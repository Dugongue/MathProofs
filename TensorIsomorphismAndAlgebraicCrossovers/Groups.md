# Polynomial-time lifting for conjugate-subgroup generation with two free elements

**Theorem.** Given the complete Cayley table of a finite group $G$, explicitly listed subgroups $H_1,\ldots,H_c$, and $r\ge 2$, one can decide in deterministic polynomial time whether
$$
G=\left\langle g_iH_i g_i^{-1}\ (1\le i\le c),\ x_1,\ldots,x_r\right\rangle
\tag{1}
$$
for some $g_i,x_j\in G$, and produce the witnesses if they exist. Fix a constant $M$. The algorithm also works for $r=1$ if every nonabelian chief factor $A$ of $G$ is simple, has order at most $M$, or has cyclic induced outer action. The last condition means that, with $A$ a minimal normal subgroup of the relevant quotient $Q_0$ of $G$, the group $Q_0/(A C_{Q_0}(A))$ is cyclic. The simple factors and the chief factors with cyclic outer action can have unbounded order.

Consequently minimum semigroup generation from an arbitrary finite Cayley table is polynomial-time Turing equivalent to the case of (1) with **exactly one free element**. It is in deterministic polynomial time when the maximal subgroups of the semigroup satisfy the stated chief-factor condition. This gives a larger polynomial-time case and narrows the unrestricted form of [Collins–Grochow–Levet–Weiß Question 8.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00133#S8). We use the convention that semigroup generators are combined by nonempty products.

## 1. Quotient lifting with prescribed subgroups

Let $N\lhd G$, write $K=G/N$, and fix a tuple of witnesses for (1) in $K$. We may test its lifts without considering any other successful tuple in $K$. To see this, for $W\le G$ surjecting onto $K$, count the lift tuples whose conjugated subgroups and free elements all lie in $W$. The count is a product across slots. If the quotient conjugator in a slot changes from $a$ to $b$, left multiplication by a $w\in W$ projecting to $ba^{-1}$ bijects the allowed conjugators in that slot. In a free slot each fiber has $|W\cap N|$ allowed values. Thus the product is independent of the generating quotient tuple. Each contributing lift generates a subgroup $V\le W$ surjecting to $K$. Subtract the counts for exact generation of each proper such $V$, inducting on $|W|$, to obtain the same independence for the number of lifts generating exactly $W$. Take $W=G$. This argument extends Efrat's subgroup lifting argument to mixed subgroup and free slots. [Efrat, *Lifting of generating subgroups*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-97-03917-8).

Choose $N$ minimal normal and recurse on $K$, with trivial group as base case. A Cayley table permits polynomial-time computation of a minimal normal subgroup, by finding the smallest nontrivial normal closure of an element. If $N$ is abelian, it is an elementary abelian $p$-group. When a lift generates $J<G$ with $JN=G$, $J\cap N$ is normalized by $J$ and by $N$, so minimality makes $J$ a complement. Every lift therefore works if the extension is nonsplit, or if some $H_i$ intersects $N$ nontrivially. To test splitting, choose a table section of $K$ and solve the linear equations correcting its factor set by a cochain in $N$. If it splits and every $H_i\cap N=1$, present $G=N\rtimes K$ and write $H_i$ as the graph of a cocycle $b_i$ on its image $K_i$.

Let $k_i,y_j$ be the fixed quotient witnesses. The lift variables are $(a_i,k_i)$ and $(z_j,y_j)$, where $a_i,z_j\in N$. They fail precisely when all images lie in a complement, the graph of a cocycle $f:K\to N$. The bad lifts are the projection onto $(a_i,z_j)$ of the affine linear system
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(u v)&=f(u)+u\cdot f(v) &&(u,v\in K),\\
f(k_i h k_i^{-1})&=k_i\cdot b_i(h)+(1-k_i h k_i^{-1})\cdot a_i &&(h\in K_i),\\
f(y_j)&=z_j &&(1\le j\le r).
\end{aligned}
$$
Elimination tests if this projection covers all lifts, and otherwise produces a vector outside it. If a nonsimple nonabelian $N$ has order bounded by a fixed $M$, the earlier bounded-chief-factor algorithm applies. In brief, enumerate $B\le N$. Any $W$ with $WN=G$ and $W\cap N=B$ lies in $N_G(B)$, and its image in $N_G(B)/B$ maps isomorphically to $K$. Enumerate the at most $M^{\log_2|K|}$ lifts in $N_G(B)/B$ of a generating list of $K$ of length at most $\log_2 |K|$. Retain the generated subgroups that map isomorphically to $K$, and take their preimages. The exact-count recursion from the preceding paragraph now has polynomially many subgroups. Fixing lift slots successively and repeating the recursion recovers a witness.

## 2. A polynomial number of forbidden local tuples

Let $N$ be nonabelian minimal normal. It has the form $T^k$ for a nonabelian simple $T$. Conjugation on $N$ gives the monolithic primitive quotient
$$
\rho:G\longrightarrow L=G/C_G(N),\qquad A=\rho(N)\cong N.
$$
The whole table of $L$ and the map $\rho$ are computable in polynomial time from the table of $G$. We use the nonabelian crown construction of Lucchini–Thakkar, specifically their statements about $R_G(N)$ before Lemma 7 and their Lemmas 7–8. It gives a quotient $G/R_G(N)$ isomorphic to the crown-based power
$$
L_{\delta}=\{(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_{\delta})\in L^\delta:\ell_1A=\cdots=\ell_\delta A\},
$$
where the image of $N$ is a single normal factor $A$, which we place last. For $J\le G$ with $JN=G$, surjecting to this crown is equivalent to $J=G$. In this identification the last projection on $G$ is $\rho$ up to an isomorphism of $L$. Indeed its kernel centralizes the last $A$, and the action there is the action on $N$ since $N\cap R_G(N)=1$. In $L$ the centralizer of $A$ is trivial. The other projections kill $N$. The proof uses this quotient without having to compute $R_G(N)$. [Lucchini–Thakkar, *The minimum generating set problem*, Sections 3–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.07633).

Expand a choice of lifts into a tagged list containing, in order, the images of every labelled element of each $H_i$ under its chosen conjugation, followed by the free elements. The entire list generates the subgroup in (1). At the crown, the first $\delta-1$ coordinate lists are fixed by the prescribed quotient choices and generate $L_{\delta-1}$ jointly. Suppose the last list generates $L$. The subgroup generated in $L_\delta$ projects onto $L_{\delta-1}$ with kernel contained in the last $A$. The last projection is surjective, so this kernel is normal in the last $L$. It is therefore either $A$ or trivial.

In the first case the generated subgroup is the full $L_\delta$. In the second case it is the graph of a surjective map $L_{\delta-1}\to L$ inducing the identity on the common quotient $L/A$. When $\delta\ge2$, the map sends each normal coordinate factor $A$ of $L_{\delta-1}$ to either $1$ or $A$. At least one factor maps onto $A$, because killing every factor would give an image of size at most $|L/A|$. Since different factors commute and $A$ is nonabelian, exactly one maps onto $A$. The map thus factors through the corresponding projection to $L$ as an automorphism inducing the identity on $L/A$. This means the last list is an automorphic image of one of the fixed earlier lists under
$$
\Gamma=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(L):\alpha\text{ induces the identity on }L/A\}.
$$
When $\delta=1$, every last list generating $L$ works. Thus at most $(\delta-1)|\Gamma|$ distinct generating lists in the last coordinate can fail to lift to a generating list of $G$.

For clarity, a coarse polynomial bound on $|\Gamma|$ suffices. Restriction to $A$ is injective because $A$ has trivial centralizer in $L$. The permutation of its $k$ simple factors induced by $\alpha$ must centralize the transitive permutation action of $L$ on those factors: $\alpha(\ell)$ and $\ell$ differ by a member of $A$, which acts trivially on the set of factors. The centralizer of a transitive action has size at most $k$ since the image of a single point determines a commuting permutation. For a fixed such permutation $\sigma$, specify the map from the first factor to its image. There are at most $|\operatorname{Aut}(T)|\le |T|^2$ choices, using two-generation of $T$. The outer class of the isomorphism on every other factor is then determined. In fact choose $\ell\in L$ carrying the first factor to that factor and compare $\alpha\circ c_\ell=c_{\alpha(\ell)}\circ\alpha$ on the first factor, where $c_\ell$ denotes conjugation. Modulo inner automorphisms of a factor, the maps induced by $\alpha(\ell)$ and $\ell$ are equal. Each other factor therefore allows at most $|T|$ choices. This gives $|\Gamma|\le k |T|^{k+1}\le |A|^3$. Finally $\delta\le\lfloor\log_2 |G|\rfloor$. The bound on forbidden lists is at most $|N|^3\lfloor\log_2|G|\rfloor$.

## 3. Enumerating enough generating lists in polynomial time

For each subgroup slot, enumerate the $|N|$ possible lifts of its quotient conjugator, compute the resulting tagged block in $L$, and remove duplicate blocks within the slot. Keep one original lift per block. For each free slot do the same for the individual values in $L$. The choices are independent across slots. Any sequence of chosen blocks and values is realizable in $G$, by using the retained lifts. All choices in a tag have the same image in $L/A$, and the tags together generate $L/A$. The first $\delta-1$ lists are constant, even when two different original lifts give the same last block. The crown criterion shows that choosing an arbitrary representative of that block preserves the success test.

For $r\ge2$, reserve the last two free slots. Every partial choice made before those slots can be completed to a list generating $L$. Choose arbitrary values for any other remaining slots. Write $d$ for the total number of expanded tags. Then $d\ge2$ and $d\ge d(L/A)$, where $d(X)$ is the minimum generating-set size of a group. By the monolithic generation theorem used by Lucchini–Thakkar, $d(L)=\max\{2,d(L/A)\}$ for a nonabelian monolith $A$. Their Lemma 5 at $t=2$ supplies generating completions by varying precisely the two remaining free entries inside their prescribed $A$-cosets. Its application treats the already chosen individual tags as an arbitrary fixed list. After all other choices are fixed, scan the at most $|A|^2$ pairs and use table generation tests in $L$. This gives an exact test of whether a prefix can extend to a generating list.

For $r=1$ and simple $N$, reserve the one free slot. Completion of a prefix is possible exactly when a nonidentity element can appear among the other tags. Indeed if all other tags are forced to the identity, one element cannot generate the nonabelian $L$. Otherwise choose those tags with a nonidentity entry $x$, and let the free slot have coset $Ay$. The prescribed-coset generation theorem gives an $s\in A$ for which $\langle x,sy\rangle=\langle A,x,y\rangle$. Together with the other tags this generates $L$, as they generate $L/A$. Testing for an available nonidentity tag in the remaining finite block lists is polynomial, and the final slot is scanned directly. The precise prescribed-coset theorem used here is attributed to Burness, Guralnick, and Harper in the introduction of Fulman, Garzoni, and Guralnick. We use the exact existence theorem for all almost simple groups. [Fulman–Garzoni–Guralnick, *Probabilistic generation of finite almost simple groups*, Section 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17291).

The same one-free prefix criterion holds for composite $N$ if $Q=L/A$ is cyclic. Write bars for images in $Q$. Let $X$ be the subgroup generated by the other chosen tags and assume $X\ne1$. Choose $x\in X$ whose image generates $B=\overline X$, taking any nonidentity $x$ when $B=1$. The monolithic group $L$ has only cyclic proper quotients. Burness–Guralnick–Harper therefore supply $w$ with $\langle x,w\rangle=L$. Both $\bar w B$ and the target $\bar y B$ generate $Q/B$. If $d=|Q/B|$, pick $e_0$ coprime to $d$ so that $\bar w^{e_0}B=\bar yB$. Since $d$ divides $|w|$, the Chinese remainder theorem permits $e\equiv e_0\pmod d$ coprime to $|w|$, by asking $e\equiv1$ modulo each prime divisor of $|w|$ that does not divide $d$. Then $w^e$ generates $\langle w\rangle$, and some $b$ gives $w^e x^b A=yA$. Replacing $w$ by $w^e x^b$ retains generation with $x$. We use only existence and scan the final coset as above. For example this clause permits $T^k$ of unbounded order acted on by a cyclic shift of its $k$ factors. [Burness–Guralnick–Harper, *The spread of a finite group*, Theorem 1](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2021/193-2/p05).

In either case depth-first enumeration of the slot lists with these exact prefix tests has polynomial delay: before the next output, each level scans at most its polynomial number of branches, and a feasible branch contains some output. Enumerate until success, exhaustion, or one more output than the bound on forbidden lists. For each output recover its original lifts and test generation in $G$. The bound proves that the cap cannot cause a false negative. Exhaustion after no success proves that the fixed quotient witnesses have no lift, and Section 1 proves the same for every quotient witness. All subgroup closures and projections use explicit tables. There are at most $\log_2 |G|$ recursive layers. For witness output the running time is polynomial in the table and list lengths and $r$. Under binary encoding of $r$, a decision algorithm can immediately accept when $r>\lceil\log_2|G|\rceil$, since repeated doubling gives enough free generators.

## 4. Consequences for minimum semigroup generation

Apply the earlier classwise reduction for ordinary semigroup generation from a Cayley table. Briefly, process Green $\mathcal J$-classes in decreasing order. Given all higher-class elements, the minimum numbers of additions in separate classes sum to the global minimum, as every factor of a word landing in a class lies at or above that class. For a nonregular class a retaining word can use at most one letter in that class, since a product of two members of the class lies strictly below it. Reachability under higher-class left and right actions yields the necessary source-component representatives after accounting for elements already generated by higher classes.

For a regular class, the Rees matrix coordinates give a labelled directed graph on the row and column vertices. Its sandwich arcs go from column to row, higher-class multipliers act on the row and column vertices, and elements already generated by higher classes supply row-to-column arcs. A selected class generator adds one arbitrary labelled row-to-column arc. The minimal number for the class is
$$
\max\{a,c-1+r_{\min}\},
$$
where $a$ is the minimum number of arcs making the base graph strongly connected, $c$ is its number of weak components, and $r_{\min}$ is the minimum $r$ in (1) for the component closed-walk groups. To see the label term, use a spanning tree in each weak component and $c-1$ new arcs to join them. Their labels independently conjugate the component closed-walk groups. Each remaining added arc supplies one free generator when labels are normalized along the resulting spanning tree. For a strongly connected directed graph allowing inverse-labelled reversed arcs preserves the group of closed-walk labels. Hence generation of the whole structural group is the remaining condition. The unlabelled graph needs the maximum of its numbers of source and sink strong components, unless it is already strongly connected. This optimum is realizable by the usual strong-connectivity augmentation since every sink component has a row vertex and every source component has a column vertex. In the earlier formula this graph includes all actions from higher classes. Gray established the corresponding Rees-matrix formula in isolation and explicitly identified the optimization over independent subgroup conjugators. [Earlier classwise proof](../FurtherPortfolioIsomorphismCrossovers/Groups.md) · [Gray, *The minimal number of generators of a finite semigroup*, Theorem 7 and Section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1307.8280) · [Eswaran–Tarjan, *Augmentation problems*](https://doi.org/10.1137/0205044).

If $c\ge2$ then $a\ge c$, so the formula only needs $\max\{1,r_{\min}\}$. If $c=1$ then $r_{\min}=0$ exactly when the single component group is the whole structural group, which can be checked directly. All thresholds $r\ge2$ now have polynomial-time decision and search by the theorem. Thus one-free decision suffices for unrestricted semigroup minimum-generation decision. In the stated chief-factor class we also construct every needed one-free witness. When $c\ge2$ and the rank at threshold $1$ is achieved with $r_{\min}=0$, the extra labelled arc permitted by the formula can carry the identity, so a threshold-$1$ witness still suffices.

One-free decision also suffices for the search problem on arbitrary input groups. To test feasibility after fixing a prefix of the conjugators, let $F$ be the subgroup generated by the corresponding fixed conjugated subgroups. Replace those slots by a single freely conjugatable slot for $F$. Any witness of the resulting instance can be conjugated globally to undo the conjugation of $F$, preserving its fixed role and leaving the remaining conjugators and free element available. Thus polynomially many oracle tests fix all original conjugators successively. Scan $G$ for the free element at the end. This extends the semigroup reduction to constructing an actual optimal generating set with the same decision oracle.

For the converse, use the earlier weighted partial-bijection construction with the supplied subgroups $H_i\le G$, padding the list with trivial ones to make $c\ge2$. Give every core point $i$ a private duplicate $i'$. The semigroup has the empty map, all weighted single arrows between core points with weights in $G$, and weighted partial identities with support $\{i,i'\}$, with weights from $H_i$ at $i$ and identity at $i'$. Weights multiply along composed paths. The top classes are the groups $H_i$; the next class is the single-arrow class, and the empty map is generated by incompatible arrows. The single-arrow base graph has $c$ weak components, each with one sandwich arc and multiplier loops carrying exactly $H_i$, so $a=c$. Its contribution is $c-1+\max\{1,r_{\min}\}$. Each top class contributes $\max\{1,d(H_i)\}$, computable in polynomial time by Lucchini–Thakkar. Comparing the whole minimum to the sum of top-class contributions plus $c$ decides the one-free problem. The constructed table has polynomial size. [Earlier reduction](../FurtherPortfolioIsomorphismCrossovers/Groups.md) · [Lucchini–Thakkar, *The minimum generating set problem*, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07633).

## Scope and novelty

The imported generation results provide crown structure and existence of free coset completions. The new algorithm uses capped enumeration of tagged subgroup choices. This handles arbitrary lengths of the subgroup list and gives deterministic search in the two-free case over unrestricted finite Cayley groups. It also covers the one-free case for simple chief factors and for unbounded composite chief factors with cyclic outer action. The ordinary unrestricted semigroup question reduces to the one-free case, where unbounded composite chief factors with noncyclic outer action remain untreated. A targeted literature check found the cited group minimum generation, subgroup lifting, and semigroup rank proofs. Publication priority for the constrained algorithm remains unestablished. The proof is informal.
