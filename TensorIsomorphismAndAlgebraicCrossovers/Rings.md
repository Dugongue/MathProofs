# Scheme-exact tensor isomorphism over arbitrary commutative rings

## A linear-size compiler to associative unital multiplication tables

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $T,T'$ be two bilinear maps $U\times V\to W$ on finite free modules of positive ranks $a,b,c$. Put $s=a+b+c$. There is an affine-linear objectwise map to multiplication tensors $\Phi(T),\Phi(T')$ of side $s+3$, using only table entries and constants $0,1$. As affine $R$-schemes their ordinary three-mode transporter is
$$
\operatorname{Trans}(\Phi(T),\Phi(T'))
\simeq
\operatorname{Trans}(T,T')\times \mathbb G_m^6 \times \mathbb A^{3s}.
\tag{1}
$$
All maps commute with arbitrary scalar extension. For $T'=T$, projection onto the source stabilizer is a split group homomorphism with smooth, geometrically connected kernel. The output multiplication is unital and associative. Its commutator ideal is a direct summand, its cube vanishes, and its quotient is $R^3$. The output tensor has an invertible contraction given by the unit in either input mode and is concise in all three modes.

View $T$ as a tensor with two dual input slots and one output slot. Thus its transporters are triples of invertible maps $(P,Q,H)$ with $H T(x,y)=T'(Px,Qy)$. Inverting and transposing the two appropriate mode matrices gives the standard three independent covariant action, with no change to the underlying scheme.

Build $A(T)$ with diagonal basis $e_1,e_2,e_3$ of orthogonal idempotents, and three direct summand spaces
$$
e_1 A e_2=U,\qquad e_2 A e_3=V,\qquad e_1 A e_3=W.
$$
The diagonal idempotents act by the indicated Peirce positions. The sole possibly nonzero product of two elements of $J=U\oplus V\oplus W$ is $uv=T(u,v)$. All products of three elements of $J$ vanish under either parenthesization. Associativity involving an idempotent follows from the indicated positions, so checking homogeneous triples establishes associativity, with unit $e_1+e_2+e_3$. The ideal generated by all commutators equals $J$, since the quotient is commutative and every $x\in e_i A e_j$ with $i\ne j$ is $[e_i,x]$. This identity also holds over every test $R$-algebra $S$.

An $S$-algebra isomorphism $F:A(T)_S\to A(T')_S$ sends $J$ to $J'$. Its quotient automorphism of $S^3$ permutes the three factors on each clopen piece of $\operatorname{Spec} S$. To recover that permutation on a piece, put $f_i=F(e_i)$ and write $\pi$ for the quotient permutation. Then $z=\sum_i e_{\pi(i)} f_i$ belongs to $1+J'$, and $z f_i=e_{\pi(i)}z$. It is invertible by nilpotence. Conjugating by $z$ sends the actual idempotents to the standard permuted ones. Reduce this normalized map to every residue field of $S$. The directed pattern of nonzero Peirce spaces between different vertices is the strict total order $1<2<3$, since $a,b,c>0$. Its only automorphism is the identity. Hence the quotient permutation is the identity on every piece and $z=\sum_i e_i f_i$ works globally. This normalizing $z$ is unique, because $1+J'$ has trivial intersection with the centralizer of all $e_i$.

The normalized isomorphisms fix the $e_i$ and act independently as $P,Q,H$ on their three Peirce spaces. Their sole extra equation is $H T(x,y)=T'(Px,Qy)$. Conversely every such triple extends to a normalized algebra isomorphism. Composing it with inner conjugation by $z^{-1}$ gives the full set of isomorphisms. Via $z\mapsto z-1$, the normalizer parameter is $\mathbb A^s$; all inverses and the reconstruction commute with every $S$. For automorphisms the idempotent-fixing subgroup is a section and the conjugations from $1+J$ form a normal subgroup. The recovery map to the transporter part is therefore a split homomorphism on stabilizers.

Now take $\Phi(T)$ to be the multiplication tensor of $A(T)$. For any associative unital algebras on finite free $S$-modules an ordinary transporter of their multiplication tensors is described by invertible $X,Y,Z$ with
$$
  Z(xy)=X(x)Y(y).
$$
Writing $u=X(1)$, $v=Y(1)$ gives $Z=L_uY=R_vX$, so the two respective multiplication operators are invertible. An element with invertible left multiplication is a unit, since $ub=1$ for some $b$ and $u(bu-1)=0$ gives $bu=1$; the right-handed argument proves the same for $v$. The map $M(x)=u^{-1}Z(x)v^{-1}$ is an algebra isomorphism, as seen by substituting $X=L_uM,\ Y=R_vM,\ Z=L_uR_vM$ in the equation. Conversely every algebra isomorphism and pair of units specifies exactly one such triple. These formulas commute with scalar extension and are regular on the stated invertible loci.

A unit of $A(T')_S$ has three invertible diagonal components and an arbitrary element of $J'_S$. Indeed its diagonal must be invertible modulo $J'$, and factoring out that diagonal leaves $1+J'_S$. Thus its parameter scheme is $\mathbb G_m^3\times\mathbb A^s$. Two unit factors and the idempotent normalizer give (1). Projection onto $M$ on the multiplication stabilizer is a split homomorphism: composition of the maps $L_u M$, $R_v M$, and $L_uR_vM$ multiplies their respective units after applying the appropriate $M$, and composes the $M$'s. The pointwise quotient of the complete projection is the displayed product of tori and affine spaces, so its kernel has the claimed geometric properties. The unit input contracts the multiplication tensor to the identity of the other input. If a vector contracted into either input annihilates all products, multiplying it by the unit shows it is zero. The image in the output mode contains the whole algebra, by multiplying by the unit. These facts prove the asserted nondegeneracy.

For any explicitly presented binary ring arithmetic, constructing the array is polynomial in the input and ring-entry bit lengths, since the construction only copies coefficients and inserts zero and one. Over finite $R$ the transporter count changes by exactly $|R^\times|^6|R|^{3s}$, with a witness projection in polynomial time in the same model. Zero-dimensional sides give trivial source equivalence between arrays of common shape; they can be handled separately in the decision reduction. Formula (1) is stated for positive side lengths.

## Determinant-controlled transport

For the same compiler, existence of a source transporter satisfying
$\det P\det Q\det H=1$ is equivalent, over every test ring, to existence of a target multiplication transporter with determinant one in every mode. This gives a linear-size reduction of the specified determinant character to the independent special-linear problem on associative unital multiplication tensors, over arbitrary commutative rings. In the usual three covariant source factors the character has exponents $(-1,-1,1)$.

The determinant of inner conjugation by $z\in1+J'$ is 1: it is the identity on each associated graded quotient for the finite filtration by path distance, whose summands are free. Specifically $[J',A]\subset J'$, $[J',J']\subset W'$, and $J' W'=W' J'=0$. In the adapted basis the matrix is block triangular with identity blocks. A normalized algebra map has determinant $d=\det P\det Q\det H$. Write $\ell=\det L_u$ and $r=\det R_v$. The determinants of $X,Y,Z$ are $\ell d,\ r d,\ \ell r d$, so their first two divided by their third gives $d$. If all three are 1 then $d=1$. If $d=1$, choose $u=v=1$ to obtain a determinant-one triple. Passing from the input-dual convention to ordinary mode matrices inverts input determinants and leaves the condition of being individually one unchanged.

## Realizing group schemes and torsors over every field

**Theorem.** Let $k$ be any field, and let $G$ be a finite-type affine group scheme over $k$. For every fpqc $G$-torsor $X$, some pair of ordinary three-tensors over $k$ has transporter scheme isomorphic to $X$ times a product of affine spaces, multiplicative groups, and general linear groups. The tensors are multiplication tables of finite-dimensional unital associative algebras. Taking $X=G$, the projection to $G$ is a split homomorphism on the resulting tensor stabilizer, with a smooth geometrically connected kernel. The statement includes nonreduced group schemes and inseparably split torsors.

### A subspace with the prescribed stabilizer

Choose a closed embedding $G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}(U)$ with $U$ finite-dimensional. Existence for affine group schemes of finite type over a field follows from a finite-dimensional stable subspace of the regular representation containing algebra generators. Pad $U$ by a trivial coordinate if necessary so that its dimension is positive. Write $I$ for the ideal of $G$ in the coordinate ring $k[x_{ij},\det(x)^{-1}]$. Multiplying a finite generating list by determinant powers gives generators in the polynomial subring itself. Take $d\geq1$ at least their greatest degree. Right translation by $\operatorname{GL}(U)$ acts linearly on the polynomials $P_{\leq d}$ of total degree at most $d$ in the $x_{ij}$. The stabilizer of $I\cap P_{\leq d}$ for this action is $G$, with equality of $S$-points for every commutative $k$-algebra $S$. Indeed, the intersection commutes with extension to $S$ by flatness over the field. A translation preserving the subspace preserves the localized ideal it generates, and evaluating the translated relations at the identity shows that the translating matrix is in $G(S)$. Conversely any $g\in G(S)$ acts by an automorphism of the closed subscheme $G_S$ and therefore preserves this subspace.

As a representation, $P_{\leq d}$ is the direct sum of the symmetric powers up to $d$ of $r=\dim U$ copies of $U$, from the $r$ rows of the coordinate matrix. The sum of corresponding tensor powers surjects onto it. Taking the inverse image of the subspace preserves exactly the same transporter condition, since this surjection has a $k$-linear section and remains surjective over each test ring. Put $W=k e\oplus U$. The sum of tensor powers embeds equivariantly as a direct summand of $W^{\otimes m}$ with $m=rd$: partition its positions into $d$ strings of length $r$, mark the row label at each of the first $j$ strings by occupying that position with $U$, and use $ke$ in all other positions. Distinct row words and lengths give distinct direct summands. This gives an explicit subspace $C\subset W^{\otimes m}$ with scheme stabilizer $G$ under $h\mapsto 1\oplus h$. This is the polynomial right-translation proof of the Chevalley stabilizer mechanism. [Milne, Sections 2.1–2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.05708v3#S2).

The $G$-torsor $X$ twists $U$ to a vector space $U_X$ of the same dimension and twists $C$ to a subspace of $(ke\oplus U_X)^{\otimes m}$. Choose a $k$-basis of $U_X$, and let $C'$ be the resulting subspace of $W^{\otimes m}$. The scheme of maps $h\in\operatorname{GL}(U)$ taking $C$ to $C'$ is the torsor $X$. Indeed $x\in X(S)$ produces the map $u\mapsto [x,u]$ on the twisted module. After a faithfully flat trivializing cover, this map identifies the two torsors with $G$, so it is an isomorphism by faithfully flat descent. This reasoning does not assume a separable splitting field.

### Encoding marked subspaces in multiplication

The following construction works over any commutative ring when the marked subspaces come with bases and are free direct summands. Use an oriented chain with vertices $0,\ldots,m$ and a copy of $W$ as the arrow space from $i$ to $i+1$. Before marking, the Peirce space from $i$ to $j$ is $W^{\otimes(j-i)}$ for $i<j$, and multiplication concatenates paths.

For each mark $M\subset W^{\otimes(j-i)}$ introduce a fresh vertex $v_M$ and arrow spaces $R a_M$ from $i$ to $v_M$ and a copy of $M$ from $v_M$ to $j$. In the path algebra impose the relation
$$
a_M b_M(w)=\iota_M(w)\qquad (w\in M)
$$
where $b_M(w)$ is the arrow labelled by $w$ and the right hand side is its specified image in the chain. These are finitely many relations from a basis of $M$. Omit a zero-rank mark. The vertices can be ordered with each detour vertex strictly between its endpoints, so the quiver remains acyclic. Replacing an adjacent detour pair by the corresponding linear combination of core paths decreases the number of detour edges. Two different detour pairs never share an edge. Substitution introduces only core arrows and preserves the endpoints, so it creates no new detour pair and commutes with substitutions at different pairs. Hence the paths without detour pairs form a free basis of the quotient: independently eliminate every internal detour pair in each path, and extend the resulting unique normal form by linearity. A product with a relation has zero normal form, since the detour can be eliminated first, and any path equals its normal form modulo the ideal. In particular the core interval spaces remain the stated tensor powers, and the two arrow spaces incident with a detour in the indicated Peirce positions remain the original rank-one space and the marked-space copy.

To make every vertex intrinsic, append to each existing vertex $v$ a private sink $\widehat v$ with an arrow space of assigned positive rank $r_v$ from $v$ to $\widehat v$, where all $r_v$ are different. Make all products of off-diagonal elements involving a private sink arrow zero. This is an associative extension: the new arrow spaces are square-zero bimodules, with left action through the diagonal at $v$ and right action through the new sink idempotent. In the Peirce support of the enlarged algebra the private sinks are exactly the vertices with no outgoing off-diagonal component. A private sink has precisely one incoming Peirce space; its rank labels the owner and the sink. Thus any quotient-permuting algebra isomorphism must fix each label after reducing to residue fields and normalizing the idempotents. The canonical commutator ideal is all off-diagonal spaces, by the same commutator formula as in the three-vertex construction. It is nilpotent by acyclicity. The same $z=\sum_i e_{\pi(i)}F(e_i)$ normalizer argument applies and is unique in $1+J$.

The normalized maps on chain arrows are $g_i\in\operatorname{GL}(W)$ for $0\leq i<m$. On the two detour arrow spaces their maps are one scalar unit $u_M$ on $a_M$ and the restriction of $g_i\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{j-1}$ to $M$, divided by $u_M$, on $b_M(M)$. The relation gives exactly this formula and forces the chain map to carry $M$ to the corresponding target mark. Conversely these data give homomorphisms preserving every relation and possessing inverse data; private sink arrow maps are independent general linear factors. This describes the full normalized transporter, because these arrows and the vertex idempotents generate the algebra.

### Synchronizing the chain

Mark the summands $ke$ and $U$ on the first arrow. Mark the flip-fixed tensors in $W\otimes W$ on every two consecutive arrows. This fixed submodule has the split basis $e_i\otimes e_i$ and $e_i\otimes e_j+e_j\otimes e_i$ for $i<j$, over any coefficient ring and in characteristic two. If $g\otimes h$ preserves it, so does $1\otimes g^{-1}h$, since $g^{-1}\otimes g^{-1}$ preserves it. Examining the first kind of basis vector shows that $g^{-1}h$ is diagonal. Examining the second kind equates all diagonal entries. The common entry is a unit because $g^{-1}h$ is invertible. Conversely scalar proportionality suffices. The two first-arrow marks make $g_0$ block diagonal. All chain arrow maps are therefore of the form $\lambda_i(1\oplus h)$, with one common $h\in\operatorname{GL}(U)$ and freely chosen scalar units $\lambda_i$. This holds also for $m=1$, when pair marks are unnecessary.

Add a mark $C$ across the full chain, or $C'$ on the target. The condition on this mark is precisely that $(1\oplus h)^{\otimes m}$ take $C$ to $C'$, since multiplying by an overall unit leaves the submodule unchanged. The preceding explicit parameterizations give the normalized algebra transporter as $X$ times the split tori of the chain and detour scalars and the general linear factors of the private sink arrows. Removing the normalization adds $\mathbb A^{\dim J}$. Multiplication tensor isotopy adds two unit factors of the algebra, each a product of a split torus and $\mathbb A^{\dim J}$ because the diagonal quotient is split. All descriptions are natural on test rings and prove the isomorphism of schemes. If the two inputs agree, the normalized maps on the arrows are multiplicative in the common $h$, and fixing the remaining parameters at their identity values defines a group section. The extra factors are smooth and geometrically connected. This completes the theorem.

### An explicit congruence consequence

For every prime $p$, there is a fixed side length depending only on $p$ with the following property. For every positive $N$ prime to $p$, two tensors over $\mathbb F_p[[t]]$ of that length are isomorphic modulo $t^j$ for every $j\leq N$, fail to be isomorphic modulo $t^j$ for every $j>N$, and are geometrically isomorphic over the fraction field. Their residual tensor stabilizers are nonreduced. In the marked-chain construction use $U=R x$, $m=p$, and the source and target lines spanned by $e^{\otimes p}+x^{\otimes p}$ and $e^{\otimes p}+(1+t^N)x^{\otimes p}$, respectively. They are free direct summands. The exact transporter equation on $u\in\mathbb G_m$ is $u^p=1+t^N$, by comparing the $e^{\otimes p}$ coefficients. It is solvable below or at the stated cutoff by $u=1$ and impossible above it since every $p$-th power has zero coefficient at $t^N$. Over an algebraic closure the equation has a root. At $t=0$ the distinguished stabilizer quotient is $\mu_p$, so the exact product description shows that its nonreduced scheme structure appears in the full tensor stabilizer.

## Scope and sources

The three-vertex map reuses the Peirce and multiplication-isotopy mechanism of [the earlier characteristic-zero torsor construction](../IsomorphismAndProofComplexityCrossovers/torsors.md). Using the commutator ideal instead of the Jacobson radical makes the normalization exact over nilpotent and mixed-characteristic coefficient rings. Grochow, Qiao and collaborators give a broader list of decision reductions for free tensors over rings with the diagonal-full property, and for general modules over finite local principal ideal rings. The present specific associative-multiplication compiler works over arbitrary commutative rings, uses linear side length, and retains the transporter scheme and its nonreduced information. [Part V, Theorems 1.4–1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S1.SS3).

Milne realizes affine group schemes as automorphism groups of bilinear algebras over fields with at least eight elements. The marked-chain construction uses his underlying stabilizer mechanism and replaces eigenvalue marks by intrinsic associative Peirce spaces. It works over the smallest finite fields and concerns the independent three-mode action. Its conclusion retains a smooth split kernel. The dimensions in the group-scheme realization depend on the degree and dimension of a chosen linear realization of $G$; the linear side-length claim concerns the preceding compiler for an input three-tensor. [Milne, Theorems 1 and 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.05708v3).

This is an informal proof. The closest literature reviewed here does not establish publication priority.
