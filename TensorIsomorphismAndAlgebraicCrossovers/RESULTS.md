# Four crossovers in tensor isomorphism and algebraic algorithms

These are informal proofs. The literature checks in the manuscripts identify the known inputs to each proof. Publication priority is unestablished.

| Result | Main theorem |
| --- | --- |
| [Tensor transporters over rings and fields](Rings.md) | A linear-side construction over every commutative ring preserves free three-tensor transporters up to explicit split factors and produces associative unital multiplication tensors. Over every field, every torsor for an affine group scheme of finite type occurs as a tensor transporter up to smooth factors. The construction includes nonreduced schemes and gives fixed-size examples with arbitrarily late failures of congruence lifting. |
| [Semigroup generation and one free element](Groups.md) | For every Cayley-table group, generation by independently conjugated subgroups and at least two free elements has a deterministic polynomial-time search algorithm. With one free element, the algorithm covers bounded nonabelian chief factors, simple factors of arbitrary order, and composite factors on which the induced outer action is cyclic. General Cayley-table semigroup minimum generation is polynomial-time Turing equivalent to exactly the one-free-element decision problem. |
| [Tensor-isomorphism completeness of cubic bent equivalence](BentEquivalence.md) | Linear, affine, extended-affine, and graph equivalence of scalar cubic bent functions in algebraic normal form have the many-one degree of binary tensor isomorphism. Hardness holds on the identity-permutation Maiorana–McFarland family. The extended-affine result covers vectorial cubic bent functions at every fixed output dimension, using same-characteristic field transfer. The coded addition-design comparison is in the succinct representation. |
| [Output-sensitive exact roots of ordered branching programs](Circuits.md) | Given a width cap and a supplied variable order, decide exact root existence and reconstruct a minimum-width root without a perfect-power promise. The algorithm takes deterministic polynomial bit time over the rationals for binary exponents, and over explicitly represented finite fields for fixed exponents for the root-up-to-scalar problem. |

The semigroup theorem extends the polynomial-time cases and reduces the unrestricted part of [Collins–Grochow–Levet–Weiß Question 8.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00133#S8) to an explicit one-free-element group problem. The root theorem gives an algorithm with an output-width cap. The structural question of a general polynomial upper bound on root width remains open.
