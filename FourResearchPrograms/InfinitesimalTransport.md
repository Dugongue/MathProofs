# Infinitesimal tensor stabilizers

Two tensors can have the same stabilizer point counts over every finite field extension and over every sufficiently shallow truncated polynomial algebra. The first distinguishing nilpotent order and the first field extension admitting a transporter are quantitative invariants. This program studies their dependence on the rank and support of an ordinary three-tensor.

For a positive-characteristic field $k$ and an affine group scheme $G/k$, define its geometric Frobenius height $h_p(G)$ as the least $h\ge0$ such that $f^{p^h}=0$ for each nilpotent $f$ of $\mathcal O(G_{\bar k})$. This measures an elementwise exponent. For geometrically isomorphic $k$-tensors $T,T'$, define their splitting degree as the least degree of a finite field extension of $k$ admitting a transporter. We prove sharp bounds for rank-one networks, transfer the examples to ordinary tensors, and give a construction in which the stabilizer recovers integral homology.

## Sharp bounds for networks of lines

A network in this theorem has $n$ named unpinned one-dimensional spaces, the ground-field line with its action fixed, and a finite list of marked vectors, linear maps and bilinear maps among these spaces. The two input networks have the same format. The slots of a record may repeat. Write $x_i$ for the automorphism scalar on unpinned line $i$, write $e_i$ for its exponent vector, and assign $e_0=0$ to the fixed line. A nonzero record of a bilinear map with inputs $i,j$ and output $\ell$ imposes the stabilizer character $x^{e_i+e_j-e_\ell}=1$. Linear maps and vectors yield the corresponding two-slot or one-slot rows. Let $L\subseteq\mathbb Z^n$ be the lattice generated by these rows, and put $r=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z} L$.

**Theorem.** The stabilizer is the diagonalizable group scheme $D(\mathbb Z^n/L)$, with the identification valid on every commutative $k$-algebra. The order of the torsion of $\mathbb Z^n/L$ is at most $2^r$. In characteristic $p>0$ its Frobenius height is at most $\lfloor r\log_p 2\rfloor$. Every geometrically positive transporter of two such networks has a splitting field of degree at most $2^r$. The characteristic-two height bound and the splitting degree bound are attained for every $n=r$.

The group-scheme identification follows by giving $D(M)$ the functor $S\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(M,S^\times)$. It also follows directly from the Laurent ring presentation. To show the bound on the torsion, we prove that each square minor of order $s$ of the row matrix has absolute determinant at most $2^s$. A bilinear row is the average of $2e_i-e_\ell$ and $2e_j-e_\ell$, still after restricting to the chosen columns. By multilinearity the determinant is an average of determinants in which every row has at most two nonzero entries, all of magnitude at most two, with opposite signs if there are two. The permitted linear and vector rows already have this property.

For each resulting matrix, construct a graph on its columns. A row supported at two columns gives an edge, and a row supported at one column gives a mark at that vertex. A zero row makes the determinant zero. Each component must have as many rows as columns for the determinant to be nonzero, since a determinant term matches every row to an incident column. In a connected component on $v$ vertices there are at least $v-1$ edges. Thus a balanced component is either a tree with one mark or an unicyclic multigraph with no marks. In the tree case orient the tree toward the mark. Each leaf away from the marked vertex occurs in only one row and column expansion removes it. The determinant magnitude is bounded by $2^v$. In the unicyclic case first remove off-cycle leaves. The remaining cycle has exactly two determinant terms, including for a cycle of two parallel edges. Each switches the chosen endpoint on every row when compared with the other. The coefficient-sign ratio for a cycle of length $a$ is $(-1)^a$, and the permutation-sign ratio is $(-1)^{a-1}$. The terms have opposite signs. Their absolute difference is bounded by $2^a$, and the removed factors have magnitude at most two each. Multiplying across components proves the bound on all minors. The product of the nonzero Smith invariant factors $d_i$ is the gcd of the $r$-minors and therefore at most $2^r$, with product one for $r=0$.

Smith coordinates identify the group scheme with a split torus times the $\mu_{d_i}$. Over an algebraic closure in characteristic $p$, $\mu_{d_i}$ has height $v_p(d_i)$. Their product has height the maximum of these values. Indeed the nilradical is generated by the respective nilpotent coordinates after splitting the prime-to-$p$ components, and the $p^h$-power map is additive and multiplicative. This gives the claimed height.

For a geometrically positive comparison, a zero record agrees with a zero record and each nonzero pair gives an equation $x^{a}=c$ for a ratio $c\in k^\times$. Geometric solvability means that every integer relation on the rows gives a true multiplicative relation on their ratios. Hence the ratios define a character of $L$. Diagonalize the lattice by integral basis changes. Solve the resulting equations $y_i^{d_i}=c_i$, adjoining one root at a time. The product of the degrees is bounded by $\prod_i d_i\le2^r$. The Smith form and ratio equations also give a concrete procedure for obtaining the residual torus and finite factors.

## Exact splitting degrees and late distinguishing jets for ordinary tensors

Take $n$ lines with $n-1$ unit-coefficient bilinear maps to impose $x_{i+1}=x_i^2$. The last bilinear map from line $n$ twice to the fixed line gives $x_n^2=t$ when its source and target coefficients have ratio $t$. The transporter scheme is exactly
$$
\operatorname{Spec} k[u,u^{-1}]/(u^{2^n}-t),
\qquad x_i=u^{2^{i-1}}.
$$
For $t=1$ this gives the stabilizer $\mu_{2^n}$. For $t=2$ over $\mathbb Q$, Eisenstein at 2 gives splitting degree exactly $2^n$. Over $\mathbb F_2(t)$, $t$-adic Eisenstein gives the same degree and the root extension is purely inseparable.

Use the prior exact transporter compiler on these networks. The compiler gives an ordinary multiplication three-tensor and identifies its transporter scheme with the source scheme times a known smooth product of affine spaces, tori and general linear groups. The smooth factor has a rational point given by the identity choices. Thus the least extension degree is unchanged, and in characteristic two the height of the compiled stabilizer is exactly $n$. The construction gives concise tensors of side and tensor rank $O(n)$. For the rank assertion, use Sections 2 and 3 of the compiler. Each reference and occurrence in the typed bilinear map has rank one. Each data, copy, evaluation and private scalar block contributes at most one nonzero coefficient. There are $O(n)$ such blocks. The projection marks each have a one-dimensional target, so there are $O(n)$ projection coefficients. The strict corners together have dimension $O(n)$, including the sentinel and paths. Their left and right diagonal actions give at most twice that many coefficients. Each diagonal vertex contributes one more. These account for the whole multiplication table, with $O(n)$ nonzero coefficients. Each coefficient is a rank-one term, and evaluation at the algebra unit makes all three flattenings concise. The rational splitting example uses coefficients in $\{0,1,2\}$. [Linear compiler, Sections 2–3](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/d6933df162f8e998f27e007a42b8b6e55bb4858b/HigherOrderUniversality/RingUniversality.md) · [Exact transporter formula](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/d6933df162f8e998f27e007a42b8b6e55bb4858b/UniversalityConsequences/ExactTransporterSchemes.md)

For a same-format jet comparison use $m+1$ lines and all $m$ squaring relations. Mark each of lines $m$ and $m+1$ by a separate bilinear record with values in the fixed line. In one network those two records have coefficients $1,0$, and in the other they have coefficients $0,1$. A zero record imposes no restriction on a stabilizer. The resulting stabilizers are $\mu_{2^m}$ and $\mu_{2^{m+1}}$, respectively. The compiler formats, including all smooth factors, agree. In characteristic two, over every test $k$-algebra with nilradical $N$ satisfying $N^{2^m}=0$, both root-of-unity functors consist exactly of $1+N$. They first differ along the standard jets over $k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2^m+1})$, where $1+\epsilon$ belongs to the second and fails the first equation. More explicitly, over a finite $k$ and $S=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^q)$, the root-of-unity factor $\mu_{2^m}(S)$ has cardinality $|k|^{q-\lceil q/2^m\rceil}$. Indeed $1+a$ belongs to it precisely when $a^{2^m}=0$, or equivalently when the coefficients of $a$ in degrees less than $\lceil q/2^m\rceil$ vanish. Hence the compiled stabilizer counts agree through $q=2^m$ and differ at $q=2^m+1$. The comparison of complete stabilizers is a natural bijection of pointed scheme functors on the indicated test algebras, via the fixed smooth-factor coordinates. The abstract group law on the full smooth extension and the embeddings into the ambient product of general linear groups are additional data.

For comparison, there is a general quadratic-in-rank height upper bound. Let $r_i$ be the three flattening ranks of a tensor over $k$. In bases adapted to the supports every stabilizer, over every test algebra, maps each support into itself. To prove this from the tensor equation, contract along the other two modes using their inverse witnesses and apply a constant right inverse of the supported flattening. The same argument for the inverse gives two-sided invertibility on the support. Thus the full stabilizer is, as a scheme, the stabilizer on the compressed format times general linear groups on the complements and affine spaces for the off-diagonal blocks. These smooth factors preserve the height after geometric base change. There are $M=2\sum_i r_i^2$ matrix entries and named inverse entries in the compressed stabilizer equations. Adjoin one equation $z-1=0$ and replace each nonzero generator $f$ of degree $b\le3$ by $z^{3-b}f$. This presents the same affine coordinate ring with all generator degrees equal to 1 or 3. Kollár's effective radical membership bound gives for each radical element some exponent at most $3^{M+1}$ taking it into the ideal. Hence
$$
h_p\bigl(\operatorname{Stab}(T)\bigr)
 \le \bigl\lceil (2\textstyle\sum_i r_i^2+1)\log_p3\bigr\rceil.
$$
In particular, $r_i\le b$ for geometric border rank $b$. The linear lower bound in border rank follows from the sparse concise examples above, where the side is at least the number of rank-one input lines of the compiler. [Kollár, Sharp Effective Nullstellensatz, Corollary 1.7](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-1988-0944576-7)

## A topological realization using thin multiplication

For a finite poset $P$, the incidence algebra has basis elements $u_{xy}$ for $x\le y$, with $u_{xy}u_{yz}=u_{xz}$ and all noncomposable products zero. Write $\Delta P$ for the order complex. Fixing the primitive diagonal idempotents $u_{xx}$ forces a unit scalar $s_{xy}$ on each strict corner. Multiplication imposes $s_{xy}s_{yz}=s_{xz}$. Thus the group scheme fixing the diagonal idempotents is
$$
D\bigl(C_1(\Delta P;\mathbb Z)/\operatorname{im}\partial_2\bigr).
$$
The short exact sequence with kernel $H_1(\Delta P;\mathbb Z)$ and quotient $\operatorname{im}\partial_1$ splits because its quotient is free. The torsion of the character group is therefore exactly the torsion of $H_1(\Delta P;\mathbb Z)$.

This determines the height of the bare tensor stabilizer as well. The strict upper ideal $J$ is intrinsic as the commutator ideal, with quotient the split diagonal algebra. A quotient automorphism is a locally constant permutation of the vertices, which must preserve the order by the corner ranks after reducing at any maximal ideal. For the identity permutation, any new orthogonal lifts $f_x$ of the idempotents align with the original ones by the unit $\sum_x f_xu_{xx}$ in $1+J$, since $f_x(\sum_y f_yu_{yy})=(\sum_y f_yu_{yy})u_{xx}$. This unit is unique in $1+J$ as the strict corners have zero diagonal entries. Every order permutation supplies a section by permuting the basis. These operations identify the automorphism scheme with the displayed diagonalizable group times affine space and the finite constant scheme of order permutations, as schemes. The stabilized multiplication tensor adds two unit-group factors by the unital associative isotopy normalization. Each unit group is a torus times the affine space for $J$, as a scheme. Consequently the exponent of the $p$-primary torsion in $H_1(\Delta P;\mathbb Z)$ is exactly $p^{h_p}$. The field-point decomposition into multiplicative, inner and poset automorphisms is classical. The scheme calculation here uses all test algebras and extracts its quantitative height. [Baclawski, Automorphisms and derivations of incidence algebras](https://ken.baclawski.org/pub/math.html) · [Drozd and Kolesnik, Automorphisms of incidence algebras](https://www.imath.kiev.ua/~drozd/Kolesnik.pdf)

To realize height $n$ in characteristic two, build a two-dimensional simplicial complex $K_n$ with $O(n)$ simplices and $H_1(K_n;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/2^n$. Start with a wedge of $n$ three-edge circles labelled $a_i$, and attach disks along the words $a_i^2a_{i+1}^{-1}$ and $a_n^2$. For an explicit simplicial model of each attachment, take a separate $6$- or $9$-edge top circle, form the mapping cylinder to the indicated edge path, and cone the top circle from a fresh vertex. Each domain edge maps to a genuine graph edge. For each oriented edge $v\to w$ in the top circle, triangulate its mapping cylinder strip with the diagonal from the image of $v$ in the graph to $w$ at the top. These strips share their vertical edges and attach along the graph edges as specified. This uses a bounded number of simplices per relation. The abelian relation matrix is triangular with diagonal entries 2 and successive off-diagonal entries $-1$. Its cokernel is the cyclic group of order $2^n$, and it is injective, so $H_2(K_n;\mathbb Z)=0$. Let $P_n$ be the nonempty face poset of $K_n$. Its order complex is the barycentric subdivision. Its incidence algebra dimension counts comparable pairs and its number of nonzero tensor coefficients counts weak three-element chains. Each chain has one maximal simplex, which has at most seven nonempty faces. Both counts are $O(n)$. This supplies the lower bound entirely within the thin incidence-algebra case.

The same face poset gives an arithmetic example with a single changed multiplication coefficient. Multiply the coefficient of a composable strict pair $u_{xy}u_{yz}$ by a unit $c_{xyz}$. The resulting table is associative for arbitrary choices of the $c_{xyz}$. Four strictly increasing elements would be needed for a nontrivial associativity constraint, and no such chain exists in $P_n$. Comparing the untwisted and twisted algebras on a field extension reduces to the equations $s_{xy}s_{yz}/s_{xz}=c_{xyz}^{\pm1}$, with orientation fixed by the direction of the map. An order permutation seen in a comparison can be removed by precomposing with a permutation automorphism of the untwisted source. Normalizing the idempotents and the unital isotopy therefore preserves the exact splitting criterion for the corresponding ordinary tensors.

The boundary map $\partial_2$ of $\Delta P_n$ is injective because $H_2=0$, and the torsion of its cokernel is cyclic of order $2^n$. In its Smith form the sole nontrivial invariant factor is $2^n$. The domain basis vector corresponding to this factor is primitive, so at least one of its coefficients in the triangle basis is odd. Twist only that triangle by 2 over $\mathbb Q$. After Smith coordinates the only nontrivial root equation is $y^{2^n}=2^a$ for an odd integer $a$, up to inverting the coefficient. Since $a$ is coprime to $2^n$, Bézout gives a $2^n$-th root of 2 from any such $y$, and conversely. Eisenstein again gives least splitting degree $2^n$. Taking the unit $t$ over $\mathbb F_2(t)$ gives the purely inseparable variant. Thus the arithmetic lower bounds also hold for thin multiplication tensors of rank $O(n)$, with the tensor support fixed across the pair.

## Research questions and verification

The sharp network bound leaves a gap between linear and quadratic Frobenius height in the geometric border rank of ordinary three-tensors. Intrinsic flags, centroid decompositions and the determinant-frame methods give distinct classes on which to seek sharper upper bounds or compute height on larger families. The relation with first homology suggests studying which finite abelian groups can occur for a prescribed tensor support size and which twisted multiplication tables realize all their arithmetic forms. The jet-count examples provide benchmarks for algorithms relying on bounded-order infinitesimal transport.

The proofs are written. The sharp character-lattice estimate and its application to quantitative tensor stabilizers are the new claims here. Smith form, diagonalizable groups, classical incidence-algebra automorphisms, and the prior tensor compiler supply the stated inputs. Publication priority of the quantitative results has not been established.
