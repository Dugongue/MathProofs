# Tensor isomorphism inside derived equivalence of three-simple algebras

**Theorem.** Over every field $k$, tensor isomorphism has a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction to derived equivalence of split basic quasi-hereditary finite-dimensional $k$-algebras of global dimension exactly three. The output is an explicit dense multiplication table. Each output has three simple modules, each with endomorphism field $k$. For each input tensor format all output algebras have the same Cartan matrix. In this family two algebras are derived equivalent if and only if they are isomorphic, and equivalently if their centers are isomorphic. Thus the image family is TI-complete as a promised comparison problem. Derived equivalence means exact $k$-linear equivalence of bounded derived categories of finite-dimensional modules. This is an informal proof.

The key construction places a commutative tensor encoding in the center of an Auslander–Dlab–Ringel algebra. The larger algebra has bounded global dimension, and its center is preserved by derived equivalence.

## 1. A commutative local algebra retaining the tensor relation

Write the tensor as $A(z):V\to U$, linear in $z\in Z$. Take three copies of the matrix pair $U,V^\vee$ and three copies of the vector pair $k,Z^*$. Pad the twelve blocks $E_1,\ldots,E_{12}$ to distinct dimensions $N+1,\ldots,N+12$, with $N\ge\max(2,\dim U,\dim V,\dim Z)$, and write $E^*=\bigoplus_iE_i$. In the mixed left-right terms of $\operatorname{Sym}^2 E^*$ take the space $B_A(Z)$ where $B_A(z)$ consists of the three copies of $A(z)$ and three copies of the universal row $Z^*\to k,\ w\mapsto w(z)$, each in its own pair of blocks. Set
$$
S_A=\bigoplus_i\operatorname{Sym}^2 E_i\ \oplus\ B_A(Z).
$$
Its dimension $r$ is fixed by the format, since the row summands make the last map injective. Write $m=\dim E$. Here $r>m\ge5$. All squares of coordinate variables occur in $S_A$, so it is basepoint-free.

For a nonzero $\ell\in E^*$, the space $C(\ell)=\{\ell':\ell\ell'\in S_A\}$ recovers the blocks. The left-right contribution of a product has matrix rank at most two, whereas every nonzero $B_A(z)$ has rank at least three from the three universal rows. Therefore all cross-block terms of a product in $S_A$ vanish. If $\ell\in E_i$, it follows that $C(\ell)=E_i$. If $\ell$ has nonzero components in two or more blocks, the two-block equations force every corresponding component of $\ell'$ to be proportional to that of $\ell$, with opposite proportionality scalars on each occupied block pair. They force all components outside the support to vanish. Thus $\dim C(\ell)\le1$. The irreducible linear components of the geometric locus $\dim C(\ell)\ge2$ are exactly the projective blocks, distinguished by dimension. Hence a congruence $S_A\to S_{A'}$ preserves the named blocks and induces a common change of slice parameter via $B_A(Z)\to B_{A'}(Z)$. The first matrix pair then supplies a source tensor transporter. To remove the padding, restrict its target map to the sum of all slice images and its source map to the quotient by the common kernel. The resulting isomorphisms extend to the original unpadded spaces by choosing complements. Conversely any source transporter extends to the padded matrix pairs and universal row pairs with the same substitution on $Z$.


Let $Q_A=\operatorname{Sym}^2E^*/S_A$. Set $B_A=k\oplus E^*\oplus Q_A$, with $k$ acting by scalars and multiplication on the positive part given by the quotient map on products of two elements of $E^*$. All positive-degree products involving $Q_A$ are zero. This gives an associative commutative local algebra since a triple product of positive-degree elements is zero. Its radical $J=E^*\oplus Q_A$ has square $Q_A$ and cube zero. The square is nonzero since some two blocks are unmatched, so their entire mixed product space survives in $Q_A$. Consequently $B_A$ has Loewy length three.

The intrinsic multiplication
$$
\operatorname{Sym}^2(J/J^2)\longrightarrow J^2
$$
has kernel exactly $S_A$. Each algebra isomorphism induces a congruence of these kernels, and conversely such a congruence directly induces an isomorphism of the graded algebras $B_A$. The block recovery above proves that $B_A\simeq B_{A'}$ over $k$ exactly when the tensors are isomorphic. This argument works in characteristic two since it uses the symmetric product in the commutative polynomial algebra, without division by $2$. Moreover the socle of $B_A$ as a module over itself is exactly $J^2$. Indeed any additional nonzero socle vector modulo $J^2$ would be a linear form $\ell$ such that $C(\ell)=E^*$. The block calculation rules this out.

## 2. An algebra with the same center and global dimension at most three

Write $U_i=B_A/J^i$ for $1\le i\le3$. Thus $U_3=B_A$. Let $M=U_1\oplus U_2\oplus U_3$ and set $\Gamma_A=\operatorname{End}_{B_A}(M)$. This is the Auslander–Dlab–Ringel algebra of $B_A$. A finite-dimensional $k$-algebra is an artin algebra, and its regular module is semilocal. The radical length here is three, so the usual ADR theorem gives quasi-heredity and global dimension at most three. [Conde on the quasi-hereditary structure of ADR algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.07923) · [Tsukamoto, Corollary 2.8](https://arxiv.org/html/1805.08085v2#S2).

For clarity, the heredity chain can also be read directly. Evaluation on $1$ identifies $\operatorname{Hom}_{B_A}(U_i,U_j)$ with $\operatorname{Ann}_{U_j}(J^i)$. Quotient by the ideal of maps factoring through $U_1,\ldots,U_{s-1}$. At the remaining vertices $i,j\ge s$ the quotient Hom space is
$$
\operatorname{Ann}_{U_j}(J^i)/\operatorname{Ann}_{U_j}(J^{s-1}).
$$
Indeed any map through an earlier $U_t$ has image of $1$ annihilated by $J^{s-1}$, and each element in that annihilator is obtained by using the natural quotient $U_i\to U_{s-1}$ followed by the map with the given image. The formula for $s=1$ uses $\operatorname{Ann}_{U_j}(B_A)=0$. The corner at $s$ is $k$ since every element of $J$ annihilates $J^{s-1}/J^s$ and no unit does. At this stage each Hom into $U_s$ has a one-dimensional quotient basis given by the canonical projection $U_i\to U_s$. Composing through this basis arrow identifies morphisms out of $U_s$ with their images in the corresponding block of the factor-through ideal. Hence multiplication identifies the factor-through ideal with $\Gamma e_s\otimes_k e_s\Gamma$ in the current quotient $\Gamma$. It is idempotent, projective on each side, and $e_s\operatorname{rad}(\Gamma)e_s=0$. Removing $s=1,2,3$ is a heredity chain. The cited dimension bound sharpens the bound four from the three-step generic heredity estimate.

The center of $\Gamma_A$ is isomorphic as a $k$-algebra to $B_A$. Any central endomorphism commutes with the summand projections, so it is block diagonal. On $U_3=B_A$ it acts by multiplication by some $b\in B_A$. For every element $u\in U_i$, the homomorphism $B_A\to U_i,\ x\mapsto xu$ is a block of $\Gamma_A$. Commuting with this block shows that the endomorphism sends $u$ to $bu$. Conversely multiplication by $b$ on every summand commutes with all homomorphisms since $B_A$ is commutative. This proves the identification of centers.


The three $U_i$ are pairwise nonisomorphic indecomposables because they have distinct Loewy lengths and their endomorphism rings are the local rings $U_i$. Each residue division algebra is $k$. Krull–Schmidt then gives that $\Gamma_A$ is split basic with exactly three simple modules. Put $d=\dim Q_A$. Using the rows for the source $U_i$ and columns for the target $U_j$, the Hom dimension matrix is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & m & d\\
1 & m+1 & m+d\\
1 & m+1 & m+d+1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
To verify the only off-diagonal entries that do not immediately come from a full target quotient, note that $\operatorname{Ann}_{U_2}(J)$ is the radical of $U_2$, $\operatorname{Ann}_{B_A}J=J^2$, and $\operatorname{Ann}_{B_A}J^2=J$. The formula proves the common Cartan matrix claim, up to the transpose convention for right and left modules.

In fact these outputs have global dimension exactly three. First $J$ cannot be a summand of copies of $M$. If it were, its Loewy length at most two and Krull–Schmidt would express it as $U_2^b\oplus U_1^a$. The socle of $J$ is $J^2$, which is also its module radical. No $U_1$ summand then occurs. The top of $J$ has dimension $m$, so $b=m$. This would force $d=m^2$, while $d\le\binom{m+1}{2}<m^2$. By Tsukamoto's Theorem 3.1, global dimension is therefore unequal to two. It is at least two because the kernel of the top map on $P_1=\operatorname{Hom}_{B_A}(M,U_1)$ cannot be projective. Indeed, as a projective kernel it would be $\operatorname{Hom}_{B_A}(M,N)$ for some $N\in\operatorname{add}(M)$, and the inclusion into $P_1$ would come from a nonzero map $N\to U_1$ by the fully faithful equivalence from $\operatorname{add}(M)$ to the projective right endomorphism-algebra modules. The map $N\to U_1$ would be injective since $M$ has the regular module as a summand. As $U_1$ is simple it would be an isomorphism, contradicting that the kernel is proper. The kernel is nonzero because the quotient $U_2\to U_1$ is an off-diagonal homomorphism in it. This proves that the global dimension is three. [Tsukamoto, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1805.08085v2#S3).

## 3. Derived equivalence and the complexity reductions

For finite-dimensional algebras over a field, $k$-linear derived equivalence preserves the Hochschild cohomology ring and in particular its degree-zero piece, the center. In our setting a bounded derived equivalence is already an equivalence on perfect complexes because of finite global dimension. Rickard's derived Morita theorem then supplies a standard derived equivalence from the associated tilting complex, to which the Hochschild invariance applies. Thus any exact $k$-linear triangulated equivalence in the decision problem forces a $k$-algebra isomorphism of the centers. No dg enhancement is supplied as input. [Rickard](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s2-39.3.436) · [Keller](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~bernhard.keller/publ/dih.pdf).

If $\Gamma_A,\Gamma_{A'}$ are derived equivalent, their centers are isomorphic, so $B_A\simeq B_{A'}$ and the source tensors are isomorphic by Section 1. Conversely any isomorphism $B_A\simeq B_{A'}$ carries radical powers and their quotient modules to one another and transports the whole endomorphism algebra. Hence the three equivalence tests coincide on the family.

The quotient space $Q_A$ and multiplication table of $B_A$ are computed by Gaussian elimination, with dimension $O(N^2)$. The summands $U_i$ are cyclic. Each Hom space embeds by evaluation in its target, so $\dim\Gamma_A\le9\dim B_A=O(N^2)$. Compute a basis of each Hom space by linear equations for its possible values on $1$. The composition of maps is found by applying the second map to the first map's value on $1$, and expressing the result in the computed basis. This yields the dense table with polynomially many field operations, also with polynomial bit cost in the usual finite field and rational or number-field presentations. Shape checks on the input pair precede the construction. The forward equivalence reduction does not need to solve an algebra isomorphism problem.

On the image family, a reverse reduction computes each center by linear equations and sends it to its ordinary multiplication tensor. For unital associative $B,B'$, any invertible linear maps $P,Q,R$ with $R(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$ imply that $B,B'$ are isomorphic. Indeed write $a=P(1)$, $b=Q(1)$. Then $R(x)=P(x)b=aQ(x)$. The surjectivity of these maps gives inverses for the left multiplication by $a$ and right multiplication by $b$, so $a,b$ are units. The map $x\mapsto a^{-1}R(x)b^{-1}$ is a unital algebra isomorphism, as direct substitution in the isotopy identity shows. The ordinary tensor equivalence acts precisely this way, with contragredients on the two input modes. This gives the claimed reverse reduction on promised inputs. [Grochow and Qiao on algebra isomorphism and TI](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309).

The construction commutes with extending the base field since the tensor data, the quotient and the linear equations defining Hom spaces do. Thus a tensor pair becomes equivalent at exactly the same field extensions as its derived-algebra pair. This transfers existing failures of local-to-global and descent to dense three-simple algebras. The ADR construction and invariance of centers are classical. The contribution is their combination with the exact tensor encoding, including the explicit multiplication tables and the constant three simples and global dimension.
