# Tensor isomorphism inside derived equivalence of directed algebras

**Theorem.** Over every field $k$, ordinary ordered three-tensor isomorphism reduces to $k$-linear derived equivalence of finite-dimensional split basic algebras of finite global dimension. The algebras are given by explicit acyclic quivers and lists of relations of path lengths two and three. For each input tensor format the quiver, all quadratic relations, and the entire graded dimension matrix are fixed. Only coefficients of cubic relations vary with the input tensor. The construction has polynomial time and output length in the explicit tensor encoding. Equivalence of bounded derived categories of finite-dimensional right modules means exact $k$-linear triangulated equivalence. Even $k$-linear equivalence of the underlying graded categories gives the same test on this family.

An isomorphism of input tensors induces an algebra isomorphism of the outputs. Thus ordinary algebra isomorphism and derived equivalence coincide on this image. The algebra presentation uses arrows and relations. Its dimension, and hence the size of its dense multiplication table, may grow exponentially. This is an informal proof.

## 1. From tensors to Fano incidence hypersurfaces

Write the tensor as $A(z):V\to U$, linear in $z\in Z$. Take three copies of the matrix pair $U,V^\vee$ and three copies of the vector pair $k,Z^*$. Pad the twelve blocks $E_1,\ldots,E_{12}$ to distinct dimensions $N+1,\ldots,N+12$, with $N\ge\max(2,\dim U,\dim V,\dim Z)$, and write $E^*=\bigoplus_iE_i$. In the mixed left-right terms of $\operatorname{Sym}^2 E^*$ take the space $B_A(Z)$ where $B_A(z)$ consists of the three copies of $A(z)$ and three copies of the universal row $Z^*\to k,\ w\mapsto w(z)$, each in its own pair of blocks. Set
$$
S_A=\bigoplus_i\operatorname{Sym}^2 E_i\ \oplus\ B_A(Z).
$$
Its dimension $r$ is fixed by the format, since the row summands make the last map injective. Write $m=\dim E$. Here $r>m\ge5$. All squares of coordinate variables occur in $S_A$, so it is basepoint-free.

For a nonzero $\ell\in E^*$, the space $C(\ell)=\{\ell':\ell\ell'\in S_A\}$ recovers the blocks. The left-right contribution of a product has matrix rank at most two, whereas every nonzero $B_A(z)$ has rank at least three from the three universal rows. Therefore all cross-block terms of a product in $S_A$ vanish. If $\ell\in E_i$, it follows that $C(\ell)=E_i$. If $\ell$ has nonzero components in two or more blocks, the two-block equations force every corresponding component of $\ell'$ to be proportional to that of $\ell$, with opposite proportionality scalars on each occupied block pair. They force all components outside the support to vanish. Thus $\dim C(\ell)\le1$. The irreducible linear components of the geometric locus $\dim C(\ell)\ge2$ are exactly the projective blocks, distinguished by dimension. Hence a congruence $S_A\to S_{A'}$ preserves the named blocks and induces a common change of slice parameter via $B_A(Z)\to B_{A'}(Z)$. The first matrix pair then supplies a source tensor transporter. To remove the padding, restrict its target map to the sum of all slice images and its source map to the quotient by the common kernel. The resulting isomorphisms extend to the original unpadded spaces by choosing complements. Conversely any source transporter extends to the padded matrix pairs and universal row pairs with the same substitution on $Z$.

Let
$$
X_A=\{(x,[q])\in\mathbf P(E)\times\mathbf P(S_A):q(x)=0\}.
$$
The equation $F_A$ has bidegree $(2,1)$. The first projection is the projective bundle of lines in the kernel of $S_A\otimes\mathcal O\to\mathcal O(2)$, surjective by basepoint freeness. Thus $X_A$ is smooth and geometrically integral. Its Picard group has basis $H_x,H_y$, the restrictions of the ambient hyperplane classes. They generate the nef cone: they are globally generated, a line in a fiber of the first projection tests the $H_y$-coefficient, and a curve in a geometric fiber of the second projection tests the $H_x$-coefficient. The latter fibers are quadrics in $\mathbf P^{m-1}$ and contain an integral curve over the algebraic closure. The two primitive ray generators have $m,r$ sections, respectively, by the divisor restriction sequences and projective-space cohomology. These numbers differ, so every abstract isomorphism preserves both classes. Their complete linear systems are the given embeddings into the two factors. Thus an isomorphism gives ambient projectivities carrying $F_A$ to $F_{A'}$ up to scalar, and induces precisely a congruence between the coefficient spaces $S_A,S_{A'}$. Conversely such a congruence yields an isomorphism. By adjunction $-K_{X_A}=(m-2)H_x+(r-1)H_y$ is ample. In particular $X_A$ is Fano. This recalls the hypotheses of the earlier MathProofs proof, *Tensor isomorphism in a smooth rational Fano family of Picard rank two*.


## 2. A full tilting bundle over the original field

Let $L_{A,b}=\mathcal O_{X}(A,b)$ and index the following direct sum by the integer strip
$$
\mathcal V=\{(A,b):0\le b\le r-2,\quad 2b\le A\le 2b+m-1\},\qquad
\mathcal T_X=\bigoplus_{(A,b)\in\mathcal V}L_{A,b}.
$$
For the projective bundle over $\mathbf P^{m-1}$, the bundles $\pi^*D^b(\mathbf P^{m-1})\otimes\mathcal O_X(0,b)$ for $0\le b\le r-2$ generate. To see the field-uniform generation, on the relative square of a projective bundle of rank $e$ take the tautological line in factor two and the tautological quotient in factor one. The map from this line to the quotient vanishes on the relative diagonal, and locally its $e-1$ equations are a regular sequence defining that diagonal. The Koszul resolution, followed by integral transformation from factor one to factor two, expresses each object in the subcategory generated by the pullbacks from the base tensored by $\mathcal O(-j)$, $0\le j\le e-1$. In this calculation flat base change identifies the pushforward of the pullback of an object on factor one with the pullback of its pushforward to the base. Tensoring the generating subcategory by $\mathcal O(e-1)$ gives the positive twists. The same argument for projective space over $k$ gives generation by any $m$ consecutive line bundles. For each fixed $b$, our $m$ summands are just such a collection tensored by the $b$-th relative twist. Thus the displayed direct sum generates $D^b(\operatorname{coh} X)$. [Orlov's projective-bundle formula](https://homepage.mi-ras.ru/~orlov/papers/1992_Izv_Math.pdf).

Write $u=A'-A$, $v=b'-b$ for a pair of strip vertices. Apply cohomology to the divisor restriction sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O(u-2,v-1)\xrightarrow{F_A}
\mathcal O(u,v)\longrightarrow\mathcal O_X(u,v)\longrightarrow0
$$
on the ambient product. If $v<0$, each of $v,v-1$ is a strictly negative twist above $-r$, so both ambient terms are acyclic in every degree. If $v=0$, the kernel has fiber twist $-1$ and is acyclic, while $u\ge1-m$, so the middle term has no higher cohomology. If $v>0$, then $u\ge3-m$ and $u-2\ge1-m$. Both ambient terms have nonnegative twists on $\mathbf P^{r-1}$ and no higher cohomology on $\mathbf P^{m-1}$. Therefore every higher $\operatorname{Ext}$ between summands vanishes. If $S=k[x_1,\ldots,x_m,y_1,\ldots,y_r]$ is bigraded by the two groups of variables, every ordinary Hom space is precisely
$$
\operatorname{Hom}(L_{A,b},L_{A',b'}) =
S_{u,v}/F_A S_{u-2,v-1}.
$$
Negative indices denote zero, and the identifications send composition to polynomial multiplication. This uses the surjectivity on $H^0$ from the same restriction sequences.

## 3. A polynomial presentation with quadratic and cubic relations

Use the common strip $\mathcal V$ as vertices. Put one arrow $x_i$ for each $i$ from $(A,b)$ to $(A+1,b)$ when both ends lie in the strip. Put one arrow $y_j$ for each $j$ from $(A,b)$ to $(A,b+1)$ when both ends lie in the strip. A path is a word in these two types of labeled arrows. The labels multiply into a monomial. First impose all differences of parallel paths of length two carrying the same monomial. We claim the component from $(A,b)$ to $(A',b')$ becomes $S_{u,v}$.

For comparable endpoints, let $h=A-2b$ be the current height. An $x$ increases height by one, and a $y$ decreases it by two. Heights lie in $[0,m-1]$. A legal path of any specified monomial is constructed by taking each next $y$ as early as possible, inserting just enough $x$'s to make the height at least two. To reach the next row the required first coordinate is at most $A'$, since the final row lies no lower in $b$ and has minimum first coordinate $2b'$. After the last $y$, use the remaining $x$'s. Hence paths exist for all monomials.

Every path can be changed to this greedy pattern by swapping an adjacent $xy$ to $yx$ each time the height before the $x$ is at least two. Both intermediate vertices are then legal. More explicitly, slide the first $y$ left past every excess $x$ before it, and repeat on each suffix. There remain no unnecessary $x$'s before any $y$. The types now occur in one fixed pattern. Their labels can also be sorted using quadratic relations. Neighboring $y$ slots are separated by at most two $x$'s. If there are $s>0$ separating $x$'s, the earlier $y$ starts at height $4-s$. Move those $s$ $x$'s before the earlier $y$, using legal commutation squares, until the two $y$'s are adjacent. The maximum intermediate height is four, allowed since $m\ge5$. Swap the two labels and reverse the pattern moves. Consecutive $x$ slots in the greedy pattern are either adjacent or separated by one $y$. This follows because once the pattern first needs $x$'s to enable a $y$, that $y$ resets height to zero, and each later $y$ needs two $x$'s. In the separated case the intervening $y$ starts at height two. Move the following $x$ to just before it, with intermediate height three, swap the adjacent $x$ labels, and reverse the pattern move. Label sorting does not change the other type's relative label order. Thus all words with the same monomial are identified. Words with distinct monomials remain independent, since the polynomial multiplication map annihilates the imposed differences. This proves the claim.


For each strip pair $(A,b)\to(A+2,b+1)$, choose a path for every monomial of bidegree $(2,1)$ and impose the linear combination of those length-three paths with the coefficients of $F_A$. The end vertex is in the strip whenever $b+1\le r-2$. Once the quadratic relations hold, the choice of representatives is immaterial. In any component with $u\ge2,v\ge1$, the pair $(A,b)\to(A+2,b+1)$ lies before the target in coordinatewise order, and each monomial of bidegree $(u-2,v-1)$ has a representing path from there to the target. Thus in this component the cubic relations generate exactly $F_A S_{u-2,v-1}$; the reverse inclusion holds because each cubic relation is $F_A$ times the local copy of the multiplicative unit. In other components both vanish. This yields precisely the displayed Hom spaces and their composition, so the resulting algebra is $\operatorname{End}_{X}(\mathcal T_X)$.

The quiver has $m(r-1)$ vertices and at most $m+r$ arrows out of any vertex. Each quadratic relation enumeration compares paths of length two. There are at most $m(r-1)(m+r)^2$ such paths to sort by endpoints and monomial, so listing all parallel differences against a representative takes polynomial size. There are at most $m(r-1)$ cubic linear combinations, each with at most $r\binom{m+1}{2}$ terms. The algorithm uses $m=O(N)$ and $r=O(N^2)$. Each coefficient is copied from the incidence equation or is $0,1,-1$. Building the quiver and its relations has polynomial cost in the explicit tensor length. The path relation ideal lies in the square of the arrow ideal and contains a power of the arrow ideal, since the quiver is acyclic. Consequently the algebra is split basic, and the displayed quiver is its Gabriel quiver.

The radical is spanned by positive-length paths. Order the vertices so each arrow strictly increases the order. The radical of an indecomposable projective has composition factors only at strictly earlier vertices or only at strictly later vertices, depending on the convention for path multiplication. Induction along the corresponding ordering gives a finite projective resolution for each simple module. Finite composition series then give finite global dimension. At fixed endpoints every element is in path degree $u+v$. The dimension of its component is
$$
\dim S_{u,v}-\dim S_{u-2,v-1},
$$
because multiplication by the nonzero polynomial $F_A$ in a polynomial ring is injective. Therefore the graded dimension matrix and in particular the ordinary Cartan matrix depend only on the tensor format.

## 4. Derived equivalence reflects the tensor relation

For the algebra $\Lambda_A$ above, the functor $R\operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathcal T_X,-)$ to right $\Lambda_A$-modules is fully faithful on the summands of $\mathcal T_X$ and all their shifts. In degree zero it identifies their Hom spaces with those of the corresponding projectives. In other degrees both sides vanish. Triangles and direct summands extend full faithfulness to their generated category, which is all $D^b(\operatorname{coh} X_A)$ by Section 2. Its image contains every indecomposable projective, so it is $D^b(\mathrm{mod}\text{-}\Lambda_A)$ by finite global dimension. This proves the tilting comparison without assuming a presentation of a dense multiplication table.

Suppose the two output algebras have $k$-linearly equivalent derived categories. The comparison just proved gives a $k$-linear graded equivalence between the perfect derived categories of the two smooth projective Fano varieties. Both varieties are connected, equidimensional, and Gorenstein. Their anticanonical sheaves are ample, so the absolute reconstruction theorem of Sancho de Salas and Sancho de Salas applies over the original field. It gives $X_A\simeq X_{A'}$ over $k$. Section 1 then gives an input tensor isomorphism. Conversely, an input isomorphism induces such an isomorphism of varieties preserving the two independent hyperplane classes. Pullback carries the indicated tilting summands to their counterparts, hence induces an algebra isomorphism and therefore a derived equivalence. [Sancho de Salas and Sancho de Salas, Theorem 1.15 and Remark 1.16](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0013091510000076).

The construction commutes with field extension. Hence every base change at which the tensors become isomorphic is exactly a base change at which the output algebras become derived equivalent. Base-field counterexamples to descent and local-to-global transfer as well. The dense algebra dimension already contains $\dim S_{m-1,0}=\binom{2m-2}{m-1}$ in a single component, so the quiver input model matters.

## Comparison and status

Quiver presentations of endomorphism algebras of tilting bundles, and reconstruction of Fano varieties from derived categories, are established techniques. The result here uses the exact tensor-to-Fano construction together with a polynomial quiver presentation whose fixed quadratic relations suffice to straighten every path and whose cubic relations contain the entire input. The closest work located on quiver GIT for tilting bundles and on presentations for Grassmannians does not supply a tensor-isomorphism reduction. Publication priority is unestablished. Federated DAG searches located the projective-bundle and related algebraic geometry source material. Exact controls checked connectivity by quadratic moves in 575 strip cases and 903,608 labeled paths. The controls cover strip heights from five through eight and words with up to seven horizontal arrows, four vertical arrows and two labels of each type. The proof is informal. [Karmazyn, quiver GIT for tilting bundles](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.5005) · [Reconstruction from Kapranov's tilting bundle](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12671).
