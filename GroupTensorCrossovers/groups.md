# Parallel construction of chief and composition series from Cayley tables

**Theorem.** Let $G$ be a finite group of order $n$ given by its Cayley table, with an arbitrary input labeling. A logspace-uniform family of polynomial-size Boolean circuits of depth $O(\log\log(n+4))$ constructs a chief series and a composition series. It outputs one input-label membership mask per subgroup, a flag for each active stage, and can give the multiplication relation of each simple section in least-original-label coordinates. A deterministic logspace transducer can also produce both series and consecutively numbered quotient tables. In the same circuit depth one can compute membership in every term of the alternating radical–socle tower.

The main group-theoretic input beyond simplicity is a normal-set compression lemma. If $X\subseteq G$ is invariant under conjugation by $\langle X\rangle$, then every element of $\langle X\rangle$ has a signed straight-line program of width at most seven and length $O((1+\log|\langle X\rangle|)^4)$ over $X\cup X^{-1}\cup\{1\}$. Thus membership in the subgroup generated by such a mask is in logspace-uniform $\mathsf{FOLL}$, with no restriction on the finite groups appearing as composition factors. Conjugation invariance is a promise about the generating mask.

## 1. Normal generating sets have bounded-width short programs

Work inside $H=\langle X\rangle$, and let $Y=X\cup X^{-1}\cup\{1\}$. This is a symmetric $H$-invariant set. The identity group is immediate. In the solvable case, Stober–Thumm–Weiß's Theorem 21 gives width four and length $O((1+\log|H|)^3)$ over $Y$. [Efficient Compression in Semigroups, Theorem 21](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04747v2#S8.SS2)

Suppose $H$ is nonsolvable. Put $R=\operatorname{Rad}(H)$, the solvable radical, $U=H/R$, and $S=\operatorname{Soc}(U)$, the product of minimal nonidentity normal subgroups. The group $U$ is radical-free, so $S$ is a nontrivial direct product of centerless nonabelian simple groups, and $C_U(S)=1$. For the last assertion, the centralizer is normal and its intersection with $S$ is $Z(S)=1$. A nontrivial centralizer would contain a minimal normal subgroup of $U$, all of which lie in $S$.

Choose $x\in X$ with nonidentity image in $U$. There is $\bar s\in S$ not commuting with $\bar x$. Pick any lift $s\in H$, and set $g=[x,s]=x^{-1}s^{-1}xs$. The image of $g$ is a nonidentity element of $S$. The lift $s$ is only used to choose the individual letter $s^{-1}xs\in X$, so the actual program for $g$ has two signed letters. Every conjugate of $g$ and every inverse of one of them is likewise a two-letter word, by conjugation invariance of $Y$.

Let $A$ be the union of these conjugates and their inverses. Let $N=\langle A\rangle\trianglelefteq H$, and let $Q$ be its image in $U$. This is a nontrivial normal subgroup of $S$, hence a product of some of its simple factors. Its kernel under the restricted map $N\to Q$ is $R(N)$: $N\cap R$ is solvable normal in $N$, and $R(N)$ is characteristic in $N$, hence solvable normal in $H$.

For each direct factor $T$ of $Q$, some $a\in\bar A$ has a nonidentity $T$-coordinate. Choose $t\in T$ that does not commute with that coordinate. Then $[a,t]$ has nontrivial support in $T$ alone, and is a word of length two in $\bar A$. All its $T$-conjugates have the same two-letter bound since $\bar A$ is invariant under $Q$-conjugation. The conjugacy class of $[a,t]$ has size at least two. Liebeck–Shalev's Theorem 1.1 expresses each element of $T$ as a product of at most $C\log_2|T|+1$ elements of this class for an absolute constant $C$. Concatenating coordinate words yields an $O(\log|Q|)$-letter $\bar A$-word for every element of $Q$. [Diameters of finite simple groups, Theorem 1.1](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/articles/11021)


Choose a lifted word $u_q$ over $A$ for every $q\in Q$, with $u_1=1$. The Schreier elements $u_q a u_{q\bar a}^{-1}$, for $a\in A$, generate $R(N)$. Indeed, along an $A$-word representing an element in the kernel, take $q$ to be the successive prefix image in $Q$. The displayed words telescope to that kernel element. Each Schreier generator has signed $X$-length $O(1+\log |Q|)$, since $A\subseteq Y^{2}$. Use the solvable width-four theorem for this generating set. A single load of a Schreier generator can be realized by its explicit signed $X$-word in two fresh registers, then copied into the load destination. The copy can be realized as multiplication by a fresh identity, reusing the other scratch register. The width becomes at most six. To obtain $h\in N$, let $q=\bar h$. Compute $u_q^{-1}h$ in that width, keep it in one register, evaluate $u_q$ in two other registers, and multiply. The resulting length is $O((1+\log |R(N)|)^3(1+\log |Q|))=O((1+\log |N|)^4)$.

The image of $Y$ in $H/N$ remains a symmetric conjugation-invariant set. Compute a width-seven program for the image of the desired target in that smaller quotient, lifting alphabet loads to $Y$. The resulting $r\in H$ is correct modulo $N$. Keep $r$ in one register and run the width-six algorithm for $r^{-1}h$ in the others. Then multiply. Width is the maximum over stages, and lengths add. The orders of the successive $N$'s and the final solvable quotient multiply to $|H|$. Omitting factors of order one gives $\sum_i(1+\log|N_i|)^4=O((1+\log|H|)^4)$. The certificates may use the correct representatives at each stage with no need for a uniform algorithm to precompute those choices.

From the Cayley table and mask, build the directed graph of width-seven register states. There are $O((n+1)^7)$ states including empty registers. Valid operations load a selected element, its inverse, or the identity, or multiply two occupied registers into a target register. All adjacency bits have constant-depth circuits, using lookup and a scan for each inverse and identity. Add self-loops. Boolean matrix squaring $O(\log\log(n+4))$ times computes all reachability within the length bound. A state containing the queried label certifies membership. The graph cannot reach an element outside the generated subgroup even when the promised mask condition fails, but the length bound uses the promise. For ordinary $\langle X\rangle$ no legal procedure here replaces $X$ by its $\langle X\rangle$-conjugates without already computing the needed subgroup. Taking conjugates under all ambient elements instead computes the corresponding normal closure. [Configuration-graph method](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00659)

## 2. Constructing a chief series without sequential subgroup queries

The algorithm can run between any supplied $N\trianglelefteq H\leq G$, represented by membership masks. For each $s\in H$ compute
$$
P_s=\langle N\cup\{h^{-1}sh:h\in H\}\rangle
$$
using the preceding circuit. Its generating mask is invariant under $H$ and hence under its generated subgroup. Every strict inclusion among the $P_s$ multiplies subgroup order by at least two. The strict inclusion graph $a\to b$ for $P_a<P_b$ therefore has paths of length at most $\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$. All edges are tested by Boolean comparisons of masks. By squaring and multiplying Boolean adjacency matrices, for each length $k\le\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$ compute which vertices end a path of exactly that length. Each fixed $k$ needs $O(\log\log(n+4))$ sequential squarings or products, and all lengths run in parallel. Let $d(s)$ be the longest length ending at $s$. Order $s\in H$ by the pair $(d(s),s)$, with input label for the second component. Strict inclusion of principal subgroups implies strictly increasing first components.

For each $s$ compute both $M_s^-$ and $M_s^+$, the joins of $N$ and the $P_t$'s strictly before $s$ or weakly before $s$, respectively. They are normal-closure queries with the union of retained membership masks as generating set, and so run in the same depth. Consider a strict increase $M_s^-<M_s^+=M_s^-P_s$. If $L<P_s$ is any proper $H$-normal subgroup containing $N$, then each $t\in L$ has $P_t\le L<P_s$. It follows that $L\le M_s^-$. In particular $P_s/(P_s\cap M_s^-)$ has no proper nontrivial $H$-invariant subgroup. Via the map to $M_s^+/M_s^-$, it is a chief factor. Removing the equality steps therefore gives a chief series from $N$ to $H$.

There are at most $\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$ strict increases. Their consecutive numbering can be obtained without a general exact counting gate. Use the increasing-order graph on strict steps, with a distinguished start point before all steps. For each prefix, the existence of a $k$-edge path confined to that prefix is equivalent to there being at least $k$ strict steps in the prefix. Compute these predicates for all $k\le\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$ by the same Boolean matrix method. Read exact ranks by differencing adjacent threshold bits. Hence the chief series has compact stage numbering and membership-mask outputs in depth $O(\log\log(n+4))$.


## 3. Refining to composition factors and recognizing their types

Take all consecutive chief terms $L<M$ in parallel. The group $M/L$ is characteristically simple, so it is either elementary abelian or a direct product of centerless nonabelian simples. Apply the algorithm of §2 with ambient $M$ and floor $L$, still using only the original table and its two membership masks. The minimal normal subgroups of an elementary abelian group are cyclic of prime order. Normal subgroups of a direct product of centerless nonabelian simple groups are products of its factors: commutating any element with a single factor isolates a nontrivial commutator in that factor when the coordinate is nontrivial. Thus all chief factors in this refinement are simple. Concatenating the refinements gives a composition series. Again there are at most $\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$ active strict steps, so sparse path counting yields consecutive stage numbers in the same depth.

For any such pair $L<M$, the membership-mask circuit tests equality of two elements in its section by $x^{-1}y\in L$, and obtains the least source label of a coset by comparing with all smaller labels at once. Products and the graph of multiplication on those representatives follow by table lookup and this equality predicate. In this circuit statement the representatives keep their original numerical labels.

There is also an explicit logspace algorithm for the two series and their consecutively numbered section tables. Use Reingold's undirected reachability on translated generating edges for the normal closures. Its adjacency is obtained from an implicit mask by recomputation. Sort $P_s$ by genuine cardinality and then by input label, computing each cardinality with a scan and an $O(\log n)$-bit counter. Cardinality strictly increases under proper inclusion, so the same proof applies. The fixed nesting of table scans and membership procedures preserves the space bound. A further scan counts the least label representatives below an index, allowing consecutively numbered section tables in logspace. [Reingold's undirected reachability theorem](https://doi.org/10.1145/1391289.1391291)

The circuit version can compare the entire multiset of composition-factor isomorphism types for two input groups in $\mathsf{FOLL}$. Babai–Kantor–Lubotzky give an absolute constant $K$ and seven generators for each nonabelian simple group, with every element a word of length at most $K\log n$ on those generators. Compare abelian simple factors by the order of a chosen nonidentity coset label. For every $1\le m\le n$ form the configuration graph of the deterministic binary powering calculation for that label and fixed exponent $m$. After reading a prefix with numeric value $e$, its state stores $e$, the bit-position counter and the current group label $u=x^e$. Reading the next bit $b$ takes the edge $(e,u)\mapsto (2e+b,u^2x^b)$, with the position advanced by one. Equality and the product use the section table. Thus the graph has polynomial size, a constant-depth adjacency predicate, and $O(\log n)$ steps. Boolean matrix squaring finds its endpoint in the same depth. Thus the least exponent yielding the identity is available by one final label scan. For nonabelian simple factors $S,T$, enumerate seven-tuples $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$ on the two sets of section representatives. For a fixed $\mathbf a$, the short-diameter property is tested by the Boolean matrix power on the $S$ Cayley graph for at most $K\log n$ steps, using the signed tuple and self-loops. For a tuple passing, run the same bounded reachability from $(1,1)$ in the product $S\times T$ with pairwise matched generators. The resulting relation is the graph of a genuine isomorphism if it is total, bijective and preserves products, all testable in constant additional depth by universal or existential label scans. Every isomorphism occurs for any fixed passing source tuple, by taking $\mathbf b$ to be its tuple image. Thus group-table equality of isomorphism types takes the same depth. Pick representatives of equivalence classes among the combined factor lists and compare multiplicities using the sparse path counting of §2, since both lengths are at most $\log n$. [Small-diameter Cayley graphs for finite simple groups](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0195-6698(89)80067-8) · [Grochow–Johnson–Levet, Theorem 9.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19777v1#S9)


## 4. Two residual predicates and the entire radical–socle tower

For a finite group $J$, let $D(J)$ be its solvable residual, the stable term of its derived series. Let $B(J)$ be its semisimple residual, the intersection of the kernels of all epimorphisms from $J$ onto nonabelian simple groups. We compute their membership masks for each normal closure $J=\langle x^G\rangle$ directly from the original table.

Every finite perfect group $P$ is normally generated by one element. To see this, take its maximal proper normal quotient maps. Their targets are nonabelian simple. The joint image in their product is subdirect, hence a product of nonabelian simples with possibly repeated diagonal coordinates. For this last fact use induction on the number of coordinates and Goursat's lemma on the final coordinate. A surjection from a product of nonabelian simple groups to one nonabelian simple group is projection onto one isomorphic simple factor followed by an isomorphism, since the factor images are commuting normal subgroups of the target. There is therefore a joint-image tuple nonidentity in every coordinate, and any preimage normally generates $P$. The trivial perfect group uses its identity. Because $D(J)$ is perfect and normal in $J$, a normal generator for it remains a normal generator inside $J$. Conversely every perfect normal subgroup of $J$ lies in $D(J)$, as its image in $J/D(J)$ is solvable and perfect. Thus $y\in D(J)$ exactly when $y$ belongs to some perfect principal normal closure in $J$. Compute all such closures and each derived subgroup by the normal-mask lemma, using the mask of commutators of elements of the closure. The membership predicate takes only a fixed number of $\mathsf{FOLL}$ operations.

To compute $B(J)$, construct the chief series of $J$ by §2. For each nonabelian chief factor $A/C$, check whether every $z\in J$ acts on $A/C$ by an inner automorphism. This means that for each $z$ there is an $a\in A$ with $zvz^{-1}C=ava^{-1}C$ for all $v\in A$. The formula uses a fixed number of element quantifiers. If all actions are inner, each simple direct factor of $A/C$ is preserved by those actions. Chief minimality implies that $A/C$ itself is nonabelian simple. Conjugation gives an epimorphism $J\to A/C$, since conjugations from $A$ already give all inner automorphisms and $A/C$ is centerless. Its kernel is the set centralizing that chief factor. Conversely, for any epimorphism $J\to T$ onto a nonabelian simple group, take the first chief term with nontrivial image. The image is all of $T$, and the preceding term maps trivially. Chief minimality implies that the induced map of the intervening chief factor to $T$ is injective. Its ambient actions are inner and the original epimorphism's kernel is exactly this factor-centralizer. Intersecting the centralizer masks computes $B(J)$.

Let $K_0=1$ and $K_i/K_{i-1}$ be the solvable radical of $G/K_{i-1}$ for odd $i$, and its socle for even $i$. Quotienting by the radical leaves trivial radical, since a solvable extension of solvable groups is solvable. Fix $x\in G$, set $J=\langle x^G\rangle$, and consider $E=G/K_{i-1}$. The image of $J$ is the normal closure of the image of $x$ in $E$. In the odd case it is solvable precisely when $D(J)\subseteq K_{i-1}$, and that is precisely when $xK_{i-1}$ lies in the radical of $E$. In the even case $E$ is radical-free. A normal subgroup of such an $E$ lies in its socle precisely when the subgroup is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups. The forward direction holds because the subgroup is normal in the socle. In the converse direction the ambient group permutes the simple direct factors of the subgroup, and the factors in each permutation orbit give a minimal nontrivial ambient normal subgroup. The image of $J$ is such a product precisely when $B(J)\subseteq K_{i-1}$: kernels of its coordinate projections give one direction, and in the other direction $J/B(J)$ is subdirect in a product of nonabelian simples, so all its quotients are products of nonabelian simples. The preceding conclusions include the trivial image and the empty product.

Thus $x\in K_i$ exactly when every label in $D(\langle x^G\rangle)$ or $B(\langle x^G\rangle)$, according to the parity of $i$, lies in $K_{i-1}$. The two source-label relations are computed just once in parallel. Make a layered directed graph with vertex $(i,x)$, an edge to $(i-1,y)$ for each indicated residual member, and self-loops for padding. The query fails precisely when there is a path from $(i,x)$ to $(0,z)$ with $z\ne 1$. Every active radical–socle round strictly decreases the order of the quotient, since either radical removal is nontrivial or the radical-free group has a nontrivial socle. Consequently $2\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$ layers suffice. Squaring the graph adjacency $O(\log\log(n+4))$ times decides all tower queries simultaneously with polynomial size. The same least-source-label comparisons give valid representatives and quotient multiplication for every level.

## 5. Uniformity and relationship to prior work

All graph indices are tuples with a fixed number of input labels, register values or counters bounded by a polynomial in $n$. The circuit constructor runs in logarithmic space and checks local wiring by scanning these indices, including the Boolean squaring layers and the root path-length schedules. Copies for different subgroup, quotient, path-length or tuple queries expand size only by fixed polynomial factors. Every dependence of one $\mathsf{FOLL}$ block on another has fixed nesting depth. The tower is the only variable-length dependency chain in the specification, and its circuit evaluates it by the single layered graph.

The original group-decomposition preprint of Chattopadhyay, Torán and Wagner gives a $\mathsf{SAC}^2$ construction with a stronger compatibility requirement between chains chosen in isomorphic groups and additional coset-representative data. The construction here selects a chain for each raw table and can select different chains under relabeling. Grochow–Kardeş–Levet's depth-$2\frac12$ isomorphism argument guesses mutually compatible composition structures. Their isomorphism witnesses still have to match the two groups. The closest bounded-width open question concerns arbitrary masks. Publication priority for the theorem remains unestablished.
