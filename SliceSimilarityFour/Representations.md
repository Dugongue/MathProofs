# Tensor isomorphism with conjugate restrictions on every small subgroup

Let $p$ be any prime, $e\ge1$, and $R=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$. Let $h\ge1$ be the prescribed number of generators allowed in a restriction test. Use a positive input tensor format $(a,b,c)$ over $k=\mathbb F_p$. The output groups come with a fixed, common abstract group and a list of matrices for its standard generators.

**Theorem.** There is a deterministic reduction from ordinary field tensor isomorphism in the declared format to conjugacy of embedded abelian $p$-subgroups of $\operatorname{SL}_N(R)$. It has the following properties.

1. The construction writes faithful maps $\rho_t:G\to\operatorname{SL}_N(R)$, with $G=(\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z)^D$ except that $G=(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^D$ when $p=2,e=1$. Here $D=3(2h+1)(a+b)$ and $N=O(D^2)$. The representations use only unitriangular matrices in a fixed coordinate order.
2. For every two inputs in that format and every subgroup $K\le G$ generated by at most $h$ elements, the two restrictions to $K$ are conjugate by one determinant-one matrix. The conjugacy respects the labels of all elements of $K$. A generator list for $K$ suffices to construct the conjugator by linear algebra.
3. Conjugacy of the full embedded images, either in $\operatorname{GL}_N(R)$ or in $\operatorname{SL}_N(R)$, is equivalent to isomorphism of the input tensors. Equivalently, full conjugacy of the homomorphisms after allowing an automorphism of their common domain is TI-complete. The full matrix conjugator recovers an input transporter.
4. If $e\ge2$, the corresponding generator matrices are identical at every smaller precision. The entire construction and the local conjugator computation are polynomial in the dense input and $h,e,\log p$.

On the $\operatorname{GL}_N(R)$-conjugacy saturation of this generator-given family, there is a polynomial upper reduction to ordinary field tensor isomorphism. No group elements have to be enumerated. For $e=1$ and odd $p$, the elementary abelian representations have constant Jordan type, including on shifted cyclic restrictions over all field extensions. [Starting slice-similarity construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/HessianFour/Tensor.md)

## 1. The quadratic space and the needed rank gap

Use the rectangular pencil of the starting construction. Given the input matrix space $M\subseteq \operatorname{Hom}(k^a,k^b)$, interpreted as the image of its $c$-dimensional slice map, put $Z=k^a\oplus k^b$, $d=a+b$. Extend $A\in M$ to $\widetilde A:Z\to Z$ taking the first summand into the second and killing the second. The rectangular space is
$$
 \mathcal B_M=\{[xI_Z+\widetilde A,\ yI_Z]:x,y\in k,\ A\in M\}.
$$
Put $m=2h+1$, $V=X\oplus Y$ with dimensions $md,2md$, and let $H_M\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2 V$ be the cross-quadratic space with coefficient matrices $B^{\oplus m}$ for $B\in\mathcal B_M$. A nonzero element of $H_M$ has cross-matrix rank at least $m$. The square monomials and all terms within either $X$ or $Y$ have zero intersection with $H_M$.

We use that $H_M$ and $H_{M'}$ are equivalent under a single change of variables precisely when the inputs are equivalent, with polynomial field witness recovery. Here is the recovery argument. Rank strictly less than $d$ in $\mathcal B_M$ picks out exactly its data matrices $[\widetilde A,0]$. Two arrow labels independent modulo this data give an invertible stacked map from $Z^2$ to $Z^2$, since the scalar stacked map is invertible and the perturbation factors through a square-zero block. Polarizing the cross quadrics gives ordered arrows that, after a coordinate permutation, are a direct sum of $m$ copies of $\mathcal B_M$'s tuple and $m$ copies of the transposed tuple. The polar rank $2m\operatorname{rank}B$ identifies the low-rank arrow space. For two labels independent modulo it, the sum of their kernels is the entire source of the rectangular copies, with zero contribution from the transposed copies. Applying either arrow to that sum gives the rectangular target. One therefore recovers the $m$-fold rectangular tuple equivalence, with the shared change of arrow labels; Krull–Schmidt cancellation gives single-copy tuple equivalence, found by the finite-field module isomorphism algorithm. Restricting to the low-rank data space and passing to its jointly supported source and target gives the original matrix-space equivalence. Extend through any kernels in the original format to recover the input tensor maps. Forward matrix-space transporters extend blockwise. This is the prior proof, with the larger replication count chosen here to absorb the quadratic terms of a group element. [Starting construction, §1](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/HessianFour/Tensor.md) · [Brooksbank–Luks, module isomorphism](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

## 2. Faithful unitriangular representations

Write $W_0=\operatorname{Sym}^2 V/H_M$, and $\pi_M$ for the quotient map. Choose common ambient dimensions
$$
 W=W_0\oplus S,\qquad
 \dim W=\dim\operatorname{Sym}^2 V+\epsilon,\qquad
 N=1+D+\dim W,
$$
where $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}$ makes $N$ prime to $p$. Inert padding $S$ has dimension at least two since $\mathcal B_M$ includes the two independent identity arrows. Put $s=p^{e-1}$ (so $s=1$ when $e=1$). Use the $R$-algebra
$$
 A_M=R1\oplus V_R\oplus W_R,\qquad
 uv=s\,\pi_M(\bar u\bar v),\qquad
 W_R(V_R\oplus W_R)=0.
$$
Here $V_R,W_R$ are free coordinate lifts of the prescribed spaces, bars denote reduction, and multiplication by $s$ identifies $k$ with $sR$. The displayed rule means apply this identification to each field coefficient. All triple products in $V_R\oplus W_R$ vanish, so the algebra is associative and commutative. Its regular representation $a\mapsto L_a$ is faithful by evaluation at $1$.

Let $v_i$ be the ordered coordinate basis of $V_R$, grouping coordinates of $X$ and $Y$. Send generator $i$ to $L_{1+v_i}$. In the order $1,V_R,W_R$ it is lower unitriangular. It has the required order because
$$
 (1+v_i)^a=1+av_i+\binom a2 v_i^2.
$$
For the usual cyclic order $p^e$, the linear term vanishes and the quadratic coefficient kills $sR$. This holds for odd $p$, and for $p=2,e\ge2$ since $\binom{2^e}{2}$ is even. In the exceptional case use order four and the same formula. The matrices commute, so the assignment defines a homomorphism. For $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_D)\in G$ and chosen integer representatives, define
$$
 \begin{split}
 u(a)&=\sum_i a_i v_i\quad\text{in }V_R,\\
 Q(a)&=\sum_i\overline{\binom{a_i}{2}}\,\bar v_i^2+
       \sum_{i<j}\bar a_i\bar a_j\,\bar v_i\bar v_j
       \quad\text{in }\operatorname{Sym}^2 V .
 \end{split}
$$
Products of three ideal terms vanish, giving
$$
 \rho_M(a)=L_{1+u(a)+s\,\pi_M(Q(a))}.
 \tag{1}
$$
This expansion is independent of the representatives, also in the exceptional case. In the ordinary cyclic-order case the $V_R$-coordinate detects $a$, so $\rho_M$ is faithful. In the exceptional case a kernel element must have all entries even. Its remaining quadratic part is the corresponding sum of the independent square monomials modulo $H_M$, hence it is zero only when all entries vanish in $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$. If $e\ge2$, mod $p^{e-1}$ every group matrix has the same coordinates for all $M$, by (1) and the algebra multiplication rule.

Over an odd prime field, each nonidentity element has nonzero $u(a)$. Equation (1) makes its centered left multiplication have chains $1\mapsto u+w\mapsto\pi_M(u^2)$ and $v\mapsto\pi_M(uv)$ for a complement to $u$ in $V$. The $D$ quadratic images are independent, since any nonzero $uv$ has cross rank at most two, less than $m$. Thus the common Jordan blocks are one of size three, $D-1$ of size two and $N-2D-1$ of size one. The same computation applies over every field extension to any nonzero linear combination of the centered generator operators, which is $L_u$. This is constant Jordan type for the elementary abelian $p$-group in the shifted cyclic sense. For $p=2,e=1$, a group element with some odd coordinate has this same block profile. A nonzero all-even element instead has one block of size two and all other blocks of size one. [Carlson–Friedlander–Pevtsova on truncated polynomial restrictions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.4474)

## 3. One conjugator on each labeled small subgroup

Let $a^{(1)},\ldots,a^{(b)}$ generate a subgroup $K$, with $b\le h$. Write $u_j=u(a^{(j)})$ and let $U\le V$ be the span of their residues. Form the following field subspace of quadratics:
$$
 \mathcal D=UV+\langle Q(a^{(1)}),\ldots,Q(a^{(b)})\rangle .
$$
Projection to the $XY$-part sends $Q(a^{(j)})$ to the product of the $X$- and $Y$-components of $\bar u_j$. This is also the cross projection of $\bar u_j(\bar u_j)_Y$ in $UV$. Therefore $\mathcal D$ and $UV$ have the same cross projection. Every element of $UV$ is a sum of at most $\dim U$ products using a basis of $U$. The cross part of one product has matrix rank at most two. Thus every cross part has rank at most $2h<m$, which implies
$$
 \mathcal D\cap H_M=\mathcal D\cap H_{M'}=0.
 \tag{2}
$$
This argument handles the carry terms $\binom{a_i}{2}$ even at $p=2$, because square monomials have zero cross part.

The map $\pi_M(q)\mapsto\pi_{M'}(q)$ for $q\in\mathcal D$ is therefore an isomorphism of the two images, and it extends to a linear isomorphism on the two ambient $W$-spaces. Take a unit-determinant lift to $W_R$: choose bases of the images, extend both to full bases, lift to $R$, and scale any one complementary output direction by the inverse determinant. The complementary direction exists since the images lie in $W_0$. Let $J$ act as this map on $W_R$ and fix $1,V_R$. It intertwines the two operators in (1) at each $a^{(j)}$. On $1$ this follows from the matching $Q$-terms. On $v\in V_R$, the only extra term is the product $s\,\pi_M(\bar u_j\bar v)$, which the map also matches. On $W_R$ the operator is the identity. Hence $J\rho_M(a)J^{-1}=\rho_{M'}(a)$ for every $a\in K$. This is simultaneous conjugacy of the labeled restrictions, and $J$ has determinant one. The construction uses at most $hD+h$ generators of $\mathcal D$ and ordinary linear equations.

## 4. Full subgroup conjugacy and recovery

A source tensor transporter gives a quadratic-space isomorphism $P:V\to V$ and an accompanying map $Q_0:W_0\to W_0'$ satisfying $Q_0\pi_M(uv)=\pi_{M'}(Pu\,Pv)$. Lift $P$ to a matrix $\widehat P$ over the cyclic-order ring of the group $G$, which is $R$ in the usual case and $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ in the exceptional case. Its determinant is a unit. In the latter case use its reduction in the ambient field. Let $a^{(i)}$ be its columns. Define an $R$-algebra map $A_M\to A_{M'}$ that fixes $1$, lifts $Q_0$ on $W_0$, and sends
$$
 v_i\longmapsto u(a^{(i)})+s\,\pi_{M'}(Q(a^{(i)})).
$$
Its cross block from $V_R$ to $W_R'$ does not change multiplication inside the augmentation ideal, since $W_R'$ annihilates that ideal. The tensor equation over $k$ therefore gives the full product equation. The map is invertible by its two invertible residue blocks and an arbitrary isomorphism of the inert complements. Scale one inert direction to make the determinant one. It sends $1+v_i$ to the group unit corresponding to the $i$-th column by (1). These columns generate the whole target group, proving full image conjugacy in $\operatorname{SL}_N(R)$.

Conversely a matrix conjugator of the full images gives an isomorphism of the $R$-algebras they generate in the ambient matrix ring. The generated algebra is exactly
$$
 \mathcal C_M=L(R1\oplus V_R\oplus s W_{0,R}).
$$
It contains $L_{v_i}$ by subtracting the identity from each generator. Products of these operators generate $L(s W_{0,R})$ since $\pi_M$ is surjective. Conversely the displayed algebra is closed under products and contains the generators and their inverses. Its augmentation ideal $B_M=L(V_R\oplus sW_{0,R})$ is the kernel of the trace of the ambient matrices, since $N$ is a unit and every multiplication by a member of the augmentation ideal is strictly triangular. Its square is $L(sW_{0,R})$, killed by $p$. Its product therefore induces a $k$-bimap
$$
 B_M/(pB_M+B_M^2)\ \times\ B_M/(pB_M+B_M^2)
       \longrightarrow B_M^2 .
$$
In the chosen construction this is precisely $(u,v)\mapsto\pi_M(uv)$ under the coefficient identification $sR\simeq k$. In particular its kernel on $\operatorname{Sym}^2 V$ is $H_M$. The map induced by a conjugator on these intrinsic subquotients gives the quadratic-space equivalence and hence the original witness by Section 1. The same proof shows that no new conjugacies between the two full images arise by passing to arbitrary ambient bases.

On promised arbitrary-basis generator lists the generated algebra can be computed by iterating additive span and multiplication by the listed generators, starting with the identity. A strict increase increases the additive logarithmic order by at least one, and the ambient order is $p^{eN^2}$. Obtain the basis-explicit quotient and multiplication maps using Smith elimination. This produces the recovered field symmetric bimap in polynomial time. Symmetric bimap isomorphism reduces to ordinary field tensor isomorphism by the ordinary tensor-system compiler, marking that the two inputs are acted on by the same map. An arbitrary-basis ordinary conjugator can be corrected to determinant one by scaling a transported inert direction, which centralizes the target subgroup. This proves the promised upper reduction for both conjugacy versions. [Grochow–Qiao, tensor-isomorphism completeness](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309)

## 5. Scope and precedent

Elementwise conjugate homomorphisms that fail global conjugacy in defining characteristic are known in work on acceptability. The comparison here is stronger in a different direction: every subgroup generated by a prescribed number $h$ of elements has a common labeled conjugator, yet global image conjugacy is already TI-complete even after permitting all domain automorphisms. The result concerns the embeddings of abelian groups. The proof also works at every prime-power precision, retaining the same lower-precision representation and providing local determinant-one conjugators. The earlier final-digit reduction to conjugacy of regular abelian subgroups did not assert labeled agreement on small restrictions. [Panja, *Acceptability of classical groups in non-zero characteristic*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01133) · [Earlier final-digit subgroup reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FinalDigitFour/Subgroups.md)

The argument is a written proof, with publication priority for the exact package unconfirmed.
