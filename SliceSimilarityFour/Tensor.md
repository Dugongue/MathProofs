# Tensor isomorphism with identical small matrix substitutions

**Theorem.** Over any field $k$, ordinary three-tensor isomorphism reduces to isomorphism of linear spaces of square slices
$$
L_t(r,u)=rI+N_{t,u}
$$
with a common parameter space $k\oplus V$, a fixed identity slice, and $N_{t,u}N_{t,v}=0$ for every $u,v$. For any chosen integer $h\ge2$, all resulting pencils of the same format satisfy the following.

- For every subspace of the full first-mode parameter space whose projection to $V$ has dimension at most $h$, one determinant-one similarity matches all corresponding slices on that subspace, including singular slices. This works after any extension of $k$.
- At every matrix substitution of size $q\le h$, using completely arbitrary and possibly noncommuting coefficient matrices, corresponding evaluated matrices have the same rational canonical form.
- The first-mode size is $D+1$, where $D=3(h+1)(a+b)+1$ for a positive input format $(a,b,c)$. The two square modes have size $O(D^2)$. There is a variant symmetric in the last two modes, with a common hyperbolic unit slice over fields. On the same first-mode subspaces its slices are simultaneously congruent by a determinant-one isometry of that metric. In this variant, substituting symmetric $q\times q$ coefficient matrices gives determinant-one congruence for $q\le h$ if $\operatorname{char}k\ne2$, and for $2q\le h$ in every characteristic.

There is a final-digit version over every finite commutative local ring $R$ with residue field $k$, nonzero maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, and supplied $0\ne s\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(\mathfrak m)$. The outputs agree modulo $sR$. In the symmetric-matrix variant the common metric over a dyadic nonfield ring has one additional norm-one line. The simultaneous similarity statement holds for every submodule whose projected residue span in $V$ has dimension at most $h$. Over finite chain rings every one-variable polynomial in each corresponding matrix substitution of size $q\le h$ has the same Smith form. Over odd-residue finite chain rings the determinant-one congruence for symmetric matrix coefficients also holds for $q\le h$. Both reductions reflect arbitrary three-mode transporters, and every field input transporter lifts to output matrices of determinant one in all three modes.

The unrestricted matrix-substitution threshold $h$ is exact for this construction. There are pairs of inequivalent outputs of the same format separated by a single substitution of size $h+1$, in each variant. The construction is polynomial in the dense input and $h$. Arithmetic is bit-polynomial over finite fields and explicit prime-power rings.

## 1. The quadratic input and a cancellation lemma

A three-tensor gives a matrix space $M\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(E,F)$ by taking the span of its slices. For matched formats, matrix-space equivalence under independent changes on $E,F$ is equivalent to tensor isomorphism. In particular an equivalence on the slice image extends to one on the slice domains by choosing complements and matching kernels.

Put $Z=E\oplus F$, and let $\widetilde A$ act by $A:E\to F$ and kill $F$. Use the oriented rectangular space
$$
\mathcal B_M=\{[xI+\widetilde A,\ yI]:x,y\in k,\ A\in M\}.
$$
Its rank-$<\dim Z$ part is precisely $x=y=0$, since $xI+\widetilde A$ is invertible if $x\ne0$, and the second block is invertible if $y\ne0$. Any two arrow labels independent modulo this low-rank data, stacked as two block rows, give an isomorphism $Z^2\to Z^2$. Indeed the scalar part is a $2\times2$ invertible matrix tensored with $I_Z$. After dividing by it the correction squares to zero, as all products of the $\widetilde A$ vanish.

Set $m=h+1$. On variables $X,Y$ of dimensions $m\dim Z,2m\dim Z$, take the space $H_M$ of quadratics $x^{\mathsf T}B^{\oplus m}y$ for $B\in\mathcal B_M$. Include one further variable $e_*$ outside the support of these quadratics. Denote the whole variable space by $V$, of dimension $D$. A nonzero element of $H_M$ has polarization rank at least $2m$, in every characteristic, because its polar matrix has off-diagonal blocks $B^{\oplus m}$ and its transpose. The same calculation holds after extending $k$. In particular,
$$
H_M\cap UV=0\qquad\text{for all }U\subseteq V,\ \dim U\le h.
\tag{1}
$$
Every element in $UV$ is a sum of at most $\dim U$ products and each product has polarization rank at most two.

For clarity, the quadratic-space reduction reflects equivalence even in the extra variable. A quadratic equivalence respects the common polarization radical in the argument space $V^*$. The radical is the line of the coordinate functional dual to $e_*$, by the two arrows $[I,0],[0,I]$. Its annihilator in $V$ is exactly the active variable space. Restricting to that support, a common relabeling of the polar tuple gives left-right equivalence of the sum of $m$ rectangular copies of $\mathcal B_M$ and $m$ transposed copies. Choose two labels independent modulo the low-rank data, which the polar-rank test recognizes. The sum of their kernels is the original rectangular source: in that part their joint map is an isomorphism, and in the transposed part each is injective. Its image under either arrow is the original rectangular target. This extracts the ordered $m$-fold tuple after the same relabeling. Krull–Schmidt cancellation for representations of the ordered parallel-arrow quiver then cancels the multiplicity $m$. Restrict to the low-rank data, quotient its common source kernel and take its target image, recovering the supported original matrix space. Complementary dimensions in the original format allow recovery on the full tensor. The forward construction extends an input transporter blockwise on $Z$ and both source copies, and hence on $X,Y$. It can use $e_*$ to make the map on $V$ have determinant one. Over finite fields, once the shared arrow relabeling is extracted, a single-copy transporter can be computed by deterministic module isomorphism on the ordered arrow matrices with their vertex projections. [Preceding high-rank quadratic construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/HessianFour/Tensor.md) · [Brooksbank–Luks, *Testing isomorphism of modules*](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf).

Write $W_0=\operatorname{Sym}^2 V/H_M$, and let $\beta(u,v)=[uv]$. The values span $W_0$, and for $u\ne0$ the map $\beta(u,-):V\to W_0$ is injective, by (1) for the line $ku$. The tensor of $\beta$ itself reflects $H_M$ under independent changes in its two inputs. Indeed if $P,Q:V\to V'$ and $C:W_0\to W'_0$ intertwine the two maps, symmetry gives
$$
(Pu)(Qv)-(Pv)(Qu)\in H_{M'} .
$$
The polarization rank of this difference is at most four, so the polynomial vanishes. Let $S=QP^{-1}$. In a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_D$ the identity $e_i S e_j=e_j S e_i$ for $i\ne j$, in the polynomial ring, forces $Se_i\in ke_i$ by divisibility by $e_i$, and then forces equality of all the diagonal scalars. Hence $Q=\gamma P$ for a nonzero scalar $\gamma$. Therefore $\operatorname{Sym}^2(P)$ transports the kernel $H_M$ onto $H_{M'}$.

## 2. The full parameter space has common simultaneous similarities

Take a padded $W=W_0\oplus S_0$, of dimension $\dim\operatorname{Sym}^2 V$, with its quotient coordinates and inert coordinate complement. This gives at least two padding coordinates since $\dim H_M\ge2$. Use $J=V\oplus W$ as the square-mode space. Over fields set $R=k$ and $s=1$. Over nonfields take free lifts $V_R,W_R,J_R$, and write $\jmath:k\to sR$, $\bar a\mapsto sa$, for the coefficient identification. It has the stated domain since the annihilator of $s$ is $\mathfrak m$. Add one more inert direction if needed so that $\dim_R J_R$ is a unit in the nonfield case.

For $u\in V_R$ define $N_{t,u}$ to take $v\in V_R$ to $\jmath(\beta(\bar u,\bar v))\in W_R$ and to kill $W_R$. Then $L_t(r,u)=rI+N_{t,u}$ has pairwise zero products of centered slices, and determinant $r^{\dim J}$. The matrix is constant modulo $sR$ in the nonfield case. For any subspace of the full parameter space, let $U$ be its projected $V$-space, or its projected residue space over $R$. If $\dim U\le h$, (1) identifies $UV$ with its two images in the two $W$-spaces. Match these images using the same products $uv$ and extend the resulting isomorphism to the whole $W$. There is an unused complement because $\dim UV\le hD<\dim W$. Rescale one complement vector to obtain determinant one, lifting the map and adjusting the determinant by a unit over $R$. The map on $J$ acting identically on $V$ and by this extension on $W$ intertwines every $L_t(r,u)$ in the given subspace. All such local witnesses follow directly from linear extension of bases.

To reflect arbitrary three-mode equivalence, suppose a first-mode map $A$ and invertible row and column maps $B,C$ give
$$
L_{t'}(Ax)=B L_t(x) C .
$$
Let $x_0=(1,0)$ and $Ax_0=(r_0,u_0)$. Invertibility of $L_{t'}(Ax_0)$ implies $r_0$ is a unit. Right-dividing the equation by $L_{t'}(Ax_0)$ gives a similarity of $L_t(x)$ to the normalized target. By the square-zero identity, the target is its scalar part $r(Ax)/r_0$ times $I$ plus a centered square-zero operator. Over a field the scalar is the unique eigenvalue. Over a nonfield, trace determines it since the matrix size is a unit. Thus $r(Ax)=r_0r(x)$. Restrict to the zero-scalar part and, over $R$, cancel $s$ by passing to residues and applying $\jmath^{-1}$. This gives an invertible simultaneous similarity of the centered residue tuples up to the factor $\bar r_0^{-1}$. Their common kernel is $W$ and their total image is $W_0$. On the supported source and target the similarity therefore induces an independent-mode equivalence of $\beta$, absorbing this scalar in one mode. Section 1 recovers $H_M$ and the input transporter.

For completeness an input equivalence gives $P$ on $V$ of determinant one and an induced map on $W_0$, extended arbitrarily to $W$. Cover it by invertible lifts over $R$, correcting the determinant of the first $V_R$-map on $e_*$ and that of the map on $J_R$ on one inert $W_R$-coordinate. These corrections are compatible: the first is incorporated in the residue map before inducing the map on $W_0$, and the second affects only the inert complement. Use the same $V_R$-map in the first mode and in $J_R$, and use similarity on the square slots. This gives determinant one in all three modes.

## 3. The symmetric-matrix variant

Replace $J_R$ by $J_R\oplus J_R^*$ with its hyperbolic evaluation metric $G$ and replace $N_u$ by $\widehat N_u=N_u\oplus N_u^{\mathsf T}$. If the residue characteristic is two over a nonfield, add a norm-one line to $G$ on which the centered maps vanish. The total size is then a unit. In odd residue characteristic over a nonfield the doubled size is already a unit. The square slices
$$
G(rI+\widehat N_u)
$$
are symmetric because the normalized operators are self-adjoint for $G$. The scalar and simultaneous-similarity arguments apply to the normalized operators. In particular the local similarities extend on the dual by their inverse transposes, give determinant-one isometries of $G$, and become simultaneous congruences. The forward determinant-one transporter extends in the same way.

After taking residues in the reflection argument and passing to common-kernel quotient and total image, the centered tuples have source $V\oplus W_0^*$, target $W_0\oplus V^*$, and arrows $\beta(u,-)\oplus\beta(u,-)^{\mathsf T}$. Choose three independent directions $u_1,u_2,u_3$. The images $\beta(u_i,V)$ have zero triple intersection. Any equality of a product with a product involving a second direction holds in $\operatorname{Sym}^2 V$ by (1) on that two-dimensional space. Thus a common product would be divisible by three pairwise nonproportional linear forms and would have degree two, forcing zero. Since the original $\beta$-arrows are injective for nonzero $u$, the sum of the three arrow kernels is exactly $W_0^*$, the annihilator of that triple intersection. Its image under the first arrow is all of $V^*$. Quotient source and target by these two subspaces to obtain the original ordered $\beta$. This reflects arbitrary transporters in the symmetric-matrix variant.

## 4. All small matrix substitutions

Let $Y_0,Y_1,\ldots,Y_D$ be $q\times q$ matrices over the base ring, and set $u_{ij}=\sum_{\nu=1}^D(Y_\nu)_{ij}e_\nu$. In the ordinary pencil the matrix evaluation is
$$
\mathcal E_t(Y)=Y_0\otimes I+\mathcal N_t(U),
\qquad
\mathcal N_t(U)_{ij}=N_{t,u_{ij}}.
\tag{2}
$$
This is the usual matrix substitution even when the $Y_\nu$ do not commute. In the symmetric-matrix version use the normalized pencil, replacing $N$ by $\widehat N$.

First use independent scalar row and column maps on the $q$-indices to diagonalize $Y_0$, via ordinary elimination over fields or Smith elimination over chain rings. The transformed $\mathcal N_t(U)$ still has the form (2) on transformed directions. For each block row $i$, the span of its $q$ directions has dimension at most $q\le h$, or its residue span has this bound. Choose the corresponding map $W_i$ of the $W$-spaces using (1). Use the direct sum of these maps on the $W$-part of the block rows and columns, acting by identity on the $V$-parts. This is a conjugation preserving the diagonal scalar blocks and matching every $V$-to-$W$ block. It gives left-right equivalence of the original evaluations. For the transpose portion of the symmetric-matrix variant, use separate $W^*$-maps indexed by columns, from the span of the $q$ directions in each column. This matches the blocks from $W^*$ to $V^*$ and gives the same result.

For every one-variable polynomial $f$, its evaluation $f(\mathcal E_t(Y))$ has scalar block part $f(Y_0)\otimes I$ and a centered block part still of the form $\mathcal N_t(U_f)$. In the expansion, every term with two centered factors vanishes even with powers of $Y_0\otimes I$ inserted between them. The remaining centered terms transform the direction entries linearly, independently of $t$. Thus the same comparison applies to all polynomials $f$. Over a chain ring this gives identical Smith forms. Over a field the nullities of $p(\mathcal E_t(Y))^j$, for all monic irreducible $p$ and $j\ge1$, determine all primary elementary divisors and therefore actual similarity. This proves the rational canonical form assertion without any bound on the field or restriction on its characteristic.

For symmetric $Y_\nu$ in the symmetric-matrix version, first reduce $Y_0$ by scalar congruence. Over an odd-characteristic field it is diagonal. In characteristic two a symmetric form has blocks of size at most two, by splitting nonzero-norm vectors and then splitting alternating hyperbolic pairs in the remainder. Over an odd-residue chain ring the scalar form is diagonalizable by congruence as well: take an entry of least valuation, obtain a diagonal entry of that valuation by using a sum of two basis vectors if needed, and eliminate its row and column using divisibility by it. Repeat on the remaining block. For a scalar block with index set $I$, choose one common $W_I$-map matching the at most $|I|q$ directions in its rows. The direction array is symmetric, so the same map suffices for the columns. Use $W_I$ and its inverse transpose on the paired $W,W^*$ directions for all indices in the block. The maps preserve the metric in these coordinates and commute with each scalar block, giving determinant-one congruence of the substituted symmetric forms. Conjugating the congruence witness back through the original scalar coordinate change preserves its determinant. This proves the respective thresholds $q\le h$ and $2q\le h$.

## 5. Exact threshold and scope

Take $a=b=2,c=1$, with the single matrix slice of rank one in one input and rank two in the other. In the rank-one case the repeated data quadric is a sum of $m=h+1$ products $x_i y_i$ in separate copies. Let $U$ be the span of those $x_i$. Then $H_M\cap UV$ has dimension one: it contains the data quadric, and every nonzero combination with an anchor has polarization rank $2m\dim Z>2m$. In the rank-two case every nonzero quadratic in $H_{M'}$ has polarization rank at least $4m$, so $H_{M'}\cap UV=0$. At size $q=m$ take $Y_0=0$ and put directions $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ in the first block row, with all other rows zero. The ranks of the two $V$-to-$W$ block maps differ by one, since their images are the images of $UV$ in the respective quotients. In the symmetric-matrix variant the added transpose block row has rank $D$ in both cases, since each nonzero $\beta(x_i,-)^{\mathsf T}$ surjects onto $V^*$. The normalized evaluations therefore differ in rank. Over a final-digit ring the same computation distinguishes the orders of their images in $sJ_R^q$, and hence their Smith forms on chain rings.

The earlier construction imposed simultaneous equivalence on subpencils in a specified coordinate block of a regular representation. The pencil here has only the unit and its $V$-directions as parameters, and reflection holds for unrestricted first-mode transformations. Ivanyos, Qiao and Subrahmanyam use matrix blow-ups to characterize and compute noncommutative rank. Here the comparison is at each explicit coefficient substitution up to size $h$ and includes every elementary divisor over fields. The rank amplification in Section 1 is an explicit construction of a subspace of quadratic polynomials that preserves transporters. The proof is informal and priority for the stated bounded-substitution theorem remains unverified. The exact control uses a rank-one and a rank-two datum with $h=2$, over $\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z$. It passed 64 matching polynomial Smith signatures at size two and four separating signatures at size three for the ordinary pencil.
