# Rank-parameterized postman tours with prescribed binary holonomy

A spanning Rural Postman tour with prescribed binary holonomy can be optimized in randomized time single-exponential in the number of required components and the rank of the cycle voltages, with no dependence on the number of physical boundary vertices in the exponent. The algorithm is exact for integer costs with a pseudopolynomial budget factor. With nonnegative rational costs, it gives a randomized $(1+\varepsilon)$ approximation in fixed-parameter time with polynomial dependence on $1/\varepsilon$. The result allows nontrivial holonomy inside required components.

## Problem and rank

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a connected loopless undirected multigraph. Every physical edge $e$ has a nonnegative weight $w_e$ and a voltage $g_e\in\mathbb F_2^k$. Given $R\subseteq E$ and $\tau\in\mathbb F_2^k$, a feasible route is a closed walk that visits every vertex, traverses every edge of $R$, and sums to $\tau$ over its edge occurrences. A repeated edge contributes its voltage on each traversal, in either direction. This is a spanning arc-routing problem. Let $r$ count the connected components of the spanning graph $(V,R)$, including isolated vertices.

Write $\partial$ for the binary vertex-edge incidence matrix and $g$ for the linear map on binary edge vectors defined by the voltage columns. Put $\rho=\dim g(\ker\partial)$. On a spanning tree, choose a root and assign each vertex $v$ the sum $b_v$ of voltages on its root path. Replacing $g_{uv}$ by $g_{uv}+b_u+b_v$ preserves closed-walk sums and zeros the tree voltages. Every remaining edge voltage is the original sum around its fundamental cycle. These voltages span $g(\ker\partial)$, so coordinates in a basis reduce all voltage labels to $\mathbb F_2^\rho$. A target outside this space is infeasible.

The gauge change is the same relabeling of graph-cover fibers that Grochow and Tucker-Foltz use in their treatment of Unique Games and cohomology localization. Here the lift of a tour starting on sheet $0$ ends on sheet $\tau$. When the cover is trivial within each required component, a spanning tree made from component trees and a quotient tree shows that $\rho$ equals the rank of the cycle voltages on the component quotient. Without interior triviality, $\rho$ also counts the voltage directions contributed by interior cycles. [Grochow and Tucker-Foltz](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06800), [Gross and Tucker](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-6632.1979.tb32796.x).

**Theorem.** For integer weights and an integer budget $C\ge 0$, a one-sided randomized algorithm in time $2^{r+\rho}\operatorname{poly}(|\mathrm{input}|,C)$ decides whether a feasible route has cost at most $C$ and returns a witness on acceptance. With probability at least $3/4$, it returns the minimum-cost feasible route among those within budget when one exists. For nonnegative rational weights and rational $\varepsilon>0$, a randomized algorithm in time $2^{r+\rho}\operatorname{poly}(|\mathrm{input}|,1/\varepsilon)$ determines feasibility and returns a feasible route whose cost is at most $(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}$ with probability at least $3/4$. The feasibility determination in the rational case is deterministic.

## Matching representation and connectivity

Represent a tour by its connected spanning even-degree multigraph of occurrences. Any edge with at least three copies can lose two copies while retaining its support, the required edges, degree parity, and voltage. This cannot increase cost. Thus a feasible optimum can use at most two copies per physical edge.

Take one copy of $R$ as a fixed baseline of weight $c_R$ and voltage $\beta$. Make one optional virtual copy of each required edge and two of every other physical edge. A virtual copy $uv$ has two auxiliary vertices, or stubs, one at $u$ and one at $v$, with an edge pairing the two stubs to each other. Also put one dummy stub at each vertex of odd degree in $R$. At each physical vertex form a clique on all of its own stubs, including its dummy when present. All auxiliary vertices and edges have distinct identities, even for parallel physical edges. The number $N$ of auxiliary vertices is even.

In a perfect matching, a copy is omitted when its two stubs are paired to one another. Otherwise both stubs must match within their respective physical vertices, and the copy is selected. A dummy can only match locally. The locally paired stubs therefore exist exactly when the number of selected copy endpoints at each vertex has the parity of its degree in $R$. Conversely, for any selection satisfying those parities, pair the omitted copies and then pair the even number of remaining stubs independently at each vertex. This gives at least one perfect matching with that selection. Different local pairings may represent the same multigraph.

For each rooted two-way cut of the $r$ required components, with component $1$ fixed to the first side, remove the local pairing edges incident to stubs of any virtual copy crossing that cut. Its own stub-pair edge stays, so the copy is forced to be omitted. Use the same underlying auxiliary graph and the same variables on all retained matching edges across all cuts. A fixed perfect matching survives precisely the cuts consistent with the components of the graph formed by the baseline and its selected copies. If that graph has $c$ components, the matching survives $2^{c-1}$ of the cuts. Summing in characteristic two keeps it exactly when $c=1$. This is the rooted-cut cancellation used for ordinary Rural Postman and in Cut & Count. [Gutin, Wahlström, and Yeo, Sections 2.6–2.10](https://arxiv.org/html/1308.2599), [Cygan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.0534).

## Voltage and cost in the Pfaffian

If $C<c_R$, reject. Otherwise let $L=C-c_R$. Work over
$K[\mathbb F_2^\rho][z]/(z^{L+1})$, with $K$ a finite field of characteristic two. Write $X^a$ for the group-algebra basis vector of voltage $a$, with $X^aX^b=X^{a+b}$. Give each auxiliary matching edge $f$ its own scalar indeterminate $x_f$. Mark one of the two stubs of each virtual copy of physical edge $e$ by $z^{w_e}X^{g_e}$. A local pairing edge receives the product of the markings of its endpoints and its scalar $x_f$. An edge pairing the two stubs of one virtual copy receives just its scalar, so omission is uncharged. Each selected copy is marked exactly once.

For each rooted cut form the alternating matching matrix of the restricted auxiliary graph and take its Pfaffian. In characteristic two all matching signs equal $1$. The sum of the $2^{r-1}$ Pfaffians, considered first with symbolic scalars, retains precisely the connected perfect matchings by the cut argument. Its coefficient at $z^d X^{\tau+\beta}$ lists the connected matchings of additional cost $d$ and target total voltage. Distinct matchings have distinct monomials in the scalar indeterminates. Consequently the coefficient polynomial is nonzero if and only if a normalized feasible route of cost $c_R+d$ exists. Normalization preserves the optimum and the budget decision. The exact degree statement is about normalized routes.

Draw all $x_f$ independently and uniformly from a field of size at least $4\max(1,N)$, once per trial, and reuse these values in all cuts. A nonzero coefficient polynomial has total degree $N/2$, so it vanishes after specialization with probability at most $1/8$ by Schwartz–Zippel. It suffices to consider the single coefficient of the true smallest feasible degree. Zero coefficient polynomials always specialize to zero, which excludes false acceptance. Evaluate the ring expression and scan for the smallest nonzero coefficient with the required voltage. The order of the field can be chosen polynomial in $N$.

For witness recovery, fix a partner for one unmatched auxiliary vertex at a time. To test a proposed partner edge, remove all other edges incident to either endpoint and reevaluate the same coefficient over all cuts at the same scalar values. The possible partners partition the matching terms of the current nonzero coefficient, so at least one branch has a nonzero coefficient. Retain any such branch. Recovery incurs only a polynomial number of additional evaluations and no additional probability of error. Finally take an Euler tour of the baseline and selected copies.

The division-free Pfaffian algorithm of Mahajan, Subramanya, and Vinay uses polynomially many operations over any commutative ring, including the truncated ring here. Direct group multiplication costs $O(4^\rho)$ polynomial-ring operations. For the stated exponent, choose a basis $e_i$ of the voltage space and put $Y_i=1+X^{e_i}$. Then $Y_i^2=0$. The products of distinct $Y_i$ form a basis, since expansion into the $X$ basis is an invertible subset zeta transform. Multiplication in the $Y$ basis is precisely disjoint-subset convolution, with values in $K[z]/(z^{L+1})$. Fast subset convolution uses $O(\rho^2 2^\rho)$ ring operations for positive rank. The rank-zero ring is just the cost-polynomial ring. Basis conversion is $O(\rho 2^\rho)$, and each operation on the truncated cost polynomials has time polynomial in $L$. Including all rooted cuts and witness recovery gives the claimed $2^{r+\rho}\operatorname{poly}(|\mathrm{input}|,C)$ time. [Mahajan, Subramanya, and Vinay](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/pascal.koiran/RefsStageM2/msv.pfaffians.pdf), [Björklund et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0611101).

An independent crossing limit $B$ can be imposed by also marking each selected cross copy by a factor of $y$ and reducing modulo $y^{B+1}$. This increases the integer-budget runtime by a polynomial in $B$. In particular, a known metric certificate guaranteeing a short-crossing optimum can be combined directly with the algorithm.

## Rational weights from one scaled optimization

In an edge-threshold subgraph, feasibility has a deterministic characterization. The graph must contain all required edges, be connected on $V$, and have $\tau$ in the image of its own binary cycle space under the voltage map. Necessity follows from any feasible route. For sufficiency, choose a binary even-degree edge set of voltage $\tau$. Add two copies of any absent required edges and of any absent edges of a spanning tree. The resulting graph is connected, even-degree, and has the prescribed voltage. It is already normalized.

Find the smallest feasible bottleneck edge weight $W_0$ by binary search on the distinct edge weights using that criterion, including the threshold zero. If no threshold is feasible, the instance is infeasible. The empty walk handles the single-vertex graph with no edges when its target is zero. If $W_0=0$, the witness from the criterion is optimal. Otherwise, writing $m=|E|$, the witness of cost $U$ at threshold $W_0$ satisfies $W_0\le\mathrm{OPT}\le U\le 2mW_0$.

Remove edges heavier than $U$ and round the remaining costs down to integers in units $\Delta=\varepsilon W_0/(2m)$. Run the integer algorithm once with total scaled budget $\lfloor U/\Delta\rfloor\le 4m^2/\varepsilon$. An original optimum remains and is within this scaled budget. A scaled optimum has true cost at most $\mathrm{OPT}+2m\Delta\le (1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}$, since it uses at most $2m$ edge occurrences. If the randomized step yields no witness, retain the deterministic feasible route. The guarantee holds whenever the optimal scaled coefficient survives, an event of probability at least $7/8$ for the stated field size.

## Finite controls and prior work

A nonflat two-part control has required edges $ab,cd$, each of weight $1$ and voltage $0$, a parallel optional $ab$ edge of weight $3$ and voltage $1$, and a crossing $ac$ of weight $2$ and voltage $0$. The required-component count is $2$ and cycle-voltage rank is $1$. Target $0$ has optimum $8$. Target $1$ has optimum $10$, obtained by traversing the two different $ab$ edges once each and both $ac$ and $cd$ twice.

A reproducible finite control compares reachable $(\text{weight},\text{voltage})$ pairs from two enumerations. In the first, enumerate physical multiplicities $1,2$ for required edges and $0,1,2$ for others, keeping even-degree connected spanning multisets. In the second, enumerate auxiliary perfect matchings in every rooted cut, key each term by its sorted list of auxiliary edge identities, and toggle that key on each appearance. Add the baseline to the weight and voltage of each surviving key. For the sampled batch, number the vertices from zero and use 96 instances, alternating two and three two-vertex parts, with consecutive vertices in each part. In order, add one required edge per part, drawing its weight from $1$–$5$ and then its two-bit voltage. Build an optional chain across consecutive parts, drawing the endpoint in each part, the weight from $0$–$7$, and then the voltage. To reach four optional physical edges, draw a vertex $u$ and a nonzero cyclic offset to obtain the other endpoint. Draw the voltage and then the weight from $0$–$7$. This allows parallel edges. The deterministic sample used seed $214327$ with the 32-bit recurrence $s\leftarrow 1664525s+1013904223$ and draws $(s\mathbin{\gg}10)\bmod n$ for a range of size $n$. The two enumerations agreed over 46,656 physical multiplicity assignments and 9,842,189 cut-compatible matching occurrences, with 1,721 reachable pairs summed over instances. A separate check compared 72 random group-algebra products at ranks $0$–$8$ over $\mathrm{GF}(256)$ with direct XOR convolution. These controls exercise the encoding and algebra, and the polynomial-time evaluation uses the cited Pfaffian theorem.

Gutin, Wahlström, and Yeo already give the ordinary, unlabelled Rural Postman connectivity sieve with a pseudopolynomial weight factor. Deppert, Kaul, and Mnich already round weights in the ordinary case by guessing the largest edge of an optimal route. The extension here keeps the prescribed voltage through the matching, cuts, and weight optimization, using the intrinsic cycle-voltage rank and a deterministic feasible bottleneck. The combined statement has a mathematical proof and finite checks. Priority for that combination remains open, and no Lean formalization was performed. [Gutin, Wahlström, and Yeo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2599), [Deppert, Kaul, and Mnich, Lemma 9 and Corollary 10](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol274-esa2023/LIPIcs.ESA.2023.39/LIPIcs.ESA.2023.39.pdf).
