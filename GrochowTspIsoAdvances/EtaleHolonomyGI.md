# Tensor isomorphism from finite-field characters of cycle holonomy

**Result.** In a recognizable class of 3-tensors with invertible matrix lines on the edges of a hidden bipartite support graph, tensor isomorphism reduces to Graph Isomorphism when the cycle holonomies commute, are semisimple, and the integral cycle lattice is generated by cycles of bounded length. The reduction has polynomial expected bit complexity for fixed length bound, with no bound on block dimensions or field size. Chordal bipartite supports satisfy the cycle condition with length bound four. For a specified support alignment, the same holonomy class admits a polynomial-time test with no cycle-length hypothesis.

The proof gives an explicit tensor isomorphism from a graph matching. It allows field factors of degree larger than one, including the field factor of a cyclic transitive projective transport. This is a mathematical proof with an exhaustive finite control. There is no Lean formalization.

## Recovering the hidden support

Let $F=\mathbb F_q$ be explicitly represented and take the image $S\subseteq L\otimes R$ of the contraction of a 3-tensor against the dual of its third leg. We consider tensors of a common format. An invertible product map between the image spaces lifts to a tensor isomorphism. Indeed, after applying the first two maps, the contraction maps have the same image. Split both surjections onto that image and identify their equal-dimensional kernels to construct the change of basis in the last leg.

Assume that $S$ decomposes as a direct sum of invertible matrix lines $F M_{uv}\subseteq U_u\otimes W_v$ across a connected bipartite graph $G$, where $L=\bigoplus_u U_u$, $R=\bigoplus_v W_v$, and all summands have dimension $r$. We require at least one edge. Each $M_{uv}$ is an invertible map $W_v^*\to U_u$. The decompositions and graph may be undisclosed in the input.

Compute the associative unital algebras of endomorphisms of $L$ and of $R$ whose action on the corresponding tensor leg preserves $S$. The conditions are linear in the endomorphism, obtained by applying it to a basis of $S$ and projecting onto the ambient quotient by $S$. The displayed block projections belong to the algebras. Each is primitive. For example, an endomorphism in the corner associated to $U_u$ must preserve an incident line $F M_{uv}$, so $aM_{uv}$ is a scalar multiple of $M_{uv}$. Invertibility gives $a$ scalar. The other leg has the same property using the dual action.

In a finite-dimensional algebra, two complete orthogonal primitive systems are conjugate by a unit after reindexing. In fact, their idempotents give indecomposable right ideals in decompositions of the right regular module. Krull–Schmidt matches these ideals and gives a module automorphism sending the first decomposition to the second. This automorphism is left multiplication by a unit and conjugates the projections. Units in the endomorphism algebras preserve $S$. Consequently any computed complete primitive systems recover the displayed decompositions up to these units. The invertible-line and equal-dimension hypotheses can be tested after projection to every pair of recovered blocks. The same conjugacy argument lets any tensor equivalence be adjusted to match the computed blocks, so it suffices to search for block-respecting equivalences. [Grochow–Qiao on Tensor Isomorphism](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110), [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, Proposition 11](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf), [Couvreur–Debris-Alazard–Gaborit on matrix-code stabilizer algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04611).

Use $U_u$ and $W_v^*$ as vertex spaces and $M_{uv}$ as the transport on the arc from $v$ to $u$, with its inverse on the reverse arc. Write $G_e$ for a matrix representative of the transport on any arc $e$. A block-respecting equivalence is exactly a part-preserving support alignment and projective vertex switches
$$
[G'_e]=[X_{\operatorname{head}(e)}G_eX_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}^{-1}]
$$
after pulling back the target along the alignment. The inverse dual switch on a right vertex gives the actual map on $W_v$. Equality up to scalar is sufficient because each summand of $S$ is a line.

## The holonomy algebra

Choose a root $o$ and a spanning tree. For every vertex $v$, let $P_v$ be the transport along the tree path from $o$ to $v$. A non-tree arc $e:u\to v$ gives the rooted fundamental transport $H_e=P_v^{-1}G_eP_u$. These matrices and their inverses generate the based closed-walk transports. This follows by inserting tree-path transports between consecutive arcs of a walk and canceling them. Tree arcs contribute the identity.

Assume the $H_e$ commute as linear maps and each has squarefree minimal polynomial. This is exactly the condition that the generated algebra $A=F[H_e]$ is commutative semisimple. Indeed, commuting semisimple matrices are simultaneously diagonalizable over an algebraic closure, so their generated algebra has no nonzero nilpotents. Over a finite field a finite commutative reduced algebra is a product of finite extension fields. The converse holds because each finite-field factor is separable over $F$. In particular,
$$
A=\prod_i A_i,\qquad A_i\cong\mathbb F_{q^{d_i}},\qquad
F^r=\bigoplus_i V_i,\qquad \dim_{A_i}V_i=m_i,
$$
where $V_i$ is the image of the identity idempotent of $A_i$. Every $m_i$ is positive, so $\sum_i d_i\le r$.

Changing an arc representative scales each closed transport by an element of $F^\times$. This leaves the generated $F$-algebra unchanged. Changing the spanning tree gives another generating tuple for the same based transports. Changing the root conjugates its holonomy algebra by transport between the roots. Under arbitrary vertex switches the root change similarly conjugates the algebra, while the other vertex switches cancel around each closed walk. These facts show that the hypothesis and factor multiplicities are intrinsic. The semisimplicity and commutation tests use matrix powers, polynomial gcds, and pairwise products. A basis of $A$ can be found by repeatedly multiplying a growing basis by generators and retaining independent matrices. Its field decomposition follows by computing primitive idempotents in $A$ itself.

For an oriented cycle $C$ starting at $v$, let $H_C=P_v^{-1}G(C)P_v$, where $G(C)$ is the transport around $C$. Any other transport path from $o$ to $v$ differs from $P_v$ by a based closed transport. Since $A$ is commutative, using that path gives the same rooted matrix. After scalar changes of arc representatives it gives the same projective class. Conjugation between different choices of root therefore identifies the projective classes of $H_C$ in their respective algebras. [Cavaleri–Donno, Theorem 3.3 and Remark 3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12428).

## Graph encoding and sufficiency

Orient each unoriented edge once and let $\Lambda=\ker(\partial:\mathbb Z^{E(G)}\to\mathbb Z^{V(G)})$ be the integral cycle lattice. Assume it is generated by signed vectors of simple cycles of length at most $\ell$. All such oriented cycles will be included, with their vertex order as part of the relation. For chordal bipartite graphs every longer simple cycle has a chord and splits as a signed sum of two shorter ones, with the chord canceled. Successive splitting gives integral generation by four-cycles. In general, express the small cycles in the fundamental basis from a spanning tree by reading their signed non-tree coordinates. The hypothesis is decidable by the Smith normal form of the resulting integer matrix. [Golumbic–Goss](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.3190020209), [Kannan–Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040), [Rybnikov–Zaslavsky on tests for balance in abelian gain graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0210052).

Construct a colored relational graph of the following data.

1. Include the base support with its two parts as colors. The encoding can also include external vertex marks or exact arc costs when comparing weighted lifts.
2. Introduce a vertex of color $(d_i,m_i)$ for each field factor $A_i$. Attach one vertex for every $F$-embedding $\phi:A_i\to K_{d_i}$, where $K_a$ is a chosen model of $\mathbb F_{q^a}$. Include directed edges $\phi\mapsto \operatorname{Frob}_q\circ\phi$. Each factor contributes a directed cycle of length $d_i$ on its embeddings.
3. For each ordered simple cycle $C$ of length at most $\ell$ and each ordered pair of embeddings $\phi:A_i\to K_d$, $\psi:A_j\to K_e$, include an ordered relation on the vertices of $C$, then $\phi,\psi$. Its color is the ratio
   $$
   R_{C,\phi,\psi}=
   \frac{\iota_{d,a}(\phi(H_{C,i}))}{\iota_{e,a}(\psi(H_{C,j}))}
   \quad\text{in }K_a,\qquad a=\operatorname{lcm}(d,e),
   $$
   where $H_{C,i}$ denotes the component of $H_C$ in $A_i$.

For the graph pair choose one common model $K_a$ for each required degree and one common $F$-embedding $\iota_{b,a}:K_b\to K_a$ for each required pair with $b\mid a$. Use the identity when $a=b$. These choices need only be shared across the two encodings. Compatibility across triples of degrees is unnecessary. Each ratio lives in a field of extension degree at most $r^2$, and no full splitting field is needed. To find the embeddings, one may find the unique degree-$b$ subfield of $K_a$ as the kernel of $x\mapsto x^{q^b}-x$ and use relative finite-field isomorphism. List the $d_i$ embeddings of $A_i$ by composing any relative isomorphism with successive Frobenius powers. Irreducible polynomial construction and algebra decomposition give Las Vegas polynomial expected preprocessing in the bit size. The degree bounds ensure all choices and labels have polynomial-size representations. [Lenstra, Theorem 9.3](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf), [Shoup](https://doi.org/10.1006/jsco.1994.1025).

A tensor isomorphism gives a support alignment and a conjugacy at the root between algebras. It induces matching factors, module multiplicities, and Frobenius cycles of embeddings. Every corresponding rooted cycle transport matches up to one scalar in $F^\times$, which cancels from each ratio. This proves invariance under all tensor basis choices and representative gauges.

Conversely, a graph match determines a support alignment. The induced map on embedding vertices commutes with Frobenius and respects factors. A matched pair $\phi,\phi'$ determines the factor map $\alpha_i=(\phi')^{-1}\phi$. Commuting with Frobenius implies that this same map works for all matched embeddings on that factor. These factor maps give one $F$-algebra isomorphism $\alpha:A\to A'$. For a small cycle $C$, put $U_C=H'_C\alpha(H_C)^{-1}$ in $(A')^\times$. The ratio constraints force the values of $U_C$ at any two embeddings to coincide after embedding them into their pair field. In particular, pair an embedding with its Frobenius successor in the same field. This forces its value to be fixed by $q$-Frobenius and hence to belong to $F^\times$. For degree one this is automatic. The remaining pair constraints force all those values in $F^\times$ to be equal. Thus $U_C$ is a single scalar in $F^\times$.

The map from based closed walks to $A^\times/F^\times$ factors through $\Lambda$ because the target is abelian. Concretely, after tree normalization it multiplies the fundamental gains to the signed exponents of the non-tree coordinates. Consequently the equality $[H'_C]=[\alpha(H_C)]$ on the short cycles gives equality on all cycles. One may even weaken integral generation to $\Lambda=\Lambda_{\le\ell}+M\Lambda$, where $M=\operatorname{lcm}_i(q^{d_i}-1)$ annihilates the projective unit group. This condition can be checked by adjoining $M$ times the fundamental basis to the integer matrix used above.

Because the module multiplicities match, $\alpha$ is realized by a linear map $K$ of the root spaces. In each $V_i$, choose a basis as an $A_i$-module, choose a matching basis in the target, and send the coefficients via $\alpha_i$. These bases can be found by accumulating independent spans over $F$. Now use the same spanning tree in the aligned graphs and set $X_v=P'_v K P_v^{-1}$. The projective switch equation holds on tree arcs directly and on the other arcs by fundamental holonomy equality. These switches construct the tensor isomorphism, which can be checked by direct multiplication on the input. With costs included they also give a weighted isomorphism of the projective covers and transport lifted ATSP tours.

For fixed $\ell$ there are at most $O(|V(G)|^\ell)$ short ordered cycles and at most $r^2$ embedding pairs. Represent bounded-arity relations as vertices with distinct colored ports for their argument positions. Encode each color ID $c\geq 0$ by attaching two private cycles of lengths $3$ and $4+c$ at its vertex. The old vertices have degree at least four and the private vertices have degree two, so an ordinary graph isomorphism recovers the colors. Thus Babai's GI bound gives an expected quasipolynomial decision algorithm on the recognized class. The exponent of the polynomial preprocessing can depend on $\ell$. The result gives no such bound for arbitrary Tensor Isomorphism, whose instances need not have invertible-line blocks, commuting semisimple holonomy, or short generators. It is a polynomial-size graph reduction on the stated class, as distinct from the general-size transfer bounds in Grochow and Qiao's linear-length reductions. [Babai](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03547), [Grochow–Qiao IV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16317).

## A polynomial test under any fixed support alignment

An alignment removes the short-cycle hypothesis. Use one common spanning tree and put the fundamental transports into ordered tuples. Choose any source embedding in any factor. For each possible target embedding of matching degree and multiplicity, compute the ratio of target to source eigenvalues on every fundamental transport. Reject the candidate if any ratio is outside $F^\times$. Otherwise divide each target transport by its proposed scalar.

For every source and rescaled target factor form the ordered tuple of eigenvalues of all fundamental transports under one embedding. Record the orbit of this tuple under simultaneous Frobenius, along with the field degree and module multiplicity. Compare the multisets of records. Matching the records constructs a factor bijection and matching embeddings, which give the algebra map and switches above. If an equivalence exists, its image of the chosen embedding is among the at most $r$ candidates and supplies exactly the scalars tried. This proves both directions. Tuple orbit size is at most the factor degree, so the test takes polynomial expected bit time and produces a directly checkable witness.

## Nonsplit finite control

Over $F=\mathbb F_3$ let $\theta^2=-1$ in $\mathbb F_9$, and let $g=1+\theta$. Its square is $-\theta$ and its fourth power is $-1$, so it has order eight. Use support $K_{2,3}$ and orient the three length-two paths from one left vertex to the other. Make the first edge on each path the identity and let the second edge act on $F\oplus\mathbb F_9$ as $1\oplus (x\mapsto g^a x)$. Set the three exponents to $(0,6,1)$. Fundamental holonomies commute and generate $F\times\mathbb F_9$.

The positive comparison $(0,2,3)$ applies a common Frobenius to the exponent list modulo eight. The negative comparison $(0,6,3)$ conjugates only the last entry by Frobenius. Support alignments permute the three paths and may reverse their orientation. Normalizing the reference path adds a common exponent shift. On the field factor Frobenius multiplies all exponents by three, so a match must send the source exponent set to the target by $x\mapsto ax+b$ modulo eight with $a\in\{1,3,5,7\}$. A closed gain has value one on the unique degree-one factor, so an additional projective scalar would violate that factor match. No such affine match exists for the negative comparison.

An independent exhaustive control tested all $12$ part-preserving support alignments and all $11{,}232$ matrices of $\operatorname{GL}(3,3)$ as root switches, checking simultaneous conjugacy up to scalar on the two fundamental gains. The positive comparison had two successful alignments with $16$ root maps each. The negative comparison had none. Comparing all oriented four-cycle ratio labels under the possible embedding matches gave the same two positive alignments and zero negative ones.

The new synthesis is the short-cycle, Frobenius-compatible encoding with witness reconstruction for recovered tensor blocks. The cited gain-graph and finite-field algorithms supply its separate ingredients. Priority for this combination remains unverified.
