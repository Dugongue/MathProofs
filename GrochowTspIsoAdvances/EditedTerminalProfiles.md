# Exact Weisfeiler–Leman width for edited TSP terminal profiles

The entire joint distribution of shortest-path metrics on $r$ marked vertices, after arbitrary edits supported on those vertices, is determined by $(r-1)$-dimensional Weisfeiler–Leman (WL) for $r\ge3$. Unmarked vertices can still be used as transit vertices. This applies to Steiner TSP, path TSP, and other route objectives computed on the resulting marked metrics. The width is exact even for the histogram of unedited TSP optima on connected diameter-two graphs. For every odd prime $r$, it is exact even for the cheapest possible bin, which counts $r$-vertex sets admitting a Hamiltonian cycle. Explicit Cai–Fürer–Immerman (CFI) witnesses also give a matching $n^{\Theta(r)}$ exponent for square-symmetric circuits in an appropriate range.

The earlier graph-edit result adds the entire anchor tuple to every pattern-decomposition bag. For shortest paths this overcounts the width. After expanding the anchor avoidance constraints, each surviving path segment attaches to at most two anchors. One central bag then carries all interactions between segments. The circuit comparison uses Grochow–Urisman's transfer from separating circuits to WL. [Grochow–Urisman, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26244v2)

## An exact theorem for simultaneous anchored edits

Let $G$ be a finite simple unweighted graph on $n$ vertices. Use all ordered injective $r$-tuples $\mathbf a=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$ as anchors. A fixed panel of edit rules can delete some anchors, insert or delete edges between remaining anchors, and change the lengths of edges between anchors to positive numbers. An edit's eligibility and choices depend only on the original adjacency bits between anchor positions. Edges with an unmarked endpoint stay unchanged except for deletion of an incident anchor. For each tuple record a joint vector containing an eligibility flag for each rule and, where applicable, all distances between retained anchors in the edited host. Use $\infty$ when no path exists. Retained positions and the original anchor adjacency pattern may be recorded as well.

**Theorem.** On $n$-vertex graphs, $(r-1)$-WL equivalence implies equality of these joint multisets for every $r\ge3$. This includes the joint distribution of closed or fixed-endpoint Steiner TSP optima on designated retained positions, which can be computed from the distance vector. For two anchors the same proof gives a 2-WL bound. The proof uses integer walk counts and does not require a bound on the treewidth of the host. [Dell–Grohe–Rattan, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.08876)

**Proof.** For each pair of distinct anchor indices $i,j$, let $W_{ij,d}(\mathbf a)$ count length-$d$ walks from $a_i$ to $a_j$ in $G$ with every internal vertex outside the entire anchor set. Record these counts for $2\le d\le n-1$, in addition to all original adjacency bits at the anchors. Consider the graph on retained anchor positions with an edge of length $\min\{d:W_{ij,d}>0\}$ when available, together with each retained or inserted direct anchor edge at its edited length. Any shortest path in the edited host splits into these kinds of segments at its successive anchors. A finite shortest path is simple since edge lengths are positive, so segments in the unedited interior use at most $n-1$ edges. Conversely each compressed segment expands to a walk of the designated length. Thus the compressed graph gives exactly the edited distances. This works jointly across all rules from the same original vector.

Every mixed moment of the original vector is a sum over injective anchor images. A product of adjacency coordinates contributes edges between anchors. A walk factor contributes a path between its two indicated anchors. At each internal path vertex $x$ impose the avoidance factor $1-\sum_{\ell=1}^r[\phi(x)=a_\ell]$, which is its indicator on injective anchor images. Expand all factors. A term pins some internal vertices to anchors. Treat every such vertex as its indicated anchor. The resulting graph consists of the $r$ anchors, a graph on those anchors, and paths whose only contacts with the anchors are their endpoints. These paths are the segments between consecutive pins in each original path. A segment can have the same anchor at both ends. An explicit self-loop, such as a length-one segment with coincident endpoints, contributes zero in a simple host. Parallel edges can be identified because adjacency bits are Boolean.

Use a central bag with all $r$ anchors. Attach a width-two decomposition for each segment. One explicit decomposition for a segment from $a$ to $b$ adds $a$ to every adjacent-edge bag along the segment, then attaches the bag containing both $a$ and $b$ to the central bag. It works also for $a=b$. The width is at most $\max\{2,r-1\}$. Finally remove the injectivity restriction by inclusion–exclusion on equalities of pairs of anchor images. This identifies anchors within the central bag and at path ends. Each resulting graph again has the same segment form and width bound.

Every mixed moment is therefore an integer linear combination of homomorphism counts from graphs of width at most $\max\{2,r-1\}$. WL equivalence in that dimension implies equality of the moments. For the two hosts being compared, the union of their observed vectors is finite. For any vector in this union, multiply univariate Lagrange indicators for its coordinate values to get a polynomial giving 1 on that vector and 0 on the others. Equality of moments gives equal multiplicity for every vector. Applying the distance and route calculations to each vector proves the theorem. $\square$

The shortest-path metric on marked vertices gives the closed or fixed-endpoint Steiner walk optimum in the host. Any route on the metric expands to a host walk. In the other direction, record the marked cities visited in order of first appearance and reserve the designated final city for the last position in the open case. Each consecutive metric distance bounds the corresponding walk segment. This proves equality of optimum values.

## Exact width with explicit witnesses

Write $C_r(G,z)$ for the number of unordered $r$-vertex sets whose closed Steiner TSP optimum is $z$ in a connected unweighted host. The theorem gives width at most $r-1$ for the histogram. We exhibit hosts of order $r2^{r-2}+1$ on which $(r-2)$-WL cannot distinguish the graphs but the histogram differs.

For a fixed isomorphism-invariant property $\Phi$ of graphs on $r$ vertices, let $F_\Phi(G)$ count the $r$-vertex sets inducing $\Phi$. As a Boolean function of the edge bits of $K_r$, $\Phi$ has a unique multilinear expansion. Its coefficient on the full edge set is
$$
b_\Phi = \sum_{J\subseteq E(K_r)}
 (-1)^{\binom r2-|J|}\Phi(([r],J)).
$$
Sum the expansion over injections of the $r$ vertices into $G$ and divide by $r!$. Inclusion–exclusion for injectivity converts each term to ordinary homomorphism counts on at most $r$ vertices. Only the complete monomial with the discrete partition contributes $\operatorname{hom}(K_r,G)$, with coefficient $b_\Phi/r!$. Every other pattern has width at most $r-2$. For a noncomplete graph on exactly $r$ vertices, choose a missing edge $uv$ and use two bags omitting $u$ and $v$, respectively. This also shows why no other $r$-vertex pattern can account for the top-width difference. [Curticapean–Dell–Marx on the homomorphism basis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.01595) · [Neuen, Theorem 2 on motif-parameter width](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17053)

For the explicit pair take Roberson's parity lifts $X_0,X_1$ of $K_r$. A vertex over $i$ is a bit assignment to incident base edges with even parity, except in $X_1$ where one fiber has odd parity. Lifted vertices over adjacent $i,j$ are joined exactly when their bits on $ij$ agree. The cited oddomorphism criterion says that differing homomorphism counts into these lifts require a weak oddomorphism from the test graph to $K_r$. The treewidth monotonicity theorem for such maps then gives equal counts for every test of width at most $r-2$. Thus $X_0$ and $X_1$ are $(r-2)$-WL equivalent. For a homomorphism of $K_r$, the projection to the base is a permutation. Adjacent bit agreement gives one bit per base edge and imposes the parity constraints at all vertices. The even system has $2^{\binom r2-r+1}$ solutions by the rank of the binary incidence matrix. The odd system is inconsistent because the sum of all desired parities is 1. Therefore the difference of clique homomorphism counts is exactly $r!\,2^{\binom r2-r+1}$. [Roberson, Theorem 3.13](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2026.07.023) · [Neuen, Proposition 16](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17053)

Adjoin one universal vertex to each lift. WL equivalence persists at the same dimension: to count homomorphisms into a graph with an added universal vertex, choose its preimage as an independent subset $S$ of the pattern and count homomorphisms of the induced subgraph of the pattern on vertices outside $S$ into the original graph. Deleting pattern vertices cannot increase treewidth. Contributions to $F_\Phi$ from sets containing the new universal vertex are counts of an induced graph property on $r-1$ vertices, so they agree between the two lifts. As a result the difference of $F_\Phi$ on the connected diameter-two witnesses is exactly $b_\Phi\,2^{\binom r2-r+1}$. An arbitrary equal number of isolated vertices can be added before the universal one. For homomorphism counts, expand over which connected pattern components use isolated vertices. The same WL equivalence and clique difference hold at every larger host order.

Two choices of $\Phi$ give exact-width route statistics.

- **The most expensive bin, for every $r\ge3$.** On a diameter-two host the metric consists of distances 1 and 2. A metric tour of length $2r$ is optimal exactly for an independent set. If the set contains an edge, some cyclic order uses it and costs at most $2r-1$. For the independent-set property $b_\Phi=(-1)^{\binom r2}$, so this bin differs on the pair.
- **The cheapest bin, for every odd prime $r$.** Length $r$ is attainable exactly when the induced graph is Hamiltonian. To prove $b_\Phi\ne0$, take the above alternating sum modulo $r$ and translate the base labels in $\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z$. Nonfixed graphs occur in orbits of size $r$. A fixed graph is the union of undirected difference classes. Each class has $r$ edges and is a Hamiltonian cycle, since every nonzero step generates the prime cyclic group. The fixed graphs with a Hamiltonian cycle are exactly the nonempty unions. Their alternating sum with sign $(-1)^{|J|}$ is $\sum_{j=1}^{(r-1)/2}\binom{(r-1)/2}{j}(-1)^j=-1$. Hence $b_\Phi\ne0$ over the integers. This uses the established alternating-enumerator method specialized to the exact cheapest-bin question. [Dörfler–Roth–Schmitt–Wellnitz and subsequent edge-monotone counting results](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.08988#A1)

## Symmetric circuits and quantitative separation

For a fixed panel of unweighted edits and closed or prescribed-endpoint terminal tours, the ordered histograms have an explicit square-symmetric arithmetic circuit on Boolean adjacency inputs. For each ordered anchor tuple construct its edited adjacency. Compute reachability from each retained anchor by a walk of at most $d$ edges for $d=0,\ldots,n-1$. The update at a vertex takes the Boolean OR of the preceding value and all products of preceding values with entering adjacency bits. Implement OR as $1-\prod_i(1-x_i)$. The difference of two successive reachability bits is the exact-distance indicator.

Use a Boolean Held–Karp recurrence with indicator states for the visited set of positions, last position and exact total cost. Every attainable cost is at most $r(n-1)$. Closing the tour or using the designated final position produces an attainable-cost vector. The first attainable entry is the optimum. For each cost bin sum the flags over ordered anchor tuples. Host permutations act on the tuple and vertex gate indices and leave the gate wiring invariant. The Boolean part is valid over every field. Counting outputs are interpreted in that field, so characteristic zero gives exact integer totals. Counting gates and wires, the family has size at most $2^{O(r)}n^{r+O(1)}$ for a fixed number of rules, with an additional factor for the size of the rule descriptions. [Held–Karp](https://doi.org/10.1137/0110015)

In characteristic zero the width witnesses imply a size lower bound $n^{\Omega(r)}$ for square-symmetric circuits computing the full histogram, and for the cheapest bin alone at odd prime $r$. This holds for sufficiently large $r$ and all $n\ge r2^{r-2}+1$, with an absolute exponent constant. For a circuit computing a numerical output that differs on the witness pair, subtract its value on one member to obtain a one-dimensional separating invariant. Apply Grochow–Urisman Theorem 5.2 with its slack parameter fixed to $1/2$. A size bound $n^k$ with $k$ a sufficiently small absolute multiple of $r$ would imply distinguishability by $O(k)$-WL below dimension $r-1$. The technical bound $k\le\sqrt n/2$ holds at these witness sizes for large $r$. This contradicts the explicit pair. The numerical circuit statement concerns Boolean inputs and the stated symmetry model. It does not require a formal symbolic identity for the Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial. [Grochow–Urisman, Theorem 5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26244v2)

The proof combines established WL and graph motif tools with a segment decomposition for edited shortest paths. The exact distribution theorem, the explicit minimum and maximum bin witnesses, and their simultaneous application to TSP are the contributions here. The mathematical proof is informal. The [control program](check_terminal_profiles.py) compares 240 edited distance tables against full-host shortest paths. It independently counts the most expensive bins at three, four, and five terminals, with differences of $-2$, $8$, and $64$ on the witness pairs. It also counts cheapest bins at three and five terminals, with differences of $2$ and $-384$. Priority for the combined result remains unverified.
