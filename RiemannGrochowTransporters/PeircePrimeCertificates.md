# Small-prime tests for multiplication tensors of square-zero algebras

Assume the Riemann hypothesis for Dedekind zeta functions. For a class of associative algebra multiplication tensors over the rationals, tensor isomorphism is equivalent to agreement at an explicit finite list of good primes. For algebras with a cycle as their geometric graph, the list and all its tests can be computed in deterministic bit-polynomial time. Two fixed direct summands give the same result for special-linear tensor isomorphism. The proof is informal.

## Statement

Let $R=\mathbb Z[1/N]$ for an integer $N\geq 1$. Let $A=S\oplus J$ and $B=T\oplus I$ be unital associative $R$-algebras given by structure constants in these decompositions. Here $S,T$ are finite free commutative unital $R$-algebras with étale generic fibers, and $J,I$ are finite free $R$-modules with their given bimodule structures. The product in $S\oplus J$ is $(s,x)(t,y)=(st,sy+xt)$, and likewise for $T\oplus I$.

Over an algebraic closure, make one vertex for each primitive idempotent $e_i$ of the commutative quotient and put weight $\dim(e_iJe_j)$ on the ordered pair $(i,j)$, allowing loops and zero weights. Suppose the two geometric weighted graphs are isomorphic to a graph $W$. Write $G=\operatorname{Aut}(W)$ and let $U$ be an integer upper bound for $|G|$. Assume that any two elementwise conjugate homomorphisms into $G$ from a finite group are globally conjugate. This condition holds for abelian groups, generalized dihedral groups, and their finite direct products. In particular it holds for the bidirected cycle of length $n\geq 3$ with every arrow of weight one, where $U=2n$.

In the supplied bases let $d_S,d_T$ be the nonzero numerators of the respective regular trace discriminants. Put $M=2N|d_Sd_T|$, $b=2U^2$, and write $\ell(z)$ for the binary length of a positive integer $z$. Define

$$
P=\bigl(4[(b-1)\ell(M)+b\ell(b)]+3b+5\bigr)^2.
$$

Then the multiplication tensors of $A_{\mathbb Q}$ and $B_{\mathbb Q}$ are isomorphic if and only if their reductions are isomorphic over $\mathbb F_p$ for every prime $p\le P$ with $p\nmid M$. The prime list is nonempty. If the two geometric graphs differ, the rational tensors and every good reduction are inequivalent regardless of the automorphism groups. If the geometric graph is common and $G$ is a finite product of abelian or generalized dihedral groups, rational nonisomorphism implies that the set of good primes distinguishing the tensors has natural density among the primes at least $1/8$. The density claim is unconditional.

## Classifying the tensors by the graph and its Galois action

Over any field, two unital associative algebra products are tensor-isomorphic exactly when the algebras are isomorphic. Write a tensor isomorphism in bilinear-map coordinates as $h(xy)=f(x)g(y)$ with $f,g,h$ invertible. In the target algebra put $a=f(1)$ and $b'=g(1)$. The identities $h(x)=f(x)b'=ag(x)$ and surjectivity show that multiplication on the appropriate side by $a$ and $b'$ is invertible. A one-sided inverse in a finite-dimensional unital algebra is two-sided, so both elements are units. Now $h(xy)=h(x)(ab')^{-1}h(y)$, and $(ab')^{-1}h(-)$ is an algebra isomorphism. Conversely any algebra isomorphism gives a tensor isomorphism. [Grochow and Qiao on tensor isomorphism and algebra isomorphism](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

Over any field where the commutative quotient is étale, the square-zero ideal is the Jacobson radical. The action of the quotient on this radical is intrinsic because changing a lift by a radical element changes its action by zero. Therefore an algebra isomorphism induces an equivariant isomorphism of the weighted graphs over the separable closure. Conversely such a graph isomorphism identifies the étale quotients by the correspondence between finite étale algebras and finite Galois sets. The bimodule can be viewed as a module over $S\otimes S$ because $S$ is commutative. This étale algebra is a product of fields. A module over it is classified by its dimension over each field factor. These dimensions are precisely the weights on the corresponding Galois orbits of ordered vertex pairs. Hence the graph isomorphism lifts to a bimodule isomorphism and then to an isomorphism of the displayed algebras.

After inverting $M$, the discriminant criterion makes $S,T$ finite étale. Over the resulting localization of $\mathbb Z$, each connected component of $S\otimes S$ is a Dedekind domain or a field. The corresponding component of $J$ is torsion-free over this domain. Indeed its underlying module is torsion-free over the base and every nonzero element of the component algebra becomes invertible in its generic field. Thus the module is locally free of constant rank on that component. The same argument applies to $T,I$. It follows that the weighted graphs specialize unchanged at every good prime, with the specialized Galois action given by Frobenius. For two common geometric graphs, choose identifications with $W$ to obtain homomorphisms $\alpha,\beta$ into $G$. The classifying condition over $\mathbb Q$ is global conjugacy of $\alpha,\beta$. Over $\mathbb F_p$ the condition is conjugacy of their values at a Frobenius element because the absolute Galois group of $\mathbb F_p$ is procyclic.

## Simultaneous conjugacy in generalized dihedral groups

Write $G=D\rtimes C_2$ with $D$ finite abelian, additively written, and the nontrivial element $s$ acting by inversion. An element of $D$ is conjugate only to itself and its negative. Two elements $as$ and $a's$ are conjugate exactly when $a-a'\in 2D$, since conjugation by a rotation changes the coordinate by twice a rotation and inversion gives no further classes.

Suppose $\alpha,\beta$ are elementwise conjugate. Their two maps to $C_2$ agree. On the common kernel $H$ their images lie in $D$. Every element lies in the kernel of either $\beta-\alpha$ or $\beta+\alpha$. A group cannot be the union of two proper subgroups, so one kernel is all of $H$. After possibly conjugating $\beta$ by $s$, the maps agree on $H$. If $H$ has index two, choose $t$ outside $H$. Its two images are conjugate by a rotation because their coordinates differ by an element of $2D$. This conjugation fixes the images of $H$ and makes the images of $t$ agree. Hence the homomorphisms are globally conjugate. In an abelian group elementwise conjugacy is equality, and for direct products the argument works componentwise. [Larsen on element-conjugate homomorphisms and acceptability](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02937514)

The same calculation gives the density estimate. If the sign maps differ, half the elements already have nonconjugate images. Otherwise let $H$ be the common sign kernel. When neither of the two rotation kernels equals $H$, their union has size at most $3|H|/4$. When both have index two they either coincide, giving union fraction $1/2$, or have an intersection of index four, giving $3/4$. If exactly one has index two, the intersection has index at most twice the index of the other, giving union fraction at most $1/2+1/6$. If both indices exceed two, the union fraction is at most $2/3$. When one rotation kernel is all of $H$, align the restrictions as above. If the homomorphisms are still globally nonconjugate, $H$ has index two and their images at $t$ differ modulo $2D$. The same discrepancy then holds for every element of $tH$. Thus in every case of nonconjugacy at least $1/8$ of the group distinguishes the maps. For an abelian group the unequal values occupy at least half. In a direct product one coordinate suffices. Apply ordinary Chebotarev to the union of distinguishing conjugacy classes.

## Bounding the first distinguishing prime

The joint action factors through the common splitting field $K$ of the two sets of vertices. Its Galois group embeds in $G\times G$, so $[K : \mathbb Q]\le U^2$, and it is unramified at all primes away from $M$ by the étale models. If the rational tensors are inequivalent and the geometric graphs agree, a conjugacy class in $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)$ has nonconjugate images by the group condition. To force the prime away from all excluded primes, use $L=K(\sqrt{\operatorname{rad}(M)})$, where $\operatorname{rad}(M)$ is the product of the distinct prime divisors of $M$. This extension of $\mathbb Q$ has degree $d\le b$, is nontrivial since $M$ is even, and is ramified at every prime dividing $M$ and no others. Its relevant classes are those above the selected class of $K$.

In the notation of Bach and Sorenson, their Theorem 5.1 with base field $\mathbb Q$ uses the zero-free right half of the critical strip for the zeta functions of $L$ and $\mathbb Q$, as specified in their Section 2. The nontrivial field $L/\mathbb Q$ is finite Galois, and the theorem gives an unramified rational prime in the prescribed Frobenius class at most $(4\log|\Delta_L|+2.5d+5)^2$. The discriminant bound for a finite Galois field gives $\log|\Delta_L|\le(d-1)\log\operatorname{rad}(|\Delta_L|)+d\log d$. The radical here is at most $M$. Since $\log z\le \ell(z)$ and $d\le b$, the prime is at most $P$. This proves rejection of the rational NO cases with a common graph. In a rational YES case the globally conjugate actions give equivalent reductions at every good prime. Taking just the quadratic field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\operatorname{rad}(M)})$ in the same bound proves nonemptiness of the prime list, including for pairs with different geometric graphs. [Bach and Sorenson, Theorem 5.1 and Section 2](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-96-00763-6) · [Mayle and Wang, Corollary 6 and Lemma 7](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.13002)

The modulus and cutoff can be computed without factoring $M$ or constructing $K$ or $L$. Discriminants are determinants of trace matrices in the supplied bases, with polynomial bit length. A sieve up to $P$ and gcd with $M$ produce the list. This is bit-polynomial whenever $U$ is polynomially bounded in the input size. The analytic hypothesis used here covers the Dedekind zeta function of the joint extension after adjoining the quadratic field. The Dirichlet-character case alone supplies this hypothesis when the extension is abelian.

## Algorithm and special-linear variant for cycles

For the promise that both geometric graphs are bidirected cycles of length $n\geq 3$, prime selection is deterministic and bit-polynomial because $U=2n$. Each modular test is also deterministic and bit-polynomial. Construct $F=\mathbb F_{p^{2n}}$ in time polynomial in $p,n$ by deterministic irreducible-polynomial construction. This splits both étale vertex algebras because the order of each Frobenius element divides $2n$. To recover all primitive idempotents, take an $F$-basis $(v_i)$ of the split étale algebra and an $\mathbb F_p$-basis $(c_j)$ of $F$. In the split algebra the elements $\sum_{a=0}^{2n-1}(c_jv_i)^{p^a}$ have coordinates in $\mathbb F_p$. They distinguish any two coordinates because some $v_i$ separates them and the field trace pairing is nondegenerate. Refine joint eigenspaces of their multiplication operators using eigenvalues in $\mathbb F_p$, by Gaussian elimination. This gives the one-dimensional coordinate lines. Normalize each nonzero coordinate-line generator to an idempotent by dividing it by the nonzero scalar by which it multiplies itself. The bimodule action then gives every weight by a rank computation. Applying Frobenius to the coefficients of the idempotents in the original $\mathbb F_p$-basis yields the vertex permutation. To check whether two cycles with these permutations are equivariantly isomorphic, try the at most $2n$ maps determined by the image of one vertex and one of its neighbors. This needs no tensor-isomorphism oracle. [Shoup on deterministic construction of irreducible polynomials](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

For the special-linear variant replace each algebra $C$ in the cycle class by $C^+=C\times k\times k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ over each ground field $k$, using the integral direct-product model. The two added vertices in the weighted graph are isolated from the cycle and are distinguished by loop weights zero and one. Thus an isomorphism of the enlarged algebras exists exactly when the original algebras are isomorphic. In the bilinear-map convention, a tensor stabilizer is a triple $(f,g,h)$ with $h(xy)=f(x)g(y)$. Left multiplication by the central unit whose coordinate in $k$ is $a$ gives a stabilizer with determinant triple $(a,1,a)$. Right multiplication with coordinate $b'$ gives $(1,b',b')$. The algebra automorphism scaling $\epsilon$ by $c$ gives $(c,c,c)$. A target determinant triple $(d_1,d_2,d_3)$ is their product with $c=d_1d_2/d_3$, $a=d_1/c$, and $b'=d_2/c$. Hence every tensor isomorphism of the enlarged tensors can be adjusted to have determinant one in every slot over its original field. Dualizing the input slots just inverts the corresponding determinants. The same modulus and prime cutoff therefore apply simultaneously to general-linear and special-linear isomorphism for these enlarged tensors.

## An example with identical étale quotients

Let $E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{11})^+$, the real cyclotomic field of degree five, and $\sigma$ be induced by $\zeta_{11}\mapsto\zeta_{11}^2$. This is a generator of its cyclic Galois group since $2^5\equiv-1\pmod{11}$ and $2\not\equiv\pm1\pmod{11}$. Use the ring of integers as the integral vertex algebra. For $s=1,2$ let the radical be two copies of this ring, with ordinary left action and right actions twisted by $\sigma^s$ and $\sigma^{-s}$, respectively. Geometrically these are the two undirected five-cycles on the same vertex set with steps $\pm1$ and $\pm2$, interpreted as bidirected graphs. A rational isomorphism would induce an automorphism of $E$. Every such automorphism is a rotation on the vertex set, which preserves the step size and cannot identify the two graphs. Thus the multiplication tensors are rationally inequivalent.

At a good prime with nonidentity Frobenius, any equivariant permutation of the five vertices is again a rotation, since the centralizer of a 5-cycle in the symmetric group is the cyclic group it generates. Therefore the modular tensors are inequivalent. At primes with identity Frobenius the graphs are equivariantly isomorphic. For instance, $3\equiv-2^3\pmod{11}$, so the prime 3 distinguishes them. By Chebotarev the distinguishing primes have density $4/5$. The étale quotients themselves are identical over the rationals and at every good prime.

## A pair of 4-cycles attaining density $1/8$

Work over $R=\mathbb Z[1/6]$. For $d=2,18$ take $S_d=R[u]/(u^4-d)$ and $J_d=R[u,i]/(u^4-d,i^2+1)$. Use the two embeddings of $S_d$ in $J_d$ taking $u$ to $u$ and $iu$ for the left and right actions. These are finite free algebras over $R$ and étale because both polynomial discriminants are units there. In particular $\operatorname{disc}(X^4-d)=-256d^3$, so the only excluded primes are 2 and 3. The algebra $S_d\oplus J_d$ has dimension 12. In the geometric fiber, the eight points of $J_d$ index the ordered pairs $(u,iu)$ on the four roots, with two choices of the image of $i$. These are exactly the directed edges of a bidirected 4-cycle, each of weight one.

Put $a=2^{1/4}$ and $t=a\sqrt3=18^{1/4}$, using positive real roots. The splitting field $K=\mathbb Q(a,i)$ of $X^4-2$ has degree eight because $X^4-2$ is Eisenstein at 2 and $\mathbb Q(a)$ is real. Its rotation $r$ sends $a$ to $ia$ and fixes $i$. Complex conjugation $s$ satisfies $srs=r^{-1}$, giving the dihedral group of order eight. The field is unramified outside 2 by the polynomial discriminant, so it intersects $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$, which ramifies at 3, in $\mathbb Q$. The common splitting field of the two quartics is $K(\sqrt3)$ because $t/a=\sqrt3$. Its Galois group is the direct product of the dihedral group and a group of order two generated by the sign change on $\sqrt3$.

Write an element of that product as $(r^x s^z,y)$ with $x\bmod 4$ and $y,z\bmod 2$. Label the roots of each quartic by multiplying its positive real root by successive powers of $i$. The two induced actions on the cycles are $r^x s^z$ and $r^{x+2y}s^z$. When $z=1$, the two reflections are conjugate because their exponents have the same parity. When $z=0$, the two rotations are nonconjugate exactly when $y=1$ and $x$ is even. This happens for two of the sixteen group elements, so the density is exactly $1/8$. It also gives the exact progression of distinguishing good primes. The conditions $z=0$, $x$ even, and $y=1$ are respectively $(-1/p)=1$, $(2/p)=1$, and $(3/p)=-1$, by the action on $i,\sqrt 2,\sqrt 3$. Quadratic reciprocity reduces these three conditions to $p\equiv17\pmod{24}$. Thus 17 is the first distinguishing prime, and the same progression distinguishes the special-linear pair after adjoining the two direct summands.

This result concerns multiplication tensors of the stated square-zero algebra presentations. The finite test compares two potentially nontrivial Galois actions. Its weighted edges retain information beyond the decomposition types of the étale quotients. The special-linear claim applies after the displayed direct-product construction.
