# Statistical zero knowledge for complex tensor isomorphism

## Theorem

Fix a polynomial bound $K(N)\geq 1$ with positive integer values. The input consists of explicit binary-encoded rational tensors $T_0,T_1$ of the same shape and bit length $N$. Let $G$ be the product of the general linear groups on the tensor factors, or the product of the special linear groups on those factors. The test is whether $T_0,T_1$ are in the same orbit under $G(\mathbb C)$.

The promise includes every negative instance. A positive instance is promised when at least one geometric irreducible component of the transporter has at most $K(N)$ conjugates under $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/\mathbb Q)$. The promise problem belongs unconditionally to $\mathsf{SZK}$, and hence to $\mathsf{AM}\cap\mathsf{coAM}$ in the promise-problem sense. It also has a randomized polynomial-time reduction to testing isomorphism over explicitly presented finite fields, using at most $K(N)$ queries over extensions of a single random prime field.

In particular, the theorem applies when one geometric stabilizer is connected or has at most $K(N)$ components. A nonempty geometric transporter is a translate of that stabilizer. A single Galois-invariant component also suffices when the total number of components is larger. For example, the multiplication tensor of the split algebra $\mathbb Q^n$ has a geometric general linear stabilizer with $n!$ components, indexed by permutations of its primitive idempotents, all defined over $\mathbb Q$. Indeed, an isotopy of a unital commutative algebra has the form $f(x)=a\varphi(x)$, $g(x)=b\varphi(x)$, $h(x)=ab\varphi(x)$ for units $a,b$ and an algebra automorphism $\varphi$. This follows by evaluating $h(xy)=f(x)g(y)$ at the unit in each argument.

## Specialization at random large primes

The transporter $Z$ is an affine algebraic set. Use matrix variables for each factor and its inverse, impose the inverse equations and impose $g\cdot T_0=T_1$. In the special linear case also impose the determinant-one equations. Clearing denominators gives a system in polynomially many variables with polynomially many equations, polynomially bounded degrees and polynomial coefficient bit lengths. These degree and height bounds also hold for the determinant equations, regardless of how many monomials occur when they are expanded. Exclude primes dividing the original denominators. At any remaining prime, reduction of the system gives the exact finite-characteristic transporter. Over an algebraic closure, we use the reduced algebraic set throughout.

Here is the quantitative specialization fact needed in the proof. Outside a set of at most $2^{P(N)}$ primes for some fixed effective polynomial $P$, the following statements hold.

- If $Z(\mathbb C)$ is empty, then $Z(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)$ is empty.
- If $Z(\mathbb C)$ has a component whose Galois orbit has size $k\leq K(N)$, the components of $Z(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)$ have a Frobenius-invariant subset of size $k$.

For the first statement use the arithmetic effective Nullstellensatz. It gives an identity $\sum_i Q_iF_i=A$ for integral polynomials $Q_i$, with $A$ a nonzero integer of logarithmic height $2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$ or less. Only divisors of $A$ can make the reduced system solvable. [Krick, Pardo and Sombra, Theorem 1](https://www.maia.ub.edu/~sombra/publications/arith-nss/maino.pdf)

For the second statement let $U$ be the union of the $k$ conjugate geometric components and let $Z_r$ be the union of all components of their common dimension $r$. Both are defined over $\mathbb Q$. Let $C$ and $Q$ be the primitive integer Chow forms of $Z_r$ and $U$, respectively. The Chow form of a union of equidimensional components is the product of their distinct absolutely irreducible Chow forms. Hence $Q$ divides $C$ over $\mathbb Q$, and therefore over $\mathbb Z$ by Gauss's lemma. Elliott and Schost give a nonzero integer $\Delta$ of logarithmic height $2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$ or less whose exclusion guarantees that $\overline C$ is the Chow form for the corresponding equidimensional union in the reduced system. It is squarefree over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, and its absolute factors are in bijection with the geometric components of that dimension. [Elliott and Schost, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02279v1)

Both Chow forms have degree at most $2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$. Their logarithmic coefficient heights have the same bound. For detail, successive geometric and arithmetic Bézout bounds applied to the defining hypersurfaces bound the degree and the nonnegative additive height of $Z$ by $2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$. At each intersection the degree is multiplied by at most the defining degree, and the height satisfies the arithmetic Bézout inequality. A hypersurface of polynomial degree and polynomial coefficient bit length in polynomially many variables has polynomial height by its Mahler-measure formula. The bounds pass to the rational union $U$ and to $Z_r$ by additivity and nonnegativity. For a primitive Chow form of an $r$-dimensional equidimensional union, the logarithmic coefficient height is at most the height of the union plus $(r+1)\log(m+2)$ times its degree, where $m$ is the ambient dimension. This is the primitive-form estimate in the proof of Elliott and Schost's Proposition 3.13. [Krick, Pardo and Sombra, Sections 1.2.1 and 1.2.4](https://www.maia.ub.edu/~sombra/publications/arith-nss/maino.pdf) · [Elliott and Schost, Proposition 3.13](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02279v1)

It remains to preserve the number of absolute factors of $Q$. Write $D=\deg Q$ and exclude primes dividing a chosen nonzero coefficient of degree $D$, so that the reduction retains its degree. Also restrict to $p>2D^2$. Substitute
\[
L_i=A_i x+B_i y+C_i
\]
for variable $i$ of $Q$, using independent formal parameters $A_i,B_i,C_i$. Write $f=Q(L_1,\ldots,L_t)$. Generic plane restriction preserves the absolute factors, in characteristic zero and after reduction. The leading homogeneous part of any factor evaluated at the independent $A_i$ is nonzero. Thus each factor of the generic restriction has positive $x$-degree, and since the characteristic is zero or exceeds $D$, $\gcd(f,f_x)=1$. The bidegree is $(D,D)$ by considering the independent $A_i$ and $B_i$ in turn. These facts apply after reduction at $p\nmid\Delta$ because $\overline Q$ divides the squarefree $\overline C$. [Gao, Theorem 5.1 and Lemma 5.2](https://www.math.clemson.edu/~sgao/papers/fac_bipoly.pdf)

Gao's factor-count theorem identifies the number of absolute factors with the nullity over the rational function field of the parameters of the coefficient matrix of
\[
 f(g_y-h_x)+h f_x-g f_y=0,\qquad
 \deg_{\rm bi}g\leq(D-1,D),\quad
 \deg_{\rm bi}h\leq(D,D-1).
\]
The matrix has $O(D^2)$ rows and columns. Its entries are integer polynomials in the parameters with coefficient sum at most $O(D)3^D\|Q\|_1$, where $\|Q\|_1$ is the sum of absolute values of the coefficients. This follows by expanding each substituted monomial and differentiating with degree at most $D$. Also $\log\|Q\|_1\leq h(Q)+t\log(D+1)$ since each of its $t$ variables has degree at most $D$. Choose a nonzero integer coefficient of one maximal nonzero minor, taking the empty minor to be $1$ if the rank is zero. Its logarithmic height is at most $2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$ by the determinant expansion and the bound on the entries. Higher minors vanish identically over the integers. Exclusion of primes dividing the chosen coefficient therefore preserves the rank and the factor count $k$. The factors of $\overline Q$ give a Frobenius-invariant subset of the factors of $\overline C$, since $\overline Q$ has coefficients in $\mathbb F_p$. This proves the second specialization statement. The logarithmic height of each exclusion integer bounds the number of its distinct prime divisors. The upper bounds are used only to choose a prime sampling interval, so constructing the Chow forms and minors is unnecessary. [Gao, Theorem 2.3 and equations (4)–(5)](https://www.math.clemson.edu/~sgao/papers/fac_bipoly.pdf)

## From components to finite-field orbits

Over a finite field, let $H$ be the reduced stabilizer of $T_0$ and $H^\circ$ its identity component. The finite field is perfect, so the reduced stabilizer is a smooth algebraic group. A nonempty geometric transporter is a right $H$-torsor and its geometric components are the orbits of the right action of $H^\circ$. If $F$ is $p$-power Frobenius, a component fixed by $F^d$ contains a point over $\mathbb F_{p^d}$. To see this, choose a point $z$ on the component, so $F^d(z)=zu$ for $u\in H^\circ(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)$. Lang's theorem gives $b\in H^\circ(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)$ with $u=bF^d(b)^{-1}$, making $zb$ fixed. Conversely, a rational point forces its component to be fixed. [Lang, Algebraic Groups Over Finite Fields](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Lang-Algebraic_Groups_Over_Finite_Fields.pdf)

At a good prime in a promised positive instance, the invariant subset of size $k$ contains a Frobenius cycle of length $1\leq d\leq k$. The two tensors are therefore in the same orbit over $\mathbb F_{p^d}$ for at least one $d\leq K(N)$. In a negative instance at a good prime they are in distinct orbits over every extension. Taking the disjunction of exact finite-field answers proves the oracle reduction.

## Reduction to Statistical Difference

Choose $L=\operatorname{poly}(N)$ so that a uniform prime $p\in[2^{L-1},2^L)$ is good with probability at least $99/100$ for every input of length $N$. This follows from the unconditional prime number theorem and the bound $2^{P(N)}$ by choosing, for example, $L=2P(N)+O(1)$ after enlarging $P$ if needed. The implicit constant handles small input sizes.

For $b\in\{0,1\}$, define a sampler that chooses this $p$ and one field representation for each $\mathbb F_{p^d}$ with $1\leq d\leq K(N)$. It chooses bits $(b_1,\ldots,b_{K(N)})$ uniformly subject to their parity being $b$. Independently it chooses uniform $g_d\in G(\mathbb F_{p^d})$. It outputs the common prime, the field representations and the tuple $(g_d\cdot T_{b_d})_{d=1}^{K(N)}$ of reduced tensors. Each orbit sample is uniform on its orbit because the fibers of the orbit map all have the same size.

Condition on the prime and field representations. In a promised positive instance at a good prime there is an index $d$ where the two orbits coincide. Flipping $b_d$ pairs the two parity classes and leaves the conditional distribution unchanged. This is the equal-or-disjoint case of the XOR construction for Statistical Difference. In a negative instance at a good prime, the two orbits at every index are disjoint. The tensor tuple determines each $b_d$ and therefore determines the parity. The conditional distributions have disjoint supports. The prime and field representations are in the output and have a common marginal. Consequently the ideal total variation distance is at most $1/100$ for promised positive instances and at least $99/100$ for negative instances.

These distributions have bounded polynomial-time fair-coin samplers up to total variation error at most $1/100$ apiece. Use bounded rejection on uniformly distributed $L$-bit candidates in the prime interval, with deterministic primality testing. The acceptance rate is $\Omega(1/L)$ and the first accepted prime is conditionally uniform. Use bounded rejection of monic degree-$d$ polynomials and deterministic irreducibility tests to represent each extension. Counting the full-degree elements in $\mathbb F_{p^d}$ gives acceptance probability at least $1/(2d)$ for sufficiently large $p$, with degree one immediate. For completeness, the union of proper subfields has size at most $\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}p^i\leq p^d/2$ for $p\geq 3$ and $d\geq2$. The exact irreducibility test uses modular powering and gcds for the prime divisors of $d$, which can be found by trial division in time polynomial in $d$. For general linear matrices, sample uniform entries and reject singular matrices. The probability of invertibility for a square matrix of size $a$ over $\mathbb F_q$ is $\prod_{i=1}^a(1-q^{-i})\geq 1-1/(q-1)$, hence is bounded below by a constant here. For special linear matrices, sample a general linear matrix $M$ and left multiply by $\operatorname{diag}((\det M)^{-1},1,\ldots,1)$, a map with equal-size fibers. Uniform residues also use bounded rejection with fair bits. Set polynomial repetition limits so that the union of all timeout probabilities is below $1/100$, and emit a designated failure output on timeout or a zero denominator. For zero denominators the ideal sampler uses the same designated output, which affects only bad primes already covered by the bound. All lengths are polynomial and the outputs can be padded to a fixed length. [Agrawal, Kayal and Saxena](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~qunfeng/papers/agrawal2002.pdf) · [Rabin, Probabilistic Algorithms in Finite Fields](https://publications.csail.mit.edu/lcs/pubs/pdf/MIT-LCS-TR-213.pdf)

Compiling both bounded samplers to Boolean circuits is a deterministic polynomial-time construction. By the triangle inequality their statistical distance is at most $3/100$ in the promised positive case and at least $97/100$ in the negative case. The complement of the promise problem reduces to Statistical Difference, which is complete for $\mathsf{SZK}$. Closure of $\mathsf{SZK}$ under complement finishes the theorem. [Sahai and Vadhan, Proposition 3.6, Theorem 3.1, and Corollaries 4.4 and 4.5](https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~salil/research/complete-abs.html)

## Scope and verification

For unrestricted rational-entry complex 3-tensor isomorphism, the equation encoding gives the established conditional $\mathsf{AM}$ upper bound via Koiran's number-field GRH theorem. Its analytic input is an effective prime ideal theorem of the type supplied by GRH for Dedekind zeta functions. The 2026 Andrews–Garg–Schost preprint gives an unconditional counting-hierarchy upper bound through the same encoding. The Boolean-complexity implication of Valiant's class collapse over $\mathbb C$ is a separate result. Under the GRH hypothesis in Bürgisser's transfer theorem, $\mathsf{VP}=\mathsf{VNP}$ implies $\mathsf{NP}\subseteq\mathsf{P/poly}$. Qiao posed $\mathsf{AM}\cap\mathsf{coAM}$ membership for unrestricted complex tensor isomorphism. The result here concerns the stated small Galois-orbit promise. It combines quantitative Chow-form specialization with finite-field orbit sampling and needs no Riemann hypothesis. The proof is in writing. The literature comparison covers these sources and does not establish priority.
