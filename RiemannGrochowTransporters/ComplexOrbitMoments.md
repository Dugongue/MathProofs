# A GRH prime-field protocol from two moments of transporter components

## Theorem

Assume the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dedekind zeta functions of number fields. Fix a positive integer-valued polynomial bound $R(N)$. Let $T_0,T_1$ be explicit binary-encoded rational tensors of the same shape and total bit length $N$, acted on by the product of general linear groups or the product of special linear groups on their factors. The decision is whether they are isomorphic over $\mathbb C$.

The promise includes every negative instance. A positive instance is promised when the geometric components of its transporter have a Galois orbit $\Omega$ for which the diagonal action of $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/\mathbb Q)$ on $\Omega^2$ has at most $R(N)$ orbits. This promise problem is in $\mathsf{SZK}\subseteq\mathsf{AM}\cap\mathsf{coAM}$. It also reduces in randomized polynomial time to tensor isomorphism using only prime fields. The Galois orbits and their sizes are absent from the input.

The bound is on the number of orbits on ordered pairs. For example, it is two when the action on $\Omega$ is 2-transitive and $|\Omega|>1$. The number of components in $\Omega$ can be exponential in the input parameters. The last section gives a family with no polynomial-size component orbit for which the ordered-pair bound holds.

## Arithmetic labels for components and pairs

Use the integer polynomial transporter equations of the [unconditional protocol](ComplexOrbitProtocols.md). Write $k=|\Omega|$, let $U$ be the union of the components in $\Omega$, and let $Z_r$ be the union of all transporter components of their common dimension $r$. Let $Q,C$ be their respective primitive integer Chow forms. The degree $D$ of $Q$, its number of variables $t$, and both Chow forms' logarithmic coefficient heights satisfy $t=\operatorname{poly}(N)$ and $D,h(Q),h(C)\leq 2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$. The arithmetic Bézout bound and the Chow form coefficient bound in the unconditional proof give these estimates without a hypothesis on $k$. The same proof of Chow form specialization and Gao's absolute-factor count applies with arbitrary $k$. Outside a set of at most $2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$ exceptional primes, $\overline Q$ has exactly $k$ absolute factors and divides the squarefree Chow form $\overline C$ of the reduced transporter in dimension $r$. [Elliott and Schost, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02279v1) · [Gao, Theorems 2.3 and 5.1](https://www.math.clemson.edu/~sgao/papers/fac_bipoly.pdf)

To give the factors labels of bounded arithmetic height, substitute $Y^{(D+1)^{j-1}}$ for the $j$th variable of $Q$. This multiplicative substitution sends distinct monomials of coordinate degrees at most $D$ to distinct powers of $Y$. Put $P(Y)$ for the resulting integer polynomial, $e=\deg P\leq (D+1)^t$, and $a=\operatorname{lc}(P)\ne0$. Normalize each absolute factor $Q_i$ of $Q$ by making the coefficient of its greatest-weight monomial one, using the same monomial weights as the substitution. The $k$ factors so normalized form a transitive Galois set. Their images are monic polynomials with product $P/a$. Each root $\rho$ of $P$ has $a\rho$ integral, since it is a root after rescaling of a monic integer polynomial. All roots satisfy $|\rho|\leq 1+\exp(h(Q))$ by the Cauchy root bound. Each coefficient of a monic factor image is an elementary symmetric function of a subset of at most $e$ roots with multiplicity. Therefore multiplying the coefficients of every $Q_i$ by $a^e$ gives algebraic integers whose archimedean absolute values at every conjugate are at most
$$
 |a|^e\,2^e(1+\exp(h(Q)))^e .
$$
The logarithm of this bound is $2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$ or less.

Represent all scaled coefficient vectors in the same monomial ordering. Choose one linear form with integer coefficients between $0$ and $\binom{k}{2}$ that takes distinct values $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k$ on these vectors. To see that a choice exists, multiply together the nonzero linear forms given by all vector differences. The resulting nonzero polynomial has total degree at most $\binom{k}{2}$, so it cannot vanish on the whole indicated integer grid. For $k=1$ the empty product is $1$ and any label works. The label map is Galois-equivariant. Every $\alpha_i$ is integral and has logarithmic archimedean size bounded by $2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$, since the vectors have at most $(D+1)^t$ coordinates and $k\leq D$.

Choose an integer $c\in[0,\binom{k^2}{2}]$ so that the ordered-pair labels $\beta_{ij}=\alpha_i+c\alpha_j$ are all distinct. Equality between two distinct ordered-pair labels excludes at most one choice of $c$, because distinct $\alpha_i$ already separate pairs with the same second coordinate. This gives a Galois-equivariant injection of $\Omega^2$ into the algebraic integers with the same logarithmic size bound. The monic global polynomials with roots all $\alpha_i$ and all $\beta_{ij}$ belong to $\mathbb Z[Y]$. Their discriminants are nonzero with log absolute values at most $2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$, by the products of squared root differences. Each individual orbit polynomial for the $\beta_{ij}$ is monic irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ and has the same discriminant bound. Let $L$ be the field generated by an $\alpha_i$ and let $L_1,\ldots,L_s$ be fields generated by one $\beta_{ij}$ from each of the $s$ ordered-pair orbits. Each field has degree at most $k^2\leq 2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$ and log absolute discriminant bounded by that of its defining monic polynomial.

Exclude the primes dividing $a$ and either global discriminant, in addition to the Chow and factor-count exceptions. The resulting total number of exclusions is $2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$ or less. At a remaining prime, reduce the normalized factors in a residue field of a number field containing their coefficients and hence all labels. This is possible because their coefficients are integral away from $a$. Each reduced factor is nonconstant because its leading monomial coefficient remains one. Their product, up to a nonzero scalar, is $\overline Q$. Since $\overline Q$ is squarefree with exactly $k$ absolute factors, each of the $k$ reduced factors is absolutely irreducible. The factor-label bijection remains injective by the excluded discriminants. Forming the labels and taking $p$th powers commute. Thus the Frobenius action on the selected geometric components of the reduced transporter is identified with its action on the reduced roots $\alpha_i$, and that on their ordered pairs is identified with its action on the reduced roots $\beta_{ij}$.

Write $J(p)$ for the number of fixed components in this subset at a nonexceptional prime and set $J(p)=0$ at the exceptional primes. At a nonexceptional prime, $J(p)$ equals the number of degree-one prime ideals of $L$ over $p$. Likewise $J(p)^2$ equals the sum of the numbers of degree-one primes over $p$ in the $L_j$. Indeed, $p$ is coprime to the discriminant of each corresponding monic integral defining polynomial, so its linear factors give exactly those ideals by the prime factorization theorem for power bases of index prime to $p$. The total degree or discriminant of the full splitting field is irrelevant to these bounds.

## Prime-field density and the statistical protocol

Choose a dyadic interval $I=[X/2,X)$ with $X=2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$. The prime number theorem for number fields under Dedekind GRH gives a uniform estimate for the number of degree-one prime ideals of a number field $M$ with rational prime norms in $I$. If $d_M=[M : \mathbb Q]$, $\delta_M$ is its absolute discriminant and $\Pi(I)$ counts rational primes in $I$, the estimate
$$
 \pi_{1,M}(I)=\Pi(I)+O\!\left(\sqrt X(\log X)^2
                 (\log \delta_M+d_M)\right)
$$
suffices. This follows from the uniform estimate for the prime-ideal Chebyshev function, discarding higher powers and residue degrees, which must lie over rational primes at most $\sqrt X$, and partial summation. The error term has an absolute effective constant. Deleting the exceptional primes costs at most $d_M$ times their number. [Grenié and Molteni, Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.4463)

The preceding bounds are uniform across $L$ and all $L_j$ and exponential in a fixed polynomial in $N$. Since $\Pi(I)=\Omega(X/\log X)$, choose the polynomial for $\log_2 X$ large enough that each remaining ideal count divided by $\Pi(I)$ lies between $3/4$ and $5/4$. On a uniform prime in $I$ this gives
$$
 \mathbb E J\geq 3/4,\qquad \mathbb E J^2\leq 5s/4.
$$
Cauchy–Schwarz applied to $J=J\mathbf1_{\{J>0\}}$ yields
$$
 \Pr(J>0)\ \geq\ \frac{(\mathbb E J)^2}{\mathbb E J^2}
 \ \geq\ \frac{9}{20s}\ \geq\ \frac{1}{4R(N)}.
$$
A fixed geometric component yields an $\mathbb F_p$-point by Lang's theorem, as proved in the unconditional protocol. Therefore at least this fraction of the prime fields exhibit isomorphism on each promised positive instance. In a negative instance, arithmetic effective Nullstellensatz makes every prime in $I$ except for at most $2^{\operatorname{poly}(N)}$ exceptions a nonisomorphism prime.

Take $20R(N)$ independent uniform primes in $I$. On promised positive inputs the chance that none supports an isomorphism is at most $e^{-5}<1/100$. Enlarge the fixed polynomial for $\log_2 X$ if necessary so that in a negative instance the probability that any sampled prime is exceptional is at most $1/100$. Testing the disjunction of finite-field isomorphisms gives the claimed oracle reduction. For statistical zero knowledge, use the parity samplers from the unconditional protocol, here with one coordinate for each sampled prime, and include the prime list in the output. Conditional on the list, a single equal pair of orbits makes the two sampler distributions identical. If every pair is disjoint, the orbit tuple determines the parity. The two ideal distributions have distance at most $1/100$ on promised positive inputs and at least $99/100$ on negative inputs. Bounded prime and group sampling gives polynomial-size distribution circuits with at most $1/100$ total variation error each. Statistical Difference completeness and complement closure give $\mathsf{SZK}$. [Sahai and Vadhan](https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~salil/research/complete-abs.html)

The algorithm uses only arithmetic in prime fields and uniform bit sampling. The decomposition, labels and orbit fields serve to prove its success probability. The analytic input is the effective prime ideal theorem for the component field and the ordered-pair orbit fields. Dirichlet GRH alone does not supply this input for general instances.

## A family outside the polynomial component-orbit promise

For every $n\geq3$ choose a monic degree-$n$ integer polynomial $f$ with squarefree factorization types $(n)$, $(n-1,1)$, $(2,1,\ldots,1)$ and $(1,\ldots,1)$ modulo four different primes of size $O(n)$, all larger than $n$. Such primes exist by repeated use of Bertrand's postulate, the required monic irreducible polynomials exist over each finite field, and the coefficientwise Chinese remainder theorem gives coefficients of $O(\log n)$ bits. Put $E=\mathbb Q[\theta]$ for a root of $f$, and let $B$ be the splitting field. The first type proves irreducibility. The second gives an $(n-1)$-cycle, so the transitive Galois group is 2-transitive. The third gives a transposition, and its conjugates in a 2-transitive group supply every transposition. Thus the group is $S_n$. The last prime $\ell$ splits completely in $B$. These conclusions use the Frobenius cycle types of squarefree reductions. [Stevenhagen and Lenstra](https://websites.math.leidenuniv.nl/algebra/chebotarev.pdf)

Let $r_1$ be a root modulo $\ell$, choose its Hensel lift $\widetilde r_1$ modulo $\ell^2$, and put $u=\theta-\widetilde r_1+\ell\in E^\times$. At any prime of $B$ over $\ell$, exactly one of the conjugates of $u$ has valuation one and all the others have valuation zero, because the roots of $f$ reduce distinctly modulo $\ell$. Transitivity lets each coordinate be the one of odd valuation at some prime. Thus the $n$ conjugates of $u$ are independent squareclasses in $B$. The Galois group of the compositum of $B$ and their square roots therefore induces the full signed permutation group $(C_2)^n\rtimes S_n$ on the $n$ pairs of embeddings of $E(\sqrt u)$, by the multiquadratic degree $2^n$ and the quotient action of $S_n$.

Take the general linear isomorphism problem for the multiplication tensors of $E\times E$ and $E(\sqrt u)$. Over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ both algebras are products of $2n$ copies of $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. The geometric components of their multiplication-tensor transporter correspond to the bijections of their primitive idempotents, since each such algebra isomorphism yields a component parametrized by the two connected groups of multiplying units. This is the unital isotopy calculation in the unconditional protocol. Both primitive idempotent sets have a map to the $n$ embeddings of $E$. The bijections respecting these maps are a Galois-invariant set $\Omega$ consisting of $2^n$ choices of a bit, one per fiber. The signed permutation group acts transitively on these choices and preserves the Hamming distance of two choices. Independent flips and coordinate permutations act transitively on each distance class, so its action on $\Omega^2$ has exactly $n+1$ orbits.

On the whole component set, the subgroup of independent target flips acts freely by postcomposition on all bijections. Every component orbit therefore has size at least $2^n$. The two tensor inputs have bit length polynomial in $n$ using the power bases and the relation $(\sqrt u)^2=u$. In these bases the table entries are obtained by reducing powers of degree $O(n)$ modulo the monic $f$ with $O(\log n)$-bit coefficients, which keeps the coefficient bit lengths polynomial. Hence this family meets the ordered-pair promise and lies outside every fixed polynomial bound on the size of a component orbit.

## Verification and scope

This is a written conditional proof for the stated promise problem. The arithmetic label construction identifies the orbit fields needed for the first two moments without using the degree or discriminant of their full splitting field. The conditional result allows exponential-size Galois orbits of components and has a prime-field reduction. The source comparison does not establish publication priority.
