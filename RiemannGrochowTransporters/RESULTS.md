# Riemann hypotheses and tensor transporters

| Result | Scope | Hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Statistical zero knowledge for complex tensor isomorphism](ComplexOrbitProtocols.md) | Ordinary and special-linear complex tensor isomorphism for rational-entry inputs are in promise-SZK when every isomorphic input in the promise has a geometric transporter component with polynomially many Galois conjugates. All nonisomorphic inputs are in the promise. | Unconditional |
| [A prime-field protocol from two moments of transporter components](ComplexOrbitMoments.md) | The same SZK bound follows when a Galois orbit of components has polynomially many orbits on ordered pairs. The reduction uses only prime fields. An explicit family satisfies this promise with at least $2^n$ components in every orbit and just $n+1$ ordered-pair orbits in the selected orbit. | Dedekind-zeta GRH gives the uniform prime-ideal counts. The family is unconditional. |
| [Factoring and determinant-one transporter search](TransporterSearch.md) | Computing a determinant-one transporter between semisimple multiplication isotopes over finite fields is polynomial-time equivalent to univariate factoring under search Turing reductions. The hardness survives a supplied ordinary transporter and queries guaranteed to have a determinant-one solution. On one split matrix block, the supplied-witness problem is equivalent to extracting roots of degree equal to the matrix size. | The reductions are unconditional. The classical GRH factoring bound gives deterministic quasipolynomial search on this locus. |
| [Small-prime tests for square-zero algebras](PeircePrimeCertificates.md) | Rational multiplication tensors with the stated weighted geometric graphs are tested by an explicit finite list of modular comparisons. The test runs in deterministic bit-polynomial time for cycles and extends to determinant-one equivalence after adjoining two fixed algebra factors. An explicit pair of dimension $12$ attains the sharp distinguishing-prime density $1/8$. | Dedekind-zeta GRH gives the prime cutoff. The density and example are unconditional. |

The notes give mathematical proofs for these scopes. The [Lean companion](ParityOrbitMask.lean) independently verifies two group-action identities used in both statistical-distance arguments. A mask on one common orbit gives a bijection of parity-conditioned samples. When the other coordinates reveal their parity and the final orbits are disjoint, the two sets of outputs are disjoint. The [verification record](Verification.json) binds both lemmas to the checked source. The geometry, analytic estimates and complexity reductions are proved in prose. Publication priority has yet to be established.
