# Matrix spaces, full generated algebras, and singular alternating spaces

The following are exact, polynomial-size classifications for the catalogue questions on arbitrary space conjugacy (GP-0232), a matched invertible anchor (GP-0237), a full generated algebra (GP-0248), and singular alternating spaces with zero common radical (GP-0242). These classifications identify the unrestricted problem to which each promise is equivalent. A polynomial-time algorithm without an oracle remains open in each case. All assertions in this note are written proofs.

## Matrix-space conjugacy and the two extra promises

Use “TI” for ordinary three-tensor isomorphism with three independent general linear groups. A matrix space in Hom(V,U) is equivalent to the tensor of a basis of that space, with left-right equivalence accounting for the first two tensor legs and mixing of the basis accounting for the third. Linear elimination removes unused slice coordinates. It also removes common kernels and restricts to the span of the images. After comparing their dimensions, these restrictions and taking complements preserve and reflect left-right equivalence.

For a matrix space S in Hom(V,U), embed it into End(U + V) as

```
N(S) = { [ 0  s ]
         [ 0  0 ] : s in S }.
```

For concise S, the sum of images of elements of N(S) and their common kernel are both U. Conjugating N(S) to N(S') therefore identifies the restrictions to U and the induced maps on the quotient V. If these identifications are A and B, the quotient-to-image map s is sent to A s B^{-1}. Conversely diag(A,B) realizes this transformation. This proves the lower reduction to matrix-space conjugacy and gives a transporter extractor after restriction. It includes zero-dimensional residual spaces with direct constant answers. Grochow and Qiao [1, Theorem A and Corollary B] already classified arbitrary matrix-space conjugacy as TI-complete. The embedding above is included to prove the extra promises.

For the two marked promises set R(S)=N(S)^perp for the nondegenerate pairing (X,Y) ↦ tr(XY) on End(U + V). The matrix units show that the form is nondegenerate in every characteristic. It is invariant under simultaneous conjugation, so taking perpendiculars preserves and reflects conjugacy. If S is proper, R(S) contains all block-diagonal matrices and all upper-right block matrices. Its allowed lower-left blocks form the trace annihilator of S in Hom(U,V). This annihilator contains a matrix L with a nonzero entry. Left and right multiplication of L by block-diagonal matrix units, followed by scalar rescaling, yields every lower-left matrix unit in the generated algebra. The other matrix units already lie in R(S). Consequently R(S) generates the full algebra and contains the identity, regardless of field characteristic. The generated algebra really is the same full subalgebra when both instances have the same dimension.

The full-space exception is decidable directly. Two full spaces with equal side dimensions are equivalent. A proper space cannot be equivalent to a full space. For constant yes or no outputs that meet both promises, use R of proper concise spaces of equal dimension in Hom(F^2,F^2). For a no pair, use span{I,E12} and span{I,E11}; the first has one rank-one projective line and the second has two, over every field. A yes pair can consist of two identical copies.

For left-right equivalence with matched invertible anchors M and M', normalize the input as M^{-1}S and M'^{-1}S'. If P M Q^{-1}=M', then M'^{-1}P S Q^{-1}=Q(M^{-1}S)Q^{-1}. The converse sets P=M'Q M^{-1}. Thus the anchored problem reduces to conjugacy, and identity as the marked element makes conjugacy on R(S) an anchored instance. For the unmarked problem on pairs generating the same full algebra, the proof also has a lower reduction: R(S) contains all diagonal blocks and I, but an unmarked left-right equivalence need not fix I. The unmarked conjugacy problem on such spaces is classified directly by the perpendicular construction. If the intended unmarked action is left-right, apply the following separate construction.

For unmarked left-right equivalence the trace annihilator also gives a direct reduction. View the rectangular S in M_{a+b} through N(S), ignore the conjugacy requirement, and observe that left-right transformations of R(S) yield left-right transformations of its trace annihilator. Precisely, (P R Q^{-1})^perp = Q R^perp P^{-1}. Left-right equivalence of N(S) reflects ordinary equivalence of S by the induced map on its image U and quotient by its common kernel U, now using the two transformations independently. Conversely ordinary equivalence of S extends to left-right equivalence of N(S) by independent block diagonal left and right factors. Removing the unused zero coordinates of N(S) gives exactly the original S. Thus the very same R(S) construction gives the unmarked left-right lower reduction. The upper reduction is ordinary matrix-space left-right equivalence to TI. This proves the two possible readings of GP-0248.

## Singular alternating-space pseudo-isometry

Regard a space of alternating matrices as the dual of the output of a surjective alternating bimap b: V × V → W. The common radical of the matrices is the intrinsic radical of b. Compute it by linear elimination and retain its dimension as an invariant. The remainder on V/rad(b) has zero radical, and any compatible isomorphism of the quotient and output lifts by choosing complements and a radical isomorphism. We use the usual definition of alternating in characteristic two, including b(v,v)=0.

We first recall the direct-sum certificate in the form needed here. The centroid of the radical-free surjective bimap consists of (A,B,C) such that b(Au,v)=b(u,Bv)=C b(u,v) for all u,v. Orthogonal refers below to vanishing cross terms for b. Each of A, B, C determines the others by zero radical and surjectivity. Alternation implies B=A, since interchanging u,v in b(Au,v)=C b(u,v) gives b(u,Av)=C b(u,v). The centroid is a commutative finite-dimensional algebra. For instance, for two centroid elements, say with first components A,A' and output components C,C', one computes b(AA'u,v) as C C' b(u,v) by pushing both maps out. Pushing the two maps out of different inputs in the two possible orders gives C C' b(u,v)=C' C b(u,v). Surjectivity and centroid injectivity give commutativity.

If e=(A,A,C) is an idempotent, V splits into its image and kernel under A, and W similarly for C. The centroid equalities give zero on cross terms and force each diagonal block into the corresponding W summand. Conversely every direct-sum splitting with independent outputs defines an idempotent. A finite-dimensional commutative algebra has a unique unordered complete set of nonzero primitive orthogonal idempotents, by its decomposition into local Artinian rings. Hence the multiset of primitive bimap summands is intrinsic and stabilizes under isomorphism. Conversely assembling summand isomorphisms produces an isomorphism. For the application, even this abstract cancellation statement suffices. It is also constructive within expected polynomial time plus the required primitive-summand transporter calls, using finite commutative algebra decomposition. This is the centroid method used by Brooksbank, Maglione and Wilson [2]. The concise-tensor commutativity and multiplication lemmas have checked historical types in the project's IntrinsicCentroidAlgebra module. The singular-padding theorem itself is a written proof.

Fix Q on a 3-dimensional space, with two independent forms e1*∧e2* and e1*∧e3*. Their common radical is zero. Each linear combination is singular, over every field, by odd dimension. Append Q⊕Q with independent outputs to each radical-free input. Any matrix in the resulting matrix space is singular because its two Q blocks are singular, including on instances where the resulting side length is even. The common radical remains zero. If the padded bimaps are isomorphic, cancel the equal contribution of Q⊕Q from the two intrinsic primitive-summand multisets to recover pseudo-isometry of the original bimaps. The forward implication adjoins the identity on Q⊕Q. Reject pre-padding mismatched dimensions using a fixed pair with different output dimensions of the same side length (for example the zero-radical span of two versus all three alternating coordinate forms in dimension 3, each padded once by Q⊕Q). One may also answer such dimension mismatches before an oracle call. Sizes increase by six in the matrix mode and four in the output mode. Thus unrestricted alternating-space pseudo-isometry and its promised singular zero-common-radical restriction have identical polynomial-reduction complexity.
