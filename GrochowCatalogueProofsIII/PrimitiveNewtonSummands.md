# Hardness of finding a promised primitive Newton summand

## GP-0612

A bit-polynomial algorithm for the requested search would imply P = NP. This holds already for explicit positive bivariate sparse polynomials given by their vertex monomials. Every positive instance in the reduction has a proper nonpoint Minkowski-indecomposable lattice summand of polynomial vertex encoding length. On this explicit polygon class the promise search is polynomial-time solvable if P = NP.

Take an instance of Gao and Lauder's NP-complete integral polygon decomposability problem, given by the integer vertices of a convex polygon $P$. Translate the vertices to nonnegative coordinates and form the polynomial whose monomials, all with coefficient $1$, are exactly those vertices. Repeated squaring gives a circuit of size polynomial in the vertex encoding. Its Newton polygon is the translated $P$. A proper nonpoint summand corresponds under translation, so the coordinate normalization is harmless. [Gao–Lauder, Proposition 14](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0012099)

Write the cyclic oriented edge vectors of $P$ as $n_i e_i$, with $e_i$ primitive integer directions. Every integer summand can, up to translation, be described by a closed list $\ell_i e_i$ in those same directions, with integers $0\le\ell_i\le n_i$. A segment uses the two opposite edge directions, and a point uses no directions. The characterization follows from the boundary edge merge under Minkowski addition. Conversely such a closed list and its complementary list define convex lattice polytopes by taking consecutive sums of their edge vectors, and their Minkowski sum agrees with $P$ up to translation. In particular, existence and propriety of a summand candidate are verifiable by polynomial-time integer computations.

If $P$ decomposes, some nonpoint proper summand can be chosen indecomposable. Minimize $\sum_i\ell_i$ over positive proper summands. A nontrivial decomposition of a minimizing summand would give a proper nonpoint summand of $P$ with smaller sum, since the lengths add in each direction and each nonpoint part has positive sum. With its first vertex placed at the origin, a minimizing summand has at most as many listed vertices as $P$ and has coordinate magnitudes at most the sum of the absolute coordinate components of the original edges. Its encoding length is therefore polynomial. A translation can put both summands in the nonnegative orthant after the original $P$ has been normalized by coordinate minima, since coordinate minima add under Minkowski addition. One may thus assume the proper primitive summand has a normalized polynomial-size encoding.

Now run the proposed promise search with a polynomial cutoff, using the proven polynomial bound on valid output size. Accept precisely when it returns a valid proper nonpoint lattice summand, as checked by the edge test. All decomposable inputs are promised inputs and succeed. On an indecomposable input no output can pass the check. This is a polynomial decision procedure for polygon decomposability. The argument uses no behavior guarantee on inputs outside the search promise. The test does not require independently certifying the output's primitivity.

For the converse on the explicit polygon class, a proposed bound on $\sum_i\ell_i$ yields an NP predicate by the same edge test. If P = NP, binary search finds its minimum positive feasible value, and the standard bit-by-bit witness search recovers the lengths. Their sum can be large but has polynomial bit length. The minimizing argument above gives a primitive proper summand. The circuit-hardness implication holds for the full input model, while this converse concerns explicit polygons.

The reduction here shows that even a complete explicit vertex list and a small primitive output cannot by themselves make lattice decomposition a polynomial-time search.
