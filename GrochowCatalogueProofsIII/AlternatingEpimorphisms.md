# Epimorphisms onto the Heisenberg group and the common-quotient approximation gap

## GP-0360 · Compact exponent-$p$ epimorphism is NP-complete

**Theorem.** For every fixed odd prime $p$, deciding whether a class-at-most-two exponent-$p$ group given by an alternating bimap surjects onto the Heisenberg group $H_p$ of order $p^3$ is NP-complete. The target is fixed and has a two-dimensional central quotient and a one-dimensional center and derived group. Consequently the decision problem with both compact alternating presentations as input is NP-complete.

Let $F=\mathbb F_p$. The Baer group of $b:V\times V\to W$ has product
$$
(v,w)(v',w')=(v+v',w+w'+\tfrac12 b(v,v')).
$$
Its bracket is $[(v,w),(v',w')]=(0,b(v,v'))$. Addition in these coordinates is recovered by multiplying group elements and subtracting half their bracket. Thus homomorphisms are exactly the $F$-linear bracket-preserving maps. A certificate consists of the matrix of such a map. Bracket preservation is tested on pairs of basis elements and surjectivity by matrix rank. For $H_p$, take the ordinary determinant on $P=F^2$ with values in $F$.

Use the polynomial-time conversion of 3-SAT to Boolean circuit satisfiability with binary AND and unary NOT gates. Index the input and internal variables by $1,\ldots,n$, including the designated output, and reserve $0$ to homogenize the equations. Put $V=A\oplus B$, with both summands having bases indexed by $0,\ldots,n$. Write $E_{ij}=e_i\otimes e_j$ and let $W=(A\otimes B)/R$. The cross brackets are the classes of $E_{ij}$, and the brackets within either summand vanish. Include in $R$ the relations
$$
E_{ij}-E_{ji},\qquad E_{ii}-E_{0i}\quad(i>0\text{ in the second family}).
$$
Also include $E_{0k}-E_{ij}$ for a gate $x_k=x_i x_j$, $E_{0k}+E_{0i}-E_{00}$ for $x_k=1-x_i$, and $E_{0k}-E_{00}$ for output $k$. Row reduction gives the compact bimap in polynomial time.

Suppose a surjection exists. The target-plane images of $A$ and $B$ are isotropic, since each summand is abelian. They therefore have dimension at most one apiece and must be distinct lines to span $P$. Choose nonzero generators $a,b$ of the two lines, and write the coordinate maps as $u_i a$ and $v_j b$. The matrix of cross bracket images is $X_{ij}=\det(a,b)u_i v_j$. It is a nonzero rank-one symmetric matrix, so $u$ and $v$ are proportional: choose $k$ with $u_k\ne 0$; symmetry of row and column $k$ gives $u_kv_j=u_jv_k$ for every $j$, and $v_k\ne0$ because $v\ne0$. Thus $X=\mu uu^{\mathsf T}$ with $\mu\ne0$. The square equations give $u_i^2=u_0u_i$ for $i>0$. If $u_0=0$ then all $u_i=0$, a contradiction. Dividing the remaining equations by the nonzero scalar $\mu u_0^2$ gives a satisfying assignment with bits $u_i/u_0\in\{0,1\}$. Conversely a satisfying assignment $x$, extended by $x_0=1$, defines the plane map $e_i\mapsto x_i a$ on $A$ and $e_j\mapsto x_j b$ on $B$ for a determinant-one basis $a,b$. The cross functional $E_{ij}\mapsto x_i x_j$ kills $R$ and descends to $W$. These maps preserve the bracket and are surjective on both target components.

If the input convention requires $V=G/Z(G)$, compute the radical $D$ of the input bimap by linear algebra. Fold $D$ into an additional summand of the center by splitting $V=D\oplus V_0$; the resulting presentation describes the same group, and $b|_{V_0}$ has zero radical. If only the stem group with center equal to the derived group is allowed, discard the central direct summand $D$: a surjection onto $H_p$ sends the whole center into the target center, and restricting to the remaining factor still surjects on the target plane. The two independent plane directions have nonzero bracket, so the restricted map also covers the target center.

If class two is required to mean class exactly two, take the direct product of each source with the fixed extraspecial exponent-$p$ group whose bracket is a nondegenerate alternating form on $F^4$ with values in $F$. This factor cannot surject onto $H_p$: a surjection would pull the determinant on $F^2$ back to a nonzero scalar multiple of the rank-four source form, while the pullback has rank at most two. In any epimorphism from a direct product onto $H_p$, the two plane images are orthogonal to one another for the determinant. Since they span the plane, one of them equals the plane and the other is zero. The factor covering the plane also covers the center by commutators. This padding therefore preserves the decision answer and ensures the source is nonabelian.

This is an odd-characteristic counterpart to the portfolio's square and commutator constraints for dihedral epimorphisms. The constructions use different defining maps. ## GP-0361 · The sharp approximation threshold for a fixed Heisenberg target

**Theorem.** Fix an odd prime $p$ and target $H_p$. The maximum base-$p$ log order of a common quotient is NP-hard to approximate to any ratio strictly below $3/2$ when the algorithm outputs a feasible quotient, or to additive error strictly below $1/2$ when it estimates the optimum. On this fixed-target problem there are deterministic polynomial-time algorithms at the respective thresholds. With general targets, no fixed ratio below $2$ or fixed additive error is achievable in polynomial time unless P = NP. For the general two-input problem an explicit polynomial-time approximation has ratio $(r+1)/2$ when $r>0$, where $r$ is the smaller abelianization dimension.

Let $G$ be the source. If it has abelianization dimension $s$, it has every elementary abelian quotient of dimension at most $s$. Any nontrivial normal subgroup of $H_p$ contains the center: a normal subgroup meeting the complement of the center contains all commutators of one of its noncentral elements, and these span the center. A nontrivial normal subgroup contained in the center equals the center. Thus the proper quotients of $H_p$ are exactly its elementary abelian quotients, and $H_p/Z(H_p)\cong F^2$. For $s\ge2$, the optimum is therefore $3$ if $G$ maps onto $H_p$ and $2$ otherwise. Append a free central factor $F^2$ to the source of GP-0360 if necessary. This leaves epimorphism feasibility unchanged: the additional central factor has central image under any epimorphism, and surjectivity on the plane already implies that the restriction to $G$ covers the center by taking commutators. The $2$-versus-$3$ gap proves both hardness claims. For $s<2$ the optimum is $s$ exactly. Otherwise output $F^2$ for the $3/2$ guarantee, or estimate $5/2$ for the additive guarantee.

For the general-target hardness, fix an integer $r\ge1$ and let $K_r$ have an $(r+1)$-dimensional quotient $F\oplus F^r$, derived group $F^r$, and alternating bracket
$$
 b((a,u),(a',u'))=au'-a'u.
$$
Every nonabelian quotient $Q$ of $K_r$ surjects onto $H_p$. Choose a nonzero functional on its derived group. Pulling the resulting scalar alternating form on $Q_\mathrm{ab}$ back to $K_{r,\mathrm{ab}}$ gives the wedge of the first coordinate functional with a nonzero functional on $F^r$, so its rank is two. Rank is preserved by pullback along the surjection to $Q_\mathrm{ab}$. Quotienting the scalar form by its radical gives the Heisenberg epimorphism, using the chosen functional on the derived group in the Baer coordinates.

In the source construction of GP-0360 retain the single left summand $A$ and replicate the right summand $B$ and the central output $W$ into $r$ copies. Use the same cross relations in each $(A,B_j,W_j)$, with the complete right summand abelian. In the satisfiable case map $A$ along the scalar coordinate of $K_r$ using $x_i$, map $B_j$ onto its $j$-th direction using the same $x_i$, and use the previous functional on $W_j$ in the $j$-th central coordinate. This gives $K_r$ as a quotient. Conversely any epimorphism of the replicated source onto $H_p$ sends $A$ and the full right summand onto independent lines in the target plane, as in GP-0360. Some right summand $B_j$ has nonzero projection, so restriction to $(A,B_j,W_j)$ still covers the target plane and hence the center. GP-0360 makes the formula satisfiable. In the negative case any nonabelian common quotient would therefore give the forbidden $H_p$ quotient. The elementary abelian group of rank $r+1$ is always a common quotient, since $K_r$ has that abelianization and the source has at least $r+1$ free abelianization directions. Thus the optimum is $2r+1$ or $r+1$, respectively. Their ratio tends to $2$ and their difference grows without bound. If exact class two is required, apply the same fixed extraspecial padding from GP-0360; it preserves the affirmative map by projection and the negative conclusion by preserving absence of a Heisenberg quotient.

For arbitrary compact class-at-most-two exponent-$p$ inputs, compute $r$ from the commutator image ranks. Both groups map onto $F^r$. Any common quotient whose abelianization dimension is $d$ has $d\le r$, and its commutator has dimension at most $\binom d2$ because its alternating bracket is spanned by pairs of representatives of a basis of the abelianization. Its log order is at most $r(r+1)/2$. When $r=0$ the optimum is zero. [Catalogue source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)
