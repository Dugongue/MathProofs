# GP-0358 · Onto lifts with a fixed action on central-extension quotients

**Theorem.** Let $A\le Z(G)$ and $B\le Z(H)$ be specified central sections. Assume $B$ is a finite abelian $p$-group with arbitrary invariant-factor heights, and a quotient epimorphism $\bar f:G/A\to H/B$ is supplied. A surjection $f:G\to H$ inducing $\bar f$ can be found in deterministic polynomial time, or reported impossible, for finite Cayley tables. The same algorithm is bit-polynomial for compact inputs giving a finite presentation of $G$, cyclic coordinates and inclusion for $B$, polynomial-time group operations and membership coordinates in $B$, abelianization coordinates for $H$ modulo $p$, and lifts of the prescribed quotient images of the source generators. In particular standard class-two compact presentations with these central subgroups and quotient action in coordinates support the algorithm without expanding the group orders.

Choose generators $g_1,\ldots,g_t$ of the source and lifts $h_i\in H$ of their prescribed quotient images. Every compatible homomorphism must send $g_i$ to $h_i z_i$ with $z_i\in B$. For each relator $r$, evaluating $r$ at the lifts gives a known central value $r(h)\in B$, and the condition on the changes is
$$
 \sum_i \operatorname{exp}_i(r)z_i=-r(h),
$$
where $\operatorname{exp}_i$ is the relator's integer exponent sum. This holds also for a presentation using binary powers because the variables are central. Polynomial-time integer Smith normal form solves the resulting simultaneous cyclic congruences, treating each modulus by an additional integer variable. If no homomorphism is found there is no surjection. Otherwise fix a solution $f_0$. Pointwise central differences identify all compatible homomorphisms with $f_0+\operatorname{Hom}(G_\mathrm{ab},B)$. Such maps send $A$ into $B$ by compatibility in the quotient. The theorem allows the map $A\to B$ to be induced by the lift.

If $L=f(G)$, then $LB=H$. Since $B$ is central, $L$ is normal and the quotient $H/L$ is a quotient of the abelian $p$-group $B$. It is trivial if and only if the projection of $L$ spans $V=H_\mathrm{ab}/pH_\mathrm{ab}$. Indeed if $H/L\ne1$, it has a nonzero homomorphism to the order-$p$ cyclic group and $L$ fails the span test.

Compute an invariant-factor decomposition of $G_\mathrm{ab}$ from the exponent-sum relation matrix, with cyclic generators $u_i$ of orders $m_i$ in terms of the original generators. Their images in $V$ under the varied lift can independently be any vectors in $a_i+U_i$, where $a_i$ is the image under $f_0$ and $U_i$ is the image of $B[m_i]$ in $V$. In a cyclic summand of $B$ of order $p^e$, the subgroup killed by $m_i$ is generated by $p^{\max(0,e-v_p(m_i))}$ times its generator. This gives $U_i$ and lifts for a basis of $U_i$ by Gaussian elimination.

Replace the $i$-th affine column space by the list $a_i,a_i+b_{i1},\ldots,a_i+b_{id_i}$, using a basis $b_{ij}$ of $U_i$. A choice of one vector per affine space can reach full rank if and only if a choice from these lists can. For the forward implication, take a nonzero full-rank minor of one affine choice and expand it multilinearly in vectors from the lists at its distinct column indices. One term is nonzero. The reverse implication holds because all list entries belong to the affine spaces. Maximum-rank selection can be solved by intersecting the linear matroid of their vectors in $V$ with the partition matroid allowing at most one vector per list index. If it spans $V$, each selected vector has a tracked lift $z_i\in B[m_i]$, which defines the compatible correction to $f_0$. Unselected indices can choose zero correction.

For Cayley tables, table-indexed symbols and multiplication relations give the source presentation, and tables give quotient operations and the required lifts by direct enumeration. Abelian quotients and section coordinates are computed by the same integer normal form on table relations. For standard class-two compact coordinates, collect a product or a power by moving generators across one another and recording the central commutator corrections, with powers evaluated by binary powering. For presentations with explicit central coordinates, this gives polynomial-time evaluations and extraction in the central subgroup by abelian congruences. In particular the algorithm has polynomial complexity in the logarithms of cyclic orders, even for central sections with arbitrarily large and mixed heights.

This extends the portfolio's full-center Cayley-table feasibility and sampling result to arbitrary prescribed central sections and a compact bit-size input model. The same algorithm supports a prescribed exact map $f_A:A\to B$ when generators of $A$ are supplied as words in the source generators. Include their required images in the affine congruence system. The difference then factors through $(G/A)_\mathrm{ab}$, and the independent-column test takes place in $V$ modulo the fixed span of the images of $f_A(A)$. The quotient is necessary because the chosen cyclic generators of $(G/A)_\mathrm{ab}$, together with the fixed images of $A$, capture the whole source span in $V$.
