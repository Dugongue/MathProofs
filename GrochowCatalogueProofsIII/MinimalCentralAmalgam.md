# GP-0473 · Minimum class-two ambient order with prescribed elementary central embeddings

**Theorem.** Let $G_1,G_2$ be compact finite groups of class at most two with elementary abelian $p$-centers $Z_i$, and fix injective maps $Z_i\hookrightarrow W$ into a common elementary abelian central group. Put $V_i=G_i/Z_i$, and view their commutator maps $b_i$ and $p$-power maps $q_i$ as $W$-valued. The minimum ambient order, among all class-at-most-two groups admitting the indicated embeddings and a central copy of $W$, is
$$
p^{\dim W+\dim V_1+\dim V_2-k},
$$
where $k$ is the maximum dimension of subspaces $U_i\le V_i$ admitting a linear isomorphism $\theta:U_1\to U_2$ that preserves both restrictions $b_i|_{U_i\times U_i}$ and $q_i|_{U_i}$. Deciding whether the minimum is at most a supplied order bound is NP-complete for standard compact bracket and power-map input, even at each fixed odd $p$ with exponent-$p$ input groups and disjoint central images.

In such a group, all commutators have order dividing $p$. The class-two identity $[x^p,y]=[x,y]^p$ puts $x^p$ in the center, so $V_i$ is elementary abelian. The commutator is alternating bilinear, and $p$-powers descend to $V_i$ because the center has exponent $p$. The class-two power formula with the coefficient $\binom p2$ shows $q_i$ is linear for odd $p$, and quadratic with polarization $b_i$ for $p=2$.

For the lower bound, embed $G_i$ into an arbitrary feasible ambient group $K$. An element of $G_i$ whose image belongs to the central $W$ was central in $G_i$ by injectivity. Thus $V_i$ injects into $K/W$. The intersection of their images identifies equal-dimensional subspaces in $V_1,V_2$. Representatives of two elements identified modulo $W$ differ by a central element of order dividing $p$, so their powers agree and the commutators of pairs agree. The intersection dimension is at most $k$. The product of the two subgroups in $K/W$ has size $|V_1||V_2|/|V_1\cap V_2|$ by counting fibers of the map from the Cartesian product onto the set product. This establishes the lower bound even if $K/W$ is nonabelian.

For the upper bound, choose maximizing subspaces and a restriction-preserving $\theta$. Form $V=(V_1\oplus V_2)/\{(u,-\theta u):u\in U_1\}$. Choose one basis of the shared subspace and independently extend it to bases of $V_1$ and $V_2$ inside $V$; their union without duplication is a basis of $V$. Extend $b_i$ to an alternating $b$ by choosing zero on pairs from the two complementary basis lists. Prescribe each basis value of $q$ from the corresponding input, and extend linearly if $p$ is odd or quadratically with polarization $b$ if $p=2$. These choices agree on the shared subspace and restrict to the original maps.

For completeness let $e_i$ be the chosen ordered basis of $V$. A central extension on $V\times W$ is obtained by adding to $(v,w)+(v',w')$ the $W$-valued cocycle
$$
 \sum_{i>j}v_i v'_j b(e_i,e_j)
 +\sum_i \left\lfloor\frac{\widetilde v_i+\widetilde v'_i}{p}\right\rfloor q(e_i),
$$
where the tildes are the digit representatives in $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$. The bilinear sum is a cocycle, and the carry sum obeys the cocycle identity because either grouping computes the same total carry in adding three digits. The coordinate lifts have the specified $p$-powers and commutators. These relations present the group, since collection orders any word as a product of the basis lifts with exponents in $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$ and a single element of $W$, and all these normal forms exist distinctly in the cocycle construction. Apply the same presentation argument to the input groups. Mapping the chosen lifts of each input basis into the corresponding cosets and mapping its center along the prescribed inclusion respects the complete presentation. The maps are injective by injectivity on the quotient and center.

The threshold problem is in NP because a pair of subspace bases indexed by one common basis verifies a witness for $k$. Test linear independence and the bracket and power equalities on basis pairs and basis vectors. If the threshold is expressed as a binary integer, compare orders or recover its integer log bound in polynomial bit time. For hardness, use a graph $\Gamma$ with $n$ non-isolated vertices; deleting isolated vertices and translating the threshold preserves Independent Set hardness. Set $V_1=F^n$ and put $b_1(e_i,e_j)=z_{ij}$ for $i<j$ adjacent, zero for nonedges, with one independent central variable $z_{ij}$ per edge. The radical is zero because each vertex occurs on an edge with its own independent bracket coordinate. Every independent set spans a totally isotropic subspace. Conversely in an isotropic subspace of dimension $s$ the coordinate functionals span the dual, and two corresponding to adjacent vertices have zero wedge. Choosing $s$ independent coordinate functionals therefore gives an independent set of size $s$.

Take $V_2=F^{2n}$ with its usual nondegenerate symplectic form taking values in an independent one-dimensional center. Set $W=Z_1\oplus Z_2$ and $q_1=q_2=0$, and use the Baer construction at fixed odd $p$. Since central images intersect trivially, the bracket restrictions agree exactly when both are zero. The symplectic input has a totally isotropic space of dimension $n$. Thus $k=\alpha(\Gamma)$, and comparison of the minimum order with $p^{\dim W+3n-t}$ decides whether $\alpha(\Gamma)\ge t$.
