# Exact and streaming induced motif counts

Throughout, an induced motif copy means a subset of the host inducing a graph isomorphic to the $k$-vertex motif $F$. For exact dynamic programs we count injective induced embeddings of the labeled $F$, then divide by $|\operatorname{Aut}(F)|$. The automorphism count can be computed in $k!\,k^{O(1)}$ time. [Tohme–Grochow, tree-shell motif counting and future directions](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38686v1#S5)

## GP-0940 — Exact near-linear counting at bounded rank-width

Given an adjacency-list graph $G$ of rank-width at most $r$, its exact induced motif count can be computed in deterministic $f(r,k)n^{1+o(1)}+O(m)$ word operations, for $n$ vertices and $m$ edges. Consequently the same algorithm is a relative-error approximation scheme for every promised positive count, and also handles zero counts. Host input size includes the $m$ explicit edges.

Use the annotated rank decomposition constructed in the proof of the almost-linear rank-width theorem of Korhonen and Sokołowski. Its rooted binary tree has one leaf per vertex. For the leaf set $X$ below a node, the annotations use a representative for each equivalence class under equality of neighborhoods in $V(G)\setminus X$. There are at most $2^r$ such classes, since the rows of the $X$-to-complement cut matrix lie in an $r$-dimensional binary space. At a binary node, child annotations give adjacency between the classes of one child and those of the other by the representative edges. They also give the projection of each child's class to its class for the parent cut. Indeed, toward the parent complement the neighborhood is the restriction of the one toward the child complement. Both adjacency and the projection are exactly the local data in the annotated-decomposition construction. [Korhonen–Sokołowski, Theorem 1.1 and Lemmas 8.4 and 8.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12364#S8)

A node state records, separately for each role of $F$, either absence from $X$ or a class of its image in $X$. The table value is the number of injective induced embeddings of the subgraph $F$ on its present roles, respecting the specified classes. Initialize singleton leaves with the empty map and the one-role choices. At a binary join take states with disjoint role sets, compare every across-child role pair against the annotated representative edges, and accept only if edges and nonedges agree with $F$. Multiply child counts and add to the parent state obtained by the class projections. Each embedding splits uniquely across the disjoint child leaf sets, and the representative test checks all remaining pairs. This proves the recurrence. The root state with all roles present gives the answer. The work per node is $k^{O(1)}(2^r+1)^{2k}$ arithmetic operations and counts are at most $n^k$. Multiword arithmetic costs only a function of $k$ using $O(\log n)$-bit words. The decomposition and this DP give the claimed total time.

## GP-0941 — Quadratic space is tight in a single adversarial edge pass

Fix a relative error $0<\epsilon<1/3$. In a one-pass insertion-only stream of the edges of a simple graph on a known $n$-vertex set, randomized space $\Theta(n^2)$ bits is necessary and sufficient to estimate the number of induced triangles within relative error $\epsilon$ with success probability at least $2/3$. The lower bound holds under the promises that the count is positive and no pendant tree types are present. It also applies to density normalized by the known $\binom n3$.

Reduce one-way randomized $\mathrm{INDEX}$ on a $q\times q$ bit table to the streaming problem. Alice's data edges are the bipartite edges $a_i b_j$ for one bits, with $q$ vertices on each side. Add two dummy vertices on each side, with every edge from a dummy vertex to the opposite side. Every vertex in this bipartite part has degree at least two, and it creates no triangle. Alice also inserts a separate baseline triangle and passes the streaming memory state to Bob. For his queried pair $(i,j)$, Bob inserts the two edges from one additional vertex $v$ to $a_i$ and $b_j$. These are the only neighbors of $v$. The final graph is its own 2-core and has exactly $1+\mathrm{INDEX}(i,j)$ triangles. The possible approximation intervals are disjoint for $\epsilon<1/3$. Hence a successful estimate solves $\mathrm{INDEX}$ on $q^2$ bits with the same success probability. The known one-way randomized lower bound gives $\Omega(q^2)=\Omega(n^2)$ bits. An adjacency matrix attains the bound and permits exact evaluation afterwards. [Kremer–Nisan–Ron, randomized one-round communication complexity](https://doi.org/10.1007/s000370050018)

This gives the tight worst-case space for a uniform connected induced motif estimator in the stated model. The bound also holds for every fixed clique motif of order at least three by replacing the queried degree-two vertex with a clique of the appropriate size, all of whose vertices meet exactly the two queried old vertices. Use a disjoint copy of the clique motif as the baseline. A full motif clique meeting the query gadget must use both queried nodes and every gadget vertex, since the old graph is bipartite and none of the gadget vertices touches another old vertex. Space can depend on the particular motif and stream model.

## GP-0942 — Logarithmic amortized updates for a bounded core

Let $F$ be fixed. Suppose an $n$-vertex host changes by edge insertions or deletions and at every step has a vertex set of size at most $c$ whose removal leaves a forest. The set may change arbitrarily and need not be supplied. The exact count of $F$ can be maintained in $f(c,k)\log n$ amortized word operations per edit. In particular this handles a bounded core with pendant trees even if all attachment types are distinct or change during an edit.

A decomposition of the forest of width at most one becomes a decomposition of the host by putting the removed vertices into every bag. Thus the graph has treewidth at most $c+1$. Start the dynamic treewidth structure from the empty graph and insert any initial edges first, since every intermediate subgraph of the initial graph has the same width bound. The structure responds to edge edits while maintaining a binary decomposition with bag size $O(c+1)$. Korhonen's theorem permits a bottom-up automaton whose transition is computed from the states and bags of the two children and the graph induced by the current bag. [Korhonen, Theorem 1.1 and §9](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02790)

For a decomposition node with bag $B$, let $X$ be the union of all bags below it, including $B$. A state consists of an active subset $S$ of motif roles and an injection specifying exactly which of these roles map into $B$ and where. The value counts induced embeddings on $S$ into $G[X]$ with these bag hits. To compute a parent value, assign each active nonbag role to a private child region. These regions are disjoint because occurrence sets of any vertex in the decomposition are connected. For each child, the roles active there are its private roles and precisely the parent bag hits in the child bag. Sum over the child bag assignments of the private roles which are consistent with this specification. The edges or nonedges within each child's active set are already checked. Check the parent bag hits against the parent bag graph. All remaining pairs between distinct private child regions, or between a private region and a parent bag hit outside the corresponding child bag, must be nonedges by the separation property of tree decompositions. Require them to be nonedges of $F$. Multiply child values for each legal tuple and sum. A leaf uses only its bag graph. The recurrence counts every induced embedding exactly once because its private region and bag hits are uniquely determined. It is a valid tree decomposition automaton with table size and per-node arithmetic bounded by a function of $c,k$. Counts take $O(k\log n)$ bits. Summing the root values with all $k$ roles active gives the maintained answer, before division by $|\operatorname{Aut}(F)|$.

## GP-0944 — Weighted and directed attachment types

Given a directed host with any prescribed core of $c$ vertices and a forest of pendant rooted trees, exact induced motif counts can be computed with weighted and oriented subtree reuse. More precisely assume each exterior tree has only one edge of its underlying undirected graph touching the core, with arbitrary labels and directions in both ordered directions on this edge. Allow arbitrary edges in the core. Use exact labels on vertices and on ordered arcs, with a distinguished symbol for absent arcs. For a fixed labeled motif on $k$ roles, the algorithm computes a sum over injective role maps: each term is the product of its chosen integral single-vertex scores and its chosen integral ordered-pair scores. Each score is a supplied exact function of the corresponding host label, and score zero may disallow that label. In particular equality tests for both arcs and nonarcs give exact induced counts of weighted or directed motifs. This model also allows integral edge weights in the objective. For unlabeled weighted motif copies with the same score on each motif-automorphism orbit, divide the labeled sum by the order of the weighted directed motif automorphism group.

Canonical types are built from the root vertex label and a sorted multiset of child types, with both ordered parent–child arc labels in each multiset item. Induction on height proves this identifies exactly the label-preserving rooted isomorphism classes. Sorting and interning the descriptors creates a DAG of types from explicit adjacency lists using $O(N\log N)$ label comparisons and operations on identifiers, with $N$ the host description length. Equality of the type is also sufficient for equality of every table below. If the input already describes the rooted types and their multiplicities explicitly, this scan of the expanded attachments is unnecessary.

For a rooted tree type $T$, store $D_T(S,j)$, where $S$ is the selected role set and $j$ is either the role at the root or a symbol indicating that the root is omitted. Count score products within the tree, respecting exactly that root status. Fix the parent root status $j$. A child table may first be decorated by summing over its root status and multiplying by every required parent–child pair score. Only the pair between the two roots can see any present arc. If the parent is selected, all its pairs with other selected vertices of that child see absent arcs in both directions. The parent vertex factor is counted separately. Every pair across distinct child trees also sees absent arcs. Thus decorated child tables combine on disjoint subsets by subset convolution with a multiplier
$$
 \prod_{a\in S,\ b\in T} F_{ab}(\mathrm{absent})F_{ba}(\mathrm{absent}),
$$
where $F_{ab}$ is the ordered-pair scoring function for roles $a,b$. This convolution is associative since, for three disjoint role sets, both associations introduce exactly the same three sets of cross-pair factors. Raise a decorated child table of multiplicity $m$ to its $m$-th power by binary powering. The convolution counts copies in the distinct child positions represented by the multiplicity and needs no enumeration of those positions. At the parent add its own selected role and score, if any. The resulting recurrence counts all score products exactly once by the unique partition of any embedding into selected child trees and the root.

Finally enumerate injections of any role subset into the core, bounded by $(c+1)^k$. Compute the factors internal to this assignment, decorate rooted attachment tables using their core parent and each other selected core role, and convolve the resulting tables across distinct attachments, again using powers for identical decorated types. The decoration is the same operation as the one-root case, with each core role outside the marked parent always seeing absence. The core may therefore have arbitrary directions and weights, and in particular may be sparse.

The per-table operations take $f(k)$ arithmetic steps. For each descriptor multiplicity $m$ they take $O(f(k)\log(m+1))$ operations. Along with the core enumeration, this is fixed-parameter arithmetic time in $k,c$, near-linear in the type DAG description and in the explicit host if unpacked. Integer scores of bit length at most $B$ yield intermediate DP counts with $O(k\log(n+1)+k^2B)$ bits since each term uses at most $k+k(k-1)$ factors and a table sums at most $n^k$ embeddings. Exact comparisons and integer arithmetic give corresponding parameter-uniform bit complexity. Even when multiplicities are encoded in binary, $\log n$ is bounded by a polynomial in the total descriptor length. The result imposes no bound on the number of distinct rooted types and gains reuse when repeated copies are present.

All four arguments are written proofs. The width algorithms and the one-way communication lower bound are published inputs.
