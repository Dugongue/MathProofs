# Flat permutation constraints on bounded-degree surfaces

## GP-0996 — Exact bounded-degree transfer

Fix an action of a finite group $G$ on a finite alphabet $A$. An oriented edge $u\to v$ with label $g$ is satisfied if $x_v=g x_u$. Reversing the orientation replaces $g$ by $g^{-1}$. For every connected unweighted constraint multigraph $I$, a deterministic polynomial-time construction gives a closed connected orientable simplicial surface with $O(|E(I)|+1)$ simplices and an absolute bound on vertex degree. Its edges have labels in the same action, every triangle has identity holonomy, and the minimum number of violations is exactly $6\operatorname{OPT}(I)$. The reduction can separately process connected components if disconnected surfaces are allowed. Every approximation factor for minimization transfers without loss.

**Bounded-degree reduction.** As in the portfolio result on [surface homology localization](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/SurfaceHomologyLocalization), split a vertex with $d>0$ incidences into the vertices of the Margulis–Gabber–Galil expander on $s^2$ points, $s=\lceil\sqrt d\rceil$. Assign the incidences injectively to copies and add six parallel equality constraints per expander edge other than loops. A loop among the original constraints uses two distinct incidences. Each copy is incident to at most 49 constraints. The $d=1$ case needs only one copy. For an expander set of at most half the copies, its edge boundary is at least $h$ times its size, for $h=(8-5\sqrt2)/2>1/3$, by the spectral bound applied to its centered indicator. Suppose $r$ copies are outside a largest label class. If that class has at least half the copies, the boundary of its complement contains at least $hr$ edges. Otherwise summing the boundary bounds for all classes and dividing by two gives at least $hs^2/2\ge hr/2$ disagreeing edges. Therefore rounding all copies to the largest class removes at least $r$ equality violations and can introduce at most $r$ old-edge violations. Constant labeling of the copies is the reverse construction and preserves cost exactly. This argument works for arbitrary permutations because only equalities were added. The transformation has linear size, preserves connectedness, and preserves the optimum. [Gabber–Galil, spectral expansion construction](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(81)90040-4)

**Vertex regions.** For each vertex of incidence degree $d$, use a triangulated sphere with $d$ holes, one disjoint triangular boundary per incidence. Store an interior anchor and two paths from the anchor to each of the three vertices on every boundary. All these paths are edge-disjoint. The only intersections are shared anchors and common designated endpoints. Such a region can be built as follows. Choose $d$ small closed disks with disjoint interiors that meet each other only at the anchor, which belongs to their boundaries. Put a hole inside each disk. Cut the resulting annulus along an auxiliary arc from the inner to the outer boundary, avoiding the anchor and the three specified vertices. The cut-open disk has all port vertices and the anchor on its boundary. Draw a fan from the anchor to the three ports in this disk, and replace each fan arc by two arcs within a thin neighborhood that share the same endpoints. Put a separate subdivision vertex on each of the resulting arcs. Glue the cut back. Take a finite triangulation relative to the marked arcs and hole edges, using interior vertices as necessary. Collars of each hole can be included in this process so that no edge joins vertices on distinct boundary components. This can also be seen by triangulating the complementary pieces of the finite embedded arc and collar diagram as polygons, adding interior vertices to avoid unwanted diagonals. For $d=0$ use a triangulated sphere. Since $d\le49$, one fixed finite family of regions suffices, so all sizes and internal vertex degrees are absolutely bounded.

**Edge cylinders.** For each edge $e=(u,v)$, join the two corresponding boundaries by a triangulated triangular prism cylinder. For cyclically numbered rings $a_0,a_1,a_2$ and $b_0,b_1,b_2$, use triangles $(a_i,a_{i+1},b_{i+1})$ and $(a_i,b_{i+1},b_i)$ with indices modulo three. Glue the boundary circles compatibly with the orientable surface orientation. Each boundary vertex meets two cross edges, and there are six cross edges in total. Label each cross edge oriented from the $u$-region to the $v$-region by $g_e$. Give all edges internal to a region the identity label. Each prism triangle contains either two same-label cross edges traversed in opposite directions or only identity edge labels along the relevant boundary segment. Hence every triangle has identity holonomy. The collar condition ensures all new cross edges are distinct from existing region edges even if both ports belong to one region. Boundary links are joined into circles by the cylinders, and each boundary circle is used exactly once. This is a closed orientable simplicial surface. Its connectedness follows from that of the constraint graph. It presents a genuine $G$-cover with the stated $A$-fiber action by the flat edge labels. [Grochow–Tucker-Foltz, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.06800#A1)

**Cost.** An assignment on the original bounded-degree graph extends constantly on each region and violates exactly six edges per violated old constraint. Conversely fix the values at the anchors as the decoded assignment. For each old edge route six paths between its two anchors. Each path uses one distinct cross edge and one allocated anchor path at each end. Every routed path has transport $g_e$ and all routes are edge-disjoint, including routes belonging to different old edges. If the decoded constraint is violated, all six paths must contain a violated edge. Thus anchor decoding divides total cost by at least six, and composing with the expander rounding proves the optimum claim.

## GP-0995 — Dichotomy for transitive actions

For every fixed finite nontrivial transitive action $G\curvearrowright A$, there exists $\epsilon_A>0$ such that $(1+\epsilon_A)$-approximation of the minimum number of violations is NP-hard on bounded-degree closed connected orientable triangulated surfaces, even for labels flat on every triangle. For a one-point action the optimum is always zero. Here nontrivial means $|A|>1$ and $G$ may be nonabelian.

Burnside's average fixed-point count is one. Since the identity has $|A|>1$ fixed points, some $g\in G$ has no fixed point. Start with an undirected graph $X$ on $m$ edges. Replace each edge $uv$ with two constraints $x_v=gx_u$ and $x_u=gx_v$. Let $H$ be the disjoint union of the simple undirected graphs obtained from the cycles of $g$, keeping a single edge for each 2-cycle.

If a 2-cycle exists, the optimum number of satisfied constraints is $2\operatorname{MaxCut}(X)$. A cut can use the two labels of a 2-cycle. In the other direction, send labels in every 2-cycle to one chosen 2-cycle by a bijection on each cycle. Keep these assignments fixed, and independently round every other vertex uniformly to the chosen 2-cycle. An edge with an endpoint outside a 2-cycle can originally satisfy at most one constraint. Such an edge has expected satisfied score one after rounding. On an edge with both endpoints in 2-cycles, originally satisfied constraints remain satisfied. This proves the claimed upper bound by expectation.

If no 2-cycle exists, at most one constraint per edge is satisfied, and it is satisfied exactly when the assigned labels are adjacent in $H$. If all cycles are even, $H$ maps homomorphically to an edge and an edge maps to $H$, giving optimum satisfied count $\operatorname{MaxCut}(X)$.

Otherwise let $\ell$ be the smallest odd cycle length in $H$. Every cycle in $H$ maps to $C_\ell$. For even cycles alternate across one edge. For a length-$r$ odd cycle, traverse $C_\ell$ once and insert $(r-\ell)/2$ backtracking pairs, obtaining a closed walk of length $r$. Also $C_\ell$ embeds in $H$. Put $L=\ell-2$. The relation “joined by a walk of length $L$” on $C_\ell$ is exactly the irreflexive complete graph. There is no odd closed walk of length less than $\ell$, and for every distinct pair one of the two paths along $C_\ell$ has odd length at most $L$ and can be padded by backtracking. Both homomorphisms respect walk-length relations. Thus the walk-length-$L$ relation on $H$ is homomorphically equivalent to $K_\ell$.

Now replace every edge of $X$ by an internally disjoint length-$L$ path before applying the two-constraint construction. For fixed original endpoints, the path can satisfy all $L$ adjacent-label relations exactly when the endpoint pair belongs to the walk-length-$L$ relation on $H$. Otherwise it can always satisfy its first $L-1$ relations, independent of the other paths, since $H$ has no isolated vertex. Hence the optimum satisfied score is $(L-1)m+\operatorname{Max}\ell\operatorname{Cut}(X)$.

In all three cases the minimum violated score is an affine decreasing function of the corresponding Max $k$-Cut score with constants depending only on $A$. The known constant additive hardness gap in the fraction of edges cut gives an additive $\Omega_A(m)$ gap in violated constraints, while the number of all constraints is $O_A(m)$. Hence there is a multiplicative gap above one. The input cut graph may be made connected by adding a tree of bridges between components. One can always recolor each component by a color permutation to cut every bridge, so the optimum increases by exactly the bridge count. After removing isolated vertices before joining, at most $m-1$ bridges are needed and the linear gap persists. Apply GP-0996 to this connected constraint instance. [Kann–Khanna–Lagergren–Panconesi, Max $k$-Cut gap](https://scholar.lib.vt.edu/ejournals/CJTCS/cjtcs/articles/1997/2/contents.html)

Both arguments are written proofs. The surface transfer uses the equality rounding in the existing abelian portfolio proof, and its routed primal constraints then give a distinct extension to arbitrary finite actions. These results establish hardness at a factor depending on the action. They do not give arbitrary-factor hardness for a single fixed action.
