# Kapovich–Millson universality on closed hyperbolic three-manifolds

The first part of Kapovich–Millson's Question 8.2 has a positive answer as printed. Their Theorem 1.1 and Corollaries 1.3 and 1.4 hold with closed hyperbolic source manifolds. In particular the \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) statement retains its dense \(\mathrm{SU}_2\) base representation and its full equivariant regular étale covering. The orientable version is false, even for reduced affine input. The rational and integral homology-sphere versions are also false. [Kapovich–Millson, Question 8.2](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~kapovich/EPR/char.pdf) · [Kapovich's formulation for closed orientable hyperbolic manifolds](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~kapovich/EPR/KapovichCHARACTERS.pdf)

## 1. Hyperbolic replacement with perfect kernel

**Theorem 1.** Let \(N\) be a closed connected three-manifold, with either orientability type. There is a closed hyperbolic three-manifold \(M\) of the same orientability type and a surjection
\[
f\colon \pi_1(M)\longrightarrow \pi_1(N)
\]
whose kernel is perfect and normally generated by one element. For every commutative base ring \(B\) and closed affine group scheme \(G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{d,B}\), pullback along \(f\) identifies \(\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(N),G)\) with an open-and-closed subscheme of \(\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(M),G)\), equivariantly for conjugation. The same \(M,f\) works for all \(B,G\). The schemes here represent homomorphisms to \(G(R)\) as \(R\) ranges over commutative \(B\)-algebras.

**Proof.** Choose a small standard unknot \(J\) in a ball in \(N\). Myers' theorem gives a knot \(K\) with excellent exterior, joined to \(J\) by an embedded ribbon concordance in \(N\times[0,1]\). The hypothesis on sphere and projective-plane boundary components in Myers' theorem is vacuous for closed \(N\). The theorem applies also when \(N\) is nonorientable. The knot \(K\) is nullhomotopic because it is concordant to \(J\), and its normal disk bundle is trivial. Write \(E\) for its compact exterior and choose a meridian \(\mu\) on the peripheral torus. The excellent interior carries a complete finite-volume hyperbolic metric by hyperbolization. [Myers, Theorem 1.1 and the discussion of excellent exteriors](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-8641(93)90038-F) · [Full text](https://openresearch.okstate.edu/bitstreams/b948a900-c087-4041-a3c5-8ccea8ee57fe/download)

We choose a longitude with a property in the cover induced by the universal cover of \(N\). Let \(U\) be that universal cover and \(\widetilde E\) the preimage of \(E\) in \(U\). Put \(L=\ker(\pi_1(E)\to\pi_1(N))\). By van Kampen the map is onto, with \(L\) the normal closure of \(\mu\), so \(\pi_1(\widetilde E)=L\). The concordance annulus has trivial image on fundamental groups in \(N\times[0,1]\), since either of its boundary circles is nullhomotopic there. Its preimage in \(U\times[0,1]\) is therefore a locally finite disjoint union of annuli, each mapping homeomorphically to the original annulus, with one lift of the knot at each end. The lifted union has a tubular neighborhood given by the lifted disk bundles. Write \(\widetilde W\) for its exterior and \(\widetilde E_J,\widetilde E_K=\widetilde E\) for the corresponding end exteriors.

Each inclusion \(\widetilde E_j\to\widetilde W\), for \(j=J,K\), induces an isomorphism on \(H_1(-;\mathbb Z)\). To see this, compare the exact segments
\[
H_2(U)\longrightarrow H_2(U,\widetilde E_j)
\longrightarrow H_1(\widetilde E_j)\longrightarrow H_1(U)=0
\]
and the corresponding segment for \((U\times[0,1],\widetilde W)\). Absolute \(H_2\) is unchanged by the end inclusion. In relative degree two, excision and the normal disk Thom isomorphism identify the two relative groups with direct sums of \(\mathbb Z\), indexed respectively by lifted knots at that end and lifted annuli. A transverse oriented disk is the generator of each summand. The end inclusion identifies the summands by the bijection of components, and carries the transverse generator to the transverse generator. The normal orientations exist on all lifts. Ordinary chains have compact support, so excision here gives direct sums also for an infinite cover. Thus the cokernels in the two exact sequences agree.

The normal plane bundle of the concordance in \(N\times[0,1]\) is oriented because the annulus is orientable and its fundamental group maps trivially to that of \(N\). Its framing extends the disk framing of \(J\), since the annulus retracts onto this end circle. The resulting parallel annulus in the exterior joins the zero-framed longitude of \(J\) to a longitude \(\lambda\) on \(K\). In the lifted unknot exterior the bottom longitude bounds the truncated disk in the lifted ball. The parallel annulus hence makes the lifted top longitude zero in \(H_1(\widetilde W;\mathbb Z)\). The end isomorphism just proved makes it zero in \(H_1(\widetilde E;\mathbb Z)\). In group terms,
\[
\lambda\in [L,L].
\]
In particular the chosen longitude lies in \(L\), also apparent because it maps to a parallel of the nullhomotopic core after meridional filling.

For an integer \(n\), fill \(E\) along the primitive slope \(\mu\lambda^n\), obtaining \(M_n\). The slopes are distinct and leave every finite set of slopes. For all sufficiently large \(|n|\), hyperbolic Dehn filling makes \(M_n\) closed hyperbolic. For a nonorientable \(E\), its orientation double cover has two tori over the peripheral torus. The slopes and standard solid-torus fillings lift to both. The orientable double cover of the filled manifold is hyperbolic by ordinary simultaneous hyperbolic Dehn filling. The free deck involution is smoothly conjugate to an isometric action by equivariant geometrization and gives a hyperbolic metric downstairs. This also checks the nonorientable case directly from the usual orientable filling theorem. [Thurston, *The Geometry and Topology of Three-Manifolds*, Chapter 5](https://library.slmath.org/books/gt3m/) · [Dinkelbach–Leeb, Theorem H](https://msp.org/gt/2009/13-2/gt-v13-n2-p15-p.pdf) · [Durán Batalla–Porti, nonorientable Dehn filling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01027)

Both \(\mu\) and \(\lambda\) die in \(\pi_1(N)\). The resulting epimorphism \(\pi_1(M_n)\to\pi_1(N)\) has kernel \(P=L/Q_n\), where \(Q_n\) is the normal closure of \(\mu\lambda^n\) **in \(\pi_1(E)\)**, a subgroup of \(L\). In \(L/[L,L]\), the class of \(\lambda\) and of each of its \(\pi_1(E)\)-conjugates is zero because \([L,L]\) is characteristic in the normal subgroup \(L\). Thus the images of the \(\pi_1(E)\)-conjugates of \(\mu\lambda^n\) in \(L/[L,L]\) are just the images of the corresponding conjugates of \(\mu\). Those classes generate \(L/[L,L]\), since \(\mu\) normally generates \(L\) in \(\pi_1(E)\). It follows that \(P/[P,P]=0\). The image of \(\mu\) still normally generates \(P\) in \(\pi_1(M_n)\). Orientability is unchanged by the two-sided solid-torus surgery. One can see this from the orientation character on the common exterior, which is trivial on the peripheral torus and extends over the filling.

For the algebraic assertion choose such an \(n\), write \(A\) for the coordinate ring of the \(G\)-representation scheme of \(\pi_1(M_n)\), and let \(I\) be the ideal of matrix entries of \(\rho_{\rm univ}(\mu)-1\). These finitely many entries cut out representations factoring through \(\pi_1(N)\), since \(\mu\) normally generates \(P\). Reduce the universal representation modulo \(I^2\). Every element of \(P\) now lies in the group of matrices \(1+T\) with \(T\) in \(\operatorname{Mat}_d(I/I^2)\). That group is abelian because \(TT'=0\) and
\[
(1+T)(1+T')=1+T+T'.
\]
Perfectness of \(P\) makes its image trivial in the reduced representation, so all generators of \(I\) belong to \(I^2\). Hence \(I=I^2\). At any prime containing \(I\), Nakayama's lemma gives \(I_{\mathfrak p}=0\). At any other prime, \(I_{\mathfrak p}=A_{\mathfrak p}\). Since \(I\) is finitely generated, \(V(I)\) is the complement of the closed support of \(I\), and its closed subscheme structure agrees with the corresponding open subscheme. The quotient is therefore open and closed, including its entire scheme structure. Pullback respects conjugation. \(\square\)

For closed orientable sources, Kawauchi's hyperbolic imitations already supply a hyperbolic replacement with a perfect-kernel epimorphism. The proof above uses Myers' concordance to give a construction for both orientability types and applies the perfect kernel to representation schemes. The totally nullhomotopic knot method gives another oriented precedent for the topological step. [Kawauchi, hyperbolic imitations, Corollary 4.3](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/8213/ojm26_04_02.pdf) · [Kawauchi, Property V](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/7102/ojm26_03_01.pdf) · [Cochran–Masters, Proposition 1.2 and the Boileau–Wang construction](https://math.rice.edu/~cochran/virtualbetti8_8.pdf)

## 2. The exact Kapovich–Millson statement

**Theorem 2.** Kapovich–Millson's Theorem 1.1 and Corollaries 1.3 and 1.4 continue to hold with closed hyperbolic three-manifold fundamental groups, with the original targets, base representations, and equivariant scheme data.

**Proof.** Start with their original witnesses for a pointed affine \(\mathbb Q\)-scheme \((X,x)\), \(x\in X(\mathbb Q)\). Apply Theorem 1 to each of the two source manifolds used for the \(\mathrm{PO}_3\) and \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) statements. Use the conjugation-equivariant open-and-closed embeddings of the full representation schemes. An open in the original scheme is therefore an open in the new scheme with the same functor of points. In particular the original invariant opens and cross-sections embed without any further restriction. The product isomorphism for \(\mathrm{PO}_3\) over \(\mathbb Q\) stays the same. The regular étale covering in Corollary 1.4 is transported using the original maps on these identical opens, including its deck group, cross-section and quotient covering. Each chosen base representation has exactly its old image because the inducing group homomorphism is onto. This preserves density in \(\mathrm{SU}_2\) for the \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) base point.

For clarity, taking a character scheme does not introduce a further neighborhood issue. In each ambient affine representation scheme the closed factor is conjugation invariant. Its complementary open-and-closed subscheme is invariant too, since conjugation preserves the factorization through the quotient group. The coordinate algebra decomposes equivariantly as a product. Taking invariants gives the product of the invariant algebras, so the old full character scheme is also an open-and-closed factor of the new one. Thus the original analytic representation and character germs, the original étale neighborhoods and all nilpotent structure are retained. For the \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) statement the resulting germ is \((X_{\mathbb C}\times\mathbb C^{3k},(x,0))\) on the character side, and \((X_{\mathbb C}\times\mathbb C^{3k+3},(x,0))\) on the representation side, for the same \(k\) as before. \(\square\)

## 3. The orientable and homology-sphere versions

**Theorem 3.** There is an affine \(\mathbb Q\)-scheme with a rational point whose complex analytic germ, after taking any smooth factor, does not occur in an \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C)\) representation scheme of a closed orientable three-manifold. The same holds at a dense unitary character in the character scheme. One may take the reduced node \(X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q[x,y]/(xy)\) at the origin.

**Proof.** For a closed oriented three-manifold, the derived stack of Betti \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)-local systems is \((-1)\)-shifted symplectic. This is the Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi mapping-stack theorem with the three-manifold fundamental class and the invariant trace pairing on \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\). Its classical truncation is the quotient stack \([\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(M),\mathrm{SL}_2)/\mathrm{SL}_2]\), with the full scheme of representations. By the d-critical truncation theorem of Ben-Bassat–Brav–Bussi–Joyce, any smooth scheme mapping to that classical stack inherits a d-critical structure. The representation scheme is a smooth atlas of this quotient stack in the relative sense, since \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) is smooth. Thus its completed germ at any complex point has a presentation as the critical locus of a function on a smooth formal space. The same critical-locus description at irreducible character points follows directly from the Heegaard intersection of two complex Lagrangians, as in Abouzaid–Manolescu. One can also use the analytic or étale quotient slice: at an irreducible representation conjugation by \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) is free and the local representation germ is the character germ times a smooth three-dimensional factor. [Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi, Theorem 2.5 and its Betti application](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.3209) · [Ben-Bassat–Brav–Bussi–Joyce, Definition 3.12 and Theorem 3.18](https://msp.org/gt/2015/19-3/gt-v19-n3-p05-s.pdf) · [Abouzaid–Manolescu, Sections 2–3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.00289)

Here is the formal obstruction, including smooth factors. Suppose a critical locus has completed local ring
\[
\mathbb C[[x,y,t_1,\ldots,t_s]]/(xy).
\]
First reduce the ambient embedding to its minimal dimension. In any formal critical-locus presentation choose coordinates splitting off a block in which the Hessian is invertible. The formal implicit function theorem solves the corresponding partial derivative equations for those coordinates as series in the remaining ones. Substitute the solution into the potential. By the chain rule the remaining derivatives of the substituted potential are exactly the remaining equations, because the eliminated derivatives vanish. Their linear parts are zero because only Hessian-null directions remain. The resulting power series ambient dimension is the embedding dimension \(s+2\). Lift the formal isomorphism of quotient rings to ambient power series rings by choosing lifts of the images of the coordinates. The lift is an isomorphism by its invertible linear part, since both ideals have zero linear terms. The chain rule carries a gradient ideal to the gradient ideal of the transformed potential.

We now have a function \(F\in\mathbb C[[x,y,t_1,\ldots,t_s]]\) whose partial derivatives generate \((xy)\). Every partial is divisible by \(xy\). For a monomial of \(F\) involving \(x\) or \(y\), divisibility of both normal partial derivatives implies that its exponents in \(x,y\) are both at least two. This coefficient comparison holds in characteristic zero. The part depending only on \(t\) has all derivatives zero, so is constant. Consequently \(F\in\mathbb C+(x^2y^2)\). Its partial derivatives then lie in \((xy)(x,y)\), which is a proper subideal of \((xy)\). This contradiction proves that the node times any smooth germ is never a formal critical locus. It cannot occur analytically either, since an analytic isomorphism induces an isomorphism of completions.

A dense unitary representation is irreducible. If its character germ were the node times a smooth factor, the corresponding representation germ would also be the node times a smooth factor by the local smooth framing factor, contradicting the preceding argument. This proves the orientable obstruction for the form of universality asked by Kapovich–Millson. A closed rational homology three-sphere is orientable because its rational top homology is nonzero. Thus both homology-sphere versions are excluded, even without requiring hyperbolicity. \(\square\)

The two conclusions distinguish the formulation in the published paper, which permits nonorientable manifolds, from the orientable formulation in Kapovich's slides. The proofs use no marking on the resulting \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) representation or character schemes.
