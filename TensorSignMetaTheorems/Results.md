# Meta-theorems for determinant constraints in tensor isomorphism

These are informal proofs. Over F₃ let P₀ be ordinary three-tensor isomorphism, P₁ and P₂ impose one and two designated determinant-one conditions, and P₃ impose all three. For positive mode dimensions write G for the product of the three general linear groups, and write s(g)∈F₂³ for the determinant signs of a transporter. Its sign profile is empty or an affine coset b+H of the source automorphism sign image. Earlier constructions realize exact linear projections of these profiles, pack independent one-sign comparisons into separate axes, and compile ordinary auxiliary constraints while retaining up to three designated signs. The unrestricted reverse reductions are open. [Sign projections and axis packing](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/SignProjections.md) · [Signed compilation](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignChain/RetainedSigns.md)

## 1. Local consistency can amplify a reduction

**Five-object theorem.** Suppose a deterministic polynomial-time procedure takes a batch of five P₃ objects and constructs five P₂ objects jointly, preserving the verdict on each pair in the batch. Then P₃ reduces many-one to P₀. The batch encodings can depend on all five inputs, so the hypothesis is weaker than a persistent object map.

For a P₁ comparison x,y, handle the easy branch of a deficient tested support or an odd tested dimension by the usual independent sign corrections. In the remaining branch the earlier format-wise independent-axis packing puts three one-sign comparisons in one P₃ comparison by an objectwise map on triples. Choose five distinct words of length three over {x,y} and encode them. If P₁(x,y) holds, the five source objects are all P₃-equivalent. If it fails, they are pairwise P₃-inequivalent. The joint encoding makes the same statements at level P₂. There are at most four P₂-orbits in a single ordinary orbit, by the index of its two determinant constraints. Thus in the negative case the five outputs cannot all be ordinarily equivalent. Determine this by four ordinary comparisons against a common reference and use ordinary conjunction packing. This reduces P₁ to P₀. Apply the seven one-sign projections and ordinary conjunction to reduce P₃ to P₀. [Earlier objectwise capacity argument](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/SignProjections.md) · [Kernel reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4775)

More generally the preserved comparisons can be the edges of any fixed connected graph with chromatic number greater than four on the distinct word objects. In the positive case its edges imply ordinary connectedness. In the negative case if all outputs share an ordinary orbit, their P₂ classes would give a proper four-coloring. On all eight words, the construction tolerates six false negative edges when the input is positive and three false positive edges when it is negative. Deleting six edges from K₈ leaves it connected, and a partition of eight objects into at most four classes has at least four monochromatic pairs.

**Criterion for a pair-dependent reduction.** Suppose f reduces P₃ to P₂, and let E_f(x,y) be the ordinary verdict on its output pair. If in each ordinary source orbit there is no set of five distinct P₃ classes with representatives all pairwise E_f-related, then the full P₃-to-P₀ reduction follows. To prove it, guard the original P₁ pair by ordinary equivalence as one packaged comparison. On the guarded negative branch the five axis-packed words belong to the same ordinary source orbit but distinct P₃ classes. Conjoin the ten ordinary verdicts E_f on those words. In the positive case all ten hold. In the negative case the hypothesized clique exclusion forces one to fail. Evaluation needs only the ordinary comparisons, so the guard and the ten tests compile together into one P₀ instance. Conversely if P₃ reduces to P₀, neutral padding gives an f whose E_f is exactly P₃, satisfying the criterion. Thus existence of a three-to-two reduction with this local property is equivalent to full collapse to ordinary TI.

One sufficient condition for the clique exclusion is compatible relative displacements on each such ordinarily positive batch. If the P₂ verdict holds exactly on zero displacements in a common group of size at most four, with displacements adding on triangles, select a root and give every object its displacement from the root. This gives at most four labels, so two objects agree. This condition makes sense for pair-dependent constructions that do not produce identical encodings of an object in different comparisons.

## 2. An exact test for when independent extra rows suffice

Fix a nonzero ℓ∈F₂³ and use the two-sign projection with kernel ⟨ℓ⟩. Let C be a family of characters χ with χ(ℓ)=1, each of whose one-sign tests is reducible to ordinary TI on the domain in question. The projected comparison conjoined with these independent ordinary comparisons is exact on the affine profiles for a family of stabilizer images H if and only if
\[
 C\cap H^\perp\ne\varnothing\quad\hbox{for every such }H\hbox{ that omits }\ell .
\]
Here the equivalence is for all affine translates of each specified H. To prove it, the only false positive of the retained two-sign test is the affine coset ℓ+H with ℓ outside H. A character in C rejects that coset exactly when it vanishes on H. Otherwise it takes both signs there, so its independent test passes. A positive original verdict passes every test. The ordinary comparisons can be absorbed into one two-sign instance using determinant-neutral conjunction.

The four characters taking value one on ℓ always suffice at the level of queries. If the image has rank zero, one suffices. If the image has rank at most one, any three suffice, and some rank-one image defeats every fixed two-element choice. Indeed the possible annihilating subsets of these four characters are all affine lines of the dual two-dimensional affine plane. If a computed upper bound K contains H and omits ℓ, one character annihilating K suffices. Thus a universal ordinary character-reading gadget on the same transporter is one route, while separately available ordinary comparisons are another route with exact hypotheses.

There is also a way to assemble partial ordinary marks directly. Suppose each of polynomially many ordinary enrichments is tied to the same raw transporter and extends every zero-sign transporter, and that their acceptability sets have empty intersection on transporters of sign ℓ. Combine the diagrams over the common raw roles, keeping private roles separate. This intersects their acceptability sets on each actual transporter. With the projection along ℓ the only possible signs on a positive output are zero and ℓ, and the latter is eliminated. This is useful even when none of the separate marks reads a determinant or supplies a total invariant.

## 3. The connected and finite contributions to a sign

Let T be rational over F₃ and let H be its reduced algebraic stabilizer in G. Removing possible nilpotents does not change geometric or rational points. Write H° for its connected component of the identity and F for Frobenius. Form the finite étale cover
\[
 \widetilde G=\{(g,z)\mid z_i^2=\det g_i,\ z_i\ne0\}
\]
with coordinatewise multiplication in z. It has central constant kernel K=μ₂³. The group is connected. In a single factor, multiply a matrix of determinant z² by diag(z⁻²,1,…,1) to obtain a variety isomorphism of the cover with SL_n×G_m. Over F₃ the projection of its rational points is exactly the all-positive sign group. Independent combinations of sign rows give the corresponding connected covers and intermediate groups, since those combinations are independent in the determinant lattice modulo two. Let \widetilde H be the inverse image of H and put A=K∩\widetilde H°.

**Connected sign theorem.** The sign image of H°(F₃) is A. Its annihilator in F₂³ consists exactly of the linear combinations of determinant characters that admit a character square root on H° over the algebraic closure.

The cover identity component surjects onto H° because the cover is finite étale and the target is connected. For a rational point h of H° choose its geometric lift in this component. Its Frobenius ratio on the three square-root coordinates is (z_i³/z_i)_i=(det h_i)_i, so the ratio is its sign in K∩\widetilde H°. Conversely for a∈A, Lang surjectivity on the connected cover component gives a lift whose Frobenius ratio equals a. Its projection is rational and realizes that sign. A coordinate character of the roots descends to H° exactly when it kills A. In that case it gives a character square root of the corresponding determinant product. Conversely if such a square root exists, its pullback and the root-coordinate character have ratio of order two. The ratio is trivial on a connected group, proving the annihilator description. The normalized root, if it exists, is unique. [Lang, algebraic groups over finite fields](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Lang-Algebraic_Groups_Over_Finite_Fields.pdf)

For a connected stabilizer the calculation can be made on one rational maximal torus when that torus and its weights are known. Characters kill the unipotent radical. In a reductive group, restrictions of characters to a maximal torus are the lattice of weights annihilating the coroots, which is saturated in the torus character lattice. A determinant product has a character square root on the group exactly when its restriction is divisible by two in that lattice. This shows why split scalar actions alone can miss sign corrections.

**Exact component equation.** Let C=π₀(\widetilde H), with its Frobenius action and the central images \bar b of b∈K. If T and S are geometrically equivalent, choose a geometric transporter in \widetilde G, denoted t. Set τ=t⁻¹F(t)∈\widetilde H. The exact raw sign profile is given by
\[
 b\hbox{ occurs}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bar\tau F(c)=\bar b\,c\quad\hbox{for some }c\in C.
\]
Every geometric lifted transporter is t h. Its raw projection is rational with sign b exactly when F(t h)=b t h, which gives the component equation. Conversely choose h₀ in the indicated component. Then τF(h₀)=b h₀u for some u in the connected stabilizer component. Replacing h₀ by h₀v asks for uF(v)=v, solvable by Lang on that connected component. This proves exactness, including when the ordinary rational transporter set is empty. If there is no geometric transporter the profile is empty. Thus an explicit polynomial-size component table, its Frobenius map, and the transporter cocycle component suffice for an exact polynomial decision. Constructing that data from arbitrary raw tensors remains a separate problem.

The component map \widetilde H→H induces a central extension
\[
 1\longrightarrow K/A\longrightarrow \pi_0(\widetilde H)
 \longrightarrow\pi_0(H)\longrightarrow1.
\]
The kernel is constant under Frobenius. Indeed the identity cover component already covers H°, and the ambiguity remaining from K is exactly A. For the absolute stabilizer sign calculation take T=S and τ=1. Any solution of F(c)=\bar b c can be raised to the odd part of its order without changing the right-hand sign, making it a 2-element. Also F²(c)=c. Thus absolute sign corrections admit a purely 2-primary search in the Frobenius-square-fixed part of the finite group. If Frobenius has odd order on this group, only \bar b=1 is possible and the connected sign image already suffices to describe the full stabilizer signs.

One may further quotient that Frobenius-square-fixed group by any odd-order F-invariant normal subgroup N, with exact preservation of the absolute sign test. To prove the reverse implication, use a 2-element solution \bar c in the quotient and let \bar P be the 2-group generated by \bar c and the central sign element. It is F-invariant by the solution equation. In its preimage the Sylow 2-subgroups project isomorphically to \bar P, since the kernel has odd order. Frobenius on the preimage has order at most two. The number of its Sylow 2-subgroups is odd, so an F-stable Sylow 2-subgroup exists. The central sign element lies in it, and the unique lift of \bar c there solves the original equation.

### Odd extensions and the actual component group

For any geometrically equivalent rational objects the rational orbit question is twisted conjugacy of the transporter cocycle in the finite stabilizer component group. This follows from the component equation and Lang, or directly by replacing a lift of each component by a connected element to solve the geometric cocycle equation. Restricting to degree f sends a cocycle c to c F(c)⋯F^{f−1}(c) and replaces F by F^f.

For a finite component group Π this restriction on twisted classes is injective if gcd(f,|Π|)=1. Here is a group proof. Work in the finite semidirect product by a cyclic group implementing F. Suppose u=cF and v=c'F have Π-conjugate f-th powers. Conjugate so u^f=v^f=w. Put K₀=C_Π(w) and J=⟨K₀,u⟩. The element v lies in J because vu⁻¹ is in K₀. The subgroup ⟨w⟩ is central in J. In J/⟨w⟩ the image of K₀ is a normal Hall subgroup and the quotient is cyclic of order dividing f. The images of u and v have order dividing f and map to the same quotient generator, so they generate complements. The Schur–Zassenhaus theorem conjugates the complements by an element of the image of K₀. After this conjugation their distinguished generators agree in the quotient. Thus a K₀-conjugate of u equals v times a power of w. That power is in Π and has f-th power one, since the f-th powers of u and v agree. Coprimality makes it trivial. This proves injectivity.

Consequently all four sign-tier comparisons descend along any odd-degree extension coprime to the order of the actual geometric stabilizer component group. In the extension, a sign refers to the quadratic character of the determinant. It coincides with a determinant-one test over the base F₃. In fact the cover Frobenius ratio for degree f is z^(3^f−1)=det(h)^((3^f−1)/2). The square-root determinant covers have no additional odd factors in their component group orders, so the same coprimality works simultaneously. This replaces coprimality with an ambient general linear group order by coprimality with the geometric component group. In particular any odd extension works when that component group is a 2-group.

Even without full orbit descent, the stabilizer sign image is invariant under every odd extension when Π=π₀(H) has a normal Sylow 2-subgroup P. For an extension of degree f, an extra sign modulo A would be a central \bar b∈K/A with F^f(a)=a\bar b for a component a of the cover. Raising a to its odd-order part leaves this equation unchanged and places its image in P. The inverse image of P in the cover component group is an F-stable finite 2-group. In that group, the central elements 1 and \bar b have the same F^f-twisted class because f is odd and the displayed equation holds. The preceding coprime injectivity makes their F-twisted classes equal. Hence the sign already occurs over F₃. Base signs persist in odd extensions because odd extension preserves square class. For any ordinarily equivalent rational pair a base transporter translates the stabilizer subgroup, so its complete determinant profile is unchanged as well.

## 4. Compute scalar sign corrections by an integer presentation

Suppose a Frobenius-permuted direct-sum decomposition into geometric blocks is explicitly recovered in each mode. Let σ be the Frobenius permutation of all block vertices, give v the dimension d_v of its block, and let E list the nonzero block triples. No simultaneous splitting field is needed for the following calculation once these combinatorial data are available.

Form the finite abelian group M presented by generators e_v and the relations
\[
 e_{\sigma(v)}=3e_v,\qquad
 \sum_{v\in e}e_v=0\quad(e\in E).
\]
It is finite and prime to three because a Frobenius orbit of length a gives (3^a−1)e_v=0. A character M→\overline{\mathbb F}_3^{\,\times} is exactly a choice of block scalars satisfying the Frobenius equations and preserving every nonzero block tensor. These are actual rational automorphisms. Define c_i=Σ_v d_v e_v over the vertices of mode i. Each c_i is killed by two since σ preserves sizes and permutes the summands. Its evaluation under the character is the corresponding determinant. Characters of a finite group of order prime to three separate its elements. The exact sign subgroup generated by these block-scalar automorphisms is therefore
\[
 \{\lambda\in\mathbb F_2^3\mid\textstyle\sum_i\lambda_i c_i=0
   \text{ in }M\}^{\perp}.
\]
Compute this by polynomial-time integer Smith normal form. A nonzero returned bit vector supplies a safe projection. This needs no search for a transporter or for explicit units. [Kannan–Bachem, polynomial Smith normal form](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0208040)

For support computation with individually represented extension orbits, compatible local fields can be chosen without constructing their full compositum. For each prime dividing some orbit degree, choose a displayed orbit field containing the largest corresponding prime-power subdegree and extract its nested subfields using Frobenius fixed spaces. For degree d take the tensor product of the selected prime-power fields for d. It is a field since the degrees are pairwise coprime. It gives compatible inclusions when degrees divide. An orbit field is identified with its model using a primitive field factor of their tensor product. Any triple of blocks requires only the model field for the least common multiple of its three orbit lengths, of degree at most their product. Evaluating all block projections and building the presentation then has polynomial cost in explicit dimensions.

For an example of a non-split correction, start with the earlier properly three-edge-colored bipartite line-support tensor on the cyclic graph with A_i connected to B_(i+s) of colors s=0,1,3, with length N at least eight and divisible by four. Build it as a bilinear tensor over F₉ and restrict scalars to F₃. Its geometric lines are the two Frobenius-permuted copies of the graph lines. They can be recovered by the Lie-stabilizer method. The shifts give no four-cycles and colors zero and one form a Hamiltonian cycle. The third matching crosses every cut whose smaller shore is a Hamiltonian interval, so there is no edge cut of size two. Cycle flows separate different geometric lines. Over F₃ compute the algebra generated by each mode's infinitesimal stabilizer projection. Over the closure it is the full diagonal algebra because its vectors separate all the lines. Its rational field factors recover the Frobenius orbits.

Each fixed-mode pair-adjoint unit is scalar on each bichromatic cycle. The number of edges of each color in one such cycle is N/gcd(N,s−t), which is even for these shifts. All its raw signs are positive. Nevertheless the six-cycle A₀ B₁ A₁ B₂ A₂ B₃ A₀ uses color counts (2,3,1). Give its edges alternating scalars a,a⁻¹ for a∈F₉× of norm −1 and give other edges scalar one. This fixes the tensor and yields the nonzero raw sign vector (0,1,1). It is a rational point of the connected non-split torus obtained by allowing the alternating cycle parameter in the two conjugate copies. The ordinary F₃-valued scalar on each orbit would contribute positive determinant because the orbit degree is even. [Earlier Lie recovery](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SLTensorProjectionPacking/LieGraphSelectors.md)

## 5. Exact diagrams turn cancellation into linear algebra

Consider a polynomially constructed tensor diagram whose roles are vector spaces with induced maps of the same raw transporter. Assume each original transporter extends to it and its diagrams enforce the claimed covariance. An ordinary readout must encode exactly the sign of its induced map, and its private roles may be attached independently of other readouts. Choose endpoint frames for all roles. Split their sign variables into the three raw signs s, ordinary-readable auxiliary bits t, and other auxiliary signs u. Suppose a collection of verifiable functorial determinant identities gives
\[
 A s+B t+C u=d .
\]
The identities include exact sequences, duals, direct sums and tensor products with their usual dimension coefficients. For a finite equivariant complex, the sum of its term signs equals the sum of its homology signs up to endpoint calibration. This follows by applying the exact sequence formula to cycles and boundaries. Endpoint constants in these identities are computed by using adapted bases and taking the recorded change-of-frame determinants.

**Elimination theorem.** In polynomial time compute the rank r of the raw-row space obtained by multiplying the displayed equations by all row vectors annihilating C. Then the sign test reduces to a single instance retaining 3−r physical signs. In particular r≥1 suffices for P₃ to P₂. This is complete for linear consequences of the given determinant identities after eliminating the other signs.

Choose r resulting identities with independent raw rows. Impose their right-hand parity conditions on t by ordinary maps. This enforces exactly that their raw rows vanish, on the same transporter. Keep a complementary basis of 3−r raw rows live using the signed compiler. Every all-positive transporter extends. Conversely all rows of a basis vanish on every accepted transporter, proving soundness. This does not require solving for any of the eliminated signs. Linear elimination computes the rows and exactly characterizes which linear identities follow from the recorded equations.

For a concrete ordinary-readable auxiliary role of dimension O(log L), attach its full exterior algebra and its top line. Give the top line a choice of frame. Use a two-idempotent algebra with one auxiliary line at each idempotent and square maps to the top line of respective coefficients +1 and −1. Preservation forces a flip exactly when the transported top-line sign is negative. The possible extensions are precisely those flips, since the auxiliary scalar squares are positive. Individually small roles may be numerous. Read their bits separately and aggregate the required parities by explicit constant-arity relations along a fixed intrinsic order of the roles. This avoids constructing the exterior algebra of their whole sum. Earlier dual pairing and evaluation-kernel calculations give examples of such determinant identities. ## 6. Compress parity observables on permutable ports

Suppose an ordinary diagram has a polynomially recovered set I of two-port roles. An actual transporter acts on bit configurations by s↦πs+δ, where π is its induced permutation of I and δ its actual port flips. If one recovers nonempty polynomial-size configuration sets permuted covariantly by these affine maps, the total parity of the flips is ordinary-enforceable. Use a coordinate algebra indexed by those configurations and a bilinear evaluation from a configuration and an index to the chosen port. The evaluations force the configuration permutation to be exactly the affine action. Map each configuration to its parity idempotent in a two-element algebra. Since the set is nonempty, the induced parity flip is exactly Σ_i δ_i. Pinning its value enforces the desired parity on the actual raw transporter. There is no need to order the indices. An intrinsic affine code of logarithmic dimension, with the stated covariance under translations as well as permutations, gives one example of such a configuration set.

There is also a quotient version. Recover an equivariant subspace C contained in the even-parity kernel, with codimension O(log L), and a covariant collection of checks spanning C^⊥. Assume each check has an ordinary two-port parity readout on its support. Support of size O(log L) supplies such a readout by an explicit assignment table. Enumerate Q=F₂^I/C by linear algebra. Include evaluation of every quotient element at every check. The readout flips force the configuration permutation on Q to be exactly q↦πq+[δ], because the spanning checks separate quotient elements. Total parity descends through C. Its readout gives the same global parity flip and the same ordinary pin. If all checks have small literal support, their union must cover all indices that affect total parity. More general already-readable checks allow large supports. The polynomial cost is governed by the size of the observable quotient and its available readouts.

## 7. Optimal affine probes admit a canonical normal form

In the abstract profile model, an unknown D is empty or affine in F_q^d. A probe asks whether D meets a specified affine space of codimension at most k. Earlier work gave the exact relationship to finite-geometric coverage and the randomized values. Here the exact deterministic number has the closed form
\[
 \left\lceil \frac{q^d-1}{q^k-1}\right\rceil .
\]
Moreover an arbitrary exact adaptive procedure in this model gives an exact nonadaptive procedure with no more probes, by following its all-positive transcript on D=F_q^d and replacing the queried spaces by their linear directions. Conjoin the resulting hits. Each negative nonempty profile lies in an affine hyperplane a(x)=1. Exactness on that hyperplane means some query on the all-positive transcript misses it. Its direction must be annihilated by a, so the new homogeneous query misses every profile in that hyperplane. The empty profile is rejected as well. The direction annihilators must cover every nonzero a, giving the counting lower bound.

To attain the bound, cover the dual space by k-dimensional linear subspaces. For d≥2k, split it as X⊕Y with dim X=k, identify Y with F_(q^(d−k)), and embed X linearly into Y. The q^(d−k) graphs of maps x↦αx cover all points with nonzero X coordinate. Recurse on Y. For k<d<2k, quotient by a fixed common subspace of dimension 2k−d. The quotient is F_(q^(d−k))², and its field-lines form a spread. Their inverse images are the k-dimensional subspaces for the cover. For d=k use the entire space. The counts in these cases give the stated ceiling.

For three binary signs and two-sign probes the answer is three. The earlier randomized sharp worst-case successes with one, two and three probes are 7/11, 7/9 and 1. These are statements about access only to an affine profile. They do not bound algorithms that inspect raw tensor entries. [Earlier profile-query theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignChain/ProfileQueries.md)

The results describe several exact interfaces for the open reduction. The batch and component criteria are conditional algorithmic interfaces. The scalar presentation and determinant-diagram elimination give constructive reductions when their stated data are available. A universal polynomial construction for arbitrary raw inputs remains open.
