# Complexity consequences of a strict determinant-one tensor-isomorphism degree

## Scope

Write $A=L_{\mathrm{SL}}$ and $B=L_{\mathrm{GL}}$ for dense determinant-one and ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over fixed $\mathbf F_3$. Write $H$ for $A\le_m^P B$. This is the uniform deterministic many-one question. The reverse reduction pads each mode by a null line, extends each map by the inverse of its determinant, and restricts any reverse transporter to the original mode supports. This gives strict many-one degree $B<_m^P A$ if $H$ fails. "$\mathrm{TI}$" is used in multiple reduction models in the literature. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5).

## Exact normal forms for the deterministic degree

**Boolean compilation.** A polynomially constructible fan-in-two monotone Boolean circuit of depth $O(\log L)$ on ordinary TI instance predicates of total polynomial description length compiles to one ordinary TI instance in polynomial time. In particular this applies to exact majority across any polynomial number of instances.

For clarity, the underlying operations can be implemented directly. View a nonzero-format tensor as a bilinear map $X\times Y\to Z$. Construct a unital triangular algebra with three diagonal scalar idempotents and radical corners $01=X$, $12=Y$, $02=Z$, whose only nonzero multiplication of two radical corners is the given map. All three corners have positive dimension. A second tensor can be encoded alongside it using two such triples and one one-dimensional bridge from the last vertex of the first to the first vertex of the second. All new products of radical elements are zero. The graph of nonzero corners has a unique topological order, which makes the roles intrinsic after aligning the complete primitive diagonal idempotents. Indeed, if $(e_i)$ and $(f_i)$ have the same quotient classes, $\sum_i f_i e_i$ is a unit congruent to 1 modulo the radical and conjugates $e_i$ to $f_i$. The corner maps after alignment give exactly the independent bilinear-map transporters. The ordered-pair algebra is centrally indecomposable because a central idempotent commutes with all the $e_i$, and every nonzero corner equates its two diagonal coordinates in a connected graph.

For two queries $(x,y)$ and $(u,v)$, conjunction is isomorphism of the ordered pairs $(x,u)$ and $(y,v)$. Disjunction is isomorphism of the algebra products encoding the unordered pairs $\{(x,u),(y,v)\}$ and $\{(x,v),(y,u)\}$. Isomorphism of products permutes the two centrally indecomposable factors. The direct matching requires $u\cong v$, while the exchanged matching requires $x\cong y$. To turn either algebra comparison into ordinary TI, take multiplication tensors. If invertible linear maps satisfy $R(ab)=P(a)Q(b)$, then $P(1)$ and $Q(1)$ are units by surjectivity at $a=1$ or $b=1$, and $a\mapsto P(1)^{-1}R(a)Q(1)^{-1}$ is a unital algebra isomorphism. These operations have output side length bounded linearly by the sum of the two input side lengths, even for distinct formats. Replace malformed or trivially empty-format comparisons by appropriate fixed instances. The polynomial bound follows by iterating a constant factor for $O(\log L)$ levels. For exact majority use the middle output wire of the explicit Ajtai–Komlós–Szemerédi sorting network, whose comparators on bits are AND and OR. [Grochow and Qiao, tensor-to-algebra methods](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309), [Ajtai, Komlós and Szemerédi, sorting network](https://doi.org/10.1145/800061.808726), [Dobrokhotova-Maikova, Kozachinskiy and Podolskii, explicit logarithmic-depth constructions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12270).

For any fixed source language $D$, the following are equivalent.

1. $D\le_m^P B$.
2. There is a bounded-error randomized many-one reduction from $D$ to $B$ using $O(\log L)$ random bits, where $L$ is input bit length.
3. $D$ has a polynomial-time nonadaptive reduction to $B$ whose final decision is computed by a uniformly constructible polynomial-size monotone Boolean circuit of fan-in two and depth $O(\log L)$.

For 2, evaluate the random map on all polynomially many tapes and compile their majority. For 3, use Boolean compilation. A deterministic many-one map satisfies both restrictions. Thus a proof of $\neg H$ also excludes these two forms of reduction. If $\overline{B}\le_m^P B$ is assumed, a deterministic adaptive polynomial-time reduction making $O(\log L)$ calls also compiles to one TI instance. Enumerate the polynomially many possible answer transcripts, retain accepting paths, and take the disjunction of conjunctions of all transcripts' literals. The true answer transcript is unique because each query is determined by its prefix. Replace negative query literals using $\overline{B}\le_m^P B$ and balance the formula. Consequently a logarithmic-query adaptive reduction together with $\neg H$ forces $\overline B\not\le_m^P B$. Unconditional compilation of adaptive transcripts with negative assertions would require a separate argument.

There is also an exact criterion for a given polynomial-randomness map $f(x,r)$ with bounded error. If a uniform polynomial-time sequence of polynomially many tapes per input length (or per input) has a strict majority of correct target-membership bits on every input, then $f$ yields a deterministic map by compilation. Every randomized map does have a nonuniform such sequence per length. Take $O(L)$ independent tapes and apply a Chernoff bound and a union bound over all $2^L$ inputs. Therefore randomized polynomial-time many-one reducibility to $B$ implies deterministic polynomial-size circuit many-one reducibility to $B$. In particular $\neg H$ and failure of nonuniform reducibility are different assertions, and only the latter rules out all bounded-error randomized many-one reductions by this argument.

An explicit generator with $O(\log L)$-bit seed that fools the predicates $r\mapsto B(f(x,r))$ to error $<1/6$ also suffices, using exact majority over its outputs. As these predicates have polynomial-size nondeterministic circuits, a suitable generator for that circuit class works without an ordinary TI circuit-size assumption. If $B\in\mathrm{P/poly}$, a standard Boolean-circuit generator suffices. In particular the exponential circuit hardness hypothesis for $\mathrm E=\mathrm{DTIME}(2^{O(n)})$ in Impagliazzo–Wigderson, together with $B\in\mathrm{P/poly}$ and any bounded-error randomized many-one reduction $A\to B$, implies $H$. The reduction enumerates generator outputs and compiles; it does not require knowing the postulated ordinary TI circuits. In the presence of $\neg H$ and a randomized many-one reduction $A\to B$, at least one of that hardness hypothesis and $B\in\mathrm{P/poly}$ therefore fails. A statement about unrelativized $\mathrm P=\mathrm{BPP}$ alone does not supply a generator for predicates containing a call to $B$. [Impagliazzo and Wigderson, exponential hardness and pseudorandom generation](https://doi.org/10.1145/258533.258590).

## Random external oracles characterize the randomized many-one degree

Fix source and target languages as unrelativized sets. The notation $\le_m^{P^R}$ gives the reduction machine access to an external oracle $R$. Give the oracle's bits independent Bernoulli distributions of parameter $1/2$. For every fixed source $D$,
$$
 D\le_m^{\mathrm{BPP}} B
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Pr_R[D\le_m^{P^R} B]=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Pr_R[D\le_m^{P^R} B]>0.
$$
The notation on the left means a single output whose membership bit is correct with probability at least $2/3$, with no oracle calls by the mapping algorithm.

For the forward implication, obtain an amplified map with error at most $2^{-2L-4}$: run $O(L)$ independent copies, then compile strict majority into one ordinary TI instance. Use oracle bits at distinct, efficiently computable addresses indexed by the input and random-bit position to supply its tape. For a fixed length $L$, the probability that any input of that length gets an incorrect answer is at most $2^{-L-4}$. Borel–Cantelli implies that almost surely there are only finitely many erroneous input strings. For each such oracle there exists a polynomial-time reduction machine hardcoding these exceptions with fixed yes/no TI instances. This is a claim about almost every oracle admitting a machine, with finite exceptional tables allowed to depend on the oracle.

For the reverse implication, enumerate polynomial-clocked oracle transducers $M$. Some fixed $M$ is correct on all inputs on a set $E$ of oracles of positive measure. That set is measurable since correctness on one input depends on finitely many bits. There exists a finite cylinder $C$ with $\Pr(E\mid C)>2/3$. For example, approximate $E$ in symmetric difference by a finite union of cylinders, with error less than $\Pr(E)/10$, and choose one partition cylinder of the finite union whose conditional frequency is at least the frequency in the union. Hardcode the cylinder's bits and simulate $M$ by generating fresh random answers to previously unqueried locations outside the cylinder and reusing answers on repetitions. For each input, this is exactly the conditional-random-oracle distribution on the machine's transcript. It yields a correct output with probability at least $\Pr(E\mid C)$ and takes polynomial time. This is the density argument used by Bennett and Gill for language recognition, applied here to the output of a transducer. [Bennett and Gill, corollary to Theorem 5](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs682/2006sp/Handouts/BennettGill.pdf).

It follows that in the $\neg H$ branch, independent random external oracles almost surely preserve the fixed-language separation if randomized many-one reducibility also fails. If the randomized reduction exists, almost every such oracle erases the separation and deterministic nonuniform many-one maps already exist without an oracle. These conclusions leave the oracle models separate from opaque tensor-query lower bounds.

## A recursive oracle where equivalence coexists with a stronger class separation

There is a recursive external oracle $\Omega$ such that $A$ and $B$ are both in $\mathrm P^\Omega$, but
$$
 \mathrm P^\Omega \subsetneq
 \mathrm{NP}^\Omega\cap\mathrm{coNP}^\Omega\cap\mathrm{SZK}^\Omega .
$$
Use $\Omega=\mathrm{SAT}\oplus C$ with separate prefixes. Cook–Levin decides the two fixed tensor languages using the SAT component. For the other component, at every $n\ge1$ encode two injections
$$
 f_{n,0}, f_{n,1}:\{0,1\}^n\longrightarrow\{0,1\}^{n+1},
$$
whose images are either equal or disjoint. Also encode a table on $\{0,1\}^{n}$ containing a single marked word, with its first bit 1 for the equal case and 0 for the disjoint case. Put $1^n$ into a language $U$ exactly in the equal case and reject nonunary inputs. Use oracle addresses with explicit unary $n$, a type tag, argument, and an output bit index for the injections. On unary inputs the unique marker certifies the correct side, so $U$ is even in $\mathrm{UP}^\Omega\cap\mathrm{coUP}^\Omega$. Its membership in $\mathrm{SZK}^\Omega$ follows by sampling the two injections with independent uniform $n$-bit inputs. The two laws are equal or disjoint. The standard honest-verifier statistical difference protocol on the disjoint branch guesses the selected side and has a perfect simulator which prepares the chosen side and argument itself. Its soundness on the equal branch is exactly $1/2$ per independent repetition. Relativized closure under complement, or the relativized Statistical Difference reduction, gives the stated membership of $U$. The simulator and verifier simply evaluate the two sampling circuits with their oracle gates. [Sahai and Vadhan, Statistical Difference and complement closure](https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~salil/research/complete-abs.html).

To make $U\notin\mathrm P^\Omega$, run the usual diagonalization over deterministic machines with polynomial clocks $p_i$. Choose $n$ beyond all previously fixed parameters with $p_i(n)<2^{n-1}$ and simulate machine $i$ on $1^n$. Give truthful SAT answers, preserve previously assigned parameters, and for other fresh parameters besides $n$ use a fixed default instance of the equal branch. At parameter $n$ assign each newly touched pair (injection side, argument) a previously unused output, even if the machine requested just one output bit, and honor that output on repeated queries. Answer all marker queries at parameter $n$ negatively. At the end the total number of touched arguments is below $2^n$; both equal-image and disjoint-image completions are possible. In the equal case take a common $2^n$-element image containing every touched value and independently complete both injections onto it. In the disjoint case divide the $2^{n+1}$-element codomain into two equal blocks extending the two disjoint sets of touched values and fill independently. Select the branch opposite to the machine's response. In either branch its required marker prefix still has an unqueried length-$n$ word, by the $2^{n-1}$ bound. Complete that table and freeze all other parameters up to a bound exceeding every parameter queried in the simulation. This preserves all responses and ensures that every parameter is eventually frozen. Finite simulations and finite table completions make $C$ recursive. This is an external-oracle separation, even when the class-separation language has unambiguous witnesses for both answers.

## Strengthening the complexity benchmark in the inequality branch

Both tensor languages are in $\mathrm{NP}$, with a transporter as certificate. Both are in $\mathrm{PZK}\cap\mathrm{coHVPZK}$ in the convention allowing expected polynomial time perfect simulators and strictly bounded polynomial-time honest verifiers. Here PZK allows malicious verifiers, and HVPZK is perfect zero knowledge for the honest verifier. Thus $\neg H$ implies the strict uniform separation
$$
 \mathrm P\subsetneq \mathrm{NP}\cap \mathrm{PZK}\cap\mathrm{coHVPZK}.
$$
Indeed $A$ witnesses strictness, since membership of $A$ in $\mathrm P$ would give a many-one reduction to $B$ by returning a fixed yes or no instance. This also separates $\mathrm P$ from $\mathrm{NP}\cap\mathrm{SZK}$.

Here are explicit perfect protocols. Let $T_0,T_1$ be the inputs and $G$ the corresponding product of GL or SL groups. Gaussian-elimination bases and mixed-radix independent-row choices give a bijection in polynomial time from $\{0,\ldots,|G|-1\}$ to $G$. For SL use the first $d-1$ rows as usual, then fix the complementary coordinate of the last row by the determinant-one equation. Exact rejection sampling of the index takes expected polynomial time. The yes protocol sends a uniformly random $\omega$ in the orbit of $T_0$, receives a fair challenge $b\in\{0,1\}$, and replies with a group element carrying $T_b$ to $\omega$. A witness $T_0\to T_1$ supplies the reply. Conditioned on $\omega$, the reply is uniform over the appropriate transporter fiber. Soundness per round on a no input is at most $1/2$, and independent sequential rounds amplify this. For a cheating verifier, fix its sampled tape and current state, guess $b$ uniformly, sample $g$ uniformly and send $gT_b$. Keep trials whose guess matches its valid challenge. For an abort or invalid challenge, retain that trial with independent probability $1/2$. Otherwise rewind the current round and try again. On yes inputs the commitment's distribution is independent of the guess, and every fixed tape and state thus has success probability exactly $1/2$. Conditioned on success the joint distribution of the commitment and reply (or abort) is the real distribution. This yields an expected polynomial-time perfect simulator round by round.

For the complementary no protocol, choose $b$ uniformly and sample a uniform group element tagged by a uniform integer $r$ in a power-of-two interval of size $2^k\ge 4|G|$. If $r$ is in the largest initial interval of length divisible by $|G|$, use its residue modulo $|G|$; otherwise accept immediately. On the sampled branch send $gT_b$ and check the returned guess $b$. The two orbits are disjoint on no instances, giving perfect completeness and an honest-verifier perfect simulator by supplying the verifier's own $b$ on the sampled branch. For a yes instance the orbit law is identical on both sides, so soundness per round is at most $1/4+(3/4)/2=5/8$. The early-accept branch keeps the verifier strictly polynomial while preserving exactness. These are the finite-group generalizations of the isomorphism and nonisomorphism protocols of Goldreich, Micali and Wigderson. [Goldreich, Micali and Wigderson, isomorphism protocol](https://doi.org/10.1145/116825.116852), [Micciancio, Ong, Sahai and Vadhan, zero knowledge without complexity assumptions](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~daniele/papers/ConcZK.pdf).

Nonuniform and O(log L)-randomness claims should remain distinct. If $A\not\le_m^{\mathrm{P/poly}}B$, then $A\notin\mathrm{P/poly}$ and in particular $A\notin\mathrm{BPP}$. An unrelativized deterministic many-one separation alone only gives $A\notin\mathrm P$. If $\mathrm P=\mathrm{BPP}$, the latter also gives $A\notin\mathrm{BPP}$.

## Two nonadaptive entropy estimates give a stronger MKTP model statement

Both $A$ and $B$ reduce by bounded-error randomized many-one maps to the total language
$$
 \mathrm{KTdiff}=\{(u,v,t):t\in\mathbb N,\;\mathrm{KT}(v)-\mathrm{KT}(u)\le \lfloor t/2\rfloor\}.
$$
Consequently both have bounded-error randomized polynomial-time truth-table reductions to $\mathrm{MKTP}$, in addition to the generic, potentially adaptive zero-error polynomial-time MKTP oracle algorithm.

For either group $G$, write $X=G T_0$, $Y=G T_1$ for the orbits. As distributions, symbols $X$ and $Y$ denote their uniform laws. Compare
$$
 P=X\times Y,\qquad Q=\tfrac12(X\times Y)+\tfrac12(Y\times X).
$$
Both laws are flat. If the two orbits agree, $P=Q$. If they are disjoint, the two products have disjoint supports of equal size $|X||Y|$, regardless of whether $|X|$ and $|Y|$ agree. Thus $H_{\mathrm{Shannon}}(Q)-H_{\mathrm{Shannon}}(P)$ is exactly 0 or exactly 1 bit, respectively. Approximating the uniform matrix-group sample by reducing a sufficiently long uniformly random binary integer modulo $|G|$ gives multiplicative error $1+\delta$ in polynomial time in the input and $\log(1/\delta)$. Products and a fair coin preserve the requisite polynomial multiplicative samplability of $P$ and $Q$. This verifies the sampling hypothesis in Allender–Grochow–van Melkebeek–Moore–Morgan's Corollary 4.5.

Take $u$ to concatenate $t$ draws from $P$ and $v$ to concatenate $t$ draws from $Q$, for a sufficiently large polynomial $t$. By the proof of that corollary for flat laws and their Proposition 2.3, $\mathrm{KT}(u)/t,\mathrm{KT}(v)/t$ are each within $1/8$ of their respective ideal entropies with probability tending to one. Use bounded-bit samplers of total statistical error at most $1/100$ on all $2t$ draws, for example by choosing the per-draw error at most $1/(200t)$. Consequently the cutoff $t/2$ distinguishes the two cases with probability at least $2/3$ for sufficiently long inputs. Hardcode decisions on the finite set of smaller inputs. No group stabilizer order is needed. To evaluate $\mathrm{KTdiff}$ using nonadaptive MKTP, ask each threshold up to a known polynomial upper bound on $\mathrm{KT}(u)$ and $\mathrm{KT}(v)$, which exists by the direct verbatim description. Take each smallest accepted threshold. All random tapes and threshold questions are prepared before any MKTP answer. This proof is compatible with the previously known $\mathrm{ZPP}^{\mathrm{MKTP}}$ algorithms and makes no one-sided or zero-error claim for the new truth-table reduction. [Allender, Grochow, van Melkebeek, Moore and Morgan, Proposition 2.3, Corollary 4.5 and Theorem 5.7](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dieter/Papers/gi-mktp-sicomp.pdf).

The same two entropy estimates give the factor by which an ordinary orbit splits into signed orbits. Draw from the ordinary and signed orbit of a single tensor with the same sketch size. The difference of their true entropies is an integer in $\{0,1,2,3\}$ because the determinant quotient has order eight and acts transitively on the signed suborbits of the ordinary orbit. Estimate to additive $1/4$ and round. For disjoint signed orbits no transporter has sign $(1,1,1)$, which is the decision question handled by the swap-product estimate above.

Both tensor languages also lie in $\mathrm{RP}^{\mathrm{MCSP}}$. Use Allender–Das's oracle preimage-finder on a bounded-bit orbit sampler for $T_1$, with a sample from the sampler for $T_0$. When the orbits differ its support is disjoint and no trial can be accepted. When the orbits agree, the two samplers of the same orbit have point probabilities within a fixed factor, so the inverse-polynomial inversion success probability transfers, and one can repeat and verify an actual matching sample. The theorem for general SZK is stated for Promise-BPP, so the one-sided claim here uses the disjoint-orbit structure specifically. [Allender and Das, Theorems 1 and 4 and text following Theorem 4](https://www.cs.rutgers.edu/~allender/papers/szk.mcsp.pdf).

The resulting metacomplexity implications depend on the lower-bound model.

- If $\neg H$ and $\mathrm P=\mathrm{ZPP}$, then $\mathrm{MKTP}\notin\mathrm P$ by the generic zero-error algorithm. If $\neg H$ and $\mathrm P=\mathrm{RP}$, then $\mathrm{MCSP}\notin\mathrm P$.
- If $A\not\le_m^{\mathrm{P/poly}} B$, then both $\mathrm{MKTP}$ and $\mathrm{MCSP}$ are outside $\mathrm{P/poly}$. Otherwise, simulate the corresponding randomized oracle decisions with polynomial-size oracle circuits, amplify and fix tapes per input length, and obtain circuits for $A$. Then output fixed affirmative or negative ordinary instances. Each oracle circuit family may supply all query lengths up to the query-length bound.
- An ordinary TI oracle that decides $\mathrm{MKTP}$ by deterministic truth-table reduction gives a bounded-error randomized truth-table reduction from signed TI to ordinary TI, by composition. If its postprocessing is monotone of logarithmic depth for $\mathrm{KTdiff}$ itself, the randomized reduction can be compiled to a single ordinary query and the nonuniform equality follows. Neither statement supplies deterministic many-one equality without further derandomization.

## An explicit input-indexed inversion consequence

Suppose $A\notin\mathrm{BPP}$, as follows from $\neg H$ together with $\mathrm P=\mathrm{BPP}$, or from a nonuniform many-one separation alone. There is an explicit polynomial-time family of functions $F_x$, indexed by signed TI instances $x=(T_0,T_1)$, for which every uniform PPT inverter fails with probability at least $1/4$ on infinitely many no instances. The failure probability is over a uniformly random input to $F_x$ and the inverter's coins. A nonuniform separation also gives the corresponding result against every nonuniform polynomial-size randomized inverter family.

Use the tagged rejection sampler above with $\Pr[\text{reject}]\le1/16$. On random bits $(b,r)$, set $F_x(b,r)=gT_b$ on acceptance and $F_x(b,r)=\star$ otherwise. On yes instances, $b$ is independent of $F_x(b,r)$. On no instances every non-$\star$ output has a uniquely determined bit $b$ across all its preimages. Given an inverter, sample $(b,r)$, invoke it on $x,F_x(b,r)$, and score 1 when the output is a valid preimage with its bit equal to $b$ and the sample was not $\star$. On yes instances the score has mean at most $1/2$. On no instances with inverter failure below $1/4$, its mean exceeds $3/4-1/16=11/16$. If only finitely many no instances violated that guarantee, one could hardcode them and distinguish the remaining cases by a constant number of repetitions with threshold $5/8$, contradicting $A\notin\mathrm{BPP}$. For nonuniform inverters the same reasoning gives randomized polynomial-size decision circuits and then, by amplification and fixing coins per length, $A\in\mathrm{P/poly}$, also a contradiction. The indices here are worst-case signed nonisomorphism inputs. The theorem places no efficiently samplable distribution on those indices and does not establish standard one-way functions. Compare the auxiliary-input conclusion in the general zero-knowledge setting. [Ostrovsky and Wigderson, *One-Way Functions are Essential for Non-Trivial Zero-Knowledge*](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/OstrovskyWig1993.pdf).

An exact decision characterization uses bit prediction without requiring inversion. $A\in\mathrm{BPP}$ if and only if a uniform polynomial-time randomized predictor, given $x$ and $F_x(b,r)$, predicts $b$ with probability at least $2/3$ conditional on a non-$\star$ sample for every no instance $x$. The forward direction compares the sample to $T_0$ using bounded-error signed TI, since on a no instance the two orbits are disjoint. The converse estimates prediction accuracy conditional on non-$\star$, which is at most $1/2$ on yes instances by independence. A strictly bounded simulation can use all draws, with a score of $1/2$ on $\star$ or a fresh fair score there. The resulting constant gap remains because the sampler rejects with probability at most $1/16$.

## Interpretation

A uniform deterministic negative answer would be a concrete $P$ versus $NP\cap PZK\cap\mathrm{coHVPZK}$ separation, while a positive answer coexists with stronger complexity separation relative to a recursive external oracle. No conclusion here resolves the actual unrelativized tensor question. Proofs are informal and use no Lean verification.
