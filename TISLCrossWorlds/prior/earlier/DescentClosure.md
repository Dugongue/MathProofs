# Determinant-constrained tensor isomorphism through intrinsic coefficient fields

[Prior descent and norms](baseline/baseline/DescentAndNorms.md) · [Atlas arithmetic entries](baseline/expansion/Descent.md)

Let \(K=\mathbb F_3\). For a bilinear map \(T:U\times V\to W\), its three-mode transporter \(g=(g_1,g_2,g_3)\) satisfies \(S(g_1u,g_2v)=g_3T(u,v)\). Signed equivalence requires each \(K\)-determinant to be one. The results below are deterministic paper proofs.

## Result

Compute the centroid of the fully nondegenerate part of each input and its primitive local summands. For a summand with residue field \(E\), let \(r_i\) be its dimension in mode \(i\) over the canonical copy of \(E\) in that local centroid. Fix a subset \(J\) of the three modes. If the ranks \(r_i\) for \(i\in J\) are \(O(\log L)\) on every summand, where \(L\) is the explicit input length, all attainable \(K\)-determinant profiles projected to \(J\) can be found by a deterministic polynomial number of nonadaptive ordinary-TI queries. When every unmonitored coordinate is independently repairable by the centroid scalar automorphisms below, this decides signed equivalence. The same decision has a deterministic polynomial many-one reduction when the maximum number of summands of any one tuple (residue degree, three relative ranks) is \(O(\log L/\log\log L)\). In particular, the many-one result holds for a local centroid, including a nonreduced one. A local centroid can have just one monitored relative rank, even and \(O(\log L)\), when the other two relative ranks are odd and arbitrarily large.

The construction uses no field size enumeration beyond the three ground-field scalars. Absolute mode dimensions and residue-field degrees are unbounded. Both directions of each comparison use one common semilinear field map on the three modes.

## 1. Intrinsic decomposition and coefficient fields

The fully nondegenerate case means that both input radicals are zero and the values span the output. Its centroid is the space of triples \(c=(c_U,c_V,c_W)\) satisfying
\[
T(c_Uu,v)=T(u,c_Vv)=c_W T(u,v).
\]
These are linear equations. The space is a unital associative algebra for coordinatewise composition. For centroid triples \(c,d\), calculating \(T(c_Uu,d_Vv)\) in the two orders gives \(c_Wd_W T(u,v)=d_Wc_W T(u,v)\). Surjectivity on the output span gives commutativity of the output operators. The zero radicals then give commutativity of the other two operators. It follows that this is a commutative finite-dimensional algebra. Isomorphisms of bilinear maps induce centroid algebra isomorphisms by conjugation.

A primitive idempotent \(e\) of the centroid gives the summand on \(eU,eV,eW\). Distinct idempotents have zero mixed products since \(T(e_Uu,f_Vv)=(ef)_WT(u,v)\). The sum of these summands is the whole tensor, and isomorphisms permute them. Conversely, isomorphisms between the summands along a bijection assemble to an isomorphism. Each summand is again fully nondegenerate. Its centroid is the corresponding local algebra, since any operator triple on the summand extends by zero to the others.

There is elementary deterministic polynomial-time calculation of the primitive idempotents over \(K\). For any finite commutative \(K\)-algebra \(C\), the space of \(x\) with \(x^3=x\) is computed by linear algebra. On the product of the local Artin factors this is precisely the product of their copies of \(K\): in one local factor the residue of a root must be in \(K\), and a nilpotent element satisfying \(n^3=n\) is zero. To split explicitly, take a basis of this fixed space and refine an initial slot \(1\) by the three value indicators of each basis element. On a current slot \(e\), the indicator with value \(a\in K\) for an element \(x\) is \(e-(ex-ae)^2\). Nonzero resulting idempotents are kept. The fixed-space basis separates every pair of primitive factors, so this terminates at the primitive idempotents. There are at most \(\dim_K C\) nonempty slots at every step.

In a local factor \(C_0\), the eventual image of the \(K\)-linear Frobenius map \(x\mapsto x^3\) is a canonical coefficient field. It stabilizes in at most \(\dim_K C_0\) steps, is reduced by injectivity of Frobenius on its stable image, and maps onto the residue field because finite-field Frobenius is surjective. Its intersection with the nilpotent radical is zero. Thus its projection onto the residue field is an isomorphism. This gives an explicit intrinsic copy \(E\) of the residue field, even for nonreduced \(C_0\). As its module action comes from the centroid, the tensor on that summand is \(E\)-bilinear.

Two copies \(E_A,E_B\) of a finite field of the same degree \(d\) over \(K\) can be identified in polynomial time without searching for generators. Form their explicit \(K\)-algebra tensor product, of dimension \(d^2\), which is a product of \(d\) copies of the degree-\(d\) field. Compute any primitive summand by the fixed-space method just given. The natural maps from \(E_A,E_B\) into this summand are unital embeddings of \(K\)-algebras of the same dimension, so their inverses give an explicit identification with one field \(E\). In each mode find an \(E\)-basis greedily by adjoining a vector outside the current \(E\)-span, computed in the underlying \(K\)-basis. The change-of-basis matrices are obtained by writing each product of an \(E\)-basis vector with a \(K\)-basis element of \(E\).

These arguments are the centroid decomposition applied to arbitrary fully nondegenerate bilinear maps. The fixed-space and stable-image implementations above apply in the present fixed finite field. [Wilson, direct products via bilinear maps](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.0548), [Brooksbank–Maglione–Wilson, centroid of a bimap](https://joshmaglione.com/papers/Genus2.pdf)

## 2. Compiling one pair of local summands

Fix a putative matching of two local summands, with the same residue degree \(d\) and same relative format. Write \(T,S\) in the bases over the common copy \(E\) just computed. A tensor-system component presents the multiplication table of \(E\) on one named space. Its map is a field automorphism \(\sigma\), necessarily a power \(F^t\) of \(F(x)=x^3\). Present the scalar actions \(E\times V_i\to V_i\) as bilinear tensors in all three modes, along with the original data map. Thus the three transporters are all \(\sigma\)-semilinear. This constraint discards no actual summand transporter since it must respect the canonical residue fields. Conversely these maps still preserve the original tensor by its own tensor-system equation.

For each monitored mode \(i\), introduce the spaces \(\Lambda_E^j V_i\) for \(1\le j\le r_i\) over \(K\), with the first space \(V_i\). Include the \(E\)-multiplies and the successive exterior product maps \(V_i\times\Lambda_E^{j-1}V_i\to\Lambda_E^j V_i\), for \(2\le j\le r_i\). Preservation forces the semilinear exterior maps by generation by pure wedges, and every semilinear map has these induced maps. In the exterior basis identify the last line with \(E\). Its map then has the form \(\ell_i(x)=a_i\sigma(x)\), where \(a_i\) is the determinant of the \(E\)-linear matrix part of \(g_i\). This is true also for \(r_i=1\), when the first and last spaces are the same.

Add \(d\) copies \(Y_j\) of the \(E\)-module line and linear maps \(F^j: \Lambda_E^{r_i}V_i\to Y_j\) in the written line bases, for \(0\le j<d\). On both sides these use identical coordinate matrices in the common copy of \(E\). Because \(F^j\) commutes with \(\sigma\), their transporter maps are forced to be \(x\mapsto F^j(a_i)\sigma(x)\). Introduce prefix lines \(P_0,\ldots,P_d\), identifying \(P_0\) with the actual field object \(E\), and impose the multiplication maps \(P_j\times Y_j\to P_{j+1}\). Present the scalar action on each line to enforce the same semilinearity. In the line frames these constraints force the last map to be
\[
p_d(x)=N_{E/K}(a_i)\sigma(x).
\]
They always extend a compatible triple on the original modes in this way.

Let \(\beta_i\in K^\times\) be the determinant of the target change of basis divided by that of the source change of basis, from the coefficient-field frames to the fixed \(K\)-frames on the two summands. For the \(K\)-determinant in the fixed frames the formula is
\[
\det_K(g_i)=\beta_i N_{E/K}(a_i)(\det_K\sigma)^{r_i}.
\]
The restriction-of-scalars determinant of an \(E\)-linear map is the norm of its \(E\)-determinant. This follows by splitting \(E\otimes_K\overline K\) into the \(d\) embedding factors and multiplying their determinants. On the same factors Frobenius permutes the embedding indices in one \(d\)-cycle. Consequently \(\det_K(F^t)=(-1)^{(d-1)t}\).

Choose \(z_i=1\) if \(d\) is odd or \(r_i\) is even. Otherwise choose a nonzero trace-zero element \(z_i\) of the unique quadratic subfield of \(E\). It can be found in the kernel of \(F+I\) by linear algebra. Indeed \(x^3=-x\) implies \(x^9=x\); on \(\mathbb F_9\) this is exactly the one-dimensional trace-zero space over \(K\), and it is present because \(d\) is even. Then \(\sigma(z_i)=(\det_K\sigma)^{r_i}z_i\) in all cases. For any specified allowed determinant value \(\epsilon_i\in K^\times\), a marked vector \(z_i\) on the source terminal prefix line and the vector \(\epsilon_i\beta_i^{-1}z_i\) on the target impose precisely \(\det_K(g_i)=\epsilon_i\). There is no extra restriction on \(\sigma\). This needs no branching over field automorphisms, including for even residue degree.

There are \(d\binom{r_i}{j}\) coordinates on the \(j\)-th exterior space and \(O(d)\) auxiliary lines for each \(i\). The total dimensions and all displayed bilinear tables are polynomial in \(L\) when \(r_i=O(\log L)\) for the monitored modes. No exterior table is formed for any other mode. All field computations are on explicit finite-dimensional \(K\)-algebras.

## 3. Turning the entire system into one ordinary comparison

Here all tensors are linear maps, bilinear maps, or marked vectors. A polynomial list of such constraints can be compressed to a fixed number of order-at-most-three tensors. Take a master copy of every named space and use private occurrence copies for every constraint. All these spaces are summands of one space \(W_*\). Use a tag space with one basis vector per summand, a cyclic nilpotent shift \(J_*\), and its first cyclic basis vector. These last two tensors force every tag to be fixed. A tag-indexed bilinear projector map enforces preservation of each summand. Use one endomorphism of \(W_*\) to hold all identity links from master copies to the private occurrences, as well as the linear constraints on private input and output copies. Each has a distinct ordered block, so equivariance imposes each one. Use one bilinear map of \(W_*\) to itself to hold the bilinear constraints on their separate private triples. Pack all marked vectors into one vector on separate private slots. Projection and the master links recover exactly the original maps and all repeated occurrences. Every original isomorphism extends by those same maps on the copies. The fixed-size tensor-system compiler applies to these six tensors with polynomial blowup. This proves a deterministic same-field many-one reduction for each local pair and any specified projected determinant tuple. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4), [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219)

## 4. Arbitrarily many summands with nonadaptive ordinary calls

Take arbitrary \(K\)-bases of every primitive summand, concatenate them in a fixed order, and record the determinant of the resulting global basis change in each mode. For every cross-input pair of summands with equal residue degrees and relative formats, form an ordinary query for each of the at most eight local projected determinants from Section 2. Different formats or degrees have empty profiles. These queries are fixed before any answers are read. There is an ordinary transporter exactly when the graph of nonempty profile edges has a perfect matching. This is because primitive centroid idempotents force a factor bijection and individual maps assemble with no mixed constraints.

If a perfect matching exists, choose one. Along that matching compute the sums, in the sign-bit vector space \(\mathbb F_2^J\), of all choices of the individual edge profiles. There are at most eight states. Add the determinant signs of the block permutation and the known changes of global frames. The result is exactly the set of profiles of transporters that use the chosen matching.

Every other matching differs from it by permuting source summands within the same ordinary isomorphism classes. Such a class is recovered from the cross-input edges: two source summands are in the same class if and only if they share a target neighbor. Necessity uses the existing perfect matching, which gives a target neighbor in the class. A transposition of two isomorphic summands can use any local isomorphism and its inverse on the two blocks, so the two local determinants cancel. Its only determinant contribution in each mode is the sign of swapping two blocks of the same \(K\)-dimension \(n_i\), namely \((-1)^{n_i}\). Conversely automorphisms that fix the summand positions are products of local automorphisms. Thus the full projected profile is the set already obtained for the chosen matching plus the \(\mathbb F_2\)-span of these swap vectors. This gives the claimed deterministic nonadaptive reduction to a polynomial number of ordinary decision calls.

If every group of summands with a common residue degree and relative format has size \(O(\log L/\log\log L)\), one ordinary query suffices for membership in the projected profile. Align the group order across the two inputs and include the global basis offset. For each group and each output profile, enumerate its compatible permutations and all local projected sign assignments giving that profile after its block-permutation contribution. The group test is the positive OR of the conjunctions of the corresponding local ordinary comparisons. The number enumerated is at most \(b!\,8^b\) for a group of size \(b\), polynomial under the stated bound. Across groups combine two half-lists by, for each desired profile \(s\), taking the OR over \(u\in\mathbb F_2^J\) of the two profile tests \(u\) and \(s-u\). A balanced binary recursion gives a positive bounded-fan-in formula of depth \(O(\log L)\) and polynomial size. Its ordinary TI leaves can be assembled into one ordinary comparison.

For completeness, binary AND uses the ordered pair encoding by triangular algebras. Represent a tensor as the multiplication of the \(01\) and \(12\) corners into the \(02\) corner, with scalar diagonal vertices, off-diagonal corners in the radical, and all other radical products zero. To represent an ordered pair, join the two three-vertex chains by a nonzero one-dimensional corner, with no additional nonzero radical products. The resulting graph is connected and has a unique topological order, so primitive diagonal classes are fixed and the algebra is centrally indecomposable. Complete lifts of the same diagonal classes are conjugate: if they are \((e_i)\) and \((f_i)\), the unit \(\sum_i f_i e_i\) conjugates the first to the second. The corner equations therefore recover the two tensor equations exactly. For OR of \((x,y)\) and \((u,v)\), compare the products of the ordered-pair algebras representing \(\{(x,u),(y,v)\}\) and \(\{(x,v),(y,u)\}\). Such products are isomorphic exactly when their factor multisets match, which happens in one of the two orderings exactly when one leaf test holds. Convert the algebras into their multiplication tensors. An invertible isotopy of a unital associative algebra can be normalized to an algebra isomorphism by the inverses of its two input maps' values at one. The two values are units because their regular multiplication maps are surjective, and associativity gives the normalized product identity. Each gate grows the total side length by at most a fixed factor, so logarithmic depth preserves polynomial size. Zero-format relations can be replaced with known ordinary YES or NO leaves. This is the same gate construction as the earlier atlas's *Complexity Transfers* chapter. [Grochow–Qiao, tensor and algebra isomorphism](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

## 5. Independently correcting unmonitored determinants

For a local summand whose canonical field is \(E\), each pair \(a,b\in E^\times\) gives the autotopism \((aI,bI,abI)\) on it, extended by the identity on every other summand. The finite-field norm \(N_{E/K}:E^\times\to K^\times\) is surjective. Consequently in sign-bit coordinates the scalar corrections include
\[
(r_1,0,r_3),\qquad (0,r_2,r_3)
\quad\text{modulo }2.
\]
Let \(C_{\rm sc}\) be their total span across all summands. If for each unmonitored mode \(i\) its coordinate basis vector belongs to \(C_{\rm sc}\), any transporter with determinant one on all monitored modes can be repaired to a signed transporter on all modes by precomposing with these source automorphisms. The converse is immediate from the monitored condition. If all three coordinate vectors are in this span, signed equivalence agrees with ordinary equivalence on the given source without any bound on relative ranks.

In a single local summand, if exactly one monitored rank is even and the two unmonitored ranks are odd, the two free scalar vectors span the two unmonitored coordinates. This establishes the one-small-relative-mode theorem. The parity uses dimensions over the coefficient field and can supply nontrivial sign repairs even when all three ground-field dimensions are even.

For an explicit family with large dimensions in all modes, let \(E/K\) have degree \(d\), take an even \(r\ge2\) and odd \(n\ge2r-1\), and let \(J_n\) be one nilpotent Jordan block of size \(n\) over \(E\). Use the \(E\)-bilinear tensor
\[
T(x,v)=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{r-1} x_jJ_n^j\right)v,\qquad
x\in E^r,\ v\in E^n .
\]
It is fully nondegenerate since the first slice is the identity and the powers \(I,\ldots,J_n^{r-1}\) are independent. Its \(K\)-centroid is exactly \(E\). To see this, a centroid triple applied to the identity slice forces the two large operators to be the same \(E\)-linear polynomial \(P(J_n)\) with \(\deg P<r\). Applying the triple to the last slice forces \(J_n^{r-1}P(J_n)\) to lie in the span of the first \(r\) powers. Since all powers through \(2r-2\) are independent, \(P\) is constant. Independence of the first \(r\) slices then makes the label operator the same scalar. If \(r=O(\log d)\) and \(n\) grows with \(d\), the large ranks \(n,n\) can both exceed every logarithmic bound in the input length, and even the absolute width of the label mode is \(dr\). The reduction needs only the \(E\)-exterior line of rank \(r\).

For non-concise data, first use the two input radical quotients and the image span in the output. Every original transporter induces a transporter on these spaces. Conversely maps there extend arbitrarily on independent complements in the output and on the two radicals in the inputs. In each mode with a nonzero discarded space its sign can be independently corrected on that space. Modes with zero discarded space retain their original frame. Comparing the respective discarded dimensions and applying the theorem to the resulting monitored support modes gives the same assertion on that larger class.

## 6. A closed pencil counterexample to stabilizer-only descent

Over \(\mathbb F_3\) take
\[
A(x,y)=\operatorname{diag}(xI_3,yI_4,(x+y)I_5),\qquad B(x,y)=A(-x,y).
\]
The displayed label reflection \(f_0=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)\) gives an ordinary transporter \((f_0,I,I)\). For either ordinary source stabilizer over an extension field, the three distinct factor multiplicities in the slice determinant force its label map to fix each factor line. Fixing the projective frame of three distinct lines makes the label map \(\lambda I_2\). Normalize by the invertible slice at \((1,1)\) for \(A\). The slice at \((1,0)\) normalized in this way gives the constant diagonal values \(1,0,2\) on the three blocks. The ordinary stabilizer of \(A\) is exactly
\[
H=\{(\lambda I_2,G,\lambda G):
\lambda\ne0,\ G\in\mathrm{GL}_3\times\mathrm{GL}_4\times\mathrm{GL}_5\}.
\]
Indeed \(G\) must commute with the normalized diagonal operator and can then be any block diagonal matrix. The twist \(f_0\) centralizes this subgroup, so \(B\)'s embedded ordinary stabilizer is literally the same \(H\). In particular it is smooth, connected and reductive in both cases. To repair the label determinant of the ordinary transporter one must have \(\lambda^2=-1\). If so one may take \(G=I\), since \(\lambda^{12}=1\). The pair is therefore signed-inequivalent over \(\mathbb F_3\) and becomes signed-equivalent over precisely the even-degree extensions.

Both signed geometric orbits are closed. Here is a direct check for this format. For a valuation specialization of a signed orbit point, write the inverse label map over its fraction field in Smith form with integral invertible outer matrices and valuations \((-a,a)\), \(a\ge0\). The primitive reductions of the three factor lines remain distinct after the first outer matrix. At most one can lose its first coordinate modulo the uniformizer. Thus the coefficient valuation of each transformed linear form is \(-a\) with at most one exception, and that exception has valuation at most \(a\). The slice determinant is integral and the product of primitive reductions of the linear factors is nonzero. The valuation of the total determinant polynomial is the sum of the multiplicity-weighted valuations, bounded above by \(a(2\cdot5-12)<0\) if \(a>0\). This forces \(a=0\), so the label can be removed by an integral signed map. The transported invertible anchor remains integral and invertible since its determinant is fixed. The resulting normalized operator has integral spectral projectors for the distinct eigenvalues \(1,0,2\). Their images give an integral direct sum basis conjugating it back to the original normalized operator. Adjust a basis column by a unit to make this conjugator special-linear, and recover the final map from the invertible anchor equation, also with determinant one. This shows every valuation specialization stays in the signed orbit and proves closedness. The same holds for \(B\), since the label reflection normalizes the signed group. [Stacks Project, valuative specialization](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/054F)

The input pairs \((A,A)\) and \((A,B)\) thus agree in ordinary answer, embedded ordinary stabilizers of the endpoints, their tangent algebras, and every geometric orbit invariant of either endpoint, while their rational signed answers differ. The comparison information missing from a stabilizer-only descent rule is the determinant coset of the rational transporter. This does not exclude reductions using the input coefficients in other ways.

## 7. Scope of a proposed faithful rank-compressing compiler

For a tensor with a local centroid and residue field \(E/K\), every ordinary automorphism embeds into the common-semilinear group \((\prod_i\mathrm{GL}_{r_i}(E))\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\). A \(2\)-elementary abelian subgroup of the \(E\)-linear kernel has rank at most \(\sum_i r_i\): its commuting involutions are simultaneously diagonalizable since their polynomial \(X^2-1\) has distinct roots in \(E\). The Galois quotient is cyclic and contributes at most one more to the \(2\)-rank. In contrast the concise scalar multiplication tensor \(K\times K^n\to K^n\) has a subgroup of \(2\)-rank \(n-1\) in its signed stabilizer, from determinant-one diagonal sign matrices acting in both large modes. A polynomial-size compiler that faithfully embeds individual stabilizers cannot force all three relative ranks to be \(O(\log L)\) in a single local target, even on this source family. This observation concerns homomorphic stabilizer embeddings and leaves open which decision reductions can avoid them. The constructive theorems above apply without any such embedding hypothesis on the original input.

The unrestricted fixed-field many-one question of Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang remains open. The present reduction fully checks the restricted semilinear determinant conditions and the stated matching regimes. No Lean verification is claimed. [Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)


## Scalar extension preserves the relative rank parameter

Centroid equations are a linear kernel, so the centroid base changes with the tensor. For a local centroid with residue field \(E\) and nilpotent radical \(J\), extension to a finite field \(L\) gives quotient \(E\otimes L\) and radical \(J\otimes L\). The quotient is reduced because finite fields are separable. Each original mode has an \(E\)-basis of size \(r_i\); after extension it is free of the same rank over \(E\otimes L\), and each new primitive summand has that same relative rank over its residue field. Consequently extending scalars, even enough to split the coefficient field, does not improve the relative rank bounds used in this compiler.
