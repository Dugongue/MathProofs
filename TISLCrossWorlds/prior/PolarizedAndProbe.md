# Direct oriented comparison for polarized square-zero matrix spaces

[Earlier square-zero equivalence](earlier/SquareZeroClosure.md) · [Prior weighted carrier](earlier/baseline/baseline/earlier/CubeZeroAndExtensions.md) · [Earlier ordinary compilation](earlier/baseline/baseline/LinearSwitchCompiler.md)

The ground field is \(K=\mathbb F_3\). Put \(A=K1\oplus X\oplus Z\) with \(J=X\oplus Z\), \(J^2=Z\) and \(J^3=0\), as in the square-zero comparison. Assume the symmetric product \(\beta:X\times X\to Z\) has zero common radical and spans \(Z\), with \(\dim_K X\) even and \(\dim_K Z\) odd. The input can be based square-zero matrix spaces rather than tables for \(A\). All results here are paper proofs.

## Main theorem

Compute the unital associative subalgebra \(D\subseteq\operatorname{End}_K(X)\) generated by the skew-adjoint operators for \(\beta\), namely the solutions of
\[
\beta(D_0x,y)+\beta(x,D_0y)=0.
\]
Consider the case where \(D\) is the product of two fields and its primitive idempotents give totally isotropic summands \(X=U\oplus V\). This hypothesis is decidable in deterministic polynomial time. It gives a common coefficient field \(E\) with respect to which the cross product \(\gamma=\beta|_{U\times V}\) balances. There is a canonical surjection
\[
\pi:U\otimes_E V\longrightarrow Z,\qquad u\otimes v\longmapsto\gamma(u,v),
\]
which is \(K\)-linear. Write \(M=\ker\pi\) and let \(M_E\) be the largest \(E\)-linear subspace contained in \(M\).

For every fixed constant \(c\), there is a deterministic polynomial many-one reduction from the square-zero even-conjugator question to one ordinary TI comparison on this class, provided in the odd-dimensional polarization case that
\[
\max\big(\dim_E M_E,\ \dim_K(M/M_E)\big)\le c\log L ,
\]
where \(L\) is the explicit input length. If the two polarizations have even \(K\)-dimension, there is no kernel bound and ordinary comparison already suffices. No ordinary transporter has to be supplied, and ordinary nonconjugate inputs return NO on this class as well. The odd-dimensional branch allows \(X,Z\), both polarizations, the full symmetric-square kernel, and the absolute dimension of \(M\) all to be arbitrarily large. The two elementary parity blocks are derived from the coefficients, without named subspaces in the input.

## 1. Extracting all orientation frames from the square-zero matrices

For \(\mathcal S\) of the displayed form, let \(W\) be its common kernel, which equals its image span, choose a complement, and write \(U_0=V_{\rm amb}/W\), where \(V_{\rm amb}\) is the original ambient space. Read the row space \(\mathcal M\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_K(U_0,W)\) from the matrices. Solve linear equations for its left idealizer
\[
C=\{D_1\in\operatorname{End}_K(W):D_1\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal M\}.
\]
In the displayed model the row \([I\ 0]\) forces each element of \(C\) to be a regular multiplication, and all such elements are in \(C\). Thus \(C\) is the algebra \(A\) and its given module \(W\) is regular. The radical \(J_C\) is \(\ker(c\mapsto c^3)\) in this commutative algebra, because \(c=s+j\) fulfills \(c^3=s^3\), with \(s\in K\) and \(j^3=0\). Pick \(w\in W\setminus J_C W\). The map \(c\mapsto cw\) gives a regular frame since \(w\) corresponds to a unit. The row space is a free rank-two left \(C\)-module. Lift a \(K\)-basis of \(\mathcal M/J_C\mathcal M\) to rows \(m_1,m_2\), and use \(u\mapsto(m_1u,m_2u)\) to identify \(U_0\) with \(W^2\). In the displayed model their residue matrix is invertible, so the lifted \(2\times2\) row matrix is invertible over the local algebra. Every row is a linear combination of these over \(C\). These choices construct an explicit ambient change of frame \(\Phi:V_{\rm amb}\to A^2\oplus A\) that sends \(\mathcal S\) to the standard space.

Choose a basis of recovered \(A\) adapted to \(1,J_C/J_C^2,J_C^2\). The product of lifts gives the required \(\beta\). The determinants of the full frame changes \(\Phi_A,\Phi_{A'}\) are computed from their matrices. An ambient ordinary isomorphism in the input frames has sign \(+1\) exactly when its canonical-frame sign is \(\epsilon=\det\Phi_{A'}/\det\Phi_A\).

For verification, in canonical frames a conjugator has diagonal blocks \(Q\) on \(A^2\), \(P\) on \(A\), with arbitrary lower shear. The condition is \(P\mathcal M(A)Q^{-1}=\mathcal M(A')\). Left idealizers show that \(P\) induces an algebra isomorphism \(\phi\), and \(P(x)=\phi(x)P(1)\), where \(P(1)\) is a unit. Strip off \(\phi\) from both blocks, then strip off this unit from \(P\). The two coordinate projection rows show that the remaining \(Q\) is \(A\)-linear and invertible. The determinant of any regular unit on \(A\) is its scalar residue to power \(\dim_K A\), and the determinant of \(Q\in\operatorname{GL}_2(A)\) after this stripping is the determinant of its residue matrix to the same power, by the radical filtration. The power is even by the input parity. Before stripping, the contribution from \(\phi\oplus\phi\) is also even. The ambient sign is therefore exactly \(\det_K\phi\). Conversely each \(\phi\) provides \(\phi\oplus\phi\oplus\phi\). Finally \(\det_K\phi=\det_K g_X\det_K h_Z\), since the scalar quotient is fixed by a unital isomorphism. Maps on the two radical layers satisfying the product equation extend to an algebra isomorphism by taking the corresponding graded map. This establishes reflection and lift from raw square-zero inputs, including the computed sign offset.

## 2. Intrinsic polarizations and the paired field

The skew-adjoint space is given by linear equations. A pseudo-isometry \((g,h)\) of \(\beta\) acts on it by conjugation by \(g\), since applying \(h\) preserves its defining zero identity. Compute its generated algebra by linear span and matrix multiplication in dimension at most \((\dim X)^2\), adding products until closed. Test commutativity on a basis. For a finite commutative \(K\)-algebra, the \(K\)-linear Frobenius \(x\mapsto x^3\) is injective exactly when the algebra is reduced, and the dimension of its fixed space is its number of field factors once reduced. The primitive idempotents can be recovered by splitting the fixed space according to the three scalar values of each of its basis elements, using the indicators \(e-(ex-ae)^2\) on a current idempotent \(e\), \(a\in K\). Test that there are exactly two factors, and that the two projected subspaces of \(X\) are totally isotropic by evaluating \(\beta\) on their bases. This recognizes the stated case. Every transporter permutes these two subspaces, and there are at most two possible matches across a pair.

For the intrinsic field pairing, write \(\gamma=\beta|_{U\times V}\). Its left and right radicals vanish, and its values span \(Z\). Consider operator pairs \((P,Q)\) with
\[
\gamma(Pu,v)=\gamma(u,Qv).
\]
Each projection is injective by the zero radicals, and its image is a subalgebra, with reversed composition in the second coordinate. Since \((P,-Q)\) is exactly the list of skew-adjoint block operators, and every skew-adjoint operator preserves the two primitive summands, those coordinate images generate the two projected fields making up \(D\). They are already closed subalgebras, hence equal those fields. The resulting anti-isomorphism between the two fields is an isomorphism because they are commutative. Use the first field as \(E\) and this pairing for its action on the second mode. The displayed relation is the balanced identity over \(E\) and gives \(\pi\) by the universal property of \(U\otimes_E V\). The field, its two actions, and \(\pi\) are computable by the same linear equations and \(E\)-basis calculation.

One source of the recognized case is any \(\gamma\) with no zero divisors, meaning \(\gamma(u,v)\ne0\) whenever both \(u,v\) are nonzero. Form \(\beta((u,v),(u',v'))=\gamma(u,v')+\gamma(u',v)\). If \(D_0\) is skew-adjoint, test \(x=y=u\in U\); the equation yields \(2\gamma(u,(D_0)_{VU}u)=0\), and forces \((D_0)_{VU}=0\). Testing \(v\) shows its other off-diagonal block vanishes. The diagonal operator \((I,-I)\) is skew-adjoint, and its two eigenprojectors lie in the generated algebra in odd characteristic. Therefore that algebra is the product of the two projected balanced-operator algebras. Any nonzero \(P\) in one projection is invertible: if \(Pu=0\) with \(u\ne0\), the no-zero-divisors property and the balancing equations force \(Q=0\), then \(P=0\). The inverse belongs to the projected algebra as a power of \(P\), since the field of coefficients is finite. Each projection is a finite division algebra and hence a field. This proves automatic recognition for this source family. The algorithm itself uses the explicit tests above and need not decide the no-zero-divisors promise.

## 3. Cancellation of both large tangent determinants

If the polarizations have even \(K\)-dimension, the automorphism
\[
(g_U,g_V,h)=(-I_U,I_V,-I_Z)
\]
is odd on \(X\oplus Z\), since \(\dim_K Z\) is odd. Thus both signs occur on every nonempty ordinary transporter and ordinary TI suffices. The polarizations have the same parity because \(X\) has even dimension.

In the remaining case \(U,V\) have odd \(K\)-dimension. The coefficient degree \(e=[E:K]\) and the relative dimensions \(a=\dim_E U,b=\dim_E V\) are therefore all odd. In one polarization-matching branch place the assigned target polarizations in the order matching \(U,V\). Identify the two coefficient fields by an explicit reference identification. This can be done deterministically by splitting \(E\otimes_K E'\) into its field factors and choosing one component, since the two natural field inclusions into that component are isomorphisms. The two-mode maps of an actual transporter are both semilinear for the same field automorphism \(\sigma\), by equivariance of the paired field. Write their matrices over \(E\) as \(P_U\sigma\) and \(P_V\sigma\). Their balanced tensor product has matrix \((P_U\otimes P_V)\sigma\), the map that induces \(h\) on \(Z\) and acts on \(M\).

In frames from the \(E\)-bases and a common \(K\)-basis of \(E\), their respective ground-field determinants are
\[
N(\det_E P_U),\qquad N(\det_E P_V),\qquad
N(\det_E P_U)^b N(\det_E P_V)^a.
\]
Indeed, extension to an algebraic closure identifies the determinant of restriction of scalars with the product over all its embeddings. Frobenius permutes these embeddings in a single \(e\)-cycle, whose sign is one since \(e\) is odd. The ordinary tensor-product determinant law then gives the third formula. Since \(a,b\) are odd, all three displayed determinant factors together multiply to one.

For exact framing, use the fixed \(K\)-frames of \(X,Z\) from Section 1. Choose \(K\)-frames of \(U,V\) from their \(E\)-bases and the chosen field basis. Let \(\xi\) be the determinant ratio of the two changes from these combined polarization frames to the fixed frames of \(X\). Let \(\eta\) be the determinant ratio of the two changes from the adapted frames of \(U\otimes_E V\) consisting of a basis of \(M\) and lifts of the fixed basis of \(Z\), to the coordinate frames from the relative tensor product. The determinant on the relative tensor product in its coordinate frames is then \(\eta\det_K(k)\det_K(h)\), where \(k:M\to M'\) is the restricted map, while that on \(X\) is \(\xi\) times the first two factors displayed above. Consequently
\[
\det_K(g_X)\det_K(h)=\xi\eta^{-1}\det_K(k).
\]
This includes a polarization exchange because the chosen order of the two target polarizations is incorporated into \(\xi\) and the tensor-product frames. Thus the required ambient sign is exactly one calibrated determinant equation on \(M\), even when both original tangent determinants are expensive to write directly.

## 4. One ordinary comparison for the remaining determinant

The largest \(E\)-linear subspace \(M_E\) in \(M\) is computed by intersecting the inverse images of \(M\) under multiplication by a \(K\)-basis of \(E\), along with \(M\) itself. This is precisely the set of \(x\in M\) for which \(Ex\subseteq M\). It is intrinsic, and \(k\) maps it to \(M'_E\) semilinearly. Use frames of \(M\) adapted to \(M_E\) and lifts of a \(K\)-basis of \(M/M_E\). They can be used as the bases of \(M\) in the calibration above. Then
\[
\det_K k=\det_K(k|_{M_E})\det_K(\bar k).
\]
In the \(E\)-frames, \(\det_K(k|_{M_E})=N_{E/K}(\det_E P)\) where \(P\) is the linear matrix part of the semilinear map. Again the field automorphism contributes sign one.

Use one typed tensor system for this branch. Include the product \(\beta\), the polarization inclusions and the two \(E\)-actions, \(E\)'s field multiplication, the balanced tensor-product bimap \(U\times V\to U\otimes_E V\), the projection \(\pi\), and the kernel, subkernel and quotient maps. All links use the same original transporter. Every original transporter in the branch extends to them by functoriality. Conversely any transporter of the system gives the given \(\beta\)-transporter and those precise induced maps, since the inclusions are injective, the tensor product is spanned by pure tensors, and the quotients are onto.

For \(M_E\) include its full relative exterior powers and successive exterior product maps. The linked map on the final \(E\)-line is \(x\mapsto\det_E(P)\sigma(x)\). Its norm is forced by copying this line through the linear Frobenius maps \(F^j\), \(0\le j<e\), and imposing successive multiplication of its copies starting from the field object \(E\), whose map is \(\sigma\). The \(j\)-th transferred coefficient is \(F^j(\det_E P)\) since the two Frobenius powers commute with \(\sigma\). The final line's map is \(N(\det_E P)\sigma\). Link the ground-field scalar line into that final line by \(t\mapsto t\,1_E\). Preservation forces its scalar map to be the required norm. On \(M/M_E\) include ordinary full exterior powers, forcing the remaining top line to act by \(\det_K(\bar k)\). For a zero-dimensional factor use a scalar line pinned to the identity.

Use a bilinear multiplication of the two resulting scalar lines into a third line, in their chosen coordinates. Pin the third line by a source vector \(1\) and target vector \(\epsilon\,\eta/\xi\). This imposes the exact product, with no independent freedom in the sign factors. The sums of dimensions of the two exterior constructions are \(e\,2^{\dim_E M_E}\) and \(2^{\dim_K(M/M_E)}\), up to the fixed tensor-system overhead. All maps outside the exterior construction have polynomial dimensions in the written input.

For clarity, a polynomial typed list of linear maps, bilinear maps and marked vectors here can be packed into a constant number of order-at-most-three tensors. Use an ordered tag space fixed by a nilpotent shift and its cyclic first vector, and a tag-indexed family of projections onto the master and private occurrence spaces in one direct sum. One endomorphism stores the links from master spaces to occurrence copies and all distinct private linear blocks. One bimap stores the separate bilinear blocks on private input and output copies. One vector stores all the marks on private summands. Any isomorphism fixes the tags, preserves each occurrence and satisfies the original maps by those linear links. Conversely any original typed transporter lifts by the same maps on the copies. The fixed-signature tensor-system reduction then gives ordinary TI in polynomial size. There are at most two polarization matches. The standard ordinary-TI disjunction combines the branch outputs. This disjunction compares the products of centrally indecomposable triangular algebras representing \(\{(x,u),(y,v)\}\) and \(\{(x,v),(y,u)\}\), where \((x,y),(u,v)\) are the ordinary leaf instances and each ordered pair is linked by a one-dimensional bridge. Factor matching gives exactly the disjunction. The multiplication tensor of a unital associative algebra expresses its ordinary isomorphism, because any isotopy can be normalized at the units. [Wilson, adjoint bimaps](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.4329), [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219), [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

## 5. An explicit unbounded family with a real sign obstruction

Choose any odd \(e\), and let \(E=\mathbb F_{3^e}\). Choose unequal odd integers \(a,b\ge13\) and write \(U=E^a,V=E^b\). Identify \(T_0=U\otimes_E V\) with \(a\times b\) matrices in fixed bases. Set \(M\) equal to the two-dimensional \(E\)-subspace of matrices
\[
A(x,y)=\operatorname{diag}(xI_3,yI_4,(x+y)I_5),\qquad x,y\in E ,
\]
placed in the first \(12\) rows and columns and zero elsewhere. Put \(Z=T_0/M\) and let \(\gamma\) be the quotient of the tensor-product bimap, used symmetrically on \(U\oplus V\). Nonzero elements of \(M\) have rank at least seven. Thus \(\gamma\) has no zero divisors, since a nonzero pure tensor has rank one. Its values span \(Z\), and the resulting \(\beta\) has zero common radical. Its dimensions have the required parity.

The balanced operator field is precisely \(E\). A balancing pair \((P,Q)\) forces \(Pu\otimes v-u\otimes Qv\in M\) for all \(u,v\). The difference has \(E\)-matrix rank at most two, so it is zero. Fixing a nonzero \(v\) then gives \(P=a_0I\) and \(Qv=a_0v\) for some \(a_0\in E\), including the zero case. Varying \(v\) gives \(Q=a_0I\). This uses no \(E\)-linearity assumption on the original \(K\)-operators. In particular the intrinsic procedure recovers the two polarizations, which cannot be exchanged because their dimensions differ.

Here \(M_E=M\) has relative rank two and absolute dimension \(2e\), while \(\dim_K(M/M_E)=0\). On \(M\), the three exceptional projective lines have distinct rank deficiencies \(3,4,5\), and the other lines have full supported rank. Any automorphism of the hyperbolic bimap must preserve the \(E\)-ranks on the relation space because the map there is induced by a semilinear left and right invertible matrix transformation. The underlying field automorphism fixes these three individual lines in the written \((x,y)\) basis. Thus the linear matrix part of its action on \(M\) fixes three projective points in a line and is a scalar matrix. Its ground-field determinant is one, since its relative determinant is a square, and Frobenius on two copies has sign one. In the calibrated formula for a self-comparison \(\xi=\eta=1\), so every automorphism is even. An ordinary equivalent partner with no even conjugator is obtained by twisting the output \(Z\) by an odd reflection and keeping the tangent basis. The reflection and lift in Section 1 transfer this to the square-zero matrix spaces. Identical copies give affirmative examples.

Taking \(e,a,b\) to grow polynomially with a common size parameter makes \(2e\), both polarized dimensions and both radical grades exceed logarithmic bounds in the input length. The full quadratic relation kernel \(\ker(\operatorname{Sym}^2X\to Z)\) also contains \(\operatorname{Sym}^2U\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2V\), so this example never meets the previous small full-kernel hypothesis. The balanced quotient and the field-relative exterior line are the two reductions in size used here.

## 6. An independent commutator criterion for symmetric products

There is also a one-query sufficient condition that does not require the two-field skew algebra. Suppose a polynomially enumerable set of candidate ordered scalar triples is given by an isomorphism-equivariant deterministic rule on the class in question. The rule can take all triples if \(\dim_K Z=O(\log L)\). For a triple \((\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\) with \(B_0=\lambda_0\beta\) nonsingular, put \(S_\lambda=B_0^{-1}(\lambda\beta)\), \(C=[S_{\lambda_1},S_{\lambda_2}]\). Construct the two alternating forms
\[
\omega_X=B_0 C,\qquad
\omega_{Z^*}(\lambda,\mu)=\operatorname{Tr}(C[S_\lambda,S_\mu]).
\]
For \(\omega_X\), alternation follows because each \(S_\lambda\) is self-adjoint for \(B_0\), so \(C\) is skew-adjoint. The trace form is bilinear and alternating by the commutator. The source condition is that at least one candidate triple have both radical dimensions \(O(\log L)\).

Choose one such source triple deterministically and list its allowed target triples. In each branch link the original map on \(Z\) to its contragredient \(\ell\mapsto\ell\circ h^{-1}\) on \(Z^*\) by the evaluation pairing, and link the three specified covectors. Each resulting \(\beta\)-transporter satisfies \(g^\top B'_0g=B_0\) and \(S'_{\ell\circ h^{-1}}g=gS_\ell\). Thus it transports \(\omega_X,\omega_{Z^*}\), the latter by invariance of trace under conjugation. Transporters on the two symplectic quotients have determinant one in symplectic frames, since the top Pfaffian of a nonsingular alternating form transforms by determinant. In the original frames each sign on \(X,Z^*\) is therefore the sign on its respective radical times a known basis offset. The sign on \(Z^*\) is the same as that on \(Z\), since determinants are signs. The two linked small radical exterior volumes and their product line give the required \(\det g\det h\) after this calibration. Their inclusion maps make the two volume choices refer to the same original transporter. The polynomial positive OR combines the candidate branches using the ordinary gate of Section 4. Every genuine transporter maps the chosen source triple to one of these branches by equivariance of the candidate rule. Thus the sufficient condition applies to unmarked square-zero inputs whenever the rule is available.

For an explicit family outside the two-field case, take \(X=\bigoplus_{j=1}^s K^{2t_j}\) and, on block \(j\), let \(P_j=\operatorname{diag}(I_{t_j},0)\) and \(Q_j=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{t_j}\\I_{t_j}&0\end{pmatrix}\), extended by zero to the other blocks. Let the scalar forms be spanned by \(I,P_j,Q_j\), all independent. Use \(B_0=I\), \(B_1=\sum_j P_j\), \(B_2=\sum_j Q_j\). The commutator is block diagonal with blocks \(\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{t_j}\\-I_{t_j}&0\end{pmatrix}\), so \(\omega_X\) is nonsingular. The only nonzero pairings on the listed \(P_j,Q_j\), up to order, are \(\omega_{Z^*}(P_j,Q_j)=-2t_j\). If none of the \(t_j\) is divisible by three the output radical is the line spanned by \(I\). With some \(t_j\ge4\), the original skew-adjoint algebra contains the noncommutative algebra generated by repeating the standard \(t_j\)-dimensional skew-symmetric matrices on both halves of that block. They commute with the entire displayed family of scalar forms and are skew for \(I\). Products of \(E_{ab}-E_{ba}\) on distinct pairs generate all off-diagonal matrix units and hence their diagonal products. Thus the algebra cannot be a product of two fields. Taking \(s=O(\log L)\) gives the polynomial unmarked candidate enumeration with growing output dimension.

## Exact remaining scope

The main reduction applies when the common skew-adjoint algebra recovers two polarizations and the stated balanced relation ranks are small, or its scalar automorphism makes orientation free. The commutator construction applies with an admissible polynomial candidate rule and two small radicals. Neither hypothesis has been shown on the unrestricted square-zero carrier family, so the promised general missing lemma and Open Question 1.9 remain open. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)
