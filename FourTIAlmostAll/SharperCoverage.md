# Inverse-polynomial rejection bounds

For every fixed finite field $\mathbb F_q$ and every fixed $M>0$, the [almost-all decision and search algorithm](FullCoverage.md) can be chosen to recognize a fraction $1-O_{q,M}(n^{-M})$ of uniform first tensors, against every target. Its running time remains polynomial in the binary input length, with exponents allowed to depend on $q,M$. The same recognition bound holds for the [Galois-ring lifting algorithm](SearchAndLifting.md), uniformly in the ring exponent.

Use actual repeated-primary commutant dimensions for the enumeration budget. The [exponential-moment proof](Distribution.md#stronger-spectral-tail-once-full-marginal-mixing-is-available) bounds $\mathbb E q^{sD_{\mathrm{comm}}}$ uniformly in $n$ for $0<s<1/2$ under a uniform matrix law, also with prescribed trace. Thus accepting $D_{\mathrm{comm}}\le K\log_q n$ costs at most $n^K$ enumeration and fails with probability $O_{q,s}(n^{-sK})$. For a fixed number of anchors one may impose the same bound on each separately and enumerate all choices with a fixed polynomial exponent.

In the invertible branch, the [marginal mixing proof](SingleAnchor.md) has error $q^{-\Omega(n)}$. The low-polar-rank exceptional term is $O_q(q^{2\epsilon n^2-ab/2})$. The high-polar-rank argument discards a dual row frame with probability $O_q(q^{a-\lceil 2\sqrt n\rceil^2})$, and its span and cross-block union bounds are $q^{-\Omega(n^3)}$ times $q^{O(n^2)}$. The Parseval error on the remaining frames is at most $q^{-3n^2/2}/2$. Hence the same inverse-polynomial rejection bound holds for these four marginals by a union bound.

For the singular branch, impose corank and selected kernel-form radical bounds $K_0\sqrt{\log_q n}$. The rank count makes the probability of either cutoff failing $O_{q,M}(n^{-M})$ for a sufficiently large $K_0$. The probability calculation may choose one random nonzero form per kernel while the algorithm enumerates the forms. Conditional on positive corank, the individual chosen forms are uniform nonzero matrices because the left and right kernel spaces are uniform over subspaces of the prescribed dimension. Existence of a passing form pair therefore fails with probability at most the probability of failure of the random form pair. For these coranks the lists still obey $q^c\le n$ for sufficiently large $n$.

The live dimensions in the old Fourier argument are at least $n-K_0\sqrt{\log_q n}$, and the codimensions of their trace-constrained subspaces are at most $K_0\sqrt{\log_q n}$. Its lower bound $(r_1-4c)(r_2^2-2c)$ for a nonscalar left coefficient, and its symmetric right bound, are therefore $\Omega(n^3)$ uniformly throughout these caps. For scalar coefficients the argument is unchanged. If a chosen form is deficient, the projected invertible-matrix block is within $O_q(q^{-2n+O(\sqrt{\log_q n})})$ of uniform by the old rank calculation. When both are full rank, the final invertibility conditioning costs only a fixed factor. Thus the live commutant dimension has the same high-probability cutoff. The [enlargement estimate](Distribution.md#stronger-spectral-tail-once-full-marginal-mixing-is-available) bounds the full budget by twice the live one plus $8u^2+2$ for a form of radical dimension $u$. Under the radical cap this remains a fixed multiple of $\log_q n$. The algorithm enumerates the actual full repeated-primary commutants and compares the whole tensor, as before.

Finally, both scalar stabilizer tests used for search and lifting fail only on a fraction $O(q^{4n^2-n^3}+q^{-n^4})$ of the uniform residue tensors. This preserves the stated inverse-polynomial rejection bound. A fixed set of small dimensions can be handled by exhaustive search, whose cost there is constant in the asymptotic field analysis.
