# One inverse anchor is asymptotically uniform in each mode

This is an informal proof for tensors over any fixed finite field $\mathbb F_q$. Randomness below is only in the analysis of the set of first inputs accepted by a deterministic algorithm.

Fix the invertible 12|34 flattening $A$ of an $n\times n\times n\times n$ tensor and set $S=A^{-T}$, using the same indices for the two arrays. The anchor on mode 3 is $E_3=\operatorname{Tr}_2(C_{23}^2)$, where $C_{23}$ contracts $A$ with $S$ in modes 1 and 4. Pick the analogous anchor in each other mode. Under a tensor mode change the anchor is conjugated by the change on its mode. The conclusion of this note is that, when $A$ is uniform in $\mathrm{GL}_{n^2}(q)$, each $E_i$ is $o(1)$ in total variation from a uniform $n\times n$ matrix, with trace fixed to the field image of $n$ in characteristic two.

## 1. A common shear and two restrictions

Write $T^0_{aj},S^0_{aj}$ for the $n\times n$ reshapes of row $(a,j)$ of a uniform invertible baseline $A_0$ and its inverse transpose. Split the first mode into halves $H,L$. Independently sample $X$ uniformly from the matrix space supported on rows $(a,j)$ with $a\in H$ and columns $(b,s)$ with $b\in L$. Then $X^2=0$, $A=(I+X)A_0$ remains uniform invertible, and $S=(I-X)^TA_0^{-T}$. All operations in the shear and in the contractions are over $\mathbb F_q$.

The crossed contraction $C_{23}$ has blocks $\sum_a T_{aj}S_{at}^{T}$ indexed by $j,t$ in mode 2. The simultaneous changes of $T$ and $S$ occur on disjoint first-mode indices. Thus $C_{23}=C^0+D(X)$ with $D$ linear. The coefficient of $X_{ar,bs}$ is
$$
 \sum_t e_{rt}\otimes T^0_{bs}(S^0_{at})^T
 -\sum_j e_{js}\otimes T^0_{bj}(S^0_{ar})^T .
$$
For a fixed coefficient matrix $P$, the polar form of $X\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(P E_3)$ only uses the quadratic part $X\mapsto\sum_{j,t}\operatorname{tr}(P D_{jt}D_{tj})$.

Use the symmetric bilinear form
$$
 \omega_P(U,V)=\operatorname{tr}(P(UV+VU))
$$
on all matrices. Its rank $r$ is the rank of $\mathcal L_P:U\mapsto PU+UP$ by the nondegenerate trace pairing. In characteristic two this is the trace-commutator form. Its restriction to traceless matrices has rank $r'\ge r-2$, since computing a restriction to a hyperplane deletes one row and one column from a matrix of the form. Write $Q_P$ for the quotient of the traceless space by the radical of that restriction. The induced form is nondegenerate of rank $r'$.

Two elementary counting facts are used throughout. For a random invertible $N\times N$ matrix, prescribe a subset of its rows and a disjoint subset of rows of its inverse transpose. Each prescribed list on one side is uniform over ordered independent tuples. Conditional on that list, the list on the other side is uniform over ordered independent tuples in the annihilator of the first list. Indeed each row has the specified dual pairings with the entire opposite basis. Every independent tuple in the annihilator can complete to a basis satisfying the prescribed dual conditions, and the stabilizer fixing the prescribed dual vectors pointwise acts transitively on all these tuples. Equivalently sample independent uniform vectors in the indicated spaces, then condition on each of the two lists being linearly independent. When their combined size is at most $N/2$, those conditioning events each cost a factor $1+O(q^{-N/2})$ in probability bounds. For rows reshaped as matrices, transpose the inverse rows so their annihilator is with respect to $\operatorname{tr}(UV)$.

The second fact is that a uniform $u\times v$ matrix over $\mathbb F_q$ with $u\le v$ has rank at most $u-\delta$ with probability at most $4\cdot q^{-\delta(v-u+\delta)}$. Indeed choose its $\delta$-dimensional left-kernel subspace, whose number of choices is at most $4\cdot q^{\delta(u-\delta)}$ by the Gaussian binomial formula, then impose its $\delta v$ independent homogeneous equations. The analogous formula holds with $u,v$ swapped.

## 2. Low-rank coefficient matrices

Put $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ and let $P$ be a nontrivial coefficient matrix with $r\le\epsilon n^2$. Nontrivial means nonscalar in characteristic two and nonzero in odd characteristic. A nonzero scalar in odd characteristic has $r=n^2$, so $P$ here is nonscalar. Write $a=b=\lfloor n/5\rfloor$, $B=ab$, and select that many indices from $H,L$, respectively. For sufficiently large $n$ define $k=\lfloor B/(2r')\rfloor$. Choose a fixed source mode-2 index $s_0$ and a disjoint set $J$ of $k$ targets. Use shear variables $x_{ij}^{(u)}$ with $i$ in the chosen part of $H$, $j$ in the chosen part of $L$, and $u\in J$, giving the entry $X_{iu,js_0}$. Set $U_j=T^0_{js_0}$, $V_{it}=(S^0_{it})^T$, and
$$
 \phi_t(x)=\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}U_jV_{it}.
$$
The matrices $\phi_t(x)$ are traceless by the disjoint dual row labels. For $u,t\in J$ one has $D_{ut}=\phi_t(x^{(u)})$. Outside those blocks only rows in $J$ or the column $s_0$ can be nonzero. Products $D_{ut}D_{tu}$ with an index outside $J$ therefore vanish, except $D_{s_0s_0}^2$. The latter is the square of a traceless matrix linear in the shear, so its phase has polar rank at most $r'$.

The map $x\mapsto(\phi_t(x)\bmod\operatorname{rad})_{t\in J}$ into $Q_P^k$ is surjective with failure probability $O(q^{kr'-B})$. To see this, fix a nonzero tuple of dual labels, represented on traceless matrices by $Z_t$ in the trace pairing. A nonzero label has a nonscalar representative $Z=Z_{t_0}$. In an unconditioned sampling model take $U_j$ independently uniform among all matrices, then take all $V_{it}$ independently uniform in $\mathcal U^\perp$ for $\mathcal U=\operatorname{span}\{U_j\}$. Conditioning both lists to be independent gives the desired law, with a bounded probability factor. For given $U_j$, annihilation by this tuple of labels requires, for each $i$, that the $k$ independent matrices $(V_{it})_t$ satisfy the $b$ equations with row coefficients $(Z_tU_j\bmod\mathcal U)_t$, one for each $j$.

The rank of these $b$ rows is at least the rank of $Z U_j\bmod\mathcal U$. There are a column vector $u$ and row functional $v$ with $vu=0$ and $vZu=1$. Otherwise $Z$ would send every vector into its own span, forcing it to be scalar. Reveal the entries of the $U_j$ except their components in the $u$ direction in each column. All $vU_j$ are fixed and $vZU_j$ remain independent uniform $n$-vectors. Project them into the quotient by $\operatorname{span}\{vU_j\}$, of dimension at least $n-b$. The tail for a rank deficit $\delta$ of the $b$ row equations is at most $4\cdot q^{-\delta(n-2b+\delta)}$. On rank $b-\delta$ the probability for the $a$ independent sets $(V_{it})_t$ is $q^{-a(b-\delta)}$. Summing over deficits gives $O(q^{-B})$, because $a+2b\le3n/5$. Union over the at most $q^{kr'}$ label tuples proves the surjectivity estimate.

On this event the $\phi_t(x^{(u)})$ can realize an arbitrary $k\times k$ array in $Q_P$. The polar form of the $J\times J$ part is the nondegenerate form pairing position $(u,t)$ with position $(t,u)$ via $\omega_P$. Its pullback therefore has rank $k^2r'$. The total polar rank on the restricted shear is at least $(k^2-1)r'$.

Here are the counting and parameter details to make this simultaneous in $P$. Over an algebraic closure let $m$ be the largest eigenspace dimension of $P$. Let $c_{\lambda,j}$ be the Jordan column lengths for eigenvalue $\lambda$. A matrix in $\ker\mathcal L_P$ intertwines $P$ and $-P$. Between blocks of opposite eigenvalues and sizes $h,j$, the intertwiner space has dimension $\min(h,j)$, and between other blocks it is zero. Consequently $\dim\ker\mathcal L_P=\sum_{\lambda,j}c_{\lambda,j}c_{-\lambda,j}\le\sum_{\lambda,j}c_{\lambda,j}^2\le nm$. Here the middle inequality applies $2uv\le u^2+v^2$ to opposite eigenvalues. Nonscalarity gives $r\ge n$. If $r\le\epsilon n^2$, there is one eigenspace of dimension at least $n-r/n>n/2$, and its eigenvalue lies in the base field by uniqueness and Frobenius. Hence such $P$ are among the matrices $\lambda I+M$ with $\operatorname{rank}M\le\lfloor\epsilon n\rfloor$, of which there are at most $q\cdot q^{2\epsilon n^2}$ by rank factorization. In particular $r'\ge n-2$, $k<n-1$, and $k\ge B/(4r')$ for sufficiently large $n$. With $B\ge n^2/36$, the polar rank is at least $B^2/(16 r')-r'\ge 4n^2$. The failure bound over all these $P$ is $O(q^{2\epsilon n^2-B/2})=o(1)$. Choosing the sets of targets as fixed initial segments of $n-1$ indices is sufficient. All these shear restrictions lie in the original full shear space.

## 3. High-rank coefficient matrices

Now use $a=b=\lfloor10^{-4} n\rfloor$ indices in the two halves. Set $B=ab$. Fix a single target index $r_0$ in mode 2 and use the other indices as the source pool $J$. The shear coordinates $x^{(s)}_{ij}$ encode $X_{ir_0,js}$, for each $s\in J$. Set $U_{jt}=T^0_{jt}$, $V_i=(S^0_{ir_0})^T$, and
$$
 \phi_t(x)=\sum_{i,j}x_{ij} U_{jt}V_i .
$$
For $t,s\in J$, $D_{ts}=-\phi_t(x^{(s)})$ and $D_{sr_0}=0$. Apart from these blocks only row $r_0$ can be nonzero. The quadratic polar form therefore consists of the $J\times J$ part, coming from
$$
 \sum_{s,t\in J}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(P\phi_t(x^{(s)})\phi_s(x^{(t)})\bigr),
$$
plus a contribution of rank at most $r$ from the square of $D_{r_0r_0}$.

Conditional on the inverse-transpose row list $V_i$, the selected rows $U_{jt}$ are independent uniform in $W=\langle V_i\rangle^\perp$, conditioned to form an independent list. The conditions on this list and on the list $V_i$ cost only $1+O(q^{-\Omega(n^2)})$. By the matrix rank bound and a union over at most $q^a-1$ nonzero combinations, with probability $1-o(1)$ every nonzero matrix in $\mathcal V=\langle V_i\rangle$ has rank at least $n-d$ with $d=\lceil2\sqrt n\rceil$. Condition on such a list $V_i$. We work in the independent $U$-row model until the final probability bound.

Take $m$ fixed source indices as a column pool and all remaining indices in $J$ as a row pool. Here $m$ is an absolute constant chosen so $mB\ge6n^2$ for sufficiently large $n$; the row pool still has $\Theta(n)$ indices. First expose all $U_{jt}$ for column-pool indices $t$. For every $P$ with $r\ge\epsilon n^2$ and every nonzero tuple $(M_t)_t$ in $\mathcal V^m$, these rows satisfy, simultaneously and with probability $1-o(1)$,
$$
 \dim\operatorname{span}\bigl\{L_{ij}=\textstyle\sum_t M_t\mathcal L_P(U_{jt}V_i)\bigr\}_{i,j}\ge B/2.
$$
For fixed $P,(M_t)_t$, expose the column-pool rows in batches indexed by $j$. If the prior span has dimension below $B/2$, failure of the next $a$ vectors to be independent modulo the prior span implies a nonzero relation with coefficient vector $(c_i)_i$. Let $V=\sum_i c_iV_i\ne0$; also choose $t_0$ with $M_{t_0}\ne0$. Given all other new rows, varying $U_{jt_0}$ contributes $M_{t_0}\mathcal L_P(U_{jt_0}V)$ to the relation. On all matrices $U$ this linear map has rank at least $r-2nd$: right and left multiplication by rank at least $n-d$ matrices lose at most $nd$ dimensions each. Restricting $U$ to $W$ loses at most $a$ and projecting modulo the prior span loses at most $B/2$. The conditional relation probability is therefore at most $q^{-r+2nd+a+B/2}$. Taking the union over $q^a-1$ coefficient vectors still gives $q^{-\Omega(n^2)}$, since $B/n^2\le 10^{-8}$. If the final span is below $B/2$, the prior span never reached that threshold and at least $b/2$ batches failed. Multiplying the conditional failure bounds along each choice of failure batches and summing over at most $q^b$ choices gives $q^{-\Omega(n^3)}$. Union over the at most $q^{n^2+am}$ choices of $P,(M_t)_t$, where $m$ is fixed, proves the asserted span event.

Fix exposed rows with this event and examine the cross submatrix of the polar form between row-pool shear groups and column-pool shear groups. For a nonzero selection $y=(y^{(t)})_t$ in the latter groups there is $j_0$ such that $M_t=\sum_i y^{(t)}_{ij_0}V_i$ is a nonzero tuple. On a row-pool group $s$, vanishing on all its shear coefficients requires the equations
$$
 \sum_t\omega_P(\phi_t(x),\phi_s(y^{(t)}))=0
 \quad\text{for every }x .
$$
Conditional on the other rows in group $s$, the equations on $U_{j_0s}$ have linear parts $U\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(L_{ij}U)$ as the coefficient of $x_{ij}$. This follows by expanding $\phi_t(x)$, using $\omega_P(B,C)=\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal L_P(B)C)$, and cycling the $M_t$ across the trace. The span estimate makes their rank at least $B/2-a$ on $W$, whose annihilator has dimension $a$. Thus the probability that fixed $y$ vanishes on all the groups is $q^{-\Omega(n^3)}$, by independence across $\Theta(n)$ groups. Union over $q^{mB}$ vectors $y$ and $q^{n^2}$ choices of $P$ gives simultaneous full column rank $mB$ with probability $1-o(1)$. This gives rank at least $mB$ for the whole $J\times J$ contribution to the polar form. The correction of rank at most $r\le n^2$ leaves rank at least $5n^2$. Transferring back from the independent row model changes the failure probability by a bounded factor.

## 4. Marginals and the deterministic algorithm

The two analyses apply to restrictions of the same full shear, so with probability $1-o(1)$ over the baseline $A_0$, every nontrivial $P$ has polar rank at least $4n^2$. In characteristic two $\operatorname{tr}(C_{23}^2)=\operatorname{tr}(C_{23})^2$, where $\operatorname{tr}(C_{23})=n^2$ in the field by the full dual row contraction. The trace of the anchor is therefore fixed. Its affine trace space has $q^{n^2-1}$ points, and its nontrivial characters correspond to nonscalar $P$ modulo scalars. In odd characteristic use the entire matrix space, in which the nontrivial characters correspond to all $P\ne0$. In both cases fix a nontrivial additive character $\psi$ of $\mathbb F_q$, writing the characters of interest as $E\mapsto\psi(\operatorname{tr}(PE))$ up to constant translation on the affine space. For a quadratic phase of polar rank $R$, squaring the modulus of its bias and averaging by the difference of two independent shear vectors bounds the squared modulus by $q^{-R}$. After fixing a difference the average in the remaining variable vanishes unless the difference is in the radical, since a nonzero $\mathbb F_q$-linear functional is onto. Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz bound total variation from uniform by $\tfrac12(q^{n^2}q^{-4n^2})^{1/2}$ for a good baseline. Averaging over all baselines proves the claimed $o(1)$ distance. Relabeling the two sides and the modes within each side proves this for each anchor. No independence between the four anchors is needed.

For the deterministic comparison, reuse Sections 4 and 5 of [Fixed-field ordinary four-tensor isomorphism](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FixedFieldOrdinaryFourTI/PROOF.md). In each mode let $d(E_i)=\sum_{m_f\ge2}\deg(f)m_f^2$, with $m_f$ the characteristic polynomial's irreducible-factor multiplicity. That proof bounds its expectation by an absolute constant for a uniform matrix, also with prescribed trace. Markov's inequality, the marginal estimates and a union bound show that $\sum_i d(E_i)\le\log_q n$ with probability $1-o(1)$. Recognize this condition exactly and align the four rational canonical forms of source and target. Centralizer algebras on repeated primary blocks of degree $e$ and multiplicity $m$ have dimension over $\mathbb F_q$ at most $em^2$. Thus there are at most $n$ simultaneous invertible repeated-block actions to enumerate. A multiplicity-one primary block has field-unit centralizer. With the other actions fixed, the same finite étale cell decomposition and exact cyclic-quotient test from Section 5 applies with four primal modes, one positive field-unit occurrence for each participating primary block, and no dual modes. The cell degrees sum to $n^4$. In particular this comparison uses polynomial time, never requires discrete logarithms, and decides equivalence against every second input. It can use just this one invertible cut.

Finally, unconditional failure in the sector with an invertible 12|34 cut is $o(1)$ because conditional failure given this cut's invertibility is $o(1)$. On the complementary sector use the singular-cut algorithm from the same proof, whose unconditional uncovered mass in the union of singular cuts is $o(1)$. Their combination gives deterministic polynomial-time exact ordinary four-tensor isomorphism on $1-o(1)$ uniformly random first inputs, with arbitrary second inputs. The algorithm may abstain on an unrecognized first input.
