# Almost-all ordinary four-tensor isomorphism over fixed finite fields

**Theorem.** Fix a finite field $\mathbb F_q$. There is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time algorithm for ordinary isomorphism of ordered $n\times n\times n\times n$ tensors which can abstain. Every answer it gives is correct. A $1-o(1)$ fraction of uniformly random first tensors are recognized, meaning the algorithm gives a correct answer for each such tensor against every second tensor.

The proof gives a certificate of applicability computable from the first input. Here ordinary isomorphism means independent invertible linear changes in the four modes. The asymptotic parameter is $n$ with $q$ fixed.

## 1. The operators and their laws

Use the prescribed cut $12\mid34$. If its matrix is singular, the [previous singular-cut algorithm](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FixedFieldOrdinaryFourTI/PROOF.md) recognizes all but an $o(1)$ fraction of that sector. If it is invertible, use its inverse transpose to make a tensor $S$ in the four dual spaces. The transformation law for the inverse transpose makes contraction of $T$ with $S$ equivariant.

In particular, on modes 2 and 3 let $C$ be the endomorphism obtained by contracting out modes 1 and 4, and set $E_3=\operatorname{Tr}_{2}(C^2)$. The inverse transformation cancels the mode-2 action in the partial trace, so $E_3$ transforms by conjugation on mode 3. Use the corresponding crossed contractions to make one operator $E_i$ on each of the other modes. Relabeling within the cut and exchanging its two sides prove that the four marginal laws coincide.

Conditional on invertibility of the prescribed cut, the flattening is uniform in $\operatorname{GL}_{n^2}(\mathbb F_q)$. The [single-operator mixing proof](SingleAnchor.md) shows that each $E_i$ is $o(1)$ from uniform in total variation. In odd characteristic the uniform law is on all $n$-by-$n$ matrices. In characteristic two it is on the hyperplane $\operatorname{tr} E_i=n^2\,1_{\mathbb F_q}$, since $\operatorname{tr}(C^2)=(\operatorname{tr} C)^2$ and $\operatorname{tr} C=n^2\,1_{\mathbb F_q}$. No joint distribution assumption on the four operators is used.

For a matrix $E$, write $m_f(E)$ for the multiplicity of a monic irreducible polynomial $f$ in its characteristic polynomial, and define
$$
 D(E)=\sum_{f\colon m_f(E)\ge2}(\deg f)m_f(E)^2 .
$$
The algorithm accepts the first tensor on this branch if $q^{\sum_{i=1}^4 D(E_i)}\le n$. This shape test uses polynomial-time field arithmetic at fixed $q$.

For completeness, if $M$ is a uniform matrix then $\Pr[m_f(M)\ge k]\le C_q q^{-k\deg f}$. Indeed the event supplies an invariant subspace of dimension $k\deg f$ with characteristic polynomial $f^k$, by the primary module filtration. Count ordered frames for the subspace. Cancelling the number of frames against the probability of any specified restricted action bounds the expected number of such subspaces by the count of size-$k\deg f$ matrices with polynomial $f^k$ divided by the order of the corresponding general linear group. This ratio is $q^{-k\deg f}\big/\prod_{j=1}^k(1-q^{-j\deg f})$ by the nilpotent primary count, derived in [Section 4 of the singular-cut proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FixedFieldOrdinaryFourTI/PROOF.md#4-bound-the-cost-of-repeated-primary-parts). Conditioning on a trace value costs at most a factor of $q$. Summing over all degrees and $k\ge2$ gives $\mathbb E D(M)=O_q(1)$ for either uniform model. For each $i$ the probability that $D(E_i)>(\log_q n)/4$ is therefore $o(1)$ by total variation and Markov's inequality. Take the union bound over four modes. This proves that the first input satisfies the shape test with probability $1-o(1)$ conditional on invertibility of its first cut.

## 2. Exact decision for every accepted first input

An invertibility mismatch at the prescribed cut rules out isomorphism. On the invertible branch, compute the four target operators. Align each source and target operator by their rational canonical forms, rejecting if any pair of forms differs. In the aligned coordinates, every candidate isomorphism has four mode maps in the centralizers of the aligned operators.

The centralizer preserves each primary summand. On a repeated $f$-primary summand, putting $e=\deg f$ and $m=m_f$, its algebra has dimension at most $em^2$. The summand is an $m$-dimensional space over the coefficient field in $\mathbb F_q[z]/(f^m)$, and a centralizing map is linear over this field. Enumerate the repeated-primary commutants by solving linear equations and discard nonunits. The gate bounds the total number of choices over all four modes by $n$. Apply each choice and label its coordinates individually for the remaining test. On a multiplicity-one primary summand the centralizer is multiplication by the units of the degree-$e$ field.

For a fixed choice, decompose the tensor into cells by tensoring the four primary decompositions and splitting the resulting products of finite fields into their field factors. Labelled scalar coordinates carry copies of the base field. Each cell is one field, and the sum of their degrees is $n^4$. The remaining variables are the field units on the multiplicity-one blocks. A variable acts in a cell either by one specified field embedding or trivially, with the first alternative applying to the blocks represented by that cell. Compare the zero support before taking ratios of the target and source nonzero coordinates. The task is to test whether the tuple of ratios belongs to the image of this homomorphism of unit groups.

Here are the constructive details. Finite étale algebras of polynomial dimension split deterministically in time polynomial in the dimension at fixed characteristic $p$. Compute the fixed algebra of the prime-field Frobenius by linear algebra. In an étale algebra with $r$ field factors this is $\mathbb F_p^r$. Split it into coordinate idempotents by applying $1-(x-\lambda)^{p-1}$ for a basis of fixed elements $x$ and each $\lambda\in\mathbb F_p$, refining the current partition by these idempotents. The same operation factors the squarefree parts of polynomials. Ordinary squarefree decomposition handles multiplicities.

Use compatible field models $K_d$ for all needed extension degrees $d$. For each prime dividing the degrees, choose a displayed field whose degree is divisible by the maximum required power of that prime. Extract the nested subfields from this one field by solving Frobenius-fixed equations. Take tensor products of the resulting prime-power towers across distinct degree primes. The tensor products are fields, and the inclusions for divisibility of degrees compose. Only polynomial-dimensional models are constructed. To identify a displayed field with its model, take a generator of the displayed field over $\mathbb F_q$ and find a root of its minimal polynomial in the model by the same étale splitting. A degree generator can be obtained by summing generators of the prime-power subfields. In one prime-power subfield a basis vector outside its unique maximal proper subfield suffices. If a Frobenius power fixes the sum, it changes each summand by a base-field scalar, by coprimality of the degrees and the tensor product basis. Each such scalar is zero when the summand degree is prime to $p$, by iterating that Frobenius power for a multiple of its orbit length prime to $p$. There is at most one remaining summand, so its change is zero as well. Thus the sum has full degree. The Frobenius twist of any actual embedding relative to the model inclusion can now be identified by testing the powers on a generator.

For decision, the following version of the [existing cyclic-quotient test](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FixedFieldOrdinaryFourTI/PROOF.md#5-exact-comparison-and-canonical-compression) uses only gcds of integer orders. An empty nonzero support passes immediately. Otherwise index the nonzero cells by $s$, let $m_s=q^{d_s}-1$ be their orders, and let $h_j$ be the order of the unit group for variable $j$. In hypothetical compatible primitive-root coordinates, an embedding of variable $j$ into cell $s$ has log coefficient $(m_s/h_j)q^\ell$, where $\ell$ is its twist. Put those explicit integers in a column for variable $j$, using zero for absence. Add the diagonal order columns $m_s e_s$, and compute an integer Smith row transform $U$ giving diagonal lattice invariants $\delta_i$. Membership of the ratio tuple, with hypothetical log vector $b$, is equivalent to $(Ub)_i=0\bmod\delta_i$ for each $i$. Neither the primitive roots nor the logs are needed in the algorithm.

The quotient of the lattice is a quotient of $\bigoplus_s\mathbb Z/m_s\mathbb Z$. Thus $\delta_i\mid\operatorname{lcm}_s m_s$. For each $i$ and cell $j$ check its congruence modulo $a=\gcd(\delta_i,m_j)$. The least common multiple of these checks is $\delta_i$. For input ratio $x_s$ compute $x_s^{m_s U_{is}/a}$, with negative powers interpreted in its unit group. The exponent is integral because $m_s e_s$ is a lattice generator and $\delta_i\mid m_s U_{is}$. The powered value lies in the common subfield of degrees $\gcd(d_s,d_j)$, since its order divides both $a$ and $m_s$. Transport it into cell $j$ using the compatible models, multiply over $s$, and check equality to $1$. To verify the test, embed the models into a hypothetical common field and choose compatible primitive roots there. The product is the primitive $a$-th root raised to $\sum_s U_{is}b_s$. Thus the checks are exactly the required congruences.

A candidate repeated-primary choice passes precisely when its remaining field units can realize the target; the argument includes necessity and sufficiency. The gate and all subsequent calculations have polynomial bit cost. The extension degrees and number of cells are at most $n^4$, the orders have $O(n^4\log q)$ bits, and Smith normal form with transformation matrices has polynomial bit cost by [Kannan and Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040). Root tests are over explicitly represented fields of polynomial degree and fixed characteristic. Combining this branch with the earlier singular branch proves the theorem.

## Scope

This is an informal deterministic decision proof. The first-input distribution is uniform and the target can be arbitrary. This strengthens the positive-density question in [Li, Li, Qiao, Tao, and Wang, arXiv v2 Problem 1.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00591v2) and the previous fixed-field construction in MathProofs.
