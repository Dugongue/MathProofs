# Relative mapping presentations of projective representation stacks

Let \(\Lambda\) be a finitely presented associative unital \(\mathbb Z\)-algebra. For a commutative ring \(R\), fix integers \(N\ge r\ge 1\), and let \(\mathcal P_{\Lambda,r,N}(R)\) denote the groupoid of representations of \(\Lambda\) on finitely generated locally free \(R\)-modules of constant rank \(r\) generated by at most \(N\) elements. Morphisms are the \(R\)-linear isomorphisms intertwining the representations. The action of \(\mathbb Z\) on each module is the scalar action. Write \(\mathcal P_{\Lambda,r}(R)\) for the groupoid with no fixed bound on the number of generators.

## Theorem

There is a cosemisimplicial family of finite anchored 4-complexes \(K_\bullet\), indexed by order-preserving injections, such that

\[
\left\|[K_\bullet,Y_R]_{\rm rel}\right\|\ \simeq\
B\mathcal P_{\Lambda,r,N}(R)
\]

naturally for every commutative ring \(R\), up to the canonical base-change identifications on modules. Here \(Y_R\) and the anchor boundary map are the fixed coefficient target and boundary of [universal relative mapping schemes](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/Top10CrossoverProofs/UniversalRelativeMappingSchemes.md). For fixed \(\Lambda,r,N\), the complex \(K_n\) uses \(O((n+1)^3)\) cells. The classifying spaces on the left have a natural filtered system, indexed by \(N\) via zero padding on their mapping-class nerves, whose homotopy colimit is \(B\mathcal P_{\Lambda,r}(R)\). In particular the construction recovers \(B\mathrm{GL}_r(R)\) as one component for \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z\), while retaining a separate component and automorphism group for every isomorphism class of nonfree modules.

## Affine objects and arrows

Fix generators \(a_j\), \(1\le j\le s\), and finitely many noncommutative polynomial relations \(f_\ell\) for \(\Lambda\). In an object, the matrix \(E\in M_N(R)\) and the matrices \(A_j\in M_N(R)\) satisfy

\[
E^2=E,\qquad
\det(T I-E)=T^{N-r}(T-1)^r,\qquad
A_j=EA_jE,\qquad
f_\ell(A_1,\ldots,A_s)=0.
\]

The last evaluation takes place in the corner algebra \(EM_N(R)E\), with the constant \(1\) represented by \(E\). The characteristic polynomial identity is a finite system of coefficient equations. Over each local ring an idempotent is conjugate to \(\operatorname{diag}(I_t,0)\) since its image and kernel are finite free over the local ring. The characteristic polynomial identity forces \(t=r\) in every residue field, because \(T\) and \(T-1\) are coprime in every characteristic. The converse also holds, since equality of each characteristic polynomial coefficient can be checked in all localizations. Thus the matrix object determines a rank-\(r\) projective representation on \(E R^N\).

For two objects \((E,A)\) and \((E',A')\), introduce matrices \(F,G\) and impose

\[
F=E'FE,\quad G=EGE',\quad GF=E,\quad FG=E',\quad
FA_j=A'_jF\quad(1\le j\le s).
\]

These equations say that \(F\) and \(G\) restrict to mutually inverse maps on the image modules and that \(F\) intertwines \(\Lambda\). Conversely, any intertwining isomorphism \(\phi:E R^N\to E'R^N\) extends uniquely to \(F\) satisfying the support equation, namely the composite of the projection \(E\), the isomorphism \(\phi\), and the inclusion \(E'R^N\subset R^N\). Its inverse yields the unique \(G\). This is natural over \(R\). The composed \(F\)-matrix is matrix multiplication and the composed \(G\)-matrix is the multiplication in reverse order.

Call the resulting matrix groupoid \(\mathcal G_N(R)\). The functor taking the image of \(E\) is fully faithful by the arrow argument. It is essentially surjective: for \(M\) generated by at most \(N\) elements, choose a surjection \(R^N\to M\), split it by projectivity, and transfer the generator action into the corner algebra defined by the resulting idempotent. The construction of the functor to modules is canonical. Base change preserves both the idempotent and the isomorphism of its image with the base change of its previous image, by the direct-sum decomposition. In the zero ring the point and groupoid descriptions both yield one isomorphism class with trivial automorphism group.

## The nerve by finite anchored complexes

An \(n\)-simplex can be specified by \(n+1\) objects, one arrow for each \(i<j\), and the equations

\[
F_{ik}=F_{jk}F_{ij}\qquad (0\le i<j<k\le n).
\]

The equations determine every nonconsecutive \(F_{ik}\) by the consecutive arrows, and every inverse \(G_{ij}\) is uniquely determined. Conversely, a composable string of \(n\) arrows gives all these labels by composition. This is an identification with the ordinary groupoid nerve in each simplicial degree and agrees with its faces. A face omitting an internal vertex keeps the arrow labeled by the new neighboring pair, which is exactly the composite specified by the triple equation.

Apply the polynomial gate construction with shared inputs. Use a fixed labeled circuit for the object equations at each vertex, a fixed labeled circuit for the arrow equations at each pair, and a fixed labeled circuit for the composition equations at each triple. Each circuit uses its named input cells and fresh auxiliary wire cells, shared anchors, and its own attached 4-cells. In particular the arrow equations see the two vertex coordinates and the corresponding arrow coordinates, and each triple sees the indicated three arrow coordinates. An order-preserving injection carries labels to labels and uses identical attaching maps, so \(K_\bullet\) is a strict covariant diagram of pairs. The number of circuits is bounded by a constant times \(n+1+\binom{n+1}2+\binom{n+1}3\). Taking relative homotopy classes recovers the algebraic nerve, since the gate coordinates are uniquely determined by the visible polynomial variables and restriction on subcircuits drops exactly the corresponding coordinate labels. The fat realization of the nerve of a discrete groupoid has the homotopy type of its ordinary classifying space, by the standard comparison for good simplicial spaces applied to discrete sets. The equivalence of groupoids \(\mathcal G_N(R)\simeq\mathcal P_{\Lambda,r,N}(R)\) completes the proof for fixed \(N\).

For variable \(N\), pad every displayed matrix by a zero row and column in the corresponding direct sum. This gives injective maps on the nerves of the matrix groupoids and induces the filtered maps of relative mapping-class nerves under the identifications already proved. The realization maps are inclusions of CW subcomplexes. Each matrix groupoid inclusion is fully faithful by the support equations. Taking the union on object and arrow sets therefore gives a groupoid still fully faithfully mapped into \(\mathcal P_{\Lambda,r}(R)\), and it is essentially surjective because every finite projective module admits a surjection from a finite free module. The ordinary colimit of these realizations is a homotopy colimit, and it identifies with the classifying space of the union groupoid. The padding maps here are on mapping-class nerves. The theorem uses only face maps for its strict topological source diagrams.

The bundle/projective-module correspondence and the classifying space of a groupoid are classical, as is the comparison of realizations. The contribution is the finite 4-dimensional fixed-target realization of the full groupoid in each degree, including isomorphisms between different idempotent frames and nontrivial stabilizers. No sheafification or descent step is required on the \(R\)-point groupoids.
