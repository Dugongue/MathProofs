# Exact Frobenius spectra for affine-encoded extension problems

**Status.** Complete informal proof. The arithmetic ingredients are classical. The conclusion computes an eventual local spectrum for affine-encoded homotopy extension and describes all spectra obtainable this way.

Ax gave the classical decision results on Diophantine congruences. Van den Dries reduced the solvability spectrum over prime fields to that of a univariate polynomial outside finitely many primes. Derakhshan and Macintyre proved decidability of the full common first-order theory of the rings \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). The conclusions here specify the effective Galois-set description of all large-prime Henselian coefficient rings simultaneously, the residue degrees, and realization of the resulting patterns by homotopy extension instances. [Ax, Solving diophantine problems modulo every prime](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1967/85-2/p01), [Van den Dries, A remark on Ax’s theorem on solvability modulo primes](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02571510), [Derakhshan and Macintyre, Decidability of the class of all the rings \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\)](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2023.62).

## Statement

Given a finitely presented affine scheme \(S/\mathbb Z\), one can compute a finite Galois extension \(K/\mathbb Q\), a finite \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\)-set \(\Omega\), and a finite set \(D\) of rational primes. For every \(p\notin D\), every \(m\ge 1\), and every Henselian local \(\mathbb Z\)-algebra \(R\) with residue field \(\mathbb F_{p^m}\),

\[
S(R)\ne\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Omega^{\operatorname{Frob}_p^m}\ne\varnothing.
\]

In particular the allowed residue degrees are exactly the multiples of the cycle lengths of \(\operatorname{Frob}_p\) on \(\Omega\). The natural density of primes with a \(\mathbb Z_p\)-point is the fraction of \(G\) that fixes a member of \(\Omega\). There is an algorithm to decide whether \(S(\mathbb Z_p)\) is nonempty at every prime and whether, in addition, \(S(\mathbb R)\) is nonempty.

Conversely, for any given \(K/\mathbb Q\) as above, every conjugation- and power-closed subset \(C\subseteq G\) is realized by an affine finite-presentation \(\mathbb Z\)-scheme with eventual local condition \(\operatorname{Frob}_p^m\in C\). Each such scheme can be chosen finite étale over \(\mathbb Z[1/N]\) for a suitable \(N\). A subset \(C\) is power-closed here when \(g^a\in C\) for every \(g\in C\) and positive integer \(a\).

By the exact affine realization theorem used in this project, each such \(S\) is represented by a finite simply connected pair of dimension at most four, an integral degree-two boundary map, and a coefficient-natural cubic-stage target \(Y_R\), the homotopy fiber of a map \(K(R,2)^2\to K(R,4)\) given by a cup product. The formula classifies existence of extensions to \(Y_R\) over every indicated local coefficient ring. The algorithm applies to extension problems presented with their affine encoding.

## Computing the Frobenius data

Work first on the underlying reduced generic fiber of \(S\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\). At each step take the irreducible components of the current reduced closed remainder. In each component take its smooth locus and delete its intersections with the other components of the remainder. These disjoint smooth geometrically irreducible open pieces are nonempty. Their union is open in the remainder and the closed complement has strictly smaller dimension. Iteration gives a finite partition by smooth geometrically irreducible locally closed strata. All choices defining the partition are intrinsic, so Galois permutes the strata.

These operations are effective by polynomial ideal operations, absolute irreducible decomposition in characteristic zero and the Jacobian criterion. Equivalently, one may enumerate number fields and decompositions and test the polynomial certificates for a partition, nonemptiness, and absolute irreducibility over the algebraic closure. Choose a computable finite Galois extension \(K\) over which all strata are defined, and let \(\Omega\) be their finite Galois set. Each representative stratum \(V_i\) descends to \(K_i=K^{G_i}\), where \(G_i\) is its stabilizer, by Galois descent of its closure and closed complement. Both descent and computation of the stabilizers reduce to linear algebra and polynomial ideal equality over number fields.

For each representative select a nonempty affine open chart \(U_i\) of \(V_i/K_i\). When its dimension is zero, it equals \(\operatorname{Spec}K_i\). In positive dimension, a transcendence basis and a primitive element present the function field of \(V_i\) as that of a geometrically irreducible affine hypersurface. The function field extension over the transcendence basis is separable in characteristic zero. There is a birational isomorphism between nonempty opens. Shrink its hypersurface side to a principal open on which some partial derivative of the defining polynomial is nonzero. This gives an explicit smooth chart \(U_i\) and retains geometric irreducibility. One can find this data by enumeration with polynomial identity checks if a birational model has not already been computed.

Spread out the partition as locally closed subsets after inverting a computable integer \(N\), including ramified primes of \(K\). The cover, disjointness, and Galois permutations are constructible-set equalities over the algebraic closure. Their verification in characteristic zero supplies finite algebraic certificates by encoding every inequality \(f\ne 0\) via \(fy=1\) and clearing denominators in the corresponding Nullstellensatz identities. The charts, their maps into \(S\), and their explicit derivative conditions also spread by clearing finitely many denominators. This gives maps into the original \(S\), since each of its original equations already holds identically on the chosen generic chart. If the generic fiber is empty, a single cleared certificate excludes all primes outside a computable finite set and one takes \(\Omega\) empty.

Further enlarge \(N\) so every positive-dimensional chart stays an open of an absolutely irreducible hypersurface and its complement stays of smaller dimension. The former assertion is effective by encoding the finitely many possible factorizations of a bounded-degree polynomial, with specified nonzero coefficients, and clearing denominators in each certificate of impossibility. The latter follows by preserving nondivisibility of the open-defining polynomial by the defining irreducible equation, using division with a fixed monomial order. After clearing denominators both assertions hold over the algebraic closures of all good residue fields. Effective Lang–Weil gives a lower bound \(q^d-O(q^{d-1/2})\) for the number of \(\mathbb F_q\)-points on a chart's hypersurface, with computable constants. The deleted part has at most a computable constant times \(q^{d-1}\) points by the bounded-degree affine point bound. Therefore every reduction of every chart has a point over each finite field of sufficiently large size containing its field of definition. The bound can be made uniform across the finitely many charts. Add every prime at most that bound to \(D\), along with the primes dividing \(N\).

### Necessity and sufficiency over local rings

Fix \(p\notin D\) and a prime of \(K\) over \(p\). The geometric special fiber is partitioned by the spread strata, and its arithmetic Frobenius permutes their labels as \(\operatorname{Frob}_p\). If \(S(R)\) has a point, the residue point lies in exactly one stratum. The action of the \(m\)-th power of Frobenius fixes that point, so it fixes the label.

For the converse, a fixed label in the orbit \(G/G_i\) is equivalent to an \(\mathbb F_{p^m}\)-point of \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_{K_i}[1/N]\). This follows by identifying this finite étale scheme's geometric special-fiber points with the cosets and using their Frobenius action. Because \(R\) is Henselian, this residue point lifts to an \(R\)-point of that finite étale scheme. The chosen chart then gives a smooth \(R\)-scheme. In dimension zero it already gives an \(R\)-point. Otherwise it has a point over \(\mathbb F_{p^m}\) by the size bound, which lifts by smooth Henselian lifting. For the explicit chart, one can also lift all coordinates other than the one with nonzero derivative, then solve the resulting one-variable equation by Hensel's lemma. The chart map gives a point of \(S(R)\).

### Density, decidability, and realization

Chebotarev gives the density from the conjugacy-invariant set of elements of \(G\) with a fixed label. If some element fixes none, infinitely many primes fail. If every element fixes a label, it remains to decide the finitely many exceptional \(\mathbb Z_p\)-point questions. Each reduces to the effective first-order theory of \(\mathbb Q_p\) by expressing valuations via squares. For odd \(p\), \(x\) is integral exactly when \(1+px^2\) is a square. For \(p=2\), replace \(p\) by \(8\). For odd \(p\) the integral case is Hensel's lemma while a negative valuation of \(x\) makes the valuation of \(1+px^2\) negative and odd. For \(p=2\) the integral case is the square criterion modulo \(8\), valuation at most \(-2\) again gives negative odd valuation, and valuation \(-1\) gives \(1+8x^2\equiv3\pmod8\). Apply quantifier elimination for real closed fields for the real point question.

For the converse, fixed-point sets for finite \(G\)-sets are closed under conjugation and powers. Every such set \(C\) is the union of the conjugates of \(\langle g\rangle\) for representatives \(g\) of the conjugacy classes contained in \(C\). For each representative take \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_{K^{\langle g\rangle}}[1/N]\) and form their disjoint union. For \(C=\varnothing\) use the empty scheme. At unramified primes outside \(N\), finite étale Henselian lifting gives the criterion for every \(R\) and \(m\). Finite disjoint unions of affines are affine and remain of finite presentation over \(\mathbb Z\).
