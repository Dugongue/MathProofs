# Affine arithmetic in two fixed compact complex flag manifolds

For every affine scheme \(X\) of finite presentation over \(\mathbb Z\), there is a finite simply connected four-dimensional CW pair \((K_X,A)\) with fixed marking \(A=S^2\vee S^2\), such that relative homotopy classes into the fixed full flag manifold \(SU(3)/T^2\) are in bijection with \(X(\mathbb Z)\). The boundary map is fixed once and for all. The target is a smooth closed simply connected complex manifold of complex dimension three. The degree-equation scheme is \(X\) after base change to \(\mathbb Z[1/3]\).

There is also a closed-source version. For each \(X\) there is a closed simply connected stably parallelizable smooth six-manifold \(M_X\) with torsion-free homology and a marked subcomplex \(A_X\) homotopy equivalent to a finite wedge of two-spheres. Relative homotopy classes into \(SU(4)/T^3\) are in bijection with \(X(\mathbb Z)\). This fixed target is a smooth closed simply connected complex manifold of complex dimension six. Its boundary maps use three fixed sphere classes and constant maps.

## Flag lifts in dimension four

For the diagonal maximal torus \(T^{n-1}\subset SU(n)\), \(SU(n)/T^{n-1}\) is the homotopy fiber of \(BT^{n-1}\to BSU(n)\). The map classifies the sum of the \(n-1\) universal line bundles and their inverse determinant. Write \(x,y\) for the two first Chern classes when \(n=3\). The weights are \(x,y,-x-y\), hence the pulled-back second Chern class is \(-q(x,y)\) for
\[
q(x,y)=x^2+xy+y^2.
\]
Take the base space of a marked pair to have a 2-skeleton that is a wedge of spheres, and attach only four-cells to it. Restrict the marking to the two anchor spheres. A map into \(BT^2\) with fixed boundary has one relative homotopy class for each list of degree pairs on the spheres extending the pinned anchor degrees. This follows from \(BT^2=K(\mathbb Z^2,2)\) and \(H^1(A)=0\). There is a relative lift to the flag precisely when \(q=0\) in \(H^4(K_X;\mathbb Z)\). Indeed \(BSU(3)\) is 3-connected and \(c_2\) induces an isomorphism on \(\pi_4\), tested on the quaternionic Hopf bundle with a trivial line added. Also \(H^4(K_X,A)\to H^4(K_X)\) is an isomorphism.

For a fixed base map, the choices of relative nullhomotopy of its composite into \(BSU(3)\), when they exist, form a torsor for \([K_X/A,SU(3)]_*\). In the path-space fiber model this follows by comparing with one chosen nullhomotopy and concatenating loops, using the prescribed paths on the marking. The group vanishes because \(H^3(K_X,A)=0\) and the only nonzero homotopy group of \(SU(3)\) in dimensions one through four is \(\pi_3=\mathbb Z\). The low-dimensional groups follow from Bott periodicity and \(SU(m+1)/SU(m)=S^{2m+1}\). Transport along a homotopy of base maps rel marking then proves uniqueness for the entire degree class. One may choose the prescribed maps on the two anchor spheres in the actual flag, pass to the homotopy-fiber model, and transport the fixed boundary by the resulting comparison. Relative homotopy classes are invariant under this target equivalence and boundary transport. [Bott, The Stable Homotopy of the Classical Groups](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/bott4.pdf)

The polarization of \(q\) is
\[
B((x,y),(x',y'))=2xx'+xy'+x'y+2yy'.
\]
For distinct sphere labels \(D,E\) with degree vectors \(d,e\), the obstruction evaluated on a four-cell attached by the Whitehead product \([D,E]\) is \(B(d,e)\). To see the coefficient and orientation, use the standard four-cell of \(S^2\times S^2\) attached by the product, and compute \(q(d\alpha+e\beta)\) on the fundamental class with \(\alpha\beta\) positive. The test is additive in attaching classes, either by pinching the attaching spheres or by evaluating the obstruction on the glued four-cells.

## Circuit construction in the six-real-dimensional flag

Pin the degree vectors of the two anchor spheres \(a,b\) to \(e_1,e_2\). Give each circuit wire \(t\) two distinct spheres \(P_t,Q_t\). Tests \([a,P_t]\) and \([b,Q_t]\) force the respective degree vectors to \(t_P(1,-2)\) and \(t_Q(-2,1)\): the equations are \(2x+y=0\) and \(x+2y=0\). Write \(v=(1,-2)\), \(w=(-2,1)\). Then
\[
B(v,e_2)=B(e_1,w)=B(v,w)=-3,\qquad B(e_1,e_2)=1.
\]
The test \([P_t,b]-[a,Q_t]\) therefore identifies \(t_P=t_Q\) over \(\mathbb Z\) and over \(\mathbb Z[1/3]\)-algebras. Use \([P_t,b]\) for pinning zero and \([P_t,b]+3[a,b]\) for pinning one. For a sum \(x+y=z\) use \([P_x,b]+[P_y,b]-[P_z,b]\). For a product \(xy=z\) use \([P_x,Q_y]-[P_z,b]\). These formulas apply to squares as \(P_x,Q_x\) are distinct. Negation is addition to the constant zero. Every defining equation of \(X\) can be represented by a finite arithmetic circuit whose output is set to zero. All auxiliary values are determined by their inputs. This gives the asserted integral bijection and equality of the degree-equation schemes over \(\mathbb Z[1/3]\) by elimination of the uniquely determined coordinates. The localization matters for the asserted scheme comparison.

## Closed six-manifold sources into a twelve-real-dimensional flag

For \(SU(4)/T^3\), write \(x,y,z\) for the three degree coordinates. The weights are \(x,y,z,-x-y-z\). Now the negative of \(c_2\) is
\[
q_3(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2+z^2+xy+xz+yz,
\]
with integral polarization \(B_3\) of matrix \(I+J\), where \(J\) is the three-by-three ones matrix. Use three anchor labels \(a_1,a_2,a_3\) with standard basis degrees. Pairing a wire sphere with each of \(a_1,a_2\) and setting both results to zero gives \(2x+y+z=x+2y+z=0\). Its degree vector is thus uniquely \(t v\), with \(v=(1,1,-3)\). Here \(B_3(v,e_3)=-4\), \(B_3(v,v)=12\), and \(B_3(e_1,e_3)=1\).

Use the sum and zero formulas from the preceding construction with \(P_t\) now the sole main wire sphere and \(b=a_3\). Pin one by \([P_t,a_3]+4[a_1,a_3]\). Use \([P_x,P_y]+3[P_z,a_3]\) for multiplication, whose value is \(12(xy-z)\). Before a square gate make a distinct duplicate of its input and use the equality test \([P_x,a_3]-[P_{\mathrm{copy}},a_3]\). Consequently every test is a sum \(\sum_{\{D,E\}}m_{jDE}[D,E]\) with only distinct-label brackets and defined integer coefficients.

Apply the framed realization to these coefficients with one new coordinate \(r_j\) per test. It produces a smooth closed stably parallelizable simply connected six-manifold \(M_X\) with torsion-free homology and zero third homology. It has a degree-two cohomology basis labeled by the circuit labels and the \(r_j\), and dual homology sphere representatives for the marked labels. Its symmetric cup trilinear form has values \(\mu(D,E,r_j)=m_{jDE}\) and their permutations, and all other values zero. For clarity, the construction starts from \(S^2\times S^4\) summands for basis coordinates and one \((S^2)^3\) summand for each signed occurrence in the coefficients. Framed surgery on primitive independent differences ties the three coordinates in each product summand to the specified two circuit coordinates and its test coordinate, using a sign on one of them. The resulting trilinear form is the restriction of the connected-sum cup form through the surgery trace. The designated sphere representatives are disjoint copies in the original summands and are retained away from the relation spheres. Join these representatives by an embedded tree to form \(A_X\). [Framed cubic realization](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ManinGrochowCrossovers/IntegralTensorManifolds.md)

Pin the degree vector on each \(r_j\) sphere to zero, and those on \(a_1,a_2,a_3\) to the standard basis. Under these pins Poincaré duality tests \(q_3\) by pairing it with each \(H^2\) basis element. Pairings on original circuit labels are zero since every nonzero trilinear term has a test label. The pairing on \(r_j\) is exactly the polarized circuit test with coefficients \(m_{jDE}\). Also \(c_3\) vanishes for every such pinned assignment: each of its terms is cubic in the three degree classes, all zero on the test labels. As \(H^6(M_X)\cong\mathbb Z\), the zero evaluation proves the claimed vanishing as a class.

It remains to show these are exactly the relative flag lift tests. The composite into \(BSU(4)\) has a fixed nullhomotopy on \(A_X\) supplied by the prescribed boundary maps to the actual flag. The comparisons of relative to absolute cohomology in degrees four and six are isomorphisms because \(A_X\) is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-spheres. By Bott periodicity in the stable range, \(\pi_j BSU(4)\) for \(1\le j\le 6\) is \(\mathbb Z\) at \(j=4,6\) and zero otherwise. As above \(c_2\) induces a unit on \(\pi_4\). The homotopy fiber of this \(c_2\) map is 5-connected and has \(\pi_6=\mathbb Z\). When \(c_2=0\), choose a relative \(c_2\)-nullhomotopy to obtain a lift to this fiber respecting the chosen boundary nullhomotopy. The remaining relative obstruction is in \(H^6(M_X,A_X;\mathbb Z)\). Its image under multiplication by \(2\), up to sign, is \(c_3\), because Bott integrality evaluates the universal \(c_3\) on the stable bundle generator on \(S^6\) as \(\pm 2\). The relative group is torsion-free, so \(c_3=0\) kills the obstruction. Conversely a nullhomotopy forces both pulled-back Chern classes to vanish. [Borel and Hirzebruch, Characteristic Classes and Homogeneous Spaces II](https://hirzebruch.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/id/eprint/160/1/22_Characteristic%20classes%20and%20homogeneous%20spaces%20II.pdf)

The relative third and fifth groups \(H^3(M_X,A_X)\) and \(H^5(M_X,A_X)\) vanish. For degree three use that \(H^2(M_X)\to H^2(A_X)\) is onto, because the marked homology classes are a subset of a basis. For degree five use \(H^4(A_X)=0\) and \(H^5(M_X)=0\). The only nonzero homotopy groups of \(SU(4)\) through dimension six are \(\pi_3=\mathbb Z\) and \(\pi_5=\mathbb Z\). Ordinary Postnikov obstruction theory shows \([M_X/A_X,SU(4)]_*\) is zero, by those relative cohomology vanishings. As in the four-dimensional case each degree class has exactly one relative lift class when the obstruction vanishes. The circuit then gives the bijection with \(X(\mathbb Z)\).
