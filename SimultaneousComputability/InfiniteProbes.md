# Infinite probe systems and exact field-entry degrees

Finite profile realization leaves three different questions. One can vary the infinitely many probes, fix a particular tower and ask what bounds the first witness, or fix the entire poset of finite extensions and classify the upward sets realized by one system. These questions have different answers even in algebraic extensions of a number field.

## 1. Universal effective probe diagrams

Let $P$ be an effectively enumerated countable preorder with a computably enumerable relation $\preceq$. A **c.e. profile on $P$** is a computably enumerable set of pairs $(e,p)$ such that membership at $p$ implies membership at $q$ when $p\preceq q$. A diagram is uniformly computable if there are computable presentations of its fields indexed by $p$, with a uniform algorithm for the inclusion on the arrows. The codes for these field presentations need not specify finite degrees or primitive elements.

**Theorem 1 (one diagram for all profiles).** There is one uniformly computable diagram of multiquadratic algebraic extensions $L_p/\mathbb Q$ with a $\mathbb Q$-embedding $L_p\to L_q$ exactly when $p\preceq q$, such that every c.e. profile is realized there by a uniform family of monic quadratics. The construction is effective from an enumeration of the preorder. On a positive probe the precise algebraic-integer witnesses are the two square roots of one fixed rational prime. The construction respects all field inclusions. A probe can have infinite degree.

**Proof.** Reserve disjoint rational primes $b_p$ for the probe indices and $a_{i,e}$ for pairs of program and input indices. Enumerate all c.e. sets $W_i$ of pairs $(e,p)$. For a probe $q$, adjoin the square roots of $b_p$ for $p\preceq q$ and the square roots of $a_{i,e}$ whenever there is $p\preceq q$ with $(e,p)\in W_i$. Distinct primes represent independent square classes in $\mathbb Q^\times/\mathbb Q^{\times2}$. A square root of a prime belongs to a generated field exactly when its prime was adjoined. In the infinite case this follows by going to a finite subfield generated by finitely many prime square roots. The reserved $b_p$ show that field inclusion reflects the preorder. Each field is normal over $\mathbb Q$, so any embedding into a field in this diagram requires inclusion in the chosen algebraic closure.

For the computational assertion there is an explicit computable ambient field of all these roots. A vector is a finite rational combination of products of distinct formal square roots, with products reduced using $(\sqrt p)^2=p$. Independence of square classes gives uniqueness and the usual field operations. For an inverse, solve the multiplication equations in the finite subfield on the involved primes. Enumerating such vectors with support in the c.e. set of adjoined primes and enumerating without repeats gives a computable presentation of each $L_p$. There are infinitely many rational elements in each enumeration. To compute an operation, translate to ambient vectors, compute there, and search the receiving enumeration for the answer. To compute an arrow, use the same ambient code and search the target enumeration. On valid arrows the search terminates, and the maps respect composition. The integral elements are also uniformly c.e. and recognizable on valid field codes by their monic minimal polynomials over $\mathbb Q$, computed in a finite containing field.

When $W_i$ is the desired upward profile, the condition with existential $p\preceq q$ coincides with $(e,q)\in W_i$. At input $e$ use $T^2-a_{i,e}$. The roots are integral and distinct. All assignments and inclusions in the construction use the original preorder, independently of this choice of program $i$. This proves the theorem.

In particular any countable effective chain or branching poset has a single universal algebraic probe diagram. The infinite profiles in this theorem need not give a finite-extension diagram. The difference is sharp under a simple and natural restriction on the equations. The construction builds on the classical coding of c.e. sets by multiquadratic subfields, for example the fields in Section 3 of Miller and Shlapentokh. The common diagram across programs and the reservation of primes that recover the exact probe order add the simultaneous features. [Miller–Shlapentokh, Computable categoricity for algebraic fields with splitting algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.1205)

**Theorem 2 (explicit versus presented finite fields).** Fix a computable increasing sequence of distinct odd primes $(p_e)$, and use the tests $T^2-p_e$. For any uniformly c.e. upward profile with each probe having only finitely many positive inputs, there is a uniformly computably presented diagram of finite multiquadratic extensions realizing the profile. It can be taken to reflect the probe order, with finitely many additional marker roots at each probe, when every principal downset is finite. Without requiring the order reflection, the following are equivalent.

1. There is an algorithm producing the full finite list of positive inputs at each probe.
2. The tests have a realization by a uniformly *explicit* diagram of number fields. Here explicit means an algorithm gives a primitive irreducible polynomial for each field. The fields need not be multiquadratic.
3. The degrees of the canonical fields generated by exactly the positive test roots are uniformly computable.

**Proof.** For a computable presentation, retain only the primes indexed by the positive row in the preceding construction, with independent marker primes for predecessors if requested. Each finite row, and each finite mark set in the order-reflecting case, generates a number field. The same enumeration and search gives the effective structure and embeddings, even without a computably bounded stopping point for the enumeration of the last root.

An explicit row gives a primitive polynomial for its multiquadratic field by exact primitive-element calculations, and its degree is $2^m$ for row size $m$. If the canonical degrees are known, enumerate positive inputs until $m$ distinct ones appear. To see necessity for any explicit number field, compute a nonzero discriminant of an integral primitive polynomial for it, scaling an original primitive generator when necessary. If $T^2-p_e$ has a root, the quadratic subfield is ramified at $p_e$, so $p_e$ divides this discriminant. There are finitely many such candidate primes, effectively listed. Factor $T^2-p_e$ over the given number field to filter the candidates. This gives the exact row. Inclusions between the canonical multiquadratic fields are the inclusions of the rows, so when the rows are explicitly given the arrows are effective as well.

For example, let the rows in a chain be the halting inputs among $0,\dots,n$ at level $n$. Each field is finite, with degree at most $2^{n+1}$ in the canonical construction. A uniform explicit description or an exact degree algorithm would decide halting by taking differences of the finite lists. Thus a computable upper bound on degrees is compatible with failure of a uniform primitive presentation.

## 2. Finite generators of field profiles

Fix a number field $K$. Let $\mathcal E_K$ be the poset of finite extension types, ordered by existence of a $K$-embedding. For $F\in\mathcal E_K$, let $\uparrow F=\{E:F\hookrightarrow E\}$. The integral profile of a system records acceptance over $\mathcal O_E$, and is an upward subset of this poset.

**Theorem 3 (exact classification for finite étale tests).** The full integral profiles on $\mathcal E_K$ of monic squarefree univariate polynomials are exactly the finite unions of principal upsets. The minimal generators form a unique finite antichain. Inclusion of such profiles is decidable, and union and intersection are effective and preserve this class.

**Proof.** The irreducible factors of a test polynomial give finite residue fields $F_i/K$. Their roots are integral, and acceptance over $E$ is precisely membership in $\bigcup_i\uparrow F_i$. Conversely choose integral primitive elements of the desired $F_i$, and multiply their minimal polynomials. Duplicate fields can be dropped. The empty set is represented by the polynomial 1. The minimal field types in a finite union are exactly the minimal elements of its generator list, which proves uniqueness. Profile inclusion is equivalent to a finite list of embedding tests on the generators. An embedding is detected by a root of the source minimal polynomial in the target.

Union concatenates lists. For intersection of principal upsets, factor the finite étale algebra $F\otimes_K G$ into fields $H_j$. A pair of embeddings of $F,G$ in $E$ gives a $K$-algebra map of that tensor product to $E$, which factors through one of the $H_j$. Conversely an embedding of a factor gives the two required embeddings. Thus $\uparrow F\cap\uparrow G=\bigcup_j\uparrow H_j$. Distribute over the finite lists and remove redundant generators.

**Corollary (smooth systems with infinitely many minimal accepting fields).** Over $\mathbb Q$, there is an affine hypersurface smooth over $\mathbb Z$, with generic fiber a nonempty principal open in affine space, whose integral profile contains every imaginary quadratic field and no $\mathbb Q$ or real quadratic points over their rings of integers. Its full profile has infinitely many minimal generators and cannot be the full profile of a finite étale test.

Indeed the system $1+t^2+u_1^2+\cdots+u_4^2=0$ already has the stated degree-at-most-two integral points. Over $E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$, take $t=\sqrt{-d}$ for $d$ positive squarefree and represent $d-1$ as four integer squares. A real embedding forbids any solution. Apply [effective integral trace smoothing](ArithmeticProfiles.md#1-effective-integral-trace-smoothing) at degree cutoff two. Every imaginary quadratic is an accepting field and has no accepting proper $K$-subfield. Thus the full profile has infinitely many different minimal elements. This separates ordinary affine arithmetic from the finite-generator profile algebra even in the simplest positive-degree range.

## 3. Locally soluble tests with any exact entry degree

For a nonempty integral profile $U\subseteq\mathcal E_K$, its **entry degree** is
$$
 m(U)=\min\{[E:K]:E\in U\}.
$$
A **protected field** is a specified finite extension $M/K$ over which the new system should have no point.

**Theorem 4 (uniform calibration).** For any number field $K$, integer $d\ge2$, and protected field $M/K$, there is an effectively constructible monic squarefree polynomial over $\mathcal O_K$ with all irreducible factors of degree exactly $d$, with a root in every completion of $K$, and with no root in $M$. Its roots at finite places can be taken integral. In particular the integral profile has entry degree $d$ and avoids all extensions that embed in $M$. There is also a hypersurface smooth over $\mathcal O_K$, whose generic fiber is a principal open of $\mathbb A^2_K$, with that same entry degree and avoidance and integral local points everywhere. All the constructions are effective uniformly in the three inputs.

**Proof.** Factor $d=\prod_\ell\ell^{a_\ell}$. For each $\ell$ choose $a_\ell+2$ distinct rational primes congruent to $1$ modulo $2\ell$, avoiding all primes ramified in $M/\mathbb Q$, and use distinct primes across all the factors. The selection is effective by trial enumeration and Dirichlet's theorem. At each chosen conductor $p$, the cyclotomic field has a unique cyclic subfield of degree $\ell$, totally ramified at $p$ and unramified at all other finite primes. The subfield is real because the degree-$\ell$ quotient kills complex conjugation when $2\ell\mid p-1$. The Chinese remainder description of distinct prime-conductor cyclotomic fields shows that their cyclic subfields have a compositum $B/\mathbb Q$ with group
$$
 G=\prod_\ell \mathbb F_\ell^{a_\ell+2}
$$
under addition. The inertia groups of the different conductor primes are the coordinate factors and generate $G$. Since $M$ is unramified at each of those primes, $B\cap M=\mathbb Q$. Indeed, any subfield of $B$ unramified at all of them is fixed by their inertia groups and so equals $\mathbb Q$. Consequently $BK/K$ has Galois group $G$ and $BK\cap M=K$. The latter also follows by computing $[BM:M]=[B: \mathbb Q]=[BK:K]$.

At every finite place, at most one of these cyclotomic factors ramifies. Its inertia contributes dimension at most one to a single $\ell$-primary group. The quotient of the full decomposition group by inertia is cyclic. In each $\ell$-primary part the decomposition group therefore has dimension at most two. This remains true after base change to $K$, since a local decomposition group over $K$ maps to a subgroup of the one over $\mathbb Q$. At an infinite place the group is trivial since $B$ is totally real. It follows that every decomposition group is contained in some subgroup $H=\prod_\ell H_\ell$, where $H_\ell$ is a two-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb F_\ell^{a_\ell+2}$.

For each such $H$, let $F_H=(BK)^H$, of degree $d$ over $K$. Choose an integral primitive element of $F_H$. Its monic minimal polynomial lies in $\mathcal O_K[T]$, and different $H$ yield distinct factors since the subfields are normal and distinct. Multiply these polynomials. For each place, choose $H$ containing its decomposition group. Then the completion embedding of $BK$ sends $F_H$ into $K_v$, so the polynomial has a root there. At a finite place integrality follows from monicity. Conversely every field containing a root contains a $K$-embedding of one $F_H$, hence has degree divisible by $d$, and cannot be $M$. The field $F_H$ itself has an integral root, so the entry degree is exactly $d$.

All the finite fields, fixed fields and primitive polynomials in the proof can be constructed with exact number-field operations. For the smooth model apply [effective integral trace smoothing](ArithmeticProfiles.md#1-effective-integral-trace-smoothing) to this one-variable equation with cutoff $\max(d,[M:K])$. This retains the first accepting degree and forbids roots over $M$ and all its subfields, as required. It also gives the stated smoothness, dimension and local points.

The polynomial antecedent is the decomposition-group construction of intersective polynomials. The claim here is precise uniform calibration of entry degree, avoidance of an arbitrary protected field, and passage to smooth integral models with that calibration. [Sonn, Two remarks on the inverse Galois problem for intersective polynomials](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.680.pdf)

**Corollary (degree and tower position are independent).** If $M_0\subset M_1\subset\cdots$ is any explicit cofinal tower of finite extensions of $K$, $d\ge2$ is fixed and $b(e)$ is any total computable function, there is a computable family of everywhere locally soluble systems of entry degree exactly $d$ with no $\mathcal O_{M_{b(e)}}$-witness. At the finite étale level the univariate polynomial degree depends only on $d$, since the number of subgroup factors is a function of $d$. After smoothing, the dimension is always two and the degree bound may depend on the protected stage. This follows by using $M_{b(e)}$ as the protected field.

**Theorem 5 (bounded-degree uniform disequality and denominator clearing).** For each $K$ and each fixed cutoff $D\ge1$, there is a single effectively chosen monic polynomial $f\in\mathcal O_K[T]$ such that for every $[E:K]\le D$ and $x\in\mathcal O_E$,
$$
 x\ne0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists t,y\in\mathcal O_E\quad xy=f(t).
$$
Consequently every system of polynomial equations with coefficients in $K$ has an effective smooth integral model whose integral profile, over all fields of extension degree at most $D$, equals the field-point profile of the original input. The model has integral local points everywhere. The equivalence concerns existence, and its witnesses give the original field point by dividing by the specified nonzero denominator.

**Proof.** In the polynomial part of Theorem 4 take $d=D+1$. It has no roots in $E$, so if $x=0$ the right-hand side is impossible. For $x\ne0$ put $a=N_{E/K}(x)\in\mathcal O_K$. At every prime of $K$ dividing $a$, $f$ has an integral local root. Polynomial evaluation respects congruences, so the Chinese remainder theorem finds $t\in\mathcal O_K$ with $f(t)\in a\mathcal O_K$. Since $a/x\in\mathcal O_E$, take $y=f(t)/x$. For that integrality assertion use the characteristic polynomial of multiplication by $x$ over $K$, whose coefficients are integral over $\mathcal O_K$ and lie in $K$. Its constant coefficient is $\pm a$, so evaluating at $x$ expresses $a/x$ as an integral polynomial in $x$.

Clear the coefficients of the original equations to lie in $\mathcal O_K$. Write every candidate field point as $(u_i/v)$ with integral numerator and one common integral denominator. After homogenizing each equation separately, encode $v\ne0$ by the displayed formula and apply effective integral trace smoothing. Every integral solution determines the original field point, and an original point can always be lifted by a common denominator and the divisibility argument. Thus the transfer works on the entire bounded-degree family of probes at once. It also handles quantifier-free Boolean combinations of polynomial equalities and disequalities before existential quantification. For disjunctions use distinct integer branch labels in the integral domain, with unused branches annihilated by their selector polynomials.

Existential disequality over a fixed ring of integers is classical. This proof supplies one explicit uniform formula for all extensions up to a given degree by calibrating the roots of an intersective polynomial. The uniform formula combines with the smooth integral transfer in this chapter. [Denef's method as cited in Mazur–Rubin–Shlapentokh, Proposition 2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.02521#S2)

## 4. Effective convergence and a discontinuous entry invariant

Let $R_0\hookrightarrow R_1\hookrightarrow\cdots$ be a uniformly computable tower of rings with computable inclusions and write $R_\infty=\bigcup_n R_n$. For a computable family of finite equation systems with coefficients in the initial ring, let $U_n$ be the accepted inputs at stage $n$ and $U_\infty=\bigcup_n U_n$. A **computable convergence bound** is a computable $b(e)$ such that $e\in U_\infty$ implies $e\in U_{b(e)}$. This bound certifies an upper limit on the probe index needed for a witness, rather than deciding acceptance at that index.

**Theorem 6 (effective cofinal sampling).** If the existential union test $U_\infty$ is decidable, then there is a computable convergence bound and a partial computable procedure that produces a stage and an actual witness on exactly the positive inputs. For the tower of all algebraic integers obtained by any explicit cofinal sequence of number fields, the bound and witness selector are uniform in the input integer-polynomial system.

**Proof.** A witness uses finitely many coordinates, so solvability in $R_\infty$ is the union of the stage tests. First decide the union test. On a negative input return 0 as the bound. On a positive input dovetail all stage witness searches and return the stage found, together with its tuple. The procedure terminates on every positive input. For the arithmetic assertion, the union is the ring of all algebraic integers, whose first-order theory with integer parameters is decidable by van den Dries. Enumerating candidates in the finite number fields supplies the joint witness search. For instance, an explicit cofinal sequence comes from composita of splitting fields of bounded-degree, bounded-coefficient integer polynomials as the bound grows. This proof is effective and claims no efficient upper bound. [Van den Dries, Elimination theory for the ring of algebraic integers](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1988.388.189)

**Proposition 7 (every c.e. degree as a two-valued entry function).** For any c.e. language $S$ and any fixed number field $K$, there is one computable bounded-format family of hypersurfaces smooth over $\mathcal O_K$, with integral local points everywhere, whose entry function is
$$
 m(e)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&e\in S,\\
 2,&e\notin S.
 \end{cases}
$$
In particular the union answer is positive on every input, and the entry degree has exactly the Turing degree of $S$.

**Proof.** Use Koymans–Pagano and MRDP to encode $S$ by a fixed-arity integral system over $\mathcal O_K$ with parameter $e$. Take its disjoint union with the equation of an integral primitive element of one fixed quadratic extension of $K$. A two-valued integral branch selector gives one polynomial system with the same point sets up to the auxiliary selector. Over $K$ the second branch has no root. Over the designated quadratic it always has a point. Smooth at cutoff two. The resulting minimum is 1 exactly on $S$, and 2 on its complement, irrespective of the other quadratic probes. [Koymans–Pagano, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01768v3#S1.SS2) · [Effective integral trace smoothing](ArithmeticProfiles.md#1-effective-integral-trace-smoothing)

Abstractly, an increasing sequence of uniformly c.e. languages has an entry index $\mu(e)\in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}$ with c.e. lower tests $\{\mu(e)\le n\}$. Conversely every such function defines a profile on a chain by those very tests. The convergence theorem controls a stage at which every positive input has entered, depending computably on the input. The two-valued example shows that determining which earlier probe first accepts may still compute any c.e. language.

## 5. Questions for the infinite theory

1. **Uniform integer tests over all degree-$D$ fields.** For each fixed $K,D$, is $\mathcal O_K$ existentially definable by one finite system throughout all $\mathcal O_E$ with $[E:K]\le D$? For $D=2$ over $\mathbb Q$, the arithmetic chapter gives a guarded uniform predicate over every quadratic probe. Extending the definition to include the rational probe is open here. A single formula working without a degree cutoff would contradict decidability over the union of all algebraic integers. [Arithmetic chapter](ArithmeticProfiles.md)
2. **A universal explicit tower.** Does some one explicitly computable tower of finite extensions of $\mathbb Q$ realize every increasing c.e. profile with decidable union by integral points of one computable family per profile? What changes if one asks for bounded-format smooth models? Theorem 1 proves a universal result when the probes themselves may have infinite degree. Theorem 6 supplies convergence bounds on an explicit cofinal tower. Neither gives the asserted finite-stage universality.
3. **Profile operations at infinite support.** Which upward sets of finite-extension types lie in the effective closure of the finite-generator algebra of Theorem 3, and which can be realized by one bounded-format smooth model? Classify the obstructions beyond computable convergence of the union.
4. **Complexity of first witnesses.** For input polynomials of bounded dimension and degree that are integrally locally soluble, how large can the algebraic degree, height and cofinal entry stage of the earliest witness be? The questions about degree and tower index are different by the calibration theorem.

The theorems are written proofs. The comparison with classical field coding, intersective polynomials and decidability of algebraic integers is part of the scope. Priority for the simultaneous formulations and their new geometric combinations is provisional.
