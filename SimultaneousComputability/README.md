# Simultaneous computability

Simultaneous computability takes a family of tests on one input and studies reductions that preserve the entire pattern of answers. It also studies witnesses that can be transported compatibly between tests. The same input map must serve all probes.

A **probe** is an object in a fixed category $\mathcal P$. A **witness system** assigns a covariant functor $W_e\colon\mathcal P\to\mathbf{Set}$ to each input $e$. Its **existence profile** $A(e)$ is the set of probes with a witness. Along each arrow in $\mathcal P$, a witness transports forward, so $A(e)$ is an upset in the reachability preorder. A **joint many-one reduction** $A\le_m^\mathcal P B$ is a total computable $f$ with $A(e)=B(f(e))$ on every input. The corresponding **witness reduction** also supplies uniformly computable natural maps $W_e\to V_{f(e)}$.

For field probes the arrows are embeddings over a fixed base, and witnesses are field or integral points of a fixed-format family of polynomial systems. The **integral trace** records the full integral tuples on the chosen probe rings. It retains their canonical transports. The **entry degree** is the least relative field degree with an integral witness, or $\infty$ if no such field exists. In an increasing tower the **entry stage** records the first accepting probe in that tower. A **progressive format** specifies a state poset and the output attached to each state. Effective approximations can only move upward in the state order. A **readout** recodes the outputs, for example by restricting an existence profile to a subset of probes.

This develops the [original definition of simultaneous computability](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/7724fc270f13b7c3f174b60b16b8901c0f84433f/FourFieldProposals/SimultaneousComputability.md) and the [original integral trace result](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/7724fc270f13b7c3f174b60b16b8901c0f84433f/FourResearchPrograms/ArithmeticTraces.md).

## Results that connect the chapters

1. **Arithmetic instances of exact visibility thresholds.** On $m$ independent quadratic probes choose the source and target profiles from [Corollary 8a](Degrees.md). Realize each by the same type of bounded-format arithmetic family using the [arithmetic profile theorem](ArithmeticProfiles.md). The two families have equivalent decision degrees after retaining $j$ of the $m$ probes exactly when $j\le k$, for any prescribed $0\le k<m$. Both families have integral local points everywhere and points over every extension field on their generic fibers.
2. **Full Galois diagrams turn decision reductions into natural witness maps.** Apply [Theorem 2](WitnessCalculus.md) to the integral point diagrams of smooth models. The reduction gives an actual effective map on integral solutions, with commuting squares for all embeddings. The result uses the complete set of intermediate-field probes. The same chapter gives an obstruction on a proper probe system, even for finite arithmetic point diagrams with equal existence profiles.
3. **An exact entry degree can coexist with arbitrary delay in a tower.** The [field-entry calibration](InfiniteProbes.md) makes the least accepting degree any prescribed $d\ge2$ and makes any prescribed finite extension reject, with local integral solvability at every place. The construction therefore separates the least possible field degree from the stage at which a particular explicit tower finds a witness.
4. **Witness structure persists under arithmetic smoothing.** The [integral trace theorem](ArithmeticProfiles.md#1-effective-integral-trace-smoothing) preserves tuples and their embedding transports simultaneously. Applying it after the orbit construction gives exact finite witness diagrams and, by the uniform realization theorem, c.e. witness systems as retracts in bounded format. The witness chapter also gives a section model for the same diagrams using graph covers.

**Corollary (many joint degrees in one readout class).** For $1\le k<m$, the joint many-one order on any $m$-probe poset contains an order-embedded Boolean lattice on $\binom{m}{k+1}$ generators in one exact-range sector. Every profile in the lattice has the same joint degree after any recoding with at most $k+1$ output colors, including restriction to at most $k$ probes. All these profiles can be realized arithmetically on $m$ quadratic probes.

*Proof.* In the simplicial-complex construction of [the degree chapter](Degrees.md), include every leaf set of size at most $k$. For each collection of $(k+1)$-element leaf sets, additionally include the fans indexed by that collection. The stronger comparison criterion from that construction says that a source fan is simulated exactly when a target fan contains its leaves. Thus the joint degrees of the resulting profiles are ordered exactly by inclusion of the freely added collections. Since $k\ge1$, all color ranges already agree in the common part. After any claimed recoding, take a source fan and choose one leaf for each of its recoded colors different from the recoded root. There are at most $k$ representatives. The fan on those representatives occurs in the common part, and it simulates the source fan under the recoding. This gives equivalence to the common part after each such recoding. Arithmetic realization is the same as in item 1 above. $\square$

**Theorem (polynomial format for calibrating entry degree).** For any number field $K$, $d\ge2$, and finite extension $M/K$, the monic squarefree polynomial in [the calibration theorem](InfiniteProbes.md) can be taken of degree
$$
d\prod_{\ell^a\parallel d}(\ell^a+1)\le d^2(1+\log_2 d).
$$
In particular, the format bound is independent of the degree of the protected field $M$. When $d$ is a prime power the degree is $d(d+1)$.

*Proof.* For each $\ell^a\parallel d$, construct $2a$ cyclic fields of degree $\ell$ over $\mathbb Q$ with distinct prime conductors, all unramified in $M$. Choose conductors successively. For a new degree-$\ell$ factor, choose its prime $p$ to split completely in the compositum of all earlier cyclic fields and the splitting fields of $X^\ell-r$ for all earlier conductor primes $r$, including $\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)$. Existence follows from Chebotarev and the search can check the splitting and avoidance effectively. The degree-$\ell$ subfield of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)$ is then split at every older conductor, because each such $r$ is an $\ell$th power modulo $p$. Impose also $p\equiv1\bmod 2\ell$ using $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2\ell})$ to keep the new field totally real. The new conductor splits in every older field. At each conductor the decomposition group of the compositum therefore comes from just its one cyclic field. At every other finite place it is cyclic because it is unramified, and it is trivial at infinity.

The compositum is disjoint from $M$ as in the [calibration proof](InfiniteProbes.md). After base change to $K$ its Galois group is additively $\prod_{\ell^a\parallel d}\mathbb F_{\ell^a}^{\,2}$. Each cyclic decomposition group projects into one line over $\mathbb F_{\ell^a}$ in each factor. Products of such lines are subgroups of index $d$, and there are $\prod_{\ell^a\parallel d}(\ell^a+1)$ choices. The product of integral primitive-element polynomials for their fixed fields gives the claimed local roots, exact entry degree and avoidance over $M$. For the bound, write the distinct primes in increasing order as $\ell_i$, so $\ell_i^a\ge i+1$. Then $\prod_i(1+\ell_i^{-a})\le\prod_{i=1}^{\omega(d)}(1+1/(i+1))=(\omega(d)+2)/2\le1+\log_2 d$. $\square$

## Conjectures

The four statements below are proposed extensions. The chapters give further open questions.

1. **Full quadratic realization.** Every uniformly c.e. monotone existence profile on $\mathbb Q$ and all its quadratic extensions is realized by one smooth integral hypersurface template. The sole additional case over Theorem 8 of the [arithmetic chapter](ArithmeticProfiles.md) is a freely prescribed rational language that can be positive. It is enough to realize one many-one-complete c.e. language identically on the base and every quadratic probe. Pull it back to the desired base language and disjoin the resulting test with the independently prescribed quadratic tests. Monotonicity absorbs each positive base input at every extension.
2. **Exact c.e. Galois realization.** For each fixed finite Galois anchor $L/K$, every uniform c.e. family of computable $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$-sets is naturally isomorphic to the integral point systems of a bounded-format family of smooth, locally soluble affine hypersurfaces over $\mathcal O_K$. Theorem 4 of the [witness chapter](WitnessCalculus.md) gives a uniform section and retraction. The conjecture requires a natural bijection of the actual integral witness sets.
3. **One explicit tower for every convergent profile.** There is an explicit increasing tower of finite extensions of $\mathbb Q$ such that every increasing uniformly c.e. profile with decidable union can be realized on it by integral tests of one computable family per profile. The [infinite chapter](InfiniteProbes.md) proves universality with effective probes of potentially infinite degree and a convergence theorem for explicit towers.
4. **Quadratic format for exact entry.** There is a constant $C$ such that for every $d\ge2$, every number field $K$ and finite $M/K$, a monic squarefree polynomial of degree at most $Cd^2$, all of whose irreducible factors have degree $d$, has an integral local root everywhere and has no root in $M$. The polynomial-format theorem above proves this for prime-power $d$ and gives a logarithmic loss for general $d$.

## Further questions

- **Higher-degree arithmetic.** Determine which effective field families admit a single Diophantine definition of rational integers, and where a parameter for the field is essential. Seek a positive classification on all fields of degree at most three.
- **Uniform finite-fold witnesses.** Can the arithmetic realization be arranged to give finitely many auxiliary tuples above every visible witness over every probe?
- **Comparison beyond finite states.** Find effective hypotheses on infinite formats under which every joint reduction has an effective causal realization. Determine how much of finite-state comparison survives without a computable finite upward cone.
- **Joint degree structure.** Determine which exact-range sectors have meets and which finite orders can be embedded into the fiber of a fixed collection of readout degrees.
- **Quantitative entry and format.** Bound the smallest degree and dimension for exact integral smoothing and for local-solubility calibration, and determine when an entry-degree constraint permits an effective bound on tower position.

The chapters give written proofs and record related work and priority limits. The [Lean file](IntegralNormSamples.lean) independently checks the interpolation lemma used in bounded-degree norm smoothing. Its statement concerns a polynomial of degree at most $n$ taking the value $1$ at $n+1$ integer test points. The complete arithmetic and computability results have written proofs.
