# Joint degrees and finite-state formats

The existence profile of one simultaneous problem can be strictly harder than every individual test makes it appear. This chapter gives an exact calculus for universal degrees of finite-state formats, then shows that even all proper coarsenings can miss an entire joint degree distinction.

## 1. The order of joint existence profiles

Let $P$ be a nonempty finite poset of probes, and let $L=\operatorname{Up}(P)$, ordered by inclusion. A profile $A\colon\mathbb N\to L$ is computably enumerable when $\{(n,p):p\in A(n)\}$ is computably enumerable. Write $A\leq_{\mathrm{sim}}B$ if $A=B\circ f$ for one total computable $f$. This is decision reducibility. Witness-preserving reductions require the additional naturality conditions of the founding definition. Mutual reducibility defines a joint decision degree.

A progressive approximation in a finite poset is a total computable sequence $n,s\mapsto x_s(n)$ which is nondecreasing in $s$. The initial state may depend computably on the input. Every such sequence stabilizes on every input. Progressive approximations in $L$ characterize c.e. profiles: use the upward closure of the probes enumerated so far, or enumerate memberships of successive labels.

For $A$ a profile, write $\mathcal R(A)=\{A(n):n\in\mathbb N\}$. This is an ordinary set of colors. Its finite cardinality does not supply a uniform algorithm to recover it from an arbitrary index.

**Theorem 1 (joins and the computable part).** Tagged disjoint union $A\oplus B$ is the least upper bound of the two degrees. Computable profiles are ordered precisely by inclusion of their nonempty color ranges. For any computable $B$ and arbitrary $A$, a meet exists if $\mathcal R(A)\cap\mathcal R(B)\ne\varnothing$, and is the computable degree with that intersection as range. If the intersection is empty, there is no common lower bound.

The two tag injections reduce each summand to the union, and two reductions to a common upper bound combine by a tag test. A reduction preserves colors. In the computable case a finite table of representatives in the target proves sufficiency, and the table need not be uniformly found from an arbitrary index. Any profile below a computable profile is computable, so the meet statement follows by applying the classification of computable profiles to all common lower bounds.

**Theorem 2 (exact range sectors).** For every nonempty $C\subseteq L$, there is a universal c.e. profile $U_C$ of range exactly $C$ for all c.e. profiles with range contained in $C$. The least degree with range exactly $C$ is the computable degree with that range. All profiles in the sector are computable exactly when $C$ is an antichain. Connected components of the comparability graph of $C$ split the exact-range degree order as a product of the exact-range degree orders of the components.

To normalize a profile with final color in $C$, run its enumeration on each input until some element of $C$ is contained in the current upset. This waiting is a total computable operation on the input because the final color will eventually be fully enumerated. Starting with the first discovered allowed color, update to any larger allowed color contained in the observed upset whenever one is eligible. At a finite stage the actual final color is eligible, and the process stabilizes exactly there. Thus every such profile has a progressive approximation with states lying in $C$. The universal format construction in Theorem 4, applied to the state poset $C$ with identity labeling, gives $U_C$. The least degree assertion follows from the finite table of representatives.

An antichain has no strict progressive moves, so its profiles are computable. If $c<d$ are allowed, start at $c$ and move to $d$ precisely when the given input belongs to a fixed noncomputable c.e. set. Pad with constant columns for any remaining allowed colors to get an exact-range noncomputable profile. To split a general profile, compute its normalized initial state and hence the comparability component of the final state. The corresponding domains are decidable and nonempty. Present each restriction on $\mathbb N$ by a computable enumeration of its domain with computable choice of an index for any domain element. For a finite domain use repetitions. Every reduction takes each component to itself. Conversely, assemble component reductions by the decidable component tag and return the selected representative in the target domain. This proves the product decomposition.

**Proposition 3 (a sufficient synchronization criterion for meets).** If the set
$$
\Gamma(A,B)=\{(i,j):A(i)=B(j)\}
$$
is nonempty and c.e., then $A$ and $B$ have a meet. Present it by any total computable surjection $n\mapsto (i_n,j_n)$ onto $\Gamma(A,B)$, with common color $A(i_n)=B(j_n)$. The coordinate maps give the two reductions. Any other common lower bound comes with a computable map to matching pairs, and searching the enumeration for those pairs reduces it to the displayed one.

Without the c.e. hypothesis, the same argument gives an exact formulation of what a meet requires. There must be one total computable sequence of matching pairs whose induced label profile dominates the induced label profile of every other such sequence. If a meet exists, take its two reductions to obtain the sequence. This criterion does not decide existence of the sequence in general.

## 2. Finite-state formats and an exact comparison algorithm

A **finite-state format** consists of a nonempty finite poset $C$ of states and a labeling $\lambda\colon C\to Q$ into a finite alphabet. Its profiles are the final labels of progressive $C$-approximations. The labels need not themselves increase. Monotone labels into $L$ give c.e. existence profiles, and arbitrary labels allow one to analyze observations that combine affirmative and negative answers.

**Theorem 4 (universal profile of a format).** Every finite-state format has a universal profile within its format. It can be constructed uniformly from the finite poset and labels, and its exact color range is the image of the labeling.

Allow inputs $(c,e,n)$, where $c$ is a valid initial state and $e$ is a program index. Simulate the successive outputs of program $e$ on $(n,0),(n,1),\ldots$, each with a bounded amount of work per construction stage. Start at $c$. If an output is invalid, fails the monotonicity check or never arrives, hold the last accepted state or halt the updates altogether. This is a progressive approximation even on faulty programs. For a genuine progressive approximation, use its program index and compute its initial state as part of the total reducing function. Every state also occurs by a constant simulation. The family of profiles of a format is precisely the set reducible to its universal profile, since one can precompose a progressive approximation with a total computable reduction.

Given formats $(C,\lambda)$ and $(D,\nu)$ into the same alphabet $Q$, define subsets $V(c)\subseteq D$ by downward induction on the finite state order:
$$
V(c)=\left\{d\in D:
\lambda(c)=\nu(d)\ \text{and}\
(\forall c'>c)(\exists d'\ge d)\ d'\in V(c')
\right\}.
$$

**Theorem 5 (finite-state comparison and uniformization).** The following are equivalent.

1. Every final-label profile of $(C,\lambda)$ admits some progressive $(D,\nu)$-presentation with exactly those final labels.
2. The universal profile of $(C,\lambda)$ reduces to the universal profile of $(D,\nu)$.
3. Every $V(c)$ is nonempty.
4. There is one causal procedure which turns every progressive $C$-approximation into a progressive $D$-approximation with matching labels at every stage.

Theorem 4 gives the first equivalence. If every $V(c)$ is nonempty, choose a viable target state initially. Each strict source move has a matching target extension in its viability set. Pick it from the finite table and keep states fixed in idle stages. This proves both statements about lifting.

For necessity, fix $c_0$ with $V(c_0)=\varnothing$. Build a single progressive $C$-approximation $B$ that defeats all candidate $D$-approximation programs. On input $e$, start in $c_0$ and simulate the successive outputs of candidate $e$ on this very input. When a candidate value $d$ with $\nu(d)=\lambda(c)$ is seen at the current source state $c$, keep the invariant $V(c)\cap\uparrow d_0=\varnothing$, where $d_0$ was the last matched target state, or initially allow all of $D$. A valid candidate is progressive, so $d\ge d_0$ when $d_0$ exists. Consequently $d\notin V(c)$. By the recurrence there is a strict $c'>c$ with $V(c')\cap\uparrow d=\varnothing$. Move to such a $c'$, record $d_0=d$, and resume the search on successive outputs. Partial or invalid candidates can be ignored by using bounded dovetailing and stopping their local updates when an order violation is detected. There are only finitely many strict source moves, so $B$ is total as a stage approximation and has a well-defined final label on every input. A valid candidate presenting $B$ would eventually match the ultimate label on input $e$. The construction would then make another strict move. This contradicts stabilization. Thus $B$ has no $D$-presentation.

This also proves that an existential, separately chosen lift for each profile automatically upgrades to one stage-by-stage lift for all profiles. On a finite order the test itself is decidable by the displayed recursion.

**Corollary 6 (lifting along a readout).** For a map $h:D\to C$ of finite state sets, every progressive $C$-profile has a $D$-lift with $h$ of the final state equal to the desired color precisely when Theorem 5 succeeds for the identity labels on $C$ and labels $h$ on $D$. An order-preserving section of $h$ is sufficient but unnecessary. For example, lift a diamond by replacing its common top by two incomparable copies, one above each middle branch in a rooted tree. Every path has a causal lift, but an order section would require one target top above both branches.

**Proposition 7 (which readouts preserve positive enumerability).** Suppose $h:C\to D$ is a map of finite posets and a $D$-profile is called progressive when it has a progressive approximation. The image of every progressive $C$-profile under $h$ is progressive exactly when $h$ is order-preserving. Sufficiency uses composition on stages. If $c<c'$ but $h(c)\nleq h(c')$, switch a profile from $c$ to $c'$ on membership in the halting set. For an upward-closed predicate on $D$ containing $h(c)$ but excluding $h(c')$, the readout is the complement of the halting set. An upward-closed readout of a progressive profile is c.e., contradiction.

**Corollary 7a (effective finite upward cones).** The comparison and causal uniformization theorem extends to computably presented, possibly infinite state posets where an algorithm lists the entire upward cone of each state as a finite set. The output alphabet may be effectively infinite, provided equality of labels is decidable. In this setting testing $d\in V(c)$ is decidable. The full comparison is equivalent to nonemptiness of every $V(c)$, even when that global condition is not decidable.

Every state trajectory is confined to the finite upward cone of its initial state. To test a pair $(c,d)$, evaluate the recursion on the two finite cones $\uparrow c$ and $\uparrow d$. Pruning to these cones is exact because every subsequent source move and target response stays within them. If every $V(c)$ is nonempty, enumerate target states to find an initial viable state, then choose subsequent viable extensions inside the listed finite cones. If some fixed $c_0$ has no viable initial state, the diagonal construction of Theorem 5 works from that state. After a match at $d$, it can find a losing strict move by checking the finite source cone and the finite target cone above $d$. Each source trajectory stabilizes. The universal-profile proof also works by guarded simulation in these orders, regardless of whether there is a global height bound.

Finite cones alone do not suffice without effective listings. Let $U,V$ be disjoint computably inseparable c.e. sets. In the source state poset, give each $n$ a root labeled $r_n$, with a single leaf labeled $a_n$ if $n\in U$, or $b_n$ if $n\in V$. Code each leaf by the unique first enumeration stage justifying its existence. This makes membership in the state presentation and its order decidable and gives finite cones. The target has two disjoint two-state branches for each $n$, with labels $r_n\to a_n$ and $r_n\to b_n$. All labels for different $n$ are distinct. Every source state has a viable matching target state in the set-theoretic recursion. Nevertheless the progressive source profile which starts at $r_n$ and moves to its leaf if found has no computable target presentation. The initial target state must already be in one of the two branches for $n$, since the final label identifies $n$ and branches are disjoint. The initial branch would give a computable separator of $U,V$. Thus this pointwise viability data does not suffice for a computable lift in the general infinite-alphabet case.


## 3. Proper observations can miss the joint degree

**Theorem 8 (sharp loss from coarsening).** For each $m\ge2$ there are two profiles with exactly the same $m+1$ colors and strictly different joint degrees, whose many-one degrees become identical under every recoding with at most $m$ output colors. They can be taken to be c.e. profiles on any $m$-element probe poset. In that realization, restriction to any proper subset of probes makes their degrees equal.

Use one format with a root labeled $0$ and $m$ incomparable leaves labeled $1,\ldots,m$. Its competitor is the disjoint union of the $m$ formats obtained by omitting one leaf, leaving the same root label in each component. Take the two universal profiles. The second reduces to the first by using the relevant subfan. The converse fails by Theorem 5: from the root of the full fan one must start in a root copy, and that copy cannot meet one of the required leaves. Under any recoding with at most $m$ labels, two labels have merged. Some leaf label can be omitted without losing any non-root recoded color: omit a leaf merged with the root, or either of two leaves merged with one another. In that target component, track the source root by the recoded root and send every leaf to any matching state above it. This proves the recoded comparison both ways, including cases in which a leaf uses the root state.

For realization on $P$, choose a maximal chain of upsets $\varnothing=U_0\subsetneq U_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq U_m=P$, adding one probe at each step. Regard these as labels $0,\ldots,m$. The state maps in the two formats respect the upset order, so the universal profiles are c.e. On any proper set of probes, at least one pair of adjacent labels is identified because one added probe is absent. Apply the coarsening assertion.

**Corollary 8a (exact visibility thresholds).** In the full-fan construction, fix $0\le k<m$ and replace the comparison target by the disjoint union of all subfans with $k$ leaves. After arbitrary recoding, the full-fan profile reduces to the union exactly when there are at most $k$ distinct recoded leaf labels different from the recoded root label. For the chain-of-upsets realization on $m$ probes, a restriction to $j$ probes makes the two degrees equal exactly when $j\le k$. Thus for every $1\le r\le m$, there is a strict gap that appears exactly at probe arity $r$, by setting $k=r-1$.

For sufficiency, select one leaf for each distinct non-root recoded color, complete the selected set to $k$ leaves, and start at the root of that component. Every source leaf can then be matched, either at a target leaf of its color or at the root. For necessity, if there are non-root colors to realize, the source root must start in a target root, since a terminal target state cannot realize any subsequent color different from its own. It then has at most $k$ distinct non-root colors available. Restricting a maximal chain of upsets to $j$ probes leaves exactly $j$ distinct non-root colors. The other comparison always holds by the branching inclusions.

More generally a finite collection of allowed leaf sets $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ yields the disjoint union of the corresponding fans. Its universal degree is ordered exactly by inclusion of the downward closures of those leaf-set collections. Each source fan must initialize at a target root capable of all its leaf colors, and can then simulate all its choices. Hence these universal degrees realize the inclusion lattice of nonempty abstract simplicial complexes on the labeled leaves, allowing omitted vertices and the complex containing only the empty face. The empty family of fans supplies a formal bottom.


**Theorem 9 (many complete marginals, many joint degrees).** For every finite probe poset $P$ with at least two probes, the nontrivial c.e. many-one degrees embed into one exact three-color sector in which *every* individual probe language is many-one complete among the c.e. sets.

Choose $\varnothing<U<P$, and fix a many-one complete c.e. halting set $K$ with nonempty complement. For each nonempty proper c.e. set $S$, on inputs $2e$ output $P$ when $e\in K$ and $\varnothing$ otherwise. On inputs $2e+1$, output $U$ when $e\in S$ and $\varnothing$ otherwise. Denote this profile by $A_S$. Every coordinate is c.e. and complete by the even inputs. A reduction $S\leq_m T$ induces $A_S\leq_{\mathrm{sim}}A_T$ by the identity on even inputs and the given reduction on odd inputs. Conversely, the $U$-fiber of $A_S$ reduces to the $U$-fiber of $A_T$ under any joint reduction. Read the output of the reduction on input $2e+1$. For an odd output use its decoded index, and for an even output use a fixed index outside $T$. This is an ordinary many-one reduction $S\leq_m T$. Hence the asserted embedding is exact. On the arithmetic realization probes of the founding theorem, both Theorem 8 and Theorem 9 therefore also hold for decision profiles of bounded-format smooth hypersurfaces with integral local points everywhere. These conclusions concern decisions and do not assert that the reductions transport arithmetic witnesses.

## 4. Exact Boolean readout levels

For a Boolean function $g:L\to\{0,1\}$, let $d(g)$ be the maximum number of value changes along an increasing chain starting at $\varnothing$. The chain may stop anywhere. Say a set is $d$-c.e. starting at bit $b$ if its characteristic function has a uniformly computable approximation starting at $b$ and changing at most $d$ times per input. This is the usual finite Ershov convention for $b=0$ and its dual for $b=1$.

**Theorem 10 (oscillation law).** The sets $g\circ A$ as $A$ varies over the c.e. profiles are exactly the $d(g)$-c.e. sets starting at $g(\varnothing)$. For one universal c.e. profile $U_L$, every Boolean readout $g\circ U_L$ is complete at its own level under ordinary many-one reductions, simultaneously for all $g$.

Use a membership enumeration starting at $\varnothing$. The state changes form an increasing chain, so its Boolean values change at most $d(g)$ times. For the converse, choose a maximizing chain and discard intermediate vertices with unchanged readout. Keep $\varnothing$ as the first vertex. This gives a chain with $d(g)$ alternating changes. Advance one position each time the given Boolean approximant changes. The resulting progressive upset approximation realizes the desired set as its readout. Completeness follows because each realizing profile is reducible to $U_L$, and composing with $g$ preserves that reduction. In particular, classical strictness of the finite Ershov hierarchy gives the sharp uniform bound in each fixed starting-bit convention.

## 5. A finite lattice of universal monotone-format degrees

**Theorem 11 (finite forest normal form).** For fixed finite output poset $L$, the universal degrees of all finite-state formats with monotone labels into $L$ form a finite distributive lattice after adjoining a formal bottom. More precisely, let $\mathcal T_L$ be the finite poset of distinct universal degrees of finite rooted trees with monotone labels into $L$. The format degrees are exactly the nonempty downsets of $\mathcal T_L$, ordered by inclusion. The formal bottom corresponds to the empty downset.

First unfold a format into a disjoint forest whose vertices are nonempty strictly increasing sequences of states, with an edge for appending a state and with label equal to that of the last state. Sending a sequence to its last state maintains all labels. In the opposite direction, follow the path of successive strict changes as they occur, starting at the one-term sequence. The two causal simulations give equivalence by Theorem 5. Next contract any edge whose endpoints have the same label. The retraction of the child to its parent is label-preserving and order-preserving because descendant order in the tree is ancestry. Surviving vertices retain their mutual ancestry, so inclusion gives the converse. Merge duplicate children with isomorphic rooted labeled subtrees in the same way. Along each remaining branch the labels strictly increase. Recursively, there are finitely many rooted tree types: at each label a tree is specified by a subset of the already available types with strictly greater root labels.

A rooted tree degree is join-prime among the format degrees. In a causal simulation into a disjoint union of two forests, choose the target initial state for the source root. Since every source state is reachable from that root, every subsequent simulated state lies in its chosen target component. Equivalently the root's viability test can be repeated within the one component with the same result. This gives a comparison with one summand alone. Conversely, a comparison with either summand suffices. The universal format of a forest is the join of its component formats, by the corresponding tagging construction. Therefore its degree is determined exactly by the downset of rooted tree degrees which lie below it. Every nonempty downset is obtained by taking the disjoint union of its maximal representatives. These assertions prove the lattice representation.

There is a direct finite procedure to compute this lattice. Generate rooted tree types by considering labels from greatest to least and choosing any subset of available greater-labeled child types. At a pair of tree nodes, a simulation begins exactly when their labels agree and every source child can be matched to a target descendant, including the current node, from which the simulation continues. This check only needs immediate children because deeper source nodes can be handled by successive simulation. To compare universal rooted degrees, permit any initial target vertex. Quotient the resulting preorder and enumerate its downsets. For chains of one, two and three colors this gives respectively $1,4,20$ nonempty downsets. For the four-element diamond it gives $157$ nonempty downsets. The intermediate rooted-tree degree counts are $1,3,8,18$, respectively. These are finite-format counts. The entire c.e. profile universe is much larger, as seen already in Theorem 9.

## 6. The witness interface

A useful comparison with the witness theory is available without changing the decision degrees. For any uniformly c.e. existence profile define its canonical witness system by using the single first-entry stage as witness at a positive probe, with no witnesses otherwise. It is understood that one has chosen an enumeration. A transport between two positive probes computes the target first-entry stage by search, and the maps are natural since each nonempty witness set is a singleton.

**Proposition 12 (decision reflection and a lifting boundary).** Every decision reduction from the existence profile of $W$ to the existence profile of $V$ lifts to a natural witness reduction from $W$ to the canonical witness system of $V$. In particular the canonical systems present exactly the decision degrees. Over a finite discrete probe poset every decision reduction lifts to the actual target witness system. For any finite probe poset with a strict comparison, passage to existence profiles can lose information even for the identity map on inputs and two systems with the same existence profile at every input.

For the first assertion, given any valid source witness, find the first-entry stage at the mapped input and probe. Commutation of the square follows from uniqueness. On a discrete poset, instead search the target's actual enumeration for any witness at that probe, with no nonidentity naturality constraints.

For the negative assertion on a two-element chain $p<q$, choose disjoint computably inseparable c.e. sets $U,V$. Give $q$ witness set $\{0,1\}$ on every input. At $p$ there is one witness exactly on $U\cup V$, with image $0$ for inputs in $U$ and $1$ for those in $V$. An input-preserving natural map from the canonical system would pick $0$ or $1$ computably at $q$ on every input, and the arrow forces it to separate the two sets. Conversely any computable separator defines such a natural map. Thus the existence-profile identity has no input-preserving lift, although every fixed input admits a natural choice. On any finite $P$ with $p<q$, the monotone map to the two-element chain that sends $\uparrow q$ to $1$ and the other probes to $0$ is a retraction on the selected chain. Pull back both systems along it and restrict any hypothetical lift back to $p<q$. This proves the claimed boundary. The example concerns lifting the identity and makes no claim about reductions allowed to change its inputs.

## 7. Questions and scope

1. **Meet problem.** In which exact-range sectors do every pair of joint degrees have a greatest lower bound? In a proposed negative example with equal full range, can one rule out a cofinal computable synchronization sequence even though there are computable lower bounds?
2. **Lifting under structural constraints.** Decide which finite-state simulations can be realized by computable maps on arithmetic witness tuples between the smooth hypersurface realizations. The decision simulation in Theorem 5 and a witness simulation can differ, by Proposition 12. Is there a single arithmetic realization chosen for all states of a given finite format so that the two comparisons coincide on that family?
3. **State complexity.** Given two monotone labeled finite-state formats, the viability algorithm decides their comparison. Determine the least number of states or branches of a format in a given equivalence class. Compare the smallest unconstrained format, its rooted-tree normal form, and its degree-theoretic ideal presentation.
4. **Effective infinite formats.** What finite-state phenomena persist for computably presented well-founded state posets in which progressive trajectories may have arbitrarily many finite changes? Can causal uniformization hold under weaker effective boundedness hypotheses than Corollary 7a, especially for a fixed finite alphabet? When does the labeled forest order require ordinal information rather than finite viability?
5. **Beyond all small readouts.** Theorem 8 gives one strict pair invisible under every proper color identification. Can one realize antichains or whole finite lattices of strict joint degrees within a fixed collection of recoded degrees, with the number of probes modest relative to the size of the desired order?

These are written arguments. The construction uses established many-one reducibility, finite Ershov approximations and classical computable inseparability. The finite-state comparison method is closely related to labeled forest methods in the fine hierarchies for partitions. The principal claims here are the exact comparison and uniformization statement for finite progressive formats, its joint coarsening obstruction, and the degree-embedding results in the probe setting. Priority of these specific formulations is provisional.
