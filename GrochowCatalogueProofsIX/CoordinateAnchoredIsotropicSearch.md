# GP-0245 — Isotropic search with hidden coordinate-edge anchors

Let $V=\mathbb F_q^n$. The input is a family of alternating forms on $V$ and a marked subfamily of nonzero rank-two forms. Suppose the marked forms, up to nonzero scalars, are distinct wedges of pairs from a single basis of $V^*$. The basis and edge labels need not be supplied. Recognize this promise in deterministic bit-polynomial time and recover one such coordinate system and graph $G$. If $\alpha$ is its independence number, one can construct a maximum-dimensional common totally isotropic subspace for the entire input in $q^{\alpha^2/6+O(n)}\operatorname{poly}(n,m,\log q)$ bit time, with $m$ the number of forms. The extra forms may be arbitrary. This improves the quadratic exponent of the unrestricted $q^{n^2/6+O(n)}$ bound whenever $\alpha\leq (1-\delta)n$ for some fixed $\delta>0$.

## Recover the hidden coordinates

View a marked form as a map $V\to V^*$. Its image $H_e$ is a two-dimensional subspace. Under the promise $H_e$ is the span of the two vertex lines of an edge. Reject duplicate planes under the distinct-edge convention. Compute and deduplicate all nonzero pairwise intersections of distinct $H_e$. These are one-dimensional and recover precisely the vertex lines of degree at least two under the promise. Require these lines to be independent and each $H_e$ to contain at most two of them.

For a plane containing two recovered lines use those endpoints. For a plane containing just one choose any other line in the plane. For a plane containing none choose any two independent lines in the plane. Check that the resulting distinct endpoint lines are globally independent. Complete representatives of the endpoint lines to a basis of $V^*$, treating the additional coordinates as isolated vertices.

If the promise holds, the last independence test cannot fail. In a plane with exactly one known vertex, the other vertex is a leaf and the new line has a nonzero component on that leaf. In a plane with no known vertex, both vertices are leaves of an isolated edge, and the new lines give a basis of its two-dimensional coordinate plane. Each leaf occurs once, so all added lines are independent modulo the known vertex lines. Conversely, success exhibits the desired basis and an edge for each marked form. A rank-two alternating form whose image is $\langle\ell_i,\ell_j\rangle$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of $\ell_i\wedge\ell_j$. The tests use linear algebra over the explicit field and apply in characteristic two.

## Cover all common isotropic subspaces by smaller spaces

Let $U$ be isotropic for all marked forms. Let $S$ be the set of coordinates $\ell_i$ with nonzero restriction to $U$. An edge on the active vertices forces its endpoint restrictions to be proportional, since their wedge vanishes on $U$. On each connected component $C$ of $G[S]$, the restrictions are therefore multiples of a single nonzero functional on $U$. Choose a root vertex and normalize the multiple there to one. In the basis of $V$ dual to the recovered coordinates, let $v_C$ be the vector supported on $C$ formed by these nonzero multiples. Then $U\subseteq\langle v_C:C\text{ a component of }G[S]\rangle$. Conversely each such component span is isotropic for the marked forms. An edge inside a component sees proportional coordinate restrictions, and any other edge has an inactive endpoint. One vertex per component is an independent set of $G$, so the component span has dimension at most $\alpha$.

Enumerate all $S$ and all normalized vectors for their components. For fixed $S$ there are $(q-1)^{|S|-c(G[S])}$ choices, so the total number is at most $\sum_S q^{|S|}=(q+1)^n$. On each span restrict the remaining forms and solve the exact maximum isotropic problem. Return a largest result. The covering argument ensures a global optimum is seen, and the converse ensures every candidate returned is feasible for the entire input.

## Residual solver and running time

The $q^{d^2/6+O(d)}$ residual solver can be obtained by the known maximal-isotropic enumeration argument. In dimension $d$, if all forms vanish return the whole space. Otherwise choose a nonzero form of rank $2r$ and divide out its radical to obtain a $2r$-dimensional symplectic space. Every isotropic subspace for the chosen form lies in the preimage of a Lagrangian. Enumerate these preimages and recurse in dimension $d-r$.

For output-polynomial enumeration choose dual Lagrangian summands $X,Y$. A Lagrangian is determined by its projection $A\leq X$, its intersection $A^\perp\leq Y$, and a symmetric map $A\to A^*$ determining its graph modulo $A^\perp$. This holds in all characteristics. For $\dim A=t$, enumerate reduced echelon forms for $A$ and all symmetric matrices in the chosen basis. The $t$-th count is bounded by an absolute constant times $q^{t(r-t)+t(t+1)/2}$, and this exponent equals $r(r+1)/2-(r-t)(r-t+1)/2$. Summing over $t$ gives an absolute constant times $q^{r(r+1)/2}$. The decrease of the potential $d^2/6+d/2$ for a step of size $r\leq d/2$ is at least $r(r+1)/2$. Adding a fixed linear term absorbs the per-node constant and a factor of two per recursion step, so the total tree size is $q^{d^2/6+O(d)}$. Apply the residual bound with $d\leq\alpha$ and multiply by $(q+1)^n$.

The graph-only isotropic correspondence and the unrestricted enumeration bound are due to [Bei, Chen, Guan, Qiao and Sun, Theorem 3 and Corollary 35](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2020.8). Here the recovered coordinates and component spans accelerate exact search with arbitrary extra forms. The stated improvement applies to inputs with the marked anchors.
