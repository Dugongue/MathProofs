# Marked epimorphisms onto semisimple abelian normal subgroups

## GP-0351

Given Cayley tables for $G,H$, normal subgroups $A\lhd G$, $B\lhd H$, and a prescribed epimorphism $\alpha:G/A\to H/B$, one can decide, count and construct epimorphisms $G\to H$ inducing $\alpha$ and mapping $A$ onto $B$ in deterministic polynomial table time, provided $B$ is elementary abelian and semisimple for the conjugation action of $Q=H/B$. The extension defining $H$ need not split, and the characteristic of $B$ may divide $|Q|$. When $|Q|$ is bounded, this also gives polynomial time for arbitrary quotient maps, including an exact total count.

The semisimplicity promise is recognizable in Cayley-table time. Find each cyclic module generated by one vector of $B$ under conjugation. Such a submodule is simple exactly when every nonzero vector in it generates the same submodule. All this can be tested by enumerating the vectors in the displayed table. Add the simple submodules greedily when they are not contained in the current sum. The intersection with the current sum is then zero by simplicity. Thus the resulting direct sum is all of $B$ exactly in the semisimple case. Use linear intertwiners to identify and match isomorphic simple types, since any nonzero intertwiner of simple modules is invertible.

Choose a normalized section of $Q$ in $H$ and write its extension cocycle as $c:Q\times Q\to B$. A candidate homomorphism inducing $\alpha$, now composed with $G\to G/A$, is exactly a solution of
$$
z(gh)=z(g)+\alpha(g)z(h)+c(\alpha(g),\alpha(h)).
$$
Decompose $B$ into a direct sum of simple types $S^m$. Each coordinate of $z$ for that type has a separate affine linear system. Reject when one is inconsistent. For the remaining systems the homogeneous solution space is the same crossed-homomorphism space $Z^1(G,S)$ in all $m$ coordinates, but the offsets can differ. Restriction to $A$ gives cosets $a_j+W$ in the common space of equivariant maps $A/(A'A^p)\to S$, where $W$ is the image of homogeneous restriction. The source quotient acts through its indicated action on $S$. With $D=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb F_p Q}(S)$, this common space and $W$ are $D$-vector spaces. Restriction of $z$ is a group homomorphism on $A$ because it is the kernel of $\alpha$, and equivariance follows by conjugating its image with any lifted element.

The combined map to $S^m$ is onto precisely when its $m$ coordinate rows are linearly independent over $D$. Indeed a proper image lies in a maximal submodule of $S^m$ and is annihilated by a nonzero $D$-linear combination of its coordinate projections. Different simple types impose independent conditions. Put $r=\dim_D\langle a_1+W,\ldots,a_m+W\rangle$ in the quotient space. By an invertible row change over $D$, arrange that the first $r$ quotient rows are independent and the remaining $m-r$ quotient rows vanish. This row change gives a bijection on the tuples of permitted top restrictions, since their independent changes range over all of $W^m$. Therefore the first $r$ lifts can be arbitrary, and the others must form an independent list in $W$. The condition is exactly $\dim_D W\ge m-r$. For each passing type the number of acceptable restriction tuples is
$$
|W|^r\prod_{i=0}^{m-r-1}(|W|-|D|^i).
$$
Multiply by the kernel size of homogeneous restriction for each coordinate, then multiply over simple types. Inverse row operations and solutions of the original linear systems give a witness. The total homomorphism is onto if and only if its image on $A$ equals $B$, because the quotient map is onto. Thus these tests settle both conditions together.

All spaces can be computed over $\mathbb F_p$ using a basis of $D$ as operators, including tests of $D$-linear rank by spanning over the prime field after applying that basis. If desired the full field $D$ can be enumerated in polynomial table time, since an endomorphism of a simple module is determined by its value at any one nonzero vector and $|D|\le |S|\le |B|$. The number of bits of the answer is polynomial. For bounded $|Q|$, enumerate all quotient homomorphisms from a generating tuple of $G/A$ of length at most $\log_2|G|$ by trying its images in $Q$, verify each on the quotient table, and sum the counts over those that are onto.
