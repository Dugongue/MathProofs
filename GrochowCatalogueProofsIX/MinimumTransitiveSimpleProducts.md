# Minimum-order transitive subgroups of products of minimal simple actions

## GP-0506

Suppose an explicit permutation action is given as a Cartesian product of supplied, nontrivial minimally transitive actions of nonabelian finite simple groups. Let $N$ be the degree of the Cartesian action, and let $b$ be the largest point stabilizer order of a factor. There is a deterministic $f(b)\operatorname{poly}(N,L)$ algorithm finding the minimum order of a transitive subgroup and constructing its generators, where $L$ is the encoded input size. The factor minimality promise can be checked in the same time bound. The cost is measured in the explicit permutation degree, even when the factors are supplied separately.

A transitive subgroup projects onto every simple factor, by minimal transitivity there. A subdirect product of nonabelian simple groups is a product of full diagonal subgroups on disjoint index sets of isomorphic factors. This follows by induction from Goursat's lemma: when adjoining the last simple factor, either it is free, or a quotient of the earlier product is identified with it. Such a simple quotient uses exactly one earlier simple diagonal, since the factors commute and their images are normal. Call the index sets blocks. One diagonal on a block is transitive on the Cartesian product of the block's domains precisely when the orbit of one product point under that diagonal has the full product degree. The diagonals on disjoint blocks are independent, so their product is transitive precisely when each block passes this test. [Previous minimal-transitivity criterion](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/3454ca222e13e2f12bf232b920771763bf8a7d1a/GrochowCatalogueProofsVIII/SimpleProductMinimalTransitivity.md)

The one-index blocks always pass. If a block has at least two indices and acts transitively, its projection to any pair of factors is a transitive diagonal for that pair. If the shared simple order is $s$, this requires $|X_i||X_j|\le s$. Since $|X_i|,|X_j|\ge s/b$, it follows that $s\le b^2$. Also every feasible block has at most $\log_2 s$ indices, since the product degree is at least two to the power of the number of indices. Therefore to test any proposed multi-index block it suffices to handle simple groups of order at most $b^2$ and block size at most $2\log_2 b$. Enumerate the isomorphisms from one base factor to all the others from their two-generator image pairs and try every tuple of choices. The number of trials depends only on $b$. Test each trial directly by a single orbit in the explicit product or, equivalently, by a table enumeration of the diagonal's action on the base point. Record a successful choice when one exists. Factor tables can be constructed in $b\operatorname{poly}(N,L)$ time by their generators, using $|S_i|=|X_i||A_i|$.

Use a subset dynamic program over the $t$ factors. The cost of a feasible block is the order of its simple diagonal. For each nonempty remaining index set, choose the block containing its least index and multiply its cost by the minimum for the complement. Take the least product and record the selected block. This computes the exact minimum, by the classification of every transitive subgroup. The choices need at most $3^t$ pairs of subsets, and $2^t\le N$. Thus the subset work is polynomial in the explicit degree. Multiply back through the chosen typed diagonals to output their generators on the Cartesian domain. The previous minimal-transitivity algorithm tests each factor with the same parameter by enumerating relevant small-index coset actions. The selection is therefore constructive on a recognizable supplied factor class.
