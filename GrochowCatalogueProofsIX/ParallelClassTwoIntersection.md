# Parallel subgroup intersection in class two

## GP-0502

Fix an odd prime $p$. Given generator permutations for $G,H\le S_n$ such that $K=\langle G,H\rangle$ has class at most two and exponent dividing $p$, a deterministic logspace-uniform NC algorithm computes generators for $G\cap H$ and a product normal form. Its input is permutation arrays and it does not expand $K$. The result also works for a supplied varying odd prime $p\le n$. The trivial case covers larger primes. [Parallel permutation-group context](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/quotient.pdf)

### Explicit coordinates for elementary abelian actions

For generators of an elementary abelian permutation group, first compute all point orbits by Boolean matrix squaring in the labeled generator graph. On any one orbit its image is regular, because an abelian point stabilizer in a transitive action fixes every point. To choose a vector-space basis of this image, compute in parallel the orbits of the least point under all generator prefixes. Retain a generator precisely when the next orbit is larger. The retained generators form a basis, since each strict step increases dimension by one. For each retained generator independently, compute the orbit of the least point under all the other retained generators. For a given point $y$, its coordinate in the retained direction is the unique $a\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$ for which applying the inverse $a$th power of that generator to $y$ enters this smaller orbit. All these tests use polynomially many graph computations and parallel permutation powers. The resulting table gives explicit additive coordinates for that orbit, and the product of the orbit translations is a faithful linear encoding of the whole elementary abelian action. [Elementary abelian parallel algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1137/0216058)

### From compact permutations to one Lie coordinate space

Let $g_1,\ldots,g_m$ be the combined input generators, and form all commutators $c_{ij}=[g_i,g_j]=g_ig_jg_i^{-1}g_j^{-1}$ for $i<j$. They generate the central elementary abelian group $C=[K,K]$. Form the orbits of $C$. The induced action of $K$ on that orbit set is elementary abelian, since it kills $C$. Let $B$ be its kernel. An element of $B$ commutes with the transitive $C$-action on every $C$-orbit. Each such restricted $C$-image is regular, and a permutation commuting with a regular abelian translation group is itself a translation. Indeed its image of one point determines all its other values. Thus $B$ is elementary abelian and embeds faithfully in the product of these orbit translation coordinates, which are computed by the preceding construction.

The class-two Lazard correspondence writes $K$ as a vector space over $\mathbb F_p$ with central Lie bracket and group product $u\cdot v=u+v+\frac12[u,v]$. Introduce one formal additive coordinate for every input generator and each of the displayed commutators. This space maps linearly onto the Lie space of $K$, and the actual permutation represented by a coordinate vector $(u_i,w_{ij})$ is
$$
g_1^{u_1}\cdots g_m^{u_m}\prod_{i<j}c_{ij}^{\,w_{ij}-u_i u_j/2}.
$$
This follows by adding the pairwise central terms in the group product. It can be evaluated by balanced products and repeated powering without knowing the additive relations. [Effective Lazard correspondence](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2011.12.009)

Find the kernel $L_0$ of the linear map of formal coordinates into the translation coordinates of the action on the $C$-orbits. Evaluate the displayed word for every vector of a computed basis of $L_0$. They lie in $B$, so read their coordinates in the product of internal $C$-orbit translation spaces from their images of the chosen least points. This gives a second linear map, now defined on $L_0$. Its kernel is exactly the additive relation space of the displayed formal generators of $K$, since the translation embedding of $B$ is faithful. Taking the quotient yields a basis and coordinates of the Lie space of $K$.

Under these coordinates the Lie space of $G$ is the linear span of its supplied generators and their pairwise brackets, and likewise for $H$. Such spans are Lie subalgebras because all brackets are central. By the group product formula they give exactly the generated subgroups. Compute their intersection by linear algebra and evaluate a basis in the displayed permutation word formula. For product normal forms choose a basis of the intersection with its subspace in the centre of the ambient Lie space placed last, or simply put its own derived subspace last. The latter subspace is obtained by computing the brackets of pairs of intersection basis vectors in the formal coordinates. For an additive coordinate vector, subtract from its central final coordinates the pairwise correction of one half the bracket sum. The resulting exponents in the ordered group generators produce the element. This operation is bijective, since the correction depends only on the earlier coordinates. It gives a unique product normal form. To recover coordinates of any supplied member permutation, first solve for its induced action on the $C$-orbits, remove the corresponding word, and then solve for the residual in the translation coordinates of $B$.

All the linear steps use deterministic uniform NC linear algebra over $\mathbb F_p$. In particular independent columns can be selected by rank tests on all prefixes in parallel, independent rows then give a full minor, and solving in that minor gives coordinates and a kernel basis. Finite-field rank has a deterministic parallel algorithm using a polynomial-degree indeterminate and a characteristic polynomial. Arithmetic for a supplied $p\le n$ also has polylogarithmic-depth circuits, using repeated squaring for inverses and standard integer arithmetic. Every point graph and linear space above has polynomial size. Only a constant number of these batches are composed, with all orbit, word and rank tests inside each batch run in parallel. [Deterministic parallel matrix rank, Mulmuley](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579205)
