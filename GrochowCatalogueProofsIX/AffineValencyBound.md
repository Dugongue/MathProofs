# Sharp factorial bound for affine primitive orbital schemes

## GP-0774

Let $V$ be a nonzero finite vector space over $\mathbb F_p$, and let $H\leq \operatorname{GL}(V)$ act irreducibly. In the coherent configuration of orbitals of the affine group $V\rtimes H$, let $k$ and $K$ be the minimum and maximum nontrivial valencies. Then $K\leq k!$. For every $k\geq 2$, equality occurs in this class. The upper bound also applies to the orbital scheme of every finite solvable primitive permutation group. This gives the optimal bound in the affine subclass of Babai's coherent-configuration analogue of the Sims conjecture. [Ponomarenko, Problem 3.1](https://www.pdmi.ras.ru/~inp/cp01.pdf)

## Proof

The valencies are the orbit sizes of $H$ on $V\setminus\{0\}$. Indeed, translate the first vertex of a pair to $0$. Its stabilizer is $H$, so the fibers of the nontrivial orbitals at $0$ are exactly these orbits. A smallest orbit $S$ spans $V$ because its span is nonzero and $H$-invariant. An element of $H$ that fixes every point of $S$ fixes the span pointwise. The induced action on $S$ therefore embeds $H$ in $S_k$, and each orbit size is at most $|H|\leq k!$.

For equality, choose a prime $p>k$ and let $H=S_k$ act on $V=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_k)\in\mathbb F_p^k\colon \sum_i x_i=0\}$ by permuting coordinates. If a nonzero invariant subspace contains $v\ne 0$, some pair $v_i,v_j$ differs since $p$ does not divide $k$. Subtracting the transposed vector yields a nonzero multiple of $e_i-e_j$. Its conjugates span $V$, proving irreducibility. The action is faithful since the $k$ vectors $e_i-\frac1k(1,\ldots,1)$ are distinct and are permuted as the coordinates are. These vectors form an orbit of size $k$. Any nonzero vector is nonconstant and has a coordinate value appearing $a$ times for some $1\le a<k$. Its positions range through all $a$-subsets under permutations, giving orbit size at least $\binom{k}{a}\ge k$. Finally $(i-(k+1)/2)_{i=1}^k$ has distinct coordinates and sum zero, so its stabilizer is trivial and its orbit has size $k!$.

For the solvable case, a nontrivial minimal normal subgroup $N$ of a finite solvable group is elementary abelian. In detail, its derived group is characteristic and proper, hence trivial by minimality. The characteristic subgroups of an abelian $N$ then force it to be a $p$-group of exponent $p$. In a faithful primitive permutation action every nontrivial normal subgroup is transitive, since its orbits form invariant blocks. A transitive abelian subgroup acts regularly because an element fixing one point fixes its entire orbit. Identifying the vertices with $N$ therefore expresses the group as $N\rtimes H$, where $H$ is the point stabilizer and acts faithfully on $N$. Every $H$-invariant proper nonzero subspace would give a nontrivial invariant coset partition. Thus $H$ is irreducible, as required. This argument concerns orbital schemes of affine groups; the general question includes configurations whose colors are not orbitals of such a group.
