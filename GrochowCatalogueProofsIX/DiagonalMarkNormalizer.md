# Simultaneous normalization of overlapping simple diagonals

## GP-0490

Let $G_i$ be permutation groups supplied by generators. Each marked coordinate has one displayed nonabelian simple normal subgroup $S_i\lhd G_i$, with an explicit table and its permutation embedding. A mark is a full diagonal subgroup across a subset of coordinates, specified by isomorphisms from one common simple group to the corresponding $S_i$. There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm constructing generators for the subgroup of $\prod_i G_i$ that normalizes every marked diagonal individually. It handles arbitrary overlaps among the marks. The same method finds a simultaneous transporter between two lists with corresponding supports. This applies, for example, to primitive almost simple coordinate actions with displayed simple socles.

Conjugation gives a homomorphism $\rho_i:G_i\to\operatorname{Aut}(S_i)$. Its image has polynomial size in the displayed table of $S_i$. Indeed a nonabelian finite simple group has a generating pair, and an automorphism is determined by the ordered image pair. Find such a pair by saturation for each candidate, then evaluate the images of the supplied ambient generators by permutation conjugation and table lookup. Generate the image group explicitly and retain a lift for every image, using traversal in these image generators. Schreier's lemma gives permutation generators for $\ker\rho_i$ from these lifts and the original generators. None of these steps enumerates $G_i$. [Finite simple two-generation](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/gkfinal.pdf)

If a marked diagonal has identifications $\phi_i:S\to S_i$ on its support, a tuple $g$ normalizes it precisely when the maps $\phi_i^{-1}\rho_i(g_i)\phi_i$ all agree on $S$. Given a target mark with maps $\psi_i:S\to S_i$, the corresponding transport equations instead require agreement of $\psi_i^{-1}\rho_i(g_i)\phi_i$. A different parametrizing simple group on the target can first be identified with $S$ through any of its coordinate isomorphisms, since the image diagonal is unchanged by a common reparametrization.

For each mark connect its support by a star. Its equations express the unknown automorphism at one endpoint of each edge as an explicit bijective function of the automorphism at the other endpoint. In the normalization case these functions are group isomorphisms given by conjugating by the coordinate identifications. In the transport case they are left-and-right compositions by fixed isomorphisms. In each connected component choose a root. Try each of its polynomially many image elements, propagate along a spanning tree, and check every remaining edge and every image-membership condition. The root value determines every other value, so the search is complete even with inconsistent cycles. For transport retain one passing assignment in each component and lift it by the previously stored permutation words. For normalization the valid assignments form a subgroup of the product of images. Keeping one lift for every valid assignment supported on each component, together with the individual kernel generators, generates its full inverse image. Unmarked factors contribute their original generators. This gives a polynomial constructive algorithm with no dependence exponential in the number of overlapped coordinates. [Permutational transporter context](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10454)
