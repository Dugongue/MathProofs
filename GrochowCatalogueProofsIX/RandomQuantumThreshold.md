# Exact quantum-symmetry transition for sparse Erdős–Rényi graphs

## GP-0969

For $G\sim G(n,p)$, let $I$ be its number of isolated vertices, and write $C(\operatorname{Qut}(G))$ for the universal $C^*$-algebra of the graph's quantum automorphism group. Fix any $C>0$. If $\log n-C\log\log n\le np$ and $p\le 1/2$, then with probability $1-o(1)$
$$
C(\operatorname{Qut}(G))\cong C(S_I^+),
$$
where $S_I^+$ is the quantum permutation group on $I$ points, with scalar algebra for $I=0$. The nonisolated induced graph then has trivial quantum automorphism group. In the critical window $np-\log n\to c\in\mathbb R$, the limiting probability that the quantum automorphism algebra is commutative is
$$
e^{-\lambda}\sum_{i=0}^{3}\frac{\lambda^i}{i!},\qquad \lambda=e^{-c}.
$$
For $0\le p\le 1/2$ it tends to $1$ if $np-\log n\to+\infty$ and to $0$ if $np-\log n\to-\infty$. This extends the dense uniform random-graph result to the sparse transition and identifies the source of the remaining quantum symmetry there. [Lupini, Mančinska and Roberson, nonlocal games and quantum permutation groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01820)

## The stable cells in the critical region

Use the stable coloring of ordinary vertex refinement started from a single color. Theorem 7.1 of Anastos, Kwan and Moore gives, with probability $1-o(1)$, pairwise distinct stable colors on the vertices of degree at least three in the 2-core of $G$. Moreover each such color is unique in the entire graph. To see this last point, the vertices left at each stage of the peeling process for the 2-core form a union of stable cells, by induction using equitability. Degrees into the 2-core are then constant on each stable cell, so one of these colors cannot also occur outside this set of vertices. For $np\ge 2\log n$, the same authors' Theorem 1.2 already gives a discrete stable coloring with probability $1-o(1)$. It thus remains to handle $\log n-C\log\log n\le np\le 2\log n$. [Anastos, Kwan and Moore, Theorems 1.2 and 7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06095)

In this range two distinct vertices of full degree at most three have distance at least three with probability $1-o(1)$, simultaneously for all pairs. Put $q=\Pr(\operatorname{Bin}(n-3,p)\le 2)=O((\log n)^{C+2}/n)$, by bounding the three binomial terms. For a prescribed adjacent pair, condition on its edge, leave aside one further vertex, and count the independent remaining edges incident to the two endpoints. The probability that both endpoints have degree at most three, with the prescribed edge present, is at most $pq^2$. For a prescribed two-edge path, condition on its two edges, omit the possible edge between the endpoints, and count edges from the endpoints to vertices outside the triple. The analogous probability is at most $p^2q^2$. A union bound gives $O((n^2p+n^3p^2)q^2)=o(1)$.

On this event, after deleting isolates and leaves, every original degree-two or degree-three vertex keeps all its neighbors. An original vertex of degree at least four loses at most one leaf, since two leaves cannot share a neighbor. The remaining graph therefore already has minimum degree two and is the 2-core. The positive-degree vertices outside the set of 2-core vertices of degree at least three are precisely the original leaves and the original degree-two vertices. Every such vertex is adjacent to a uniquely colored vertex, and no two of these low-degree vertices share a neighbor. Equal stable colors would require equal counts into each singleton stable cell, which is impossible. Thus the isolates, if any, constitute the only stable cell that can have more than one vertex.

## The quantum algebra and the limiting law

A magic unitary commuting with the adjacency matrix preserves stable colors. Indeed it fixes the all-ones vector, and fixing a cell indicator implies it fixes its neighbor-count vector. If a vector $f$ is fixed, row orthogonality gives $(f_x-f_y)u_{xy}=0$, so the refinement splits into invariant cells. In particular, for any graph with $I$ isolates the magic unitary is block diagonal between the isolates and the nonisolated vertices. The two remaining block relations are independent, since the isolate block and the blocks between the two sets are zero in the adjacency matrix. The graph's quantum algebra is therefore the unital free product of $C(S_I^+)$ and the quantum automorphism algebra of the nonisolated induced graph. Equivalently, the block calculation applies after deleting the isolates, since their color contributes zero neighbor counts on every other vertex and the degree-zero split may be performed first. On the event just proved, each nonisolated vertex is fixed and the second factor is scalar. [Prior quantum permutation background](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503461)

A three-by-three magic unitary is commutative. To check this for entries in different rows and columns, relabel them as $a=u_{11}$ and $b=u_{22}$, and put $d=u_{33}$. Row and column sums and orthogonality give $ab=ad$, and cyclic permutation of the indices gives $bd=ba$ and $da=db$. Taking the adjoint of the last equality gives $ad=bd$, so $ab=ba$. Two-by-two and smaller cases follow directly or by adding fixed points. With at least four points, the universal magic matrix can map to a block diagonal matrix with two blocks of the form $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}P&1-P\\1-P&P\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ using two noncommuting projections in $M_2(\mathbb C)$, and fixed points for the rest. Thus the algebra is commutative exactly for $I\le 3$.

If $np-\log n\to c$, the factorial moments of $I$ equal $(n)_k(1-p)^{k(n-k)+\binom{k}{2}}$ for fixed $k$ and converge to $e^{-kc}$. Hence $I$ converges to a Poisson variable of mean $e^{-c}$, giving the displayed limit. If $np-\log n\to+\infty$, Markov's inequality gives $I=0$ with probability tending to one. If $np-\log n\to-\infty$, $p=O(\log n/n)$ and $\mu=\mathbb E I=n(1-p)^{n-1}\to\infty$. Computing the joint isolation probability of two distinct vertices gives $\operatorname{Var}(I)/\mu^2\le \mu^{-1}+p/(1-p)=o(1)$, so $I\ge 4$ with probability tending to one. The isolated block yields a noncommutative quotient even outside the structural range. The upper-density transition follows by complementation, since a magic unitary commutes with $I_n$ and $J_n$, and therefore commutes with $A$ exactly when it commutes with $J_n-I_n-A$.
