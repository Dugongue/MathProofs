# Rational binomial feasibility is in deterministic polynomial time

**GP-0755, rational binomial specialization.** Given equations over $\mathbb Q$, each with at most two nonzero monomials and with binary exponents, existence of a common zero over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ is decidable deterministically in polynomial bit time. Variables may vanish and no coordinate-support promise is needed. This is a restricted version of rational Hilbert Nullstellensatz.

## Proof

Combine identical monomials and discard zero equations. For a point $x$, let $S=\{j\mid x_j\ne0\}$. A monomial is nonzero exactly when its variables all lie in $S$. For a binomial with nonzero coefficients, its two monomials must therefore be either both zero or both nonzero. If their variable sets are $U,V$, impose the implications $U\subseteq S\Rightarrow V\subseteq S$ and $V\subseteq S\Rightarrow U\subseteq S$. For a single-monomial equation, its variable set may never be contained in $S$. Constants have empty variable set.

Starting with the empty set, repeatedly apply all implications to produce the least closed support $S_*$, in polynomial time. Every feasible solution has support containing $S_*$. Reject if $S_*$ contains a forbidden single-monomial support. For each binomial whose supports are both contained in $S_*$, divide its monomials on the torus to obtain $x^{a_i}=c_i$, where $a_i\in\mathbb Z^{S_*}$ and $c_i\in\mathbb Q^\times$. Every larger candidate support must still solve all these equations on the same coordinates. Conversely, any solution of this Laurent system on $S_*$ extends by zero to the original system, because every other binomial has both terms zero by closure. Thus feasibility reduces to just one Laurent system.

Let $A$ be the matrix with rows $a_i$. The Laurent system has a solution over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ exactly when $\prod_i c_i^{u_i}=1$ for each $u\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}(A^{\mathsf T})$. One direction follows by multiplying equations. For sufficiency, compute unimodular row and column transformations bringing $A$ to Smith form. Row operations replace the constants by products of the $c_i$, with exponents given by the corresponding transformation rows. Column operations give invertible monomial coordinate changes on the torus. In the transformed system each nonzero diagonal entry yields an equation $y_j^{d_j}=c'_j$, solvable by algebraic closedness. The zero rows require $c'_j=1$. Their transformation rows form an integer basis of the relation lattice. The matrices of these transformations have polynomial bit length and are computable in polynomial bit time. [Kannan and Bachem, polynomial Smith and Hermite normal forms](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0208040).

The relation tests can be performed without expanding products of binary powers or factoring their bases. Separate signs and write each positive rational base as an integer numerator over an integer denominator. Maintain these integers as products of a shared set of distinct factors, initially the distinct nonunit numerators and denominators. If factors $a,b$ have $d=\gcd(a,b)>1$, replace them by $d,a/d,b/d$, omit units, merge duplicates, and update the factorizations. The sum of base-2 logarithms of the distinct factors drops by at least $\log_2 d\ge1$, so only polynomially many splits occur. The multiplicities representing any initial integer are bounded by its bit length. At termination the factors are pairwise coprime. A rational product of binary powers equals 1 exactly when its sign is positive and the total exponent of each factor is zero. Both tests use polynomial-size integer arithmetic. This completes the bit-time bound without computing the roots $y_j$.

**Scope.** This proof handles arbitrary rational binomial systems under the displayed sparse encoding. The broader rational Hilbert Nullstellensatz question allows polynomials with more terms. [Eisenbud and Sturmfels, Laurent binomial ideals and partial characters](https://arxiv.org/abs/alg-geom/9401001), [Cattani and Dickenstein, counting affine solutions for binomial complete intersections](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0510520).

## Sparse radical membership and a succinct sample point

The same method has two further consequences. A feasible binomial system has a polynomial-size sample representation over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ by rational constants, roots of equations $y_j^{d_j}=c'_j$, and integer monomial maps. One may choose any root of each displayed nonzero constant, take every free Smith coordinate equal to 1, and map back before putting zeros outside $S_*$. The transformed constants are products of binary powers of original rationals. Verifying that the description denotes a solution uses just the transformations and the product tests already given, without representing a minimal polynomial for each final coordinate.

Given any sparse rational query $h$, the criterion $h\in\sqrt{I}$, where $I$ is the input binomial ideal, is decidable in randomized polynomial bit time with one-sided error. A verdict that $h\notin\sqrt I$ is always correct, and the probability of missing nonmembership can be made arbitrarily small.

For each monomial of $h$, seed the support closure with that monomial's variables. Compute the resulting least support $T$ and keep the distinct supports so produced. A feasible stratum with support $T$ is tested by the Laurent system on $T$. These polynomially many strata suffice to test vanishing everywhere. Indeed, fix any solution $x$ with support $S$. If $T\subseteq S$ is closed, zeroing the coordinates of $x$ outside $T$ gives another solution $x_T$. The left and right monomials of each binomial either both become zero or both retain their old values. No single-monomial equation becomes active. Group the monomials of $h$ by their least closed supports and write $h_T$ for a group. Then $h(x_T)=\sum_{U\subseteq T}h_U(x)$. If every tested stratum has $h=0$, induction on the finite inclusion order of tested supports inside $S$ gives $h_T(x)=0$ for each such $T$. All other groups of monomials vanish at $x$. Thus $h(x)=0$ for every solution.

For a nonempty stratum, the Smith coordinates give equations $y_j^{d_j}=c'_j$ with $d_j>0$ and leave the remaining coordinates free and nonzero. Reduce a surviving query monomial by dividing its exponent on coordinate $j$ by $d_j$ with remainder in $[0,d_j-1]$, putting the quotient in the binary power of $c'_j$. Group the reduced terms by their exponent vectors. These exponent vectors index a basis of the resulting Laurent coordinate ring. The ideal is radical in characteristic zero because the univariate polynomials $y_j^{d_j}-c'_j$ have distinct nonzero roots and each remaining coordinate is free. Hence the query vanishes on the stratum exactly when every grouped rational coefficient equals zero. Each is a sum of polynomially many compressed rational power products.

For the grouped tests, discard any prime dividing an original numerator or denominator that is inverted in the calculations, and evaluate the power products modulo a random prime of sufficiently large polynomial bit length. There is a polynomial $b$ in the input length such that a common-denominator numerator and denominator for each group have at most $2^b$ bits. This follows from the polynomial bit bounds on the binary exponents, from the explicit rational bases, and from the polynomial count of terms and operations used to form each group. In particular a nonzero numerator has at most $2^b$ distinct prime factors. The interval of primes of bit length $2b+O(\log(1/\epsilon)+\log(s+2))$, where $s$ is the input length, contains sufficiently many primes to make the failure probability at most $\epsilon$ after adjusting the absolute constants. Uniform prime sampling in the interval by primality testing uniform integers and rejecting composites takes expected polynomial time. It may be truncated with an additional arbitrarily small failure probability by returning the vanishing verdict when the sampling budget expires. Rational zero evaluates to zero at every allowed prime, so a nonzero modular evaluation is a valid certificate of geometric nonvanishing. This proves the claimed bit bound and error direction. [Eisenbud and Sturmfels, Laurent coordinate rings and partial characters](https://arxiv.org/abs/alg-geom/9401001), [Agrawal, Kayal and Saxena, deterministic primality testing](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2004/160-2/p12).
