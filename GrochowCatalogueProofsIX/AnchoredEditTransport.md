# Exact budgeted edit alignment with preprocessed anchors

## GP-0866

Let $G$ be an $n$-vertex graph and $t$ a nonnegative edge-edit budget. Suppose there are $1\le L<n$ top-degree vertices $a_1,\ldots,a_L$, in decreasing degree order, with gaps greater than $t$ between successive anchors and between the last anchor and the largest degree outside the anchors. Also suppose that the binary adjacency strings of the nonanchors to the ordered anchors have pairwise Hamming distance greater than $t$. For $L=O(\log n)$, $G$ has polynomial deterministic preprocessing, after which each arbitrary $n$-vertex adjacency-matrix query takes $O(n^2+nL)$ word operations. It returns the unique relabeling with at most $t$ edge disagreements if it exists, with the exact edit locations. This gives a sufficient certificate in the noisy version of the isomorphic-priors model. [Dorfman et al., the isomorphic-priors model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37979)

The certificate also implies that the coherent adjacency algebra of $G$ separates all vertices. In fact, the algebra generated by $A,I,J$ under ordinary and entrywise multiplication and linear combinations contains the diagonal degree matrix $I\circ(AJ)$. Interpolation gives the diagonal projector $E_{aa}$ of every anchor. For each anchor, $I\circ(AE_{aa}J)$ marks the vertices adjacent to it. Intersecting the bit projectors or their complements with the nonanchor projector isolates every nonanchor. The matrices $E_{xx}JE_{yy}$ then give all matrix units.

## Proof of the query bound

An edit changes the difference of two vertex degrees by at most one. In particular, an edit between the two vertices changes both degrees equally. Thus a feasible relabeling carries the anchors to the top $L$ target vertices in the same order, and there can be no relevant tie. Reject any query with a tie within those top ranks or at their boundary.

Put $r=\lfloor t/2\rfloor$. In preprocessing, make a table on all $2^L$ strings that stores the source nonanchor with signature at distance at most $r$, or a null entry. It can be filled by exhaustive comparisons in $O(2^L nL)$ operations, and there is at most one hit per string since $2r\leq t$. On a query, read the target nonanchor signatures and apply the table. Relative to a feasible relabeling the sum of the string discrepancies is at most $t$, since each discrepancy is a distinct anchor to nonanchor edit. Hence at most one target vertex can disagree with its true source signature by more than $r$. At all the other vertices, the table returns the true label.

If all table outputs form a bijection, verify that bijection against the entire adjacency matrix. A different feasible answer could not differ from this bijection at just one vertex. If exactly one table output is null and the other outputs are distinct, fill the missing source label and verify. If no output is null and there is exactly one duplicate pair and one missing label, try putting the missing label in each of the two duplicate positions. Every other pattern is impossible with a single incorrect or null output. These rules verify at most two permutations. Sorting degrees, reading strings and checking the candidate permutations fits the claimed bound. A feasible alignment is unique independently of the algorithm: two distinct feasible alignments would have the same anchors and disagree at at least two nonanchors. For each disagreement, the sum of the two signature distances is greater than $t$ by the triangle inequality. This exceeds their joint total budget $2t$.

## Certificates for a logarithmic edit budget

For all sufficiently large $n$, choose $L=\lceil32\log n\rceil$, with natural logarithm, and construct a random bipartite graph with $L$ prescribed anchors and $n-L$ nonanchors. Independently join each nonanchor to anchor $i$ with probability $1/2+i/(4L)$. The gaps between successive anchor degree means are $(n-L)/(4L)$. Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound give simultaneous deviations below $(n-L)/(16L)$ with probability tending to one. The resulting gaps exceed $t=\lfloor L/8\rfloor$, and all anchor degrees are higher than the nonanchor degree bound $L$ by more than $t$. For a fixed pair of nonanchors the disagreement bits are independent with means at least $3/8$. Hoeffding bounds the probability of Hamming distance at most $L/8$ by $\exp(-L/8)$. A union bound over pairs proves existence for $t=\Theta(\log n)$. The result extends degree-rank and signature comparisons from the dense vertex-deck argument to exact search under a total edit budget.
