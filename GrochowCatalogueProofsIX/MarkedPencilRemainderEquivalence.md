# GP-0240 — Marked split simple pencils with an arbitrary remainder

Let $\mathcal U,\mathcal U'\subseteq \operatorname{Mat}_n(\mathbb F_q)$ be linear spaces, $n\geq3$. Each comes with a marked two-dimensional subspace generated by $(D_0,D_1)$ or $(D'_0,D'_1)$. Assume $D_0,D'_0$ are invertible and $D_0^{-1}D_1,(D'_0)^{-1}D'_1$ have split simple spectrum. Suppose their eigenvalues are supplied. There is a deterministic bit-polynomial algorithm deciding whether some $X,Y\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$ send $\mathcal U$ to $\mathcal U'$ by $M\mapsto XMY^{-1}$ and carry the marked pencil onto the marked pencil. There is no restriction on the dimension of the remainder or its support.

## Residual group after the pencil is matched

By an invertible left-right transformation, put each pencil in the form $\langle I,\Lambda\rangle$ or $\langle I,\Lambda'\rangle$ with $\Lambda,\Lambda'$ diagonal. The eigenbases come from ordinary linear systems using the supplied eigenvalues. A transporter then satisfies
$$
XY^{-1}=aI+b\Lambda',\qquad
X\Lambda Y^{-1}=cI+d\Lambda'
$$
with the coefficient matrix invertible. This is possible precisely when $aI+b\Lambda'$ is invertible, and
$$
Y\Lambda Y^{-1}=(aI+b\Lambda')^{-1}(cI+d\Lambda').
$$
In particular the fractional linear map $z\mapsto(c+dz)/(a+bz)$ bijects the target spectrum onto the source spectrum.

Enumerate the ordered preimages of any three distinct source eigenvalues. Their uniqueness as projective points determines at most one fractional linear map, found by solving a fixed-size homogeneous linear system. Check the candidate for poles, invertibility and the spectral bijection. This yields at most $n(n-1)(n-2)$ candidates. Scaling the coefficient representative by a nonzero field element scales all images $XMY^{-1}$ by the same factor and has no effect on equality of linear spaces.

For each candidate choose the unique permutation matrix $P$ realizing the required bijection. Since the spectrum is simple, every $Y$ giving this permutation is $P\operatorname{diag}(u_1,\ldots,u_n)$ with all $u_i\ne0$. The known factors are $P$ and $aI+b\Lambda'$. Apply their inverse transport to $\mathcal U'$. The remaining question is whether diagonal conjugation sends $\mathcal U$ to the transformed target. This acts on each coordinate $M_{ij}$ by the scalar $u_i/u_j$.

## Deciding the diagonal-correction problem

Vectorize the matrices. Choose any coordinate subset $I$ of size $\dim\mathcal U$ on which projection is an isomorphism. Reject if the target has different dimension or its projection onto the same $I$ is singular, since both properties are preserved by diagonal coordinate scaling. The two subspaces now have unique systematic row generator matrices with identity on $I$. For pivot coordinate $p\in I$ and nonpivot coordinate $j$, the systematic entry $m_{pj}$ scales by $d_j/d_p$, where $d_\ell=u_i/u_h$ when coordinate $\ell$ stands for matrix entry $(i,h)$. Require the same zero pattern, then impose $d_j/d_p=m'_{pj}/m_{pj}$ on the nonzero entries. This gives a system of monomial equations in $(u_1,\ldots,u_n)$ with exponents of absolute value at most two.

For integer exponent matrix $E$, compute Smith normal form with unimodular multipliers, $L E V=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_t,0,\ldots,0)$ in rectangular notation. Multiplication by $L$ is an invertible monomial operation on equations, applied also to the right-hand constants. Changing variables by $V$ is an invertible monomial operation on the multiplicative group of the field. Thus a zero row requires its transformed constant to equal one, and a nonzero row is $w_i^{s_i}=c_i$. Cyclicity gives the exact test
$$
c_i^{(q-1)/\gcd(s_i,q-1)}=1.
$$
This decides feasibility without a discrete logarithm. Smith form with multipliers has a bit-polynomial algorithm, and constant transformations use modular exponentiation with exponents reduced modulo $q-1$. Success in at least one pencil candidate is necessary and sufficient.

The theorem concerns marked pencils with supplied split simple spectra and a decision problem. For an unmarked pencil inside an arbitrary larger space, the search for its image is outside this claim. The two ingredients connect the genus-two pencil setting in [Brooksbank, Maglione and Wilson](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.03033) with [Kannan–Bachem integer normal forms](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040).
