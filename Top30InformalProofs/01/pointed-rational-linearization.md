# Pointed rational linearization and a probe obstruction

Two scoped results toward the mixed finite-diagram trichotomy. The general trichotomy remains open.

These two results address the fixed-height, fixed-source-dimension diagram program. Diagrams are indexed by a fixed finite category. Diagram pairs are projectively cellular, or presented via the corresponding coherently resolved pair. Boundary maps are relative maps in the same model, and the probes used in the hardness construction below observe only integral bottom-degree periods. No completeness claim is made for the full trichotomy. The arguments are informal.

## A natural rational linearization

Let $Y$ be a finite-height diagram of objectwise simply connected spaces of finite type. Suppose $Y_{\mathbb Q}$ has a coherent point and a Mal'cev operation in the homotopy category of diagrams. Then $Y_{\mathbb Q}$ is naturally weakly equivalent to the underlying diagram of a bounded Dold–Kan complex of rational coefficient diagrams. The complex may have nonzero derived extension classes between its homology diagrams.

The coherent point supplies a unit for the multiplication obtained by fixing the middle Mal'cev input. Let $s$ be its square map. The augmentation of the rational chain diagram splits by the chosen point in the derived category. Its augmentation fibre gives the reduced chains, and $s$ induces an endomorphism of that fibre by preservation of the augmentation. This construction does not require a strictly pointed representative of $s$. Truncate the reduced chain complex at the fixed Postnikov height $N$ to obtain $C$ in the derived category of rational $I$-modules.

Objectwise rational H-space cohomology is free graded-commutative on the dual rational homotopy groups. The action of $s^*$ is multiplication by two on the indecomposables and multiplication by $2^w$ on the word-length-$w$ quotients of the augmentation ideal. In degrees at most $N$ the filtration is finite. Thus $R(s)=\prod_{w=1}^{N}(s-2^w)$ is zero on the homology of $C$. A homology-zero endomorphism $v$ of a bounded derived object is nilpotent. In cohomological indexing, the restriction of $v$ to a truncation at $k$ factors through the truncation at $k-1$, because its composite with the projection to $H^k[-k]$ vanishes by truncation orthogonality and zero action on $H^k$. Functoriality of the truncations allows these factorizations to iterate. After at most the length of the homological range, $v$ vanishes. Hence a fixed power of $R(s)$ is zero in the derived category itself. [Golub, power-map weights](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34822)

The Chinese remainder theorem supplies a polynomial in $s$ that is an idempotent projecting to generalized eigenvalue two. Split it in the derived category, with image $C^{(1)}$. Compose the unit into rational chains, the projection to reduced chains, and the projection to $C^{(1)}$. By the linearization adjunction this gives $Y_{\mathbb Q}\to\mathrm{DK}(C^{(1)})$ in the homotopy category of diagrams. At each object the eigenvalue-two cohomology pairs isomorphically with the indecomposable rational homotopy generators. Rational Hurewicz therefore shows that the composite is a homotopy equivalence at each object. No semisimplicity of the coefficient category is used.

## The NP conclusion with explicit boundary conversion

Assume the integral diagram has the effective principal obstruction presentation of the fixed-height program. Choose a bounded finite-dimensional rational chain model for $C^{(1)}$ in degrees at least two, and choose free finite-rank lattices stable under the differential and the finitely many arrows. The comparison $Y\to\mathrm{DK}(C^{(1)})$ is adjoint to a map of rational chains. In the bounded range, derived Hom from the integral chain object can be computed using a finite window of a finite-type projective resolution over the noetherian ring $\mathbb ZI$. Tensoring that finite Hom calculation with $\mathbb Q$ and clearing a denominator yields a map $p:Y\to K=\mathrm{DK}(C_{\mathbb Z})$ which is still a rational equivalence. Use unreduced chains here if only the rational diagram has a coherent point.

The relative homotopy groups of $p$ are finite. The base is simply connected. A possible first relative fibre group is the finite abelian cokernel on $\pi_2$, and the first total stage is simply connected by the same long exact sequence. The later total stages also have trivial fundamental group. Thus the relative invariants are ordinary diagram cohomology classes with finite coefficient diagrams in this principal presentation.

Write $K_m=\mathrm{DK}(C_{\mathbb Z}/mC_{\mathbb Z})$. For $m'=km$, the fibres of reduction are $\mathrm{DK}(m'C_{\mathbb Z})$ and $\mathrm{DK}(mC_{\mathbb Z})$, and the comparison acts by multiplication by $k$ in lattice coordinates. In mod-$p$ cohomology, multiplication by $p$ sends the positive-degree ideal of the cohomology of this connected abelian space into its square. The axis terms in the pullback by addition sum to zero and all remaining terms use at least two positive-degree factors. Iteration kills every bounded positive-degree range. Coefficient exact sequences give the same eventual vanishing with finite $p$-primary coefficients. Thus the positive fibre rows of the comparison Serre spectral sequences vanish in the colimit over sufficiently divisible moduli. The edge maps identify the colimit of finite-residue cohomology with the cohomology of the fixed total space. The diagram bar spectral sequence transfers this conclusion to diagram cohomology because its terms in any bounded range use finite products over strings of arrows. The bases at the finite stages remain simply connected. In the first relative stage the connecting homomorphism from the integral base surjects onto the degree-one group, hence so does its descended version. Higher stages do not change this fact.

Descend the finite-coefficient relative invariants one at a time, increasing $m$ as needed. A descended cohomology class has a representative on a projective bar model, and taking its principal homotopy fibre retains finite simplex sets in each degree. This gives a fixed homotopy pullback presentation $Y\simeq K\times_{K_m}F_m$ with $F_m$ degreewise finite. A fixed range of simplices suffices for any bounded-dimensional instance. [Effective diagram obstruction models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10152)

Guess the finite labels on the source and check faces, arrows and the prescribed boundary. The exact comparison with $K$ imposes integer linear chain equations and residue conditions on a free lattice, plus linear probe equations and the original equality–congruence arithmetic. Fixed bottom-degree probes are linear under this comparison: their rational classes are in the eigenvalue-two summand, and clearing a fixed denominator gives exact integral equalities on integral cycles. Torsion cohomology classes have zero evaluations on such cycles. For general higher probes this proof needs the same eigenvalue-two condition for the chosen global doubling map. Polynomial-bit feasibility follows from integer normal forms, after guessing finite arithmetic labels and Boolean branches and avoiding finitely many proper affine hyperplanes for disequalities. This proves the NP conclusion of A8 in the fixed residue presentation. For maps into a fixed finite simplicial target presentation, a fixed table on the relevant degrees of its projective bar model gives a polynomial boundary conversion, using a further bounded bar on an already resolved boundary when needed. An arbitrary existing binary tower encoding needs a separate polynomial conversion argument.

This linearization uses a coherent rational point. There are unpointed Mal'cev diagrams, for example affine torsors over the indexing category classified by a nonzero higher diagram cohomology class. Their affine linearization does not follow by choosing unrelated points at the objects. The slice and unpointed cases therefore remain outside this argument.

## Universal-cover probes can miss rational nonlinearity

Consider the finite indexing category $a\rightrightarrows b$ with two parallel arrows $f,g$. Both targets are $K(\mathbb Z,2)\times K(\mathbb Z,4)$ with cochain coordinates $(u,v)$. Set $f(u,v)=(u,v)$ and $g(u,v)=(2u,4v+u^2)$. This is a strict diagram, with no nonidentity composable arrows. It has a rational Mal'cev failure at stage 4. Indeed a natural Mal'cev operation, using the zero section, induces a natural unital multiplication. The identity arrow makes the two local products agree up to homotopy. Degree and unit conditions force their cohomology functions to be $u_1+u_2$ and $v_1+v_2+c u_1u_2$. The two compositions involving $g$ differ by $2u_1u_2$ in the top coordinate. The lower stage is linear.

Over $\mathbb Q$ let $L$ have maps $1,2$ and let $G$ and $M=\operatorname{Sym}^2L$ have maps $1,4$. Give the two-dimensional coefficient diagram $E$ the identity along $f$ and the matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}4&1\\0&4\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ along $g$. The target is the pullback of $K(E,4)\to K(M,4)$ along the square $K(L,2)\to K(M,4)$. This is a concrete identification in the normalized cocycle models: the second coordinate of the lift to $E$ is $u^2$, and its first coordinate is $v$. Its relative obstruction is the connecting class of $u^2$ for $0\to G\to E\to M\to0$.

Take any finite projectively cellular probe pair $(F,F_0)$ and any prescribed boundary giving a nonempty component of its relative mapping space. Anchor the component at a lift. Relative mapping spaces into the lower and obstruction abelian stages are translates of simplicial abelian mapping groups. The universal cover of the lower stage after rationalization is $K(U,2)$ with $U=H_I^0(F,F_0;L)$. Ordinary homotopy groups follow from the relative diagram cochain complex. A class of $U$ is literally a compatible locally constant assignment on the objectwise components, zero on components meeting $F_0$. By the natural relative Künneth decomposition, the universal lower family over that cover is $u_0+\theta$, where $\theta=\sum_i u_i z_i$ for a basis $u_i$ of $U$ and the degree-two parameter classes $z_i$. In total degree two there are only the anchored constant and the $H^0$-label contributions.

Each coefficient of $\theta^2$ is a product label $r$ transforming by $4$ along $g$ and by $1$ along $f$. It has a compatible lift $(h(r),r)$ to $E$, where $h(0)=0$ and $h(r)=r v_2(r)/8$ for nonzero rational $r$. Indeed $h(4r)=4h(r)+r$. This lift is a locally constant assignment and vanishes on the boundary. Each coefficient lifts individually, which is all the connecting homomorphism requires. Therefore the quadratic part of the obstruction on the cover vanishes. The remaining variable part is the connecting class of $2u_0\theta$, linear in the parameter classes. Parameter degrees above four cannot contribute to the degree-five obstruction. The rational obstruction map on the cover is affine-linear into a product of Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces, so its homotopy fibre and the universal covers of its components are rationally such products. For integral probes, compare the integral and rational lower and obstruction mapping groups by finite cellular cochains. Tensoring their homotopy groups with $\mathbb Q$ gives the corresponding rational groups. The homotopy fibre exact sequence gives the same comparison in degrees at least two for the simply connected covers. This proves that every universal-cover probe misses this rational Mal'cev failure.

Direct hardness nevertheless holds at projective source dimension five, with empty boundary and ordinary bottom periods. Let $C=\#_2(S^2\times S^2)$. On its wedge of four two-spheres double every sphere generator. The Whitehead attaching sum is multiplied by four, so the map extends with degree four on the top class. Use the identity and this map for the source arrows, and replace them by two mapping cylinders so that the pair is projectively cellular of dimension at most five. The identity arrow identifies the two classes $v$. The second arrow then imposes $u^2=0$ since the source self-map acts by four on $H^4(C;\mathbb Z)$. The degree-two compatibility is automatic. The four ordinary periods $(x,y,-z,1)$ give top square evaluation $2(xy-z)$. This enforces exact integer multiplication with the prescribed observations. Diophantine representation gives computably enumerable hardness and effective cochain enumeration gives membership. The example disproves the universal-cover probe formulation of rational detection. A general direct hardness argument in the same dimension and with the same probes remains open. [Matiyasevich](https://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/~yumat/papers/21_paper/21_eng.pdf)

## A separate warning about extending probe degrees

If higher coordinate periods are permitted, the underlying target Mal'cev condition alone cannot imply tractability of the mixed problem. Over a category with a single nonidentity arrow, take the invertible arrow $(a,b,c)\mapsto(a,b,c+a\smile b)$ between identical split targets $K(\mathbb Z^2,d)\times K(\mathbb Z,2d)$, where $d$ is even. The diagram is isomorphic to a constant diagram and carries a natural integral Mal'cev operation. All three coordinate readouts are closed classes. On a representable identity source with carrier $S^d\times S^d$, read $a,b$ on distinct axes with periods $x,y$ and pin their other periods to zero. Read the source $c$ period as zero and the target $c$ period as $z$. Compatibility gives $xy=z$. This warning concerns higher probes. The degree-two detection counterexample above uses exactly the bottom-period interface.
