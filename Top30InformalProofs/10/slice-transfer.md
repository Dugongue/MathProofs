**Premise A** (as in W4: hexagon 2611.00954v1, Thms 1.1 and 1.4, not re-verified here). Over C, for n ≥ n₀, every circuit for Perm_n, and every circuit over C((ε)) approximating it, has more than n^{ε(log n)^{1/3}} gates. Gates count variables and constants. Constants are arbitrary complex numbers, so this is stronger than the bit-size measure of GIMOWW Remark 1.4.

Notation:
- L(f) is circuit size (division-free, arbitrary constants); L̄(f) is border size.
- [f]_d is the homogeneous degree-d part. Recall the standard fact L([f]_d) ≤ 3(d+1)²·L(f), which also holds for L̄.
- U_{a,b,c} = C^a⊗C^b⊗C^c, acted on by G = SL_a×SL_b×SL_c (TI_SL).

### Theorem 1 (slice transfer of generator hardness)

Let G be reductive over C, acting linearly on U. Let w₀ ∈ U have a closed orbit, and let H = G_{w₀}, which is reductive by Matsushima. Let N be an H-stable complement to g·w₀ in U, and let N₁ be an H-stable direct summand of N. Suppose C[N₁]^H has no nonzero homogeneous elements in degrees 1, …, m−1, and that its degree-m part is the line C·P.

Then every generating set {g_i} of C[U]^G, finite or not, contains an element g with

  [g(w₀ + n₁)]_m = λ·P(n₁), with λ ≠ 0 and n₁ ranging over N₁.

Hence L(P) ≤ 3(m+1)²·(L(g) + 2·dim U·(dim N₁+1)), and likewise for L̄. Every succinct encoding C(x,y) of generators therefore has size ≥ L(P)/(3(m+1)²) − 2 dim U(dim N₁+1).

*Proof.*
1. **The maps.** φ: C[U]^G → C[N]^H, f ↦ f(w₀ + ·), is well defined because n ↦ w₀+n is H-equivariant. Restriction ρ: C[N]^H → C[N₁]^H is surjective: compose an H-invariant on N₁ with the H-equivariant projection N → N₁.
2. **Luna.** By Luna's étale slice theorem, the induced morphism N//H → U//G is étale at the image of 0. So φ induces an isomorphism of completed local rings Ĉ[U]^G_{w₀} ≅ Ĉ[N]^H_0. The map ρ̂ is surjective, since completion is exact on finitely generated modules.
3. **Generators of the maximal ideal.** Put c_i = g_i(w₀). Every f ∈ C[U]^G is a polynomial Q(g), so f − f(w₀) = Q(g) − Q(c) lies in the ideal (g_i − c_i). Thus the maximal ideal at w₀ is generated by the g_i − c_i. Their images h_i := ρφ(g_i − c_i) therefore generate the maximal ideal m̂₁ of R̂₁, where R₁ = C[N₁]^H and R̂₁ = ∏_k (R₁)_k. Finitely many h_i suffice (Noetherian).
4. **Degree count.** By hypothesis every element of m̂₁ has order ≥ m, and m̂₁² has order ≥ 2m. Write P = Σ a_i h_i with a_i ∈ R̂₁. Taking degree-m parts gives P = Σ a_i(0)·[h_i]_m, and each [h_i]_m ∈ (R₁)_m = C·P. So some [h_i]_m = λP with λ ≠ 0. Finally [h_i]_m = [g_i(w₀+n₁)]_m, because m ≥ 1 and subtracting the constant c_i does not change it.
5. **Cost.** Substituting the affine map n₁ ↦ w₀ + n₁ costs ≤ 2 dim U (dim N₁+1) gates. Extracting [·]_m multiplies the size by 3(m+1)². Both steps commute with ε-approximation. For succinct encodings, apply this to the generating set {C(x,α)}_α. ∎

*Special cases (sanity checks).*
- w₀ = 0 (so H = G and N = U) recovers the lowest-degree argument of GIMOWW Thm 4.4 and W4 F1/F2.
- U = V⊕W with w₀ = (v, 0): here W ⊆ N, which recovers the use of DM-exp Thm 1.5 for hardness rather than degree.

**Checked:** steps 1, 3, 4, 5, and both special cases.
**Assumed:** Luna's étale slice theorem for linear actions, in the form "N//H → U//G is étale at 0, with the linear slice w₀+N" (Luna 1973; Popov–Vinberg §6). Also: an étale morphism of C-varieties induces an isomorphism of completions.
**Novelty:** DM-exp use Grosshans surjections only to transfer *degree* bounds, and only for direct sums V⊕W. Using Luna completions to transfer *circuit hardness of every generating set* within a single representation appears new. It is easy for experts. **Confidence 85%** (the risk is in the exact form of Luna quoted).
