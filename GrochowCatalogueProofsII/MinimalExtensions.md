# Minimum extensions of codes and permutation actions

## GP-0343 · Minimum permutation-equivalent extension of linear codes

**Theorem.** Given equal-length binary linear codes and a budget, deciding whether each can be extended by at most that budget of linear parity coordinates so that the two extensions are permutation equivalent is NP-complete. This already holds for full-row-rank generators. The reduction allows a permutation to mix old and new coordinates.

Let the full-row-rank $k\times n$ generator matrices be $A,B$. If ranks differ, linear parity coordinates cannot equalize the ranks. For $P\in {\rm GL}_k$, let $m(P)$ be the size of the multiset intersection of the columns of $PA$ and $B$, with multiplicities. Then the exact minimum number appended to each is
$$
n-\max_P m(P).
$$
Indeed any permutation witness and its row change give a common extension multiset containing both column multisets, hence containing their multiset union. Conversely take that union and append precisely the needed columns, using $P^{-1}$ to convert the appended columns on the first side into its original coefficient coordinates. These are legal linear parity coordinates. Guessing $P$ and checking its overlap proves NP membership.

For hardness, take a simple graph $H$ on $k\ge4$ vertices with $m\ge k$ edges, and let $J$ be the $k$-cycle. Graphs with fewer than $k$ edges can be rejected directly and mapped to a fixed no-instance. Both multisets contain $M$ copies of every standard basis column $e_i$ of $F_2^k$. In the first include $e_i+e_j$ for each edge of $J$, and in the second include $e_i+e_j$ for each edge of $H$. Pad the first with $m-k$ copies of $e_1+e_2+e_3$. Set $M>2m$. A linear map that fails to permute the set of basis vectors achieves fewer than $Mk$ common columns. It misses at least $M$ basis-to-basis matches, and all matches with a nonbasis endpoint total at most $2m$, the total count of nonbasis columns on both sides. The identity gives at least $Mk$ matches. Thus an optimum uses a permutation of the basis. Its extra overlap counts cycle edges mapped to edges of $H$, because the padding has weight three and cannot match a basis or edge column. The maximum is at least $Mk+k$ exactly when $H$ has a Hamiltonian cycle. In the extension instance the budget is $m-k$. The construction has polynomial size. The same proof works over an explicitly presented finite field with binary $0,1$ entries, because the recognized basis columns are exact vectors and the graph edge columns have two nonzero entries. [Undirected Hamiltonian cycle hardness](https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~madhusudan/courses/Fall2020/Lectures/L19-after.pdf) · [Generator-matrix formulation of permutation equivalence](https://coding.yonsei.ac.kr/publications/international_journal/pdf/1596no.pdf)

## GP-0511 · Minimum duplication of existing action orbits

**Theorem.** Given two faithful permutation actions of different degrees, deciding whether they can become permutationally isomorphic after duplicating only already occurring orbits at total cost at most a binary budget is NP-complete. Hardness holds when both groups are elementary abelian of exponent two and every orbit has size one or two. The cost may count added points or added orbit copies.

For any named group identification, transitive $G$-set types carry multiplicities. Duplication preserves the set of types present. Equalizing multiplicities to their pointwise maxima is optimal when the support sets agree after the identification. The number of points in the common action is bounded by the sum of original action degrees. Therefore even for a binary budget one can guess polynomially sized lists of orbit duplications and a relabeling between the resulting domains. Membership of permutations in generated groups checks whether relabeling conjugates one enlarged subgroup to the other. This gives NP membership on generator input.

For hardness let $V=F_2^k$ as an additive group. For each nonzero form $\ell\in V^*$ let the associated two-point transitive action be $v\cdot b=b+\ell(v)$ on $F_2$. Two such actions have the same type precisely when their forms agree, by equality of stabilizers over $F_2$. Any abstract identification between actions on two copies of $V$ gives an invertible map on $V^*$, and all such maps occur. Use the same set of forms on both sides: the basis forms $e_i$ and every form $e_i+e_j$ for $i<j$. Since the basis forms span $V^*$, the actions are faithful. Each basis form occurs $M$ times. A pair form occurs once or twice according to whether the pair is an edge. Use the edges of a $k$-cycle $J$ on the first side and the edges of the input graph $H$ on the second. Include one singleton orbit on the first side and two on the second so the total degrees differ by an odd number.

For any linear identification $T$ taking the set of forms to itself, the optimal number of two-orbit additions is
$$
\sum_{\ell}|a_\ell-b_{T\ell}|,
$$
where $a,b$ are the input multiplicities. A map taking a basis point to a pair point has cost at least $M-2$ in this sum. In contrast any basis permutation has cost at most $\binom{k}{2}$. Choose $M>\binom{k}{2}+2$. Every optimum permutes the basis and hence acts by an ordinary vertex permutation on pair forms. The sum now equals the symmetric difference size of the two edge sets. Its minimum is at most $m-k$ exactly when $H$ contains a Hamiltonian cycle, for $m=|E(H)|\ge k$. Graphs with smaller $m$ can be mapped to a fixed no-instance. The corresponding threshold is $1+2(m-k)$ for points or $1+(m-k)$ for orbit copies. Generator permutations are explicit: for each basis vector of $V$, swap exactly those two-point copies whose form has value one on it. The construction has polynomial degree. An output-sensitive polynomial-time optimum algorithm would decide these instances in polynomial time because their optimum actions have polynomial degree. [Permutation-subgroup conjugacy background](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10454)

The code proof uses overlap of actual generator columns and a common multiset union. The action proof uses overlap of orbit types under an unknown group identification, with faithfulness supplied by the basis forms. The large multiplicities preserve the same vertex-permutation constraint in both representations.
