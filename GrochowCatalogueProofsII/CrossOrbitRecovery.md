# Sharp noise tolerance and linear queries for two-orbit Gram recovery

These are exact and perturbed versions of the joint two-orbit observation in [Mixon and Vose, Theorem 2.12 and discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17434#S4). The separation parameter below refers to the cross-orbit labels. Two generic seed orbits of a faithful real orthogonal action are free and have cross block
$$
M_{g,h}=\langle gx,hy\rangle=f(g^{-1}h),\qquad f(s)=\langle x,sy\rangle.
$$
Genericity makes the $n$ values of $f$ pairwise distinct. For two distinct group operators $s,t$, equality of the two values would be the zero set of the nonzero bilinear form $\langle x,(s-t)y\rangle$. Thus it is enough in both algorithms that the orbits are free and the displayed $n$ values are distinct. No labels $g,h$ are given to the algorithms.

## GP-0948. Sharp universal constant $1/2$

**Theorem.** Let $\Delta=\min_{s\ne t}|f(s)-f(t)|$ and let every observed entry of the joint Gram matrix have absolute error at most $\epsilon$. For $\epsilon<\Delta/2$ the regular multiplication law is recoverable deterministically in polynomial time. No fixed constant $c>1/2$ in $\epsilon<c\Delta$ admits an algorithm that succeeds uniformly over generic models, even with both marginal Gram matrices supplied as part of the noisy joint matrix.

For the upper bound, sort the entries of each observed cross row. Each row contains one copy of every true value. The order of any pair in a row remains correct, since their difference changes by at most $2\epsilon<\Delta$. Replace each entry by its observed rank in the row. The resulting matrix is the exact cross table with its $n$ real values replaced by their global ranks. Fix a reference row. For each row $g$, the matching of ranks from the reference row $g_0$ to row $g$ permutes the columns by $h\mapsto gg_0^{-1}h$. These are exactly the regular left translations, with one translation for each row. Choose any column as the identity. Composing the translations and reading their image of this column gives the table. The bound needs no prior knowledge of $\Delta$.

For the lower bound choose groups $A=C_4$ and $B=C_2^2$. Label both sets on four indices so they have the same map to $C_2$, using parity in $C_4$ and one coordinate in $C_2^2$. Use their faithful left regular representations in $\mathbb R^4$. At initial $x=e_1$, cross values are an arbitrary prescribed coordinate vector of $y$, up to the permutation that takes each element to its inverse. Choose a common four-vector of desired cross values, with $0,\delta$ on the two even indices and $3\delta,4\delta$ on the two odd indices. Then $\Delta=\delta$ in each model. At each fixed matrix entry of the two cross blocks, the difference labels $g^{-1}h$ have the same parity. The two cross entries therefore differ by at most $\delta$. Both marginal $x$ Gram matrices are the identity. The two marginal $y$ Gram matrices differ entrywise by at most $2\|y\|_2^2=52\delta^2$. Fix $0<\delta<1/52$.

The initial data may be perturbed to genuine generic observations within each model. For fixed $x$ close to $e_1$, the map from $y$ to the four cross values remains invertible. Hence a neighborhood of the initial $(x,y)$ is parameterized by $x$ and the chosen cross values. This open neighborhood admits generic pairs in each regular representation, including free seeds and avoidance of every non-identical polynomial equality of Gram labels. Choose the perturbations small enough that the max entrywise discrepancy between the exact joint matrices is at most $(1+\eta)\delta$, and each gap is at least $(1-\eta)\delta$, where any fixed $c>1/2$ allows $\eta>0$ satisfying $(1+\eta)/(2(1-\eta))<c$. The midpoint of the two exact joint matrices is positive semidefinite and satisfies the allowed error bound for both models. Since their abstract groups differ, an algorithm receiving that midpoint cannot recover the correct law in both cases. This proves sharpness of the universal constant, including when algorithms use the within-orbit observations.

## GP-0949. Linear worst-case sufficiency for adaptive cross entries

**Theorem.** For two free orbits of size $n$ and pairwise distinct cross labels, at most $6n-1$ adaptively chosen exact cross entries suffice for full regular multiplication recovery. The bookkeeping between queries is polynomial time. This answers worst-case sufficiency and makes no claim that the leading constant or query count is optimal.

Read one whole reference row and one whole reference column. This takes at most $2n-1$ queries. The $n$ values on the reference row are names for the $n$ group elements after choosing the reference intersection as the identity. To verify the convention, write the reference row and column as $g_0,h_0$. Set $c=g^{-1}g_0$, $b=g_0^{-1}hh_0^{-1}g_0$, and $f'(s)=f(sg_0^{-1}h_0)$. Then $M_{g,h}=f'(cb)$. The reference row gives a dictionary from each column to its $b$-label and the reference column gives a dictionary from each row to its $c$-label. Subsequent entries are therefore queries for multiplication $cb$ in a single identified set of labels, with the identity already recognized.

Maintain a subgroup $K$ together with the left and right multiplication permutations $L_k,R_k$ on all labels for every $k\in K$, starting with the identity. If $K\ne G$, pick a label $a$ outside $K$. The unknown $L_a$ commutes with all $R_k$. Choose one representative $b$ of each right $K$-orbit and query $ab$. This determines $L_a$ everywhere via $a(bk)=(ab)k$, at $n/|K|$ queries. In the same way, query $ba$ on one representative of each left $K$-orbit to obtain $R_a$. Use products and inverses of the now-known permutation arrays to build all left and right arrays for the subgroup generated by $K$ and $a$, without more queries. Ordinary subgroup closure in the regular action suffices, and the value of each multiplication permutation at the identity identifies its element.

Each extension at least doubles $|K|$. The additional number of entries is bounded above by $2n(1+1/2+1/4+\cdots)=4n$. The method constructs all left multiplication arrays, which are the requested multiplication table. It does not require either marginal Gram matrix.
