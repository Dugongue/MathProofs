# GP-0327 · Cycle-index computation for a code automorphism group

**Theorem.** Computing every nonzero cycle-index coefficient of a binary linear code's permutation automorphism group is #P-hard even when full group generators are supplied and the number of possible cycle signatures is polynomial in the code length. Computing the coefficient for a specified signature is #P-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions.

Use the [Boolean fiber-product group and graph realization](InvolutionGeneration.md) of GP-0342. For a system of $J$ homogeneous equations, the group $E$ has a central kernel with exactly $2^J$ elements over every common vector $x$. Every member of the fiber over $x$ squares to one precisely when all $Q_j(x)=0$. Thus counting elements with square one counts the quadratic solutions after division by $2^J$. Construct the instance a second time with the additional equation $x_0^2=0$. The difference of the two solution counts leaves precisely the solutions with $x_0=1$. Every satisfying 3-CNF assignment extends to one assignment to the circuit gates. This recovers #3SAT via two counts. [Counting satisfiability](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208032)

Let $\Gamma$ denote the uncolored graph of GP-0342 with $v$ vertices and automorphism group $E$. Its minimum degree is at least one. For $h\ge5$, form $\Gamma_h$ by adding two internally disjoint pendant paths of lengths $1$ and $h-2$ at each old vertex. The old vertices now have degree at least three, and new vertices have degree at most two. Each of the two attached lengths is distinguished at an old vertex. Every automorphism of $\Gamma_h$ is therefore a unique extension of some $g\in E$, and the vertex action consists of $h$ parallel copies of the original action. If $g$ has $o(g)$ vertex orbits on $\Gamma$, it has $h\,o(g)$ on $\Gamma_h$.

Apply the duplicated-basis code construction of GP-0342 to $\Gamma_h$. The full permutation automorphism group is a split extension $K_h\rtimes E$ with $K_h=(C_2)^{hv}$. The action on the kernel coordinates is by vertex permutation. For $g^2=1$, a lift $(a,g)$ has square $(a+ga,1)$, which equals one exactly when $a$ is constant on each vertex orbit of $g$. Hence there are $2^{h o(g)}$ such lifts. No lift of an element of order greater than two has square one. Thus the sum of coefficients of signatures with only one-cycles and two-cycles is $F(2^h)$ for the polynomial
$$
F(X)=\sum_{g\in E,\;g^2=1} X^{o(g)} .
$$
It has degree at most $v$. Querying $v+1$ consecutive integers $h$ starting at five yields distinct interpolation arguments $2^h$. All inputs and answers have polynomial bit length, and exact interpolation returns $F(1)$ in polynomial bit time. This is the required number of elements of $E$ with square one.

Each element of $E$ has order dividing four. The fourth power of a code automorphism lies in the exponent-two kernel and its eighth power is one. The only permitted cycle lengths are $1,2,4,8$, giving $O(N^3)$ possible signatures at length $N$. An algorithm with polynomial delay including termination would therefore finish in polynomial time on these instances. A single-coefficient oracle also suffices since there are at most $N/2+1$ square-one signatures. A specified coefficient belongs to #P in the supplied-group model by guessing a permutation in a unique encoding, checking group membership, and checking the cycle signature. [Permutation-group membership](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf)

Goldberg gives specified-coefficient hardness for general supplied permutation groups. The reduction here uses full binary-code automorphism groups and keeps the number of possible output signatures polynomial. [Goldberg, Theorem 21](https://doi.org/10.1006/inco.1993.1045)
