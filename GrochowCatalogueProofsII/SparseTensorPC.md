# Sparse polynomial-calculus lower bounds for tensor isomorphism

**GP-0737.** Over each fixed finite field and over the rationals, there are geometrically nonisomorphic pairs of ordinary three-tensors whose ordinary polynomial-calculus refutations require monomial size $2^{\Omega(m)}$, where $m$ is the largest side length. There are such pairs obtained by one specified rank-one perturbation of a pair with a polynomial-size refutation. The perturbation preserves geometric nonisomorphism. The lower bound applies to the original tensor equations with matrix inverse variables and no field axioms. Both statements are written proofs.

## Sparse substitutions preserve proof size

Suppose a substitution from target-proof variables to source polynomials maps each variable to zero or a scalar times one monomial of degree at most a fixed constant. Suppose each substituted target axiom has a constant-degree polynomial-calculus derivation from the source axioms. A polynomial-size monomial proof in the target then gives a polynomial-size monomial proof in the source.

Indeed, the image of each line has no more monomials. A rule multiplying a previous line by a variable becomes a sequence of at most the constant degree bound many variable multiplications, with one scalar operation if needed. Zero images are harmless. Linear combinations remain linear combinations. For the initial axioms, any degree-$D$ derivation in $N$ source variables has a polynomial-size simulation when $D$ is fixed. To see this, retain only independent lines in the vector space of polynomials of degree at most $D$. Whenever a new independent line needs a discarded premise, recover that premise from the retained basis by successive linear combinations. There are at most $\binom{N+D}{D}$ independent lines, and each recovered line has at most that many monomials. This also works for a family with polynomially many target axioms.

## A graph extension that orders the ends of each edge

Use the actual hard pairs in the proof of Grädel–Grohe–Pago–Pakusa, Theorems 6.2–6.3. Their graphs are the undirected $J(A_n)$ and $J(B_n)$ of Lemma 5.3, where $J$ has an inverse interpretation on the CFI structures. Their underlying undirected graphs are therefore already nonisomorphic. The retained color classes in the size lower bound have constant cardinality. The standard colored ISO equations use only equal-color mapping variables and require monomial size $2^{\Omega(n)}$ on $O(n)$ vertices. The pairs have matching color and edge counts since they come from the same ordered CFI base. We include row and column collision monomials in the ISO system. The same theorem applies to this first-order-definable encoding with a linear number of variables. [Lemma 5.3 and Theorems 6.2–6.3](https://lmcs.episciences.org/5119/pdf)

For either underlying undirected graph $G$, build a bipartite $\widehat G$. Replace vertex $i$ by anchors $L_i,R_i$ in opposite parts. Every original edge $ij$ gives edges $L_iR_j$ and $L_jR_i$. Tie each $L_i$ to its $R_i$ by a private length-three path. Attach $5$ leaves to $L_i$ and $\Delta+7$ leaves to $R_i$, where $\Delta$ is a common constant degree bound. The two anchor degree intervals $[6,\Delta+6]$ and $[\Delta+8,2\Delta+8]$ are disjoint and all other degrees are at most two. After deletion of all anchors, a connector component is incident to two anchors and identifies the prescribed pair. Direct edges between anchors recover original adjacency. Thus uncolored isomorphism of the extensions is exactly uncolored isomorphism of the underlying graphs. Each extension has $O(n)$ vertices and edges. The source proof system still retains colors, which suffice for the sharper source size lower bound.

Every new vertex has its original owner $i$ and a fixed role in that owner's gadget. For the ordered pair of extended graphs substitute the target GI variable for $i,\rho\mapsto j,\tau$ by $z_{ij}$ when the owners have equal colors and roles $\rho,\tau$ agree, and by zero otherwise. For each row and column this pulls its sum equation back to the corresponding colored sum equation. Boolean axioms and collision axioms become zero or source axioms or their immediate multiples. For a local adjacency obstruction consider the two owners on each side. If owner equality fails to match, a collision monomial kills the image. Otherwise adjacency is prescribed by shared roles and owner equality except for direct anchors, where adjacency is exactly original adjacency. Thus every nonzero image of an obstruction is a source obstruction. All variable images from one part to the opposite part are zero.

## Monomial pullback through the published TI reduction

Apply Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She Propositions 5.4 and 5.5 to $\widehat G,\widehat H$. Their construction goes through the code generator $[I\mid I\mid I\mid D]$ for the incidence matrix $D$, then places two perpendicular identity entries behind each code column. It preserves and reflects isomorphism over the algebraic closure, including in finite characteristic. In the first proof substitution, an edge correspondence is the sum of the two endpoint matchings. Order all edges by their bipartition. After our vertex substitution the reversed matching vanishes term by term, so each edge entry is zero or one degree-two monomial. The other entries are copied vertex correspondences, transposes and zeros. In the second proof substitution every entry is a copied entry, its square, zero or one. The composite substitution therefore sends each original TI or inverse variable to a single monomial of degree at most four, or zero. Constant-degree derivations of the images of the tensor equations follow from those propositions and the explicit variable map for the extended GI equations. [Propositions 5.4–5.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S5)

By the sparse-substitution argument, a monomial-size $s$ TI refutation would give a colored ISO refutation of size $O(s)+n^{O(1)}$. Thus there are ordinary geometrically nonisomorphic tensors with each dimension $O(n)$ and refutation size $2^{\Omega(n)}$. This gives a superpolynomial lower bound for the size part of Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She Question 7.2. Since the largest side length is $m=O(n)$, the lower bound is $2^{\Omega(m)}$. This rules out a universal subexponential bound in the largest side length. The dense input length is a different parameter. [Open Question 7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS2)

## One rank-one change switches from easy to hard

The first flattening of every output tensor has full row rank. In the old rows this follows from the identity columns of the code generator. Each gadget row has its own disjoint coordinate specified by its column and slice. Let the hard pair be $(T,S)$. Pad each mode by one zero coordinate, denoted by $*$ in that mode, and add $e_*\otimes e_*\otimes e_*$ only to the source to give $T^+$. The padded source $T^+$ has full first flattening rank while the padded target has a zero $*$-row.

This rank discrepancy has a polynomial-size constant-degree PC refutation in the original equations. Write $A,B,C$ for the three unknown transporter matrices. The tensor equations at the $*$-row of the target state that the first-mode row vector from $A$ multiplying the source flattening gives zero after the other two mode changes. Multiply the entries by the corresponding entries of the inverse variables $B',C'$ and sum over the two target indices. Expand and use $B'B=I$, $C'C=I$. This derives the linear equations which say that the $*$-row of $A$ annihilates the source flattening. Constant Gaussian elimination gives the zero row for $A$. The corresponding diagonal entry of $AA'=I$ gives the contradiction. Each multiplication before substitution uses at most two additional variables and the summation ranges are polynomial.

Remove the rank-one tensor from the source. The zero-padded pair is still geometrically nonisomorphic because its intrinsic contraction supports recover the original factors. A refutation of the padded pair specializes to a refutation of the unpadded equations by taking the new block of each transporter and inverse to be the identity and all cross terms zero. This is a monomial substitution and preserves the size lower bound. The single supplied rank-one perturbation thus destroys polynomial-size refutability while preserving nonisomorphism.
