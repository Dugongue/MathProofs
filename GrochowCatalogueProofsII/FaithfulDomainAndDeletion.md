# Faithful action extension and singleton deletion

## GP-0378 · Minimum-domain faithful extension

**Theorem.** For generator-given permutation $G$, given subgroup $M$, and a prescribed finite $M$-set, deciding whether that action extends faithfully with zero additional points is NP-complete. The decision problem with a bound given in unary is NP-complete too. For every fixed $L\ge 1$, it is NP-hard to distinguish a zero optimum from an optimum at least $8L$. The gap construction has size polynomial in the source size and $L$, and all instances admit an extension after finitely many points are added.

Start with the compact fixed-target extension problem for $D_8$ from the first catalogue packet. Its input consists of the natural faithful explicit permutation action of a group $G_0$ on $\Omega_0$, a subgroup $M_0\le G_0$, and a specified homomorphism $\rho:M_0\to D_8$. Deciding whether $\rho$ extends to $G_0$ is NP-complete by the marked commutator reduction. The reduction from that problem below applies to arbitrary such input triples. [Wuu, Homomorphism Extension](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.08656#S1.SS2)

Let $A=D_8\times C_L$, and let $\iota:D_8\to A$ denote its first-factor embedding. Set $G=G_0\times A$ and $M=M_0\times A$. Give $G$ by its faithful generator action on $\Omega_0\sqcup A$ with $G_0$ acting naturally on $\Omega_0$ and $A$ acting by left multiplication on itself, with each factor fixing the other's summand. The prescribed $M$-set $X$ has those same points but different action on the second summand. On $\Omega_0$ use the natural $M_0$-action, with $A$ trivial. On $A$ require
$$
(m,a)\cdot x=a x\,\iota(\rho(m))^{-1}.
$$
The commuting left and right regular actions make this a homomorphism.

If $f:G_0\to D_8$ extends $\rho$, use the same block formulas with $f$ on the second summand and the original full $G_0$-action on the first. This extends $X$ on exactly its existing points. If $(g,a)$ acts trivially, its action on $\Omega_0$ forces $g=1$ and then its left regular action forces $a=1$. The extension is faithful.

Conversely suppose $X$ embeds $M$-equivariantly into a $G$-set using fewer than $|A|$ extra points. Identify the old points with their image. The second factor $A$ has a regular orbit on the old anchor and fixes all old points of $\Omega_0$. The additional points form an $M$-invariant subset, so no new $A$-orbit has size $|A|$. Since $G_0$ commutes with the whole second factor, it preserves the unique regular orbit. Every permutation of $A$ commuting with left multiplication is right multiplication, as is seen by evaluating it at the identity. Therefore the action of $G_0$ on this orbit gives a homomorphism to $A$ via $h\mapsto(x\mapsto xh^{-1})$ and its inverse on the image. Its restriction to $M_0$ is $\iota\rho$. Compose with $A\to D_8$ to obtain an extension of $\rho$. This implication holds even without a faithfulness promise on the putative larger action.

There is always a finite feasible extension. Induce $X$ as $G\times_M X$, use the injection $x\mapsto[1,x]$, and take the disjoint union with the given faithful $G$-action. The construction above maps a negative extension instance to an optimum at least $|A|=8L$. If an optimization routine had guaranteed additive error strictly less than $4L$ on these instances, thresholding its reported optimum at $4L$ would solve the decision problem. At $L=1$ the hardness follows already for the class-two exponent-four compact sources from the first packet.

For NP membership with a unary added-point bound, guess one permutation for each generator on a domain with at most the allowed number of points. Include the coordinates of the old action by a relabeling of its embedded image. Generate the subgroup of the product of the given and guessed permutation groups on disjoint domains from these pairs. Its projection to the given group is onto. Equal group orders make that projection injective, so the pair subgroup is exactly a homomorphism graph. Checking the image-group order verifies faithfulness. Evaluate the subgroup marks by constructive permutation-group membership and compare their restrictions to the old labels. All of these checks are polynomial in the stated encoding. [Permutation-group algorithms](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf)

### Dependence on the first catalogue packet

The fixed-target reduction uses a compact class-two group generated by involutions $a_i,b_j$. It is built in $D_8^{m^2}$ on factors indexed by $(i,j)$, with $a_i$ acting by one generating reflection along its row and $b_j$ by the other along its column. Each $[a_i,b_j]$ is central and has a single nontrivial block. Mark the commutators enforcing symmetry, diagonal normalization and the homogeneous Boolean equations as trivial, and mark $[a_0,b_0]$ as the central involution. Under an extension the two commuting families in the central quotient of the target each lie on one line, and the marked commutator forces independent lines. The resulting cross-commutator matrix is rank one. Symmetry and diagonal normalization recover a Boolean assignment with anchor one. Conversely powers of two generating reflections realize every satisfying assignment. Collecting words into products of the displayed involutions and independent central commutators gives exactly the needed presentation of the compact source. The prior Lean file **DihedralEpimorphismCore.lean** checks the commutator formula and the Boolean rank-one recovery. The action-extension and hardness arguments here are written proofs. [Earlier class-two group–tensor correspondence](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/ef3248c44a28fcc2e97f90392942083b33dce716/FourIsomorphismResults/GroupTensorEquivalence.md)

## GP-0496 · Conjugacy after deleting invariant singleton orbits

**Theorem.** For two permutation-generator actions, let $s_1,s_2$ be their numbers of globally fixed points and let $H_1,H_2$ be the induced permutation subgroups on their moving supports. The minimum total number of invariant singletons that must be deleted to make the remaining actions permutationally conjugate is $|s_1-s_2|$ if $H_1,H_2$ are conjugate, and is infeasible otherwise. Thus the optimization is polynomial-time equivalent to ordinary permutation-group conjugacy under oracle reductions. Its feasibility predicate is many-one equivalent to ordinary comparison.

A point is globally fixed exactly when every given generator fixes it. Removing such points leaves all nontrivial orbits unchanged and the restriction of each represented group is faithful. Any conjugator of remaining actions maps global fixed points to global fixed points, and restricts to a conjugator of the moving-support groups. Conversely a moving-support conjugator and any bijection of remaining fixed points combine into a conjugator. Equalizing fixed-point counts requires at least $|s_1-s_2|$ deletions and needs exactly that many on the side with surplus. All singleton choices give equivalent actions. On an ordinary equal-degree input pair, add one globally fixed point to each side to force intransitivity. Conjugacy of the augmented moving supports then implies conjugacy of the original groups because equal original degrees give equal fixed-point counts. Small trivial inputs can be decided directly. [Permutation-subgroup conjugacy and canonization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10454)
