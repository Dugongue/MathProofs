# Quaternionic orientations and explicit orbit reconstruction

This note treats numerical Gram graphs of free real orthogonal orbits. “Generic” refers to the simultaneous dense-open conditions on each competing representation described below. The proof uses the point-fixing rigidity already obtained in [the previous one-orbit recovery proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/ef3248c44a28fcc2e97f90392942083b33dce716/GrochowOrbitAndGroupAdvances/recovery.md) and answers the numerical questions of [Mixon and Vose](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17434#S4).

## GP-0946. Exponential upper bound independent of group order

**Theorem.** Fix a faithful finite real orthogonal representation of dimension at most $d$ and a generic seed. Let $r$ be the number of inequivalent irreducible constituents of quaternionic type and real dimension four, counting each isotype once. The numerical Gram graph of the orbit has at most $2^r$ possible regular permutation groups from faithful generic models, on that common observed vertex set. Consequently it has at most $2^{\lfloor d/4\rfloor}$ possible abstract group types. Together with the [exponential construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/ef3248c44a28fcc2e97f90392942083b33dce716/GrochowOrbitAndGroupAdvances/ambiguity.md), the maximum grows as $2^{\Theta(d)}$.

Work in the Euclidean span $W$ of the observed cloud and pick an observed basepoint $x$. A Gram isomorphism identifies every competing model with this same cloud by an isometry of spans, and the basepoint can be moved to the competing seed by its group action. Restriction to the observed span preserves the actual regular permutation action. Write $G$ for the original restricted group, $H$ for a competing group, and $C=\sum_z zz^{\mathsf T}$ for the orbit covariance. For each cloud point $z$, denote the unique operators of $G,H$ sending $x$ to $z$ by $g_z,h_z$, respectively. Both groups commute with $C$.

We recall the point-fixer argument with the exceptional factors retained. Exclude accidental equality between nonequal functions $y\mapsto\langle a y,b y\rangle$ of the seed, for $a,b\in G$. For any Gram permutation $T$ fixing $x$, the permutation of labels $g x\mapsto f(g)x$ extends linearly to an invertible map $L$ on the real operator algebra $A=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}G$ of the original representation. Indeed the Gram equality holds as an identity in $y$, so a linear combination of operators with zero action on every $y$ has image with zero squared norm on every $y$. Also $L(I)=I$, $L(S)^*L(S)=S^*S$, and $T(Sx)=L(S)x$. These equalities hold when projecting onto the observed span as well. The algebra $A$ is the product of its real simple blocks $M_a(D)$, where $D=\mathbb R,\mathbb C,\mathbb H$, and $*$ is the adjoint. The norm identity forces $L$ to preserve each block. Applying it to $S+I$ identifies the self-adjoint part, then the Euclidean matrix norm implies that $L$ fixes every self-adjoint element. If $U^*=U$ and $V^*=-V$, applying the identity to $U+V$ gives $[U,L(V)-V]=0$.

Except in $M_1(\mathbb H)$, the commutant of the self-adjoint elements in an individual block is its center. For $a\ge2$, use the diagonal projections and the off-diagonal self-adjoint matrices with entries $d,\bar d$ for arbitrary $d\in D$. This makes the commutant a real scalar for real and quaternionic blocks, and a complex scalar for complex blocks. The skew center is zero except for complex blocks. In $M_a(\mathbb C)$ with $a\ge2$, the norm identity fixes every trace-zero skew element and permits only a sign on $iI$. A negative sign would change the norm operator of $V+iI$ for a nonzero trace-zero skew $V$, since $(V+iI)^*(V+iI)-(V-iI)^*(V-iI)=-4iV$. Thus $L$ is the identity there too. In $M_1(\mathbb C)$, $L$ is the identity or conjugation. In $M_1(\mathbb H)$, it is the identity on the real axis and orthogonal on the imaginary subspace.

Under the generic covariance condition each positive covariance eigenspace is one real irreducible, and each isotype contributes at least one. To recall why, an isotype for an irreducible $D^a$ is represented as $a$-by-$m$ matrices over $D$, with the group acting on the left. On this isotype the covariance is the right multiplier by $|G|X^*X/\dim_{\mathbb R} D^a$, where $X$ is the seed matrix. This follows by testing its operator trace against equivariant Hermitian right multipliers. A generic $X$ has maximal rank and distinct positive singular values, and the positive covariance values on different isotypes are distinct after independent scalings. Each resulting positive covariance eigenspace has a nonzero projected seed. In dimension two, the projected cloud of a cyclic image is a regular polygon, and the projected cloud of a dihedral image is not regular when the seed avoids finitely many exceptional directions. Thus the regular projected polygons are intrinsic, and both $G$ and $H$ preserve their plane orientations. If $T=g_z^{-1}h_z$, conjugation on any $M_1(\mathbb C)$ would reflect one such plane and is impossible. The same point-fixer argument applies with $H$ as the reference representation. Hence $T$ is trivial outside the four-dimensional quaternionic isotypes for each representation.

For each original quaternionic isotype of the exceptional type, select one positive four-dimensional covariance eigenspace $E$. Fix quaternion coordinates on $E$ sending the projected seed to a positive multiple of $1$. The original action on $E$ is left multiplication by unit quaternions. If every $g_z^{-1}h_z$ is the identity on $E$, its induced map on that whole operator factor is the identity, since left multiplication by $a$ evaluated at the projected seed determines $a$. The actions then agree across the whole isotype for each corresponding vertex.

Otherwise some $g_z^{-1}h_z$ is nontrivial on $E$. Since the covariance eigenspaces are intrinsic and the point-fixer argument also applies to $H$, $E$ is quaternionic of the exceptional type for $H$ too. Its action on $E$ is conjugate to unit left multiplication by an orthogonal map fixing $1$ in the chosen coordinates. Such a map acts on the imaginary subspace by an orthogonal transformation. Positive determinant preserves the dot and cross product in the quaternion multiplication formula and gives a quaternion automorphism. Negative determinant reverses the cross product and gives a quaternion anti-automorphism. It follows that the entire competing action on $E$ consists either of left multiplications or of right multiplications, uniformly in $z$. If $b_z$ is the quaternion at $z$ divided by the projected seed length, then $h_z|_E$ is respectively $v\mapsto b_z v$ or $v\mapsto v b_z$.

Thus each chosen orientation determines the relative point-fixer on $E$, and hence on the whole exceptional isotype by the operator-factor argument. It determines $h_z$ for each $z$, since outside those isotypes it agrees with $g_z$. Each competing regular group is therefore determined by the $r$ orientation bits. This proves the stated bound. The result concerns generic observations of both compared actions and requires no bound on the group order.

## GP-0947. A single quaternionic isotype leaves no abstract ambiguity

**Theorem.** If $r\le1$ in the preceding theorem, the numerical Gram graph determines the abstract group among all faithful generic models.

There is nothing to prove when all orientations agree. Suppose the one exceptional factor uses opposite orientations in the two models. Their shared vertex set identifies the true group with a subdirect product $R\le B\times D$, where $B$ is its image on the chosen four-dimensional eigenspace and $D$ its image on the complementary isotypes in $W$. This embedding is faithful because the action on any other copy of the exceptional isotype is determined by its action on that chosen copy. The competing group is represented by the same subset $R\subset B^{\mathrm{op}}\times D$, using right multiplication on the chosen copy. The first projection set $B$ is the same by evaluation at $1$.

By the elementary fiber-product description of a subdirect product, there are surjective homomorphisms $\pi_B\colon B\to Q$, $\pi_D\colon D\to Q$ with $R=\{(b,d):\pi_B(b)=\pi_D(d)\}$. One obtains $Q$ by quotienting $B$ by $\{b:(b,1)\in R\}$ and identifying it with the corresponding quotient of $D$. Closure under both multiplication conventions forces $Q$ abelian: products of $(b,d),(b',d')\in R$ give $\pi_B(bb')=\pi_D(dd')=\pi_B(b'b)$, and the projection onto $B$ is surjective.

Every finite subgroup $B$ of the unit quaternions admits an automorphism $\alpha$ inducing inversion on $B^{\mathrm{ab}}$. For a cyclic group use inversion. A binary dihedral group has presentation $a^{2m}=1,\ b^2=a^m,\ bab^{-1}=a^{-1}$. Its abelianization has exponent two for $m$ even. For $m$ odd the abelianization is cyclic of order four generated by $b$, and the automorphism $a\mapsto a,\ b\mapsto b^{-1}$ inverts it. The exceptional groups are the binary tetrahedral, octahedral and icosahedral groups. In each case the central element $-1$ is a commutator in the quaternion subgroup of order eight, so abelianization agrees with that of the corresponding rotation group $A_4,S_4,A_5$. For $A_4$, odd conjugation in $S_4$ inverts the quotient of order three, and this conjugation lifts using the binary octahedral overgroup. For $S_4$ or $A_5$, inversion is already the identity on the abelianization. This exhausts the [finite unit-quaternion groups](https://jvoight.github.io/quat/quat-book-v0.9.14.pdf).

As $Q$ is abelian, $\pi_B(\alpha(b))=\pi_B(b)^{-1}$. Consequently
$$
(b,d)\longmapsto(\alpha(b^{-1}),d)
$$
maps $R$ to the same fiber product in $B^{\mathrm{op}}\times D$. It is a bijective homomorphism, proving that the abstract groups agree. The concrete regular subgroups can have different left and right actions.

## GP-0951. Polynomial bit-time recovery for generic rational data without exceptional factors

**Theorem.** Given a rational numerical Gram matrix of a faithful generic finite real orbit without four-dimensional quaternionic irreducibles, one can recover its regular action and multiplication table deterministically in polynomial bit time. The generic promise is the one used above, together with avoidance of accidental inner-product equalities separately on each nonzero projected irreducible orbit. This extra condition holds generically: in each isotype fix the positive singular values of the seed matrix and its right singular basis, then vary the left singular frame. Each nonzero projected seed direction can thereby be varied over its whole sphere. The finite list of forbidden quadratic equalities is proper on each such sphere. The result concerns the generic rational recovery question and does not extend to arbitrary isolated nongeneric models.

Let $K$ be the rational Gram matrix, in arbitrary vertex order. Each distinct positive eigenvalue $\lambda$ gives one real irreducible projected orbit. Its Gram matrix is $K P_\lambda$, where $P_\lambda$ is the spectral projector of $K$. Indeed, for the matrix $Z$ with the observed orbit points as columns, $(ZZ^{\mathsf T})Z=ZK$, so the same spectral polynomial projects the column image. If $P(t)$ is the squarefree characteristic polynomial of $K$, use
$$
P_\lambda=\frac{P(t)/(t-\lambda)\vert_{t=K}}{(P(t)/(t-\lambda))\vert_{t=\lambda}} .
$$
All entries lie in the single real algebraic number field $\mathbb Q(\lambda)$, of degree at most the matrix order. One can compute and distinguish these projectors by polynomial-time rational characteristic polynomial, root isolation and polynomial arithmetic. Deduplicate each projected cloud by zero squared distance. Choose one observed basepoint $x$; in a projected cloud denote its image again by $x$.

For each value $c$ of the inner product with $x$ on a projected cloud, form the vector sum of its shell. In an irreducible representation of image group $J$, genericity makes the shell consist precisely of the $g x$ for which $S_g=(g+g^*)/2$ has one fixed value. The inverse map preserves that shell and cancels the skew parts of the operator sum. Thus its vector sum is $|\mathrm{shell}|\cdot S_g x$. As the real linear span of $J$ is $M_a(D)$, the shell sums span the images of $x$ under all self-adjoint operators. In quaternion, complex or real coordinates this subspace is exactly $\{y:x^*y\in\mathbb R\}$. To see sufficiency, rotate $x$ to the first coordinate, then make the corresponding first column the prescribed vector and fill the first row by adjunction. Its real codimension is $\dim_{\mathbb R}D-1$, at most three.

Fix any observed target vertex $z$. On each projected cloud a true transporter $x\mapsto z$ is an isometry that maps each shell sum at $x$ to the corresponding shell sum at the projection of $z$. Choose a basis from those sums and extend it by at most three projected cloud points. Enumerate possible images of the added basis vectors among projected points. Each choice determines a linear map, and exact Gram tests verify whether it is an isometry that maps the projected cloud onto itself and sends the projected basepoint correctly. For irreducible $M_1(\mathbb C)$ use only determinant $+1$ isometries, because the image acts on its regular polygon by rotations. The regular polygon is detected from the equal two nearest distances from a projected vertex, which distinguishes it from the alternating unequal gaps of the generic dihedral projected cloud. For every other permitted irreducible, the point-fixer proof above gives trivial stabilizer. Consequently this search yields exactly the true projected transporter, in all cases. For each $z$ combine the resulting tuple of actions by locating the unique observed vertex having the prescribed projected images. Uniqueness holds because the positive covariance spaces span the observed cloud; the algorithm needs no joint number field. Repeating this for every $z$ gives the regular permutations; composition, read at the basepoint, gives their multiplication table.

There are at most cubically many candidate basis completions on a block, and polynomially many blocks, points and shell computations. Shell comparisons, spans, basis selection, image tests and matching use only rational arithmetic or exact arithmetic in one number field at a time. Polynomial-degree rational matrix operations bound coefficient and denominator bits through minors. Resultants or standard gcd and isolating interval computations provide the needed exact tests on each specified real root. This yields the claimed polynomial bit bound.
