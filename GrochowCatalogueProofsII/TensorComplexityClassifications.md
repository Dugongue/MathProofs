# Complexity classifications by ordinary tensor isomorphism

## GP-0171 · Nonsymmetric forms with nonsemisimple asymmetry

**Theorem.** Over every finite field, tensor equivalence under products of the groups preserving supplied bilinear forms is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to ordinary tensor isomorphism. The hardness already holds for nondegenerate nonsymmetric forms whose asymmetry operators have nontrivial Jordan blocks of length two and repeated primary factors. It holds when the form in each mode is a fixed function of that mode dimension. The reduction preserves and recovers the ground-field transporter.

For the upper reduction, the tensor together with the supplied bilinear forms is a fixed-signature tensor system of the kind handled by the Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk compiler. Two such systems are isomorphic exactly when there are simultaneous variable changes carrying the displayed tensor and each displayed form to its mate. For the same fixed form in the pair, these variable changes are precisely its isometries. The argument also gives the upper bound for degenerate forms.

For the converse, first restrict a nonzero input tensor to its three contraction supports. Equivalence in the original formats is preserved, because a factor isomorphism of equal-dimensional supports extends to an ambient one, and any ambient transporter carries each support to its mate. Thus each input tensor may be assumed concise and paired support dimensions must match.

Write $F$ for the ground field. Take $E=F$ if $|F|>3$, and choose $\lambda\in F^\times$ with $\lambda\ne\lambda^{-1}$. Over $F_3$ instead take a root of $x^2+x+2$ and $E=F_9$, and over $F_2$ a root of $x^3+x+1$ and $E=F_8$. In each of these two cases the polynomial is irreducible and its normalized reciprocal is a different polynomial. Thus $\lambda$ and $\lambda^{-1}$ belong to distinct Frobenius orbits. These small field extensions have constant degree.

For a mode of concise dimension $n$, let $X=E^n\oplus E^n$ viewed over $F$, and put
$$
A(x,y)=(\lambda x+y,\lambda y),\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\ A&0\end{pmatrix}
$$
on $X\oplus X$, using any fixed coordinate bases over $F$. Then $B$ is invertible, and its asymmetry $B^{-1}B^\top$ is $\operatorname{diag}(A^{-1},A^\top)$. Since the two summands have relatively prime minimal polynomials, an isometry of $B$, which necessarily commutes with its asymmetry, preserves each half. Such isometries are precisely $\operatorname{diag}(P,P^{-\top})$ for $P\in\operatorname{GL}_F(X)$ commuting with $A$. This follows by multiplying the blocks in $G^\top BG=B$.

Inside the first $X$ take the marked subspace consisting of $F^n$ in its first $E^n$ coordinate. Embed the concise tensor in these three subspaces, supplying the form $B$ in each mode. Every original factor map $P_0\in\operatorname{GL}_n(F)$ extends to $P=\operatorname{diag}(P_0,P_0)$ on $X$, acting by the same coefficients over $E$, and hence to an isometry of $B$. Conversely a transporter of the embedded tensors must take the contraction support in each mode onto its mate. Those supports are exactly the marked $F^n$'s by conciseness. Restricting the isometry matrices to these $F$-subspaces gives a ground-field input transporter. In particular, ambient field extension in the two small characteristic cases creates no false positives. All sizes and bit times are polynomial. ## GP-0080 · Morita equivalence with arbitrary radicals

**Theorem.** For unital finite-dimensional algebras given by structure constants over a finite field $F$, $F$-linear Morita equivalence is ordinary tensor-isomorphism complete. Both directions are deterministic polynomial reductions for each fixed finite field. In a variable finite field there is a deterministic lower reduction, and the upper reduction is Las Vegas with polynomial expected bit time. From a transporter returned for the upper tensor instance one obtains an explicit Morita equivalence bimodule in polynomial bit time. The radical length and its representation are unrestricted.

Compute the Jacobson radical $J(A)$, and compute a matrix decomposition of $A/J(A)$ over its finite extension fields. These are the standard finite-algebra decomposition algorithms, with finite-field factoring as the randomized step for variable fields. In each simple block choose one rank-one matrix idempotent. Their sum $\bar e$ lifts to an idempotent $e$ of $A$. A polynomial lift is obtained by repeated application of $u\mapsto 3u^2-2u^3$ to any lift of $\bar e$, since the error $u^2-u$ squares at each iteration and belongs initially to $J(A)$. This identity is univariate and works in every characteristic.

The image of $AeA$ is all of $A/J(A)$, so $AeA$ contains an element congruent to $1$ modulo $J(A)$ and hence a unit. Thus $e$ is full. The algebra $A_b=eAe$ is basic, with semisimple quotient a product of fields, and is Morita equivalent to $A$. For finite-dimensional algebras the basic algebra is unique up to $F$-algebra isomorphism in its $F$-linear Morita class: the Morita category identifies the nonisomorphic indecomposable projective covers of the simple objects, and the opposite endomorphism algebra of the direct sum of one of each recovers the basic algebra. This reconstruction uses the regular module decomposed into primitive-idempotent left ideals, with a single term from each isoclass. Therefore $A,B$ are Morita equivalent exactly when $A_b,B_b$ are isomorphic. Compute corner structure constants by linear algebra and give the multiplication tensors to ordinary TI. A multiplication-tensor transporter $L(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$ determines a unital algebra isomorphism $x\mapsto P(1)^{-1}L(x)Q(1)^{-1}$. Both displayed inverses exist: multiplication by the corresponding element is bijective by the transporter and thus has a one-sided inverse, and injectivity gives the other side. From the isomorphism $A_b\to B_b$ form $Ae_A\otimes_{A_b}e_BB$, with the left $A_b$-action on $e_BB$ twisted by that isomorphism. The tensor quotient and its $A,B$ actions are ordinary linear algebra on spaces of polynomial dimension.

For hardness use the project tagged acyclic quiver encoding of an ordinary three-tensor, with a scalar bypass path and a relation equating that path to the tensor in the main path. The proof of functorial tensor reductions gives a polynomial-sized unital algebra $A_T$ with $A_T\cong A_U$ exactly when $T\cong U$. The positive-path ideal is nilpotent, because the quiver is acyclic, and the quotient by that ideal is a product of copies of $F$. Thus this ideal is the radical and $A_T$ is already basic. Morita equivalence of these outputs therefore forces actual algebra isomorphism, so the original tensor equivalence is reflected. This combines the basic-algebra theorem with the existing reduction and leaves the complexity of unrestricted TI itself open.
