# Tensor centroids, finite-field descent and matrix-space direct sums

## Centroid computation

Let $b:U\times V\to W$ be a bilinear map over a field $F$. Assume that the image spans $W$ and both kernels are zero. These conditions can be tested by the three flattening ranks. Define
$$
C(b)=\{(A,B,D):\ b(Au,v)=b(u,Bv)=D\,b(u,v)\text{ for every }u,v\}.
$$
Compute it by homogeneous linear equations on the three endomorphisms. It is a unital algebra under coordinatewise product. Applying one centroid element in each input slot shows that the two output endomorphisms commute: $b(Au,B'v)=DD'b(u,v)=D'Db(u,v)$. The spanning condition proves $DD'=D'D$. Either input coordinate is determined by the output coordinate using the zero kernel conditions, so both input coordinates commute as well. Thus $C(b)$ is commutative, and projection to each coordinate is injective. The centroid is transported by conjugation when the bilinear map changes by an isomorphism.

## GP-0162 · Exact geometric-to-rational orbit splitting in an étale ensemble

**Theorem.** In the ensemble of multiplication tensors of $F[x]/f$ for a uniformly drawn monic squarefree $f$ of degree $n\ge2$ over $F=\mathbb F_q$, all tensors are geometrically isomorphic. The rational orbits are indexed exactly by the multiset of irreducible degrees. For two independent draws, the probability of geometric equivalence with no rational transporter is
$$
1-\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}
\left(\frac{\prod_{d=1}^n\binom{I_q(d)}{m_d(\lambda)}}{q^n-q^{n-1}}\right)^2,\qquad
I_q(d)=\frac1d\sum_{a\mid d}\mu(a)q^{d/a}.
$$
It is at least $1-\min(1,4/n)$, uniformly in $q$. In addition there is a deterministic polynomial bit-time orbit decision algorithm on every source from this ensemble against every target, even after an arbitrary basis change in each factor. The algorithm needs no polynomial factoring.

For multiplication $b$ of a finite commutative unital algebra $A$, its centroid identifies with $A$ acting by multiplication in all coordinates. Use $v=1$ and then $u=1$ in the defining identities to obtain $A_{\text{coord}}=B_{\text{coord}}=D$ as linear maps, and set $u=1$ again to see that $D$ acts by multiplication by $D(1)$. Thus equivalent tensors have isomorphic algebras. Conversely any algebra isomorphism gives an equivalence of multiplication tensors. An étale algebra splits as $\overline F^n$ over the algebraic closure. Over $F$ it is a product of fields whose degrees are the irreducible degrees of $f$. This yields the orbit classification and shows that the geometric orbit splits for every $q,n$ in the given range, since types $n$ and $1+(n-1)$ both occur.

If $m_d$ denotes the multiplicity of degree $d$, the number of squarefree polynomials of this type is precisely $\prod_d\binom{I_q(d)}{m_d}$ by unique factorization. The total squarefree count for $n\ge2$ is $q^n-q^{n-1}$, for example from the generating function $(1-qt^2)/(1-qt)$. Two independent draws have the same type with the stated sum of squares. Since $dI_q(d)\le q^d$, a type has probability at most $((1-q^{-1})z_\lambda)^{-1}$, where $z_\lambda=\prod_d d^{m_d}m_d!$. One has $z_\lambda\ge n/2$. Indeed, the product of a list of integers at least two dominates their sum, apart from the empty list, by induction. The factorial of the number of ones dominates the number of ones when nonzero. Combining these two bounds dominates the larger subtotal, at least $n/2$. The cases with one empty subtotal satisfy the same inequality directly. The collision probability is no larger than the greatest type probability.

To decide an arbitrary target of the same format, reject if any flattening is deficient. Compute its centroid $C$ and reject unless $\dim_F C=n$ and the $q$-power Frobenius $F_C$ on $C$ is injective. The latter test is a matrix rank test because $C$ is commutative and Frobenius is $F$-linear. It is equivalent to reducedness for a finite-dimensional commutative algebra. Since $F$ is finite, the reduced algebra is a product of finite fields. The action of $C$ is faithful in each mode by the centroid calculation. For $C=\prod_i E_i$, every $E_i$ must act nontrivially on each mode. That mode has dimension $\sum_i[E_i:F]=n$, so each factor acts on an $E_i$-space of dimension one. Identify the three modules with $C$. The original bilinear map is $C$-linear in each input and therefore multiplication by some $c\in C$ on the ordinary product. Its image spans the third mode precisely if $c$ is a unit. Thus every accepted target is a multiplication tensor of its computed centroid.

Read the degrees of the factors from $h_k=\dim_F\ker(F_C^k-I)$ for $1\le k\le n$. Over a factor of degree $d$, that kernel has dimension $\gcd(k,d)$. The matrix $M_{kd}=\gcd(k,d)$ is invertible over the rationals because $M=L\operatorname{diag}(\varphi(1),\ldots,\varphi(n))L^\top$, where $L_{ke}=1_{e\mid k}$ is unit lower triangular. Solve $h=M m$ over the rationals for the factor multiplicity vector. Integer matrix entries and all dimensions have polynomial bit size. Frobenius and its powers use polynomially many ring multiplications and linear operations. Apply the invariant to the source as well, so no witness polynomial or hidden basis is needed. This proves the decision algorithm at its exact all-target scope. ## GP-0239 · Intrinsic direct-sum factors of a matrix space

**Theorem.** For a matrix space $S\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(U,V)$ over any finite field, compute its unique finest embedded direct-sum decomposition after removing its common kernel and unused output directions. Here a direct sum has independent summand spaces $S_i$ supported on disjoint pairs $U_i\to V_i$, and all cross-actions vanish. The algorithm is deterministic polynomial time over each fixed finite field and uniform Las Vegas polynomial bit time over variable fields. A transporter exists exactly when there is a matching of the intrinsic indecomposable pieces by local left-right transporters, along with equality of the two removed dimensions. Finding a decomposition is at least as hard as squarefree polynomial factoring over variable fields under a deterministic polynomial search reduction.

Let $K=\bigcap_{M\in S}\ker M$, $W=\sum_{M\in S}\operatorname{im} M$, and use $b:S\times (U/K)\to W$ given by evaluation. This is concise. The centroid of $b$ is equipped with all three faithful representations. Its primitive idempotents are unique up to relabeling, as in every finite-dimensional commutative algebra. A primitive idempotent $e$ projects all three modes, and $b(e x,(1-e)y)=e(1-e)b(x,y)=0$. Therefore the images of the idempotents give the desired matrix-space factors. Conversely the projections of any embedded independent-space decomposition satisfy the centroid equations. Such a decomposition refines if and only if one of those projections is nonprimitive. This proves intrinsic uniqueness. Dimension-zero cases have no nonzero summands.

To compute the projections, obtain the radical of the centroid as the kernel of a sufficiently large Frobenius power. Factor the reduced quotient into fields by the finite-field algebra decomposition algorithm. Use orthogonal idempotents in that quotient and lift them uniquely across the nilpotent commutative radical. The update $u\mapsto3u^2-2u^3$ squares the idempotent error ideal and gives a lift in logarithmically many iterations. Uniqueness follows because a nilpotent idempotent is zero and two commuting idempotents congruent modulo a nilpotent ideal have zero differences on each other's complements. This also forces orthogonality and sum one of the lifted idempotents. Gaussian elimination gives their three projected spaces. A reduced transporter must permute the primitive projections, and a choice of local transporters over a matching gives the direct-sum witness. It extends over complementary ambient subspaces by any maps of the prescribed dimensions. This derives a transporter recovery theorem without requiring canonical choices of bases or a separate oracle for the matching labels.

For the factoring lower bound, give the space of left multiplication operators in $A=F[x]/f$ for a monic squarefree input $f$. Its evaluation tensor is multiplication after identifying $S=A$. The computation of its embedded direct summands recovers the primitive factors $e_i A$. On each such summand compute the minimal polynomial of multiplication by the residue class of $x$. This polynomial is precisely the corresponding irreducible factor of $f$. All reductions are polynomial in the original bit size. The decomposition considered here splits the matrix-space mode itself. A block diagonal tuple in which every indexed slice can have several diagonal blocks has a different ordered-tuple decomposition, governed by primitive idempotents in the usually noncommutative endomorphism algebra and its Krull–Schmidt theorem. The intrinsic centroid decomposition is the one for independent slice spaces.
