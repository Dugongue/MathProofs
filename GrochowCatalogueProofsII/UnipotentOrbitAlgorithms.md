# Constructive unipotent tensor orbits

## GP-0288 · Coefficient networks with explicit finite unipotent actions

**Theorem.** Let $F=\mathbb F_q$ with an explicit field representation of characteristic $p$. Given generators of a unipotent subgroup $G\leq\operatorname{GL}(V)$ and two vectors $v,w\in V$, one can decide orbit equivalence and compute a transporter, stabilizer generators and the exact transporter count in deterministic polynomial bit time in the matrix input. Thus the coefficient-network problem has these algorithms when its explicit group generators act unipotently on the displayed finite-field coefficient space. This includes two-step groups with supplied commutator data, without needing a separate calculation from those data.

The generator promise guarantees a simultaneous upper unitriangular basis over $F$. One proof uses that a finite unipotent subgroup is a $p$-group, and every nonzero $F$-representation of a finite $p$-group has a nonzero invariant vector, by induction using a central element of order $p$. The fixed space is the intersection of the kernels of generator minus identity. Take a nonzero fixed vector and repeat on the quotient to construct a full invariant flag. Convert all generators to its basis.

Use the ambient upper unitriangular group's central filtration. Order its strict upper diagonals by increasing distance from the diagonal, and within each diagonal order entries and their $\mathbb F_p$-basis coordinates. Prefix-zero conditions define a chain $H_i$ of normal subgroups with successive quotients $\mathbb F_p$ on the next digit. Indeed modulo the next strict upper diagonal, a prescribed diagonal adds entrywise and conjugation is trivial.

There is an induced generating sequence for $G$ with at most one pivot per ambient digit. To construct it, sift each input generator from early to late digits. At its first nonzero digit, cancel by the matching power of the pivot there when present. If absent, insert the residual as that pivot. Repeat sifting pivot powers $g_i^p$ and commutators $[g_i,g_j]$ until no new pivot appears. This needs at most $\dim_F(V)(\dim_F(V)-1)[F\colon \mathbb F_p]/2$ insertions. One pass checks polynomially many matrix products and powers, with exponents handled in binary. All inserted elements are in $G$ and all input generators belong to the generated subgroup. At termination write the ordered pivot list as $g_1,\ldots,g_s$. The suffix groups $G_i=\langle g_i,\ldots,g_s\rangle$ admit independent exponents $0,\ldots,p-1$ in order. To verify this, induct upward from the trivial suffix. Sifting a pivot power rewrites it with deeper pivots, and a commutator rewrites with pivots strictly deeper than both of its inputs. Hence $g_i$ normalizes the next suffix and its $p$-th power belongs to it. The first digit distinguishes the $p$ cosets and proves uniqueness. This certifies the sequence and the order at each level.

The following operation applies to this or any induced sequence. Given a homomorphism $\phi:G\to A$ to an additive $\mathbb F_p$-space, solve for any target image using the span of the generator images and multiply the corresponding powers. To generate the kernel with another induced sequence, scan the pivots in reverse order. At pivot $g_i$, if $\phi(g_i)$ is in the span of the deeper pivot images, choose a correction $h_i$ from deeper pivots with $\phi(h_i)=-\phi(g_i)$. Retain $g_ih_i$ as a kernel pivot. Otherwise omit $g_i$. A nonzero first exponent at an omitted pivot cannot be canceled by deeper images. At an included pivot every first exponent can be removed using the new pivot with exactly the same leading digit. This proves that the retained sequence describes the kernel, in the same ambient filtration.

Write the invariant vector flag in descending order $V=F^0V\supset\cdots\supset F^\ell V=0$, so each action is trivial on $F^iV/F^{i+1}V$. Maintain $g_0v\equiv w\bmod F^iV$ and an induced sequence for $K_i=\{h\in G:hv\equiv v\bmod F^iV\}$. At the next step use
$$
\phi_i: K_i\to F^iV/F^{i+1}V,\qquad h\longmapsto hv-v.
$$
It is a homomorphism because $h$ acts trivially on the indicated quotient. Test whether $g_0^{-1}w-v$ belongs to the image. A negative answer is exact. Otherwise take an image preimage $h$, replace $g_0$ by $g_0h$, and use the kernel procedure for $K_{i+1}$. At $F^\ell V=0$ this gives an exact transporter and a point stabilizer sequence. The coset then has $p^{s'}$ elements where $s'$ is the final stabilizer sequence length. Its repeated parameters are bounded by the ambient unitriangular rank throughout, so the algorithm remains polynomial in bit size even if $p$ varies.

## GP-0289 · Canonical filtrations for arbitrary finite nilpotent residual algebras

Suppose the residual action is $G=\prod_{j=1}^d(1+J_j)$ on $V=\bigotimes_j V_j$, where each $J_j$ is an explicitly given nilpotent associative subalgebra of $\operatorname{End}_{F}(V_j)$. Let $I=\sum_j \widetilde J_j\,\prod_{i\ne j}(F\,1+\widetilde J_i)$ inside the algebra generated by the lifted factor matrices. The lifted factor algebras commute. If $J_j^{n_j}=0$, then $I^{1+\sum_j(n_j-1)}=0$, since in every word of that length some factor occurs at least $n_j$ times. The canonical descending filtration $F^kV=I^kV$ depends only on the residual algebra action, and each group element acts trivially on its graded quotients. Compute it by starting with $V$ and taking the sum of images under each displayed prime-field or field basis element of each $\widetilde J_j$ at each step. The latter procedure gives $I^{k+1}V$ from $I^kV$, since this is closed under the full algebra.

In this case an initial induced sequence in the **source group** is immediate. Use the layers $1+J_j^i$, with each basis refinement of $J_j^i/J_j^{i+1}$ over the prime field. The lift $1+x$ of a basis vector $x$ has the required leading entry. These quotients are central since commutators with $1+J_j$ have depth at least $i+1$. Any vector of a layer is the reduction of a product of these lifts, and successive layers give all elements, with unique choices of exponents. Take the product of the factor sequences. Run the preceding linear image and induced-kernel steps along $I^kV$, retaining entries as tuples in the factor groups. This avoids any loss of stabilizer multiplicity from the kernel of the tensor representation and returns actual separate factors. All powers or inverses are computed in the displayed matrix algebras with polynomial bit complexity. The number of linear image problems is at most the nilpotence bound above, or at most $\dim_F V$ nonzero steps.

The algorithms address nilpotent residual actions once the residual groups and their action are given. The polynomial bounds are in the explicit coefficient-space input, not a list of exponentially many group elements.
