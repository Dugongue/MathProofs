# Persistence, mapping degrees, and spherical boundary extension

The results below address the nilmanifold question for all finite sources, extend the two-stage linear estimate to every fixed component, and give mapping-degree and manifold boundary constructions. They build on the preceding proofs where identified. [Earlier proofs](ManinGeometry.md)

## 1. Polynomial persistence bounds for maps to nilmanifolds

**Theorem.** Fix a finite piecewise Euclidean complex $X$ and a compact nilmanifold $N=\Gamma\backslash G$, with a left-invariant metric on its simply connected nilpotent cover. There are constants $C,D$ depending only on $X$ and $N$ with the following property, simultaneously in all homological degrees. A class in the space of $L$-Lipschitz maps $X\to N$ that vanishes in the full mapping space already vanishes through maps of Lipschitz constant at most $C(L+1)^D$. For a finite parameter pair $(Z,B)$, every component of $Z$ meeting $B$, a continuous family with $L$-Lipschitz slices on $B$ is homotopic relative to $B$ to a family with this polynomial bound on all slices. For an arbitrary continuous filling, the proof uses only its representation in the space of starting points. This supplies a group-dependent polynomial replacement for the free-loop estimate in Question 1.4, as well as an extension to all fixed finite source complexes. [Block–Manin–Weinberger, Question 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S1.SS3)

It suffices to treat a connected $X$, with a vertex $x_0$, fixed paths in its one-skeleton, and a fixed finite set of based loop generators $\gamma_i$. Over the universal cover $\widetilde X$, a map in the component represented by $\rho:\pi_1(X)\to\Gamma$ has a lift $\widetilde f$ satisfying $\widetilde f(hy)=\rho(h)\widetilde f(y)$. Lifts with this same $\rho$ differ by left multiplication by the discrete simultaneous centralizer $C_\Gamma(\rho)$. Evaluation at a chosen lift of $x_0$ defines a continuous map to $E_\rho=C_\Gamma(\rho)\backslash G$. Locally in the uniform topology a nearby map lifts to a nearby lift with the same representation, using the unique short geodesics in $N$ and their lifts. This proves the asserted continuity and shows that the conjugacy class of the representation is locally constant.

### Canonical equivariant representatives

For any vertices $g_i$ of a simplex in $G$, interpolate using the unique $g$ solving
$$
\sum_i t_i\log(g^{-1}g_i)=0
$$
for the barycentric weights $t_i$. Existence, uniqueness, and polynomial dependence in logarithmic coordinates follow by the lower central series. In layer $j$, the left side is $-(\log g)_j+\sum_i t_i(\log g_i)_j$ plus a polynomial in lower layers, by Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff. Solve for the layers in order. This interpolation respects faces and commutes with left multiplication. In particular it is a concrete finite-measure version of the exponential barycenter. [Barycenters in free nilpotent Lie groups](https://doi.org/10.1137/23M159024X)

For each vertex of $X$ choose a lift, including the chosen base vertex. At a start $x\in G$ assign $\rho(h)x$ to the translate by $h$ of each chosen vertex lift. Interpolation on simplices gives a continuous equivariant map, and hence a section of evaluation over $E_\rho$. It also gives a canonicalization homotopy on any original map, using the pointwise interpolation $g\exp(t\log(g^{-1}g'))$ from the old lift to the canonical one. Both operations are independent of the permitted changes of lifts. In particular every map with the designated representation is homotopic to its canonical representative, and $E_\rho$ is a model of that component.

These operations have fixed polynomial Lipschitz bounds on bounded sets of logarithmic coordinates. To see this uniformly for low maps, put $R=L+1$ and left-translate the target coordinates by $x^{-1}$. Every generator displacement $x^{-1}\rho(\gamma_i)x$ has distance from the identity at most $CL$, by lifting its based loop. All words needed for the finitely many chosen simplex representatives therefore have polynomially bounded logarithms. The old map on those simplices also has logarithm bounded by a polynomial in $R$, since their points can be reached from the chosen base vertex by paths of uniformly bounded length. A path in $G$ of speed at most one and length $S$ has logarithmic coordinates bounded by a polynomial in $S+1$, by integrating its left-invariant velocity equation one lower central layer at a time. The conversion between coordinate derivatives and left-invariant velocities is polynomial in either direction by the same triangular Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formulas. Consequently on the old low maps the coordinate Lipschitz bounds, the interpolation and homotopy Lipschitz bounds on each simplex, and the bounds after projecting to $N$ are all polynomial in $R$. Taking the finitely many simplices together gives the assertion for the polyhedral metric. The same reasoning bounds canonical maps whenever the generator displacement logarithms are bounded by a polynomial in $R$, with no hypothesis on the start $x$ itself.

### Polynomial contractions of simultaneous conjugacy orbits

Consider a component with at least one low map. Conjugate its chosen lattice representation by an element of $\Gamma$ so that the lift of the base vertex of one such map lies in a fixed compact fundamental set. Then all the elements $\rho(\gamma_i)$ have distance at most $C R$ from the identity. Let $a=(a_i)$ be their logarithms. Choose rational coordinates adapted to the lower central series. The $a_i$ have bounded denominators independent of $R$. A Mal'cev basis for $\Gamma$ writes lattice elements as fixed ordered products of integer powers, and the logarithm of this product is a polynomial with fixed rational coefficients. The numerators are polynomially bounded by the preceding metric estimate. [Mal'cev coordinates and polynomial operations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03888)

Write $\mathfrak g_j$ for the lower central filtration, with $\mathfrak g_{c+1}=0$. Define $H_j$ to be the stabilizer of the tuple $a$ modulo $\mathfrak g_{j+1}$ under simultaneous adjoint action. Thus $H_1=G$ and $H_c=C_G(\rho)$. Its Lie algebra is
$$
\mathfrak h_j=\{v:[v,a_i]\in\mathfrak g_{j+1}\text{ for all }i\}.
$$
Indeed on the truncated algebra $\exp(\operatorname{ad}v)-1$ is $\operatorname{ad}v$ times an invertible polynomial in that operator. For $j\ge2$, the layer-$j$ change of the tuple under $\exp v\in H_{j-1}$ is the linear map
$$
v\longmapsto ([v,a_i]\bmod\mathfrak g_{j+1})_i.
$$
Choose a linear right inverse on its image. It can be chosen rational, with entries bounded by a fixed polynomial in $R$. First choose a rational nullspace basis for $\mathfrak h_{j-1}$ by a nonzero minor. Then choose a nonzero minor of the layer map in that basis to solve on the image. Each matrix dimension is fixed and each nonzero minor of a rational matrix of polynomial height has inverse of polynomial height.

For a point $b$ in the tuple orbit of $a$, find $v_2$ by this inverse from the layer-two part of $b-a$. The residual $\operatorname{Ad}_{\exp(-v_2)}b$ is in the orbit of $H_2$ through $a$. Indeed it lies in the $G$ orbit and agrees with $a$ modulo $\mathfrak g_3$, so any element taking $a$ to it belongs to $H_2$. Repeat through layer $c$. The product $s(b)=\exp(v_2)\cdots\exp(v_c)$ satisfies $\operatorname{Ad}_{s(b)}a=b$ and $s(a)=e$. The construction is polynomial and continuous in $b$, and its coordinate values on tuples with logarithms polynomially bounded in $R$ are themselves bounded by a fixed power of $R$. The bounds follow because each step applies one polynomially bounded linear map and one fixed adjoint polynomial. This gives a global section of the simultaneous orbit with a quantitative bound.

For a start $x$ let $b=\operatorname{Ad}_{x^{-1}}a$. Then $x s(b)\in C_G(\rho)$, and $(C_\Gamma(\rho)x s(b),b)$ are product coordinates on $E_\rho$. Move the orbit coordinate from $b$ to $a$ through $\operatorname{Ad}_{\exp((1-t)\log s(b))}a$ while keeping the centralizer coordinate fixed. In the resulting start homotopy the displacements of all generators stay polynomially bounded whenever the initial tuple was polynomially bounded. Thus any low family can be canonicalized, then moved into the canonical section over the fiber at $a$, with a polynomial cutoff. The same operations, without a quantitative bound, apply to every continuous family. At the centralizer fiber the displacement tuple is always $a$, so the canonical section there satisfies the cutoff uniformly.

To prove the relative family assertion, use any original family on $Z$, choose the bounded representative separately on each component, and apply the section and starting-point homotopy to move the whole family to the centralizer fiber. The resulting family over $Z$ is bounded. The entire homotopy over $B$ is bounded by the same fixed polynomial. Attach a parameter collar to $B$ carrying this homotopy, and use the homotopy extension property of the parameter pair to transfer the new family back relative to the prescribed maps on $B$. One can make the transfer by precomposing the bounded family on the collared complex with a map of pairs extending the inclusion of the outside copy of $B$. Such a map is homotopy inverse to collar collapse, by the homotopy extension property. Precomposition affects only the parameter variable. This proves the assertion for finite CW pairs. Equivalently, for homology alone, apply evaluation to a bounding chain, project it along the product to the fiber, and append the bounded prisms on the original cycle. The exponent is independent of the homological degree.

### Linear bounds for finite graphs in class two

If $X$ is a finite graph and $N$ has nilpotency class at most two, the conclusion holds with $D=1$. In a connected graph, collapsing a spanning tree gives a bouquet of circles. There are fixed homotopy inverse Lipschitz maps, with homotopies whose slices have a uniform Lipschitz constant. These maps transfer families and sublevels with fixed factors, so work on the bouquet. Its lifted maps are tuples of paths from a common $x$ to $\rho(\gamma_i)x$. Canonicalize each path by retaining its initial segment and replacing the rest by a canonical path with length and speed bounded by the displacement of that remaining segment plus a constant. A fixed reparametrization of the two pieces controls all slices by $C(L+1)$. The starts of the resulting canonical tuple lie above the simultaneous conjugacy orbit, whose logarithmic coordinates in class two are the affine tuple $a_i+[a_i,\log x]$. The intersection of this affine orbit with the weighted coordinate box defined by the low-loop bounds is convex. Project orbit coordinates into this intersection while keeping the centralizer coordinate fixed. Canonical paths give a bounded family over the projected starts. Attaching the parameter collar as above preserves the original boundary data. This extends the linear estimate for class-two free loops to every fixed finite graph. [Canonical paths and the class-two loop bound](ManinGeometry.md#4-based-and-free-persistence-on-nilmanifolds)

### A linear cutoff in each fixed free-loop component

For every compact nilmanifold, each fixed free-loop component has a linear cutoff, with a constant allowed to depend on the component. Infinite bars within a fixed component also have uniformly bounded births. Fix the lattice element and use the orbit and product start coordinates above. The orbit in logarithmic coordinates is semialgebraic by elimination, since it is the image of the polynomial adjoint map on the Lie algebra. The weighted maximum of the absolute logarithmic coordinates, raised to the inverse layer weights, defines a continuous semialgebraic height $h$. By Hardt’s theorem there is $T$ such that $h$ is trivial over $(T,\infty)$, or the orbit has bounded height. For $R>T$, in the trivialization move the height coordinate linearly to $\min(h,R)$ and use the identity below $R$. This gives a deformation relative to the sublevel at $R$. Use it in the product start space with the centralizer coordinate fixed. Canonical loop speed is linear in $h+1$, and canonicalization of a low loop stays at $C(L+1)$. Clamping at the larger of $T+1$ and a fixed multiple of $L+1$ proves the linear cutoff. Clamping instead at $T+1$ proves the birth bound. [Hardt’s semialgebraic triviality theorem](https://doi.org/10.2307/2374240) · [Weighted canonical paths](ManinGeometry.md#canonical-paths-and-starting-points-of-lifts)

## 2. Arbitrarily large sharp persistence exponents in class three

For each pair of integers $m,s\ge1$ there is a compact class-three nilmanifold $N_{m,s}$ of dimension $(2s+1)m+1$ with the following properties, for any fixed Riemannian metric. In every homological degree, a class represented by loops of Lipschitz constant at most $L$ and vanishing in the full free-loop space already vanishes at $C(L+1)^{1+m/3}$. This exponent is sharp simultaneously in degrees
$$
s-1\le i\le s-1+m(s+1)+1.
$$
In degrees $0\le i<s-1$ the cutoff is $C(L+1)$. Thus the first homological degree exhibiting unbounded log-length finite bars can be prescribed, and the sharp cutoff exponent can be arbitrarily large in that degree even in class three. Coefficients can be taken in any field, or integral coefficients for the stated finite-death classes. The result strengthens the Engel and seven-dimensional estimates and applies directly to the free-loop part of Block–Manin–Weinberger Question 1.4. [Block–Manin–Weinberger](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S1.SS3)

### The groups and their conjugacy orbits

Take a totally real number field $K$ of degree $m$, and write $E=K\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R=\mathbb R^m$ using the real embeddings $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_m$, with coordinatewise multiplication. Such a field exists for every $m$. For $m\ge2$, choose a prime $\ell$ and a large positive integer $B$. The polynomial $\prod_{j=1}^m(x-\ell B j)-\ell$ is Eisenstein at $\ell$ and has one real root within distance 1 of each $\ell B j$. The last assertion follows because the product has opposite signs of absolute value bigger than $\ell$ at the two endpoints of each of these intervals. For $m=1$ use $\mathbb Q$.

Write $U=Q=E^s$, $P=E$, $V=P\oplus Q\oplus\mathbb R$, with central coordinate $w\in\mathbb R$. Use the pairing
$$
\langle u,q\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^s u_jq_j\right)
=\sum_{\nu=1}^m u^{(\nu)}\cdot q^{(\nu)} .
$$
For $u\in U$ define $A(u)$ on $V$ by $A(u)p=pu$ in $Q$, $A(u)q=2\langle u,q\rangle$ in the central coordinate, and $A(u)w=0$. These operators commute since $A(u)A(v)p=2\langle u,pv\rangle$ is symmetric in $u,v$. Each $A(u)^3=0$. The semidirect product $G=U\ltimes V$ has multiplication $(u,x)(a,b)=(u+a,x+e^{A(u)}b)$. Its lattice $\Gamma$ uses $\mathcal O_K$ in each of the $2s+1$ copies of $E$, and $\mathbb Z$ in the central coordinate. Indeed $I+A(u)+A(u)^2/2$ and its inverse preserve the lattice of $V$ for integral $u$, since the quadratic term has central coefficient $\langle u,pu\rangle$. One obtains a compact fundamental region by first reducing the $U$ coordinate and then reducing $V$ by its lattice. The two nontrivial derived layers of the lower central series are $Q\oplus\mathbb R$ and $\mathbb R$, since multiplication with $p=1$ is onto $Q$ and the trace pairing is nonzero. Put $N_{m,s}=\Gamma\backslash G$.

Use the fixed weighted gauge $\|u\|+\|p\|+\|q\|^{1/2}+|w|^{1/3}$ in semidirect coordinates. It is equivalent up to uniform multiplicative and additive constants to distance from the identity in a left-invariant Riemannian metric. The gauge also bounds the speeds of continuous choices of canonical paths from the identity up to the same type of constants. To apply the general gauge and path construction in logarithmic coordinates, use the coordinate change $(u,v)\mapsto(u,(I+A(u)/2+A(u)^2/6)v)$. The change and its inverse are triangular and preserve the indicated weights. A different metric on the compact quotient changes all function levels by fixed factors. [Weighted path construction](ManinGeometry.md#canonical-paths-and-starting-points-of-lifts)

For $\gamma=(a,(p,q_0,w_0))$ the orbit parametrized by $x\mapsto x^{-1}\gamma x$ has $a,p$ constant and is obtained by
$$
x=(u,v),\qquad x^{-1}\gamma x=(a,e^{-A(u)}(p,q_0,w_0)+(e^{A(a)}-I)e^{-A(u)}v).
$$
If $a\ne0$, the image of $e^{A(a)}-I$ equals the image of $A(a)$ and contains the center since $\langle a,-\rangle\ne0$. Therefore $w$ varies freely and $q$ varies over the affine space with translate $q_0$ and direction $pE^s+aE$. The orbit is affine. If $a=p=0$, the orbit is again affine, with $q=q_0$ and $w=w_0-2\langle u,q_0\rangle$. In the remaining case $a=0$ and $p\ne0$ in $K$, multiplication by $p$ on $E^s$ is invertible. The orbit is the graph over $q\in E^s$ given by
$$
q=q_0-pu,\qquad
w=w_0-2\langle u,q_0\rangle+\langle u,pu\rangle
=c+\sum_{\nu=1}^m\frac{\|q^{(\nu)}\|^2}{\sigma_\nu(p)},\qquad
c=w_0-\langle q_0,p^{-1}q_0\rangle .
$$
In this case $C_G(\gamma)=\{0\}\times V$, so its starting-point quotient is $C_\Gamma(\gamma)\backslash G=T^{m(s+1)+1}\times E^s$. In the affine cases the orbit map likewise gives a trivial bundle over the affine orbit with fiber $C_\Gamma(\gamma)\backslash C_G(\gamma)$. For the quotient description, a loop with deck multiplier $\gamma$ has a lift beginning at $x$ and ending at $\gamma x$, and discrete changes of lift preserving $\gamma$ give exactly $C_\Gamma(\gamma)$. The affine bundle is a product because the orbit is smoothly an affine space and the centralizer map admits local sections. Equivalently, right translation of the preimage by $G$ identifies the local maps with the submersion at the identity.

### Upper bound in all degrees

Put $R=L+1$. A low loop's start has displacement in a box with $\|a\|,\|p\|\le C R$, $\|q^{(\nu)}\|\le C R^2$ for each embedding, and $|w|\le C R^3$. In an affine orbit, project to the intersection of this convex box with the orbit and keep the fiber coordinate fixed. For the graph orbit, take the convex hull of the $q$-coordinates from the box intersected with the orbit, and project onto that hull. Each such graph has quadratic part of operator norm at most $C R^{m-1}$, since $p$ is an algebraic integer and
$$
1\le |\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb Q}(p)|
=\prod_{\nu=1}^m|\sigma_\nu(p)|,\qquad |\sigma_\nu(p)|\le C R .
$$
If $q=\sum_j\theta_j q_j$ is in the convex hull and $w=f(q)$, the difference between $f(q)$ and $\sum_j\theta_j f(q_j)$ is bounded in absolute value by $C R^{m-1}\sum_j\theta_j\|q_j-q\|^2$. Thus $|w|\le C R^{m+3}$ throughout the lifted hull and the resulting displacement gauge is at most $C R^{1+m/3}$. Projection fixes the original starts in each case.

Any finite singular filling decomposes into conjugacy components. Evaluate its loops at starts, apply this projection and use the canonical paths at the new starts. The resulting chain is a filling of the canonicalized original cycle at the specified upper level. The controlled homotopy from each original loop to its canonical path adds a prism along the boundary at Lipschitz level $O(L+1)$. For that homotopy, retain the initial part of the lifted path and replace the remaining part by a canonical path bounding the distance between endpoints of the unaltered part. The retained and replacement parts can each be allocated half the time, with a preliminary and final reparametrization. This proves the uniform all-degree upper bound.

### The matching lower bounds

By Dirichlet's unit theorem, the logarithms of the positive units form a full lattice in the hyperplane $\sum_\nu t_\nu=0$. The totally positive subgroup is finite index in the unit group, as the sign group is finite. Hence, for every $R\ge2$, there is a unit whose logarithmic embedding vector differs by $O(1)$ from $(-(m-1)\log R,\log R,\ldots,\log R)$. Fix an algebraic integer with first embedding negative and all others positive and multiply the unit by it, producing $p_R$. Such a fixed integer exists because $K$ is dense in $E$ as a rational vector space and multiplication by a positive integer clears denominators. Thus the first embedding of $p_R$ is negative of magnitude comparable to $R^{-(m-1)}$, and all others are positive of magnitude comparable to $R$. The argument includes $m=1$ by taking $p_R=-1$. [Dirichlet’s unit theorem](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2017fa/LectureNotes15.pdf)

Take $\gamma_R=(0,(p_R,0,w_R))$ with $w_R=\lceil R^{m+3}\rceil$. In the $q$-space, let $q^{(\nu)}=0$ for $\nu>1$ and $\|q^{(1)}\|=\sqrt{w_R|\sigma_1(p_R)|}$. This is an $(s-1)$-sphere of starts in the orbit chart at which $w=0$ and $\|q\|=O(R^2)$. Its canonical loops, with all torus starting-point coordinates, have Lipschitz constant $O(R)$. When $q^{(1)}=0$, the positive sign of the other embeddings forces $w\ge w_R$. Every loop at such a start has Lipschitz constant at least $cR^{1+m/3}$ for a uniform $c>0$. Below that level, the starting-point map followed by keeping its torus coordinate and normalizing $q^{(1)}$ takes values in $T^{m(s+1)+1}\times S^{s-1}$. On the displayed low sphere times the torus this is the identity.

For $s\ge2$, take the homology classes obtained by crossing the fundamental class of this sphere with arbitrary torus classes. For $s=1$, use the reduced degree-zero class given by the difference of the two points. In every degree $i=s-1,\ldots,s-1+m(s+1)+1$ this gives independent classes of rank $\binom{m(s+1)+1}{i-s+1}$, surviving below the specified level by the starting-point map. The sphere fills by its ball, crossed with the same torus cycles, because the $q$-space of starts is all of $E^s$. Over the ball the $w$ coordinate lies in $[0,w_R]$, so canonical loops on this filling have the matching $O(R^{1+m/3})$ Lipschitz bound. This proves sharpness and eventual death of each stated class.

### Linear bound in the lower homological degrees

It remains to treat $i<s-1$ in the graph orbit. Let a finite singular $i$-cycle be represented at level $L$, and evaluate its starts in the product of the centralizer torus with the $q$-space. Select the convex set of squared radii
$$
\bigl\{(x_1,\ldots,x_m):0\le x_\nu\le (2C R^2)^2,\
     |c+\textstyle\sum_\nu x_\nu/\sigma_\nu(p)|\le 2C R^3\bigr\}.
$$
Here $C$ is chosen to contain the starts of all original low loops with a factor of 2 to spare. The space above this set consists of choosing a vector of norm $\sqrt{x_\nu}$ in the $\nu$-th $s$-dimensional block. All points above it have displacement gauge $O(R)$.

Approximate the $q$-coordinates of the finitely many parameter simplices by piecewise affine maps, preserving identical face restrictions. Namely use a common sufficiently fine edgewise subdivision on the parameter simplices, interpolate their original values at vertices, and use the same tiny translation in each block across all simplices. The error can be made arbitrarily small, so the straight homotopy to this approximation remains in the enlarged box. After a generic tiny translation the finitely many affine pieces of each block avoid zero, since their dimension is at most $i<s$. Their positive cones have dimension at most $i+1<s$. Hence the normalized directions jointly omit a point of $S^{s-1}$. Contract the directions in the complement of that point while keeping all radii fixed, and then interpolate the squared radii to one fixed point of the above convex set, using the fixed directions. These homotopies keep the torus coordinates fixed and respect face equalities. Applying canonical loops gives a chain homotopy at level $C'R$ from the original cycle to a cycle over a single $q$-coordinate.

If the original cycle eventually bounds, its projection to the torus bounds there. The projected filling can be placed over the fixed $q$, where the canonical paths are still uniformly $O(R)$. This proves the linear result in all the asserted lower degrees. In affine orbits every degree already has this linear bound by convex projection.

### Other source spaces

The lower bounds also apply to the Lipschitz mapping space from any connected finite polyhedron $X$ with nonzero first Betti number. Pick a primitive class in $H^1(X;\mathbb Z)$, represent it by a Lipschitz map $r:X\to S^1$, and choose a Lipschitz representative $j:S^1\to X$ of a loop on which it evaluates to 1. The composition $rj$ is homotopic to the identity by a fixed Lipschitz homotopy. Precomposition by $r$ sends the low cycles and their high fillings into maps from $X$, with a fixed Lipschitz factor. If one of these cycles were to fill too early, restricting along $j$ and attaching the prism from $rj$ to the identity would give a loop filling at a fixed multiple of that level, contradicting the sharp lower bound.

The analogous discrete conjugator length can have arbitrarily large exponents already in class two. Here class-two loop spaces have the linear cutoff from affine real conjugacy orbits, whereas class three admits arbitrary exponents. [Bridson–Riley, Linear Diophantine equations and conjugator length in 2-step nilpotent groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01239)

## 3. Linear persistence on each fixed two-stage component

**Theorem.** Let $X$ be a finite simplicial complex and $Y$ a closed simply connected Riemannian manifold with two-stage real minimal model $(\Lambda(V\oplus W),d)$, where $dV=0$ and $dW\subset\Lambda V$. Fix a component $\mathcal C$ of $\operatorname{Map}(X,Y)$ and an integer $r$. There is $C=C(X,Y,\mathcal C,r)$ with the following property. If $(Z,B)$ is a finite simplicial pair with $\dim Z\le r$ and $F:Z\times X\to Y$ a continuous family in $\mathcal C$ whose slices over $B$ are $L$-Lipschitz, then $F$ is homotopic relative to $B\times X$ to a family of $C(L+1)$-Lipschitz slices. The same holds in a fixed relative mapping component for a finite pair $(X,A)$ with a fixed Lipschitz restriction $g:A\to Y$, also relative to $Z\times A$.

In every fixed component and each homological degree, finite persistence intervals for $\log_+\operatorname{Lip}$ have bounded length and infinite intervals have bounded birth levels. Any failure of the global bound in Block–Manin–Weinberger Question 1.3 must therefore range over different components. [Block–Manin–Weinberger, Question 1.3 and Theorem 9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S1.SS3)

### Reference model and the affine cohomological ambiguity

Apply the fixed Lipschitz thickening of $X$ to a closed manifold, family smoothing, and the parameter boundary collar as in the earlier proofs. Denote the resulting spatial manifold by $M$ and the smooth-in-space, piecewise smooth-in-parameter pullback of a smooth minimal model by $\phi=F^*m_Y$. In the relative case use the paired whole-boundary thickening and the relative Hodge contraction on the odd forms of the double. Smooth the fixed restriction $g$ once and transport across the spatial collars as in the earlier relative proof. Reattach the controlled smoothing and transport homotopies at the end to recover the original boundary. [Absolute preparation](ManinNewProofs.md#5-higher-persistence-for-two-stage-targets) · [Relative preparation](ManinGeometry.md#5-relative-persistence-with-a-prescribed-boundary)

Choose a fixed smooth reference in the transformed component and let $a$ denote its model, independent of $Z$. Write $K,P$ for the bounded spatial Hodge contraction and projection, with product signs so that $dK+Kd=1-P$. In the relative case use them on differences of forms with equal spatial-boundary restrictions. Let $\mathcal H$ be the absolute spatial cohomology in the absolute case and its relative counterpart in the relative case. On every slice the closed-stage difference $\phi(v)-a(v)$ has zero class in $\mathcal H$ because the slices are homotopic to the reference within the component.

Let $J: \bigoplus_{v\in V}\mathcal H^{|v|-1}\to\bigoplus_{w\in W}\mathcal H^{|w|}$ take the cohomology class of the coefficient of $ds$ in $dw$ evaluated at $a(v)+ds\wedge h_v$. The $a(v)$ supply absolute cohomology classes acting on the relative classes when applicable. Two formally homotopic models whose $V$ images both equal $a(v)$ differ on $W$ by a class in $\operatorname{im}J$. To see this, on any algebraic homotopy in $s$, normalize each closed $V$ image $\psi(v)$ using the exact-form homotopy $-d(\tau K(\psi(v)-a(v)))$ and extend along $W$ by integration in $\tau$. Its endpoint restrictions in $s$ do not change since the restricted correction vanishes there. In the normalized homotopy the $V$ images are $a(v)+ds\wedge h_v(s)$ with $h_v(s)$ spatially closed. Taking the horizontal cohomology of the parameter-degree-one equation on $W$ and integrating in $s$ gives the assertion. These operations preserve the spatial boundary whenever the algebraic homotopy is relative to it.

### Radial interpolation and a degree filtration

Use an auxiliary coordinate $t$ and define on $Z\times M\times[0,1]$
$$
\Phi(v)=a(v)+P(\phi(v)-a(v))+d(t^{|v|}K(\phi(v)-a(v))),
\qquad
\Phi(w)=\phi(w)+\int_1^t\iota_{\partial_s}\Phi(dw)\,ds.
$$
The homotopy identity verifies the differential equation and agreement with $\phi$ at $t=1$. For a fixed $z$, each horizontal $V$ image is $a(v)+t^{|v|}(\phi(v)_{\rm hor}-a(v))$. Put $m=|w|$ and $b=m+1$. In the $dt$ coefficient of $\Phi(dw)$, if the factors taken from the difference, including the one carrying $dt$ via $K$, have assigned degrees summing to $j$, the factor of $t$ is $t^{j-1}$. Thus for suitable spatial forms $\beta_{w,j}$ continuous in $z$,
$$
\Phi(w)_{\rm hor}=\phi(w)_{\rm hor}+\sum_{j=1}^b(t^j-1)\beta_{w,j}.
$$
On a neighborhood $N$ of $B$, let $R=L+1$. The original pullback is bounded horizontally by $C R^{|\cdot|}$ on its generators. The same fixed bound for $K$ gives $\|\beta_{w,j}\|\le C R^j$, since the reference forms are fixed. If $j<b$, at least one reference factor of degree at least two occurs, so $j\le m-1$.

Set $\eta_w=\Phi(w)_{\rm hor}|_{t=0}$. Equivalently,
$$
\Phi(w)_{\rm hor}
=t^b\phi(w)_{\rm hor}+(1-t^b)\eta_w+
\sum_{j<b}(t^j-t^b)\beta_{w,j}.
$$
At $t=0$ the horizontal $V$ images coincide with $a$, so $\eta_w-a(w)$ is closed. The slices are algebraically homotopic to $a$, and the ambiguity result gives $u=([\eta_w-a(w)])_w\in E=\operatorname{im}J$. For each degree $m$, the corresponding block satisfies $\|u_m\|\le C R^{m+1}$ on $N$.

Let $E_{\ge d}$ consist of vectors whose blocks in degrees below $d$ vanish. Split $E_{\ge d}=E_{[d]}\oplus E_{\ge d+1}$ using fixed complements and write $u=\sum_d u^{[d]}$. Projection to degree $d$ is injective on $E_{[d]}$. Recursively recover $u^{[d]}$ from the degree-$d$ projection after subtracting previously recovered pieces. The bounded inverses on the projected subspaces give $\|u^{[d]}\|\le C_E R^{d+1}$ on $N$. The spaces and inverses are fixed by the reference and the source. The proof uses only finitely many generators through the relevant total dimension.

### Cancel the uncontrolled primitive

Use a fixed right inverse of $J$ and fixed closed representatives to choose spatially closed forms $h_v(t,z)$ with
$$
J[h(t,z)]=-\sum_d(1-t^{d+1})u^{[d]}(z).
$$
Specifically choose the forms by applying those linear operators to the vector displayed, so $h_v(1,z)=0$. Replace $\Phi(v)$ by $\Phi'(v)=\Phi(v)+dh_v$ with the full differential, then recompute the $W$ images by the integration formula. This leaves horizontal $V$ images unchanged and leaves exact agreement with $\phi$ at $t=1$. The horizontal change $\Delta_w$ of the $W$ image is closed because its horizontal differential is unchanged. In the $dt$ coefficient the new term comes from $\partial_t h_v$. All other factors have the same absolute cohomology class as $a(v)$. Integrating therefore gives
$$
[\Delta_w]=-\sum_{d\le m}(1-t^{d+1})(u^{[d]})_w.
$$
The spatially closed form $\Delta_w+(1-t^b)(\eta_w-a(w))$ has class
$$
e_w(t,z)=\sum_{d<m}(t^{d+1}-t^b)(u^{[d]})_w.
$$
This is $O(R^m)$ on $N$, as $d+1\le m$, and it vanishes at $t=0,1$. Subtract from the total $W$ image the full differential of the spatial $K$-primitive of that closed form. This changes the horizontal form by its exact part because $d_X K=1-P$ on closed forms, and leaves the full differential equation intact. The horizontal form now equals
$$
t^b\phi(w)_{\rm hor}+(1-t^b)a(w)
+\sum_{j<b}(t^j-t^b)\beta_{w,j}+\alpha e_w(t,z),
$$
where $\alpha$ is harmonic representation. Its norm is $O(R^m)$ on $N$, and at $t=0$ it exactly equals $a(w)$ everywhere. It still has full pullback $\phi(w)$ at $t=1$, since the subtracted primitive vanishes there identically. All corrections are relative to the spatial boundary when required.

Choose $t=t(z)$ equal to one near $B$ and zero off $N$, and pull back onto its graph. The horizontal norms satisfy the ordinary dilatation estimates $O(R^{|v|})$ and $O(R^{|w|})$. Interpolating graph functions from 1 to $t(z)$ gives an algebraic homotopy relative to the parameter boundary, and to the spatial boundary in the relative case. Stretch the parameter metric to absorb all mixed form norms and the parameter Lipschitz constant on the boundary. Ordinary relative shadowing on the bounded-geometry triangulated product gives an actual family, homotopic relative to the prescribed pieces, whose slices are $C R$-Lipschitz. The fixed-factor transfer through the thickening and smoothing collars proves the theorem. [Ordinary relative shadowing](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S6.SS2)

A finite singular bounding chain yields a finite parameter pair with its uncanceled faces. The theorem gives a controlled filling. For a closed parameter chain use $t(z)=0$ everywhere, even if $B$ is empty. The horizontal model is then just the reference on every slice, so the Lipschitz sublevel after parameter stretching and shadowing depends only on the component, domain, target and degree. This bounds the births of infinite intervals as well.

The same constant can be used for components represented by a single real Sullivan homotopy class. Indeed the cohomological and exact-form arguments require only formal homotopy to the common reference. The shadowing step is applied relative to the original family and retains its genuine homotopy class. The relative statement uses the corresponding real Sullivan homotopy class with the prescribed restriction.

## 4. Mapping-degree spectra in arbitrary connectivity

**Theorem.** For every even $n\ge 2$ there is a fixed $(n-1)$-connected closed smooth stably parallelizable $3n$-manifold $M$ for which the degree set $D(M,(S^n)^3)$ is computably enumerable complete. More generally, fix $k\ge2$ and a computably enumerable divisibility-upward set $A$ of positive integers containing every multiple of every prime $k$-th power. There is such a source with
$$
\{0\}\cup\{\pm r_n^3a:a\in A\}\ \subseteq\ D(M,(S^n)^3)\
\subseteq\ \{0\}\cup\{\pm a:a\in A\},
$$
where $r_n$ depends only on $n$. In dimension six one may take $r_2=1$. The six-dimensional case therefore realizes the full exact spectra on stably parallelizable sources.

### Framed realization of cubics

Use the homogeneous quadratic gate system $q_j(x)=0$ and distinguished coordinate $U$ from the earlier arithmetic construction. [Homogeneous gates](ManinGeometry.md#homogeneous-gates-with-integral-decoding) Its absolute nonzero $U$ values are exactly $A$. For the rational and higher-dimensional obstruction calculation no change in those equations is needed. Prescribe the cubic
$$
F=6Ubc+K\left[\sum_jy_jq_j(x)+z\left(\sum_j y_j^2+z^2\right)\right]
$$
with $K$ a positive multiple of six. The resulting trilinear form is integral by polarization, and every monomial has a coefficient divisible by six.

In a closed oriented $(n-1)$-connected $3n$-manifold the value of the third cup power of an integral degree-$n$ class is a cubic because $n$ is even. Start with one summand $S^n\times S^{2n}$ for each underlying coordinate and one summand $(S^n)^3$ for each monomial $6d\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3$ in $F$, allowing repeated coordinate forms. The latter summand's own three coordinates $a_1,a_2,a_3$ contribute $6a_1a_2a_3$. Impose $a_1=d\ell_1$, $a_2=\ell_2$ and $a_3=\ell_3$ by surgery on the dual $n$-cycles. These cycles give a primitive independent subgroup because the coefficient matrix in the auxiliary coordinates is an identity matrix. By Hurewicz choose sphere representatives. The inequality $2n<3n$ lets general position make them embedded and mutually disjoint. The connected sum is stably framed, as follows by extending the product stable framings across the connecting 1-handles. The normal bundle of each sphere is stably trivial because both the ambient tangent bundle and the sphere tangent bundle are stably trivial. Its rank is $2n$, so it is trivial as an actual bundle over $S^n$ by the inclusion $SO(2n)\to SO$ in the stable range. Choose its framing to make the ambient stable framing extend across the surgery trace. The discrepancy lies in $\pi_n(SO)$, and changing the normal framing changes it by the image of $\pi_n(SO(2n))$, which is all of that group since $n\le 2n-2$. Thus the result is stably parallelizable.

The trace has index $n+1$ handles from the original side and index $2n$ handles from the new side. The new boundary remains $(n-1)$-connected, by the relative connectivity of both handle inclusions. The restriction of $H^n$ of the trace to the original manifold is exactly the sublattice annihilating the attaching cycles, and its restriction to the new manifold is an isomorphism since $n+1<2n$. Evaluating a triple cup product on the two boundary cycles gives the same integer. Hence the new manifold has the prescribed cubic $F$. The $n$th homology is free by primitivity of the surgery subgroup. In dimension six the third Betti number is zero, since each surgery lowers the Euler characteristic and twice the second Betti number by two. Poincaré duality and the universal coefficient theorem then give torsion-free homology in dimension six.

By Poincaré duality, a class has square zero precisely when all first derivatives of $F$ at its coordinates vanish. Indeed they are three times its cup square paired with the basis elements. The $z$ derivative forces $y_j=z=0$, and the other derivatives then impose $q_j(x)=0$ and $bc=Uc=Ub=0$. In particular every such class occupies at most one of the $U,b,c$ axes. A map to $(S^n)^3$ supplies three square-zero classes. Their triple pairing is its degree, and the only contributing part of $F$ is the polarization of $6Ubc$. Thus every nonzero degree is a multiple of an allowed nonzero $U$, and belongs to $\{\pm a:a\in A\}$. Conversely, for each witness with $U=a\in A$, the class supported on its $x$ coordinates and the two pure axis classes $b=1$, $c=1$ are all square-zero. Their triple pairing is exactly $a$.

### A uniform dilation for sphere lifts

Let $P$ be the homotopy fiber of the map $K(\mathbb Z,n)\to K(\mathbb Z,2n)$ representing the cup square of the universal class. This simply connected space has a finite-type CW model. For example its homology is finitely generated in each degree by the Serre spectral sequence of the fibration with fiber $K(\mathbb Z,2n-1)$, and the simply connected CW construction by relative Hurewicz yields finite skeleta. The sphere generator classifies a map $S^n\to P$ because its square is zero. This map is a rational equivalence for even $n$. The rational model for $P$ is free on generators in degrees $n$ and $2n-1$, and the differential of the second is the square of the first, as for the even sphere. The map takes the first generator to a generator.

A model of $S^n_{\mathbb Q}$ is the mapping telescope of sphere maps of respective degrees $2,3,4,\ldots$. To see this, the stage maps to the localized sphere can be chosen by composing the rationalization map with the inverse of the cumulative degree map. This gives compatible maps up to homotopy, hence a map from the telescope. The source and target are simply connected, and this comparison gives an integral homology isomorphism since the telescope homology in degree $n$ is the direct limit $\mathbb Q$. Whitehead's theorem gives the equivalence. The map from the finite $3n$-skeleton of $P$ to $P_{\mathbb Q}\simeq S^n_{\mathbb Q}$ thus factors up to homotopy through one sphere stage by boundedness of its image in telescope height. If that stage has cumulative degree $r_n$, the resulting map into the integral sphere pulls back its generator to $r_n$ times the universal class. The comparison holds integrally because degree-$n$ cohomology of the skeleton is freely generated by the universal class. Every integral square-zero class on a complex of dimension at most $3n$ comes from a map into this skeleton by obstruction theory and cellular approximation. Hence its $r_n$ multiple is represented by a genuine sphere map. Applying this to the three classes above supplies all the asserted degrees $r_n^3a$. [Manin on rational rescaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02173)

For the halting example, number the primes not dividing $r_n$ by machine indices, choose $k>\max_{p\mid r_n}v_p(r_n^3)$ with $k\ge2$, and let $A$ consist of multiples of all prime $k$-th powers and multiples of primes with halting indices. Then $r_n^3p$ is $k$-free, and belongs to $A$ exactly when the corresponding machine halts. By the two inclusions it belongs to the degree set exactly on those indices. Simplicial approximation enumerates the degree set, so it is many-one complete. [Comparison with earlier undecidability for degree problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02824)

### Exact square-zero lifting on stably parallelizable six-manifolds

For a simply connected closed stably parallelizable six-manifold, let $L$ be a complex line with $c_1(L)=u$. The fiber sequence $S^2\to BU(1)\to BSp(1)$ sends $L$ to its quaternionification. As a complex bundle this is $L\oplus L^{-1}$, with second Chern class $-u^2$. Since $Sp(2)/Sp(1)=S^7$ and later successive sphere quotients are higher-dimensional, stabilization $BSp(1)\to BSp$ loses no information on a six-dimensional domain. Thus $u$ is pulled back from $S^2$ exactly when $q(L-1)=0$ in reduced symplectic K-theory.

If $u^2=0$, this stable class lies in filtration six of the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence. The first reduced group is in filtration four, detected by ordinary second Chern class, and the possible filtration-five group vanishes since $H^5(M;\mathbb Z/2)=0$. The filtration-six group is a quotient of $H^6(M;\mathbb Z/2)$ because $\widetilde{KSp}^0(S^6)=KO^{-10}(\mathrm{pt})=\mathbb Z/2$. Its generator, if nonzero, is the pullback along a degree-one collapse $c:M\to S^6$ of the sphere generator, by naturality of the filtration and the isomorphism in top mod-two cohomology.

A stable framing yields a Pontryagin–Thom stable map from $S^6$ to $M_+$ whose composite with $c$ has degree one. Pullback along this stable map therefore detects the whole filtration-six subgroup. The stable operation of quaternionification commutes with this pullback. The complex K-theory pullback of $L-1$ vanishes, since its Chern character is $u$ when $u^2=0$, the pullback is graded on rational cohomology, and $\widetilde{KU}^0(S^6)$ is torsion-free. Hence the symplectic pullback vanishes as well, proving $q(L-1)=0$. One can construct the stable map using a normal framing of dimension divisible by eight. In this model the commutation follows directly from the fact that quaternionification commutes with multiplication by the image of a real Bott class. This proves the exact lifting criterion used for $n=2$. ## 5. Density and comparison of mapping-degree spectra

**Theorem.** The natural densities of the positive degree sets into the fixed target $(S^2)^3$, among degree sets with a natural density, are exactly the left-computably enumerable reals in $[0,1]$. Every such density occurs for a stably parallelizable simply connected source whose spectrum is decidable. Other sources satisfying the same promises have decidable spectra with no natural density.

For any oriented closed triangulated source, the positive degree set into this target is computably enumerable and is closed upwards under divisibility, by composition with degree maps on one sphere factor of the target. The Davenport–Erdős theorem says that the lower natural density of a set of multiples is the supremum of the densities of its finite subunions of sets of multiples. Each finite union is periodic with computable rational density. Thus the lower density, and in particular the natural density whenever it exists, is left-computably enumerable. [Davenport and Erdős](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1936-04.pdf)

Fix $k\ge2$ and a set of primes $E$, and put into $A$ the multiples of all prime $k$-th powers and the multiples of the members of $E$. Its complement has density
$$
\zeta(k)^{-1}\exp\left(-\sum_{p\in E}\log(1+p^{-1}+\cdots+p^{-(k-1)})\right).
$$
For finite restrictions this comes from the Chinese remainder theorem. The tail contribution from the prime $k$-th power restrictions is bounded uniformly by $\sum_{p>y}p^{-k}$. If $\sum_{p\in E}1/p$ converges, the tail of the extra prime restrictions is bounded by $\sum_{p\in E,\ p>y}1/p$, proving the displayed formula. If the sum diverges, the finite-prime upper bounds for the complement tend to zero, again proving it.

The positive computable weights $w_p=\log(1+p^{-1}+\cdots+p^{-(k-1)})$ tend to zero and have divergent sum, as each is at least $\log(1+1/p)$. Every nonnegative extended left-computably enumerable real $t$ is a subseries sum of these weights with a decidable set of summands. Process the primes in their increasing order. At prime number $i$ use a rational upper bound with error at most $2^{-i}$ for the sum of the weights accepted so far plus the new weight. Accept if that bound is strictly below the $i$th rational lower approximation to $t$. The sum never exceeds $t$. If the limit were less than $t$, every sufficiently large prime would be accepted, since the error and the next weight tend to zero and the lower approximation eventually exceeds the putative limiting sum by a fixed margin. The divergent sum of all weights contradicts that. This also works when $t=\infty$, using lower bounds that tend to infinity. Since each prime decision is made on a finite schedule, $E$ is decidable.

For a desired positive left-computably enumerable density $\delta$ take $k$ so large that $1-\zeta(k)^{-1}<\delta$, and use $t=-\log((1-\delta)\zeta(k))$, interpreted as infinity at $\delta=1$. This is left-computably enumerable since the displayed transformation is computable and increasing, with an effective increasing approximation on positive finite arguments. The empty positive degree set giving density zero is realized by $S^6$. The exact six-dimensional theorem realizes the prescribed $A$, which is decidable by factoring the tested integer and deciding membership of its prime factors in $E$.

For failure of natural density start instead with the nonsquarefree integers, of density $b=1-1/\zeta(2)$, and add the multiples of every integer in a sequence of disjoint windows $(m_j,2m_j]$. Besicovitch's interval result gives arbitrarily large $m$ for which the periodic set of multiples of $(m,2m]$ has arbitrarily small density. Choose each window effectively with density at most $\epsilon 2^{-j}$ by computing exact finite-period densities. After choosing that window, choose $N_j>2m_j$ where the fraction occupied by the nonsquarefree integers together with all windows chosen so far is at most $b+2\epsilon$. Such $N_j$ exist by the density bounds and can be found by direct counting using computable $b$. Choose the next window beyond $N_j$. At $N_j$ later windows make no contribution, so the lower density is at most $b+2\epsilon$. At $2m_j$ the entire upper half is included as well as the nonsquarefree integers in the lower half, giving upper density at least $(1+b)/2$. Take $\epsilon<(1-b)/4$. The resulting upward set is decidable because membership below a tested integer only depends on the nonsquarefree test and finitely many effectively chosen windows. The exact six-dimensional realization supplies the claimed source. [Ford on Besicovitch’s divisor windows](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2008/168-2/p01)

## 6. Extension on the boundary of one punctured sphere product

**Theorem.** For every $n\ge2$, let $W=(S^n\times S^n)\setminus\operatorname{int}D^{2n}$, given its smooth boundary sphere. There is a fixed finite $(n-1)$-connected complex $Y$ of dimension $2n$ and an effective affine family of classes $\beta_0+m(\beta_1-\beta_0)$ in $\pi_{2n-1}(Y)$ for which extension from $\partial W$ to $W$ is halting-complete. Every boundary class in the family extends after each prime localization of $Y$, and every suspended boundary class is zero. The dimension $2n$ is the first one outside the general decision range for an $(n-1)$-connected target. [Čadek et al., stable range and Whitehead-product reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.2370) · [Manin, fixed-target extensions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10632)

Take the removed disk inside the top cell. The punctured product retracts onto $S^n\vee S^n$ and its boundary inclusion gives the attaching class $[\iota_1,\iota_2]$, with compatible orientations. A map out of the wedge into $Y$ with values $v,w\in\pi_n(Y)$ thus extends the prescribed boundary exactly when $[v,w]$ equals the prescribed class. Homotopy extension turns an equality of boundary classes into extension of the exact boundary map.

To construct $Y$, first choose a wedge $V$ of $n$-spheres with basis labels $a,b,L_i,R_i$; the index $i$ will run over the variables of one fixed quadratic gate system. Sending the wedge generators to the standard basis identifies $\pi_n(V)$ with the integral lattice on these labels. Put $\epsilon=(-1)^n$. The Hilton–Milnor splitting in degree $2n-1$ has the individual $\pi_{2n-1}(S^n)$ summands and one free coordinate for each cross Whitehead product. The cross coordinate of $[v,w]$ at the ordered labels $i,j$ is
$$
B_{ij}=v_iw_j+\epsilon v_jw_i,\qquad B_{ji}=\epsilon B_{ij}.
$$
For even $n$, the Hopf invariant of the component at $i$ gives $2v_iw_i$, because the self Whitehead square has Hopf invariant $2$. Thus $B_{ij}$, and for even $n$ the integral diagonal coordinate $2v_iw_i$, are values of fixed homomorphisms out of $\pi_{2n-1}(V)$. In the following, the column at label $i$ is $(v_i,w_i)$. The displayed pairing on columns is a split symmetric form for even $n$ and the determinant for odd $n$.

Require $B_{ab}=1$, and for even $n$ require that the diagonal coordinates at $a,b$ vanish. The two anchor columns form a basis over the integers. In the symmetric case they are isotropic and must be the two coordinate vectors in one order with compatible unit signs. In the alternating case they are a basis of determinant $1$. Require $B_{a,L_i}=B_{b,R_i}=0$. In both parities each anchor column spans its own orthogonal space, so the column at $L_i$ equals $x_i$ times the anchor at $a$, and the column at $R_i$ equals $y_i$ times the anchor at $b$. The readings are now
$$
B_{L_i,b}=x_i,\qquad B_{a,R_i}=y_i,\qquad B_{L_i,R_j}=x_i y_j.
$$
Add $B_{L_i,b}-B_{a,R_i}=0$ to identify the two copies of each variable. These are exact integer assertions, since the anchor pairing is $1$. Conversely arbitrary choices of the common variables give columns with these properties by taking the two standard coordinate anchors in order. The same statements hold over every $\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}$, using determinant $1$ or the symmetric equations in an integral domain.

Choose a Diophantine polynomial whose integral solvability at input $m\ge0$ is automatic for $m=0,1$ and for $m\ge2$ equivalent to halting of machine $m-2$. This is the Diophantine representation theorem, allowing unrestricted integer variables by the four-square substitution if needed. Multiply the polynomial by $(2u-1)(3v-1)$ with fresh variables. The factors are never zero on integers, and the polynomial becomes zero for a supplied witness after inverting either 2 or 3. Compile this polynomial into a fixed system of quadratic gates, including its input variable. Every gate is a linear combination of the clone, product, and constant readings, with $B_{ab}$ the constant reading. Let
$$
\Theta:\pi_{2n-1}(V)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^s
$$
record all the anchor, orthogonality, clone, and gate equations, and separately the input reading. The desired right sides are $1$ at the anchor, $m$ at the input, and zero at all other readings. They are sufficient and necessary for the prescribed arithmetic predicate for a bracket $[v,w]$.

Attach $2n$-cells along any finite generating set of $\ker\Theta$. The long exact sequence and relative Hurewicz identify $\pi_{2n-1}(Y)$ with $\operatorname{im}\Theta$. The attachment leaves $\pi_n(Y)=\pi_n(V)$. A finite simplicial model of dimension $2n$ can be obtained using simplicial mapping cones for the attaching maps. Witnesses at $0,1$ give brackets realizing classes $\beta_0,\beta_1$ with precisely the required readings at those inputs. Hence their affine combination has the required readings at every $m$. It is represented effectively using sphere pinches, the two fixed representatives, and precomposition by integer degree maps. By the quotient, a pair of elements of $\pi_n(Y)$ has bracket equal to the prescribed class precisely when its readings satisfy the system. This proves the reduction, and extension is computably enumerable by relative simplicial approximation.

For a prime $\ell$, all the homotopy groups here localize by tensoring with $\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}$. The homomorphism from the bracket quotient to the reading module stays injective under localization. Use an explicit masked solution in $\mathbb Z[1/2]$ or $\mathbb Z[1/3]$ according to which denominator is invertible. Its columns then realize the prescribed localized boundary class. Finally the two initial classes are represented by Whitehead products, whose suspensions vanish. The suspended affine family is therefore null and extends over the suspended pair. [Hilton–Milnor and the cross-product coordinates](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.2370)

### A cyclic family on punctured projective planes

For $n=2,4,8$, take the punctured complex, quaternionic, or octonionic projective plane, respectively. For each computably enumerable set $E$ of positive squarefree integers, there is a fixed finite $(n-1)$-connected $2n$-dimensional target and a single class $\beta$ such that $q\beta$ extends precisely for $q\in E$ when $q$ is positive and squarefree. Every instance extends at every prime, and all the boundary classes suspend to zero.

The punctured projective plane retracts onto $S^n$, with boundary class the Hopf-invariant-one attaching map $\eta$. A candidate map of the spine is an arbitrary vector $v$ into a target wedge of $n$-spheres. The individual Hopf invariants of the components of $v\circ\eta$ are $v_i^2$, and its cross Hilton–Milnor coordinates are $v_iv_j$. The first assertion follows from naturality of the cup-square definition of Hopf invariant. The second is the Hopf–Hilton formula for composition with a sum, in which the cross coefficient is the Hopf invariant of $\eta$, equal to $1$. These coordinates therefore read every homogeneous integer quadratic on $v$ by a homomorphism out of the wedge's $\pi_{2n-1}$.

Use a fixed gate system recognizing $E$ at $T_0/U$, homogenized with coordinate $U$ after the coprime-denominator masking step above. Introduce new variables $X,T,Z$ and equations
$$
XU=U^2,\qquad X(T-T_0)=0,\qquad T^2=UZ,\qquad UT=q .
$$
Only the last row is inhomogeneous, and no other row uses the monomial $UT$. For positive squarefree $q$, the last two equations imply $|U|=1$, since a prime dividing $U$ would divide $T$ and then its square would divide $q$. The first two equations give $X=U$ and $T=T_0$, so the old gates decode integrally at $T_0/U=q$. Conversely an integral witness gives a solution with $U=X=1$ and $T=T_0=q$. The same choice gives witnesses at each prime using the coprime-denominator mask.

Let $\Theta$ read these rows and the original homogeneous gate rows. Attach $2n$-cells to kill its kernel, as above. The cross bracket of the distinct wedge generators at $U,T$ reads $1$ on the $UT$ row and zero on all others. Let $\beta$ be its class after attaching the cells. The extension $q\beta$ is now equivalent to solving exactly the displayed system and the original gates, and it suspends to zero because $\beta$ is a Whitehead bracket. Using the images of halting indices under prime enumeration for $E$ gives the stated cyclic halting family.

### Mapping degrees as exact boundary extension spectra of manifolds

**Theorem.** Let $P$ and $M$ be closed connected oriented smooth $d$-manifolds with $d\ge3$, and suppose $P$ is simply connected. Write $V=P\setminus\operatorname{int}D^d$, $W=M\setminus\operatorname{int}D^d$, and $\beta:S^{d-1}\to V$ for the target boundary inclusion with the orientation of the removed disk. Then
$$
D(M,P)=\{k\in\mathbb Z: \text{the boundary class }k\beta\text{ extends to }W\to V\}.
$$
Equivalently, a specified degree-$k$ boundary map $\partial W\to\partial V$ extends to a map of compact manifold pairs if and only if $k\in D(M,P)$.

A compact connected smooth manifold with nonempty boundary has the homotopy type of a CW complex of dimension at most $d-1$, by using an absolute handle decomposition without top-index handles. The pair $(P,V)$ is obtained by attaching one $d$-cell, and both are simply connected. It is $(d-1)$-connected, with $\pi_d(P,V)\cong H_d(P,V)\cong\mathbb Z$ by relative Hurewicz. Choose its generator from the target disk. For $F:M\to P$, its restriction to $W$ is homotopic to a map into $V$ by the connectivity of $V\to P$ and the spine dimension. Extend the homotopy over $M$ by the cofibration property. The source disk now maps into the relative target pair, and its relative class is $\deg(F)$ times the target disk class, by naturality on $H_d$ and by excision. Its boundary class is therefore $\deg(F)\beta$. Conversely, a prescribed representative $\beta\circ d_k$, where $d_k$ has sphere degree $k$, has a relative disk filling into $(P,V)$ by coning $d_k$ in the target disk. Gluing this filling to an extension over $W$ gives a map of degree $k$. Homotopy extension allows all choices of representatives in the indicated class. This proves the exact equality.

For $P=(S^n)^3$, $V$ retracts onto the fat wedge obtained by removing the unique top cell. The fixed degree-spectrum realization then gives a fixed compact smooth source and a fixed compact smooth target, each with just one spherical boundary, with a halting-complete set of extendible boundary degrees. For $n=2$ this holds for simply connected six-manifolds. All prescribed boundary maps are genuine maps of their boundary spheres. The target can also be made closed by inclusion in the double of $V$, because the fold retraction of the double onto $V$ preserves the prescribed boundary data. For the fat-wedge model the suspended top attaching map vanishes, since the suspended product splits as the wedge of smash products over the nonempty subsets of the factors, compatibly with the fat wedge consisting of the proper subsets. The conclusion holds also with the stably parallelizable higher-connectivity mapping-degree sources whenever the corresponding degree-spectrum theorem is used. [Degree-spectrum construction](ManinGeometry.md#1-a-halting-complete-mapping-degree-set-for-fixed-six-manifolds) · [Stable product and polyhedral-product splitting](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.4684)

For $P=\mathbb{CP}^4$, its puncture retracts onto $\mathbb{CP}^3$, whose embedded top attaching boundary class $S^7\to\mathbb{CP}^3$ is a generator of $\pi_7(\mathbb{CP}^3)$ by the Hopf fibration. The fixed-target minimum-degree gap thus becomes a gap $1$ versus $K$ in the least positive extendible boundary multiple on smooth punctured eight-manifolds. Their boundary complex lines are abstractly trivial and all stabilize into $\mathbb C^5$. The integral degree-$K$ witness and the prime-local degree-one witnesses transport through the same construction. For localization, $V_{(\ell)}\to P_{(\ell)}$ is still $(d-1)$-connected and relative Hurewicz computes its relative group as $\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}$, with the same orientation generator. This gives precisely the localized boundary reading. [Projective-space degree gap](ManinGeometry.md#2-an-undecidable-gap-in-the-minimum-positive-degree-to-complex-projective-space)

## 7. A rational Postnikov transfer for persistence

**Theorem.** Let $p:Y\to W$ be a map of simply connected finite complexes inducing isomorphisms on rational homotopy groups through degree $d$. Fix a finite simplicial complex $X$ of dimension at most $d$. Suppose families in a component of $\operatorname{Map}(X,W)$ can be homotoped relative to their prescribed parameter boundary to families of $C(L+1)$-Lipschitz slices whenever the original boundary slices are $L$-Lipschitz. Allow $C$ to depend on the fixed data and the parameter dimension. Then the same conclusion holds for any component of $\operatorname{Map}(X,Y)$ mapping into that component. The transfer preserves the homotopy class of the entire original family relative to its parameter boundary.

In particular, if $W$ has positive weights, finite intervals in the null mapping component for $Y$ have bounded length with respect to $\log_+\operatorname{Lip}$, and infinite ones have bounded births in each homological degree. The result applies to the twelve-dimensional Mimura–Toda target, which has no positive weights, for every source of dimension at most ten. [Block–Manin–Weinberger, Question 1.2 and Lemma 7.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S7.SS2)

### Transfer through the rational Postnikov range

Use piecewise Riemannian metrics and a Lipschitz representative of $p$. One may replace each target by a smooth thickening with boundary and use the Lipschitz maps and homotopies in both directions, since these transfer linear cutoffs. Prepare a given finite family $F:Z\times X\to Y$ as in the earlier argument. It can be taken Lipschitz on the whole parameter product, up to a controlled smoothing homotopy on the parameter boundary $B$. Here the spatial domain stays the original $X$ of dimension at most $d$. For example, once the targets are thickened, embed them in a Euclidean neighborhood retract and use piecewise linear approximations on a fine spatial and parameter subdivision. On slices over $B$ the approximated family and the short homotopy to it have spatial Lipschitz constant $O(L+1)$. This follows by using vertex samples and shape-regular spatial subdivisions of size proportional to the approximation scale, then applying the neighborhood retraction. The original boundary will be recovered by appending this short homotopy.

Write $M_W,M_Y$ for the real minimal models and $P:M_W\to M_Y$ for the model of $p$. The subalgebras generated through degree $d$ are identified by $P$. Indeed on indecomposables through degree $d$ the map is an isomorphism and its decomposable corrections only involve lower degrees. Let $A$ denote this identified subalgebra. Minimality and simple connectivity make $A$ closed under the differential. Choose the target-model realization $m_Y:M_Y\to\Omega^*(Y)$ so it agrees on $A$ with the literal pullback $p^*m_W$. To do so start with any model realization and homotope it on $A$ to the indicated pullback, as both realize the same model of $p$. Extend this algebraic homotopy over the remaining generators of $M_Y$ inductively. For a generator $\xi$ whose differential is already handled and whose starting value is $\psi_0(\xi)$, set
$$
\Psi(\xi)=\psi_0(\xi)+\int_0^t\iota_{\partial_s}\Psi(d\xi)\,ds.
$$
The homotopy formula for the closed form $\Psi(d\xi)$ gives the required differential. This also works relatively because a constant restriction on the earlier generators gives no $dt$ coefficient there.

Apply the family property in $W$ to $pF$ and obtain a homotopy $H$ relative to $B\times X$ to a family whose spatial Lipschitz norm is $O(L+1)$. The homotopy can be Lipschitz on the compact parameter product after relative approximation. The final family can be approximated first with spatial Lipschitz norm $O(L+1)$, preserving its boundary, and then the homotopy with its endpoints and boundary fixed. This uses the same finite polyhedral approximation and target neighborhood retraction, since the starting and ending maps on the prescribed pieces are Lipschitz. For the null component of a positive-weight target the family property follows from Lemma 7.4 by induction over the parameter skeleta, with the boundary subcomplex kept fixed and the interior vertices moved to a common constant map.

The pullback $H^*m_W$ on $A$ now gives an algebraic homotopy starting at $F^*m_Y$ restricted to $A$. Use the same generator integration formula to extend it to $M_Y$, preserving the original model over the parameter boundary. On the final model, the forms on every generator of degree at most $d$ have the correct horizontal ordinary-dilatation bounds by the controlled $W$ family. All higher generators restrict to zero on a horizontal slice because $\dim X\le d$. All mixed form norms are finite on this particular finite parameter product. Stretch the parameter metric sufficiently to control these norms and the parameter cost of the original boundary. Ordinary relative shadowing then gives an actual family with the stated spatial bound and in the given relative homotopy class. Recover the original boundary by the smoothing homotopy. For closed parameter cycles one carries out the same construction with empty boundary and the constant-bound family from the positive-weight skeleton induction. This also bounds the births of infinite intervals. [Relative shadowing](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S6.SS2)

### The nonpositive-weight example

Put $B_0=S^3\vee\mathbb{CP}^2$, let $\iota_j$ denote the inclusions of the degree-$j$ generators for $j=2,3$, let $h:S^5\to\mathbb{CP}^2$ be the Hopf map, and write $\omega=[\iota_3,\iota_2]$. Up to the Whitehead convention one can take the Mimura–Toda attaching map as the sum of three classes
$$
f_1=[[[\omega,\iota_3],\omega],\iota_3],
\qquad f_2=[[\iota_3,h],h],
\qquad f_3=[[\omega,h],\omega].
$$
The complex $Y=B_0\cup_{f_1+f_2+f_3}e^{12}$ lacks positive weights by Mimura–Toda's nonuniversality theorem and the characterization of positive-weight rational types by $p$-universality. Write $W$ for the complex obtained by attaching three separate $12$-cells to $B_0$, one along each $f_i$. The sum is null in $W$, so the identity on $B_0$ extends to $p:Y\to W$. The two attachments leave the homotopy groups through degree ten unchanged. [Mimura–Toda, §4](https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=com-001%3A1971%3A46%3A%3A11) · [Body–Mimura–Shiga–Sullivan, Theorem A](https://doi.org/10.1007/S000140050063)

To prove that $W$ has positive weights, first define an actual self-map of $B_0$ by a degree-eight map on $S^3$ and homogeneous coordinate squaring on $\mathbb{CP}^2$. The latter multiplies $H^2$ by two and the Hopf generator of $\pi_5$ by eight. One check of the Hopf scaling uses the minimal model $(\Lambda(x_2,y_5),dy=x^3)$, where scaling $x$ by two scales $y$ by eight. This computes the integral coefficient because $\pi_5(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\mathbb Z$ by the Hopf fibration. Naturality and bilinearity therefore give exact multipliers $2^{14},2^9,2^{11}$ on the attaching classes, respectively. In fact their multipliers for degrees $r$ on the second homotopy group and $s$ on the third sphere are $r^2s^4$, $r^6s$, and $r^5s^2$. Extend the self-map across the cells by cones on sphere maps of these degrees, inserting the attaching homotopies. The resulting self-map of $W$ acts on every positive-degree rational cohomology group with eigenvalues positive powers of two. In degrees $2,3,4$ these are the eigenvalues from $B_0$, and in degree $12$ they are the indicated three relative cellular degrees.

For completeness, any such cohomology action gives a positive weight decomposition of a simply connected rational minimal model $M$. Let $T$ be the endomorphism of $M$ induced by the map and let $M_{<j}$ be the subalgebra on generators of degrees below $j$. It is invariant. For the degree-$j$ indecomposable vector space $V^j$ there is a $T$-equivariant sequence
$$
H^j(M)\longrightarrow V^j\longrightarrow H^{j+1}(M_{<j})
$$
exact at $V^j$. The second map takes the class of the differential of a generator, and its kernel comes from closed classes by subtracting a lower-degree primitive. Inductively the eigenvalues on the right are products of positive powers already occurring at smaller degrees. Exactness then forces the same form for every eigenvalue on $V^j$, and hence on $M^j$. The rational generalized eigenspaces for $2^w$ give the desired weight-$w$ grading. The differential preserves these spaces and multiplication adds weights, since it intertwines the tensor-product endomorphism. This establishes the positive weights for $W$ and completes the dimension-ten example.
