# Open questions on extension, persistence, and degree spectra

The first two chapters concern Manin's nilpotent-target direction and his question about classifying extensions in the critical dimension. The later chapters give results on self-mapping degree spectra and on the persistence questions of Block, Manin, and Weinberger. Each theorem states the range proved.

## 1. The extension dichotomy for finite nilpotent targets

**Theorem.** Fix a connected finite nilpotent complex $Y$. The extension problem for maps $A\to Y$ from finite simplicial pairs $(X,A)$ is decidable if $Y_{\mathbb Q}$ is a product of rational Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces. In every other case it is many-one complete for computably enumerable sets. This gives the nilpotent-target extension of the existence criterion in Manin’s Theorem A and Theorem 8.1. The decision procedure is for each fixed target. [Manin, introduction and Theorem A](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.10632#S1) · [Manin, Theorem 8.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.10632#S8)

We use the earlier coefficient-only bilinear theorem. For each nonzero integral bilinear map $B:L_1\times L_2\to L_3$ on free finite-rank lattices, the satisfiability of finite systems $\sum a_{ij}B(u_i,v_j)=c$ is halting-hard, even for a fixed coefficient array with designated varying constants. Killing torsion in a homotopy group means multiplying by the exponent of its torsion subgroup. Higher homotopy groups of a connected nilpotent finite complex are finitely generated. [Bilinear theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/c5fef3140265c1dff49ad6e18c7f40b71f6a8ff0/BilinearDiophantineUndecidability/PROOF.md) · [Fixed arithmetic templates](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/c5fef3140265c1dff49ad6e18c7f40b71f6a8ff0/PortfolioTop30/ManinExtensions.md)

### Nonabelian rational fundamental group

Write $\Gamma=\pi_1(Y)$. If $\Gamma$ is not virtually abelian, Garreta–Miasnikov–Ovchinnikov existentially interpret a ring of integers of a number field in $\Gamma$ by equations. Koymans–Pagano existentially define the ordinary integers in that number ring. Using an integral Diophantine representation of halting thus gives many-one hardness for systems of group equations with constants in this fixed $\Gamma$. Make the constants fixed boundary circles at a common vertex, let unknowns be further circles, and attach one two-cell for each word equation. The attaching loop is null in $Y$ exactly when that equation holds in $\Gamma$. This proves the claimed reduction on two-dimensional sources. A finitely generated nilpotent group has abelian rationalization exactly when it is virtually abelian. [Garreta–Miasnikov–Ovchinnikov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04085) · [Koymans–Pagano](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01768)

### Nontrivial rational action on higher homotopy

Suppose some $\pi_n(Y)\otimes\mathbb Q$, $n\ge2$, has nontrivial $\Gamma$-action. Put $L=t\pi_n(Y)$ for a positive integer $t$ killing its torsion. Let $\mathcal I$ be the augmentation ideal of $\mathbb Z[\Gamma]$ and let $U=\{u\in L: \mathcal I^2u=0\}$. Nilpotence of the action implies that $\mathcal I U\ne0$. In fact, if $s\ge1$ is maximal with $\mathcal I^sL\ne0$, then $\mathcal I^{s-1}L\subset U$ and $\mathcal I^sL\ne0$. For $u\in U$ the assignment $\gamma\mapsto(\gamma-1)u$ is additive under composition because $(\gamma-1)(\delta-1)u=0$. It factors through $\Gamma_{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathrm{torsion}$, since $L$ is torsion-free. Hence
$$
B:(\Gamma_{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathrm{torsion})\times U\longrightarrow L,\qquad
B([\gamma],u)=(\gamma-1)u
$$
is a nonzero integral pairing. All lattice elements on the left have loop representatives.

Use a wedge of unknown loops and unknown $n$-spheres, with boundary loops representing a fixed finite generating set $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ of $\Gamma$ and boundary $n$-spheres representing the right sides. Replace each unknown sphere label $x$ in the equations by its multiple $u=tx$, allowing every element of $L$. The conditions $(g_i-1)(g_j-1)u=0$ for all $i,j$ impose $u\in U$. Indeed each $(g_j-1)u$ is then fixed by all of $\Gamma$, so inverses introduce no new differences and every group difference of $u$ is a sum of the displayed differences with signs. Attach $(n+1)$-cells measuring these conditions and the bilinear equations, using the usual based fundamental-group action on the $n$th homotopy of the wedge. Each such sphere can be formed by dragging a representative along the indicated loop, taking additive differences, and pinching. Extension over the cells is exactly solvability in the lattices. The cell dimensions used are $1,n,n+1$, together with the common vertex. This branch applies even when the universal cover is a rational H-space.

### A finite-coefficient rescaling fact

For a finite product $E$ of spaces $K(\mathbb Z^r,i)$ with $i\ge1$ and a finite abelian group $A$, some multiplication map $[m]:E\to E$ annihilates positive-degree cohomology with coefficients $A$ through any specified degree $D$. Over $\mathbb F_p$, use convolution on graded cohomology with the multiplication coproduct. The identity is the sum of its counit part and a map vanishing in degree zero. Its $p^a$th convolution power in characteristic $p$ has only the counit and the $p^a$th power of the latter map. The latter vanishes in degrees less than $p^a$, because the coproduct terms that survive have positive degree in each slot. This proves the claim over $\mathbb F_p$. The coefficient long exact sequences and composition supply the claim for finite abelian groups. All powers can be bounded effectively from $D$ and the group order.

We also use the relative version for $(Z\times E,Z\times\{0\})$ with a finite local coefficient system pulled back from $Z$. Filter by the skeleta of $Z$. The product relative spectral sequence has positive $E$-degree, and the preceding powers kill its terms in total degrees at most $D$. Iterating at most $D+1$ times kills the filtered relative group in those degrees. If a finite local system on the product is initially nontrivial along $E$, first rescale enough to kill that monodromy. The commuting images from the two factors show that the remaining system is pulled back from $Z$. An exponent of the automorphism group of the coefficient group bounds this preliminary step.

### A nonzero rational Postnikov invariant

Now assume $\Gamma$ is virtually abelian and the rational higher actions are trivial. The remaining case for hardness is a first nonzero rational Postnikov invariant $k$ in degree $n+1$, $n\ge2$. Choose a finite-index central torsion-free subgroup $A_0\le\Gamma$. Such a subgroup exists because a finite-index normal free abelian subgroup has finite-order conjugations, which are also unipotent by nilpotence and therefore trivial. Use the cover $\widehat Y$ with group $A_0$. It has the same rational Postnikov type, since this finite-index inclusion induces the isomorphism of rationalized fundamental groups and the cover preserves higher homotopy groups. The lower rational type is a product of Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces. Taking integral multiples of its coordinate classes gives a map
$$
q:(\widehat Y)_{n-1}\longrightarrow E=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}K(\mathbb Z^{r_i},i)
$$
which induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$ and a rational equivalence. Its homotopy fiber is connected with finite homotopy groups, and its fundamental group is abelian by the homotopy exact sequence.

Some multiplication map on $E$ lifts up to homotopy through $q$. To prove this use the relative Moore–Postnikov tower of $q$, starting from the identity on $E$. At each stage the obstruction lies in positive-degree cohomology with finite local coefficients. A preliminary multiplication kills the monodromy and a further multiplication kills the obstruction by the rescaling fact. Compose the stages successively, pulling the prior lifts back along each multiplication. There are finitely many stages because both spaces are Postnikov sections. Let $\rho$ be the resulting multiplier.

Express $k$ as a polynomial in the rational coordinates of $E$, with values in $\pi_n(Y)\otimes\mathbb Q$. The algebra of these coordinates is graded commutative and the polynomial has a smallest nonzero monomial length $\kappa\ge2$. Select the degrees $d_1,\ldots,d_\kappa$ of one such monomial, so $\sum d_i=n+1$. In the product $\prod S^{d_i}$ let the variable sphere classes lie in integral multiples of the homotopy lattices of $(\widehat Y)_{n-1}$ such that their $q$-images are divisible by $\rho$. Choose the allowed lattices inside the image of the lift of multiplication, as detailed next. Then each assignment extends to the fat wedge in $\widehat Y$. Map the full product to $E$ using the independent sphere coordinates of preimages under that lift, then apply the lift, and finally lift the fat wedge into $\widehat Y$ since its dimension is at most $n$. The resulting factor maps can be adjusted to the prescribed representatives by homotopy extension.

For this refinement, the lift of $[\rho]$ induces a homomorphism on each $\pi_i(E)$ with image a full-rank lattice. The image of this homomorphism contains a sufficiently divisible full multiple of $\pi_i(\widehat Y)$. Take a multiple also divisible by the torsion exponent to make the allowed group a free lattice. All members have fat-wedge realizations as above. Indeed for a lattice homomorphism with full rational rank, the cokernel is finite, so a sufficiently large full multiple of the target lies in the image. At degree one use a multiple of $A_0$. If $d$ kills $\Gamma/A_0$, the set of $d$th powers lies in $A_0$ and is a full-rank subgroup there. To see closure, write $\Gamma$ modulo its finite torsion subgroup, which is abelian since its commutator is finite by centrality of $A_0$. Two elements of $A_0$ with the same image in this quotient coincide. Hence the product of two $d$th powers is the $d$th power of the product. Use sufficiently large further powers of $\Gamma$ for the allowed loop sublattice.

For any map from the fat wedge into the cover, the rational lift of its lower-stage map across the top disk is unique up to homotopy relative to the fat wedge, since $E$ has no homotopy in degrees $n$ and $n+1$. The rational obstruction on the disk is $k$ evaluated on the product fundamental class. In this evaluation all terms of length greater than $\kappa$ vanish. For terms of length $\kappa$ only the pure coordinates of the individual sphere factors contribute. Indeed on the product every positive-degree cohomology monomial uses some factors, and a product contributing to the top class must use each of the $\kappa$ distinct factors exactly once. This shows that the obstruction is the $\kappa$-linear polarization of the length-$\kappa$ part of $k$, independent of the mixed classes and the fat-wedge choices. Relative obstruction theory identifies it, up to a fixed orientation sign, with the rational homotopy class of the attaching sphere image in $\pi_n(Y)\otimes\mathbb Q$. In particular one can select the last $\kappa-2$ sphere arguments to make the remaining bilinear map nonzero. Its values on the allowed first two lattices are integral in $\pi_n(Y)/\mathrm{torsion}$, because all assignments have genuine fat-wedge lifts. This reasoning includes factors $S^1$, using their degree-one cohomology coordinates and trivial rational local coefficients.

Encode a system in this fixed pairing exactly as in Manin’s fat-wedge construction. Use an independent copy of the fat wedge for each pair of variables, identified along its factor spheres, a prescribed sphere for each right side, and one $(n+1)$-cell per equation. Multiply the equation attaching class by the exponent of the torsion in $\pi_n(Y)$, so rational equality is equivalent to the actual nullity needed for extension. Impose the allowed multiple on a higher-dimensional variable by a degree mapping cylinder. Impose the allowed power on a loop variable by adding an auxiliary root circle and a separate two-cell identifying the variable with this power. Excluding those auxiliary root circles and their defining two-cells, the core of the construction has all its loop generators mapping into $A_0$, so it lifts to the finite cover. Conversely every allowed loop value has a root in $\Gamma$, so a solution on the core extends over these separate two-cells. The covers and fat-wedge maps may be chosen based at the fixed boundary vertex. Thus the equations are necessary and sufficient for actual extension, proving the remaining hardness case.

### Decision in the rational H-space case

Assume $Y_{\mathbb Q}$ is a rational H-space. Its rational type is a finite product of rational Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces, all in odd degrees, since a nonzero even factor would have unbounded rational cohomology and infinitely many odd factors would contradict finite-dimensional total cohomology. The group $\Gamma$ is virtually abelian. Choose again a central finite-index free abelian subgroup $A_0$, and a map
$$
q:Y\longrightarrow B=K(\Gamma,1)\times E_+,\qquad
E_+=\prod_{i\ge2}K(\mathbb Z^{r_i},i),
$$
whose first part is the classifying map and whose second part is given by integral multiples of the rational coordinates. This induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups and a rational equivalence on higher homotopy. As above its homotopy fiber $F$ is connected with finite homotopy groups and trivial fundamental group, since the higher factors of $E_+$ start in degree three. There is no need to recognize the hypothesis uniformly from a target presentation. The integral coordinate cocycles, a word problem algorithm for this fixed virtually abelian group, and the finite central extension data are fixed input-independent data.

Let $E_0=K(A_0,1)\times E_+$. It acts by translations on $B$, using the central subgroup in degree one and addition in the higher factors. For each dimension $D$ there is an effective integer $N_D$ so that, for a pair of dimension at most $D$, the possibility of lifting a base extension through $q$ relative to the prescribed $Y$-map is invariant under translation by $[N_D]e$ for any map $e:(X,A)\to(E_0,0)$. To see this start with the map $Y\times E_0\to B$ obtained by translating $q$ and the prescribed lift on $Y\times\{0\}$. Use the relative Moore–Postnikov tower of $q$ through degree $D$. At each stage precompose by $\operatorname{id}\times[m]$ to remove the coefficient monodromy in the second factor and then annihilate the finite relative obstruction. This retains the prescribed lift and multiplies only the translation parameter. Apply the final lift to any given lift on $X$ and the translation map $e$. Since the fiber stages retained suffice in dimension $D$, it gives the new relative lift. Using the negative translation gives the reverse implication. The product of the finite rescaling bounds is effective from $D$ and the orders of the fiber groups through that range.

Here are the effectiveness details for those groups and the finite lifting checks. The universal cover of $Y$ is also the universal cover of its finite cover with group $A_0$. Its cellular chains there are finite free in each degree over a Laurent polynomial ring over $\mathbb Z$. Module operations and elimination over this ring give finite presentations of its homology modules. Their underlying abelian groups are finitely generated, since the universal cover is simply connected with finitely generated homotopy groups. To recover generators as abelian groups, enumerate successively larger Laurent translates of module generators and dovetail tests for the span being stable under multiplication by the Laurent variables and their inverses. Each such positive test uses finite integer combinations and equality in the module. Some test terminates by finite generation. The lattice of integer relations among the resulting generators is computed by intersecting the Laurent relation module with the constant coordinates using elimination. Equivalently use ordinary integer polynomial variables for the generators and their inverses and adjoin the relations that their products are one.

This also gives effective homology in the chain-homotopy sense. Use a finite-type free chain complex of the free and cyclic homology groups and map it to the universal-cover chain complex using cycle representatives and bounding chains for torsion relations. These chains can be found by enumeration with the known homology classes. The mapping cone is an effectively enumerable acyclic free complex. On its basis in increasing degree, construct a contracting homotopy by finding a primitive of the basis vector minus the already computed homotopy of its boundary, which is a cycle. Each primitive exists, so the search terminates. The resulting chain equivalence gives effective homology and works on normalized simplicial chains as well, using the standard cellular-to-simplicial equivalence. [Universal-cover chains over an abelian group ring](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06357#S7) · [Effective homology and Whitehead towers](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~sergerar/Papers/Constructive-AT.pdf)

The fiber $F$ is equivalent to the homotopy fiber of the induced map $\widetilde Y\to E_+$. It is simply connected since $\pi_2(E_+)=0$. Use the effective homology of $\widetilde Y$ and the standard effective path fibration of $E_+$, with fiber the product $\prod_{i\ge3}K(\mathbb Z^{r_i},i-1)$, to compute effective homology of the pullback. The effective Whitehead tower computes its homotopy groups and evaluates maps of spheres on them. These constructions use the standard effective homology of a twisted product with simply connected base, with locally effective simplicial sets. [Effective homotopy of fibrations](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~sergerar/Papers/AAECC-Effective-Homotopy-of-Fibrations.pdf)

To decide a fixed base lifting problem, work cell by cell using a path-space fibration model of $q$. Over a disk contract its base map to the center to identify the boundary obstruction with an element of the homotopy group of $F$. This class and its nullity are computable by the preceding constructions. On a cell with zero obstruction a filler can be found by enumeration. The relative homotopy classes of fillers with the prescribed boundary are obtained from one filler by the action of the corresponding finite fiber homotopy group, whose representatives can be enumerated and identified by the effective computations. When a candidate lift is homotopic to a retained one on the lower skeleton, the fiberwise homotopy extends over its higher cells by the homotopy extension and fibration lifting properties. Thus retaining one representative per local choice suffices. Keep all possibilities through the finitely many cells, respecting the prescribed lift on $A$. This gives a finite decision algorithm. Local coefficient twisting needs no global trivialization in this procedure, since each step uses the based paths and the contraction on its own disk.

Finally, it suffices to check finitely many base maps. Represent the first factor of $qf$ by its edge labels in the group nerve and its higher factors by integral cocycles on $A$. Enumerate the finitely many assignments of labels in $\Gamma/A_0$ on the edges of $X$, matching the restriction and the triangle equations. For each assignment choose any fixed lifts on the other edges and solve the remaining triangle equations as linear equations in central $A_0$-labels. The solutions, when present, form a coset of the relative $1$-cocycle lattice with coefficients in $A_0$. For each higher factor solve the integral cocycle equations with the prescribed restriction, obtaining a coset of its relative cocycle lattice. Smith normal form gives one representative of each coset modulo $N_D$ in these lattices. The omitted base maps differ by a translation for which the relative lifting decision is invariant. These representations also cover disconnected $A$ because all edge assignments and cochains agree on $A$ itself. The finite checks decide extension. Positive extension instances are always enumerable by relative simplicial approximation, which also gives completeness in the hard cases.

## 2. Computing all extension classes in the critical dimension

**Theorem.** Let $Y$ be a simply connected finite simplicial complex, $D\ge2$, and suppose that its $(D-1)$st Postnikov stage is rationally a product of Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces. For a finite simplicial pair $(X,A)$ of cohomological dimension at most $D$ and a specified map $f:A\to Y$, there is a finite computable description of $[X,Y]^f$, the homotopy classes of extensions relative to $A$. The index set is a finite disjoint union of explicitly presented finitely generated abelian groups. Over each index the extension classes form a torsor for an explicitly computable finitely generated abelian quotient. A finite list of polynomial matrices and residue tables specifies that quotient at every index. Maps and torsor coordinates can be computed in both directions. For $Y=S^{2n}$ this applies in dimension $4n-1$ and gives the critical-dimension description asked for in the remarks following Manin's Theorem A. [Manin's question and Theorem A](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.10632#S1)

### Lower classes and their mapping-space loops

Write $P=Y[D-1]$, $Q=Y[D]$, and $M=\pi_D(Y)$. Write $f_P$ and $f_Q$ for the given boundary map composed with the chosen Postnikov maps. Work with models in which $Q\to P$ is the principal Eilenberg–Mac Lane fibration classified by $k\in H^{D+1}(P;M)$. The required towers and cochains are computable by effective Postnikov theory. Let
$$
H=\prod_{j=2}^{D-1}K(\pi_j(Y),j),\qquad
G=\prod_{j=2}^{D-1}H^j(X,A;\pi_j(Y)).
$$
Manin's lower-stage algorithm gives a computable rational equivalence $v:P\to H$, an action map $\mu:H\times P\to P$ with strict unit $\mu(0,z)=z$ and homotopy associativity, representatives $g_i:X\to P$ of the finitely many $G$-orbits in $[X,P]^{f_P}$, and their stabilizers $S_i\le G$. In particular the lower index set is the finite disjoint union of $G/S_i$. A chosen representative $\alpha\in G$ of an index in the $i$th quotient is realized by a map $a_\alpha:X\to H$ with $a_\alpha|_A=0$, and the corresponding lower map is $g_{i,\alpha}=\mu(a_\alpha,g_i)$. [Effective Postnikov stages and finite types of lifting](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.10632#S5) · [Manin's lower-stage action algorithm](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.10632#S6)

We need explicit generators of the fundamental group of the relative mapping space at each of the finitely many $g_i$. Use the mapping-space fibration induced by $v$, after replacing $v$ by its mapping-path fibration. Its total and base spaces consist of maps with prescribed restrictions $f_P$ and $v f_P$. The fibre over $b_i=v g_i$ is the space of lifts of $b_i$ with the prescribed lift on $A$. The homotopy fibre of $v$ has finite homotopy groups, since the two stages have finitely generated groups and $v$ is a rational isomorphism in every degree. Manin's finite-fibre lifting algorithm, Theorem 5.1(8), gives representatives of the finitely many components of the space of lifts. It also gives representatives for the finitely many based loop classes at $g_i$ in that fibre, by applying the algorithm to $X\times I$ with both ends fixed to $g_i$ and the family prescribed on $A\times I$. Work over the fixed map $b_i$ throughout in the latter application.

The fundamental group of the base mapping space at $b_i$ is the explicit finitely generated abelian group
$$
J=\prod_{j=2}^{D-1}H^{j-1}(X,A;\pi_j(Y)).
$$
Its generators have concrete cochain representatives as based loops. Lift each generator at every chosen vertical component to compute its permutation action on the finite set of components. The lifts exist by homotopy lifting with the prescribed boundary. In the mapping-path model this operation is especially direct, since it concatenates the path of the lift with the supplied base path. The resulting endpoint class can be identified among the finite representatives by the relative effective fibrewise homotopy test, Theorem 5.1(7). Thus the image of the fundamental group of the total mapping space in $J$ is the computable stabilizer of the vertical component of $g_i$. Generators of this finite-index stabilizer can be obtained by Schreier rewriting on its finite orbit. Represent each of these generators by a concrete concatenation of base loops, lift it at $g_i$, and close the lift by a vertical path to $g_i$. Such a path can be found by enumeration using the already-decided equality of vertical components. The resulting based loops, together with the representatives from the fundamental group of the vertical fibre, generate the fundamental group of the total at $g_i$ by the exact sequence. Projection to $P$ gives concrete generators $\gamma_{ij}$ in the original mapping space. All finite obstruction computations here concern the map $v$ between effective simply connected finite-type spaces as in Manin's Theorem 5.1. The mapping-path presentation is only a way to make transport explicit.

Translation $z\mapsto\mu(a_\alpha,z)$ on relative mapping spaces is a homotopy equivalence with inverse translation by $-a_\alpha$. Indeed the associativity homotopy for $\mu$ can be taken relative to $\{(0,0)\}\times P$. If a chosen homotopy is only an ordinary one, its restriction to this subspace is a loop $\ell_t$ at the identity of $P$. Prepend the inverse loop obtained by composing the first composite with $\ell_t$ on the target. Restriction is then a backtrack, and the homotopy extension property of the subspace inclusion straightens the homotopy to be constant there. Pull back along $a_\alpha,-a_\alpha$ and use the strict unit. Both relative homotopies fix the restriction on $A$. Consequently the loops $\mu(a_\alpha,\gamma_{ij})$ generate the fundamental group at $g_{i,\alpha}$.

### The integral quotient over each index

Every lower representative $g_{i,\alpha}$ lifts to $Q$ with the prescribed restriction on $A$. The only obstruction is in $H^{D+1}(X,A;M)=0$. Lifts up to homotopies over that specific lower map form a torsor for
$$
B=H^D(X,A;M).
$$
This follows either from the cochain model of the principal stage, where one solves the affine equation for the last cochain, or from the standard description using nullhomotopies of $k\circ g_{i,\alpha}$. The class of a change of lift is computed by subtracting last cochains.

Allowing the lower map to move by homotopy acts on this torsor by translations. The translation of a loop $\gamma$ of lower maps is the slant product of $\gamma^*k$ with the parameter circle, with relative coefficients on $(X,A)$. More precisely, choose a cocycle representing $k$ and integrate its pullback to $X\times S^1$ over the circle, where $\gamma$ is constant in the parameter on $A$. In a simplicial prism calculation, chains over $A$ have degenerate parameter because the map is constant there, so the slant cochain is relative. Equivalently subtract the pullback for the parameter-constant family before taking the relative product. The principal cochain equation identifies this slant class with the difference between the transported and initial top lifts, up to the overall choice of orientation sign of the circle. As a consequence the homotopy classes over the lower index form a torsor for
$$
B\big/\langle\,c_{ij}(\alpha):j\,\rangle,\qquad
c_{ij}(\alpha)=\operatorname{slant}_{S^1}\!\left(\mu(a_\alpha,\gamma_{ij})^*k\right).
$$
This uses actual integral cochains, including the torsion in $M$. Smith normal form computes the quotient when the columns are given.

### A finite specification of all relation columns

Choose a computable splitting $B\simeq\mathbb Z^r\oplus B_{\rm tor}$. Each free coordinate of $c_{ij}(\alpha)$ is a rational polynomial in the free coordinates of $\alpha\in G$. To prove this, split $\mu^*k$ over $\mathbb Q$ as the pullback from $P$ plus a class relative to $\{0\}\times P$. The relative part has a rational Künneth decomposition into positive-degree classes on $H$ times classes on $P$. Rational cohomology of $H$ is the free graded-commutative algebra on its Eilenberg–Mac Lane generators. Pullback by $a_\alpha$, which is zero on $A$, makes these monomials into products of the relative primary cohomology classes of $\alpha$. Pullback on the $P$ part is fixed by $\gamma_{ij}$. Slant product is linear and preserves the claimed polynomial dependence. Degree $D+1$ is a uniform upper bound on the polynomial degrees. The coefficients can be computed either in the effective rational cohomology rings or by evaluating the cochain formula at a finite interpolation grid of integral values for the free coordinates.

Let $e$ be the exponent of the finite group $B_{\rm tor}$, with $e=1$ in the trivial case. Reduction of coefficients identifies $B/eB$ with $H^D(X,A;M/eM)$. For a pair of cohomological dimension at most $D$ this follows from the two coefficient short exact sequences for multiplication by $e$, since all their obstructions in degree $D+1$ vanish. In particular the torsion coordinate in the chosen splitting can be read from this reduction.

Reduce $k$ mod $e$. On $H\times H\times P$, the difference between pulling $\mu^*k$ back by $(u,w,z)\mapsto(u+w,z)$ and by $(u,w,z)\mapsto(w,z)$ vanishes as a cocycle on $\{0\}\times H\times P$. Regard the difference as a relative cohomology class with finite coefficients $M/eM$. By Manin's Lemma 3.6(i) a multiplication map by some positive integer $s$ on the first $H$ factor kills this class. Such an $s$ can be computed by checking the effective relative cohomology class for successive multipliers. For $a_\alpha,a_\beta$ zero on $A$, pull back this vanishing along $(a_\beta,a_\alpha,\gamma_{ij})$, then take the slant product. Relative vanishing ensures that the equality holds in $H^D(X,A;M/eM)$. It says precisely that $c_{ij}(\alpha+s\beta)$ and $c_{ij}(\alpha)$ have the same residue. A finite table over $G/sG$ therefore specifies every torsion coordinate. The multipliers can be chosen once for all columns. This proves the asserted finite polynomial-and-residue presentation of the relation matrices. [Manin's finite-order cohomology lemma](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.10632#S3)

### Effective coordinates and scope

Choose normal forms for $G/S_i$ and for the displayed quotients of $B$. For a chosen lower index, solve the affine top lifting equation for one origin. Add a last cochain representing a requested element of $B$ to obtain its top representative. For an input map, compute its lower index using the effective lower action, or enumerate translates and test equality until its representative is found. Compute a lower homotopy to this representative and transport the top cochain. Subtract the chosen origin, then reduce the resulting class in the displayed quotient. Any choice in the transport changes the result by one of the allowed monodromy translations, so these coordinates are well defined.

The map $Y\to Y[D]$ induces a bijection of extension homotopy classes for a pair of cohomological dimension at most $D$. The higher lifting and relative homotopy obstructions vanish, since they are in degrees above $D$ on $(X,A)$, or above $D+1$ on its relative cylinder. An actual representative into the finite target can be recovered from a top representative by enumeration of relative simplicial representatives and the effective homotopy test in $Y[D]$, which must find one. This yields the two-way computable description.

For $Y=S^{2n}$, all rational groups between degrees $2n$ and $4n-1$ vanish, so $Y[4n-2]$ is rationally $K(\mathbb Q,2n)$ and $D=4n-1$ is the first critical dimension. Even for $S^2$ the torsor varies with the lower class: the top invariant is the square of the universal degree-two class, and its slant along a loop with class $h\in H^1(X;\mathbb Z)$ is $2u\smile h$ at primary class $u$. Thus on $S^1\times S^2$ the quotient is $\mathbb Z/(2u)$, as in Manin's example. The finite relation matrices above retain all torsion in higher even dimensions and apply also to a general target at its first critical stage. Earlier stable descriptions of all maps into a sphere stop in dimensions at most $4n-2$ for $S^{2n}$. [Stable range of Čadek–Krčál–Matoušek–Sergeraert–Vokřínek–Wagner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.6257)

### An exact reduction to equations one dimension higher

For the same target hypothesis and $\operatorname{cd}(X,A)\le D+1$, the existence of an extension into $Y$ is equivalent to a finite list of alternatives, each a finite system of rational polynomial equations on an integral lattice and a finite residue condition. Use the same finite lower-stage orbits and write a lower map as $\mu(a_\alpha,g_i)$. Extend its prescribed last boundary cochain arbitrarily to a cochain $\hat b$ on $X$. The only obstruction to lifting this lower map to $Q$ is
$$
[\,\mu(a_\alpha,g_i)^*k-d\hat b\,]\in H^{D+1}(X,A;M).
$$
Here the pullback and the differential agree on $A$ by construction. Its difference from the obstruction at $g_i$ is the pullback of the same relative class $\mu^*k-\operatorname{pr}_2^*k$ used above. The free coordinates are rational polynomials and the torsion coordinates are periodic, by the Künneth argument and the finite-coefficient annihilation argument in degree $D+1$. A choice lifts if and only if all the resulting equations and residue conditions hold. Any lift to $Q$ lifts further to $Y$ because the remaining relative obstructions lie in degrees above $D+1$. This gives an exact computable presentation of the arithmetic obstruction, which can itself be undecidable.


## 3. Powers of all integers prime to a fixed modulus

**Theorem.** Let $M$ be a simply connected closed oriented manifold with a self-map of absolute degree greater than one. Then there are positive integers $a,e$ such that $t^e\in D(M)$ for every positive integer $t$ coprime to $a$. In particular, no simply connected closed oriented manifold has $\{0,1\}$ together with an infinite geometric progression as its self-mapping degree set. This answers the simply connected case of Question 2.5(2). [Question 2.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11922#S2)

### A nonnegative grading from one self-map

Let $n=\dim M$, let $f$ be the given self-map, and choose a prime $p$ dividing $|\deg f|$. Poincaré duality makes $f^*$ on rational cohomology injective, hence bijective. The rational Whitehead theorem gives a rational self-equivalence, represented by a Sullivan model automorphism $\alpha$. Work on the subalgebra generated in degrees at most $n$, which models the rational $n$th Postnikov stage. The eigenvalues on each indecomposable vector space are algebraic integers, because the action there is dual to the integral action on $\pi_i(M)$ modulo torsion. The word-length filtration, which is finite in each degree, shows that each eigenvalue on the subalgebra is a product of such eigenvalues.

Take a finite Galois splitting field $E$ for the eigenvalues on the finitely many generating indecomposable spaces. For an eigenvalue $\xi$ assign the integer weight
$$
w(\xi)=v_p\left(|N_{E/\mathbb Q}(\xi)|\right).
$$
These weights are nonnegative, additive under multiplication of eigenvalues, and invariant under Galois conjugation. The product of two generalized eigenspaces belongs to the generalized eigenspace for the product eigenvalue, because multiplication intertwines $\alpha\otimes\alpha$ with $\alpha$. The differential preserves generalized eigenspaces. Grouping them by weight and descending gives a rational grading of the model. The action on the cohomological orientation line has weight $w_0=[E: \mathbb Q]v_p(|\deg f|)>0$. Scaling the part of weight $i$ by $t^i$ defines a rational self-equivalence $\lambda_t$ for each positive integer $t$, acting on the orientation by $t^{w_0}$. The nonnegative weights allow weight-zero generators.

### An integral Postnikov model carrying the dilations

Use the Moore–Postnikov lattice construction of Manin, Lemma 5.1, for the rational $n$th Postnikov stage $P$. We need only its lattice step, which works for nonnegative weights. Its input is the rational action $\lambda_t$ on homotopy and homology and the facts that it has a rational eigenbasis and that $t^i$ preserves the integer lattice on an eigenline of weight $i$. The strict positivity used to kill mod-$p$ homology in Manin's Lemma 5.2 is unnecessary here. The proof of Lemma 5.1 gives stages $K_j\to P$ which are rational equivalences, with integral free homotopy lattices in degrees through $j$ and rational homotopy groups above $j$. At each stage it constructs self-maps $r_{q,j}$ for every prime $q$, realizing $\lambda_q$ on $P$, with eigenvalues $q^i$ on the homotopy lattices and a common lattice eigenbasis. [Manin, Lemma 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.02173v4#S5)

In this construction we can also ensure a map $g_j:M\to K_j$ over the given rationalization into $P$. At the initial stage take the rationalization itself. In the next lattice step Manin's calculation constructs a class $\kappa\in H^{j+1}(K_j;(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)^r)$ and intertwines it with the self-maps on $K_j$ and the diagonal actions on the coefficient group. Explicitly, as a homomorphism on homology it is zero on the chosen complementary summand and is multiplication by an integer $N$ followed by reduction modulo the eigenlattice on the divisible summand. The computation allows replacing $N$ by a multiple. Alternatively, once $\kappa$ intertwines the actions, so does any integer multiple of it. The image of $\kappa\circ(g_j)_*$ is finite because $H_{j+1}(M;\mathbb Z)$ is finitely generated and the coefficient group is torsion. Choose the multiple to annihilate that finite image. The chosen multiple of $\kappa$ has trivial pullback, by the universal coefficient theorem with the injective coefficient group. Thus $g_j$ lifts to its homotopy fibre $K_{j+1}$. The multiple leaves the new homotopy group a full free eigenlattice, since its rational Hurewicz-to-coefficients composite still has kernel a full eigenlattice and is surjective. This gives $g:M\to K_n$ and $r_q:K_n\to K_n$ for all primes $q$. Write $K=K_n$ and $b:K\to P$. Then $b g$ is rationalization into $P$, and $b r_q\simeq\lambda_q b$.

The space $K$ is a simply connected $n$-type with finitely generated homotopy groups, so it has finite-type homology. For a positive integer $t$, compose the $r_q$ according to its prime factorization and call the result $r_t$. This realizes $\lambda_t$ on $P$. When a prime $\ell$ does not divide $t$, $r_t$ is an $\ell$-local homotopy equivalence, because it acts invertibly on the homotopy lattices after localization. No commutation identities among the integral $r_q$ are required.

### Restoring the torsion and lifting to the manifold

The homotopy fibre of $g$ is connected. Its homotopy groups through degree $n-1$ are finite by the exact sequence, because the adjacent homotopy groups of the source and target are finitely generated and $g$ is a rational isomorphism through degree $n$. Replace $g$ by a fibration and take its fibrewise $(n-1)$st Postnikov section $E'\to K$, with fibre $F$. Let $S$ be the finite set of primes dividing the orders of the homotopy groups of $F$.

Classify this fibration by a map to $B\operatorname{haut}(F)$, where $\operatorname{haut}(F)$ is the space of unbased homotopy self-equivalences. Since $K$ is simply connected, its classifying map lifts to the simply connected cover $T$ of $B\operatorname{haut}(F)$. The space $T$ is an $n$-type whose homotopy groups are finite and supported at the primes in $S$. Indeed for positive homotopy degrees the space of equivalences has the same homotopy groups as $\operatorname{Map}(F,F)$ at the identity. The mapping-space Postnikov tower computes these groups in successive extensions of subquotients of cohomology groups of $F$ with finite homotopy coefficients of the target. This description follows by applying the mapping-space functor to each Postnikov fibration, with the local coefficient system induced by the identity. The $K(\pi_1F,1)$ stage has identity-component mapping space with only $\pi_1$, a subgroup of the finite group $\pi_1F$. The cohomology groups at the remaining stages are finite, since $F$ has a finite-type CW model, and their prime support is contained in that of the coefficient groups. The highest possible positive homotopy degree of the equivalence space is at most $n-1$, as the target is an $(n-1)$-type. These facts prove the claim about $T$. [Fibration classification by homotopy equivalences](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.1470)

The set $[K,T]$ is finite by ordinary Postnikov obstruction theory and finite type of $K$. If $t$ is coprime to all primes in $S$, then $r_t$ is an equivalence after localization keeping exactly the primes in $S$, by its homotopy lattice actions. The target $T$ is already local for this localization. Consequently precomposition by $r_t$ permutes $[K,T]$. Put $b_0=|[K,T]|!$, so $r_t^{b_0}$ preserves the class of the fibration for all these $t$. An equivalence with its pullback gives a map $E'\to E'$ covering $r_t^{b_0}$ up to homotopy.

The canonical map $M\to E'$ is $n$-connected, since its homotopy fibre is the fibre of the map from the original fibre to its $(n-1)$st Postnikov section. Therefore the composite $M\to E'\to E'$ lifts up to homotopy to $M\to M$ because $M$ has the homotopy type of an $n$-dimensional CW complex. This lift induces $\lambda_t^{b_0}$ on the rational cohomological orientation, hence has degree $t^{b_0w_0}$. Taking $a=\prod_{\ell\in S}\ell$ and $e=b_0w_0$ proves the positive degree theorem. In particular self-map degrees include powers of infinitely many distinct primes, whereas an infinite geometric progression of integers has prime factors in a fixed finite set. Question 2.5(2) for arbitrary fundamental groups remains open here.

### The explicit nonrealizability question

Let $p_e$ be the $e$th prime and let $H$ be the diagonal halting set. The multiplicative set
$$
\{0\}\cup\{m>0:\ \text{all prime factors }p_e\text{ of }m\text{ have } e\notin H\}
$$
contains 0 and 1. Its complement is computably enumerable, by listing all negative integers and all multiples of the primes whose indices enter $H$. The set itself is not computably enumerable, since its prime trace is the complement of $H$. Every mapping-degree set is computably enumerable, by Löh–Uschold. This gives an explicit example for both parts of Question 2.4, using the prime-factor monoid from the question's paper and the earlier enumerability obstruction. A decidable explicit example remains open here. [Question 2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11922#S2) · [Löh–Uschold, Appendix A](https://loeh.app.uni-regensburg.de/preprints/l2_comp.pdf#page=29)


## 4. Linear persistence in the first nonclosed Sullivan degree

Let $X$ be a finite simplicial complex of dimension $n$ and let $Y$ be a simply connected finite complex. Suppose every indecomposable of degree less than $n$ in the real minimal Sullivan model of $Y$ is closed. Then the finite bars of $PH_i(\operatorname{Lip}(X,Y),\log_+\operatorname{Lip})$ have uniformly bounded length for every $i$, with a bound independent of the mapping component. In fact, for each parameter dimension $r$ there is $C(X,Y,r)$ such that any family $F:Z\times X\to Y$ over a finite simplicial pair $(Z,B)$ with $\dim Z\le r$ and every component of $Z$ meeting $B$ is homotopic relative to $B\times X$ to a family of spatial Lipschitz constant at most $C(X,Y,r)(L+1)$, provided its slices on $B$ have Lipschitz constant at most $L$.

In particular, this applies if $Y$ is rationally $(k-1)$-connected and $\dim X\le 2k-1$. A differential is decomposable and so has degree at least $2k$. This adds the first dimension with possible nonlinear Sullivan differentials to the all-component persistence range, and does not require scalability or a two-stage model above the spatial dimension. The preceding abelian-stage dimensional range for homotopy thickness is discussed by Chambers, Manin, and Weinberger, Corollary 6.1. [Chambers–Manin–Weinberger](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03513) · [Block–Manin–Weinberger, Question 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S1.SS3)

### A preliminary model

Through degree $n$, write the model as $\Lambda(V\oplus W)$ with $dV=0$, $W$ concentrated in degree $n$, and $d:W\to\Lambda V$ injective. Move the kernel of this differential into $V$. All subsequent modifications on this subalgebra extend to algebraic homotopies on the full Sullivan model by following the generator order. For the next generator $u$, take its initial value plus the interval integral of the parameter component of the already defined image of $du$. This solves the differential equation and preserves any relative restrictions of the homotopy. The horizontal values of all these subsequent generators vanish for dimensional reasons.

Put $R=L+1$. The preliminary family argument for two-stage targets, before shadowing, gives an algebraic model $\phi$ on a finite parameter product, with boundary matching an actual prepared family, satisfying
$$
\|\phi(v)_{\mathrm{hor}}\|_\infty\le C R^{|v|},\qquad
\|\phi(w)_{\mathrm{hor}}\|_\infty\le C R^{n+1}.
$$
The boundary horizontal $W$ bound is $C R^n$. For clarity, the preliminary argument can be run directly on $X$. Work in a fixed Lipschitz manifold thickening of the target, with a fixed inclusion and retraction. Approximate the family in a fixed Euclidean embedding of the thickened target by tensor-product Bernstein polynomials on the pairs of parameter and spatial simplices, then apply the smooth neighborhood retraction. Samples on a face alone enter the restriction to that face, so the approximants respect all faces. On $B$, the parameter samples entering each value lie in $B$, and their spatial Lipschitz constants are at most $L$. The derivative formula for the spatial Bernstein polynomials uses the spatial degree times first differences of samples separated by the reciprocal of that degree. This bounds the spatial derivative by $C R$ on $B$, and hence by $2 C R$ on a neighborhood of $B$ in the finite family. The approximation can be made uniformly as close as needed by increasing the degrees. The short homotopy between the old and prepared boundary families also costs $O(R)$ spatially and is appended at the end. These operations give a piecewise smooth prepared family, with parameter derivative bounds allowed to depend on the family.

For the cochain contractions on $X$, one can use the barycentric Dupont operators, followed by a fixed finite-dimensional splitting on simplicial cochains. The resulting operators $K,P$ satisfy $d_XK+Kd_X=1-P$, with $P$ landing in fixed closed representatives and $K,P$ bounded on spatial coefficient sup norms. These bounds follow directly from Dupont's formula, which uses finitely many vertex radial homotopy integrals and multiplication by fixed Whitney forms on each simplex. They act on parameter families by extending in the spatial variables with the product signs. [Dupont contraction formulas](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v170-n1-p07-p.pdf)

On each parameter component choose the model $a$ of one low boundary slice. The closed generator difference $\delta_v=F^*m_Y(v)-a(v)$ has zero horizontal spatial cohomology. Homotope $F^*m_Y(v)$ to $a(v)+P\delta_v$ by subtracting $d(tK\delta_v)$ on each $v$ and integrating the already defined $d w$ over $t$ on each $w$. The resulting $V$ horizontal images equal $a(v)$. On low slices and along this homotopy over $B$ the $W$ images are $O(R^{n+1})$, by the total degree $n+1$ of $d w$ and the bounded operators. Let $u_w(z)$ be the spatial cohomology of the resulting $W$ image minus $a(w)$. Since the closed-stage horizontal images are fixed, differentiating in the parameter shows that $d_Zu$ lies in the image of the fixed linear map $J$ that inserts one class $h_v\in H^{|v|-1}(X;\mathbb R)$ into $d w$ with the other factors $[a(v)]$. The term involving the spatial differential of the mixed $W$ image integrates to zero in cohomology. Thus $u(z)\in\operatorname{im}J$ on the whole parameter component, since it is zero at the chosen reference slice. If the preparation creates a reference-form exact discrepancy at this slice, its class is still zero.

Trim $u(z)$ to $\chi(z)u(z)$, with $\chi=1$ near $B$ and supported in a neighborhood where its norm is $O(R^{n+1})$. Choose spatially closed representatives $h_v(z)$ lifting $(\chi-1)u$ through a fixed right inverse of $J$ on its image. Add $d(t h_v(z))$ on the closed stage and extend over the second stage by integration. Horizontal $V$ images do not change, and the $W$ cohomology now equals $[a(w)]+\chi u_w$. Subtract the exact horizontal discrepancy on $W$ by a full differential using $K$. The possible size of the inverse of $J$ affects mixed coefficients only. Match the prepared boundary maps by using the original low slices, the first normalization homotopy, and the boundary exact-form normalization on an external parameter collar. The latter is also bounded by $C R^{n+1}$ on horizontal $W$ forms because both the normalized form and its fixed cohomology representative have that bound. Later corrections can be applied to that entire collared product, whose outer boundary is still actual and whose horizontal bounds are the displayed bounds. The model is formally homotopic to the prepared family relative to this outer boundary. Equivalently, one can insert the collar by using a cutoff in the homotopy parameter near $B$ and delaying the normalization there. The relative family argument and the collar construction are also given in the earlier higher-persistence proof. [Earlier two-stage family preparation](ManinNewProofs.md#5-higher-persistence-for-two-stage-targets)

### Local corrections in the top degree

Choose orientations on the $n$-simplices. In the interior of each choose finitely many disjoint small coordinate balls. Precompose the preliminary family by a fixed spatial squeeze collapsing a neighborhood of each ball to a point and equal to the identity near all simplex faces. A fixed simplexwise smooth Lipschitz homotopy to the identity performs this squeeze. The pullbacks of the horizontal $V$ forms on the balls are now zero. The squeeze changes the prepared boundary by a homotopy of spatial cost $O(R)$, which is undone on a parameter collar after shadowing.

In a single oriented ball, let $\beta_v$ be compactly supported smooth forms of degree $|v|-1$. Add $d((s\lambda)^{|v|}\beta_v)$ to the full image of $v$, with $s$ an auxiliary interval parameter and $\lambda\ge 0$ allowed to depend on the family parameter. Lift over $w$ by integration as above. For a fixed parameter slice, on the support of this change there are no background horizontal $V$ factors. Hence the change of the $W$ integrals over that spatial simplex is exactly $\lambda^{n+1}T(\beta)$, where $T(\beta)\in W^*$ is given on $w$ by transgressing $dw$ along $v\mapsto d(s^{|v|}\beta_v)$ and integrating over the ball. Its fixed-coordinate horizontal cost on $v$ is $O(\lambda^{|v|})$.

The vectors $T(\beta)$ span $W^*$. In detail, for a monomial $v_1\cdots v_\ell$ in $dw$ the transgression integral at weight $n+1$ equals
$$
\int \beta_{v_1}\wedge d\beta_{v_2}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\beta_{v_\ell}.
$$
Every term of the interval contraction agrees with this one after integration by parts, including its contraction sign. Their coefficients are $|v_j|/(n+1)$, with sum one. A nonzero monomial gives a nonzero functional after polarization in occurrences of any repeated generator. Repeated generators have even degree, so the polarized terms all agree under permutations of those occurrences. To test the polarized functional, put a compactly supported bump times a coordinate form on the first slot, and take the other $d\beta$'s to be complementary constant coordinate forms on its support. Such constant forms have compactly supported primitives after cutting off their usual local primitives outside this support. The resulting top integral is nonzero. For a nonzero linear combination of monomials, independently rescaling each generator separates the distinct multidegrees, so its functional cannot vanish identically. Apply this to $dw$ for every $0\ne w\in W$, using injectivity of $d|_W$. This proves the span claim.

Use copies of a fixed basis of these $T$ vectors, with one oriented ball of each sign for each basis vector, in each top simplex. Orientation reversal changes the integral sign. Write $I_\sigma(z)\in W^*$ for the original vector of top integrals on the simplex. Its norm is $O(R^{n+1})$ everywhere and $O(R^n)$ on a neighborhood of the outer parameter boundary. Trim it to $\rho(z)I_\sigma(z)$ with $\rho=1$ in a smaller neighborhood of the boundary and supported in the low neighborhood. Resolve the required difference into the fixed basis. For a coefficient $c(z)$, choose the two amplitudes from its positive and negative parts to realize $c(z)$ to a uniformly bounded error and to be identically zero near the boundary. For example use the Lipschitz function that is zero for $x\le0$, is $x$ for $0\le x\le1$, and is $x^{1/(n+1)}$ for $x\ge1$, on $c$ and $-c$. Its $(n+1)$st power differs from the positive part by at most one. Piecewise smooth approximation of the parameter coefficients to within one, vanishing on a smaller boundary neighborhood, suffices if needed. The amplitudes are $O(R)$ and additions on the disjoint balls do not interact. Thus all $V$ bounds remain ordinary linear bounds, and every top-simplex $W$ integral is now $O(R^n)$, with exact agreement retained near the boundary where additions are zero.

### Ordinary shadowing

For each top simplex the difference between a closed top form and the Whitney top form with the same integral is exact relative to its faces. The simplex Dupont operator gives compatible primitives, since its faces see the restriction of a top form as zero. On each $w$, subtract the full differential of this spatial primitive, with a parameter cutoff zero near the boundary and one off a slightly larger low neighborhood. Horizontally, the image becomes a combination of its unchanged low-boundary values and the top Whitney forms with the new bounded integrals. Its norm is $O(R^n)$ on the whole parameter product. The exact-form homotopy fixes the boundary. All mixed coefficients and weak derivatives are finite for the chosen family. If using a Lipschitz parameter amplitude, the identities hold in flat forms, or one may smooth the amplitude with bounded integral error and preserve its vanishing near the boundary.

Stretch the parameter metric until the mixed coefficients and boundary parameter Lipschitz constants satisfy ordinary dilatation bounds, without changing any spatial slice metric. Relative shadowing applies to a bounded-geometry subdivision of the product. It gives an actual family of spatial cost $CR$, in the prescribed genuine relative homotopy class, since the algebraic changes started from the original family and were made by homotopies relative to its prepared boundary. Undo the initial spatial squeeze and boundary smoothing on the controlled collars. These collars can be transferred back to the original parameter pair by a homotopy inverse to collar collapse relative to the outside copy of $B$. This uses the homotopy extension property and affects only parameter coordinates. The uniform spatial bounds therefore persist. A finite singular bounding chain is such a finite parameter family after identifying its canceled faces, which proves the finite-bar assertion. [Relative shadowing, Theorem 6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S6.SS2)

This proves the all-component linear bound in the indicated dimension range. When a nonclosed generator lies strictly below the top spatial degree, a local correction can also change smaller-degree primitives, and the argument above gives no compatibility mechanism for those changes. The all-source statement of Question 1.3 remains open here.

### Uniform linear persistence for suspension sources

Let $X$ be a finite pointed suspension with a fixed polyhedral metric, and let $Y$ be a closed simply connected Riemannian manifold with a two-stage real minimal model. For each $r$ there is $C(X,Y,r)$ such that any continuous family $F:Z\times X\to Y$ over a finite simplicial pair $(Z,B)$, with $\dim Z\le r$ and every component of $Z$ meeting $B$, is homotopic relative to $B\times X$ to a family of $C(X,Y,r)(L+1)$-Lipschitz spatial slices, provided the original boundary slices are $L$-Lipschitz. In the free case the homotopy can preserve the evaluation at the suspension basepoint. The conclusion holds also for finite pointed complexes Lipschitz homotopy equivalent to such a suspension. Consequently every finite bar in every homological degree has bounded log length, uniformly over all mapping components. Chambers, Manin, and Weinberger, Corollary 1.3, prove linear spatial thickness for individual paths between endpoint maps from suspensions, with a separate time estimate. The assertion here concerns higher parameter families and preserves the prescribed relative homotopy class. [Chambers–Manin–Weinberger](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03513) · [Block–Manin–Weinberger, Question 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S1.SS3)

#### Based translation

Write $f*g$ for concatenation in the suspension coordinate, with the two halves assigned fixed equal lengths, and $f^{-1}$ for reversal in that coordinate. For maps taking the basepoint to the same point of $Y$, these operations are defined by precomposing with fixed Lipschitz maps of the suspension. Their spatial cost is at most a fixed multiple of the maximum input cost, and they are continuous in the uniform topology. The standard reparametrization and cancellation homotopies also have this spatial control. For cancellation, shorten the backtracking path by retaining opposite traversals of a progressively shorter final subinterval of the reversed map's suspension coordinate.

First fix the value at the basepoint. On each component of $Z$, choose a boundary slice $g=F_b$, of cost at most $L$. The based family $F*g^{-1}$ lies in the component of the constant map, because every original slice is based homotopic to $g$. Its prescribed boundary has spatial cost $O(L+1)$. Apply the linear family theorem in the fixed null component of the relative mapping space for $(X,\{*\})$, with fixed restriction the basepoint value. The result is a family of spatial cost $O(L+1)$ homotopic to $F*g^{-1}$ relative to $B$, preserving the basepoint. The constant here is independent of $g$ and of the original component, since the translated component is always the fixed component of the constant map. For different values of the basepoint in a fixed compact target the constants can be taken uniform by taking finitely many coordinate balls and using uniformly Lipschitz changes of basepoint within them. [Fixed-component relative linear cutoff](ManinPersistenceAndDegrees.md#3-linear-persistence-on-each-fixed-two-stage-component)

Translate the new family back by right concatenation with $g$. Its boundary is $(F*g^{-1})*g$. Fixed reassociation and cancellation give a controlled homotopy back to the original $F$ on the boundary. Appending this homotopy on a parameter collar fixes the original boundary exactly. This preserves the actual relative homotopy class, since before the controlled compression the same reassociation and cancellation homotopy is defined over the entire parameter space. The homotopy extension property transfers the collared family back to the original pair relative to the prescribed boundary, involving only reparametrization of $Z$. This proves the uniform based theorem. A fixed pointed Lipschitz homotopy equivalence transfers these operations and costs by precomposition and the analogous collars from its inverse homotopies.

#### Free families with fixed evaluation

Let $e:Z\to Y$ be evaluation at the suspension basepoint in the original free family. Choose a finite cover of $Y$ by coordinate balls on which a point $y$ can be moved to that ball's center by a smoothly varying diffeomorphism $h_y$ isotopic to the identity. These diffeomorphisms and inverses have uniformly bounded Lipschitz constants on smaller balls still covering $Y$. They give local trivializations of the evaluation map of the free mapping space. Subdivide $Z$ so the image of each simplex under $e$ lies in one smaller ball. Such subdivision is finite by continuity and does not increase its dimension.

At each vertex outside $B$, choose a low slice at one point of $B$ in its parameter component. Move the value at the basepoint of that low slice to $e(z)$ by a uniformly Lipschitz diffeomorphism isotopic to the identity. There is a uniform bound because the target is compact and connected. One can use local diffeomorphisms to move to and from the centers of finitely many balls, and fix a diffeomorphism between each ordered pair of centers by pushing along a fixed path. The resulting based map is based homotopic to the original slice at that vertex, since it is freely homotopic and the ambiguity in turning free homotopies into based homotopies is the action of $\pi_1(Y)$, which is trivial. Thus all vertices can be controlled while keeping their evaluation points fixed.

Induct over the positive-dimensional simplices outside $B$. Over a simplex, use $h_{e(z)}$ to rebase its family to that chart center. The boundary is already controlled, so the based theorem gives a controlled replacement relative to the boundary of the simplex. Undo the change of basepoint. This keeps the evaluation point $e(z)$ at every parameter. At each stage the homotopies of sections on the already treated skeleton extend to homotopies of sections on the whole parameter complex. To see this, extend cell by cell in the local product coordinates for the evaluation bundle using the ordinary homotopy extension property of the simplex-boundary pair. Thus every step respects the original family homotopy class and fixes $B$. The bound changes by a fixed factor per parameter dimension, independent of the number of simplices. This proves the free-family assertion. Apply it to a finite bounding chain to get the uniform persistence statement.

This gives a full all-component answer to Question 1.3 for suspension sources, without scalability. Arbitrary source complexes can lack the domain concatenation used in this argument, so the full question remains open here.

## 5. Positive weights are unnecessary in arbitrarily high finite Postnikov ranges

**Theorem.** For each odd integer $t\ge5$, set $D=(3t+3)t+2$. There is a $t$-connected finite $D$-dimensional complex $Y_t$ without positive weights for which every finite source $X$ with $\dim X\le D-2$ satisfies the conclusion of Block–Manin–Weinberger Theorem C. In particular the finite bars in each degree in the null mapping component have bounded log length. Infinite bars also have bounded births. The constants may depend on the target, source, and degree. This gives arbitrarily large ranges, reaching two dimensions below the target dimension, in which positive weights are unnecessary. [Theorem C and Question 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S1.SS3)

### A family of attaching relations

Let $b=t+1$ and $P_b=S^b\cup_{[\iota,\iota]}e^{2b}$, where $b$ is even. Its rational cohomology is $\mathbb Q[x]/(x^3)$ with $|x|=b$, since the Hopf invariant of the Whitehead square is 2. A model is $(\Lambda(x,y),dy=x^3)$ with $|y|=3b-1$. For example, choose a closed form representing $x$ and take $y$ to zero. The cube of the form vanishes by dimension, and the map is a cohomology isomorphism. The rational homotopy Lie algebra of the loop space is abelian on generators in degrees $t$ and $3t+1$, by the absence of a quadratic part in the Sullivan differential.

Put $B=S^{2t+1}\vee P_b$. In its rational loop homotopy Lie algebra let $a,x,h$ denote the generator of the sphere and the two generators of $P_b$, respectively. They have degrees $2t,t,3t+1$. We also choose integral representatives for the generators used in attaching maps, taking $h$ to be a nonzero integral multiple of its rational generator. By the Adams–Hilton model for a wedge and the Milnor–Moore theorem, the rational loop homotopy Lie algebra injects into the graded associative algebra
$$
H_*(\Omega B;\mathbb Q)\cong \mathbb Q\langle a,x,h\rangle/(x^2,\ xh-hx).
$$
The wedge gives a free product of chain algebras. Over a field, passage to homology preserves the free product by the direct sum decomposition into alternating tensors of augmentation ideals. The factors in this case are the tensor algebra on the single even $a$ and the graded-commutative algebra on the odd $x$ and even $h$. The displayed formula also shows that the positive degrees $t,2t,3t+1$ of this associative algebra are each one-dimensional. At degree $2t$ the only additional candidate is $x^2=0$. At degree $3t+1$, all words not using $h$ have degrees divisible by $t$, and multiplying $h$ by a positive-degree element is too large. [Adams–Hilton on the loop chain algebra](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02564350) · [Milnor–Moore on the loop homotopy Lie algebra](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970615)

Write $\omega=[a,x]$, an odd element of degree $3t$. Let $j=(3t+1)/2$, $j'=(3t-1)/2$ and use $\operatorname{ad}_u(v)=[u,v]$. The integral iterated Whitehead products represented by the following loop Lie words all have loop degree $D-2$:
$$
f_1=\operatorname{ad}_a^{j-2}[\omega,\omega],\qquad
f_2=\operatorname{ad}_a^{j'-5}\operatorname{ad}_{\omega}^{4}(a),\qquad
f_3=\operatorname{ad}_h^{t}(a).
$$
All exponents are nonnegative for the specified $t$.

For clarity, these three elements are rationally independent. The associative subalgebra on $a,\omega$ is free on those symbols. Within a fixed multidegree of a polynomial in $a,\omega$, expand $\omega=ax-xa$. The maximal expanded word, in lexicographic order with $a>x$, is obtained by replacing each $\omega$ with $ax$. The code words $a,ax$ give unique parsing from right to left. All these maximal words survive the relation $x^2=0$, since no two $x$ are adjacent. Hence the largest of the distinct maximal words cannot cancel. This proves freeness, separately in each multidegree. Now $[\omega,\omega]=2\omega^2$ and $\operatorname{ad}_\omega^{4}(a)=\operatorname{ad}_{\omega^2}^2(a)$. The expansions of $f_1$ and $f_2$ have nonzero coefficients on $a^{j-2}\omega^2$ and $a^{j'-4}\omega^4$, respectively, by commuting through the even $a$ and $\omega^2$. The subalgebra on $a,h$ is also free because neither algebra relation involves only those symbols. Thus $f_3$ is nonzero by its coefficient on $h^ta$. Finally the three distinct multidegrees in $a,x,h$ make the words independent.

### One relation defeats a grading

Define $Y_t=B\cup_{f_1+f_2+f_3}e^D$. Its connectivity is $t$ by the cell dimensions. The inclusion of $B$ induces rational homotopy isomorphisms through $D-2$ and has kernel exactly the span of $f_1+f_2+f_3$ in degree $D-1$. Indeed the pair is $(D-1)$-connected, and its relative homotopy group in degree $D$ maps to the top relative homology group by the relative Hurewicz isomorphism. Under the boundary homomorphism a generator goes to the attaching map.

Let $T$ be any rational self-equivalence of $Y_t$. It sends $x$ to $rx$ and $a$ to $sa$ for nonzero rationals $r,s$, by the one-dimensional computations. On $h$ it acts by $r^3$. To see this last assertion, collapse the odd sphere in $B$ to get a map to $P_b$. This map kills every $f_i$ and extends to $Y_t$. It is an isomorphism on the one-dimensional rational homotopy group spanned by $h$. Compose the inclusion of $P_b$ with $T$ and this extended projection. A self-map of the rationalized $P_b$ acting by $r$ on its degree-$b$ rational homotopy must act by $r^3$ on the other generator by $dy=x^3$.

The action on the images of $f_1,f_2,f_3$ therefore has multipliers $r^2s^j,r^4s^{j'}$, and $r^{3t}s$. Their sum is zero in $Y_t$, with exactly this one relation in their span. Thus the three multipliers must coincide. The first equality gives $s=r^2$. Substituting gives $r^{3t+3}=r^{3t+2}$ and hence $r=s=1$. A positive weight grading supplies a rational self-equivalence that acts by a positive power of 2 on the one-dimensional indecomposables corresponding to $x$. That contradicts the preceding conclusion, so $Y_t$ has no positive weights. This is the same mechanism as the Mimura–Toda example, with independent word multiplicities adapted to arbitrary connectivity. [Mimura–Toda, §4](https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=com-001%3A1971%3A46%3A%3A11)

### Separate relations give the required positive-weight target

Let $W_t$ be obtained from $B$ by attaching three $D$-cells along the three classes separately. The identity of $B$ extends to $Y_t\to W_t$ by the nullity of their sum in $W_t$. Both sets of attachments leave every homotopy group through $D-2$ unchanged.

There is a self-map of $B$ of degree two on the odd sphere and acting by two on $H^b(P_b)$. For the second factor start with a degree-two map of $S^b$. Naturality and bilinearity of the Whitehead square let it extend over the top cell by a cone of degree four. Choose the integral $h$ above to be a nonzero multiple of a lift of its rational generator annihilating the torsion ambiguity, by multiplying by the order of the torsion subgroup of the finitely generated group $\pi_{3b-1}(P_b)$. The self-map then acts by eight on this $h$ exactly. It acts on each $f_i$ by its individual positive power of two and hence extends over the separate cells by maps of those degrees. The resulting self-map on $W_t$ has positive powers of two as its eigenvalues on all positive cohomology. For the top cohomology this follows from the relative cellular group in degree $D$, since $B$ has no $(D-1)$- or $D$-cells. For the lower cohomology it follows from the stated restrictions.

An endomorphism $T$ with this cohomology property yields positive weights on the simply connected minimal model $M$ of $W_t$. Let $M_{<k}$ be the subalgebra generated below degree $k$ and $V^k$ the degree-$k$ indecomposables. The sequence $H^k(M)\to V^k\to H^{k+1}(M_{<k})$ is equivariant and exact at $V^k$. The second arrow is given by the differential, and an element in its kernel can be made closed by subtracting a primitive from $M_{<k}$. The differential lands in $M_{<k}$ by decomposability and simple connectivity. Induction on $k$ shows that the eigenvalues on $V^k$ are positive powers of two, since those on the right arise from products of such eigenvalues on lower-degree generators. The same holds on the positive-degree part of $M$. Its rational generalized eigenspaces for the eigenvalues $2^w$ give a weight grading, since the differential commutes with $T$ and the product intertwines the tensor action. All weights on the positive-degree part are positive.

Apply the rational Postnikov transfer for persistence to $Y_t\to W_t$ in every source dimension at most $D-2$. This gives both assertions about the null mapping component. The argument supplies a dimension-range result for Question 1.2. The all-source question for a single target without positive weights remains open here. [Rational Postnikov transfer](ManinPersistenceAndDegrees.md#7-a-rational-postnikov-transfer-for-persistence)

## 6. Rational fiberwise H-spaces and effective lifting

**Theorem.** Fix a map $p:Y\to B$ between simply connected finite simplicial complexes, with simply connected homotopy fiber. Use its fibration model, and suppose it has a section. Fix a dimension $n$. Suppose the fiberwise rationalization followed by fiberwise $n$th Postnikov truncation admits a fiberwise pointed multiplication with unit up to fiberwise homotopy. Then for finite pairs $(X,A)$ of dimension at most $n$, a base map $g:X\to B$, and a prescribed lift on $A$, one can decide whether the lift extends over $X$. One can also compute the relative homotopy classes of lifts in a finite description by finitely generated abelian coordinates and finite labels, with effective conversion in both directions. The algorithm is uniform in finite presentations of $p$ and $n$ under the stated promise. This answers the rational fiberwise form of Manin's question in the simply connected finite setting, including dimension-bounded problems. The condition is on multiplication over the base, as proposed in §1.4, and need not imply fiberwise triviality. [Manin, §1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.10632#S1.SS4) · [Lupton–Smith, definition and rationalization](https://arxiv.org/html/1208.4073#S2)

### A rational linear comparison by the power map

Write $T\to B$ for the rational fiberwise $n$-type with its section. In this argument a fibration over $B$ is regarded as a local system of spaces on the fundamental homotopy groupoid of $B$. This keeps the coherent higher transport in the fibration as well as ordinary loop transport. Based spaces are used for sectioned fibrations. Apply reduced rational chains to the fibers of $T$, and truncate above degree $n$, to obtain a local system $C$ of connective rational chain complexes. There is a natural map from the fiberwise space to the underlying space of this local system, induced by sending a point to its reduced chain generator. For example the construction can be made using the derived reduced free simplicial rational vector space functor on the local system followed by normalized chains and Dold–Kan.

The fiberwise square map $\psi=\mu\Delta$ acts on $C$. On each fiber, the rational H-space theorem identifies the rational cohomology algebra with the free graded commutative algebra on the duals of the rational homotopy groups. If $I$ is its augmentation ideal, the unit axioms imply $\psi^*=2^k$ on $I^k/I^{k+1}$. Indeed the induced coproduct takes a positive-degree element to the two terms in which just one factor is positive, plus terms positive in both factors. On a product of $k$ indecomposables this gives the claimed factor. There are finitely many possible $k$ through any specified degree. Since those eigenvalues are distinct, the induced action on reduced cohomology in each degree is diagonalizable. The eigenspace at $2$ pairs perfectly with the rational Hurewicz image of rational homotopy, by the free-algebra description and because $\psi$ induces multiplication by $2$ on homotopy.

Choose a rational polynomial $P$ that takes value $1$ at $2$ and $0$ at the other powers of $2$ that can occur through degree $n$. Take the telescope of $P(\psi)$ on $C$ in the category of chain-complex local systems. Homotopy colimits and homology here are computed fiberwise, and the homology of this telescope is the eigenspace at $2$. Thus the natural map from $T$ to the underlying space of the telescope induces an isomorphism on fiber homotopy through degree $n$, and both sides have no higher fiber homotopy. In particular this gives a rational comparison over the original base to a local system of rational simplicial abelian groups whose fibers have homotopy concentrated in degrees $2,\ldots,n$. This argument uses the actual power map over $B$. A multiplication solely on a typical fiber would not give this endomorphism of the local system.

### An integral form of the linear comparison

Every local system of rational connective chain complexes on a finite simply connected $B$, with finitely many finite-dimensional homology groups, admits an integral form in which the homology of the typical fiber is free of matching ranks. To see this, choose the chain complex $L=\bigoplus_i\mathbb Z^{r_i}[i]$ with zero differential. Its Dold–Kan simplicial abelian group splits into the indicated shifts. Consider the simplicial group of upper triangular changes of coordinates with identity diagonal, denoted by $U_{\mathbb Z}$. The entry from degree $i$ to degree $j>i$ is the simplicial mapping space of maps of simplicial abelian groups between the two corresponding summands. Composition is strict, and positive triangular matrices are nilpotent off the diagonal, so every such change of coordinates is invertible. This group acts by additive automorphisms on the Dold–Kan group of $L$.

The corresponding $U_{\mathbb Q}$ is weakly equivalent to the identity component of the space of homotopy automorphisms of the rational chain complex $L\otimes\mathbb Q$. Indeed the simplicial mapping spaces compute the derived Hom for these free chain complexes. An entry from degree $i$ to degree $j$ has homotopy concentrated in degree $j-i$ when $j\ge i$. For $j=i$, the identity component is contractible, and for $j<i$ the whole entry space is contractible. Thus strict positive triangular matrices capture exactly the homotopies of automorphisms in the identity component. Since $B$ is simply connected, the classifying map of the rational local system lifts to the classifying space of this identity component. We use the usual classification of local systems by the classifying space of homotopy automorphisms, equivalently the classification of families of objects in a homotopy theory after fixing the equivalence type of a fiber. Here the homotopy theory is that of connective rational chain complexes, so all the mapping spaces just used have the explicit Dold–Kan models. [Dwyer–Kan, classification of diagrams](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/dwyekan2.pdf) · [Schwede–Shipley, monoidal model categories](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0209342)

For every positive integer $m$, scaling the entry from $i$ to $j$ by $m^{j-i}$ defines a group endomorphism of $U_{\mathbb Z}$. It preserves products because the exponents add under composition. The telescope of the corresponding maps on $BU_{\mathbb Z}$, with each prime repeated infinitely often, maps by a weak equivalence to $BU_{\mathbb Q}$. On degree $k>0$ homotopy of $U_{\mathbb Z}$ the transition is multiplication by $m^k$, so the direct limit is the rational entry group. Both classifying spaces and the telescope are simply connected, and the maps to the rational group at successive stages use the inverse of the cumulative scaling. A map from the finite complex $B$ into the telescope is homotopic into one stage, by compactness and the retraction of a finite mapping telescope onto its last stage. Over $\mathbb Q$ the cumulative scaling is conjugation by the invertible diagonal map acting by $m^i$ on degree $i$, where $m$ is the product of the scalars used. Such conjugation is just a change of fiber coordinates and leaves the unmarked local system equivalent. The finite-stage classifying map therefore provides an integral simplicial abelian fibration $E\to B$ whose fiberwise rationalization is the comparison just constructed.

The map from $Y$ into $E_{\mathbb Q}$ over $B$ can in turn be made integral up to scaling. The fiberwise telescope of the ordinary scalar maps on $E$ is its fiberwise rationalization, by the same homotopy group argument on each fiber. The finite complex $Y$ maps into a bounded segment of that telescope, which retracts over $B$ to a single stage. This supplies a comparison over $B$ up to a specified homotopy. Rectify it to a map $q:Y\to E$ over $B$ by the lifting property of the fibration. It induces rational isomorphisms on fiber homotopy through degree $n$. The homotopy fiber of $q$, between total spaces, is also the homotopy fiber of the comparison on a typical fiber. Its groups through degree $n$ are finite, since the comparison is a rational isomorphism through that degree and $E$ has no fiber homotopy in degree $n+1$.

The comparison can be found effectively by search. Compute the rational homotopy ranks of the fibers of $p$ through $n$ using the effective Moore–Postnikov construction for a map of simply connected spaces. These are the ranks for $L$. Enumerate classifying maps into the locally effective Kan model of $BU_{\mathbb Z}$ and maps $q$ over $B$ into the associated simplicial abelian fibration. Simplicial approximation after subdivision and use of fibration models suffice for this search. Check the rational isomorphism condition using the effective homotopy groups and induced maps of the comparison on fibers. All total spaces used here have effective homology. For the associated fibration this follows from twisted-product effective homology over the simply connected finite base and the standard effective Dold–Kan Eilenberg–Mac Lane fibers. The finite presentation and explicit simplicial law in the triangular matrix construction provide all needed locally effective data. A simplicial entry from degree $i$ to degree $j$ in a $k$-simplex is a chain map from the normalized chains of the tensor of the free simplex with the degree-$i$ Dold–Kan group to the shifted degree-$j$ lattice. Only normalized degrees $j$ and $j+1$ enter those linear equations. The existence proof ensures the search terminates. [Čadek–Krčál–Vokřínek, effective Moore–Postnikov construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.6444) · [Effective homotopy of fibrations](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~sergerar/Papers/AAECC-Effective-Homotopy-of-Fibrations.pdf)

### Periodicity and computation of the relative classes

Fix the input $(X,A),g$ and the boundary lift. At the comparison stage $E$, exact sections over $g$ extending the given section on $A$ are either empty or a torsor for the simplicial abelian group of sections zero on $A$. One can test for a section by integer linear algebra. For each simplex of $X$ choose its trivialization in the associated twisted simplicial product. Its fiber coordinate belongs to a finite-rank, explicitly given abelian group in that dimension. All face and degeneracy equations and all prescribed values on $A$ are linear. The same computations on $X\times\Delta^j$ with $A\times\Delta^j$ fixed give the simplicial abelian group of differences. Its $\pi_0$ and $\pi_1$, representatives, and homotopies are computable using the normalized Moore complex and Smith normal form. Thus the base components form a torsor for a computable finitely generated abelian group $G$, with computable fundamental group at any component.

Replace $q$ by its relative $n$-type $P\to E$. This preserves relative lift classes for sources of dimension at most $n$, by the connectivity of the stage map. There is a uniform integer $N$ and a map $P\times_BE\to P$ lifting addition by $N$ times the second variable and restricting to the identity at the zero section of that variable. Construct it by lifting the map to $E$ given by addition, relative to $P$. At each finite Moore–Postnikov stage, the obstruction has finite local coefficients. The projection $P\times_BE\to P$ has simply connected abelian-group fiber and a section. The relative Serre spectral sequence of that section has only positive fiber degrees. Multiplication by a sufficiently divisible integer on the fiber annihilates finite-coefficient cohomology in positive degrees through the prescribed bound. This follows from the characteristic-$p$ convolution formula for the power map and then the exact sequences of finite coefficients, as in the finite-coefficient rescaling argument above. Therefore this multiplication annihilates the associated graded groups of the obstruction in the relative spectral sequence. Iterating at most one more than the degree annihilates the obstruction itself. At each stage precompose already chosen lifts with this multiplication, preserving the prescribed lift and changing only the cumulative multiplier on the second coordinate. This constructs the asserted map. An admissible $N$ is effective from $n$ and the finite fiber homotopy groups, even though one does not need to compute the universal translation map itself.

For a section $e$ of the difference group, this lifted translation induces bijections between the relative lift classes over comparison components $s$ and $s+Ne$. To see this on each point of $X$, join $e(x)$ to zero in the connected fiber of $E\to B$. The universal lifted translation along that path is a homotopy over the path of translated targets, starting at the identity on the fiber of $P\to E$. Thus, after transport, it induces a homotopy equivalence of the two homotopy fibers. The resulting map of pullback fibrations over $X$ is a fiberwise weak equivalence and is the identity over $A$, so it induces equivalences on vertical lifting spaces. Applying this argument with $s$ varying over its base component gives the equivalence on classes covering that component as well. Hence the number of classes over a base component is constant on the finitely many cosets modulo $NG$.

For completeness, the finite classes over any single comparison component are algorithmically determined. Take one exact representative $s$ of the component and compute the vertical relative lifting classes through $q$ over $s$ by the finite obstruction search. On each disk, a base contraction identifies the obstruction with a homotopy element of the homotopy fiber of $q$, computable by the effective homotopy algorithms. If the obstruction is zero, the distinct relative fillers are obtained by its finite homotopy group in the cell dimension. Keeping all branches gives a finite list that covers all vertical classes. Equality can be decided by the same relative obstruction test on the cylinder, with its two ends prescribed. If the fiber has nonzero $\pi_1$, it is the finite abelian cokernel of the degree-two map between the simply connected total spaces. For higher homotopy computations one can replace $E$ by the homotopy fiber of $E\to K(\pi_2(E)/q_*\pi_2(Y),2)$ and lift $q$ to it. This gives the simply connected cover of its homotopy fiber and has effective homology by the standard path-fibration construction. The low-degree tests use the computed finite cokernel. The groups needed for the cylinder obstruction are finite through degree $n$.

The fundamental group of the comparison component acts on this finite vertical set by monodromy, and its orbits are exactly the desired relative homotopy classes covering that component. Use the computed generators of the comparison fundamental group, lift the loops by the homotopy lifting property, and identify the resulting vertical classes with the finite equality test. Thus all orbits and representatives are effective. Checking just the base components modulo $NG$ decides existence. For the full description, keep the finite table of the cardinalities of the orbit sets in those cosets. Over any component in a fixed coset, order its finite output by a fixed enumeration, giving coordinates by the abelian component and a finite label with the corresponding table size. Forward conversion recomputes the vertical orbit set at the indicated component, and inverse conversion projects a lift and uses comparison homotopy and the finite monodromy test. This gives a finite disjoint union of copies of cosets of $NG$, with an explicit action of $NG$ by translation in these coordinates.

### A non-split finite smooth example

Let $\xi$ be the oriented rank-six bundle on $S^3_b\times S^3_x$ obtained by pulling back $TS^6$ along a degree-one collapse to $S^6$. Its sphere bundle followed by projection to $S^3_b$ is a smooth fibration with fiber $S^3\times S^5$ and has a section over the slice $x=*$, where the bundle is trivial. Its Euler class is $2bx$ with the indicated degree-three generators. A linear simplicial abelian model over the base, using a fixed integral cocycle $b$, has section coordinates $(x,u)$ with $dx=0$ and $du=2b\smile x$ in degrees three and five. This is the homotopy fiber of the Euler-class map in the comparison. The sphere bundle maps to it over $S^3_b$, using the nullhomotopy of the Euler class after pullback to the sphere bundle. The induced map on the $S^5$ factor has degree $1$, by naturality of the Euler transgression and since $2bx\ne0$. The $S^3_x$ coordinate is the generator, so this is a rational equivalence on fibers. The differential $du=2b x$ also shows that the fibration is rationally non-split over $S^3_b$. For example the degree-five fiber class transgresses to the product involving $b$ and thus is not in the image of cohomology restriction to the fiber. Nevertheless the theorem decides all finite lifting-extension problems for it.
