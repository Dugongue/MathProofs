# Five extensions of Manin’s homotopy computability results

Let $Y$ be a simply connected finite complex. Write $Y_{(p)}$ for its localization at the prime $p$, and write $Y[1/q]$ for localization at $\mathbb Z[1/q]$. We use Manin’s extension algorithm on the positive side, his Whitehead-product construction on the negative side, and the preceding integral bilinear undecidability theorem. The five results below are written proofs. [Manin, Theorem A and §8](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.10632) · [Bilinear theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/c5fef3140265c1dff49ad6e18c7f40b71f6a8ff0/BilinearDiophantineUndecidability/PROOF.md) · [Earlier fixed-pair theorem and arithmetic compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/c5fef3140265c1dff49ad6e18c7f40b71f6a8ff0/PortfolioTop30/ManinExtensions.md)

## 1. When Lipschitz extension bounds are computable

**Theorem.** Fix a simply connected finite piecewise Euclidean complex $Y$ and $d\ge2$. The following are equivalent.

- $Y$ is a rational H-space through Postnikov degree $d$.
- For every fixed finite simplicial pair $(X,A)$ with $\dim X\le d+1$, there is a total computable function $b : \mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ such that every extendable $L$-Lipschitz boundary map $A\to Y$ has an extension with Lipschitz constant at most $b(L)$.

At the first failing degree there is one fixed pair whose worst-case required extension Lipschitz constant eventually exceeds every total computable function. The lower bound still holds if an extension is allowed to realize the boundary map only up to homotopy. Metrics are fixed piecewise Euclidean metrics, with finite, computably specified distances assigned between components if needed.

### Positive direction

Use finite simplicial models and replace the metrics by bi-Lipschitz equivalent metrics with computable constants. A finite simplicial realization of $Y$ in a Euclidean simplex has a Lipschitz neighborhood retraction $r$. One explicit construction takes a barycentric subdivision of the ambient simplex, on which the subdivided $Y$ is a full subcomplex. In each simplex project to the face spanned by vertices of the subcomplex by normalizing their barycentric coordinates. Restrict to where their total weight is at least $1/2$. The local formulas agree on intersections, and the retraction is Lipschitz on a smaller closed neighborhood. Thus one can fix a rational tube radius $\delta>0$ in the Euclidean affine span and a computable Lipschitz constant. If needed, first project onto the ambient simplex. Write $m$ for the ambient Euclidean dimension.

For integer $L$, enumerate a finite set of simplicial maps on one sufficiently fine subdivision of $A$ into a fixed fine subdivision of $Y$. The set contains a map $g$ within $\delta/(8\sqrt m)$ of every $L$-Lipschitz map $f$. For effectivity, refine the target to have stars of sufficiently small diameter, bound the Lebesgue number of its open vertex-star cover from the finite piecewise linear coordinate functions, and refine the source so vertex stars have diameter less than that number divided by $\max(1,L)$. Simplicial approximation then supplies $g$. Sufficiently close maps are homotopic via the straight-line interpolation followed by $r$.

Manin’s Theorem A decides which enumerated $g$ extend. The theorem can use the dimension of the pair if it lies below $d+1$, with padding in the very low-dimensional cases. For each extendable $g$, enumerate simplicial approximations $G:X\to Y$ from finite subdivisions until $\|G|_A-g\|_\infty<\delta/(8\sqrt m)$. This test is decidable on a common triangulation by convexity of the Euclidean norm on each simplex. Arbitrary uniform approximation of a continuous extension ensures termination. Record an effective upper bound $K_g$ for the Lipschitz constant of $G$.

For an extendable $f$, choose a close enumerated $g$. It extends by the homotopy extension property. Extend the $m$ real coordinate functions of $f-G|_A$ across $X$ by McShane’s extension formula, then clamp them at $\pm\delta/(4\sqrt m)$. The resulting correction $\Delta$ has Euclidean norm at most $\delta/4$, agrees on $A$ with the needed correction, and has Lipschitz constant at most $\sqrt m (L+K_g)$ in the chosen ambient metrics. The map $r(G+\Delta)$ is an actual extension. Taking the maximum of the resulting bounds over the finite list and rounding up gives a total computable $b$. This argument covers exact prescribed Lipschitz boundary values.

### Negative direction and growth

Apply the fixed-pair construction to the halting relation $K=\{\operatorname{code}(p,w):p(w)\text{ halts}\}$, where a fixed program has a fixed binary prefix followed by the unaltered binary input. Use the fixed section version with one parameter $n$ and a varying boundary class $n\gamma$. Choose effective representatives $f_n$ with Lipschitz constant at most $C(n+1)$. For instance, a degree $n$ sphere map can use $n$ disjoint balls of radii bounded below by a constant times $1/(n+1)$ along an arc, collapse their complement to the basepoint, and realize degree one on each ball. Compose with a fixed piecewise linear representative of $\gamma$.

There is a total computable predicate $Q(n,B)$ with two properties. If any $B$-Lipschitz map $X\to Y$ restricts to the class of $f_n$, then $Q(n,B)$ accepts. If $Q(n,B)$ accepts, $f_n$ extends. To construct it, enumerate simplicial maps $X\to Y$ on the finite mesh sufficient for simplicial approximation of all $B$-Lipschitz maps. Compare the restriction of each map with $f_n$. In the original fixed pair $A$ is a wedge of simply connected spheres, so each comparison asks equality of finitely many elements in fixed computable homotopy groups of $Y$. Based and free tests agree because $Y$ is simply connected. The approximation represents the correct restriction up to homotopy. Conversely an accepted comparison provides an extension by the homotopy extension property. [Effective homotopy groups of simply connected finite complexes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.2337)

A computable extension bound gives a computable budget for $Q(n,B)$, and would decide halting. For the eventual growth claim take any total computable $h : \mathbb N\to\mathbb N$. By the recursion theorem choose a program $p$ that, on input the binary representation of $N$, finds its own coded index $n=\operatorname{code}(p,\operatorname{bin} N)$ and halts exactly when
$$
Q\!\left(n,\max_{j\le 2^N}h(j)\right)
$$
rejects. Acceptance implies extendability and hence halting, which contradicts the program definition. Thus the test rejects and the program halts. Every extension realizing the prescribed class has Lipschitz constant above the budget. For sufficiently large integer $L$, take $N=\lceil\log_2 L\rceil$. The fixed prefix gives $n=O_p(N+1)$, so the prescribed boundary map has Lipschitz constant at most $L$ while every extension exceeds $h(L)$. On each individual extendable class the optimal cost is attained by Arzelà–Ascoli. A uniform limit preserves the exact boundary in the exact problem. In the homotopy version sufficiently close restrictions are homotopic, so it also preserves the specified class. This gives strict domination for the infimum as well.

This gives a quantitative form of Manin’s computability dichotomy. In the language of persistent topology, the lower bound concerns the time at which a boundary component enters the image of restriction from bounded-Lipschitz maps. Block–Manin–Weinberger study persistent homology of the spaces of maps themselves. [Persistent homology of function spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16907v2) · [Quantitative nullhomotopy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03513)

## 2. Undecidable gluing of local extensions

**Theorem.** For every fixed simply connected finite $Y$ outside rational H-space type, there is one fixed finite pair $(X,A)$ and an effective family $f_t:A\to Y$ varying by $t\gamma$ on one sphere. The integral extension question is halting-complete. Every $f_t$ extends after inverting 2, and after inverting 3. In particular it extends after localizing at each prime and over the rationals. All the suspended boundary maps $\Sigma f_t$ are homotopic to one another and extend over $\Sigma X$.

Deciding whether the two localized extension spaces contain a pair of extensions that agree up to homotopy relative to $A$ in $Y[1/6]$ is halting-complete. Each localized extension space individually has a point on every input.

### Choose a fixed family with two rational witnesses

Use the nonzero integral bilinear map $B$ from Manin’s §8 for this $Y$. In its adjoint center $C$, select a nonzero integral $s$ in a minimal nonzero ideal of $C_{\mathbb Q}$. Its annihilator defines a residue field, and $Ts=ns$ is equivalent to the field image of $T$ being the integer $n$. Use the earlier number-ring interpretation to encode the fixed halting predicate by equations $F_j(T,x)=0$ for this residue-field image. Multiply each residual by $(2u-1)(3v-1)$. Multiplication by either of these factors on the finite free integral lattice $C$ is injective at every integral assignment. In fact the determinant of $kM_a-I$ is congruent to $\pm1$ modulo $k$. Thus the masked predicate has the same integral solutions on its input as the original.

Keep the gate $Z=Ts$ and the single reading equation unmasked. For frame indices $a,b$ choose $\delta=B(P_s e_a,f_b)\ne 0$ and require $B(P_Ze_a,f_b)=t\delta$. The masked predicate implies that $T$ has integral residue and the reader forces that integer to be $t$. Over either localization use $T=t1_C$, $Z=ts$, $x=0$, and respectively $(u,v)=(1/2,0)$ or $(0,1/3)$. Evaluate all constants and residuals before multiplying by the zero mask. On these assignments every intermediate coordinate has denominator dividing $q$ for the corresponding $q=2,3$. Computing $2u$ and $3v$ by addition before multiplying the mask factors ensures this bound. Integral frames and integral splittings of the radical quotients preserve it in the original pairing coordinates.

Compile with the existing frame and center equations. All right sides of the resulting raw system are sums of pairings, which have homotopy lifts in Manin’s construction. Only the reader’s right side varies. Multiplying every left and right variable of either rational witness by its $q$ gives an integral solution to the system with every right side multiplied by $q^2$.

### Undo the scaling after localization

Let $X_0$ be Manin’s complex before the final equation cells. Define a self-map $h_q$ on the wedge of spheres by degree $q$ on the two variable lists and their primed spheres, degree $q^2$ on each equation sphere in $A$, and identity on the fixed lower-dimensional spheres. Extend over each fat wedge by the corresponding product of sphere maps. Extend over each mapping cylinder by a based homotopy between the two sphere maps of degree $q\rho_i$.

For the product pair $(P,T)$ of spheres and its fat wedge, there is just one relative cell, of dimension $d+1$. Its relative homotopy generator has boundary the attaching class $\varphi_{ij}$. Relative cellular approximation and relative Hurewicz identify this generator with the relative integral homology generator. The product map acts by $q^2$ on that generator, so $h_q$ sends $\varphi_{ij}$ to $q^2\varphi_{ij}$ in $\pi_d(X_0)$, including torsion. The same scaling applies to each final attaching class in Manin’s formula. Extend $h_q$ over those cells with relative degree $q^2$ by matching the attaching homotopies to the degree maps of disks.

The resulting $h_q$ is a homology equivalence over $\mathbb Z[1/q]$. On the initial wedge, the fat wedges, and the mapping cylinder pieces this follows from their sphere and product cell bases. Gluing along their wedge or sphere intersections preserves the equivalence by Mayer–Vietoris, and the final relative cells have degrees $q^2$. The restriction to $A$ is also a homology equivalence over this ring. Since all spaces in the pair are simply connected, both maps induce homotopy equivalences after localization.

The cleared witness supplies an actual extension $G_q:X\to Y$ of the map with all equation classes scaled by $q^2$ and the lower boundary classes fixed, by Manin’s theorem. This boundary map is homotopic to $f_t\circ h_q|_A$. After localizing the target, precomposition with $h_q$ is invertible on maps up to homotopy from $X$, and also from $A$. Undo it on the localized $G_q$. The result restricts to the class of the localized $f_t$, and homotopy extension gives the exact boundary value. Each prime divides at most one of 2 and 3. Both local extensions have effective finite certificates as localization roofs: enumerate a piecewise linear representative of each integral extension of the scaled boundary and precede it by the inverse of the fixed map $h_q$ in the localized category. [Manin’s cells and attaching maps](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.10632#S8) · [Localization and its mapping property](https://web.math.ku.dk/~jg/papers/cattop.pdf)

### Suspension and compatibility

The suspension splitting of a product of spheres has summands indexed by nonempty subsets of factors. For the fat wedge it has the same proper-subset summands. Under these natural splittings its inclusion into the product splits. Therefore the suspended attaching map of the top product cell is null. The varying class $\gamma$ can be chosen to be the actual class from the corresponding higher Whitehead product. Its suspension is null, since it is the composite of that attaching map and a fat-wedge map. Consequently all $\Sigma f_t$ are homotopic. Some integral instance with a halting index extends, so every suspended boundary map extends. [Natural suspension splitting](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.4689)

For an abelian group $G$ the diagonal and difference maps give the exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow G[1/2]\oplus G[1/3]\longrightarrow G[1/6]\longrightarrow 0.
$$
For $\mathbb Z$ this follows from coprimality of powers of 2 and 3, and the general case follows by tensoring since the final term for $\mathbb Z$ is flat. Apply this to all homotopy groups in the long exact sequence of the homotopy pullback. It proves that $Y$ is the homotopy pullback of $Y[1/2]\to Y[1/6]\leftarrow Y[1/3]$. The groups in degrees zero and one also agree, by simple connectivity and surjectivity of the difference map in degree two. Mapping out of $X$ and $A$ preserves homotopy limits. Taking the homotopy fiber of restriction over $f_t$ gives the same pullback description of the extension space. Because $(X,A)$ is a cofibration, these fibers model actual extensions and homotopies relative to $A$. Thus a pair of individually localized extensions together with the indicated compatibility exists exactly when an integral extension exists.

This uses the ordinary localization square and a hard family in its relative mapping spaces. The new content is the simultaneous promise of actual extensions at both coprime localizations and after one suspension, for every target in the negative class.

## 3. Undecidability on the whole boundary of a smooth manifold

**Theorem.** For every fixed simply connected finite target $Y$ outside rational H-space type, there is one fixed simply connected compact smooth manifold $W$ with connected simply connected boundary, such that the universal computably enumerable relation is the extendability relation of a two-parameter family of maps from the whole of $\partial W$ to $Y$. The localized and suspended promises of Theorem 2 also survive on a fixed manifold pair.

### Transfer to a whole boundary

Start with the fixed PL pair $(X,A)$ for the desired family, using finite simplicial models of the corresponding CW pair. This is a simply connected pair. Subdivide to make the boundary subcomplex full. Embed $X$ in a high-dimensional Euclidean half-space, with vertices of $A$ on its boundary plane and all other vertices at positive height. Generic horizontal coordinates give an embedding, and fullness makes its intersection with the plane exactly $A$.

A relative regular neighborhood gives a compact PL thickening $N$ of $X$ with $Q$ the regular neighborhood of $A$ in the boundary plane. It has maps of pairs
$$
(X,A)\xrightarrow{i}(N,Q)\xrightarrow{r}(X,A),\qquad ri=\mathrm{id}.
$$
The relative collapse provides the retraction, and it can be taken PL. $Q$ is a codimension-zero patch of $\partial N$. In $\partial(N\times I)$ remove the relative interior of $Q\times I$, including its interior along the two end faces where they meet the side. More explicitly the remaining manifold is
$$
W=(N\times\{0,1\})\cup((\partial N\setminus\operatorname{int}_{\partial N}Q)\times I).
$$
Its whole boundary is $P=(Q\times\{0,1\})\cup(\partial Q\times I)$, the double of $Q$. Projection $(W,P)\to(N,Q)$ and bottom inclusion $(N,Q)\to(W,P)$ compose to the identity. Thus any boundary map $f:A\to Y$ pulls back to all of $P$ via projection and $r$. An extension on $X$ pulls back to $W$, and any extension on $W$ restricts back along the section to $X$ with boundary $f$. This works for any target including the localized targets. Suspension preserves the paired maps and their identities.

The thickenings have spines $X$ and $A$, so $N$ and $Q$ are simply connected. Choose the ambient dimension greater than both spine dimensions by at least three. The regular-neighborhood handle presentation has indices at most the spine dimensions, with no dual handles of index zero or one. In particular $\partial N$ and $\partial Q$ are connected. The complement of the interior of $Q$ in $\partial N$ is connected as well. Each of its components meets the connected $\partial Q$, unless it is an entire separate component of $\partial N$. The two copies in $W$ now glue across a connected side, so van Kampen gives $\pi_1(W)=0$. The same argument for the double of $Q$ gives $\pi_1(P)=0$.

The PL manifolds with boundary here are codimension-zero locally tame regions in the respective ambient manifolds. Round $N$ as a Euclidean region, then round the corners of $\partial(N\times I)$ and the boundary of its codimension-zero region $W$. Relative smoothing in codimension one gives a compatible smooth model of the pair. For effectiveness the fixed PL triangulation and the PL retractions already give the encodings of the boundary maps on that fixed pair. The domain and target therefore stay fixed even when the input is required to be a map on the entire boundary. [Hudson–Zeeman on relative regular neighborhoods](https://www.lms.ac.uk/sites/default/files/1964%20On%20Regular%20Neighbourhoods.pdf) · [Wall, Differential Topology, §2.6](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~hekmati/Books/Wall.pdf) · [Earlier fixed-source or fixed-target results](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.2370)

## 4. Nonzero mapping degree at the first nonformal dimension

**Theorem.** For every $k\ge2$, ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb Q$ is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to deciding whether a nonzero-degree continuous map exists between members of the following promised family, given by explicit rational invariant tables. The members are closed oriented $(k-1)$-connected smooth $(4k-1)$-manifolds with zero rational Pontryagin classes. One cup-product ring serves every input of a fixed tensor format. The tables give the rational cup products and Bianchi–Massey tensor. The same decision criterion holds in the reverse direction between the two manifolds. The rational mapping degree set is exactly $\{0\}$ or $\mathbb Q$ on each pair.

The result uses regrading of the seven-dimensional construction for even $k$, a new exterior-square construction for odd $k$, and Manin’s positive-weights theorem to obtain actual continuous maps. [Seven-manifold construction and invariant encoding](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/c5fef3140265c1dff49ad6e18c7f40b71f6a8ff0/TopologyAndInvariantCrossovers/Geometry.md) · [Crowley–Nordström](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04184) · [Manin, Positive weights and self-maps](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02173)

### Regraded cohomology and the odd-degree construction

Place $X$ in degree $k$. Let $E$ be a subspace of the graded symmetric square $S^2_{\mathrm{gr}}X$, and put $D=S^2_{\mathrm{gr}}X/E$. The cohomology groups in degrees $0,k,2k-1,2k,3k-1,4k-1$ are respectively $\mathbb Q,X,D^*,D,X^*,\mathbb Q o$. Use the quotient $q:S^2_{\mathrm{gr}}X\to D$ for multiplication of two elements of $X$, and duality products $x\xi=\xi(x)o$, $\alpha b=\alpha(b)o$ for $x\in X,\xi\in X^*,\alpha\in D^*,b\in D$. Define $(x\alpha)(x')=\alpha(q(x'x))$, and extend by graded commutativity. All products in omitted degrees vanish. These rules are associative. The only possible nonzero triple product of positive-degree elements is obtained from two elements of $X$ and one of $D^*$, and both associations give $\alpha(q(x'x))o$ with the signs specified by their order. This is a rational Poincaré duality algebra.

The top Bianchi–Massey source is
$$
\mathcal B^{4k}=\ker\big(\operatorname{Sym}^2 E\longrightarrow S^4_{\mathrm{gr}}X\big).
$$
Indeed the first possible quadratic cup-product relation has degree $2k$. In this degree the full relation space, including the unit, is the graph of minus the quotient map. Choose a splitting of the quotient, so the graph breaks into $E$ and relations with a nonzero unit term. In a symmetrized product of two such graph elements, the coefficients of $1^2\operatorname{Sym}^2D$ first force all terms using two unit relations to vanish, and the coefficients of $1\cdot (S^2_{\mathrm{gr}} X)D$ then force all mixed terms to vanish. What remains is the displayed kernel. For even $k$ the original seven-manifold construction and its automorphism argument apply with this regrading. They single out one Plücker-relation component on four marked blocks $U_1,\dots,U_4$.

For odd $k$, use $X=\bigoplus_{i\in C_8}V_i$, eight blocks of pairwise distinct dimensions at least two, each above the tensor side bound. Let $E\subseteq\Lambda^2X$ be the span of $V_i\wedge V_j$ for distinct nonadjacent vertices of the eight-cycle. For $x\ne0$ with block support $S$, put
$$
h(x)=\dim\{y:x\wedge y\in E\}=c(C_8[S])+\sum_{i\notin N[S]}\dim V_i.
$$
Here $N[S]$ is the closed neighborhood and $c$ counts components. To verify the formula, in each occupied block $y_i$ must be proportional to $x_i$ because the within-block wedge survives the quotient. On occupied adjacent blocks the scalars must be equal. On unoccupied blocks with occupied neighbors the corresponding $y_i$ must vanish, and remaining blocks are free. Thus $h$ depends only on the support.

Adding a vertex to a support never increases $h$. If isolated from the old support it contributes one component but loses a free block of dimension at least two. If adjacent it cannot increase the number of components or shrink the neighborhood. Adding a second vertex to a singleton decreases $h$ strictly. In the adjacent case the closed neighborhood strictly grows in the eight-cycle. Therefore in the nonzero rank loci $\{x:h(x)\ge a\}$ together with the origin, the inclusion-minimal nonzero irreducible components across all thresholds are exactly the individual block spaces. In fact the loci are unions of coordinate support subspaces, and the singleton is a maximal support at its own threshold. Every automorphism preserving $E$ preserves these loci and thus each $V_i$ by its distinct dimension. Conversely all independent block maps preserve $E$.

Take the four alternating blocks as $U_1,\dots,U_4$. In the multidegree using one element in each of these blocks, $\operatorname{Sym}^2 E$ has three summands indexed by the perfect matchings. Identify each with $U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_4$. Their products in $\Lambda^4X$ are the same canonical tensor up to the sign of the matching. The kernel is two copies of that tensor product, with the copies given by choosing two fixed relations between the matchings. The blockwise action preserves each copy. This gives the component needed in the odd case.

In either parity, pad the three input factors by zeros inside the first three marked blocks. Put $T\otimes u_4^*$ on one chosen kernel copy for a fixed nonzero covector $u_4^*$ and put zero on all other components. Independent changes of basis on the data blocks produce precisely tensor isomorphism on this component, up to a nonzero scalar from the top class and, in the even construction, from the auxiliary two-dimensional space. Both can be absorbed into one data block. Conversely any table equivalence preserves the chosen multidegree and, in the odd case, the fixed matching relations, so comparing the two separated factors reflects tensor isomorphism. Padding preserves the comparison by restricting to the essential supports and extending maps across complements. The table construction is polynomial in the tensor format, with fixed $k$. Crowley–Nordström, Theorem 1.5, realizes these tables by smooth manifolds in dimension $4k-1\le5k-3$ with Pontryagin data zero. Their Theorem 1.2 gives the exact rational equivalence criterion. The reverse polynomial reduction packages graded spaces, cup products, the inclusion of the Bianchi–Massey source, and its functional into a fixed typed tensor system, then uses the existing ordinary tensor-isomorphism compiler. ### Positive weights and continuous maps

Give $X$ weight one, both $D$ and $D^*$ weight two, $X^*$ weight three and $o$ weight four. Scaling by $2^w$ on weight $w$ preserves the cup and Bianchi–Massey operations. Crowley–Nordström realize that compatible cohomology automorphism by a rational self-equivalence. Let $A$ be the resulting automorphism of the simply connected rational minimal model.

For completeness, the positive weights on cohomology lift to the model. In degree $j>0$, filter by the subalgebra $M_{<j}$ on lower-degree generators, and let $V^j=M^j/M_{<j}^j$. There is an equivariant sequence
$$
H^j(M)\longrightarrow V^j\xrightarrow{d}H^{j+1}(M_{<j}).
$$
It is exact at $V^j$. A generator whose differential is exact in the lower algebra can be adjusted by a lower term to be closed, and a closed class maps to the kernel. Minimality and simple connectivity put the differential in the lower algebra. Induct on degree. Eigenvalues on lower monomials are products of lower eigenvalues, and every eigenvalue on $V^j$ comes from one of the two displayed spaces. Consequently all eigenvalues in positive model degree are $2^w$ with $w>0$. Their generalized eigenspaces over $\mathbb Q$ grade the differential algebra. Multiplication multiplies these eigenvalues, since it intertwines the tensor product action. This is a positive weight decomposition.

Manin’s Theorem B now gives continuous maps inducing rational cohomology isomorphisms between any two representatives of the same rational type. They have nonzero degree. Conversely a nonzero-degree map $M\to M'$ gives an injection on rational cohomology by Poincaré duality. For $x\ne0$ choose a dual class $y$ with nonzero top product. Its pullback product is multiplied by the nonzero degree, so the pullback of $x$ cannot vanish. The ranks here are equal degree by degree, giving an isomorphism and therefore a rational homotopy equivalence. To see the rational degree assertion, within one rational type multiply the fourth marked block by a chosen nonzero $\lambda\in\mathbb Q$ and the top class by $\lambda$, and take the induced action on the remaining algebra, including complementary duals. The Bianchi–Massey functional has exactly one input from the fourth block. Crowley–Nordström therefore realize the resulting automorphism by a rational self-equivalence of degree $\lambda$. The constant map realizes zero.

The first dimension at which this fixed-cup-ring rational separation can occur is $4k-1$, since Miller’s formality theorem makes the rational cup algebra sufficient through dimension $4k-2$. The polynomial-time claim uses invariant-table input. The same equivalence between nonzero mapping degree and table isomorphism also holds for any two such manifolds with equal Betti numbers whose rational type on at least one side has positive weights. The result concerns genuine continuous maps between the promised manifold realizations. [Miller’s range as stated by Crowley–Nordström](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04184) · [Manin’s lifting theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02173)


## 5. Higher persistence for two-stage targets

**Theorem.** Let $X$ be a finite simplicial complex. Let $Y$ be a closed simply connected Riemannian manifold with a two-stage real minimal Sullivan model $\Lambda(V\oplus W)$ with $dV=0$ and $dW\subseteq\Lambda V$. For each $i$ and each coefficient field there is $C$ such that every singular $i$-cycle in $\operatorname{Lip}_{L}(X,Y)$ that bounds in $\operatorname{Lip}(X,Y)$ already bounds in $\operatorname{Lip}_{C(L+1)^{4/3}}(X,Y)$. Here function spaces have the uniform topology. If $Y$ is scalable, the bound improves to $C(L+1)$. Thus the scalable closed-manifold case of Block–Manin–Weinberger Question 1.3 holds in all homological degrees and all components. Their Theorem 9.1 treats degree zero under the scalability assumption. [Block–Manin–Weinberger, Question 1.3 and Theorem 9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S1.SS3)

### Put a finite filling into differential forms

First take $X$ to be a closed smooth manifold. A null-homologous finite singular cycle has a finite singular filling. Realize its summands on a finite parameter complex $Z$ with face identifications for identical singular faces, retaining the coefficients. Let $B\subset Z$ stand for the subcomplex generated by the uncancelled boundary faces. This produces a continuous family $F:Z\times X\to Y$, with slices on $B$ of Lipschitz constant at most $L$. Any component of $Z$ disjoint from $B$ contributes no boundary and can be omitted. If the face identifications use a delta complex, subdivide its cells to get a simplicial model and use the corresponding subdivided chains. The subdivision maps and their chain homotopies preserve the indicated boundary sublevels.

After refinements, approximate $F$ by a family piecewise smooth in $Z$ and smooth in $X$, with slices on $B$ of Lipschitz constant at most $C_0(L+1)$. To see the needed control, embed $Y$ smoothly with a tubular retraction. Sample the slices on a fine parameter mesh which has $B$ as a subcomplex, smooth the finitely many sampled slices in the $X$ coordinates, then interpolate in the ambient Euclidean space and retract. For a sample on $B$, smoothing by local convolution and a fixed partition of unity keeps the $X$ Lipschitz constant at most $C_0(L+1)$. The other samples only need smoothness. Uniformly close approximations allow the ambient interpolation and retraction on each simplex. The straight-line homotopy followed by retraction carries the original boundary cycle to the approximated boundary within the same stated bound up to another fixed factor. Thus it suffices to fill the approximated cycle.

On $X$ take harmonic projection $P=\alpha\pi$ and the Hodge contraction $K=d^*G$ for the Green operator of the Hodge Laplacian. Thus $dK+Kd=1-P$, $K^2=KP=PK=0$. Both $P$ and $K$ are bounded in the supremum norm on smooth forms. The harmonic projection has a smooth finite-rank kernel. The order $-1$ operator $K$ has a kernel whose singularity is locally integrable, giving the supremum bound on compact $X$. Extend the contraction in the $X$ direction of $Z\times X$, with sign $(-1)^a$ on parameter degree $a$. It then anticommutes with the parameter differential. The projection acts on the $X$ factors. These operations commute with restriction to parameter faces. [Hodge and Green operators](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~demailly/manuscripts/hodge-ams.pdf)

Take a smooth real model map $m_Y$ and let $\phi=F^*m_Y$. Only the finitely many generators in degrees at most $\dim Z+\dim X$ enter the estimates. We work in the same algebraic homotopy class by explicit homotopies starting from $\phi$. Throughout, a horizontal form means its restriction to a fixed $X$-slice. Norm estimates concern that restriction until we stretch the parameter metric.

### Bound the horizontal forms throughout the filling

First normalize every $v\in V$ by setting
$$
\Phi(v)=\phi(v)-d(\tau K\phi(v))
$$
over a homotopy parameter $\tau\in[0,1]$. Its endpoint is $P\phi(v)$. Extend over each $w\in W$ by $\Phi_\tau(w)=\phi(w)+\int_0^\tau i_{\partial_s}\Phi(dw)\,ds$, with zero $d\tau$ component for the chosen generator image. The homotopy identity on the closed form $\Phi(dw)$ implies $d\Phi(w)=\Phi(dw)$ on the product with the homotopy interval. On $B$ the horizontal norms throughout this homotopy are $O((L+1)^{|v|})$ and $O((L+1)^{|w|+1})$, respectively. In the second estimate, the product defining $dw$ has total degree $|w|+1$, and at most one factor supplies the contraction term from the homotopy. Boundedness of $K$ proves the required estimate for that factor. Forms with parameter differentials do not contribute horizontally.

Thereafter, write $a_v$ for the horizontal part of $P\phi(v)$ on a connected component of $Z$. It is constant in the parameter, since its harmonic derivative in parameter degree one is zero by closedness. Every retained component meets $B$, so $\|a_v\|=O((L+1)^{|v|})$. The horizontal part $a(dw)$ is exact on $X$ because an image of $w$ already exists. Fix $\sigma_w=K(a(dw))$, which has derivative $a(dw)$ and norm $O((L+1)^{|w|+1})$. Replace the image of $w$ by its image minus $dK(\phi(w)-\sigma_w)$ by an exact-form homotopy, with $V$ fixed. Its horizontal part is now $\sigma_w+\alpha u_w(z)$, where $u_w(z)$ lies in $H^{|w|}(X;\mathbb R)$. During this normalization the horizontal terms on $B$ are bounded by $O((L+1)^{|w|+1})$. Indeed the horizontal difference from $\sigma_w$ is closed, so subtracting $d_XK$ interpolates it to its harmonic projection with uniformly bounded norm.

Read the parameter-degree-one part of $d\phi(w)=\phi(dw)$ and project to $H^*(X;\mathbb R)$. It says
$$
d_Z u=J\xi .
$$
Here $u=(u_w)_w$, $\xi$ consists of the mixed parameter-degree-one parts of $P\phi(v)$ expressed in harmonic coordinates, and $J$ is the linearization of the polynomials $dw$ on cohomology at the constant list $[a_v]$. The signs are those of graded multiplication, using the same ordering on both sides. The part from $d_X$ of the parameter-degree-one component of $\phi(w)$ projects to zero. On the right, each product has one mixed factor and all others horizontal, yielding exactly the linearization. Thus $u$ varies in a single affine translate of $\operatorname{im}J$ on each component.

For each component fix one point $z_0$ of $B$. Choose a piecewise smooth cutoff $\chi$ with values in $[0,1]$, equal to one near $B$ and supported where the finitely many $u_w$ still obey twice their boundary bounds plus one. Such a neighborhood exists by continuity, with no required width. Set $u'=u(z_0)+\chi(u-u(z_0))$. Each $u'_w$ now obeys the desired bound throughout the component. The difference $u'-u$ lies in $\operatorname{im}J$, and it vanishes near $B$. Use a linear right inverse on this image to lift the difference to a piecewise smooth list $p=(p_v)_v$ of classes in $H^{|v|-1}(X;\mathbb R)$, zero near $B$. The size of this lift need not be uniformly bounded.

Make the second homotopy on $V$ by adding $d(\tau\alpha p_v)$ to $\phi(v)$ and extend on $W$ by the same integration rule. On an $X$-slice its $V$ images remain $a_v$. The homotopy-direction component is $\alpha p_v$, so the change in the horizontal cohomology coordinate of $w$ is precisely $(Jp)_w$. Use the sign convention of the parameter-degree-one equation for the homotopy interval. Normalize the $W$ forms once more with the same exact-form homotopy. The horizontal images are now $\sigma_w+\alpha u'_w$ and have the claimed bounds on all of $Z$. Near $B$, the second correction is the identity, and the second normalization fixes the already normalized first image since $KP=0$ and its horizontal part is fixed. The full second normalization on $B$ fixes the first image as a form, since the operator $dK$ is idempotent on the total forms and $\sigma_w$ is fixed in the parameter.

### Keep the actual boundary and apply shadowing

Attach a collar $B\times[0,1]$ to $Z$ with coordinate zero at its outer edge. Extend the reference family $F$ constantly along it. On this collar place the restriction of the concatenated algebraic homotopy just constructed, with its interpolation parameter taken to be the collar coordinate. This agrees with the modified model at the inner edge and with $F^*m_Y$ at the outer edge. Using the product of the homotopy and collar parameters gives an algebraic homotopy relative to the outer edge from the original reference family to the new model. All horizontal terms on the collar obey the controlled boundary estimates.

Fix a particular finite filling. Its mixed components have finite norms. Multiply every distance in the parameter complex, including the collar, by a sufficiently large integer. Each form component with positive parameter degree decreases in norm under this change. This also reduces the parameter Lipschitz cost of the outer boundary family to the desired scale. Subdivide edgewise at approximately unit scale and triangulate the product with the fixed $X$. Within the fixed total dimension the resulting shapes have bounded distortion from standard simplices. This is the same parameter stretching used in the proof of Block–Manin–Weinberger’s higher-degree results. The size of the mesh and stretching can depend on the filling. The shadowing estimate depends on the total dimension, the target and the dilatation, so its bound on $X$-slices is uniform in that mesh. [Block–Manin–Weinberger, §§6.2 and 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S6.SS2)

For ordinary shadowing, $V$ horizontal norms grow as $(L+1)^{|v|}$. Move any zero-differential $W$ generators into $V$ at the outset. For the remaining $w$, minimality and simple connectivity give $|w|\ge3$. Their horizontal norms therefore contribute at most $O((L+1)^{4/3})$ to ordinary dilatation. Theorem 6.2 gives the claimed filling relative to the outer boundary. In the scalable case use the weights $|v|$ and $|w|+1$ of the formal two-stage model, as in the proof of Theorem 9.1. The $U$-dilatation is then $O(L+1)$ and Theorem 6.4 gives the linear estimate. If using a smooth model in place of the flat scalable realization fixed for shadowing, insert one more collar carrying the pullback of a fixed homotopy between these model maps. The smooth-to-flat de Rham quasi-isomorphism gives the model homotopy by Sullivan lifting. Its horizontal estimates are bounded by the pullback of the fixed forms along the controlled slices. Use the collar parameter as above to make that change relative to the outer boundary. [Improved relative shadowing and the two-stage weights](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16907v2#S9)

The genuine shadowing map gives a continuous singular family in the stated Lipschitz sublevel. The enlarged parameter complex and collar prisms give the required chain with actual prescribed boundary. Reattaching the initial boundary approximation homotopy recovers the original cycle.

For a finite simplicial $X$, embed it as a Euclidean polyhedron and choose a smooth compact domain inside a Lipschitz-retract neighborhood of $X$ and containing $X$ in its interior. Double that domain to a closed smooth manifold $M$. Folding and retracting gives a Lipschitz retraction $M\to X$, with Lipschitz section. Pull the entire family back to $M$, apply the result there, and restrict the controlled filling along the section. Both operations cost fixed Lipschitz factors. Hence the bounds hold for finite $X$. In the scalable case, a linear change in the Lipschitz level bounds the lengths of all finite bars in $\log_+\operatorname{Lip}$, using the barcode interpretation in the cited work.
