# Coordinate-permutation and quadratic-form comparisons between ordinary and one-sign tensor isomorphism

Work over the fixed field F₃. Write O for ordinary three-tensor isomorphism and A for three-tensor isomorphism with determinant one on one ordered mode. Every arrow below denotes a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction. The statements are informal. Write O∧E for the problem of requiring both a supplied ordinary tensor comparison and an independent E comparison to succeed. Thus the ordinary test is part of those inputs.

There is an explicit chain

    O ≤ O∧Gₚ ≤ O∧Gᵥ ≤ O∧C ≤ O∧U ≤ O∧M ≤ Q ≡ Qₛ ≤ A.

The displayed order concerns reductions. The seven non-equivalence arrows have no reverse reduction proved here. The definitions give six candidate intermediate degrees on this displayed chain, up to the indicated equivalence. The reductions by themselves do not establish any strict inequality. Several plausible extra rungs collapse under the explicit constructions below.

## The comparisons

| Problem | Input and required isomorphism |
| --- | --- |
| Gₚ | Colored groups given by full Cayley tables, with even permutation on one union of designated colors of elements |
| Gᵥ | Colored graphs, with even permutation on one marked union of vertex colors |
| C | Colored permutations taking one ternary linear code onto another, with even permutation on one marked union of coordinate colors |
| U | Isomorphism of two colored indexed lists of subspaces of a finite-dimensional ternary vector space, with even permutation on the marked indices. Dimensions and multiplicities are arbitrary. |
| M | TI with the change of basis on a colored slice-label mode restricted to monomial matrices. The underlying permutation on the marked labels must be even. The two matrix modes have unrestricted invertible maps. |
| Q | Isomorphism of finite explicitly specified tensor systems of arity at most three, including one designated nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form. The transporter must take the designated form to the target form and have determinant one on its vector space. Ordinary tensor data can share that vector space. |
| Qₛ | The same tensor-system comparison with a common scalar multiplier for the designated form. The multiplier acts on a specified one-dimensional output role and may participate in the other tensor data. |

A tensor system here has named vector-space roles, explicit linear or bilinear maps, and optionally marked vectors. A system transporter uses one invertible map per role and respects every displayed map and mark. A scalar-valued form in Q has its value line pinned by a named nonzero vector. A form in Qₛ takes values in a one-dimensional role that can have further linear or bilinear constraints. These conventions keep each comparison closed under composing its eligible transporters.

The chain places several different questions between O and A. Moving from C to U replaces column lines by arbitrary-dimensional subspaces with no basis supplied as part of the match. Moving from U to M allows the slices to interact on a shared input space, while the U construction below places their input spaces on separate summands. The question for Q concerns sign-one isometries on an arbitrary quadratic space as a role in a linked diagram. Its obvious metric gives the form test, while its orientation remains a separate condition.

## The lower reductions

For O≤O∧Gₚ attach any fixed positive group comparison. To reduce Gₚ to Gᵥ, make a vertex for each group element and a record for each true multiplication triple. Connect a record through three separately colored position nodes to its ordered arguments and output. Permutation on the original element vertices must preserve precisely the multiplication relation. The three slots remain distinguished even when two entries of a triple are equal. Group colors and the marked subset are retained on the original element vertices.

For Gᵥ≤C, use the code with codewords

    (x,(x_u+x_v)_{e={u,v}}),   x∈F₃^V.

Color each vertex coordinate according to its original color in one palette and all edge coordinates in a separate palette. Keep the mark on the vertex coordinates. A candidate colored coordinate permutation has vertex component π and edge component σ. Equivalence of the codes forces the linear functional for each target edge to be the sum of its two coordinates transported along π. Sums of two distinct coordinate functionals distinguish unordered vertex pairs. Thus precisely graph isomorphisms extend, with the same permitted vertex parity. The construction handles isolated vertices and empty edge sets.

Permutation-code comparison C is equivalent to monomial-code comparison with the sign of the *underlying coordinate permutation*. Let the latter variant be C′. For C≤C′, replace a code X by

    D(X) = {(x,x+t·1,t) : x∈X, t∈F₃}.

Distinguish the two copies of each original coordinate color and color the last coordinate separately. Use the second copy for the marked permutation. Any monomial equivalence takes the codewords with first copy zero to the corresponding codewords, and their line is spanned by (0,1,1). Thus all scalars in the second copy equal its last-coordinate scalar. The last-coordinate-zero subspace then forces the second-copy permutation to take X to the target code. Conversely an old permutation extends with scale one and the same permutation on both copies.

For C′≤C use

    P(X) = {(t,t+x,t−x) : x∈X, t∈F₃^n}.

The first coordinate group is separately colored from the other two. Use the same color for the two coordinates t_i+x_i and t_i−x_i, distinguishing original colors between groups of pairs as needed. These two positions are the unique pair of distinct coordinates of their group for which the sum of the two coordinate functions on P(X) is 2t_i. Indeed the t_i are independent. A colored permutation must therefore preserve the pairs and move them by the same permutation as the first group. A swap inside the pair multiplies x_i by −1. Conversely every monomial equivalence gives these swaps and a permutation. The mark is on the first group, so its parity is exactly the required underlying parity. Both transformations have linear overhead in code length.

For C′≤U, take a full-row-rank generator matrix and replace a column by its span. Keep the color, mark and copy index of every column, allowing zero subspaces and repeated equal lines. An invertible ambient map taking one line list to the other gives a coordinate scaling for every nonzero column. Zero columns can be given any scaling. Conversely a row operation together with a monomial coordinate map gives exactly such a transporter. The old coordinate permutation is unchanged.

For U≤M, use one slice label for every list entry. If (S_i) is a list in V, give the common matrix input space the direct-sum form W=⊕_i S_i, with one chosen coordinate basis per summand. Slice M_i maps W into V by projection to S_i followed by its inclusion. Every permitted tensor transporter must take the image of M_i to the image of its target slice. Conversely an ambient isomorphism that matches the list extends to the matrix input by its restriction on each summand. Choose the slice scalars to be one. Slice permutation and parity match exactly, including zero-dimensional summands.

Every construction in this section keeps an independent O input unchanged when applied to its conjunction.

## From a permutation of slice labels to an oriented isometry

To show O∧M≤Q, keep the independent ordinary tensor data in disjoint ordinary roles. In the M component, suppose the slice-label space is L=F₃^n with labeled basis indexed by i. Introduce the coordinate algebra E=F₃^n, with primitive idempotents e_i, and include its multiplication and its action on L by coordinate projection. An algebra automorphism permutes the e_i. Preservation of the action forces the linear map on L to take each one-dimensional space e_iL to the line named by the same permutation. Conversely every monomial map on L gives that permutation and preserves the action. Include the sums of primitive idempotents for each prescribed color and require that they match the target color sums.

Introduce the marked ideal E_* with its inclusion into E. Give it the form for which its supported primitive idempotents form an orthonormal basis. The induced transporter on E_* is the requested permutation, so it is automatically an isometry and its determinant is the desired sign. This is exactly one Q instance, together with all the original tensor constraints. An empty marked set can use a pinned one-dimensional form role. All arrays and names use polynomial space.

For Q≤Qₛ use the same form but pin a nonzero vector of its scalar output line. This forces the multiplier to equal 1 and leaves other witness conditions unchanged.

The reverse Qₛ≤Q uses an exterior square of polynomial dimension. Retain the original form as a bilinear map into its unpinned value line, hence retain every original condition. Let n be the metric dimension and let λ be the scalar of the value-line transporter in fixed input and output bases. If n is positive and even, adjoin the role Λ²V and the alternating bilinear map V×V→Λ²V. It forces the exterior-square map of the original transporter, since decomposable wedges span. Choose the usual induced symmetric form on Λ²V, with Gram matrix given by the two-by-two minors of the original Gram matrix in the chosen value-line basis. It is nondegenerate because it is the exterior square of the invertible Gram identification V→V*. Compared using the original frames, its multiplier is λ²=1. Therefore the new isometry requirement on this role holds for every original transporter. Its determinant is det(g)^(n−1)=det(g), as n is even. The new designated metric thus imposes exactly the old sign. All arrays have polynomial size.

If n is odd, taking determinants in gᵗQ'g=λQ forces λ=det(Q')/det(Q), because det(g)²=1 and λⁿ=λ. Pin the originally allowed value-line map to that computed ratio by marking the chosen source line basis vector and λ times the target line basis vector. Now designate Q in its chosen source coordinates and λ⁻¹Q' at the target. The resulting isometry condition is precisely the original form equation. If zero-dimensional metrics are allowed, an extra pinned one-dimensional form role can carry the identically positive determinant. These cases establish Q≡Qₛ.

Apply the earlier [exact one-sign tensor-system compiler](SignedCompiler.md) to the designated metric role for Q≤A. It retains the determinant of this original moving role. This final arrow uses the earlier informal one-sign compiler and does not require a tensor-isomorphism decision oracle. [Classical actions and ordinary tensor-system compilation](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

There is also an ordinary-TI lower bound for the simpler problem with just one metric on one physical mode and the signed isometry condition on that mode. Place the first tensor role V in the first summand of the hyperbolic space V⊕V*, and give the tensor zero support on its second summand. Any ordinary first-mode map a extends by diag(a,a⁻ᵗ), an isometry of determinant one. Conversely the supports of the inflated tensors are intrinsic, so restricting any inflated transporter to them and extending across the unused original coordinates yields ordinary equivalence. This simpler problem sits between O and Q even with the form supplied in standard hyperbolic coordinates.

## Further structural candidates and exclusions

- Even isomorphism of ternary represented matroids has degree C when its ground-set mark and colors are carried through. The needed uniqueness of ternary representation also has a short check. Put representations on a common pivot basis in identity form, with remaining block N. The matroid determines the zero pattern of N by exchanges of one basis element. On the bipartite graph of nonzero entries, row and column sign changes can align two sets of entries if and only if their sign ratios multiply to one on each cycle. It suffices to test chordless cycles. Their corresponding square submatrix has two determinant terms, each ±1 in F₃, so cancellation is equivalent to a prescribed product of its entry signs. Singularity of this submatrix is determined by the basis-exchange test for the matroid. Thus every chordless cycle is consistent. This determines row and column signs up to connected-component freedom, and pivot-column signs restore the identity block. Ternary matroid parity and even-code equivalence are therefore two formulations of one candidate. [Semple and Whittle, Partial Fields and Matroid Representation](https://www.math.canterbury.ac.nz/~c.semple/papers/SW96a.pdf)
- The cone construction also places marked graphic-matroid parity between Gᵥ and C. To the original simple graph adjoin a clique on three new anchors and join every vertex to every anchor. The result is 3-connected for a nonempty graph. Color anchor triangle edges individually and color each anchor-to-original edge by its anchor and the old vertex color. Mark just the edges from one fixed anchor to the marked original vertices. The 3-connected case of Whitney's theorem identifies every cycle-matroid isomorphism with a graph isomorphism on the same edges, and the three colored triangle edges fix the anchors. Hence the corresponding marked permutation is precisely the original one. The upper reduction uses oriented incidence matrices over F₃ and the preceding ternary uniqueness calculation. An input to the graphic-matroid comparison can specify its graph representation, with no constraints on the recovering graph beyond its cycle matroid and marked edges. This extra candidate admits all such graphic inputs, including graphs with 2-separations. Restricting to 3-connected simple graphs alone gives reverse reduction to Gᵥ by the same Whitney theorem and graph incidence, so the 3-connected restriction introduces no extra degree. [Whitney's theorem in the graph-to-code construction](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/782.pdf)
- Restricting M to rank-one and zero slices gives degree C. Factor each nonzero slice into a rank-one column and row. Its image line and coimage line must match under one common permutation. Their two independent signs are compensated by the permitted slice scale. To combine the two projective line comparisons into one ordinary permutation-code comparison with a common permuted mark, use the code of words (t,t+x,t−x,t+y,t−y) for x and y in the two respective full-row-rank codes. Use separate colors for the two pairs of groups. Both use the same t_i and hence the same original index permutation by the sum test above. Color zero slices separately. This removes a natural-looking candidate from the chain and isolates the role of higher-rank shared maps.
- Changing the ambient permutation data from graphs to directed graphs, bounded-arity relational structures or colored incidence hypergraphs preserves the degree Gᵥ when arbitrary colors and the single marked set are permitted. The ordered record construction preserves the exact action on the marked base points. Even isomorphism on a derived set such as conjugacy classes of a Cayley group remains a separate comparison from the element-parity definition. It reduces to Gᵥ by marking class vertices tied to the element vertices by membership. Parity on maximal subgroups of an explicitly presented 3-group has the same upper reduction: enumerate the hyperplanes of the Frattini quotient, of which there are fewer than the group order, and mark the corresponding subgroup vertices. Cayley-table group input is essential to this size estimate.

To collapse C to O in the displayed chain it is sufficient to reduce even ternary code equivalence to ordinary TI in one instance, using ordinary conjunction for the independent O test. Analogous tests apply to U and M. The upper endpoint question for Q is whether every signed TI query can be encoded with a nondegenerate symmetric form on the same orientation-carrying role. Neither of these questions is answered by the constructions above. Checking parity after an isomorphism is supplied would already give a polynomial-time verification algorithm for these problems. The degree comparisons require a single output decision instance on raw inputs.

An abstract existence statement follows from Ladner's density theorem. If either endpoint is strictly above the other in the polynomial-time many-one degrees of decidable languages, there are arbitrarily long strictly intermediate chains in that interval. The proof of existence supplies delayed-diagonalization languages. The comparisons above instead aim to identify explicit isomorphism problems with a concrete shared-transporter condition. [Ladner, On the Structure of Polynomial Time Reducibility](https://doi.org/10.1145/321864.321877)
