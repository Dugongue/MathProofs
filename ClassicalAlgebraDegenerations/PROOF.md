# Associative algebra degenerations in classical matrix conjugacy

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of any characteristic. For two unital associative algebras \(A,A'\) of the same positive dimension, consider degeneration in the variety of unital multiplication tables on the fixed-dimensional space, with the unit allowed to vary. Use the earlier hyperbolic encoding \(\mathcal H(A)\) as a matrix subalgebra. Then \(A'\) belongs to the orbit closure of \(A\) if and only if \(\mathcal H(A')\) belongs to the orbit closure of \(\mathcal H(A)\) in the Grassmannian of subspaces of the ambient matrix algebra. The latter closure can use any one of the general linear, split symplectic, or determinant-one split quadratic conjugation actions. The statement compares pairs inside the constructed family. It combines the exact classical transporter construction with recovery of corners in a flat one-parameter family.

## The encoding and its behavior on families

For \(n=\dim A\) write \(r=n+1\). Set
\[
B(A)=\begin{pmatrix}k & A\oplus k^r\\0&A\times k\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathcal H(A)=\{\operatorname{diag}(L_b,L_c^*): b,c\in B(A)\}.
\]
Here the right action on the top right is the coordinatewise action and \(L_b\) is left multiplication. The ambient module is \(B(A)\oplus B(A)^*\) with its two standard hyperbolic forms. The construction over a local base of finite free multiplication tables gives a subbundle of the endomorphism module. In each regular block evaluation at the unit splits the embedding as a module map, and the same is true of the dual block by transposition. Thus a family of algebra degenerations gives a family of Grassmannian points, and the earlier hyperbolic doubling of algebra isomorphisms gives conjugators preserving both forms and having determinant one. This proves preservation for all three actions.

For clarity, for orbit closure in either of the indicated finite-type varieties we use the usual curve-selection formulation. A point in the closure of an orbit is the special point of a curve whose generic point lies in the orbit, since the orbit contains an open subset of its irreducible closure. One may obtain such a curve by slicing through the special point while avoiding components lying in the closed complement. Normalize at a point over the special point and complete the local DVR. If necessary pass to a finite extension of its fraction field to choose an actual transporter at the generic point, and take the completed valuation ring in that extension. Such a finite extension exists because a nonempty finite-type transporter over the fraction field has a closed point with finite residue extension. The residue field remains \(k\). The same argument can be applied to an irreducible component of the orbit closure meeting the desired orbit points if the acting group is disconnected.

## Reflection by lifting orthogonal idempotents

Suppose now that \(\mathcal H(A')\) is a degeneration under the general linear group. Over the resulting complete DVR \(\mathcal O\), the family is a finite free associative algebra \(\mathcal C\) of matrices. Closure under multiplication and containment of the unit are closed conditions on a Grassmannian chart, so they persist. Its special fiber is \(B(A')\times B(A')^{\rm op}\), and its generic fiber is \(B(A)\times B(A)^{\rm op}\).

Lift the two ordered central idempotents of the special product to complementary idempotents of \(\mathcal C\). The required lifting holds for every finite free algebra over a complete DVR. For a lift \(u\) of an idempotent, \(2u-1\) is a unit since its square is 1 modulo the maximal ideal. Newton correction for \(u^2-u\), performed in the commuting subalgebra generated by \(u\) and the inverse, squares the order of the error and converges. Lift a family one member at a time inside the complementary corners. In the present case the two off-diagonal corners of the lifted pair reduce to zero and are direct summands of a finite free module, so they vanish by Nakayama's lemma. Thus the lifted pair is central.

Write \(\mathcal D\) for the summand specializing to \(B(A')\). The two field algebras \(B(A)\) and \(B(A)^{\rm op}\) are indecomposable. This follows from the connected weighted radical graph of the earlier construction, since the top scalar vertex joins the last scalar vertex and every simple factor of \(A/J(A)\). The algebra \(\mathcal D\) therefore has one of those two generic fibers. Inside \(\mathcal D\) lift the three orthogonal idempotents \(s,t,e=1-s-t\) of \(B(A')\), where \(s,t\) are the top and last scalar vertices. Every corner defined by these three lifts is a direct summand and its rank agrees with the dimension of its special fiber. In particular the two diagonal corners \(s\mathcal D s,t\mathcal D t\) have rank one, \(s\mathcal D t\) has rank \(r\), and \(s\mathcal D e\) has rank \(n>0\).

These three dimensions determine the orientation in the generic fiber. To see this, in either \(B(A)\) or its opposite, any complete orthogonal family is conjugate by a unit in \(1+M\) to its projections in the diagonal algebra, where \(M=A\oplus k^r\) is the square-zero off-diagonal ideal. This follows by the sum-of-products alignment of families agreeing modulo \(M\). A diagonal idempotent with one-dimensional diagonal corner must be supported on just one of the three diagonal factors. The only pair of distinct orthogonal idempotents with such corners and an off-diagonal corner of dimension \(r>n\) is the ordered pair of scalar vertices in the direction of the large arrow. All corners internal to \(A\) have dimension at most \(n\), and all corners involving \(A\) and a scalar vertex similarly have dimension at most \(n\). In the opposite algebra the source of the large arrow has zero outgoing corner to the complementary vertex. This contradicts the rank \(n\) just computed. It follows that \(\mathcal D\) has generic fiber \(B(A)\) with the lifted \(s,t\) aligned to their named vertices after conjugation.

Hence \(e\mathcal D e\) is a finite free unital algebra with generic fiber \(A\) and special fiber \(A'\). Choosing a free basis provides a one-parameter family of multiplication tables proving \(A'\in\overline{\operatorname{Orb}(A)}\). For the smaller classical groups, reflection follows because their orbits lie inside the general linear orbit. Thus the construction preserves and reflects the degeneration preorder, not only isomorphism of individual objects. This proof is informal.
