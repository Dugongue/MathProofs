# Contracting graph covers with a certified loss bound

A weighted Unique Games instance can be compressed by replacing a connected region with one label variable whenever the cover is trivial over the region and its interior has enough capacity to route its boundary weights to an anchor. Saturating max-flows certify that these replacements preserve the optimum on any quotient. A single directed min-cut computes a tighter, sharp bound on the loss from simultaneous rounding under a signed-parity condition. The algorithm applies to finite permutation constraints and specializes to weighted Max-2Lin, signed Ising ground states, and minimum-support representatives of abelian cohomology classes.

## From topology to an optimization certificate

Grochow and Tucker-Foltz interpret a Unique Games labeling as a section of the label-extended graph over the edges it satisfies. Their Theorem 14 proves Unique Games-completeness of a gap form of 1-homology localization on cell decompositions of closed orientable surfaces with coefficients in $\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z$, with $k$ depending on the gap. Observation 15 identifies a surface Max-2Lin instance coming from a 1-cocycle with 1-cohomology localization. The graph-cover interpretation also gives the right transformation for an algorithm. Use path transport to identify the fibers in a region, then ask how much the optimization objective can change if the region uses a single sheet. [Grochow and Tucker-Foltz, SoCG 2018](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.SoCG.2018.43), [full version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06800).

Gao, Brodzki, and Mukherjee use flat principal bundles and holonomy to characterize exact synchronizability. Cycle-edge message passing by Lerman and Shi estimates corrupted group ratios from cycle consistency and gives recovery guarantees under hypotheses on corrupted cycles. Either cycle statistics or a candidate synchronization can guide the search for regions. The test here checks a proposed region against all its edges. It can be used even when no group-valued planted solution or corruption model is available. [Gao, Brodzki, and Mukherjee](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.09051), [Lerman and Shi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.11347).

## Weighted contraction theorem

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite loopless multigraph with nonnegative rational edge weights $w_e$. Each vertex has an alphabet $[q]$, where $q\ge 2$. Give each edge one orientation $u\to v$ and a permutation $\pi_{uv}$ of $[q]$. The reverse orientation uses its inverse. An assignment satisfies the edge when $\ell(v)=\pi_{uv}(\ell(u))$. Write $F(\ell)$ for the satisfied weight. Partition $V$ into connected parts $P_i$ and choose an anchor $r_i$ in each. Write $E_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $E_{\mathrm{out}}$ for the edges within and between parts.

Assume the cover has trivial holonomy inside each part. To test this, choose a spanning tree in $P_i$ and let $T_v$ be the product of edge permutations along the tree path from $r_i$ to $v$. Check $T_v^{-1}\pi_{uv}T_u=\operatorname{id}$ for every interior edge. This costs $O(q(|V|+|E|))$ permutation-entry operations with explicitly tabulated permutations. Using $T_v^{-1}\ell(v)$ as the label at $v$ gives the same objective and makes interior constraints equalities. For a general permutation cover a section along one sheet only certifies one common fixed point of the holonomy, whereas this test verifies the behavior on every sheet.

Form the quotient by contracting each part to a vertex and retaining every cross edge with its transformed permutation. Let $I=w(E_{\mathrm{in}})$ and let $Q$ be the maximum satisfied cross-edge weight in the quotient. A quotient assignment lifts by assigning its part label in the transported coordinates at every vertex of that part.

For bits $x_v$ pinned to $0$ at anchors, set $C(x)=\sum_{uv\in E_{\mathrm{in}}}w_{uv}[x_u\ne x_v]$. Define $B_q(x)$ by summing $w_{uv}[x_u\lor x_v]$ over cross edges if $q\ge 3$, and $w_{uv}[x_u\ne x_v]$ if $q=2$. Here brackets indicate the indicator of the enclosed condition. Set $D=\max_x\bigl(B_q(x)-C(x)\bigr)$. The all-zero assignment gives $D\ge 0$.

**Theorem.** The optimum of the original instance lies in the interval $[I+Q,I+Q+D]$. The value $D$ is the largest possible loss of anchor rounding over all choices of the cross permutations and all input labelings on the given weighted partitioned graph.

For the bound, start with any assignment in the transported coordinates and replace each label by its anchor label. Mark changed vertices by $x_v=1$. Every interior edge with differently marked endpoints was violated and becomes satisfied, so the gained weight is at least $C(x)$. A cross edge loses weight only if at least one endpoint changes. For two labels, a change flips the bit. Flipping both bits preserves satisfaction of every binary permutation constraint, which sharpens the bound to $B_2(x)$. The rounded score is at least the original score minus $D$. All rounded scores are at most $I+Q$, and the optimum quotient assignment lifts to this value.

For sharpness, fix a maximizing $x$. Give label $0$ to every unmarked vertex and label $1$ to every marked vertex, naming the alphabet elements from $0$. The interior gain under rounding is exactly $C(x)$. If $q\ge 3$, choose a cross permutation that satisfies the original edge and violates the rounded edge whenever an endpoint is marked. With just one marked endpoint, every permutation satisfying the original edge already has this property. With both marked endpoints, fix label $1$ and transpose labels $0$ and $2$. On every other cross edge, choose the identity. If $q=2$, choose every cross permutation to satisfy the original edge, so precisely the cross edges with one marked endpoint become violated. This realizes rounding loss $D$ in either case. The loss is for this rounding map. The optimal quotient can compensate by changing the anchor labels.

## How to compute the certificate

For a part $P_i$ let $d(v)$ be the weight of cross edges incident to $v$, and let $D_i$ be the maximum of $\sum_{v\in S}d(v)-w(\delta_{P_i}(S))$ over $S\subseteq P_i\setminus\{r_i\}$, where $\delta_{P_i}(S)$ is the internal cut. Make $r_i$ the sink of a flow network on the part, include two oppositely directed arcs for each interior edge, and add a source with an arc of capacity $d(v)$ to each vertex $v$. A cut whose source side meets $P_i$ in $S$ has capacity $\sum_{v\notin S}d(v)+w(\delta_{P_i}(S))$, with the sum over vertices of the part. Thus $D_i$ is the total source-arc capacity minus the maximum flow. Taking $x$ supported in just $P_i$ gives $D\ge D_i$. Counting boundary exposures separately in each part gives $D\le\sum_i D_i$. Consequently $D=0$ exactly when all these flows saturate the source arcs. The result holds even when the quotient has odd cycles. Positive values still give the upper bound $I+Q+\sum_i D_i$.

For the tighter simultaneous budget, suppose the vertices other than the anchors admit bits $h_v$ which agree across every interior edge joining two such vertices and disagree across every cross edge joining two such vertices. A breadth-first search with parity constraints tests this condition. A bipartite quotient suffices. Take $W=w(E_{\mathrm{out}})$. Then $W-D$ is the minimum of $C(x)+W-B_q(x)$. Eliminate the pinned anchor bits and write $x_v=y_v\oplus h_v$ at the remaining vertices. Inside parts the binary terms remain cut penalties $w[y_u\ne y_v]$. For a cross edge whose endpoints have $h_u=0,h_v=1$, the $q\ge 3$ term becomes $w(1-y_u)y_v$, the cost of a directed arc $v\to u$ when bit $1$ denotes the source side. When $q=2$, the cross term becomes $w[y_u\ne y_v]$. Terms involving eliminated anchors are constants or unary preferences. Add unary preferences with arcs to and from the terminals. This gives $D$ from a single minimum cut and an explicit additive constant. With a supplied set of $p$ nonanchor vertices whose deletion yields parity consistency, fix their $2^p$ possible bit assignments, reduce their incident terms to unaries or constants, and take the best of $2^p$ cuts. The construction uses standard binary graph-cut representability. [Kolmogorov and Zabih](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~rdz/Papers/KZ-PAMI04.pdf).

The additive loss bound also applies to principal finite-group covers, whose edge permutations are left translations. To prove sharpness under the signed-parity condition for a group with at least three elements, assign the identity to unmarked vertices and distinct nonidentity elements $g_0$ and $g_1$ to marked vertices with parity $0$ and $1$. Interior edges between marked vertices originally agree. A cross edge with a marked endpoint originally has distinct labels at its ends. For each cross edge $u\to v$, use left translation by $\ell(v)\ell(u)^{-1}$, which satisfies the original assignment and violates the rounded one exactly when the original labels differ. This realizes $D$. The binary proof already uses only translations in the group of order two.

## Examples and operating regimes

Take parts $\{a,u\}$ and $\{b,v\}$ anchored at $a$ and $b$. The interior edges $au,bv$ each have weight $1$. The cross edges $uv,ub,va$ have weights $1,3,3$. Moving just $u$ or just $v$ gives a rounding deficit of $3$. Moving both gives the maxima $D=5$ for $q=3$ and $D=4$ for $q=2$. The separate patch bounds sum to $6$. Exhaustively checking all $216$ ternary and $8$ binary cross-permutation choices reproduces the worst losses $5$ and $4$. The largest actual optimum gap between the full and contracted problems is $2$ in each enumeration. This distinguishes robust rounding loss from the smaller gap a reoptimized quotient can achieve.

For an exact family, use unit-weight cliques of size $m\ge 2$ with trivial interior holonomy. If a clique's total incident cross weight is at most $m-1$, every nonempty cut of its interior has weight at least that much, so the anchor flow saturates. Connect the cliques in a cycle with a nonidentity net shift on the quotient, so that the instance can have genuine frustration. The original graph has treewidth at least $m-1$ even though the quotient has treewidth $2$. Given a quotient decomposition of width $t$, storing one entry for each assignment to each bag and counting each cross-edge objective term at one bag solves the weighted quotient in $q^{t+1}\operatorname{poly}(|V|+|E|)$ elementary operations. A heuristic quotient score $L$ and an independently valid upper bound $U$ also suffice to lift a solution of score $I+L$ and certify the interval $[I+L,I+U+D]$. [Freuder](https://www.aiinternational.org/Library/AAAI/1990/aaai90-001.php).

For abelian translations, orient each edge and write its shift as $a_e$. Its violation cost is the weight of the support of $a-\delta\ell$, where $(\delta\ell)_{uv}=\ell(v)-\ell(u)$. Minimization is weighted nearest-coset decoding for the code of graph coboundaries. When $a$ is a 1-cocycle on a 2-complex, the original objective is weighted cohomology localization in the fixed class $[a]$. On a closed orientable surface, dual cells identify this with weighted homology localization on the dual, with inherited edge weights. Subtracting the certified bounds on satisfied weight from the total edge weight gives corresponding bounds on the minimum support weight. Topological methods supply other parameterizations, including algorithms for a shortest representative in a prescribed binary homology class with dependence exponential in genus. [Grochow and Tucker-Foltz, Observations 10 and 15](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06800), [Erickson and Nayyeri](https://jeffe.cs.illinois.edu/pubs/pdf/homcover.pdf).

## Relation to existing algorithms

Lange, Andres, and Swoboda already use objective-improving maps, cuts, and contraction for persistency in Max-Cut and multicuts. Their Max-Cut subgraph test can choose which side to switch, while the map studied here fixes the anchor side. The contribution here is the simultaneous finite-cover rounding analysis, the sharp distinction between binary and larger alphabets, and the cut formulation of the joint loss after transporting the labels. The checked sources establish the related techniques, and priority for the combined statement remains open. [Lange, Andres, and Swoboda, Sections 4–6](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.01426).

Kolla's spectral algorithm uses eigenvalues of the label-extended graph and recovers efficient algorithms for expander instances. Bafna, Barak, Kothari, Schramm, and Steurer use low-degree sum-of-squares certificates for expansion or global hypercontractivity. Iwata, Wahlström, and Yoshida use half-integrality and flow for parameterized edge-deletion Unique Label Cover. These give distinct algorithmic baselines. The contraction theorem concerns a supplied partition and verifiable local capacity, and the quotient may still require an exact or approximate solver. It gives no unconditional improvement to the general Unique Games approximation threshold. [Kolla](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.2300), [Bafna et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09969), [Iwata, Wahlström, and Yoshida](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.2841).

The theorem and its algorithmic consequences have the proofs above. The exhaustive checks cover the four-vertex example. The result has no Lean formalization.
