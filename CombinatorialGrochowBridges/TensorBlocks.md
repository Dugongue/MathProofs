# Recovering graph structure from tensor isomorphism

**Result.** A basis of a tensor subspace determines one associative algebra for each tensor leg. Primitive idempotents of those algebras recover a product decomposition up to changes of basis that preserve the subspace. Over a finite field, bounded dimensions of the recovered summands suffice for a polynomial-size reduction to Graph Isomorphism. Small treewidth of their incidence graph gives a fixed-parameter algorithm. For two tensor legs with a forest of invertible matrix lines, the summands can have arbitrary dimension.

The proofs below are mathematical arguments with constructive witnesses. They have no Lean formalization.

## 1. The starting points in the literature

- **Tensor, algebra, and group isomorphism.** Grochow and Qiao showed that higher-order tensor isomorphism reduces to 3-tensor isomorphism and established polynomial-time equivalences with algebra isomorphism and form equivalence. Their linear-length reductions transport algorithmic advances without inflating group tables by a quasipolynomial factor. For example, Part IV transfers Sun's subquadratic-exponent algorithms to further classes and proves that polynomial-time Graph Isomorphism would imply a single-exponential bound in the number of variables for cubic form equivalence over a fixed finite field. The construction below uses an intrinsic parameter of a particular tensor instance to obtain a graph of polynomial size. [Grochow–Qiao I](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110), [Grochow–Qiao IV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16317), [Sun](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10485538).
- **Hamming and rank-metric codes.** Sendrier's support splitting refines coordinate signatures to recover a permutation in favorable cases. Grochow and Qiao reduce monomial Hamming-code equivalence to 3-tensor isomorphism. Couvreur, Debris-Alazard, and Gaborit define the left and right stabilizer algebras of a matrix code and show that two-sided matrix-code equivalence is at least as hard as monomial Hamming-code equivalence. Their polynomial or zero-error polynomial algorithm addresses right equivalence. Here both stabilizer algebras are used to retain the coupling between left and right actions in an exact two-sided test. [Sendrier](https://www.paris.inria.fr/secret/PUBLICATIONS/RESUMES/Sendrier00.html), [Grochow–Qiao II, Prop. 7](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol200-ccc2021/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.16/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.16.pdf), [Couvreur–Debris-Alazard–Gaborit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04611).
- **Graphical matrix spaces and refinement.** Li, Qiao, the two Wigdersons, and Zhang relate graphical matrix spaces to matching, conjugacy, and congruence. Li and Qiao also adapt graph individualization and refinement to average-case alternating matrix-space isometry. Grochow and Levet use group Weisfeiler–Leman refinement to improve parallel upper bounds on families of group isomorphism. In the construction below, graph refinement can reject or guide search after the reduction. An exact graph-isomorphism algorithm or a verified discrete matching supplies the decision. [Graphical matrix spaces, Theorem 1.15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04815), [Li–Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04501), [Grochow–Levet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11487).

## 2. Recovering the summands

Fix a finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$ and spaces $V_1,\ldots,V_k$ of positive dimension. The input is a basis of $S\leq V_1\otimes\cdots\otimes V_k$. The equivalence relation is $S'=(\bigotimes_i g_i)S$ with independent $g_i\in\operatorname{GL}(V_i)$. An ordinary $(k+1)$-tensor defines $S$ as the image of the contraction map from the dual of its last leg. For two tensors of the same format, equivalence of these images is sufficient for tensor isomorphism. After applying the first $k$ maps, both contraction maps surject onto the same image. Use splittings of the surjections to identify their quotient spaces and any isomorphism to identify their equal-dimensional kernels. The resulting invertible change on the dual of the last leg gives the remaining tensor map. This also works when the contractions have nontrivial kernels.

Define
$$
A_i(S)=\{a\in\operatorname{End}_F(V_i)\mid
 (1\otimes\cdots\otimes a\otimes\cdots\otimes1)S\subseteq S\}.
$$
Each is a unital associative algebra computable by linear equations. For each basis element of $S$, apply the operator with symbolic entries and require its image to vanish in the ambient quotient by $S$. Compute structure constants by multiplying the resulting matrix basis. The inverse of a unit of $A_i(S)$ is in the algebra, so that unit acting in leg $i$ fixes $S$ as a set.

Take a complete system of nonzero pairwise orthogonal primitive idempotents $e_{i,j}$ in $A_i(S)$. Completeness means $\sum_j e_{i,j}=1$. Primitivity is in $A_i(S)$ and means an idempotent cannot split there as the sum of two nonzero orthogonal idempotents. An algorithm of Chistov, Ivanyos, and Karpinski finds such a system in Las Vegas polynomial time or in deterministic time polynomial in the input size and the characteristic. Set $U_{i,j}=e_{i,j}V_i$. For each tuple $\alpha=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)$, applying the projection $\bigotimes_i e_{i,j_i}$ to $S$ computes a local subspace $S_\alpha\leq\bigotimes_i U_{i,j_i}$. The projections preserve $S$, are pairwise orthogonal, and sum to the identity, so $S=\bigoplus_\alpha S_\alpha$. [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, Prop. 11](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf).

**Independence of the chosen projections.** In any finite-dimensional unital algebra $A$, two complete primitive orthogonal systems $e_j$ and $f_\ell$ give decompositions of the right regular module $A_A$ into indecomposable right ideals $e_jA$ and $f_\ell A$. Indeed, the endomorphism algebra of $e_jA$ identifies with $e_jAe_j$, in which a nontrivial idempotent would split $e_j$. By Krull–Schmidt, we can match the two lists of summands and take a direct sum of isomorphisms between the matching summands. This regular-module automorphism is left multiplication by a unit $u$, since a right-module map $A\to A$ is determined by its value on $1$. The automorphism conjugates the corresponding projections, which are left multiplication by $e_j$ and $f_\ell$. Hence $ue_ju^{-1}=f_\ell$ under the matching.

An equivalence $g=\bigotimes_i g_i$ from $S$ to $S'$ conjugates $A_i(S)$ to $A_i(S')$. Apply the preceding argument inside each target algebra to match the transported system to the computed one. The units used for this adjustment fix $S'$, so the adjusted equivalence sends summands to summands and local subspaces to local subspaces. Conversely, leg-wise block permutations with linear maps matching all local subspaces assemble to a tensor-subspace equivalence. In particular, the maximum rank $b$ of the primitive projections is intrinsic. It is also the minimum possible largest summand dimension across product decompositions that split $S$ across their block tuples. In any such decomposition the coordinate projections lie in the respective $A_i(S)$ and can be split into primitive projections by refining in their corners.

## 3. Exact reduction to graph isomorphism

Form a support incidence graph $H$ with one vertex for each summand, colored by leg, and one vertex for each $\alpha$ with $S_\alpha\neq0$, adjacent to the summands in $\alpha$. Keep the isolated summands. To represent the local maps consistently, expand $H$ as follows.

1. At every summand $U$ of dimension $r$, introduce a vertex for each ordered basis $B\colon F^r\to U$. Link it to the summand vertex, which is colored by leg and dimension. For every $h\in\operatorname{GL}(r,q)$, add the directed relation $B\mapsto Bh$ with label $h$ in a common field representation.
2. At every occupied tuple $\alpha$, introduce one relation for each tuple $(B_1,\ldots,B_k)$ of ordered bases of its summands. Link the relation vertex to the occupied tuple vertex and give the relation ordered ports to those basis vertices. Its label is the unique reduced row echelon matrix for $S_\alpha$ in the tensor product of the bases, with row length included in the label.
3. Implement a relation as a vertex with an incident port vertex of distinct color for each argument position. A directed labeled edge is a two-port relation. Shared colors refer to equal descriptors in the input pair. To obtain ordinary uncolored graphs, attach two private cycles to every core vertex, of lengths $3$ and $4+c$ for a common nonnegative integer color ID $c$. The cycles share only that core vertex. Core vertices have degree at least $4$, and every private vertex has degree $2$. Deleting the private cycles recovers the colored core. This encoding adds polynomial size, increases maximum degree by four, and raises treewidth to at most the larger of its former value and $2$.

A graph isomorphism matches summands and induces a map $\phi$ on their ordered bases. The labeled edges enforce $\phi(Bh)=\phi(B)h$, so $\phi(B)B^{-1}$ is independent of $B$ and is the unique induced linear map on that summand. The local relation labels enforce that the tensor products of the induced maps carry every occupied $S_\alpha$ onto its target. The graph bijection also matches occupied tuples in reverse, which handles the zero tuples. Conversely a system of block permutations and linear maps matching all the local subspaces maps every basis and port by construction. A graph isomorphism therefore yields matrices witnessing the original equivalence, and the witness can be verified directly by applying those matrices and comparing subspaces. A graph decision oracle also suffices for search via successive vertex individualization in the colored graph.

There are fewer than $q^{r^2}$ bases for $r\geq1$, and each occupied tuple has at most $q^{kb^2}$ basis tuples. The dense input size $N$ bounds the product of the mode dimensions when $S$ is nonzero. The zero space is handled separately. The construction uses $q^{O(kb^2)}\operatorname{poly}(N)$ time and graph size, including port vertices and color encoding. For fixed $q,k,b$ it is a deterministic polynomial-time reduction to GI. Already $k=2,b=1$ gives GI-hardness for fixed $q$: send a simple graph with at least one edge to its bipartite vertex-edge incidence graph and take the span of the coordinate matrix units indexed by incidences. The coordinate rank-one projections lie in the one-sided algebras and form primitive systems. The recovered support is exactly the incidence graph up to part-preserving isomorphism. Babai's bound gives a quasipolynomial algorithm for the restricted tensor class. [Babai](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03547).

If each summand participates in at most $\Delta$ occupied tuples, the graph has maximum degree at most $f(q,k,b)(\Delta+1)$ for a computable $f$. Bounded-degree graph isomorphism therefore gives polynomial time for fixed $q,k,b,\Delta$. If $H$ has treewidth $t$, put the basis vertices and their internal port relations in the fiber of their summand vertex, and the local relations and their ports in the fiber of their tuple vertex. Every core edge stays within a fiber or goes between fibers adjacent in $H$. Replacing each bag of a tree decomposition by the union of its fibers gives width at most $f(q,k,b)(t+1)-1$. The bound after attaching private cycles is $\max\{2,f(q,k,b)(t+1)-1\}$. Bounded-treewidth GI yields an algorithm fixed-parameter tractable in $q,k,b,t$ with the characteristic charged to $q$. [Grohe–Neuen–Schweitzer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04659), [Lokshtanov–Pilipczuk–Pilipczuk–Saurabh](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.0818), [Improved bounded-treewidth GI](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06858).

## 4. Gain graphs and a polynomial case with large summands

Take $k=2$ and assume every occupied $S_{uv}$ is the line of an invertible square matrix $M_{uv}$ in local bases. In a connected support component all adjacent summands then have a common dimension $r$. This condition is decidable from the computed blocks. Choose any alignment of two dimension-colored bipartite supports. A block map acts on matrices as $M_{uv}\mapsto L_uM_{uv}R_v^{\mathsf T}$. Taking projective classes and writing $x_u=[L_u]$, $x_v=[R_v]^{-\mathsf T}$ turns the equation into $[M'_{uv}]=x_u[M_{uv}]x_v^{-1}$. Thus the residual test under an alignment is switching equivalence of gains in $\operatorname{PGL}(r,q)$.

Orient each edge from left to right and use its inverse gain for reverse traversal. On a spanning tree one root switch determines all others by the edge equations. For any closed walk from the root, the intervening switches cancel so the original and target products must be conjugate by that same root switch. The fundamental cycles suffice, since each contains one off-tree edge and its constraint supplies exactly the omitted edge equation. This is the established gain-graph switching criterion. Applied to the recovered support, it shows that for forest supports, colored forest isomorphism decides tensor equivalence in polynomial time with arbitrary summand dimensions. Start with identity at each root, propagate switches, lift each switch to a linear map, and apply the construction for the last tensor leg. The algorithm is deterministic for fixed characteristic and Las Vegas polynomial for arbitrary finite fields. [Cavaleri–Donno, Theorem 3.3 and Remark 3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12428).

For a concrete family, assign a copy of $F^r$ to each vertex of a bipartite forest without isolated vertices and put $\langle I_r\rangle$ on each edge. Each standard block projection has rank $r$ and is primitive. An endomorphism in its corner must multiply an incident invertible matrix line into itself, so that corner consists of scalars. The same argument applies with arbitrary invertible matrices on the edges. Cycles yield additional information even if the support and dimensions match. Over $\mathbb F_3$, take dimension $2$ at each vertex of a four-cycle. All edges set to $I_2$ give trivial cycle gain. Replacing a single edge by $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ gives nontrivial cycle gain under every support alignment and hence an inequivalent tensor.

The contribution here is the combination of one-sided stabilizer algebras, conjugacy of primitive systems, and local graph relations to recognize disguised block structure and obtain the stated sufficient algorithmic conditions. The graphical-matrix, idealizer, gain-switching, and graph-isomorphism ingredients have the separate precedents cited above. The construction applies to the independent general-linear actions specified in Section 2. Using it after a reduction from a different isomorphism problem requires checking the block and support parameters on the reduced instance. Priority for this combination remains unverified.
