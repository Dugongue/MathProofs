# Raw determinant profiles from bounded rectangular pencil components

An informal sufficient theorem for TI versus SL-TI over every fixed finite field. The algorithm starts from raw tensors in arbitrary independent bases, allows rectangular or everywhere singular pencils, and computes the full determinant profile by a deterministic polynomial-time Turing reduction to ordinary TI. It uses the factor supports of maximal determinantal divisors of small indecomposable summands. No regular square summand is assumed.

## 1. Covariant finite covector lists

Write the slice presentation as \(M:U\to\operatorname{Hom}_F(X,Y)\), with transporter equation \(M_B(fu)=hM_A(u)g^{-1}\). Define its ordered-pencil module on \(X\oplus Y\) by the two vertex projections and the linear span of arrow operators \(u\mapsto M(u)\), extended by zero on \(Y\). Use one named arrow per raw basis vector. Relabeling by an invertible \(f\) only changes the basis of the generator span.

Compute the ordered-pencil end algebra \(D\) by linear equations. Compute \(J=\operatorname{Rad}D\), an explicit product of full matrix algebras for \(D/J\), and the quotient map. This is deterministic polynomial time at fixed finite characteristic. Lift the primitive diagonal matrix idempotents of this product one at a time, working in the corner complementary to the preceding lifts. For a lift \(a\), use \(a^{p^e}\) once \(p^e>\dim_F D\), since \((a^{p^e})^2-a^{p^e}=(a^2-a)^{p^e}=0\). The complementary idempotent after all lifts is in the radical, hence zero. Each image module is indecomposable because its endomorphism corner is a division algebra modulo a nilpotent ideal. This gives a Krull–Schmidt decomposition in deterministic polynomial time. A genuine tensor transporter sends any such decomposition to one on the target after relabeling the arrows. The computed target decomposition differs from it only by equality of ordered-module isomorphism types and by permutation, by Krull–Schmidt for finite-dimensional modules. [Finite algebra algorithms, Ivanyos–Qiao Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#S3.SS5.SSS1)

Fix \(b\). For every indecomposable summand of both vertex dimensions at most \(b\), let \(r\) be the generic rank of its slice matrix. At rank zero it contributes no mark. For \(r>0\) take the gcd of its nonzero \(r\)-minors, up to scalar. Cauchy–Binet shows that the ideal generated by these minors is unchanged under invertible row and column basis changes, by applying the minor expansion and its inverse. Therefore the gcd is intrinsic. The label map acts on it by linear substitution. The small-summand filter is intrinsic because it only uses vertex dimensions.

Here is a self-contained bound for extracting absolute factors from these divisors. The \(b^2\) matrix entries span a space of at most \(b^2\) linear forms in the raw labels. Compute a basis by Gaussian elimination and write them in those independent coordinates. Enumerate all minors of the constant-size matrix and expand them exactly in these variables. This determines \(r\), including zero. Every nonzero maximal minor is homogeneous of degree at most \(b\). Normalize its absolute factors to be monic in a fixed monomial order. Frobenius permutes these factors, so an individual field of definition has degree at most \(b\) over \(F=\mathbb F_q\). All such factors of any of the small minors are therefore defined over the same fixed field \(K=\mathbb F_{q^{\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,b)}}\). This field can be precomputed, or constructed by exhaustive search at constant \(q,b\). Enumerate all nonzero monic homogeneous polynomials in at most \(b^2\) variables of positive degree at most \(b\) over \(K\). This is a finite constant-size search for fixed \(q,b\). Extract minimum-degree exact divisors by polynomial division and repeat. Since all geometric factors are already defined over \(K\), this gives the absolute factors. Compare factor lines in the same coordinates and take minimum multiplicities across the nonzero maximal minors. Adjoining unused raw variables does not change the factorization because the selected coordinates extend to a linear coordinate system.

For each nonconstant factor \(\phi\) let \(T_\phi\) be the space of formal translation directions, those \(v\) for which \(\phi(z+s v)=\phi(z)\) as a polynomial in \(z,s\). It is a linear subspace by the addition and scaling identities of translation. Its annihilator is the useful space of linear forms \(E_\phi\). Indeed in a basis adapted to the translation space, translating in each of its basis directions proves independence of that coordinate, in all characteristics. Conversely a presentation using given linear forms is translation invariant along their common annihilator. Compute this space by trying all directions in the at most \(b^2\) active variables over the fixed field \(K\), testing formal coefficient equality, and lifting its annihilator through the original linear forms. This avoids replacing a formal polynomial by its finite-field evaluation function.

The smallest \(F\)-space containing \(E_\phi\) after base change is obtained by expanding a \(K\)-basis of \(E_\phi\) coefficientwise in an \(F\)-basis of \(K\) and taking the span of the resulting \(F\)-covectors. In any given containing \(F\)-space every such coefficient covector belongs to that space. Conversely their \(K\)-span contains \(E_\phi\). The resulting space is thus independent of the chosen bases and transports contragrediently. Its dimension is at most \(b^2\) because all these coordinates come from the \(F\)-span of the block's entries.

Collect all \(F\)-covectors in these spaces into a set \(\mathcal S\). There are at most \(b q^{b^2}\) contributed per summand. Under \(f\), its image by \(f^{-\mathsf T}\) is exactly the target set, including for arbitrary choices of bases in the Krull–Schmidt blocks. The extraction is exact on both sides for this filter, with no heuristic factor candidates. Let \(L\ge2\) denote the raw input length. The sufficient hypothesis on the source is that \(S=\langle\mathcal S\rangle\) has codimension at most \(C\log_q L\) in \(U^*\), for fixed \(C\). Every ordinarily isomorphic target has the same dimension and covector-list properties.

## 2. Read the full determinant profile by bounded image searches

Let \(W=S^\perp\). Find one ordinary transporter as follows. First test ordinary TI. Choose an ordered basis of \(S_A\) consisting of covectors from \(\mathcal S_A\). For each in turn, try every possible target covector in the polynomial-size \(\mathcal S_B\) under the contragredient convention. Keep a value for which ordinary TI with the accumulated pins still returns YES. These pins are equations \(\lambda_Bf=\lambda_A\), realizable by a linear map to a fixed coordinate line. Then \(f(W_A)=W_B\) and the map on the full quotient is fixed. Next try all \(q^{\dim W_B}\) images for each successive basis vector of \(W_A\), keeping earlier pins. This is polynomial. The full map on \(W_A\) is now fixed as well.

The unknown images of quotient lifts are shears into \(W_B\). Recover one scalar coordinate of one shear at a time. For a paired quotient lift \(u,u'\) and nonzero covector \(\ell\) on \(W_B\), use the invariant correspondence \(W_A+Fu\to W_B+Fu'\). Its quotient by the corresponding kernels of \(\ell\) on the marked subspaces is two-dimensional. Fix the induced map on this two-dimensional quotient to impose just the chosen value of \(\ell(fu-u')\), with all other scalar coordinates unrestricted. Try the \(q\) field values, retaining an extendible one. Once the label map is fully known, constructive ordered module isomorphism gives actual \(g,h\). The pins, subspace inclusions and quotient arrows can be packed into a tensor system with a fixed number of vector-space types and tensors of order at most three. For a coordinate space with a specified basis, force its action to be the identity by attaching the nilpotent shift sending each basis vector to the next and the vector at the start of that chain. Commuting with the shift and fixing its cyclic vector fixes every basis vector. Package all prescribed image vectors in one arrow from such a coordinate space to the label space, and all prescribed covector equations in one arrow from the label space to a fixed-coordinate space.

For the simultaneous shear flags, let (V_i) be each included subspace and (Q_i) its prescribed two-dimensional quotient. Use one space (V_oplus=igoplus_iV_i) and one fixed-coordinate space (Q_oplus=igoplus_iQ_i), with bases paired by the prescribed quotient maps. If (P_i) is the projection to the (i)-th summand in (V_oplus), attach the tensor (sum_i P_iotimes e_i) in (operatorname{End}(V_oplus)otimes E), where (E) is a fixed-coordinate space with basis (e_i). Its preservation is exactly preservation of the individual summands. Use the sum of inclusions (V_oplus	o U) and the direct sum of quotient projections (V_oplus	o Q_oplus). The first arrow forces the action on each summand to be the restriction of the label map, and the second fixes the requested quotient maps. Conversely, a label map meeting the pins extends to the actions on these spaces. These constructions use a fixed signature even when there are polynomially many constraints, so the tensor-system compiler makes each query an ordinary TI query in polynomial time. [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219), [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4), [Brooksbank–Luks, Testing Isomorphism of Modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

To get all determinant values rather than just the value of one transporter, compute the determinant image of the source automorphism group. Run the same prefix search against the source itself. At every prefix of the chosen basis covectors and the subsequent basis of \(W_A\), first fix the earlier marks pointwise. For every possible next image, test feasibility by ordinary TI. For each attainable image the preceding continuation algorithm constructs one automorphism realizing it. These representatives give a transversal of the current group modulo the next stabilizer. A polynomial number of image trials and continuation searches therefore provides a generating list up to the terminal stabilizer.

The terminal stabilizer acts trivially on \(W_A\) and \(U_A/W_A\). Its label-image group is a subgroup of the additive group \(\operatorname{Hom}_F(U_A/W_A,W_A)\), so has \(p\)-power order. Its determinant-triple image is already contributed by the label kernel, since the determinant target \((F^\times)^3\) has order prime to \(p\). The kernel is the unit group of the ordered-pencil end algebra \(D\), with trivial label determinant. Compute its determinant image by the \(J\)-filtrations on \(X\) and \(Y\). If a simple factor \(\operatorname{Mat}_e(E)\) of \(D/J\) occurs with total natural-module multiplicities \(m_X,m_Y\) on the grades, its contribution to the two determinants is
\[
 \big(a^{m_X},a^{m_Y}\big),\qquad a\in F^\times .
\]
Indeed each simple determinant over \(E\) contributes its norm to \(F\), and determinants multiply through the filtration. Norms are surjective and all quotient units lift through \(J\). These multiplicities are computed by applying the central idempotents on each grade and dividing their dimensions by \(e[E:F]\). Multiply the resulting subgroup by the determinant triples of the prefix representatives to generate the entire raw stabilizer image. Translate by the determinant triple of the found raw transporter. This is the exact full raw determinant profile, and testing whether it contains the identity gives SL-TI. There are polynomially many marked TI calls and polynomial overhead at fixed \(F,b,C\).

## 3. Balanced examples with no square regular summand

Over \(F=\mathbb F_3\), take \(k\) independent groups of nine label coordinates. In group \(j\) set
\[
 A_j=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,a_2,a_3)+b_1E_{21}+b_2E_{32}+b_3E_{13},\qquad
 B_j=\operatorname{diag}(x,y,z)+(x+y+z)P,
\]
where \(P e_1=e_2,P e_2=e_3,P e_3=e_1\). Coordinates for different groups are distinct. Use a shared \(t=a_1-a_2\) from the first group. For \(D=A_j\) put \((\ell_1,\ell_2)=(b_1,b_2)\), and for \(D=B_j\) put \((\ell_1,\ell_2)=(x-y,y-z)\). Form
\[
 R_D=\begin{pmatrix}D&0\\ t e_1^{\mathsf T}&(\ell_1,\ell_2)\end{pmatrix}
 \quad(4\text{ rows},5\text{ columns}).
\]
Use the direct sum of \(R_{A_j}\) and \(R_{B_j}^{\mathsf T}\) for all \(j\). The three tensor dimensions are all \(9k\).

The only nonzero maximal minors of \(R_D\), up to sign, are \((\det D)\ell_1\) and \((\det D)\ell_2\), since the first three rows need all of the first three columns. The maximal-minor gcd is exactly \(\det D\). For \(A_j\), this is the absolutely irreducible cubic \(a_1a_2a_3+b_1b_2b_3\), primitive and linear in \(a_1\). For \(B_j\) it is \(xyz+x^3+y^3+z^3\). A reducible homogeneous cubic over the closure has a linear divisor. If its \(z\) coefficient is nonzero, substitute \(z=\alpha x+\beta y\). The mixed coefficients \(x^2y,xy^2\) force \(\alpha=\beta=0\), but then the two cubes do not vanish. If the alleged divisor has zero \(z\) coefficient, the restricted \(z^3\) coefficient survives. This proves absolute irreducibility. The first partial derivatives of each polynomial are independent on its support coordinates, so each support is exactly the corresponding six- or three-dimensional coordinate space.

Each \(R_D\) is indecomposable. At the common slice with all \(a_i,x,y,z\) equal to \(1\) and all \(b_i=0\), its matrix is \(\operatorname{diag}(I_3,0)\) in rectangular block form. The ordered endomorphism equations force the domain map to have form \(\begin{pmatrix}G&0\\ V&C\end{pmatrix}\) and the range map form \(\begin{pmatrix}G&H\\0&d\end{pmatrix}\). The top-right equation gives \(H(\ell_1,\ell_2)=0\), hence \(H=0\). The top-left equation gives \(GD=DG\) for all slices. The slices of either \(D\) generate all \(3\times3\) matrices. For \(A_j\) they supply the diagonal units and directed cyclic units. For \(B_j\), polynomials in the difference of the first two coefficient matrices give all coordinate projections because the diagonal entries \(1,-1,0\) are distinct. Subtracting the projections from the coefficient matrices gives \(P\). Thus \(G=gI\). The bottom-right equation gives \(C=dI\) by independence of the two \(\ell_i\). The bottom-left equation now is
\[
 (d-g)t e_1^{\mathsf T}=(\ell_1,\ell_2)V.
\]
The three linear forms are independent on every block, so \(d=g\) and \(V=0\). This end algebra is scalar, hence the block and its transpose are indecomposable. Therefore the full ordered pencil has only rectangular indecomposable summands and its full determinant is identically zero. The tensor is concise because each block has no common source kernel or proper common image, and the \(A_j,B_j\) jointly detect every label.

The algorithm recovers the small cubic support spaces whose span is all of \(U^*\). Each contributes at most \(3^6\) covectors. Every regular-determinant-only method gets no factor marks on this family, whereas the maximal-minor method computes the exact determinant profile from raw coordinates.
