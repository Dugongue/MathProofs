# Tensor isomorphism on degree-minimal permutation presentations of exponent-prime groups

Fix an odd prime $p$. This is an informal proof.

**Theorem.** Three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb F_p$ is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to abstract isomorphism of permutation-generator groups of class at most two, exponent dividing $p$, and all orbits of size at most $p^2$. The hard instances have center equal to derived group, are polynomial-time recognizable from the raw permutations, and are given in faithful permutation actions of minimum possible degree. The instances corresponding to concise tensors preserve exactly the transporter maps on three characteristic framed sections. An orbit bound below $p^2$ forces these groups to be abelian.

The known tensor-to-group completeness results concern commutator or matrix group presentations. Here each group is supplied by literal permutation generators, with neither its multiplication table nor its hidden sections supplied for ordinary isomorphism. [Grochow and Qiao, Tensor Isomorphism-Completeness](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

## Bracket construction

Use the matrix notation of [the exponent-$p^2$ group construction](GroupTransfer.md#the-exact-optimal-orbit-cap-for-ti-completeness). In brief, a concise tensor gives a matrix space $D\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(R,L)$ with its given label frame. Set $U=L\oplus R$, and set $P=\mathbb F_p B\oplus N_D\subset\operatorname{End}(U)$, with $B=\operatorname{diag}(I_L,-I_R)$ and $N_M:R\to L$ the strictly upper block of $M\in D$. In $P$ the nilpotent locus is exactly $N_D$, since $(cB+N_M)^2=c^2I$. Its images span $L$, by conciseness. Consequently the conjugacy of the spaces $P$ is equivalent to the original equivalence of matrix spaces $D$. Conjugacy induces the maps on $L,U/L,N_D$, and any original tensor transporter extends by its block-diagonal maps on $L,R$. Take $Y=P^*$, write $a=\dim U$, $b=\dim Y\ge2$, and define the linear map $q:U\to U\otimes Y$ by $(1\otimes v)q(u)=M_vu$ for $v\in Y^*=P$. This $q$ is injective because $B$ is invertible.

Introduce spaces $J,K$ of respective dimensions $j,k$ with $k>b+1$ and $j>a+k$. For example $k=b+2$ and $j=a+k+1$ suffice. Use the tangent and central spaces
$$
    V=F\oplus S,\quad F=U\oplus J\oplus K,\quad S=Y\oplus\mathbb F_p t,
    \qquad Z=(U\otimes Y)\oplus(J\otimes Y)\oplus K .
$$
Define an alternating pairing $\beta$ on $V$ by its four nonzero block maps in the displayed central summands:
$$
 [u,y]=u\otimes y,\qquad [u,t]=q(u),\qquad
 [j_0,y]=j_0\otimes y,\qquad [k_0,t]=k_0 .
$$
Every unlisted block bracket is zero, and reversing the order of an input pair negates its value. The group on $V\oplus Z$ has law $(x,z)(x',z')=(x+x',z+z'+\frac12\beta(x,x'))$. The bilinear cocycle gives associativity and the indicated commutator. The exponent divides $p$ by alternation. Its center is $Z$, because an element with nonzero $S$-coordinate brackets nontrivially with $J$ or $K$, and a nonzero $F$-coordinate brackets nontrivially with $Y$ or $t$. The bracket is onto $Z$, so the derived group is $Z$ as well.

## Recover the tensor and all framed maps

The subspace $F$ is the unique largest common isotropic subspace for $\beta$, meaning the pairing vanishes on it. If an isotropic subspace $H$ contains a vector $x$ outside $F$, the restriction $[x,-]$ to $F$ has rank at least $\min(j,k)>b+1$ by its pairing with the dummy $J$ or $K$. Hence $H\cap F$ has codimension greater than $b+1$ in $F$, whereas $H/(H\cap F)$ has dimension at most $b+1$. Thus $\dim H<\dim F$. This makes $F$ and $V/F$ intrinsic, with $V/F$ identified with $S$. The cross bracket $F\times(V/F)\to Z$ is well-defined by isotropy.

The nonzero rank-one contractions of elements of $F$ are exactly the nonzero elements of $K$. A nonzero $U$ or $J$ component already gives an injection on $Y$ in the first or second central summand, of rank $b\ge2$. A nonzero $K$ component alone acts just on $t$, with rank one. Thus $K$ is intrinsic and its annihilator in $V/F$ is exactly $Y$. The $t$ line in $V/F$ is also intrinsic. Its nonzero contractions on $F$ have rank $a+k$ by injectivity of $q$ and the separate $K$ summand. Every element with a nonzero $Y$-coordinate has rank at least $j>a+k$ on $F$. Then $J$ is the kernel of contraction by a nonzero vector of the $t$ line. The subspace $Z_0=[J,Y]+[K,\mathbb F_p t]$ is intrinsic. The induced bracket on $F/(J+K)$ times $Y$ identifies $(F/(J+K))\otimes Y$ with $Z/Z_0$, recovering $U\otimes Y$. The induced bracket on $F/(J+K)$ times the $t$ line into $Z/Z_0$ recovers $q$ up to the scalar used to choose $t$. Its contraction image $P\subseteq\operatorname{End}(U)$ is unchanged by that scalar.

More precisely, any group isomorphism induces maps $f:U\to U'$, $g:Y\to Y'$ and $t\mapsto\lambda t'$ on the quotient $V/F$. The map on $Z/Z_0$ is $f\otimes g$, and preserving the bracket with $t$ gives $(f\otimes g)q=\lambda q'f$. Thus $fM_{g^*v'}f^{-1}=\lambda M'_{v'}$ for every $v'\in (Y')^*$. This is a conjugacy of the intrinsic spaces $P,P'$, with the exact induced maps on $L,U/L,N_D$. The original three maps are recovered using the input coordinate frames there, with duals if required by the tensor action convention. Conversely, an original slice transporter gives a block-diagonal $f$ conjugating $B,N_D$ to their target counterparts. Take $g$ to be the inverse dual of this conjugacy on $P$ and take $\lambda=1$. Extend with the identities on the dummy coordinates and the evident induced maps on $Z$. The four brackets are preserved, and therefore the group law is preserved. Both directions are exact on the three framed section maps.

## Realize the group by permutations

Choose the basis of $P$ formed by $B$ and the matrices $B+N_i$ for a basis $\{N_i\}$ of $N_D$. All basis matrices square to $I$, so each $M_v$ in the basis has an eigenbasis of functionals on $U$ over $\mathbb F_p$. For each such eigenfunctional $u^*$, with eigenvalue $\epsilon$, the central functional $u^*\otimes v$ on the first central summand gives the scalar alternating form
$$
                  u^*\wedge(v+\epsilon t^*)
$$
on $V$. These functionals form a basis on that summand. Each coordinate functional on $J\otimes Y$ or on the central $K$ gives a decomposable form by the corresponding single dummy bracket. The whole list is therefore a basis of $Z^*$ consisting of scalar forms of rank two.

Each such central functional and its two tangent linear factors give a quotient onto the class-two exponent-$p$ Heisenberg group of order $p^3$, using the displayed half-pairing law in two tangent coordinates and one central coordinate. A noncentral subgroup of order $p$ in that quotient is disjoint from its center and therefore core-free: a nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite $p$-group meets its center. The action on its cosets is faithful of degree $p^2$. Pull these actions back along the quotient maps. Their kernels intersect $Z$ trivially, because the chosen functionals are a basis of $Z^*$, so the whole action is faithful. All quotients have fixed order for fixed $p$, and the generator images and coset actions are obtained by finite-field arithmetic and their constant-size tables. The total permutation degree is $p^2\dim Z$.

This degree is minimal. By the independent central-mark argument of [the minimum degree formula](GroupTransfer.md#the-exact-minimum), a minimum action has $\dim Z$ orbits with independent marks. Every orbit detecting a nonzero direction of $Z=[G,G]$ has degree at least $p^2$, since any index-$p$ orbit has cyclic image. The exhibited action attains the lower bound. More generally a faithful action of a $p$-group with all orbits smaller than $p^2$ embeds it in a product of cyclic groups of order $p$, because its nontrivial orbits then all have size $p$.

## Full reverse and raw section extraction

For any inputs in the stated class, compute the elementary abelian spaces $C=Z(G)$ and $A=G/C$ and the alternating bilinear commutator $\beta:A\times A\to C$ in polynomial time by constructive permutation-group membership and linear algebra. One can first compute the elementary abelian derived subgroup from pairwise generator commutators, then get the center as the kernel of the vector of commutators with the generators. The elementary section coordinates are obtained by successive adjoining of independent generators and reading exponents in constructive membership expressions. An isomorphism is exactly a pair of linear maps in the $A,C$ roles preserving the bracket. This uses the class-two group law with $\frac12\beta$, which recovers every such group via its elementary abelian additive Lie ring. Any unused part of $C$ is retained as a central vector-space complement to the commutator image. The fixed-order tensor-system compiler maps this two-role comparison to one ordinary three-tensor comparison. The forward direction begins with ordinary support compaction before the displayed concise construction, with trivial branches handled by fixed outputs. This finishes the unrestricted ordinary equivalence. [Seress, Permutation Group Algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511546549) · [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, tensor-system compiler](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

On a hard instance, the promised intrinsic spaces also admit polynomial-time extraction, without searching isotropic subspaces. Extend the field to cardinality greater than $\dim V$. In the space of scalar alternating contractions of $\beta$, start with zero. Whenever the kernel of the current form is not commonly isotropic, choose two kernel vectors and one available scalar form pairing them nontrivially. This can be done using bases. The determinant on these two vectors and a nonsingular complement of the old kernel, after adding a scalar multiple of the chosen form, has a nonzero coefficient of degree two in that scalar. It is the product of the old nonsingular determinant and the determinant on the selected kernel pair. Scan the extension field to find a rank increase. In the constructed family the process cannot stop until the cross rank is $\dim S=b+1$: at a smaller cross rank the kernel contains a nonzero $S$ vector and a subspace of $F$ of codimension at most $b$, which that vector cannot centralize by the $j,k$ bounds. At full cross rank the kernel is contained in $F$, of codimension $b+1$ there. Its common centralizer is exactly $F$ by the same bounds. Descend by restricting these linear equations to original-field coordinates.

Compute $K\subset F$ by a Frobenius linear-algebra test. The $2\times 2$ minors of the matrix of $[x,-]$ on $V/F$, for a variable $x\in F$, are homogeneous quadratic polynomials in $x$. Form the space spanned by their products with all monomials of degree $p-2$. The span of quadratic minors is invariant under output and input basis changes by the Cauchy–Binet formula. In template bases, choose two different coordinates of $Y$. For each coordinate of $U$, take the two rows of $U\otimes Y$ for its two chosen $Y$ positions. The minor on those rows and the two $Y$ columns is the square of the given $U$-coordinate. The same holds for each $J$-coordinate by the rows of $J\otimes Y$. Multiplying by powers of the linear coordinates gives their $p$-th powers in the degree-$p$ span. That includes powers of all their linear combinations by Frobenius. Conversely every element of the span vanishes on $K$, because all the minor factors do. Thus the preimage of the span under the map taking $p$-th powers of linear forms is precisely the annihilator of $K$. Compute this preimage linearly in monomial coordinates over $\mathbb F_p$. The dimensions are polynomial at fixed $p$.

Recover $Y\subset V/F$ by annihilating $K$. To recover the $t$ line, choose a complement generator $t_0$ to $Y$. For a candidate $t_0+y$, the image on $F$ lies in $Z$, and its rank can be read using the coordinates already recovered. There is a polynomial **linear** search for the unique low-rank candidate. Put $Z_K=[K,V/F]$ and $\bar Z=Z/Z_K$, and use the identification
$$
   (F/K)\otimes Y\simeq\bar Z
$$
through the restriction of the cross pairing. In the fixed template $\bar Z=(U\oplus J)\otimes Y$. Form $T:F/K\to(F/K)\otimes Y$ by contraction with $t_0$, followed by quotient by $Z_K$. The true $t$ yields a map of rank $a$, vanishing on $J$, and $T$ differs from that map by $x\mapsto x\otimes y_0$ for some $y_0\in Y$. In arbitrary bases, contract $T$ against a basis $v_i$ of $Y^*$ with operators $T_i$. In the template, $T_i$ has eigenvalue $v_i(y_0)$ on the $j$-dimensional space $J$, where $j>a$. A linear operator on a space of dimension $j+a$ has at most one eigenvalue with eigenspace dimension greater than half the dimension. Enumerate the $p$ eigenvalues in the base field for each $T_i$, find the unique one with that property and recover $y_0$ by its coordinates. Consequently $t_0-y_0$ spans the $t$ line. Recover $J$ as the full kernel of $[- ,t]$. In $F/K$ the contractions of $[- ,t]$ into $(F/K)\otimes Y$ have joint image exactly the original $U$-summand. Each image lies there, and the contraction corresponding to $B$ is onto that summand. This recovers the complement to $J$ in $F/K$ by linear algebra. Lift its basis to $F$. Its bracket with $Y$ already specifies the old central summand $U\otimes Y$, independent of the lifts because $[K,Y]=0$. Its bracket with $t$ can be made to land in that central summand by subtracting the unique correction in $K$, since $[- ,t]$ on $K$ identifies $K$ with $Z_K$. Thus the full displayed frame can be recovered, including the unquotiented bracket values. The companion proof supplies the remaining polynomial matrix-space recognition for $P$. Check that the center equals the commutator image, that the prescribed role dimensions satisfy the positivity conditions and $k>b+1,\ j>a+k$, and that all displayed bracket identities hold in the constructed role frames, including the zero brackets on both $F$ and the chosen complement to $F$. An isotropic complement to $F$ can be recovered by linear adjustment of a section of $V/F$, since $F$ itself is isotropic. Therefore malformed inputs cannot pass, and on the promised construction all tests pass.

The central information replacing the original power map is the bracket with the one-dimensional $t$ role. The eigenfunctional argument is what keeps all orbit sizes at $p^2$ despite adding that extra bracket.
