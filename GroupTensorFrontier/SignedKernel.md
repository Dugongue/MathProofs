# A linear-side tensor kernel preserving the full determinant profile

**Theorem.** Fix a finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$. There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm taking any two explicit three-tensors of positive dimensions over $F$ to one raw three-tensor pair with the same set of attainable determinant triples in $(F^\times)^3$, including preservation of emptiness. The output has largest side $O_q(r+w+1)$. Here, after support compaction, $w$ is the label dimension and $r$ is the sum of both vertex dimensions of one representative of each distinct indecomposable of the fixed-label slice representation. The common parameters agree on positive ordinary instances, and any choice of the label mode is allowed. In particular, $w\le r^2$ and the output has $O_q((r+1)^6)$ entries.

Every feasible label map on the compacted input has an output realization with exactly the same attainable matrix-side determinants in the calibrated frames. Conversely, every output transporter restricts to such a label map and has matching determinant data. It follows that the same preprocessing is a kernel for ordinary TI, one-mode SL-TI, two-mode SL-TI, all-mode SL-TI, and any specified predicate on the three determinants. The result is informal.

## 1. Support compaction and conventions

In the bilinear slice convention write $M:W\times U\to V$. A transporter is a triple $(s,p,q_V)$ satisfying $q_V M(w,u)=M'(sw,pu)$. In the usual tensor convention with the ordered modes corresponding to $(W,U,V)$, the physical determinants are $(\det(s)^{-1},\det(p)^{-1},\det(q_V))$, because the two input maps are inverse transposes.

Tensor supports are intrinsic. Different ordered raw dimensions or support ranks give a fixed ordinary no pair. Compact to the three supports and save the coordinate charts. If the compact tensor is zero, the raw pair is either ordinarily inequivalent or both tensors are zero and all determinant triples are feasible. These cases have fixed outputs. For every deficient mode, any map on the support extends with every desired determinant by varying its unused quotient action. At the end, appending one unused coordinate to the corresponding output mode has exactly this effect. In each full mode use the original frame up to recorded chart determinants.

In a nonzero compact pair the slice map is injective, the common kernel is zero, and the images jointly span $V$. All dimensions below are those of the compact comparison unless marked otherwise.

## 2. Reduce copy counts modulo $q-1$

For the fixed basis of $W$, regard $M$ as a two-vertex quiver representation, with one arrow $U\to V$ per slice. Compute its indecomposable direct-sum decomposition. The fixed-label endomorphism algebra is obtained by linear equations in $\operatorname{End}(U)\times\operatorname{End}(V)$. Computing its radical and semisimple factors gives primitive diagonal idempotents in the quotient. Lift each idempotent in the remaining orthogonal corner and take a sufficiently high power of $q$: if $x^2-x$ is radical and $q^k$ bounds the nilpotence index, then $(x^{q^k})^2-x^{q^k}=(x^2-x)^{q^k}=0$. The images give the indecomposable summands. Compare them using constructive fixed-label module isomorphism and choose an ordered representative for each type. Give all isomorphic copies the same representative coordinates using the computed isomorphisms. Record the determinants of the resulting basis changes at both vertices. All these operations run in deterministic polynomial time over the fixed field. [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18) · [Brooksbank and Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

If a type occurs $m>0$ times, retain $\nu(m)$ copies, the unique integer in $\{1,\ldots,q-1\}$ congruent to $m$ modulo $q-1$. Retain the actual $m$ as its multiplicity color, shared at both vertices. Write $A:W\to\operatorname{Hom}(U_0,V_0)$ for the resulting pencil and $a=\dim U_0,\ b=\dim V_0$. Its matrix sides sum to at most $(q-1)r$. Its slice map remains injective, since a slice vanishes on the full module precisely when it vanishes on one copy of each type. Its common kernel is zero and its images span the new output, as these assertions hold on the original direct sum. In particular $w$ is at most the product of the two one-copy matrix dimensions, and $w\le r^2$. Compare the sorted multiplicity lists and dimensions of matching color parts at both endpoints. A mismatch gives an ordinary no pair.

Fix any $s:W\to W'$. Applying $s$ to the target slices is an invertible change of the arrow list, so it preserves decompositions and fixed-label equivalence. By Krull–Schmidt, the full representations match at $s$ exactly when the trimmed representations match with this same $s$ and their vertex maps preserving the specified multiplicity parts. To prove determinant equality at such an $s$, choose one isomorphism for each matched type and repeat it across its copies. For each vertex the determinant of this reference is the product of the determinant powers for those isomorphisms times the determinant of the block reordering. The powers $m$ and $\nu(m)$ agree in $F^\times$. Reordering transposes blocks of dimensions $d_i m_i$ and $d_j m_j$ with determinant $(-1)^{d_i m_i d_j m_j}$. When $q$ is odd, $\nu(m_i)\equiv m_i\bmod 2$; in characteristic two the transposition sign is one. Thus in the saved decomposed frames the two reference determinants agree.

All fixed-label maps at this $s$ differ from the reference by fixed-label automorphisms. For the full representation the semisimple quotient of its endomorphism algebra is a product of factors $\operatorname{Mat}_{m_i}(K_i)$, where $K_i$ is the residue field of the endomorphism ring of indecomposable type $i$. The corresponding factors of the color-preserving trimmed representation are $\operatorname{Mat}_{\nu(m_i)}(K_i)$. The determinant on the two vertices factors through the semisimple quotient, since units congruent to one modulo the radical act unipotently. To compute the factor contribution, lift a quotient unit blockwise to matrices over the endomorphism ring of the associated indecomposable. On the filtration of each vertex of that indecomposable by its local endomorphism radical, the simple residue module is $K_i$. The total multiplicity of this residue module is $a_i/[K_i:F]$ in the first vertex and $b_i/[K_i:F]$ in the second, where $a_i,b_i$ are the one-copy dimensions. Thus the factor with determinant $z\in K_i^\times$ contributes
$$
\big(\operatorname{Norm}_{K_i/F}(z)^{a_i/[K_i:F]},
      \operatorname{Norm}_{K_i/F}(z)^{b_i/[K_i:F]}\big).
$$
Every $z$ is possible, and the formula is independent of the number of copies. This proves equality of the fixed-label determinant subgroups and hence the complete relative profiles at each $s$. Color preservation can eliminate radical maps between differently colored summands, but it retains every semisimple factor.

## 3. Encode the multiplicity colors in one raw pencil

Let $c$ be the number of colors. Encode the sorted color indices in $t=\lceil\log_2 c\rceil$ bits. At each vertex let $\Pi^U_j,\Pi^V_j$ be the projection onto the sum of the color parts having bit $j$ equal to one. Vertex maps preserve the color parts exactly when they intertwine all these projections, since each part is a common coordinate eigenspace.

Put $D=2(q-1)$, and use an annotation space $F^k$ with the following coordinates. Use $D$ identity coordinates for each vertex, and $t$ color coordinates for each vertex. Add dummy coordinates if needed to make $k$ divisible by $q-1$. In particular $k\ge4$. On input $U_0\oplus V_0^{\oplus D}$ and output $V_0\oplus U_0^{\oplus D}$ use the main pencil
$$
 B(w,z)=\begin{pmatrix}A(w)&R(z)\\ L(z)&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
In $L(z):U_0\to U_0^{\oplus D}$, the $i$-th identity coordinate gives the identity into copy $i$; the $j$-th color coordinate gives $\Pi^U_j$ into copy 1. In $R(z):V_0^{\oplus D}\to V_0$, the corresponding identity coordinates project from the indicated copy and the $j$-th color coordinate gives $\Pi^V_j$ acting on copy 1. Dummy coordinates have zero main contribution.

The annotation label maps must be fixed up to one common scalar. To enforce this, list all $k$ linear forms $z_i$ and all $k-1$ forms $z_i+z_{i+1}$. Call the resulting distinct projective forms $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_{2k-1}$. For each $j$ choose an annotation linear form $\eta_j$ independent of $\ell_j$. Add to the pencil a disjoint diagonal block of $j$ copies of
$$
 \ell_j(z) I_D+\eta_j(z) J_D
$$
where $J_D$ is one nilpotent Jordan block of size $D$. The same formula is used at each endpoint. Call the complete pencil $\widehat B$ and let $S=D\sum_j j$ be the total stub side.

Over an algebraic closure, the main pencil has rank at most $a+b$ for every label: the top block has only $b$ rows and the bottom output is supported on $a$ input coordinates. Off the union of the hyperplanes $H_j=\{\ell_j=0\}$, both second identity coordinates are nonzero. Then $R(z)$ is surjective and $L(z)$ is injective, so the main rank is $a+b$. All stubs are invertible there. On any $H_j$, its $j$ blocks lose at least $j$ ranks, so the full rank is below $S+a+b$. The locus of submaximal rank is therefore exactly the union of these hyperplanes.

Generically on any individual $H_j$, the main rank is still $a+b$. For each vertex, the second identity coordinate is generically nonzero unless it defines that very hyperplane. On its own hyperplane, the first identity coordinate is unrestricted, and the first copy map is that variable times the identity plus fixed linear terms from the color coordinates; its determinant is monic in the first identity coordinate, so it is generically invertible. The analogous statement holds for the other vertex. All other $\ell_i$ and $\eta_j$ are generically nonzero on $H_j$. Thus the generic rank on $H_j$ is exactly $S+a+b-j$. These rank arguments are valid over the infinite algebraic closure even for small $F$, and an $F$-linear pencil isomorphism extends to that closure.

An invertible label map $\widehat s$ of the complete pencil preserves submaximal rank and each hyperplane individually, since the hyperplanes are its irreducible components and their generic ranks are distinct. Their common intersection is $W$. On the quotient by $W$, preserving the coordinate hyperplanes makes the label map diagonal. Preserving each adjacent-sum hyperplane forces consecutive diagonal entries equal. Hence $\widehat s(w,z)=(s w+f(z),\lambda z)$ for some $s,f,\lambda$, with $s$ invertible and $\lambda\ne0$. The determinant on the label role is $\det(s)\lambda^k=\det(s)$.

## 4. Exact recovery of both matrix determinants

First remove the stubs conceptually for a fixed label map $\widehat s$ as above. The main pencil has no nonzero direct summand on which all the $W$-labels vanish. Indeed, that summand's input lies in the common $W$-kernel $V_0^{\oplus D}$, and its output is disjoint from $V_0$ because the other summand carries all $W$-images spanning $V_0$. Each of the $D$ right identity labels maps the summand's input into $V_0$, so must vanish on it. This forces its input to be zero. Its output is then also zero, since the full main pencil has images spanning its entire output. In contrast every stub is killed by $W$. This distinction is preserved by $\widehat s$. Krull–Schmidt therefore gives a fixed-label isomorphism of the complete pencil exactly when it gives one on the main pencil and one on the stubs. One may choose such a reference isomorphism blockwise.

Each Jordan stub with one block is indecomposable: the two independent coefficient functions allow the two fixed arrows $I_D,J_D$, so its endomorphism ring is the local algebra $F[J_D]$. This follows from the cyclic Jordan generator. The residue field is $F$, and both vertex dimensions are multiples of $q-1$. The determinant subgroup formula from Section 2 shows that automorphisms on the sum of stubs have both determinants one, including any repeated isomorphic stubs. There are no common indecomposable types between main and stubs, so the semisimple determinant formula also shows that arbitrary fixed-label automorphisms of the full pencil have the same determinant subgroup as the main automorphisms. It accounts for any off-diagonal Hom maps as well. Under $\widehat s$ every stub is multiplied by the common $\lambda$; the reference identity map on the input and $\lambda I$ on the output has determinants one. Thus the stubs change neither matrix determinant.

In the main pencil any transporter at $\widehat s$ preserves the common $W$-kernel on the input, and the span of $W$-images on the output. Write $p$ for the induced map on $U_0$ and $q_V$ for the induced map on $V_0$. They intertwine the original trimmed data at $s$. The left identity annotations, in the quotient of the output by $V_0$, force the map on $U_0^{\oplus D}$ there to be $D$ copies of $\lambda p$. The right identity annotations, restricted to the common $W$-kernel, force the input map on $V_0^{\oplus D}$ to be $D$ copies of $\lambda^{-1}q_V$. In both equations a shift $f(z)$ affects only the upper left block and hence has no effect. The color annotations in the same quotient and restriction force $p\Pi^U_j=\Pi^{U'}_j p$ and $q_V\Pi^V_j=\Pi^{V'}_j q_V$. By these intrinsic subspaces the matrix maps are block triangular. The $D$ repeated diagonal maps have determinant product one on each side, so the complete main pencil has matrix determinants precisely $\det(p)$ and $\det(q_V)$.

Conversely every color-preserving trimmed witness $(s,p,q_V)$ extends with $f=0,\lambda=1$. On the main input and output use $p\oplus q_V^{\oplus D}$ and $q_V\oplus p^{\oplus D}$; on the stubs use the identity. Its three determinants are exactly those prescribed. This proves exact per-label correspondence, including all determinant triples and ordinary inequivalence.

The main sides have sizes $a+Db$ and $b+Da$. The stubs use $O_q((1+\log c)^2)$ coordinates and $c\le r$. The label role uses $w+O_q(1+\log c)$ coordinates. This gives the linear bound.

## 5. Restore physical determinants and certify the scope

For any full mode, let the recorded coordinate charts back to the physical input be $C_{\rm src},C_{\rm tgt}$, expressed in that physical tensor mode. A transporter in the adapted coordinates has its physical determinant multiplied by $\det(C_{\rm tgt})/\det(C_{\rm src})$. If a recorded chart was in a dualized bilinear input role, use its inverse transpose as the physical chart. Before this calibration, both original and compressed bilinear input-role determinants are inverted by the same translation to physical tensor mode determinants. For each full mode multiply the output target tensor by a diagonal basis transformation with determinant exactly the chart ratio. Left-composing its mode transporter with this matrix multiplies all attainable physical determinants by the required ratio. For each deficient mode append one unused coordinate to both output tensors, as in Section 1. The profile after these operations is literally the original raw input profile. No witness is used in constructing the output.

The result extends the earlier MathProofs ordinary repeated-slice compression by determinant-periodic truncation and the explicit rank-hyperplane encoding of the multiplicity constraints. It uses the module algorithm and algebraic decomposition cited above. The same low-dimensional pencil provides one signed decision instance with all constraints on one shared witness. It makes no worst-case claim when every indecomposable type occurs once. A publication-priority check for this exact determinant-preserving kernel remains pending.
