# Tensor isomorphism on tiny permutation orbits and the hardness of minimizing those actions

Fix an odd prime $p$. Inputs are explicit permutation generators. All reductions are deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions at fixed $p$. This is an informal proof.

**Theorem 1.** There is a polynomial-time recognizable class of class-two groups of exponent dividing $p^2$, given by permutations with all orbits of size at most $p^3$, with the following properties.

1. Abstract group isomorphism in the class is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb F_p$. The construction continues to preserve the exact transporter maps, and therefore all determinant characters, on its three framed characteristic sections.
2. $\mu(G)$, the least degree of any faithful permutation action of $G$, is given by an exact rank-one span formula. In particular the threshold problem $\mu(G)\le M$ is NP-complete on these inputs. The threshold concerns alternative actions of the abstract group and $M$ can be supplied in binary. Approximating $\mu(G)$ from above within any fixed factor $c<2p/(p+1)$ is NP-hard.

The proof builds on the earlier MathProofs encoding of tensor isomorphism by bounded-orbit permutation groups. Its signed transporter property is retained verbatim. The new faithful actions use point stabilizers in the tiny quotient groups, and the exact optimization uses the geometry of the rank-one matrices in the original tensor's output slicing. The usual correspondence between tensor isomorphism and class-two groups supplies context. [Grochow and Qiao, Tensor Isomorphism-Completeness](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

## The encoded group

Let $m:X\times Y\to W$ be a bilinear map with $a=\dim X>0$ and $b=\dim Y>0$. Write $E=X\otimes Y$ and $K=\ker(E\to W)$. Choose a complement $I$ to $K$ in $E$ and a complement $Q$ to the image in $W$. Put $k=\dim K$, $\ell=\dim Q$, and take the additive central group
$$
 C=(\mathbb Z/p^2)^k\oplus I\oplus C_Y\oplus Q,
$$
where $C_Y$ is a copy of $Y$ and the last three summands are elementary abelian $p$-groups. Embed $E$ as $p(\mathbb Z/p^2)^k\oplus I$, using the given identifications so that $K=pC$. The group $G=G_m$ is a central extension of $A=X\oplus Y$ by $C$ whose commutator pairing is
$$
 \beta((x,y),(x',y'))=x\otimes y'-x'\otimes y\quad\text{in }E\subset C .
$$
In coordinates $(v,c)\in A\times C$ use the product
$$
 (v,c)(v',c')=(v+v',c+c'+\tfrac12\beta(v,v')+\operatorname{car}(y,y')),
$$
where $\operatorname{car}$ applies the addition carry in base $p$ to each coordinate of $Y$ and takes values in $C_Y$. The carry is a cocycle. The alternating pairing is bilinear, so its half is also a cocycle. The commutator image is $E$, its common radical is zero, and consequently $Z(G)=C$. The $p$-th powers and commutators generate $E+C_Y$. More precisely, the $p$-th power of $(x,y,c)$ is $y+pc$ in $C_Y+K$. These facts follow by collecting the bilinear and carry terms along a repeated product. The group has class at most two and exponent dividing $p^2$.

For completeness, put $q(v)=g^p\bmod pC$ for any lift $g$ of $v\in A$. This gives characteristic sections
$$
 X=\ker q,\qquad Y=A/X,\qquad W=(C[p]/pC)/q(A),
$$
canonically identified with the corresponding tensor roles once their coordinate frames are fixed. The restriction of the commutator to $X\times A$, projected to $W$, is precisely $m$ on $X\times Y$. The projection is independent of the representative of $Y$ by $\beta(X,X)=0$.

Every group isomorphism preserves this map, and every tensor transporter $(f,g,h)$ lifts. For the latter assertion use $f\oplus g$ on $A$. On $E$, $f\otimes g$ preserves $K$ and gives $h$ on $E/K$. Lift its restriction to $K$ to the long central cyclic generators, act by $f\otimes g$ on the short $I$ inside $E$, use $g$ on $C_Y$, and use any $h$-compatible lifts on the short $Q$ summand. These choices give an invertible map $C\to C'$: the diagonal blocks on the long and short components modulo $p$ are invertible. The constructed maps preserve the commutator and the power map modulo $pC'$. Choose a lift of each image of a basis element of $A$, and multiply it by a central element to correct its actual $p$-th power by the required element of $pC'$. These images preserve all defining relations. Those relations consist of the relations of $C$, centrality, the commutators and the $p$-th powers of the basis lifts. Collection gives at most $|A||C|$ normal forms, and the displayed extension has exactly that many. Thus the images define an isomorphism. This proves exactness on the three characteristic sections for relative pairs and for stabilizers.

### Recognition from the displayed permutations

The intrinsic recognition tests are the following. Check class at most two, exponent dividing $p^2$, that $[G,G]$ and $G/C$ are elementary abelian, and that $pC\subseteq E=[G,G]$. Require $q(A)$ to be disjoint from $E/K$ in $C[p]/K$, with $K=pC$. Require that $X=\ker q$ and $A/X$ have positive dimension, that $X$ is isotropic for the commutator and that an isotropic complement of $X$ exists. Finally require that the induced map $X\otimes(A/X)\to E$ is an isomorphism. Also check the actual orbit bound on the given permutations.

Here is how to carry out the group steps. Check that generator powers $s_i^p$ have order dividing $p$ and that pairwise commutators are central of order dividing $p$. These conditions assure the promised class and exponent by collection, since $p$ is odd. The group generated by the powers and commutators is central elementary abelian and the quotient by it is elementary abelian. Build bases of the subgroup and quotient by successive constructive permutation-group membership. Read coordinates by evaluating straight-line expressions in the chosen bases in these elementary abelian groups. The pairing on the quotient has radical lifting to $C$; lifts of a basis of that radical, together with the central generators already computed, generate $C$. Compute a cyclic decomposition of $C$ by first choosing central generators whose $p$-th powers form a basis of $pC$, then completing their classes to a basis of $C/pC$. Correct the additional lifts by long generators so those lifts have order dividing $p$. This computes all the subspaces and the power map in the tests. Test the existence of an isotropic complement by starting with any section $j:A/X\to A$ and solving for a linear adjustment $t:A/X\to X$. The pairing on the adjusted section is $\beta(j,j)+\beta(t,j)+\beta(j,t)$, since $\beta(X,X)=0$, so the equations are linear. All computations are polynomial in the written permutations and use standard constructive membership. [Seress, Permutation Group Algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511546549)

These tests exactly characterize the constructed abstract family within the stated orbit promise. Use the isotropic complement for the $Y$ section, identify the commutator image with $X\otimes Y$, and choose a central basis with long generators over $pC$, short generators for the remaining part of $E$, short lifts of $q(Y)$, and short generators for the rest of $C[p]$. The disjointness test permits these independent short choices. The commutator and power data are then as in the construction, and the defining relations show that the recovered group lies in the family. The recovered $m$ is computed in polynomial time. In the signed version choose the frames of the characteristic sections from the supplied bases as words in the permutation generators, verifying them with the same calculations. The equivalence of ordinary decision problems follows by applying the two directions to the respective raw inputs. If a tensor data role has dimension zero, its comparison is decidable directly and the reduction uses a fixed answer instance.

## Realizing the group on orbits of size at most $p^3$

Write $S=C[p]$, the order-$p$ socle of the center. It is $E\oplus C_Y\oplus Q$. A collection of subgroup coset actions is faithful exactly when the intersections of their point stabilizers with $S$ have zero intersection. Indeed each such intersection is central and belongs to the kernel of its orbit action. Conversely a nonidentity normal subgroup of a finite $p$-group meets the center nontrivially, by the class equation for conjugation on that normal subgroup. Any nonidentity central $p$-subgroup contains an element of order $p$. This proves the equivalence.

Every coordinate functional $\varphi$ of the tensor-product basis of $E$ has rank one as a bilinear form on $X\times Y$. More generally take any nonzero rank-one $\varphi(x,y)=u(x)v(y)$. If $\varphi$ kills $K$, extend it to $\lambda:C\to\mathbb F_p$ killing $C_Y,Q$, and use the quotient on $\mathbb F_p^2\times\mathbb F_p$ that sends $(x,y,c)$ to $(u(x),v(y),\lambda(c))$. Its central cocycle is the half alternating pairing and the carry vanishes in the quotient. This is a surjection onto a group of order $p^3$ with its center equal to the third coordinate. The subgroup of order $p$ spanned by $(1,0,0)$ intersects the center trivially, so the action on its cosets is faithful by the preceding normal subgroup argument. It has degree $p^2$.

If $\varphi$ does not kill $K$, use $\lambda:C\to\mathbb Z/p^2$ with $\lambda|_E=p\varphi$ and $\lambda$ zero on $C_Y,Q$. To construct it, prescribe an arbitrary lift of $\varphi$ of the $p$-multiple of each long generator, and use $\lambda=p\varphi$ on the short $I$. This gives a quotient on $\mathbb F_p^2\times\mathbb Z/p^2$ of order $p^4$, with the half pairing now multiplied by $p$; its center is again just the third coordinate. The subgroup spanned by $(1,0,0)$ still has order $p$ and intersects the center trivially. Its coset action has degree $p^3$ and is faithful. In both cases the pullback coset action has central socle functional $\varphi$ on $E$ and zero on $C_Y,Q$.

For every coordinate of $C_Y$, the map to $\mathbb Z/p^2$ taking the representative of the corresponding $y$-coordinate plus $p$ times that $C_Y$-coordinate respects addition by the carry rule. Its regular action of degree $p^2$ gives the requested $C_Y$ functional. Every coordinate of $Q$ similarly gives a cyclic action of degree $p$. Using the tensor-product coordinate functionals and these $C_Y,Q$ marks gives a basis of $S^*$, so the resulting disjoint union is faithful. Each chosen quotient has constant order at fixed $p$; the permutations on its cosets are computed by its displayed multiplication rule. The full permutation degree is at most $p^3 ab+p^2 b+p\ell$, so this constructs the promised input explicitly, without enumerating $G$. The old regular action of the $p^4$-element quotient is thereby replaced by one of degree $p^3$ while preserving the entire tensor interface.

## The exact minimum

Let $r$ be the dimension of the span in $E^*$ of all rank-one matrices in $K^\perp$. Orthogonality here is the annihilator under the usual pairing $E^*\times E\to\mathbb F_p$. Then
$$
                 \mu(G)=p\,\ell+p^2(b+r)+p^3(ab-r).
$$

First, every orbit in a minimum-degree faithful action contributes exactly one independent functional on $S$. For a point stabilizer $H$, write $D=H\cap S$. If $\operatorname{codim}_S D=0$, remove that orbit while preserving faithfulness. If its codimension is at least two, choose two nonzero complementary subspaces in $S/D$ and lift them to $S_1,S_2$ with $S_1\cap S_2=D$. Replace $H$ by the two subgroups $HS_1,HS_2$ in two separate orbits. Their intersections with $S$ are $S_1,S_2$. Hence faithfulness is preserved, and each replacement index is at most $[G:H]/p$. Since $p>2$, the cost strictly decreases. In a minimum action all codimensions are therefore one. The corresponding functional lines are independent: any dependent line can be omitted without losing the spanning condition on $S^*$.

Next classify which central socle functionals are detectable at small orbit indices. An index-$p$ subgroup of a finite $p$-group is normal and has cyclic quotient of order $p$. Its mark kills $E=[G,G]$ and $C_Y\subseteq G^p$, so the span of these marks lies in $Q^*$. The coordinate quotient actions above show that it equals $Q^*$.

Now take an index-$p^2$ subgroup $H$ with nontrivial socle mark and put $D_C=C\cap H$. The index $[C:D_C]$ is the orbit size of $C$ on the base point, so it is $p$ or $p^2$. If it is $p^2$, the image of $C$ is a transitive abelian permutation group. It acts regularly, because an element fixing one point then fixes all points. The centralizer of a regular abelian permutation group is that group itself: a commuting permutation is determined by where it sends the base point, and each possible destination is obtained by the regular action. Since the image of $G$ commutes with $C$, it is abelian in this case. Thus the mark kills $E$.

If $[C:D_C]=p$, the quotient $C/D_C$ gives a character killing $pC=K$. Its restriction $\varphi$ to $E$ satisfies $\varphi\beta(v,w)=0$ for any two $v,w$ in the image $HC/C$, since their lifts in $H$ have commutator in $H\cap C$. The index $[G:HC]$ is $p$, so this image is an isotropic hyperplane for the alternating form $\varphi\beta$. An alternating form vanishing on a hyperplane squared has rank at most two, by writing its matrix in a basis of that hyperplane followed by one more vector. In the splitting $A=X\oplus Y$ the rank is twice the matrix rank of $\varphi$. Thus its restriction to $E$ is zero or rank one, and lies in $K^\perp$. This proves that the span of the marks detectable at index at most $p^2$ lies in the direct sum of $Q^*$, $C_Y^*$ and the specified rank-one span. The actions already constructed for the $Q,C_Y$ coordinates and for each rank-one form in $K^\perp$ prove equality.

In an independent basis of marks, at most $\ell$ cost $p$ and at most $\ell+b+r$ cost at most $p^2$. The remaining marks each cost at least $p^3$ because a $p$-group has power-of-$p$ orbit sizes. This gives the displayed lower bound. To attain it, use a basis of each of the cheap spans from the explicit actions. Extend that basis to $S^*$ by tensor-product coordinate functionals of $E^*$, since those span $E^*$; these extensions cost at most $p^3$. This proves equality and realizes the minimum with orbits of size at most $p^3$. The global optimum formula concerns the abstract group and is unchanged by the original chosen faithful action.

In particular, if $K\ne0$, every faithful action has an orbit of size at least $p^3$. All socle marks at smaller indices kill $K$. When $K=0$, the rank-one span has dimension $ab$ and the construction uses only orbits of size at most $p^2$. This is the exact orbit threshold for this encoding family.

## Complexity of minimizing the action

For membership in NP, first recognize the group and recover $m$. The threshold determines how many independent rank-one matrices are needed in $K^\perp$ by the formula. If that number is positive and at most $\dim E$, give the required independent list of matrices as a certificate. Verify rank one, annihilation of $K$, and independence by linear algebra. The remaining threshold cases are immediate from the formula. This verification uses the actual input group and does not rely on a certificate that another permutation presentation describes the same group.

For NP-hardness, the following reduction from Boolean circuit satisfiability gives an explicit matrix space $L\subseteq\operatorname{Mat}_{n+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ with a nonzero rank-one element exactly when the circuit is satisfiable. Use one index for each input bit and gate output, together with an index 0. Require symmetry and the linear conditions
$$
 M_{ii}=M_{i0}\quad(i>0),\qquad
 M_{z0}=M_{xy}\quad(z=x\wedge y),\qquad
 M_{z0}=M_{00}-M_{x0}\quad(z=\neg x),\qquad
 M_{\mathrm{out},0}=M_{00}.
$$
AND and NOT suffice to express the circuit. A nonzero symmetric rank-one matrix over a field has the form $s u u^{\mathsf T}$ with $s\ne0$. The diagonal equations force $u_0\ne0$, since otherwise they would force every $u_i=0$. Normalize $u_i/u_0$. Each normalized value satisfies $x^2=x$ and is therefore zero or one. The remaining linear matrix equations are exactly the gate equations and the accepting-output condition after dividing by $s u_0^2$. Conversely a satisfying bit assignment gives the rank-one matrix $u u^{\mathsf T}$ with $u_0=1$. Compute a basis of $L$ by elimination.

Let $X=Y=\mathbb F_p^{n+1}$. Use a basis of $L$ as the coefficient matrices of the bilinear map $m:X\times Y\to\mathbb F_p^{\dim L}$. Thus $K^\perp=L$ and $Q=0$. In the constructed group define the baseline $M_0=p^2b+p^3ab$. The circuit is satisfiable if and only if
$$
                 \mu(G_m)\le M_0-(p^3-p^2).
$$
The fixed prime, polynomial number of matrix entries, and explicit bounded-orbit representation give a polynomial-time reduction. This proves NP-completeness on a recognizable group family for which ordinary group isomorphism has exactly the TI many-one degree. The reduction shows that the minimum-action problem extracts rank-one information which ordinary isomorphism of the given presentations need not compute.

### Constant-factor approximation hardness

Fix $1\le c<2p/(p+1)$. By approximation from above within $c$ we mean any reported value $v$ satisfying $\mu(G)\le v\le c\mu(G)$, whether or not an action is returned. Adjoin $m$ free Boolean coordinates to the circuit variables, with their diagonal equations $M_{ii}=M_{i0}$ but with no gate constraints involving them. If the circuit is unsatisfiable then $r=0$ as before.

If the circuit is satisfiable, fix one satisfying assignment of its original variables. Vary the new free coordinates over $\{0,1\}^m$. Project the resulting rank-one matrices onto the principal submatrix indexed by 0 and the new coordinates. In that projection the rank-one spans contain the matrix supported at $(0,0)$ by evaluating at the zero vector. Subtracting that matrix from evaluations at each coordinate vector supplies $m$ independent matrices each with its own diagonal and corresponding $0,i$ entries. For each distinct pair $i,j$, the evaluations at $e_i+e_j,e_i,e_j,0$ with signs $+,-,-,+$ supply precisely the symmetric matrix supported on $(i,j)$ and $(j,i)$. These independently supported matrices show
$$
              r\ge 1+m+\binom{m}{2}\ge m^2/2.
$$
Let $n$ be the number of original circuit variables including all its gate outputs. Set $m=M_\ast(n+1)$ for a fixed positive integer $M_\ast$, and put $N=n+m+1$. The no-case degree is $B=p^3N^2+p^2N$. In the yes case the formula gives
$$
\frac{\mu(G)}{B}
\le 1-\frac{(1-1/p)m^2}{2N^2(1+1/(pN))}
\le 1-\frac{(1-1/p)M_\ast^2}{2(M_\ast+1)^2(1+1/(p(M_\ast+1)))} .
$$
The final bound tends to $(p+1)/(2p)<1/c$ as $M_\ast$ increases. Choose $M_\ast$ making it strictly less than $1/c$. Thus any $c$-approximation from above is strictly below $B$ in the yes case and at least $B$ in the no case. The factor $M_\ast$ is fixed, so this is still a polynomial-time reduction from circuit satisfiability. In particular over $\mathbb F_3$ every fixed approximation factor below $3/2$ is NP-hard.

Minimal faithful actions of finite $p$-groups have long been studied via their central socle. The splitting argument here supplies the relevant independent-mark statement directly. The family-specific calculation is the identification of the optimal central marks with the rank-one span in the given tensor slice space. Publication priority for this calculation and the complexity statement is unverified. [Elias, Silberman and Takloo-Bighash, Finding minimal permutation representations of finite groups](https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~rtakloo/papers/group-paper/delta.9.pdf)

## The exact optimal orbit cap for TI completeness

**Theorem 2.** Fix an odd prime $p$. Abstract isomorphism of class-at-most-two groups of exponent dividing $p^2$ given by permutation generators with all orbits of size at most $p^2$ is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb F_p$. The hard instances have elementary abelian centers equal to their derived groups. They admit a polynomial-time recognizer from the raw generators, and in the frames corresponding to concise tensors the construction preserves all three induced section maps of every transporter. Any smaller orbit bound forces the represented $p$-groups to be abelian. Thus $p^2$ is the first possible orbit cap permitting nonabelian hardness within this class. Theorem 1's sharp $p^3$ bound concerned a different encoded group family.

### Encode a matrix space using a power map

Support compaction reduces ordinary three-tensor isomorphism to concise tensors, preserving ordinary equivalence and the order of the modes. Pairs with different support dimensions and zero cases can be decided directly. Let a nonzero concise tensor be written as an injective slice map $w\mapsto M_w$ from its label space $W$ into $\operatorname{Hom}(R,L)$, with image $D$. Thus the images of its slices span $L$ and their common kernel in $R$ is zero. All three roles have positive dimension. Write $U=L\oplus R$ and define a matrix space
$$
 P=\mathbb F_p B_0\oplus N_D\subseteq\operatorname{End}(U),\qquad
 B_0=\begin{pmatrix}I_L&0\\0&-I_R\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 N_M=\begin{pmatrix}0&M\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad(M\in D).
$$
Here $N_D=\{N_M:M\in D\}$. Let $Y=P^*$, $A=U\oplus Y$, and let $E=U\otimes Y$ be elementary abelian and central. Use the universal cross commutator
$$
 \beta((u,y),(u',y'))=u\otimes y'-u'\otimes y .
$$
The linear power map $q:A\to E$ is zero on $Y$. On $U$, define $q$ by the condition $(1\otimes v)q(u)=M_vu$ for every $v\in Y^*=P$, where $M_v$ is the corresponding matrix in $P$. This specifies a unique tensor in $U\otimes Y$ for every $u$. Use the central extension built as above with $A,E,\beta$ and with a carry term given by applying $q$ to the coordinatewise base-$p$ carries of the two input tangent vectors in a chosen basis of $A$. The $p$-th power of a lift of $a\in A$ is exactly $q(a)$, and the commutator pairing is $\beta$. The cross pairing has zero radical and spans $E$, giving $Z(G)=[G,G]=E$. The class and exponent conditions follow.

Because $B_0$ is invertible, $\ker q=Y$, a characteristic subspace of $A=G/E$. Write $U=A/Y$ intrinsically. The commutator induces the canonical isomorphism $U\otimes Y\to E$, since $Y$ is isotropic, and $q$ itself factors through $U$. Thus the contractions of $q$ by $Y^*$ recover the same matrix space $P$. A group isomorphism induces invertible $f:U\to U'$, $g:Y\to Y'$, and $f\otimes g:E\to E'$. Preservation of $q$ is precisely
$$
          M'_{v'} f=f M_{g^*v'}\qquad(v'\in (Y')^*).
$$
In particular $f$ conjugates $P$ to $P'$.

The nilpotent elements of $P$ are precisely $N_D$. Indeed $N_M^2=0$, while $(tB_0+N_M)^2=t^2I$ for every $t\in\mathbb F_p$, using anticommutation. The subspace $L$ is recovered as the span of the images of $N_D$. Every conjugacy of $P$ therefore gives maps $f_L:L\to L'$, $f_R:U/L\to U'/L'$, and carries $M\in D$ to $f_L M f_R^{-1}$. The nilpotent spaces $N_D,N_{D'}$ have the label frames of the original tensors. Taking the dual where appropriate translates this into the exact original physical tensor convention.

Conversely every matrix-space equivalence $f_L,f_R$, with its induced label map, extends by $f=f_L\oplus f_R$. This conjugates $B_0$ to $B'_0$ and acts on $N_D$ by the indicated formula. Select $g$ to be the inverse dual of this conjugacy on $P$, so the displayed equation holds. The block map $f\oplus g$ on $A$ and $f\otimes g$ on $E$ preserve all commutators and powers. The corresponding basis lifts define a group isomorphism by the same normal-form argument as above. Hence the attained maps in the three sections $L,U/L,N_D$ are exactly the original tensor transporters in their indicated frames. In particular constraints on their determinants can be read on the same group isomorphism.

### Faithful permutation generators with all orbits of size at most $p^2$

The space $P$ has a basis consisting of $B_0$ and the matrices $B_0+N_i$ for any basis $N_i$ of $N_D$. Every basis matrix squares to the identity. Since $p$ is odd, each of these matrices admits an eigenbasis over $\mathbb F_p$, also on the dual of $U$. For each such basis label $v\in P=Y^*$, take a dual eigenbasis $u_1^*,\ldots,u_{\dim U}^*$, with $u_i^*M_v=\epsilon_i u_i^*$, $\epsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}$. The collection of central functionals $u_i^*\otimes v$, over all these choices, is a basis of $E^*$.

Fix one such functional $\lambda=u_i^*\otimes v$. The quotient candidate has two elementary tangent coordinates $(x,y)\in\mathbb F_p^2$ and one elementary central coordinate. Give it commutator $xy'-x'y$ and power $\epsilon_i x$. This defines a group of order $p^3$ by the half-pairing and carry construction. Its center is the central coordinate. The maps $A\to\mathbb F_p^2$, $(u,y)\mapsto(u_i^*(u),v(y))$, and $E\to\mathbb F_p$, by $\lambda$, preserve the commutator and powers, because
$$
          \lambda(q(u))=u_i^*(M_vu)=\epsilon_i u_i^*(u).
$$
Send each standard generator lift to the lift of its displayed image, and each central generator according to $\lambda$. The normal-form presentation gives a homomorphism with these formulas on generators, regardless of the two chosen carry charts. It is surjective since its images span the tangent quotient and reach the entire center. The second tangent basis vector has a lift of order $p$. Its cyclic subgroup avoids the center, so the coset action is faithful on the quotient and has degree $p^2$. Restrictions to the center of the kernels of these pulled-back actions are exactly the kernels of the respective $\lambda$. Their basis of central characters therefore gives a faithful union of the actions by the socle criterion.

At fixed $p$, compute each tiny quotient by its constant-size table and print the action of each generator on the cosets. Construct the eigenbases by Gaussian elimination. The combined degree is $p^2\dim U\dim Y$, and the group order need never be tabulated. A nonabelian group of $p$-power order has orbit sizes that are powers of $p$; if all orbits have size smaller than $p^2$, each is singleton or of size $p$. A transitive $p$-subgroup of $\operatorname{Sym}(p)$ is cyclic of order $p$, because $p!$ has $p$-valuation one. Any subgroup of a product of those orbit images is abelian. This proves the claimed sharp change in the possible group structures.

### Recognize the hard family

Using the group steps of Theorem 1, compute $C=Z(G)$, $E=[G,G]$, $A=G/C$, the power map and the commutator. Require $C=E$ elementary, $A$ elementary, $Y=\ker q$ isotropic with an isotropic complement, and $(A/Y)\otimes Y\to E$ an isomorphism through the commutator. Require positive dimensions on both tangent roles. The complement check is the same linear adjustment as in Theorem 1. Set $U=A/Y$. Recover $P$ by contracting $q$ and check that $Y^*\to P$ is injective.

For the matrix-space shape check, choose an invertible matrix from the computed basis of $P$, or reject if there is none. In the target family some basis matrix is outside the nilpotent hyperplane and hence invertible. Require that the chosen matrix square to a nonzero scalar square times the identity, normalize it to square to the identity, and try both signs for the resulting $B$. On the target family $B=\pm B_0+N$ for some $N\in N_D$; its eigenspaces are a valid split basis of $U$, with the recovered $L$ as one of the eigenspaces. For each sign check that $BM+MB$ is scalar for every $M\in P$. Then its zero locus $N$ is a hyperplane since $BB+BB=2I$. Require $N$ to take the negative eigenspace into the positive eigenspace and vanish on the positive eigenspace. Test that its images span the positive eigenspace and its common kernel is the positive eigenspace. Both eigenspaces must have positive dimension. These checks are linear in the chosen bases, recover the concise matrix slice space, and exactly characterize the template among the groups passing the preceding conditions. The group is reconstructed from its commutator and power data by the presentation argument.

### Upper bound for the full bounded-orbit class

The following upper bound applies to all class-at-most-two groups of exponent dividing $p^2$, without the displayed orbit restriction. Since $p$ is odd, the associated additive Lie ring uses $x+y=xy[y,x]^{1/2}$, with $1/2$ represented by an integer modulo $p^2$, and its bracket is the group commutator. The class-two commutator identities give an abelian sum and a biadditive bracket, and recover the group product as $x+y+\tfrac12[x,y]$. Thus abstract group isomorphisms are exactly Lie-ring isomorphisms. The Lie ring can be recovered in cyclic coordinates in polynomial time from the permutation generators. Obtain the central derived subgroup by the generator commutators. For that central abelian group, adjoin generators using constructive subgroup membership, testing relative order $p$ or $p^2$ and collecting exponent-sum relations. Smith normal form supplies cyclic coordinates. The map $x\mapsto([x,s_i])_i$ into a power of the derived subgroup has kernel the center, and its image and kernel are obtained by abelian linear algebra in the computed cyclic coordinates. Central lifts and derived generators generate the center. Compute cyclic coordinates of the center similarly, then adjoin lifts of the quotient by the center with their central power relations in the additive Lie ring. The bracket table on these lifts is given by their group commutators. These operations use polynomially many words represented as straight-line expressions and have polynomial bit complexity.

For a finite additive group $M$ of exponent dividing $p^2$, let $I$ be the augmentation ideal in $\mathbb F_p[M]$. Mark the multiplication, unit and augmentation on the truncated group algebra $B=\mathbb F_p[M]/I^{p+1}$. Also mark the diagonal and the bilinear tensor-product map into the total-degree truncation $B'=(B\otimes B)/\sum_{i+j\ge p+1} I^i\otimes I^j$. In a cyclic basis the variables $z_i=[e_i]-1$ give monomials of total degree at most $p$, with $z_i^p=0$ for factors of order $p$. The dimension is polynomial for fixed $p$. The actual group elements remain distinct, since their first nonzero base-$p$ digits give a nonzero linear term in degree $1$, or a nonzero pure term in degree $p$ if all first digits vanish. They are precisely the augmentation-one elements satisfying the group-like equation given by the two marks. For the reverse, the first nonconstant homogeneous term in such an element must be primitive for $z_i\mapsto z_i\otimes 1+1\otimes z_i$. In degrees at most $p$ the only primitive possibilities are linear terms or pure $p$-powers. A mixed monomial has a nonzero split of its exponents giving a mixed tensor term unique to that monomial. A pure power of degree between $2$ and $p-1$ has nonzero first split coefficient. Multiplication by the inverse of a true group element removes the first nonconstant term, preserving the group-like equation, and induction proves the assertion. Mark the bilinear extension of the bracket by sending $([x],[y])$ to the group-algebra basis element indexed by $[x,y]$. In either argument for the other fixed to a group basis element, this extends an additive homomorphism, so the bilinear map descends through the augmentation truncation in both arguments. Its table on monomials is computable by bounded-degree expansion in the cyclic coordinates. Marked-structure isomorphisms now restrict exactly to additive group isomorphisms preserving the bracket. Conversely each Lie-ring isomorphism extends by functoriality.

All marks are tensors of order at most three with a fixed total number of roles. The tensor-system compiler reduces this comparison to one ordinary three-tensor comparison over $\mathbb F_p$. This proves the reverse reduction on the full exponent-bounded class, hence on all of the stated raw bounded-orbit inputs. The truncation is the fixed-prime-power reverse reduction used in the previous MathProofs group encoding. ## The polynomial-to-NP-complete transition for minimizing permutation degree

**Theorem 3.** Fix an odd prime $p$. For class-at-most-two groups of exponent dividing $p^2$ presented by permutations with every orbit of size at most $p^2$, a minimum faithful permutation action can be computed in polynomial time. At the next larger $p$-power orbit bound, $p^3$, the degree threshold problem is NP-complete by Theorem 1. Thus the same input orbit bound that first permits tensor-complete abstract isomorphism still permits efficient minimization.

Let $S=Z(G)[p]$, let $\Phi=G^p[G,G]$ be the Frattini subgroup of the finite $p$-group $G$, and put
$$
         s=\dim_{\mathbb F_p} S,\qquad
         a=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\bigl(S/(S\cap\Phi)\bigr).
$$
Then the exact minimum is $\mu(G)=pa+p^2(s-a)$. The independent central-mark proof from Theorem 1 applies to any finite $p$-group at odd $p$. Index-$p$ subgroups are kernels of nonzero linear functionals on the elementary abelian quotient $G/\Phi$, so their marks on $S$ span exactly the annihilator of $S\cap\Phi$, of dimension $a$.

For the next level use the given action itself. For each input orbit, let $H$ be its point stabilizer and $D=H\cap S$. The annihilators $D^\perp$ together span $S^*$ by faithfulness and the central socle criterion. For a nonzero $\lambda\in D^\perp$, the subgroup $H\ker\lambda$ has index at most $[G:H]\le p^2$ and intersects $S$ precisely in $\ker\lambda$. Thus all central directions can be realized at cost at most $p^2$, and at most $a$ independent ones have cost $p$. This proves the formula, taking the optimal action of the trivial group to be the empty action, with the usual optional degree-one convention for that group.

The argument constructs the minimum. Compute $S$ and the images of a basis of $S$ in $G/\Phi$ using constructive group membership and abelian coordinates. In this class the center and its cyclic decomposition were constructed in the proof of the upper bound in Theorem 2. Generate $\Phi$ using the derived generators and the $p$-th powers of the input generators, because after quotienting by the derived subgroup taking powers is a homomorphism. Coordinates in $G/\Phi$ are computed by successively adjoining independent generators and collecting exponents of constructive membership expressions. Find a basis of the cheap marks on $S$, and extend each to a character on $G/\Phi$ by linear algebra. Their actions on $\mathbb F_p$ are given by the resulting translation generators. For each input orbit compute $H$ by standard permutation-group stabilizer algorithms and compute $D$ as the kernel of the action of $S$ on that orbit. This kernel is computable by taking the image of each $S$ generator and finding dependencies in the elementary abelian image using constructive membership. Select a basis from the resulting lists for the $D^\perp$'s, extending the already chosen cheap basis. The corresponding groups $H\ker\lambda$ are given by their generators. Each small coset action is computed by enumerating cosets using group membership. There are at most $s$ chosen characters, and all operations and the total printed size are polynomial in the input length.

The hard family from Theorem 2 has $S=Z(G)=[G,G]\subseteq\Phi$, so its minimum is $p^2\dim E$. The printed action has exactly this degree. Thus the ordinary TI-completeness also holds when the input permutation actions are themselves of minimum faithful degree and have every orbit of size at most $p^2$. This input condition is polynomial-time checkable using the formula.

Kantor and Luks asked in §13, Problem 1 whether a quotient of a permutation group embeds in $\operatorname{Sym}(m)$, specifically highlighting the nilpotent case. Theorem 1 establishes NP-hardness for that nilpotent-quotient problem as stated by taking the normal subgroup to be trivial. In conjunction with the known NP upper bound for the quotient problem, this gives NP-completeness, even on the class-two exponent-$p^2$ lower-bound instances. The input here consists of permutation generators. The construction does not give the same bound when given full Cayley tables, whose length can be exponential in the length of these permutation presentations.
