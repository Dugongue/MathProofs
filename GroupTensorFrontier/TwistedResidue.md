# Polynomial-time signed isomorphism for local cube-zero multiplication with a one-dimensional radical square

**Theorem.** Over $\mathbb F_3$, consider raw ordered three-tensors isotopic to multiplication in finite-dimensional unital local associative algebras $R$ with $J^3=0$, where $J=\operatorname{rad}(R)$. Write $K=R/J$. If $\dim_K({}_KJ^2)=1$, the entire attainable three-bit determinant profile of a relative tensor transporter is computable in deterministic polynomial time in the written input length. Witnesses for the attained profiles can also be found in polynomial time. Both inputs can have independent raw frames. There is no bound on the residue degree, no nonsingularity assumption on the tangent product, and the left and right residue actions may differ arbitrarily. In the independent gate $A\land F$, where $A$ is one-sign tensor isomorphism and $F$ is an odd-automorphism test, this gives a single $A$ comparison when either conjunct belongs to the indicated family. The proof is informal.

## 1. Recover the raw algebra and its twist data

For a raw candidate with equal positive side dimensions $d$, write its slices as $M(u):X\to Y$. Require $u\mapsto M(u)$ to be injective. Compute the fixed-label intertwining algebra $E=\{(P,Q): QM(u)=M(u)P\text{ for every }u\}$. Require $\dim E=d$ and compute regular $E$-module charts on $X,Y$ using constructive module isomorphism. In the left regular charts each slice is right multiplication by its value at the unit, and these values give an invertible chart on the first mode. This is multiplication in $E^{\mathrm{op}}$. Conversely a unital algebra multiplication tensor has just this intertwining algebra because the identity slice identifies $P,Q$, and commuting with all left multiplications forces right multiplication. Thus the test recovers all three raw charts. Compute $J$, test the field-quotient and cube-zero conditions, and check the claimed dimension of $J^2$. [Brooksbank and Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf) · [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

Choose a section $K\hookrightarrow R$. With a generator of the residue field in its polynomial presentation, lift the generator to $x\in R$ and let $P$ be its irreducible polynomial. Correct $x$ twice by $x\mapsto x-P(x)P'(x)^{-1}$. The commutative subalgebra generated by $x$ contains the inverse, because the invertible element $P'(x)$ has finite order. The errors square at each iteration and $J^3=0$. Thus the corrected element is a root and determines the section. For a polynomial-time coordinate version of choosing a field generator, one may use a generator from the finite-field structure algorithm. Choose a $K$-bimodule complement to $W=J^2$ in $J$ by solving for a projection onto $W$ commuting with both field actions. Such a projection exists because $K\otimes_{\mathbb F_3}K$ splits as a product of fields. In the resulting charts
$$
 R=K\oplus V\oplus W,\qquad V=J/J^2.
$$
The product on the radical is determined by $b : V\times V\to W$. The two field actions and the field multiplication give all other entries of the algebra product.

An algebra isomorphism can be taken to align the chosen field sections by conjugating by $1+J$, by Wedderburn–Malcev. This conjugation has determinant one, since it is identity on every radical grade. The remaining compatibility equations are precisely that the field isomorphism $\sigma$ and the induced actions $g,h$ on $V,W$ intertwine the two field actions and satisfy $b'(gx,gy)=h b(x,y)$. Every such triple extends by the block map in the chosen splitting. Any shear on these filtration grades has determinant one. Two different residue degrees reject, and all $e=[K : \mathbb F_3]$ candidate $\sigma$ can be enumerated using one field isomorphism and its Frobenius translates. [Wedderburn–Malcev in Lam, A First Course in Noncommutative Rings](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4419-8616-0) · [Lenstra, Finding isomorphisms between finite fields](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

For a fixed $\sigma$, transport the source coefficients to the target field $K$. Put $\tau_i(s)=s^{3^i}$, with $i$ modulo $e$. The intrinsic $K$-bimodule decomposition $V=\bigoplus_i V_i$ is given by $xs=\tau_i(s)x$ on $V_i$. The corresponding summands are kernels for the right action of one field generator minus its twisted left action. Since $W$ has left $K$-dimension one, its right action is a single twist $\tau_k$. The dimensions of all types have to agree at the two endpoints. Fix a left $K$-basis on each $V_i$, and identify $W$ with $K$ by a nonzero coordinate vector. After $\sigma$ has been incorporated, the map on $W$ is multiplication by a common $c\in K^\times$.

Associativity with the field actions gives $b(x,sy)=b(xs,y)=\tau_i(s)b(x,y)$ on $V_i\times V_j$. Also $b(x,ys)=b(x,y)s$, requiring $\tau_i\tau_j=\tau_k$ on every nonzero type pair. Write $\bar i=k-i$ modulo $e$. Therefore the only blocks are
$$
 b(x_i,y_{\bar i})=x_i^{\mathsf T}B_i\tau_i(y_{\bar i}).
$$
The involution $i\mapsto\bar i$ divides the tangent space into one- or two-type summands. Their maps are independent for fixed $c$, and share only that scalar. Write $B_i,C_i$ for the target and source matrices after the field identification. The equations on the summand are $G_i^{\mathsf T}B_i\tau_i(G_{\bar i})=c C_i$. This field-section and twisted-matrix description has a structural antecedent in [Chikunji, Enumeration of Finite Rings with Jacobson Radical of Cube Zero, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9905030).

## 2. The fixed-multiplier subroutine

For prescribed $c$, a deterministic polynomial-time test finds a compatible congruence, or reports failure, and gives the whole set of determinants on $V$. This holds for a single type orbit and for the complete list of types. Here are the algorithm and the sign calculation.

Over $\mathbb F_3$, use the coordinate components of the bilinear maps, together with their transposes, as a tuple of source and target forms. Here the source tuple already includes multiplication by $c$. Constrain the unknown maps to commute with the paired fixed field action operators. Constructive module isomorphism on $V^*\oplus V$ gives a left-right comparison $P^{\mathsf T} B_\nu Q=C_\nu$ for the entire transpose-closed tuple. The arrows $V\to V^*$ are the form matrices, and unary marks are the two projections, the field action operators on $V$, and their transposes on $V^*$. A module isomorphism $\operatorname{diag}(P^{-\mathsf T},Q)$ has the claimed property. Both $P,Q$ commute with the required field actions between the charts. If none exists the fixed-$c$ test fails. Write $D=QP^{-1}$; transpose closure gives $D^{\mathsf T}B_\nu=B_\nu D$.

Use the algebra of pairs $(z,a)$ satisfying $a^{\mathsf T}B_\nu=B_\nu z$ and commuting with the target field action operators in both entries. Its product is $(z,a)(w,b)=(zw,ba)$ and its involution exchanges the entries. Transpose closure makes the involution well defined. The matrix representation $\operatorname{diag}(z,a^{\mathsf T})$ is faithful even for singular tuples. The element $\Delta=(D,D)$ is invertible and self-adjoint. A congruence $g$ is equivalent to a unit solution of $x^*x=\Delta$: take $z=gP^{-1}, a=Dz^{-1}$ in one direction, and $g=zP$ in the other. The identities follow by multiplying $z^{\mathsf T}B_\nu z=B_\nu D$ by $z^{-\mathsf T}$ and using $D^{\mathsf T}B_\nu=B_\nu D$.

Solve the norm equation in the semisimple quotient, separately on exchange, orthogonal, symplectic and unitary factors, by finite-field algebra and form algorithms. A quotient solution lifts: if $u$ is an arbitrary lift and $v=(u^*)^{-1}\Delta u^{-1}$, then $v=v^*$ lies in $1+\operatorname{rad}(E)$ and has odd order dividing a computable $3^j$. Raise $v$ to an inverse of two modulo $3^j$ to obtain a self-adjoint square root $y$. Then $(yu)^*(yu)=\Delta$. In particular the unitary group of the quotient is the full image of the unitary group upstairs. The kernel is unipotent on $V$, so has determinant one. On the first representation the determinant of a simple factor $M_m(F)$ on all radical grades is $\operatorname{Norm}_{F/\mathbb F_3}(\det_F z)^a$, where $a$ is the multiplicity of its natural module summed over the grades. Compute these multiplicities by linear algebra from the projections to simple factors. On an exchanged pair the two norm determinants are inverse, so odd is possible precisely when the two multiplicities have odd sum. On a fixed orthogonal factor odd is possible precisely when $a[F : \mathbb F_3]$ is odd. The symplectic and unitary factors contribute only positive signs. This gives the full sign image, by multiplying the sign of one witness by the computed isometry sign subgroup. A witness for each feasible sign can be obtained by using an appropriate reflection or exchange-factor element in the quotient, followed by the stated lifting. [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18, Proposition 23 and the finite-field forms algorithms](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

## 3. Regularization preserving Frobenius types

For one orbit $V_i\oplus V_{\bar i}$ or $V_i$ use the left radical $L=\{x:b(x,V)=0\}$ and the left annihilator $U=\{x:b(x,L)=0\}$ of $L$. Both are graded by the types, and $L\subseteq U$. The restriction to $U$ vanishes on $L$ in both arguments, so it induces the same kind of graded form on $U/L$. Any twisted congruence with common scalar multiplier induces a congruence with the same multiplier on that quotient. Repeat while the current form is singular, with the zero-dimensional form as a permitted endpoint. Each singular step strictly decreases dimension. The endpoint is an intrinsic nonsingular subquotient. This allows a necessary condition for a multiplier to be read on the endpoint.

We also need the case in which the endpoint is zero. Here is a constructive refinement valid for twists of any order. It shows that the original form can be put, by a type-preserving change of basis, into a direct sum of the endpoint form and directed paths. A path has basis $v_1,\ldots,v_\ell$, with $b(v_s,v_{s+1})=1$ for $1\le s<\ell$ and all other entries zero. The types along a path alternate between $i,\bar i$, allowing equality of types.

Choose graded complements $H$ of $L$ in $U$ and $P$ of $U$ in $V$. In order $H,P,L$, the matrix is of the form
$$
 \begin{pmatrix} A & B & 0 \\ C & D & E \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.
$$
On every relevant ordered pair of types the block $E$, from rows $P_i$ to columns $L_{\bar i}$, has full row rank by definition of $U$. Choose bases on the $L$ types to put each such block in the form $[I\ 0]$. The field automorphism on those columns is bijective, so any desired invertible column multiplier is available. By adding appropriate $L$-coordinates to the $H,P$-coordinates of basis vectors, clear $C,D$. The companion row operations add only zero rows, and the $H$ rows are unchanged since their $L$ columns vanish. The $H$ rows of $[A\ B]$ are independent, since any dependence would give a vector in $H\cap L$.

Inductively put $A$ into nonsingular-plus-path form by a basis change on $H$ alone. All of its nonzero rows of a given type are independent. Use their columns in $H$ to clear the entries of $B$ on those rows by a change adding $H$-coordinates to $P$-basis vectors of the matching type. This is a paired row and column shear. Its additional effect is confined to the $P$ rows in the $H,P$ columns. Clear that effect again using $E$ and the zero $L$-rows. The entries of $B$ on the zero rows of $A$ were untouched, and remain independent in each relevant type by independence of $[A\ B]$. These zero rows are the terminal vertices of the old paths. A basis change in each $P$ type now puts their blocks of $B$ in the form $[I\ 0]$. Restore the corresponding $[I\ 0]$ blocks of $E$ by rechoosing bases in the $L$ types. Each $P$ and $L$ type occurs in only one such column block, so the normalizations are simultaneous. The remaining entries extend each old path by two vertices, give two-vertex paths for the remaining $P$ vertices, and give isolated vertices for the remaining $L$ coordinates. The nonsingular endpoint is unaffected. This inductive construction handles zero-sized blocks and uses only polynomially many field operations. Its form is a twisted, type-preserving version of regularization for bilinear and involutory sesquilinear forms. [Horn and Sergeichuk, A regularization algorithm for matrices of bilinear and sesquilinear forms](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.0852)

If the intrinsic endpoint is zero, every nonzero scalar $c$ is a multiplier of a type-preserving similitude of the form. On a path use nonzero diagonal coordinates $t_s$, beginning with $t_1=1$, and set $t_{s+1}=\tau_{\operatorname{type}(v_s)}^{-1}(c/t_s)$. This makes every edge acquire multiplier $c$. Thus a comparison of two zero-endpoint forms either admits all multipliers or admits none. One test at $c=1$ decides which. If the endpoints have different type dimensions no multiplier is possible.

## 4. Compute the entire multiplier coset of each type orbit

For a type orbit the feasible $c$'s are either empty or a coset of the target form's scalar similitude subgroup $H_i\le K^\times$. This follows by composing one relative witness and the self similitudes. We compute one representative and the exact order of $H_i$.

For a self-paired type of odd twist order, the map $t\mapsto t\tau_i(t)$ has image the squares. Indeed if $i$ denotes a Frobenius exponent, the greatest common divisor of $3^e-1$ and $1+3^i$ is two. Any common divisor makes $3^i$ both an element of odd order and equal to minus one. Scaling all tangent coordinates of this orbit by $t$ changes the multiplier by $t\tau_i(t)$. Test $1$ and a nonsquare, and count how many classes succeed. This works with singular forms as well. At fixed characteristic a nonsquare is deterministic polynomial time. [Ivanyos and Qiao, finite-field forms algorithms](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

For all remaining orbits consider the nonsingular endpoint, assuming it is nonzero. In a two-type orbit it has equal dimensions on the paired types, since each block must separately be invertible. Put $a=\tau_i$, $b=\tau_{\bar i}$, and $\rho=ab$. In a self type put $a=b=\tau_i$. Write $B,B'$ for the endpoint matrices of the target ordered blocks starting at $i,\bar i$, and $C,C'$ for the source. In a self type $B=B'$ and $C=C'$. With $G=G_i$, $Q=G_{\bar i}$, the equations are $G^{\mathsf T}B\,a(Q)=c C$ and $Q^{\mathsf T}B'b(G)=c C'$. Transpose the first, apply $a$ to the second, and cancel their common left factor. This gives
$$
 G^{-1} A_B\rho(G)=\frac{a(c)}{c} A_C,\qquad
 A_B=(B^{\mathsf T})^{-1}a(B'),\quad A_C=(C^{\mathsf T})^{-1}a(C').
$$
Let $r=\operatorname{ord}(\rho)$, $K_0=K^\rho$, and $N_\rho=\operatorname{Norm}_{K/K_0}$. Iterating the semilinear identity gives
$$
 G^{-1}M_B G=\eta(c)M_C,\qquad
 M_B=A_B\rho(A_B)\cdots\rho^{r-1}(A_B),\quad
 \eta(c)=a(N_\rho(c))/N_\rho(c),
$$
with $M_C$ defined in the same way.

If $s$ is the side of $M_B$, all admissible $\eta(c)$ are among the at most $s^2$ roots of
$$
 P(t)=\operatorname{Res}_X(\chi_{M_C}(X),\chi_{M_B}(tX)).
$$
Indeed both matrices are invertible. Over an algebraic closure this resultant is the product of $\chi_{M_B}(t\lambda)$ over the eigenvalues $\lambda\ne0$ of $M_C$, with multiplicities. The product is nonzero, and the displayed conjugacy forces vanishing there. List its $K$-roots by taking the gcd with $t^{|K|}-t$ using modular exponentiation, then using the three values of the trace pairing against each element of a field basis to split the squarefree product into linear factors. Nondegeneracy of that pairing proves completeness of the splits. Degrees and residue-bit lengths are polynomial. For each nonzero $\eta$ solve $a(u)=\eta u$ by $\mathbb F_3$-linear algebra for nonzero $u\in K_0$, and find one $c$ with $N_\rho(c)=u$. This last step uses unconditional deterministic norm inversion over finite fields. [Van de Woestijne, Deterministic equation solving over finite fields, Theorem 7.1](https://math.leidenuniv.nl/scripties/Woestijne.pdf)

It remains to show that one choice of $c$ for each possible $\eta$ suffices on the full unreduced orbit. The following gauges are simultaneous across both blocks and include their singular parts.

- **Two different types.** Every gauge $\delta\in\ker(\eta)$ can be supplied by multiplying the maps on $V_i,V_{\bar i}$ by $t,s$, respectively. The desired equations are $t a(s)=s b(t)=\delta$. Put $s=\delta/b(t)$. The remaining equation becomes $\rho(t)/t=a(\delta)/\delta$, solvable by cyclic Hilbert 90 exactly when the norm of the right side is one. That condition is $\eta(\delta)=1$. Conversely every gauge of this uniform form must satisfy the same equation.
- **One self-paired type of even order.** Now the gauge subgroup is the image of $t\mapsto t a(t)$. It is contained in $\ker(\eta)$ since $a^2=\rho$. The kernel of the gauge map lies in $K_0$ and is the quadratic norm-one subgroup of $K_0/K^a$, of order $|K^a|+1$. The quotient map $\eta$ has image of the same order, because $N_\rho$ is onto and $a$ on $K_0$ has order two. Therefore the gauge subgroup is exactly $\ker(\eta)$.

For each represented $\eta$, apply the full fixed-multiplier isomorphism test to the original orbit. Feasibility is constant on each coset of the gauge subgroup and every feasible gauge class appears in the resultant list. Thus the exact size of the feasible coset is the number of successful distinct $\eta$'s times
$$
 |\ker(\eta)|=\frac{(|K|-1)(|\operatorname{Fix}_{K_0}(a)|-1)}{|K_0|-1}.
$$
The fixed-field sizes are computable by linear algebra. The formula follows from surjectivity of $N_\rho$ and the fixed-field description of the kernel of $u\mapsto a(u)/u$. For the zero endpoint case the size is $|K|-1$ if the fixed-multiplier test at one succeeds. For the odd-order self-type gauge the successful class count is multiplied by $(|K|-1)/2$. There is no need to compute a primitive root or a discrete logarithm. The one-type nonsingular monodromy calculation extends an earlier MathProofs research note. The grading, regularization and coset assembly here remove the single-type and nonsingularity requirements.

## 5. Merge the cosets before evaluating determinants

Intersect all feasible cosets over the orbit list. Suppose two cosets have representatives $c_1,c_2$ and subgroup orders $m_1,m_2$. Set $\ell=\operatorname{lcm}(m_1,m_2)$ and $z=c_2/c_1$. Their intersection is nonempty exactly when $z^\ell=1$, since the product of the two subgroups is the unique subgroup of order $\ell$ in the cyclic group $K^\times$. When nonempty, choose integers $u,v$ with $u+v=1$, where $u$ is a multiple of $\ell/m_1$ and $v$ is a multiple of $\ell/m_2$. They exist by Bézout. Then $c_1z^u=c_2z^{-v}$ is a representative for the intersection and the new subgroup order is $\gcd(m_1,m_2)$. Iterate. All integer exponents have polynomial bit length.

If the final intersection is nonempty, let $c_0$ be its representative and $H$ its cyclic subgroup. This is exactly the scalar image of the target algebra automorphisms with identity residue action, since the orbit witnesses at each scalar can be combined freely. Use the fixed-multiplier routine on the whole comparison to lift $c_0$. A witness of any particular residue map differs from this one by a target automorphism with identity residue action. Such automorphisms have determinant group on the whole algebra generated by the following tests.

1. Evaluate the determinant image at scalar $1$. This reduces to the determinant image of the tangent isometry group already computed by the fixed-multiplier routine. If it contains both signs we are done.
2. Otherwise determinant is trivial on the kernel of the scalar projection, hence factors as a character of $H$ into $\{\pm1\}$. If $H$ has odd order the character is trivial. If $H$ has even order, find an element generating $H/H^2$. A nonsquare of $K^\times$, raised to the odd part of $|K|-1$, has the full two-primary order in $K^\times$. A further power of two gives the two-primary order of $H$, so the resulting scalar lies in $H$ and generates $H/H^2$. Lift this scalar by the fixed-multiplier routine on the self comparison. Its algebra determinant is the tangent determinant times $\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb F_3}(c)$, and determines the character.

This gives the exact algebra determinants at each feasible $\sigma$, including a witness per sign, without enumerating a potentially exponentially large scalar similitude group. Compute the determinant of the found $\sigma,g,h$ block map in the chosen endpoint bases, and use multiplication by the target sign subgroup to obtain its coset. Finally take the union over the $e$ field charts.

## 6. Restore the entire raw tensor sign profile

An isotopy of unital associative multiplication is of the form $L_u\phi,R_v\phi,L_uR_v\phi$, with units $u,v$ and an algebra isomorphism $\phi$. This follows by evaluating the product equation at either identity, which both shows the indicated multiplications invertible and proves the normalized map multiplicative. If $R$ has left $K$-dimension $d_0$, left or right multiplication by a unit of residue $s$ has determinant $\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb F_3}(s)^{d_0}$ by the radical filtration. If $d_0$ is even, the units contribute only positive determinants. If it is odd, the two unit determinants range independently over $\{\pm1\}$, since finite-field norms are onto. The raw signs arise from the algebra determinant in all three coordinates, the two independent unit corrections (their product in the output mode), and the three computable ratios of the source and target chart determinants. Tensor dualization does not change a sign over $\mathbb F_3$. Norm inversion also produces units realizing the supplied signs if witnesses are required.

All procedures run in deterministic polynomial time in the dense input length. The checks start from raw tensors, the number of field maps and type orbits is at most $e$, and each candidate list is quadratic in its own core matrix dimension. The proof uses the lists one at a time through scalar cosets. It does not form their Cartesian product. For $A\land F$, decide the eligible conjunct by this algorithm and then return the remaining comparison if it succeeds or a fixed negative instance otherwise. An unrestricted odd-automorphism comparison can be expressed as a one-sign comparison by reflecting its target in the tested mode. This result applies to a radical square of unbounded $\mathbb F_3$-dimension whenever it is one-dimensional over its left residue field. It does not establish unrestricted odd-automorphism absorption.


## 7. Several radical-square coordinates with independently recoverable tangent pieces

The same algorithm applies to the following larger recognizable family with no restriction on $\dim_K W$. Retain $J^3=0$, and decompose both $V$ and $W$ by Frobenius types. Each unordered pair $\{i,j\}$ of tangent types has its two ordered products landing in the single output type $i+j$. Require the $K$-span $W_{ij}$ of those products to have dimension at most one. Distinct pairs can give the same line, or give dependent lines. Impose the following additional condition on the tangent product.

For each $i$, let $D_i\subseteq V_i$ be the common two-sided radical against all tangent types. Let $P_{ij}\subseteq V_i$ be the two-sided radical against all types other than $j$. Thus $D_i\subseteq P_{ij}$, and $x\in P_{ij}$ has $b(x,V_\ell)=b(V_\ell,x)=0$ for every $\ell\ne j$. Require the images of $P_{ij}$ to span $V_i/D_i$. They then form a direct sum there. To see this, in any dependence project the product equations against $V_j$ on both sides. Only the $P_{ij}$ term can contribute, so it must also annihilate $V_j$ both ways and therefore belongs to $D_i$. All tests use linear equations and ranks over $K$. In particular the condition holds when each type interacts with at most one other type, counting a self-pair as an interaction.

The product now splits, on the intrinsic quotient by the common two-sided radical, into independently acted-on one- or two-type pieces indexed by the active unordered pairs. Indeed the decomposition $V_i/D_i=\bigoplus_j P_{ij}/D_i$ is fully preserved by compatible maps. Independent piece maps lift to a map on $V_i$ by choosing any charts for this quotient and for $D_i$. The arbitrary invertible maps on $D_i$ and shears into $D_i$ do not change the products. Each active piece has a common multiplier on the line $W_{ij}$ for its two ordered products. The multiplier algorithm in Section 4 works for each piece separately with the twists $\tau_i,\tau_j$ and a scalar on $W_{ij}$. In particular it gives its entire feasible coset, without deciding determinants first.

For each fixed residue map $\sigma$, identify the source coordinates with the target field as above. Check that the active pair labels, tangent piece dimensions and common radical dimensions match. The labeled lines $W_{ij}$ over all active pairs span $W$, because $J^2$ is generated by products. The problem remaining for the output map $h$ is to send each such line to its counterpart, subject to the feasible scalar cosets. Here is a polynomial-time procedure for all those constraints at once.

Work in one Frobenius type of $W$. Choose an ordered basis of vectors from the source labeled lines, and choose nonzero vectors on the corresponding target lines. Reject if the latter fail to be a basis. Write the chosen nonzero source and target vectors on line label $\ell$ in their respective basis coordinates $a_{j\ell},a'_{j\ell}$. An invertible map sending the basis lines to their counterparts acts by factors $t_j\ne0$ in these two bases. It sends all the labeled lines correctly exactly when its line factors $s_\ell\ne0$ satisfy $t_j a_{j\ell}=s_\ell a'_{j\ell}$ for all $j,\ell$. The two coefficient supports must agree. On their common nonzero positions these equations are prescribed ratios between $s_\ell$ and $t_j$. Use the finite bipartite graph with one vertex for each basis index and each labeled line and edges for these nonzero positions. Set one unknown to one per connected component, propagate along a spanning tree, and verify all other edges. Failure excludes any map with the given line scheme. On success all solutions are obtained by independent nonzero scalars $u_\nu$, one per component, multiplying the propagated solution. This is true even for repeated lines. In particular each active pair's line multiplier is its known propagated factor times the $u_\nu$ on the component containing its label.

Divide each piece's feasible multiplier coset by that propagated factor. Apply the cyclic intersection algorithm to the resulting cosets separately on every $u_\nu$. Different components and different output types do not share a scalar. An empty intersection anywhere excludes the given residue map. Otherwise choose one intersection representative per component. They give $h$ constructively. The feasible output maps form a coset of a group which, in the target chart, acts by one common scalar on each of the spans of component basis lines. Its factors are the cyclic subgroups given by the component intersection orders. The spans are intrinsic to the labeled lines, since these groups of basis vertices precisely describe the allowed independent line rescalings. For computation it suffices to repeat the same algorithm on the target self comparison with identity residue map to get the component description in the target chart. This self comparison always succeeds.

For prescribed $h$, the fixed-multiplier subroutine works verbatim by preapplying $h$ to the complete source product tuple. First compute one full relative witness for the chosen $h$. For stabilizers with identity on the residue, the kernel of the output projection is computed by setting $h=1$ in the full fixed-multiplier test, including the common radical pieces. If this kernel has only positive determinants, the determinant of the algebra automorphism factors through the product of the cyclic component groups. Evaluate it on a generator modulo squares of each even-order factor, using one full fixed-$h$ lift per factor. Here $h$ is the corresponding scalar on that component's basis vectors and the identity on the others. The determinant of $h$ itself is computed in its actual chart. This covers the entire sign image, since the product of the cyclic groups modulo squares is generated by these elements. Restore the raw three-bit profile and witnesses as before. The recognition and all comparisons remain deterministic polynomial time with no bound on the residue degree or number of active pairs.

For the main theorem with $\dim_K J^2=1$, the separate cube-zero hypothesis can be dropped. Indeed $J^3\subseteq J^2$ is a left $K$-subspace once a residue section is chosen. A finite-dimensional local algebra over a finite field has a section by Wedderburn–Malcev. If $J^3=J^2$, then $J^r=J^2$ for every $r\ge2$, contradicting nilpotence because $J^2\ne0$. Hence $J^3=0$.
