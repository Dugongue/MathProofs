# Rational matrix realization of nonlinear algebraic tests

Let \(A\) be an explicit finite-dimensional unital algebra over \(L=\mathbb Q(t_1,\ldots,t_d)\), with multiplication coordinates given by rational circuits. Its rank and the number of parameters may be parts of the input. Consider a finite list of rational-function circuit computations over \(A\), with commuting central variables \(x\), and tests between the resulting elements. Products may be noncommutative. If supplied, an \(L\)-linear anti-involution can be used as a gate. Allow inverses and substitutions wherever the indicated expressions are defined over \(L(x)\).

There is a deterministic polynomial-time translation to rational matrix circuits with the same \(x\)-variables, preserving all designated identities and nonidentities. For substitutions the size parameter includes the explicit substituted circuit occurrences. It simultaneously preserves the generic ranks of designated matrices in the regular representation, including every minor's vanishing pattern. If there is an involution, it becomes adjunction for a fixed split form. The output uses circuit-encoded rational scalars. The proof combines the previous deterministic function-field specialization with the split adjoint representation.

## Faithful multiplication and involution

Represent \(a\in A\) by its left multiplication matrix \(\rho(a)\) in a basis beginning with 1. This homomorphism is unital and faithful, since evaluation on the unit vector recovers \(a\). In the involutive case use
\[
\widetilde\rho(a)=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\rho(a),\rho(a^*)^{\mathsf T}\bigr).
\]
Anti-multiplicativity and transposition make this a homomorphism as well. Taking the adjoint for the symmetric hyperbolic form exchanges the two diagonal blocks and transposes each, so it carries \(\widetilde\rho(a)\) to \(\widetilde\rho(a^*)\). The same identity holds for the alternating hyperbolic form on block diagonal matrices. This step preserves every operation, including formal inversion by matrix inverse where an inverse is defined. Substitution in the central variables commutes with forming the matrix expressions.

Compose the arithmetic gates using shared matrix entries. Represent all entries by integer-polynomial numerator and denominator circuits, with adjugate and determinant for inverse matrices. Tests between elements are reduced to tests on matrix entries. A fixed list of block matrices may be included as outputs. Use shared denominators at the scalar and matrix stages. Substitution nodes can be handled by circuit substitution, with cost polynomial in the written circuit data and the sizes of the explicit substituted occurrences. Equivalently the input may directly specify the rational circuits for every substitution instance. Collect the denominators needed by the resulting circuits, including those needed after substitution. They are nonzero by the premise.

## One point preserving all designated minors

Clear denominators separately in each designated matrix, using one shared nonzero multiplier for every entry of that matrix. Suppose its resulting entries have total degree at most \(D_0\) and integer coefficient absolute sums at most \(B_0\). A \(k\)-minor has total degree at most \(kD_0\) and coefficient absolute sum at most \(k!B_0^k\). These bounds follow directly from the determinant formula and apply uniformly to every choice of rows and columns. The denominators and the individual test residuals have bounds of the same form obtainable from their circuits. By the usual degree and coefficient recurrence for a division-free circuit, one can take a degree bound \(2^m\) and coefficient norm bound \(2^{(b+1)2^m}\), with \(m,b\) polynomial in the explicit circuit and matrix sizes. For the minors take \(m\) large enough to cover the polynomial cost of a determinant circuit for any one selection of entries. It is unnecessary to enumerate those selections.

Set \(D=2^m+1\), \(H=2^{(b+2)2^m}\), and \(t_i=H^{D^{i-1}}\). Exponent vectors in the \(t\)-variables are mapped injectively to powers of \(H\) by base-\(D\) expansion. For each fixed monomial in \(x\), its nonzero coefficient polynomial in \(t\) survives, since the leading power of \(H\) exceeds the sum of the lower powers in absolute value. The same applies to the denominators, so all translated expressions are defined as rational functions. The assigned integers have polynomial-size circuits via repeated squaring. Inserting those circuits yields the desired polynomial running time without expanding their numerical values.

Every designated rank over \(L(x)\) is therefore preserved over \(\mathbb Q(x)\), as are augmented-rank tests of consistency for specified linear systems. The specialized representation stays faithful on the specialized basis because the first columns of the regular action of the basis elements are the coordinate vectors of those elements. Thus all multiplication relations and the optional involution descend to an actual rational matrix algebra. Here the preservation is for generic rank in the remaining variables and does not assert uniform pointwise rank at arbitrary assignments of \(x\). This proof is informal.
