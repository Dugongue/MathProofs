# A binary parity marker for explicit permutation frames

## Statement

There is a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb F_2$ for the following problem. The input consists of two three-tensors over $\mathbb F_3$ with prescribed coordinate frames. The allowed mode maps are monomial in those frames. In addition to equality of the transformed tensors, require any specified determinant signs on the three mode maps. A sign condition on any selected subset of the modes, or the product of those signs, is also allowed. The resulting binary tensor is one ordinary TI instance. The same reduction works for an explicitly tabulated finite relational system, with any fixed number of named sorts and bounded relation arity, when the restrictions are signs of the sort permutations.

This is an exact result for supplied permutation frames and for those raw-input subproblems where such frames are recovered naturally and all allowed witnesses preserve them. Computing frames or transferring the unconstrained general linear action between characteristics is a separate requirement.

## The parity marker

Fix an ordered finite set $S=\{1,\ldots,s\}$ with $s\ge2$. Give the binary vector space $W=\mathbb F_2^{\{(i,j):i\ne j\}}$ its coordinate-permutation action. The subspace $E$ of symmetric entries consists of functions on unordered pairs, and the subspace
$$
 E_0=\{x\in E\mid\textstyle\sum_{i<j}x_{ij}=0\}
$$
is invariant under every permutation. Write $v$ for the vector with $v_{ij}=1$ if $i<j$ and $0$ otherwise. For a permutation $\sigma$, the vector $\sigma v-v$ belongs to $E$. For each unordered pair it has value $1$ precisely when the order of its two source labels was reversed by $\sigma$. Thus $\sigma[v]=[v]$ in $W/E_0$ exactly when $\sigma$ is even. Taking the target mark to be $[v+e]$, where $e$ has value $1$ on the two orientations of any one pair, imposes odd sign. When several named sorts must contribute to a product of signs, use the direct sums of their pair spaces and their symmetric spaces, taking the kernel of the **single sum** of the unordered-pair coordinate sums. The same proof applies. Singleton and empty sets contribute sign $+1$.

Here are tensor-system arrows that enforce the intended quotient map. Put $A_S=\mathbb F_2^S$ with coordinatewise multiplication. Its algebra automorphisms permute precisely its primitive idempotents, so the map on $A_S$ must be a coordinate permutation. Include the linear map $A_S\otimes A_S\to A_S$ of coordinatewise multiplication, the quotient by the diagonal span $\langle e_i\otimes e_i\rangle$, the inclusion of that span, and the inclusion and quotient maps for $E_0$ in the resulting off-diagonal space $W$. Link the tensor product by its bilinear evaluation. Every map on the pair space and its quotient is forced by the coordinate permutation. Conversely every permutation preserves the defining maps and $E_0$. Mark a vector in the last quotient by a linear arrow from one fixed scalar line. These spaces and maps have polynomial dimension. The construction uses characteristic two in the symmetric difference of the two orientations.

## Framed three-tensors over $\mathbb F_3$

For each mode $i$, write $L_i$ for its set of coordinate lines and $P_i=L_i\times\{+1,-1\}$ for the corresponding signed basis vectors. Keep both sets as named sorts, with projection $P_i\to L_i$ and the sign-flip involution on $P_i$. Any compatible permutations are given by a line bijection $\pi_i$ and scalars $\lambda_{i,j}\in\{+1,-1\}$, acting by
$$
           (j,\epsilon)\longmapsto(\pi_i(j),\lambda_{i,j}\epsilon).
$$
These are exactly the monomial maps. One may use the lists as dual basis vectors and take the inverse-transpose maps on the original tensors. A sign equals its inverse, so this convention does not change the determinant requirement.

For signed dual labels $u_i=(j_i,\epsilon_i)$, color the triple by the field element
$$
             \epsilon_1\epsilon_2\epsilon_3 T_{j_1j_2j_3}.
$$
Keep separate relations for the three values. These colors encode the trilinear evaluation on every choice of signed dual basis vectors. Their preservation is equivalent to the tensor equation for the associated monomial maps, since the basis triples determine all tensor coefficients. The underlying permutation on $L_i$ contributes $\operatorname{sgn}(\pi_i)$. On $P_i$, lifting $\pi_i$ gives an even permutation because each exchanged block has two elements, and the scalar flips contribute $\prod_j\lambda_{i,j}$. Thus the permutation sign on $L_i\sqcup P_i$ is precisely the matrix determinant sign in the two chosen frames. Apply the parity marker to this union for each specified constraint.

The relational encoding is an exact tensor system over $\mathbb F_2$. Give each sort its coordinatewise algebra and include linear maps induced by the projection, the sign flip and inclusions into the named disjoint unions. A relation of arity $r$ is the binary $0$-$1$ indicator vector in the $r$-fold tensor product of the corresponding sort spaces. Include it as a mark in that space, with the tensor space actions linked by successive bilinear tensor-product maps. There are fixedly many sorts and bounded arity, and the dense arrays are polynomial in the frame dimensions. Pin the scalar line and compile all arrows and marks to a single ordinary 3TI instance using the [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk tensor-system construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219). The forward and reverse implications follow from the exact correspondence between system witnesses and compatible permutations just proved.

For frames supplied in the original vector-space coordinates, compute the changes of basis $M_{T,i}$ and $M_{T',i}$. The determinant in the displayed target and source frames differs from the original determinant by the known factor $\det(M_{T,i})/\det(M_{T',i})$, if the mode map is represented from source to target in the primal frames. Equivalently calibrate using the reciprocal for the inverse-transpose dual maps. Including these constants in the requested parity makes the test independent of which frame bases are chosen.

A raw-input sufficient hypothesis is a polynomial-time algorithm computing a complete direct sum of unordered lines from each tensor such that **every** ordinary transporter between any two tensors in its applicability domain takes each recovered line family to the corresponding family. A deterministic applicability test should succeed for both tensors in any ordinary orbit it accepts. To use the reduction, choose nonzero representatives and an ordering by Gaussian elimination and apply the above construction with calibrated signs. On the accepted domain this computes ordinary SL-TI, not merely the monomial-SL diagram problem. Frame invariance under all transporters is the substantive hypothesis. Supplying a line decomposition which is merely compatible with some transporter is insufficient for preserving all signs.

## Odd finite coefficient fields

For fixed odd $q$, use $P_i=L_i\times\mathbb F_q^\times$ and include the named scalar multiplication permutations on this set. Their simultaneous commutant on each line fiber consists exactly of the scalar translations. Color the triple by its trilinear evaluation, with one named relation for each field value. The sign of a scalar translation by $\lambda$ on $\mathbb F_q^\times$ equals the quadratic character $\chi(\lambda)=\lambda^{(q-1)/2}$ interpreted as $+1$ or $-1$. Indeed a cyclic generator acts as a $(q-1)$-cycle of odd sign. The permutation of line fibers contributes nothing to this sign because $q-1$ is even. If $\chi(-1)=-1$, include the line-label set as a second contribution, and if $\chi(-1)=+1$, omit it. The resulting total parity is exactly $\chi(\det g_i)$. This supplies a binary ordinary-TI reduction for prescribed quadratic determinant characters on the framed domain. Exact determinant-one is equivalent to the square-character condition when $q=3$.

## Higher two-primary determinants on explicit line frames

The binary target can test the whole two-primary part of a monomial determinant for any fixed odd $q$. Let $\mu$ be the two-primary quotient of $\mathbb F_q^\times$, of order $m=2^a$, and let $B=\mathbb F_2[\mu]$. Write $\varepsilon:B\to\mathbb F_2$ for augmentation and $J=\ker\varepsilon$. A generator $z$ of $\mu$ identifies $B$ with $\mathbb F_2[z]/((z-1)^m)$, so $J^m=0$.

For an underlying line set $L$, let $B_i$ be a copy of $B$ on line $i$. The labels are the scalar labels modulo the odd-order subgroup, linked to the existing signed-label carrier by the quotient map. Work in
$$
 N=\bigoplus_{\substack{S\subseteq L\\ |S|<m}}\ \bigotimes_{i\in S}B_i .
$$
The direct-sum action permutes the line subsets and shifts each local $B_i$ by the image of its scalar multiplier. Build each fixed-size subset-label carrier from the ordered label tensor power, by quotienting out tuples with repeated line labels and identifying tuples that differ only by reordering. These are invariant subspaces under the actual labelled action, specified by their bases and inclusion or quotient maps. Thus the action on $N$ is linked to the frame action and uses only polynomial size for fixed $q$.

Let $C\subseteq N$ be the space of compatible families of marginals. Applying $\varepsilon$ to slot $i$ of the $S$-coordinate is required to equal the $S\setminus\{i\}$-coordinate. It is invariant under local shifts and line permutations. Let $M\colon\bigotimes_{i\in L}B_i\to C$ be the map taking the retained marginals. It is surjective. For each retained set $S$, split each $B_i=\mathbb F_2\cdot1\oplus J_i$ and use the all-$J_i$ projection of its $S$-coordinate as the $S$-term. Marginal compatibility reconstructs every retained coordinate from these terms. Inserting $1$ outside each $S$ lifts the family to the full product.

The map from the full product to $B$ multiplying its factors vanishes on terms involving $J_i$ at $m$ or more positions. Consequently it factors through a surjection $\Psi:C\to B$ given explicitly by summing the products of the retained all-augmentation projections. The full product need not be constructed. On the full product, multiplication is equivariant for the output action of multiplication by $\prod_i\overline\lambda_i$ on $B$, where $\lambda_i$ are the local scalar shifts and bars denote the quotient in $\mu$. This identity descends by the surjectivity of $M$. Therefore $\ker\Psi$ is invariant, and the marked class of the family of all identities in $C/\ker\Psi$ is transported by precisely the desired scalar product.

To add the permutation sign, take the two-dimensional subspace of the binary parity quotient spanned by its even and odd marked classes. On it the two classes are independent and the parity action swaps them. For a singleton line set take the one-dimensional trivial carrier instead. Tensor this space with $C/\ker\Psi$, then quotient by the kernel of the linear map to $B$ which selects multiplication by $1$ on the even class and by the image of $-1$ on the odd class. This kernel is invariant because the two induced shifts commute. The final marked class transforms by the full two-primary determinant. It decides exact framed determinant-one whenever $q-1$ is a power of two.

The same marginal construction over $\mathbb F_\ell$ computes the $\ell$-primary scalar determinant component for any prime $\ell\mid(q-1)$. The permutation sign contributes only at $\ell=2$. The full determinant test on a framed domain thus reduces to a fixed list of ordinary TI instances across prime fields for fixed $q$. Indeed the determinant image of any nonempty transporter coset is a coset of a subgroup of $(\mathbb F_q^\times)^3$. A subgroup of this finite abelian group is the product of its primary subgroups, so the separate primary tests are jointly sufficient. When $q-1$ is a prime power, a single instance over its prime field suffices.

## Scope of the analogous same-field quotient

The ordered-pair marker relies on the binary translation action. Here is a precise limitation of the corresponding idea in odd characteristic. Let $V=k^s$ be the coordinate permutation module for $S_s$ and $k$ have odd characteristic. Restrict to the elementary abelian group generated by $b=\lfloor s/2\rfloor$ disjoint transpositions. Every weight of $V$ and its dual has support of size at most one on these generators. Thus a direct sum of tensor powers of degree at most $r$, and every subquotient, has only weights of support at most $r$. Separability follows from the fact that the subgroup order is a power of two and hence invertible in $k$.

If the alternating group fixes a point $x$ in such a module and an odd transposition $\tau$ moves it, then $x-\tau x$ is a nonzero sign vector. The alternating group fixes it by normality, and odd elements negate it. Its restriction to the disjoint transpositions has support $b$. Hence $r\ge b$ for such a point marker. In particular degree two fails once $s\ge6$. For a subspace marker the required degree is at least $\lceil b/2\rceil$ by the analogous weight restriction argument. Two weights of support at most $r$ with $2r<b$ cannot agree on the even-product subgroup of these transpositions without agreeing on the whole subgroup. The weight projectors on the module can therefore be computed from even permutations alone, showing stability under every transposition of the subgroup. Stability under the alternating group and one transposition gives stability under the full symmetric group.

These arguments leave more general input-dependent constructions available. They explain precisely why the low-degree binary parity quotient cannot simply be reused over $\mathbb F_3$.
