# Residue signs from nonsplit determinant frames

## Statement

Let $T,S$ be three-tensors over $\mathbb F_3$ of equally sized odd mode dimensions $n,n,n$. Write $M_T(x)$ for the matrix obtained by contracting a specified dual mode against $x$, and similarly for $S$. Suppose the determinant polynomial of the source pencil is, over the algebraic closure, a product of $n$ independent linear forms. Record their Frobenius-orbit multiplicities $m_d$, with $m_d$ orbits of size $d$. If $m_d\le C\log L$ for all $d$, one can test special-linear equivalence with **one polynomial-size ordinary 3TI instance over $\mathbb F_3$**. Individual orbit degrees may grow without bound. With arbitrary multiplicities, the same extraction yields a polynomial-time Turing reduction to ordinary TI over $\mathbb F_3$.

## Polynomial extraction of the regular étale algebra

Use an extension $E$ of $\mathbb F_3$ of size greater than $n^3+n^2$ and at most three times that bound, using degree at least one. Construct it by enumerating and testing monic irreducibles. Scan the moment curve $(1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1})$ over $E$ to find $u$ with $f_T(u)=\det M_T(u)\ne0$. A nonzero linear form on the curve excludes at most $n-1$ points. Use the same scan to find $v$ so the ratios $\lambda_j=\ell_j(v)/\ell_j(u)$ are distinct and nonzero. Each collision or zero excludes at most $n-1$ points since the geometric factors $\ell_j$ are independent. For this test just take the characteristic polynomial $p$ of $M_T(u)^{-1}M_T(v)$ and check squarefreeness and a nonzero constant coefficient.

Work over $B=E[t]/(p)$, and let $\lambda$ be its class of $t$. For each label basis vector $w$ evaluate the directional derivatives of $f_T$ at $u-v/\lambda$ in directions $w$ and $v$. These evaluations use the coefficient of $\epsilon$ in a division-free determinant over $B[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$. In each geometric coordinate exactly one factor vanishes at $u-v/\lambda$, so the derivative in direction $v$ is invertible. Multiplying the ratio of derivatives by $\lambda$ gives the value of the corresponding form on $w$, normalized by its value at $u$. This produces a linear isomorphism from the $E$-extended label space to $B$. Pull back the multiplication operators of $B$ to the endomorphisms of the label space. Over the algebraic closure these are precisely the diagonal operators in the coordinates given by the geometric forms. The diagonal algebra is Galois invariant, since Galois permutes the forms up to scale. Intersect the computed $E$-space with the rational endomorphisms by linear equations. This gives $A_T$, a rational étale algebra of dimension $n$ acting in its regular representation on the original dual labels.

At fixed field size one can compute the residue factors and cyclic vectors for a finite-dimensional algebra in deterministic polynomial time. For this étale algebra one can also compute primitive idempotents by solving $x^3=x$ and splitting with $1-(x-\lambda)^2$, then take one nonzero vector in each component of the regular module and sum them. Choose such a cyclic vector $u_T$, ordered bases for the factors of $A_T$, and let $\Psi_T(x)$ be the unique $a\in A_T$ with $a u_T=x$. Then $f_T(x)=\kappa_T\operatorname{Norm}_{A_T/\mathbb F_3}(\Psi_T(x))$, where $\kappa_T=f_T(u_T)\ne0$. Define $J(T)=\kappa_T\det\Psi_T$. Calculate the same data for $S$, with a failed test leading to rejection. An ordinary equivalent target inherits the source promise, so no equivalent target is lost. No assertion of recognizing the entire promise on arbitrary unpromised pairs is needed. [Finite-field algebra algorithms](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf), [division-free determinant](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0020019084900188).

## The exact character and a small same-field compiler

Write $g_i$ for the original transporter, taking the first mode to be the label mode, and write $\beta=g_1^{-\mathsf T}$. Let $a=\det(g_2)\det(g_3)$. Then $f_S(\beta x)=a f_T(x)$. The map $\beta$ takes the geometric frame to the geometric frame and conjugates $A_T$ onto $A_S$, by an algebra isomorphism $\sigma$. In regular module coordinates it acts as
$$
 \Psi_S\beta x=b\,\sigma(\Psi_T x)
$$
for a unit $b\in A_S$, obtained by evaluating at $u_T$. Substituting $u_T$ into the determinant identity gives $\kappa_S\operatorname{Norm}(b)=a\kappa_T$. Taking determinants of the regular module equation and canceling $\operatorname{Norm}(b)$ gives
$$
 \prod_{i=1}^3\det(g_i)=a/\det(\beta)
   =\det(\sigma)^{-1}\,J(S)/J(T).
$$
Central scalar automorphisms with product one realize every triple of determinant signs of product one, because $n$ is odd. Thus special-linear equivalence holds exactly when one ordinary transporter has total determinant one.

Inside the product of the degree-$d$ field factors, let $U_d$ be the product of their prime subfields if $d$ is odd, or of their quadratic subfields if $d$ is even. This is a canonical subspace of dimension $m_d$ or $2m_d$, respectively, found by solving $x^3=x$ or $x^9=x$ in that product. Any field-factor isomorphism is, in common field coordinates, a Frobenius power. One Frobenius step on $\mathbb F_{3^d}$ has determinant $(-1)^{d-1}$: after extension to an algebraic closure it acts by one $d$-cycle on the embeddings. Hence it has the same determinant on the chosen prime or quadratic subfield. To compute $\det(\sigma)$ between distinct inputs, order factors by degree, choose the same model and basis for each degree, and use the corresponding subfield basis for every copy. Similar deterministic conversion between explicit finite fields of fixed characteristic follows by factoring a generator's minimal polynomial in the common model, or one can use arbitrary bases and account for their known change-of-basis determinants. A swap of two degree-$d$ factors contributes $(-1)^d$, the same sign as swapping their copies in $U_d$. Therefore in the common basis convention
$$
 \det(\sigma)=\prod_d\det(\sigma|_{U_d}).
$$

Keep the original tensor in a system over $\mathbb F_3$. Include the evaluation map $A\otimes V_1^*\to V_1^*$ so the map on $A$ is forced to be actual conjugation by the label-dual transporter. Include $U_d\hookrightarrow A$ for each degree. Link each $U_d$ to its exterior powers with the surjective wedge maps $\Lambda^j U_d\otimes U_d\to\Lambda^{j+1}U_d$. The map on each exterior power is forced by the wedge maps, starting at $j=1$. In particular the map on the top degree is exactly the determinant scalar. Multiply these lines using a chain of tensor-product maps of two lines to one line, and mark $J(T)$ times the basis vector of the final line, with $J(S)$ on the target. Preservation is exactly $\det(\sigma)=J(S)/J(T)$. All added maps are forced by and exist for the ordinary transporters with the required determinant character. The summed dimensions of the exterior spaces at any degree are at most $2^{2m_d}$. The hypothesis on $m_d$ and the bound $n\le L$ give polynomial size, and the order-three tensor-system compiler yields one ordinary same-field TI instance. [Tensor-system compilation, Theorem 4.1 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2).

## Arbitrary multiplicities with adaptive queries

Use the common field models above to list a polynomial-size set in $A_T$, consisting of all Frobenius conjugates of the standard basis elements in each individual field factor, extended by zero to the other factors. Remove duplicates. This set spans $A_T$, has at most $\sum_d m_d d^2$ points, and is carried into the same list for $A_S$ by every algebra isomorphism. Thus its permutation records the complete $\sigma$. Each prescribed point image is a vector mark in the tensor system containing the original tensor and the algebra evaluation map.

Use marked TI queries to find a reference permutation on this list. For each successive source point, try all possible target images, retain the first that permits a completion with all preceding marks, and proceed. If there is no ordinary transporter, reject. Similarly, for the source automorphisms, fix the first $i-1$ points, and for each achievable image of point $i$, complete one such permutation. These are a set of coset representatives between successive pointwise stabilizers. The combined list generates the entire induced automorphism group since the subgroup fixing the whole list acts trivially there. Compute the matrices of their actions on $A_T$ from the marked points and take their determinants. If at least one source automorphism has negative determinant on $A_T$, either sign is attainable in the ordinary transporter coset. Otherwise the sign is the reference sign. This procedure checks the calibrated criterion exactly with polynomially many adaptive queries. [Deterministic finite-field isomorphisms in multiplication coordinates](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf).

## Assemble the same-field test with the local-ring compiler

Let $R$ be a finite commutative local $\mathbb F_3$-algebra with residue $\mathbb F_3$, and suppose it admits a supplied $\mathbb F_3$-linear derivation $D\colon R\to R$ with kernel $\mathbb F_3$. This includes arbitrary split square-zero extensions and $\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^e)$ for $e\le3$, where the Euler derivation separates the principal units. Consider tensors in the tensor product over $R$ of three free modules of rank $n$, with $n$ odd. Suppose the source residue tensor has the determinant-frame promise above and logarithmic multiplicities. For any three prescribed determinants $\delta_i\in R^\times$, the problem of an $R$-linear transporter with exactly those determinants reduces to **one ordinary 3TI instance over $\mathbb F_3$**.

Here is the joint system. Pin the ring $R$ pointwise in a tensor system by including a cyclic shift and its marked cyclic vector on its underlying vector space. Include scalar multiplication arrows on each mode $V_i=R^n$, and include the $R$-balanced tensor product as an explicit linear quotient of the tensor product over $\mathbb F_3$. The latter is formed with two bilinear tensor-product arrows; the quotient map forces the induced action. Mark the original tensor in this quotient. These constraints retain exactly the invertible $R$-linear transporters. Include the quotient maps $V_i\to V_i/\mathfrak m V_i$, which force the residue mode maps to be induced by those same transporters. Link the frame and exterior maps of the residue system to these quotient spaces, using the residue total sign $\prod_i\overline{\delta_i}$ in the final line mark.

Independently, on each original $V_i$ let $D_{V_i}$ differentiate coordinates, $E_i=\operatorname{End}_R(V_i)$, $E_i^0=\ker\operatorname{tr}_R$, and $M_i=E_i+\mathbb F_3 D_{V_i}$. Use the evaluation map $M_i\otimes V_i\to V_i$ and the quotient map $M_i\to M_i/E_i^0$. They force the conjugation action and its induced quotient. For an $R$-linear mode map $g_i$ one has
$$
 g_i D_{V_i}g_i^{-1}=D_{V_i}-(Dg_i)g_i^{-1},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{tr}_R((Dg_i)g_i^{-1})=\frac{D(\det g_i)}{\det g_i}.
$$
The second identity follows by differentiating the permutation formula for the determinant and using the adjugate. For $b_i=D(\delta_i)/\delta_i$, mark the class of $D_{V_i}$ at the source and of $D_{V_i}-\operatorname{diag}(b_i,0,\ldots,0)$ at the target. This enforces the indicated logarithmic derivative, so $\det g_i/\delta_i\in\mathbb F_3^\times$. If $D=0$, the kernel hypothesis means $R=\mathbb F_3$ and these mark conditions are vacuous.

The residue total sign requires $\prod_i(\overline{\det g_i}/\overline{\delta_i})=1$. Hence the three quotients $\det g_i/\delta_i$, already in $\mathbb F_3^\times$, have product one. Multiplying each mode map by the inverse of its quotient preserves the full tensor equation, because the scalars have product one. Its determinant is now exactly $\delta_i$, since $n$ is odd. Conversely every exact witness supplies the two sets of marks. The underlying field dimensions, including the $R$-balanced product and the residue exterior spaces, are polynomial in the explicitly represented ring and tensor dimensions. All arrows have order at most three, so the joint system compiles in one call. This argument requires no separate choice of compatible residue and principal-unit witnesses. [Tensor-system compilation](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4).
