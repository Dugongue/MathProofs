# Residue sign in functorial lifting constructions over F3

This note analyzes two direct ways to use the local-ring lifting proof for determinant sign. The positive principal-unit reduction is in the separate determinant-lifting proof. The arguments below delimit residue-layer constructions, while the arbitrary raw-pair reduction remains open.

## Arbitrarily many tensor-construction steps

Let G=GL_n(F3), and let D be its diagonal sign subgroup. Its characters correspond to subsets S of [n], through the product of the diagonal entries indexed by S. D has order 2^n, so its representation on any F3 vector space splits as a direct sum of character spaces. Restriction, taking subquotients and taking direct sums behave exactly on these character spaces. Duality gives the same weights, because each character has order at most two. Weights in a tensor product are symmetric differences of weights of the factors.

Consider constructions beginning with any number of copies of the standard module, its dual and fixed trivial modules. Allow arbitrary many steps of direct sums, duals, invariant subquotients, and tensor products with at most r inputs per step, where r≥2 is fixed. Allow affine actions whose linear parts are such modules, and linearizations of those affine modules by adjoining a fixed scalar coordinate. These operations include invariant relation spaces and quotients in the local lifting obstruction. Assume all spaces carry a specified action of the full G throughout.

If a constructed module contains the full-set character of D, then some intermediate module has dimension at least the binomial coefficient n choose ceil(n/(r+1)). Indeed permutations in G permute D-weights. A weight of size s forces all size-s weights to occur and hence demands dimension at least n choose s. Put a=ceil(n/(r+1)) and suppose n>r+1. Choose the first stage at which any support has size at least a. The initial modules have weights of sizes zero or one. The first such stage can only arise from a tensor product, since restriction, quotient, sum and dual do not increase the maximum support size. Its weights are symmetric differences of at most r supports of size ≤a−1. Thus the first offending size lies in [a,r(a−1)]. The inequality r(a−1)≤n−a follows from (r+1)(a−1)<n. The minimum of n choose s on this interval is n choose a, which is exponential in n for fixed r. If one constructs only a subquotient of the indicated tensor product, its weights are still among those of the parent product, so the same argument proves the bound.

The affine additions do not provide new weights. In the homogenization of an affine representation, the original linear module is a submodule and the extra quotient is trivial. On D this exact sequence splits. Equivalently every affine D-action in characteristic three has a D-fixed point, found by averaging the orbit of any point over D. Recenter there and compare its linear weights.

Now let D^+ be the kernel of the sign product on D. A character of D trivial on D^+ is exactly the trivial character or the full-set product. Thus in the absence of a full-set weight every vector fixed by D^+ is fixed by D. The same statement holds for affine marks using the fixed origin just described. A universal stabilizer construction aiming to retain the entire SL_n must make every final data mark fixed by D^+. If the marks are tensors of bounded order, include their spaces as stages of the construction. Below the dimension bound the marks are then fixed by every diagonal sign matrix, including matrices of determinant −1. Therefore a functorial group controller of this form cannot enforce determinant one with all intermediate spaces of polynomial dimension. This applies to any number of successive nilpotent layers and arbitrary affine cocycles on the displayed modules. The hypothesis of a full G-action excludes input-dependent flags or solution-specific choices.

For example, on a single residue-layer quotient made from a tensor product of at most r standard or dual factors, every weight has support size at most r. For n>r an arbitrary affine cocycle in this quotient cannot distinguish the even and odd diagonal signs by an SL_n-fixed mark. This addresses the affine cocycle as well as its linear part.

## Mixed-characteristic transport to characteristic two

Let n≥3 and E be the elementary abelian subgroup of G consisting of I+e_1v^T with v_1=0. Then E≅C_3^{n−1}. Its normalizer contains block diagonal matrices with lower block GL_(n−1)(F3). This group is transitive on the nonidentity characters of E over a splitting field of characteristic two.

For any characteristic-two representation of G, extend scalars to an algebraic closure and diagonalize E. Its order is odd, so this weight decomposition is exact. If some nontrivial character occurs, its full normalizer orbit occurs. There are 3^{n−1}−1 such characters and their weight spaces are disjoint, giving that dimension lower bound. Otherwise E acts trivially. Conjugates of E contain every elementary transvection. These generate SL_n(F3), so the representation factors through the determinant quotient C_2. In characteristic two such a quotient can be represented by a unipotent involution, but the map from a source matrix to that involution already uses the sign.

The bound also applies through a lifted action with a normal 2-group kernel. Let Γ surject onto G with such a kernel K and consider a characteristic-two representation of Γ of dimension below the bound. Every irreducible constituent is trivial on K: a finite 2-group acting in characteristic two has a fixed vector, and normality makes its fixed space Γ-invariant. To see the fixed-vector assertion, pass to a central involution, take its nonzero fixed space and induct on the group order. Consequently each constituent descends to a characteristic-two representation of G, factors through C_2 by the preceding dimension argument, and is trivial. It follows that there is a full Γ-invariant flag whose quotient actions are trivial. The image is a finite subgroup of an upper unitriangular group in characteristic two, and is a 2-group. A Sylow 3-subgroup of the preimage of E maps onto E by counting the 3-parts of their orders, yet acts trivially in that image.

In a finite local residue-characteristic-two ring, the kernel of matrix reduction is itself a 2-group, using its central congruence filtration. Thus a polynomial-dimensional linear lift across such a ring cannot retain the source elementary transvections through its residue action or congruence kernel under this extension model. The claim concerns group actions and their linear or affine lifts. It neither assumes nor decides the existence of an arbitrary pair-dependent reduction between ordinary and special-linear TI.

## What transfers constructively

For split dual numbers the original determinant problem over the ring separates into the determinant sign at residue and an additive trace constraint on the nilpotent coefficient. The latter has a polynomial ordinary-TI encoding and the former survives unchanged. More generally let a determinant increment group be a p-group, as in the successive square-zero layers. An additive homomorphism from the prime-to-p residue determinant subgroup to that group is trivial by order. The bounded-arity argument also rules out encoding the same sign through an equivariant affine mark on the ordinary low-degree residue modules. The available positive transfer therefore consists of the principal-unit compiler, the complete determinant-constrained lifting algorithm with the residue subgroup supplied, and the earlier constructions for inputs with recoverable orientation data.
