# Orthogonal tensor isomorphism at marginal multiplicity two

Informal proof. This addresses the real orthogonal multiplicity-two case of Chizewer, Everett, Mithal and Qiao's Open Problem 5. The algorithm uses the original rational arrays, works with arbitrary zeros in the tensor, and uses randomized polynomial bit time. The unitary case and multiplicities above two remain separate. [Original question](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27128v1#S1.SS5)

**Theorem.** Fix the tensor order (in particular, order three). For rational tensors with equal ordered formats, orthogonal isomorphism is in BPP on the promise that each eigenvalue of each mode Gram matrix of the first input has multiplicity at most two. The error probability can be bounded by any fixed positive constant, uniformly on all inputs satisfying the promise. The residual decision in exact spectral coordinates uses polynomially many arithmetic operations over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two. With rational Gram eigenbases orthonormal over Q, the method gives a deterministic polynomial bit-time decision.

## Complexification and eigenblocks

For real arrays, complex orthogonal orbit equality is equivalent to real orthogonal orbit equality. To see this, take a complex orthogonal transporter with polar decompositions g_j = u_j p_j. For a complex orthogonal g, the positive Hermitian matrix H = g* g satisfies H^T = H^(-1). Its unique positive square root p therefore satisfies p^T = p^(-1) (transpose preserves positive Hermitian matrices). Thus p is orthogonal, and u is both unitary and orthogonal, so u is real orthogonal. Put P = tensor product of the p_j. Since A and B are real and tensor product of the u_j is real, PA is real. Also conjugate(P) = P^(-1), since P is Hermitian and complex orthogonal. Hence P^2 A = A. Positive definiteness gives PA = A, and the real u_j transport A to B.

The Gram matrices here use transpose, so they are covariant also under complex orthogonal action. Use exact rational characteristic polynomials and reject a mismatch in any mode. Each Gram matrix is semisimple over C and its distinct eigenspaces are orthogonal and nondegenerate. In matching ordered eigenblock coordinates, make independent orthonormal bases and replace the two vectors in each two-dimensional block by an isotropic pair with mutual pairing one. The local orthogonal group then acts by diag(z,z^(-1)) and the swap, generating all of O(2) since its only isotropic lines are those two lines. On one-dimensional blocks the action is just a sign. Thus the residual group is T semidirect E, where T is an algebraic torus and E is an elementary abelian 2-group. In an eigentuple block of tensor coordinates the action is monomial and the block has at most 2^d positions for order d. All blocks are preserved setwise. Each coordinate action has a weight in the character lattice of T, one entry 0, 1 or -1 for each rotation variable. The argument applies over every algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two when matching Gram matrices have the stipulated semisimple block sizes.

## Processing the reflection coset one block at a time

For any fixed e in E, a torus query on any union of blocks asks whether some t in T maps eA to B on that coordinate set. This can include arbitrary same-format auxiliary values in a new block. After checking support equality, divide corresponding nonzero entries to obtain equations chi_i(t) = r_i. A Smith normal form computation gives an integer basis of all relations among the weights chi_i. Feasibility is equivalent to product_i r_i^(v_i) = 1 for each relation vector v. In fact unimodular changes in source and target turn the character map into independent power maps followed by coordinates fixed to one. Powers are surjective over an algebraically closed field. The computation of the integer bases and their binary lengths is polynomial in the weight table dimensions, by polynomial-time integer normal form. This is the usual torus orbit criterion. [Bürgisser, Doğan, Makam, Walter and Wigderson](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07727)

Maintain the set of feasible e on the processed blocks as an affine coset e_0+H in E, with a basis for H found by binary linear algebra. Initially this is all E. Indeed H is the image in E of the stabilizer S of A on those blocks and the transporter projects to a coset of H. Suppose the next block has b coordinates. Write K = S intersect T and D for the image of K among its b diagonal coordinates. The image of S in the monomial group M_b on the new block normalizes D, since K is normal in S. The map S -> N_(M_b)(D)/D descends to a homomorphism on S/K = H. Denote its kernel by J.

Its image is elementary abelian of exponent two. Its permutation image has order at most b factorial. Its elements with identity permutation lie in the 2-torsion of the diagonal torus modulo D, which has size at most 2^b. To justify that bound, K is cut out by monomial equations, so D is a closed diagonalizable subgroup. The quotient of the diagonal torus by D is a torus, with character lattice the free subgroup of characters trivial on D. This also follows by integer normal form for the defining monomial equations. Consequently [H:J] <= b! 2^b, a constant for fixed tensor order.

The algorithm tests membership in J without producing a lift. Given e in H, check that its permutation on the new block is identity. Then ask whether it can stabilize A on the old blocks and map the all-ones tuple to itself on the whole new block. For identity permutation, the latter says every diagonal multiplier is one. Existence is equivalent to the image of some lift lying in D, since a lift with image in D can be corrected by an element of K. Thus the test is exactly membership in J. For each basis vector of H in turn, compare it with each already represented coset modulo J using this test. If it matches, the difference supplies a kernel vector. Otherwise adjoin a representative and double the list of represented cosets by addition. The list stays of constant size. This constructs all coset representatives and a generating set for J.

Test e_0+h for each representative h by a torus query on the old and new blocks using the actual tensors. All members of the same coset modulo J give the same answer. A lift of their difference stabilizes the old A and is identity on the full new block, so it can be composed on the source side of any witness. If none succeeds return NO. If some succeed, the feasible flips are again a coset by the transporter argument. A new representative is any successful flip, and the new subgroup is generated by J and differences between successful representatives. Repeat through the blocks. The procedure never enumerates all reflection choices and makes polynomially many torus queries with binary exponents of polynomial length.

For orthonormal spectral frames over Q one can use the asymmetric isotropic basis u+i v, (u-i v)/2, giving pairing one and literal swap in the complex group. All residual entries then belong to Q(i). The same holds for auxiliary queries. Product-of-powers equality over Q(i) is polynomial by the Gaussian gcd-free basis method in the torus paper. This proves the stated deterministic bit-time case. For arbitrary raw rational input, computing a common exact number field for all frames can cost exponential time. The next argument bypasses that field.

## Finite-field torus queries without a large compositum

Work in an algebraic closure of F_p for odd p. Given matching semisimple Gram matrices, factor their characteristic polynomials over F_p. For each irreducible factor of degree e at most the relevant side length, list its roots in F_(p^e) and match the roots for A and B using the same list. Find bases of the corresponding eigenspaces by linear algebra. Orthogonalize inside each nondegenerate space by symmetric Gaussian elimination and normalize using square roots in F_(p^(2e)). A nonzero norm pivot can be found among basis vectors and sums of pairs since the characteristic is odd. The isotropic-pair construction only adds i, available in F_(p^2). Each transformed coordinate, and each ratio used in a torus query, therefore lies in an extension of degree polynomial in the input dimensions for fixed tensor order. Taking the compositum of the bounded number of fields used for one coordinate does not require forming the compositum for all coordinates.

Here is an explicit compatible field convention. For each prime power l^a needed in the extension degrees, construct one field of the largest such degree for l. In that field obtain the nested smaller fields as kernels of x -> x^(p^(l^j)) - x by F_p-linear algebra. For a composite degree e take the tensor product over F_p of its selected prime-power fields; these degrees are pairwise coprime, so the product is a field. Natural inclusions give compatible embeddings for divisibility and hence the same result along any composition. Field operations, passage to the image of a smaller field, and conversion between bases cost polynomial time in the represented degrees and log p. Degrees have polynomial numerical size, so their integer factoring is also polynomial by trial division. Randomized polynomial-time finite-field polynomial factoring and irreducible-polynomial construction provide the small field models and roots. One can cap trials with arbitrarily small failure probability because there are only polynomially many operations. See [Bosma, Cannon and Steel](https://www.math.ru.nl/~bosma/pubs/JSC1997FiFi.pdf) for compatible finite-field lattices.

It remains to evaluate a product y = product_i y_i, where y_i includes the signed exponent of an input ratio by modular exponentiation and y_i belongs to F_(p^(d_i))*. The simultaneous field degree may be very large, but the integer

    M = lcm_i (p^(d_i)-1)

has polynomial bit length and y^M=1. Compute a gcd-free basis for the integers p^(d_i)-1, expressing each as a product of powers of pairwise coprime integer bases g_j. This does not require prime factorization of those integers. [Bernstein, Factoring into coprimes](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgor.2004.04.009)

Write M as a product of pairwise coprime factors t_j, each the maximal occurring power of g_j. For each j choose an index k with t_j dividing p^(d_k)-1. By the Chinese remainder theorem compute E_j congruent to one modulo t_j and zero modulo each other t_l. Every y_i^E_j belongs to the t_j-torsion, hence lies both in F_(p^(d_i)) and in F_(p^(d_k)). It can be pulled back through the common subfield of degree gcd(d_i,d_k) and pushed forward to F_(p^(d_k)), using the compatible coordinates. Multiply the resulting elements there to test y^E_j=1. All these tests pass exactly when y=1, since the sum of the E_j is one modulo M. This provides polynomial bit time for each finite-field torus query, with no dependence on the numerical size of the common extension degree.

## Transferring the raw rational input by random primes

For a rational input pair of length L, fix conceptual orthonormal and isotropic frames over the algebraic numbers after the exact Gram spectra check. This step is for the analysis and is not computed by the algorithm. The chosen field containing all frames can be built by adjoining all roots of the mode characteristic polynomials, the square roots needed for orthonormalization, and i. Its degree is at most 2^poly(L): there are polynomially many roots of individually polynomial degree and polynomially many quadratic adjunctions. Polynomial-length linear algebra expressions using the eigenvalues give the frames. All these coefficients have logarithmic algebraic height at most 2^poly(L), by the height bounds for sums, products, inverses, and roots. The same estimates hold for the raw input, all transformed coordinates, and every product-of-powers comparison for any possible torus query below. Each such comparison uses a straight-line expression of polynomial length by binary powering. These bounds need no common-field construction.

For clarity one can preselect a deterministic integer-kernel-basis algorithm, and consider all its relation tests for all prefixes of blocks, all individual flip vectors, and both types of torus query (actual transporter or stabilizer plus all-ones local probe). There are at most 2^poly(L) such prefix and flip choices. Each involves polynomially many nonzero coordinates or nonzero tested differences in the chosen characteristic-zero frames. For every nonzero algebraic value in this finite list, exclude primes dividing the leading or constant coefficient of its primitive minimal polynomial. Include the raw denominators, all nonzero differences between distinct eigenvalues in a mode, and p=2. The logarithm of the absolute value of any such nonzero integer is at most 2^poly(L), since it is bounded by a polynomial in the algebraic degree times the logarithmic height and a degree term. Thus the number of excluded primes is at most 2^P(L) for an explicit polynomial P. Such a polynomial is obtained by bounding the count of operations, root adjunctions and exponent bit lengths in the listed procedures, then applying the elementary height recurrences. Constant-sized formats can be handled separately or by increasing the bound.

At a nonexcluded prime all the frame coordinates are integral at every prime of the chosen number field above p, and the nonzero listed values stay nonzero. Reduction in a residue field therefore gives matching, orthonormal eigenframes with the same block sizes and the same support tests. Zero equalities stay zero. The torus tests in those reduced frames have the same truth values as in characteristic zero, so the reflection-coset decision has the same output. The actual finite-field algorithm can choose any orthonormal frames and any consistent ordering of the matching eigenvalues: its output is exactly the intrinsic orbit comparison in characteristic p and is thus independent of the frame choices used in the analysis. In particular the finite-field algorithm need not find the residue field of the conceptual characteristic-zero frames.

Choose p uniformly among primes with sufficiently many polynomially many bits, for instance in an interval [2^B,2^(B+1)] with B larger than 2P(L) by a suitable absolute constant. A uniformly sampled integer in the interval can be tested for primality, and the prime number theorem gives acceptance probability Omega(1/B) and a negligible chance that an accepted prime is excluded. Cap the number of trials and the randomized finite-field subsidiary procedures, counting their failure in the error budget. Reduce the rational arrays modulo p and run the finite-field procedure just proved. If denominators fail or reduced matrices lack the required spectral structure, the algorithm can output a fixed verdict on that trial. On nonexcluded primes these failures cannot occur. Repetition controls the total error. For real tensors the complexification argument identifies the resulting answer with the original real orbit question. This proves the claimed BPP bound.

The proof uses the Gram-eigenspace reduction of Chizewer, Everett, Mithal and Qiao. The new steps are the bounded-index stabilizer projection for sparse blocks and the modular power-product evaluation across many small fields. No deterministic raw-input bound or result for two-dimensional unitary eigenspaces is asserted.
