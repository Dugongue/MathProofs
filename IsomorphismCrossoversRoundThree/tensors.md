# Dimension-preserving finite-field descent for tensor isomorphism

**Status.** Informal proof. Publication priority is unestablished.

**Theorem.** Fix a characteristic prime $p$ and present $k=\mathbb F_q$ as an extension of $\mathbb F_p$. Given two 3-tensors over $k$ of equal positive format $(n_1,n_2,n_3)$, one can decide whether they are isomorphic over $k$ by a deterministic nonadaptive reduction to tensor isomorphism over larger finite fields of the same characteristic. Every query has exactly the original format and only extends the scalars of the input tensors. More precisely, let $s=\sum_i n_i^2$ and let $m_0\ge1$ be a desired minimum extension degree. Choose distinct primes $\ell\ge m_0$ with product greater than $3^{2s}$. The original tensors are isomorphic over $k$ if and only if they are isomorphic over every $\mathbb F_{q^\ell}$ in the list. The list can be chosen from an interval $[L,2L]$ with $L=O(s+m_0+1)$, using $O(1+s/\log(2+s+m_0))$ queries. Runtime and query bit lengths outside the oracle are polynomial in the explicit input length, $\log q$, and the numerical parameter $m_0$.

In particular, a uniform worst-case algorithm over fields of size at least $n^C$, with bit-time bound $T(n)\operatorname{poly}(\log q)$ for a fixed constant $C$, gives the bound $T(n)\operatorname{poly}(n)$ over every fixed smaller field of the same characteristic, where $n=\max_i n_i$. The reduction does not assume any distribution on the input tensors.

## 1. Prime degree descent for torsors

Write $F$ for the $q$-power Frobenius on the algebraic closure $\bar k$. Suppose a nonempty variety $X$ over $k$ has a right action of an algebraic group $S$ over $k$ such that translation by any chosen point of $X(\bar k)$ identifies the reduced geometric varieties $S_{\bar k}$ and $X_{\bar k}$. Let $C=\pi_0(S_{\bar k})$ be its finite group of geometric connected components, and let $Y$ be the set of geometric connected components of $X$. Then $Y$ is a torsor for $C$, equivariantly for Frobenius. We work with the reduced group and variety. Reduction descends over the perfect field $k$, and neither reduction nor extension to the algebraic closure changes the geometric point sets used in the argument.

For $j\ge1$, a point of $X(\mathbb F_{q^j})$ gives a component fixed by $F^j$. Conversely, if a component $Y_i$ is fixed by $F^j$, choose a geometric point $x\in Y_i$. Then $F^j(x)=x a$ for some $a\in S^\circ(\bar k)$. The identity component descends to $k$ and is connected. Lang's theorem over $\mathbb F_{q^j}$ gives $h\in S^\circ(\bar k)$ with $hF^j(h)^{-1}=a$, so that $F^j(xh)=xh$. Thus rational existence is equivalent to fixing a component.

Now suppose $X(\mathbb F_{q^\ell})$ is nonempty for a prime $\ell$. Its set $Y^{F^\ell}$ of fixed components is a nonempty torsor for the subgroup $C^{F^\ell}$, since the action is equivariant and simply transitive. Its size therefore divides $|C|$. On $Y^{F^\ell}$ Frobenius acts with order dividing $\ell$. All nontrivial cycles have length $\ell$. If $\ell\nmid|C|$ the size of this set is not divisible by $\ell$, and at least one component is fixed by $F$. The previous paragraph then gives a rational point over $k$. In particular, when $X(k)$ is empty every successful prime degree extension has degree dividing $|C|$.

## 2. Apply descent to an arbitrary pair of tensors

For a tensor $A$, let $S=\operatorname{Stab}(A)$ in $\prod_i\operatorname{GL}_{n_i}$. Let $X=\{g:gA=B\}$. It is empty geometrically or a torsor for $S$ by right multiplication. In the geometric empty case every extension query rejects. In the other case, the previous argument applies.

The geometric component group of $S$ has order at most $3^{2s}$. Write the matrices $P_i$ and their inverses $Q_i$ as $2s$ variables in affine space. The equations $P_iQ_i=I$ and $(P_1\otimes P_2\otimes P_3)A=A$ have degree at most three and define an affine variety isomorphic to $S$ on geometric points. An affine algebraic set in $N$ variables given by equations of degree at most three has at most $3^N$ irreducible components. To see the bound, cut successively by $N$ generic linear combinations of the defining equations over $\bar k$. At each stage choose a combination that does not vanish identically on any of the finitely many current components outside the proposed zero set, which is possible over an infinite field. Those components decrease dimension under each cut, while components contained in the proposed zero set are preserved in the intersection. After $N$ cuts, every component outside it is at most zero-dimensional. Thus every irreducible component of the proposed zero set is among the components of this intersection. The sum of degrees of all current components increases at most threefold under each cut, by projective Bézout applied to each component not contained in the cutting hypersurface; components contained in it retain their degree. This proves the required estimate. Connected components are no more numerous than irreducible components.

If $A,B$ are not isomorphic over $k$, and all prime-degree extension tests accept, the product of those distinct primes divides $|C|$ by Section 1. This contradicts the bound. If the tensors are isomorphic over $k$, the same witness works in every extension. This proves the exact decision reduction for every input pair.

## 3. Effective choice of fields and inputs

Choose an integer $L\ge\max(m_0,s,2)$ and enumerate primes in $[L,2L]$ with a sieve. Double $L$ until their product exceeds $3^{2s}$. The prime number theorem gives $\sum_{L\le\ell\le2L}\log\ell=\Theta(L)$, so the first successful $L$ has size $O(s+m_0+1)$ and the selection takes polynomial time, without knowing an asymptotic constant in advance. Retain only a prefix of the primes ending when its product first exceeds the threshold. Each prime contributes at least $\log L$ to the logarithm of the product. The resulting count is at most $1+2s\log 3/\log L$, up to rounding, which gives the stated query bound.

For fixed $p$, Shoup's Theorem 4.1 constructs an irreducible polynomial of each requested degree over $k$ in deterministic polynomial bit time in the requested degree and $\log q$. Use the quotient-field presentation and map each original coefficient by the constant-coordinate embedding. Tower arithmetic uses polynomial-time polynomial arithmetic modulo the two supplied irreducibles. If the oracle expects a single modulus over $\mathbb F_p$, one can convert the tower as follows. Write $N=[\mathbb F_{q^\ell}:\mathbb F_p]$. For each prime $r\mid N$, find the $\mathbb F_p$-linear fixed subspace of $x\mapsto x^{p^{N/r}}$, using repeated Frobenius and linear algebra. An element generates the field as an algebra over $\mathbb F_p$ exactly when it lies outside every such subspace. For a coordinate prefix, count completions outside their union by inclusion-exclusion, with intersections computed by affine linear systems. There are at most $2^{\omega(N)}\le N$ subsets, and each count has $O(N\log p)$ bits. Choose its coordinates one at a time, trying the $p$ possible values, maintaining a nonzero count. The resulting element has degree $N$, so its powers give a basis and its minimal polynomial follows from the relation on the next power. Express the original embedded coefficients in this basis by linear algebra. This provides the usual flat representation in polynomial bit time for fixed $p$.

For prime characteristic supplied as a variable, the mathematical criterion holds without change. A deterministic reduction polynomial in the bit length of the field then needs a suitable deterministic field-construction routine or the requested extension presentations and embeddings as auxiliary data. The argument here uses Shoup's unconditional fixed-characteristic bound. All queried fields and formats depend on the given field, format, and numerical minimum degree. No transporter or component group computation is required.

For the large-field corollary choose the minimum degree at least $C\log_q n$. The chosen extension degrees are $O(n^2+1)$, so each oracle sees only polynomially many field bits and unchanged tensor sides. Multiplying the uniform bound by the polynomial query count and adding the construction cost proves the claimed preservation of the dependence on $n$. This applies to solvers correct on all inputs over the larger fields. Scalar-extended inputs need not be uniform in the larger-field tensor space.

## 4. Two coprime extension degrees can both give false positives

The use of a list with a sufficient product of primes reflects a real obstruction. In dimension twelve the distinct étale $k$-algebras

$$
A=k^6\times\mathbb F_{q^6},\qquad
B=(\mathbb F_{q^2})^3\times(\mathbb F_{q^3})^2
$$

become isomorphic both over $\mathbb F_{q^2}$ and over $\mathbb F_{q^3}$. Their base-field multiplication tensors are not isomorphic. To establish the last claim under independent general-linear actions, use the following observation. If two finite-dimensional unital associative algebras admit invertible linear maps $P,Q,R$ with $R(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$, put $a=P(1)$ and $b=Q(1)$. The identities $R(x)=P(x)b=aQ(x)$ make right multiplication by $b$ and left multiplication by $a$ invertible. Finite-dimensionality and associativity imply $a,b$ are units. The map $x\mapsto a^{-1}R(x)b^{-1}$ is then a unital algebra isomorphism. These independent changes are exactly those induced by ordinary tensor isomorphism on the multiplication tensor, with contragredient action on the input dual slots.

More generally, for any nonempty prescribed list of distinct primes $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r$, put $m=\prod_i\ell_i$. For each divisor $d\mid m$, take signed field-factor multiplicity $a_d=(-1)^{\omega(m/d)}m/d$ at $\mathbb F_{q^d}$, where $\omega$ counts distinct prime factors. The positive and negative parts each have total dimension $m2^{r-1}$. Their distinct field-factor multiplicities prevent a base-field isomorphism. Under extension of degree $\ell_i$, pair the contributions for $d$ and $\ell_i d$ with $\ell_i\nmid d$. The first stays a single degree-$d$ factor over the new base, and each degree-$\ell_i d$ factor splits into $\ell_i$ degree-$d$ factors. The signed contributions cancel by $a_d+\ell_i a_{\ell_i d}=0$. Thus both sides become isomorphic after every prescribed extension. The argument provides ordinary tensor counterexamples by the multiplication-tensor observation.

## Attribution and comparison

The argument uses Lang's theorem and a finite prime-order orbit count, together with a degree bound on the stabilizer. The Mathlib group-action sources include the reusable congruence between a finite set's cardinality and the number of fixed points of a prime-power-order action. This source-level reference is not a Lean verification of the descent theorem. Earlier same-characteristic tensor reductions allow additional tensor data that enforce the base field. The reduction above uses only unchanged-coordinate scalar extensions and applies to the full worst-case decision relation. A targeted search did not establish priority for the dimension-preserving algorithm.

Exact finite controls checked the prime-order component argument on 289,010 affine permutation and prime pairs from cyclic groups of orders 2 through 65 and inner automorphisms of $S_2,S_3,S_4$. They also checked both base types and all extension multiplicities for prescribed lists $(2)$, $(2,3)$, $(2,3,5)$ and $(3,7,11)$, yielding dimensions 2, 12, 120, and 924. All controls passed.
