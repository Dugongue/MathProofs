# Special-linear orbit closure intersection for matrix pencils

AI-assisted informal proof. Orbit closures and transporters are over the complex numbers. Algorithms receive densely encoded rational input. The classification by matrix-pencil invariants is due to Pervouchine. The proposed contribution is an explicit, uniform efficient algebraic reduction to a fixed mixed-tensor signature, including a linear-side construction for binary forms of growing degree.

**Theorem.** For arbitrary positive $m,n$, orbit closure intersection on $\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^m\otimes\mathbb C^n$ under $\mathrm{SL}_2\times\mathrm{SL}_m\times\mathrm{SL}_n$ belongs to TOCI under the efficient algebraic reductions of Lysikov and Walter. There is also a deterministic polynomial bit-time decision algorithm on rational inputs. This gives the complete one-mode-dimension-two case of their Question I.13. No genericity or nonsingularity promise is imposed. [Question I.13 and Definition III.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04639v2).

The algebraic reduction in the square case maps a pencil first to its determinant binary form and then to one tuple in $\operatorname{End}(W)\oplus\operatorname{End}(W\otimes W)$ under $\mathrm{GL}(W)$, where $\dim W=O(n)$. In the rectangular case the same target signature with $\dim W=1$ represents a single determinant or a constant. This defines a uniform map for the multivariate dimension parameters $(m,n)$. TOCI membership as defined in Definition IV.21 permits arbitrary polynomial-time Karp reductions. The object-wise polynomial map established here additionally meets the stronger condition in Definition III.3.

## 1. Classical pencil quotients

We recall the quotient classification to make all input and computational conditions explicit. The square invariant comparison appears in Pervouchine's 2002 and 2004 papers. The rectangular classification is in the 2004 paper. [Pervouchine, 2002](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=im&option_lang=eng&paperid=405&wshow=paper) · [Pervouchine, 2004](https://jolt.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/jolt.345/).

Identify a tensor with the pencil $A x+B y$ of maps from $U=\mathbb C^m$ to $V=\mathbb C^n$, replacing the action on the domain by its inverse transpose. Thus the two matrix sides act by $h(A,B)g^{-1}$. The arrow-space group acts by the natural action on the binary variables $x,y$.

**Square pencils.** When $m=n$, write $f(x,y)=\det(xA+yB)=\sum_{j=0}^n a_jx^{n-j}y^j$. The left and right special-linear invariant ring is $\mathbb C[a_0,\ldots,a_n]$. One verification is to localize at $a_0=\det A$. Left special-linear multiplication brings $A$ to $D_b=\operatorname{diag}(b,1,\ldots,1)$ with $b=\det A$, and the stabilizer of $A=D_b$ acts on $D_b^{-1}B$ by conjugation. Invariants of one matrix under conjugation are generated by its characteristic coefficients: the restriction to the dense set of diagonalizable matrices is symmetric in the diagonal entries, and elementary symmetric polynomials extend to the characteristic coefficients. Conjugation by $\mathrm{SL}_n$ and $\mathrm{GL}_n$ has the same orbits because scalar matrices act trivially. The same argument with $b$ as a Laurent variable applies over the whole invertible chart. Its invariant ring is therefore $\mathbb C[a_0^{\pm1},a_1,\ldots,a_n]$. The reciprocal chart $\det B\ne0$ yields $\mathbb C[a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1},a_n^{\pm1}]$. Companion matrices show that the $a_j$ are algebraically independent: every coefficient tuple with $a_0\ne0$ occurs. Intersecting the two rings in their common fraction field yields the polynomial ring asserted above. The determinant map is equivariant for the $\mathrm{SL}_2$ arrow action. Thus the full invariant ring is the pullback of the binary-form invariant ring and the two orbit closure intersection relations coincide by reductive invariant separation. In particular a pencil with identically zero determinant maps to the zero form; no normalization step assumes a nonsingular slice.

**Rectangular pencils.** Transpose to assume $n>m$. Write $d=n-m$, $m=rd+t$ with $0\le t<d$. Let $P_j$ be the pair of maps $\operatorname{Sym}^{j-1}\mathbb C^2\to\operatorname{Sym}^{j}\mathbb C^2$ given by multiplication by $x,y$, with $P_0$ meaning the zero domain and one-dimensional target. The pair $(d-t)P_r\oplus tP_{r+1}$ has an open dense orbit for the full left-right general linear group. Indeed $\dim\operatorname{Hom}(P_a,P_b)=\max(0,b-a+1)$. For $a,b>0$, the two intertwining equations on the monomial basis imply that the first map has values $x^{a-1-i}y^i p$ on the basis $x^{a-1-i}y^i$, where $p$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $b-a$. To see this, the overlap relations are $yF_i=xF_{i+1}$, and coprimality of $x,y$ gives $x^{a-1}\mid F_0$ before solving the recurrence. The target map is then determined on the products by $x,y$, which span its domain. If $a=0$, the target map is arbitrary; if $b=0<a$, both maps vanish by the same spanning property. Hence the endomorphism algebra on the displayed sum has dimension $(d-t)^2+t^2+2(d-t)t=d^2$. Its invertible elements form an open subset, so the orbit has dimension $m^2+n^2-d^2=2mn$ and is open dense.

A special-linear invariant decomposes into semi-invariants for the two remaining determinant characters. Every nonzero semi-invariant is nonzero on this dense orbit and its character must be trivial on the stabilizer. In particular the stabilizer can rescale any one shift summand simultaneously on its domain and target. If both types occur, a character $\det(g)^a\det(h)^b$ must satisfy $ar+b(r+1)=a(r+1)+b(r+2)=0$, giving $a=b=0$. Only constants remain.

If $t=0$, then $r\ge1$, and the characters are integral powers of the primitive character $\det(g)^{-(r+1)}\det(h)^r$. A nonzero semi-invariant for this character is the determinant of the matrix of multiplication by the transpose pencil,
$$\Delta(A,B)=\det\left(V^*\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{r-1}\mathbb C^2\stackrel{xA^T+yB^T}{\longrightarrow}U^*\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^r\mathbb C^2\right).$$
The map is square of size $r(r+1)d$. On $P_r$, its kernel equations are $xf_i+yf_{i+1}=0$ for $0\le i<r$, where the $f_i$ have degree $r-1$. They force $y^r\mid f_0$, hence every $f_i=0$. Direct sums then prove nonvanishing on the dense orbit. Every possible character power is nonnegative because the scalar action $h=sI$ scales this determinant by $s^{nr}$ and a polynomial has nonnegative homogeneous degrees. For each power, two semi-invariants of that weight are proportional on the open orbit and therefore globally. Thus the ring is $\mathbb C[\Delta]$. The determinant is invariant under the arrow-space $\mathrm{SL}_2$, since every symmetric power of a special-linear two-by-two matrix has determinant one. This proves the full rectangular quotient description and yields its constant or scalar polynomial reduction. A scalar is a fixed one-dimensional trivial tensor representation.

## 2. Binary forms of growing degree in a fixed tensor signature

Let $A=\mathbb C^2$ and $f\in\mathrm{Sym}^n(A)$ for $n\ge1$. Construct a binary addition chain for $n$ by reading its binary digits from the most significant one, successively doubling the current integer and adding one when needed. The chain starts at $1$ and each new integer $j$ is the sum $a+b$ of two preceding entries, with repetitions permitted. Put a named block $W_j=\operatorname{Sym}^j(A)$ for each chain entry, so $W_1=A$, and put $W=L\oplus\bigoplus_j W_j$ with one extra named line $L=\mathbb C\ell$. The sum of all chain entries is $O(n)$, since there is a doubling every one or two steps, so $\dim W=O(n)$.

In the first target coordinate fix $D\in\operatorname{End}(W)$ with distinct rational eigenvalues on the named blocks and on $L$. Its conjugacy orbit is closed, cut out by its characteristic polynomial and its squarefree annihilating polynomial. Its stabilizer $K$ is the product of the block general linear groups. The orbit map has regular local sections: apply spectral interpolation polynomials of the variable conjugate of $D$ to standard basis vectors, choose projected columns that form bases, and restrict to the open chart where the resulting basis matrix is invertible.

In the second coordinate $E\in\operatorname{End}(W\otimes W)$ use the following marked ordered blocks. Each has an inclusion into the full endomorphism by setting all other blocks to zero.

1. **Volume.** Fix an isomorphism $\bigwedge^2 A\to L\otimes L$, extend by zero on $\operatorname{Sym}^2 A$, and include the inverse into $\bigwedge^2 A$. Thus the two blocks $A\otimes A\leftrightarrow L\otimes L$ have products $I_{L\otimes L}$ and the alternating projector on $A\otimes A$. The simultaneous orbit of these blocks under $K$ is closed, defined by the two product equations and their zero-on-complement and image conditions. Each such pair is obtained by rescaling the volume using a diagonal matrix on $A$. A section is regular because the scalar and its reciprocal are supplied in the two blocks. The stabilizer now satisfies $\det(g_A)=\lambda^2$, where $g_L=\lambda$.
2. **Addition chain.** For $j=a+b$ place multiplication $m_j:W_a\otimes W_b\to W_j\otimes L$ and its split inclusion $i_j:W_j\otimes L\to W_a\otimes W_b$ in the two corresponding ordered blocks. Their prescribed products are the identity on $W_j\otimes L$ and the equivariant projector $P_j$ onto the copy of $\operatorname{Sym}^j A$ in $W_a\otimes W_b$. The splitting is explicit: polarize the symmetric tensor into factors of lengths $a$ and $b$, or take the bidegree-$(a,b)$ part of $f(x+x',y+y')$ and divide by $\binom ja$. Multiplication composed with this splitting is the identity. Coefficients in the monomial bases are rational with $O(n)$ bits.
3. **Data.** Put the input form in the block $L\otimes L\to W_n\otimes L$, sending $\ell\otimes\ell$ to $f\otimes\ell$.

These are disjoint ordered matrix blocks even when $a=b$. Chain source pairs never involve $L$, chain target pairs are $W_j\otimes L$ with distinct new $j$, and the only chain stage with input $A\otimes A$ has output $W_2\otimes L$. The volume output $L\otimes L$ and the data input $L\otimes L$ therefore create no collisions.

After fixing all earlier stages, the stabilizer acts on $W_a,W_b$ by a common scalar $\lambda$ times the respective symmetric powers of $g_A/\lambda$. It therefore preserves $P_j$. The next pair has closed orbit precisely the pairs with products $I,P_j$. In fact if $M,I'$ are such a pair then $I'$ identifies $W_j\otimes L$ with the image of $P_j$, and the inverse on that image is $M$. The free change of basis of the new $W_j$ reaches this pair from the fixed pair, leaving all prior blocks untouched. Taking the new block matrix to be $M i_j$ in the fixed identification by $\ell$ produces that change of basis and gives a regular section. This proves closedness and the stabilizer identity
$$g_{W_j}\otimes g_L\ =\ \lambda^2\operatorname{Sym}^j(g_A/\lambda)$$
on the chain output. Equivalently $g_{W_j}=\lambda\operatorname{Sym}^j(g_A/\lambda)$. The induction starts with $g_A=\lambda(g_A/\lambda)$. Here $g_A/\lambda$ is special linear by the volume equation. Every element of $\mathrm{SL}(A)$ occurs with $\lambda=1$. The induced action on the data block is exactly the full special-linear action on the binary form after the two scalar factors cancel.

For clarity, the closed-orbit argument used at every marker is the following. Let $J$ act on $Z$, let $p:Z\to B$ be equivariant and let $Jb$ be closed. If the orbit map to $Jb$ has regular local sections, then over each section chart $U$ the preimage is $U\times p^{-1}(b)$, via $(u,z)\mapsto s(u)z$. A $J$-orbit through a point in the fiber becomes $U$ times its orbit under $J_b$. Taking closures in this product proves that the closure restricted to the marker fiber is exactly the $J_b$-orbit closure. Any common point of two closures can be moved to this fiber. This proves preservation and reflection of intersection. Apply this first to $D$, then to volume, then to the chain pairs in order. The affine data family in the final fiber is a closed invariant linear translate, so its closure agrees with the intrinsic closure of the binary-form representation. This proves correctness for every $f$, including zero. Uniform evaluation takes polynomial bit time and the target is a fixed balanced tensor-tuple signature. Composition with the determinant binary form from Section 1 is an efficient algebraic reduction as claimed.

## 3. An exact polynomial bit-time algorithm

In the square case the determinant binary form is computed by $n+1$ evaluations and rational interpolation, or by polynomial matrix arithmetic. The coefficients have polynomial bit length. We describe exact binary-form comparison to specify the computational cost independently of computing separating generators.

The Hilbert–Mumford criterion for binary forms gives three cases. The zero form and forms with a linear factor of multiplicity greater than $n/2$ have closed orbit representative zero. If every multiplicity is strictly less than $n/2$, the orbit is closed. Indeed the weights of the diagonal subgroup on $x^iy^{n-i}$ are $2i-n$, and all nontrivial one-parameter subgroups of $\mathrm{SL}_2$ are conjugate to a power of this diagonal subgroup. Absence of all half-multiplicity factors gives strict Hilbert–Mumford inequalities. In the boundary case $n=2k$ and the largest multiplicity is $k$. Move such a factor to $x$ by a determinant-one change of basis and then use the diagonal subgroup to take the limit $c x^k y^k$, with $c\ne0$. This limit is a closed orbit representative: the standard unitary-invariant Hermitian form on the symmetric power gives zero moment map on $x^ky^k$, since its diagonal weight is zero and the off-diagonal terms move to orthogonal weights. Its orbit equivalence parameter is $c$ modulo multiplication by $(-1)^k$, because the only possible projective actions on the two factor lines are diagonal and a swap. Only forms in the same one of the three cases can pass the comparison.

Compute the linear factor lines, with multiplicity, by squarefree decomposition and certified complex root isolation after dehomogenizing, keeping the point at infinity separately. In the boundary case at most one selected algebraic factor line per form is needed to choose a determinant-one basis and compute $c$, and then test the allowed signs. For two strict-case forms, there are at least three distinct factor lines. Fix an ordered triple from the first form, enumerate ordered triples in the second, and form the unique projective map carrying one triple to the other. If its representative is $M$ and $M\cdot f=a f'$, membership in an $\mathrm{SL}_2$ orbit requires
$$a=(\det M)^{n/2}\quad\text{for even }n,\qquad a^2=(\det M)^n\quad\text{for odd }n.$$
This follows by trying the two scalars which normalize $M$ to determinant one. Compare the coefficients directly for proportionality and compute $a$ using a nonzero coefficient of the target. There are at most $O(n^3)$ candidate triples.

The exact checks involve at most six complex roots per candidate, so the field degree is at most $n^6$ and one never constructs the splitting field. Here is a bit bound that avoids any primitive-element search. After clearing the denominators, each equality is an equality to zero of an integer polynomial in those at most six roots, of degree $O(n)$ and polynomial coefficient bit length. This follows from the explicit rational formulas for the projective triple map and the degree-$n$ substitution. To test it, multiply by a known integer of polynomial bit length making its evaluated value an algebraic integer, by scaling each root by the leading coefficient of its defining integer polynomial. Every conjugate is bounded by $2^{\mathrm{poly}}$ using the elementary root bound. If the value is nonzero its norm is a nonzero integer; since the field degree is polynomial, its absolute value is bounded below by $2^{-\mathrm{poly}}$. Use certified root approximations with the resulting polynomial precision to distinguish zero from a value above the lower bound. Squarefree decomposition and the distinction of factor lines are also polynomial bit time. [Sagraloff, certified complex root isolation and refinement](https://www.algebra.uni-linz.ac.at/people/mkauers/risc-stuff/issac2013usb/papers/p283-sagraloffA.pdf). This completes the algorithm including the unstable and exactly half-multiplicity cases.

## Prior art and scope

Pervouchine established the classical invariant descriptions for these pencils. We do not claim those descriptions or the binary-form stability criterion as discoveries. The construction here combines them with a bounded-arity, linear-side symmetric-power compiler. The proof applies to every format with one mode two in the exact complex model of Question I.13. It does not give containment for three unbounded modes or solve the general question. No Lean proof, independent human validation, or publication-priority result is claimed.
