# Epimorphisms of Abelian Sylow towers by projective covers and exact marked counts

Informal proofs for complete Cayley tables. Grochow, Srivastava, and Thakkar ask in Open Question 3(4) whether epimorphism testing for all Abelian Sylow tower groups is in polynomial time. This note proves a symmetric source-or-target theorem and a deterministic $N^{O(\log\log(N+4))}$ bound on **all two-prime** tower instances, including mixed exponents on both sides. It also gives exact counting in both regimes. The full polynomial-time question remains open. [Original question](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)

## 1. A marked module counting lemma without exponent restrictions

Let $A,B,C$ be finite abelian $p$-groups with specified action by a finite $p'$-group $K$. Let $\rho:A\twoheadrightarrow C$, $\psi:B\twoheadrightarrow C$ be specified equivariant epimorphisms. On their explicit tables, one can count all equivariant epimorphisms $f:A\to B$ with $\psi f=\rho$, in deterministic polynomial time, and recover one when the count is positive. There is no hypothesis on the exponents or their multiplicities in either group.

Use the coprime decomposition of $A$ into homocyclic lifts $S_j$ of simple $\mathbb F_p K$-modules. Each lift of exponent $p^{t_j}$ is free over $\mathbb Z/p^{t_j}\mathbb Z$ and is projective over the corresponding group ring. The latter follows by lifting over the scalar ring and averaging a linear lift over $K$, whose order is invertible. Distinct simple types do not interact: each composition factor of a homocyclic lift is its simple residue type, and a nonzero map would have a common composition factor in its image. Decompose $B$ and $C$ by the same types. [Coprime decomposition and unmarked quotient counts](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/PortfolioIsomorphismCrossovers/GroupAlgorithms.md)

Fix a simple type $S$ and put $\mathbb D=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb F_p K}(S)$, a finite field. This block's head $B/pB$ is a direct sum of copies of $S$. Each $f_j:S_j\to B$ is chosen independently subject to the linear congruences $\psi f_j=\rho|_{S_j}$ and equivariance. Solve them by Smith form. In a nonempty system its variations are exactly the maps into $Z=\ker\psi$. The residue of such a map in the entire head $B/pB$ corresponds to a column in the multiplicity space over $\mathbb D$; its space of variations is the column space $U_{t_j}$ corresponding to the image of $Z[p^{t_j}]$ in $B/pB$. To verify equality, every map lands in $Z[p^{t_j}]$, and any map into its head image lifts by projectivity of $S_j$ over $(\mathbb Z/p^{t_j}\mathbb Z)[K]$. Every allowed residue has the same number of lifts, computed by the homogeneous system with zero residue. No exceptions need be separated from the target head.

The spaces $U_{t_j}$ are nested when the source columns are ordered by nondecreasing $t_j$. Write the allowed columns as $v_j+U_{t_j}$. The rank distribution of their independent choices is found by keeping only the current rank $r$. Modulo $U_{t_j}$, the preceding span is always the span of the preceding offsets, since their variation spaces are contained in $U_{t_j}$. Let its dimension be $d$. If the present offset projects outside it, all $|U_{t_j}|$ choices increase the rank. If it projects inside, exactly $|\mathbb D|^{r-d}$ choices lie in the preceding span; their affine intersection is a translate of the intersection of the preceding span with $U_{t_j}$. Thus all transition counts depend only on $r$ and computable offset data. Count full target rank, multiply by the uniform residue completion counts, and multiply over the simple types. Surjectivity modulo $p$ is equivalent to surjectivity on $B$ by iterating the powers of $p$. A witness can be constructed by trying possible residue columns one at a time and recomputing the positive completion counts, then solving the individual congruences. It suffices to scan at most $|B|$ residues for a single column. Each arithmetic calculation and counts' bit lengths are polynomial on the explicit tables. This is a deterministic fixed-mark count, with no enumeration of possible marks.

### The same fixed-mark lemma in compact bit complexity

The local theorem remains polynomial in the binary input size when the homocyclic source decomposition and the simple head decompositions are supplied in compact coordinates. For an abelian primary module, give its cyclic moduli in binary, its action matrices on a generating set of the specified coprime group, and the matrices of $\rho,\psi$. Source blocks are explicitly free over the corresponding $\mathbb Z/p^{t_j}\mathbb Z$. Give the residue simple modules and the identifications of their copies in the source and target heads. The module promises and the action of a $p'$-group are as in the preceding theorem. No enumeration in the order of a module or of a coefficient field is needed.

All additive lift and equivariance equations use the cyclic generator images as variables, with the order relations of each generator included. A system of congruences is converted to integer equations by adding a slack variable for each modulus. Polynomial-time Smith form computes feasibility, solution group coordinates, and each projection's kernel order. Head residue variations can either be obtained as the projection of the solution group or from the image of $(\ker\psi)[p^{t_j}]$. To do these operations on compact inputs, represent subgroups of finite abelian groups by generator matrices or congruence systems. Smith form converts between the representations and computes the orders without listing elements.

The finite field $\mathbb D$ is computed as the commutant on one supplied simple module by linear equations on the action generators. An additive basis and the multiplication constants from composition of matrices form a compact field representation. Inversion is a linear solve for multiplicative inverse, and all basis and rank calculations over $\mathbb D$ have polynomial bit complexity. The rank dynamic program needs at most one state per possible dimension at each column. Every exact count has bit length at most the sum of the log sizes of the choices of images of cyclic generators, hence polynomial in the compact input.

A witness can also be recovered without scanning residues. Compute suffix completion counts for each current rank. With the previously selected columns spanning $M$, choose the next rank with positive suffix count. To keep the rank, solve the affine linear intersection $(v_j+U_{t_j})\cap M$ and take any point. To increase it, use $v_j$ if it is outside $M$. If it is inside, any basis vector of $U_{t_j}$ outside $M$, added to $v_j$, works. Such a vector exists whenever the increasing transition has a positive count. The future projection data depends only on the fixed offsets and the nested variation spaces, so the positive suffix count remains valid for that witness choice. Solve the congruences with the selected residues to recover all original generator images.

The same proof works over a finite commutative principal chain ring $R$ given by compact additive coordinates and multiplication constants, with specified uniformizer $\pi$, residue field, and module quotients $R/(\pi^t)$. Require the acting finite group to have order invertible in $R$, the source blocks to be free over their respective $R/(\pi^t)$, and their simple heads and target head decompositions to be supplied. Each $R$-linearity equation can be enforced on the additive generators of $R$, through its explicit scalar action. Compact additive presentations of the ideal quotients and the head coordinates are computed by Smith form, using the additive generators of $(\pi^t)$ obtained by multiplying $\pi^t$ into the additive generators of $R$. Replace every $p^t$-torsion condition by $\pi^t$-annihilation; the projectivity proof and nested rank count are unchanged. This version concerns supplied decomposed modules. It makes no claim about extracting the decomposition in polynomial time from arbitrary compact generators of an action. [Polynomial integer Smith form](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

### Truncated-projective sources over arbitrary finite rings

Let $R$ be a finite unital ring, possibly noncommutative, and $J$ its Jacobson radical. Give finite left $R$-modules $A,B,C$ and specified epimorphisms $\rho:A\to C$ and $\psi:B\to C$. Use compact abelian additive coordinates with binary moduli for the ring and all modules, multiplication and action constants on additive generators, and matrices for the maps. Supply $J$ by additive generators, primitive idempotents for the semisimple head structure, and the relevant simple decompositions of the heads in action coordinates. Suppose the input supplies a source decomposition $A=\bigoplus_j P_j$ and a descending chain of two-sided ideals $I_j\subseteq J$ such that each $P_j$ is annihilated by $I_j$ and is projective over $R/I_j$. This is a structural promise. There is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time algorithm giving the number of epimorphisms $f:A\to B$ with $\psi f=\rho$, and giving a compact witness on a positive instance. The target and mark may be arbitrary modules in these coordinates.

For any $j$ the admissible maps $P_j\to B$ are an independently specified affine congruence system. Its variation maps have image in
$$
Z[I_j]=\{z\in Z:az=0\text{ for every }a\in I_j\},
\qquad Z=\ker\psi.
$$
This is a submodule because $I_j$ is two-sided, and the chain condition makes these submodules nested. Let $U_j$ be their images in $B/JB$. Reduction of a variation map factors through $P_j/JP_j$, with image in $U_j$. Conversely every homomorphism $P_j/JP_j\to U_j$ lifts: precompose with the head projection, and use projectivity of $P_j$ for the surjection $Z[I_j]\twoheadrightarrow U_j$ of $R/I_j$-modules. It follows that all the head columns of a block can vary independently in the corresponding simple-type parts of $U_j$, even when the head of $P_j$ contains several simple copies. This is the exact image description. The number of original solutions per admissible head solution is the uniform kernel size of its projection, computed by additive congruences.

The ring $R/J$ is semisimple, and in each simple type its multiplicity coordinates lie over the finite field $\operatorname{End}_R(S)$. The endomorphism field of a given simple is computed by linear equations on the action matrices and matrix composition. Indeed the underlying additive group of $S$ has prime exponent: its primary components and then $pS$ are submodules, and simplicity and finiteness force a single prime and $pS=0$. Thus its coordinates are over that prime field even when the full ring has mixed additive characteristic. Every field inversion is a linear solve in the endomorphism algebra's additive basis. The nested head-image spaces give the same rank dynamic program above, with at most one state per possible rank for each ordered column. A map is onto if its head map is onto, by Nakayama's lemma or iteration of the nilpotent radical. Construction from positive suffix counts uses only affine-subspace intersections in the head and solving the original lift congruences.

All linear equations in the original modules use cyclic generator images and their order relations. Enforce $R$-linearity on the additive generators of the ring and the source; these relations suffice by biadditivity. Head calculations use $JB$ and $JP_j$ generated by products of the given radical and module generators. Annihilator tests use additive generators of $I_j$. Smith form computes generators, images, and affine fiber sizes without expanding the underlying groups. The log of the count is bounded by the number of source additive generators times $\log|B|$, which is polynomial in the specified input lengths. Head dimensions and integer modulus lengths are likewise in the written input. This is consequently a bound in binary coordinate size, not in the ring or module cardinalities.

For example, given a finite-dimensional algebra over any explicit finite field and supplied idempotents $e_i$ lifting primitive idempotents of its semisimple quotient, one may take source blocks
$$
P_j=Re_{i_j}/J^{t_j}Re_{i_j},\qquad I_j=J^{t_j}.
$$
Each block identifies with the left ideal generated by the corresponding idempotent in $R/J^{t_j}$, hence is projective there. Order by nondecreasing $t_j$ to get the required chain. This applies to modular group algebras as well as the coprime case. No algorithm is assumed for recovering an unspecified source decomposition of this kind from compact group generators. For decompositions with supplied simple heads one may also check nesting directly on the computed head variation spaces; when it holds, the same rank algorithm works even without a supplied projectivity proof. [Finite-ring module algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08365) · [Polynomial Smith form](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

## 2. A source-or-target condition for arbitrary tower length

Write the source tower in a fixed prime order as $G_i=A_i\rtimes G_{i+1}$, with $A_i$ a normal abelian Sylow $p_i$-subgroup. A target compatible tower has $H_i=B_i\rtimes H_{i+1}$ in the same order, using trivial layers when $p_i$ does not divide its order. If a target Sylow order exceeds its source counterpart, reject. Both towers and their complements can be computed in polynomial table time for a chosen order. There are at most $|G|$ prime orders to try, since $t!\le\prod_{i=1}^t p_i\le |G|$. Complements are conjugate by the normal Hall subgroup, so the module property below is independent of the choices of complements. [Babai–Qiao](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2012.453)

Call a coprime Sylow module $s$-regular in exponent if for every simple residue type, all but at most $s$ of its homocyclic simple lifts have one common exponent for that type. This includes ordinary homocyclicity when $s=0$, and it allows unrelated exponents in different simple types. Use the determinant-free marked-quotient theorem already proved for targets with this property. Its local orbit algorithm lists a complete set of equivariant epimorphic marks $B^*\to B$ modulo automorphisms of $B^*$ in at most $(|B^*||B|)^s$ branches when $B^*$ has the property. Duplicate orbits can be merged with the fixed marked test applied to $B^*\to B^*$. The group algorithm lifts recursively over these relative quotient marks. [Prior projective-layer proof](ProjectiveLayers.md) · [Earlier marked recursion and quotient witnesses](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SylowTowerActionRanks/PROOF.md)

**Theorem.** Fix $s$. Suppose some compatible prime order has the property that for each layer $i$, at least one of the two modules $A_i$, $B_i$ is $s$-regular in exponent under its respective complement. Then deterministic polynomial time suffices to decide, construct and count epimorphisms from $G$ to $H$. No bound is imposed on the number of layers, the exponents, the total ranks or the action ranks. The property is recognized by trying the prime orders and computing the coprime modules from the raw tables.

### Cover enumeration

At a layer with suitable target $B_i$, one may simply use $B_i^*=B_i$ and $\gamma_i=1$, the identity map. At a layer where the theorem uses the source property, enumerate candidates $B_i^*$ with action of $L=H_{i+1}$ and epimorphisms $\gamma_i:B_i^*\twoheadrightarrow B_i$. Only simple types occurring in $B_i$ are used. For each type list a sorted, nonempty list of the exponents of candidate homocyclic summands. The sum of their lengths, weighted by the dimensions of their simple types, must be at most $\log_{p_i}|A_i|$. This ensures $|B_i^*|\le |A_i|$. Keep only candidates satisfying the bound $s$.

This enumeration has polynomial cardinality without an assignment of source simple types to target labels. Ignoring the dimension weights increases the count. With a fixed order of target labels, any candidate is then a composition of an integer at most $a=\lfloor\log_{p_i}|A_i|$, with selected boundaries between its parts giving the simple-type blocks. There are at most $4^{a+1}$ possibilities for the composition and the boundary subset. Sorting and the dimension weights are enforced by direct tests. The local target label list is explicit, and only those labels are used. Thus there is no additional enumeration in the order of the complement.

For clarity the required lift of each labeled simple action is effective. Given its representation modulo $p_i^j$, lift its matrices arbitrarily modulo $p_i^{j+1}$. The multiplicative defects at the next layer form an additive two-cocycle in a matrix space over $\mathbb F_{p_i}$ with conjugation action. Summing the cocycle equation in its third argument and dividing by $|L|$ gives a cochain whose coboundary is that defect. Correct the representatives by the cochain. Iterate to the specified exponent. Each operation uses polynomial matrix and table time. The lift of a fixed simple module of given length is unique up to module isomorphism: average a lift of the identity residue intertwiner over $L$; its reduction is invertible, hence so is the lift. Construct the direct sums for each exponent list. Enumerate $\gamma_i$ up to precomposition by $L$-module automorphisms by the local orbit algorithm above, and merge duplicates. For each fixed order the product of the branch bounds is polynomial because $\prod_i|A_i|=|G|$ and $s$ is fixed. The trivial target layer has only the trivial module.

Build a whole cover $H^*$ with these layers, starting at the top complement and working down. Its $i$-th layer is $B_i^*\rtimes H_{i+1}^*$, where the action on $B_i^*$ is pulled back along the already constructed epimorphism $H_{i+1}^*\to H_{i+1}$. The maps $\gamma_i$ therefore assemble into an epimorphism $\gamma:H^*\to H$. Each table has order at most $|G|$. The cover's target modules satisfy the bounded-exception property, since pulling back along an epimorphism preserves the simple types, their equality relations and dimensions, and their exponent multiplicities. The prior marked-quotient solver decides whether $G$ surjects onto each cover.

### Exact coverage of the original fibers

Every epimorphism to $H^*$ composes with $\gamma$ to one onto $H$. Conversely fix an epimorphism $f:G\to H$. On one layer the source $A_i$ maps onto $B_i$, since its image is a normal $p_i$-group and the quotient by the image has order prime to $p_i$. The image of the complement is a complement in $H_i$. Conjugating by an element of $B_i$ makes this the chosen complement. Denote the resulting maps by $\varphi:A_i\to B_i$ and $\beta:G_{i+1}\twoheadrightarrow H_{i+1}$. The first is equivariant through the second.

At a padded layer retain from $A_i$ its **entire** isotypic modules whose residue types occur among the pullbacks of target types under $\beta$. All other components map to zero, since their composition factors differ. On each retained homocyclic lift, $\ker\beta$ acts trivially on the head. Its image on the lift lies in the kernel of reduction modulo $p_i$ of an invertible matrix group over $\mathbb Z/p_i^a$, which is a $p_i$-group by its congruence filtration. Since $\ker\beta$ is a $p_i'$-group, the action is trivial. Thus each retained isotypic module with all of its exponent lengths actually descends to the target complement. The direct sum of these descended modules is one of the $B_i^*$ candidates, and $\varphi$ factors through its projection followed by an equivariant epimorphism to $B_i$. The prescribed orbit representatives include this latter map up to automorphism of $B_i^*$. At an unchanged layer use $B_i^*=B_i$. Use induction to lift $\beta$ into the upper cover. The action on the current $B_i^*$ factors through $\beta$, so the projection and change of basis on the current source module combine with this upper lift. Undo the normalization conjugation by any lift in $B_i^*$ of the conjugating element. This proves that the specified $f$ itself lifts to one of the enumerated covers.

This proof also gives a per-type decision and search refinement. An individual target simple type that is already $s$-regular may be left unchanged while other types at that same prime are padded. If every possible source match of each such other type is $s$-regular, the same completeness argument applies. One recognizes a sufficient condition by comparing only source types of the same residue dimension and whose length list admits the target length list as a quotient. Equivariance of an actual map implies both conditions. The resulting cover list keeps the same polynomial bound. The whole-layer version gives the simpler counting implementation below.

### Exact counts for whole-layer choices

For any padded layer, count only maps $G\to H^*$ that retain each matched source isotypic module in full. This restriction is computable in the target-side marked recursion: at that layer retain only the $K$-module quotient types which keep every nonzero surviving isotypic component with all of its original exponent lengths. The condition depends on the quotient type, since an epimorphism between modules of equal order is an isomorphism. It imposes no restriction on a layer left unchanged. The weighted recursion counts kernels under these filters, followed by all isomorphisms to $H^*$, as usual. Its local fixed-mark fibers can in particular be counted by Section 1. [Weighted quotient recursion](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/PortfolioIsomorphismCrossovers/GroupAlgorithms.md) · [Isomorphism cosets](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26748v1#S1)

For a specified original epimorphism $f$, the full-isotypic condition determines one cover type and one mark orbit at every padded layer. Indeed, which source simple modules match the fixed target labels is determined by its quotient map $\beta$, and their full sizes and types must be preserved in any counted lift. The prescribed map $\varphi$ on these modules determines the mark orbit over $B_i$. Conjugating by an element of the abelian $B_i$ in order to normalize the complement does not change its restriction to $B_i$ or its induced map on the quotient, so this determination is independent of normalization. Therefore the full-isotypic lifts of $f$ all belong to the same deduplicated cover choice.

For a fixed cover and original $f$, count extensions at an individual layer, after first normalizing $f$'s image complement to the chosen one. For a fixed lift of its upper quotient map, normalized lifts on a padded $A_i$ are precisely isomorphisms of its full retained module with $B_i^*$ inducing its prescribed $\varphi$. They form a torsor under the group
$$
\{u\in\operatorname{Aut}_{H_{i+1}}(B_i^*):\ \gamma_i u=\gamma_i\}.
$$
Its cardinality is obtained by the general fixed-mark count. At an unchanged layer that factor is one. To count lifts whose complement image is not chosen, note that complements in $H_i^*$ are parametrized by $B_i^*/(B_i^*)^{H_{i+1}}$. The chosen image complement downstairs is preserved precisely when the conjugating element belongs to $\gamma_i^{-1}(B_i^{H_{i+1}})$. Thus the number of possible upstairs image complements is
$$
\frac{|\ker\gamma_i|\ |B_i^{H_{i+1}}|}{|(B_i^*)^{H_{i+1}}|}.
$$
This is an integer and is independent of $f$, as is the normalized lift factor. Each possible image complement gives the same number of lifts by conjugation, and the resulting maps are disjoint. The original $f$'s number of lifts is the product of these factors over the layers. Divide each count of maps to a cover by its product and sum over covers. Each $f$ is then counted exactly once. The counts, fixed spaces, and products all have polynomial bit length in the table sizes.

## 3. An unrestricted-exponent algorithm for two-prime towers

Suppose the source and target each have the form $G=A\rtimes K$, $H=B\rtimes L$ with $A,B$ abelian $p$-groups, $K,L$ abelian $q$-groups, $p\ne q$. This includes all promised two-prime inputs after trying the two orders and taking trivial layers for absent target primes. Set $N=|G|+|H|$. Deterministic $N^{O(\log\log(N+4))}$ time suffices for decision, search and exact counting, with no restriction on exceptions anywhere.

Normalize an epimorphism by conjugating its complement image into $L$. It then consists of epimorphisms $\varphi:A\to B$ and $\beta:K\to L$, with $\varphi$ equivariant through $\beta$. A simple residue module of an abelian acting group is over a field: the commutative image of the group algebra is a finite field $\mathbb F_{p^d}$, where $d$ is the simple dimension, and the action is multiplication by a character into that field's units. Compute these fields as spans of the action matrices, so their orders are at most the metered source or target module size. If two equal-dimensional types can be matched, the permitted field identifications are the $\mathbb F_p$-isomorphisms of their action fields. There are at most $d$. They are found by choosing a field generator and scanning roots of its minimal polynomial. An intertwiner exists for a specified action equality precisely when the fields are identified in this fashion, since the module is one-dimensional over the action field.

Enumerate injections from target simple labels to source labels. Require equal dimensions and quotient-compatible lists of exponent lengths for each pair. For each allowed injection choose the field identifications. The product of the identification counts is at most $\prod d\le |B|$. Both simple label lists have lengths at most $\log_2 N$. Thus enumerating injections costs at most $(\log N)^{O(\log N)}$. The conditions on $\beta$ within a branch are simultaneous equations of the form $\chi_j(\beta(k))=\eta_j(\chi_{i(j)}(k))$. Combine the target character maps into $\sigma:L\to C$, its action image in a product of unit groups. Compute $C$ by scanning $L$; it has at most $|L|$ elements. The proposed source combined map must have image $C$, since $\beta$ is onto. Now finding and counting $\beta$ is exactly the fixed-mark problem $K\twoheadrightarrow L$ over the specified map $K\twoheadrightarrow C$, with trivial acting group. Section 1 handles arbitrary exponents on both sides.

For every such $\beta$, the isomorphism types of the source module and the pulled-back target module are precisely the prescribed simple labels and exponents. Therefore the number of allowed $\varphi$ is the unmarked equivariant epimorphism count for those module types, independent of $\beta$ in the branch. Compute it by the earlier coprime-module method, or Section 1 with trivial mark. Count different branches disjointly. A given $\beta$ determines unique matched simple labels and unique field identifications, since the character values span the action fields. Finally multiply by $|B|/|B^L|$ to restore the choices of image complement. All witnesses are concrete maps on table labels. This proves the stated bound and the exact count. It applies in particular to the two-prime binary-code family without any dimension promise, although its time there is no better than the $2^{O(r\log r)}$ permutation bound. [Earlier binary subcode correspondence](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SylowTowerActionRanks/PROOF.md) · [Subcode equivalence](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1749)

### Polynomial time when the target action kernel splits

In the two-prime setting above, assume $E=\ker(L\to\operatorname{Aut}(B))$ is a direct summand of the abelian complement $L$, so $L=E\oplus D$. This is checkable by solving additive congruences for a section of $L\to L/E$. Then decision, search and exact counting all run in deterministic polynomial time, with arbitrary mixed Sylow exponents on both sides.

Enumerate only the at most $|A|$ distinct isomorphism types $B'$ of equivariant module quotients of $A$. For one such $B'$, let $C$ be the faithful action image of $K$ on $B'$, and $\rho:K\to C$ its quotient map. Compare the action-faithful groups
$$
X=B'\rtimes C,\qquad Y=B\rtimes D
$$
by the polynomial-time Abelian Sylow tower group-isomorphism algorithm, with its isomorphism coset. An isomorphism identifies the normal $p$-groups and, after conjugating by the normal $p$-group on the target, gives an isomorphism of complements $\alpha:C\to D$. Conversely exactly these normalized maps give the required action equivalence on the two modules. From any full epimorphism with lower quotient type $B'$, the induced complement controls the two faithful action images and therefore gives such an isomorphism.

For a fixed $\alpha$ the complement epimorphisms must be
$$
\beta(k)=(\nu(k),\,\alpha\rho(k)),
$$
where $\nu:K\to E$ is a homomorphism. Such $\beta$ is onto exactly when $\nu(\ker\rho)=E$. This condition and count are independent of $\alpha$. Thus one isomorphism of $X,Y$ suffices to determine the complement feasibility and count by Section 1, using the marked problem $K\to E\oplus D$ over $\alpha\rho$. A nonempty result gives a full group witness.

More explicitly let $b_{\rm lift}$ be this count of complement epimorphisms for one $\alpha$. Let $a_{\rm epi}$ be the number of equivariant epimorphisms $A\to B'$ and $a_{\rm aut}=|\operatorname{Aut}_K(B')|$, obtainable from Section 1 with trivial mark. For each normalized isomorphism $(g,\alpha):X\to Y$, combine any complement map above and any of the $a_{\rm epi}$ maps with $g$ to construct the normalized full map. For each fixed full normalized map the quotient identifications with $B'$ count $a_{\rm aut}$, and precisely those choices produce it. The number of normalized isomorphisms $X\to Y$ is $|\operatorname{Iso}(X,Y)|/(|B|/|B^D|)$, since potential image complements are conjugate with stabilizer $B^D$. Undoing normalization for original maps to $H$ multiplies by $|B|/|B^L|$, with $B^D=B^L$. Hence the contribution of this $B'$ is
$$
\frac{a_{\rm epi}\,|\operatorname{Iso}(X,Y)|\, b_{\rm lift}}{a_{\rm aut}}.
$$
These contributions have disjoint quotient types. The groups $X,Y$, the module tables, and the character-free action quotients have polynomial size in the original input. The isomorphism coset supplies the count of isomorphisms via its automorphism generators. Thus the displayed formula is an exact polynomial-time count. The target splitting condition concerns a direct summand of its abelian complement, so it holds for many mixed-exponent targets without a bound on the number of exceptional summands. [Babai–Qiao isomorphism test](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2012.453) · [Isomorphism coset form](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26748v1#S1)

## 4. Route audit and remaining scope

Explicitly listing all unmarked quotient types of an arbitrary tower cannot yield a universal polynomial bound. The earlier family with a single source complement $(\mathbb Z/4)^r$ already has quotient types indexed by binary codes up to coordinate permutation, giving $2^{\Omega(r^2)}$ types from $2^{O(r)}$ table size. The new cover construction lists only types built from the fixed target's labeled simple modules and uses the source's controlled exponent shape, before selecting a compatible lift. It therefore avoids this obstacle in the stated cases.

Direct joint module compatibility without mark compression works for two primes because the complement is abelian and the simple action fields can be identified individually. Its remaining enumeration is the injection between the simple labels. In the binary-code case the compatibility equations can encode unrestricted permutation subcode containment, so a faster full two-prime algorithm needs a way to test this interaction without trying a factorial-size family. The known NP-completeness of the compact code problem does not settle the Cayley-table running time. This note contains no resolution of unrestricted Question 3(4) or 2. The concrete gains are the symmetric source/target theorem, the general fixed-mark exact count, the all-two-prime bound, and the arbitrary-exponent polynomial algorithm when the target action kernel splits. No Lean verification or publication priority claim is made.


## 5. Relation to known counts

Classical Hall polynomials count subgroups of one abstract type with quotient of another abstract type. Even for finite abelian $p$-groups those types cannot determine the prescribed-map count in Section 1. Hill's example takes $M=C_p\langle a\rangle\oplus C_{p^2}\langle b\rangle\oplus C_{p^3}\langle c\rangle$ and kernels $K_1=\langle a,pc\rangle$ and $K_2=\langle pb,a+pc\rangle$. Both kernels and both quotients have type $C_p\oplus C_{p^2}$, but $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(K_1[p]\cap pM)=1$ while $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(K_2[p]\cap pM)=2$. Let $\psi:M\to M/K_1$ be the quotient map. There is a surjective solution of $\psi h=\rho$ when $\rho=\psi$. There is none when $\rho$ has kernel $K_2$, since a surjective $h:M\to M$ is an automorphism and would carry $K_2$ to $K_1$. The five abstract types in the two marked diagrams agree. Haglund's rank formula for matrices supported on Ferrers boards covers the homogeneous rank calculation when all affine offsets are zero and coordinates make the nested variation subspaces coordinate subspaces. For the prescribed-map problem, the fixed offsets outside each variation space can alter the rank transitions. Matei and Suciu's Lemma 4.4 counts lifts of a fixed group epimorphism through one elementary abelian minimal normal subgroup, and their Corollary 4.8 sums over earlier lifts for a chief series. These are precedents for rank counting and group epimorphism lifting.
