# Independent audit of repeated characteristic-two norm lifting

The theorem in Round2Matrix.md is sound under its nondegenerate-marking and minimum residual-dimension promise. This audit supplies the binary irreducibility and centralizer argument rather than assuming absolute irreducibility over the extension field. It also identifies a small correction to one optional symplectic evaluation list.

## 1. The orthogonal natural module over the prime field

Let $E$ be a finite field of characteristic two and let $(R,Q)$ be a quadratic space with nondegenerate polar form $B$ and even dimension $n\geq 8$. A nonsingular direction $w$ gives the orthogonal symmetry
$$
s_w(x)=x+\frac{B(x,w)}{Q(w)}w.
$$
It has Dickson invariant one because $s_w-1$ has rank one. The group $\Omega(Q)$ is the kernel of the Dickson invariant. In this range it is perfect, so the only abelianization of $O(Q)$ is its Dickson quotient. [Dickson invariant and simplicity in the classical groups](https://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/don/papers/gcg.pdf)

**Orbits.** If $Q(v)=Q(v')$ for nonzero $v,v'$, Witt's theorem extends the marked line isometry to an element of $O(Q)$. If necessary, postcompose with a symmetry fixing $v'$ to make the Dickson bit zero. The space $(v')^\perp$ has dimension $n-1>n/2$ and contains a nonsingular direction, since a totally singular space cannot exceed half the ambient dimension. Thus $\Omega(Q)$ is transitive on the nonzero singular vectors and on each nonzero norm level.

**Binary simplicity.** A nonzero $\mathbb F_2$-submodule containing a nonsingular $v$ also contains a nonzero singular vector $u$. Choose $u$ in $v^\perp$ and a maximal totally singular space, whose dimension is at least $n/2-1$. This intersection has positive dimension. Then $v+u$ has the same nonzero norm as $v$ and hence belongs to the submodule, which also contains their sum $u$. All nonzero singular vectors now belong to the submodule. They span $R$ over $\mathbb F_2$. In a Witt decomposition, every vector on a hyperbolic basis line is singular, and for a vector $w$ in the remaining anisotropic plane, the vector $w+e+Q(w)f$ is singular, where $e,f$ is one hyperbolic pair normalized by $B(e,f)=1$. Thus $w$ is a sum of three singular vectors.

**Binary endomorphisms.** Fix a nonzero singular $e$. The point stabilizer $\Omega(Q)_e$ fixes exactly $Ee$. To see this, fix $x\notin Ee$. Nonsingular vectors span $e^\perp$, since each singular $y\in e^\perp$ is the sum of $z$ and $z+y$ for a nonsingular $z\in e^\perp\cap y^\perp$, which exists by the dimension bound. Choose a nonsingular $w\in e^\perp$ with $B(x,w)\neq0$. Also choose a nonsingular $z\in e^\perp\cap x^\perp$, again possible because its dimension is at least $n-2>n/2$. The product applying $s_z$ then $s_w$ belongs to $\Omega(Q)_e$ and moves $x$. Thus a binary endomorphism commuting with the group must send $e$ to $ae$ for one $a\in E$. Transitivity and $E$-linearity of the group give multiplication by $a$ on every nonzero singular vector, hence on their binary span. Both claims for $O(Q)$ follow as it contains $\Omega(Q)$.

The same claims for the full symplectic group follow from transitivity on nonzero vectors and from the fact that every $E$-line is an image of a transvection minus identity. The quotient of an explicit binary sum of trivial and natural copies is consequently semisimple with precisely those two isotypic types. In the natural part its submodules correspond to $E$-subspaces of the multiplicity space. Intersecting with one fixed $E$-coordinate line in each copy computes that multiplicity subspace and selecting an $E$-complement gives effective quotient coordinates.

## 2. Cohomology and recovering the parameters

Kantor and Liebler's Theorem 2.14(i) has $H^1(\mathrm{Sp}(R),R)$ of $E$-dimension one and $H^1(\Omega(Q),R)=0$ in the stated even-dimensional range. The latter includes both quadratic types. Binary cohomology of the underlying module is the same cocycle quotient as $E$-cohomology, with its scalar structure retained. In the orthogonal case $\Omega(Q)$ has no natural fixed vectors, so inflation–restriction gives $H^1(O(Q),R)=0$. Trivial binary modules have zero cocycle on $\Omega(Q)$ and on the stated symplectic group by perfectness. On $O(Q)$ they have precisely the Dickson multiples claimed. [Kantor–Liebler, Theorem 2.14(i)](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/KantorLiebler.pdf#page=8)

The separating symmetry-products list works. Choose nonsingular spanning directions $v_i$. If all $s_i s_j$ fix a vector $x$, then all $s_i(x)-x$ are equal. Each lies on $Ev_i$, and two independent directions force that common vector to be zero. Spanning then gives $x=0$. One can construct a nonsingular spanning list in polynomial time from a basis. Include a nonsingular basis vector directly. For each singular basis vector $v$, include nonsingular $z,z+v$ using a nonsingular vector $z$ in $v^\perp$. To find a nonsingular vector in the latter space, test basis vectors and their pairwise sums. A quadratic form vanishing on these vanishes identically. Consequently the list recovers the coboundary vector uniquely without enumerating the group. Evaluating one symmetry then gives the Dickson coefficient for $O(Q)$.

The optional symplectic list using only a symplectic basis and one additional direction $e_1+e_2$ need not generate the full symplectic group. It does separate the normal-form parameters, which is all that is used. For direction $v$ and nonzero parameter $a$, let $T_v(a)$ be the transvection $x\mapsto x+aB(x,v)v$. In the Frobenius-normalized module the difference of quadratic refinements contributes $\sqrt{a^2Q_0(v)+a}$ along the $v$-line, while the ordinary coboundary contributes $aB(z,v)$. If $|E|>2$, two distinct nonzero parameters give distinct ratios $\sqrt{Q_0(v)+a^{-1}}$. The homogeneous equations imply the cohomology coefficient is zero. If $E=\mathbb F_2$, a nonzero homogeneous coefficient would give $B(z,v)=Q_0(v)+1$ on $e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2$, impossible by additivity on the first two orthogonal vectors. Then the standard basis directions force $z=0$. The paper can also simply use a standard full root-generator set. This optional-list clarification was sent to its author.

## 3. Physical corrections, parity and exactness

Put $K_k=\{u\in 1+J:c^u=c\bmod J^k\}$. The map $u\mapsto c^u-c\bmod J^{k+1}$ is an additive homomorphism on $K_k$, because the linear action of $1+J$ on $J^k/J^{k+1}$ is trivial. The image is a binary submodule of the current residue group. More explicitly, conjugating $K_k$ by an actual lift of a congruence stabilizer preserves $K_k$, and conjugation acts on its image by that lift's residue. Computing the image from actual generators therefore removes exactly all identity-residue freedoms. The trace image is included by infinitesimal radical corrections, so using the quotient of the Tate module is justified.

Given a residue unit, choose any unit preimage and solve the remaining congruence modulo $J^k$ in $1+J$. Its generators are obtained from binary bases of successive $J^i/J^{i+1}$. Sun's deterministic transporter algorithm applies after restriction of scalars to $\mathbb F_2$, in a direct sum of congruence and faithful regular multiplication representations, with the regular vector coordinate zero. This faithfully retains the original elements and all stabilizer generators. Every chosen allowed residue does have a preimage by the previous exact-image invariant. For the new correction, solve the additive image equation on the actual $K_k$ generators and multiply the selected generators to obtain the physical element. [Sun, Appendix A, Lemma A.24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05920#page=75)

After successful affine transport, the next group is the affine stabilizer of the **target** zero inside the previously retained image. The span of its natural marks is extracted from the normal-form parameters. When this span is nondegenerate, its pointwise stabilizer splits exactly as the identity on the span and an arbitrary classical element on its polar complement. A determinant or parity test elsewhere cannot contribute an additional condition in the symplectic phase. In the orthogonal phase, the only additional trivial-module condition is the Dickson bit. The invariant of an isometry supported on the complement is its complement bit by the rank formula. A nonsingular symmetry there adjusts the bit and fixes all marked vectors. For a zero complement the isometry is uniquely determined and its bit is checked. Hence the recursive groups are exactly the stated $\mathrm{Sp}$, $O$ and $\Omega$ groups, including the terminal trivial case.

The written input promise allows repeated grades but tests nondegeneracy and the residual dimension on the actual extracted spans. All required finite-field operations are polynomial in the explicit coordinate input. The argument supports the conditional Las Vegas polynomial-time theorem and does not resolve the unrestricted matrix-tuple problem. This is an independent informal audit, with no Lean check.
