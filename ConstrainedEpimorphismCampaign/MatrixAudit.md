# Independent audit of the symplectic multi-grade norm argument

Reviewed the informal proof of characteristic-two norm lifting when a full symplectic residue factor participates at at most one obstruction grade. The single-factor affine transporter and the residue-product induction are sound after supplying the explicit binary Tate-module reduction and the faithful implementation of physical lifts below. The proof has the advertised single-grade scope. It gives no reduction of the unrestricted matrix-tuple problem.

## 1. Cohomology and affine transport

Kantor–Liebler Theorem 2.14(i) gives native-field dimension one for natural-module $H^1(\operatorname{Sp}(2m,q),V)$ when $q$ is even and $m\ge3$. The relevant group $\operatorname{Chev}(V)$ is the full symplectic group in this regime. The zero-polar quadratic forms representation is a dual Frobenius twist of the natural module, so yields the same one-dimensional cohomology. Both field conventions can be treated over $\mathbb F_2$: coefficient squaring is an $\mathbb F_2$-linear intertwiner for the Frobenius twist. The underlying natural binary module is simple because the symplectic group is transitive on its nonzero vectors. Any binary endomorphism commuting with the group preserves every native-field line, by commuting with transvections and preserving their one-line images. A binary-linear map preserving every line is a common scalar on all lines, by adding vectors from two independent lines. Its endomorphism field is therefore the native field.

The refinement cocycle $\kappa(g)=Q_0\circ g-Q_0$ is nonzero in cohomology. A coboundary would produce a symplectic-invariant refinement of the defining alternating form. By transitivity such a refinement would be constant on nonzero vectors. Two independent perpendicular vectors show the constant is zero in characteristic two. Its polar form would then vanish, a contradiction. Thus the one-dimensional cohomology bound identifies every affine action as a sum of the stated translations and quadratic-refinement changes.

The simultaneous equations on quadratics are exact. Once one equation with nonzero polar part has been isolated, subtract its proportional polar component from the rest. Taking coefficient square roots converts the remaining zero-polar quadratic equations into linear markings. The necessary partial isometry checks are linear relations, pairings, and, in the quadratic case, quadratic values on a basis of the marked span. Such data determines the restrictions on all their combinations. Equality of the ambient quadratic type and Witt extension then give sufficiency.

The constructive Witt call has a polynomial implementation without enumerating the field. First split a nonsingular part of the marked span by symplectic elimination. Its polar radical admits a basis of isotropic vectors and at most one anisotropic vector, since the square root of the quadratic restriction on the radical is linear. For each marked isotropic vector choose a dual partner by linear equations and make it isotropic by adding a multiple of that marked vector. Require the partner to be perpendicular to all other marked radical vectors. Split off these planes successively. If a marked anisotropic line remains, scale its vector to quadratic value one. A partner of pairing one can be adjusted along the line by solving $t^2+t=b$. If the complement of the proposed plane has positive dimension, adjusting further by a vector in that complement can change the quadratic value by any scalar, since it has some nonzero represented value and all field elements have square roots. If that complement is zero, equality of ambient types guarantees equality of the two absolute-trace classes, so the first adjustment works. This aligns a plane containing the anisotropic line and leaves same-type orthogonal complements. Build a basis of each remaining nonsingular complement by splitting hyperbolic planes. In dimension at least four, two perpendicular nonsingular planes each have a nonzero represented value, which can be made equal by scaling, yielding a nonzero isotropic vector in their sum. The final dimension-two form is handled by its Artin–Schreier type. All these operations use Gaussian elimination and square-root or Artin–Schreier solving in the explicit finite field. [Kantor–Liebler, §2.14](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/KantorLiebler.pdf#page=8) · [Cherlin–Hrushovski, Fact 3.3.6](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~cherlin/Paper/2003FSFT.pdf#page=54)

## 2. The grade Tate modules

This calculation is necessary because the actually achieved correction image $R_k$ may be only binary-linear, and because the base field of the algebra need not equal the center of a residue factor. Let the grade $I=J^k/J^{k+1}$ be an $S$-bimodule from the semisimple residue $S$. For the product of first-kind factors, off-diagonal central blocks $e_i I e_j$ and $e_j I e_i$ are exchanged under the involution, and their Tate quotient is zero. For one diagonal matrix factor $M_n(E)$, split further by the relative Frobenius types of the left and right center actions $E\otimes_FE$, where $F$ is the base field. The involution exchanges the type $\sigma$ with its inverse. Distinct exchanged types have zero Tate quotient. On a self-paired nonidentity twist, $\sigma$ has order two and the involution is $\sigma$-semilinear over the center. Choose $t\in E$ with $t+\sigma(t)=1$. For a fixed vector $v$, the trace of $tv$ is $v$, so this piece also has zero quotient.

In the untwisted diagonal type, the bimodule is a direct sum of copies of the usual matrix bimodule. In tensor notation the involution is the symplectic adjoint on the matrix factor times a linear involution on the multiplicity space. This follows by removing the fixed adjoint from the action on the matrix factor and using bimodule Schur's lemma, whose intertwiner endomorphisms are the center $E$. In characteristic two the multiplicity involution has Jordan blocks of lengths one and two. A length-two block has a basis exchanged by the involution. On its associated pair of matrix copies every fixed element is a trace, so it contributes zero. A length-one block gives the fixed adjoint matrices modulo their traces. Identifying a matrix $X$ with the bilinear form $B(u,Xv)$ sends adjoint to transpose. The quotient is symmetric bilinear forms modulo alternating bilinear forms, exactly the squared dual natural representation via $X\mapsto (v\mapsto B(v,Xv))$. In particular it is a direct sum of nontrivial simple modules over $\mathbb F_2$. This proves the natural-copy statement needed to derive the same conclusion for quotients by the actual correction images.

## 3. Physical lifts and compatibility through grades

Sun's Lemma A.24 applies with $R=\mathbb F_2$ to the action of $1+J$ on the relevant additive quotient. Congruence is linear in the form being moved. If this action has kernel, append the right regular linear action $a\mapsto au$ of $u\in1+J$ on the algebra with zero as the auxiliary test vector. Alternatively convert both actions to left actions using inverses. It is faithful, unipotent, and preserves the auxiliary coordinate automatically. Elements returned in that representation can be read back by the image of the regular unit. Generate $1+J$ by adding one-element lifts of binary bases of all the successive ideals' quotients, since multiplication is additive on each quotient. The same faithful computation supplies generators of each underlying physical stabilizer $K_k$. Evaluating $\delta_k$ on its generators gives the complete binary-linear image. Taking a product with coefficients from a solution of the resulting linear system constructs a prescribed preimage. [Sun, Lemma A.24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05920#page=75)

Use $H_k$ as the full congruence stabilizer of the normalized form $c$ modulo $J^k$, among units with residue in the full classical product. $K_k$ is its identity-residue kernel. The affine cocycle in its residue group is based at $c$. Since identity-residue units act trivially on the grade, the quotient by $R_k$ makes this cocycle well-defined and makes the next residue image exactly the affine stabilizer of **zero**. This stabilizer can differ from the stabilizer of the current source offset. At an active grade, every unused simple factor acts trivially on previous grades and has no additive quotient, so stays available in full. Earlier factors and inactive full factors act trivially linearly on the present grade. Commuting them with each current full factor shows their cocycle values are fixed by all current factors, and the binary module calculation proves their simultaneous fixed space is zero. The factorwise affine tests therefore give the next residue image as a product as claimed.

At each successful grade keep an actual correcting unit. If any full solution $z$ exists and a correction $g$ has just aligned the forms modulo the current ideal, then $g^{-1}z$ is a correction from the current aligned form and belongs to the next full congruence stabilizer. Thus the arbitrary choice of a successful grade transporter cannot lose a global continuation. The algorithm can restrict each new search to the currently active factors, since all discarded actions and translations on that grade vanish and $R_k$ accounts for every available identity-residue correction. Reversing the initial normalization $c\mapsto(a^*)^{-1}ca^{-1}$ sends the normalized solution $w$ to $wa$. This checks the product order.

## Audit status

The cited natural-module cohomology input, physical unipotent procedure, factorwise affine reduction and through-grade compatibility pass with the additions above. An arbitrary marked stabilizer can acquire nonsemisimple natural-module quotients, so the attempted repeated use of the full symplectic cohomology theorem does not extend this scope. This audit is mathematical and informal.
