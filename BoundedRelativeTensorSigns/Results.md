# Exact determinant profiles for tensors with one bounded relative mode

These are informal proofs. Given two explicit three-tensors over a finite field K of fixed odd characteristic, their determinant profile is the set of the three simultaneous determinant square classes of their transporters. In characteristic 3 the square classes are the determinant signs over the prime field. The algorithm below determines the exact profile in deterministic L^{O(k²+1)} bit time when one mode has dimension k over K and L is the written input size, including the field. Its field dependence is polynomial in log |K| for every fixed k. Rectangular slices, identically singular nets, and positive-dimensional label stabilizers are all allowed.

For raw F₃ input under the earlier relative-mode promise, one can recover the coefficient field of each local block and apply the algorithm there. In particular the deterministic polynomial-time result for relative mode dimension two extends to dimension three and every fixed relative dimension. Two further reductions below address classes in which all three mode dimensions grow.

## 1. The linear equations after fixing one mode

Use the convention that a tensor is a linear map from its label space to the space of matrices from X to Y. In bases write source slices M_i and target slices N_i. For an invertible candidate label map g, set N_g(i) = Σ_j g_{ji}N_j. The other two transporter matrices P and Q must satisfy
\[
 N_g(i)P=QM_i
\]
for each i. Write these as A(g)x = 0 in the joint coordinates x of (P,Q). The entries of A(g) are affine linear in the entries of g.

Compute the fixed source algebra E of pairs (U,V) satisfying M_iU=VM_i for every i. This is a matrix algebra under componentwise composition. The space W(g) = ker A(g) is a right E-module under (P,Q)(U,V)=(PU,QV). If one invertible pair x₀ exists, composition identifies W(g) with the regular right E-module x₀E. The invertible pairs are then exactly x₀E^×.

Compute J = rad E and E/J = ∏_j Mat_(d_j)(K_j), with explicit maps and lifts, by finite-field algebra algorithms. Compute the exact subgroup H of attainable pairs of determinant square classes of E^× on X and Y. On the filtration by powers of J, the contribution of a quotient matrix U_j in factor j is Norm_(K_j/K)(det U_j) raised to the total simple multiplicity on the selected mode. Thus the two parity multiplicities give one generator per factor. The norm is onto and quotient units lift. This computes H once for the source, because 1+J acts unipotently. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

## 2. Polynomial formulas spanning the live kernel

For linearly independent polynomials f_1,…,f_r of degree less than m, consider the r by r matrix with entries f_j(ω^i t), for 0 ≤ i < r. Suppose the multiplicative order of ω is at least m. Its determinant is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most r(m−1). To prove this, make an invertible change of basis so the f_j have distinct leading degrees. The leading coefficient of the determinant is then a product of the leading coefficients times a Vandermonde determinant at the distinct powers of ω corresponding to those degrees. A grid of more than r(m−1) scalars therefore contains a nonzero evaluation. The same proof works when polynomial coefficients are in an extension field and ω is in K. [Folded Vandermonde rank condensers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.7455)

Apply this fact first to r linearly independent columns of a rank-r matrix A and then to the transpose of the compressed matrix. It supplies a polynomial list of pairs of constant matrices L_r,R_r of shapes r by rows(A) and columns(A) by r. At rank r, at least one pair makes B=L_r A R_r invertible. Include r=0 with the empty determinant equal to one. For each such candidate with A=A(g), write D=det B, and let w_1(g),…,w_m(g) be the columns of
\[
 D I-R_r\operatorname{adj}(B)L_r A(g).
\]
These are polynomial formulas of degree polynomial in the input size. At any actual rank-r instance with D≠0, they span ker A(g). Indeed L_r is injective on the image of A(g) and A(g)R_r spans that image. The displayed matrix has image in the kernel, and restricts to multiplication by D on the kernel.

## 3. A polynomial list of live invertible pairs

Choose fixed lifts e^(j)_(1a)∈E of the elementary units in rows 1 and columns a of each quotient factor j. For each candidate from Section 2, add a second grid parameter t and form the polynomial pair
\[
 x_t(g)=\sum_j\sum_{a=1}^{d_j}
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\omega^{a-1}t)^{i-1}w_i(g)\right)e^{(j)}_{1a}.
\]
All operations here are on explicit matrices. There is a polynomial-size grid of t for which one formula gives an actual invertible pair whenever one exists for g.

For the proof use any true invertible x₀ at that g and identify W(g)=x₀E. On a correct rank choice, the first columns of the quotient projections of x₀⁻¹w_i span K_j^{d_j}. Multiplication by e^(j)_(1a) places the first column in column a. Hence the j-th quotient matrix of x₀⁻¹x_t has determinant equal to a folded Vandermonde determinant for d_j linearly independent polynomials. Its polynomial is nonzero of degree at most d_j(m−1). The sum of those degree bounds over all factors is polynomial. A grid larger than that sum contains a t for which every quotient factor is invertible. An element invertible in E/J is invertible in E. Thus x_t is invertible and satisfies the two remaining mode equations. This simultaneous construction avoids branching separately on every simple factor.

Choose the grid sizes and a bound M on the order required for ω from the explicit matrix dimensions and factor sizes. All are polynomial in L. If |K| is below a sufficiently large polynomial threshold, enumerate all label matrices, at cost |K|^{k²}, and use constructive fixed-label module isomorphism. Otherwise scan a polynomial number of field elements until ω^j≠1 for each 1≤j<M. The polynomial ∏_(j<M)(z^j−1) has fewer than M² roots. The field also has enough elements for the grids. Both searches therefore terminate without enumerating a large field. For the small-field branch, the fixed-label module algorithm is elementary given the finite-algebra theorem. Form the endomorphism algebra of the direct sum of the two tuples. The two projections represent isomorphic summands precisely when their ranks agree in every matrix factor of its radical quotient. Choose mutually inverse corner maps in the quotient, lift them and invert their products in the corners. This gives one actual invertible pair. Its other sign options are its translate by H.

## 4. Three sign tests using a single bounded-variable equation

For every polynomial candidate x_t(g)=(P_t(g),Q_t(g)), impose the direct equations A(g)x_t(g)=0 and
\[
 u\,\det g\,\det P_t(g)\,\det Q_t(g)=1 .
\]
These checks make every accepted candidate sound even at parameters where its rank choice fails. For a requested square-class triple choose c_i equal to one or a fixed nonsquare to match the requested bit. Impose three further equations z_i²=c_i D_i, where the D_i are the three determinants. All these constraints are on the same candidate pair and the same label map. If checking a requested profile, also try its translations by the fixed-label subgroup H on the two remaining modes. Completeness follows because at each feasible g one candidate supplies a true x_t, and all invertible pairs for that g differ by a unit of E. Soundness follows by composing positive candidates with the actual units realizing H.

There are k²+4 variables. The number of equations and their degrees are polynomial in the written tensor input size. Pack all s equations f_i=0 into one polynomial over K as follows. Build a cubic Artin–Schreier tower whose degree h over K satisfies s≤h<3s. At each step in characteristic 3, choose an element a of nonzero trace to F₃ by testing basis vectors, and adjoin a root of Z³−Z−a. The image of x↦x³−x is the trace-zero subspace, so this polynomial has no root and is an irreducible cubic. The trace is nonzero as a linear map since its polynomial degree is less than the field order. Use s independent elements b_i in the resulting extension. Compute
\[
 F=\operatorname{Norm}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_i f_i\right)
\]
as the determinant of its multiplication matrix. On K-rational parameter values, F=0 if and only if every f_i=0. The extension is used to construct the equation, with all solutions counted over K itself. For another fixed odd characteristic p the same construction uses the degree-p Artin–Schreier polynomial. A nonsquare can be found deterministically by repeatedly extracting a square root of −1 until there is no further root, using fixed-characteristic univariate factoring. Each step doubles the order.

Apply Lauder–Wan to count the K-points of F=0 and test positivity of the count. Proposition 36 with the torus decomposition of Section 6.4 supplies a single-field point count whose exponent is linear in the variable count. The norm determinant and all other polynomial formulas have polynomial degree, and their dense representations take L^{O(k²+1)} time and space using polynomial arithmetic and division-free determinants. This gives the stated bit-time bound. The constant case F identically zero or a nonzero constant is handled directly. [Lauder–Wan, Proposition 36 and Section 6.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0612147)

This handles fixed characteristic with the field supplied as an irreducible polynomial over its prime field. A field given by a multiplication table converts deterministically to that form. For each prime power ℓ^a exactly dividing its degree e, find an element of the unique subfield of degree ℓ^a outside its subfield of degree ℓ^(a−1) by Frobenius fixed-space linear algebra. Sum those choices. If a Frobenius power fixes the sum, each summand's difference lies in the intersection of its subfield with the compositum of the others, hence lies in the prime field. For ℓ different from the characteristic, iteration through a power-of-ℓ orbit forces that difference to vanish. The remaining difference vanishes by the sum relation. Thus the sum has degree e. Its minimal polynomial is obtained by linear dependence of powers.

## 5. Apply the profile algorithm to raw F₃ blocks

On concise input solve the linear equations saying the centroid actions in the three roles agree through the tensor. The centroid is commutative: applying two centroid maps in different input roles gives commuting output actions, and spanning and zero annihilators give the same commutations in the input modes. Its cube-fixed subalgebra is a product of copies of F₃ with one factor per local summand. For each basis element a of that subalgebra, the three projectors 1−(a−c)² for c∈F₃ split its coordinate values. Refinement finds its primitive idempotents. Their images in the modes are the primitive tensor blocks. The radical is the kernel of a sufficiently high iterated cubing map.

For a block with residue degree e the coefficient field is the fixed subspace of x↦x^(3^e) of its local centroid. Reduction injects that field into the residue field because no nonzero nilpotent is fixed. Any residue lifts to a fixed element by repeatedly applying this power until the nilpotent error vanishes. This coefficient field acts through the tensor in its three roles and gives a bilinear map over K. All original transporters are semilinear with one common field map. A reference field identification between the endpoints is obtained from a primitive factor of the tensor product of the two fields, via the same fixed-space idempotent construction. Enumerate the e possible identifications and compute K-linear profiles in the resulting frames. The raw determinant bits are the computed square classes, plus the determinants of the chosen field and raw frame changes. Restriction of scalars sends the determinant to the norm, whose square class over F₃ is the original character. Dualizing a mode leaves the sign unchanged.

If every primitive block has some relative mode of size at most a fixed k, compute the local profiles for all pairs of blocks. A perfect matching in the graph of nonempty profiles gives a base profile. The subgroup of possible corrections is generated by the local self-profiles and by the format-parity vector for each ordinary type that occurs more than once. The latter arises by swapping two copies using a block isomorphism and its inverse. Every other block permutation factors into such transpositions. After adding the frame calibration, test membership of the all-positive triple in the resulting affine binary space. Nonconcise inputs first compact to the intrinsic support, and each deficient ambient mode permits an independent sign on its unused complement. This computes the exact raw verdict in deterministic polynomial time. A fixed yes or no two-sign pair gives a many-one reduction.

## 6. A structural partition that reduces the number of live variables

The proof works more generally if there are polynomially many explicit polynomial matrix maps g(y) covering all possible label choices, up to a known sign correction subgroup. The actual tensor equations discard any extra choices. The same argument costs L^{O(b+1)} when there are b scalar parameters and all chart degrees are polynomial. In the small-field branch enumerate the b-parameter values. This gives an intrinsic partition by the pair-adjoint module, even when every raw and relative mode is large.

In particular take a pair-adjoint algebra A acting on two modes, with remaining mode fixed. On concise input both projections are faithful, by the defining adjoint equation. Consider one of its acted modes U as the label mode in the algorithm above, using dual conventions if needed. Put J=rad A and choose a semisimple complement S of J in A. Below we give the construction and conjugacy argument. Use b=dim_K End_S(U). Write A/J=∏_j Mat_(d_j)(K_j), with U of simple multiplicities r_j when restricted to S. Then
\[
 b=\sum_j[K_j:K]\,r_j^2.
\]
All r_j are positive by faithfulness. Thus the number of factors and their center degrees are bounded. One can enumerate the factor correspondences and center isomorphisms in constant count for fixed b. Matrix units and the centers construct a reference complement isomorphism φ₀ over each choice. An actual transporter conjugates A to its target counterpart and sends S to a semisimple complement there. First conjugate its image complement to the chosen target one by a unit in 1+J at the target, which has positive determinant. Its resulting complement map with those center data differs from φ₀ by conjugation by a target complement unit. This follows by transporting matrix-unit bases in each simple matrix algebra. Compose the actual transporter with the associated pair-adjoint unit to normalize its complement map to φ₀. Compute the exact signs of the pair units by the multiplicity formula in Section 1.

In a normalized transporter the label map is an S-module intertwiner through φ₀. The possible linear intertwiners form an explicit linear space. If it contains an isomorphism then it has dimension exactly b, by composition with that isomorphism. Discard references with a larger dimension and parameterize any remaining space by its computed basis. Test actual tensor equations and all three signs with the prescribed correction subgroup. Completeness is the normalization just proved, and soundness uses the actual normalized transporter and actual pair-unit corrections.

For construction of the complement, lift the diagonal matrix-unit idempotents of the quotient successively in complementary corners. If a lift x is idempotent modulo J, a sufficiently high characteristic-power of x is idempotent because x²−x is nilpotent and commutes with x. In each matrix factor lift the off-diagonal units E_(a1), E_(1a) to P_a,Q_a in the corresponding corners. Replace Q_a by (Q_aP_a)⁻¹Q_a. Then Q_aP_a and P_aQ_a are the matching corner identities. For the second equality use that the product is idempotent and invertible modulo J, hence is the identity. The products P_aQ_b form exact matrix units. Lift a field generator of K_j into the first diagonal corner and apply Newton iteration to its separable minimal polynomial. The derivative is invertible there because it is invertible modulo the radical. Corrections are polynomials in the chosen lift, so the error squares at each step and reaches zero. This gives an explicit copy of K_j in that corner. Transfer it using the matrix units to obtain S.

For conjugacy of complements inducing the same quotient identifications, let E_(ab) and F_(ab) be their exact matrix units. The unit u=Σ_(j,a) F^(j)_(a1) E^(j)_(1a) is one modulo J and satisfies F_(cd)u=uE_(cd) in each factor. After this conjugation align the field copies in each first corner. For two field maps i,j agreeing modulo J, use the separability idempotent ∑_h x_h⊗y_h of K_j/K with ∑_h x_h y_h=1. It can be constructed as the primitive idempotent of the multiplication factor of K_j⊗_K K_j. The element v=∑_h j(x_h)i(y_h) is one modulo the corner radical and j(a)v=vi(a) by the separability identity. Copy v into the other diagonal corners using the matrix units. The product correction over the factors is a unit in 1+J and identifies the complements. These constructions take polynomial time by finite-field linear algebra and the quotient computation. Thus the parameter b is independent of all choices.

For fixed cutoffs, partition by the two intrinsic numbers given by the smallest relative mode dimension and the smallest b over the pair adjoints and their acted modes. Compare each to its cutoff. Three of the four cells close, since a small value of either parameter suffices. The cell in which both parameters exceed their cutoffs remains open in general. This partition is determined by mode dimensions and the represented simple factors of pair-adjoint algebras.

For example, choose growing even d,m and use the bilinear map
\[
 ((u_i)_{i=1}^d,(v_i)_{i=1}^d)\longmapsto
 \sum_{i=1}^d u_i\otimes v_i
\]
with u_i∈K⁴ and v_i∈K^m. The relative mode dimensions are 4d, md and 4m. The pair adjoint on the first two modes is Mat_d(K), which follows by comparing the d by d operator blocks in the two adjoints and using that the base outer product has scalar pair adjoint. The commutant on the first mode has dimension 16. Both acted dimensions of a single copy are even, so the determinant signs of every pair unit are positive on both modes. The partition therefore includes wide examples even in the absence of a usable sign-changing unit from that pair.

## 7. A direct sign relation on wide square slice spaces

There is a different intrinsic condition that enforces a sign without any parametrization of the label group. Work in fixed-field coordinates, or in recovered local field coordinates with the raw sign calibrated by the determinant norm. Let the square slice space S be a right translate M A of a unital matrix algebra A, with M invertible. Recover A as {a: S a ⊆ S} by linear equations. Recognize the promise by the constructive right-module isomorphism test between S and the regular right A-module. The image of one generates S. If the promise holds then all such generators differ by right multiplication by a unit and are invertible. Test the found generator for invertibility. This gives the exact raw recognition and an anchor.

Write A/rad A=∏_j Mat_(d_j)(K_j) and let m_j be the total multiplicities in the radical filtration of the natural module X. For a slice x let a(x)=M⁻¹M(x) and let a_j(x) be its component in the quotient. The determinant on X is the product of the norms of det a_j(x) raised to m_j. Thus the raw determinant polynomial has the form
\[
 \det M(x)=\det(M)\, f(x)\,h(x)^2,
 \qquad f(x)=\prod_{j\mid m_j\ {\rm odd}}\operatorname{Norm}_{K_j/K}(\det a_j(x)).
\]
The line of f is intrinsic. A different anchor multiplies each quotient matrix by a fixed unit on the left, which scales its determinant by a constant. A transporter conjugates the represented input algebra, permutes its factors, and respects their multiplicities. The coordinate Frobenius has the same dimension exponent in the two square matrix modes, so those factors cancel in the comparison.

If every m_j is even, the product of the two raw matrix-mode signs is the known ratio of the two anchor determinant signs, including frame calibration. Drop this fixed character, keeping two complementary rows. The matrix sizes and the number and size of factors may grow. For example, take S given by Mat_r(F₃) acting on two copies of its natural module, with r even. Its label dimension r² and its two matrix dimensions 2r all grow, and the anchor test gives the sign relation.

More generally if w=Σ_(j:m_j odd) d_j[K_j:K] is bounded, compute f in the explicit degree-w polynomial space on the label. Under the actual label map the one-dimensional line of f has a multiplier whose norm supplies precisely the missing matrix-mode character, up to the known anchor ratio. To see this, take the ratio of the two determinant polynomials under the action relation. Their squared parts contribute a square. The transported f is a scalar times the original f after the common coefficient identification. The ratio of the squared parts is constant because the remaining factors are constant, so it is a square in K. Mark the required square class on the one-dimensional K-line by a square-root multiplication map from two copies of an auxiliary K-line. In endpoint coordinates its extension condition is that the multiplier differ from the calibrated class by a square. Use the fixed-sign compiler to keep the two other independent characters on the same raw transporter. Since w is bounded, the symmetric-power role and its multiplication maps are polynomial size. This supplies a cut by actual determinant multiplicities even when the large label group is never recovered. ## 8. Scope of the geometric routes

For dimension three, a finite projective frame in the determinant factors or in the finite rank-drop set also gives a direct finite-candidate algorithm. Such a frame is unnecessary for the general bounded-width theorem. Rank geometry alone can leave out the needed information. For example, the two nets xI+yE₁₂+zE₁₃ and xI+yE₂₁+zE₃₁ in size four both have determinant x⁴, and their ranks at every projective label agree. On the intrinsic singular line x=0 the first has total slice-image span of dimension one and the second has total slice-image span of dimension two. That span is preserved by every transporter. Thus these nets are inequivalent despite identical rank geometry.

The full arbitrary-width three-to-two reduction remains open in this work. The bounded-width proof replaces an enumeration with polynomial dependence on log |K|, and the two wide families give concrete sufficient domains by intrinsic structure. The proof does not require a novelty claim for these statements. A baseline for the ordinary comparison by label-map enumeration appears in the tensor-isomorphism-completeness paper. [Grochow–Qiao](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full)
