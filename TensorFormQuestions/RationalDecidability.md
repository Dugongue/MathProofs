# Decidability of rational linear-algebraic orbit equivalence

This note records a classical consequence of Sarkisyan's algorithmic Galois cohomology theorem and the Kneser–Harder–Chernousov Hasse principle. It explains the affirmative answer to the rational cubic-form decidability question discussed by Agrawal and Saxena and the equivalent tensor-isomorphism question discussed by Grochow and Qiao. The argument gives a terminating algorithm and is informal. [Agrawal–Saxena](https://www.cse.iitk.ac.in/%75sers/manindra/algebra/algebras-cubicforms2.pdf) · [Grochow–Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

**Theorem.** Let \(k\) be a number field specified in an exact algebraic encoding. Given a linear algebraic \(k\)-group \(G\), an explicitly specified algebraic action on an affine \(k\)-variety \(V\), and \(v,w\in V(k)\), it is decidable whether there is \(g\in G(k)\) with \(g\cdot v=w\). If the answer is affirmative, an algorithm can produce such a \(g\). The data of the group and its action may vary with the input.

Define the stabilizer \(H=\{h\in G:h\cdot v=v\}\) and transporter \(X=\{g\in G:g\cdot v=w\}\). Embed \(G\) in a general linear group and use an extra variable for the inverse determinant to write both sets by polynomial equations. If the transporter is geometrically empty, reject. This case is decidable by Gröbner basis calculation. Otherwise, for any geometric point \(x_0\), multiplication on the right identifies \(X_{\bar k}\) with \(H_{\bar k}\) by \(h\mapsto x_0h\). In characteristic zero a linear algebraic group is smooth, and its geometric connected components are the translates of its identity component \(H^\circ_{\bar k}\). Each is irreducible, and the components are pairwise disjoint. The same properties hold for \(X_{\bar k}\).

Compute the geometric irreducible components of \(H\) and \(X\) by absolute primary decomposition. Their ideals have generators over an effectively computable finite extension, which can be enlarged to be Galois over \(k\). Applying its finite Galois group to the generators and comparing ideals computes the permutation of components. A point rational over \(k\) belongs to exactly one component, so its component must be Galois-fixed. Each Galois-fixed component descends effectively to \(k\), by linear Galois descent on a Galois-stable finite-dimensional space of generators. The identity component is identified by testing the identity matrix and is defined over \(k\).

For each Galois-fixed transporter component \(C\), find an algebraic point \(x_0\in C\) by enumerating tuples over \(\bar k\) and testing its equations. This terminates because \(C\) is nonempty. Enlarge the finite Galois extension to \(L/k\) containing its coordinates. For \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)\), form

\[
c_\sigma=x_0^{-1}\sigma(x_0).
\]

The two points lie on the same component \(C=x_0 H^\circ\), so \(c_\sigma\in H^\circ(L)\). The relation \(c_{\sigma\tau}=c_\sigma\,\sigma(c_\tau)\) makes this a finite description of a Galois 1-cocycle. Its class in \(H^1(k,H^\circ)\) is trivial exactly when \(C(k)\) is nonempty. Indeed, if \(c_\sigma=b^{-1}\sigma(b)\) for \(b\in H^\circ(\bar k)\), then \(x_0b^{-1}\) is Galois-fixed and belongs to \(C(k)\). Conversely, if \(x\in C(k)\), take \(b=x^{-1}x_0\).

Sarkisyan gives an algorithm to decide whether explicitly specified Galois 1-cocycles of a linear algebraic group over a number field are cohomologous, under the Hasse principle for the simply connected cover of the semisimple quotient. Apply it to the connected algebraic group \(H^\circ\), the finite cocycle above and the identity cocycle. Its hypothesis holds by Kneser–Harder–Chernousov for every semisimple simply connected group over a number field. This test applies uniformly to the computed algebraic group and cocycle data. The input transporter has a rational point exactly when at least one of the finitely many component tests accepts. On an affirmative instance, enumerate \(k\)-points in the ambient affine coordinates until the exact equations hold. [Sarkisyan, Galois cohomology and some questions of the theory of algorithms](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/sm2659) · [Kneser–Harder–Chernousov as stated by Borovoi, Theorem 5.0.3](https://archive.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/id/eprint/1514/1/preprint_1989_90.pdf)

For ordinary tensor isomorphism, use the product of the mode general linear groups and their tensor product action. For special-linear tensor isomorphism, replace each mode group by its special linear subgroup. For homogeneous form equivalence, use the general linear action on the coefficient vector space. For a finite-dimensional algebra in a basis, view its multiplication as a tensor in \(V^*\otimes V^*\otimes V\) under simultaneous change of basis, and add a unit vector if needed. Each action is polynomial in matrix entries and inverse determinant coordinates. The theorem therefore gives decidability for all these basis-explicit problems over number fields, including \(\mathbb Q\). It does not establish a polynomial-time reduction between the decision problems or address algebras that are finitely generated but infinite-dimensional over the field.
