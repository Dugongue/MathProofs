# Linear-size realization of tensor isomorphism extension spectra

For two tensors over \(\mathbb F_3\), the ordinary extension spectrum records the positive degrees \(f\) over which they are isomorphic after extending scalars to \(\mathbb F_{3^f}\). The special-linear spectrum instead requires literal determinant one in all three modes over that field. Write \(d\mathbb N_{>0}\) for the set of positive multiples of \(d\).

**Theorem.** Let \(d_1,\ldots,d_s\) be positive integers and \(U=\bigcup_i d_i\mathbb N_{>0}\). There are two same-format concise multiplication tensors of unital associative \(\mathbb F_3\)-algebras whose ordinary and special-linear extension spectra are both \(U\). Their mode dimension is

\[
O\left(1+s+\sum_{i=1}^s\sum_{p\mid d_i}p^{v_p(d_i)}\right),
\]

where the inner sum is over distinct primes. The underlying algebras have square-zero Jacobson radical. The tables are deterministically constructible in polynomial time in \(1+\sum_i d_i\) from unary generators, or in polynomial time in the displayed dimension bound and the length of the factored list when the prime-power factorizations are supplied. No field of degree \(\operatorname{lcm}(d_1,\ldots,d_s)\) is constructed.

The earlier [MathProofs extension-spectrum characterization](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/OptimalMaxRankAndDescent/ExtensionSpectra.md) addresses which sets can occur. This construction fuses the inverse direction with graph parity, finite-field descent and determinant-neutral matrix amplification to control the size. For instance, choosing distinct primes gives the union of their multiples with dimension linear in their sum. The splitting degree of the displayed Frobenius permutation in that example is their product.

## 1. One parity constraint combines all divisibility tests

Set \(k=\mathbb F_3\). First construct two finite directed multigraphs \(Q_0,Q_1\), each equipped with a designated Frobenius permutation \(\sigma_0,\sigma_1\). All nonloop arrows described below occur once. We use directions and a constant number of vertex types. To encode a vertex type, assign it a distinct number of loops from a fixed finite set. A vertex permutation respecting arrow multiplicities necessarily preserves types. The two multigraphs have the same numbers of vertices and arrows.

For \(s\ge 1\), there are anchor vertices \(p_0,\ldots,p_s\), of one type, with arrows \(p_i\to p_{i+1}\). The directed path of anchors forces each index to be preserved. At \(p_i\) put a prefix bit pair \(Y_{i,0},Y_{i,1}\) with an arrow from \(p_i\) to each, using a second vertex type. At \(p_i\) for \(i\ge1\), put a choice bit pair \(X_{i,0},X_{i,1}\) in a third type, likewise linked from their anchor. In a fourth type, add four relation vertices at \(p_i\), one for each \((a,b,c)\in\mathbb F_2^3\) with \(a+b+c=0\), and link them from \(p_i\). Their outgoing nonloop arrows go respectively to \(Y_{i-1,a},X_{i,b},Y_{i,c}\).

Add one pin vertex at each endpoint, with an arrow to it from the endpoint anchor and an arrow from it to a single prefix bit at that endpoint. At the first endpoint both graphs pin \(Y_{0,0}\). At the last endpoint \(Q_0\) pins \(Y_{s,0}\) and \(Q_1\) pins \(Y_{s,1}\). The pins use a fifth type.

For each maximal prime power \(r=p^{v_p(d_i)}\) in \(d_i\), attach to each \(X_{i,b}\) a tag of a sixth type, with an arrow from the bit to the tag. Each tag in turn points to \(r\) vertices of a seventh type. Different tags at one bit have different \(r\), recognizable by these outdegrees. In both graphs the Frobenius permutation cycles the \(r\) vertices at the tag on \(X_{i,0}\) in one \(r\)-cycle. It fixes the \(r\) vertices at the corresponding tag on \(X_{i,1}\) individually. It fixes all other vertices. It takes an arrow to the same-index arrow with its endpoints moved, using loop labels when several parallel arrows occur.

Consider a multiplicity-preserving vertex isomorphism \(Q_0\to Q_1\) that intertwines \(\sigma_0^f\) and \(\sigma_1^f\). Let \(u_i\) be its swap bit on the prefix pair at \(p_i\), and let \(v_i\) be its swap bit on the choice pair. The pins give \(u_0=0,u_s=1\). The relation vertices give exactly \(u_{i-1}+v_i+u_i=0\), because translating the three bit positions by these swaps preserves the set of even triples precisely under that condition. An unswapped choice pair always extends over its tags. A swapped one extends over its tags exactly when \(r\mid f\) at every tag on the pair, since an \(r\)-cycle to the \(f\)-th power is conjugate to the identity precisely then. Thus \(v_i=1\) is available exactly for \(d_i\mid f\). The prefix equations require an odd number of available swaps to be used, which is possible exactly when at least one is available. Conversely, for any such choice the indicated bit shifts, translation of the relation vertices and suitable bijections of tag leaves give an equivariant vertex isomorphism. This is the parity gadget of [Cai–Fürer–Immerman](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~immerman/pub/opt.pdf) arranged along a path, with permission to perform each swap supplied by a finite-field degree constraint. The MathProofs [graph parity reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GraphGroupDeterminantTI/GraphParity.md) gives another use of parity in an isomorphism compiler.

The number of vertices and arrows is \(O(1+s+\sum_{i,p}p^{v_p(d_i)})\), including loops. The proof uses no search for an isomorphism of the multigraphs.

## 2. The equivariant test is an ordinary TI test over every extension

For either quiver \(Q=(V,E)\) over an algebraic closure \(\bar k\), use the algebra

\[
\bar A_Q=\bar k^{V}\oplus\bar k^{E}.
\]

The vertex part is coordinatewise multiplication, the arrow part has square zero, and an arrow \(a\) has left and right vertex actions from its head and tail coordinates. This is the path algebra with all products of length at least two set to zero. Its Frobenius is the semilinear map cubing coefficients and applying \(\sigma\) to vertices and arrows. This is an instance of [Deng–Du Frobenius descent for quivers](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0307256). Explicitly, the fixed points on a cycle of \(\ell\) basis positions have coordinates \((z,z^3,\ldots,z^{3^{\ell-1}})\) for \(z\in\mathbb F_{3^\ell}\). Fixed points give a \(k\)-algebra \(A_Q\) of dimension \(|V|+|E|\) and base-changing it to \(\bar k\) recovers \(\bar A_Q\). The last claim follows on each cycle from \(\mathbb F_{3^\ell}\otimes_k\bar k\cong\bar k^\ell\) via all embeddings. Over \(K=\mathbb F_{3^f}\) the base change corresponds to the fixed points of the \(f\)-th power of semilinear Frobenius.

Use \(B_Q=M_2(A_Q)\) for the final tensor. Over \(\bar k\), its radical is the arrow part tensored with \(M_2(\bar k)\), of square zero, and its semisimple quotient is \(\prod_{v\in V}M_2(\bar k)\). If \(B_{Q_0}\otimes K\cong B_{Q_1}\otimes K\), extension to \(\bar k\) permutes the simple quotient labels and commutes with the \(f\)-th Frobenius action on them. The quotient acts intrinsically on the radical from both sides because the radical squares to zero. Its radical corner of type \((v,w)\) has dimension four times the arrow multiplicity from \(w\) to \(v\). Thus the induced vertex permutation preserves all arrow multiplicities. Conversely, such an equivariant vertex map extends to an equivariant arrow map by mapping the same-index parallel arrows. The resulting permutation of basis labels is an algebra isomorphism before matrix amplification, commutes with the \(f\)-th semilinear Frobenius and descends to \(K\). Its matrix amplification gives the required isomorphism of the \(B\)'s.

For unital associative algebras, their multiplication tensors are isomorphic exactly when the algebras are isomorphic. Indeed, an ordinary tensor isomorphism can be written as an isotopy \(P(x)Q(y)=R(xy)\). Setting \(a=P(1),b=Q(1)\) gives bijective left multiplication by \(a\) and right multiplication by \(b\), so they are units. Then \(\theta(x)=a^{-1}R(x)b^{-1}\) is a unital algebra isomorphism, with \(P=L_a\theta,Q=R_b\theta,R=L_aR_b\theta\), where \(L_a,R_b\) denote the two multiplications on the target. Multiplication tensors are concise because of the unit on both inputs. This proves the ordinary spectrum claim.

For explicit tables, use a copy of \(\mathbb F_{3^\ell}\) for each coordinate cycle of length \(\ell\). In this construction the incident arrow and vertex cycles have the same length or one endpoint cycle has length one. With representatives chosen at the incident vertex, vertex actions on arrow coordinates are multiplication in the cycle field, through the identity or the inclusion of \(k\). Other representative orders use known Frobenius powers. Fixed-characteristic irreducible polynomial construction is deterministic polynomial time in \(\ell\) by [Shoup](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf). Computing these tables and their matrix amplifications is polynomial in the stated bounds. For unary generators their factorizations can be found by trial division. The dense output count is polynomial in the mode dimension.

## 3. The same witnesses have literal determinant one

On the vector space of \(\bar A_Q\) put the standard symmetric pairing with the vertex and arrow labels as orthonormal basis. This pairing is invariant under simultaneous transport of both vectors by the label permutations, and descends to a nondegenerate pairing over \(k\), because over \(\bar k\) its Gram matrix is the identity. Let its Gram matrices in the two chosen \(k\)-bases be \(G_0,G_1\). The graph-induced algebra isomorphism \(\theta\) over \(K\) preserves these pairings, giving

\[
(\det_K\theta)^2=\det G_0/\det G_1\in k^\times.
\]

Entrywise amplification to \(M_2(A_Q)\), in ordered matrix-entry bases, has determinant \((\det_K\theta)^4=1\) since \(k=\mathbb F_3\). Apply that same algebra isomorphism in each of the three multiplication roles. Using duals on the two input roles in the covariant tensor convention preserves the determinant-one condition. Every ordinary yes degree therefore has an exact special-linear witness. This is the trace-Gram normalization from the preceding MathProofs [inverse spectrum construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IsomorphismDescentAndObservations/Results.md), now on both vertices and arrows.

For the empty union use the same-format multiplication tensors of \(M_2(k)\) and \(k^4\). They cannot be isomorphic after any extension because their center dimensions differ.

## 4. Simultaneous inverse theorem for a finite family

Let \(I\) be finite. Prescribe sets \(S_{ij}\subseteq\mathbb N_{>0}\) with \(S_{ii}=\mathbb N_{>0}\), symmetric in \(i,j\), each a finite union of sets of multiples. Suppose \(i\sim_f j\) when \(f\in S_{ij}\) is an equivalence relation for each \(f\). These conditions are sufficient for all \(S_{ij}\) to occur simultaneously as both ordinary and exact special-linear spectra on one finite family of equal-format concise multiplication tensors. They are necessary by transitivity at each field and the [Frobenius component characterization](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/OptimalMaxRankAndDescent/ExtensionSpectra.md). This statement imposes only the displayed conditions on the data, and does not claim the linear bound of the two-object construction.

For sufficiency let \(M\) be the least common multiple of the finitely many generators, with \(M=1\) if none are needed. The relation at \(f\) equals that at \(\gcd(f,M)\). For each proper divisor \(e\) of \(M\), give distinct integer labels to the \(\sim_e\) classes and let \(c_e(i)\) be the label of \(i\); put \(c_M(i)=0\) for all \(i\). Define the fixed-count function \(F_i(f)=M c_{\gcd(f,M)}(i)\). For each \(m\mid M\), put

\[
a_m(i)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{e\mid m}\mu(m/e)F_i(e),
\]

an integer because \(m\mid M\). Add a common nonnegative integer to \(a_m(i)\) for each \(m\) so all resulting counts are nonnegative. Let \(E_i\) be the étale algebra with those counts of degree-\(m\) factors. Its Frobenius permutation has fixed-point counts \(F_i(f)\) plus a common function. At \(M\) all these fixed-point counts equal the total number of geometric labels, so all \(E_i\) have equal dimension. If necessary add one common scalar factor to make them nonzero.

If \(i\sim_M j\), then over degree \(f\) the two étale algebras match exactly when their Frobenius permutations powered by \(f\) have the same cycle type. The fixed counts of all positive powers determine cycle type by divisor inversion. Hence equality holds exactly when \(F_i(fh)=F_j(fh)\) for every \(h\ge 1\). If \(f\in S_{ij}\), all its multiples belong and the counts match. If \(f\notin S_{ij}\), then \(\gcd(f,M)\ne M\), so the counts already differ at \(h=1\). For indices in different \(\sim_M\) classes, \(S_{ij}\) is empty. To enforce those empty spectra, after forming \(M_2(E_i)\) attach a separate marker algebra by direct product. For \(h\) eventual classes indexed by \(r=0,\ldots,h-1\), use \(r\) copies of \(M_4(k)\) and \(16(h-r)\) copies of \(k\). These markers have common dimension, and the number of geometric size-four simple factors distinguishes classes over every extension. The size-two simple factors are all in the core, so a same-class comparison is exactly comparison of the two étale cores. For such a comparison the étale label isomorphism yields an exact determinant-one map on \(M_2(E_i)\) by Section 3's Gram computation, and extend it by the identity on the identical marker. This proves simultaneous realizability.

## 5. Independent ordinary and special-linear spectra on a single pair

Given two finite divisibility unions \(V\subseteq U\), there are two same-format concise multiplication tensors with ordinary spectrum \(U\) and exact special-linear spectrum \(V\). For this corollary use the étale-core version of the ordinary realization, as its determinant property holds for every algebra isomorphism. Specifically choose equal-dimensional étale algebras \(E_+,E_-\) with isomorphism spectrum \(U\) by divisor inversion as in Section 4 if \(U\ne\varnothing\). Set \(C_\pm=M_2(E_\pm)\). Every algebra isomorphism of the \(C\)'s over any extension has determinant one in the ordered matrix-entry bases. It induces the isomorphism of centers, whose entrywise amplification has determinant one by the trace-Gram argument. What remains fixes the center and is inner on each size-two matrix factor by Skolem–Noether. Inner conjugation has determinant one, as left multiplication by the conjugating matrix and right multiplication by its inverse contribute reciprocal determinants.

For the second factor use the preceding MathProofs [odd local algebra and reflected multiplication construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorIsomorphismComposition/Results.md), Section 3. Its construction gives an algebra \(D\) that is a product of one or two odd-dimensional local factors, each with nonzero radical and remaining local after every finite scalar extension. Over degree \(f\), \(D\) has an algebra automorphism of determinant \(-1\) exactly when \(f\in V\). For nonempty \(V\) the two factors have equal odd dimension, intrinsic automorphism determinant one, and a swap precisely when their underlying étale algebras become isomorphic. For empty \(V\) take the single odd local factor with all automorphism determinants one. These are exactly the properties of the earlier construction used here.

Form \(C_+\times D\) and \(C_-\times D\). The local factors of \(D\) cannot mix with simple matrix factors, a distinction preserved by isomorphisms over every extension. The two products are ordinarily isomorphic exactly when the matrix sectors match, so the ordinary spectrum is \(U\). Every isomorphism has determinant equal to the determinant of its restriction to \(D\) because the matrix-sector determinant is one. Reflect the output mode of the second multiplication tensor by the linear map \(S\) that multiplies one odd local factor by \(-1\) and fixes everything else. This changes the target product to \(S(xy)\), still a unital associative product because \(S\) is multiplication by a central involutory unit. It leaves the ordinary spectrum unchanged.

If the reflected comparison has an exact special-linear witness, strip its output reflection to get an unreflected isotopy with \(\det P=\det Q=1\) and \(\det R=-1\). Section 2's factorization gives \(\det\theta=\det R/(\det P\det Q)=-1\). Conversely, if an algebra isomorphism of determinant \(-1\) exists for the unreflected product, in that isotopy formula take both \(a,b\) to be the target central unit that acts by \(-1\) on one odd local factor and by one elsewhere. Then \(\det P=\det Q=1\) and \(\det R=-1\), and the reflection makes the output determinant one. Hence the precise special-linear spectrum is \(U\cap V=V\). If \(U\) is empty then both prescribed spectra are empty and the pair in Section 3 suffices.

These are informal proofs. The compact construction is a size-controlled realization of extension-degree behavior, and the last two results give simultaneous inverse theorems. They leave the complexity of unrestricted same-field SL-TI unchanged. The cited ingredients are classical or previously proved as indicated. Publication priority for the combined statements remains unverified.
