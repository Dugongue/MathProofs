# GP-0277 · Scheme-preserving reduction from order d to order three

**Result.** For each fixed d there is a deterministic polynomial-size reduction from ordinary order-d tensor isomorphism over a field k to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over k. For each input, the output stabilizer group scheme is an explicit iterated semidirect product with the input stabilizer group scheme. The transporter construction and recovery are valid over all commutative k-algebras, including nonreduced ones. The proof is written.

Use the project's tagged acyclic quiver construction. For d ≥ 2 the quiver has core vertices v_0,…,v_d,z, a main path whose i-th arrow space is V_i, and a second path v_0 → z → v_d with scalar arrows a,b. Attach positive pairwise distinct numbers of private outward leaves to the core vertices. For T ∈ ⊗V_i set A_T=kQ/(ab−T). Each coefficient of T is viewed in the main-path space. Use the path basis omitting ab and paths extending ab into a leaf. The relation has coefficient one on each eliminated path and therefore gives the same basis after arbitrary commutative scalar extension. In particular the multiplication tensor lives in A_T^* ⊗ A_T^* ⊗ A_T, with all three modes free over the base. For d=1 use a second one-dimensional dummy path slot with its independent scalar absorbed into the original mode.

Let R be any commutative k-algebra. Write A for R ⊗ A_T and N for the ideal generated by positive-length paths. The ideal N is nilpotent and equals the two-sided commutator ideal. Indeed, a path p with distinct endpoints is [e_src,p] in the traversal convention, and the quotient by N is the commutative coordinate algebra R^Q_0. Therefore the ideal and its successive powers are functorially intrinsic over R, including nilpotent R.

An R-algebra isomorphism induces an automorphism of R^Q_0, which is a locally constant permutation of the vertex idempotents on Spec R. Explicitly its coefficients are idempotents, and their products decompose R into finitely many factors on each of which the map is a fixed permutation. After any localization at a prime, the quotient N/N² identifies arrow spaces between each pair of vertices. The nonterminals are exactly the core vertices and their distinct numbers of outgoing terminal neighbors identify each core vertex. It follows on each nonzero factor of the permutation decomposition that the core vertex labels are fixed, and the only possible permutations are within each family of private leaves. These permutations act on the quiver by canonical lifts, with the chosen arrow bases.

After correcting the permutation, take e'_i to be the images of the vertex idempotents and set u=Σ_i e'_i e_i in the target. Then u≡1 modulo N and u e_i=e'_i u. Since N is nilpotent u is a unit. It is the unique element of 1+N conjugating the original family to the image family: an element of N commuting with all vertex idempotents has only diagonal blocks, which all vanish. This provides, naturally over R, the normal kernel 1+N of the factorization by conjugation and a split complement consisting of automorphisms that preserve the family up to the leaf permutations.

An isomorphism that fixes the idempotents restricts to independent GL(V_i ⊗ R) matrices P_i on adjacent main arrow spaces, unit scalars α,β on the tag arrows, and independent unit scalars on the leaf arrows. No other entries can be chosen because these arrows generate the algebra. Preservation of the tag relation is exactly (⊗P_i)T=αβ T'. Scaling P_1 by (αβ)^{-1} gives the desired original transporter. Conversely the original transporter, with free choices of the two tag unit scalars and leaf units, gives every fixed-vertex isomorphism. This is natural over R, and on stabilizers is an isomorphism of a product with a torus. The leaf group permutes the independent leaf coordinates. It fixes the two tag coordinates and the original tensor factor.

It remains to pass from A to its ordinary three-tensor of multiplication. Under the usual dual-mode convention a transporter is a triple (P,Q,L) of invertible R-linear maps satisfying L(xy)=P(x)Q(y). Write a=P(1), b=Q(1). From L(x)=P(x)b=aQ(x), multiplication on the right by b and multiplication on the left by a are linear automorphisms. Consequently a and b are units. For example surjectivity gives a right inverse to a, and injectivity of left multiplication forces it to be a left inverse. Then f(x)=a^{-1}L(x)b^{-1} is a unital algebra isomorphism and P(x)=a f(x), Q(x)=f(x)b. Every such triple (a,b,f) gives an isotopy. Composition is

    (a,b,f) (a',b',f') = (a f(a'), f(b') b, f f').

For the output stabilizer this gives a semidirect product of the two unit groups of A (with the second opposite) by Aut(A). The latter is (1+N) extended by the original tensor stabilizer, the tag and leaf torus, and the constant group scheme of private leaf permutations, with the actions just described. Every factor identification is a natural isomorphism on the functor of points, proving the claimed scheme statement. For d=1 the dummy path scalar is absorbed into the first mode and contributes one further independent multiplicative coordinate. All path counts, output multiplication coordinates, and calculations required to recover a transporter over k are polynomial for fixed d.
