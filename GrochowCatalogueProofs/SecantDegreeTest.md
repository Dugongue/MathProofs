# GP-0194 · A nondefective secant for which the degree test cannot improve the bound

**Result.** The universal statement in the catalogue is false, even in a balanced concise format with generic border rank above the largest mode dimension. The proof is written.

Take the rank-one Segre secant X=σ_1 in the three-tensor space V=C³⊗C³⊗C³. The Segre itself is nondefective and proper. The generic border rank g in this format exceeds 3 by parameter dimension: the projective Segre has dimension 6 and the expected dimension of σ_3 is at most 20, below dim P(V)=26. Take a generic tensor T with border rank g.

For any linear subspace L of V containing T, and any Y ⊂ L∩X with the cone convention, at least one flattening minor of order two gives a nonzero degree-two polynomial on L vanishing on Y. It is nonzero because some flattening of T has rank at least two. Multiplying by powers of a nonzero linear form on L gives a nonzero vanishing polynomial in every degree q≥2. Thus the hypothesis of Lee's Theorem 3 applied to this secant can only hold at q=1. At q=1 the hypothesis states that Y spans L, so a basis of L can be chosen from rank-one tensors in Y. This gives R(T) ≤ dim L, so dim L ≥ g. With rank bound r=1 and q=1, the upper bound of Theorem 3 is exactly dim L. It cannot strictly improve on g.

This answers the catalogue wording for all proper nondefective secants. A question restricted to higher or penultimate secants would still require a different argument.
