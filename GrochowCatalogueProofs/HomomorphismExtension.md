# Homomorphism extension on eight points

**Theorem.** Homomorphism extension between generator-given permutation groups is NP-complete even with fixed target $D_8$ of order eight and with source of nilpotency class at most two and exponent dividing four. The symmetric-target version is also NP-complete even with fixed target $S_8$. This answers [Angela Wuu, Open Problem 1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.08656#S1.SS2), and establishes NP-completeness of GP-0379 already when the target domain has size eight and each given subgroup action has one orbit. On the same instances, counting all extending homomorphisms and counting their conjugacy classes are #P-complete under polynomial-time metric reductions. This gives the counting hardness alternatives for GP-0381 and GP-0377.

## 1. A compact group carrying the Boolean relators

Take a Boolean quadratic system obtained from a 3-CNF formula as in the [dihedral epimorphism proof](DihedralEpimorphism.md). There are $m=n+1$ homogeneous coordinates including anchor $0$. Each 3-CNF satisfying assignment extends to exactly one gate assignment for the quadratic system. Write $D_8=\langle r,s\rangle$ where $r$ is a rotation of order four and $s$ is a reflection, and let $z=r^2$. Set $t=sr$.

Use a disjoint union of $m^2$ regular eight-point $D_8$ actions, indexed by $(i,j)$, so their direct product has a faithful permutation representation of degree $8m^2$. Let $G$ be the subgroup generated by these explicit permutations:

- $a_i$ acts by $s$ in every block of row $i$ and trivially elsewhere.
- $b_j$ acts by $t$ in every block of column $j$ and trivially elsewhere.

The cross-commutators $c_{ij}=[a_i,b_j]$ are independent central involutions, with $c_{ij}$ acting by $z$ in exactly its own block. Involutions $a_i$ commute amongst themselves, and so do involutions $b_j$. The abstract group presented by these relations together with centrality and order two of the $c_{ij}$ has order at most $2^{2m+m^2}$, by collecting each word to a fixed ordered product of binary powers of these generators. The concrete group has that many elements. Indeed in each factor the projection to $D_8/Z(D_8)$ records the row bit and the column bit in independent directions. Taken together these projections recover every exponent of an $a_i$ and a $b_j$, after which the independent central involutions recover every exponent of a $c_{ij}$. Thus the presentation with these relations is exact for the compact permutation group.

Inside the elementary abelian central subgroup generated by all the $c_{ij}$, let $R$ be the span of the Boolean equation relators and of the symmetry and normalization relators. Set $M=\langle R,c_{00}\rangle$. Prescribe a homomorphism $\rho:M\to D_8$ taking $R$ to $1$ and $c_{00}$ to $z$. Gaussian elimination on the explicit commutator coordinates decides whether this is well defined. If $c_{00}\in R$, the Boolean system cannot be feasible since a satisfying assignment would give $c_{00}=z$ and kill every element of $R$. Map this branch to a fixed no-instance. For example take a generator $g$ of $C_4$, and prescribe that $g^2$ map to the reflection $s$. A reflection is not a square in $D_8$.

## 2. Extension and exact multiplicity

Every extension $G\to D_8$ of $\rho$ has nontrivial commutator $z$ in its image. Every proper subgroup of $D_8$ is abelian, so the extension is onto. The rank-one argument of GP-0349 then gives a Boolean solution with anchor $1$. Conversely every solution maps $a_i$ to $s^{x_i}$ and $b_i$ to $t^{x_i}$, which respects the exact presentation of $G$ and agrees with $\rho$.

More precisely, each solution yields exactly $2^{2m+1}$ extensions. There are two choices of which of the two reflection lines in $D_8/Z(D_8)$ belongs to the $a$-half. The $b$-half must then use the other line. The rotation line is unavailable to either nonzero half because its lifts have order four, while $a_0,b_0$ are involutions. Following a choice of lines there are two lifts in $D_8$ for each of the $2m$ generator cosets. For zero coset these are $1,z$. For nonzero reflection coset both lifts are reflections. These lifts satisfy the involution and within-half commuting relations, all central relations hold, and their cross-commutator bits are independent of the lifts. Thus every choice is valid and distinct, and every extension has been counted.

This gives the decision and counting claims for fixed target $D_8$. It also gives a polynomial-time metric reduction from #3SAT to the count: divide the exact extension count by the efficiently known $2^{2m+1}$.

## 3. Fixed symmetric target and simultaneous local actions

Identify the eight-point target domain with the elements of $D_8$. Write $\lambda(h)(x)=hx$ and $\tau(h)(x)=xh^{-1}$ for left and right regular actions. Both are homomorphisms from $D_8$, and the image of $\tau$ is exactly the centralizer of the image of $\lambda$. To see this, a permutation commuting with all left translations is determined by its value at the identity, and its action on every other point is right multiplication by that value.

Replace the source by $G\times D_8$, realized by adding one disjoint regular action of the second factor. Prescribe the action on $M\times D_8$ by $(m,h)\mapsto \tau(\rho(m))\lambda(h)$. An extension to $S_8$ must send $G\times\{1\}$ into the centralizer of the prescribed second-factor action, hence into $\tau(D_8)$. Restriction to that factor and inversion of the faithful representation $\tau$ give a bijection with the earlier extensions $G\to D_8$. Conversely such an extension combines with $\lambda$ to give the required $S_8$ action. The fixed no-instance extends to this construction by using $C_4$ in place of $G$ and its marked order-two subgroup in place of $M$.

For GP-0379, take this prescription as the first local action and the left regular action on the second factor alone as the second. The two maps agree on their overlap and each has a single orbit. Consequently a run time fixed-parameter tractable in the target degree plus the number of local orbits would put an NP-complete problem in P. The hardness holds even though the marked maps are globally compatible on the subgroup they already generate.

For GP-0381, prescribe the single block consisting of the whole eight-point domain. It has fixed size eight and imposes no additional restriction on the extensions. Any supplementary disjoint blocks with prescribed fixed actions may be omitted. Thus counting extensions with a fixed block size is already #P-hard.

## 4. Membership and conjugacy-class counts

For membership in NP, a certificate consists of the images of the input generators. On the disjoint union of the source and target domains, generate the group of pairs of the input generators and their proposed images. It projects onto the source group. Its order equals the source group order precisely when the projection is one-to-one, in which case the pair group is the graph of a homomorphism. Permutation-group order and membership are computable in polynomial time. For each marked subgroup generator test that its paired prescribed image belongs to the graph, and check block preservation if required. For exact counts on labeled actions, accepting witnesses can encode each tuple of generator images exactly once. This gives membership in #P in the explicitly represented permutation model of these problems. [Permutation-group algorithms](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf)

For GP-0377, equivalence between extensions is conjugation by the centralizer of the prescribed action. In the construction above this centralizer is $\tau(D_8)$: the left regular subgroup forces containment, and the remaining marked images are right translations by central elements. Conjugation of an extension by this centralizer has stabilizer $\tau(Z(D_8))$, since the image of its first factor is all of $\tau(D_8)$. Every class therefore has four labeled extensions. Dividing by four gives exactly $2^{2m-1}$ classes per satisfying assignment.

For membership in #P for the general class count, there is a polynomial-time deterministic choice of one compatible labeled extension in each class. Fix the input generator order. Encode an action on an explicitly given finite domain as a directed Schreier graph with edges labeled by generator and direction. In each orbit, an ordered breadth-first traversal from a specified root produces a numbered rooted code, including every generator transition. Minimize over the possible roots and sort the resulting orbit codes to compute a canonical action on the ordered target domain. This is possible in polynomial time because one rooted traversal uniquely determines the rooted action code.

The canonical action is defined up to all target relabelings. To transport it back to a compatible representative, evaluate the marked subgroup generators in this action, using constructive membership in the source group to obtain words in its generators. Run the same Schreier procedure for the two marked actions, that is, the canonical action restricted to the subgroup and the prescribed marked action. Since an extension was supplied, these restrictions are isomorphic. Match orbits with identical codes in order of their least domain labels and pick the least root realizing the chosen canonical code in each orbit. The rooted traversals then give a deterministic isomorphism of the two marked actions. Transporting the full canonical action through this bijection gives an extension depending only on its original conjugacy class and on the fixed marked input. For two genuine extensions all target isomorphisms automatically commute with the fixed marked subgroup action, so no distinct classes collapse in this canonicalization. Accept only generator tuples equal to the constructed representative. This is one accepting witness per class, proving #P membership and hence completeness with the stated reduction convention.

The hardness constructions and counting analyses above are written proofs. The transport from compact commutator coordinates uses the [class-two group–tensor work](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/b0947ecc8675432a7292628b99e945b7d50b216e/FourIsomorphismResults/GroupTensorEquivalence.md). The independent Lean core of the dihedral proof checks the Boolean rank-one recovery and order-eight commutator formula, while the compact construction and the complexity claims here use the written arguments.

Chlopecki and Samperton study counting all maps to the order-eight dihedral group from tensor-encoded class-two groups. Here a prescribed central commutator gives the extension and conjugacy-class counts. [Samperton–Weiß, §§1.5–1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02885v2#S1.SS5)
