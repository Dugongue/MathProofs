# Groups, codes, and succinct actions

These are written proofs for catalogue questions. The methods for GP-0336, GP-0501, and GP-0503 use established algorithmic primitives. The counterexample for GP-0335 concerns the unrestricted additive comparison itself. An algorithm that makes one unrestricted oracle query and also does extra work on the original inputs is a broader model.

## GP-0362 · Uniform sampling of central epimorphic lifts

**Theorem.** Given finite Cayley tables for $G,H$ and a prescribed epimorphism $\bar f:G/Z(G)\to H/Z(H)$, existence of an epimorphism inducing $\bar f$ is decidable in deterministic polynomial time. Feasible instances have an exactly uniform sampler in expected polynomial time. A strictly bounded polynomial-time sampler may report failure with any prescribed probability $\delta>0$, and its conditional distribution is exactly uniform.

Choose a lift $h_g\in H$ of every coset image $\bar f(gZ(G))$. Write every possible group homomorphism inducing $\bar f$ as $f(g)=h_g z_g$. The variables $z_g$ lie in the finite abelian group $Z(H)$. The equations for products are
$$
z_{gg'}=(h_{gg'}^{-1}h_gh_{g'})z_gz_{g'}.
$$
The coefficient before $z_gz_{g'}$ lies in $Z(H)$ and all these equations are affine linear in cyclic invariant-factor coordinates. Compute their solution or inconsistency by integer Smith normal form, using one modulus for each cyclic coordinate and slack variables for the corresponding congruences. Centers, derived subgroups, abelian quotients, invariant-factor coordinates, and quotient sections can all be computed in polynomial time from the tables. For example, a finite abelian Cayley table presents the abelian group by generators $[g]$ and relations $[g]+[g']=[gg']$, so Smith form gives cyclic coordinates and can be used together with direct table tests. If a solution $f_0$ exists, all solutions have the form $f_0 z$ for $z\in\operatorname{Hom}(G_\mathrm{ab},Z(H))$, with no duplications.

Write $B=f(G)$. Since $BZ(H)=H$, conjugation by $H$ normalizes $B$, and $H/B$ is abelian. Thus $f$ is surjective precisely when its induced map $G_\mathrm{ab}\to H_\mathrm{ab}$ is surjective. Test generation of $H_\mathrm{ab}$ modulo $pH_\mathrm{ab}$ for all primes dividing its order. This is equivalent because a proper subgroup of a finite abelian group is contained in a maximal subgroup of prime index. In the explicit-table model, primes can be enumerated by trial division.

Fix such a prime $p$, choose cyclic generators $g_i$ of $G_\mathrm{ab}$ of orders $m_i$, and put $V=H_\mathrm{ab}/pH_\mathrm{ab}$. The allowed image of $g_i$ is an affine space $a_i+U_i$ in $V$. Here $a_i$ is the image under $f_0$, and $U_i$ is the image of $Z(H)[m_i]$. The columns can be chosen independently: a map out of the cyclic summand of order $m_i$ is determined by an arbitrary element killed by $m_i$. For a basis $u_{i1},\ldots,u_{ik_i}$ of $U_i$, replace its affine set by the finite list $a_i,a_i+u_{i1},\ldots,a_i+u_{ik_i}$. The list spans the affine set. If a full-rank selection exists, expand a nonzero maximal minor multilinearly in vectors of these lists. Some term is nonzero, showing a full-rank selection from the lists at distinct indices. The converse is immediate. A maximum independent selection from the union of tagged lists is an intersection of a linear matroid and the partition matroid with capacity one at each index. Standard deterministic augmenting-path matroid intersection, with Gaussian elimination as a rank oracle, decides whether such a selection spans $V$. Prime components can be lifted and combined independently because $Z(H)$ is the direct product of its Sylow subgroups and summands of order prime to $p$ vanish in $V$. This proves deterministic feasibility. [Independence transversals and matroid intersection](https://egres.elte.hu/www/tr-08-10.html) · [Catalogue source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)

Now sample independently and uniformly from $Z(H)[m_i]$ for all $i$ and test the resulting lift. These choices yield a uniform distribution on all compatible homomorphisms. At a prime $p$, columns are uniform on their allowed affine spaces and their coordinates are affine functions of independent uniform field parameters. On feasible inputs choose any full-rank minor that is nonzero on at least one assignment. Its determinant has degree at most $d_p=\dim V$, and its function on the full parameter cube is nonzero. A nonzero polynomial function over $\mathbb F_p$ expressible in degree at most $d$ is nonzero with probability at least $p^{-d}$ under uniform inputs. To prove this, first reduce all variable degrees below $p$ using $x^p=x$, which preserves the function and does not increase degree. For a reduced polynomial of positive degree, write it as a polynomial of degree $k>0$ in one occurring variable. Its leading coefficient is a nonzero reduced polynomial of degree at most $d-k$. A reduced nonzero polynomial represents a nonzero function by induction in the number of variables and the univariate root bound. Conditional on a nonzero leading coefficient, the univariate polynomial is nonzero at a fraction at least $1/p$ of inputs. Induction on total degree gives probability at least $p^{-(d-k+1)}\ge p^{-d}$. A nonzero constant starts the induction.

Prime components in the random choices are independent. Therefore the probability of an epimorphism is at least $\prod_{p}p^{-d_p}\ge1/|H_\mathrm{ab}|$. Rejection sampling is exactly uniform on epimorphisms and has polynomial expected cost. Testing existence first handles infeasible inputs. With $O(|H|\log(1/\delta))$ independent trials, bounded rejection has failure probability at most $\delta$. Sampling a table-listed subgroup uniformly from random bits can itself use either expected bounded rejection or a capped power-of-two sampling step with an independent failure budget. Truncation at complete independent trials preserves exact conditional uniformity.

## GP-0477 · Sparse alternating presentations requiring quadratic witness support

**Theorem.** Over any finite field and for input dimension $2m$, there are pseudo-isometric alternating presentations of output dimension $m$ with $O(m)$ nonzero input entries and at least $m(m+1)/2$ nonzero entries in every input-side matrix transporting the stated source to the stated target.

Use a dual basis $e_1,\ldots,e_m,f_1,\ldots,f_m$, write $e_0=0$, and compare the slice spaces
$$
S=\langle e_i\wedge f_i : 1\le i\le m\rangle,\qquad
T=\langle (e_i-e_{i-1})\wedge f_i : 1\le i\le m\rangle.
$$
Both use a linear number of alternating wedge terms. The two ordered families of supporting covectors are bases, so there is a congruence. In each space the rank of a linear combination is twice the number of nonzero slice coefficients. This is immediate in the respective split bases, where every slice has its own $2$-dimensional block. It holds for arbitrary characteristic when $u\wedge v$ denotes the alternating bilinear form $u(x)v(y)-v(x)u(y)$.

Let $A$ be any input map transporting $S$ to $T$, so $A^*T=S$, where $A^*$ denotes pullback. It must permute the rank-two lines. The image in the dual of the linear contraction of a nonzero rank-two form is its supporting plane. Thus $A^*$ takes the supporting planes of the second family to distinct coordinate planes of the first. Put $u_j=e_j-e_{j-1}$. For every $i$, $A^*e_i=\sum_{j=1}^i A^*u_j$. These $i$ nonzero summands lie in disjoint coordinate planes, so the sum has at least $i$ nonzero coordinates. Sum these inequalities across the $m$ relevant columns of $A^*$. Since transposition preserves entry count, the same bound holds for $A$. This is an obstruction in the stated direction of transport. [Catalogue context](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2)

## GP-0479 · Identical rank-drop schemes can hide different class-two groups

**Theorem.** For every odd prime $p$ and every $t\ge4$, there are nonisomorphic class-two exponent-$p$ groups of order $p^{2t+4}$ with elementary abelian center and derived group both of dimension $2$, common radical zero, and identical affine determinantal rank-drop schemes over $\mathbb F_p$ and every extension.

For $e\ge1$, take $L_e(x,y)$ with $e$ rows and $e+1$ columns, with $x$ on its diagonal and $y$ one step above. Let
$$
K_e(x,y)=
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & L_e(x,y)\\ -L_e(x,y)^\top &0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The two pencils are $P=K_1\oplus K_{t-1}$ and $Q=K_2\oplus K_{t-2}$. Each coefficient pair is linearly independent, so its alternating bimap onto a two-dimensional target is surjective. The common radical is zero: the first $e$ and last $e$ coordinates of the longer summand must vanish by the two shifts in $L_e$, and multiplication by the transpose of the $x$ coefficient kills no vector of the shorter summand. These statements persist under direct sum.

For $k\le e$ and $0\le a\le k$, take the first $k$ rows of $L_e$ and columns $1,\ldots,a,a+2,\ldots,k+1$. The resulting determinant is $x^a y^{k-a}$. This follows by splitting at the omitted column into triangular blocks of sizes $a$ and $k-a$, with diagonals $x$ and $y$ respectively. Row and column permutations separately turn $K_e$ into a block diagonal matrix with blocks $L_e$ and $-L_e^\top$, with rectangular blocks of complementary sizes. In the full pencil the four such blocks have capacities $1,1,t-1,t-1$ or $2,2,t-2,t-2$. Any total minor size $k\le2t$ can be distributed among the blocks within their capacities, and any exponent $0\le a\le k$ distributed among the selected sizes. Combining the indicated monomial minors by disjoint rows and columns gives $x^a y^{k-a}$ up to sign. Every minor is homogeneous of degree $k$, so both determinantal ideals are exactly $(x,y)^k$. All larger minors vanish since each block has rank at most its capacity over the fraction field. Ideals, and hence schemes, also agree after base change.

The polynomial kernel of $K_e$ over $\mathbb F_p[x,y]$ is generated by the vector in the longer summand whose coordinates are $((-y)^e,x(-y)^{e-1},\ldots,x^e)$. Indeed the short summand is killed only by the zero vector, and the recurrence $xv_i+yv_{i+1}=0$ forces the displayed multiples by relative primality of $x$ and $y$. Thus the first pencil has a nonzero homogeneous kernel vector of degree $1$ and the second has none. This is preserved by any invertible congruence of input basis and invertible linear substitution of $x,y$. It certifies failure of pseudo-isometry.

For either bimap $b$, use the multiplication
$$
(v,z)(v',z')=(v+v',z+z'+b(v,v')/2).
$$
Bilinearity gives associativity, and alternation gives exponent $p$ and commutator $b$. Zero common radical gives $Z(G)=W$, and surjectivity gives $G'=W$. Any group isomorphism would induce linear maps on $G/Z(G)$ and $G'$ preserving their commutator, hence a prohibited pseudo-isometry. This argument uses the same commutator-coordinate interpretation as the project’s [class-two group–tensor equivalence](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/b0947ecc8675432a7292628b99e945b7d50b216e/FourIsomorphismResults/GroupTensorEquivalence.md), with a finer obstruction than fieldwise rank alone.

## GP-0498 · Minimum-support permutation roots in a specified subgroup

**Theorem.** For every fixed exponent $e\ge2$, deciding whether a specified permutation subgroup contains an $e$-th root of $g$ of support at most $K$ extending a prescribed partial bijection is NP-complete. This holds for $g=1$ in an elementary abelian group presented by generators consisting of products of disjoint cycles of one fixed prime length. The extra prescribed cycle marks can be empty, or can be the blocks in this construction.

Choose a prime $p\mid e$. Reduce Exact Cover by 3-Sets with universe size $m$ divisible by three. Give each input triple and each universe element a disjoint block of $p$ points with a fixed $p$-cycle. For triple $j$, the subgroup generator advances its private triple block and the three universe blocks that it meets. The private blocks make the coefficient vector $x\in\mathbb F_p^{s}$ unique. All subgroup elements are $e$-th roots of the identity. With incidence matrix $A$, the advances on universe blocks are $Ax$, and the support is $p(\operatorname{wt}(x)+\operatorname{wt}(Ax))$. Prescribe the advance of a single point to its successor in every universe block, forcing $Ax=\mathbf 1$.

At threshold $K=p(m+m/3)$, a feasible vector uses at most $m/3$ nonzero triple coefficients. Each universe point must be covered by a nonzero triple, since the required coefficient there is $1$. The support triples must therefore be disjoint and cover the universe. Each selected coefficient is then forced to be $1$. Conversely an exact cover gives precisely such a vector. Verification of any candidate permutation uses polynomial-time permutation-group membership, exponentiation, and checks of support and marks. The optimization and witness problem has an $\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ upper bound by threshold queries and progressively pinning permutation images. [Exact cover hardness context](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9) · [Permutation-group membership](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf)

## GP-0501 · Polynomial-delay maximal normal subgroup enumeration

**Theorem.** A generator-given permutation group $G\le S_n$ admits deterministic polynomial-delay enumeration of its maximal proper normal subgroups, each given by generators. No full group enumeration is needed.

First compute a chief series $1=H_0<\cdots<H_s=G$ by Kantor–Luks, P11(iii). For every nonabelian layer calculate the full inverse image $C_i$ of the centralizer of $H_i/H_{i-1}$ in $G/H_{i-1}$, by their P6(i), and use quotient composition algorithms to retain precisely candidates with $G/C_i$ nonabelian simple. Deduplicate this polynomial-sized list by mutual membership of generating sets. It contains every desired nonabelian-quotient kernel: for such a maximal normal $N$, choose the first $H_i$ with nontrivial image in $G/N$. Its image is a nontrivial normal subgroup of the simple quotient, hence all of it. The kernel of the map from $H_i/H_{i-1}$ onto $G/N$ is $G$-normal, so chief minimality forces it to vanish. Thus $H_i\cap N=H_{i-1}$. An element of $N$ centralizes the chief layer because its commutators with $H_i$ lie in $N\cap H_i$. Conversely a centralizing element maps to the center of $G/N$, which is trivial. Hence $N=C_i$. [Kantor–Luks, P6, P10 and P11](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/quotient.pdf)

For an abelian simple quotient the index is prime, necessarily $p\le n$. Compute $N_p=[G,G]G^p$ as the normal closure of pairwise commutators and $p$-th powers of input generators. The resulting quotient is abelian of exponent dividing $p$, which proves it is exactly $G/N_p$. Membership in subgroups generated by $N_p$ and previously accepted lifts allows greedy selection of a basis of the quotient from input generators. Enumerate the nonzero linear functionals in which the first nonzero entry is $1$. They enumerate hyperplanes without duplication; standard basis vectors with the pivot coordinate corrected by the indicated scalar give a basis of each hyperplane. Return $N_p$ generators plus corresponding products of lifted basis elements. Dealing with $O(n)$ primes, precomputing the nonabelian list, and advancing the normalized vector enumeration all take polynomial time per gap. Empty input or zero-rank quotient yields no hyperplanes.

## GP-0503 · Coset transport of points in uniform NC

**Theorem.** Let $K\lhd G\le S_\Omega$ be given by generators, with supplied representative $t$ of a coset $tK$. In uniform NC, one can compute all pairs $(a,b)\in\Omega^2$ with nonempty $tK$-transporter, an explicit representative per nonempty transporter, and generators for the pointwise stabilizer common to the witness differences for any supplied set of source marks. The same statements hold for any fixed number of simultaneous marked pairs.

For left actions, $tk$ sends $a$ to $b$ precisely when $k$ sends $a$ to $t^{-1}b$. The undirected graph of $K$'s generators and inverses therefore suffices. Boolean matrix powering gives distances up to $|\Omega|-1$ in polylogarithmic depth and polynomial size. For each target, each unreached vertex is marked and each reached vertex other than the target selects a least generator that lowers distance by one. Standard parallel doubling computes the product of the generators along this selected path, storing an actual permutation at every edge. The target has an identity self-loop. Doubling composes permutations using polylogarithmic-depth indexing and only $O(\log|\Omega|)$ rounds. Thus the outputs can include full witness permutations, not just existence bits. This is the orbit analogue of the project's generator-edge reachability bridge, formalized as cayley_reachable_iff_mem_closure. [Lean reachability bridge](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/b0947ecc8675432a7292628b99e945b7d50b216e/LogspaceMinimumGenerationLean/Mathproof/MinimumGenerationCayley.lean#L27)

For a given nonempty constraint $\pi(a)=b$, all witnesses are $\pi K_a$ for any one witness $\pi$. More generally for a nonempty common transporter on a set $S$ of source marks the right multiplier group is $K_{(S)}=\bigcap_{s\in S}K_s$. Compute its generators in NC by Babai–Luks–Seress Theorem 1.3, independently of whether a simultaneous witness is requested. For constant-size tuples, find such a witness by applying the same graph argument on the diagonal power of $\Omega$, which has polynomial size. [Babai–Luks–Seress](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf)

## GP-0535 · Uniform AC⁰ recovery for a row with one missing entry

**Theorem.** For a partial Cayley table on $[n]$ with at most one hole in each row and a valid completion, all holes can be recovered by uniform polynomial-size constant-depth Boolean circuits.

For a hole in row $i$, let $P_s$ be the assertion that none of the present entries in row $i$ equals symbol $s$. Build equality to the binary encoding of $s$ by conjunction of literals. Mask it by the present bit and take a NOR over the entire row. Because every complete Cayley row is a permutation of the alphabet and there is exactly one hole, exactly one $P_s$ holds and it identifies the missing entry. For output bit $b$, OR the $P_s$'s over symbols with their bit $b$ equal to $1$. A mask preserves the original entries. The construction has constant depth and polynomial size, and its gate list is generated in logspace by counters for row, column, symbol and bit. With power-of-two padded tuple-addressed input and gate labels, these wiring tests are also DLOGTIME uniform. The Latin-row property alone suffices. [Group-table circuit context](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26257v2)

## GP-0336 · Binary hardness with no duplicated projective columns

**Theorem.** Binary monomial code equivalence is graph-isomorphism hard under polynomial many-one reductions even at repeated-projective-column parameter zero.

For a simple graph $X$ on at least one vertex, form $Y=X\vee K_3$, using three universal vertices. This is 3-connected, since removing fewer than three vertices leaves a universal vertex. Use the row span of the vertex–edge incidence matrix over $\mathbb F_2$ as the code. Delete one redundant row to obtain a generator matrix with independent rows. All edge columns remain nonzero and distinct: edges incident with the deleted vertex have distinct singleton support, and all others have distinct two-element support. Therefore the parameter is zero. An isomorphism between graphs induces a coordinate equivalence between both binary codes. Conversely a code permutation carries the kernel of a generator matrix to the corresponding kernel, so induces an isomorphism of cycle matroids: their circuits are exactly the inclusion-minimal nonzero dependency supports. Whitney’s theorem for simple 3-connected graphs implies isomorphism of $Y$'s. Deleting any three universal vertices recovers the isomorphism type of $X$, since universal vertices are mutually interchangeable. Empty graphs with no vertices can be treated separately. The reduction proves the hardness alternative stated in the catalogue, without a complexity assumption. The broader code-equivalence hardness is classical. [Whitney](https://doi.org/10.2307/2371127) · [Petrank–Roth](https://cris.technion.ac.il/en/publications/is-code-equivalence-easy-to-decide-2/)

## GP-0335 · What one unrestricted block-additive comparison leaves undecided

**Theorem.** There exist length-eight $\mathbb F_{32}$-linear codes whose prime-field restrictions are block-additively equivalent and that have no semilinear monomial equivalence. For a single supplied block-additive transporter, computing whether its coordinate permutation lifts to a semilinear monomial transporter reduces to consistency of explicit congruences on one Frobenius exponent. Under connected support this lift always exists.

Let $\lambda\in\mathbb F_{32}\setminus\mathbb F_2$ and let $C(\lambda)$ be the rank-two linear code with generator columns $\infty,0,1,\lambda$ interpreted as distinct projective points. Two such ordinary monomial classes coincide exactly when the unordered quartets are projectively equivalent, i.e. the fourth parameters differ by one of the six anharmonic fractional linear transformations permuting $0,1,\infty$. This follows from unique projective normalization of an ordered triple; the possible permutations of four points yield those six values. For $\lambda^2$ to be in the same anharmonic orbit, a transformation $u$ over $\mathbb F_2$ of order $r=1,2$, or $3$ would satisfy $u(\lambda)=\lambda^2$. The transformation commutes with Frobenius, so $\lambda^{2^r}=u^r(\lambda)=\lambda$, impossible for an element of extension degree $5$.

Now compare $C(\lambda)\oplus C(\lambda)$ and $C(\lambda)\oplus C(\lambda^2)$. Acting as the square map on all four symbols of one summand and as identity on the other shows block-additive equivalence after restriction to $\mathbb F_2$. The projective column dependence matroid has exactly two connected components, each the rank-two uniform matroid on four points. A monomial semilinear equivalence must permute the components. One global Frobenius makes the two source components land in the same ordinary monomial class, whereas the two target components lie in different ordinary classes. Therefore a bare yes answer to unrestricted additive comparison does not certify a semilinear witness.

For a general comparison with a supplied block-additive transporter, first apply its coordinate permutation, then choose the same pivot coordinate set on both sides. Pivots for the two systems coincide because a block-additive bijection preserves injectivity and surjectivity of projections onto coordinate sets. Use systematic generator matrices with those pivots. If the coefficient from pivot $i$ to nonpivot $j$ is $a_{ij}$ on the first side and $b_{ij}$ on the second, the coordinate maps $F_i,F_j$ of the additive witness satisfy
$$
F_j M_{a_{ij}}=M_{b_{ij}}F_i.
$$
Here $M_x$ is multiplication by $x$ on $\mathbb F_q$ as a prime-field vector space; the equation comes from test codewords with only pivot $i$ nonzero. Nonzero coefficient support is identical. Rescale each pivot and nonpivot coordinate on each side along a spanning forest of this support graph so that corresponding tree-edge coefficients become $1$. The corresponding conjugated witness is constant on symbol maps within each connected component. In one component this common map conjugates the multiplication operators of all normalized coefficients, so it gives a field isomorphism on the subfields generated by them. A finite field has a unique subfield of each admissible order, and each such isomorphism is a restriction of a Frobenius power. If the component's subfield degree over the prime field is $d$, its restriction imposes a single explicit congruence $s\equiv s_0\pmod d$ on the global Frobenius power $s$. The degree and restriction can be found by trying the finitely many at most $[\mathbb F_q\colon\mathbb F_p]$ powers. The generalized Chinese remainder criterion decides whether the congruences have a solution. In normalized coordinates the corresponding global Frobenius itself gives a transporter exactly when all component conditions hold. Undo the coordinate scales to obtain a semilinear monomial map with the original permutation. This criterion checks the supplied permutation. Other permutations may have different conditions.
