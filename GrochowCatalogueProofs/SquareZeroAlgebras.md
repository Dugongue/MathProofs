# GP-0076 · Graph-isomorphism classification of radical-square-zero algebras

**Result.** Over each fixed finite field, isomorphism of explicitly based associative algebras with square-zero radical and arbitrary semisimple top is deterministically polynomial-time many-one equivalent to Graph Isomorphism. Over explicitly presented variable finite fields the reduction to graphs is uniform Las Vegas polynomial bit time, and deterministic with finite-field factorization. The reverse reduction remains uniformly deterministic. This class would have a polynomial-time algorithm over fixed fields exactly if Graph Isomorphism does. The proof is written.

Let A be a finite-dimensional unital algebra over F_q and J its radical, with J²=0. Put S=A/J. By Wedderburn–Malcev, A is isomorphic to the split square-zero extension S⊕J. The left and right S-actions on J are intrinsic, since different lifts differ in J, whose square is zero. Consequently A and A' are isomorphic precisely when there is a semisimple-top isomorphism S→S' with respect to which the radical bimodules are isomorphic. This uses only the existence of the complement, so no complement need be computed. For nonunital inputs one can unitize and mark the character of the augmentation; the original algebra is its kernel. In the graph below mark the corresponding one-dimensional split vertex.

Write S=∏_i M_{n_i}(K_i), K_i=F_{q^{e_i}}. Over an algebraic closure, the i-th summand gives e_i matrix block vertices indexed by field embeddings K_i→the closure. There is a permutation F of these vertices given by q-Frobenius. Color each vertex by n_i. For each ordered vertex pair (i,a),(j,b), record the multiplicity of the corresponding simple bimodule in J after extension. This is a weighted directed graph with a second edge type indicating F. Its size is polynomial because Σ_i e_i≤dim A and all multiplicities are at most dim A.

Every F_q-algebra isomorphism induces such a graph isomorphism. Conversely a vertex bijection preserving the Frobenius permutation matches full cycles, their matrix sizes, and choices of cyclic phase, so it is induced by an isomorphism of S over F_q. Matrix-factor inner automorphisms do not change bimodule types. For this isomorphism, equality of the graph edge weights implies equality of the bimodule multiplicities already over F_q. To see this explicitly, a pair of center fields satisfies

    K_i ⊗ K_j ≅ ∏_{s mod g} F_{q^l},  g=gcd(e_i,e_j), l=lcm(e_i,e_j).

Each of the g components has l embeddings over F_q, forming a single orbit of ordered pairs under simultaneous Frobenius. The corresponding component of the enveloping algebra S⊗S^op is M_{n_i n_j}(F_{q^l}). Its unique simple module contributes one copy to each split vertex pair of that orbit after extension. Thus multiplicities over the algebraic closure are precisely the multiplicities of these simple F_q-bimodules. Since S⊗S^op is semisimple, these data determine J, as required.

## Computing all edge labels without a large common splitting field

Compute J, the simple ideals of S and their centers using finite-algebra algorithms. Their matrix sizes are recovered from dim_Fq(S_i)=n_i² e_i. The central idempotents of S and their representative lifts in A give left and right actions of each K_i on the block J_{ij}=1_i J 1_j. No lift choices affect these actions.

Fix coherent reference subfields for the prime-power divisors of the e_i. For each prime ℓ occurring in the e_i, take some center with maximal ℓ-adic exponent k. Its unique subfield of degree ℓ^k and all subfields of degree ℓ^a are found by solving the linear fixed-point equations for the corresponding Frobenius powers. In every ℓ^a subfield with a>0 choose a generator over F_q by taking an element outside the unique maximal proper subfield. Use a root of its minimal polynomial to embed the maximal required reference subfield into each K_i, and restrict that embedding to its subfields. Root extraction uses finite-field factorization. These embeddings are simultaneously coherent: pieces of distinct prime-power degrees inside the same K_i have coprime degrees, so their tensor product is their compositum, K_i. Thus each center has an embedding into the algebraic closure that identifies the chosen reference subfields exactly. The numerical labels a mod e_i may now consistently be interpreted as successive Frobenius powers of these embeddings.

For the phase s mod g of the pair (i,j), and each ℓ^a dividing g as its maximal ℓ-power, take the generator α of the reference subfield of degree ℓ^a. In J_{ij} impose the homogeneous linear equation

    left(α_i) v = right(α_j^(q^s)) v,

using the chosen embeddings into K_i and K_j. In split coordinates this equation says that the left embedding index a_i is congruent to a_j+s modulo ℓ^a for a nonzero vector. The intersection over the prime powers selects exactly the desired diagonal Frobenius orbit by the Chinese remainder theorem. If g=1 take the full J_{ij}. Its F_q dimension divided by l n_i n_j is the edge weight to assign to each pair in that orbit. All relevant field degrees are individually at most dim A and there are polynomially many pairs, phases and equations.

Weighted directed graphs with these colors reduce to Graph Isomorphism by separate colored vertices for directed-edge tails, heads and relation types, followed by standard removal of vertex colors. The integer weights are bounded by dim A, so this step has polynomial size. Rónyai's finite-algebra algorithms give polynomial-time radical computation and deterministic reductions for the semisimple preprocessing to finite-field factorization. Finite-field factorization is Las Vegas polynomial bit time in general and admits deterministic polynomial time in fixed characteristic. Therefore the claimed algorithmic bounds follow. Conversely, given a simple graph, take S=F_q^V and a radical J with one basis vector in each bimodule direction of each edge, with all products inside J zero. Any isomorphism of the algebra is exactly a permutation of the top vertex idempotents preserving these edge multiplicities up to its choice of radical isomorphism. This is graph isomorphism, giving the deterministic return reduction.

The graph encoding extends the known split abelian-top case to arbitrary matrix and extension-field factors. The Frobenius constraint keeps the classification over the input field.
