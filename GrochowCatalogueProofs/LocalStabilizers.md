# GP-0108 · Constructive tensor stabilizers over prime-power rings

**Result.** For each fixed prime p, including p=2, there is a deterministically recognizable family of dense tensors over Z/p^e whose full stabilizer and kernel of reduction modulo p have explicit matrix generators computable in polynomial bit time. The family allows unbounded precision, all three mode dimensions, number of blocks, and block sizes. The proof is written.

Put R=Z/p^e and consider tensors with matrix slices which, after independent bases on the first and second mode, have the form

    B(s)=⊕_j λ_j(s) I_{d_j}.

The d_j are pairwise distinct positive sizes. The coefficient forms λ_j are pairwise nonproportional modulo p and some m of them form an R-basis of the dual of the m-dimensional slice space. Choose this frame by greedily selecting independent residue forms in increasing order of d_j. In this frame require each remaining coefficient to be zero or a unit. For example any system of distinct nonzero 0/1 coordinates containing the coordinate vectors has these properties. For any number of blocks there are such examples, with no requirement to present the frame or blocks.

Recognition uses the shared adjoint equations XᵀB_i=B_iY. For a valid tensor, the two by two scalar equations from nonproportionality modulo p have determinant a unit. They force the off-diagonal blocks of X and Y to vanish. On each block X_j is arbitrary and Y_j is the prescribed adjoint. Thus projection to X identifies the joint adjoint algebra with ∏ M_{d_j}(R), up to opposite multiplication. Compute its additive generators and the center by solving linear equations over the principal ideal ring R. For completeness, choosing a nonzero entry of least p-adic valuation as a pivot permits clearing its row and column by elementary operations, since the pivot divides every entry in its submatrix. Repeating gives diagonal invariant factors from which kernels, image membership and lifts follow in polynomial bit time. This also allows an injectivity check on projection. To extract primitive central idempotents, reduce the computed center modulo p, enumerate eigenvalues in the fixed prime field, and refine the common eigenspaces of a basis. On a valid input the residue algebra is F_p^r; the common eigenspaces are its projectors' images. Solve linear equations to express each projector as a residue of a central element. In the commutative center the iteration x↦3x²−2x³ squares the idempotent error ideal. Thus within logarithmically many iterations it lifts each residue projector to an idempotent. Idempotent lifts are unique because the kernel of reduction is nilpotent and the center is commutative, so the idempotents are orthogonal and sum to 1. For matched second-mode blocks use the Y component of the same adjoint idempotents.

Choose free block bases using columns whose residues are independent. The lifted block projectors and their complements have ranks given by these residues. Validate the block sizes, vanishing of off-diagonal slices, existence of one invertible block slice on each block, scalar proportionality of the others, and the required conditions on coefficient forms. The invertible block slice normalizes to I by independent left and right actions. The explicit validation makes the recognition algorithm sound on arbitrary dense inputs.

An input stabilizer permutes the joint-adjoint blocks in both modes. Since sizes are distinct, every block is fixed. Let H act on third-mode coefficients. Its required preservation of the chosen frame lines makes H diagonal in that frame. Because other coefficients are units or zero, H preserves their lines exactly when it has a constant scalar across the support of each coefficient vector. Build the graph whose vertices are frame coordinates and in which two occur together if some support contains both. Each of its c connected components receives one free scalar. Let u_j be the scalar received by λ_j. Writing P_j for the first-mode action, the second-mode action must be Q_j=u_j^{-1} P_j^{-T}, and every such choice stabilizes the tensor. Thus the full stabilizer is

    (∏_j GL_{d_j}(R)) × (R^×)^c.

If coordinate conventions replace H by its contragredient, the determinant and congruence conditions are changed by inversion only. The displayed coordinate isomorphism identifies the kernel modulo p with the product of the principal congruence subgroups in the displayed factors. Changing to and from the computed bases transports these generator matrices to the original coordinates and preserves the congruence condition.

For GL_d(R) use transvections I+E_ij and single-coordinate diagonal matrices with unit generators. All additive coefficients in R are integer multiples of 1, so powers of the listed transvections give all elementary operations. Elimination over a local ring then proves generation. For the congruence kernel use I+pE_ij and diagonal generators for principal units: elimination to a diagonal matrix requires only p-divisible off-diagonal operations, because all current diagonal pivots are congruent to 1 modulo p. For odd fixed p take any generator of F_p^×, lift it to its unique (p−1)-st root of unity by Hensel lifting, and add 1+p for the principal units. For p=2 and e≥3 principal units are generated by −1 and 5, as follows by the elementary valuation identities v_2(5^{2^j}−1)=j+2. The cases e=1,2 are immediate. For odd p the identity v_p((1+p)^{p^j}−1)=j+1 follows by binomial expansion. These lists yield polynomially many matrices and the entire computation is polynomial in dimensions and e log p for each fixed p. The center extraction and choice of residue unit generators here use fixed p. Uniform arbitrary-prime complexity is a separate question.

This combines the joint-adjoint recovery for alternating spaces with exact local nilpotent lifting and explicit congruence groups.
