# GP-0247 · Tensor-isomorphism classification for quadratic refinements in characteristic two

**Result.** Pseudo-isometry of a space of quadratic forms over a finite field of characteristic two, with arbitrary change of output basis, is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over that field. The lower bound still applies if polarization is injective on the space of forms, so the input can instead be specified by an alternating space equipped with refinements of its basis. The bounds are uniform in the explicit field presentation. The proof is written.

For the upper reduction, write V for the variable space and S for the parameter space of the quadratic forms. Include the canonical multiplication map V*×V*→Sym²(V*) as a bilinear structure tensor and the input linear map S→Sym²(V*). An isomorphism of the multiplication forces the action on Sym²(V*) to be the symmetric square of the action on V*: every degree-two monomial is a product of two degree-one elements, including pure squares. Thus an isomorphism of the two specified maps is exactly a simultaneous variable change and arbitrary output-basis change of the quadratic map, with no assumption on the rank of polarization. The fixed-signature tensor compiler gives the upper reduction. This uses formal quadratic polynomials. The evaluation function also determines the coefficients, even over F_2, by values on basis vectors and on their pairwise sums.

For the lower reduction, start with a three-tensor T whose matrix slices A_1,…,A_l are linearly independent, after compressing the slice support. For an input pair, differing support dimensions give an immediate negative case, and changing to each compressed support preserves and reflects tensor isomorphism in equal original dimensions. Use the marked alternating space of Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Part IV, Theorem 2.1. The variable blocks of its construction are the two data blocks U and W, a dual copy U' of U, and a private hyperbolic block H⊕H'. Its data slices have only U–W pairings and its extra slice is the sum of the standard U–U' and H–H' pairings. All slices are therefore off-diagonal with respect to the same bipartition

    L=U⊕H,    R=W⊕U'⊕H'.

On an alternating form B with this property, take the quadratic refinement q_B(x+y)=B(x,y) for x∈L,y∈R. It is the unique quadratic refinement whose restrictions to both L and R are zero. The assignment B↦q_B is linear, and polarization is its inverse on this image. In particular the constructed quadratic-space input has injective polarization.

If the original tensors are isomorphic, the explicit forward transporter of Theorem 2.1 acts block diagonally on U,W,U',H,H', with the contragredient action on U' correcting the anchor pair and identity on the private hyperbolic pair. Thus it respects the common bipartition, so it sends the assigned quadratic refinements exactly as it sends their polarizations. The same arbitrary mixing of data slices and the fixed anchor scalar are allowed. In the other direction, any quadratic-space isomorphism polarizes to an isometry of the marked alternating spaces. The reflection direction of Theorem 2.1 then gives the original three-tensor isomorphism. Hence every quadratic-space transporter is sound even if it does not preserve the chosen bipartition. This proves the classification and transfers finite-field TI algorithms without inheriting exhaustive search as part of the reduction itself.

Compared with forgetting the pure-square coordinates, the upper compiler retains the entire quadratic map. Compared with demanding a change of basis fixing the quadratic-form labels, both directions allow unrestricted output mixing.
