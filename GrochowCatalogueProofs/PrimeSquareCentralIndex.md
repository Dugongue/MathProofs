# Epimorphism to groups of prime-square central index

**Theorem.** Fix a nonabelian finite group $H$ with $|H:Z(H)|=p^2$ for some prime $p$. Given a finite presentation of a group, deciding whether it surjects onto $H$ is NP-complete. In particular this holds for the quaternion group $Q_8$ and for any fixed group of prime-cube order that is nonabelian.

The quotient $V=H/Z(H)$ has order $p^2$. It cannot be cyclic because any group with cyclic central quotient is abelian. Thus $V$ is two-dimensional over $\mathbb F_p$. The group $H$ is class two, so commutators give an alternating bilinear map on $V$ into $Z(H)[p]$. Its image generates a cyclic subgroup of order $p$: the map is determined by its value on one basis pair of $V$, and that value is nontrivial since $H$ is nonabelian. Upon identifying its image with the additive group of $\mathbb F_p$, the pairing is therefore nondegenerate.

Take the finite-presentation reduction of [the dihedral case](DihedralEpimorphism.md), with the following modifications. Encode the input Boolean circuit over $\mathbb F_p$ using $z=xy$ for AND and $z=1-x$ for NOT, then homogenize every equation to degree two. Keep the two commuting halves, the symmetry constraints $c_{ij}c_{ji}^{-1}=1$, and the normalization constraints $c_{ii}c_{0i}^{-1}=1$ for $i\ge1$, with $c_{ij}=[a_i,b_j]$. Omit the involution relators. Add a constant number of auxiliary generators, sufficient to generate $Z(H)$, with relators forcing each to commute with every generator. Interpret each homogeneous coefficient as an exponent of the corresponding $c_{ij}$, as an integer representative modulo $p$. The target and $p$ are fixed, so the construction is polynomial in the circuit size.

In an onto assignment, every auxiliary element has image in $Z(H)$ because it commutes with all generator images. The images of the two commuting halves must therefore span the quotient $V$. Each half spans a totally isotropic subspace of dimension at most one, so they span distinct lines. Choose generators $u,v$ of the two lines, and write the quotient images as $x_i u$ and $y_i v$. Their pairing is a nonzero scalar $\beta$. The cross-commutator matrix is $\beta xy^{\mathsf T}$. It is nonzero and symmetric, so $y$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of $x$. The normalization equations imply $x_i^2=x_0x_i$ for every $i\ge1$. Since the matrix is nonzero, $x_0\ne0$. Thus $x_i/x_0\in\{0,1\}$, and the homogeneous equation relators recover the Boolean circuit constraints when divided by the common nonzero scalar $\beta(y_0/x_0)x_0^2$. This establishes soundness.

Given a satisfying assignment, set $x_0=1$ and choose fixed lifts in $H$ of any two independent quotient vectors. Assign $a_i,b_i$ their respective lifted vectors to the Boolean powers $x_i$. Use the auxiliary generators to generate $Z(H)$. The chosen images generate $H$, and the commutator relators hold because every relevant homogeneous equation vanishes. Membership in NP is by guessing and checking images in the fixed finite target.

Nonabelian groups of order $p^3$ have center of order $p$, so they satisfy the hypothesis. This extension uses the same alternating-central-commutator structure as the [class-two group–tensor correspondence](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/b0947ecc8675432a7292628b99e945b7d50b216e/FourIsomorphismResults/GroupTensorEquivalence.md). For related results on counting all homomorphisms into nonabelian targets and the distinction between counting and deciding surjectivity, see [Samperton–Weiß, Sections 1.5–1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02885v2#S1.SS5).
