# One ordinary comparison from polynomial branches, with an alternating-form sign test

All statements below use explicit arrays over a fixed finite field, with the sign statement over the field of three elements. A basis change on a covariant mode inverts its determinant, which preserves its sign.

**Theorem.** An explicitly supplied polynomial-size monotone Boolean formula with ordinary TI comparisons at its leaves can be reduced to one ordinary TI comparison in deterministic polynomial time. There is no oracle evaluation of the leaves during the construction.

In particular, one-sign TI reduces to one ordinary TI comparison on the following class. From a raw input tensor T, suppose one can compute in polynomial time an intrinsic subspace B(T) of alternating forms on the tested mode, of dimension O(log L) for raw length L. Require B(T) to contain a nonsingular form, and require every ordinary transporter to carry these subspaces to one another by the induced action on forms. A single reduction covers both flexible tensors with a negative stabilizer sign and rigid tensors whose stabilizer signs are all positive. An example below has scalar pair adjoints and exponential projective point orbits, and exhibits both signs regimes.

## Keep the gates inside one algebra category

Use finite-dimensional unital associative algebras with radical J satisfying J³=0 and with quotient a product of copies of the base field. The vertex idempotents lift its coordinate idempotents. They can be aligned under an algebra isomorphism by an inner conjugation. In fact two complete orthogonal lifted systems f_i,e_i with matching images have f_i u=u e_i for u=Σ f_i e_i, and u equals 1 modulo J, hence is invertible. Thus all dimensions of the spaces e_iJe_j and all induced corner algebras are independent of the lifts up to isomorphism. In particular the sequence of labels given by the dimensions of e_iJe_i is well defined up to the induced permutation of vertices.

Encode a bilinear tensor T:U×V→W in a three-vertex algebra with the nonzero off-diagonal radical spaces e_0Je_1=U, e_1Je_2=V, e_0Je_2=W. Put the bilinear tensor in their multiplication and make all other radical products zero. Give the three vertices private radical loop spaces of dimensions 1,2,3 to distinguish them. An algebra isomorphism induces exactly an ordinary tensor transporter, and conversely any such transporter extends by arbitrary invertible maps on the loops. This holds even if some off-diagonal spaces vanish. The construction uses no isomorphism decisions.

For two algebras A,C in this category form an ordered tuple algebra P(A,C). Start with their direct product, add three vertices r,a,c, and add one radical arrow from r to each anchor a,c. Add one radical arrow from a to every vertex of A and one from c to every vertex of C. All products between radical elements involving a new arrow are zero. Equivalently the new arrow spaces are simple bimodules for the enlarged semisimple quotient, with the indicated vertices acting as their endpoints. Add private radical loops of three distinct dimensions above the maximum of the old vertex loop dimensions. These tags, which can be fixed by construction depth, distinguish r,a,c, and the arrows from a,c identify the two child vertex sets. The formula for the multiplication and the bimodule construction prove associativity, and the enlarged radical still has cube zero.

Every isomorphism of these tuple algebras sends the tagged vertices to the identically tagged vertices. After aligning lifts, it sends the corner algebra on the child a vertices to the corresponding whole copy of A, and the child c vertices to the whole copy of C. Conversely two child algebra isomorphisms extend, since they permute each child's vertices and hence extend to the arrows from its anchor. Thus tuple isomorphism means coordinatewise isomorphism.

The tuple algebra is indecomposable as a direct product. A central idempotent reduces modulo its radical to the sum of some vertex idempotents. Its commuting relation with any nonzero space e_iJe_j forces the coefficients at i and j to agree. For the new arrows this follows even modulo J², since every new arrow survives that quotient. The anchors and root connect every vertex, so the coefficients are either all one or all zero. A central idempotent reducing to zero lies in the nilpotent radical and vanishes, and one reducing to one is one by the same argument applied to its complement. This proves indecomposability without recognizing the original children's product decompositions.

If (A,B) and (C,D) are two comparison pairs, implement AND by comparing P(A,C) and P(B,D). Implement OR by comparing

    P(A,C) × P(B,D)     and     P(A,D) × P(B,C).

A product isomorphism matches primitive central factors. Each displayed factor is indecomposable. Matching them in order is possible exactly when C≅D. Matching them across is possible exactly when A≅B. Conversely either equality supplies the corresponding product isomorphism. This is the prior unordered-pair identity. The split basic algebras here give an explicit gate implementation with linear additive dimension at each gate. [Earlier OR identity and anchor comparison](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/GapOne.md)

For a formula of logarithmic depth, complete the bottom-up construction with fresh three-label tags assigned by depth, so that all compared variants agree on new tags. Each gate copies each child a bounded number of times and adds at most one arrow per child vertex, plus O(depth) loop dimensions. Thus the dimension grows by at most a constant factor per level, plus a polynomial amount. Logarithmic depth gives polynomial dimensions and dense multiplication tables. An arbitrary monotone formula of polynomial size can be balanced with polynomial overhead. Choose a subformula G whose number of leaves is between one third and two thirds of the total, by descending the larger child until it enters this interval. Monotonicity gives the original value as

    F(G) = F(0) OR (G AND F(1)).

Simplify constants immediately. The three recursive formulas each have at most two thirds of the starting leaf count after simplification, unless constant. Repeating gives O(log size) depth and polynomial growth in size. True and false leaves can be implemented with fixed pairs. Finally reduce the one resulting algebra-isomorphism comparison to one ordinary TI comparison by the bounded-order tensor-system compiler. This compiler is applied once to the finished tables. [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219) · [Grochow–Qiao, tensor isomorphism completeness](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/21M1441110)

## Compile the alternating-form criterion to one ordinary comparison

Enumerate B(T) to find a nonsingular alternating form b. This is polynomial over the field of three elements at logarithmic dimension. Compute its Pfaffian using symplectic elimination or by building a paired symplectic basis. Rank and Pfaffian are measured in the raw coordinate frames of the tested mode. Enumerate the forms b' in B(S) with the same Pfaffian as b. For each, make the ordinary comparison of the tensor with the added bilinear-form marker b on the source and b' on the target. The marker includes its actual value, using the same tested mode maps on both form arguments and a fixed scalar output. A fixed-signature tensor-system compiler produces an ordinary TI instance for each branch. [Tensor-system compiler](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

If the branch admits a transporter with tested map g, it satisfies g^t b' g=b, hence Pf(b)=det(g) Pf(b'). The matching nonzero Pfaffians force det(g)=1. Conversely any positive transporter sends b to a target form b' by the inverse congruence. Covariance puts that form in B(S), and the Pfaffian identity puts it in the tested list. That transporter extends to the marked comparison. The prior section combines all branches by OR before one final TI query. This proves correctness without needing to find a reference transporter or to classify either stabilizer. One can also compute the full one-sign profile by repeating the argument once for each prescribed Pfaffian ratio.

A direct linear-algebra source of possible form spaces is the space of all alternating forms annihilated by a functorial Lie subalgebra on the mode. If a derivation-derived algebra has basis X_j, the equations are X_j^t b+bX_j=0. They are linear in b and conjugacy covariance makes the solution spaces intrinsic. The logarithmic dimension and existence of a nonsingular form remain actual conditions of this branch. The proof also works for any polynomial-size intrinsic list of nonsingular forms, supplied explicitly instead of enumerating a subspace.

## A high-dimensional family with flexible and rigid cases

Let V have even dimension n≥6 and choose a nonsingular alternating bivector P∈Λ²V, viewed as an invertible alternating map V*→V. Consider

    T(x,y) = [x∧y] ∈ Λ²V / F P.

The tested mode is the first. All inputs may be independently rebased in all three modes. On raw arrays T:U×V→W find the space of f:U→V satisfying T(x,f(x))=0 for all x. These are linear conditions on f. Testing the coordinate vectors and their pairwise sums suffices, by bilinearity. In standard coordinates they imply x∧f(x)=0 because a nonzero decomposable bivector has rank two, whereas every nonzero multiple of P has rank n. Thus f is scalar. This follows by using coordinate vectors to obtain diagonal form and their pairwise sums to equate the diagonal entries. In raw coordinates the space is one-dimensional with an invertible generator f.

Choose any such generator. The map T(x,f(y)) is alternating and defines a linear map on Λ²U. It is onto W and its kernel is a line of nonsingular bivectors. This construction recognizes the class from raw arrays: require the one-dimensional f-space with invertible generator, require surjectivity, and require the kernel to be a single line of nonsingular alternating bivectors. Then the resulting quotient is exactly the stated family, since all nonsingular alternating forms of the same dimension are congruent. Invert a generator of the kernel to get a covariant alternating form b on U. The line F b is intrinsic. Indeed a transporter takes the original f-line to the target f-line, and altering the chosen f by a nonzero scalar does not affect the kernel line.

For the standard tensor, write an autotopism as T(Ax,By)=C T(x,y). Setting y=x forces x↦A⁻¹Bx to preserve every line, hence B=λ A. Descending the exterior action forces Λ²A to preserve the line of P. Conversely every such A and nonzero λ gives an autotopism with the induced quotient map. Thus the tested map group is the symplectic similitude group. If A^t b A=μ b, then det A=μ^(n/2) by the Pfaffian, and both multipliers occur by scaling the first half of a paired symplectic basis. Therefore this tensor is rigid precisely for n divisible by four, and flexible for n equal to two modulo four. In the rigid case, the two generators of F b have equal Pfaffian. The ratio between the source and target Pfaffians determines the unique possible relative sign. In the flexible case there is always a positive transporter whenever ordinary TI holds. Thus the raw recognition even yields a deterministic direct algorithm on pairs in this family because shared format entails ordinary equivalence.

All pair-adjoint algebras are scalar. For the two input modes a relation T(Ax,y)=T(x,By) lifts to the same wedge equality before quotient, since the difference of two decomposable bivectors has rank at most four and cannot be a nonzero multiple of P. Comparing coefficients at x=e_i,y=e_j forces A,B diagonal and their diagonal scalars equal across all distinct i,j. For an input and the output, setting y=x forces each line to be preserved by the input map, and then spanning forces the same scalar on the output. The same proof works over extensions. Nonetheless the full symplectic group acts simultaneously on both input modes, and is transitive on the nonzero vectors in each. In either input mode every nonempty invariant projective list therefore contains every projective point. This gives an exponential obstruction to invariant point lists inside a family covered by the alternating-form test.

The unrestricted missing statement is a polynomial-size ordinary comparison or polynomially many sound positive branches covering every raw tensor. The construction gives both signs regimes on the alternating-form class, and removes OR packaging from any extension of that branch. It supplies no universal intrinsic alternating forms. Proofs are informal and priority for this combination is unverified.
