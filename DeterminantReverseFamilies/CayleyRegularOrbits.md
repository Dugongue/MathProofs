# Parity on arbitrarily many regular 2-group orbits

All groups are given by full Cayley tables with named element colors. This is an informal deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction to ordinary tensor isomorphism over F₃. Overlaps among marks are allowed when each mark is a union of named colors. The parity convention uses the displayed orders on the marked sets.

**Theorem.** Suppose each color occurring in a parity test carries an explicit functorially recovered free action of a noncyclic 2-group P. P may depend on the color. It must have a polynomial-size element set and be carried to the corresponding group under every isomorphism of the original colored tables, with equivariance of the displayed action. Then all the requested parity tests reduce to one ordinary TI instance. The number of P-orbits in a color can grow arbitrarily.

For one concrete raw-input recognition procedure let R be the right-translation stabilizer of a color S, namely the set of g with Sg=S. Compute P=O₂(R) and use p·s=sp⁻¹. The action is free. This applies to arbitrary ambient group tables and to every mark that is a coset of a noncyclic 2-subgroup, as well as unions of arbitrarily many cosets with the appropriate right stabilizer. As in the 3-group construction, one may also try left translations, or use the conjugation and left-right stabilizers when their computed 2-cores act freely.

## A group isomorphism sign on 2-groups

Write Φ(P)=P²[P,P] and let d=dim_F₂ P/Φ(P). The sign of translation on a finite 2-group K is positive unless K is cyclic and the translating element is a generator of this cyclic group. Indeed the sign on the regular action is (-1) raised to |K| minus |K| divided by the element order, which is negative exactly in that case.

If d≥3, arbitrary isomorphisms of P have positive permutation sign in independently chosen adapted orders. Here an adapted order starts with ordered basis lifts g_i of P/Φ(P). It lists the cosets in lexicographic binary basis coordinates, uses the representatives r(v)=g_1^v_1 ⋯ g_d^v_d, and within every coset uses the same independently chosen order of Φ(P). For Φ(P)=1 the result is the parity of a linear bijection of F₂^d. Its transvections generate it and each swaps 2^(d−2) pairs, hence is even for d≥3.

If Φ(P) is nontrivial, the block permutation appears to the power |Φ(P)| and the internal subgroup map appears to the power 2^d, both even. Thus the entire sign is the product of signs of the translations in the coset fibers. If Φ(P) is noncyclic these are positive. If Φ(P) is cyclic, project the characteristic subgroup Φ(P) to Φ(P)/Φ(P)². This quotient has order two. Its conjugation action in P/Φ(P)² is trivial, since a group of order two has no nontrivial automorphism. Put z for its nonidentity element. In P/Φ(P)² the representative product has the form

    r(v)r(w) = r(v+w) z^b(v,w),

where b is bilinear over F₂. To see this, move the lifts into increasing index order, accounting for one central commutator per inverted pair. Reducing intermediate exponents from 2 to 0 accounts for a central square on the corresponding diagonal term. These operations respectively contribute products v_i w_j and v_i w_i. This proof works regardless of the chosen basis lifts.

For an isomorphism F:P→P′ with quotient map L, write F(r(v))=r′(Lv)z′^h(v) in the target quotient. Substituting the ordered product of the images of the basis lifts and using the bilinear representative rule shows that h(v) is a Boolean polynomial of degree at most two. Its linear terms are the individual discrepancies on the basis lifts. Its quadratic terms arise from the target bilinear representative rule evaluated on two basis images. There is no term of higher degree. The sign of the coset translation at v is (-1)^h(v). Each monomial of degree at most two is supported on an even number of points of F₂^d for d≥3. Thus the sum of the translation exponents is zero. This completes the adapted-order parity proof. Any other written order differs by a computable calibration, obtained by enumerating the adapted order from the table.

For a noncyclic 2-group with d=2 we can attach its exact one-dimensional permutation-sign line without enumerating the group of arbitrary permutation witnesses. Enumerate the at most |P|² ordered generating pairs. For each pair (x,y) enumerate the elements by breadth-first traversal from the identity with the two outgoing multiplications in their specified order, and write the Cayley table in that induced order. Group closure and this traversal are polynomial in |P|. Retain precisely the pairs yielding the lexicographically least such table. This family is intrinsic under group isomorphism. The traversal order is functorial on a chosen pair, since group maps preserve word evaluations and equality. For each retained pair t put ε_t equal to the sign of its traversal order relative to a preferred order on P. An isomorphism F therefore sends ε_t to ε_F(t)=sign(F) ε_t using the preferred orders. Bundle the retained pair positions into a coordinate algebra and attach a linear map sending coordinate e_t to ε_t times a coordinate vector of a one-dimensional space. The map is surjective and forces the induced action on that line to be exactly sign(F). The pair permutation itself is forced by its two projection maps to the group-element coordinate space. Every group isomorphism extends, since it sends the minimal-table pair set to the minimal-table pair set with this sign relation. Noncyclicity excludes d<2.

## Remove parity on an entire color

Choose an arbitrary basepoint in every P-orbit and enumerate its elements by applying P in its preferred order to that basepoint. Under a compatible isomorphism of original data between the two endpoints, each orbit correspondence is expressed by one and the same group isomorphism on P followed by a translation in P′. All translations are even since P is noncyclic. Since all orbit sizes are even, exchanging two whole orbit blocks contributes a positive sign, regardless of the permutation of orbits. In concatenated orbit orders the color sign is therefore sign(F:P→P′)^m where m is the common number of orbits. There is an additional known factor for converting the concatenated order to the raw color order. The calibration does not require an isomorphism search: both permutations of written orders are explicit. For d≥3 choose the adapted group orders above, so the computed color sign is simply the calibration. For d=2 use the one-dimensional line just constructed if m is odd, and a trivial pinned line if m is even.

For a union of colors take their fixed color order and concatenate, using the raw mark order for the final calibration. The product of color signs is then the desired mark sign. A running sequence of bilinear product maps on one-dimensional lines, with its first and final vectors pinned, imposes the calibrated sign. An explicit binary system of parity conditions uses a separate sequence per equation. This also works if the same color line appears in several equations, so the witness is not required to be independently chosen.

## Recover and compile the data

From an explicit Cayley table compute R by setwise tests and O₂(R) by testing whether each element's normal closure is a 2-group. The union of the elements passing is exactly the largest normal 2-subgroup, because products of normal 2-subgroups are again normal 2-subgroups. These computations commute with color-preserving group isomorphisms. Subgroups of G×G used for left-right actions have polynomial cardinality. Compute free orbits by direct enumeration and reject any format mismatch relevant to the isomorphism test. The group rank and adapted orders are computed with the Frattini quotient, using subgroup closure and linear algebra over F₂ on explicit group cosets; these calculations do not require a characteristic change of any isomorphism instance.

The ordinary tensor-system compiler uses the same two group products and tag bundles as in the companion result for odd 3-group orbits. Coordinate algebras with parent maps force action on group elements and ordered tuples, and their tied projections force the action on the direct sums of color lines and equation states. For each tensor sort all subgroup, pair and line data of that sort are bundled into direct sums tagged by their parent records. The coefficients ε_t in the d=2 branch constitute one linear map across the tagged bundle. Fixed position names for the signs and the equation products, with the initial and terminal pins, yield a fixed number of roles and maps of polynomial dimension. Consequently the bounded-order tensor-system compiler gives one ordinary F₃ TI instance. Extra independent ordinary TI conditions can be included with their own fixed roles. All original admissible isomorphisms extend and only such isomorphisms extend. [Ordinary tensor-system compiler, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

The theorem depends on the free noncyclic action. For a cyclic even-order action, translations can contribute additional odd signs independently of the automorphism of the acting group, so that case is outside this construction.
