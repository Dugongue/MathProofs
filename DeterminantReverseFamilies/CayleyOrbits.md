# Removing marked Cayley-table parity on large homogeneous colors

Work over F₃. The input consists of two explicit Cayley tables, arbitrary named element colors, and parity equations on the permutations of specified color sets. All reductions are deterministic polynomial-time many-one, and the proofs are informal. An element mark is given by a union of named colors and by its ordering.

**Theorem.** For each color that occurs in a parity equation, let a 3-group act on its elements, with the actions and their stabilizers obtained functorially from the table and colors. If the color has O(log L) orbits under this action, where L is the input length, all the required parity constraints can be removed in one ordinary F₃ tensor isomorphism comparison. The group actions used here need only have explicit domains of polynomial size. In particular, the following are polynomially recognizable applications.

1. Use the largest normal 3-subgroup of the setwise conjugation stabilizer of each color, acting by conjugation.
2. Use the largest normal 3-subgroup of the subgroup of G×G preserving the color by left-right translations, acting by (g,h)·x=gxh⁻¹.
3. Use a distinguished 3-subgroup acting by conjugation on the color if the subgroup is already preserved by the input data.

The construction allows a polynomial number of distinct colors, and each orbit may be arbitrarily large. An arbitrary color union is covered when all its constituent active colors pass one of these tests. As a special case, parity on any one conjugacy class in a Cayley-table 3-group reduces to ordinary F₃ tensor isomorphism. The whole unrestricted marked Cayley-table rung need not pass the orbit bound.

## 1. Orientation lines for small-order 3-groups

For a finite set T with ordered elements define D(T) to be a one-dimensional space whose map for a bijection is multiplication by its sign in the chosen orders. This can be viewed as the top exterior line of F₃^T. We will avoid building the exterior algebra on F₃^T when T is large.

For a table 3-group K with at most polynomially many elements, take the characteristic iterated Frattini series K_0=K, K_{i+1}=K_i³[K_i,K_i]. The quotients V_i are elementary abelian 3-groups of dimension at most log₃|K|. Choose a basis in each quotient and enumerate the group elements in adapted recursive coset orders. An isomorphism f of these groups permutes quotient blocks by the linear quotient map and acts inside each matched block by the subgroup isomorphism followed by a translation. The translation is even because it has odd order. The coset sizes and their number are odd, so the sign of f on K is the product of the permutation signs on the V_i. Each quotient permutation sign equals the determinant of its linear map: transvections have order three and one-coordinate negations swap 3^(dim V_i−1) pairs. These generate the general linear group. For an independent raw order, incorporate the change-of-order sign at each endpoint.

A tensor diagram can therefore realize D(K) using the exterior algebra on each V_i, including the degree-one embedding and its top line. Exterior dimension is 2^dim V_i, polynomial in the table size. The determinant-product line is carried by a sequence of bilinear maps from the next top line and a running line to a new running line. The starting line is pinned to its unit. The final line has the required sign action and is free to participate in further equations. These are the same exterior and determinant-product tools used for Frattini layers.

## 2. Compute the orientation of a transitive orbit with no pinned point

Let a 3-group P act on an orbit O. The orbit has odd size. For each s∈O, let H_s=P_s be its point stabilizer. The map P→O given by p↦p·s has fibers r_x H_s, for x∈O. Use a chosen order on O, the adapted orders of P and H_s from Section 1, and independently chosen coset representatives r_x. Define k_s=±1 to be the sign of reordering P from the fiber listing (r_x h)_(x∈O,h∈H_s) to its chosen adapted order. Changing the representatives does not change this sign: it multiplies each h-list by a left translation of the odd-order group H_s.

Consider an isomorphism of the original colored objects, with induced isomorphism f_P:P→P′, stabilizer map f_H:H_s→H′_{f(s)}, and orbit bijection f_O:O→O′. In the two fiber listings, the sign of f_P is the sign of f_O times the sign of f_H. In fact the cosets have odd size, so an exchange of two blocks gives the same sign as their exchange on O. The internal sign of f_H occurs an odd number of times, one for each block, and the discrepancy between f_P(r_x) and r′_{f(x)} gives only even translations. Thus, in the three chosen order pairs,

    sign(f_P) k_s = k′_{f(s)} sign(f_O) sign(f_H).

Introduce a free one-dimensional space L_O for each orbit, with fixed coordinate vector at each endpoint. For every s∈O include a bilinear map L_O×D(H_s)→D(P), sending the two selected coordinate vectors to k_s times the selected coordinate vector. All these maps force the action on L_O to be precisely sign(f_O). The displayed identity also proves that every original isomorphism lifts on these lines. Every s is included, so the original isomorphism is free to move all basepoints. This is the additional construction relative to the earlier coset and Frattini results.

## 3. Combine moving orbits

Fix one input color. Order its P-orbits independently at the endpoints, and order each orbit's elements independently. For the permutation of the color in the resulting concatenated orders, the sign is the product of the orbit signs times the sign of the permutation of the orbits. All odd block sizes give the same factor as a permutation of the corresponding orbit indices. This remains true if sizes vary: swapping two adjacent blocks incurs the sign (-1)^(s t) for their two sizes s,t, and both sizes are odd.

Take the direct sum of its orbit lines L_O with its summand labels tied to the actual orbit permutation. Its determinant is exactly this product. The assumed logarithmic orbit count means the full exterior algebra of that direct sum is polynomial size. Its top line therefore implements the color sign. Changing to the supplied raw mark orders contributes precomputable calibration signs. For several marked unions or simultaneous parity equations, compute the sign product of the corresponding color lines in each equation. Use one running product per equation and pin the final vectors to the calibrated desired signs. A disconnected marked set requires no choice of a matching of its orbits. The orbit permutation is induced by the single original isomorphism.

## 4. Effective group and tensor data

Using a table, setwise conjugation stabilizers are subgroups obtained by checking every candidate element, and left-right stabilizers are subgroups obtained by checking all pairs. The latter uses the coordinatewise product on G×G. An explicit multiplication table for any resulting subgroup has polynomial size when the ambient set of candidates does. The largest normal 3-subgroup of any such table group K is polynomial-time computable: test for each element x whether its normal closure in K is a 3-group. The positive elements exactly comprise O₃(K) because the product of normal 3-subgroups is again a normal 3-subgroup. Normal closures are computed by table conjugation and subgroup closure. All steps commute with table isomorphisms preserving the colors. The color orbits and stabilizers themselves are found by direct enumeration. When choosing between two permitted actions, use the same fixed tie rule based on the orbit counts. In a 3-group the whole group preserves each conjugacy class and acts transitively there. A prior example, a coset rH with H a 3-subgroup, also passes the left-right test. The right H action is a transitive normal 3-subgroup of its left-right stabilizer since any right component of a preserving pair normalizes H=(rH)⁻¹(rH).

The original group-colored binding is encoded by the two products on F₃^G, coordinate multiplication and e_g e_h=e_(gh) for the second product. The first forces permutation of the coordinate basis and the second forces the group isomorphism. A map to a split color algebra with a shift fixes the color names. For actions inside G×G, add the map e_g⊗e_h↦e_(g,h); its values span the pair role and force the corresponding pair permutation. The P-orbits carry a permutation tied to the map that sends an element to its orbit coordinate. All data for P and each H_s is tied to the old group maps through inclusion and quotient maps, so the only permissible actions are the induced ones.

Here is how to keep the number of tensors fixed as the number of colors, stabilizers, and layers grows. For each kind of index set use its split coordinate algebra, with basis of primitive idempotents indexed by its records. Put spaces of the same kind into the direct sum indexed by those records and mark the coordinate algebra action by projections. Include the parent index maps by sending each idempotent to the idempotent of its color, orbit, element, or position in an ordered list. The tuple of these parent labels identifies each record uniquely. The old coordinate permutations and the fixed position labels thus force the permutation of records. Include subgroup and Frattini quotient maps in bundles, so that the map on each tag is its original inclusion or surjection. Use componentwise exterior multiplication. For the maps in Section 2, a single bilinear map on the sums of orbit and stabilizer lines is declared zero unless the point lies in the orbit, in which case the output is in the corresponding P line with coefficient k_s. The running products in Sections 1 and 3 can also be indexed by position and by parent, using one bilinear map and a pinned initial line for each bundle. Copying a line into several summands uses its explicit inclusions. Thus there are a fixed number of types of linear and bilinear maps and a fixed number of roles, all of polynomial dimensions. In particular the tensor-system construction has no hidden dependence on a growing signature. The ordinary bounded-order tensor-system compiler then gives one ordinary tensor comparison. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

The isomorphism extension and restriction arguments show exact preservation of feasible old isomorphisms. Fixed negative outputs handle mismatched visible data. The result does not make an assumption on the number of elements in a color or on independent choices of witnesses across its orbits. It leaves open colors with too many orbits under the permitted actions.
