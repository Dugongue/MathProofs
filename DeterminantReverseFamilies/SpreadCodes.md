# Marked spread arrangements reduce to one marked graph on two algebraic families

Work over F₃. The input is a colored indexed list of subspaces of dimension d in a space of dimension 2d, with a union of colors marked in input index order. Distinct subspaces in the list are complementary. Repetitions are allowed. There are at least three distinct subspaces. A comparison asks for an ambient linear isomorphism and a matching permutation even on the marked indices. Both reductions below are deterministic polynomial-time many-one and preserve exactly the feasible permutations of the input indices. They are informal proofs.

## Charts from three subspaces

For each ordered triple of indices whose spaces are distinct, call the corresponding spaces U∞, U₀, U₁. Split the ambient space as U₀⊕U∞. The space U₁ is the graph of an invertible map T:U₀→U∞. Use T to identify the second summand with the first. Every input subspace other than U∞ is a graph of an endomorphism Mᵢ of U₀, called its slope. The normalized slopes of U₀ and U₁ are 0 and 1. Indices whose subspace equals U∞ get the separate value ∞.

For a map carrying one ordered triple to another, preservation of U₀ and U∞ makes it block diagonal in their normalized coordinates. Preservation of the identity graph makes its two blocks equal. Thus an ambient map carrying both triples and all remaining labels exists exactly when one invertible g on the first summand satisfies gMᵢg⁻¹=M′_{σ(i)} for all the finite slopes and the ∞ labels match. Arbitrary initial coordinates on U₀ change all the slopes by a common conjugation, leaving this criterion unchanged.

In the graph encodings below include one vertex for every original index, with its color and mark, and every chart vertex is linked to its three original index vertices by separate slot-colored intermediate vertices. This forces a graph isomorphism to use the image ordered triple under its single permutation of the original indices. The mark is only on original index vertices. Each chart carries auxiliary vertices described below. Producing all chart vertices simultaneously removes the need to combine a disjunction of parity queries. Any arrangement isomorphism extends to the data for every chart. A graph isomorphism gives an ambient map by reading any one of its matched charts.

## Case I. Commutative semisimple slope algebras

Assume every chart generates a commutative semisimple algebra A inside End(U₀), using its finite slopes and the unit. There is no bound on the number of field factors or on their degrees. Compute A by multiplication and linear-span enlargement until the span stabilizes, of dimension at most d². Verify commutativity on a basis. On the resulting commutative algebra the cube map is F₃-linear. It is injective exactly when there are no nonzero nilpotents: a nilpotent element eventually vanishes under iterated cubing, whereas x³=0 for some nonzero x immediately certifies nilpotence. A finite-dimensional commutative reduced algebra over the finite field is a product of fields.

For completeness, its field factors can be computed by linear algebra. In the fixed subalgebra of cubing the field factors each become F₃, so this fixed subalgebra is F₃^s. Split the unit idempotent along every element of a linear basis of the fixed subalgebra by applying the three Lagrange polynomials for values 0,1,2. Iteratively multiply these spectral idempotents by each current idempotent and discard zero products. This gives exactly the primitive idempotents of the product, since two factors have distinct evaluations on some basis element. The number of nonzero pieces at every step is at most s. For each field factor Eⱼ compute its degree rⱼ over F₃ and the rank of its module eⱼU₀ over Eⱼ. The latter is dim_F₃(eⱼU₀)/rⱼ. The fields act through the given inclusion A⊆End(U₀), so these modules are faithful factorwise.

For each degree r used in either output choose a common reference field Fᵣ from a computed factor of that degree. This choice may depend on the pair. For every factor E of degree r, compute its r isomorphisms to Fᵣ as follows. The tensor product E⊗Fᵣ over F₃ is the product of r copies of Fᵣ, by separability and the splitting of every degree-r irreducible polynomial over Fᵣ. Apply the same cube-fixed procedure to find the r primitive idempotents. On each corresponding field component, the two canonical inclusions from E and Fᵣ are unital injective maps between spaces of dimension r, so give an explicit isomorphism E→Fᵣ. Distinct components correspond exactly to the r possible isomorphisms. All these computations are polynomial in r.

For each chart put a child vertex for every field factor, colored by its degree and its module rank. For each isomorphism of that factor to its reference field add a further child vertex. For every original label add a record vertex connecting this last vertex to that label. The record color gives the field value of the corresponding slope component under this isomorphism, or the distinguished value ∞ for an infinite slope. Use a common coordinate encoding of reference field values on the two sides, and distinct palettes for different kinds of vertices. All field and chart data are computed before asking the graph comparison.

A simultaneous conjugation of all slopes induces a field-factor matching preserving the module ranks, with factor isomorphisms carrying every slope component to its target. Precomposing the reference-field maps with the inverses of these factor maps extends the match to the entire graph chart. Conversely a match on graph chart vertices gives a factor bijection. On each matched pair of factors any matched reference-field child gives a source and target field map with the same recorded values at all correspondingly labeled slopes. Composing one with the inverse of the other gives a field isomorphism carrying all slope components. The ranks agree, so choosing field bases yields semilinear module isomorphisms, whose sum is one F₃-linear g carrying all slopes in that chart. This is precisely the needed ambient map from the chart criterion. Hence the marked graph retains exactly the input permutations.

The assumption is recognizable in polynomial time by testing every chart, and is invariant under permutations and ambient isomorphism by the chart criterion. There are O(m³) charts for m indices, all their factor and reference data have polynomial size, and each child adds m records. This family includes configurations of distinct lines of a vector space of dimension two over F_{3^r}, viewed as F₃-subspaces: in each chart the normalized slopes are multiplication by field elements, so generate a subfield. Choosing slopes 0,1 and a full-degree element together with ∞ gives examples with arbitrarily large indecomposable dimension. Indeed every endomorphism preserving 0,1,∞ and the full-degree slope acts diagonally on the two summands and commutes with the full multiplication field. Its endomorphism algebra is a field, which excludes nontrivial decomposition. These examples have no nonzero intersection of two distinct input spaces.

## Case II. A bounded number of actual slopes generate the algebra

Fix a constant k. Suppose for every chart the normalized algebra is generated by at most k of its finite slopes and the unit. No commutativity, semisimplicity, or bound on the algebra dimension beyond d² is imposed in this case. Use chart vertices for all ordered anchor triples together with all ordered lists of at most k original indices whose normalized slopes generate that chart algebra. Link the chosen generator indices by separate ordered slots to the original index vertices as well. This eligibility can be checked in deterministic polynomial time by enumerating the O(m^(k+3)) possibilities and computing the generated spans. Ordinary isomorphisms preserve it. The proof would also work if one used only charts that admit this generating tuple and required at least one such chart at each endpoint.

For each generated algebra construct a canonical list of basis words in its ordered generators, as follows. Begin with the identity word. In discovery order, right multiply each discovered basis vector by each named generator in the fixed generator order, keeping a word exactly when its evaluation is linearly independent of the previously kept ones. Keep each word by its parent and letter, so the recording effort is polynomial. If a product is redundant, no extension of it is needed: the entirety of the retained span at termination is closed under right multiplication by each generator, hence contains all words. Under simultaneous conjugation of the named generators the resulting word lists match position by position, because the tests are linear dependence tests.

Color the chart by the simultaneous similarity class of its ordered named generator tuple. Assign these colors across both outputs by polynomially many deterministic strict module-isomorphism tests. One may view the ordered generators as the specified actions of free algebra generators, or take the explicitly generated algebra of the corresponding block-diagonal matrices on the direct sum of the two modules. A module intertwiner is exactly a simultaneous similarity, obtainable by the constructive module isomorphism algorithm over the fixed field. [Chistov, Ivanyos and Karpinski](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf)

For every original label add a record vertex joined to the chart vertex and its label vertex. Color it by the coordinate list of that slope in the chart's basis words, or by ∞ when applicable. Matching charts now guarantees some common invertible conjugator on the generator tuples, and the matched slope coordinate lists force that same map to conjugate every slope. Conversely any simultaneous conjugation carries all generator choices, all chart colors and every slope coordinate list correctly. Thus this graph has precisely the same possible marked label permutations. No algebra basis needs to be selected canonically without the ordered chart labels.

These graph reductions compose with the previously established marked graph-to-ternary-code construction, while any independent ordinary TI comparison in the lower ladder is carried along. They resolve the subspace-to-code reverse on the two specified families. They place no width bound on the subspaces themselves.

The basis-word method is related to the algorithm for labeled projection algebras discussed by King, Mixon, and Waldron. Here we use strict finite-field similarity and preserve the marked permutation while the generator selections can move. [Testing isomorphism between tuples of subspaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03448)
