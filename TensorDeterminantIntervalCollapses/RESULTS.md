# Further collapses among the thirty determinant TI candidates

All results concern deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions on explicit tensors over F3, unless a fixed conjunction or affine probe model is stated. Write O, A, B and S for ordinary TI, one-sign TI, two-sign TI and SL-TI. The unrestricted chain has not collapsed. The proofs are informal.

## Exact reductions on the lower catalogue

The two commutative families L3 and L4 both have the full degree A. The reduction starts with a calibrated castling step that moves the first sign to a total determinant. A commutative local algebra with cube-zero radical then carries that determinant exactly. A two-dimensional marker distinguishes the old tangent subspace by a rank cutoff while its determinant contribution cancels. Thus requiring commutativity or scalar-locality on the active multiplication family leaves the one-sign degree unchanged.

The L5 socle-one problem and its L7 radical-index restriction have the same degree. The reduction identifies the socle with the tops of long nilpotent strings. These raise the radical index to exactly three times the enlarged tangent dimension plus one. The original algebra and determinant are recovered despite possible shears, and every original isomorphism lifts. With prior results, the lower catalogue is now grouped as follows.

For the common Frobenius degree, an intrinsic pairing moves the determinant to an invariant quotient annihilated by the radical, together with at most the socle line. This gives a single ordinary TI instance when the residual dimension is logarithmic in the explicit input length. A separate symmetric-power calculation handles a large middle layer if its multiplication map has small kernel and the ambient symmetric-power determinant is neutral. The notes distinguish the prior odd-socle-degree case and give an example where the residual stays large. [Frobenius quotient proof](Frobenius.md)

## Middle and upper comparisons

The shared-reference comparison M3 packages two independent one-sign tests on the promise that the two sources have the same sign flexibility. A known flexible source instead permits one one-sign instance. The odd-stabilizer candidates M4 and M5 are exactly the independent conjunction with an ordinary transporter supplied for the second comparison. The unary odd-stabilizer problem is between O and A and is closed under polynomial-length disjunction. These statements leave the general middle gap open. [Middle interval proofs](Middle.md)

Every upper sign-sum comparison with a certified nonzero surviving stabilizer sign reduces to one two-sign instance. This includes the plane-balance families when a tested mode in any slot is odd or support-deficient. The correction candidate U2 suffices for SL-TI using a conjunction of three fixed conjugate U2 comparisons. Tilted-line balance and two-plane balance reduce to a conjunction of six one-sign instances, with six sharp in the aggregate-affine oracle model. On the other hand, within uniform affine mixing of independent profiles, an arrangement reduces to another exactly when one common linear change of coordinates embeds its slot subspaces into target slots. That statement explains why copying and adding signs alone cannot remove the general upper gap. [Upper interval proofs](Upper.md) · [Original upper definitions](PriorUpper.md)

The remaining unrestricted comparisons include the published endpoint question of [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5). The sign projection and ordinary packing used here are prior MathProofs results and the published low-order tensor compiler. The accompanying dependency files are included for reference. Publication priority has not been established.
