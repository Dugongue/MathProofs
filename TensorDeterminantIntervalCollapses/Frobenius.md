# Reducing a Frobenius-algebra determinant to an intrinsic quotient

Work over $\mathbb F_3$. Consider the active branch of L5 in the thirty-candidate catalogue. Each input tensor is ordinarily isomorphic to multiplication of a finite-dimensional commutative local algebra $R$ with scalar residue and one-dimensional socle $S=\operatorname{Ann}J$. Here $J$ is its Jacobson radical. The branch asks for an ordinary transporter with determinant one in the first physical mode. The earlier regular-module recognition computes each underlying algebra and also the coordinate changes, so comparisons of determinant signs retain the original raw bases.

**Theorem.** There is a deterministic polynomial-time, intrinsic procedure producing an ideal $L$ with $L^2=0$ and a quotient $M=\operatorname{Ann}L/L$ annihilated by $J$. If $\dim M=O(\log N)$, for dense input length $N$, the active branch reduces to one ordinary TI instance. This includes every pair whose associated graded algebras have odd last degree and perfect pairings of complementary grades, regardless of tangent dimension. The rest of L5 remains unresolved.

## Pair the two outer spaces

Choose a nonzero socle vector $\omega$ and a linear functional $f$ with $f(\omega)=1$. The symmetric form $b(x,y)=f(xy)$ is nondegenerate. Indeed any nonzero principal ideal contains a nonzero socle element, by multiplying successively by the radical until the last nonzero product. The socle is one-dimensional. This also proves that for any ideal $I$, its orthogonal complement under $b$ is $\operatorname{Ann}I$. If $xI\ne0$, choose a nonzero product $xy$ with $y\in I$, and multiply again into the socle. The resulting element of $I$ pairs nontrivially with $x$.

Let $L^2=0$, write $i=\dim L$, and choose frames for $L$ and $M=\operatorname{Ann}L/L$. Lift the second frame into $\operatorname{Ann}L$ and choose a final frame for $R/\operatorname{Ann}L$ dual to the frame of $L$ with respect to $b$. The concatenation is an explicit basis of $R$. Use separate such frames on source and target. An algebra isomorphism $\phi:R\to R'$ has, in these frames, determinant
$$
   \det\phi=\lambda^i\det(\phi_M),\qquad \phi(\omega)=\lambda\omega'.
$$
To verify this, $f'\circ\phi=f(u\,\cdot)$ for a unique $u$ by nondegeneracy, and $u$ has residue $\lambda$ since multiplication by its radical part kills the socle. It is a unit. The outer pairing after applying $\phi$ is therefore the old pairing with the map $x\mapsto ux$ on $L$. That map has determinant $\lambda^i$ on $L$ because the remaining summand is nilpotent. Thus the product of the two outer determinant contributions is $\lambda^i$. The middle map contributes $\det(\phi_M)$ by the filtration.

Every ordinary transporter of a multiplication tensor is an algebra isomorphism followed by left and right multiplication by units. This follows by evaluating the tensor equation at the unit on both sides. In even algebra dimension all such unit multiplications have determinant one, since their determinant is the scalar residue to that dimension. In odd dimension multiplication by the unit $-1$ corrects the first determinant freely. In the even case, the formula gives the raw condition
$$
   \lambda^i\det(\phi_M)=\delta,
$$
where $\delta$ is the ratio of the source and target raw frame determinants, including the recovered multiplication coordinates. In $\mathbb F_3^\times$ inverse and direct ratios have the same value.

## Construct an intrinsic small target for the sign

First test for the odd last-degree perfect-pairing case described below and use its half-dimensional ideal when available. Otherwise start with $L=0$. On $M=\operatorname{Ann}L/L$, the form induced by $b$ is perfect and balanced under the action of $R$. If $JM\ne0$, use the largest $t\ge1$ with $J^tM\ne0$. The subspace $I=J^tM$ is an $R$-submodule and isotropic. A pairing of two such products can be rewritten with a factor from $J^{2t}$ acting on $M$, and this is zero by maximality of $t$. Replace $L$ by the preimage of $I$ in $\operatorname{Ann}L$. It is an ideal, and isotropy means its square is zero by the same ideal-annihilator argument. This strictly decreases $\dim M$ by $2\dim I$. Repeat until $JM=0$. Every step uses only image, annihilator and quotient operations on explicit linear spaces. The ideals, quotient modules and dimensions are invariant under every algebra isomorphism, independently of the choice of $f$. The raw basis calibration only needs the final $L$, not the intermediate choices of frames.

Put $Z=M$ when $i$ is even and $Z=M\oplus S$ when $i$ is odd. The desired determinant is exactly $\det(\phi_Z)=\delta$. Build a typed system that includes the multiplication and unit, the intrinsic subspaces giving $M$ and $S$, their inclusions and the quotient map, and the exterior algebra of $Z$ with its degree-one inclusion. Direct-sum inclusions link the same $M$ and $S$ actions to $Z$. Mark the top wedge in the source and $\delta$ times the corresponding coordinate top wedge in the target. Multiplication with degree-one inclusion forces every exterior action, and the marked volume forces exactly the required sign. Conversely every original algebra map of that sign extends to these tensors. If $Z=0$, its sole determinant is one and the sign prescription can be checked in preprocessing. There are a bounded number of tensor components of order at most three, including the exterior multiplication. Dense size is polynomial in $N$ and $2^{\dim Z}$. Apply the ordinary fixed-signature compiler to obtain a single ordinary TI instance. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

If the associated graded multiplication pairs grades $j,s-j$ perfectly into $J^s=S$ and the last degree $s$ is odd, the intrinsic ideal $I=J^{(s+1)/2}$ has square zero and dimension exactly half the algebra dimension. Use it directly, so $M=0$. The hypothesis is checked by linear algebra on radical powers. This odd last-degree case is a reuse of the earlier MathProofs paired-radical-layer reduction. General context for perfect pairings on intrinsic subquotients is given by [Iarrobino and Macias Marques](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03586). Both forms of this theorem work from raw recognized multiplication tensors. The arbitrary terminal dimension need not be logarithmic, and the logarithmic condition cannot be discarded in this compilation.

## A further case with a large middle grade

Suppose the associated graded multiplication has perfect complementary-grade pairings and last degree $s=2r$, where $r\ge1$ is fixed. Take the intrinsic square-zero ideal $L=J^{r+1}$. Its annihilator is $J^r$, by the dimension equality from perfect pairings, and the middle quotient is $J^r/J^{r+1}$. Let $V=J/J^2$, $e=\dim V$, and let $K$ be the kernel of the surjective multiplication map $\operatorname{Sym}^r V\to J^r/J^{r+1}$. In compatible source and target frames the middle determinant equals
$$
 \det(\phi_V)^{\binom{e+r-1}{r-1}}\det(\phi_K)^{-1}.
$$
This follows by taking determinants in the exact sequence and using the determinant character of the symmetric power. The latter identity follows on a diagonal map by counting exponents of the monomial basis, on elementary shears by triangularity, and then on all invertible matrices by generators. Relative frame changes are computed from the two multiplication maps by linear algebra. Therefore if $\binom{e+r-1}{r-1}$ is even and $\dim K=O(\log N)$, the full sign constraint lies on $K$ and possibly the socle line. The same marked exterior construction imposes it in one ordinary TI query, even when the middle quotient is large.

For fixed $r$, all symmetric powers can be linked by their canonical products in a bounded typed system of polynomial size. The kernel is linked by its inclusion. This makes the computation independent of any chosen splitting of the radical filtration. All statements in this note are informal.

## A large residual can occur

For even $m$, consider $R_m=\mathbb F_3[x_1,\ldots,x_m]/(x_1^2,\ldots,x_m^2)$. Independent sign changes of the variables act on the squarefree monomials by distinct characters. Any ideal preserved by all automorphisms is therefore a span of monomials, since the diagonal sign group is split and has order prime to three. Variable permutations force this span to contain all monomials in each included degree. Multiplication by variables then forces every later degree, so a nonzero characteristic ideal is a radical power. Complementary monomials give $\operatorname{Ann}J^j=J^{m-j+1}$. The largest characteristic square-zero ideal is $J^{m/2+1}$, and its residual has dimension $\binom{m}{m/2}$. The input algebra dimension is $2^m$, so this residual exceeds logarithmic size. This example concerns the intrinsic quotient strategy. It does not imply a lower bound for deciding oriented isomorphism of this algebra family.
