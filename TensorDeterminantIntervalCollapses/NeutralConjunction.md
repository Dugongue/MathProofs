# Determinant-neutral padding and certified one-instance conjunctions

Work over $\mathbb F_3$. Let $A$ be the one-sign tensor isomorphism problem and $B$ the two-sign problem, with the definitions and equivalences of the preceding comparison packet. The proofs here are informal.

## 1. One signed query absorbs any polynomial list of ordinary TI tests

There is a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction to $A$ for the conjunction of one arbitrary $A$-instance and a polynomial-length list of ordinary TI instances. The reduction permits the list and the signed instance to be computed jointly from the input.

We first construct witnesses for the required neutral padding.

**Local algebra encoding.** For an ordinary tensor pair compact to its intrinsic supports. Unequal support dimensions exclude equivalence. On nonzero concise input write its bilinear map as $\mu:U\times V\to W$. For a parameter $L\ge4$ form the algebra
$$
 B_{\mu,L}=F1\oplus U\oplus V\oplus W\oplus tF[t]/t^L F[t].
$$
The only product between the first three nonunit spaces is $\mu$ in the order $UV\to W$. The $t$-branch has the usual truncated algebra product and all mixed products with the other radical spaces vanish. This algebra is associative, has scalar residue, and has nilpotent radical $J$ equal to the direct sum of the nonunit spaces. Its isomorphisms recover the tensor. On $J/J^2$, the set where the cubic power vanishes in $J^3/J^4$ is exactly the old tangent $U\oplus V$: the only possible degree-three term is the $t$-coordinate cubed times $t^3$. That criterion is well-defined on tangent vectors. Multiplication on this old tangent into $J^2/J^3$ has image $W$. Its left and right annihilators recover $V$ and $U$, by conciseness. Conversely an ordinary tensor isomorphism extends with the $t$-branch fixed. Thus the extra length can grow freely, with no additional ordinary equivalences.

**A cubic tensor with neutral determinants.** Put $R_\mu=\operatorname{Mat}_2(B_{\mu,L})$ and use its unital algebra multiplication tensor. An algebra isomorphism of two such matrix algebras induces one of the underlying $B$'s. Here is the elementary matrix-unit argument. Their radicals are matrices over the respective radicals of $B$. The scalar quotient is $\operatorname{Mat}_2(F)$, whose $F$-algebra automorphisms are inner. After composing an isomorphism with a lifted conjugation, its image matrix units $f_{ij}$ agree with the standard units $e_{ij}$ modulo the radical. Then
$$
                 u=\sum_i f_{i1}e_{1i}
$$
is a unit, since it is one modulo the radical, and $f_{jk}u=u e_{jk}$. A second conjugation makes the isomorphism fix all standard units. It is then obtained by applying one isomorphism of the $e_{11}$-corner to all four entries. Conversely an isomorphism of the $B$'s plainly extends.

The determinant of left multiplication by any unit of $R_\mu$ is one: it acts by two copies of the same invertible linear map on the columns. Right multiplication uses the analogous two row copies. Thus conjugations by units have determinant one. The coordinatewise algebra isomorphism from the previous paragraph also has determinant one, as it acts by four copies of the same map. This proves trivial determinant for every algebra transporter in the chosen matrix-entry frames.

An ordinary transporter of multiplication tensors is an isotopy $p(x)q(y)=r(xy)$. Setting $u=p(1)$ and $v=q(1)$ gives $r(x)=p(x)v=u q(x)$. The invertible maps in this equality show that $u,v$ are units. Then $u^{-1}p(x)=q(x)v^{-1}$ defines a unital algebra isomorphism, and $p,q,r$ are its left and right unit modifications. Therefore every ordinary tensor transporter of the $R_\mu$ multiplication tensors has determinant one in all three modes. The multiplication tensor is concise by its unit, and its centroid is its algebra center, as one sees by substituting the unit in the three centroid equations. The center of $R_\mu$ is the center of $B_{\mu,L}$, which has no nontrivial idempotents even over an algebraic closure. Hence this cubic tensor is absolutely indecomposable.

**Absorb the list.** Build such a neutral tensor pair for each ordinary comparison, using lengths $L$ giving distinct cubic formats larger than all sides of the signed input. All lengths and output side lengths remain polynomial in the joint input length. If the tested mode of the signed input has full support, compact the other modes without touching its basis, then direct-sum the neutral cubes to both sides. The intrinsic centroid summands identify every neutral cube by its format. Thus an output transporter exists only if every ordinary comparison succeeds, and the determinant in the tested mode is exactly the determinant on the original signed data: the attached blocks cannot supply a correction. In the reverse direction any allowed signed transporter extends using independently chosen ordinary auxiliary maps, all neutral. If the tested mode is support-deficient, its sign is freely adjustable, so all comparisons are ordinary and can be packed using just neutral cubes. Zero and malformed branches are handled by fixed outputs or by omitting trivial yes comparisons. This proves the assertion.

In particular $A^{\wedge2}$ reduces to one call to $A$ on any directly recognizable family where one of the two signed comparisons already admits an ordinary TI reduction. The other input can remain arbitrary. For example a tested mode of dimension $O(\log N)$, where $N$ is input length, makes its sign test reducible to ordinary TI: attach its entire exterior algebra and a marked basis vector of the top exterior power to the typed tensor system containing the input. Multiplication and the degree-one inclusion force all exterior actions; pinning the top vector forces determinant one. The algebra has $2^{O(\log N)}$ dimensions, and the number of tensor components is fixed. The fixed-signature compiler therefore makes an ordinary instance of polynomial size. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

## 2. A raw sign-flexibility certificate for shared transporters

For the two-sign problem, let $D$ be the image of the transporter set on the two required sign coordinates. If ordinary isomorphism exists it is an affine coset of the source stabilizer's projected subgroup. One can compute a certified subgroup $K$ of the latter in polynomial time, as follows.

* Add any freely adjustable determinant on a support complement and the image of scalar stabilizers.
* For each choice of a mode $i$ to fix pointwise, form the pair-adjoint algebra of the corresponding slice tuple. With slices $M_j:X\to Y$, this is the linear solution algebra $E=\{(p,q):qM_j=M_j p\ \forall j\}$. Its units are exactly the stabilizer maps fixing the label mode, before any projection on signs.
* Compute its radical $J$ and its Wedderburn factors $E/J=\prod_\nu \operatorname{Mat}_{d_\nu}(F_\nu)$. On $X$ and $Y$, form the radical filtrations $J^k X,J^k Y$. Each successive quotient has a semisimple module structure. Let $m_{X,\nu},m_{Y,\nu}$ be the total numbers of copies of the natural simple $F_\nu^{d_\nu}$ module through these grades. A unit whose $\nu$-component is $u_\nu$ induces the determinant $N_{F_\nu/F}(\det u_\nu)^{m_{X,\nu}}$ on the corresponding parts of $X$, and likewise for $Y$. This is the determinant of a tensor product of the simple action with the multiplicity identity. Norms are onto, and all quotient units lift through the nilpotent radical. Thus the exact signs available from units of this pair-adjoint algebra are the span of the parity vectors of these multiplicities, with zero in the fixed mode. Compute the multiplicities by dimensions of the factor projections on each grade. All finite algebra steps are polynomial at fixed field. [Ivanyos and Qiao, finite-field algebra algorithms](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

Project those sign subgroups onto the two tested coordinates and take their linear span. Every generated sign is realized by a stabilizer product. If $K$ is nonzero, the entire raw two-sign comparison reduces to one signed instance. If $K$ is two-dimensional, ordinary TI suffices. If $K$ is a line, take its unique nonzero annihilating character in the two tested coordinates. A nonempty $D$ is invariant under addition by $K$, so meeting the kernel of that character is exactly equivalent to containing zero. Empty transporter sets fail both tests. The prior character equivalences give one ordinary-format $A$ instance. The result also handles fixed coordinate rebasings for prescribed signs by applying the prescribed sign shift before the test.

This is a raw, polynomially recognizable sufficient domain. It covers the case where there is an observable sign-changing stabilizer even when no relative transporter can be produced in polynomial time. The certificate is allowed to be zero even when the full stabilizer has nontrivial signs. On the independent conjunction encoding it can discard either signed test whose required orientation is already freely adjustable. If both sign subgroups are certified trivial by other arguments, one cannot infer a determinant correction from this criterion.

## Scope

The mixed conjunction theorem holds unconditionally, and the paired-sign reduction is exact on the stated domains. Neither theorem supplies a deterministic one-instance conjunction of two unrestricted signed queries. The techniques reused here are local-algebra encoding, intrinsic centroid summands, and the radical-layer determinant formula.
