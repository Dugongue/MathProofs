# A single ordinary tensor instance from filtered quadratic relations

Let \(F=\mathbf F_3\). Write a symmetric bimap as a surjective linear map \(b\colon \operatorname{Sym}^2X\to Z\), in the given input and output bases. Put \(R=\ker b\) and assume \(n=\dim X\) is even. A transporter is a pair of invertible maps satisfying \(h b=b'\operatorname{Sym}^2 g\). The required sign is \(\det g\det h\). The square-zero normal form in the referenced reduction identifies this sign with the ambient conjugator sign. If an endpoint is first expressed in a new ambient basis, its change-of-basis determinant must also be carried into the requested sign. The construction below works for either requested sign. [Square-zero reflection and lift](../TISLSquareZeroDirect/earlier/SquareZeroClosure.md) · [Scalar-centroid promise](../TISLSquareZeroDirect/ScalarCentroid.md)

## The filtered reduction

Choose the same polynomial-time intrinsic filtration rule at the two endpoints, giving an ordered flag \(0=X_0<\cdots<X_t=X\). Intrinsic means every transporter carries each member to the corresponding target member. Examples are the radical flags computed in the companion proof, the flag obtained by splitting at the support of \(R\), and their common refinement by the nested spaces \(X_{i-1}+(X_i\cap Y_j)\) for a second flag \(Y\). The support of a relation space is the span of its contractions by all covectors, using symmetric matrices with off-diagonal coefficients divided by two. Write \(D_i=X_i/X_{i-1}\), of dimension \(d_i\). [Intrinsic flags](ProbeGroups.md)

Order pairs \(1\le i\le j\le t\) by increasing \(j\), then by increasing \(i\). Let \(F_{ij}\) be the sum of the images of \(X_a X_b\) through that pair in \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 X\). For each grade let \(H=F_{ij}/F_{\rm prev}\) and let \(T\) be the image of \(R\cap F_{ij}\) in \(H\). An adapted monomial basis identifies \(H\) naturally with \(D_i\otimes D_j\) for \(i<j\), and with \(\operatorname{Sym}^2D_i\) for \(i=j\). Write \(Q=H/T\).

**Theorem.** Fix \(c>0\), put \(k=\lfloor c\log_2 L\rfloor\) for dense input length \(L\), and compare all graded dimensions at the endpoints. A mismatch implies an ordinary NO. For each grade, make either of two choices.

- Keep \(T\) when \(\dim T\le k\).
- Keep \(Q\) when \(\dim Q\le k\), and include \(H\) in a product of ambient determinant characters.

For a selected ambient diagonal grade \(ii\), add \(d_i+1\) to the integer \(e_i\). For a selected off-diagonal grade \(ij\), add \(d_j\) to \(e_i\) and \(d_i\) to \(e_j\). All \(e_i\) start at zero. If there is a choice with \(e_i\) even for every \(i\) of dimension \(d_i>k\), there is a deterministic polynomial-time reduction to **one ordinary three-tensor isomorphism instance** over \(F\). The number of grades, the dimension of \(R\), and the sum of the retained dimensions can grow without a logarithmic bound.

Finding the choices uses only linear algebra. A grade offering one choice forces its bit. A grade offering both contributes a free bit to the parity equations. If neither is available, this sufficient test does not cover the pair. Solve the remaining equations over \(\mathbf F_2\) and select one solution. The two endpoints use the same solution. Large tangent grades and large relation spaces can therefore participate without requiring their exterior algebras.

## Why the compiled sign is exact

The determinant of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2g\) is \((\det g)^{n+1}\), by diagonal scalings, swaps and transvections, or the symmetric-square determinant formula. The exact sequence of \(b\) gives \(\det(\operatorname{Sym}^2g)=\det(g_R)\det h\) in compatible frames. Since \(n\) is even and all nonzero ground scalars square to one, the desired sign transfers to the determinant on \(R\), with a computable endpoint basis correction. This is the relation transfer already used in the earlier duality result. [Quadratic relation duality](../ScalarCentroidOrientation/DualRelationOrientation.md)

The filtration expresses \(\det(g_R)\) as the product on the \(T\) grades. On a grade where \(Q\) is retained, exactness gives the determinant on \(T\) as that on \(H\) divided by that on \(Q\). In \(F\) a sign is its own inverse. The tensor-product determinant formula on the off-diagonal grades and the symmetric-square formula on diagonal grades give precisely the exponents \(e_i\). Thus only the small retained spaces and the small \(D_i\) with odd \(e_i\) contribute variable signs.

Here is a basis calibration algorithm that does not presume a transporter. Choose adapted bases for \(X\), for all filtration steps of \(R\), and for the short exact sequences used above. In the original monomial basis compare a basis of \(R\) followed by lifts of the given basis of \(Z\). In the relation filtration compare the chosen basis with the concatenated lifts of the successive grade bases. In a selected ambient grade compare a basis of \(T\) followed by lifts of a basis of \(Q\) with the tensor or monomial basis from \(D_i,D_j\). Include the determinant of the change from the displayed \(X\)-basis to its adapted basis when applying the symmetric-square identity. Equivalently, start with the displayed generator of \(\det X\otimes\det Z\) and transport it through the canonical determinant-line isomorphisms just described. An even tensor power of a one-dimensional ternary space has canonical generator \(v^{\otimes 2a}\) for any nonzero \(v\). Each determinant-line operation thus consists of a polynomial-size change-of-basis determinant, multiplication or inversion of a nonzero ternary scalar. This calculates an endpoint sign times the product of the chosen small volume generators, in a fixed order. The two endpoint signs need not agree.

Build a linear diagram containing \(b\), symmetric multiplication into \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 X\), and all used inclusions, quotient projections, and induced maps. Include the bilinear tensor and symmetric products identifying the ambient grades. Surjections and injections force every induced map from the original \(g,h\). Conversely a genuine transporter extends to every displayed subquotient. For each contributing small space \(V\), adjoin its full exterior algebra with unit, multiplication, the inclusion of \(V\) as degree one, and the one-dimensional top subspace. An isomorphism on \(V\) extends uniquely as an algebra map and induces its determinant on top. Degree zero also works for \(V=0\). Chain the top lines using one-dimensional tensor products with their bilinear maps. Mark the calibrated generator on the final line at each endpoint, multiplying one by the requested sign when it is negative. When there are no contributing spaces use the based ground-field line for the empty product. A diagram isomorphism is now equivalent to a transporter of exactly the requested sign. Every exterior algebra has dimension at most \(2^k\).

For clarity, a polynomial number of named sorts and arrows can be placed in a fixed signature before applying the tensor-system theorem. Make a role for each sort and separate occurrence copies for every arrow slot. On the direct sum of the roles, act by the coordinate algebra \(F^s\). Mark its first coordinate idempotent and the linear shift sending each standard idempotent to the next and the last to zero. This fixes all role labels. A single linear map sends occurrence copies identically to their master roles and is zero on the masters. Intertwining it forces the same basis change on an occurrence and its master. Aggregate all unary arrows into one block map and all binary arrows into one bilinear map using the private slots. Aggregate constants into a vector in their separate output slots. All unspecified blocks are zero. Isomorphisms are exactly the original compatible families. This fixed signature has function arities at most two. Its basis-explicit size is polynomial, so the established single-pair compiler gives one ordinary TI pair. [Grochow–Qiao, Theorem 7.3, after Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

## Two direct special cases

If \(U\subseteq X\) is intrinsic of odd dimension, put \(P=UX\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2X\). The natural map \(R\to\operatorname{Sym}^2(X/U)\) has kernel \(R\cap P\) and cokernel \(Z/b(P)\). Because \(\dim(X/U)\) is odd, the determinant of its symmetric square has even exponent. The two kernel and cokernel volumes alone give the sign after calibration. Thus logarithmic dimensions of those two defects suffice, regardless of the size of \(U,X,Z\). This also follows directly from the two exact sequences of this map and needs no hypotheses on the individual grades within \(P\).

If \(B=\operatorname{supp}R\) has odd dimension, \(R\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2 B\) and the same neutral determinant argument applies to the inclusion. Here a single bound \(\dim(\operatorname{Sym}^2 B/R)\le k\) suffices. When this quotient vanishes, adapted bases already decide the answer. The executable compiler implements this latter case on dense based input, including independent basis changes at the two ends. [Compiler](compiler.py)

For example take \(X=A\oplus B\) with \(\dim A=\dim B=b\equiv3\pmod4\) and \(R=\operatorname{Sym}^2 B\). The output dimension \(b^2+b(b+1)/2\) is odd. The perfect cross pairing gives an unmarked split nonsingular scalar slice. The common radical is zero. For a centroid pair, every scalar form makes its input map self-adjoint. The squares of all \(A\)-covectors force every such covector to be a left eigenvector and hence force a common scalar on that copy of \(A^*\). For a mixed form \(\ell t+t\ell\) with \(\ell\in A^*,t\in B^*\), subtract this scalar. Self-adjointness forces the resulting covector \(tP\) to be proportional to \(\ell\); varying \(\ell\) forces zero. Thus the centroid is scalar. Every automorphism preserves \(B\), and its determinant on \(R\) is the even power \((\det g_B)^{b+1}\). The self-pair has an even transporter. Reflecting one output coordinate yields an ordinary-isomorphic pair with only odd transporters. The first instance has \((\dim X,\dim Z)=(6,15)\).

These are written reductions. The full scalar-centroid, split-slice promise does not supply the flag choices, defect bounds, or output labels required by these constructions.


## Starting from a conjugated matrix space

For a raw input \(\mathcal S\subseteq\operatorname{End}(V)\) in the promised orbit, compute the common kernel \(W\) and \(U=V/W\). The operators give \(\mathcal M\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(U,W)\). Compute the left idealizer \(A\subseteq\operatorname{End}(W)\) by linear equations. On the model \(\mathcal M=[L_a\ L_b]\) this is exactly the regular algebra by applying the idealizer to the first projection. Thus on the raw input it is the same abstract local algebra. Compute \(J=\operatorname{rad} A\), its square, \(X=J/J^2\), and \(Z=J^2\) with the induced product. These operations are polynomial over the fixed field.

For each basis element \(a\) of the idealizer solve \(\ell T_a=a\ell\) for all \(\ell\in\mathcal M\). Existence follows on the model by diagonal multiplication on its two copies. Uniqueness follows from the zero common kernel on \(U\). This recovers \(U\) as a free rank-two \(A\)-module. Similarly \(W\) is a free rank-one module. Choose a basis of \(A\) in the order \(1\), lifts of a basis of \(X\), then a basis of \(Z\). Choose any lift of a nonzero class in \(W/JW\) and lifts of a basis of \(U/JU\). They are module bases by Nakayama and the known free ranks. Apply the displayed \(A\)-basis to each module generator, lift the two ordered \(U\)-blocks to \(V\), and append the \(W\)-block. Let \(\delta\) be the determinant of this frame in the given basis of \(V\).

On the model between these frames, every conjugator induces an algebra isomorphism \(\phi\). After removing the triple lift of \(\phi\), its diagonal blocks are a unit on \(W\) and an invertible \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrix over \(A\) on \(U\), by the two coordinate projections. Their ground determinants are respectively the residue unit and the residue matrix determinant each raised to \(\dim A\), an even power. Off-diagonal shears have determinant one. Conversely a transporter of the recovered bimap extends to an algebra isomorphism by the chosen grade lifts, and its triple lift conjugates the model spaces. Thus the set of original ambient signs is exactly \((\delta'/\delta)\) times the set of recovered combined signs. The endpoint correction uses only computed bases. If a compact group is presented by generators \(1+N_i\), take the span of their differences from the identity first.
