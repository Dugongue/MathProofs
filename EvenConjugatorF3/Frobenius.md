# Frobenius probes for the determinant sign of a symmetric bimap

Let \(F=\mathbb F_3\). Given two based surjective symmetric bimaps \(\beta:X\times X\to Z\) and \(\beta':X'\times X'\to Z'\), the question is whether \(h\beta=\beta'(g,g)\) for invertible \(g,h\) with \(\det g\det h=1\). Set \(n=\dim X\), \(m=\dim Z\), and let \(N\ge2\) be the dense input length. Dimensions must agree across the two endpoints. A scalar slice is \(B_z=z\circ\beta\) for \(z\in Z^*\). Assume a nonsingular scalar slice exists. Its coordinates need never be given or fixed.

**Theorem.** Suppose the formal determinant polynomials at both endpoints split into powers of ground-field linear forms. Label each projective factor line by its multiplicity when that multiplicity occurs exactly once. If multiplicity profiles disagree, the answer is no. Otherwise form the linear space of maps \(H:Z\to Z'\) taking each uniquely labelled line into its counterpart. If \(d\) is the dimension of this space, the even-transporter decision takes deterministic \(3^d\operatorname{poly}(N)\) time. Thus \(d\le c\log_3 N\), for any fixed \(c\), gives a polynomial many-one reduction to one ordinary ternary TI instance. A projective frame of unique labels on each endpoint gives \(d\le1\) whenever the same labelled points are frames on both sides. Without the splitting assumption, enumerating all output matrices gives the same conclusion when \(m^2\le c\log_3 N\). The theorem applies in particular to the scalar-centroid square-zero promise with even \(n\), odd \(m\) and a split nonsingular slice. Complete splitting of the determinant is an additional hypothesis. This is a written proof.

## Recover every factor from a univariate probe

Choose an explicit power basis \(b=(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{m-1})\) of \(K=\mathbb F_{3^m}\), and let \(b^{(3)}\) be its entrywise cube. Here \(m>0\), as in the square-zero promise. Constructing this field takes deterministic polynomial time in \(m\) at fixed characteristic. [Shoup, construction of irreducible polynomials](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

Compute the univariate polynomial \(P(T)=\det(B_{b^{(3)}}-T B_b)\) over \(K\), for example by division-free characteristic-polynomial algorithms. Write a factor of the original determinant as \(z\mapsto z(\lambda)\), with coefficient vector \(\lambda\in F^m\setminus\{0\}\). Its evaluation \(a=b(\lambda)\) is nonzero by basis independence. It contributes the root \(a^3/a=a^2\) to \(P\). Two points produce the same root precisely when their evaluations are equal up to sign, hence precisely when their \(F\)-lines coincide. Thus factoring \(P\) gives the original multiplicities, even for multiplicities divisible by three. A square root of each root, expanded in the power basis, recovers its coefficient line. Field representation uses \(m\) ternary coordinates throughout. Univariate factoring in fixed characteristic is deterministic polynomial time. Indeed the elements fixed by cubing in the product of the residue fields of a square-free polynomial form a computable \(F\)-space of tuples of ternary scalars. A basis separates all components by gcd tests with its elements minus the three base scalars. Standard square-free decomposition handles repeats. Apply the same algorithm to the quadratic for each square root. The earlier determinant-frame method addresses unrestricted left/right changes of basis. Here the two Frobenius-related probes supply labels for the coupled congruence and determinant test. [Earlier determinant-coordinate result](../TensorCoordinateRecovery/DETERMINANT.md)

An actual transporter identifies the determinant polynomials by the dual output substitution and the nonzero scalar \((\det g)^2\). It therefore matches all unique labels. Requiring \(H\lambda\in F\lambda'\) gives homogeneous linear equations. Enumerate the resulting vector space, discard singular \(H\), then test the full bimap and sign for \(h=H\) as follows. The split-factor assumption is a promise for the factor extraction. A factor profile or a candidate output map alone never suffices for acceptance.

## Determine both possible input determinants for a fixed output action

For given \(h\), apply constructive simultaneous congruence to the tuples of scalar forms in \(h\beta\) and \(\beta'\). This returns one \(g_0\), or proves the transporter empty. Over the fixed field \(F\), the finite-field algorithm with the characteristic-dependent deterministic bound is polynomial. The other possible \(g\) are \(g_0u\) with \(u\) a pointwise isometry of the original form tuple. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 7(1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03495)

Compute the adjoint algebra \(A\subseteq\operatorname{End}_F X\) consisting of maps \(a\) possessing a mate \(a^*\) on every slice, by solving the simultaneous adjoint linear equations. Zero common radical gives uniqueness. Symmetry makes the mate operation an involution. Then \(u\) is a pointwise isometry exactly when \(u\in A\) and \(u^*u=1\). For \(J=\operatorname{rad}A\), compute \(A/J=\prod_i M_{s_i}(K_i)\) and its induced involution. On each layer \(J^k X/J^{k+1}X\), the central factor idempotents project onto sums of natural modules over \(K_i\). Sum the projected dimensions divided by \(s_i[K_i:F]\) to obtain the composition multiplicity \(t_i\) in \(X\). All these data are computed by finite-field algebra algorithms and linear algebra, in deterministic polynomial time at characteristic three. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18 and §§3.4–3.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03495)

Reduction of unitary elements \(U(A)\to U(A/J)\) is onto. Lift a quotient unitary element to \(a\in A^\times\), put \(v=a^*a\in 1+J\), and choose a power \(3^s\) at least the nilpotency index of \(J\). Then \(v^{3^s}=1\) in characteristic three, and \(a v^{(3^s-1)/2}\) is unitary. The kernel is a subgroup of the finite 3-group \(1+J\), hence has determinant one. On \(X\), a quotient unitary element with components \(a_i\) has determinant
\[
 \prod_i N_{K_i/F}(\det_{K_i}a_i)^{t_i}
\]
by multiplication of determinants on the layers. The involution is adjoint to the nonsingular scalar slice, so \(X\) is isomorphic to its involution-twisted dual as an \(A\)-module. Exchanged factors have equal multiplicities and inverse norm determinants, which cancel. On symplectic factors every determinant is one. On a factor of unitary type the determinant has norm one to the fixed subfield and consequently to \(F\). On an orthogonal factor the determinant can independently take either sign, since reflection in a nonsingular vector is an isometry. Consequently a negative input determinant occurs in the pointwise isometry group exactly when some orthogonal factor has \(t_i[K_i:F]\) odd. Combined with \(\det g_0\det h\), this decides whether there is an even transporter. Emit the ordinary \(1\times1\times1\) tensor pair \((1,1)\) on acceptance and \((0,1)\) on rejection. This is a decision-based many-one reduction and uses no TI queries internally.

## An infinite scalar-centroid family with a genuine negative instance

For odd \(m\ge3\), take \(Z=F^m\), and let \(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{m+1}\) be its coordinate vectors followed by their sum. Choose \(\mu\in Z\) on none of these lines. Pick distinct positive integers \(r_i\) with at least one odd, and put \(X=\bigoplus_i(F^{r_i}\oplus F^{r_i})\). Declare distinct blocks orthogonal. On block \(i\) use
\[
 \beta_i((u,v),(u',v'))=\lambda_i(u\cdot v'+v\cdot u')+\mu(u\cdot u').
\]
The image spans \(Z\). Choose \(s\in Z^*\) with every coordinate nonzero and with coordinate sum nonzero. The all-ones vector works unless \(3\mid m\), in which case change one sign. Then \(B_s\) is nonsingular and split, since the joint second halves are totally isotropic of half the dimension. The determinant form equals \(\pm\prod_i z(\lambda_i)^{2r_i}\), by the block determinant formula. Thus every output automorphism fixes the projective frame, giving \(h=\varepsilon I\), \(\varepsilon=\pm1\).

For the centroid identity \(\beta(Px,y)=Q\beta(x,y)=\beta(x,Py)\), testing distinct blocks shows that \(P\) preserves each by their zero common radical. Hence \(Q\) preserves each output plane \(\langle\lambda_i,\mu\rangle\). Comparing the \(\mu\) and \(\lambda_i\) coefficients in the displayed block formula forces \(Q\lambda_i=a_i\lambda_i,\ Q\mu=b_i\lambda_i+a_i\mu\), and \(P_i(u,v)=(a_i u,b_i u+a_i v)\). Frame preservation by this linear endomorphism, including in the singular case, forces all \(a_i\) equal. The same equations give all \(b_i=0\). The centroid is \(F\).

For an automorphism, \(g\) commutes with all ratios \(R_t=B_s^{-1}B_t\) because it scales every \(B_t\) by the same \(\varepsilon\). On block \(i\), the ratio is \(t(\lambda_i)/s(\lambda_i)\) times the identity plus a multiple of \(N_i(u,v)=(0,u)\). Distinct \(i\) give distinct joint characters. The intersection over a basis of \(t\) of the kernels of \((R_t-t(\lambda_i)/s(\lambda_i))^2\) recovers block \(i\). Thus \(g\) preserves it. Take \(t\) vanishing on \(\lambda_i\) and nonzero on \(\mu\). Its ratio restricts to a nonzero multiple of \(N_i\), so \(g_i(u,v)=(Au,Cu+Av)\) with determinant \((\det A)^2=1\). The same \(B_t\) on that block gives \(A^\mathsf{T}A=\varepsilon I\). An odd \(r_i\) forces \(\varepsilon=1\) by determinants. Every automorphism therefore has positive combined determinant. Transporting this bimap by a reflection on \(X\) and the identity on \(Z\) gives an ordinarily isomorphic pair all of whose transporters have negative combined sign. Comparing the bimap with itself gives a positive pair. Both dimensions can grow without bound.

## Intrinsic output lines and higher-dimensional label spaces

**Theorem from raw coordinates.** A nonsingular scalar slice need only exist. For each output line \(L\subset Z\), put \(V_L=\{x\mid\beta(x,X)\subseteq L\}\). The complete list of lines with \(V_L\ne0\), with their subspaces \(V_L\), is deterministically computable in polynomial time. Give a line its dimension label \(\dim V_L\) when that dimension occurs exactly once. The homogeneous constraints \(H(L)\subseteq L'\) from paired labels define a linear space of candidate output maps. Thus the preceding fixed-\(h\) proof gives time \(3^d\operatorname{poly}(N)\) for residual dimension \(d\), including for inputs with irreducible determinant factors. Mismatched dimension multisets certify an empty transporter. The same result holds after refining by the intrinsic spaces below.

Compute the ratio algebra \(E=\langle B^{-1}S\rangle_{\rm alg}\) directly from the raw scalar slice space \(S\) by the blow-up and projection construction. This uses the existence of some nonsingular \(B\in S\). Every transporter conjugates the computed algebra to its counterpart. [Raw ratio algebra construction](ProbeGroups.md)

For the proof only, write \(B=B_s\). A nonzero \(x\in V_L\) has nonzero functional \(B(x,-)\), hence \(s(L)\ne0\). Write \(v\) for the unique vector on \(L\) with \(s(v)=1\). Then for every \(t\in Z^*\), \(B_t(x,-)=t(v)B(x,-)\). Equivalently \(B^{-1}B_t x=t(v)x\), which says that \(x\) belongs to the eigenspace of a unital algebra character \(\chi:E\to F\). Conversely an eigenvector for such a character satisfies these equations with \(t(v)=\chi(B^{-1}B_t)\). This is linear in \(t\) and equals one at \(s\). It follows that the full character eigenspace is exactly \(V_{Fv}\). Distinct characters with nonzero eigenspaces give distinct lines because the ratios generate \(E\).

A character factors through \(E/\operatorname{rad}E\); the only simple matrix factors allowing a unital map to \(F\) are copies of \(F\), each supplying exactly one character by projection. Use the explicit algebra decomposition to obtain all of them. For a basis \(\{a_j\}\) of \(E\), compute \(\bigcap_j\ker(a_j-\chi(a_j)I)\), dropping the zero results. Taking the span of the bimap values on that space against \(X\) recovers \(L\) in the actual output coordinates. No choice of a scalar slice is made by this algorithm.

For additional linear constraints, let \(J=\operatorname{rad}E\). On every layer \(J^kX/J^{k+1}X\) compute the isotypic summands for all simple factors \(i\) of \(E/J\) using their central idempotents. For each factor record the tuple consisting of its matrix size, its center degree, and its multiplicities in these ordered layers. Pool factors with identical tuples. For each class and each layer take the preimage in \(J^kX\) of its pooled summands. Also take the individual class summands in the socle \(\{x:Jx=0\}\), on which \(E/J\) acts directly. All of these subspaces are invariant under transport with their labels. Pooling avoids any choice between indistinguishable simple factors. Add \(X\) itself and the individually dimension-labelled \(V_L\) to this dictionary. For every ordered pair \(U,V\) in it compute \(W_{UV}=\operatorname{span}\beta(U,V)\). Transport forces \(h(W_{UV})=W'_{UV}\); require equal dimensions and impose \(H(W_{UV})\subseteq W'_{UV}\) linearly. There are polynomially many factor classes, layers, pairs, and constraints. The fixed-action decision now uses the dimension remaining after *all* these constraints. Kernel intersections or isotypic components alone are never used to certify a transporter.

In the frame family above, exactly the \(\lambda_i\)-lines have nonzero \(V_L\). A vector with nonzero first half on any block has local evaluation image the full output plane. With all first halves zero, nonzero second halves on two different blocks produce two distinct output lines. Thus \(V_{F\lambda_i}\) is precisely the second half of block \(i\), of dimension \(r_i\). The raw line test consequently leaves one scalar parameter.

For an example with a determinant having an irreducible factor, adjoin an orthogonal block \(K^2\) for \(K=\mathbb F_9\), with product \((u,v),(u',v')\mapsto\iota(uv'+vu')\), where \(\iota:K\hookrightarrow Z\) is \(F\)-linear. Choose its image plane so the earlier split slice \(s\) restricts nontrivially to it. Every nonzero vector of the new block has evaluation image that whole plane. Restricting a general vector's evaluation to the new block therefore shows that the old rank-one output lines and their dimensions are unchanged. The old frame still forces scalar centroid output, and the zero common radical forces scalar action on the new block as well. The restriction of \(B_s\) to the new block is nonsingular and split because it is the trace pairing between the two copies of \(K\) with a nonzero scalar coefficient. More explicitly, \(t\circ\iota(w)=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(c_t w)\), with \(c_t\) varying surjectively in \(K\). The determinant on \(K^2\) is a nonzero constant times \(N_{K/F}(c_t)^2\), since each off-diagonal block is a trace pairing multiplied by \(c_t\). This norm is an irreducible binary quadratic over \(F\), and stays irreducible after adjoining the unused output coordinates. The new instance thus satisfies the raw line theorem independently of the rational splitting promise.
