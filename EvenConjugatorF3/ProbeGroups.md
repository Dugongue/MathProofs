# Two polynomial-time tests for an even conjugator over the ternary field

Let \(X,Z\) be based spaces over \(\mathbb F_3\), and let \(\beta:\operatorname{Sym}^2 X\twoheadrightarrow Z\) have a nonsingular scalar slice. The two endpoints may have different bases. A transporter consists of \(g:X\to X'\) and \(h:Z\to Z'\) with \(h\beta=\beta'\operatorname{Sym}^2g\). The desired bit is \(\det(g)\det(h)\) in the supplied bases. On the previously established square-zero operator carrier this bit is its conjugator determinant, with the frame correction from deterministic recovery if the input consists of raw matrix spaces. Write \(L\) for dense input length. The algorithms below compute the entire attainable bit set and one witness for each attainable bit. They apply in particular when the centroid is scalar, the input grade is even-dimensional, the output grade is odd-dimensional, and the slice is split. Status is a written proof, without an end-to-end Lean check. [Square-zero carrier and frame recovery](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/TISLSquareZeroDirect)

## 1. A characteristic flag removes all guessing of slice images

**Theorem with raw slice data.** Fix a constant \(b\). Form the following algebra directly from a basis of the scalar slice space \(S=\beta^*(Z^*)\):
\[
 C_{\rm all}=\{u\in\operatorname{End}(X):\ \text{some }v\in\operatorname{End}(X)\text{ satisfies }Bu=v^t B\ \text{for every }B\in S\}.
\]
Solve the simultaneous linear equations for \((u,v)\) and project to the first coordinate. This space contains the identity and is closed under products because mates multiply in reverse order. If \(g^tS'g=S\), the target mates of \(gug^{-1}\) and \(gvg^{-1}\) satisfy the same equations, so every transporter conjugates the computed algebra to its target counterpart. The flag of the powers of its Jacobson radical on \(X\) is therefore intrinsic. When every successive quotient of this flag has dimension at most \(b\), the comparison has a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm on raw bimap data. Neither endpoint needs to exhibit any scalar slice. The flag-stabilizer algorithm below applies verbatim with this algebra in place of \(E\).

When a nonsingular slice \(B_0\) exists, the raw algebra is exactly the commutant of \(\langle B_0^{-1}S\rangle_{\rm alg}\): substituting \(v^t=B_0uB_0^{-1}\) in the mate equations gives commutation with every ratio. This identity uses existence only, independently of the algorithm for the raw flag.

**A second flag from raw slices.** Fix \(b\). Suppose each endpoint has a nonsingular scalar slice. Write \(S=\beta^*(Z^*)\) for the space of symmetric slice matrices. Form the associative unital matrix algebra \(E=\langle B^{-1}S\rangle_{\rm alg}\), with \(B\in S\) nonsingular, and \(I=\operatorname{rad}(E)\). If each successive quotient of the filtration \(X\supset IX\supset I^2X\supset\cdots\supset 0\) has dimension at most \(b\), there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for the signed comparison. There is no bound on \(\dim Z\), the length of the flag, the radical dimension, or the number of nonsingular slices. A supplied slice serves only to compute the flag.

For another choice \(B_1=Br\), the matrix \(r\) lies in \(E\) and is invertible. Its inverse belongs to \(E\) by Cayley–Hamilton. Thus \(B_1^{-1}S=r^{-1}B^{-1}S\subset E\); exchanging the two slices proves equality of their generated algebras. If \(g^tS'g=S\), choose \(g^t B'g\) temporarily as the first slice. The ratio algebra on \(X\) is then \(g^{-1}E'g\). Every actual transporter carries the displayed flag to the target flag. The raw and ratio flags can be tried separately.

To compute \(E\) when a nonsingular scalar slice is supplied, use multiplication and linear span until stable. Even when only existence is promised, the raw ratio algebra is deterministically computable. Invoke the finite-field constructive noncommutative rank theorem on \(S\), whose noncommutative rank is full by the scalar-slice promise. It returns, over the original field, an invertible matrix \(W\in M_d(S)\) for polynomial \(d\); here \(M_d(S)\) consists of block matrices with blocks in \(S\). Generate \(\mathcal E=\langle W^{-1}M_d(S)\rangle_{\rm alg}\) by linear algebra. The change-of-base-slice argument above works for every matrix space, without symmetry, and hence gives the same algebra after replacing \(W\) in the proof with \(I_d\otimes B\) for an uncomputed nonsingular \(B\in S\). It follows that \(\mathcal E=M_d(E)\): the normalized space lies in \(M_d(E)\) and contains each matrix unit tensored with the identity, together with \(I_d\otimes r\) for every \(r\in B^{-1}S\). Projecting a basis of \(\mathcal E\) to its first diagonal block and taking linear span yields the desired algebra over \(\mathbb F_3\). This calculation does not produce, mark, or require an ordinary scalar slice at either endpoint. [Ivanyos, Qiao and Subrahmanyam, finite-field constructive noncommutative rank, Corollary 3](https://www.cmi.ac.in/~kv/IQS2.pdf)
 Compute its Jacobson radical over the fixed finite field and obtain the flag by iterated matrix-space action. The deterministic radical algorithm uses the structure constants of the algebra. If the two flag types differ, return the empty bit set. Otherwise choose any invertible map \(a\) aligning adapted bases. Every possible \(g\) lies in \(aP\), where \(P\) is the full stabilizer of the source flag. In adapted bases, generators of \(P\) are elementary off-diagonal transvections in allowed blocks, diagonal sign changes, and elementary transvections within the diagonal blocks. Its block-unitriangular kernel is a 3-group and its quotient is \(\prod_j\mathrm{GL}_{b_j}(3)\) with \(b_j\le b\). Therefore its nonabelian composition factors embed in a symmetric group of fixed degree, and every prime in its order is bounded in terms of \(b\). Section 3 completes the test. [Friedl and Rónyai, radical computation](https://www.cs.uoregon.edu/frames/reports.php?report=TR-1985-003)

## 2. A bounded list of probe images with a large joint commutant

Fix constants \(b,c\). Select \(t+1\) covectors with scalar slices \(B_0,\dots,B_t\) and \(B_0\) nonsingular. Put \(R_i=B_0^{-1}B_i\) and \(C=\operatorname{End}_{R_1,\ldots,R_t}(X)\), the simultaneous matrix centralizer. Suppose each simple component of \(C/\operatorname{rad}C\) either has ternary dimension at most \(b\), or is a field of ternary dimension at most \(c\log_3 L\). Obtain a complete polynomial-size list of possible images of the ordered probes at the other endpoint. A concrete sufficient regime is \((t+1)\dim Z\le c\log_3L\), in which all \(3^{(t+1)\dim Z}\) target tuples are enumerated. One may also search all source tuples within this bound to locate a suitable seed if one exists. Larger probe lists are allowed when their construction and total size satisfy the same polynomial budget. An arbitrary supplied nonsingular slice need not be mapped to the supplied target slice.

**Theorem.** Under these conditions there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for the signed comparison. In particular, the radical of \(C\), its total semisimple dimension, and the number of its simple factors can all grow with \(L\).

Discard target tuples with singular first slice. For each remaining tuple \(B'_0,\ldots,B'_t\), compute \(R'_i=(B'_0)^{-1}B'_i\). Constructive simultaneous module isomorphism produces an invertible \(a\) with \(aR_i=R'_ia\) or rules out that branch. All invertible intertwiners in it are exactly \(a C^\times\). Every true bimap transporter occurs in such a coset: for its actual target covectors, \(B_i=g^tB'_ig\) implies \(gR_i=R'_ig\). Cosets may contain more maps, a harmless enlargement because the final relation-space test enforces the full tensor identity. [Brooksbank and Luks, constructive module isomorphism](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021869308003748)

For completeness, compute generators of \(C^\times\) within the stated bound. Compute \(J=\operatorname{rad}C\). In the center of \(C/J\) the equation \(z^3=z\) is ternary linear and its solutions form \(\mathbb F_3^r\), where \(r\) is the number of simple components. Split the identity by multiplying with the three Lagrange indicator polynomials of each element in a basis of this subalgebra, retaining nonzero pieces. Coordinates corresponding to different simple factors separate, so the result is the primitive central idempotents. In each factor enumerate its elements separately, test units by linear algebra and extend each unit by the identity on the other components. The total count is bounded by \(r\max(3^b,L^c)\). Lift these elements to \(C\) by linear algebra; units lift through a nilpotent ideal. To generate \(1+J\), include \(1+x\) for lifts of ternary bases of all \(J^k/J^{k+1}\), \(k\ge 1\). These generate each successive additive quotient and hence the kernel. All steps are deterministic.

Every nonabelian composition factor of \(C^\times\) is a subquotient of the unit group of a component with dimension at most \(b\), hence embeds in a symmetric group of degree at most \(3^b\) by its regular action. The other components contribute cyclic groups. Every prime in \(|C^\times|\) is at most \(\max(3,3^b,L^c)\). Thus Section 3 applies to each \(a C^\times\). With a prepared source seed and \(M\) enumerated target tuples the time is \(M\,\mathrm{poly}_{b,c}(L)\), plus the time to generate the list. Testing up to \(M\) source seeds first costs another \(M\,\mathrm{poly}_{b,c}(L)\) in the full enumeration regime. The polynomial exponent from the matrix-group theorem depends on the fixed composition-factor bound. This argument supplies no uniform fixed exponent for growing \(b\).

## 3. Extract both determinant bits inside a matrix coset

Write \(K=\ker\beta\subset \operatorname{Sym}^2 X\), \(K'=\ker\beta'\), and let \(\rho(g)=\operatorname{diag}(g,\operatorname{Sym}^2g)\) on \(T=X\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2X\). Let \(aG\) be a candidate coset from either theorem. Put \(U=0\oplus K\) and \(V=0\oplus(\operatorname{Sym}^2a)^{-1}K'\) in \(T\). A candidate \(a s\), \(s\in G\), transports the bimap exactly when \(\rho(s)U=V\). Indeed, a map carrying kernels induces the unique invertible output map \(h\) between the quotients. Linear algebra recovers \(h\) in the given output bases. Retaining \(X\) in \(\rho\) makes the representation faithful and lets us read off \(s\).

Use Miyazaki's deterministic polynomial-time subspace stabilizer for matrix groups whose nonabelian composition factors embed in \(S_D\) for fixed \(D\), with a polynomial bound on all primes in the group order and on the field characteristic. These hypotheses hold above, and also for \(H=\rho(G)\wr S_2\), acting on \(T\oplus T\). Compute the stabilizer of \(U\oplus V\) in \(H\). Its projection onto the two-copy permutation group is nontrivial exactly when \(U\) is carried to \(V\) by some \(\rho(s)\). Inspect the stabilizer generators for one swapping copies, then read its block from the first copy to the second. Conversely any \(s\) carrying \(U\) to \(V\) gives the swap with off-diagonal blocks \(\rho(s),\rho(s)^{-1}\). The ambient dimensions are \(O((\dim X)^2)\) and all matrices have polynomial-size explicit encodings. [Miyazaki, Polynomial-Time Computation in Matrix Groups](https://www.cs.uoregon.edu/frames/reports.php?report=PHD-199911-Miyazaki)

If the branch succeeds, compute the source stabilizer of \(U\) in \(\rho(G)\) as well. The set of solutions in the branch is one representative times this source stabilizer. Each generator gives a source automorphism \(k\) with induced output action \(\bar k\). The map \(k\mapsto\det k\,\det\bar k\) is a group character. If a generator has value \(-1\), composing it with the representative realizes the other bit as well. Otherwise the representative's bit is the only one. Union these sets across branches. To encode the decision as one ordinary TI instance, emit the equal pair of one-entry unit tensors if the target bit was attained, and the one-entry zero tensor paired with the unit tensor otherwise. This is a decision-first many-one reduction and needs no complement oracle.

## 4. Explicit scalar-centroid family with an unbounded output grade

Let \(n\) be even, \(m\ge3\) odd, and \(2m-2<n\). On \(X=\mathbb F_3^n\) let \(B\) be the reverse-identity matrix and \(N\) the single nilpotent Jordan shift with superdiagonal ones. Use the \(m\) independent scalar slices \(B,BN,\ldots,BN^{m-1}\). They are symmetric, \(B\) is hyperbolic, independence gives surjectivity, and the common radical is zero. Put \(V=\langle 1,N,\ldots,N^{m-1}\rangle\). For an element \((d,h)\) of the symmetric bimap centroid the equation for the \(B\)-slice gives \(Bd\in BV\), so \(d\in V\). Compatibility with all slices forces \(Vd\subset V\). If \(\deg d=k>0\) then \(N^{m-1}d\) has degree \(m-1+k\) between \(m\) and \(2m-2<n\); contradiction. Thus \(d\) and \(h\) are the same scalar. Conversely scalars belong to the centroid.

Here \(E=\mathbb F_3[N]\), and the radical filtration is the full Jordan flag, of width one. The raw algebra is also \(\mathbb F_3[N]\), since the centralizer of the single Jordan block is that algebra. The raw-flag theorem therefore handles arbitrarily large odd \(m\) with neither enumeration nor a supplied slice. Every pointwise isometry commutes with \(N\), hence equals \(u(N)\). Since \(N\) is \(B\)-selfadjoint, preservation of \(B\) gives \(u(N)^2=1\). In the local ring \(\mathbb F_3[N]\) this gives \(u(N)=\pm1\), both with positive determinant in even dimension. Thus this family genuinely has no pointwise odd correction. Its existence does not imply that all scalar-centroid inputs have bounded radical-flag width.
