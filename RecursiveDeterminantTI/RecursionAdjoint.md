# Determinant descent through principal adjoint algebras

Let \(F=\mathbf F_3\). Fix \(s\in\{1,-1\}\) and a constant \(c>0\). A concise \(s\)-symmetric bimap \(\beta:X\times X\to Z\) has \(\beta(y,x)=s\beta(x,y)\), zero common radical, and values spanning \(Z\). A transporter \((g,f)\) satisfies \(f\beta(x,y)=\beta'(gx,gy)\). Its sign in the given input bases is \(\det(g)\det(f)\).

The adjoint algebra \(A\subseteq\operatorname{End}_F(X)\) consists of the operators \(a\) with a mate \(a^*\) satisfying \(\beta(ax,y)=\beta(x,a^*y)\). The mate is unique by conciseness. Swapping \(x,y\) proves that the mate operation is an involution, and composing operators proves that it reverses products. Write \(J\) for the radical of \(A\).

**Theorem.** Suppose \(A\cong F[t]/(t^h)\) with \(h\ge 2\), and let \(\nu\in\{1,-1\}\) be the involution's action on \(J/J^2\). There is a canonical polynomial-size filtration of the balanced carrier, with cells indexed by triples \((j,a,b)\) of radical degree and two module lengths. Give each cell \(D_\ell\) its exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow K_\ell\longrightarrow D_\ell\longrightarrow Q_\ell\longrightarrow0
\]
from the bimap, as below. Let \(N\ge2\) be the dense input length. In each cell choose either the kernel or the quotient, subject to the chosen space having dimension at most \(c\log_2 N\). Let \(S\) be the set of cells where the kernel was chosen. Let \(V_u\) be the module residue of length \(u\), with dimension \(r_u\). Define
\[
e_u=u+\sum_{\ell\in S}w_u(\ell),\qquad
w_u(j,a,b)=
\begin{cases}
r_b & a<b,\ u=a,\\
r_a & a<b,\ u=b,\\
r_a+s\nu^j & a=b=u,\\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
If \(r_u\le c\log_2 N\) for every odd \(e_u\), there is a deterministic polynomial-time reduction of a prescribed-sign transporter test to one ordinary three-tensor isomorphism instance over \(F\). The source and target may have unbounded radical length, unbounded module multiplicities, and distinct module lengths. The records use the sign of the same transporter on \(\beta\). Every transporter of the prescribed sign extends to an isomorphism of the records.

One may also use the coarser filtration by radical degree. In that version put \(r_{>j}=\sum_{u>j}r_u\), let \(S\) be the kernel-read degrees, and replace \(e_u\) by
\[
u+\sum_{j\in S,\ j<u} (r_{>j}+s\nu^j).
\]
The choice of \(S\) and all its bounds are recognized by linear algebra. A cell with only one small side forces its corresponding choice. A cell with both sides small gives a binary variable. For every \(u\) with \(r_u>c\log_2 N\), impose the equation \(e_u=0\bmod 2\). These are affine equations over \(\mathbf F_2\); use a fixed elimination order to choose \(S\) when the system is consistent. If an invariant differs between the two inputs, the comparison returns a fixed negative ordinary instance. For a cube-zero local algebra \(F\oplus X\oplus Z\) with products in its radical given by symmetric \(\beta\), the same sign is the determinant of the algebra transporter in adapted bases. In raw algebra bases the associated frame factors are known.

## 1. Intrinsic module and carrier filtrations

Compute \(A\) and the involution by linear equations. To recognize the stated algebra, test commutativity and compute on an \(F\)-basis the powers \(a^{3^m}\) with \(3^m\ge\dim_F A\). On a principal algebra of the stated form these are scalar multiples of the identity, so the resulting residues define a linear map \(A\to F\). Verify that it is a unital algebra map with nilpotent kernel \(J\). This forces \(J\) to be the radical since \(A/J=F\). Require \(\dim J/J^2=1\). For any \(t\) mapping to a generator, \(J=Ft+J^2\) implies that \(J^k/J^{k+1}\) is generated by \(t^k\) for every \(k\ge1\). Thus \(1,t,\ldots,t^{h-1}\) is a basis, where \(h\) is the nilpotence index. One may take \(t=(v+\nu v^*)/2\) for a lift \(v\), so \(t^*=\nu t\). The operator \(t\) is nilpotent on \(X\). A nilpotent Jordan basis expresses \(X\) as a sum of modules \(F[t]/(t^u)\), with \(r_u\) copies of length \(u\).

Put \(T=X/JX\), \(T_{\le u}=(\operatorname{ann}_X J^u+JX)/JX\), and \(V_u=T_{\le u}/T_{\le u-1}\). In a Jordan basis \(V_u\) has the images of the generators of chains of length \(u\) as a basis. Write \(L=J/J^2\), and let \(\lambda\) be the scalar of the actual transported map on \(L\) in chosen bases. The map on \(A\) is forced to be \(a\mapsto gag^{-1}\) and preserves \(*\), since transporting the adjoint equations gives the adjoint equations on the target. Let \(\delta_u\) be the determinant on \(V_u\). The map induced on \(J^jX/J^{j+1}X\) is the map on
\[
 L^{\otimes j}\otimes (T/T_{\le j}).
\]
The identification follows by multiplying \(j\) lifts from \(J\) and a lift from \(X\), and the chain basis verifies bijectivity. Thus in layer-adapted bases,
\[
\det(g)=\lambda^{\sum_j j r_{>j}}\prod_u \delta_u^u.
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(C\) be the quotient of \(X\otimes_F X\) by \(y\otimes x-sx\otimes y\) and \((ax)\otimes y-x\otimes(a^*y)\). Let \(C_{\ge j}\) denote the image of \(X\otimes J^jX\), and \(K=\ker(C\to Z)\). The map \(C\to Z\) is surjective. For \(j<h\) there is a canonical identification
\[
 C_{\ge j}/C_{\ge j+1} \cong
 L^{\otimes j}\otimes \mathcal S_{s\nu^j}(T/T_{\le j}),
 \quad
 \mathcal S_+(W)=\operatorname{Sym}^2 W,\quad
 \mathcal S_-(W)=\Lambda^2 W .
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed the map sends the class of \(l_1\otimes\cdots\otimes l_j\otimes \bar x\otimes\bar y\) to the class of \(x\otimes (l_1\cdots l_j)y\), using arbitrary lifts. A different lift of \(l_i\), or a factor from \(JX\) in a lift of \(x,y\), changes the value by \(C_{\ge j+1}\). A lift in \(\operatorname{ann}_X J^j\) contributes zero by the adjoint identity. Swapping the two last factors multiplies by \(s\nu^j\). To check there are no further relations, use the chain generators \(e_i\) of lengths \(u_i\). The reciprocal map on the generators of \(C\) sends \(t^p e_i\otimes t^q e_k\) to \(\nu^p[e_i,e_k]_{p+q}\), with zero for \(p+q\ge\min(u_i,u_k)\). Its target has one basis symbol for each unordered pair and allowed degree, subject to \([e_k,e_i]_d=s\nu^d[e_i,e_k]_d\), so a diagonal symbol vanishes when its sign is negative. This reciprocal map respects swap and all balancing relations, and its inverse sends \([e_i,e_k]_d\) to \(e_i\otimes t^d e_k\). The identification (2) follows.

For the finer filtration, fix \(j\), write \(W=T/T_{\le j}\), and let \(W_a\) be the image of \(T_{\le a}\) for \(a>j\). Enumerate pairs \(j<a\le b\le h\) in increasing lexicographic order of \((b,a)\). In the degree-\(j\) carrier, at each step add the image of \(L^{\otimes j}\otimes W_a\otimes W_b\). The increment for \(a<b\) is \(L^{\otimes j}\otimes V_a\otimes V_b\), and for \(a=b\) it is \(L^{\otimes j}\otimes\mathcal S_{s\nu^j}(V_a)\). Lower factors in either argument have an earlier sorted length pair. In a chain basis the new independent symbols are exactly those of the claimed increment. This also proves the identifications are induced by the displayed canonical maps. Lift these steps to a filtration of \(C\) by running \(j\) from \(h-1\) to \(0\) and pulling back each degree-\(j\) step along its quotient map. Empty cells are omitted.

An increment \(C_i/C_{i-1}\) in this ascending filtration is \(D_\ell\). Its quotient \(Q_\ell\) is the corresponding increment in the image filtration of \(Z\), and \(K_\ell\) is \((K\cap C_i)/(K\cap C_{i-1})\) included in \(D_\ell\). The exact sequence follows by restricting \(C\to Z\) and taking the two successive images. The same argument applies to the coarse degree filtration.

## 2. Sign identity

Use chain bases to give bases to the residues \(V_u\) and to the carrier cells in (2). In a cell \(D_\ell\) the determinant of the action is
\[
 \lambda^{j\,\dim D_\ell}\prod_u\delta_u^{w_u(\ell)} .
\tag{3}
\]
For \(a<b\) use the determinant formula for tensor products. For the diagonal use \(\det(\operatorname{Sym}^2 P)=(\det P)^{r+1}\) or \(\det(\Lambda^2 P)=(\det P)^{r-1}\) on \(r\) dimensions. These formulas follow by triangularization over an algebraic closure, where every diagonal eigenvalue of \(P\) occurs in \(r+1\) or \(r-1\) products respectively. For the coarse case the operator on \(T/T_{\le j}\) is triangular in its flag of length residues, giving exactly the coarse exponents.

For each \(\ell\), choose a basis of \(K_\ell\), a basis of \(Q_\ell\), and lifts of the latter to \(D_\ell\). Let \(d_\ell\) be the determinant of the concatenated kernel and lifted quotient basis relative to the specified basis of \(D_\ell\). Choose the basis of \(Z\) by concatenating lifts of the \(Q_\ell\) bases through its filtration. Let \(S_X,S_Z\) be the matrices of the adapted \(X,Z\) bases relative to the raw bases. For a kernel-read cell, the exact sequence says that the determinant on its quotient is the determinant in (3) divided by the determinant on its kernel, multiplied by \(d_\ell/d'_\ell\). For a quotient-read cell use its quotient determinant directly. Since sums or decompositions of distinct flag grades are used only to compute triangular determinants, arbitrary choices of lifts create no further conditions on \(g\). Thus, with
\[
 \eta=\det(S_X)\det(S_Z)\prod_{\ell\in S} d_\ell^{-1},
 \qquad
 b=\sum_j j r_{>j}+\sum_{(j,a,b')\in S}j\,\dim D_{(j,a,b')},
\]
the raw sign is
\[
 \frac{\eta'}{\eta}\,
 \lambda^b \prod_u\delta_u^{e_u}\,
 \prod_{\ell\in S}(\det g_{K_\ell})^{-1}
 \prod_{\ell\notin S}\det f_{Q_\ell}.
\tag{4}
\]
In the coarse case the second sum defining \(b\) is over kernel-read degrees with their increment dimensions. Every determinant in (4) is over \(F\), so an even power equals \(1\) and an inverse equals the original value.

## 3. Same-field one-instance compilation

Retain \(\beta\) with both input occurrences tied to \(X\), and the evaluation \(A\times X\to X\) with its two \(X\) occurrences tied to \(X\). Because evaluation is faithful, the action on \(A\) is the actual conjugation induced by \(g\). Retain the quotient \(X\times X\to C\), with both input occurrences again tied to \(X\); its values span, so this forces the action on \(C\). Keep inclusions and quotient maps for the filtration pieces and the exact sequences used in (4), including the maps defining \(L\) and the relevant \(V_u\). A map on a subspace linked by its inclusion is forced, and the same is true for a quotient linked by its quotient map. Every genuine transporter induces all those maps.

For each logarithmic space \(M\) used in (4), attach the full exterior algebra \(\Lambda^\bullet M\), its multiplication, the degree-one inclusion of \(M\), and the top line inclusion. Its dimension is \(2^{\dim M}\le N^c\). The exterior algebra is generated by \(M\) as a unital algebra, so the top line has exactly the actual determinant of the induced map on \(M\). A zero-dimensional determinant can be omitted. Use the cotangent line \(L\) when \(b\) is odd. Multiply the selected lines in a fixed order via a chain of bilinear isomorphisms of one-dimensional spaces. For the final product line with chosen generators \(z,z'\), mark \(\eta^{-1}z\) on the source and \(\sigma(\eta')^{-1}z'\) on the target, where \(\sigma\) is the desired raw sign. This compares its multiplier to \(\sigma\eta/\eta'\), as required by (4). If all factors are omitted, test the known frame ratio and use the ordinary comparison or a fixed negative pair as appropriate. This construction extends exactly the signed transporters as typed isomorphisms.

There are polynomially many named roles and records. They can be packed into fixed arity and a constant number of tensor records as follows. Form the direct sums \(H\) of all roles, \(O\) of a separate copy for each slot occurrence, and the split coordinate algebra \(E\) with one idempotent for every summand label in \(H,O\). Keep multiplication on \(E\), its diagonal actions on \(H,O\), and its action on an additional module having distinct positive multiplicities \(1,2,\ldots,\dim E\) for its idempotents. The extra module forces each idempotent to remain in its given position. Include the map \(O\to H\) sending each slot copy identically into its actual role. Combine linear arrays on their separate occurrence blocks into one linear map on \(O\), and bilinear arrays into one bilinear map on \(O\), extended by zero on unused blocks. Marked vectors combine into one vector with their occurrence slots as components. This fits a constant-size tensor signature of order at most three, with polynomial dimensions. Its isomorphisms enforce the original typed relations in both directions. Apply the same-field tensor-system compiler to this fixed signature. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

For a cube-zero algebra, an isomorphism fixes the scalar unit, acts by \(g\) on the radical modulo its square, and by \(f\) on its square. The lift on the radical can vary by an arbitrary map into the square, which is triangular and leaves the determinant unchanged. Conversely each bimap transporter extends by the identity on the unit and the direct sum of \(g,f\) in adapted lifts.

## 4. An unbounded sign-rigid family with both centroids scalar

Take \(s=1\), \(h\ge6\) with \(h\equiv2\pmod4\), and \(r\ge8\) divisible by \(4\). Write \(B=F[t]/(t^h)\), \(t^*=-t\), and \(X=B^r\). Build the balanced symmetric carrier using this given \(B\)-action and let \(\tau:X\times X\to C\) be its universal map. Use the basis of Section 1 to identify the even coefficient grades with symmetric \(r\times r\) matrices and odd grades with alternating matrices. This identification sends classes of paired vectors to their symmetrized or skew-symmetrized tensors, using \(1/2\). Put \(I=I_r\) and \(H=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,\ldots,1)\). Take \(K\subset C\) to be the direct sum of the line of \(I\) in degree \(0\) and the line of \(H\) in each positive even degree. Set \(Z=C/K\) and \(\beta=\tau\bmod K\). Each degree kernel is a line or zero even though \(\dim K=h/2\) is unbounded.

**The actual adjoint algebra is \(B\).** First consider adjoint pairs \((P,Q)\) of \(\tau\). Evaluate on \(u e_i,v e_j\) for \(i\ne j\), where \(e_i\) are the \(B\)-basis vectors. The coefficient in the unordered \(\{i,j\}\) channel is \(u^*v\), up to applying \(*\) when changing the order. For \(k\ne i\), choose \(j\) distinct from \(k,i\) and take \(v=1\). Comparing the \(\{k,j\}\) coefficient forces the \((k,i)\) block of \(P\) to vanish. The same argument for the second variable makes \(Q\) diagonal. In the two-variable cross equation one now has
\((P_i u)^*v=u^*Q_j v\) for all \(u,v\) and \(i\ne j\). Substituting \(u=v=1\) identifies the corresponding constants across all indices because \(r\ge3\). Substituting \(u=1\) or \(v=1\) then gives \(P\) equal to multiplication by one \(B\)-scalar and \(Q\) equal to multiplication by its mate.

For adjoints of \(\beta\), test candidate \(P,Q\) on the \(F\)-basis elements \(t^p e_i,t^q e_j\). In a fixed degree, each of the two terms in
\(\tau(Px,y)-\tau(x,Qy)\) is a symmetrized or skew-symmetrized tensor of a coefficient vector paired with a coordinate vector. Each term has matrix rank at most \(2\). The difference has rank at most \(4\) in every degree. Any nonzero element of \(K\) has rank \(r>4\) in some degree. Thus the differences vanish in \(C\), and all adjoints of \(\beta\) are already those of \(\tau\). To see conciseness, for arbitrary \(x\) and any \(y=e_j\) every degree of \(\tau(x,y)\) has rank at most \(2\). If all \(\beta(x,e_j)=0\), then all \(\tau(x,e_j)=0\). For each \(i\), taking \(j\ne i\) and inspecting the \(\{i,j\}\) channel gives \(x_i=0\). The values of \(\beta\) span by construction.

**The centroid of \(\beta\) is scalar.** The action on \(X\) of a centroid element is an adjoint equal to its mate. It is multiplication by an even polynomial in \(t\). For its two compatible centroid actions to exist, its induced action on \(C\), by applying it to the first placement, has to preserve \(K\). If the polynomial has a nonzero coefficient of positive degree, apply its nonconstant part to \(I\) in degree zero. In its lowest nonzero degree the resulting matrix is a nonzero multiple of \(I\), outside the specified line of \(H\). This is impossible. The scalar on \(X\) forces the same scalar on \(Z\).

**The centroid of the dual relation bimap is scalar.** Set \(R=\ker(\operatorname{Sym}^2_F X\to Z)\), so dualization gives a symmetric bimap on \(X^*\) with output \(R^*\). The space \(R\) contains \(tX\odot W\) for \(W=\ker t\), and every \((t x)\odot y+x\odot(t y)\). These follow by balancing, since \(t^*=-t\). Here \(W\subset tX\), \(\dim W=r\), and \(t^2X\ne0\). This bimap has zero radical. If \(\xi\in X^*\) has zero contraction with \(R\), first use the squares of vectors in \(W\) and then \(tX\odot W\) to obtain \(\xi|_{tX}=0\). Contraction with \((tx)\odot x\in R\) gives \(\xi(x)tx=0\), hence \(\xi=0\). The bimap spans its output by duality.

For an operator on \(X^*\) in the centroid, let \(P\) be its transpose on \(X\). Balancing on \(R\) requires \((P\otimes 1-1\otimes P)q=0\) for all \(q\in R\), viewing symmetric tensors inside \(X\otimes X\). Applying this to \(w^2\) for all \(w\in W\) gives \(P|_W=d I\), since a linear map preserving every line is scalar. For \(v\in tX\), applying it to \(v\odot w\) gives \((Pv-dv)\wedge w=0\), so two independent \(w\)'s give \(Pv=dv\). Put \(D=P-dI\). Applying the balancing equation to \((tx)\odot x\) gives \(tx\wedge Dx=0\) for all \(x\). Since \(D\) kills \(\ker t\subset tX\), it factors through \(t:X\to tX\) by a map carrying every line in \(tX\) to itself. Thus \(D=\alpha t\). Since \(D\) kills \(tX\) and \(t^2X\ne 0\), one has \(\alpha=0\). This makes the centroid scalar on both roles.

**The sign matters.** Every ordinary self-transporter acts on the two lines in degrees \(0\) and \(2\) by congruence on \(I,H\) through the same residue matrix \(P\). The cotangent scalar contributes \(\lambda^2=1\) in degree \(2\) and likewise in all even degrees. Write \(PIP^{\mathsf T}=aI\), \(PHP^{\mathsf T}=bH\). Then \(PHP^{-1}=(b/a)H\). The two eigenspaces of \(H\) have different dimensions, so \(a=b\) and \(P\) preserves the unique one-dimensional \(-1\) eigenspace. On this coordinate line, \(P^{\mathsf T}P=aI\) gives \(a\) equal to a nonzero square in \(F\), hence \(a=b=1\). All kernel-line actions are therefore \(1\). In the coarse rule one can choose all cells kernel-read for sufficiently large dense inputs. The residue exponent is even because \(h\) is even and the \(h/2\) positive and \(h/2\) negative parity contributions add to \(hr\). The cotangent exponent is even because \(r\) and \(\binom r2\) are even and only odd grades affect its parity. Formula (4) now gives sign \(1\) for every self-transporter. Reflecting one vector of the raw \(Z\) basis yields an ordinarily isomorphic pair with only sign \(-1\) transporters. Here \(\dim X=hr\) is even, and \(\dim Z=(h/2)(r^2-1)\) is odd. The resulting algebra is even dimensional and has radical square equal to \(Z\).

**Mixed-length extension of the examples.** Add any number of chains of lengths \(2,\ldots,h-1\), with the multiplicity of each length a multiple of \(4\). Retain the kernel lines in the longest block exactly as above. In every degree the carrier is the space of symmetric or alternating matrices on the active chains, and each nonzero kernel grade still has rank \(r\). The rank test for preservation of the actual adjoint therefore still applies. To compute the adjoints of the universal carrier, make the same off-diagonal block test with its probing index \(j\) a longest chain distinct from \(i,k\). The \(\{k,j\}\) channel sees the full coefficient in the \(k\)-th chain, so every off-diagonal block vanishes. The diagonal blocks on longest chains are a single scalar and its mate by the same cross equations. Testing a shorter diagonal block against a longest chain recovers precisely the reductions of those scalars to the shorter coefficient ring. The common radical is zero by probing each nonzero coordinate against a different longest chain. Both centroid arguments are unchanged because \(W=\ker t\subset tX\) holds for chain lengths at least \(2\), and the grade-zero kernel matrix of the longest block still detects any positive even scalar coefficient.

Every coarse grade can again read its kernel. For each length \(u\), the parity of \(e_u\) is the parity of \(\sum_{j<u} r_{>j}\), which is even. The cotangent exponent is even because \(r_{>j}\) and \(\binom{r_{>j}}2\) are even for all \(j\). On the longest-length residue \(V_h\), the induced actions on the two distinguished lines again project to congruences with \(I,H\). This projection is valid because the \((h,h)\) cell is the last cell in its degree and the chosen lines inject into it. The same eigenvalue argument forces both multipliers to be \(1\), and hence forces all kernel multipliers to be \(1\). All carrier grades have even dimension. Since \(\dim K=h/2\) is odd, \(\dim Z\) is odd. These are mixed-length even-dimensional algebras with two scalar centroids and sign-rigid comparisons.

This proof is informal. It gives a sufficient class for the determinant-reduction problem with two scalar centroids and a nonsemisimple adjoint algebra. The principal ring and the logarithmic/parity conditions are hypotheses. The adjoint ring construction is standard and the tensor-system compiler is prior work.
