# Sharp Polynomial Calculus degree for tensors with split determinantal frames

**Theorem.** Let $T,S$ be same-format three-tensors over any field $k$ that are not isomorphic over an algebraic closure. Suppose the three flattening supports of $T$ have equal dimension $n$. On its supported format, suppose the determinant of the matrix slice pencil in each of two modes is a nonzero scalar times a product of $n$ linearly independent linear forms over the algebraic closure. Then the literal cubic tensor action equations and the two-sided inverse equations have an ordinary Polynomial Calculus refutation of degree $O(n+1)$. There are no field equations, auxiliary witness variables or assumptions on $S$. In particular the degree is linear in the geometric border rank of $T$ on this class.

The condition includes the tensors $\sum_i e_i\otimes e_i\otimes e_i+\sum_{i<j<k} t_{ijk}e_i\otimes e_j\otimes e_k$ for arbitrary coefficients, since in every mode the slice pencil is triangular with the coordinate variables on its diagonal. The bound is sharp on this class in characteristic zero and in every fixed positive characteristic, both on the rank and the border-rank scales. This settles the quantitative degree on a class in Questions 7.1 and 7.4 that contains tensors outside geometric sums of rectangular matrix multiplication. [Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She,

2.3 and 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19320)

## 1. A Laurent-binomial refutation with localized degree

Let $K$ be algebraically closed and write $x_i,y_i$ for variables with axioms $x_i y_i-1$, $1\le i\le m$. For $a\in\mathbb Z^m$, set $M(a)=\prod_i x_i^{a_i^+}y_i^{a_i^-}$. Consider axioms $M(a_j)-c_j M(b_j)$ with $c_j\in K^\times$ and $\max(\|a_j\|_1,\|b_j\|_1)\le D$. If the axioms have no solution, they have a PC refutation of degree $O((m+1)D)$.

The difference $M(a)M(b)-M(a+b)$ follows from the inverse axioms in degree at most $\|a\|_1+\|b\|_1$, by replacing each opposing factor $x_i y_i$ by one, one product at a time. Hence each binomial gives $M(d_j)-c_j$ for $d_j=a_j-b_j$, by multiplication by $M(-b_j)$ and cancellation. Multiplication by $M(-d_j)$ and cancellation also gives $M(-d_j)-c_j^{-1}$. These derivations use degree at most $O(D)$.

Inconsistency means there is an integral relation $\sum_j \ell_j d_j=0$ with $\prod_j c_j^{\ell_j}\ne1$. Indeed, if all such products were one, the $c_j$ would define a homomorphism from the lattice generated by the $d_j$ to $K^\times$. Smith normal form and divisibility of $K^\times$ extend it to $\mathbb Z^m$, giving a solution by evaluating the basis and taking inverses. Take $|\ell_j|$ copies of the step $\operatorname{sgn}(\ell_j)d_j$ with associated scalar $c_j^{\operatorname{sgn}(\ell_j)}$. This is a zero-sum family of vectors of $\ell_1$ norm at most $2D$. The Steinitz lemma in $\ell_1$ orders the family with partial sums of norm at most $2mD$. [Bárány, *A matrix version of the Steinitz lemma*](https://doi.org/10.1515/crelle-2024-0008)

Write $s_t$ for the partial exponent sum and $C_t$ for the corresponding product of constants. To update the line $M(s_t)-C_t$, multiply the next step equation $M(d)-c$ by $M(s_t)$, cancel the opposing factors, and add $c$ times the previous line. This derives $M(s_t+d)-c C_t$, with degree at most $2mD+O(D)$. The final line is the nonzero constant $1-\prod_j c_j^{\ell_j}$. This proof bounds the degree without bounding the total number of steps or the degree of a Nullstellensatz certificate.

## 2. Work on the supported format

It suffices to work over an algebraic closure. To descend a final bounded-degree PC proof to $k$, take a finite extension containing its coefficients and a $k$-basis of that extension containing $1$. Replace every line by all its $k$-coordinate polynomials. Multiplication by a variable commutes with extracting coordinates, and linear combinations are implemented by the multiplication table for coefficient scalars in the extension. Initial $k$-axioms occupy only the $1$-coordinate. This descends the final $1$ without increasing degree.

Write $P_i$ for the forward and $P_i'$ for the named inverse in mode $i$. From the literal action equations derive the reverse action by applying the three named inverses and telescoping their three adjacent inverse products. This has a constant-degree certificate. If one flattening rank differs, choose a nonzero minor of order one more than the smaller rank on the larger side. The product expression for that flattening under the forward or reverse action has all such minors identically zero by Cauchy–Binet. Telescope the determinant difference using the flattening action equations to refute in degree $O(n+1)$, since the smaller rank is at most $n$.

Otherwise use separate constant supported bases for $T,S$. In the forward flattening identity cancel the other two actions by their named inverses. The bottom rows of the target supported flattening vanish, so applying a constant right inverse of the source's active full-row-rank flattening derives that the off-support block of $P_i$ from the source support into the target complement is zero. Similarly, using the reverse action, derive the analogous block equation for $P_i'$. The top-left blocks of both inverse equations then give two-sided inverse equations on the $n$-dimensional supports, and the top supported action equations give the compressed tensor action. All compressed axioms have constant-degree derivations. Constant linear changes of variables preserve PC degree, since multiplication by a linear form is implemented by variable multiplications and addition. Thus the proof on the supported format lifts with only constant additive degree overhead. The zero case is immediate. If $n=1$ and the supports agree, the two nonzero supported scalar tensors are isomorphic.

## 3. The determinant recognizes the mode frame in bounded degree

Work with the supported tensors, each concise of format $n\times n\times n$, $n\ge2$. For a mode $i$, let $F_T(z)$ be the determinant of its linear contraction pencil with covector $z$, and similarly $F_S(z)$. Define the constant linear space
$$
\mathcal L_T=\{A\in M_n(K): D_A F_T(z)=\operatorname{tr}(A)F_T(z)\},
\qquad D_A f(z)=[t]f(z+tAz).
$$
Specify $\mathcal L_S$ similarly. In source covector coordinates where $F_T=\lambda\prod_j z_j$, the space $\mathcal L_T$ is the full diagonal algebra. The diagonal coefficients already give the required trace multiple. Each off-diagonal entry $A_{ij}$ gives the distinct monomial $z_j^2\prod_{\ell\ne i,j}z_\ell$ with coefficient $\lambda A_{ij}$, so all those entries are zero. This calculation is characteristic-independent.

With $H=P_i^{\mathsf T}$, $K_i=(P_i')^{\mathsf T}$, and $d=\det(P_j)\det(P_\ell)$ for the other two modes, the action gives the coefficient identities
$$
F_S(z)=d F_T(Hz).
$$
Every coefficient of the difference has a degree $O(n)$ certificate. To obtain it, use the literal slice matrix identity $S(z)=P_j T(Hz)P_\ell^{\mathsf T}$ and telescope its determinant column by column. Differentiate the displayed coefficient identities along $B=K_i A H$ with $A\in\mathcal L_T$ fixed. This only takes linear combinations of the original coefficient identities with multipliers of bounded degree in $B$. In the derivative of the right side use $HB=AH$, obtained by the inverse equation $HK_i=I$, and use $\operatorname{tr}(B)=\operatorname{tr}(A)$ by the same identity. Consequently the fixed linear equations defining $\mathcal L_S$, evaluated on $B$, are derivable with degree $O(n)$. For example, the error in replacing $HBz$ by $AHz$ occurs linearly in the directional input to $\nabla F_T(Hz)$, of witness degree $n-1$, and the determinant factor has degree $2n$. All statements about $z$ here mean coefficient identities. No $z$ variables are added to the proof. The reverse argument follows from the reverse tensor action.

Choose a constant linear projection onto each of $\mathcal L_T,\mathcal L_S$. The difference between the identity and the projection factors as a constant linear map through the defining equations of the space. We may therefore use the degree-two coordinates of the projected conjugations $\Phi:\mathcal L_T\to\mathcal L_S$ and $\Psi:\mathcal L_S\to\mathcal L_T$, with errors certified in degree $O(n)$. The two compositions are the identities by the named inverses. They transport the unit and products whenever those products lie in the indicated spaces, by the same conjugation identities.

These certificates either refute the original system or identify matching diagonal algebras. If $\dim\mathcal L_S\ne n$, the mutual inverses $\Phi,\Psi$ give a rectangular rank contradiction. Even if $\dim\mathcal L_S>n$, use just an $(n+1)$-minor of the larger identity. If $\mathcal L_S$ is not closed under products, choose $a,b\in\mathcal L_S$ whose product is outside it. Transport their projected preimages into the source diagonal algebra, multiply there and transport back. This gives a polynomial expression for $ab$ in the fixed target space with error derivable in degree $O(n)$, contradicting a constant separating linear functional. If the target algebra is noncommutative, use $\Psi(a)\Psi(b)=\Psi(b)\Psi(a)$ in the same argument. Both transports preserve the unit.

If the target is a commutative algebra that is not separable, transport the source diagonal separability element $\sum_i E_{ii}\otimes E_{ii}$ to the target using $\Phi\otimes\Phi$. Its multiplication is the identity. The equation $(a\otimes 1)e=(1\otimes a)e$ for each fixed target basis element $a$ follows by using $\Psi(a)$ in the source and transporting the product identities. Thus coordinates of the transported element would solve the fixed linear separability system. That system is inconsistent for a nonseparable algebra, so a constant linear combination refutes. To see the usual criterion here, a solution $\sum u_i\otimes v_i$ averages a linear projection $p$ onto a submodule into the module projection $w\mapsto\sum u_i p(v_iw)$. This forces semisimplicity. Over the algebraically closed field the commutative semisimple algebra of dimension $n$ is $K^n$. Its faithful inclusion into $M_n(K)$ forces each nonzero primitive summand to act on a one-dimensional space. Thus a constant basis change makes $\mathcal L_S$ diagonal as well. Apply this in each of the two marked modes and use the dual bases on physical vectors.

All product, projection, trace and separability certificates in this step substitute polynomial expressions of bounded degree into the conjugation identities, and determinants used for a rank contradiction have order at most $n+1$. In each determinant expansion, telescope the difference one matrix entry at a time, multiplying a single certified error by products of the other entries. These derivations preserve the bound $O(n)$.

## 4. Eliminate the third mode after the two frame permutations

In each of the two marked physical modes, transport of the two diagonal algebras gives
$$
P E_i P'=\sum_j f_{ji}E'_j,\qquad
P' E'_j P=\sum_i g_{ij}E_i ,
$$
where the coefficient polynomials are of degree at most two in the witness variables. Their projected equalities have degree $O(n)$ derivations from Section 3. Multiplication of idempotents and the unit identities give $f_{ji}^2=f_{ji}$, orthogonality across columns within a row, and each row sum equal to one. The reverse in $g$ gives the other sums and orthogonalities after identifying $g_{ij}=f_{ji}$: multiply the first equality by $E_j'$, conjugate back, and read its $i$-component to get $g_{ij}=f_{ji}g_{ij}$. The corresponding calculation in reverse gives $f_{ji}=g_{ij}f_{ji}$. These are bounded-degree substitutions in the certificates of Section 3.

For a permutation $\pi$ let $e_\pi=\prod_i f_{\pi(i),i}$. The equations give $\sum_\pi e_\pi=1$ in degree $O(n)$. To derive it, multiply the column sums, telescope the product against one, and remove every term with a repeated row using row orthogonality. The derivation may be long, but each term is a product of $n$ polynomials of degree at most two. On this branch, $e_\pi f_{ji}=0$ if $j\ne\pi(i)$ and $e_\pi f_{\pi(i),i}=e_\pi$ by orthogonality and idempotence. Multiplying the conjugation identities by $P$ and $P'$ respectively therefore gives, after multiplying by $e_\pi$, the equations that all off-permutation entries of $P,P'$ vanish. The named inverse equations give $x_i\overline{x}_i-1=0$ after multiplication by $e_\pi$, where $x_i=P_{\pi(i),i}$ and $\overline{x}_i=P'_{i,\pi(i)}$.

Use the analogous selector for a permutation $\sigma$ of the second marked mode. For each pair of choices use their product $e=e_\pi e_\sigma$. The sum of these products is one with degree $O(n)$, by expanding and using each partition identity. On the branch define $a_i,\overline a_i,b_j,\overline b_j$ from the two marked pairs of action matrices. Use $R,R'$ for the third action and inverse. Write $v_{ij}$ for the third-mode vector of $T$ at the first two coordinates $i,j$, and $w_{ij}$ for the vector of $S$ at coordinates $\pi(i),\sigma(j)$. The literal action equations and the branch equations give
$$
e\big(a_i b_j R v_{ij}-w_{ij}\big)=0.
\tag{1}
$$
The third support is full, so choose a basis $v_{s_h t_h}$, $1\le h\le n$, from the $v_{ij}$. If the corresponding $w_{s_h t_h}$ are linearly dependent, pick a constant nonzero relation among them, sum (1) with these coefficients, and apply $R'$. Extracting its coordinates in the source basis forces $e\lambda_h a_{s_h}b_{t_h}=0$ for each relation coefficient $\lambda_h$. A nonzero $\lambda_h$ and the branch inverse equations give $e=0$.

Otherwise use the two bases to write $v_{ij}=\sum_h c_{ijh}v_{s_h t_h}$ and $w_{ij}=\sum_h d_{ijh}w_{s_h t_h}$. Derive the following branch equations by cancelling the factors on the basis rows of (1) with the named inverses, substituting the resulting expressions for $Rv_{s_h t_h}$ into (1), extracting the target basis coefficients, and clearing the indicated factor:
$$
e\big(c_{ijh} a_i b_j-d_{ijh}a_{s_h}b_{t_h}\big)=0.
\tag{2}
$$
These operations use $e f$ lines linearly and multiply them by at most a bounded number of application or inverse variables. For example the cancellation on the basis row gives $e(Rv_{s_h t_h}-\overline a_{s_h}\overline b_{t_h}w_{s_h t_h})=0$, and clearing its factor in the $h$-th extracted coordinate uses two variables. The claimed degree is $O(n)$ throughout. If exactly one of $c_{ijh},d_{ijh}$ is zero, cancellation in (2) gives $e=0$. Omit equations where both vanish. All others are branch copies of Laurent binomials with constant degree and invertible coefficients.

An invertible assignment to the $a_i,b_j$ satisfying these binomials would define an invertible third-mode map by $Rv_{s_h t_h}=(a_{s_h}b_{t_h})^{-1}w_{s_h t_h}$. Equations (2) without $e$ ensure that this map satisfies every original vector equation, producing an isomorphism over $K$. The branch binomials are therefore inconsistent. Apply the Laurent-binomial proof of Section 1, multiplying every line by $e$. All its initial weighted axioms have just been derived. Multiplication by a variable and field-linear combinations simulate each step, with the selector adding only its $O(n)$ degree. Its final line gives $e=0$. Do this for each pair of permutations, then add and use the partition identity to obtain $1=0$. This completes the all-field upper bound.

The hypotheses also hold outside geometric sums of rectangular matrix multiplication. For example, take the side-three diagonal tensor plus $e_1\otimes e_2\otimes e_3$. In mode one its slice matrices in the bilinear convention are $E_{11}+E_{32},E_{22},E_{33}$. Their adjoint pair algebra consists of the two equal diagonal operators with second and third entries equal. The module Hom space from the second input to the output over that algebra has dimension $1^2+2^2=5$, while the slice space has dimension three. Thus the slices do not fill the full Hom space required for geometric matrix multiplication sums. This calculation works over every field. For each rectangular matrix multiplication block, comparing its matrix-unit slices gives a common column-action algebra, and every module map for that algebra is a slice. The same property holds componentwise for a direct sum, since the disjoint input and output supports force the pair maps to preserve the summands.

## 5. Sharpness over characteristic zero

There is a linear-rank, degree-preserving reduction from bounded-degree colored graph isomorphism to the tensors above. Vertex-color labels and edge-color labels are treated as disjoint. Use graph pairs with the same sets of labels, all occurring on each side, as in the bounded-degree Cai–Fürer–Immerman construction. Enumerate these labels $1,\ldots,m$, with $m\ge2$. Given a graph $G$ on $N$ vertices, use a single common ordered index set for the three modes. It consists of one index $p$, two copies $a_v,b_v$ of each vertex, and one index $c_j$ for each label. Order them as $p<A<B<C$, with $c_1<\cdots<c_m$. Add to the unit-coefficient diagonal tensor the following unit-coefficient support:
- $(a_v,b_v,c_j)$ whenever $j$ is the vertex color of $v$.
- $(a_v,b_w,c_j)$ whenever $v,w$ are adjacent by an edge of color $j$, taking both orders.
- $(p,c_j,c_{j+1})$ for $1\le j<m$.

All added triples are strictly increasing, so the tensors satisfy the determinant hypotheses in every mode. The diagonal triples are the unique perfect matching of the support viewed as a three-partite hypergraph. In fact, number the common indices in their chosen order. The sum of the first indices, sum of the second indices and sum of the third indices of any perfect matching must all equal the sum of the numbers of all indices. Since every support triple is weakly increasing, equality of the sums requires every triple of the matching to be diagonal. Every tensor isomorphism is monomial in all three modes because the three diagonal spaces of Section 3 are intrinsic. It therefore preserves this unique perfect matching and uses the same permutation of indices in all modes.

This permutation preserves the non-diagonal triples. The indices occurring in the third position of those triples are exactly the label indices $C$. The only index with an incident triple in the first position and with second position in $C$ is $p$. Thus $p$ is fixed and the directed path $(p,c_j,c_{j+1})$ pins every $c_j$. The remaining first-position and second-position indices are respectively $A$ and $B$. The vertex-color triples tie the two copies of each vertex to the same image and mark its color. The edge triples then require exactly a colored graph isomorphism. Conversely, a colored graph isomorphism extends by the same permutation on the two copies and by the identity on the other indices, with all scalar factors one. In particular a colored graph nonisomorphism gives a geometric tensor nonisomorphism over every field.

For the PC reduction, substitute the same block permutation matrix $\operatorname{diag}(1,X,X,I_m)$ for all three forward matrices, and its transpose for all three named inverses, where $X$ consists of the graph-isomorphism variables. Off-color entries can be set to zero using the original color equations. The standard graph-isomorphism axioms are the row and column sums, individual Boolean equations, and quadratic products of pairs of vertices that violate injectivity, equality, and colored adjacency. The inverse projections follow from these in degree two. A diagonal tensor triple reduces to the corresponding target diagonal coefficient in degree at most three: off-diagonal images vanish by collision equations and a remaining diagonal coefficient is a row sum after replacing a cube by the variable. Vertex-color matching coefficients reduce to the color-restricted row sums or zero, again via collisions. At each edge label $j$, the action coefficient is $\sum_{v,w} A_j(v,w)X_{a v}X_{b w}$ for target indices $a,b$, where $A_j$ is the source adjacency matrix of color $j$. Every term with $A_j(v,w)\ne B_j(a,b)$ vanishes by a colored adjacency axiom. The sum of all products $X_{a v}X_{b w}$ is one by the two target row sums, so the image is the required target adjacency entry $B_j(a,b)$. The path is preserved identically. These are degree-at-most-three derivations of the projected tensor axioms, and substitution itself does not increase degree.

Berkholz and Grohe's Theorem 6.2 gives colored graph pairs with degree lower bound $\Omega(N)$ over every field of characteristic different from two. Its construction uses a fixed-regularity base graph, a bounded number of vertices per base edge or vertex, and bounded degree, so it has $O(N)$ edges and labels. Our tensors have side $1+2N+m=\Theta(N)$ and $O(N)$ nonzero entries. Their flattening ranks are the side length, while their ordinary rank is at most the number of nonzero entries. Thus both ordinary rank and geometric border rank are $\Theta(N)$. The lower bound transfers with bounded degree overhead and matches the all-field upper bound in characteristic zero. [Berkholz–Grohe, Theorem 6.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05912)

For each fixed positive characteristic, the diagonal rank-gap family with active ranks $3q,4q$, where $q$ ranges over sufficiently large powers of the characteristic, already gives a matching linear lower bound in the maximum rank or border rank. The tensors are diagonal on their supports and satisfy the determinant hypothesis. [Earlier diagonal lower bounds](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/fa1001c4a32d65711c2864f4bdfa1257a44e333b/TensorSparsePCBounds/Proof.md)

## 6. Unequal sides and determinantal subspaces

There is an unequal-format version. Let $n_i$ be the three support dimensions of $T$, and put $n=\max_i n_i$. For each of two selected modes $i$, choose a positive integer $s_i$ at most the lesser of the other two support dimensions. Let $W_{T,i}$ be the linear span of the size-$s_i$ minors of the rectangular slice pencil in that supported mode, viewed as homogeneous polynomials of degree $s_i$ on the covector space. Suppose that for both selected modes the space
$$
\mathcal L_{T,i}=\{A\in M_{n_i}(\overline k):D_A W_{T,i}\subseteq W_{T,i}\}
$$
is conjugate to the full diagonal algebra. Then the same $O(n+1)$ degree bound holds, still with no assumption on $S$.

Here one uses the minor space and its covariance in place of the single determinant and its trace character. Specifically, choose fixed bases $q_b$ of $W_{T,i}$ and $q'_a$ of $W_{S,i}$. Cauchy–Binet applied to the slice matrix identity, together with constant expressions of minors in these bases, gives
$$
q'_a(z)=\sum_b c_{ab}\,q_b(Hz),\qquad
q_b(Hz)=\sum_a d_{ba}\,q'_a(z)
\tag{3}
$$
modulo certificates of degree $O(s_i+1)$. In the first identity $c_{ab}$ are sums of products of order-$s_i$ minors of the forward maps on the other two modes. For the second, apply the reverse action and substitute $Hz$ for its covector. The products of inverse maps cancel, yielding coefficients $d_{ba}$ of the same degree scale. Determinant telescoping certifies the error of each minor, and replacing its covector linearly adds at most $s_i$ in witness degree. These equalities mean coefficient identities in the original witness variables.

Differentiate the first equality of (3) along $K_i A H$ for $A\in\mathcal L_{T,i}$. Cancelling $HK_i=I$ gives a combination of the pullbacks $D_A q_b(Hz)$. Since $D_A q_b\in W_{T,i}$, the second equality of (3) expresses this as a polynomial coefficient combination of the fixed $q'_a$. Projection onto coefficients outside $W_{S,i}$ certifies the target linear stabilizer equations in degree $O(s_i+1)$, as does the reverse argument. Proceed with algebra recognition and the two permutation partitions in Section 3–4, using $n_i$ idempotents in each respective mode and a vector basis of size $n_j$ in the remaining mode. All selector degrees and the number of Laurent coordinates are at most $O(n)$, so the same proof applies. If supports mismatch, the proof in Section 2 still uses just a minor of order at most $n+1$. The source condition is itself checkable by linear algebra, testing that the stabilizer has dimension $n_i$, is an associative commutative algebra and has a solution to its separability system. A split commutative algebra of dimension $n_i$ is faithfully embedded in $M_{n_i}$ only as a diagonal algebra up to change of basis.

For example, let $n_0\ge4$ and take the tensor of format $n_0\times n_0\times(n_0+1)$
$$
\sum_{j=1}^{n_0} e_j\otimes e_j\otimes e_j
+(e_1\otimes e_2+e_3\otimes e_4)\otimes e_{n_0+1}.
$$
All three supports are full. In mode one the maximal-minor span is generated by the monomial $f=\prod_{j=1}^{n_0} z_j$ and the two monomials obtained by replacing its $z_2$ by $z_1$, or replacing its $z_4$ by $z_3$, since these are the two nonzero entries of the sole additional matrix column. Differentiating $f$ shows that the only possible off-diagonal coefficients in a stabilizer are those for the indicated replacements. Apply either replacement to the monomial for the other. Its resulting double replacement is outside the span, and the coefficient of the replaced variable was one, so the remaining off-diagonal coefficients also vanish. The same calculation in mode two has the arrows reversed. Thus the unequal-side theorem applies in every characteristic to this source.

## 7. One frame and a full matrix algebra generated by slice ratios

In the minor-space criterion of Section 6, it suffices for the commutant of each marked Lie space to be conjugate to the full diagonal algebra. In fact, the covariance argument certifies projected conjugation maps between the source and target Lie spaces, prior to any use of the algebra hypothesis. For a fixed $B$ in the source commutant and a fixed $A'$ in the target Lie space, the projection of $HA'K_i$ into the source Lie space commutes identically with $B$. Replace that projection by $HA'K_i$ using its degree-$O(s_i+1)$ error certificates. Conjugate the resulting commutator by $K_i,H$ to certify $[K_iBH,A']=0$ with the same degree order. The reverse derivation is identical in content with source and target exchanged. The commutants are fixed linear spaces, so projection, mutual inverses and recognition of the split diagonal algebra proceed as in Section 3. This also supplies a degree-$O(n+1)$ partition into frame permutations for a tensor with just one marked mode.

**One-frame theorem.** Let the tensor $T$ have supported format $m\times d\times d$. Suppose mode one has a minor space whose Lie stabilizer has diagonal commutant as above. In its physical frame, denote the slices by $T_i$, with $0\le i<m$. Suppose $T_0$ is invertible and the ratios $B_i=T_iT_0^{-1}$ generate $M_d(\overline k)$ as a unital algebra. Then for any same-format $S$ geometrically nonisomorphic to $T$, the literal equations admit an ordinary PC refutation of degree $O((m+1)d^2+1)$, without field equations or extra variables. In particular this is cubic in the source's geometric border rank. The bound holds in every characteristic.

First handle support mismatches and transport the single frame, exactly as in Sections 2–3 with the commutant argument above. Their degree cost is $O(\max(m,d)+1)$. Fix a selector $e$ in the resulting degree-$O(m)$ partition and write $S_i$ for the slice on its target axis paired with axis $i$. Let $\lambda_i,\bar\lambda_i$ be the named branch scalar and reciprocal, and $Q,Q'$ and $R,R'$ the other mode maps and named inverses. The action gives the weighted equations $e(S_i-\lambda_i Q T_i R^{\mathsf T})=0$. If $S_0$ is singular, left-multiply by a nonzero constant row annihilating it, then cancel $\lambda_0,Q,T_0,R^{\mathsf T}$ on the appropriate sides to force this row times $e$ to be zero. If $S_0$ is invertible, put $C_i=S_iS_0^{-1}$ and $\rho_i=\lambda_0\bar\lambda_i$. The weighted action identities and reciprocal relations yield
$$
e\left(QB_iQ'-\rho_i C_i\right)=0.
\tag{4}
$$
For example, from the zero-indexed slice equation derive $e(S_0^{-1}-\bar\lambda_0(R')^{\mathsf T}T_0^{-1}Q')=0$, use it in the product $S_i S_0^{-1}$, and clear the branch scalar factor. These are constant-degree operations in addition to the selector.

The increasing spans of words in the $B_i$ of length at most $\ell$ stabilize only when they reach the full algebra. Indeed a span that equals the preceding span is stable under left multiplication by all generators and contains the identity. Thus there are $d^2$ basis words $w_j$ of length at most $d^2-1$, including the empty word. For every word $w$ of length at most $d^2$, (4) gives $e(QB_wQ'-\rho_w C_w)=0$, where $\rho_w$ is the product of the indicated $\rho_i$ and $\rho_{\varnothing}=1$. Telescope the word product, using $Q'Q=I$ between factors, at additive degree $O(|w|+1)$. All weighted derivations use only polynomial multiplications and differences of two expressions with the same selector. Whenever a calculation uses $eF=0$ to replace a factor $F$ after one expansion, the identity for that replacement follows by multiplying this line by the other factors. This avoids an extra selector in each step.

If the $C_{w_j}$ are dependent, choose a nonzero constant dependence vector. Multiply each weighted basis-word identity by the monomial for $\rho_{w_j}^{-1}$, cancel branch reciprocal factors and sum with those coefficients. Cancel $Q,Q'$ and read the coefficient of a word in the source basis with nonzero coefficient in the dependence. This forces $e$ times an invertible branch monomial to vanish, hence $e=0$. If the $C_{w_j}$ are independent, both sets are bases of the full matrix space. Write
$$
B_i B_{w_j}=\sum_h a_{ijh}B_{w_h},\qquad
C_i C_{w_j}=\sum_h b_{ijh}C_{w_h}.
$$
Compare weighted conjugation of the first identity with the second and extract coefficients in the target basis. It follows that
$$
e\left(b_{ijh}\rho_i\rho_{w_j}-a_{ijh}\rho_{w_h}\right)=0
\tag{5}
$$
in degree $O(m+d^2)$, apart from the initial frame transport certificates. A single zero coefficient gives $e=0$ by reciprocals, and two zero coefficients give no equation. The other equations are Laurent binomials with exponent $\ell_1$ norms at most $2d^2$ in $m$ coordinates.

If those binomials had a solution over the algebraic closure, send $B_{w_j}$ to $\rho_{w_j}C_{w_j}$ to define a linear isomorphism $\Theta:M_d\to M_d$. This maps the identity to the identity. Equations (5) say precisely $\Theta(B_i X)=\rho_i C_i\Theta(X)$ on basis vectors $X$, and so on all $X$. Iterating over generators and using that their words span proves multiplicativity. Every such matrix-algebra automorphism is inner. To see this directly, its images of the standard matrix units are matrix units as well. Pick a nonzero vector in the image of the first diagonal unit, and apply the images of the column-one matrix units to obtain a basis in which the images act as the original units. Thus there is an invertible $Q$ with $Q B_i Q^{-1}=\rho_i C_i$. The equation $R^{\mathsf T}=\lambda_0^{-1}T_0^{-1}Q^{-1}S_0$ then reconstructs an invertible third action, making $S_i=\lambda_i Q T_i R^{\mathsf T}$ for all $i$. This would be a tensor isomorphism, so the Laurent-binomial system cannot have a solution.

Apply Section 1 with the selector to obtain $e=0$ in degree $O((m+1)d^2+1)$. The partition makes the sum of the $e$'s equal to one, which proves the theorem.

**Sharper length bound.** More generally, if words of length at most $L$ span the ratio algebra, the proof gives degree $O((m+1)(L+1)+d)$. Only the word length and not the number of basis words enters the Laurent exponents. Šitov's matrix-algebra length theorem gives $L=O(d\log(d+1))$ over any field, improving the bound to $O((m+1)d\log(d+1)+1)$, or $O((r+1)^2\log(r+2))$ in source border rank $r$. [Šitov, *An improved bound for the length of matrix algebras*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.09310)

For an example with an irreducible slice algebra, take the pencil $z_0 I_d+\sum_{i=1}^d z_i E_{i,i+1}$, with the indices read cyclically, $d\ge3$, and characteristic not dividing $d$. Its determinant is $z_0^d+(-1)^{d-1}\prod_{i=1}^d z_i$. Each off-diagonal directional derivative produces a distinct monomial outside the determinant line with nonzero coefficient. The determinant-line Lie stabilizer is therefore the diagonal hyperplane $d A_{00}=\sum_{i=1}^d A_{ii}$. Its $d+1$ diagonal coordinate functions are pairwise distinct, so its commutant is the full diagonal algebra. The slice $T_0=I_d$ is invertible and products of the directed cycle units give every matrix unit. The supported format is $(d+1)\times d\times d$, with a diagonal frame in mode one and with the ratio algebra acting irreducibly on the other two modes.

## Scope

This is a written proof. The linear bound uses two intrinsic diagonal mode algebras recognized from determinants or minor spaces. A single frame and a full matrix algebra of slice ratios suffice for the $O((r+1)^2\log(r+2))$ bound. The raw equations supply the frame transporters and the remaining Laurent-binomial equations. The unrestricted polynomial degree clause of Question 7.1 remains open here.
