# Linear Polynomial Calculus degree from an independent rank-one slice frame

Let $k$ be any field. A geometric negative instance is a pair of explicit three-tensors that are inequivalent over an algebraic closure of $k$. Use the cubic tensor action equations and the quadratic two-sided inverse equations of Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She. No coordinate field equations are used. The rank bound below depends only on the rank of one tensor. [Question 7.1 and the literal encoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19320)

**Theorem.** Suppose one tensor of a geometric negative instance, in some choice of slice mode and after removing zero padding, has a basis of slices $a_i b_i^{\mathsf T}$, $1\le i\le r$, over an algebraic closure. Suppose that the algebra of infinitesimal linear transformations preserving the span of the slice pencil's $2\times2$ minors is exactly the diagonal matrix space in this basis. Then the instance has a Polynomial Calculus refutation of degree $O(r+1)$ over $k$. The same bound holds for Nullstellensatz degree. There is a sparse PC refutation of size $\operatorname{poly}(N)2^{O(r\log(r+1)+r\log(N+1))}$, where $N$ is the maximum ambient mode dimension. For already concise tensors this is $2^{O(r\log(r+1))}$. The other tensor is arbitrary. The source has both rank and border rank exactly $r$.

A sufficient condition for the hypothesis is that the $\binom r2$ tensors
$$
(a_i\wedge a_j)\otimes(b_i\wedge b_j),\qquad i<j,
$$
are linearly independent. This is the second-compound Khatri–Rao condition for a CP decomposition whose slice factor has full column rank. It includes the Kruskal inequality $k_A+k_B\ge r+2$, where the two numbers are the largest sizes of independent subsets guaranteed for the columns of the two factors respectively. This is a polynomial-degree case of the published question with unbounded mode sizes and with no restriction to sums of matrix multiplication tensors. [Domanov–De Lathauwer on the compound and Kruskal conditions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.4602)

## 1. Rank support and the intrinsic slice algebra

All identities below are polynomial certificates. An equality with a degree-$D$ certificate means that its difference is a linear combination of the literal axiom polynomials with each product of degree at most $D$. Such a certificate yields a PC derivation of the same degree by expanding and multiplying one variable at a time.

Compress to the three mode supports. A tensor of rank $r$ has support dimensions at most $r$. Independently choose bases of source and target taking those supports to initial coordinate blocks. These are constant invertible linear changes of the witness variables. For each mode, in the raw tensor action equation cancel the actions in the other modes using their named inverses. Restrict to the rows outside the target support. Then the forbidden block in the forward matrix times the supported source flattening is zero. The supported flattening has full row rank, so a constant right inverse yields the vanishing of the forbidden block. Apply the reverse action for the corresponding inverse block. The reverse action follows by applying the three inverses to the forward action and telescoping. This uses constant-degree certificates. The supported blocks of both inverse equations follow by omitting products with a forbidden block. If support dimensions differ, one obtains a pair of rectangular inverse equations. A minor of order one larger than the smaller dimension vanishes on the rectangular product by Cauchy–Binet and is one on the identity. This gives a refutation of degree $O(r+1)$ even if the target has much larger support. Otherwise both tensors have the same concise shape with all sides at most $r$. If a tensor is zero, one nonzero entry on the other side gives an immediate or reverse-action refutation.

Use the equivalent matrix-pencil convention
$$
S(Ax)=U T(x)V,
$$
with named inverse matrices $A',U',V'$. It follows from the original action tensor equality by inverses or transposes in the indicated modes and constant-degree cancellations. The reverse identity is $T(A'y)=U'S(y)V'$. In the source $T(x)=\sum_i x_i a_i b_i^{\mathsf T}$. All such slices are nonzero. Since the first flattening has rank $r$ and we have an $r$-term rank-one decomposition, both rank and border rank are $r$.

For a slice pencil $M$, write $Q_M$ for the span of its $2$-minor polynomials in the slice variable. For an operator $h$ on the slice space, let $D_h q$ be the coefficient of $t$ in $q(x+t h x)$. Define $E_M=\{h:D_h Q_M\subseteq Q_M\}$; it is the solution space of a constant linear system. Expansion of a minor of $T(x)$ gives exactly the terms $x_i x_j$ with coefficient vector $(a_i\wedge a_j)\otimes(b_i\wedge b_j)$. Independence of these vectors therefore makes $Q_T$ the span of all the mixed monomials. For $r\ge2$, the coefficient of $x_i^2$ in $D_h(x_i x_j)$ is $h_{ji}$ for $i\ne j$. It must be zero, and diagonal operators preserve the mixed monomials. This identifies $E_T$ with the full diagonal algebra, including in characteristic two. For $r=1$ every operator is already diagonal. For the Kruskal claim, fix $i,j$ and partition the other indices into sets of sizes at most $k_A-2,k_B-2$. On each wedge space a linear functional can annihilate every wedge with a column from its assigned set and take a nonzero value on the chosen pair. Indeed the pair is independent after quotienting by that assigned set. The product of the two functionals isolates the chosen wedge tensor among all the pairs.

## 2. Low-degree transport to the target, even when its slice algebra is initially unknown

Cauchy–Binet and both pencil equations transport the coefficient spaces of $2$-minors. More explicitly, modulo constant-degree certificates, $q_S(Ax)$ is a linear combination of a fixed basis of $Q_T$ with polynomial coefficients of constant degree in the witnesses, and $q_T(A'y)$ is a corresponding combination of $Q_S$. To obtain the certificates, compare $2$-minors entrywise by telescoping each product, apply the two-sided Cauchy–Binet formula, and extract the coefficients in the formal $x$ or $y$. The operation of extracting coefficients leaves an identity with the same bound in the witness degrees. The constant vectors used here to identify a basis of a space of quadrics can be incorporated by constant linear maps.

For $h\in E_T$, the derivation $D_h$ takes $q_S(Ax)$ into the polynomial span of $Q_T$ modulo these identities. Moreover
$$
(D_{A h A'}q_S)(y)=\bigl(D_h(q_S(Ax))\bigr)(A'y)
$$
modulo the inverse axioms. This follows by coefficient extraction in $t$ after $A(x+thx)$ is evaluated at $x=A'y$. Using the reverse transport of $Q_T$ shows that $A h A'$ lies in the polynomial extension of $E_S$ modulo constant-degree certificates. Namely, if $L$ is the constant linear system defining membership in $E_S$, fix a linear projection $\pi_S$ onto its kernel. There is a constant linear map $H$ with $I-\pi_S=HL$, by choosing a complement to the kernel. Thus the projected operator can replace the unprojected operator with the same degree bound. Likewise use $\pi_T$ in reverse. These projections give polynomial-coordinate maps
$$
\Phi:E_T\longrightarrow E_S,\quad h\longmapsto\pi_S(AhA'),\qquad
\Psi:E_S\longrightarrow E_T,\quad h'\longmapsto\pi_T(A'h'A).
$$
Their entries have degree at most two, and the maps are inverse modulo constant-degree certificates by the inverse axioms and the identities just proved.

If $\dim E_S\ne r$, a minor of order one larger than the smaller dimension contradicts one of these two inverse-map identities. Since $\dim E_T=r$, the required order is at most $r+1$, and the certificate has degree $O(r+1)$. Suppose the dimensions agree. For any constant $h_1,h_2\in E_S$, the product $h_1h_2$ equals $\Phi(\Psi(h_1)\Psi(h_2))$ modulo constant-degree certificates. This follows by expanding the conjugations and cancelling the inverse matrices, then substituting the certified projections. The product inside $\Phi$ lies in $E_T$ because the source is diagonal. If $h_1h_2\notin E_S$, a fixed separating linear functional yields a constant-degree contradiction. If the target algebra is not commutative, the same comparison with $\Phi(\Psi(h_2)\Psi(h_1))$ yields a contradiction. The identity matrix belongs to $E_S$ because $D_I q=2q$.

Now $E_S$ is a commutative unital algebra of dimension $r$. The linear equations for a separability element $e=\sum a_l\otimes b_l$ are
$$
\sum_l a_l b_l=1,\qquad
\sum_l c a_l\otimes b_l=\sum_l a_l\otimes b_l c\quad(c\in E_S).
$$
The source has the element $\sum_i E_{ii}\otimes E_{ii}$. Transporting its two factors by $\Phi$ satisfies the target equations modulo constant-degree certificates. In the linear equation for each constant basis element $c$, transport the source identity applied to $\Psi(c)$, expanding in its fixed source basis. If the target linear system is inconsistent, a constant inconsistent combination gives a refutation. If it is consistent, $E_S$ is geometrically a product of fields. For example, over the algebraic closure every module subspace that is a submodule has an algebra-linear projection obtained by sending a linear projection $p$ to $v\mapsto\sum a_l p(b_lv)$. Thus every submodule of the regular module splits, showing semisimplicity. The dimension $r$ gives $r$ primitive orthogonal projectors. Their images are nonzero since the algebra has a faithful action on the $r$-dimensional slice space, and the images sum directly to that space. Therefore each image is a line. Use a target slice basis in which the projectors are the diagonal coordinate projectors. All source and target basis choices can be made over a finite algebraic extension and subsequently descended by coefficientwise linear functional.

## 3. Branch on a permutation of the canonical lines

Write $E_i$ and $F_j$ for the coordinate projectors in these two bases. Let
$$
\Phi(E_i)=\sum_j f_{ji}F_j,\qquad
\Psi(F_j)=\sum_i g_{ij}E_i.
$$
The coefficients have degree at most two. Multiplicativity and the unit imply that within each row of $f$ the entries are idempotent, pairwise orthogonal, and sum to one. The same holds for $g$. The two descriptions are transposes because applying $\Psi$ to $F_j\Phi(E_i)=f_{ji}F_j$ and taking coordinate $i$ gives $g_{ij}=f_{ji}g_{ij}$. Applying $\Phi$ to $E_i\Psi(F_j)=g_{ij}E_i$ and taking coordinate $j$ likewise gives $f_{ji}=g_{ij}f_{ji}$. Consequently both rows and columns of $f$ have sums one and have pairwise orthogonal entries. Every identity here has a constant-degree certificate. We also have $A E_i A'=\sum_j f_{ji}F_j$ from the conjugation certificate.

Fix a permutation $\sigma$ and temporarily add $f_{\sigma(i),i}=1$ for every $i$. Orthogonality implies $f_{ji}=0$ when $j\ne\sigma(i)$ in column $i$. Multiply the transported-projector equality by $A$ on the right, cancel the inverse and apply to coordinate $i$. It gives $Ae_i=\lambda_i e'_{\sigma(i)}$, where $\lambda_i=A_{\sigma(i),i}$, with constant-degree certificates relative to the branch axioms. From $A'A=I$ it follows that $\nu_i\lambda_i=1$, with $\nu_i=A'_{i,\sigma(i)}$. The pencil equation now gives
$$
U a_i b_i^{\mathsf T} V=\lambda_i S_{\sigma(i)} .
$$
If one target slice on these lines has rank at least two, apply a nonzero $2$-minor there. The left-hand minor vanishes identically because it factors through a rank-one matrix, so the certificate gives $\lambda_i^2=0$; the named inverse $\nu_i$ gives a constant-degree branch refutation. A target slice cannot have rank zero since the target first flattening is concise.

Otherwise write $S_j=a'_j(b'_j)^{\mathsf T}$. Let $\mathcal U_\sigma$ be the linear space of matrices $B$ such that $B a_i$ lies on the line $k a'_{\sigma(i)}$ for all $i$, working in the field containing the two bases. Let $\mathcal V_\sigma$ be the linear space of matrices $C$ such that $C^{\mathsf T}b_i$ lies on $k b'_{\sigma(i)}$. The branch equations derive $U\in\mathcal U_\sigma$ and $V\in\mathcal V_\sigma$ with constant-degree certificates: multiply the pencil equality by $V'$, then by a constant covector annihilating $a'_{\sigma(i)}$ and a constant vector on which $b_i^{\mathsf T}$ is nonzero. For the other direction multiply by $U'$ and use the analogous constant tests on the other factor.

If both linear spaces contain invertible matrices over an algebraic closure, those two matrices send the original slice $i$ to an invertible scalar multiple of the target slice $\sigma(i)$. Taking these scalars for the monomial-mode entries produces an actual tensor isomorphism, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence one of the two linear spaces has identically zero determinant. Choose the linear projection onto that space along a constant complement. Its difference from the identity is a fixed linear combination of the relevant defining linear equations. Telescoping the determinant by columns shows that the determinant of the corresponding witness matrix vanishes with degree at most $O(r+1)$ under the branch equations. Telescoping the determinant of the matrix inverse equation shows that the determinant times the determinant of the named inverse is one with degree $O(r+1)$. This gives a branch Nullstellensatz refutation of degree $O(r+1)$.

## 4. Remove the branch axioms in linear degree

For the branch $\sigma$, let $p_\sigma=\prod_i f_{\sigma(i),i}$. Multiply its certificate of $1$ by $p_\sigma$. The effect of each branch axiom $f_{\sigma(i),i}-1$ is the ordinary certified idempotence $f_{\sigma(i),i}^2-f_{\sigma(i),i}$ multiplied by the other $r-1$ factors. Thus $p_\sigma=0$ has a certificate of degree $O(r+1)$ with respect to the literal equations. Expanding
$$
1=\prod_i\Bigl(\sum_j f_{ji}\Bigr)
$$
using the column sums, every choice with a repeated target row is zero by row orthogonality, and the remaining choices give exactly $\sum_\sigma p_\sigma$. The expansion and cancellations have degree $O(r+1)$. Substitution of the certificates for $p_\sigma=0$ completes the refutation. No bound on the number of branch choices other than $r!$ is needed for degree. Summing certificates for the same original axiom consolidates it to at most a polynomial number of multiplier polynomials. Counting all degree-$O(r+1)$ monomials in $O(N^2)$ witness variables gives the asserted sparse bound, including implementation of a multiplier by successive variable multiplications. A finite extension containing all constants suffices. Apply a $k$-linear functional from that extension to $k$ taking $1$ to $1$ coefficientwise to descend the identity, with no increase in degree.

## 5. Scope and comparison

For example take $T(x)$ with slices $a_i e_i^{\mathsf T}$, where the $a_i$ are nonzero, pairwise nonproportional, and span a space of dimension $d<r$. The compound vectors are independent because the wedges $e_i\wedge e_j$ distinguish the pairs and $a_i\wedge a_j\ne0$. Such source tensors can be outside the earlier class of geometric direct sums of rectangular matrix multiplication. In that earlier class the adjoint on the two matrix factors is separable and the pencil fills the whole corresponding module Hom space. Here the pair adjoint, for these slice matrices considered as maps to the $a_i$ space, has second component diagonal and consists of operators on the first space preserving each specified line. Choose the $a_i$ to contain a basis and the sum of all its basis vectors, with $d\ge2$. The only such first-space operators are scalars, so this adjoint is scalar; its Hom space has dimension $dr>r$. Thus the pencil is outside that earlier class. [Earlier direct-sum certificates](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs)

For positive characteristic fixed in advance, the order of the upper bound cannot be improved on this class: the older diagonal padded examples have two different support ranks of order $r$, satisfy the frame condition on their supports, and force PC degree $\Omega(r)$. The new argument extends the upper bound to the frame class and to arbitrary targets. It is a written proof, with provisional priority. The unrestricted polynomial-degree question is outside this theorem. [Earlier diagonal lower bound](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/927d262c8992bd305b625ad56ca25564555700f0/TensorSparsePCBounds/Proof.md)

## 6. A quadratic degree bound without an independent CP factor

**Theorem.** Let a geometric negative instance have one source tensor with a CP decomposition with $r\ge2$ terms, written
$$
T(x)=\sum_{i=1}^r \ell_i(x) a_i b_i^{\mathsf T}.
$$
Work geometrically for this hypothesis. Assume that one factor is in projective general position in its support. More explicitly, if the $\ell_i$ span a $K$-dimensional space, assume that any $K$ of them are independent. Assume the Kruskal inequality $k_A+k_B+K\ge 2r+2$. Then the literal TI instance has a PC and Nullstellensatz degree-$O(r^2)$ refutation, with no coordinate field equations and with arbitrary target. The Kruskal theorem makes $r$ the tensor rank. In particular this result allows all three factor matrices to have fewer than $r$ rows and none to have full column rank. [Kruskal's criterion and compound CP reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.2848)

If $K=r$, Section 1 applies. Otherwise $2\le K<r$. Put $d=r-K+1$, $m=d+1$, and compress source and target as before. The supported slice dimension is $K$. Indeed the $a_i\otimes b_i$ are independent by the same partition argument as for wedge products, now with one chosen index, because $k_A+k_B\ge r+1$. The source support dimensions are at most $r$, and differing dimensions are refuted as above.

### The finite configuration from the higher minors

The $m$-minors of $T(x)$ span the degree-$m$ polynomials $\prod_{i\in I}\ell_i(x)$ for all $m$-subsets $I$. Cauchy–Binet gives the coefficient vectors
$$
\left(\bigwedge_{i\in I}a_i\right)\otimes
\left(\bigwedge_{i\in I}b_i\right).
$$
They are independent. For a chosen $I$, partition the other indices into two subsets of sizes at most $k_A-m,k_B-m$. This is possible since $k_A+k_B\ge r+m$ by the hypothesis. After quotienting the two vector spaces by their respective assigned columns, the chosen wedge is nonzero on both sides, while every other pair of wedges has a zero side. This supplies the required separating linear functional. These are statements over fields of arbitrary characteristic.

For each $(K-1)$-subset $H$, choose $u_H\ne0$ in the common kernel of $(\ell_i)_{i\in H}$. This kernel is a line, and every other linear form is nonzero there, by projective general position. Define
$$
p_H=\prod_{i\notin H}\ell_i \quad\text{of degree }d.
$$
The evaluations $p_H(u_J)$ vanish for $H\ne J$ and are nonzero on the diagonal. There are $\binom{r}{K-1}$ such polynomials, precisely $\dim \operatorname{Sym}^d((k^K)^*)$, since $K+d-1=r$. They therefore form a basis of that space over the field in which the decomposition is defined. For $H\ne J$, the product $p_H p_J$ is divisible by an $m$-fold product of distinct linear forms $\ell_i$. Thus the product lies in the degree-$2d$ part of the ideal of $m$-minors.

For a pencil $M$, let $J_M$ be the ideal of its $m$-minors in its slice coordinates. Define $W_M=(\operatorname{Sym}^d((k^K)^*))^*$, the dual space of degree-$d$ slice forms. A vector $v$ in the original slice space gives the evaluation vector $\operatorname{ver}_d(v)$ in $W_M$. Multiplication of degree-$d$ forms identifies each quadric on $W_M$ with a degree-$2d$ form on the original space, by evaluation along $\operatorname{ver}_d$. Let $Q_M^*$ be the preimage of the degree-$2d$ part of $J_M$ under this linear multiplication map. For $T$, the coordinate forms $p_H$ on $W_T$ are a basis and $Q_T^*$ is exactly the span of their distinct mixed products. We already proved containment of the mixed products. The image of any quadric in $Q_T^*$ vanishes at each $u_H$, so its coefficients on $p_H^2$ must all vanish by the diagonal evaluations.

### Transport on the lifted space with degree bounded in the original variables

Let $\widehat A$ be the induced map of evaluation spaces, so that $\widehat A\operatorname{ver}_d(v)=\operatorname{ver}_d(Av)$. Its entries are polynomials of degree $d$ in $A$. Functoriality is a polynomial identity and, by telescoping products of degree $d$, the two inverse laws for $\widehat A,\widehat{A'}$ have certificates of degree $O(d+1)$ in the original equations.

The pencil action transports the $m$-minor spans, with certificates of degree $O(m+1)$, by Cauchy–Binet and telescoping the $m$ factors in each determinant term. Multiplying by the remaining forms of degree $2d-m$ gives transport on the degree-$2d$ pieces of the ideals, still in degree $O(r)$. The commutative square for multiplication of degree-$d$ forms under pullback then gives both inclusions between the pulled-back spaces $Q_T^*,Q_S^*$, with certificates of degree $O(r)$. For specificity, compute a fixed linear system for the preimage by composing the multiplication map with the quotient by the appropriate degree-$2d$ ideal piece, and apply the fixed projection method of Section 2.

On $W_T$, the infinitesimal stabilizer of $Q_T^*$ is again the diagonal algebra, with coordinate projectors onto the lines of $\operatorname{ver}_d(u_H)$. Apply the argument of Section 2 to the lifted witnesses and quadratic spaces. The quadratic covariance and all projection, product, commutativity, unit, inverse-map, and separability identities there have certificates of degree $O(r)$ in the original variables. Each uses a bounded number of products of witness entries, each now of degree $d$, as well as the just-certified transport of $Q_M^*$. Unlike a matrix dimension cutoff, the fact that the lifted spaces may have $\binom r{K-1}$ coordinates has no effect on these degrees. As before, if the target infinitesimal stabilizer is not a commutative separable unital algebra, one obtains a refutation of degree $O(r)$.

Suppose the target passes these tests. Its geometric primitive projectors $F_j$ have images whose dimensions may initially exceed one. Let $E_i$ denote the rank-one source projectors in the lifted basis. Write $\widehat A E_i\widehat {A'}=\sum_j f_{ji} F_j$ modulo certificates; the same formula applies with the projected conjugation replacing the raw one. In an adapted target basis, if $\operatorname{rank}F_j\ge2$, a $2\times2$ minor within its block gives $f_{ji}^2=0$ for every $i$, since the left matrix factors through a line. The idempotence relation gives $f_{ji}=0$, contradicting that row's sum one. Thus all target projectors have rank one. They sum to the identity on the lifted space, so their number is exactly the source dimension. This proves the needed dimension equality without an exponentially large determinant. The argument of Section 3 also supplies $g=f^{\mathsf T}$ and the orthogonality and sum axioms, now with degrees $O(r)$ and $\deg f_{ji}\le2d$.

### Pin only a polynomial number of projective directions

Choose $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_K$ as a basis of the linear forms. The dual coordinate axes are among the lines of $u_H$. Choose one more column $\ell_{K+1}$. All its coordinates in this basis are nonzero because every $K$-subset is independent. For each $j=2,\ldots,K$, the intersection using $\ell_{K+1}$ and all the basis forms other than $\ell_1,\ell_j$ gives another line $u_H$ supported on dual coordinates $1,j$, with both coefficients nonzero. These $2K-1$ lines have the following property: every endomorphism carrying each line to itself in the sense of containment is scalar. The axes force it to be diagonal and the other lines make all diagonal entries equal. Use only the lifted projectors for these $2K-1$ lines as anchors.

Branch on the assignment of images of just these anchors to target lifted projector lines. Under the equations $f_{ji}=1$ for the chosen assignments, the lifted conjugation and the inverse law imply $\widehat A\operatorname{ver}_d(u_i)=\lambda_i w'_j$ for a nonzero fixed vector $w'_j$ on the target line and a polynomial $\lambda_i$ that has a named polynomial inverse of degree $d$, modulo degree-$O(r)$ branch certificates. Here the inverse is the appropriate entry of $\widehat{A'}$ in the two line bases, with a constant scaling from $\operatorname{ver}_d(u_i)$.

A lifted line $k w'_j$ is a line of evaluations if and only if its quadratic monomial-product relations hold. To see this, write $z_\alpha$ for evaluation coordinates indexed by monomials of degree $d$ in $K$ variables. These quadrics are $z_\alpha z_\beta-z_\gamma z_\delta$ with $\alpha+\beta=\gamma+\delta$. Any two products of the same number of variables $z_\alpha$ with the same total multidegree agree under these relations: swapping one original variable occurrence between any two degree-$d$ groups is one of the quadratic relations and these swaps connect all partitions of the same occurrences. In particular $z_\alpha^d=\prod_i z_{d e_i}^{\alpha_i}$. A nonzero vector satisfying the quadrics has a nonzero pure coordinate, say $z_{d e_h}$. The same swap argument gives $z_\alpha z_{d e_h}^{d-1}=\prod_i z_{(d-1)e_h+e_i}^{\alpha_i}$. Dividing by the nonzero coordinate gives $z$ proportional to the evaluation of the vector with coordinates $z_{(d-1)e_h+e_i}/z_{d e_h}$. This reasoning works even when the characteristic divides $d$.

If an assigned $w'_j$ violates one such quadric, its difference evaluates to a nonzero constant times $\lambda_i^2$ on the branch, whereas the evaluation vector $\widehat A\operatorname{ver}_d(u_i)=\operatorname{ver}_d(Au_i)$ satisfies all these quadrics identically. The inverse of $\lambda_i$ gives a degree-$O(r)$ branch refutation. Otherwise choose a vector $v'_j$ with $\operatorname{ver}_d(v'_j)$ spanning $k w'_j$, absorbing their constant proportionality into $\lambda_i$. Choose $h$ with $(v'_j)_h\ne0$. The equality on the pure coordinate gives
$$
(Au_i)_h^d=\lambda_i(v'_j)_h^d.
$$
This certifies a polynomial inverse for $(Au_i)_h$, by multiplying its $(d-1)$-st power by the inverse of the right side. Evaluating on $X_s X_h^{d-1}$ for each $s$ and using that inverse gives the linear equations $Au_i\in k v'_j$, with certificates of degree $O(r)$.

In an anchor branch let $\mathcal A$ be the linear space of matrices satisfying all these line conditions. If $\mathcal A$ consists of singular matrices, telescope its determinant against a fixed linear projection onto $\mathcal A$ and use the inverse axiom of $A$ to refute in degree $O(r)$. Otherwise choose $A_0\in\mathcal A$ invertible over an algebraic closure. Composing every candidate on the left with $A_0^{-1}$ fixes all source anchor lines. Thus $\mathcal A=k A_0$, and the branch derives $A=\mu A_0$ for a linear function $\mu$ of $A$. The original inverse equations also give a linear function of $A'$ serving as the inverse of $\mu$, by looking at a diagonal entry of $\mu A_0 A'=I$.

The pencil equation is now $U T(x)V=\mu S(A_0x)$. Cancel $V$ on the right to obtain $U T(x)=S(A_0x)(\mu V')$. The pairs $(D,E)$ satisfying $D T(x)=S(A_0x)E$ form a fixed linear space. If it contained a pair with both matrices invertible, $A_0,D,E^{-1}$ would give a genuine isomorphism. Therefore $\det D\det E$ vanishes as a polynomial on the space. Use a constant linear projection onto it and telescope the determinant product, substituting $(D,E)=(U,\mu V')$. Both determinants are certified units by the inverse equations of $U,V$ and the certified inverse of $\mu$. This finishes the branch in degree $O(r)$. All geometric constants just used can be taken in a finite algebraic extension of the original coefficient field.

Finally expand the product of the column sums over only the anchor columns. Repeated target indices are killed by row orthogonality. For every surviving product, multiply the corresponding branch refutation by it. As in Section 4 each branch axiom is discharged by idempotence. The degree of that product is at most $2d(2K-1)=O(r^2)$, so the assembled degree is $O(r^2)$. Descend coefficients as before. This is a sufficient class beyond the independent-frame case, while the full polynomial bound of Question 7.1 remains open.

## 7. The classical Kruskal class

**Theorem.** Suppose that one tensor of a geometric negative instance has a rank-$r$ CP decomposition satisfying the classical Kruskal inequality
$$
k_A+k_B+k_C\ge2r+2.
$$
No general-position assumption on any full support is required. Then the literal TI system has a PC and Nullstellensatz refutation of degree $O(r^2)$ over every field, without coordinate field equations and against an arbitrary target. The class and the inequality are taken over the algebraic closure. The output is a proof-degree statement and does not require computing the decomposition efficiently.

Here is a proof in which all lifted-space comparisons use a bounded number of products of witness matrices. The sizes of the lifted spaces affect only the number of linear combinations, not the degree.

### The canonical derivative space of the minor ideal

Choose the largest of the three Kruskal ranks as the slice factor, and denote it by $k_0$. Write $K$ for the dimension of its span. The Kruskal inequality gives $k_0\ge (2r+2)/3>r/2$. For $r=1$ use Section 1. Otherwise put $d=r-k_0+1$, $m=d+1$. Then $m\le k_0$, because $r\ge2$. The supported slice dimension of $T$ is $K$: the $a_i\otimes b_i$ are independent by the one-index partition argument. If support sizes differ from the target, use Section 1.

The same compound proof as in Section 6 applies, now using $k_0$ instead of $K$. The $m$-minor span is exactly the span of all $m$-fold products of distinct $\ell_i$, since $k_A+k_B\ge r+m$. The linear span of their directional derivatives is exactly the space $P_T$ spanned by all distinct $d$-fold products $p_H=\prod_{i\notin H}\ell_i$ with $|H|=k_0-1$. To see the reverse containment, extend a chosen $d$-subset by one more index. The resulting $m$ forms are independent. Choose a direction annihilating the $d$ original forms and evaluating to one on the extra form. The derivative in this direction is exactly the chosen product, in every characteristic.

Although $P_T$ need not be the whole degree-$d$ space, the products $p_H$ are independent. On the common kernel of $(\ell_i)_{i\in H}$, each other $\ell_i$ is nonzero as a linear form by the Kruskal rank. Over the algebraic closure choose a vector $u_H$ there avoiding the finitely many other zero hyperplanes. Then $p_J(u_H)$ is nonzero exactly for $J=H$. Also $p_H p_J$ for $H\ne J$ is divisible by an $m$-fold product.

For both pencils let $P_M$ mean the derivative space of the $m$-minor span, and $W_M=P_M^*$. Use the preimage of the degree-$2d$ ideal of $m$-minors along $\operatorname{Sym}^2(P_M)\to \operatorname{Sym}^{2d}((k^K)^*)$ as the intrinsic quadric space. For $T$ this is precisely the span of all distinct mixed products in the basis $p_H$, by the same evaluations as Section 6. Both spaces $P_M$ are intrinsic. Pullback by $A$ transports the minor spans as before. For each constant direction $v$ use the coefficient identity
$$
(D_v q)(Ax)=D_{A'v}(q(Ax))
$$
modulo $AA'=I$ to transport their derivative spans. Choose linear projections to $P_T,P_S$ from the degree-$d$ forms. The projected pullbacks and their dual maps have polynomial entries of degree $d$. They have two-sided rectangular inverse identities, from the original inverse laws and degree-$O(r)$ projection certificates. Their actions on the two intrinsic quadric spaces then have certificates of degree $O(r)$, using multiplication and the two degree-$2d$ ideal pieces exactly as above.

Denote the induced map on the dual spaces by $\widehat A:W_T\to W_S$. It sends an evaluation at $x$ to evaluation at $Ax$, modulo the derivative-space projection identities. If $W_S=0$, the inverse identity on the nonzero $W_T$ already refutes the system in degree $O(r)$. Transport the diagonal infinitesimal algebra from $W_T$ as in Section 6. We do not need a large determinant even if the two lifted spaces initially have unequal dimensions. All projection, inverse-map, unit, multiplicativity and separability arguments in Section 2 are valid for rectangular two-sided inverses. If the target algebra passes, its primitive projector of rank greater than one gives a direct contradiction as before by $2$-minors of the images of rank-one source projectors. Otherwise all projectors have rank one. Their numbers need not be compared: the source-to-target projector coefficients $f_{ji}$ still have orthogonal rows and columns summing to one, by the two inverse-map identities and the equalities $g_{ij}=f_{ji}$ from Section 3. Their degrees are at most $2d$.

Choose at most $r$ distinct products $p_H$ that together contain every original factor $\ell_i$, and assign one of those products to each factor it contains so that every factor has one assigned product. Branch on the $f$-selectors for only these chosen products, using column sums and row orthogonality. Each assignment pins the corresponding projector. In target dual coordinates write $p'_j\in P_S$ for the coordinate form dual to the chosen target lifted projector line. The equality $F_j\widehat A=\widehat A E_i$ shows that the coordinate form $p'_j$ pulls back to a scalar multiple of $p_H$:
$$
p'_j(Ax)=\lambda p_H(x).
\tag{7.1}
$$
Here $\lambda$ is a degree-$d$ polynomial with a degree-$d$ inverse given by an entry of the reverse lifted map. The coordinate equality in (7.1), including the substitution in the initial nonprojected pullback, has certificates of degree $O(r)$. We keep the outer branch axioms available in the next steps.

### Recover one factor line inside each pinned product

The $d$ original factors of $p_H$ are independent since $d< m\le k_0$. Its directional derivative kernel is their common kernel, as is seen by making the factors the first $d$ coordinate forms. For a fixed $p'_j$, define its derivative kernel by the linear map of first directional derivatives, even if $p'_j$ is initially unrelated to a product. Differentiating (7.1) in constant directions and composing with $A'$ gives $A$ mapping the source derivative kernel into the target one, with certificates of degree $O(r)$. Use the reverse equality obtained from (7.1) and its certified inverse scalar to get the reverse containment for $A'$. Passing to the quotients gives two-sided inverse laws for their induced witness blocks, whose entries are linear in the original witnesses. If the quotient dimensions differ, a rectangular minor of order at most $K+1$, together with the degree-$O(r)$ identities for the block inverse laws, gives a branch refutation of degree $O(r)$.

If the dimensions agree, the quotient on either side has dimension $d$. We can assume $p'_j$ itself factors through this quotient. Here is a certificate for the alternative, important in positive characteristic. Choose the constant projection $\pi'$ onto a complement of the target derivative kernel. Since $A'$ carries that kernel into the source kernel, each original factor $\ell_i(A'y)$ occurring in $p_H(A'y)$ is unchanged on replacing $y$ by $\pi'y$, modulo the certified containment equations. Telescoping their product, multiplying by $\lambda$, and using the inverse equality from (7.1) gives $p'_j(y)=p'_j(\pi'y)$ with degree $O(r)$. If a coefficient of the constant difference is nonzero there is a refutation. Otherwise use the actual quotient polynomials.

On the $d$ source quotient variables, $p_H$ is the coordinate monomial $z_1\cdots z_d$. Its infinitesimal line stabilizer, defined by $D_h p_H\in k p_H$, is exactly the diagonal matrix space in every characteristic. A nondiagonal entry produces a different monomial with one missing coordinate and one squared coordinate, and its coefficient must vanish. For $d=1$ the operator space is already diagonal. Transport this stabilizer to the target quotient using (7.1), its inverse, and the differentiation and substitution argument of Section 2, now for a polynomial line rather than a quadratic space. The ambient degree of the form is $d$, the quotient witness entries are linear, and only a bounded number of differentiated/substituted identities are used, so the certificates have degree $O(r)$. For example
$$
(D_{\bar A h\bar A'}p'_j)(y)=D_h[p'_j(\bar A x)](\bar A' y)
=\lambda (D_h p_H)(\bar A' y)
$$
modulo the established branch equations, where bars denote the quotient blocks. If $D_h p_H=c p_H$, the right side is $c p'_j(y)$. The reverse uses the certified inverse of $\lambda$. Use fixed projections to compute the induced polynomial maps on operator coordinates.

As before, transfer the algebra closure, commutativity, and separability equations. Those give immediate branch refutations if they fail. For any target primitive projector of rank at least two, use the $2$-minor and row sum argument, giving another branch refutation. In the remaining case the rank-one projectors give selector coefficients with degree at most two in the original witness entries, with all idempotence, sums and orthogonality certificates of degree $O(r)$.

For each original $\ell_i$ assigned to this product, pin just its projector in this second-stage selector matrix. In an inner branch, if $F'_j$ is the selected target projector, the identity $F'_j\bar A=\bar A E_i$ gives proportionality of the pullback of its corresponding target coordinate functional to $\ell_i$. To see this, take row $j$, since $\ell_i$ is the coordinate covector of the source projector $E_i$. Composing the functionals with the quotient maps gives certified linear conditions on the original $A$, one for each source $\ell_i$. The membership certificates have degree $O(r)$ in the two levels of branch axioms.

### Remaining scalars and labelled linear maps

Let $\mathcal A$ be the fixed linear space of candidate slice changes satisfying the resulting $r$ proportionality conditions. If there is no invertible element, refute the inner branch by the determinant of $A$ as above. Otherwise choose an invertible $A_0\in\mathcal A$. Each condition can then be rewritten as $\ell_i A_0^{-1}A\in k\ell_i$, since the corresponding target form composed with $A_0$ is a nonzero multiple of $\ell_i$. These are conditions on the primal slice matrix $A_0^{-1}A$, and the $\ell_i$ here are its covectors.

Choose a basis from the $\ell_i$. A matrix fixing all basis lines on the dual is diagonal. Each remaining form forces equality of diagonal entries across the basis coordinates on which it is supported. Form the graph with basis coordinates as vertices, joining two if some remaining form is supported on both. There is at most one nonsingleton component. Indeed a remaining form supported on $s$ basis coordinates gives a dependent set of $s+1$ columns, so $s\ge k_0$. Every nonsingleton component has such a form. Two of them would have at least $2k_0>r$ vertices, while the basis contains at most $r$. A singleton component contains only its own basis form among the columns, because proportional columns are excluded by $k_0\ge2$.

If there is no nonsingleton component, all the forms are a basis and Section 1 applies. Otherwise use the fixed primal projectors $E_D$ onto the nonsingleton component and $E_s$ onto the singleton components. The linear branch conditions therefore give
$$
A=A_0\left(\mu E_D+\sum_s\lambda_s E_s\right).
$$
Each scalar has a polynomial inverse of degree one modulo the branch equations. Use a diagonal coordinate of $A_0^{-1}(AA')A_0=I$ in its component.

Put $H=\mu V'$. On $x$ in the range of $E_D$, the branch equations give $U T(x)=S(A_0x)H$. On a singleton coordinate direction $e_s$ the source slice is $a_s b_s^{\mathsf T}$ and $U T(e_s)V=\lambda_s S(A_0 e_s)$. If this target slice has rank at least two, its nonzero $2$-minor and the scalar inverse give a branch refutation. If it is zero, cancel $U,V$ to contradict the nonzero source slice. In the remaining case write it as $a'_s(b'_s)^{\mathsf T}$. The branch implies $U a_s\in k a'_s$ and $H^{\mathsf T} b'_s\in k b_s$. For the first multiply on the right by $V'$ and test with a covector killing $a'_s$. For the second use the same cancellation, test with a constant covector taking a nonzero value on $a'_s$, and cancel the scalar $\lambda_s$ using its inverse. Then multiply by $\mu$. All these identities have certificates of degree $O(r)$ or better.

The conditions on the pair $(U,H)$ are linear with fixed coefficients. If two invertible matrices satisfied them, choose $\mu=1$, $V=H^{-1}$ and the singleton scalars that match their rank-one slice products. The singleton scalars are freely chosen and nonzero. Together with $A_0$ this gives an actual tensor isomorphism. Hence the product of the two determinants is identically zero on the fixed linear solution space. Compare it with its value on the projected variables $(U,\mu V')$ by telescoping. The degree is $O(r)$ and both determinants have explicit certified inverses by the original inverse laws and the branch inverse of $\mu$. This refutes the inner branch.

The inner branch selects at most $r$ individual factors, so multiply by products of at most $r$ second-stage coefficients of degree at most two. Discharge the inner branches by their idempotence and column sums while retaining the first-stage branch axioms. This has degree $O(r)$. The outer branch selects at most $r$ products using coefficients of degree at most $2d$, so discharge it in total degree $O(r^2)$, by the same mechanism. This also covers a branch that was already refuted at any earlier step. Descend coefficients to the original field by the linear-functional argument. The proof covers the classical Kruskal class and does not assume the full-column-rank hypothesis on any one factor.
