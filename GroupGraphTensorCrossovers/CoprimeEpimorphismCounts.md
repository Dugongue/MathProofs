# Counting epimorphisms by colored code equivalence

**Theorem.** Let $G$ and $H$ be finite groups given by Cayley tables. Suppose $G$ is a semidirect product of a direct product of elementary abelian normal Hall Sylow subgroups by an elementary abelian group at a different prime $q$. There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm that counts all epimorphisms $G\to H$ and produces one when the count is positive. The target is arbitrary. The algorithm uses colored code equivalence and preserves the requirement of one common quotient map across all Hall Sylows.

Grochow, Srivastava, and Thakkar establish polynomial decision bounds for these classes in Corollary 1 and Theorem 4. The proof below gives explicit accounting for the selected columns and a single quotient map, and gives the exact count and a witness. It addresses this class of their counting question, Open Question 2. [Algorithms for Finite Group Epimorphism Testing, §5.2 and §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S5.SS2)

## 1. Quotient maps and weight multiplicities

Begin by returning count zero unless $|H|$ divides $|G|$. Fix a decomposition $G=N_G\rtimes E_G$, with $E_G$ elementary abelian at $q$ and $N_G$ a product of elementary abelian groups at primes $p\ne q$. It is enough for $H$ to admit the same type of decomposition $H=N_H\rtimes E_H$, allowing trivial factors, since this property is preserved under quotients. These decompositions are computable from the tables. For a chosen $q$, the normal Hall subgroup must be the set of elements of order prime to $q$. Check closure and the requisite abelian Sylows, and check that the quotient is elementary abelian at $q$. Try the prime divisors of $|G|$, and handle the case $G=N_G$ by the elementary abelian direct product algorithm, after checking that $H$ is also such a product. For a valid choice on $G$, any quotient must satisfy the corresponding test for $H$.

One can obtain an actual splitting without a subgroup search. A section $s:E\to G$ gives a conjugation action on $N$, independent of the section, and a cocycle $c(x,y)$ using additive notation in $N$. The function $b(x)=|E|^{-1}\sum_z c(x,z)$ obeys
$$
c(x,y)=b(x)+x\cdot b(y)-b(xy).
$$
This follows by summing the cocycle equation over $z$. The division is well defined by coprimality. Adjusting the section by $-b(x)$ gives a complement. Quotients, orders, and elementary abelian coordinates can all be built by polynomially many table operations.

Every epimorphism is determined by a surjection $\phi:E_G\to E_H$, a surjective intertwiner $\rho:N_G\to N_H$ for the action pulled back through $\phi$, and a cocycle $u:E_G\to N_H$ for that action. Indeed the normal Hall subgroup maps into the target Hall subgroup by element orders. The induced quotient map is onto. An element of the target Hall subgroup lifted to $G$ can have quotient coordinate only in $\ker\phi$. In the split source, an element of the complement has $q$-power order, so if it maps into the Hall subgroup it maps to the identity. Thus $\rho$ is onto. Conversely these surjectivities and the two compatibility equations give an epimorphism by the semidirect product law.

For each prime $p\ne q$ of the source normal Hall subgroup, extend scalars on the $p$-Sylow modules to a field containing the $q$-th roots of unity. If the source action on that block is trivial, only the zero weight occurs. A nontrivial target action on this block then rules out an epimorphism. Otherwise use $L_p=\mathbb F_{p^e}$ with $e=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)$, and pick a root $\zeta_p$ of order $q$. A weight $\chi\in E^*$ denotes the one-dimensional character $x\mapsto\zeta_p^{\chi(x)}$. The actions are simultaneously diagonalizable because the commuting matrices satisfy the squarefree polynomial $X^q-1$. Record each weight as a column of a matrix, with the number of repetitions equal to the simultaneous eigenspace dimension. Color the columns by $p$.

These multisets are explicitly computable in polynomial time in the table orders. A nonzero weight appears with its full Frobenius orbit of length $e$, so when the block is nontrivial the dimension $a_p$ of the source block is at least $e$. Thus $p^e\le |N_G|$. One may find an irreducible degree-$e$ polynomial by exhaustive enumeration and the finite-field irreducibility test, then find the primitive root by scanning the resulting field. Enumerate all members of $E^*$, at most $|E|$ possibilities, and solve the simultaneous eigenvector equations on a basis of $E$.

For a given onto $\phi$, the dual injection $\theta=\phi^*:E_H^*\to E_G^*$ carries the target weights to the weights of the pulled-back target module. Consequently a surjective $\rho$ exists precisely when, separately at each color, the image multiset lies inside the source multiset. This criterion descends to the prime-field modules. Each Frobenius orbit of nonzero characters is a simple module of dimension $e$ whose endomorphism field is $L_p$, and the zero character is a simple module with endomorphism field $\mathbb F_p$. This follows by taking the corresponding simple components of the commutative semisimple group algebra of the elementary abelian complement. On each component the intertwiner is just a matrix over that field, possibly rectangular.

## 2. Finding and counting the common dual injections

Enumerate all subsets of the source column positions with the correct cardinality in each color, and deduplicate the resulting colored multisets. The number of subsets is at most $2^{\sum_p a_p}\le |N_G|$. A candidate multiset $B$ is retained exactly when it equals the target multiset under some linear injection $\theta$ into $E_G^*$.

Let $r$ be the dimension of the span of all target columns, using all colors together and one common coordinate space. Reject a candidate if its column span has a different dimension. A color-preserving matching of the columns induces a linear isomorphism of the two spans precisely when it preserves every linear dependence. Equivalently the matching carries the row space of the target column matrix to the row space of the selected source matrix. This is colored coordinate-permutation code equivalence. Use disjoint copies of the alphabet $\mathbb F_q$ for the colors and trivial symbol actions in the twisted-code algorithm of Grochow, Johnson, and Levet. The code length is at most $\sum_p a_p\le \log_2|G|$. Each code can be provided explicitly, since its dimension $r$ is at most $\dim E_H^*$, so it has at most $|E_H|$ words. Theorem 4.3 gives the transporter coset by a representative and subgroup generators in polynomial time in a bound for these table orders. Its subgroup order is computable by the permutation-group order algorithm. [Grochow–Johnson–Levet, Theorem 4.3 and §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19777v1#S4)

If the transporter exists, divide its cardinality by $\prod_{p,x}m_{p,x}!$, where $m_{p,x}$ is the multiplicity of the exact target column $x$ at color $p$. Indeed a transporter determines the linear map on the span uniquely. For any such span map, the remaining choices of matching are the independent bijections among copies of each column of the same color. Thus the quotient is exactly the number of allowable span maps.

Write $\ell_G=\dim E_G$, $\ell_H=\dim E_H$. A span map extends to an injection on all of $E_H^*$ in exactly
$$
\prod_{j=r}^{\ell_H-1}(q^{\ell_G}-q^j)
$$
ways if $\ell_H\le\ell_G$, and in zero ways otherwise. Choose basis extensions to see this. Different selected colored multisets are disjoint cases, since the injection determines its selected image multiset. This counts exactly all compatible dual injections, retaining the count for each image multiset $B$. A transporter representative and a basis extension also construct an injection in any nonempty case.

## 3. Counting and constructing the full homomorphisms

Fix one compatible $\theta$ and its associated image profile $B$. At a source irreducible component with endomorphism field of size $Q$, let $a$ be the source multiplicity and $b$ the desired target multiplicity, as read from $B$ on this Frobenius orbit. The number of local surjective module maps is
$$
\prod_{j=0}^{b-1}(Q^a-Q^j).
$$
For the zero weight use the field of size $p$. The product of the local counts is independent of the particular $\theta$ within the same profile. One can construct a surjective module map by splitting into the simultaneous primary components and using bases over their endomorphism fields. These bases and their action matrices can be computed by linear algebra: on a nonzero Frobenius orbit choose one group generator whose weight is nonzero. Its action generates the endomorphism field on that component. Use the corresponding irreducible factor of $X^q-1$ to project onto that eigentype and intersect with the simultaneous type for the other generators. Equivalently one can take over $L_p$ the sum of the simultaneous eigenspaces in the entire orbit and descend that Frobenius-stable subspace by row reduction. Its Frobenius-stable reduced row echelon basis lies in the prime field. In either construction the source and pulled-back target component have the same field action, so a coordinate projection gives the desired module map.

The complement cocycle choices contribute $|N_H|/|N_H^{E_H}|$ for every $\phi$. To verify this, in a coprime action a cocycle $u$ satisfies $u(x)=v-x\cdot v$ with $v=|E_G|^{-1}\sum_y u(y)$, by averaging the equation $u(xy)=u(x)+x\cdot u(y)$. Two such $v$ give the same cocycle exactly when they differ by a fixed vector. Surjectivity of $\phi$ identifies invariants under $E_G$ with invariants under $E_H$. A witness can use the zero cocycle in the computed splittings.

Multiply the dual-injection count, the module-map count and the cocycle count for each profile $B$, then sum over the distinct profiles. Each epimorphism appears once. All exponents and products have polynomial bit length, since the answer is at most the number of functions between the group tables. The completely abelian case handled initially uses the same surjective-matrix formula independently on each Sylow. The construction and count therefore have deterministic polynomial bit complexity. This is an informal proof.
