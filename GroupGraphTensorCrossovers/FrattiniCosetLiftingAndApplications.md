# Frattini lifting under bounded composition factors

Write $\mathcal C_b$ for the finite groups whose nonabelian composition factors have order at most a fixed constant $b$. This class is closed under subgroups, quotients, and extensions. For subgroups, intersect a composition series with the subgroup and refine it. A nonabelian factor comes from a subgroup of one of the bounded-order factors. Every group in $\mathcal C_b$ belongs to Luks and Miyazaki's $\Gamma_b$, since each such nonabelian factor embeds in the symmetric group of degree $b$.

**Theorem 1.** Given Cayley-table groups $G,H$, an isomorphism $\alpha_0:G/\Phi(G)\to H/\Phi(H)$, and permutation generators for $P\le\operatorname{Aut}(G/\Phi(G))$ in $\mathcal C_b$, one can compute the coset of all isomorphisms inducing a map in $\alpha_0P$ in deterministic polynomial time. A nonempty output gives a representative, generators for the source automorphism group of the coset, and its exact cardinality.

**Theorem 2.** Fix $b$. There are deterministic polynomial-time decision, search, counting, and automorphism-generator algorithms for Cayley-table groups whose Frattini quotient has no nontrivial abelian normal subgroup and whose Frattini quotient's socle has each nonabelian simple isomorphism type at most $b$ times. The Frattini kernel is arbitrary.

**Theorem 3.** Fix $b,r$. The same algorithms work for Cayley-table groups for which, writing $Q=G/\Phi(G)$ and $V=F(Q)$ for its Fitting subgroup, the group $Q/V$ is at most $r$-generated and every simple module in each primary component of $V$ has multiplicity at most $b$ under conjugation by $Q/V$.

The coset theorem extends the companion fixed-map Frattini lifting algorithm. The second theorem lifts the classical socle-normalizer approach through an arbitrary Frattini kernel. The third uses the multiplicities of the Frattini quotient and overlaps known abelian-kernel techniques on that quotient. [Cannon–Holt](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(02)00133-5) · [Babai–Codenotti–Qiao, ICALP 2012](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/papers/icalp12.pdf) · [Skresanov](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15497v2)

## 1. Coset lifting through characteristic layers

First compute the Frattini subgroup from the table. For every prime $p$, compute $W=\Omega_1(Z(O_p(G)))$, an elementary abelian normal subgroup. In the quotient $G/W$, push the extension class forward by every module map from $W$ to the semisimple regular module. The intersection of the kernels of maps giving zero cohomology class is $W\cap\Phi(G)$, by the correspondence between split simple quotients of $W$ and maximal supplements. Precisely, if a maximal subgroup $M$ misses $w\in W$, then $G=WM$, the quotient $W/(W\cap M)$ is simple and its pushed-out extension splits. Conversely a zero-class map nonzero on $w$ projects onto a simple summand nonzero on $w$. A complement in that pushed-out extension pulls back to a maximal subgroup missing $w$. The zero-class maps are computed by a joint linear system for the module map and the coboundary cochain. Iterate by quotienting by a nonzero $W\cap\Phi(G)$ until none remains. If the remaining $\Phi(G)$ is nontrivial, its nilpotence gives a minimal normal elementary abelian $p$-subgroup inside it, which lies in $O_p(G)$ and is centralized by $O_p(G)$ by the fixed-point property of $p$-groups on simple modules in characteristic $p$. It would lie in the corresponding $W$, a contradiction. The algorithm finds all normal $p$-cores by retaining the elements whose normal closures have $p$-power order, and the calculation uses $\Phi(G/N)=\Phi(G)/N$ for each $N\le\Phi(G)$. The Jacobson radicals of the relevant group algebras are computed by Friedl–Rónyai. [Friedl–Rónyai](https://www.cs.uoregon.edu/Reports/TR-1985-003.pdf)

Build the characteristic series from $\Phi(G)$ to $1$ by processing each Sylow $p$-subgroup and recursively taking its own Frattini subgroup. Refine each resulting elementary abelian section by the powers of the Jacobson radical of $\mathbb F_p[G]$ until all layers are semisimple. The series is canonical under isomorphism by processing primes in numeric order, and incompatible layer sizes and primes can be rejected. At a layer with quotient $Q=G/L_i$ and kernel $E=L_i/L_{i+1}$ of characteristic $p$, the kernel lies in the Frattini subgroup of the extension. With extension class $c\in H^2(Q,E)$ and $R=\mathbb F_p[Q]/\operatorname{rad}(\mathbb F_p[Q])$, use the subspace
$$
 X(c)=\{f_*c:f\in\operatorname{Hom}_Q(E,R)\}\subseteq H^2(Q,R).
$$

For a simple $Q$-module $S$, any nonzero $f:E\to S$ pushes the class to a nonzero class. Otherwise the quotient by $\ker(f)$ would split with a nontrivial Frattini kernel. Decompose $R$ into copies of every simple module. For each $S$, equality of the displayed subspaces yields equal injective images of the maps $f\mapsto f_*c$ into $H^2(Q,S)$. These identifications are linear over the endomorphism division ring of $S$ and induce isomorphisms of the corresponding isotypic parts of the two kernels. Explicitly, maps from an isotypic part to $S$ are rows over this division ring, and an invertible linear transformation of rows acts by precomposition by a kernel isomorphism. Together the isotypic isomorphisms give a module isomorphism carrying one class to the other. Conversely any such isomorphism gives equality. The same injectivity ensures that an extension automorphism inducing the identity on $Q$ acts trivially on $E$. The automorphisms over the quotient identity are therefore translations by the additive group $Z^1(Q,E)$.

Maintain a quotient isomorphism $\alpha_i$ and the group $B_i$ of source-quotient automorphisms inducing a member of $P$ on the initial quotient. Start with $\alpha_0$ and $B_0=P$. The next lift exists exactly when the two displayed subspaces, after transporting the target by $\alpha_i$, lie in the same orbit under $B_i$. An automorphism of $Q$ acts by inverse pullback on cochain arguments and its natural group-algebra action on values. This preserves the radical and gives a linear representation on $H^2(Q,R)$. Apply Luks–Miyazaki subspace transport to this representation, with $B_i$ represented faithfully on the underlying group elements of $Q$.

Lift a transporter and generators of the subspace stabilizer by solving the module-map and coboundary equations. Any solution for the kernel map is invertible. If it had proper image, projection of the target onto a simple quotient annihilating that image would split a nontrivial Frattini extension. Include the translation generators from $Z^1(Q,E)$. The resulting group $B_{i+1}$ is an extension of a subgroup of $B_i$ by an abelian group, and contains all the allowed automorphisms at its layer. Thus it remains in $\mathcal C_b$. Use deterministic permutation stabilizer chains to compress generators and compute the final order. All cochain and group-algebra dimensions, field characteristics, and permutation degrees are polynomial in the explicit input length. The number of nontrivial layers is at most logarithmic in the group order. [Luks–Miyazaki, Proposition 3.17](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/531/download)


## 2. Constructing the quotient coset from socle factors

Put $Q=G/\Phi(G)$ and $S=\operatorname{Soc}(Q)$. For a Fitting-free group, $S$ is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups. The centralizer of $S$ in $Q$ is trivial: any nontrivial normal subgroup contains a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup of $Q$, while $C_Q(S)\cap S=Z(S)=1$. Thus conjugation embeds $Q$ faithfully into $\operatorname{Aut}(S)$. Every quotient isomorphism restricts to a socle isomorphism, which identifies the embedded quotients by conjugation. Conversely any socle map conjugating one embedded quotient to the other yields a quotient isomorphism. Two quotient maps with the same restriction are identical by the faithful conjugation action. [Cannon–Holt, §§2–3](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(02)00133-5)

List all normal closures of individual nonidentity elements. The inclusion-minimal ones are exactly the minimal normal subgroups, so they generate the socle. Repeating the procedure inside the socle identifies its simple direct factors. Nonabelian finite simple groups are two-generated. Find a generating pair and test all possible images to find isomorphisms between simple factors. This lists all automorphisms of each factor and gives their explicit multiplication tables, since a factor of order $u$ has at most $u^2$ automorphisms. The socles can either be distinguished or identified with one common $S$. [Babai–Codenotti–Grochow–Qiao, §2](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/papers/soda11.pdf)

For a simple factor $T$, construct a solvable subgroup $D_T\le\operatorname{Aut}(T)$ with $\operatorname{Aut}(T)=\operatorname{Inn}(T)D_T$. Start with $E=\operatorname{Aut}(T)$ and $N=\operatorname{Inn}(T)$. Maintain that $E$ surjects to $\operatorname{Out}(T)$ with kernel $N$. If $N$ is nonnilpotent, choose a Sylow subgroup $L$ not normal in $N$, and replace $E$ by $N_E(L)$ and $N$ by $N_N(L)$. The Frattini argument gives $E=N N_E(L)$ before replacement, so the new $E$ still surjects, while $N$ strictly decreases. Eventually $N$ is nilpotent and the final $E=D_T$ is solvable by the Schreier theorem that $\operatorname{Out}(T)$ is solvable. All of these groups are subsets of the explicit polynomial-size automorphism table. To build a Sylow subgroup in a table, start from the trivial subgroup and enlarge a $p$-subgroup by a cyclic subgroup of order $p$ in its normalizer quotient. This is possible whenever it is smaller than Sylow, by the normalizer property inside a Sylow subgroup containing it. Scanning the quotient table finds the required element. [Luks–Miyazaki, proof of Theorem 1.3](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/531/download)

Choose fixed coordinates for each simple type $T$ in $S$, of multiplicity $m_T$. The factor permutations give the product of wreath products $\operatorname{Aut}(T)\wr S_{m_T}$ for $\operatorname{Aut}(S)$. Thus
$$
 \operatorname{Aut}(S)=I D,\qquad I=\operatorname{Inn}(S),\qquad
 D=\prod_T D_T\wr S_{m_T}.
$$
Give $D$ by individual factor generators and factor swaps. Its nonabelian composition factors have order at most $b!$, because $m_T\le b$ and $D_T$ is solvable. Represent $D$ and the two quotient images as permutations of the elements of $S$.

The group $I$ lies inside either embedded quotient. Consequently conjugacy of the two quotient subgroups under $\operatorname{Aut}(S)$ is equivalent to conjugacy under $D$. Luks–Miyazaki Theorem 1.2 finds a transporter in $D$ or proves none exists. This gives a quotient isomorphism $\alpha_0$. Their Theorem 1.1 finds the normalizer of the embedded source quotient inside $D$, which induces $P\le\operatorname{Aut}(Q)$ by conjugation. The full source quotient automorphism group is $IP$, writing $I$ now for its induced group on $Q$. Every element of $I$ is inner on $Q$, so lifts to an inner automorphism of $G$. If there is any full-group isomorphism, compose it with a lifted inner automorphism to put its quotient map in $\alpha_0P$. Theorem 1 solves that coset.

For counting, process $G$ against itself. Write $L$ for the resulting group of automorphisms inducing members of $P$, and $A$ for the image of the full automorphism group on $Q$. Since $I\le A$ and $\operatorname{Aut}(Q)=IP$, one has $A=I(A\cap P)$ and
$$
 |\operatorname{Aut}(G)|=|L|\,|I|/|I\cap P|.
$$
Enumerate $I$ using the elements of $S$ and test their membership in $P$. Generators of $L$ together with conjugations by lifts of generators of $S$ generate all of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. For any isomorphic pair, the number of isomorphisms is this order. [Luks–Miyazaki, Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/531/download)



## 3. Listing quotient maps from bounded module multiplicities

Here $Q=G/\Phi(G)$ has trivial Frattini subgroup. Its Fitting subgroup $V$ is the direct product of the characteristic normal $p$-cores $O_p(Q)$. Each of these is elementary abelian. Indeed, $\Phi(O_p(Q))\le \Phi(Q)$: a maximal subgroup of $Q$ missing an element of $\Phi(O_p(Q))$ would supplement this characteristic subgroup. Intersecting that product with $O_p(Q)$ would make the intersection a supplement to its own Frattini subgroup, hence the whole $O_p(Q)$, a contradiction. Thus $V$ is abelian. Each primary component $V_p$ is semisimple as a module; apply the Frattini intersection formula from Section 1 to $V_p\unlhd Q$ using $\Phi(Q)=1$. The zero-class module maps to a semisimple regular module jointly separate all nonzero vectors, and hence embed $V_p$ in a finite direct sum of such modules. Conjugation factors through $Q/V$ since $V$ is abelian.

Compute $V$ and $A=Q/V$ from the table, and likewise for the target. Enumerate candidate isomorphisms $\beta:A\to A'$ by the possible images of a generating tuple of at most $r$ elements. For each candidate, transport the target kernels' actions and extension cocycles by $\beta$. Compute the intertwining maps $V_p\to V'_p$ by linear algebra. Their dimension is at most $b\dim_{\mathbb F_p} V_p$. Indeed if a simple module $S$ has multiplicities $m_S,m'_S$ in source and target, its contribution is $m_Sm'_S\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\operatorname{End}(S)$. This is at most $b m_S\dim_{\mathbb F_p}S$ because any nonzero vector generates the simple module, so endomorphisms are determined by their value there. Sum over $S$. Hence for each $\beta$ there are at most $|V|^b$ intertwining maps between the whole kernels, including noninvertible ones. Enumerate and retain the invertible maps.

For each retained map, find compatible section corrections by solving the coboundary equation. The solutions are empty or a coset of $Z^1(A,V')$. A crossed homomorphism is determined by its values on the chosen generators of $A$, so there are at most $|V'|^r$ solutions and they can be enumerated from the affine linear solution space in the primary coordinates. This lists exactly the lifts of $\beta$ and, processing all candidates, all possible isomorphisms between the Frattini quotients in polynomial time. Use the fixed-Frattini-map algorithm of Theorem 1 with $P=1$ on each listed map. Recover a witness from any nonempty fiber and count by summing over the disjoint fibers. When comparing one group with itself, lift one representative of every nonempty fiber and adjoin the kernel generators from Theorem 1, to generate the full automorphism group. [Skresanov, abelian extension algorithms](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15497v2)

This is an informal proof in the explicit Cayley-table model. The complexity exponents may depend on the fixed bounds $b,r$.
