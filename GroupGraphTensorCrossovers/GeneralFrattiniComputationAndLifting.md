# Computing and lifting through the Frattini subgroup

**Theorem.** Given the Cayley table of a finite group $G$ of order $s$, one can compute $\Phi(G)$ in deterministic $s^{O(1)}$ time. Given two finite Cayley-table groups $G,H$ and an explicit isomorphism $\alpha:G/\Phi(G)\to H/\Phi(H)$, one can in deterministic polynomial time decide whether $\alpha$ lifts and return a lift if one exists. For a liftable $\alpha$, the algorithm also returns generators for the automorphisms of $G$ acting trivially on $G/\Phi(G)$ and the exact number of lifts.

The first statement answers the polynomial-time Cayley-table question posted in 2023, which asked for the solvable case. Eick and Neumann-Brosig gave a practical algorithm for polycyclic groups. The extension lifting framework of Cannon and Holt predates this proof. The complexity claims here use explicit Cayley tables and polynomial-time subspace transport in solvable permutation groups. [2023 polynomial-time question](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/4768500/is-there-an-algorithm-to-check-whether-given-subgroup-contained-inside-the-fratt) · [Eick–Neumann-Brosig](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2021.09.035) · [Cannon–Holt](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(02)00133-5)

## A linear computation of the Frattini intersection

Fix a finite group $K$ and a normal elementary abelian $p$-subgroup $W$. Put $Q=K/W$, let $J$ be the Jacobson radical of the group algebra $\mathbb F_p[Q]$, and let $R=\mathbb F_p[Q]/J$ as a left $Q$-module. It is semisimple and contains every simple $\mathbb F_p[Q]$-module as a direct summand. Let $c\in H^2(Q,W)$ be the class of the extension $K$, with the conjugation action on $W$. Then
$$
 W\cap\Phi(K)
 =
 \bigcap_{\substack{f\in\operatorname{Hom}_Q(W,R)\\ f_*c=0}}\ker f. \tag{1}
$$

If a maximal subgroup $M$ of $K$ misses an element $w\in W$, then $K=WM$. The subgroup $M\cap W$ is normalized by $M$ and centralized by $W$, hence is normal in $K$. The quotient $W/(M\cap W)$ is a simple $Q$-module, since a proper intermediate module would give a proper subgroup strictly containing $M$. The quotient extension by this simple module splits, with complement $M/(M\cap W)$. The map from $W$ to the simple module and then to its copy in $R$ has $f_*c=0$ and $f(w)\ne 0$.

Conversely, suppose $f(w)\ne 0$ for a map on the right of (1). Project $R$ onto a simple summand on which $f(w)$ has a nonzero coordinate. This gives a surjective $Q$-linear map $f_0:W\to S$ with $(f_0)_*c=0$. The quotient of $K$ by $\ker f_0$ is a split extension with kernel $S$. A complement to $S$ is maximal because $S$ is simple as a $Q$-module. Its preimage in $K$ is maximal and misses $w$. This proves (1).

Choose a section of the quotient map and use its normalized cocycle $c(x,y)$ to represent the class. Solve jointly for a $Q$-linear map $f:W\to R$ and a normalized $1$-cochain $u:Q\to R$ with $f c=\delta u$. All equations are linear over $\mathbb F_p$, including the intertwining and coboundary conditions. Project a solution-space basis onto the map coordinates and intersect the kernels of those maps. This computes the right side of (1). The group algebra has dimension $|Q|$, and its radical is computable by the deterministic algorithm of Friedl and Rónyai. Its structure constants come from the table, and the cochain arrays have polynomial length. [Friedl–Rónyai](https://www.cs.uoregon.edu/Reports/TR-1985-003.pdf)

## Computing all of the Frattini subgroup

For each prime $p\mid |K|$, compute the largest normal $p$-subgroup $P=O_p(K)$. It is generated by those elements whose normal closure has $p$-power order. Each normal closure is obtained by generating a subgroup from the conjugates of one element, which takes polynomial time in the table. Let $W_p$ be the elements of order dividing $p$ in $Z(P)$. This is a characteristic elementary abelian subgroup of $K$.

Every minimal normal $p$-subgroup $E$ of $K$ lies in $W_p$. It is elementary abelian and lies in $P$. The conjugation action of the $p$-group $P$ has nonzero fixed points on $E$: the number of fixed elements is congruent to $|E|$ modulo $p$ and includes the identity. Since $P$ is normal in $K$, these fixed points form a normal subgroup of $K$ inside $E$. Thus all of $E$ is centralized by $P$, as needed.

Compute $W_p\cap\Phi(K)$ by (1). If all these intersections vanish, then $\Phi(K)=1$. Indeed the Frattini subgroup of a finite group is nilpotent. If it were nontrivial, it would contain a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup of $K$. Such a subgroup is elementary abelian of prime-power order, by the characteristic Sylow decomposition and the characteristic derived series of a nilpotent group. The preceding paragraph would force it into one of the $W_p$.

Otherwise quotient by a nontrivial $W_p\cap\Phi(K)$ and repeat. For a normal subgroup $L\le\Phi(K)$ all maximal subgroups contain $L$, so $\Phi(K/L)=\Phi(K)/L$. Therefore the inverse image of the subgroup accumulated when the process stops is exactly $\Phi(G)$. Every nontrivial quotient reduces order by at least two, so there are at most $\log_2 s$ repetitions. Prime divisors can be found by trial division in time polynomial in $s$. A classical proof of the finite-group nilpotence fact uses a Sylow subgroup $P$ of $\Phi(K)$ and the Frattini argument $K=\Phi(K)N_K(P)$. This forces $N_K(P)=K$ for each such Sylow subgroup. [Finite-group Frattini facts](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/grouptheory/subgpseries2.pdf)

## The group of automorphisms fixing the Frattini quotient

For any finite group $L$, let $B_L$ be the automorphisms that induce the identity modulo $\Phi(L)$. Then $B_L$ is nilpotent. If $\Phi(L)$ is a $p$-group, an automorphism in $B_L$ of prime order $q\ne p$ has a fixed point in each coset of $\Phi(L)$, by applying orbit-size divisibility to that coset. Its fixed-point subgroup would then surject onto $L/\Phi(L)$, and would be all of $L$ by the defining property of $\Phi(L)$. Cauchy's theorem therefore implies that $B_L$ is a $p$-group. For general $\Phi(L)$, let $\Phi(L)_{p'}$ be the product of its Sylow subgroups at primes other than $p$. These are characteristic. The actions on all quotients $L/\Phi(L)_{p'}$ embed $B_L$ into a direct product of the respective kernels, since the intersection of the $\Phi(L)_{p'}$ is trivial and since $\Phi(L/\Phi(L)_{p'})=\Phi(L)/\Phi(L)_{p'}$. Each respective kernel is a $p$-group. A subgroup of a finite nilpotent group is nilpotent.

Construct a characteristic series descending from $\Phi(G)$ to $1$ whose nontrivial sections are elementary abelian and semisimple under conjugation. Process the Sylow subgroups of $\Phi(G)$ in ascending prime order. For each current nontrivial characteristic $p$-subgroup $P$ in that process, the quotient $P/\Phi(P)$ is elementary abelian. Refine it by the descending filtration by powers of the Jacobson radical of $\mathbb F_p[G]$ acting on that quotient, and then replace $P$ by $\Phi(P)$. All its successive sections are semisimple. The radical is invariant under algebra automorphisms, so all filtration steps are characteristic in $G$. For each such step $L_i\ge L_{i+1}$, its kernel $E=L_i/L_{i+1}$ is a semisimple module for $Q=G/L_i$ because conjugation by $L_i$ is trivial on the abelian section. Each $E$ is contained in $\Phi(G/L_{i+1})$. The same construction is made for $H$, and incompatible section sizes or primes preclude a lift. The series, conjugation maps, and quotient tables use only subgroup closure, field linear algebra, and the radical algorithm. The count of nontrivial sections is at most $\log_2 s$.

## Comparing extensions along a known quotient map

At a step suppose $E$ is a semisimple $\mathbb F_p[Q]$-module, the normal kernel of an extension of $Q$, lying in its Frattini subgroup. Let $c\in H^2(Q,E)$ be its extension class, and set $R=\mathbb F_p[Q]/J$. Record the subspace
$$
 X(c)=\{ f_*c : f\in\operatorname{Hom}_Q(E,R)\}\subseteq H^2(Q,R). \tag{2}
$$
It is defined and computed by linear algebra. For every simple $Q$-module $S$, the map
$$
 \operatorname{Hom}_Q(E,S)\longrightarrow H^2(Q,S),\qquad f\longmapsto f_*c
$$
is injective. Indeed a nonzero $f$ is onto. If $f_*c=0$, quotienting the extension group by $\ker f$ would give a split extension by $S$, with $S$ still contained in the Frattini subgroup. A complement contradicts this containment.

For two such extensions $c_1,c_2$ of the same $Q$, with potentially different semisimple kernels $E_1,E_2$, equality of (2) holds exactly when there is a $Q$-module isomorphism $B:E_1\to E_2$ with $B_*c_1=c_2$. One implication follows by composing the maps to $R$. For the other, decompose $R$ as a direct sum of copies of simple modules $S$, containing at least one copy of each. Project (2) onto the cohomology of one such copy. Equality says that the coefficient maps from $\operatorname{Hom}_Q(E_1,S)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_Q(E_2,S)$ have the same image. They are injective and linear for postcomposition by $\Delta=\operatorname{End}_Q(S)$. Their identification is therefore an isomorphism of left $\Delta$-spaces. In coordinates on the $S$-isotypic parts of the two kernels, a map to $S$ is a row over $\Delta$. A left $\Delta$-linear isomorphism of the rows is right multiplication by an invertible matrix, which corresponds to precomposition with an isomorphism of those two isotypic parts. This gives the block of $B$ for $S$. The blocks together define $B$, and the cohomology equality after every projection to a simple summand implies $B_*c_1=c_2$ by additivity in coefficients.

The argument also shows that any automorphism of $E_1$ stabilizing $c_1$ is the identity, since it preserves every map $E_1\to S$ by injectivity. A concrete isomorphism of extensions over the quotient identity can be recovered by solving the linear equations for a module map $B$ and a cochain $v$ satisfying $B c_1-c_2=\delta v$ at the cocycle level. Every solution $B$ is surjective: if its image is proper, a projection of $E_2$ onto a simple quotient annihilating the image would send $c_2$ to zero. The kernels have equal size, so $B$ is invertible. The section, $B$, and $v$ give the isomorphism on every element. The lifts of any one quotient map differ by the normalized $1$-cocycles $Z^1(Q,E_1)$, which are computed by linear equations. These act by the usual translation of the kernel coordinate, with identity maps on $Q$ and $E_1$.

## Polynomial lifting through the whole series

At the initial quotients $G/\Phi(G)$ and $H/\Phi(H)$, the given map is the representative and its source stabilizer is trivial. At each next stage maintain one quotient isomorphism realizing the prescribed map on the initial Frattini quotient and a generating set for the group of all source-quotient automorphisms fixing that initial quotient. Such a group is nilpotent by the previous argument, since $\Phi(G/L_i)=\Phi(G)/L_i$.

Transport the target extension to the source quotient using the representative. For $a\in\operatorname{Aut}(Q)$, the natural action on $\mathbb F_p[Q]$ preserves its radical and descends to $R$. It combines with the pullback on cochains to transport (2). Taking pullback by the inverse for a left-action convention gives a linear representation of the maintained source group on $H^2(Q,R)$. Its generator matrices are computed by linear algebra on cochains and by applying the quotient permutation to basis elements of the group algebra.

Apply the subspace transporter algorithm of Luks and Miyazaki to the two spaces (2) under the maintained group, represented faithfully as permutations of the elements of $Q$. The algorithm produces a transporter and generators of the subspace stabilizer, or proves that there is no transporter. Its hypotheses apply because a nilpotent permutation group is solvable, and the ambient cohomology dimension and characteristic are bounded polynomially in $s$. A transporter gives a compatible adjusted quotient isomorphism. The equality criterion and linear one-step lifting recover its lift and lifts of all subspace-stabilizer generators on the source. Add generators for the kernel obtained from a basis of $Z^1(Q,E)$. The resulting permutations generate exactly the source-quotient automorphisms at the next stage fixing the initially prescribed quotient. Deterministic permutation group stabilizer-chain algorithms compress the generating set to polynomial size. [Luks–Miyazaki, Proposition 3.17 and Problem 22](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/531/download)

Repeating through the characteristic series gives the required isomorphism and source kernel generators, or proves that none exists. When a lift exists, all lifts form a coset of that kernel group. Its order is computed from the permutation stabilizer chain. All polynomial bounds here are in the explicit Cayley-table model.
