# General theorems for constrained isomorphism

These are informal proofs. They address four general questions about isomorphism reductions. Each gives a precise way to transfer, assemble, or compress conclusions, with a tensor application. They use the previous ternary tensor sign projections, independent-axis packing, and determinant-preserving ordinary compiler. The unrestricted reduction from special-linear to ordinary tensor isomorphism remains open. [Earlier tensor sign constructions](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/SignProjections.md) · [Previous determinant and Frobenius theorems](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignMetaTheorems/Results.md)

## 1. Ambiguous batch reductions and covering designs

Let E be a fine comparison equivalence and O a coarser equivalence on the same target objects. Assume every O-class contains at most b E-classes. A batch construction outputs a list of at most r target objects per source position. It encodes a positive source comparison by existence of an E-matching pair across the two lists. The lists may depend jointly on the full batch. Let J be a fixed graph of tested comparisons on the source positions. Our two reference cases are that all edges in J are positive or all are negative.

For each ordinary target class appearing in the lists, let S_j be the set of source positions whose list meets that ordinary class. Each vertex lies in at most r sets S_j. The following is an exact analysis of the ordinary-equivalence observations in this model.

- A completion of the data by fine equivalences making all tested edges positive exists iff the S_j cover every edge of J. Necessity follows from the existence of a fine match on each edge. Sufficiency assigns one fine class to every ordinary class.
- A completion making all tested edges negative exists iff each induced graph J[S_j] can be colored with b colors. For necessity, select one listed fine class per position in S_j and color it by that class. Any adjacent positions must receive distinct colors. For sufficiency, assign each position's objects in that coarse class to its selected color class. We treat list entries as tokens with the observed ordinary relation. Repeated occurrences of an identified token, if available as additional data, may impose more constraints.

Thus the exact ambiguity is an edge cover by subsets inducing b-colorable graphs, with vertex replication at most r. When there is no such cover, the desired positive case reduces to a fixed positive formula of ordinary comparisons. Precompute all W⊆V(J) with χ(J[W])>b. For each W, test whether one coarse class meets all its lists by taking the OR over choices of one object per list, testing each chosen object for ordinary equivalence to a common reference. Take the OR over W. For fixed J and r this uses a constant number of conjunctions and disjunctions.

### Sharp two-candidate capacity for ternary tensors

For complete J and two candidates per position, the all-positive lists, interpreted as sets of fine labels, are pairwise intersecting. Either there is a common label or all sets are edges of one triangle. A singleton forces a common label. Otherwise in the absence of a common label, take two distinct sets {a,b},{a,c}. A set omitting a must be {b,c}, and the three pairwise intersection conditions then force every set to be a triangle edge. With every label occurring at most b times, there are at most floor(3b/2) triangle lists by counting their incidences. This is attained by taking multiplicities floor(b/2), floor(b/2), and ceil(b/2) on the three edges. For b=4, at most six lists can therefore form an ambiguous batch. Seven suffice, and six is sharp in this observation model.

An ordinary ternary tensor orbit contains at most four two-sign classes. Suppose a polynomial procedure takes seven SL-TI objects and produces a list of at most two two-sign objects for each, preserving every pair by existential matching. Then SL-TI reduces to ordinary TI. For a one-sign pair x,y, use the earlier objectwise independent-axis packing to encode seven distinct length-three binary words over {x,y} as SL-TI objects. In the positive case all are equivalent, and in the negative case they are all distinct at the signed tier. The capacity theorem gives the required ordinary comparisons. Polynomial ordinary conjunction and disjunction pack the fixed formula into one ordinary TI instance. The earlier seven character probes then reduce SL-TI to ordinary. The converse holds by determinant-neutral singleton outputs, so existence of this seven-object two-list reduction is equivalent to full many-one collapse. [Earlier axis construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/SignProjections.md)

With three candidates per position, ambiguity can persist on all eight words. Use the seven lines of the Fano plane as three-element lists and duplicate one line. They are pairwise intersecting and no label occurs more than four times. By taking distinct tokens for different positions, the coarse incidences admit both completions from the general theorem. This is an exact boundary for inference from these coarse observations, independent of how a reduction on raw tensors might exploit additional structure.

### Independently randomized encodings

Suppose there is instead a sampler producing one target object from each source object. Draw independently at the two endpoints. Assume that the E-verdict agrees with the source verdict with probability at least γ>5/9, including independent draws in each self-comparison. Then the preceding capacity argument has a bounded-error randomized analogue with only b+1 source positions.

For each source object, its fine-class probability distribution has collision probability at least γ. Its largest mass p is at least γ because the collision sum is at most p. Also it is at most p²+(1−p)², so p≥η=(1+√(2γ−1))/2>2/3. Suppose two objects have different largest classes with masses p,q≥η. Their cross-collision is at most p(1−q)+(1−p)q. To see this, allocate the nonleading mass of each distribution first against the other's leading class, which can only increase the product sum because each leading mass exceeds any possible competing nonleading mass. Equivalently, with residual masses x,y outside the two largest classes, the product sum is at most p(1−q)+q(1−p)−px−qy+xy. Thus distinct leading classes give collision at most 2η(1−η)=1−γ, and a positive pair must share its leading class. A shared leading class gives collision at least η²>1−γ, so a negative pair must have different leading classes.

With a constant number of samples from each of b+1 objects, for fixed b and fixed γ>5/9, the leading ordinary class has a strict sampled majority in each list simultaneously with probability at least 2/3. In the positive case these majority classes coincide. In the negative case the b+1 distinct leading fine classes cannot fit into one ordinary class. A constant OR of ordinary equality conjunctions tests whether one class has strict majority in every list. For ternary tensors the five-word packing gives a bounded-error randomized many-one reduction to ordinary TI under this sampler hypothesis. Independent endpoint randomness and the self-comparison guarantee are essential hypotheses of the theorem.

## 2. One finite permutation controls every extension degree

Consider a finite nonempty set X on which a finite group C acts freely and transitively on the right. Let F be an automorphism of C and let P be a permutation of X satisfying P(xc)=P(x)F(c). Call f positive if P^f has a fixed point. If f is positive, its fixed set is a torsor for C^{F^f}: choose one fixed point x and observe that xc is fixed exactly when F^f(c)=c.

**Degree spectrum theorem.** The set of positive degrees is a union of sets of multiples of divisors of |C|. More precisely,
\[
 f\text{ is positive}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \gcd(f,|C^{F^f}|)\text{ is positive},
\]
and |C| can replace |C^{F^f}|. If C is nilpotent, there is a single d dividing |C| such that the positive degrees are exactly the multiples of d. In that case any two positive degrees imply positivity of their greatest common divisor.

To prove the first assertion, choose a divisibility-minimal positive divisor e of a positive f. Every cycle of P contributing a point to Fix(P^e) has length exactly e, since its length is a positive divisor of e. Therefore e divides |Fix(P^e)|=|C^{F^e}|, which divides both |C^{F^f}| and |C|. A multiple of a positive degree is positive, proving both equivalences. For nilpotent C, choose a basepoint of X. The map P is c↦aF(c), and it decomposes across the characteristic Sylow factors C_p. On each factor every positive degree has a positive divisor which is a power of p. All positive p-powers are a chain under divisibility, so the least positive p-power d_p divides every positive degree for that factor. Such a power exists because a finite permutation has some positive iterate. The product of all d_p is the single d for X.

### Apply the theorem to comparisons with a central constraint

Let G'→G be a finite central étale isogeny of connected affine groups over a finite field, with kernel Z. Let x,y be rational objects in the same geometric G-orbit. The geometric lifted transporters form a torsor under the lifted geometric stabilizer H of x. Its set X of connected components is a torsor under C=π₀(H). All stabilizers can be taken reduced over the perfect base, since passing to this subgroup does not change geometric or rational points. Frobenius acts on this finite torsor.

For b∈Z over the algebraic closure, set P_b(t)=F(t)b⁻¹ on the geometric lifted transporters. On components it is a permutation compatible with Frobenius on C. Its f-th iterate twists F^f by the inverse of N_f(b)=b F(b)⋯F^{f−1}(b). A fixed component for that iterate has an actual fixed lift. Choose a representative t of the component and write P_b^f(t)=t u with u in the connected stabilizer. Correct t by a connected element h solving uF^f(h)=h, using Lang's theorem. Each such component supplies exactly |H°(F_(q^f))| twisted-fixed lifts because any two differ by a rational point of the connected stabilizer over that extension. [Lang, algebraic groups over finite fields](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Lang-Algebraic_Groups_Over_Finite_Fields.pdf)

Every rational G-transporter with central obstruction class represented by N_f(b) has exactly |Z(F_(q^f))| such lifts. Indeed a geometric lift has a Frobenius ratio in Z. Adjusting the lift by an element of Z changes the ratio by a Frobenius coboundary. A solution exists precisely in the specified class, and its solution set is a torsor for the Frobenius-fixed part of Z. Thus the exact number of such rational transporters is
\[
 \#\operatorname{Fix}(P_b^f\mid X)\,
 \frac{|H^\circ(\mathbb F_{q^f})|}{|Z(\mathbb F_{q^f})|}.
\]
The normalized count sequence is the fixed-point sequence of a single permutation. Its zeta series is the product of (1−t^l)⁻¹ over the cycle lengths l, as follows by taking the logarithm and summing the contribution of each cycle. With explicit component data, all degrees and all arithmetic thresholds are thus read from one finite object.

If the ordinary geometric stabilizer component group is nilpotent, the lifted group C is also nilpotent. Indeed the map between component groups has central kernel, a quotient of Z, and a central extension of a nilpotent group is nilpotent. The theorem therefore gives a single divisibility threshold for every fixed central-obstruction comparison, with target obstruction propagated by norm. In particular, any two successes at coprime extension degrees imply base success. One can use this test on the nilpotence promise without computing the component group or the threshold.

The least degree d_1 for a refined comparison is a multiple of the ordinary least degree d_0. Its ratio d_1/d_0 divides the size of the kernel of the map between component groups. To prove this, take one fixed ordinary component at degree d_0. Its lifted component fibre is a torsor under this central kernel. The iterate P_b^(d_0) acts affinely on it. It has a positive degree r dividing the kernel size, so the global refined threshold divides d_0r. When the kernel is constant the fibre map is a translation, so the ratio divides its exponent. In particular each determinant-square-class refinement over F₃ has least degree d_0 or 2d_0, even if all three signs are imposed. Over extensions, the signs here are quadratic characters of determinants. They coincide with determinant-one tests over F₃.

Nilpotence is substantive. In the constant group (C₃×C₅)⋊C₂ with simultaneous inversion by the involution, the generator pair (a,b) and (a,b⁻¹) are not conjugate, but their cubes are conjugate and their fifth powers agree. These are ordinary Frobenius classes that agree at both extension degrees 3 and 5 and differ at degree one. They are realizable in a homogeneous-space comparison by embedding the finite group as a constant permutation subgroup of a general linear group and using Lang surjectivity to obtain the two rational orbit types.

The permutation, count, and degree-spectrum statements also hold for a geometrically surjective morphism of connected affine groups with a noncentral kernel K, if the prescribed obstruction is a twisted conjugacy class in K. For a representative b use the same map P_b(t)=F(t)b⁻¹. Its action on the lifted transporter torsor is affine for θ=Ad(b)F on the stabilizer. Its f-th power uses the ordered norm B_f=bF(b)⋯F^{f−1}(b) and θ^f=Ad(B_f)F^f. To lift a fixed component, apply Lang for θ^f on the identity stabilizer. This follows from ordinary Lang by choosing s in the connected ambient group with s⁻¹F^f(s)=B_f. Conjugation by s transfers θ^f to F^f on the conjugate connected stabilizer. Each fixed component then supplies |(H°)^{θ^f}| lifts, and each admissible underlying witness has |K^{θ^f}| lifts. The finite affine permutation theorem applies without centrality. The central hypothesis in the preceding application ensures nilpotence upstairs from nilpotence downstairs.

### A uniform extension reduction without computing any components

Suppose only an upper bound B on the component group order of the lifted stabilizer is available. Pick distinct prime degrees whose product exceeds B. At least one prime degree is coprime to the actual component group order. Hence base positivity is equivalent to positivity at **every** selected degree with the target obstruction propagated by norm. Base positivity gives all extension witnesses, and the gcd theorem descends at least one positive extension comparison to degree one. This criterion holds without nilpotence. If there is no geometric transporter, every extension comparison is negative.

For explicit ternary tensors, B can be chosen with polynomial bit length using only the input dimensions. The stabilizer equations prescribe the tensor entries after the three matrix actions, each of degree at most three in the matrix entries. Add three equations making the determinants invertible, using one inverse variable per mode, and up to three square-root determinant equations for the selected sign constraints. If there are s equations of maximum degree D, an affine Bézout bound gives at most D^s geometric irreducible components, hence at most that many connected components. To see the bound, start with affine space of degree one and intersect one hypersurface at a time. The degree of a component already contained is unchanged, and the sum of degrees of the pieces of the others increases by at most the hypersurface degree. Both s and D are polynomial in the dense input size. [Affine Bézout inequality](https://www.maia.ub.edu/~sombra/publications/arith-nss/maino.pdf)

It suffices to take s⌈log₂ D⌉+1 distinct odd prime degrees. The first that many odd primes have polynomial numerical size by the usual prime-count estimates and are found by trial division. In fixed characteristic the extension fields have deterministic polynomial-time explicit presentations of the required degrees. Thus all signed tiers over F₃ have a uniform deterministic reduction to a conjunction of polynomially many square-class comparisons over extensions of polynomial degree. The degrees are chosen without knowing the actual component group or any transporter. This does not itself remove signs in those extension comparisons. [Shoup, deterministic construction of irreducible polynomials in small characteristic](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1990-54-189/S0025-5718-1990-0993933-0/S0025-5718-1990-0993933-0.pdf)

## 3. Exactly when separate quotient witnesses combine

A groupoid is a category whose morphisms are all invertible. A multiplicative grading by a finite group G assigns a group element to every morphism and respects composition. For any nonempty transporter set, the set of grades is a coset xH, where H is the image of the source automorphisms. This follows by fixing a transporter and composing it with all source automorphisms. A normal subgroup K_i defines a quotient observation π_i:G→G/K_i.

**Quotient theorem.** The following three properties are equivalent.

1. For every subgroup H≤G, one has ⋂_i HK_i=H.
2. The entire grade coset D of any comparison is determined by its quotient images and equals ⋂_i π_i⁻¹(π_i(D)).
3. Simultaneous existence of the independent identity-grade witnesses in all quotients is equivalent to existence of an actual identity-grade witness, for all abstract coset profiles.

Indeed for D=xH the inverse image of π_i(D) is xHK_i. The identity tests therefore agree exactly under condition 1. If condition 1 fails, choose x∈⋂HK_i outside H. The positive coset H and the negative coset xH have identical entire quotient images, so even complete adaptive observations of those images cannot distinguish them. A nonempty list of quotient observations detects an empty profile directly.

For a finite abelian G the criterion is equivalent to every prime-power-order character occurring in the annihilator K_i^⊥ of at least one kernel. Duality rewrites condition 1 as L=Σ_i(L∩K_i^⊥) for every dual subgroup L. A cyclic subgroup of prime-power order has one maximal proper subgroup, so its generators must be covered by one annihilator. Conversely the prime-power-order members generate every finite abelian subgroup. This proves the description.

For arbitrary finite G, the exact subgroup criterion can be stated using primitive actions. For every subgroup J≤G and every maximal subgroup M<J, at least one J∩K_i must lie in the core of M in J. This is equivalent to the corresponding channel preserving a faithful copy of the primitive quotient action on cosets of M. Indeed J∩K_i is normal in J, so inclusion in M is inclusion in its core. If no such inclusion holds, M(J∩K_i)=J for all i and M is indistinguishable from J in all quotients. Conversely a failure of condition 1 yields J=⟨H,x⟩ and a maximal M containing H but omitting x. Each projection already has π_i(J)=π_i(H), so the primitive condition fails.

For exactly two channels the criterion simplifies to K_1∩K_2=1 and gcd(|K_1|,|K_2|)=1. Trivial intersection follows by checking H=1. The disjoint normal kernels commute. If their orders share a prime, choose one cyclic subgroup of that order in each and take the diagonal subgroup in their product. Its grade information in the two quotients misses the diagonal constraint. Conversely if x=h_1 k_1=h_2 k_2 with h_i∈H and k_i∈K_i, then h_2⁻¹h_1=k_2k_1⁻¹ lies in H. Coprimality lets powers isolate either component, so k_1 and k_2 lie in H and x does too. For coordinate observations of a finite direct product, the same diagonal obstruction gives necessity of pairwise coprime factor orders. Under that condition one can extract each coordinate of any subgroup element by powering, proving sufficiency.

### Construct a genuine combined witness

In the abelian case, given actual transporters g_i with identity grade in each respective quotient, take g_0=g_1 and use the automorphisms g_0⁻¹g_i. Their grades generate H'≤H. The smaller coset s(g_0)+H' still meets every kernel because it contains all observed witness grades. The universal separation property applies to H' and makes this coset contain zero. Solve the modular linear equations for a suitable combination of the grades by Smith normal form, and implement it as a product of powers of the actual relative automorphisms. This yields one actual jointly valid transporter. No stabilizer generators beyond these relative automorphisms are needed. Separation only promised for H would not suffice for this constructive argument, since the subgroup H' must also be separated. [Kannan–Bachem, polynomial Smith normal form](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0208040)

One tensor application concerns a common coefficient field K/F_q of degree e. A semilinear transporter is graded by its three norm determinants together with its Frobenius alignment. The grading is multiplicative because the Galois action preserves the norm. If gcd(e,q−1)=1, the independent determinant and alignment constraints combine, since their grade groups have coprime orders. This is a statement about the actual transporter, conditional on the two independent witnesses existing. When e is odd over F₃, the determinant of coordinate Frobenius is positive, as its order is odd, so these norm signs are the raw signs after the endpoint frame calibration.

Shared primes can give actual coupling. For the multiplication tensor of an even-degree K/F₃, every isotopy is (aσ,bσ,abσ) for a,b∈K× and one common field automorphism σ. To see this, evaluate at the unit in each input and then divide out the two resulting units, leaving a unital multiplicative map. The product of the three raw determinant signs is the sign of σ, since both unit norms occur twice. The generator Frobenius permutes a normal basis in an even-length cycle, so its determinant is negative. Hence the positive-sign constraint and the generator alignment each have a witness, but they have no joint witness.

## 4. The residual group of a determinant certificate

Let A be a finite abelian group of raw orientation data. Let T be a finite abelian group of auxiliary data that can be ordinarily read on the same transporters. Suppose exact functorial identities, after eliminating any unreadable variables, assert that every actual witness has (a,t) in an explicit affine relation R⊆A⊕T. The algorithm may impose a constraint on t using ordinary maps, as long as every favorable witness with a=0 survives. It may then retain characters of a as explicit output conditions. This abstraction includes the binary exact-sequence equations and more general cyclic determinant values.

**Residual group theorem.** Put T_0={t:(0,t)∈R}. If it is empty, the constrained comparison is false. Otherwise the most selective mark on auxiliary data justified by these identities is t∈T_0. After imposing it, the possible raw grades in the identity model are exactly
\[
 N=\{a\in A\mid(a,0)\in R-R\}.
\]
In particular the minimum number of characters into a cyclic group of exponent equal to the exponent of A that can still distinguish zero exactly on this residual group is the minimum generator number of N.

The first claim follows because any mark sound for all favorable pairs consistent with the identities has to allow T_0. After the mark, (a,t)∈R and (0,t)∈R imply (a,0)∈R−R. Conversely add (a,0) in the difference group to any (0,t)∈R to obtain the corresponding admissible raw grade. Thus the possible grades are exactly N in the certificate model. This does not assert that all such grades are realized by tensor transporters.

Write M for the exponent of A. The minimum generator count d(N) is max_p dim_Fp N[p]. Fewer characters into Z/M cannot be injective on N[p] for a prime attaining this maximum. Conversely embed N into (Z/M)^d(N) using its invariant factors and extend the coordinate characters to A. For existence of the extensions, extend one generator a at a time. If a has order d and its coset modulo the subgroup already covered has order m, solve m x=χ(ma) for an x killed by d. The image of multiplication by m on the d-torsion of Z/M is exactly its (d/m)-torsion, so the equation is soluble. This gives a well-defined character extension and proves optimality. Integer modular linear algebra and Smith normal form compute the residual group, its invariant factors, and the required characters from an explicit finite abelian presentation.

Ordinary enforcement of the auxiliary mark and exact character retention are construction hypotheses. For binary tensor signs the previous compiler supplies both for ordinary-readable bits arranged in fixed roles, and the theorem recovers Gaussian elimination on the determinant equations. In a cyclic orientation group, coprime residual parts can be read by one character. For example a residual C₃×C₄ needs only one character of modulus twelve although it may occupy two originally designated coordinates. It is the group of remaining possibilities that determines the optimal number of retained characters in this model.

The four theorems abstract different aspects of the earlier tensor results. They describe what local consistency, field extension, quotient observation, and an exact determinant identity imply once their hypotheses hold. Polynomial extraction of such data or an unrestricted signed-tensor reduction remains open.
