# Determinant correction over finite fields and on a local tensor isomorphism hard class

Over a finite field of fixed characteristic, determinants of units in the three pair adjoint algebras give a polynomial-time test for when ordinary tensor transporters can be corrected in chosen modes. This gives one ordinary tensor isomorphism comparison for every prescribed determinant triple if the correction subgroup contains the coordinate axes of the large modes. The class with corrections on all three axes remains tensor-isomorphism complete even among concise tensors with local centroid and equal mode dimensions. The latter construction works over every field.

The unrestricted reduction from special-linear tensor isomorphism to ordinary tensor isomorphism in Open Question 1.9 remains open. The results below establish the reverse inclusion over every field and the forward reduction on the stated recognizable class. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, *Actions by classical groups*, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

## 1. The subgroup generated by unit determinants

Write the input as a trilinear form $T:V_1\times V_2\times V_3\to F$. A transporter $g:T\to T'$ satisfies $T'(g_1x_1,g_2x_2,g_3x_3)=T(x_1,x_2,x_3)$. For $1\le i<j\le3$, let
\[
 A_{ij}(T)=\{(a,b)\in\operatorname{End}_F(V_i)\oplus\operatorname{End}_F(V_j)
       : T(\ldots,a x_i,\ldots)=T(\ldots,b x_j,\ldots)\}.
\]
The equality is required for all input vectors, with the other arguments unchanged. The product on this algebra is $(a,b)(c,d)=(ac,db)$. Indeed move $a$ across first, then move $c$ across. It contains $(1,1)$ and is represented faithfully by $(a,b)\mapsto a\oplus b^{\mathsf T}$. A unit gives the actual tensor automorphism that acts as $a$ on mode $i$, as $b^{-1}$ on mode $j$, and as identity on the remaining mode. Let $H(T)\le (F^\times)^3$ be the subgroup generated by the determinant triples of all these units for all three pairs.

Suppose $F=\mathbb F_q$. For one pair let
\[
 A_{ij}/J\simeq \prod_s M_{d_s}(E_s),\qquad J=\operatorname{rad}(A_{ij}),
\]
with $E_s/F$ finite fields. The first raw space is a left module and the second is a right module. Write $m_{i,s},m_{j,s}$ for their multiplicities of the natural simple module for factor $s$. A unit with semisimple components $a_s\in\operatorname{GL}_{d_s}(E_s)$ contributes
\[
 \left(\prod_s N_{E_s/F}(\det_{E_s}a_s)^{m_{i,s}},
 \quad
 \prod_s N_{E_s/F}(\det_{E_s}a_s)^{-m_{j,s}}\right)
 \tag{1}
\]
on modes $i,j$. To see this, filter each module by its powers of $J$. The diagonal blocks are its semisimple graded modules and the determinant is the product of their determinants. Restriction of scalars takes the determinant to its field norm. This applies to the right module as well, and the second exponent sign is from the inverse in the tensor automorphism. Units lift from $A_{ij}/J$, since an element that has a two-sided inverse modulo the nilpotent ideal $J$ has a two-sided inverse in the algebra. Conversely the norm of the determinant of an invertible matrix over each $E_s$ can be any element of $F^\times$, independently for each $s$, by the surjectivity of the finite-field norm and diagonal matrix determinants.

Consequently $H(T)$ is described exactly by the matrix of columns
\[
 m_{i,s}\mathbf e_i-m_{j,s}\mathbf e_j
 \tag{2}
\]
over $\mathbb Z/(q-1)$, taking the columns from all three pairs. Here $\mathbf e_i$ is the vector with $1$ in coordinate $i$ and $0$ in the others. Equivalently, for each column $v$ we include every triple $(u^{v_1},u^{v_2},u^{v_3})$ with $u\in F^\times$. In particular, testing whether $H(T)$ contains a full coordinate axis asks whether the corresponding $\mathbf e_i$ belongs to the lattice spanned by (2) and $(q-1)\mathbb Z^3$. No choice of a primitive element or discrete logarithm is needed.

The three algebras and their structure constants are obtained by linear algebra on the tensor coefficients. The finite-field Wedderburn algorithm computes the radicals, simple quotient models, and the quotient maps in deterministic time polynomial in the matrix dimensions, $\log q$, and the characteristic of $F$. This is polynomial bit time for fixed characteristic with the field given in an explicit polynomial basis. To compute the multiplicities, form the left or right radical filtration by successively taking spans of products by a basis of $J$. On each quotient, the action of any lift of a central quotient idempotent projects to the associated simple block. Its image dimension divided by $d_s[E_s:F]$ gives the count of simple modules there. Sum over the layers. These operations and integral lattice membership via Smith normal form are polynomial time. This calculation uses the algebra decomposition result without any involution. [Ivanyos–Qiao, *Algorithms based on $*$-algebras*, finite-field Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03495#page=18)

## 2. A single ordinary comparison

**Theorem 1.** Fix a characteristic and a constant $c$. Let $L\ge2$ be the dense input length. Given two ternary tensors over an explicit finite field of that characteristic and prescribed determinant values $d_i\in F^\times$, suppose that, for every mode with dimension greater than $c\log_2 L$, $H(T')$ contains the full coordinate axis on that mode. Then the prescribed-determinant comparison reduces in deterministic polynomial time to one ordinary three-tensor isomorphism instance over the same field. If $H(T')=(F^\times)^3$, the ordinary instance is just the original pair.

Put $I=\{i: \mathbf e_i\notin H(T')\}$, interpreting this membership in the exponent description above. These modes all have dimension at most $c\log_2 L$. Retain the usual trilinear tensor record, and for each $i\in I$ attach the exterior algebra $E_i=\bigwedge V_i$ as one role. Use its single bilinear multiplication record, the degree-one inclusion $V_i\to E_i$, and the top basis volume vector marked as a tensor of order one. Mark the source volume $\omega_i$ and the target volume $d_i\omega_i'$. The exterior algebra is generated by its degree-one space and unit. A record isomorphism extending $g_i$ therefore acts by $\bigwedge g_i$ and carries the marked vector precisely when $\det g_i=d_i$. Conversely every such $g_i$ extends to these records. This construction works in characteristic two using the usual exterior algebra with $v\wedge v=0$.

There are constantly many records of order at most three, including the full multiplication table of each exterior algebra as one bilinear record. Each attached space has dimension at most $2^{c\log_2 L}$, so their dense records have polynomial size. The tensor-system compiler gives one ordinary TI instance of polynomial size. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, *Actions by classical groups*, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

A transporter satisfying all three prescribed determinants already passes the records. Conversely a record transporter has the correct determinants on $I$. To correct any remaining mode, use the corresponding coordinate axis in $H(T')$. By definition each element of this subgroup lifts to a product of actual target automorphisms. Thus one can compose on the target to correct the remaining determinants while leaving the determinants on $I$ unchanged. The decision reduction needs no witness search or constructive norm inversion. In the case $H(T')=(F^\times)^3$ there are no additional records.

## 3. A hard class with local centroid and all three correction axes

**Theorem 2.** Over every field, ordinary three-tensor isomorphism reduces to isomorphism of concise multiplication tensors with equal mode dimensions, local centroid, and full determinant correction subgroup generated by the three pairs. The source and target have a transporter if and only if the new tensors have a transporter with any prescribed determinant triple. The multiplication tensors come from unital associative algebras whose Jacobson radical has cube zero and whose semisimple quotient is $F\times F$. The output side length is linear in the sum of the concise input side lengths.

First crop the mode radicals of the input $T$, which can be done by linear algebra. In each mode the radical is the kernel of the flattening, and the trilinear form factors through the quotient. For equal original dimensions, ordinary isomorphism is equivalent to equality of radical dimensions and isomorphism of the concise quotient tensors. The forward direction is intrinsic, and any quotient isomorphisms lift by arbitrary invertible maps on the radicals. Write the resulting concise form as a product map $T:U\times V\to W$, taking $W$ dual to the third raw mode.

Define $B=F\oplus (U\oplus V\oplus W)$ with its displayed scalar summand furnishing the unit. The only nonzero radical products are $uv=T(u,v)$ for $u\in U,v\in V$. These products lie in $W$, which annihilates the radical on both sides, so the algebra is associative. It is local, with radical $J_B=U\oplus V\oplus W$ and $J_B^3=0$. Conciseness gives $J_B^2=W$, and
\[
 \operatorname{Ann}_{\ell}(J_B)\cap J_B=V\oplus W,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Ann}_{r}(J_B)\cap J_B=U\oplus W .
\]
For example a radical element annihilates $J_B$ on the left exactly when its $U$ component is zero, since the radical of the first tensor mode is zero. The multiplication on the resulting intrinsic subquotients recovers exactly the product map $U\times V\to W$. Thus algebra isomorphisms of these $B$ give tensor transporters of the original concise inputs, and tensor transporters extend to algebra isomorphisms of $B$ by acting on the indicated summands.

Let $b=\dim_F B$, and take $C=F\oplus F^{b-1}$, with square-zero radical. Write $\epsilon_B,\epsilon_C$ for the two residue maps. Form the triangular algebra
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}B&F\\0&C\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The off-diagonal copy of $F$ is a $B$-$C$ bimodule via the residue maps. In coordinates multiplication is $(b_0,z,c_0)(b_1,w,c_1)=(b_0b_1,\epsilon_B(b_0)w+z\epsilon_C(c_1),c_0c_1)$. The ideal with diagonal entries in the radicals and arbitrary upper-right entry is nilpotent of cube zero, and the quotient is $F\times F$, so this ideal is exactly the radical. The two diagonal idempotents have a nonzero mixed corner only in the forward direction.

An isomorphism between two such algebras must carry their radical quotients to each other. Lifts of an ordered pair of diagonal idempotents in the quotient are simultaneously conjugate to the chosen diagonal idempotents. In fact for idempotents $f,e$ equal modulo the radical, the unit $u=fe+(1-f)(1-e)$ satisfies $ue=fu$ and aligns both complementary idempotents. After this adjustment the mixed-corner dimensions rule out exchanging the two quotient labels. Restriction to the first diagonal corner recovers an isomorphism of the original $B$ algebras. Conversely an isomorphism of $B$ preserves its residue map and extends to $A$ using the identity on $C$ and on the mixed corner. Hence the construction reflects and preserves tensor isomorphism through algebra isomorphism.

The center of $A$ consists of diagonal pairs from $Z(B),Z(C)$ of equal residue. Indeed commuting with the diagonal idempotents removes mixed entries, and commuting with the mixed corner imposes that single residue equality. This center is local because the common residue map to $F$ has nilpotent kernel and each nonzero residue element is invertible in the center.

Now use the trilinear multiplication tensor
\[
 \Theta_A(x,y,\lambda)=\lambda(xy)\qquad (x,y\in A,\ \lambda\in A^*).
\]
It is concise by the existence of a unit. A centroid element is a triple whose three separate actions on the respective arguments induce the same trilinear form. Setting one of $x,y$ equal to the unit shows that the first two maps must act by right and left multiplication by the same element $a$. The third map then acts dually by multiplication by $a$, and the two unit substitutions force $a$ to be central. Conversely every central element gives a centroid element. Thus the centroid is $Z(A)$ and is local.

Ordinary TI of unital associative multiplication tensors reflects algebra isomorphism. A tensor transporter gives invertible maps $g_1,g_2,f:A\to A'$ with $f(xy)=g_1(x)g_2(y)$, where $f$ is inverse dual to the map on the third mode. Set $a=g_1(1)$ and $b_0=g_2(1)$. Left multiplication by $a$ and right multiplication by $b_0$ are invertible linear maps by the identities with one input equal to the unit, so $a,b_0$ are units. Then
\[
 f(xy)=f(x)b_0^{-1}a^{-1}f(y).
\]
The map $x\mapsto b_0^{-1}a^{-1}f(x)$ is a unital algebra isomorphism. Conversely a unital algebra isomorphism gives a tensor transporter by using its inverse dual on the third mode. This is the unital case of the normalization for associative algebra tensors. [Bläser–Lysikov, *On degeneration of tensors and algebras*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04253)

Finally the unit $u=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha 1_B,\beta 1_C)$ gives
\[
 \det L_u=\alpha^{b+1}\beta^b,\qquad
 \det R_u=\alpha^b\beta^{b+1}.
\]
The mixed coordinate accounts for the extra $\alpha$ on the left and extra $\beta$ on the right. Associativity gives the pair adjoints $(R_u,L_u)$ on modes $1,2$, $(L_u,L_u^*)$ on modes $1,3$, and $(R_u,R_u^*)$ on modes $2,3$. Using diagonal units with $(\alpha,\beta)=(s,s^{-1})$, $(s^{-1},s)$ and $(1,s)$, respectively, provides the determinant triples
\[
 (s,1,s^{-1}),\qquad (1,s,s^{-1}),\qquad (s^{b+1},s^{-b},1)
\]
from the appropriate pairs. The first two types generate every triple of product one. The third has product $s$, which is arbitrary in $F^\times$. Thus these corrections generate all determinant triples over every field, without taking roots. This proves the claimed equivalence for any prescription.

Here $\dim A=2b+1$, which is linear in the original three concise dimensions. All structure constants are copied from $T$ or are $0$ and $1$. Cropping the radicals and forming the dense multiplication tensor are polynomial time. For mismatched dimensions or flattening ranks one can output a fixed negative pair from this same class. For example take the above construction on the concise multiplication tensors of $F^2$ and $F[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$. Those tensors are nonisomorphic by the same unital normalization, since the algebras have different nilpotent elements. The construction preserves this difference. This shows that the full-correction class of Theorem 1 is TI-complete even on the stated local-centroid subclass.

## 4. Unrestricted reverse inclusion and simultaneous cubic transport

The reverse inclusion in Open Question 1.9 holds over any field without an algebra construction. Add one new zero-support coordinate in each mode to both tensors. Every original isomorphism extends by multiplication by the inverse of its determinant on each new coordinate. Conversely, an isomorphism of the padded tensors preserves the mode radicals and induces an isomorphism of the concise quotients, hence of the original tensors, whose original radical dimensions agree. This yields a polynomial-time many-one reduction from ordinary TI to special-linear TI.

There is also a fixed two-dimensional determinant correction for simultaneous trilinear transport. Use the trilinear form
\[
 C(x,y,z)=x_1y_1z_2+x_1y_2z_1+x_2y_1z_1.
\]
Its simultaneous stabilizer contains $\operatorname{diag}(s,s^{-2})$, of determinant $s^{-1}$, for every $s\in F^\times$. Appending $C$ as a direct summand preserves and reflects ordinary simultaneous isomorphism after cropping the common radical. Here the common radical is the intersection of the three mode radicals in the single raw space. It is intrinsic for the simultaneous action, and any isomorphism of quotients lifts by arbitrary maps on equally sized common radicals.

For cancellation, on a form with zero common radical use the diagonal centroid, the set of endomorphisms whose action gives the same trilinear form in each argument. It is a commutative algebra. Moving two endomorphisms between the three positions shows that their commutator takes values in each of the three radicals, hence is zero. Their product then belongs to the diagonal centroid by moving both successively. The projections on a direct-sum decomposition belong to the diagonal centroid, and commuting with those projections forces other centroid maps to restrict independently to the summands. The primitive idempotents of this finite commutative algebra are canonical and give an orthogonal direct sum of the forms. Mixed contractions vanish by the centroid balancing identities. Therefore simultaneous isomorphism preserves the multiset of the resulting primitive components, so a fixed summand cancels. This proves that ordinary simultaneous trilinear comparison is equivalent to any prescribed total determinant on the calibrated forms. When $6$ is invertible the same construction for commutative cubic forms uses $x_1^2x_2$, with the reflection proof given by polarization.

The proofs are informal.
