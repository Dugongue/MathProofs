# Exact pole-cap cancellation and linear amplification under tensor powers

Let \(A_m^+\) and \(A_m^-\) be the two based algebras of the sparse Möbius-telescope construction, and denote their multiplication tensors by \(T_m^+\) and \(T_m^-\). Tensor products of three-tensors below are regrouped into three modes. Thus the multiplication tensor of \(A\otimes B\) is the regrouped product of the multiplication tensors of \(A,B\). A triple of nonnegative integers \((p_1,p_2,p_3)\) is feasible for a pair if some formal Laurent witness of that degeneration over \(k((t))\) has forward entry pole order at most \(p_i\) in mode \(i\). There is no bound on inverse matrices.

**Theorem.** Fix any coefficient field \(k\), and \(m,r\ge1\).
1. For \(T_m^{+\otimes r}\rightsquigarrow T_m^{-\otimes r}\), every feasible triple satisfies
\[
 p_1+p_2+p_3\ \ge\ \frac{r\,2^{m-1}}3.
\]
There is a witness with \(p_1+p_2+p_3=O(r2^m)\). The result is for arbitrary changes of basis on the regrouped modes. In fact every feasible triple for the powered pair is attained by monomial diagonal matrices.
2. Tensoring both sides with the multiplication tensor of any nonzero finite-dimensional separable semisimple \(k\)-algebra \(B\) leaves the entire feasible region of the powered pair exactly unchanged. This includes full matrix algebras of arbitrarily large size and nonsplit separable semisimple algebras.
3. Taking arbitrarily many identical direct-sum copies of these pairs also leaves the feasible region unchanged. More generally for equidimensional unital algebra pairs with each center \(k\), the region is unchanged by adding a fixed direct-product catalyst whose factors have center \(k\) and dimensions different from the dimension of the compared algebras.

The corresponding least order of a polynomial-matrix degeneration presentation is unchanged in part 2 and for repeated copies in part 3. It is at least \(r2^{m-1}/3\) and at most \(O(r2^m)\). A generic source arc of the displayed powered family is the \(r\)-th tensor power of the previous linear arc, so the tensor entries have degree at most \(r\), including after adjoining a constant tensor-product catalyst. [Sparse exponential pole construction](SparseSeed.md)

## 1. Intrinsic filtered corners after regrouping and matrix amplification

Write \(A_\pm=(A_m^\pm)^{\otimes r}\). In the earlier \(A_m^\pm\) there is a fixed finite vertex list, with diagonal basis \(e_i\) and one-dimensional strict corners \(k p_{ij}\) for the supported edges \(i\to j\). All edges point forward in the prescribed total vertex order, which is also the reachability order. Every strict basis element has degree \(1\) or \(2\), according as it is outside or inside \(J^2\) for the strict ideal \(J\). The same based choices work on both endpoints, and \(J^3=0\). Both tables are homogeneous for this grading. All retained products have coefficient \(1\), and the target deletes exactly one triangular product.

In \(A_\pm\) use vertices \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_r)\). A nonzero corner \(x\to y\) is the tensor product of the factor corners, and has dimension one over \(k\). The convention includes \(e_x\) for the diagonal corner. The strict ideal is the sum of the ideals with \(J\) in one factor. Its \(h\)-th power is the sum of the products of \(h\) such ideals, so it has precisely the basis elements whose total degree in the factors is at least \(h\). Indeed those products give all products \(\bigotimes_i J^{h_i}\) with nonnegative \(h_i\) and \(\sum h_i=h\), using \(J^0=A_m^\pm\). Thus every radical power has the same based rank on the two endpoints. The reachability order of the entire strict support is the product of \(r\) copies of the total order. Every poset automorphism permutes its coordinates: the nonzero join-irreducibles are the disjoint chains of tuples with precisely one nonminimal coordinate. A poset automorphism permutes those chains, fixes each order within a matched chain, and is determined by that action since each tuple is the join of its coordinate elements. Every such permutation lifts to a constant permutation of the indicated tensor factors.

We also allow the common matrix amplification \(A_\pm\otimes M_d(k)\), for any \(d\ge1\). Let \(I\) be its strict ideal, namely the strict ideal of \(A_\pm\) tensored with \(M_d(k)\). It can be read intrinsically as the two-sided ideal generated by values of the standard polynomial \(s_{2d}\). The inclusion into \(I\) follows from the Amitsur–Levitzki identity on the quotient \(\prod_x M_d(k)\). For the reverse, put the first \(2d-1\) matrix arguments at the source endpoint of any strict corner, using
\[
 E_{11},E_{12},E_{22},E_{23},\ldots,E_{d-1,d},E_{dd},
\]
and the last argument in that corner. Only terms with the corner argument last can be nonzero. The preceding list has just one ordering with nonzero product, namely the displayed order, whose product is \(E_{1d}\). Choose the corner argument so that the full product is a nonzero matrix unit, then multiply on both sides by diagonal-endpoint matrices to span the entire corner. This proves the ideal identity in every characteristic, including \(d=1\). [Amitsur–Levitzki, standard polynomial theorem](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1950-0035758-5)

Now let \(R=k[[t]]\), \(K=k((t))\), and take an arbitrary ordinary Laurent witness, written as \(m(x,y)=C\mu_+(Ax,By)\). Put \(p_A=p_1,p_B=p_2,p_C=p_3\). As in the original unital normalization, \(R_e x=m(x,e)\), \(L_e y=m(e,y)\) are integral invertible since their reductions are the identity. Let \(h\) be an integral invertible map sending \(e\) to \(m(e,e)\) and reducing to the identity, obtained by \(h=I+(m(e,e)-e)\ell\) with a fixed \(\ell(e)=1\). Normalize the law by \(\nu(x,y)=h^{-1}m(R_e^{-1} h x,L_e^{-1}h y)\). It reduces to the fixed target law and has fixed unit \(e\). Over \(K\) set \(a=Ae,b=Be\). The identities \(R_e=C R_b A\), \(L_e=C L_a B\) show that \(a,b\) are units. The generic algebra transporter \(H=h^{-1}C L_{ab}\) and the three unchanged-pole maps are related by
\[
 \widetilde A=A R_e^{-1}h=L_{ab}R_{b^{-1}}H^{-1},\qquad
 \widetilde B=B L_e^{-1}h=L_b H^{-1},\qquad
 \widetilde C=h^{-1}C=H L_{(ab)^{-1}}. \tag{1}
\]
These identities follow by substituting the two slice identities. In particular \(\nu=H\cdot\mu_+\) is associative and unital, and those identities hold integrally because the underlying \(R\)-modules are free. [Bläser–Lysikov, normalization for unital multiplication tensors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04253)

In \(\nu\) use \(I_R\) generated by the values of \(s_{2d}\), and its powers. Each is the image of a map between finite free modules, using multilinearity, products of such values, and the two outside multipliers. Its generic and special ranks agree by the endpoint descriptions above. A full-rank minor modulo \(t\) and elimination split off a free image with the indicated reduction: after eliminating its unit block any remaining matrix block is zero by the generic rank. Thus the filtration and all its graded modules are free direct-summand constructions.

The quotient of \(\nu\) by \(I_R\) splits as \(\prod_x M_d(R)\) with matrix units lifting the written special matrix units. Here is a direct proof. In the complete free quotient lift all standard diagonal idempotents, successively in complementary corners. For a lift \(f\), \(f^2-f\) lies in \(t\), and \((2f-1)^2=1+4(f^2-f)\) is invertible. The update \(f-(2f-1)^{-1}(f^2-f)\) squares the error and converges to an idempotent. The Peirce modules of these primitive idempotents are direct summands whose ranks agree with those of their reductions. They have rank one within each matrix factor and rank zero between factors. Within a factor take lifts \(f_i,g_i\) of the matrix units \(E_{i1},E_{1i}\). Normalize the unit \(g_i f_i\) in the first diagonal corner to its identity. Then \(f_i g_i\) is an idempotent in the \(i\)-th rank-one corner reducing to its identity, so it is that identity. The products \(f_i g_j\) are the required units. This argument also works at \(d=1\).

Every positive graded corner of \(\nu\) supported at \((x,y)\) of a given degree identifies with \(M_d(R)\) as bimodule for its two quotient blocks. Choose a generator of its \((11,11)\) corner reducing to \(p_{xy}\otimes E_{11}\). Left and right multiplication by the lifted matrix units gives a full basis since its reduction is the stated basis. All other positive corners are zero by rank. Thus \(H\) induces the same labelled support permutation as a product-grid automorphism. Precompose the generic transporter with the inverse constant source factor permutation to fix the labels. This changes no entry pole bounds. In these frames the quotient maps of \(H\) are \(X\mapsto S_x X S_x^{-1}\) for \(S_x\in\mathrm{GL}_d(K)\), by Skolem–Noether. On a positive corner its map is \(X\mapsto\eta_{xy}S_x X S_y^{-1}\) with \(\eta_{xy}\in K^\times\), since removing the two conjugating matrices leaves a bimodule endomorphism of the regular matrix bimodule, which is scalar. Take \(\eta_{xx}=1\) for diagonal corners. [Skolem–Noether theorem](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/074P)


## 2. Extracting integer pole caps, including for matrix catalysts

For every triple with nonzero source product of its supported corner basis elements, write \(c_{xyz}\) for the coefficient of matrix multiplication in the corresponding integral graded target product. This product is exactly \((X,Y)\mapsto c_{xyz}XY\): the graded product is balanced over the middle matrix block and equivariant over the outer ones, so it is determined by its value at the two \(E_{11}\)'s. Multiplying by matrix units gives the stated formula. Its reduction is \(1\) for retained products and \(0\) for deleted products. The equation for \(H\) gives
\[
 c_{xyz}=\eta_{xz}/(\eta_{xy}\eta_{yz}).
\]
Thus its valuation is zero for retained products and at least one for deleted ones.

For any matrix \(M\), write \(v(M)\) for its least entry valuation. If \(M\) is invertible then \(v(M)+v(M^{-1})\le0\), by evaluating the product at the identity. Write \(a_x,b_x\) for the images of the source isotope units in its quotient matrix block \(x\). Define the following integers:
\[
 s_x=v(S_x),\quad u_x=v(b_xS_x^{-1}),\quad q_x=v(a_xb_xS_x^{-1}),
 \qquad
 w_{xy}=v(\eta_{xy})+s_x-s_y,\quad
 \beta_x=u_x+s_x,\quad \alpha_x=q_x-u_x.
\]
The added vertex difference cancels on each composable triangle. Consequently \(w_{xz}-w_{xy}-w_{yz}\) has the required zero or positive defect at all products. Also \(w_{xx}=0\).

The graded maps in (1) on the \((x,y)\) corner are the following, written on matrix variables \(X\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widetilde A &: X\longmapsto \eta_{xy}^{-1}\,(a_xb_xS_x^{-1}) X(S_yb_y^{-1}),\\
 \widetilde B &: X\longmapsto \eta_{xy}^{-1}\,(b_xS_x^{-1}) XS_y,\\
 \widetilde C &: X\longmapsto \eta_{xy}\,\big(S_x(a_xb_x)^{-1}\big)XS_y^{-1}.
\end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]
These formulas apply on the quotient diagonal as well. Higher-degree parts of a radical unit contribution do not affect the graded maps. Bases adapted to the integral filtration, lifting the chosen graded bases, are integral with integral inverses. Hence the entry bounds on (1) also bound these graded matrices. The least entry valuation of the matrix of \(X\mapsto MXN\) is exactly \(v(M)+v(N)\), because each entry is one product of a coefficient of \(M\) and one of \(N\), and the two coordinate pairs range independently.

It follows that the extracted integers satisfy all the original scalar cap inequalities:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \alpha_x&\ge -p_A,& \beta_x&\ge -p_B,& \alpha_x+\beta_x&\le p_C,\\
 \alpha_x+\beta_x-\beta_y-w_{xy}&\ge -p_A,&
 \beta_x-w_{xy}&\ge -p_B,&
 w_{xy}-\alpha_x-\beta_x&\ge -p_C.
\end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
For the first edge expression substitute \(q_x-u_y-v(\eta_{xy})\), which is at least the valuation of the first map in (2), since \(-u_y\ge v(S_y b_y^{-1})\). The middle expression is exactly the valuation of the second map. For the last expression substitute \(v(\eta_{xy})-q_x-s_y\); this is at least the valuation of the third map since \(-q_x\ge v(S_x(a_xb_x)^{-1})\) and \(-s_y\ge v(S_y^{-1})\). Applying the same three inequalities on \(x=y\) gives the first row. This extraction is integral even though determinants of matrix blocks, if divided by block ranks, would give rational weights.

The integer conditions are sufficient on the unamplified power. Take \(H_0\) diagonal, of weight \(w_{xy}\) on its basis corner \(p_{xy}\) and zero on idempotents. Put \(a_0=\sum_x t^{\alpha_x}e_x\), \(b_0=\sum_x t^{\beta_x}e_x\) and use
\[
 A_0=L_{a_0b_0}R_{b_0^{-1}}H_0^{-1},\qquad
 B_0=L_{b_0}H_0^{-1},\qquad C_0=H_0 L_{(a_0b_0)^{-1}}.
\]
They are monomial diagonal matrices with the three cap bounds by (3). Their tensor action is the algebra law \(H_0\cdot\mu_+\). Retained structure coefficients are \(1\), and the deleted ones gain strictly positive exponents, so the result is integral with the desired reduction. Zero coefficients stay zero and idempotent products are unchanged. The same construction works over the original coefficient field even if the weights were extracted over a field extension. This proves feasibility equivalence for each \(M_d(k)\), and diagonal attainment in the unamplified case \(d=1\). The other direction tensors the base witness with three identity matrices.

## 3. Additive circulation on the diagonal cycle

For the powered pair, take the integers just extracted. The weights on the supported strict corners can be evaluated along walks, using a minus sign on traversing an edge backward. Holding all but one coordinate fixed gives the original edge graph. For each such fiber the retained face defects are zero and the omitted face defect is positive. Thus the earlier homology computation gives a bottom-cycle circulation on \(C_m\) of magnitude at least \(2^{m-1}\), with the **same sign** in every fiber and coordinate, since the boundary evaluation of the omitted face is the negative of its positive defect in the fixed orientation.

For two moves in distinct factors there is a square, with a common diagonal corner along the two forward moves. Both paths to that corner multiply with coefficient one in the source and in the target. Thus their sums of weights agree. In signed path notation, the flux around the square is zero. In particular any adjacent moves in distinct coordinates commute as far as the total signed weight is concerned, even when a move is backward. Likewise, for a simultaneous forward edge in several coordinates, the weight on the diagonal corner is the sum along its subdivision into one-factor moves. This follows by multiplying the distinct-factor steps consecutively. Every partial multiplication is retained with coefficient one. For a backward diagonal edge apply the same statement to its reversal.

Traverse the six-edge diagonal cycle in the product, assigning each vertex of \(C_m\) to the tuple with that vertex in every coordinate. Subdivide every move into one-factor moves. Now commute different-coordinate steps until all the moves of each factor are consecutive. This is possible by adjacent square exchanges, preserving signed total weight and the relative order within each coordinate. The result is a sum of \(r\) original bottom-cycle traversals, all based at the appropriate fixed coordinate vertices. Their signs agree by the omitted-face condition, so the diagonal circulation has magnitude at least \(r2^{m-1}\).

At each of the three original vertices of the subdivided circle, its two incident circle edges are oriented toward the edge barycenters. The same is true in the diagonal cycle. Thus the circulation is a sum of three signed differences of edge weights with the same starting vertex within each difference. For each \(x\), (3) puts all its outgoing weights in
\[
 [\,\alpha_x+\beta_x-p_C,\ \beta_x+p_B\,],
\]
whose length is at most \(p_A+p_B+p_C\) because \(\alpha_x\ge-p_A\). This proves the lower bound \(p_A+p_B+p_C\ge r2^{m-1}/3\). Tensoring the original diagonal witness with itself \(r\) times gives the matching \(O(r2^m)\) upper bound. Its displayed orbit tensor arc is the tensor power of the previous linear arc.

## 4. Splitting direct sums and finishing semisimple cancellation

Here is an exact center argument for direct sums, independently of the matrix-block analysis. Suppose the source and special algebras in a witness are direct products of finitely many algebras with one-dimensional centers. Suppose the number of factors is the same on the two sides. Normalize the arbitrary ordinary Laurent witness as in (1). The center of the integral law is the kernel of the linear commutator map against a full basis. Generic and special ranks agree, so by unit-minor elimination the center is a free direct summand with the prescribed fibers. Lift the split coordinate idempotents of its special center by the commutative version of the idempotent iteration above. Since they are central they split the whole integral law into actual algebra summands, each reducing to its specified special summand. Choose integral bases of these summands reducing to the specified target bases. The map \(H\) necessarily sends source central factors to their matched lifted factors, with ranks agreeing. Each of the multiplication-by-unit maps in (1) preserves the corresponding factors, so all three normalized maps restrict in the same matching. Their entry pole bounds on each restricted block are unchanged by integral basis changes and projection. The restriction gives an ordinary Laurent witness between the two individual matched algebras.

For identical repeated copies this yields one copy of the original comparison. In the dimension-distinguished catalyst statement the factor to compare is forced by its dimension and the unchanged catalyst dimensions. Conversely a block diagonal witness gives feasibility on the full direct product, with identity maps on the unchanged factors. This proves the claimed exact region comparisons without assuming the original witness is block diagonal.

For \(A_\pm\), the center is \(k\): commuting with every vertex idempotent forces a central element to be diagonal, and the connected support forces its scalars equal. For \(A_\pm\otimes M_d(k)\) the center remains \(k\) since it is \(M_d(A_\pm)\). A separable semisimple \(B\) is a product of full matrix algebras after some field extension. Extend an alleged Laurent witness to a splitting field, which does not change valuation bounds. Apply the center argument to the resulting product of matrix amplifications, and choose any matched factor. Its source and target matrix sizes agree because the center-factor dimensions are \(\dim(A_\pm)d^2\), with \(\dim(A_+)=\dim(A_-)\). The integer extraction and the monomial sufficiency in Section 2 give an unamplified witness back over the original field with all the same caps. Conversely tensor with the identity actions on \(B\). This proves cancellation of the whole semisimple catalyst, as well as the repeated-copy claims, for the exact feasible region.

Finally all the multiplication tensors here are concise since the algebra is unital. For a polynomial-matrix presentation whose leading coefficient is the target, the three polynomial matrices are generically invertible by that target's full flattening ranks. Dividing one matrix by the leading power of \(t\) yields a Laurent witness with pole sum at most that order. Conversely the minimum feasible sum is attained by Laurent monomial matrices in the powered comparison and can be kept after adjoining the catalyst or taking repeated copies. Clearing the poles separately gives a polynomial presentation of that order. Thus the minima coincide exactly in these cases.

## Scope

The integer circulation argument and the sparse tensors are from the preceding exponential-poles construction. Here the new conclusions are amplification linear in tensor-power degree, exact cancellation of separable semisimple factors for the entire forward-pole region, and the center-based direct-product cancellation. Matrix-unit normalization and the integer gauges are applied to the actual recovered transporter, rather than assuming it respects the auxiliary tensor decomposition. These are written proofs for relative degeneration from the indicated source tensor. They do not bound relative order from a different source, or establish catalysis cancellation for arbitrary nonsemisimple tensor-product factors. [Comparison of degeneration order with fixed rank source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10179v1)
