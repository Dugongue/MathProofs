# The geometry of partially observed laws

A partial observation generally leaves many compatible states. For a parameterized response, the primary object is the **entire family of compatible responses**, rather than the single state selected by a fitting procedure. The field asks which laws hold throughout that family, which laws are excluded, where violations first occur, and what further observations settle the law. For example, concavity of a phase-moment response can survive on every proper coordinate face and fail when all coordinates are present.

## What to study in any instance

1. **Observation fiber.** Specify admissible states, permitted probes, their refinement and cost, and the exact or noisy trace. A symmetry or extendibility condition also changes the fiber, even when it supplies no new measured values.
2. **Response geometry.** Take the joint image of the fiber under all requested evaluations. When the image is convex, its support function gives simultaneous bounds on every linear combination. Equality at a particular evaluation gives only a one-dimensional part of this geometry.
3. **Law profile.** Compare the response family with the law. A law is forced, excluded or undetermined at each feasible trace. For regional laws, record the inclusion-minimal regions where a compatible response fails.
4. **Certification.** Find the least observation cost or sample budget that settles the law. Distinguish exact probes, noisy calibration, and statistical queries that return fresh samples. Determining the response, deciding a law, and constructing a violating state can have distinct costs.

The same data structure admits classical probability laws, quantum density operators, and finite linear experiments. An example begins with random phases $\xi_i$ on the unit circle. With $k=2m$, the response is $F(x)=\mathbb E|\sum_i x_i^{1/k}\xi_i|^k$ on the nonnegative orthant. The original theorem realized every $k$-uniform hypergraph as the minimal concavity failures while all $(k-1)$-coordinate marginals and all lower even responses agreed with independent Haar phases. [Original proposal and theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/FourFieldProposals/PartiallyObservedLaws.md)

## A worked separation between marginal order and failure size

Take the fourth moment. Give every three-coordinate term with one doubled exponent a coefficient $\alpha>0$, and every squarefree four-coordinate term a coefficient $-\beta$; add these to the independent response. Scale $\alpha$ and $\beta$ together to a sufficiently small size. The result comes from a smooth positive phase density, with product-Haar pair marginals and the independent second moment. Its restriction to any face of size $s\ge4$ is concave exactly when $\beta\le 6\alpha/(s-3)$. For example, with $\beta=7\alpha/4$, every face on at most six coordinates is concave and every seven-coordinate face fails. The same fourth-order response construction can move the first failure to any larger size. [Threshold and proof](PhaseMoments.md)

This separates three quantities. The **interaction order** is how many coordinates appear together in a single term. The **observation order** is how many can be measured jointly. The **minimal failure size** is how many coordinates must act together before the law fails. The first can remain fixed while the last increases without bound.

## Conjectures that organize the next mathematical questions

- **Failure families on the original ground set.** For fixed $k=2m$, determine whether every antichain whose members have size at least $k-1$ can occur with all $(k-2)$-marginals and all lower even moments fixed to independence, without adding coordinates. The private-coordinate realization and the exact threshold are positive cases. Nonnegativity of the weighted boundary polynomials is a concrete proposed criterion to test against interior curvature. [Phase questions](PhaseMoments.md)
- **The intermediate statistical regime.** For fresh samples of $b$-coordinate probes and one hidden edge, let $K=\binom b{2m}$. The proposed adaptive cost at fixed risk is $\Theta((D/K)\epsilon^{-2}\log(K+1))$. The exact endpoint at $b=2m$ and the full-observation endpoint support this formula. The general fractional-cover bound and the nonadaptive fixed-budget rate give benchmarks. [Sampling conjectures](Inference.md)
- **Sharp higher-order exchangeability.** At each fixed $m\ge3$, the proposed limiting most negative coefficient, multiplied by $N^m$, is $-\lfloor m/2\rfloor!\lceil m/2\rceil!$. The scalar optimum is proved and the fourth-order case attains it. The higher-order question is whether a phase law with the exact required marginals can realize the limiting quadrature law. [Exchangeability conjectures](Extensions.md)
- **Cost of decomposing observed information.** For acyclic marginal scopes, ask whether every observed integrable function decomposes into functions of its $s$ scopes with sum of norms at most $C\sqrt{s}$ times its own norm. The uniform binary path has this exact scale. The unbounded-alphabet and unbounded-scope statement is open in this treatment. The same chapter asks about finite shape-test grids and approximate classical representations of observed quantum response regions. [Foundational conjectures](Foundations.md)

## Relationship to prior work and verification

The natural predecessors are identified sets, marginal polytopes and moment duality, Fourier methods for limited independence, and joint numerical ranges. The program developed here links their respective notions of visibility and compatibility to a law profile, minimal failure regions, and exact observation costs. The sharp collective-curvature threshold, the access-structure cost, the specific exact-versus-statistical phase separation, and the phase exchangeability constructions are the main additions.
