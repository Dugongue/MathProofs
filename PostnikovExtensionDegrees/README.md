# Unbounded source dimension precludes a general three-tier extension theorem

This note gives obstructions to removing the fixed source-dimension and explicit finite Postnikov-data hypotheses from a relative extension classification. The targets are simply connected simplicial abelian groups with finitely many simplices in every degree. In particular they admit strict Mal'cev operations. The proofs are informal.

## Every computably enumerable Turing degree occurs

**Theorem.** For every computably enumerable set $E\subseteq\mathbb N$, there is a fixed effectively presented simplicial abelian group $Y_E$, simply connected with finitely many simplices in every degree, such that unrestricted relative extension into $Y_E$ is computably enumerable and has Turing degree $\deg_T(E)$. For each fixed source-dimension bound, the corresponding nonuniform slice is in P.

Let $r_n=2n+2$. Construct a chain complex $C$ over $\mathbb F_2$ with a generator $a_n$ in degree $r_n$ for every $n$, and a generator $b_n$ in degree $r_n+1$ exactly when $n\in E$. The nonzero differentials are $\partial b_n=a_n$. The basis symbol $b_n$ includes the first stage at which the chosen enumerator outputs $n$. Checking that certificate requires only a finite simulation. One may write the stage in unary. In each degree the possible nonzero vector has a unique valid code, and all chain operations on valid codes are computable. This presentation does not promise a uniformly computable list of all generators in a given degree.

Let $Y_E=\Gamma C$ under Dold–Kan. The explicit formula in degree $q$ is the direct sum of $C_k$ over the monotone surjections $[q]\twoheadrightarrow[k]$. Thus every degree is finite, valid sparse simplex codes and their face and degeneracy operations are computable, and $Y_E$ is Kan. Its homotopy groups are the homology groups of $C$. They are zero in degrees zero and one and either zero or $\mathbb F_2$ in each positive degree. The operation $x-y+z$ is simplicial and satisfies the Mal'cev identities strictly. [Dold–Kan correspondence](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/019D) · [Homotopy groups of simplicial abelian groups](https://people.math.binghamton.edu/malkiewich/spectra_book_draft.pdf)

Use finite simplicial pairs and exact boundary maps. By applying the free simplicial $\mathbb F_2$-vector-space functor and then normalized chains, a map $X\to Y_E$ is exactly a chain map $N\mathbb F_2[X]\to C$. This identification commutes with restriction to $A\subseteq X$. As boundary encoding one may list the images of the nondegenerate generators of $A$ in normalized chains and verify the chain equations. This specifies a simplicial boundary map by the equivalence.

To reduce membership in $E$ to extension, use $(X,A)=(\Delta^{r_n+1},\partial\Delta^{r_n+1})$. On the boundary send one $r_n$-face to $a_n$ and the other nondegenerate generators to zero. This is always a valid chain map because $\partial a_n=0$. The top simplex can be filled exactly when $a_n$ is in the image of $\partial:C_{r_n+1}\to C_{r_n}$, which holds exactly when $n\in E$. No query to $E$ is needed to write the boundary. Conversely an oracle for $E$ determines the finite target chain complex in the degrees used by any given input. Extension then reduces to a finite system of linear equations over $\mathbb F_2$. Hence the extension language is Turing equivalent to $E$.

To semidecide without an oracle, enumerate the $b_n$ through the source dimension. Once all certificates used by the prescribed boundary have appeared, test the linear system after each finite stage. Each stage embeds into the next as a subcomplex. An extension uses only finitely many generators and is found at a finite stage. For each fixed $D$ the true chain tables through degree $D$ can be built into a machine, so each fixed-dimension slice has a polynomial-time linear algebra algorithm. The family of these algorithms need not be computable uniformly from $D$. For an intermediate computably enumerable Turing degree, the unrestricted extension language is undecidable and fails to be computably enumerable complete. It therefore lies outside P, NP-complete and computably enumerable complete. This already happens for the diagram with a single object. [Friedberg's solution to Post's problem](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC528418/)

## Even computable lists of finite levels do not give the complexity trichotomy

**Theorem.** There is a fixed simply connected simplicial abelian group $Y$ for which all simplex sets can be listed effectively degree by degree and the unrestricted extension problem is decidable outside NP. This holds with ordinary explicit finite simplicial complexes as sources.

Choose a decidable unary language $L$ outside deterministic double-exponential time. Such a language follows by diagonalizing over clocked double-exponential-time deterministic machines on their indexed unary inputs. Use the same chain complex pattern, now with both $a_n$ and $b_n$ always present. Define $\partial b_n=a_n$ if $1^n\in L$ and zero otherwise. The chain groups have an effective full finite basis in every degree, and the differentials are uniformly computable. Dold–Kan gives effective full finite simplex lists in every degree. The normalized linear system gives decidability of extension by computing the finitely many required differential entries.

The simplex-boundary construction above reduces $1^n\in L$ to an extension instance with length and construction time at most exponential in $n$, even if every face of the simplex is listed. The boundary requires no differential computation from $L$. If extension were in NP, deterministically simulating its nondeterministic verifier on this instance would decide $L$ within double-exponential time in $n$. This contradicts its choice. Since extension is decidable, it is also outside the computably enumerable complete branch. With a normalized simplicial-set encoding one can instead use the quotient $\Delta^{r_n+1}/\Lambda_i^{r_n+1}$ of a simplex by a horn and the image of its boundary. There is only one surviving $r_n$-face and one top nondegenerate simplex above the point, giving a polynomial-size version of the same reduction.

These results concern unrestricted source dimension. They leave the fixed-height, fixed-dimension mixed finite-diagram conjecture with explicitly supplied Postnikov data open. They show why a general theorem must retain a dimension bound or impose a uniform resource bound on the effective presentation across dimensions.
