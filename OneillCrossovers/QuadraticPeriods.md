# Exact quotients for four-coloring quadratic-difference graphs

Let $\Gamma_7$ be the graph on $\mathbb Z^2$ with undirected steps $(d,\pm d^2)$ for $1\le d\le7$. Define $K=\langle(8,0),(4,8)\rangle$. For any subgroup $\Lambda\le\mathbb Z^2$, the quotient graph $\Gamma_7/\Lambda$ is four-colorable if and only if $\Lambda\le K$. In particular, the smallest periodic quotient has 64 vertices, and the marked graph on any finite cyclic quotient is never four-colorable. This uses the finite forcing certificates in the quadratic van der Waerden release and gives an exact classification of the periodic local-constraint model. [O’Neill, Kirkby and Astra, Proposition 7 and Lemmas 12, 15 and 16](https://charlesponeill.com/maths-releases/quadratic-differences/quadratic-differences.pdf).

Let $c$ be a proper global four-coloring. Apply Lemma 16 to the 348 even points in the indicated $12$ by $58$ rectangle. It gives a base point $(u_0,v_0)$ and a forcing configuration on a parity coset $C$ of $L=\{(x,y):x+y\equiv0\bmod2\}$ in coarse coordinates, with embedding $(x,y)\mapsto(u_0+2x,v_0+4y)$. In these coordinates the coarse steps are $(d,\pm d^2)$ for $d=1,2,3$. Lemma 15 uses only the edges in the finite coarse box $[-6,8]\times[0,9]$ on that coset. All are available in the global graph. Hence $c$ agrees, up to a renaming of colors, with

$$
\phi(x,y)=\bigl(x\bmod2,\ \lfloor(y-x)/2\rfloor\bmod2\bigr)
$$

throughout the $3$ by $10$ coarse block on $C$.

For each target in the next column in that strip, each of the three other $\phi$ values can be reached by a choice of two earlier neighbors. Their vertical offsets have opposite signs and magnitudes $(1,9)$, $(9,1)$, or $(4,4)$, respectively, by the color changes in Lemma 12. Each pair has combined span at most ten, so at least one of the two neighbors stays in rows $0$ through $9$. For clarity, if both offsets missed, their magnitudes would sum to at least eleven. This determines the next column. Reflection and the same color differences determine the earlier columns as well. For a target above the strip, the offsets $(1,-1),(-1,-1),(2,-4)$ go to already determined rows and have the three distinct nonzero color differences. Negate these for a target below the strip. Induction determines the whole coset $C$.

In the original coordinates the coarse coset is a translate of $H=\{(2a,4b):a+b\text{ even}\}$, and its equal-color translations are exactly $2H=K$, by Lemma 12. If $v$ is a global period of $c$, let $\ell$ be the order of its image in $\mathbb Z^2/H$. This order is a power of two since $H$ has index 16. Then $\ell v\in H$ is a global period, so $\ell v\in 2H$. If $\ell>1$ then $(\ell/2)v\in H$, contradicting minimality. Hence $v\in K$. Pulling back a proper quotient coloring gives a global coloring with every element of $\Lambda$ a period, proving necessity. Quotient loops in this convention forbid proper colorings.

For sufficiency, the $8$ by $16$ tile in Proposition 7 is invariant under $(4,8)$. This is checked by shifting each of its first four rows horizontally by eight entries and obtaining the corresponding last four rows. It is already periodic under $(8,0)$, so it descends to $\Gamma_7/K$ and to each finer quotient. The generators of $K$ have determinant 64 and their entries have gcd 4. Smith normal form gives $\mathbb Z^2/K\simeq\mathbb Z/4\times\mathbb Z/16$.

Equivalently, if a finite abelian group $A$ is generated by a marked pair $(a,b)$, its constraint graph with steps $da\pm d^2b$ is four-colorable precisely when $\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z^2/K$ factors through the marked surjection $\mathbb Z^2\to A$. This gives a test by two integer relation generators for the marked pair and applies without any restriction on the aspect ratio of the quotient.

## Degree-350 refutations over every field

Encode the four colors by Boolean indicators, with exactly-one equations, pairwise color exclusions and edge exclusions. Every finite quotient with $\Lambda\not\subseteq K$ has a Polynomial Calculus refutation of degree at most 350, over every field.

Use the fixed 348-point rectangle $R$ from Lemma 16. By the exactly-one axioms, expand the product of the color sums of its points as $1=\sum_\rho M_\rho$, where $\rho:R\to\{1,2,3,4\}$ and $M_\rho$ is the product of the indicated quotient variables. This derivation uses degree at most 348. Repeated variables from different lifts of the same quotient vertex cause no problem. If $\rho$ violates an edge, its monomial vanishes by an edge exclusion.

For a proper assignment $\rho$, Lemma 16 supplies the seed. For each seed point assigned color $c$, Boolean equations give $M_\rho X_{v,c}=M_\rho$. If a neighboring point $w$ has already been forced to color $d$, multiply its conditional equality by $X_{v,d}$ and use the edge exclusion to get $M_\rho X_{v,d}=0$. With three differently colored neighbors, the exactly-one equation forces the remaining color. These derivations use degree at most $348+2$ and apply to every propagation step above.

An offending vector of $\Lambda$ has some multiple in $H\setminus K$ by the minimal-order argument. Some two points of the forced coarse coset then agree in the quotient and have different prescribed colors. Both are forced in finitely many steps from the seed. Their two conditional equalities and the pairwise color exclusion give $M_\rho=0$. Summing the vanishing branches gives $1=0$. This also gives a polynomial-size derivation, with an absolute constant depending on the fixed rectangle. In each branch, use a worklist on the finite quotient. Each vertex needs at most one stored color until the first clash. A finite forcing sequence exists by the lifted propagation, so saturation or an earlier clash suffices.
