# Circular edge-coloring thresholds with prescribed computational complexity

This theorem concerns the representation of a fixed real comparison parameter in the finite-diagram trichotomy program. Its graph input is the interval-realization theorem for connected simple 9-regular graphs. [O’Neill, Kirkby and Astra, Corollary 14](https://charlesponeill.com/maths-releases/circular-index-denominators/circular-index-denominators.pdf).

Put $I=(19/2,29/3)$. For $r\in I$, let $L_r$ consist of connected simple 9-regular graphs $G$ with $\chi'_c(G)\le r$. Then $L_r$ is Turing-equivalent to $\{q\in\mathbb Q:q\le r\}$. Moreover, some irrational $r\in I$ is polynomial-time computable as a real and has $L_r$ decidable outside NP.

To compute $\chi'_c(G)$, enumerate cyclic orders of its edges and assign a nonnegative gap to each consecutive pair. For each adjacent pair of edges in the graph require the sum on each of the two arcs between them to be at least $1$. Minimize the sum of all gaps by rational linear programming. A circular coloring induces one of these orders, including an arbitrary order within ties. Conversely, a feasible gap vector gives a circular edge-coloring. The minimum over the finitely many orders is the exact rational index. This gives the reduction from $L_r$ to the lower cut. For the reverse reduction, compare immediately outside $I$. For a rational $q\in I$, enumerate connected simple 9-regular graphs and evaluate their indices until an index equal to $q$ is found. The cited realization theorem guarantees termination. Membership of this graph in $L_r$ is exactly membership of $q$ in the cut.

For the second claim, enumerate all polynomially clocked nondeterministic machines $M_i$. Start with a dyadic center $q_1$ strictly inside $I$. Choose a sufficiently large starting tick so that all later movements fit inside $I$. At stage $i$, find a graph $G_i$ of index $q_i$ by the terminating procedure above. Run the finite computation tree of $M_i$ on $G_i$. At its completion tick $t_i$, move the center by $2^{-t_i-2}$ upwards if $M_i$ rejects and downwards if it accepts. Insert enough idle ticks that successive move times differ by at least two. Future changes have total absolute size at most one third of the current jump. Thus the limiting $r$ lies strictly on the chosen side of each $q_i$. On $G_i$ the machine $M_i$ gives the wrong answer.

Use a fixed ordinary machine to perform the search and computation-tree simulations incrementally, charging its work to ticks. The current center can be stored as a dyadic with $O(t)$ bits at tick $t$, and updating it costs polynomial time in $t$. Replaying the first $n$ ticks and outputting the current center takes polynomial time in $n$. The tail is bounded by $\sum_{t>n}2^{-t-2}<2^{-n}$, so this is a polynomial-time Cauchy presentation.

If $r$ were rational, $L_r$ would be in NP. Guess a cyclic edge order and solve the rational linear program for that order with an added total-length upper bound $r$. Consequently the diagonalized $r$ is irrational. Comparing its successive approximations with the exact rational $\chi'_c(G)$ terminates, which proves decidability. This language therefore falls outside all three tiers of the proposed P, NP-complete, and $\Sigma^0_1$-complete classification. The conclusion concerns models allowing exact comparison with a fixed real presented by a polynomial-time approximation algorithm.
