# Optimal uniform splitting probes for cyclotomic forms

This sharpens the arithmetic probe question in the mixed trichotomy and tensor-isomorphism programs. It concerns a common panel chosen for all forms with the same conductor bound. The small-prime bound uses the least-prime release. [O’Neill, Kirkby, Astra and Fable, Theorem 1](https://charlesponeill.com/maths-releases/least-prime-ap/least-prime-ap.pdf).

Let $A=(\mathbb Z/m)^\times$. Suppose that a probe at a prime $p\nmid m$ asks whether $p$ splits completely in a subfield of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)$. The exact minimum size of a nonadaptive panel identifying every subfield is the number of nontrivial cyclic subgroups of $A$ of prime-power order.

By cyclotomic Galois theory a field fixed by $H\le A$ answers yes exactly when the residue $g=p\bmod m$ belongs to $H$. For each cyclic prime-power subgroup $C$, let $C^-$ be its unique maximal proper subgroup. Any panel distinguishing $C$ and $C^-$ must query a generator of $C$, because $C\setminus C^-$ is exactly the set of its generators. A residue generates only one subgroup. This proves the lower bound. For the upper bound, query a generator of each such $C$. The yes answers recover precisely the cyclic prime-power subgroups contained in $H$. They generate $H$: the prime-power components of each element of $H$ are powers of that element and multiply to it. Dirichlet’s theorem realizes each required residue by an unramified prime.

If the family is restricted to the split quadratic algebra and the quadratic subfields, put $r=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}(A/A^2)$. Then exactly $r$ splitting probes identify all $2^r$ forms, even allowing adaptivity. Evaluating all quadratic characters on basis elements gives the upper bound. A binary decision tree of depth less than $r$ has too few leaves. On quadratic multiplication tensors this is exactly the test of tensor isomorphism to the split form at each prime. Every inert answer gives the degree obstruction in [the quadratic certificate proof](QuadraticTensorCertificates.md).

The cited theorem gives absolute $C,\delta>0$ such that for more than 58.7% of the moduli in every sufficiently large dyadic interval, all probes in either optimal panel can be chosen with $p\le C m^{2-\delta}$. It suffices to take the least prime in each chosen reduced residue class. The panel cardinality assertions use no distribution estimate.

## Error-correcting local signatures

The proof of Lemma 4 yields more than a least prime for the moduli it keeps. Maynard’s estimate there gives $\gg m^{1-\delta}/\log m$ distinct primes in each reduced residue class below $4m^{2-\delta}$. In particular a common panel of $O(r)$ distinct primes can realize a binary linear code of dimension $r$ and positive relative distance, even if some generator columns repeat. For any chosen generator matrix, interpret its columns as elements of $A/A^2$, and choose a fresh prime in a representative class for each column. The vector of inertness bits of a quadratic form then equals its message vector times that generator matrix.

For example an $r$ by $8r$ matrix of independent fair bits has a choice for which every nonzero message has output weight at least $r$. The probability that a given nonzero message has smaller weight is at most $2^{-8r(1-h_2(1/8))}$ by the binomial entropy bound, where $h_2$ is binary entropy. The union bound over fewer than $2^r$ messages is less than one. Thus the chosen matrix has full row rank and realizes a bounded-prime panel correcting up to $\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor$ erroneous answers. No extra number-theoretic hypothesis is needed beyond those in the least-prime manuscript.
