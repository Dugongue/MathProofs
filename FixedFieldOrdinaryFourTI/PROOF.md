# Ordinary Four-Tensor Isomorphism at Fixed Field Size

**Theorem.** For every fixed finite field $\mathbb F_q$, there is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time algorithm for ordinary isomorphism of two ordered $n\times n\times n\times n$ tensors that may abstain. Every answer it returns is correct. For a uniformly random first input and every second input, its recognition probability tends to
$$
 1-c(q)^3,\qquad c(q)=\prod_{j\geq1}(1-q^{-j}).
$$
More precisely it recognizes all but an $o(1)$ fraction of the first inputs with at least one singular pair flattening, and its recognizer abstains on the all-invertible stratum. A single specified cut suffices for coverage $1-c(q)-o(1)$. On the recognized class it also gives an objectwise reduction to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism of total dimension $O(n^{4/3})$. All proofs here are informal.

The limit is about 97.59% for $q=2$ and is $3/q+O(q^{-2})$ as $q$ grows after the fixed-field limit. In particular it is $\Theta(1/q)$ with absolute constants, answering [Li–Li–Qiao–Tao–Wang, arXiv v2 Problem 1.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00591v2) affirmatively. That question is numbered 1.6 in v1. Their Theorem 1.3 uses dimension-three kernel codes to get an inverse-polynomial-in-$q$ fraction. Our algorithm uses all fixed positive coranks, including singular forms in one-dimensional kernels, and does not require squarefree spectra. The remaining all-invertible sector has limiting mass $c(q)^3$.

## 1. Use kernel forms to turn four basis changes into two conjugations

Choose the $12\mid34$ cut. Its flattening has side $N=n^2$. Write $c$ for its corank and require $1\leq c<n$ and $q^c\leq n$. These tests permit enumeration of every nonzero form in each of the two $c$-dimensional kernels. The left and right kernel spaces are spaces of bilinear forms on the respective pair of mode spaces.

For a chosen left kernel form $L:V_1\times V_2\to\mathbb F_q$ of rank $r=n-k$, embed $V_1$ into a space $E$ of dimension $n+k$ and embed $V_2$ into $E^*$. The restriction of the evaluation pairing to the two images is $L$. Explicitly, choose complements to the radicals $R_1,R_2$ of $L$, put $E=V_1\oplus R_2^*$, and map $y\in V_2$ to $(L(-,y),\operatorname{pr}_{R_2}y)\in E^*$. Do the same on the other pair with the chosen right kernel form and a space $F$.

Every isometry of the chosen form pair extends to a simultaneous primal and dual change of basis of the enlarged pair. To see this on one pair, write $p:E\to V_2^*$ for the surjection induced by the embedding of $V_2$, and $p'$ for the target surjection. Its kernel is the left radical and lies inside $V_1$. If $g_1,g_2$ preserve the chosen pairing, $p'g_1=g_2^{-\mathsf T}p$ on $V_1$. The maps on the quotients $E/V_1\to V_2^*/p(V_1)$ and their target counterparts are isomorphisms. Extend $g_1$ on a complement to satisfy $p'\widetilde g_1=g_2^{-\mathsf T}p$ there, which is possible by surjectivity and yields an invertible extension on the quotient. The action on $V_2$ is then the restriction of the dual action. This also proves independence of the choices of complements in the construction.

For each form choice the embedded tensor $Z$ belongs to $\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes\operatorname{End}(F)$, and we compare such tensors under independent conjugations on the two endomorphism factors. Enumerating both form choices on both inputs accounts for the entire original isomorphism group. A source isomorphism carries the two kernel spaces contragrediently to the target spaces, so it transports every exact form choice to a target choice with the same ranks. For reflection, every singleton support in the original tensor is full: otherwise the pair flattening has rank at most $n(n-1)$, contradicting $c<n$. In the enlarged format these supports are exactly the images of the four original spaces. Therefore any conjugation equivalence of embedded tensors restricts to an original tensor isomorphism. This argument requires no invertible representative in the kernel.

## 2. The only large random blocks that matter

Let
$$
 A(Z)=\operatorname{Tr}_F(Z^2),\qquad B(Z)=\operatorname{Tr}_E(Z^2).
$$
These conjugate on the respective factors. In the block order of live complement, left radical, and added space for a chosen form, every matrix in the first enlarged pair has shape
$$
 \begin{pmatrix}X&0&C\\ D&0&H\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The live block $X$ has side $r$. Products stay in this shape, with live block the product of live blocks, and traces come entirely from the live block. Project $Z$ to $Z_0$ on the live blocks of the two pairs. The live blocks of $A(Z)$ and $B(Z)$ are exactly the two quadratic partial traces of $Z_0^2$. The full characteristic polynomials of $A$ and $B$ are those of the live blocks multiplied by $z^{2k}$ for the respective radical dimensions $k$. Off-diagonal radical entries need not vanish and will be handled by the exact algorithm.

Condition on the two exact kernel spaces of the original cut, and on deterministic choices of a form from each. In bases of their annihilators, of dimension $m=N-c$, the tensor is a uniform invertible coefficient matrix. This follows because it represents an isomorphism of the right-kernel quotient onto the annihilator of the left kernel. The projected live spaces $S_1,S_2$ have codimension at most $c$ in their respective full matrix spaces, because the original annihilators had codimension $c$ and projection is onto before those constraints. Both live spaces are trace-zero, by the two chosen kernel equations.

If at least one chosen form is singular, then its live dimension is at most $(n-1)^2$, at least $2n-c-1$ below $m$. In suitable bases, $Z_0$ is a rectangular coordinate block of a uniform invertible $m$-by-$m$ matrix. It is exponentially close in total variation to uniform on $S_1\otimes S_2$. Indeed if the number of projected columns is $b<m$, the first $b$ columns are uniform among independent columns, with total variation distance at most $q^{b-m}/(q-1)$ from independent uniform columns, by sequential rank counting. Projection of rows cannot increase total variation. If the small side is the row side use transposition. If both forms are invertible, there is no projection loss. In that case we first analyze a uniform coefficient matrix and then condition on invertibility, an event of probability at least $c(q)>1/4$.

For each fixed $c\geq1$, the two kernel spaces are independent uniform $c$-spaces. A uniform nonzero form chosen from either space is a uniform nonzero $n$-by-$n$ matrix. Its radical dimension is at most $(\log_q n)^{1/4}$ with probability $1-o(1)$ by the elementary rank count. We use this random choice only in the proof of coverage. The algorithm enumerates all choices. It remains to prove that the live quadratic partial traces typically have a small repeated-primary budget, even after these conditionings.

## 3. The live partial traces have almost-uniform joint law

For now let $Z_0$ be uniform on $S_1\otimes S_2$. Write $r_i$ for the two live side dimensions. In odd characteristic the two partial traces of $Z_0^2$ have equal trace. In characteristic two their traces are both zero, since $\operatorname{tr}(Z_0^2)=(\operatorname{tr}Z_0)^2=0$. Let $\mathcal H$ be the corresponding space of pairs of matrices. We show the distribution is exponentially close to uniform on $\mathcal H$.

Use the trace pairing $\langle X,Y\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(XY)$ on each full matrix space. The radical of its restriction to $S_i$ has dimension at most $c$, because $S_i$ has codimension at most $c$. Take a complement $U_i$ to that radical. The pairing is nondegenerate on $U_i$, with $u_i=\dim U_i\geq r_i^2-2c$. A Fourier character of $\mathcal H$ is represented by matrices $P,Q$ and the functional $\operatorname{tr}(PA)+\operatorname{tr}(QB)$, followed by a fixed nontrivial additive character of $\mathbb F_q$. For the quadratic expression in $Z_0$, restriction of the polar form to $U_1\otimes U_2$ has operator
$$
 L_P\otimes I+I\otimes L_Q ,
$$
under the tensor product trace pairing. Here $L_P$ is $X\mapsto PX+XP$ restricted and trace-orthogonally projected to $U_1$, and similarly for $L_Q$. This follows by expanding the two terms $Z_0 Z_0'+Z_0' Z_0$ on pure tensors.

If $P$ is nonscalar, the full matrix map $X\mapsto PX+XP-\lambda X$ has rank at least $r_1$ over an algebraic closure for every $\lambda$. Choose $v$ with $Pv$ outside its line. Restrict to $X=v\otimes f$ with $f$ arbitrary and project the output modulo matrices whose range is contained in the line of $v$. The projected map $f\mapsto(Pv\bmod v)\otimes f$ is injective. Restriction and projection to $U_1$ together lose at most $4c$ ranks, so $L_P-\lambda I$ has rank at least $r_1-4c$. Triangularizing $L_Q$ gives $u_2$ diagonal blocks of this form in the tensor sum. A block triangular matrix has rank at least the sum of the ranks of its diagonal blocks, by projection to the last block and induction. The same argument works with the two factors exchanged. If both $P,Q$ are scalar, the character is trivial on $\mathcal H$ in characteristic two. In odd characteristic it is trivial when their scalar coefficients sum to zero, and otherwise the polar operator has full rank on $U_1\otimes U_2$.

For fixed $c$ and $r_i=n-o(n)$, every nontrivial character therefore has polar rank $\Omega(n^3)$. Squaring the quadratic character sum and averaging over differences bounds its squared modulus by $q^{-\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{polar})}$, including characteristic two. Plancherel and Cauchy–Schwarz, with $|\mathcal H|\leq q^{2n^2}$, give total variation $q^{-\Omega(n^3)}$. The individual uniform marginals on $\mathcal H$ are uniform matrices in odd characteristic and uniform trace-zero matrices in characteristic two. This Fourier step uses the mechanism of [MathProofs Problem 1.8](../Problem18/README.md) with anticommutator rank in place of the original Gram rank calculation.

## 4. Bound the cost of repeated primary parts

For a matrix $A$ let $m_f(A)$ be the multiplicity of a monic irreducible $f$ in its characteristic polynomial. Put
$$
 D(A)=\sum_{f : m_f(A)\geq2}(\deg f)m_f(A)^2 .
$$
For uniform matrices, even conditioned on a prescribed trace, $\mathbb E D(A)=O(1)$ with an absolute bound. Here is a count that avoids a squarefreeness assumption.

Fix $f$ of degree $e$ and $k\geq2$. The event $m_f(A)\geq k$ implies the existence of an invariant $ek$-dimensional subspace whose restriction has characteristic polynomial $f^k$, by a composition series of the primary module. Count injective ordered frames of length $ek$, multiply by the number of restrictions with that polynomial and by $q^{-ek r}$ for a specified restriction in ambient dimension $r$, and divide by $|\mathrm{GL}_{ek}(q)|$. The normalized frame count is at most one. The expected number of such subspaces is therefore bounded above by the number of size-$ek$ matrices with characteristic polynomial $f^k$ divided by $|\mathrm{GL}_{ek}(q)|$. Conditioning on a prescribed trace costs at most a factor $q$.

That normalized count equals $Q^{-k}/c_k(Q)$, with $Q=q^e$ and $c_k(Q)=\prod_{j=1}^k(1-Q^{-j})$. Indeed, $\mathbb F_q[z]/f^k$ is $\mathbb F_Q[\epsilon]/\epsilon^k$. Its coefficient field consists of the $Q$-Frobenius fixed lifts of residue elements. A fixed lift exists by iteration of Frobenius until the nilpotent discrepancy is killed, and it is unique. A generator of the maximal ideal gives $\epsilon$. The similarity classes are thus nilpotent classes over $\mathbb F_Q$ with the same centralizer groups. To count the latter, decompose every endomorphism uniquely into complementary nilpotent and invertible summands by its Fitting decomposition. Dividing by $|\mathrm{GL}_k(Q)|$ gives
$$
 \frac{1}{c_k(Q)}
   =\sum_{j=0}^k\frac{\#\{\text{nilpotent size-}j\text{ matrices}\}}{|\mathrm{GL}_j(Q)|}.
$$
Subtract the formula at $k-1$ to obtain the claim. Consequently $\Pr(m_f(A)\geq k)\leq4q^{1-ek}$ with or without the trace condition. There are at most $q^e$ irreducibles of degree $e$. Since $m^2\mathbf1_{m\geq2}\leq\sum_{k=2}^m 2k$, we get
$$
 \mathbb E D(A)\leq 8\sum_{e\geq1}\sum_{k\geq2} e k\,q^{1-e(k-1)}=O(1).
$$
The series is bounded by its convergent value at $q=2$.

Thus the two live operators have $D(A_0)+D(B_0)=o(\log_q n)$ in probability by Markov's inequality, also after the total variation error. When both kernel forms are invertible, conditioning on invertibility of the coefficient matrix increases the failure probability by at most $1/c(q)<4$. Otherwise use the rectangular projection estimate from Section 2. In the enlarged operators the only added primary multiplicity is $2k$ at $f=z$ in each mode. Its contribution is bounded by a constant times the old repeated budget plus $1+k^2$. Since the two chosen kernel forms have $k\leq(\log_q n)^{1/4}$ with probability $1-o(1)$, the enlarged budget is still $o(\log_q n)$ in probability. Hence, conditional on any fixed corank $c\geq1$, there is with probability $1-o(1)$ a pair of forms for which
$$
 q^{D(A)+D(B)}\leq n.
$$
This is the remaining test of the recognizer. It depends only on characteristic polynomial multiplicities, even when the actual operators are highly nonsemisimple.

## 5. Exact comparison and canonical compression

For every accepted form pair of the source, enumerate the target form pairs of matching ranks in the same cut. Unequal cut coranks rule out isomorphism. If the source is accepted, an original isomorphism would send its successful exact forms to an enumerated successful pair in the target. No global scalar enumeration is needed because every nonzero form representative is already included. For a fixed comparison, align the rational canonical forms of $A$ and $B$. A mismatch rejects that comparison. After alignment the remaining maps are precisely in the products of the two operator centralizers.

Each centralizer preserves its primary spaces. On a repeated $f$-primary space of dimension $em$ the commutant algebra has dimension at most $em^2$: view its endomorphisms as a subspace of the $m$-by-$m$ matrices over the lifted coefficient field of degree $e$, as in Section 4. Compute it by linear equations. Enumerate its elements and discard singular ones. Over both operators the total count is at most $q^{D(A)+D(B)}\leq n$. On a nonrepeated primary space the centralizer is the unit group of a field.

For each repeated-part choice, apply the chosen maps and treat those coordinates as labelled scalar coordinates. Identify a nonrepeated primal component with its field and the dual component with the same field by the nondegenerate field trace. A local field unit acts as multiplication on the primal and inverse multiplication on the dual. Split the tensor product of the field factors into finite field cells, keeping the scalar coordinates separately labelled. The sum of the degrees of the cells equals the number of tensor coefficients in the enlarged format, at most $(2n)^4$. Compare zero supports. On the nonzero support, the remaining test is membership of the vector of coefficient ratios in a signed image of local field units.

The following describes the exact quotient and also supplies a compact canonical record. It reuses [Tensor Frame Recovery and Cyclic Quotients](../TensorFrameRecovery/CYCLIC-QUOTIENT.md) and the [growing-field ordinary TI proof](../GenericOrdinaryFourTI/PROOF.md). Make compatible models for all required field degrees by tensoring nested prime-power subfield towers of pairwise coprime degrees. Obtain the towers by taking Frobenius-fixed subfields in an available field of maximal valuation at that degree prime. The tensor product of the selected towers is a field, and divisor embeddings compose by construction. Identify each displayed field with the corresponding model by adjoining a field generator and finding a root of its minimal polynomial. Compute the Frobenius twist of any actual embedding by testing the at most degree-many powers on a generator. All used field dimensions are at most the tensor coefficient count.

With target cell orders $m_s$ and source unit orders $m_j$, the abstract log column for one variable has entry
$$
 \frac{m_s}{m_j}\sum_{\text{occurrences in cell }s}(\pm q^\ell),
$$
where $\ell$ is the twist and the signs encode primal versus dual. With no occurrence the entry is zero. The same variable may occur with both signs in a cell. This formula follows from compatible primitive roots in a hypothetical common field. Neither these primitive roots nor the common field are computed.

Use these explicit columns and $\operatorname{diag}(m_s)$ as lattice generators. A Smith row transform $U$ reduces the lattice to $\operatorname{diag}(d_i)\mathbb Z^S$. Membership is exactly the zero condition on the transformed abstract logs modulo the corresponding $d_i$. To evaluate it without logs, gcd-split all the orders into pairwise coprime atoms, and check each atom power $a\mid d_i$ separately. For an input cell value $x_s$, compute $x_s^{m_s U_{is}/a}$. The exponent is integral because the lattice includes the $s$-th order column. The result has order dividing $a$ and $m_s$. It therefore lies in the gcd-degree subfield common to its own field and any target slot whose order is divisible by $a$. Transport by the compatible embeddings, multiply the contributions in that slot, and check equality to 1. This computes the desired congruence without discrete logarithms, integer factoring or a large compositum.

For canonical packing, for each gcd-free atom match its powers in the $d_i$ to powers in the cell orders in descending order. Each quotient exponent is bounded by the corresponding input exponent. To prove this, take any prime dividing the atom and use the surjection on successive primary layers of the quotient of the product of cyclic groups. The layers bound the number of factors with each minimum valuation. Thus each matched cell receives at most one power for a given atom. Pack the evaluated transformed residues into the matched cells. The atom powers sharing one cell have coprime orders, so multiplying their values preserves every residue. Together with the zero support, these values are a complete orbit record in $O(n^4)$ field symbols. The deterministic choices depend on the rational canonical descriptors and the support alone.

Minimize over the repeated-part choices, then over all accepted kernel form pairs and cuts. Include the cut, enlarged dimensions, and the full rational canonical descriptors in every candidate record. Two isomorphic tensors give the same sets of records, since their intrinsic kernel forms and the accepted choices correspond. Conversely equality of records gives conjugation equivalence for corresponding enlarged tensors and hence original isomorphism. Pad the fixed-format record and place it in a three-way cube. Mark each mode by a fixed cyclic shift and its cyclic vector to pin its basis, and apply the [balanced mixed-tensor compiler](../BalancedOrdinaryReduction.lean). This gives ordinary 3TI with total dimension $O(n^{4/3})$ on the recognized class.

There are at most $n$ choices in each kernel and at most $n$ repeated-part choices in each aligned comparison. The field computations, rational canonical forms and integer normal forms are polynomial for fixed $q$. The integer orders have polynomial bit length. The algorithm may abstain immediately when $q>n$, since then no positive $c$ can satisfy the existing cap. Thus a bound polynomial in $n,q$ on the remaining branch is polynomial in the binary input length uniformly. Deterministic finite-field factoring at fixed characteristic can use the prime-field Frobenius-fixed idempotents of a finite étale algebra, as in the cited proofs. The output on recognized sources is exact. Unrecognized sources may abstain.

## 6. Simultaneous invertibility of the three cuts

Consider left kernels for cuts 12|34,13|24,14|23. For fixed nonnegative c_i, count tuples of c_i-dimensional test spaces L_i in the respective paired duals. Their kernel conditions are orthogonality to U_i=L_i⊗the two complementary dual spaces. When each L_i has minimum nonzero matrix rank greater than sum of the other c_j, the U_i form a direct sum. Indeed contract any dependence over the two complementary modes of one U_i. Its slice belongs to L_i and is a sum of at most sum_{j≠i}c_j rank-one matrices, since every other cut shares one constrained mode and one complementary mode. Thus every such slice vanishes. Do this for each i.

For arbitrary test spaces the codimension loss is O(n) with constant depending on c_i. To see it, select c_i coordinate pairs on which restriction of L_i is injective. The corresponding slices determine every tensor in U_i. For tensors also in the sum of other U_j, each of those slices lies in a sum of subspaces of dimension at most n sum_{j≠i}c_j, since in each U_j the shared coordinate is fixed and its other constrained mode has at most c_j possible vectors. This bounds U_i intersect sum of others by O(n). A random fixed-dimensional L_i contains a low-rank nonzero matrix of any fixed rank cutoff with probability q^(-n²+O(n)), by union bound and the elementary rank count q^O(n) at fixed rank. Hence the exceptional spaces' q^O(n) probability multiplier from lost constraints is negligible.

Consequently the joint Gaussian kernel-count moments converge to the product of the usual square-matrix moments m_c=q^(-c²)/prod_{j=1}^c(1-q^(-j)). For a corank K, the partial sum S_r(K)=sum_{c≤r}(-1)^c q^(c(c-1)/2)[K choose c]_q equals (-1)^r q^(r(r+1)/2)[K-1 choose r]_q when K>0, by the Gaussian Pascal recurrence, and equals 1 at K=0. Thus even partial sums majorize 1_{K=0}, and odd partial sums minorize it. The product of three even majorants is an upper bound. Its excess over the product of the indicators is bounded by the sum over one slot of its even-minus-next-odd difference times the other two even majorants. Expectations of all these fixed polynomial expressions converge by the joint moments. The error tends to zero as the cutoff grows because q^(c(c-1)/2)m_c decays as q^(-c(c+1)/2). Single-cut invertibility tends to c(q), so the limit for three cuts is c(q)^3. Combining with the singular-anchor theorem yields the claimed recognition density.
