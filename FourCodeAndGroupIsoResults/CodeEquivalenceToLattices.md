# Nonprime-field linear code equivalence reduces to lattice isomorphism

**Theorem.** Let $q=p^s$, with the field given in a power basis over its prime field. Linear code equivalence over $\mathbb F_q$, meaning equivalence under arbitrary nonzero coordinate scalings and permutations, has a deterministic many-one reduction to lattice isomorphism in time polynomial in the block length and $q$. This gives a polynomial-time reduction over every fixed finite field, including every nonprime field. The main intermediate reduction takes the code pair to one permutation-equivalence instance over $\mathbb F_p$.

This answers the nonprime-field LEP-to-LIP question posed in Section 6 of Horlemann–Mazumder–Schaller–Weger and the nonprime-field reduction question of Cheraghchi–Shagrithaya–Veliche. The survey poses a separate question about reductions uniform in the bit length of the field. The bound here for arbitrary monomial scalings is polynomial in $q$. We prove in addition a bound polynomial in the block length and $\log q$ for prime-field descent of permutation equivalence. [Survey, Section 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12870v2#S6), [Cheraghchi–Shagrithaya–Veliche](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/208).

## 1. Four vector configurations force the field structure

Write $E=\mathbb F_q$, $F=\mathbb F_p$, and view $W=E^k$ as an $F$-space. Let $v_i$ be the columns of a full-row-rank generator matrix. Discard zero columns after comparing their numbers in the two inputs. Equal code lengths and dimensions can also be checked beforehand. A zero-dimensional input after these checks is immediate. For a nonprime field let $\alpha$ be its given power-basis generator, so in particular $\alpha\ne0,1$.

Make the multiset $\mathcal M$ containing all $a v_i$ for $a\in E^\times$ and all retained column indices $i$. In particular each nonzero point of $E v_i$ has the same multiplicity, equal to the multiplicity of that projective column. The multiset spans $W$ over $F$, since the original columns span over $E$.

In $W\oplus W$, give distinct names to the following four multisets, where $v$ runs through $\mathcal M$ with multiplicity:
$$
 (v,0), \qquad (0,v), \qquad (v,v), \qquad (v,\alpha v).
$$
A named-multiset equivalence is an invertible $F$-linear map carrying each multiset to its counterpart. The first two sets span their respective summands and force such a map to be $g_1\oplus g_2$. The third enforces $g_1(v)=g_2(v)$ on a spanning set, so $g_1=g_2=g$. From the fourth, $g(\alpha v)=\alpha g(v)$ on the same spanning set. By linearity $g$ commutes with multiplication by $\alpha$ everywhere. As $\alpha$ generates $E$ over $F$, it commutes with every $E$-scalar, and hence is $E$-linear. Conversely every $E$-linear $g$ carrying $\mathcal M$ to the target multiset extends to all four by $g\oplus g$.

For such an $E$-linear map, preserving $\mathcal M$ is exactly preserving the multiset of projective columns, since distinct $E$-lines have disjoint nonzero points. A bijection of these columns gives a coordinate permutation. The nonzero ratios between chosen representatives give precisely the permitted coordinate scalings. This proves both directions including arbitrary repeated columns.

## 2. Remove the names in one ordinary code instance

The four vector supports are disjoint, since $v\ne0$ and $\alpha\ne0,1$. Number their types $j=1,2,3,4$. For each distinct vector of type $j$ with multiplicity $m$, put $4m+j$ copies as columns of a generator matrix over $F$. The residue of this count modulo $4$ recovers the type, and then the count recovers $m$. Because row-equivalence under coordinate permutations is exactly invertible linear equivalence of its column multiset, this removes the names without losing any condition. The number of columns is at most eight times the original cardinality of the four multisets, hence $O(nq)$. If their lengths differ we can immediately return a fixed no instance. Shape or zero-column rejections and the dimension-zero case can likewise be represented by fixed no or yes instances.

Each of the four original multisets is invariant under multiplying by an element of $F^\times$, with the same multiplicities. The uncolored output is therefore invariant as well. Equivalence of these outputs by signed coordinate permutations is the same as equivalence by coordinate permutations. Indeed a left row map carries entire sets of $\{\pm1\}$-orbits to entire sets of such orbits. On each nonzero sign class the multiplicities are uniform, so matching the signed multiplicities is equivalent to matching the literal vector multiplicities. The assertion includes $p=2$, where each sign class is a singleton.

## 3. Turn the prime-field code into one lattice

Let $B$ be the uncolored prime-field output code. In the nonprime case, every nonzero word has Hamming weight greater than $p^2$ already. A word is the evaluation of a linear functional on $W\oplus W$. If it is nonzero, the functional is nonzero on one of the two axes, hence on some $E$-line used by that axis's multiset. Its restriction to this $s$-dimensional $F$-space has exactly $q-q/p$ nonzero evaluations. Every one occurs at least five times in the output generator, so the weight is at least $5(q-q/p)>p^2$ for $s\ge2$.

In the integer ambient space of dimension equal to the length $b$ of $B$, take the Construction A lattice
$$
 \Lambda_B=\{x\in\mathbb Z^b:x\bmod p\in B\}.
$$
Its shortest nonzero vectors are exactly the signed coordinate vectors $\pm p e_i$. Indeed every vector outside $p\mathbb Z^b$ has at least $p^2+1$ nonzero coordinates and squared norm above $p^2$. Therefore a lattice isomorphism must be a signed coordinate permutation. Such a map is a lattice isomorphism exactly when it is a signed-permutation equivalence modulo $p$, by the construction. This is the shortest-vector argument used by Bennett–Win in the prime-field case. An integer basis is obtained in polynomial bit time by putting the prime-field code in systematic form $[I\,\vert\, A]$; a basis of the lattice consists of the integer lifts of these rows and $p e_i$ on all nonpivot coordinates. This basis spans the lattice because any vector can first have its pivot coordinates removed by the lifted rows, after which its nonpivot coordinates are all multiples of $p$. [Bennett–Win reduction](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/782), [survey, Section 4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12870v2#S4.SS4).

For $s=1$, the single multiset $\mathcal M$ gives a prime-field code. Repeat its generator horizontally $p^2+1$ times to enforce the same distance condition. Signed equivalence checks equality of the multiplicities of sign classes after an invertible common row map, and repetition multiplies all multiplicities by the same positive integer. This preserves and reflects the comparison. In the nonprime case, the lattice rank is $O(nq)$ with no additional repetition. The code and lattice constructions have bit time polynomial in $n,q$. Fixed yes and no cases can be represented by the lattice pairs $(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z)$ and $(\mathbb Z,2\mathbb Z)$.

## 4. Uniform descent for permutation equivalence and bounded scaling groups

For permutation equivalence over $E$, let $\mathcal B$ be the literal column multiset, with zero columns removed as before. Form the auxiliary multiset $\mathcal M=\{\alpha^j v: v\in\mathcal B,\ 0\le j<s\}$. It spans the whole $F$-space $W$, since the power basis spans $E$. Use the four named multisets from Section 1 based on this $\mathcal M$ and a fifth multiset $(v,0)$ for $v\in\mathcal B$. The first four force an $E$-linear map, and the fifth then forces it to act on the literal columns, rather than just the augmented list. Conversely one $E$-linear map preserving the literal columns automatically preserves all four auxiliary marks. Allow the names to overlap at a vector and encode the five multiplicities as digits of its total count in base $sn+1$. Each digit is less than the base, so one ordinary permutation code over $F$ again enforces every condition, and the number of columns is polynomial in $n,s$. All field computations here take polynomial bit time in $\log q$.

More generally let a permitted scaling subgroup $U\le E^\times$ be supplied as a list. Take $\mathcal B=\{av_i:a\in U\}$ with copies and use the same five lists. Distinct $U$-orbits on nonzero vectors are disjoint, so exact $E$-linear preservation of $\mathcal B$ implies the original $\operatorname{LEP}(U)$ comparison, keeping the same multiplicities and recovering a permutation and a permitted multiplier at each position. Its running time and output length are polynomial in $n,|U|,\log q$. This supplies a prime-field permutation reduction without listing the whole extension field when the allowed subgroup is small.

The field descent uses the same linked-map pattern as tensor-system reductions, with the graphs restricted to the span supplied by the input code. The scalar closure over a single field and Construction A step have established prior art. The added field-linearity configurations are the contribution here. The version with unrestricted scalings leaves open the separate $\operatorname{poly}(n,\log q)$ requirement.

## A finite check where field-linearity matters

In $E^2$ for $E=\mathbb F_4$, assign the five projective lines distinct multiplicities from one to five, and compare this with its coordinatewise Frobenius image. Frobenius fixes the lines with slopes $0,1,\infty$ and exchanges the other two. Any projective linear transporter would have to fix the same three distinctly weighted lines and hence be the projective identity. It cannot exchange the others. Thus the codes are inequivalent over $E$, while the unmarked scalar-closure vector lists become equivalent by a binary linear map. The marked construction separates them. A check of all $20{,}160$ invertible binary $4$-by-$4$ matrices found three unmarked transporters and no marked transporters after enforcing the block and diagonal identities. This example tests the field-linearity condition rather than the entire theorem.
