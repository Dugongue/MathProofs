# Intrinsic tensor summands and determinant tests from principal centroid ideals

Informal mathematics over a fixed finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$. Put $m=q-1$, let $L$ denote dense input bit length, and write $T\in V_1\otimes V_2\otimes V_3$ in the covariant convention. Every raw tensor has a determinant-exact decomposition into intrinsic concise indecomposable summands. Given local polynomial many-one determinant tests, they combine into one ordinary TI instance for logarithmic invariant-bucket width. Separately, a principal ideal of a local centroid yields an exact one-instance reduction whenever its determinant-active Jordan multiplicities are logarithmic.

## 1. Recover the intrinsic summands

Compute the support $S_i\subset V_i$ as the image of flattening in mode $i$. The induced tensor in $\bigotimes_i S_i$ is concise. Every transporter carries $S_i$ onto $S_i'$. Every supported transporter lifts to an arbitrary block upper triangular matrix with the supported map and an independently chosen invertible complement map as diagonal blocks. Thus a nonzero complement supplies any determinant in that mode, independently of the others, after known basis changes. Unequal support dimensions obstruct ordinary equivalence. Two zero tensors admit arbitrary determinants in positive-dimensional modes and determinant one in zero-dimensional modes, and precisely one zero tensor precludes equivalence.

On the support, solve the linear equations $a_iT=a_jT$ for triples $a$ of mode endomorphisms. Conciseness makes each projection faithful. For $a,b$ satisfying these equations and distinct $i,j,k$,
$$
 a_i b_iT=a_i b_jT=b_j a_iT=b_j a_kT=a_k b_jT=a_k b_iT=b_i a_kT=b_i a_iT .
$$
Thus their projections commute. Moving both factors to another mode now proves closure under componentwise products, so the centroid $C(T)$ is commutative and unital.

Compute its radical $J$ and the finite-field factors of $C(T)/J$ in deterministic polynomial time at fixed characteristic. An arbitrary lift $x$ of a residue idempotent has $x^2-x\in J$, so $x^{p^a}$ is idempotent for a sufficiently large $p^a$, where $p=\operatorname{char}F$. Idempotents lifting the same residue are equal, since their asymmetric products are idempotents in $J$. The resulting primitive idempotents are orthogonal and sum to one. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

Write $S_i=\bigoplus_b e_{b,i}S_i$ for the primitive centroid idempotents $e_b$. The centroid equations give $e_{b,i} e_{c,j}T=e_{b,i}e_{c,i}T=0$ for $b\ne c$. Thus the tensor has only same-label blocks, each concise on its three projected support spaces. A further direct sum of a block would give a further centroid idempotent by projections extended by zero on the other blocks. Each block is therefore indecomposable and its centroid is the corresponding local centroid factor. Every transporter conjugates the two centroids and hence permutes their primitive idempotents. Conversely compatible block maps and a bijection of labels give a global transporter. These statements also apply to nonsplit residue fields.

In concatenated block bases, the global determinant in mode $i$ is the product of the block determinants times
$$
 \prod_{b<c,\ \pi(b)>\pi(c)}(-1)^{d_{b,i}d_{c,i}},
$$
where $\pi$ is the matching and $d_{b,i}=\dim_F e_{b,i}S_i$. This is the sign of interchanging coordinate intervals and gives exact raw determinants after including the known source and target basis matrices.

## 2. One ordinary TI instance for logarithmic invariant-bucket width

Assume local prescribed determinant tests have deterministic polynomial many-one reductions to ordinary TI. Their ordinary equivalence tests are the positive ORs over all determinant triples in $D=(F^\times)^3$. Partition blocks on each side by any polynomially computable ordinary-isomorphism invariant, refining by their three dimension parities. Assume each resulting bucket has at most $C_0\log(2+L)$ blocks on each side for fixed $C_0$. There is no bound on the number of ordinary isotypes. Use a common ordering of the invariant values for concatenation and account for regrouping in the raw basis calibration. Unequal bucket cardinalities exclude ordinary equivalence.

Fix a bucket with $k$ blocks on each side. Enumerate $|D|^k$ assignments of charges $a_i\in D$ to its source blocks, grouping by product. For a chosen assignment let $E_{ij}$ be the local test from block $i$ to block $j$ at charge $a_i$. For each source subset $S$, the condition that it has at least $|S|$ neighbors is the positive OR over target subsets $R$ of size $|S|$ of $\bigwedge_{j\in R}\bigvee_{i\in S}E_{ij}$. AND over all $S$. This is exact by Hall's theorem: an imperfect maximum matching permits an alternating search from its unmatched source vertices, yielding more reached source vertices than reached target vertices and including all their neighbors. Empty gates use the usual Boolean constants. The OR over assignments and the Hall tests have size $2^{O(k)}$ and balanced binary depth $O(k+\log(2+k))$.

The aggregate local determinant coset is the same for every ordinary matching before applying its permutation sign. Within each ordinary isotype choose an abstract common block $R_0$, and fix maps $f_i:A_i\to R_0$ and $g_j:B_j\to R_0$. Every matching $\pi$ is realized by $g_{\pi(i)}^{-1}f_i$, whose base determinant product is $(\prod_i\det f_i)(\prod_j\det g_j)^{-1}$. All local choices give this product times the subgroup generated by the local stabilizer determinant groups. These groups are invariant under conjugating by a transporter. The argument establishes the identity without computing reference maps.

A repeat predicate is the positive OR of same-side ordinary equivalence tests for distinct source blocks in the bucket. If true and a matching exists, swapping the target images of two equivalent source blocks toggles the permutation sign while preserving the available aggregate determinant coset. If false and a matching exists, partners are unique. For each $i<i'$, test inversion by OR over $j>j'$ of the two corresponding ordinary equivalence tests joined by AND. Test non-inversion using $j<j'$. Compute inversion parity by balanced simultaneous even and odd formulas and OR the repeat predicate into both possibilities. This is exact on every bucket for which a matching exists. A sign contributes $((-1)^{p_1},(-1)^{p_2},(-1)^{p_3})$ in the negative case, for the bucket parity vector $(p_1,p_2,p_3)$.

Combine each bucket's sign and charge table and then multiply the constant-size tables across buckets by balanced Boolean convolution. Incorporate the basis calibrations and omit requirements on independently free support determinants. The resulting monotone circuit has polynomial size and binary depth $O(\log L)$.

Such a circuit on ordinary TI leaves compiles into one ordinary TI pair. AND uses ordered pairs. OR uses the equality of multisets $\{(A,C),(B,D)\}=\{(A,D),(B,C)\}$, which holds exactly if $A=B$ or $C=D$ as isomorphism types. Unfold to a term tree of polynomial size since every node increases size by at most four and depth is logarithmic. Coordinatewise label algebras and linear incidence maps encode its colored child relations. In each tensor mode take the direct sum of leaf spaces tagged by their leaf idempotents, and include the sum of the leaf tensors. The incidence maps force a tree isomorphism and the leaf idempotents force the corresponding local maps. Conversely any such tree and leaf maps give an isomorphism of the system. The signature has a fixed number of components of order at most three. Apply the fixed-signature compiler once. This reuses the MathProofs positive Boolean packing construction. The ordered and unordered pairing method goes back to [Jenner, Köbler, McKenzie and Torán](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.190/Mitarbeiter/toran/completeiso.pdf). [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4.Thmtheorem1)

## 3. Read determinants through exact-length spaces of a principal centroid ideal

Consider a concise block whose centroid $C$ is local with radical $J$. It has a canonical coefficient field $E$. If $f=[C/J:F]$, a sufficiently large iterate of $q$-Frobenius on $C$ with iterate index divisible by $f$ kills $J$ and fixes its residue, so its image maps isomorphically to $C/J$. Compute it by $F$-linear algebra. A transporter $g_i:V_i\to V_i'$ induces one common isomorphism $\alpha:C\to C'$ satisfying $g_i(av)=\alpha(a)g_i(v)$, and hence a common map $\sigma:E\to E'$. Align the two coefficient fields in fixed coordinates. Their degrees must agree on a feasible pair. [Lenstra, Finding isomorphisms between finite fields](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

Let $I$ be a nonzero principal ideal contained in $J$, selected by a deterministic polynomial-time isomorphism-invariant rule. An explicit polynomially computable example is $I=J^a$ with $\dim_E(J^a/J^{a+1})=1$. Any lift of a basis vector generates $I$ by Nakayama's lemma, and the same index selects the target ideal. Choose generators $t,t'$ of $I,I'$. Every feasible $\alpha$ satisfies $\alpha(t)=u t'$ for a unit $u\in C'^\times$. Its residue $\lambda\in E'^\times$ is exactly the semilinear multiplier on the $E$-line $I/IJ$ relative to the two generator classes. The residue is independent of the particular unit, since the annihilator of $t'$ contains no unit.

In each mode use the nilpotent operator given by $t$. Put $K_\ell=\ker t^\ell$, $K_0=0$. For $k$ at least the nilpotence index set $K_{k+1}=V$. The exact-length spaces are
$$
 Q_\ell=K_\ell/(K_{\ell-1}+tK_{\ell+1})\qquad (1\le \ell\le k).
$$
On an individual nilpotent Jordan chain this quotient has dimension one exactly at the chain length and dimension zero otherwise. Hence $b_\ell=\dim_E Q_\ell$ counts the chains of length $\ell$. Since $u$ commutes with $t'$ and is invertible, the kernels and the $tK_{\ell+1}$ transport exactly. In chosen $E$-bases write the induced transporter on $Q_\ell$ as $G_\ell\sigma$.

Lift the chosen $Q_\ell$ bases to $K_\ell$ and take the $t^j$-iterates for $0\le j<\ell$. These give a basis adapted to the determinant calculation. Indeed
$$
 (K_{\ell-1}+tV)\cap K_\ell=K_{\ell-1}+tK_{\ell+1},
$$
since $t^\ell(y+tz)=0$ with $y\in K_{\ell-1}$ implies $t^{\ell+1}z=0$. The lifts therefore give a basis of $V/tV$. By nilpotence their iterates span, and counting Jordan chains gives the exact required number of vectors. Order the bases consistently by $j$, then by $\ell$, and expand in the aligned $F$-basis of $E$.

Filter $V$ by $t^jV$, and filter the $j$-th grade $t^jV/t^{j+1}V$ by the images of $t^jK_\ell$ in increasing order of $\ell$. For $\ell>j$, its next successive quotient is $Q_\ell$ via $x\mapsto t^jx$. This follows in the adapted basis because exactly the chains of length $>j$ survive in grade $j$. Its actual transporter is $(u^j|_{Q_\ell'})G_\ell\sigma$ by $g(t^jx)=\alpha(t)^jg(x)$. Multiplication by $u$ has determinant $\lambda^{b_\ell}$ over $E'$: write $u=\lambda(1+j_0)$ with $j_0\in J'$, and observe that $j_0$ induces a nilpotent endomorphism on this quotient. Commutativity ensures it preserves the spaces defining the quotient. Applying the norm determinant identity and multiplying on all grades gives the adapted-basis determinant in each mode,
$$
 (\det_F\sigma)^{\dim_E V}\,
 \mathrm N_{E'/F}(\lambda)^{\sum_\ell b_\ell\binom{\ell}{2}}\,
 \prod_\ell\mathrm N_{E'/F}(\det_{E'}G_\ell)^{\ell}.
$$
Here each $Q_\ell$ occurs $\ell$ times and its powers $j$ sum to $\binom{\ell}{2}$. The $F$-determinant of an $E$-linear map is the norm of its $E$-determinant, as follows by checking elementary and diagonal generators. Applying $\sigma$ coordinatewise contributes $(\det_F\sigma)^{b_\ell}$ each time. If $B,B'$ are the column-basis matrices of the adapted frames, multiply the formula by $\det B'/\det B$ to recover the raw-coordinate determinant. This includes the Frobenius contribution even in even extension degree.

## 4. Build the local instance

Retain the entire tensor, the centroid and its actions, the coefficient field, and the ideal inclusion and quotient maps. Include the inclusions and quotient maps for the selected $Q_\ell$. The module equations force the shared centroid map since its mode representations are faithful. Every actual tensor transporter extends compatibly to these intrinsic spaces. The original tensor keeps all cross-layer constraints.

For a selected $Q_\ell$ of dimension $b_\ell$ over $E$, introduce its exterior algebra with multiplication, $E$-action and degree-one inclusion. The forced top-line multiplier is $\det_{E'}G_\ell$. The line $I/IJ$ gives $\lambda$. Take the norm of any such multiplier $z$ by copies of its $E$-line connected by the $F$-linear $q$-Frobenius. Their forced multipliers are $z,z^q,\ldots,z^{q^{f-1}}$ since $\sigma$ commutes with Frobenius after alignment. A chain of binary multiplication maps and a final $F$-line inclusion extract the norm. Force $\det_F\sigma$ by a coordinatewise label algebra on a full Frobenius orbit $w^{q^j}$ of a degree-$f$ element $w$. Map its idempotent $e_j$ both to that orbit element and to $\gamma^j$ in an $F$-line, where $\gamma$ is the determinant of $q$-Frobenius. A Frobenius shift by $v$ on the labels forces the line multiplier $\gamma^v$. Full-degree elements can be found deterministically at fixed characteristic by selecting coordinates to avoid the maximal proper subfields: after each fixed coordinate keep the expected number of forbidden memberships at most its current value, computing the counts by linear elimination. For $f\ge2$ the starting expectation is at most $\omega(f)q^{-\lceil f/2\rceil}<1$, where $\omega(f)$ counts distinct prime divisors. Each maximal proper subfield has degree at most $\lfloor f/2\rfloor$. For $f=1$ use $w=1$. These line constructions reuse the MathProofs shared semilinear norm method.

Multiply the forced norm factors and $\det_F\sigma$ powers by a chain of maps between $F$-lines. Mark a terminal vector with the calibrated target value. Conversely every genuine transporter with the requested determinant extends to the system with exactly these line actions. Testing the full determinant triple needs an exterior algebra only when $\ell\not\equiv0\bmod m$, because every norm belongs to $F^\times$. For a character with weights $w_i$, replace that condition in mode $i$ by $w_i\ell\not\equiv0\bmod m$. An active multiplicity bound $O(\log L)$ makes the size of every exterior space $[E:F]2^{b_\ell}$ polynomial. Each remaining map is computed by linear and finite-algebra computations, and the number and size of attachments are polynomial.

To use a fixed number of tensor components, place the auxiliary spaces into direct sums with summands tagged by a coordinate algebra whose idempotents are fixed by a shift and its first idempotent. Use separate sums for input and output occurrences and maps linking each occurrence to its original summand. Put all linear maps into one component and all bilinear maps into one component, with zeros between unrelated occurrences. Dual pairings force the appropriate contragredient actions. The fixed-signature compiler gives one same-field ordinary TI instance. The construction and exactness hold for arbitrary bases, and all three determinants are trivial when $q=2$.

Several intrinsic principal ideals can be used in one system. For one determinant coordinate and a logarithmic width bound define
$$
 b(I)=\gcd\bigl(m,\{\ell:\dim_E Q_\ell\text{ exceeds that bound for }I\}\bigr).
$$
Use $b(I)=m$ for the empty set. Raising the determinant formula for $I$ to the power $m/b(I)$ kills every large factor. The forced powered test detects the determinant up to $\langle\zeta^{b(I)}\rangle$ for a generator $\zeta$ of $F^\times$. If the least common multiple of these $b(I)$ is $m$, the intersection of the undetected subgroups is trivial. Conjoin the powered tests on the same actual transporter for the exact determinant. The same argument uses exponents $w_i\ell$ for a character, collecting large lengths over its modes. All radical powers with a one-dimensional nonzero grade can be computed and used concurrently.

## 5. An unbounded-width example and scope

For $k\ge3$ let $C=E[t,s]/(t^k,s^2,st)$ and $W=C\oplus\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^k C/(s,t^\ell)$. Let $B$ be the upper triangular algebra with both diagonal rings $C$ and off-diagonal bimodule $W$, using the same commutative action on either side. A central element must commute with the two diagonal idempotents, hence be diagonal. Its two entries coincide since $W$ contains a faithful $C$-module. Therefore its center is $C$. The centroid of the multiplication tensor is this center. In the bilinear convention, putting the unit in each centroid identity forces the endomorphisms to be left and right multiplication by the same central element. The covariant input-factor actions are duals of the bilinear input actions.

The radical $J=(t,s)$ is two-generated and $I=J^2=(t^2)$ is intrinsic principal. On $C/(s,t^\ell)$, $t^2$ has up to two chains with lengths $\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor,\lceil\ell/2\rceil$. Thus each exact length occurs at most four times across those summands. The three regular copies of $C$ contribute at most nine additional chains, including the singleton for $s$. Duals have the same Jordan lengths. The required exterior dimensions are bounded, while $\dim_E B=\Theta(k^2)$ and $\dim_E B/JB=k+3$. So ordinary radical grade width and total coefficient-field mode width can both exceed the logarithmic bounds in earlier methods.

The raw decomposition has no promise. The one-instance assembly requires logarithmic invariant-bucket width and local many-one tests. Without that width, prior MathProofs packing obtains logarithmically many adaptive ordinary TI queries by computing the combined ordinary-equivalence count first. The new principal-ideal criterion gives local many-one tests under its active-width hypothesis, including the example above. It does not cover scalar centroids, and neither unrestricted one-instance assembly with arbitrarily large unknown-type buckets nor unrestricted primitive local determinant tests are established here. Publication priority is unverified.

## 6. Reduce the unrestricted problem to indecomposable local tests without matching

There is a separate way to handle unrestricted input if the local many-one test is available for all concise indecomposable multiplication tensors produced by the truncated-corner normalizer. This construction changes the blocks, so it does not require a local tester for the actual input summands or any isotype histogram.

Take any character $\chi:(F^\times)^3\to F^\times$. Apply the existing truncated-corner character construction to the arbitrary input tensor pair and stop before the optional direct-product field padding. It gives explicit same-dimension unital triangular algebras $A_T,A_S$ such that
$$
 \{\det\theta:\theta:A_T\xrightarrow{\sim}A_S\}
   =\{\chi(\det g_1,\det g_2,\det g_3):g\cdot T=S\}.
$$
The proof of this equality is entirely in the unpadded construction: program the corner lengths modulo $q-1$, take determinants on the diagonal and corner filtrations, and use the chosen diagonal lengths to cancel the common change of the truncation parameter. A residue transporter extends coefficientwise. The three corner lengths are at least two, and positive-dimensional formats may be assumed after resolving the zero cases. This uses the three-corner encoding of Grochow and Qiao with the independently weighted truncations of the prior MathProofs proof *Every finite-field determinant character reduces to unit-side special-linear tensor isomorphism*. [Grochow–Qiao, Tensor Isomorphism-Completeness](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

Each of the two resulting algebras has local center. A central idempotent commutes with each standard diagonal idempotent, so it is diagonal. Each truncated diagonal ring is local, which leaves only zero or one in each diagonal slot. Its equality on incident slots follows from commuting with their nonzero Peirce corner. Therefore the only central idempotents are zero and one. A finite commutative algebra with just those two idempotents is local by its forced residue factorization. The multiplication tensor is concise by the unit identities. Its centroid is the center by the same unit evaluation used above, so it is indecomposable.

For a pair of unital algebras the determinant-one algebra-isomorphism test equals the special-linear test on their three multiplication-tensor modes in every dimension. Indeed, every bilinear multiplication isotopy has maps $L_a\theta,R_b\theta,L_aR_b\theta$ for units $a,b$ and an algebra isomorphism $\theta$. The identities with a unit argument first show that $a,b$ are units and then give $\theta(x)=a^{-1}h(x)b^{-1}$ for the output map $h$. In the covariant tensor convention the first two maps are inverse duals. The product of the three determinants is then $(\det\theta)^{-1}$. Conversely a determinant-one $\theta$ itself gives the special-linear isotopy, with $a=b=1$. Thus the unit-side congruence condition and its padding are unnecessary for this test.

The determinant profiles of ordinary transporters are empty or one coset of the stabilizer determinant subgroup of $(F^\times)^3$. A nonempty coset contains the identity precisely when its image under every character does, since characters separate points of the corresponding quotient. Include the trivial character to reject an empty profile. There are only $(q-1)^3$ characters. Apply the preceding indecomposable multiplication construction to each, then use the assumed local many-one test and conjoin the resulting ordinary TI instances with the ordered-system compiler. This is one same-field ordinary TI output. It gives unconditional preprocessing from arbitrary raw tensor input to a constant list of raw concise local-centroid multiplication tests, and conditional one-instance assembly from a many-one solver on that local class. This route avoids matching unknown isotypes entirely. It leaves the actual unrestricted local determinant computation open.
