# Uniform determinant correction over varying finite local rings

There is a witness-preserving polynomial-time reduction from ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $R$ to determinant-one correction over the **same** ring for each of the following families, with $R$ allowed to vary with the input.

- All finite commutative local nonfields of characteristic 2, presented as finite-dimensional $\mathbb F_2$-algebras.
- Local rings with maximal ideal $2R$ and characteristic at least 4, including the unramified Galois rings $\operatorname{GR}(2^a,f)$ with both $a$ and $f$ variable.
- All finite commutative local rings of any one fixed odd residue characteristic $p$, with the residue degree allowed to vary.

For the last two families use an explicit ring presentation supporting polynomial-time arithmetic, unit inversion, and reduction to the residue field, with the residue field size given and $\log |R|$ bounded by the presentation length. The reduction supplies an ordinary algebra isomorphism of genuine free unital associative multiplication tables with the same unit vector. It has polynomial size uniformly in the ring precision and residue degree. The earlier fixed-ring theorem used amplification width $|R^\times|$, which does not give this uniform bound. ## A power-subgroup calibration

Put $U=R^\times$. Suppose an even integer $s\ge2$ and a unit $\rho\notin U^s$ are given with finite order $d\mid s$. For any two equal positive-shape tensors $T,T'$, we construct a based algebra $E$ with exact determinant image
$$
\det\operatorname{Aut}_R(E)=
\begin{cases}
U^s,&T\not\cong_R T',\\
U^s\langle\rho\rangle,&T\cong_R T'.
\end{cases}
$$
The dimensions are polynomial in $s,d$, and the sum of the input side lengths. For $\rho$ of order 2 one obtains exact orbit counts $|U[s]|$ and $|U[s]|/2$, respectively, where $U[s]=\ker(u\mapsto u^s)$.

The underlying three-vertex tensor algebra $S(T)$ has three scalar diagonal copies of $R$, and upper spaces $U_1,U_2,U_3$ in positions $(1,2),(2,3),(1,3)$, with the only product of upper blocks the given bilinear map $T$. The projection onto its first diagonal coordinate is an augmentation. Its isomorphism type recovers $T$ up to ordinary tensor isomorphism. After aligning the three vertex idempotents by a radical conjugator, the nonzero directed corners recover their order, and the three induced maps intertwine $T$.

Use a scalar source and scalar sink, an outer source-to-sink space $H=R^3$, and $d$ data blocks $M_s(S(T_i))$, with $T_0=T$ and all other $T_i=T'$. Every data block has an incoming row and outgoing column of rank $s$, acted on via the augmented matrix quotient $M_s(R)$. Their product pairs the row and column into the line generated by $(\rho^i,1,0)$ in $H$. Add three marked middle matrix algebras $M_{2s}(R),M_{4s-2}(R),M_{s-1}(R)$, with standard row and column spaces of their respective ranks. Their pairings have images the first, second, and third coordinate lines of $H$. All remaining strictly upper outer products vanish. The balanced pairings make this associative. The center is $R$, since the outer diagonal centers are all $R$ and the nonzero free arrows force every central diagonal scalar to agree.

Here are the alignment facts used in the fixed-ring construction, with their consequences for this modification. The radical consists of the strictly upper blocks and maximal-ideal diagonal entries after refining every data block into three matrix vertices. An automorphism permutes the corresponding simple residue blocks. For a transported idempotent family $f_j$ aligned with target residues $e_{\pi(j)}$, conjugate by the inverse of $\sum_j f_j e_{\pi(j)}$. Its diagonal entries are exactly the identity since a diagonal idempotent congruent to 0 or 1 is equal to 0 or 1. Its left and right multiplications therefore have determinant 1 on the original free module, using triangular orders on the Peirce spaces. The refined width-$s$ vertices recover each whole data block by the connected components of their induced directed support viewed as undirected. Widths pin the first two markers and the third marker when $s-1>1$. When $s=2$, the third marker has width one but has both incoming and outgoing corners, unlike the scalar source and sink. Thus the three axes are pinned in every case.

The action on $H$ is now $\operatorname{diag}(x,y,z)$. Port lines are equal precisely when their affine coordinates are equal, since their second coordinates are units. Hence the permutation on the data labels is a translation $i\mapsto i+j$ with $x/y=\rho^j$. If the input tensors are inequivalent, only the identity translation can occur. Indeed a data block isomorphism would give $S(T)\cong S(T')$. To cancel a matrix amplification, transport its full matrix units in the free right module. The transported rank-one corner module $P$ satisfies $P^s\cong S(T')^s$. Krull–Schmidt for finite-length modules identifies $P$ with the right regular module, so taking the endomorphism corner cancels the matrix amplification.

For the translation kernel $x=y=t$. Every data block automorphism has determinant an $s$-th power. After conjugating to fix the matrix units, it acts entrywise with an $s^2$-th power determinant. Left and right multiplications in the conjugation separately repeat on $s$ rows or columns and thus also have $s$-th power determinants. On each marker matrix algebra, the two determinants of the inner conjugation cancel. The two arrow determinants for a perfect row-column pairing of rank $m$ with multiplier $a$ multiply to $a^m$, by the determinant of the scaled identity. The total contribution from the first two marked pairings and the first two coordinates of $H$ is $t^{2+2s+4s-2}=t^{6s}$. Each data pairing contributes $t^s$. The remaining marker and the third coordinate together contribute $z^{(s-1)+1}=z^s$. Therefore the kernel determinant image is contained in $U^s$. It is also all of $U^s$, since the third coordinate may be scaled by any $z\in U$ and only its incoming row needs the same scaling.

When $T\cong_R T'$, choose identifications of all the matrix-algebra copies with a common model, preserving the augmentations. A translation by one on the data blocks is implemented by these identifications, the corresponding row and column permutations, and the map $\operatorname{diag}(\rho,1,1)$ on $H$. Scale only the incoming row of the first marker by $\rho$ to preserve its product. The identification determinants cancel around the cycle. All permuted block ranks are even since $s$ is even. The remaining determinant is $\rho^{2s+1}=\rho$, since $d\mid s$. This proves the exact determinant formula.

Twist the multiplication tensor by the same coordinate map $P$ on both input legs and the output leg in the bilinear-map convention, where $P$ scales one coordinate of $H$ by $\rho^{-1}$. This preserves the unit vector and is the supplied ordinary algebra isomorphism. For any unital associative multiplication isotopy $(X,Y,Z)$, evaluating the product at 1 gives the normalized algebra isomorphism of determinant $\det X\det Y/\det Z$. Therefore the twist admits determinant-one correction exactly when $\rho$ is in the computed automorphism determinant image. The full left and right unit determinant images are $U$, witnessed by scaling only the sink and only the source respectively. The number of special-linear orbits in the ordinary orbit is consequently the index of the automorphism image in $U$. For $d=2$, $\rho\notin U^s$ makes that index drop by exactly 2. In general it drops by the order of $\rho U^s$ in $U/U^s$.

For witness recovery, normalize a successful correction and compose with $P^{-1}$. The resulting automorphism has determinant $\rho$ and therefore has nontrivial label translation, because the identity translation has determinant in $U^s$. Thus it sends the exceptional input to a copy of $T'$. Align block idempotents as above and align matrix units within each diagonal block. The latter is effective over a local ring: take a column of the transported $F_{11}$ with nonzero residue and apply the $F_{i1}$ to obtain a basis. The three $(1,1)$ upper matrix corners then give invertible maps carrying $T$ to $T'$. Conversely a transporter for $T,T'$ gives the augmentations-preserving identifications needed to build a correction. All operations use explicit ring arithmetic and polynomial-sized tables.

## Small choices of the power over varying rings

**Characteristic 2, with nonzero maximal ideal.** Write $|R|=2^n$ and take $s=2^{\lceil\log_2 n\rceil}$. Since $R$ is a nonfield local algebra, $n\ge2$. The maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ satisfies $\mathfrak m^n=0$, by the strict dimension decrease along its nonzero powers. The Frobenius map $x\mapsto x^2$ is $\mathbb F_2$-linear. A nonzero square-zero element can be found in its kernel by linear algebra, since a highest nonzero power of $\mathfrak m$ consists of elements with square zero. For such an element $b$, the unit $\rho=1+b$ has order 2. Every principal unit satisfies $(1+c)^s=1+c^s=1$. The residue unit group has odd order, so $U^s$ meets $1+\mathfrak m$ trivially: if $u^s$ reduces to 1, then $u$ itself reduces to 1. Thus $\rho\notin U^s$. The bound $s<2n$ makes the reduction polynomial uniformly. The exact orbit counts are $|1+\mathfrak m|$ versus $|1+\mathfrak m|/2$.

**Unramified residue characteristic 2, with $\operatorname{char} R\ge4$.** Take $s=2$ and $\rho=-1$. Any square root of $-1$ would reduce to 1 in the residue field. Since $\mathfrak m=2R$, such a unit has form $1+2c$, whose square is $1+4(c+c^2)$ and hence is 1 modulo $4R$. The unit $-1$ is not 1 in that quotient, since $2\in4R$ would imply $2(1-2c)=0$ for some $c$, and $1-2c$ is a unit. Thus $-1$ is a nonsquare principal unit, and the width is constant even at unbounded precision. In $\operatorname{GR}(2^a,f)$ with $q=2^f$, $|U[2]|=q$ for $a=2$ and $2q$ for $a\ge3$. Indeed every square root of 1 has the form $1+2c$, with $c$ taken modulo $2^{a-1}$. For $a\ge3$ its square is 1 exactly when $c(c+1)\in2^{a-2}R$. The residue of $c$ must be 0 or 1. In the first case $c+1$ is a unit and $c$ is divisible by $2^{a-2}$, yielding $q$ choices. In the second the roles reverse. This gives explicit orbit-count pairs $(q,q/2)$ and $(2q,q)$.

**Fixed odd residue characteristic $p$.** If the residue field has size $q=p^f$, take $s=2^{v_2(q-1)}$ and $\rho=-1$. The residue multiplicative group is cyclic and its $s$-th powers exclude $-1$. Thus $-1$ is also outside $U^s$. The standard valuation identity for $p^f-1$ gives $s\le C_p f$ for a constant $C_p$ depending only on $p$. For odd $f$, $v_2(p^f-1)=v_2(p-1)$ by the factorization with an odd number of odd summands. For even $f=2^h m$ with $m$ odd, the first squaring contributes $v_2(p-1)+v_2(p+1)$ and each subsequent one contributes one more. This gives the bound. The principal unit group has odd order, so $|U[s]|=s$ and the two counts are $s$ and $s/2$.

For a concrete constant-width refinement, if $R=\mathbb F_{2^f}[t]/(t^a)$ and $a=2$ or $a\ge4$, take $s=2$ and $\rho=1+t^r$ for any odd $r$ with $a/2\le r<a$. Such an $r$ exists, its square term vanishes, and any square has only even-degree terms. For $a=3$, take $s=4$ and $\rho=1+t$, of order 4 and outside the fourth powers. Thus the amplification width is at most four throughout that whole equal-characteristic family.

The full reductions above have written proofs. Lean checks the characteristic-two Frobenius identity and the obstruction to squaring to $-1$ modulo 4. The finite controls check the prescribed power images and indices on 39 ring cases, and 320 width bounds for odd residue degrees. Bibliographic priority for these uniform reductions remains open.
