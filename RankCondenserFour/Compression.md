# Lossless compression of sharp matrix-substitution profiles

**Theorem.** Let $k=\mathbb F_Q$ and let $H\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2 V$ be one of the quadratic relation spaces used in the square-zero pencils. Write $D=\dim V$, $W_H=\operatorname{Sym}^2 V/H$, and choose a largest substitution size $r\le D$. Put
$$
a_r=rD-r(r-1)/2 .
$$
There is one explicit compressed pencil over a finite extension of $k$ whose product-output dimension is at most $a_r$. It preserves all **base-defined** substitutions through size $r$, including rational expressions in them. In precise terms, every compressed normalized evaluation, after adding a known scalar-only summand, has the same rational canonical form over the extension as the original evaluation after scalar extension. For final-digit pencils over finite chain rings the corresponding statement holds for Smith forms after unramified extension. Rank and Smith comparisons descend exactly.

For a finite family of pencils one can use one common extension $E/k$ of degree $\nu=\max_H\dim W_H$. Construction from a presentation of $E$ is deterministic and polynomial in the explicit dimensions and field arithmetic. A randomized construction trades output width against extension degree. If $b\ge0$, a random width-$(a_r+b)$ map over an extension of degree $e$ works simultaneously for $\lambda$ specified pencils with failure probability at most
$$
8\lambda Q^{\,r(D-r)-e(b+1)} .
\tag{1}
$$
Thus extra output width can eliminate the extension. The deterministic Moore bound is optimal among extension-linear maps of minimum width preserving all base-defined subspaces of that dimension in their source. The output width $a_r$ is itself attained as a necessary width for the substitution problem at $r=h+1$ in the earlier pencils. This last width lower bound concerns compressors applied to the product-output block.

As an application, for rank-one-generated input matrix spaces the compressed transcripts through $h+1$ recover the full ordinary transporter condition when relabeled by a single base-field map respecting the two-dimensional anchor span. More generally, levels through $r$ do so whenever the relevant quadratic relation space is generated by its product-length-at-most-$r$ part and any supplied anchor space. This is an equivalence of measured profiles under a base-field relabeling. It is not a characterization of unrestricted isomorphism between the compressed pencils.

## 1. A one-map condenser over a finite extension

Let $W$ be a $k$-space of dimension $\nu_H\le\nu$, and fix any $k$-linear injection $\iota:W\hookrightarrow E=\mathbb F_{Q^\nu}$. If $a_r\ge\nu_H$, retain $W_E$ unchanged. Otherwise put $t=a_r$ and define
$$
\Psi_H:W_E\longrightarrow E^t,\qquad
w\longmapsto\big(\iota(w),\iota(w)^Q,\ldots,\iota(w)^{Q^{t-1}}\big)
\quad(w\in W),
$$
extending $E$-linearly from a $k$-basis. The display on base vectors determines the matrix. On general $E$-vectors the map is that matrix, rather than entrywise powering of the vector.

It is injective on $U_E$ for every $k$-subspace $U\subseteq W$ of dimension $d\le t$. For independent elements $x_1,\ldots,x_d$ in the image of $U$, the first $d$ rows of their Moore matrix are invertible. If these rows were dependent, a nonzero polynomial $\sum_{i=0}^{d-1} c_i X^{Q^i}$ would vanish on all $x_j$, and hence on their $Q^d$-element $k$-span by additivity and $k$-linearity. Its degree is at most $Q^{d-1}$, which is impossible. This proof also shows that $\Psi_H$ has output rank $t$. The matrix is computed by repeated powering in the represented finite field. The theorem takes the finite extension in an effective presentation. It does not assume a uniform deterministic polynomial-bit irreducible-polynomial algorithm for every encoding of every varying $k$.

This is a single map for all base subspaces, with the field extension doing the work of the seed family. The underlying fact is the classical Moore determinant. Forbes and Guruswami use families of maps over the working field to preserve each low-rank source somewhere in the family. Here all the relevant base-defined sources survive in one extension-linear map. [Gabidulin, *Theory of Codes with Maximum Rank Distance*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/ppi/v21/i1/p3) · [Forbes–Guruswami, *Dimension Expanders via Rank Condensers*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.7455).

The universal extension-degree bound is sharp. Suppose $\nu>t>0$ and an $E$-linear map $E^\nu\to E^t$ is injective on the extension of every base $t$-space. Write $e=[E:k]$ and expand kernel vectors in a $k$-basis of $E$ as $e\times\nu$ matrices. A vector whose coefficient matrix has rank at most $t$ belongs to the extension of its base row space. Thus every nonzero vector in the kernel gives a matrix of rank greater than $t$. If $e\le t$ this is impossible because the kernel is nonzero. If $t<e<\nu$, deleting $t$ rows is injective on this code: two words with the same retained rows would differ in a matrix of rank at most $t$. Its $k$-dimension is at least $e(\nu-t)$ and at most $(e-t)\nu$, forcing $e\ge\nu$, a contradiction. The Moore construction attains $e=\nu$. This argument is the rank-metric Singleton puncturing bound applied to the kernel. It concerns all base subspaces, so it does not give a necessary extension degree for the more restricted product subspaces below.

## 2. Exact compression of the measured matrices

The ordinary normalized pencil uses $J_H=V\oplus W_H\oplus S_H$, where $S_H$ is inert padding to match formats. Put $\beta_H(u,v)=[uv]$ in the quotient $W_H$. Replace the product by
$$
\widetilde\beta_H(u,v)=\Psi_H\big(\beta_H(u,v)\big)
$$
over $E$. Retain $V_E$ and the scalar unit slice. The centered operators still multiply pairwise to zero. In the version symmetric in the square legs retain the hyperbolic companion by using the compressed centered operator together with its transpose.

For any base $U\subseteq V$ of dimension at most $r$ the image $\beta_H(U,V)$ is a base subspace of dimension at most $a_r$. Indeed the products $UV$ have dimension
$$
a_j=jD-j(j-1)/2,\qquad j=\dim U,
$$
by using a basis extending one of $U$ and counting monomials involving one of its first $j$ basis variables. The compressed map is injective on the extension of this image. In particular it preserves every linear relation within it, not just its dimension.

A size-$q$ base substitution, $q\le r$, has the normalized form
$$
 \begin{pmatrix}
 A\otimes I_V&0\\
 B_H(\mathcal U)&A\otimes I_W
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 (B_H(\mathcal U)v)_i=\sum_j\beta_H(u_{ij},v_j).
\tag{2}
$$
Pad the compressed $W$-space with inert directions to the original total $W$-dimension. The removed summand in the unpadded evaluation is thus exactly $A\otimes I$ of known size. Diagonalize the common scalar matrix $A$ by independent row and column operations over $k$, applied on the $q$-leg. The new array still has base directions. Each centered block row uses a span of at most $q$ such directions. On its image in the original $W_E$ the compressed map is injective. Extend the image map to an isomorphism of the equally padded $W_E$-spaces, separately for each row index. The resulting direct sum of maps acts identically on the $V$-parts and commutes with the scalar diagonal part. It conjugates the two transformed evaluations. Undo the independent scalar row and column operations to obtain left-right equivalence of the original and padded compressed evaluations over $E$. For the transpose companion use base column spans on the dual part, giving the same conclusion. Any supplied inert norm-one line in the dyadic variant only supplies another scalar block.

For free rational expressions the same argument applies to each output. By pairwise vanishing of centered products, evaluating a written expression acts on common parameters by
$$
  (A,\mathcal U)(C,\mathcal T)=(AC,A\mathcal T+\mathcal U C),\qquad
  (A,\mathcal U)^{-1}=(A^{-1},-A^{-1}\mathcal U A^{-1}),
$$
where the inverse exists exactly when $A$ is invertible. The base scalar data and the array of directions throughout remain in $k$, even with arbitrary fixed base matrix coefficients on the $q$-leg. Thus the syntactic domains are the same and every resulting matrix again satisfies the comparison of (2). Apply it also to every scalar polynomial of a defined matrix value. Ranks of the powers of each irreducible polynomial in the value determine its elementary divisors, proving equality of rational canonical forms over $E$ for the padded evaluations. The normalized symmetric companion is compared as an operator. This proof does not assume that a left-right equivalence itself is a similarity.

Rank descends because the original matrix is base-defined. Likewise the comparison of two base rational canonical forms can be made over $E$: the base modules over $k[X]$ are determined by their ranks of powers of base irreducible polynomials, unchanged under field extension. Consequently equality after extension is exactly equality over $k$. The power ranks or Smith data of the scalar-only summand are known and subtract from the corresponding padded data, allowing measurement on the small pencil alone. [Earlier square-zero rational calculus](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SharpSubstitutionFour/Functions.md).

Over a finite commutative chain ring $R$ with residue $k$, form an unramified extension $R'$ with residue $E$ by a monic lift of an irreducible polynomial for $E/k$. It is finite free, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak mR'$. Its ideal powers still form the chain, since every nonzero element is a power of a uniformizer times a unit. The original nonzero socle element $s$ annihilates $\mathfrak mR'$. Lift the matrices of $\Psi_H$ arbitrarily and use them on the residue product before multiplying by $s$; the result is independent of the lifts. In (2) use base Smith elimination on $A$ and the residue spans of the remaining directions. The row maps over $E$ lift to invertible maps over $R'$. Their action on the $s$-part depends only on the residue maps. Thus left-right equivalence, and the same comparisons for every rational expression and polynomial in its value, hold over $R'$. Field coefficients in the centered array multiplication are then residues of the scalar blocks. The Smith exponents of the original base matrices are unchanged by unramified extension, as their diagonal forms over $R$ are still diagonal forms with the same uniformizer. This gives exact descent in the final-digit case.

## 3. Width, extension degree and lossless membership

The width $a_r$ is the correct worst-case width for the product block at $r=h+1$ in the repeated construction. A nonzero relation quadratic is a sum of identical blocks on the disjoint variable sets of the $m=h+1$ copies. Choose an $m$-plane $U$ disjoint from the variables of one full copy, inside the span of the other copy variables and the possible inert direction. Its dimension is available since $m\ge3$ and each copy has at least two variables. Every nonzero relation has nonzero projection on that untouched copy, so $H\cap UV=0$. The first-row size-$m$ centered evaluation on a basis of $U$ has rank precisely $a_m$, forcing at least that much product-output width. The same argument works at any smaller size. This is a lower bound on a single product-output width under the stated lossless measurement requirement.

For the tradeoff, use any finite extension of degree $e$, and independently choose a uniform $(a_r+b)$-row matrix for each of the $\lambda$ given quotient spaces. It suffices to preserve the images of the $r$-planes, since every smaller plane extends to one. There are at most $4Q^{r(D-r)}$ such planes by the Gaussian binomial formula. For an extension subspace of dimension $j\le a_r$, a uniform map fails to be injective only if one of its at most $2|E|^{j-1}$ projective lines is killed. Each line is killed with probability $|E|^{-(a_r+b)}$. The union bound gives (1). With $b=0$ this gives optimal width and a degree linear in $r(D-r)$ up to logarithmic terms, sometimes below the universal Moore degree. With $e=1$, slack $r(D-r)+O(\log_Q(\lambda/\delta))$ gives failure probability at most $\delta$. This is a probabilistic guarantee for simultaneously preserving an entire family of probes, without testing or enumerating those probes. When the desired width exceeds the given quotient dimension use identity instead. The bound concerns dimension. A large extension can increase the description length per coordinate.

For exact quadratic recovery assume $H$ excludes nonzero sums of two linear products. The membership probe of the preceding construction for $f=\sum_{i=1}^q u_i v_i$ uses $q\le r$ and obtains rank $qD-\mathbf 1_{f\in H}$ from the centered ordinary evaluation. The compressed evaluation has precisely that same rank without padding, since its scalar block is zero. Thus all product-length-at-most-$r$ membership tests retain their exact bit. If both spaces are generated by their short relations and by a marked anchor subspace $A_0$, consider one base proposed relabeling $g:V\to V'$ carrying $A_0$ to its counterpart under $\operatorname{Sym}^2 g$. Compare the compressed transcripts under $u\mapsto gu$. Each membership probe is carried to the corresponding probe for $\operatorname{Sym}^2(g) f$, so equality of the compressed transcripts forces equality of the short relation sets and hence $\operatorname{Sym}^2(g)H=H'$. Conversely the latter equality induces an uncompressed product equivalence and therefore equality of all compressed transcripts in this size range, after the fixed scalar padding. This describes the exact qualified simultaneous transporter condition without assuming that a chosen compression commutes with $g$ on raw arrays.

In the anchored repeated construction with $m=h+1$, if $M$ is spanned by matrices of rank at most $\rho$, its data quadratic subspace is generated by relations of product length at most $m\rho$. The two scalar anchor quadratics are supplied as $A_0$. Take $r=m\rho$ when this is at most $D$. Original tensor isomorphisms lift to relabelings respecting the anchor span, by the block extension on the two oriented source copies. Conversely the preserved full quadratic kernel reflects original tensor isomorphism by the previous recovery from oriented source and target spaces. Thus for rank-one-generated $M$, the compressed profiles at the prescribed first detecting size already recover the ordinary equivalence decision under one common base relabeling. This assertion is about exact profile information and does not give an efficient algorithm to search all relabelings.

## Comparison and verification

Moore matrices and rank-metric puncturing are established coding tools. The specific extension here is their use on the images of $UV$ in the quadratic quotient, where the bound is $rD-r(r-1)/2$. It preserves the matrix-substitution calculus, including descent of rank and Smith data and the same membership threshold. The earlier substitution theorem evaluates the full quadratic quotient. The compression preserves its base-defined tests, whereas tests using arbitrary extension-field directions may have additional rank losses. The randomized dimension–extension tradeoff uses the product-subspace count. This proof is informal and publication priority for the application remains unverified.
