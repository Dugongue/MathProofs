# Orientation from intrinsic adjoint grades of a square-zero pencil

Work over \(F=\mathbb F_3\). Let \(D=F\Omega\oplus J\) be an alternating pencil on \(W\), with its singular members forming \(J\) and the common radical \(R\) of \(J\) Lagrangian for the nonsingular \(\Omega\). We compare based instances of the same type and prescribe the determinant of the transported map on \(J\). The square-zero normal form of the ternary one-sign problem supplies exactly this input. The algorithm below also works when the quotient dimension is even and the prescribed sign is directly on \(J\).

Put \(U=W/R\), so the descended \(J\subseteq\bigwedge^2 U^*\) has zero common radical. Every ordinary pencil congruence induces a congruence \(p:U\to U'\) on the descended forms, by \(f=\bigwedge^2(p^{-\mathsf T})|_J\). Conversely, choose an \(\Omega\)-Lagrangian complement to \(R\) and identify \(R=U^*\) by \(\Omega\). Then \(p\oplus p^{-\mathsf T}\) on the hyperbolic decompositions gives a pencil congruence with the prescribed action \(f\) on \(J\). These decompositions can be computed by alternating elimination. For the odd-quotient-dimension normal form, the raw product sign is the radical sign up to known frame calibration, as detailed below.

## Theorem

Let \(L\) be the dense input bit length. There is a polynomial-time construction of an intrinsic flag on \(U^*\), with successive quotients \(S_i\) of dimensions \(a_i\), from the adjoint algebra of the descended alternating map. Form the induced flag on the exterior square, whose grades \(E_\lambda\) are \(S_i\otimes S_j\) for \(i<j\) and \(\bigwedge^2 S_i\) for \(i=j\). Write \(J_\lambda\) for the associated subquotient of \(J\) in \(E_\lambda\).

Given \(b\ge 0\), test the following condition by linear algebra over \(\mathbb F_2\).

- For each grade \(\lambda\), select either \(J_\lambda\) or \(E_\lambda/J_\lambda\). The selected space must have dimension at most \(b\).
- Each selected quotient \(E_\lambda/J_\lambda\) contributes a vector of ambient exponents. It contributes \(a_j\) at \(i\) and \(a_i\) at \(j\) for \(i<j\), and it contributes \(a_i-1\) at \(i\) for \(i=j\). Sum these exponent vectors modulo two. The resulting entry must vanish at each \(i\) with \(a_i>b\).

On the class satisfying this test, the radical determinant question reduces to a single ordinary three-tensor isomorphism instance over \(F\), with construction time and dimension polynomial in \(L\) and \(2^b\). In particular, \(b=O(\log L)\) gives a same-field polynomial-time many-one reduction. The total label dimension and total codimension of \(J\) can both be quadratic in \(\dim U\), even when \(b=2\) and there is a genuine sign obstruction. The chosen cutoffs refer to based subquotients and their actual actions.

## Recovering the flag

Write \(\beta:U\times U\to J^*\) for the alternating map \(\beta(x,y)(B)=B(x,y)\). Its common radical is zero. Compute
\[
 \mathcal A=\operatorname{Adj}(\beta)
 =\{(P,Q):\beta(Px,y)=\beta(x,Qy)\ \text{for all }x,y\},
\]
with multiplication \((P,Q)(P',Q')=(PP',Q'Q)\). Projection to the first coordinate is injective, since a difference in the second coordinate with first coordinate zero has image in the common radical. We may use the first-coordinate realization of \(\mathcal A\) on \(U\). Every congruence conjugates this algebra to its target counterpart, as one sees by transporting the defining identity with \(p\) and the induced map on \(J^*\).

The deterministic fixed-field finite-algebra algorithms compute \(\mathfrak r=\operatorname{rad}\mathcal A\) and the primitive central idempotents of the semisimple quotient \(\mathcal A/\mathfrak r\). The defining equations are linear and the algebra dimension is at most \((\dim U)^2\). On each \(\mathfrak r^t U/\mathfrak r^{t+1}U\) the action factors through \(\mathcal A/\mathfrak r\). For each simple factor \(M_h(\mathbb F_{3^e})\), use the signature \((h,e,\text{dimension of its projection on each radical grade in order})\). A transporter must preserve the signature. On each radical grade group the summands with equal signature, sort the signature groups lexicographically and take their successive partial sums. Taking preimages in the radical filtration gives an intrinsic flag on \(U\). Passing to the reversed annihilator flag gives \(0=V_0\subset V_1\subset\cdots\subset V_s=U^*\), with \(S_i=V_i/V_{i-1}\). Omit zero-dimensional steps. This construction uses no choice of an ordering amongst factors with the same signature. It works even when the adjoint algebra is semisimple and the full radical filtration has just one nonzero grade. [Wilson, adjoints of bilinear maps](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.4329) · [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18, fixed-characteristic structural algorithms](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18).

Order pairs \(i\le j\) by increasing \(j\), then increasing \(i\). For \(i<j\), the exterior filtration stage is \(\bigwedge^2 V_{j-1}+V_i\wedge V_j\), and for \(i=j\) the stage is \(\bigwedge^2 V_j\). The successive grades identify naturally with \(S_i\otimes S_j\) and \(\bigwedge^2 S_i\) respectively. This follows by choosing any basis adapted to \(V_\bullet\) and keeping each wedge according to the grade pair of its two basis vectors. The action on that stage modulo earlier wedges is the tensor product or exterior square of the quotient actions. Intersect these stages with \(J\) to obtain the spaces \(J_\lambda\) and their specified inclusions in the exterior grades. No requirement that \(J\) split as a direct sum of its associated subquotients is used.

Different intrinsic signatures, flag dimensions or \(J_\lambda\) dimensions on the two sides certify a negative ordinary comparison. In the remaining cases the dimensions agree. Each choice in the theorem is forced if only one of the two spaces has dimension at most \(b\), unrestricted if both do, and impossible if neither does. The second condition is a linear system in the unrestricted switches with the forced contributions moved to the right-hand side. A deterministic row-reduction convention selects the same choices from the common dimension profiles. The test itself requires only polynomial time even if it fails.

## The determinant and the single query

Let \(g_i:S_i\to S_i'\) be the actual quotient action of \(p^{-\mathsf T}\). In based tensor-product coordinates, \(g_i\otimes g_j\) has determinant \((\det g_i)^{a_j}(\det g_j)^{a_i}\), and \(\bigwedge^2 g_i\) has determinant \((\det g_i)^{a_i-1}\). These formulas hold by the standard determinant identities for tensor products and exterior powers, including when the exterior grade is zero-dimensional. Multiply determinants over the intrinsic flag on \(J\). On every grade where the quotient was selected, replace its \(J_\lambda\) determinant by the determinant on \(E_\lambda\) divided by the determinant on \(E_\lambda/J_\lambda\), with a frame factor. In \(F^\times\), inversion is the identity and even exponents vanish. Thus the sign depends only on the determinant lines of the selected small spaces and of those \(S_i\) still having an odd exponent.

Explicitly choose bases of each \(S_i\) and the corresponding tensor or wedge basis of \(E_\lambda\). Independently choose bases of \(J_\lambda\) and \(E_\lambda/J_\lambda\), and choose any lifts of the quotient basis. Let \(A_J\in F^\times\) be the determinant of the adapted \(J\)-basis, obtained by lifting and concatenating the \(J_\lambda\) bases in flag order, in the original basis of \(J\). For a quotient choice let \(B_\lambda\in F^\times\) be the determinant of the concatenated \(J_\lambda\) basis and quotient lifts in the tensor or wedge basis of \(E_\lambda\). Let primes denote the corresponding target constants. Then
\[
 \det f=\kappa
     \left(\prod_{\lambda}\det h_\lambda\right)
     \left(\prod_{i:e_i=1}\det g_i\right),
 \qquad
 \kappa=\frac{A_J'}{A_J}
        \prod_{\lambda\text{ using quotient}}\frac{B_\lambda}{B_\lambda'}.
\]
Here \(h_\lambda\) is the map of the selected space and \(e_i\) is the computed ambient exponent modulo two. The ratio \(A_J'/A_J\) converts a determinant in adapted coordinates to the raw \(J\) coordinates. The factor \(B_\lambda/B_\lambda'\) solves the determinant equation for \(J_\lambda\) inside \(E_\lambda\). All of these constants are computable by elimination.

Keep \(\beta\) and the actual dual pairings of \(U,U^*\) and of \(J,J^*\) in a typed system. Add the alternating surjection \(U^*\times U^*\to\bigwedge^2 U^*\), the true filtration inclusions and quotient maps, and the actual \(J\hookrightarrow\bigwedge^2 U^*\). Include the inclusion and quotient maps defining each \(J_\lambda\) and \(E_\lambda/J_\lambda\). These diagrams force every auxiliary map to be the one induced by the underlying congruence. Conversely every underlying congruence transports the flags and extends to all the indicated maps. At each selected space and each \(S_i\) with \(e_i=1\), attach its exterior algebra with the given degree-one inclusion, the unit and the top line. Multiplication forces the action on that line to be its actual determinant in its chosen frame. On the product of these lines use a chain of scalar multiplication maps and a terminal mark with the sign divided by \(\kappa\). If there are no factors, the product has value one. This keeps the single sign condition on the product, allowing determinant signs of intermediate spaces to cancel.

The dimensions of the exterior algebras are at most \(2^b\), and the numbers of maps and roles are polynomial in \(L\). For completeness, a single ordinary query follows by first packing the polynomial list into a constant-size tensor-system signature. Take a split commutative coordinate algebra \(F^t\) with a marked module in which the primitive idempotents have distinct ranks \(1,\ldots,t\). This fixes the ordered role labels. Use the coordinate algebra action on the direct sum of all roles, including distinct occurrence copies of each input and output of each linear or bilinear map. A single linear map on the sum can tie each occurrence to its named role by an identity block. Its remaining blocks can encode all linear tensors using distinct input and output occurrence labels. Sum the bilinear maps on their distinct occurrence triples. A constant role with fixed multiplication implements marked vectors by linear maps from that role. These blocks are recovered by the fixed idempotent projections. Any linked transporter of the original system extends to this packed system and conversely. Thus the number of tensors before applying the ordinary three-tensor compiler is constant and all tensors have order at most three. The compiler of Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1, gives dimension polynomial in the packed size. There is no change of field or adaptive query. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4).

For the raw one-sign normalization with odd \(m=\dim U\), choose the adapted label frames \((\Omega,\mathcal B_J)\) and \((\Omega',\mathcal B_{J'})\). If their change-of-basis determinants relative to the original pencil bases are \(A_D,A_D'\), then the product sign equals
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega)}{\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega')}\,
 \frac{A_D'}{A_D}\,\det f.
\]
Indeed the scalar \(\epsilon\) on the anchor quotient contributes \(\epsilon\) to the label determinant, whereas Pfaffian covariance gives ambient factor \(\epsilon^{-m}\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega)/\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega')\). Their \(\epsilon\)-powers cancel for odd \(m\). This calibration also handles unstandardized bases of a pencil in the same normal form.

## An unbounded family with a genuine sign restriction

Let \(U=X\oplus Y\oplus Z\) with \(\dim X,\dim Y\) distinct positive even integers, \(\dim Z\) odd and at least three, and all three dimensions growing. Inside \(X\otimes Y\), let \(C\) be a two-dimensional matrix space in which every nonzero matrix has rank at least three. Set
\[
 J=C^\perp\ \oplus\ \bigwedge^2 Z^*
 \ \subseteq\ (X^*\wedge Y^*)\oplus\bigwedge^2 Z^*
 \ \subseteq\ \bigwedge^2 U^*.
\]
The common radical is zero. For the cross part, a nonzero \(x\in X\) or \(y\in Y\) pairs nontrivially with a vector from the other side because its rank-one product cannot lie in \(C\). The \(Z\)-part has zero common radical by exterior multiplication.

The projected adjoint algebra here is exactly \(F\times F\times F\), with first-coordinate factors acting by scalars on \(X,Y,Z\). To verify the cross summand, take the alternating output in \(X\otimes Y/C\). For two inputs in \(X\), the adjoint equation has difference of matrix rank at most two before reduction modulo \(C\). Therefore it already holds in \(X\otimes Y\). For independent \(x,x'\) its two rank-one terms have independent \(X\) directions, forcing the off-diagonal adjoint maps \(X\to Y\) to vanish. The same argument on \(Y\) kills the maps \(Y\to X\). For \(x\in X,y\in Y\), the remaining equation has the form \(P_X x\otimes y=x\otimes Q_Y y\) and similarly with the roles reversed. The same rank argument removes the quotient, and each pair of maps must be multiplication by one scalar. The two scalars are independent. Mixed adjoint equations between \(X\oplus Y\) and \(Z\) have opposite sides in independent output summands, so the common-radical condition kills both off-diagonal blocks. Finally \(Pz\wedge z'=z\wedge Qz'\) on \(Z\) implies \(Pz\in\operatorname{span}(z,z')\) for every \(z'\) independent of \(z\), after wedging with \(z\). Since \(\dim Z\ge3\), the intersection of these planes is \(Fz\), forcing a scalar operator. This proves the asserted adjoint algebra, and the dimension signatures distinguish its three factors.

Choose the quotient in the cross grade and in the full \(Z\) exterior grade, and choose the zero \(J\)-subspace in every other grade. In the cross grade the quotient is \(C^*\), of dimension two, and the full \(Z\)-grade quotient is zero. The only ambient exponents are \(\dim Y,\dim X,\dim Z-1\), all even. Thus \(b=2\) suffices. For comparable dimensions of the three summands, both \(\dim J\) and \(\operatorname{codim}_{\bigwedge^2 U^*}J\) are quadratic in \(\dim U\).

For an explicit such \(C\) when \(\dim X,\dim Y\ge 18\), use block-diagonal matrices \(A(a,b)\) whose four scalar blocks are \(aI_3,bI_4,(a+b)I_5,(a-b)I_6\), with remaining entries zero. The four projective lines of \((a,b)\) over \(F\) have distinct matrix ranks \(18-3,18-4,18-5,18-6\). An automorphism acting on \(C\) by invertible left and right matrix operations preserves rank, hence fixes all four projective lines. Its action on this two-dimensional space is scalar and has determinant one. The ambient exponents just computed show that the action on \(J\) also has determinant one for every automorphism. Reversing one basis vector of \(J\) on the target side preserves ordinary equivalence and reverses the attainable sign. Taking the hyperbolic square-zero construction gives these examples on the pure-power Pfaffian locus. This demonstrates a real orientation restriction outside both the small-radical and the small-total-complement regimes.

## An exact duality for the unrestricted endpoint

For any odd-dimensional \(U\), set \(C=J^\perp\subseteq\bigwedge^2 U\) by the natural perfect pairing. The map \(p\mapsto p^{-\mathsf T}\) gives a bijection between congruence transporters of \(J\) and congruence transporters of \(C\) regarded as alternating forms on \(U^*\). The sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow\bigwedge^2 U
   \longrightarrow J^*\longrightarrow 0
\]
identifies the determinant of the \(C\)-action with the determinant of the \(J\)-action up to a computable frame ratio. The ambient action has determinant \((\det p)^{\dim U-1}=1\), and the quotient determinant is the inverse of the \(J\)-determinant in dual bases. Use a basis of \(C\) followed by lifts of the basis dual to the given \(J\)-basis to compute the frame ratio. If \(C\) has a common radical as a form space on \(U^*\), quotient by it. Unequal radical dimensions give a negative comparison. For equal dimensions every congruence on the quotient lifts to a congruence before quotienting by choosing any isomorphism of the radicals and lifts of complements. The action on \(C\) is unchanged. When \(C=0\), this determinant condition is just the frame comparison. Otherwise the quotient form space has zero common radical and yields an anchored hyperbolic square-zero pencil exactly as above, in quotient dimension at most \(\dim U\).

Consequently the unrestricted orientation test on the original odd-quotient square-zero instances can always be presented on a form label space of dimension at most
\(\min\{\dim J,\binom{\dim U}{2}-\dim J\}\), allowing either parity of the new quotient dimension and calibrating the prescribed radical sign. The adjoint-grade theorem additionally lets small subquotients and small grade deficits coexist in one query even when this minimum is large.

## Scope and prior work

The intrinsic flag and linked top exterior lines build on the existing thin-layer method. The new test uses near-full exterior grades as well as thin \(J\)-grades, with a linear system selecting grade complements that cancel all large ambient determinants. The square-zero ratio algebra itself has no trace information on its radical. The adjoint algebra used here comes from the actual descended alternating map and is computed before the query. The proof covers a polynomially recognizable sufficient domain of the ternary sign problem. The unrestricted reduction in Open Question 1.9 remains unresolved. These are written proofs. Exact finite controls checked 200 independent sign calibrations on exterior flags, solved the adjoint equations on an explicit \(4+6+3\)-dimensional example, and checked all 48 elements of \(\mathrm{GL}_2(F)\) against the four distinct rank labels. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5).
