# Class reduction for finite-group epimorphism at an odd prime

**Theorem.** Let \(p\) be an odd prime. There is a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction from epimorphism testing for two Cayley-table groups of exponent dividing \(p\) and class at most \(p\) to ordinary epimorphism testing of two groups of exponent dividing \(p\) and class at most two. The same prime is used. Output orders are polynomial in the input orders, with an absolute exponent. A witness in the output transports back to an epimorphism of the input groups. This is an epimorphism reduction, distinct from the earlier instancewise isomorphism reduction. It makes no assumption on the dimensions of the center or the derived subgroup. [Earlier isomorphism reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightFollowup/groups.md) · [Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar, epimorphism questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)

## 1. The compact bilinear data

For clarity, the needed parts of the augmentation construction are as follows. Put \(E_G=\mathbb F_p[G]/I^{p+1}\) for the augmentation ideal \(I\) of the group algebra, and \(C_G=\gamma_p(G)\). The Jennings formula identifies \(C_G\) with the kernel of the map to the unit group modulo \(I^p\) and makes the map from \(G\) to \(E_G^\times\) injective. Let \(A_G\) be the Lazard Lie algebra of \(Q_G=G/C_G\), realized as the logarithm of the group image modulo \(I^p\). Let \(D_G\) be the logarithm of \([G,G]\) in \(E_G\), also an ordinary Lazard Lie algebra, since \(1+I^2\) has class less than \(p\). Logarithms here are the polynomial
\[
\ell(g)=\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{r+1}(g-1)^r/r.
\]
The augmentation powers, these spaces, and the required linear maps are computed in ambient dimension at most \(|G|\) by ordinary field linear algebra. [Jennings](https://doi.org/10.2307/1989916) · [Grochow–Qiao on the ordinary Lazard range](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S7.SS2)

On \(A_G\) record its Lie bracket \(B_G\) and the additional alternating bimap \(\beta_G:A_G\times A_G\to D_G\), obtained by taking the associative commutator of two representatives \(\ell(g)\) in \(E_G\). Changes of lifts lie in \(I^p\) and do not affect the commutator. To see that it belongs to \(D_G\), it is the coefficient of \(st\) in \(\ell([g^s,h^t])\). The expression has mixed total degrees at most \(p\), so its individual degrees are at most \(p-1\). Every coefficient is a linear combination of values on \(\mathbb F_p^2\), by interpolation. Modulo \(I^p\) the map \(j_G:D_G\to A_G\) obeys \(j_G\beta_G=B_G\) and has kernel \(\ell(C_G)\).

The full logarithm of each group commutator is recovered from \(j_G,\beta_G\) by a Lie polynomial. In rational formal variables it is the Lie polynomial through degree \(p\) in
\[
\log(\exp X\exp Y\exp(-X)\exp(-Y)).
\]
Terms with only one variable cancel, so each exponential factorial used in a remaining term through degree \(p\) is prime to \(p\). The logarithm uses only denominators at most \(\lfloor p/2\rfloor\), since the commutator has no linear term. In each degree the integral free Lie algebra is a direct summand of the free associative algebra by the homogeneous Hall and PBW bases, so its Lie coefficients are \(p\)-integral as well. Reduce modulo \(p\). At each nested bracket apply \(\beta_G\), taking intermediate values back to \(A_G\) via \(j_G\). The resulting value is the actual logarithm in \(E_G\). The span of the values of \(\beta_G\) is Lie-closed since \([d,d']=\beta_G(j_Gd,j_Gd')\), and the full commutator formula puts every group commutator logarithm in this span. Those logarithms generate \(D_G\) as a Lie algebra. Thus \(\beta_G\) spans \(D_G\) and \(j_G\beta_G=B_G\) determines \(j_G\).

## 2. Surjections of the data are exactly group epimorphisms

An epimorphism \(G\to H\) induces a pair of surjective linear maps \(\alpha:A_G\to A_H,\delta:D_G\to D_H\) preserving \(B,\beta\), by functoriality of the augmentation filtration and because lower central series commute with surjective images.

Conversely let \(\alpha,\delta\) be surjective and preserve \(B,\beta\). They respect \(j\) because \(\beta_G\) spans \(D_G\). The map \(\alpha\) gives a group epimorphism \(a:Q_G\to Q_H\) by Lazard. The map \(\delta\) gives a derived-subgroup epimorphism \(\psi:[G,G]\to[H,H]\) because the brackets on those Lie algebras are \(\beta(jd,jd')\). The full commutator formula now shows \(\psi([g_1,g_2])=[h_1,h_2]\) whenever \(a(g_iC_G)=h_iC_H\). In particular \(\psi\) restricts on \(C_G\) to a surjection \(c:C_G\to C_H\). Indeed, lift the quotient classes of the entries of any iterated \(p\)-fold commutator in \(H\) using \(a\), and apply the displayed commutator identity inductively using compatibility with \(j\). These iterated commutators generate \(C_H\), and their lifts lie in \(C_G\).

Take the fiber product
\[
P=\{(g,h)\in G\times H:a(gC_G)=hC_H\}.
\]
Its derived subgroup is the graph of \(\psi\), since every commutator pair lies in that graph and the first projection \(P\to G\) is surjective. Quotient \(P\) by the central graph \(\{(x,c(x)):x\in C_G\}\) to obtain \(K\). The central extension \(K\to Q_G\) has kernel meeting \([K,K]\) trivially. Thus \(\gamma_p(K)=1\), as its image in \(Q_G\) is trivial and it lies in the derived subgroup. Its exponent divides \(p\). Consequently \(K\) and \(Q_G\) lie within the ordinary Lazard range. The induced Lie extension has a section, obtained by inverting its map on derived ideals and extending arbitrarily to a linear section. This extension preserves the bracket because brackets lie in the derived ideal, where the quotient map is injective. Exponentiating gives a group section.

The inverse image in \(P\) of that section is a subgroup \(M\). The projection \(M\to G\) is surjective by choosing lifts of each quotient class and adjusting the first coordinate by the central graph. This projection is injective because the section meets the central kernel only in the identity, and the graph has trivial intersection with \(1\times H\). The second projection \(M\to H\) is onto \(Q_H\) by surjectivity of \(a\) and contains \(C_H\) by surjectivity of \(c\). Thus it is surjective. Identify \(M\) with \(G\) using the first projection to recover an actual epimorphism.

## 3. Ordinary class-two target with padding

The task now is to preserve the surjectivity condition while forgetting the two names in the diagram. A necessary dimension test is \(\dim A_G\ge\dim A_H\) and \(\dim D_G\ge\dim D_H\). On failure output the trivial source and the target of order p. On success pad the two pairs of primal spaces to equal dimensions, so each side has padded spaces \(A^+,D^+\) and respective quotient projections \(\pi_A,\pi_D\) to its own \(A,D\). The primal core is \(A^+\oplus D^+\oplus k u\) for \(k=\mathbb F_p\). The central base is a copy \(\bar A\oplus\bar D\) of the unpadded spaces. Bracket two elements in \(A^+\) by \( (B(\pi_A x,\pi_A y),\beta(\pi_A x,\pi_A y))\). Bracket elements of \(A^+\) and \(D^+\) with \(u\) by \(\pi_A,\pi_D\) in their respective central copies, and put all other core brackets zero apart from alternation.

Use two private-pair markers for each of the three primal blocks. A marker on \(V\) is the evaluation form between \(V\) and a new private dual \(V^*\), plus a fixed symplectic form on a private even-dimensional heavy block. It has a separate central line. If \(d\) is the core dimension, take the six heavy dimensions \(w_i=2(10+i)(3d+1)\), \(1\le i\le6\). These dimensions are the same in both groups because of padding. All added terms are alternating. Form the class-two exponent-\(p\) groups by the half-bracket law \((x,w)(y,z)=(x+y,w+z+[x,y]/2)\). Their derived subgroups are exactly the named central carriers, since brackets with \(u\) span the base and heavy pairings span each heavy line. [Private-pair construction and rank recovery](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowResearchSynthesis/CLASS-TWO-BLACK-BOX.md)

An epimorphism induces surjections \(h\) on abelianizations and \(k_0\) on derived subgroups. Each alternating form of the target, pulled back by \(h\), occurs as a source form and has unchanged rank since \(h\) is surjective. Its contraction image is the image under \(h^*\) of the target contraction image. The proof of rank recovery is short. If several heavy coefficients are present, their ranks add to the sum of their heavy dimensions, with nonheavy error at most \(3d\). The singleton bands are disjoint from each other, from the zero band, and from all bands containing two or more coefficients. Each pure target marker therefore pulls back to a form with just its own nonzero heavy coefficient. Projection to \(V^*\) and the dual of its private copy is onto by the evaluation pair, so the nonheavy rank is \(2\dim V\) plus the rank of the residual form on the complement of that pair. The target rank is exactly the heavy rank plus \(2\dim V\), and the \(V\) dimensions agree by padding. Hence the residual form vanishes. Two marker images for a block intersect in exactly its core dual block, because a common vector has zero outputs on both private duals and both heavy spaces. Within either image this forces the \(V\) input to vanish, and the zero residual leaves just the \(V^*\) output. Varying the private input attains every such output. Since \(h^*\) is injective and preserves intersections on subspaces, it sends each named target core dual block onto the corresponding source block of equal dimension.

Thus \(h\) gives invertible quotient maps on the padded core roles and the anchor scalar \(t\ne0\). A base form supported on the target core pulls back to a base form supported on the source core. Dualizing gives a surjection \(k_{\rm base}\) of base outputs. The maps to the two central copies are forced by the separate brackets with \(u\). For example
\[
 k_{\rm base}(\overline{\pi_A x})=t\,\overline{\pi_A'(h_Ax)}
\]
on the padded \(A^+\) block. Since \(h_A\) is onto, this induces a surjective map \(\bar h_A:A_G\to A_H\), with the analogous map \(\bar h_D\). The induced base action on the two copies is \(t\bar h_A,t\bar h_D\). Evaluating the bracket on two padded \(A\)-inputs proves that \(t^{-1}\bar h_A,t^{-1}\bar h_D\) preserve \(B,\beta\). These are the required surjections of the unpadded data.

Conversely a surjective diagram morphism lifts to invertible padded role maps. Extend the corresponding surjective map to the smaller quotient by sending its kernel isomorphically to the target padding of exactly that dimension, using chosen complements. More generally one matches the kernels of the two displayed padded-to-unpadded quotient composites. Fix \(u\), extend the private maps by inverse duality and take the heavy blocks identically. Use the stipulated surjections on the base output and the identity on each heavy output. This preserves all brackets and gives an epimorphism of the ordinary output groups.

The decoder extracts the quotient and central maps from a supplied group epimorphism, computes the padded role and base maps by linear algebra, and uses the reconstruction in Section 2. The fiber product has at most \(|G||H|\) elements, and its Lazard linear algebra can use the group algebra modulo the \(p\)-th augmentation power. All primal and central dimensions in the encoded groups are \(O(\log_p(|G|+|H|))\) with an absolute constant, including the private markers. Tables are built directly by the half-bracket law. Trivial inputs can be decided directly. These are paper proofs.

## 4. Deterministic parallel transversals

Fix a constant \(c\). Given permutation generators for \(H\leq G\leq S_n\) with \([G:H]\leq n^c\), a deterministic logspace-uniform NC circuit constructs a right transversal for \(H\) in \(G\). The same bound holds for left transversals. This answers Babai–Luks–Seress Problem 5, Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar Problem 12.6, and Question 8.2 in Michael Levet, *Parallel Algorithms for Group Isomorphism via Code Equivalence*, which ask for the same polynomial-index result. Kantor–Luks–Mark Proposition 3.13 gives a quasipolynomial-order case and announces a complete solution in work listed as in preparation. The recent paper posing Question 8.2 reports that it could not locate that announced solution. The proof here uses the universal sequences in Reingold's Theorem 5.4. [Babai–Luks–Seress](https://doi.org/10.1145/28395.28439) [Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar, Section 12](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039#S12) [Question 8.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13953#S8) [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Section 3.5](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf) [Reingold, Theorem 5.4](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/sl.pdf)

**Universal candidate list.** Make an alphabet of \(D\) slots from the input generators, their formal inverses, and three identity slots. Let \(s_i\) be the permutation in slot \(i\), and let \(\pi\) pair each formal inverse with its original slot and fix the identity slots. The graph on right cosets has an edge from \(Hg\) to \(Hgs_i\) for each slot. This is well defined because if \(g'=hg\) for \(h\in H\), then \(Hg's_i=Hgs_i\). The inverse step is in slot \(\pi(i)\). The graph is connected because the slots generate \(G\).

For an application to simple graphs, take vertices \((Hg,i,b)\), where \(i\) is a slot modulo \(D\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}\). Outgoing ports \(0\) and \(1\) move the slot forward and backward around its cycle. Port \(2\) takes the matching edge
\[
 (Hg,i,b)\longmapsto (Hgs_i,\pi(i),1-b).
\]
The cycle has length at least three. The matching edge is an involution and changes layers, so the graph is simple and 3-regular. Cycle edges permit choosing any generator slot, and an identity slot allows changing layers while keeping the coset fixed. Thus the graph is connected. The incoming port is always the image of the outgoing port under the fixed involution swapping \(0,1\) and fixing \(2\).

For each integer \(m\) between \(1\) and \(n^c\), use Reingold's algorithm on a unary vertex count \(2mD\) and this fixed port involution. It outputs a polynomial-length sequence which visits all vertices of any connected graph of that size with this consistent labeling, from any start. Start at \((H,0,0)\). The slot updates are determined solely by the port sequence and the formal inverse-slot involution. A logspace transducer can therefore stream a word in generator slots, outputting the current slot for port \(2\) and an identity for ports \(0,1\). All prefixes of all these words represent elements of \(G\). For \(m=[G:H]\) the prefixes visit every coset. This argument requires neither a membership test nor an explicit coset graph to construct the words.

**Parallel evaluation and compaction.** Compute inverse input permutations in parallel and evaluate each prefix by a balanced product tree, with the empty prefix included. Permutation composition on arrays is implementable using comparisons and bounded fan-in selection, so all prefix permutations have polynomial total circuit size and polylogarithmic depth. For every pair of resulting permutations \(a,b\), compute \(ab^{-1}\) and test membership in \(H\) with the uniform NC permutation-group membership algorithm of Babai–Luks–Seress. The test is true exactly when \(Ha=Hb\). Keep the earliest candidate in each coset. A parallel prefix sum of the keep bits packs the output and records its length. Inverting the kept representatives yields a left transversal. Reingold's words can be built into the circuit by a logspace uniformity machine simulating the transducer on the relevant unary size and retrieving the requested output symbol. Padding and masked outputs accommodate variable list lengths. The general construction with an explicit candidate bound \(B\) instead of \(n^c\) has size polynomial in \(B\) and the permutation-input length and depth polylogarithmic in their sum. [Reingold](https://doi.org/10.1145/1391289.1391291) [Babai–Luks–Seress](https://doi.org/10.1145/28395.28439)

## 5. Minimal faithful permutation representations in deterministic NC

Given a Fitting-free group \(G\leq S_n\) by generating permutations, a deterministic uniform NC algorithm constructs the images of the input generators in a faithful permutation representation of smallest degree. This derandomizes Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar Theorem 1.2(b), a question explicitly posed in their Section 12. It also derandomizes the corresponding construction for Cayley-table input via the NC regular representation. [Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039)

Use their Theorem 5.4 assembly and Theorem 5.2 deterministic NC construction of minimum-degree actions for the almost-simple components. In the notation of their proof, if a minimal normal subgroup is a product of \(\ell_i\) simple factors, the subgroup \(N_G(S_{i1})\) has index \(\ell_i\). Indeed, conjugation is transitive on those factors because any orbit product is normal in \(G\). This index is at most \(\log_2|G|\leq\log_2(n!)\), since all factors have order at least two. The generating sets for the normalizers and the wiring for the wreath-product action are constructed in NC in their proof. Replace the randomized call constructing each normalizer transversal by Section 4 and run these calls in parallel. All the required coset lookups use ordinary NC membership, as in their proof. The wreath-product embeddings and the kernel and degree calculations are exactly those of their deterministic assembly, so the resulting faithful degree equals \(\mu(G)\). This uses the cited published subroutines and yields an unconditional construction for permutation and Cayley-table input. [Theorems 5.2 and 5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039#S5)

## 6. Parallel subgroup intersection when one index is polynomial

Let \(G,K\leq S_n\) be given by permutation generators, and suppose \([G:G\cap K]\leq n^c\) for a fixed \(c\). A deterministic uniform NC algorithm outputs generators for \(G\cap K\). This supplies the polynomial-index case of Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar Problem 12.3, for which the question assumes \(G\) is a \(p\)-group but permits any index. [Problem 12.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039#S12)

Write \(H=G\cap K\). The universal list from Section 4 uses only the generators of \(G\) and the index bound. For candidates \(a,b\) in \(G\), the element \(ab^{-1}\) lies in \(G\), so membership in \(K\) decides whether \(Ha=Hb\). Carry out all tests using the NC membership routine for \(K\), yielding a transversal with identity in the identity coset by ordering the empty prefix first. For each representative \(r\) and generator slot \(s\), find by parallel tests the unique representative \(\overline{rs}\) with \(rs(\overline{rs})^{-1}\in K\). Output these products. Each lies in \(H\), and for a word representing an element of \(H\), multiplying these products for consecutive prefixes telescopes to that element because the initial and final representatives are the identity. This proves generation with polynomial total output and polylogarithmic depth. The three results of Sections 4–6 are paper proofs.
