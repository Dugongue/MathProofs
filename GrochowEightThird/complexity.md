# A depth-four reduction for matrix-symmetric algebraic circuits

Work over a field of characteristic zero. Let `n` be the number of rows and columns and `d` the total degree of the output polynomial. Suppose a matrix-symmetric circuit, after rigidification, has supports of size at most `k`, where `k >= 2` and `n >= 2k+1`. Then its polynomial has a matrix-symmetric circuit of depth at most four and orbit size at most `n^(2 sqrt(2kd)+2)`. In particular every family in symVP has depth-four matrix-symmetric circuits of orbit size `n^O(sqrt(d))`. This gives a quantitative depth-four reduction in the orbit-size model of the first future-work question. [Dawar–Dwivedi–Pago–Seppelt, Section 1.3 and Theorem 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.23541#S1.SS3)

## Bounded-treewidth expansion

Apply their Theorem 3.5 with any support depth large enough for the given circuit, for example its depth. The output is a finite linear combination of bipartition-respecting homomorphism polynomials of bipartite multigraphs of treewidth at most `k-1`, since every forestwidth bound implies the same treewidth bound. Each homomorphism polynomial is homogeneous of degree its edge count, including edge multiplicity. Discard terms of degree greater than `d` by taking the homogeneous components of the identity. Remove isolated vertices, absorbing their factor of `n` per vertex into the coefficient. Every nonconstant pattern `F` now has at most `2d` vertices. Constant terms are input constants.

## A separator with bounded remaining components

Fix `b >= 1` and a tree decomposition of `F` with bags of size at most `k`. Root the decomposition and assign each vertex of `F` to the highest bag containing it. The connected-bag condition gives a unique highest bag. Give each bag weight equal to the number of vertices assigned to it. Scan from the leaves towards the root. At each node add its weight to the residual weights of the children, and mark the node and reset its residual to zero whenever the total exceeds `b`. Each mark discards at least `b+1` previously uncounted units of weight, so at most `|V(F)|/(b+1)` nodes are marked. After deleting the marked nodes, every tree component has weight at most `b`, by considering its highest node.

Let `S` be the union of the marked bags. Every vertex outside `S` has all of its bags in one remaining tree component. An edge outside `S` belongs to a bag, so its two endpoints belong to the same tree component. It follows that each component of `F-S` has at most `b` vertices and `|S| <= k|V(F)|/(b+1)`.

## Circuit construction

Take an outer sum over the pattern terms and over all bipartition-respecting assignments `a` of each separator `S`. For each such assignment form a product gate containing the coefficient, the edge factors internal to `S`, and one factor for every component `U` of `F-S`. The factor belonging to `U` is the sum over all assignments `u` of its vertices of the product of the edge factors inside `U` and from `U` to `S`, evaluated using `(a,u)`. Each edge occurs exactly once. The gate layers are product, sum, product, sum, giving depth at most four.

Permuting host row and column labels acts on every gate by relabeling its assignments. This is a circuit automorphism because all assignments occur and the variables on each wire transform by the prescribed action. The pattern, component, and position of each edge are fixed tags under the action. An outer product or a component-sum gate uses at most `|S|` assigned labels, and a bottom product uses at most `|S|+b`. An input variable has orbit size `n^2` and inputs labeled by constants are fixed. The selected automorphisms therefore give orbits bounded by `n^max(2,|S|+b)` even for a sum with arbitrarily many patterns. To control all automorphisms extending label permutations, merge equal input labels and then identify gates bottom-up whenever their operation labels and multisets of merged children agree. These identifications preserve polynomial values and depth. They are equivariant for the selected action because it preserves operation labels and wire multiplicities. The resulting reduced circuit has a unique extension for each host permutation, by Lemma 2.3 of the same paper. Its entire orbit of a merged gate is thus the image of a selected orbit of one original representative, and obeys the same bound. Choosing `b = ceil(sqrt(2kd))` if `d >= 1` yields the stated bound. The depth-four construction gives an orbit bound. The number of pattern terms in the expansion may affect the total number of gates.

For symVP the fixed support bound `k` follows from the support theorem in the same paper for all sufficiently large `n`, since input orbit size is polynomial in `n`. Finitely many smaller values of `n` can be covered by ordinary expansion into monomials and their orbits. The paper's lower bound at fixed depth rules out a polynomial-orbit guarantee for all symVP families. The optimal depth-four exponent and the depth-three tradeoff remain open here. The proof is informal.

# Unrestricted symbolic subgraph counts characterize the parameterized clique circuit question

Over a characteristic-zero field, the unrestricted version of Grochow–Urisman's Open Question 1.11 has an affirmative answer if and only if every family in `VW[1]` has fixed-parameter-size algebraic circuits. It suffices to assume the asked-for bound for matchings, a family of treewidth one. Equivalently, the question asks whether the node-and-edge weighted parameterized clique polynomials admit fixed-parameter-size circuits. The equivalence leaves whether they do open. [Grochow–Urisman, Open Question 1.11](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26244v2#S1.SS3) [Bläser–Engels, Definition 4.4 and Theorem 7.22](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.12287)

Write `Mat(n,m)` for the sum of the edge monomials of all `m`-edge matchings on `n` labeled vertices. Write `Cli(n,k)` for the sum over `k`-subsets of the product of their vertex weights and of their pair weights. Fixed-parameter size means a bound `f(k) n^c` for one constant `c` and some function `f`, as usual for the nonuniform model. Theorem 7.22 of Bläser–Engels shows that `Cli` is complete for `VW[1]` under fixed-parameter c-reductions, which preserve fixed-parameter circuit size by replacing oracle gates with circuits.

## Matching circuits give clique circuits

For `k >= 2`, put `m = binom(k,2)`. Use host vertices `(i,a,s)`, where `i` is a color in `[k]`, `a` is an original vertex in `[n]` and `s` is a replica index in `[t_i]`. The integers `t_i` will range from `0` to `k-1`. A pair between colors `i < j` has weight zero if its originals are equal, and otherwise carries the original pair weight. Choose one incident color-pair for each color. On that pair multiply the edge weight by the vertex weight for that endpoint. This gives substitutions of degree at most three. Pairs within any color have weight zero.

Require the matching to use exactly one edge of each color-pair type. This requirement is enforced by `2^m` evaluations of `Mat` with chosen types set to zero, using inclusion–exclusion to discard matchings that omit any type. There are exactly `m` edges, so all surviving types occur once. The count is also valid for the empty host, where the result is zero.

Expand the resulting polynomial by choosing the originals for both endpoints of every color-pair. A fixed choice contributes its weight product times `prod_(i,a) (t_i)_(j_(i,a))`, where `j_(i,a)` is the number of endpoint occurrences at original `a` in color `i` and `(t)_j = t(t-1)...(t-j+1)` is a falling factorial with `(t)_0=1`. Indeed the endpoints at every given `(i,a)` must receive distinct replica indices, and these choices are independent across groups. Its degree in `t_i` is at most `k-1`, so interpolation on the given grid recovers any coefficient, using substitutions on at most `k(k-1)n` host vertices and a number of evaluations depending only on `k`.

The coefficient of `prod_i t_i` vanishes unless every color uses a single original, because each nonempty `(i,a)` group contributes a factor of `t_i`. In the remaining terms each original is distinct across different colors, since all color-pair types occur and equal endpoints were given weight zero. The coefficient from every such endpoint assignment is `((-1)^(k-2) (k-2)!)^k` times the weighted clique monomial. Every `k`-subset arises from `k!` colorings. Divide by both nonzero constants to obtain `Cli(n,k)`. For `k=0,1` the clique polynomials are computed directly. Thus fixed-parameter-size matching circuits imply fixed-parameter-size clique circuits over characteristic zero.

## Clique circuits give every subgraph circuit

Let `H` be a simple pattern on `k` vertices. In `Cli(kn,k)`, use one color for each vertex of `H` and `n` original vertices per color. Set every vertex weight to one. Set a pair weight to zero if the colors or original vertices agree. Otherwise use the ordinary host pair variable for pairs of colors corresponding to edges of `H`, and use one for the other pairs. Each surviving clique selects exactly one original per color, and the nonzero terms sum precisely over injective maps from `H` to the host. Dividing by `|Aut(H)|` gives the usual subgraph-count normalization. This handles isolated pattern vertices as well. For edgeless patterns the polynomial is already the constant `binom(n,k)`. For every other pattern the bound `f(k)(kn)^c` implies the requested `f'(H)n^O(tw(H))` since its treewidth is at least one. Conversely the proposed bound applies to all matchings, which have treewidth one. This completes the equivalence by informal proof.
