# Four routes for simultaneous computability

Each route asks when many witnesses or certificates together prove something stronger than a single probe can. The four routes use field-degree gluing, cohomological restriction, transfer under minors, and counting prime reductions. The distinction between an unconditional theorem, a resolved scope of a published question, and a sufficient route to a larger target matters here.

| Route | Unconditional result | Published targets and scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Glue local witnesses at the exact degree](WitnessRoute.md) | For a connected reductive group over a local or global field, if the action on its algebraic fundamental group has cyclic Sylow subgroups, the least degree of a splitting field equals the cohomological period and the index. Over global fields, the extension is assembled from exact local degrees and can avoid a protected field. | This gives a positive answer in the local and global field cases of Gordon-Sarney–Suresh Question 1.4 for absolutely almost simple groups. Their counterexamples to the broader semisimple question are prior, and the manuscript credits them. The proof-complexity application gives scalar descent of sparse certificates for the same equations. [Question 1.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00516) · [Prior period–index bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07659). |
| [Control which everywhere-local obstructions survive extension](ArithmeticRoute.md) | Everywhere-locally trivial classes of connected linear groups over number fields can be killed simultaneously by one cyclic extension of the exact common period, with specified split places. For locally cyclic abelian norm-one tori the entire restriction profile is described by transfer. | There is a negative answer to van Hoften's prescribed-degree injectivity question, for every prescribed degree greater than one, already for a norm-one torus. The same point profiles occur in ordinary tensor isomorphism. This concerns fixed-input existence in Grochow–Qiao's field-comparison question. [van Hoften, Question 2](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/361972/field-extensions-that-preserve-given-cohomology-classes) · [Grochow–Qiao, Question 10.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S10.SS2). |
| [Transport certificates through matroid minors](CertificateRoute.md) | One signed substitution preserves degree and sparse size across all fields in the quadratic bracket encoding. Duality preserves the degree and direct sums take its pointwise minimum. The exact worst-case PC degree is three over $\mathbb F_2$ and five over $\mathbb F_3$ with field equations. | This transfers excluded-minor structure in matroid theory to quantitative proof complexity. For the open explicit quinary classification, it gives degree at most $757$ whenever a known obstruction on at most ten elements occurs. The unlisted exclusions and general tensor proof bounds remain separate questions. [Brettell, quinary excluded minors](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.14614v2#S1) · [Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She, Questions 7.1 and 7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7). |
| [Transfer growing finite-field searches to characteristic zero](AsymptoticRoute.md) | For bounded-format witness equations, one arithmetic Nullstellensatz estimate controls all false positives simultaneously. It gives density-one convergence for truncated asymptotic tensor rank and fractional Haemers bounds with diverging budgets. | This gives sufficient modular targets for $\omega_{\mathbb Q}=2$ and for the Shannon capacity of the complement of the Schläfli graph to equal six. Both numerical targets remain open in this work. The graph manuscript proves the first block-matrix ansatz has optimum seven in every characteristic. [Matrix multiplication](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/281/) · [Capacity bounds](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9858314/). |

The local witness gluing theorem is the strongest result for an existing open-question formulation. The growing-prime theorem gives the clearest use of a single sufficient mechanism in two distinct domains. Both applications require small modular witnesses built from the algebraic structure specific to each problem. The current proof transfers witnesses at tensor-power window $O(\log\log X)$ and graph block-size window $O(\sqrt{\log X})$, with sufficiently small constants. It gives no new numerical record.

The notes are manuscript proofs. The [Lean companion](PrimeCover.lean) verifies the finite counting step of the growing-prime theorem. The matroid proof includes a reproducible finite-field rank computation. Priority remains provisional for the new statements, with known prior results credited in their respective notes.
