# Exact arithmetic profiles from duality and transfer

Let $K$ be a number field. For a $K$-torus $T$, write $M=X^*(T)$ for its character lattice. Write $\Sha^i(K,-)$ for classes trivial after restriction at every place of $K$. A locally trivial torsor $X$ under $T$ has a class $\alpha\in\Sha^1(K,T)$, and $X(E)\ne\varnothing$ exactly when $\alpha$ restricts to zero. This note computes that profile by a finite transfer calculation and constructs degree-optimal extensions realizing its positives.

## 1. A finite transfer formula for every extension

**Theorem 1.** Let $L/K$ be a finite Galois splitting field of $T$, with group $G$, and $E/K$ any finite extension in a common algebraic closure. Write $F=E\cap L$, $H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)$, and $e=[E:F]$. Identify $\operatorname{Gal}(LE/E)$ with $H$. For either $(U,J)=(K,G)$ or $(E,H)$ define
$$
 B_U=\ker\left[H^2(J,M)\longrightarrow
                       \prod_{w\in\Omega_U} H^2(D_w,M)\right],
$$
using decomposition groups for $L/K$ or $LE/E$, respectively. Inflation identifies $B_U=\Sha^2(U,M)$. With respect to the Poitou–Tate pairing over $K$,
$$
 \ker\bigl[\Sha^1(K,T)\longrightarrow\Sha^1(E,T)\bigr]
       =\bigl(e\,\operatorname{cor}_{H}^{G}(B_E)\bigr)^\perp .
 \tag{1}
$$
In particular, the entire positive and negative profile of a given locally trivial torus torsor is decided by the finite classes in this formula.

*Proof.* Over a splitting field the lattice is a finite sum of copies of $\mathbb Z$. For any number field $N$, one has $H^2(N,\mathbb Z)=H^1(N,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$ because positive-degree cohomology with trivial $\mathbb Q$-coefficients vanishes. A continuous character of the absolute Galois group of $N$ which is zero at every place is zero by Chebotarev. Thus every class in $\Sha^2(U,M)$ becomes trivial over its splitting field. Since $H^1(N,M)=0$ over a splitting field, inflation–restriction in degree two identifies the global kernel of restriction with $H^2(J,M)$, and analogous local inflation maps from $H^2(D_w,M)$ are injective. This proves the identifications. The product of restriction tests in $B_U$ can be taken over all cyclic subgroup classes, which occur by Chebotarev, and the finitely many ramified and archimedean decomposition groups.

For $\beta\in B_E$, its class at $E$ is the restriction of the inflation of the same element of $H^2(H,M)$ over $F$. That inflated class over $F$ need not be locally trivial. Restriction followed by corestriction from $E$ to $F$ multiplies the full cohomology class by $e$. For $F/K$, corestriction commutes with inflation from the finite quotients $H\le G$, since $H$ has as preimage the absolute Galois group of $F$. Thus on the displayed groups the full corestriction $E/K$ is $e\,\operatorname{cor}_{H}^{G}$. Its image lies in $B_K$ by the compatibility of corestriction with localization.

The perfect pairing $\Sha^1(U,T)\times\Sha^2(U,M)\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ satisfies $\langle\operatorname{res}\alpha,\beta\rangle_E=\langle\alpha,\operatorname{cor}\beta\rangle_K$. The adjunction uses the cohomological cup-product projection formula in its construction and the equality of local invariants under Brauer corestriction. The annihilator criterion now gives (1). $\square$ [Tate, cohomology of tori in Galois extensions](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/nagoya-mathematical-journal/article/cohomology-groups-of-tori-in-finite-galois-extensions-of-number-fields/81A85EFA72AA0B0937DF1019F89373B1) · [Harari–Szamuely, arithmetic duality including tori](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0304480) · [Brown, group cohomology and transfer](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~kbrown/papers/cohomology_hangzhou.pdf)

## 2. Simultaneous cyclic splitting of the exact least degree

**Theorem 2.** For any finite family of tori $T_i/K$ and any subset $A_i\subseteq\Sha^1(K,T_i)$, let $m\ge1$ be the least positive integer annihilating every $A_i$. The smallest degree of an extension that trivializes all these classes is $m$. One such extension can be chosen cyclic, linearly disjoint from any prescribed finite extension of $K$, and totally split at any prescribed finite set of places. More precisely, for each degree $d\ge1$ there is such a controlled cyclic extension $E/K$ for which the kernel on each $\Sha^1(K,T_i)$ is exactly its $d$-torsion subgroup.

*Proof.* Choose a common finite Galois splitting field $L/K$ for all the lattices and demand $E\cap L=K$. Demand that each ramified and archimedean place of $L/K$ split completely in $E$. The identification $\operatorname{Gal}(LE/E)=G$ then preserves the local decomposition groups at these places, and at the remaining places the possible cyclic groups on both sides are exactly the same by Chebotarev. Thus $B_E=B_K$ for each lattice in Theorem 1, and (1) says that $\operatorname{res}\alpha=0$ exactly when $d\alpha=0$. On arbitrary finite extensions the period of a class divides the degree of any extension splitting it, by $\operatorname{cor}\operatorname{res}=[E:K]$ on $H^1(K,T_i)$. This proves sharpness and reduces existence to the controlled cyclic extension.

For $d>1$, write $S$ for the finite set of rational primes below the specified finite places and the ramified places of $L/K$. Choose a rational prime $\ell$ splitting completely in a finite Galois extension of $\mathbb Q$ containing $\zeta_{2d}$, a $d$-th root of every $q\in S$, and the normal closure of the protected extension and of $L$ over $\mathbb Q$. It can be taken away from $S$, and infinitely many choices remain by Chebotarev. Let $C_\ell$ be the unique degree-$d$ field in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)$. It is totally real since $2d\mid\ell-1$, and each $q\in S$ splits because $q$ is a $d$-th power modulo $\ell$. Every nontrivial subfield of $C_\ell$ is ramified at $\ell$, whereas the protected normal closure is unramified there. Thus $C_\ell$ is linearly disjoint from that closure. Take $E=KC_\ell$. It is cyclic of relative degree $d$, satisfies the prescribed local splitting, and is unramified outside the places above $\ell$. The degree-one case is immediate. $\square$

**Corollary 3.** Let $X$ be an everywhere locally trivial torsor under any smooth connected affine group over $K$. There is a canonically attached period, given for a reductive group by the Borovoi–Reichstein power operation. It is the least degree of a splitting extension and divides the degree of every splitting extension. A minimum-degree extension can be taken cyclic and subject to the disjointness and local splitting conditions of Theorem 2. For finitely many torsors the simultaneous minimum is the least common multiple of their periods.

*Proof.* In characteristic zero the unipotent radical is split, as are its forms, and its first cohomology is trivial. Levi decomposition reduces the assertion to the reductive quotient without changing the profile at any number-field extension. For a connected reductive group $G_0$, take a central $z$-extension $1\to P\to J\to G_0\to1$ with $P$ quasi-trivial and $J^{\rm der}$ simply connected. The obstruction to lifting the class of $X$ lies in $H^2(K,P)$, and is locally trivial because $X$ is. It vanishes by the Brauer Hasse principle applied to each factor of the quasi-trivial torus. The lift $\xi\in H^1(K,J)$ is locally trivial since $H^1(K_v,P)=0$. Over every finite extension, triviality of the lift is equivalent to triviality of the original class, again by the trivial $H^1$ of $P$.

Put $T=J/J^{\rm der}$ and let $\alpha\in\Sha^1(K,T)$ be the image of $\xi$. The abelianization of $H^1(U,J)$ is $H^1(U,T)$ over each number field $U/K$, because the derived group is simply connected. Borovoi's Cartesian square of abelianization with the real localization map says that on the everywhere locally trivial classes, triviality of $\xi_U$ is equivalent to triviality of $\alpha_U$. The power operation is compatible with $J\to G_0$, with abelianization, and with the real localization map. Hence the period of $X$ is exactly the order of $\alpha$, with no dependence on the choice of $z$-extension. Apply Theorem 2 to the torus images, and use restriction–corestriction for the sharp lower bound. $\square$ [Borovoi, abelianization as stated in Borovoi–Reichstein–Gille §4, and the power operation in §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.07659) · [Colliot-Thélène, connected reductive resolutions](https://archive.numdam.org/articles/10.1016/j.crma.2004.06.012/)

In particular, an everywhere locally trivial connected-group torsor that has a zero-cycle of degree $d$ has a closed point of degree dividing $d$. All residue degrees in the zero-cycle are divisible by the period. The controlled cyclic extension of degree equal to the period supplies an actual closed point of that degree, since the residue field of its image has degree both divisible by and at most the period.

## 3. Exact norm and tensor profiles from homology

**Theorem 4.** Let $L/K$ be Galois with all decomposition groups cyclic and $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\cong(\mathbb Z/m)^r$, $r\ge2$. Let $T$ be the norm-one torus of $L/K$. Identify $\Sha^1(K,T)$ with $\bigwedge^2 G$. Given $E/K$, use $F,H,e$ as in Theorem 1. Choose a basis $u_1,\ldots,u_r$ of $\mathbb Z^r$ in which the inverse image of $H$ under $\mathbb Z^r\to G$ has basis $s_i u_i$, where $s_i\mid m$. Put $v_i=s_i u_i\bmod m\in H$. The restriction of a class $\alpha$ is $e$ times the map
$$
 u_i\wedge u_j\longmapsto
       \Bigl(\prod_{k\ne i,j}s_k\Bigr)\,v_i\wedge v_j
       \quad\text{from }\bigwedge^2 G\text{ to }\bigwedge^2H .
 \tag{2}
$$
In particular, if $m=p$ is prime, each nontrivial locally trivial norm torsor splits over $E$ exactly when $p\mid[E:K]$.

*Proof.* The character sequence is $0\to\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z[G]\to M\to0$. Restricted to any subgroup the middle module is a sum of regular permutation lattices and is acyclic in positive cohomological degrees. Dimension shifting gives $H^2(H,M)\simeq H^3(H,\mathbb Z)$ naturally for restriction and corestriction. Since $H^3(C,\mathbb Z)=0$ for finite cyclic $C$, all the localization conditions in Theorem 1 are automatic over $K$ and every $E$. Now $H^3(H,\mathbb Z)\cong H^2(H,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)\cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_2(H,\mathbb Z),\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$. Its dual identifies with $H_2(H,\mathbb Z)=\bigwedge^2 H$. Corestriction above is dual to homology transfer.

To compute the transfer use its naturality in the pullback square of the finite-index subgroups $H\le G$, $\widetilde H\le\mathbb Z^r$, where $\widetilde H$ is the preimage. The lattice inclusion $\widetilde H\hookrightarrow\mathbb Z^r$ maps the $ij$-th basis wedge to $s_i s_j$ times the ambient $ij$-th wedge. The inclusion after transfer is multiplication by the index $\prod s_i$. Since the inclusion on lattice wedges is injective, this gives the coefficient $\prod_{k\ne i,j}s_k$. The square descends the formula to $G,H$. The extra factor $e$ is exactly the corestriction factor in (1). For $m=p$, a proper subgroup has some $s_k=p$, which either makes one of the two output wedge factors zero or makes the coefficient zero modulo $p$. If $H=G$, (2) is multiplication by $[E:K]$. This proves the last assertion. $\square$ [Pollio–Rapinchuk, Tate's $H^3$ description of the norm obstruction](https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/ixqrlw/files/multinorm_reprint_01_9.pdf) · [Koymans–Rome, local groups and exterior powers](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12978)

**Corollary 5.** For every prime $p$ and finite $s$, one can select $s$ probes of equal degree $p^2$ over any number field $K$ such that each binary pattern on those probes is the exact profile of an everywhere locally trivial norm-one torus torsor with least point degree $p^2$. A single torus suffices for all these patterns.

*Proof.* Build a locally cyclic Galois extension with group $(C_{p^2})^{2s+2}$ by successively using the cyclic cyclotomic fields in the proof of Theorem 2. Require the new auxiliary rational prime to split completely in the previous fields, and require every previous auxiliary prime to split in the new field. These conditions reduce to one finite Chebotarev prescription at each step. At any ramified place at most one of the cyclic factors contributes a nontrivial local extension, while all other places have cyclic decomposition by unramifiedness. Each cyclic factor is linearly disjoint from the previous compositum and from $K$ by total ramification at its new prime.

For probe $i$, take the degree-$p^2$ subfield corresponding to the subgroup $H_i$ in which only coordinates $2i-1,2i$ must be divisible by $p$. Write $w_j$ for the standard basis of $(\mathbb Z/p^2)^{2s+2}$ and $r=2s+2$. Use the locally trivial class
$$
 \alpha=w_{r-1}\wedge w_r+\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_jw_{2j-1}\wedge w_{2j}
 \qquad(c_j\in\{0,1\}).
$$
Its period is $p^2$. On $H_i$ every other displayed term acquires a factor of $p^2$ and vanishes. Its $i$-th term maps to $c_i$ times a generator of a summand of order $p$. Thus probe $i$ is positive exactly if $c_i=0$. Theorem 2 gives the claimed least degree. $\square$

## 4. Applications and scope

**Preserving the global-to-local kernel.** Pol van Hoften asks whether, for every connected reductive $G_0/\mathbb Q$, degree $d$, and prescribed prime $q$, one can find a cyclic totally real tamely ramified extension of degree $d$, with $q$ inert, on which the restriction of the global-to-local kernel is injective. The natural arithmetic question is explicitly posed in item 2 of the researcher's post. The following counterexample works even in the absence of every restriction on the extension other than its degree. Take the norm-one torus of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{17},\sqrt{89})/\mathbb Q$. This extension is locally cyclic. The primes are both $1\bmod8$, each splits in the other's quadratic field by reciprocity and $89\equiv4\bmod17$, and the other finite places are unramified. Its kernel $\Sha^1$ has order two by Theorem 4, and every quadratic extension annihilates it. More generally, choosing a locally cyclic $(C_p)^2$-extension as in Corollary 5's construction gives a counterexample whenever $p\mid d$. The construction of degree-$d$ extensions realizing the exact kernel in Theorem 2 also gives a sufficient retention criterion in response to item 1. [Van Hoften's research question and Borovoi's reduction comments](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/361972/field-extensions-that-preserve-given-cohomology-classes)

**Zero-cycles and the minimum splitting degree.** Totaro's general question for torsors and homogeneous spaces has counterexamples by Gordon-Sarney–Suresh, including over number fields. Corollary 3 treats the locus of everywhere locally trivial torsors under arbitrary connected affine groups over number fields and gives exact periods, simultaneous cyclic extensions, and local control. The general period/index results of Borovoi–Reichstein–Gille include period divisibility and power bounds and treat the split fundamental-group case. The argument here uses preservation of the lattice's global-to-local cohomology on selected probes instead of requiring split fundamental group. This is an affirmative subcase with extra control, while the published general statement is already false. [Totaro, Question 0.2](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~totaro/papers/public_html/e8.pdf) · [Gordon-Sarney–Suresh and erratum cited in Borovoi–Reichstein–Gille §8.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.07659#page=21)

**Ordinary tensor isomorphism.** For a geometrically isomorphic pair of ordinary tensors, the isomorphism scheme is a torsor under the source stabilizer. When that stabilizer is connected and all completion tests are positive, Corollary 3 determines the least field degree of an isomorphism and supplies cyclic witnesses disjoint from protected fields. The norm torsors and their profiles in Theorem 4 and Corollary 5 also transfer to ordinary three-tensors through the established affine-group-torsor reduction: Milne realizes the affine group scheme as the automorphism group of a finite-dimensional algebra, twisting realizes the torsor as the algebra isomorphism scheme, and the field-uniform Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk construction transfers each field's existence test. This last transfer makes no claim about connectedness of the resulting tensor stabilizer. These are controlled arithmetic results for the scalar-extension aspect of Grochow–Qiao's across-fields question. Complexity reductions between arbitrary base fields are a separate question. [Grochow–Qiao, across-fields Open Question 10.6 in the combined preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S10.SS2) · [Milne](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05708) · [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219)

The arithmetic inputs are classical duality and finite-group cohomology. The claims made here are the uniform profile formula, the controlled simultaneous minimum, and the identified specializations. This is a manuscript derivation and priority beyond the sources checked remains undetermined.
