# Characteristic-zero asymptotic bounds from growing finite-field probes

This route tests one theorem in algebraic complexity and zero-error information theory. Its inputs are bounded-format algebraic descriptions of witnesses. The resource bound may grow with the primes, and the witnesses may vary from prime to prime. We target matrix multiplication exponent two and the Shannon capacity of the complement of the Schläfli graph.

## 1. A uniform transfer bound

Let $\mathcal S$ be a finite list of systems of integer polynomial equations. Weak inequalities are not needed. Nonvanishing, if needed, can be encoded by an inverse variable. Suppose each system has at most $N$ variables, at most $Q$ equations, degree at most $D\ge2$, and logarithmic coefficient height at most $H$. Put
$$
 U=4N(N+1)D^N\bigl(H+\log Q+D(N+7)\log((N+1)D)\bigr).
$$
Increase $N,Q$ to at least two, pad the variables and equations if necessary, and treat identically zero equations as having height zero.

**Theorem 1.** For $X>1$, at most $|\mathcal S|U/\log X$ primes in $[X,2X]$ admit a solution over any extension of their residue field to some member of $\mathcal S$ that has no solution over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. In particular, if more primes than this each solve at least one system on the list, some system has a characteristic-zero solution. The list, the format, and the selected system may all depend on $X$.

*Proof.* For each characteristic-zero negative system, the effective arithmetic Nullstellensatz gives an integer $a\ne0$ in the ideal generated by its equations, with $\log|a|\le U$. At any prime where that system specializes to a positive answer, the prime divides $a$. The product of the distinct prime divisors in $[X,2X]$ is at least $X^k$ if there are $k$. Thus $k\le U/\log X$. Sum over the negative systems. The certificate degree is irrelevant to this counting argument. Lean verifies the finite-union implication in [PrimeCover.lean](PrimeCover.lean). [Krick–Pardo–Sombra, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9911094). $\square$

If $D$ is constant, $H+\log Q+\log |\mathcal S|=O(N+\log\log X)$, and $N=o(\log X)$, the number of exceptional primes is $X^{o(1)}$ for the two lists below. More generally their format estimates permit $N\le c\log X$ with a sufficiently small $c>0$, giving $O(X^{1/2})$ exceptional primes for large $X$. Both are negligible compared to the $X/\log X$ primes in the interval. One can use a finite numerical threshold directly by evaluating $U$. This conclusion depends on the counts of distinct successful primes, so an arbitrary number of successful extensions of a single prime count as one success.

## 2. Tensor rank in a growing power window

For a fixed integral $k$-tensor $T$, let $T_p$ denote reduction at a rational prime and write
$$
 \rho_F(T)=\inf_{m\ge1}\operatorname{rank}_F(T^{\boxtimes m})^{1/m}.
$$
For $B=\max(2,\max_i\dim V_i)$, a witness of rank at most $r$ in the $m$th power has $r\sum_i(\dim V_i)^m$ scalar coordinates. Entrywise equality to its sum of $r$ simple tensors is a degree-$k$ system with at most $B^{km}$ equations and logarithmic height $O(m)$. The trivial rank bound is $B^{km}$. Thus all bounded-power rank probes $1\le m\le M$ and $0\le r\le B^{km}$ constitute a list of length $\exp(O_T(M))$ with at most $\exp(O_T(M))$ variables per system. Theorem 1 applies simultaneously to the whole list with at most $X^{o(1)}$ exceptional primes if $M=o(\log\log X)$. The same conclusion holds with $M=\lfloor c_T\log\log X\rfloor$ for a small enough positive constant $c_T$, since the variable bound is then $O((\log X)^\theta)$ for some $\theta<1$.

**Corollary 2.** Fix $c_T$ as above and take the minimum rank-root value of $T_p$ over the power window $1\le m\le\lfloor c_T\log\log X\rfloor$. Over the primes $p\in[X,2X]$ these values converge in relative density to $\rho_{\mathbb Q}(T)$. This holds whether ranks are taken over prime fields or over algebraic closures. In particular, each positive-density improvement within the window yields a characteristic-zero bound at the same threshold.

*Proof.* At every prime outside the exceptional set, every rank witness in the window implies a witness of the same rank and power over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. By definition, each rank-root value is therefore at least $\rho_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(T)$.

For the other direction fix $\varepsilon>0$, choose a single $m$ and a decomposition of rank $r$ with $r^{1/m}<\rho_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(T)+\varepsilon/2$. Its coefficients lie in a number field $K$ of degree $e$. For all primes outside a fixed finite set, the witness reduces at a prime of $K$ over $p$, over a residue field of degree at most $e$. In any finite field extension of degree at most $e$, a rank-$r_0$ witness for a base-field $k$-tensor descends to a decomposition of rank at most $e^k r_0$: expand each tensor factor into extension-basis coordinates and apply a base-field linear functional on the extension that sends $1$ to $1$. Use the witness to the $s$th power before descent, with $s$ fixed large enough that $(e^k r^s)^{1/(ms)}<\rho_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(T)+\varepsilon$. This single exponent $ms$ eventually belongs to the window. The identical descent argument over $\mathbb Q$ proves equality of the two characteristic-zero asymptotic ranks. $\square$

**Open target.** For the integral tensor $T$ of $2$-by-$2$ matrix multiplication, $\rho_{\mathbb Q}(T)=2^{\omega_{\mathbb Q}}$. Thus $\omega_{\mathbb Q}=2$ is equivalent to the following bounded search statement. For each $\varepsilon>0$, at a positive proportion of primes in infinitely many intervals $[X,2X]$, some $m\le c_T\log\log X$ has an actual rank-$\lfloor 2^{(2+\varepsilon)m}\rfloor$ or smaller decomposition. Necessity holds at all sufficiently large primes. Sufficiency follows by applying Theorem 1 to that list of rank thresholds. The route replaces a characteristic-zero witness with many modular witnesses and a quantitative power budget. It does not supply the modular decompositions. [Matrix multiplication exponent and the $9/4$ upper bound, Shpilka](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/281/) · [Asymptotic rank and polynomial characterization, Christandl–Hoeberechts–Nieuwboer–Vrana–Zuiddam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15789).

## 3. Fractional Haemers bounds in a growing block window

For a graph $G$, a fitting matrix has square blocks of size $b$, identity blocks on the diagonal and zero blocks at distinct nonadjacent vertices. Its minimum rank divided by $b$, infimized over $b$, gives the fractional Haemers bound $h_f^F(G)$, an upper bound on Shannon capacity. A rank-at-most-$r$ fitting matrix is $UV$, where $U,V$ have sizes $|V(G)|b$ by $r$ and $r$ by $|V(G)|b$. The fitting requirements impose $O_G(b^2)$ quadratic equations of constant height in $O_G(b^2)$ variables, since $r\le |V(G)|b$. [Bukh–Cox, Definition 1 and Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00476).

**Corollary 3.** For a small enough $c_G>0$, the minimum over $b\le c_G\sqrt{\log X}$ of the modular fitting-rank ratios over primes in $[X,2X]$ converges in relative density to $h_f^{\mathbb Q}(G)$. Ranks over either prime fields or their algebraic closures give the same limit.

*Proof.* Apply Theorem 1 to all $O_G(\log X)$ pairs $(b,r)$, with at most $O_G(c_G^2\log X)$ variables per system. Choose $c_G$ so the exceptional-prime bound is $O_G(X^{1/2})$ for sufficiently large $X$. For the other inequality pick a fixed fitting matrix over a number field $K$ whose ratio is within $\varepsilon$ of $h_f^{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(G)$, and reduce its factorization outside a finite set of primes. Restriction of scalars of the underlying linear maps multiplies both the block size and the rank by the residue degree, so preserves the ratio. The resulting block size is bounded uniformly in $p$. The same restriction over $\mathbb Q$ proves $h_f^{\mathbb Q}(G)=h_f^{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(G)$. $\square$

**Open target.** Let $G$ be the complement of the Schläfli graph. Its independent set of size six gives $\Theta(G)\ge6$, and Haemers's matrix gives $\Theta(G)\le7$. For each $\varepsilon>0$, a positive-density modular construction of fitting rank at most $(6+\varepsilon)b$ in the above block window would prove $\Theta(G)=6$. It would suffice to give a modular construction for one fixed $\varepsilon<1$ to improve the upper bound. The statement about capacity is one-way since fractional Haemers need not attain Shannon capacity. [Alon, explicit graph and Haemers bound](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/shann3.pdf) · [Sason, Remark 17 and the $6$–$7$ bounds](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9858314/).

**Exact test of the first construction.** The entire block family $I\otimes I_b+A\otimes C$, where $A$ is the adjacency matrix of $G$ and $C$ is arbitrary, has optimal ratio $7$ over every field and for every $b$. More generally, extend the field to an algebraic closure, put $C$ in Jordan form, and reorder by its Jordan blocks. A length-$j$ block at eigenvalue $\lambda$ produces a block triangular matrix with $j$ copies of $I+\lambda A$ on the diagonal. Using the same nonzero rank minor on each diagonal gives at least $j\operatorname{rank}(I+\lambda A)$ rank. A scalar $C=\lambda I_b$ attains that ratio whenever $\lambda$ belongs to the field.

For this graph the integral adjacency matrix has eigenvalues $10,1,-5$ with multiplicities $1,20,6$. This follows from the strongly regular parameters $(27,10,1,5)$: $A^2=5I-4A+5J$, and use the trace on the orthogonal complement of the constant vector. For characteristic different from $2,3,5$ the three eigenvalues remain distinct, with unchanged multiplicities, since the characteristic polynomial and the polynomial $(A-10I)(A-I)(A+5I)=0$ reduce integrally. The smallest possible rank is $7$, at $\lambda=-1$. For characteristic $2,3,5$, every nonzero occurring eigenvalue reduces to $1$. Thus the only possible singular scalar is again $-1$. The matrix $I-A$ has rank at most seven in all fields because its integer $8$-minors vanish. Its principal minor on the six edges $\{1,j\}$ for $3\le j\le8$, followed by $\{3,4\}$, has diagonal blocks $I_6,1$, and off-diagonal vector with exactly two entries $-1$. Its determinant is $1-2=-1$. Here vertices are pairs from $\{1,\ldots,8\}$ other than $\{1,2\}$, with same-type disjoint pairs and different-type intersecting pairs adjacent, and type records incidence with $1$ or $2$. This verifies the lower bound in all characteristics. Therefore a successful modular improvement via fractional Haemers needs fitting matrices with genuinely edge-dependent blocks. [Alon, graph model](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/shann3.pdf).

The arithmetic input is classical. The contribution in this route is the growing-window statement and the same sufficient transfer across two different witness optimizations. The windows and graph-family optimum are proved here in manuscript form. Neither open numerical target has been closed.
