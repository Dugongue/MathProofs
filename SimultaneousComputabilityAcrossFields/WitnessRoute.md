# Exact splitting degrees and sparse certificates from compatible local data

## 1. A local field can avoid a Sylow-cyclic action at any degree

Call a finite group **Sylow-cyclic** if all its Sylow subgroups are cyclic. Let $F$ be a nonarchimedean local field and $T/F$ a finite Galois extension with Sylow-cyclic group. For every positive integer $d$ there is a finite separable field $E/F$ of degree $d$ linearly disjoint from $T$.

To prove this, use the Zassenhaus structure theorem to write $\operatorname{Gal}(T/F)=C_a\rtimes C_b$ with $\gcd(a,b)=1$, where $C_a$ is the normal subgroup. A normal series of prime factors for $C_a$, followed by the preimages of a prime-factor series for $C_b$, shows the group is supersolvable. All maximal subgroups of a finite supersolvable group have prime index. For completeness, induct using a normal subgroup of prime order. If a maximal subgroup contains it, pass to the quotient. If it does not, adjoining the normal subgroup gives the full group, and the index is its prime order. Thus every nontrivial intermediate field contains a field of prime degree over $F$. [Drinfeld, On the pro-semisimple completion of the fundamental group, Appendix C](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2017.06.029).

For each prime $\ell$, the index-$\ell$ subgroups of $\operatorname{Gal}(T/F)$, if they exist, are conjugate. If $\ell\mid b$, every such subgroup contains $C_a$ and is the preimage of the unique subgroup of index $\ell$ in $C_b$. If $\ell\mid a$, each has intersection with $C_a$ equal to its unique subgroup of index $\ell$, and surjects onto $C_b$. Modulo that intersection these subgroups are complements to the normal group $C_\ell$. They are conjugate by Schur–Zassenhaus. The corresponding degree-$\ell$ intermediate fields therefore have a common ramification type, either unramified or totally ramified.

Factor $d=ef$ as follows. Assign the whole $\ell$-part of $d$ to $f$ when the degree-$\ell$ subfields of $T/F$ exist and are ramified. Assign it to $e$ when they are unramified. Other parts can go to either factor. Take the unramified extension of $F$ of degree $f$, then a totally ramified extension of degree $e$ given by an Eisenstein polynomial. If $e>1$ and $\varpi$ is a uniformizer, $X^e+\varpi X+\varpi$ is separable and Eisenstein even in equal characteristic dividing $e$. The resulting $E$ has ramification degree $e$ and residue degree $f$. It contains none of the prime-degree subfields of $T$, since those would divide the opposite ramification or residue factor. Hence $E\cap T=F$, proving the assertion.

## 2. The index of a reductive torsor is attained at the period

Let $G$ be a connected reductive group over a nonarchimedean local or global field $K$. Let $M=\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(G)$ and let $\Theta$ be the finite image of $\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{sep}}/K)$ acting on $M$. Assume $\Theta$ is Sylow-cyclic. For $\xi\in H^1(K,G)$, the degrees of finite separable fields that split $\xi$ are exactly the positive multiples of $\operatorname{per}(\xi)$, the period of the Borovoi–Reichstein power operation. In particular a field of degree $\operatorname{ind}(\xi)=\operatorname{per}(\xi)$ splits $\xi$, and the torsor has a closed étale point of that degree. Over a global field the splitting extension can also be required linearly disjoint from any prescribed finite separable extension of $K$. [Borovoi–Reichstein, The power operation in Galois cohomology, Theorem 8.7, Lemma 10.1 and Proposition 12.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07659).

For the local statement, the Kottwitz identification sends $H^1(K,G)$ to the torsion of the Galois coinvariants $M_{\Gamma_K}$, with restriction corresponding to transfer. In these coordinates the period of $\xi$ is the additive order of its image, by Borovoi–Reichstein Proposition 7.1. Take any target degree $d$ divisible by this order. Apply §1 to the Galois action field of $M$. Over the resulting degree-$d$ extension the image of Galois on $M$ is still $\Theta$, so transfer on the common coinvariant group is exactly multiplication by $d$. Thus the class vanishes. The other inclusion follows from period dividing index.

For the global statement, localization of $\xi$ is trivial at all but finitely many places. The finitely many local periods divide the global period, by compatibility of the power operation with restriction. At each finite place with nontrivial localization choose a separable local splitting field of degree exactly $d$ using the local result and the fact that the local Galois action group is a subgroup of $\Theta$. At a real place with nontrivial localization, the period is two, so prescribe the étale algebra $\mathbb C^{d/2}$. Complex places give no constraint. Add one finite auxiliary place splitting completely in the normal closure of the prescribed auxiliary extension and in the action field, which exists by Chebotarev.

Choose separable monic polynomials of degree $d$ for these local étale algebras. At real places use a product of distinct irreducible quadratics. By Krasner's lemma and stability of simple real roots and nonreal pairs, the prescribed local isomorphism types hold on open coefficient neighborhoods. At the auxiliary place prescribe an Eisenstein polynomial. In positive characteristic also choose its linear coefficient to have valuation one for $d>1$, so the global polynomial's derivative is nonzero when the characteristic divides $d$. Weak approximation for the coefficients meets every condition and gives a global extension of degree $d$. The chosen auxiliary prime splits in the desired avoidance field, so the polynomial stays Eisenstein there, proving linear disjointness. The case $d=1$ uses $K$ itself.

The global class restricted to the constructed field is locally trivial everywhere. The action group remains Sylow-cyclic under every separable extension. Borovoi–Reichstein Proposition 12.5 therefore makes the restricted class trivial. Period divides index over global fields as well, proving exactness. The index in their Definition 8.1 is unchanged if all finite splitting extensions are allowed, so inseparable closed points in positive characteristic do not change the lower bound. A rational point over a degree-period splitting field has an image closed point on the torsor. Its residue degree divides the period and is a splitting degree, hence equals the period. This proves the closed-point assertion.

The same result holds for simultaneous splitting of finitely many torsors, possibly under different groups, when the action on the direct sum of their fundamental groups is Sylow-cyclic. Apply the theorem to the product group and torsor. Alternatively, take $d$ a multiple of all their periods and use the joint action field at each finite probe, then apply the Hasse principle for each factor. The common splitting degrees are precisely the multiples of the least common multiple of their periods.

## 3. Consequence for the absolutely simple form of Totaro's question

Gordon-Sarney and Suresh's Question 1.4 asks whether a torsor under a smooth absolutely simple linear algebraic group with a zero-cycle of degree $d$ has a closed étale point of degree dividing $d$. For an absolutely almost simple group the action on the algebraic fundamental group factors through the based Dynkin diagram action. Indeed the Weyl group acts trivially on the coweight lattice modulo coroots, and the intermediate fundamental group is the corresponding submodule for the given central isogeny type. Irreducible Dynkin diagram automorphism groups are trivial, cyclic of order two, or $S_3$ in type $D_4$. Their subgroups and quotients are Sylow-cyclic. Section 2 therefore answers Question 1.4 over all nonarchimedean local fields and all global fields, for every central isogeny type. It also gives an actual point of the index degree and complete splitting degree spectra under its broader reductive hypothesis. Over $\mathbb R$ the conclusion is immediate, with the only possible nonsplit index equal to two. For an arbitrary field, the local coinvariant description and the global Hasse step used here do not apply. [Gordon-Sarney–Suresh, Totaro's Question on Zero-Cycles on Torsors, Question 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.00516).

This use of the local avoidance lemma differs from the cyclic splitting result of Borovoi–Reichstein Corollary 12.6. Their extension is cyclic of degree $n\,\operatorname{cp}(n,|\Theta|)$ for $n=\operatorname{per}(\xi)$, while here the extension has degree $n$ and may be non-Galois. Their Corollary 8.8 already treats degree-one descent for all reductive groups over these fields. Bayer-Fluckiger–Lee–Parimala's broader homogeneous-space result likewise treats degree-one zero-cycles, in Proposition 8.1 and Remark 8.4. Ofek–Reichstein–Scavia's Theorem 1.5 concerns groups defined over an algebraically closed field and torsors over its iterated Laurent series fields. [Borovoi–Reichstein](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.07659) · [Bayer-Fluckiger–Lee–Parimala](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.12532) · [Ofek–Reichstein–Scavia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01595).

### An explicit pair of probe degrees outside the hypothesis

The broader semisimple negative result is already known, including the rank-eight dyadic example and a globalization, in Gordon-Sarney–Suresh Theorem 3.5 and Examples 3.6–3.7. Here is a direct choice of probes of degrees four and six for this phenomenon.

Set $K=\mathbb Q_2$ and $L=K(\sqrt{-1},\sqrt2,\sqrt5)$. The square classes of $-1,2,5$ form a basis for $K^\times/K^{\times2}$, by parity of the valuation and the criterion that an odd unit is a square exactly when it is $1\bmod 8$. Thus $[L:K]=8$ and $L$ contains every quadratic extension of $K$. Let $D$ be the quaternion division algebra over $L$, of invariant $1/2$. The $K$-torsor $X=\operatorname{Res}_{L/K}\operatorname{Isom}_{L\text{-alg}}(M_2(L),D)$ is a torsor under $\operatorname{Res}_{L/K}\operatorname{PGL}_2$, connected and semisimple, with eight geometric simple factors. An $E$-point is precisely splitting of $D$ on every field factor of $L\otimes_K E$. Any quadratic $E/K$ is contained in $L$, so these factors are copies of $L$. An odd-degree $E$ is disjoint from $L$ and leaves the invariant $1/2$ nonzero. In particular the index is even and degree two never occurs.

To get two small splitting fields, let $N/K$ be the splitting field of $X^4+2X+2$. The polynomial is Eisenstein. Its cubic resolvent $X^3-8X-4$ is irreducible. For a proposed root of valuation $v\in\mathbb Z$, the valuations of its three terms are $3v,3+v,2$. When $v\le0$ the first is uniquely least, and when $v\ge1$ the last is uniquely least. The discriminant is $16\cdot101$, a nonsquare since $101\equiv5\bmod8$. Transitivity of the quartic and cubic actions gives group order divisible by four and three, and the nonsquare discriminant excludes $A_4$, hence the group is $S_4$. The fixed fields of a point-stabilizer $S_3$ and of a $C_4$ generated by a four-cycle have degrees four and six. Neither subgroup is contained in the unique index-two subgroup $A_4$, so neither field contains a quadratic subfield of $K$. As $L/K$ is multiquadratic, both fields are disjoint from $L$. Their even relative degrees multiply $1/2$ to zero. The gcd of these witnessed degrees is two, so $X$ has index two.

For an example over $\mathbb Q$, use $L_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-1},\sqrt2,\sqrt5)$, with one place above $2$. The rational prime $41$ splits completely in $L_0$: it is $1\bmod 8$ and $1\bmod5$, so $-1,2,5$ are all squares modulo $41$. Prescribe the invariant $1/2$ at the place above $2$ and at one place above $41$, and zero elsewhere. These invariants sum to zero and determine a degree-two division algebra by Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether. For each $d=4,6$, prescribe at $2$ the local degree-$d$ splitting field just constructed, and at $41$ the unramified extension of degree $d$. Weak approximation for the coefficients of monic polynomials and Krasner's lemma furnish those simultaneous completions on a degree-$d$ number field, since the polynomial is locally irreducible at $2$. Thus every local invariant vanishes over the field compositum with $L_0$. The two nontrivial global Brauer supports are split, giving the two global points. For any odd-degree number field some local factor above $2$ has odd degree, and for a quadratic number field the factor degrees are one or two. In either case the obstruction above $2$ persists. The two probe degrees again give index two without a quadratic point. [Gordon-Sarney–Suresh, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.00516) · [Chernousov–Rapinchuk–Rapinchuk, The genus of a division algebra, §3.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13373-013-0037-z).

## 4. Transporting sparse Polynomial Calculus certificates

Let $E/K$ be a field extension. Keep the polynomial axioms $F\subset K[x]$ fixed, and let $P_1,\ldots,P_t=1$ be a Polynomial Calculus refutation over $E$. Put $s_i=|\operatorname{supp}P_i|$, and $S=\sum_i s_i$. There is a degree-preserving refutation over $K$ with size at most $\sum_i c_i s_i$, where $c_i$ is the number of distinct projective coefficient functionals demanded at line $i$ by the following recursion.

A coefficient functional is a $K$-linear map $\lambda:E\to K$, applied coefficientwise. Start at the final line with a functional satisfying $\lambda(1)=1$. For an inference $P_i=x_k P_j$, propagate the same functional to $j$. For an inference $P_i=aP_j+bP_k$, propagate $\lambda\circ\mu_a$ to $j$ and $\lambda\circ\mu_b$ to $k$, where $\mu_a(z)=az$. Ignore zero functionals and identify all nonzero functionals that differ by a nonzero $K$-scalar. Fix one representative of each demanded projective class, normalizing it to have value one at $1$ whenever this value is nonzero. The recursion is finite because it runs backwards along the given refutation.

Now derive all demanded projections forwards. Multiplication by a variable commutes with a coefficient projection. At a linear-combination inference, its demanded projection is
$$
(\lambda\circ\mu_a)(P_j)+(\lambda\circ\mu_b)(P_k).
$$
Replace each nonzero summand by a $K$-scalar multiple of the already-derived representative of its class. The projections of axioms are the same axiom or zero because coefficients are in $K$. Zero projected lines may be omitted, interpreting multiplication or linear combinations on only their nonzero parents when needed. Thus all steps are legal and the final representative gives $1$. The support and degree of each projected line are bounded by those of the corresponding given line. No additional polynomial with a larger support is used in this simulation.

For $K=\mathbb F_q$ and $E=\mathbb F_{q^e}$ this gives size at most $(q^e-1)/(q-1)\cdot S$. More specifically, let $U$ be the subgroup of $E^\times$ generated by the nonzero scalars used in the linear-combination steps. The projective orbit of the terminal functional under $U$ has size $|U/(U\cap K^\times)|$. Indeed, if $\lambda\circ\mu_a=c\lambda$ with $c\in K^\times$, then $\lambda\circ\mu_{a-c}=0$, and $a-c\ne0$ would make $\mu_{a-c}$ surjective and force $\lambda=0$. The actual demand sets are subsets of this orbit. The projective bound can therefore remain small even when the coefficient field is large.

There is a complementary bound $O(e^2 S^2)$ for any explicit finite extension of degree $e$. At each line maintain all $e$ coordinate projections in a $K$-basis of $E$ beginning with $1$. At a linear-combination step each coordinate is a sum of at most $2e$ scaled parent coordinates, given by the $K$-matrices of multiplication by $a,b$. Binary addition implements these combinations, with every intermediate monomial in the union of the two parent supports. After omitting zero lines there are at most $S$ original lines, and each input support union has size at most $S$. The bound follows, with no increase in maximum degree. For comparison, a Nullstellensatz identity can be projected once using any $\lambda(1)=1$, because the axioms commute with coefficient projection in each product. Thus it has no increase in the degree or sparsity of its coefficient polynomials, over arbitrary field extensions.

In Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She's Open Question 7.2 on sparse PC size for tensor nonisomorphism, this shows that subexponential-size upper bounds are equivalent over a base coefficient field and extensions of degree $\exp(o(n))$, provided the tensor-isomorphism axioms are kept fixed and $n$ is the parameter of the bound. In the extension-to-base direction use the coordinate bound, and in the reverse direction reuse the original derivation. The transfer does not decide whether those upper bounds exist. It does not change $\mathbb F_q$-point equations into $\mathbb F_{q^e}$-point equations. Replacing the field axioms changes the polynomial system, and geometrically soluble arithmetic negatives remain beyond coefficient descent without such axioms. [Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She, Open Question 7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS2).

## Verification status

These are written proofs. The arithmetic argument uses the published transfer and Hasse results with the stated action-group hypothesis. The example with probe degrees four and six gives explicit small witness choices for a known negative phenomenon. The local/global absolutely simple result and the sparse PC size transfer have provisional priority based on the compared sources and earlier portfolio. No Lean verification is claimed.
