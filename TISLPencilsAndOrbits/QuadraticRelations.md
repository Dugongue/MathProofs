# The orientation of the quadratic relations in the ternary square-zero construction

Consider the existing reduction over $\mathbb F_3$ to the following based algebra. Its vector space is $A_\beta=K1\oplus X\oplus Z$. Its radical products are determined by a symmetric surjection $\beta:\operatorname{Sym}^2 X\to Z$ with zero common radical. Every product of $Z$ with the radical vanishes. The dimensions $n=\dim X$ and $z=\dim Z$ are even and odd respectively. Put $U_\beta=\ker\beta$. A basis for $U_\beta$ and lifts of the fixed ordered basis of $Z$ give an ordered basis matrix $B_\beta$ (kernel first) in the standard monomial basis of $\operatorname{Sym}^2X$. Write $b_\beta=\det B_\beta$.

**Exact criterion.** The two algebras admit a determinant-one isomorphism exactly when some $P:X\to X'$ carries $U_\beta$ to $U_{\beta'}$ under $\operatorname{Sym}^2P$ and the induced map on these based relation spaces has determinant $b_\beta/b_{\beta'}$. In particular, if $r=\dim U_\beta$, there is a deterministic same-field reduction in time $2^{O(r)}\operatorname{poly}(N)$ to a single ordinary TI pair. This applies to the square-zero conjugacy construction even when $X$ and $Z$ are large. Comparing unequal dimensions gives a fixed negative instance.

Indeed any unital algebra isomorphism fixes $1$, preserves the radical and its square, and induces $P$ on $X$ modulo $Z$ and $Q$ on $Z$. Every admissible $P$ induces the unique $Q$ with $Q\beta=\beta'\operatorname{Sym}^2P$. The diagonal map with blocks $1,P,Q$ is an algebra isomorphism. A possible shear $X\to Z$ leaves the determinant unchanged. If $G$ denotes the map on the based kernels, writing the symmetric-square map in the two adapted frames gives
$$
(\det P)^{n+1}=\det(\operatorname{Sym}^2P)
   =\frac{b_{\beta'}}{b_\beta}\det G\det Q.
$$
The first identity follows by triangularizing $P$ over an algebraic closure and multiplying its diagonal weights, and hence holds over the original field. Consequently the algebra determinant is $(b_\beta/b_{\beta'})(\det P)^{n+2}/\det G$. The even exponent kills the sign in $\mathbb F_3^\times$, proving the criterion. Both chosen relation-space bases appear in this formula, which makes the assertion independent of their choice.

For the many-one reduction, encode symmetric multiplication $X\times X\to\operatorname{Sym}^2X$ and the inclusion of $U_\beta$. This forces the ambient symmetric-square action and its restriction to $U_\beta$. The exterior products on the latter through degree $r$ impose the required determinant by marking source vector $1$ and target vector $b_\beta/b_{\beta'}$ in the top lines. In dimension zero the determinant is one by convention and the ratio is checked directly. Use the [encoding note](Encoding.md) to combine the data. The full construction is polynomial when $r=O(\log N)$.

For completeness the square-zero space in the earlier reduction is $\{(u,v,w)\mapsto(0,0,au+bv):a,b\in A_\beta\}$ on $A_\beta^3$. Its common kernel and total image are the last copy. On that copy a conjugator acts by an algebra isomorphism followed by multiplication by a unit. This follows by recovering the left regular algebra as the left idealizer of the rows $[L_a\ L_b]$, using the row $[I\ 0]$. On the quotient its map is two copies of the same algebra isomorphism followed by an invertible $2\times2$ matrix over the target algebra, as follows by taking the two coordinate projections. Unit multiplication has determinant $s^{\dim A_\beta}=1$, with $s$ its residue. A two-by-two algebra matrix has determinant $(\det \overline M)^{\dim A_\beta}=1$ on the vector space, by the radical filtration. Hence the ambient sign is exactly the algebra sign, and a given algebra isomorphism acts on all three copies. This proves the claimed application without being given a conjugator.

### What the reformulation buys on the full ternary construction

The change of presentation is reversible in polynomial time for unrestricted relation dimension. Given $U\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2 X$ with an ordered basis and allowance $d\in\mathbb F_3^\times$ for the determinant on the relation spaces of a pair, use the quotient by $U$ for $Z$. Adjoin any complement basis to the ordered basis of $U$ to make $B_\beta$. Scaling one complement vector adjusts its determinant to either sign, since $z$ is positive. Thus source and target quotient bases can be chosen with $b_\beta/b_{\beta'}=d$. Define the quotient maps using precisely these frames. The required common-radical condition is checked by linear equations. Now the signed algebra question is exactly the proposed based subspace question. This includes $r=0$, where orientation allowance $-1$ is impossible and the calibration still realizes a negative algebra pair.

Consequently an ordinary-TI compiler for orientation on the quadratic relation space, with **unrestricted** change of basis on $X$, suffices for the project's full $\mathbb F_3$ reverse reduction using its existing seven-character square-zero reduction and conjunction construction. Conversely a full reverse reduction would supply this compiler. Indeed signed algebra comparison for these based algebras equals signed comparison of their multiplication tensors: a three-map isotope of unital commutative algebras has its first and second maps equal to a common algebra isomorphism followed by multiplication by units $u,v$, and its third by $uv$. This follows by evaluating at $1$ in each argument. The even algebra dimension makes all these unit determinants one. The seven-character assembly is an input from the earlier project proof; the equivalence between the based quadratic-space test and its individual signed algebra comparison is proved here. The unbounded-dimension reformulation has not removed the last sign.

For the free quadratic algebra $U_\beta=0$, changing $\beta$ to $-\beta$ with $z$ odd changes $b_\beta$ by $-1$. The criterion rejects this pair directly, regardless of $n$. This recovers the project example on which short corresponding tuples lose the sign. General relation spaces can have dimension $\Theta(n^2)$. The exterior construction above can have exponential size there.
