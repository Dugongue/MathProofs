# Determinant constraints on adjoint multiplicity spaces

Informal mathematics.

Fix a finite field F of order q. The target is a polynomial-time Turing reduction from special-linear isomorphism of concise three-tensors with local centroid to ordinary tensor isomorphism over F. The block assembly theorem then extends such a reduction to arbitrary inputs over F. The theorem below gives a reduction on a computable class of these inputs, with a parameter bound on the remaining cases. The unrestricted target remains open.

## The theorem

Let T and S be concise trilinear forms over F of the same format. Given prescribed determinants delta in D = (F*)^3, there is a deterministic ordinary-TI oracle algorithm that decides whether a transporter with these determinants exists. Its work and total query length are polynomial in L + 2^w for fixed q, where L is the dense input length and w is defined below. In particular this gives a polynomial-time Turing reduction on every class with w at most C log(L+2) for a fixed C.

If a mode has dimension zero, both forms are zero. The determinant image is then F* in each positive-dimensional mode and {1} in a zero-dimensional mode. The rest of the proof assumes positive mode dimensions.

The algorithm can compute the entire determinant coset of the transporters. It handles nonsplit pair adjoints, radicals, and permutations of their simple factors. The parameter uses only multiplicity spaces whose determinant contributions survive the available adjoint corrections. There is no requirement that the simple factors, radical layers, or number of center labels be bounded. Locality of the centroid is unnecessary for the theorem.

We use the convention T(x_1,x_2,x_3) = S(g_1 x_1,g_2 x_2,g_3 x_3). Ordinary tensor isomorphism and the determinant-one condition are unaffected by passing between form and array conventions. For other prescribed determinants, the convention determines whether to invert the three coordinates.

## 1. The algebras, the available corrections, and the parameter

For each pair p = ij, with i < j, form the pair adjoint algebra A_p. Its elements (a,b) satisfy T(...,a x_i,...)=T(...,b x_j,...) for every input. Its product is (a,b)(a',b') = (aa',b'b). Conciseness makes the two projections faithful, one as a representation and the other as an opposite representation. These algebras are computed by linear equations. The two incident algebras commute on each common mode. Indeed, moving the two operators to their distinct opposite endpoints allows their order on the common mode to be interchanged. Conciseness then identifies the two compositions.

A unit u=(a,b) of A_p gives an automorphism of T with a in mode i, b inverse in mode j and identity elsewhere. Its action on A_p by conjugation is the inner automorphism by u. It acts trivially by conjugation on the other pair algebras, by the preceding commutation. Let K_T be the subgroup of automorphisms generated by all three groups of pair units. Write D_0 = det(K_T), a subgroup of D. Conjugating by any transporter takes K_T onto K_S, so the images are the same when the tensors are GL-isomorphic.

Here is the known explicit calculation of D_0 used in the algorithm. Write A_p/J_p as a product of full matrix algebras over finite extension fields, with J_p the Jacobson radical. For a factor Mat_d(E), let a_i and a_j be its composition multiplicities in the respective mode modules, using the opposite representation at the second endpoint. The available determinant triples from this factor are x raised to a_i and -a_j in the two modes, and 1 in the third, for all x in F*. To see this, take successive layers of the J_p filtration. A simple E^d contributes Norm_{E/F}(det_E u) to the determinant, and its inverse contributes the reciprocal. Units of the quotient lift to units of A_p since the radical is nilpotent. Every norm value occurs because the finite-field norm is surjective. Factors are independent, and taking the subgroup generated by the columns from all three pairs gives D_0. The layers and multiplicities are computed by spans and idempotent images. At fixed q the full subgroup can be listed. This is the earlier adjoint saturation calculation, not a new determinant-image formula.

For mode i, let B_i be the abstract tensor product of the two incident pair algebras, taking opposites where needed so that both factors act on the left. Their commuting actions give a B_i-module on V_i. Put R_i = J_p tensor A_r + A_p tensor J_r, with the same opposite conventions. This ideal is nilpotent by expansion of a long product, since sufficiently many radical factors must occur on at least one side. The quotient Q_i = B_i/R_i is semisimple. In fact a tensor product of full matrix algebras over finite extensions is a full matrix algebra over the tensor product of the centers, and the tensor product of finite separable fields is a product of fields. Hence the radical layers W_{i,t} = R_i^t V_i / R_i^{t+1} V_i are explicit Q_i-modules. Any transporter induces on these layers an intertwiner for the product of its two induced quotient-algebra maps.

For each simple factor Mat_d(E) of Q_i in each layer, its module is a sum of m natural copies of E^d. Call the factor in this layer relevant if m > 0 and the triples with x^d in mode i and 1 elsewhere, for x in F*, do not all belong to D_0. Define w as the maximum of the relevant m on the two inputs, or zero if the list is empty. This is invariant under GL-isomorphism. The sizes of all algebra tables and module presentations used here are polynomial in L. For instance each pair algebra embeds in a mode matrix algebra, and the abstract product of two such algebras has at most the product of their dimensions.

For deterministic construction, use finite-field algebra decomposition in the form of Theorem 18 in arXiv:1708.03495, which allows polynomial dependence on the characteristic and hence polynomial time for fixed F. It returns explicit matrix coordinates and quotient/preimage maps. The same algorithm applies to a table algebra via its faithful left regular representation. Compute R_i powers on the mode by spans and quotient bases. For the determinant condition we first test ordinary isomorphism, so we can assume D_0 agrees on the two inputs.

## 2. Obtain the label actions using decision queries

Use a finite label set for each pair quotient that records its center maps. For every simple factor of center degree f, identify its center with a common degree-f model. Include f labels, each consisting of the primitive central idempotent and the corresponding element of the Frobenius orbit of one common field generator. The factor with f=1 has a single label. Across both inputs use the same model for each f. The total label count N is polynomial in L. Every induced center isomorphism acts on these sets, and the induced label map determines the center map on every factor.

For completeness, the center identifications can be made deterministically at fixed F. In an existing degree-f finite field, use Frobenius kernels to find each subfield of degree l^a exactly dividing f for a prime l, and choose an element outside its subfield of degree l^(a-1) by linear algebra. Sum these elements to get a generator. To check the sum step, suppose x,y have coprime degrees and a Frobenius power fixes x+y. The two differences under this power lie in the intersection F and are negatives of each other. The order of the Frobenius power on one of the two fields is prime to the characteristic, so iteration forces its difference to be zero. The power then fixes both, and its exponent is a multiple of both degrees. Induct on the prime-power factors. For degree one use 1. Take the minimal polynomial of a generator in one reference center by linear algebra. Factoring this polynomial over each of the other degree-f centers gives a root and therefore an explicit identification. Finite-field factoring has deterministic polynomial complexity in the degree, log of the field order, and characteristic, which suffices here.

To ask whether an ordinary transporter extends a partial label map, use a typed tensor system containing T, the pair algebra multiplications, their module actions, the quotient sorts, and the quotient maps. The pair action tensors force the map on an algebra to be the one induced by the two mode transformations, because the projections are faithful. Include the relevant markings on the quotient elements for each prescribed label. A marked element is encoded by a map from a scalar sort fixed by its field multiplication. These are maps of arity at most two and hence tensors of order at most three. Use the prior fourteen-component uniform packing and the fixed-component ordinary-TI compiler. Any original transporter with the given label constraints extends by its induced algebra and quotient maps. Any solution of the compiled instance supplies existence of such a transporter. No search answer from the oracle is used.

By trying the at most N target labels for each source label in turn, these queries find one full label map pi_0 for T to S, if they are GL-isomorphic. To generate the image P of Aut(T) on its labels, use a stabilizer chain in the fixed source label order. For each position and every candidate image of that label, query with the preceding labels fixed. When the answer is yes, extend that partial map to a full label permutation by the same procedure. The representatives generate P because at each step any element of the point stabilizer has the same image of the next point as some representative, and dividing by that representative fixes one additional point. At the end the action on all labels is the identity. There are polynomially many queries, and only permutations of the polynomial-size label set are recovered.

## 3. Normalize a prescribed label map

Consider one of these full label maps pi, or the identity map on T. In the quotient of every pair algebra choose the matrix-algebra isomorphism phi_p^0 that applies its prescribed center map entry by entry and takes the chosen matrix units to the corresponding units. Incompatible pair factor sizes reject the query. Any quotient isomorphism with that center map differs from phi_p^0 by conjugation by a quotient unit. Indeed the images of matrix units define a basis of the natural module by translating a vector from the first diagonal image with the first-column units. This identifies the two systems of matrix units by conjugation. The map left over fixes the center and every matrix unit, so is the identity.

Lift the correcting quotient unit to a target pair adjoint unit, and compose with the corresponding target tensor automorphism. This normalizes the quotient map without changing the label map. It changes the determinant only by a member of D_0. Corrections for the three pairs do not interfere since each acts trivially on the other pair algebras by conjugation. Thus the set of determinant classes in D/D_0 of transporters with label map pi equals the set for those whose three quotient-algebra maps are the prescribed phi_p^0. For a normalized decision query, add markings for a full basis of the pair quotients, with the prescribed images on the target. This is still a polynomial-length typed system.

## 4. Read determinants from normalized layer maps

For a normalized transporter, the map on W_{i,t} intertwines the prescribed product isomorphism psi_i of Q_i. Choose source matrix units, center basis and simple-module bases for every factor of Q_i. On the target use their transported units and center basis given by psi_i. On each summand choose an E-basis of the multiplicity space, which is the first diagonal matrix unit's image in that layer. Transport this basis through the other first-column units to obtain a basis of the entire summand. Multiplicity mismatches reject. All these frames are computed from the two tensors and pi, independently of any unknown transporter. To compute an E-basis of a module with an explicit field action, append vectors whose E-spans increase the F-span until it is the whole space.

In these aligned coordinates any intertwiner acts on that summand by I_d tensor h, with h an E-linear invertible map on the multiplicity space. The matrix-unit equations force the same map in all d copies. In F-coordinates its determinant is Norm_{E/F}(det_E h)^d. This determinant identity follows for diagonal elementary generators by the defining multiplication determinant of the norm. For elementary transvections the F-map is unipotent with determinant one, so the same identity follows for every invertible h by elimination.

Choose orders of the summands so that matching summands have the same position in the source and target frames. Lift the ordered layer bases to full adapted mode bases. For each mode let c_i be the determinant of the target basis matrix divided by the determinant of the source basis matrix, in the original coordinates. A filtered map is triangular in these bases with its induced layer maps on the diagonal, so its determinant is exactly c_i times the product of the displayed norm powers. The c_i values include the signs from every layer and factor permutation and every center-field change. An irrelevant factor's norm power belongs, in its single mode, to D_0 and can be dropped when testing the class in D/D_0. No other factor is dropped.

For a relevant factor, link its multiplicity space to the graded layer by the actual inclusion of the first diagonal image. The markings of Section 3 force that image to map to its prescribed counterpart. Realize det_E h by the top line of the exterior algebra of that E-space. Explicitly include its multiplication, its scalar E inclusion, the degree-one inclusion, and the top-line inclusion. Mark the E basis, in the aligned field coordinates on both sides. Exterior multiplication and the included generators force exactly the exterior extension of h. The top line has one E dimension and acts by det_E h. These operations work also in characteristic two, using the alternating exterior algebra.

For an E-line acted on by lambda, realize its norm in an F-line as follows. Include copies of the E-line linked by the explicit F-linear maps z -> z^(q^j) for j from 0 to [E:F]-1. The action on the j-th copy is then multiplication by lambda^(q^j). Include E-multiplication maps along a chain of prefix lines, forcing the last line to scale by the product of these conjugates. Its value is in F*. The inclusion F -> E in this last line forces the map on an auxiliary F-line to be multiplication by the norm. Every h extends through these maps. Conversely each scalar action just described is forced by the specified linear and multiplication tensors. Repeated products on F-lines now give the d-th powers and their product per mode. If a mode has no relevant factors, its product is the unit of a fixed scalar line.

For any requested determinant class, enumerate its representatives delta' in D. For each, require the three final products to be delta'_i/c_i by a vector marking on the final F-lines. A normalized transporter with the required class exists exactly when one of those queries accepts, because the dropped factors contribute only D_0. The exterior algebras have F-dimensions [E:F] times 2^m, and even their dense multiplication tables and all linked maps have size polynomial in L + 2^w. All field extensions in this construction are represented as F-vector spaces with explicit F-bilinear operations. The final TI instances stay over F.

## 5. Assemble the determinant coset

Run the class tests for the identity label map on T to obtain the full determinant-class image of the label kernel. For each computed generator of P, find one realized determinant class of a lift by trying all classes. These lists have constant size at fixed q. The kernel image and the selected lift classes generate det(Aut(T))/D_0. Indeed any automorphism's permutation is a word in the generators, so dividing by the same word in their lifts leaves an element of the kernel. For the computed label map pi_0 from T to S, find one realized determinant class of a lift. The full transporter coset is that class multiplied by the stabilizer determinant subgroup, and then lifted back to D. This test decides the specified determinant triple. It uses no tensor search oracle or enumeration of label permutations.

For arbitrary tensors over fixed F, the prior direct-sum theorem reduces the decision to concise local-centroid blocks, with calibrated determinant targets on each block. Whenever the resulting blocks have logarithmic relevant multiplicity, this algorithm discharges those queries by ordinary TI. This gives a polynomial-time reduction for that entire recognized class, including inputs whose full centroid is not local.

## 6. A large-multiplicity example with no volume cost

Let T_0 be the concise form with slice space spanned by the n coordinate diagonal units and the all-ones matrix, for n >= 3. Its pair adjoints are scalars, as proved in the project's rank-one contraction result. Let M_b be the trace-of-matrix-product form on three copies of Mat_b(F). Its pair adjoints are the natural full matrix factors, with the two incident factors acting on opposite sides on each mode. This follows by setting one matrix at a time to the identity in the adjoint equation and using trace nondegeneracy.

Take the modewise tensor product T = T_0 tensor M_b. Each of the two single-mode action maps is injective for concise factors, and the intersection of two product subspaces is the product of the two intersections, by extending bases of the intersections. Therefore the pair adjoints tensorize. The pair adjoints of T are Mat_b(F), and in each mode their product acts as the full Mat_(b^2)(F) on the matrix factor, with multiplicity the mode dimension of T_0. The centroid is scalar since its action must lie in the intersection of the two endpoint factor actions on each mode. These are commuting left and right full matrix actions with scalar intersection, and conciseness forces the same scalar in all modes.

For q > 2 choose b divisible by q-1. Each pair exponent is b times a mode dimension of T_0, hence divisible by q-1, so the pair-unit determinant image is trivial. The multiplicity determinant powers on the layers are b^2, also divisible by q-1. Therefore w=0 even while the multiplicities grow without bound. Every stabilizer determinant is one since center labels are trivial, the normalized determinant powers are all one, and source and target calibration for a stabilizer can be taken equal. Applying a determinant change on one mode thus gives GL-equivalent but SL-inequivalent pairs. The algorithm decides these pairs without a volume space, including in arbitrarily changed input bases.

## Attribution and scope

The determinant coset and direct-sum assembly, the pair-unit image calculation, the split semisimple multiplicity method, and the thin intrinsic filtration compiler are prior MathProofs results. The new combination uses ordinary TI to recover only quotient center-label permutations, normalizes inner automorphisms by pair units, and places volumes on relevant multiplicity spaces of the joint radical layers. Its exact claim is the parameterized reduction above. For scalar pair adjoints, Q_i is F, its sole simple has dimension one, and the layers give no multiplicity compression. This argument does not give a polynomial bound for all concise local-centroid inputs over q > 2. Over F_2 the determinant group is trivial and ordinary TI is already the decision problem.
