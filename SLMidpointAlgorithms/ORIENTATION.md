# Determinant cosets from a flag in one tensor mode

Informal mathematics. The construction gives a deterministic polynomial-time Turing reduction to ordinary tensor isomorphism over each fixed finite field when one mode has a computable intrinsic filtration with logarithmic successive dimensions. It also applies to supplied flags, with an exact flag-preserving output contract. The other two modes need no flags or adjoint restrictions. The proof includes a family in which all mode dimensions grow with scalar pair adjoints and a genuine special-linear obstruction.

## 1. Statement and conventions

Let $F=\mathbb F_q$ be fixed. Given two order-three tensors of the same format, write $A_T(x)\colon W_T^*\to V_T$ for contraction of $T\in U_T\otimes V_T\otimes W_T$ by $x\in U_T^*$. Use analogous notation for $S$. A transporter $(g_1,g_2,g_3)$ from $T$ to $S$ has induced first-mode covector map $f=(g_1^{-1})^*\colon U_T^*\to U_S^*$, where the star denotes the dual map. Its equation is

$$
g_2 A_T(x)=A_S(fx)h,\qquad h=(g_3^*)^{-1},\qquad x\in U_T^*.
$$

The original determinant triple, in the input coordinates, is $(\det(f)^{-1},\det(g_2),\det(h)^{-1})$.

Give $U_T^*$ and $U_S^*$ flags $0=F_0\subset F_1\subset\cdots\subset F_s=U^*$ with matching dimensions. Their width $w$ is the maximum of the successive quotient dimensions. The algorithm computes the entire set of determinant triples of transporters sending each source flag subspace onto its target counterpart. It returns the empty set if none exist. Given the flags, its total work, apart from the time to answer ordinary-TI queries, and its total dense query size are polynomial in $L+q^w$, where $L$ is the dense input length including the flags. This also gives the bound for the unflagged problem whenever the flags are computable at that cost and every ordinary transporter sends one to the other. A single-step flag gives the bound with $w=\dim U^*$, hence a polynomial reduction when one mode has logarithmic dimension. Zero-dimensional modes can be handled directly because then the tensor is zero.

## 2. Recover the first-mode map by marking quotient vectors

All queries include the tensor, the flag subspaces, the necessary subquotients $F_j/F_k$ with $0\leq k<j$, and their inclusions and quotient maps. Pairing identifies the action on the dual mode. Inclusion and quotient equations force every auxiliary action to be induced by the same original first-mode map. To impose a specified image for a vector in a subquotient, mark the two vectors with a fixed scalar sort. These are explicit typed tensor systems of order at most three. The earlier uniform mixed-system packing, followed by the fixed-component ordinary-TI compiler, handles a polynomial number of components. Any flag-preserving transporter respecting the marks induces an isomorphism of the system, and conversely. No oracle returns a witness.

Choose an adapted basis $e_{j,a}$ for the source first mode, where the vectors at level $j$ lift a basis of $F_j/F_{j-1}$. For each basis vector list its class in $F_j/F_{j-1},F_j/F_{j-2},\ldots,F_j/F_0$, in that order, and concatenate these lists. To find one full $f$, begin by asking whether the unmarked flagged pair is isomorphic. If it is, greedily pin the image of the next mark while retaining every previous pin. The image of the first mark for a vector has at most $q^w$ candidates. Each later candidate is a lift of the already fixed image in the immediately shorter quotient. The kernel of that quotient projection has dimension at most $w$, so there are again at most $q^w$ candidates. Enumerate candidates by linear algebra. A yes query leaves a nonempty set of consistent transporters. Since the final marks are the adapted basis vectors in the full mode, the selected images specify a single realized $f$, including all of its off-diagonal blocks.

Use the same list of source marks to generate the image $P$ of the flagged stabilizer on the first mode. For each index, consider the stabilizer of all earlier marks. Its candidate images of the next mark again lie in a set of size at most $q^w$, because its image in the immediately shorter quotient is fixed whenever that quotient occurs. For every candidate realized by a marked self-isomorphism query, greedily extend to a full first-mode map. The resulting maps generate $P$. Indeed, in the subgroup fixing the first $r-1$ marks, divide an element by a chosen representative for its image of mark $r$. The result fixes mark $r$ as well. Iterating ends with the identity on the first mode. With $M\leq (\dim U)^2$ marks there are at most $O((M+1)^2 q^{2w})$ queries, including the searches for full representative maps. This also bounds the total explicit quotient dimensions and matrix data by a polynomial in $L+q^w$.

There is a separate useful case with no flag beyond $0\subset U^*$. If a polynomial-size, polynomial-time enumerable set of covectors spans $U^*$ and is carried onto its target counterpart by every ordinary transporter, take a basis from the set and try images only in the target set. The same argument recovers the first-mode map and stabilizer-image generators. In that case replace $q^w$ in the marking cost by the maximum set size.

## 3. Complete a fixed first-mode map by linear module algebra

For a fixed $f$, use a basis $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ of $U_T^*$. The two lists of arrows $A_T(x_a)$ and $A_S(fx_a)$ define two representations $M,N$ of the same two-vertex $m$-arrow Kronecker quiver. The vertices are the domain and codomain of each arrow. A module isomorphism is a pair $(h,g_2)$ of invertible vertex maps satisfying the displayed arrow equations, exactly the required two remaining mode maps.

For completeness there is a deterministic constructive algorithm over fixed $F$. Form the algebra $C=\operatorname{End}(M\oplus N)$ by linear equations and matrix multiplication. Let $e,f_0\in C$ project to $M,N$, respectively. Compute the radical $J$ and explicit matrix-algebra coordinates of $C/J$ by finite-algebra decomposition. In each simple factor the two idempotents have equal rank exactly when there are mutually inverse linear maps between their image spaces. If any rank differs, no isomorphism exists, since inverse corner elements would descend to such maps. Otherwise choose inverse corner maps in each factor and lift them to $f_0 C e$ and $e C f_0$, by taking preimages and multiplying by the corresponding idempotents. If $a,b$ are the lifts, $ba-e$ and $ab-f_0$ are in the nilpotent radical. Both products are invertible in their corner rings by a finite geometric series. Thus $a$ has a left inverse and a right inverse and is the requested module isomorphism. The algebra lives in the matrix algebra on the direct sum of the vertex spaces. All these computations have deterministic polynomial cost in the dense size for fixed $F$, including the finite-algebra decomposition.

When $T=S$ and the first-mode map is the identity, the transporter kernel is the module automorphism group $\operatorname{End}(M)^\times$. Compute its two vertex determinant characters by decomposing the endomorphism algebra modulo its radical. To justify the calculation, filter each vertex space by powers of that radical. On its graded layers, each simple factor $\mathrm{Mat}_d(E)$ acts through copies of the natural $E^d$ module. If the total multiplicities at the two vertices are $a,b$, its determinant contributions there are $t^a,t^b$ for $t=\operatorname{Norm}_{E/F}(\det_E u)$. Norms of finite fields are surjective and all quotient units lift. The determinant triple in original mode coordinates is therefore generated, over the simple factors, by the contributions $(1,t^a,t^{-b})$, with $a$ for $V_T$ and $b$ for $W_T^*$. The filtration and multiplicities are computed by spans and idempotent images. Over fixed $F$ this subgroup is explicitly enumerable.

## 4. Assemble the coset

Obtain one full transporter for the greedy first-mode map from $T$ to $S$ by the module algorithm. For each stabilizer-image generator from Section 2, apply the same algorithm on $T,T$ to obtain one actual lift. The kernel group and these lifts generate the flagged stabilizer, because division by a word in the lifts can remove the first-mode action of any stabilizer element. Their determinant triples and the computed kernel image therefore generate the determinant image of the whole flagged stabilizer. Multiplying that subgroup by the determinant triple of the one relative transporter gives the exact requested coset. The determinant group $(F^\times)^3$ has constant size for fixed $F$. In particular this decides membership of $(1,1,1)$, and also answers prescribed determinant targets in a primitive block assembly. The method uses polynomially many adaptive ordinary-TI queries and polynomial dense size within the claimed parameter bound.

## 5. Recover a flag from contraction-rank bands

Suppose prescribed rank thresholds in the first mode give nested linear subspaces $F_r=\{x\mid \operatorname{rank}(A_T(x))\leq r\}$, and consecutive members, including zero and the full space, differ by dimension at most $w$. These are intrinsic because invertible left and right slice maps preserve rank. Recover them in descending order from the full space. Given a basis of an upper member $F$ and a threshold for the next lower member $F'$, scan that basis while maintaining representatives of an independent list in $F/F'$. For the next vector $v$, try all linear combinations of the current representatives and check whether subtracting one puts $v$ in $F'$ by computing its contraction rank. If a combination works, keep the resulting difference as a kernel basis vector. Otherwise append $v$ as a quotient representative. Each step is a triangular basis change on the vectors processed so far. At the end the collected differences give a basis of $F'$. There are at most $q^w$ trials per step. This is a promised-class algorithm for the selected rank thresholds, with no need to list the vectors of the full mode or to decide whether an arbitrary rank sublevel set is linear.

## 6. A scalar-adjoint family with growing dimensions

Let $D e_i=e_{i+1}$ be the forward nilpotent shift of dimension $n$, with $D e_n=0$, and put $P=E_{12}$. Fix an integer $s\geq4$. For $k\geq1$ and $n-sk\geq4$, use the pencil space

$$
\mathcal W=\langle I,D,D^s,D^{2s},\ldots,D^{ks},P\rangle
$$

as the image of the first-mode slice map. The format is $(k+3,n,n)$, and the tensor is concise because these slices are independent and $I$ is among them. For a nonzero polynomial part with least exponent $j$, its rank is $n-j$, since the residual polynomial has invertible constant term. Adding a multiple of $P$ changes the rank by at most one. The thresholds $n-js+1$ for $1\leq j\leq k$, together with threshold $1$, therefore give the flag consisting of the successively shorter power spans with $P$ and finally the line $FP$. All successive jumps have width one, except the top jump of width two for $I,D$. Section 5 finds it in arbitrary input bases.

All three pair adjoints are scalar. The adjoints of the two large modes are the common centralizer of the slices, since one slice is $I$. Commuting with $D$ makes a matrix a polynomial in $D$, because $e_1$ is cyclic. Commuting also with $P$ forces all nonconstant coefficients to vanish: the first column vector of such a polynomial must lie in $Fe_1$, the image of $P$. An adjoint of the slice mode and either large mode corresponds to a one-sided multiplier $Q$ preserving $\mathcal W$. From $I\in\mathcal W$ it follows that $Q\in\mathcal W$. The condition $QD\in\mathcal W$, or $DQ\in\mathcal W$ on the other side, forces its nonscalar coefficients to vanish, since $D^2,D^{s+1},\ldots,D^{ks+1}$ and $PD=E_{11}$ or $DP=E_{22}$ are independent modulo $\mathcal W$. The centroid is consequently scalar.

For $q>2$, take $n$ divisible by $2(q-1)$. Every tensor stabilizer then has determinant one in both large modes. To prove this, write a slice as $aI+bD+cP+R(D)$ with $R(D)$ supported on the powers of degree at least $s$. Its determinant polynomial is $a^{n-2}(a^2-bc)$ by the lower block triangular form with leading $2$-by-$2$ block. The quadratic is irreducible over $F$, since it is primitive and linear in $c$ over the fraction field of $F[a,b]$. Under any invertible left-right pencil stabilizer $X\mapsto LXR$, the induced coordinate map preserves this formal polynomial up to scalar. It therefore preserves the hyperplane $a=0$, the quadratic up to scalar, and the line $FP$, which is the unique rank-one line by the rank bounds. In coordinates its first three outputs have the form $a'=\alpha a$, $b'=\beta a+\gamma b+B(z)$, $c'=\delta a+\epsilon b+\zeta c+C(z)$, where $z$ records the higher powers. Here $\alpha,\gamma,\zeta\ne0$. For $\gamma$ this follows by comparing the $bc$ coefficient of the quadratic. Comparing all terms containing $c$ forces $\beta=0$ and $B=0$, and the terms $ab,b^2,bz$ then force $\delta=\epsilon=0$ and $C=0$. Thus $LR=\alpha I+O(D^s)$ and $LDR=\gamma D+O(D^s)$, where the remainder denotes a polynomial in the indicated higher powers of $D$.

It follows that $LDL^{-1}=(\gamma/\alpha)D+O(D^2)$. Hence $L$ preserves each kernel of $D^j$, since a polynomial with leading nonzero linear coefficient has the same kernel flag as $D$. In the displayed basis $L$ is lower triangular. Comparing the first subdiagonal in the intertwining equation shows that its diagonal entries are $t,tr,\ldots,tr^{n-1}$ with $r=\gamma/\alpha$. Therefore $\det L=t^n r^{n(n-1)/2}=1$, and $\det(LR)=\alpha^n=1$ gives $\det R=1$ as well. Transpose or inversion of these two mode maps does not affect the conclusion. A nontrivial determinant twist in one large mode gives a GL-isomorphic pair with no SL transporter. Taking $k=\lfloor n/(2s)\rfloor$ for growing $n$ keeps the first-mode flag width at two while all three dimensions grow. Scalar pair adjoints leave full mode-size multiplicities in the adjoint test, and their determinant contributions cannot remove the obstruction in either large mode here.

## Attribution and scope

This argument combines the project's vector-marking decision reductions, prior uniform mixed-system packing, and adjoint radical determinant calculation. The additional step is the nested subquotient marking scheme on a single mode with a constructive Kronecker-module completion in the other two. Its guarantee does not require a thin flag in those two modes. Relevant published ingredients are the finite-algebra decomposition theorem in [Theorem 18 of the bilinear form isometry paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03495) and the fixed-component [tensor-system compiler, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2). The conclusion is a restricted same-field Turing reduction. An unrestricted local-centroid reduction and a many-one construction remain open. Publication priority is unestablished.
