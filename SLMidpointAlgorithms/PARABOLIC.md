# Determinant cosets for tensor transporters with block flags

AI-assisted informal mathematics. This algorithm works with supplied flags or generators. Its unrestricted SL-TI consequence applies when the flags are intrinsic and computable with the stated width bound.

## The theorem

Let F be a presented finite field of characteristic p, and let H be a subgroup of the product of three block-upper-triangular matrix groups over F. The ordered block bases and generators of H are given. For each individual diagonal block, take the group generated by the projections of the input generators to that block. Let s be the maximum order of any of these groups. There is a deterministic algorithm, polynomial in the dense input length, s and p, that finds an H-transporter between two three-tensors or proves that none exists. It also outputs generators of the H-stabilizer, so the determinant image of the transporter set is given exactly by a coset representative and generators. For fixed F it decides any prescribed determinant triple in the same bound.

In particular, with full flag-preserving groups and all block dimensions at most b, the time is polynomial in the dense input length and q^(b²), where q = |F|. This allows b = O(sqrt(log L)) at fixed q and input length L. The more general bound in s can apply even to blocks of unbounded dimension. The algorithm uses no TI oracle.

## 1. A small-index chain for keeping generating sets small

Work in the ambient group P of block-upper-triangular matrices whose diagonal blocks lie in the individual groups specified above. These groups can be enumerated by multiplication by generators and their inverses. Their product is allowed in P even if the diagonal projections of H are correlated. First take successive kernels of the projections of P to the individual diagonal groups, one at a time. Each index is at most s. This leaves the block-unitriangular radical. In the prescribed bases it is an ordinary upper unitriangular matrix group on the direct sum of the three mode spaces.

In that radical successively require that each basis column equals the identity column, processing rows from bottom to top. Once the lower rows of a column j agree with the identity, the next row is shifted additively by the corresponding entry of a left multiplier. Indeed the lower coordinates in the same column already equal those of e_j, and the current diagonal entry of the left multiplier is one. Refine each additive coordinate requirement into its F_p digits using a field basis. Each digit constraint in its parent is a kernel of a map to the additive group of F_p, so its index is at most p. This gives a chain P = P_0 >= ... >= P_m = 1 with polynomial length and computable coset labels, at most max(s,p) per stage. A diagonal stage uses the diagonal block itself as label. A digit stage uses the current digit of the column. Two elements in the same parent with the same label differ on the right by an element of the child, since the label maps on the parent are homomorphisms.

For clarity, here is a generating-set compression algorithm for any subgroup K of P supplied by generators. At level j maintain one representative per label already reached, including the identity for the identity label. To sift an element in P_j, left-multiply it by the inverse of the representative at its label and continue to level j+1. Insert it at the first missing label. Sift each input generator from level zero. Every inserted matrix lies in the input subgroup and the resulting representatives generate a group containing all input generators. To complete the tables, at each level compute the orbit of the identity label under left multiplication by all stored representatives at that level and deeper, and their inverses. Insert an element reaching any missing label. For each action edge between labels with representatives r and r', sift (r')^(-1) a r, where a is the acting generator, from the next level. Repeat scans until there is no insertion. At that point the Schreier lemma shows that the group generated by the deeper levels is exactly the stabilizer of the identity label in the group at the current level. There are at most m max(s,p) insertions, and every scan, label computation and matrix product is polynomial. The union of tables is a generating set with at most that many elements. This can be rerun after every subgroup computation below.

## 2. Process the tensor blocks

Each tensor is the direct sum of coordinate blocks with index triples (i,j,k). A matrix from P acts by a block-upper-triangular map in lexicographic block order. Its diagonal action on block (i,j,k) is the tensor product of its three corresponding diagonal blocks. Process tensor blocks from last to first. Maintain an H-element moving the processed suffix of A to that of B and the subgroup K of H fixing that suffix of B. Initially these are the identity and H. K acts on the next block of the affine fiber over the fixed suffix by

```text
x -> M_g x + t_g
```

where t_g is obtained from the suffix and M_g from the diagonal blocks. This is a genuine affine action because the fixed suffix is invariant under K and earlier coordinates do not enter the update. The group of linear parts has order at most s³. Compute it by a Cayley-graph traversal storing a lift in K for each linear part. Apply the Schreier lemma to the action on this quotient to obtain generators of the kernel of the linear-part map, denoted R. Compress these generators using Section 1.

Each element of R acts by a translation. The set W of translation values is the F_p-linear span of the translation values of the generators. Linear algebra gives membership in W and returns a lift of each member as a product of powers of those generators. In particular this handles a translation group of size exponential in the size of the block without listing its elements.

For every linear-part lift r, compute the image of the current next block of the transported A under r. The target block is reachable exactly when it differs from one such image by an element of W. Every element of K is a linear-part lift preceded by an element of R. This proves both directions of the test. If it passes, apply the linear-part lift first and the correcting translation second. Left-multiply the current transporter by their product in K.

To update K, first compute the kernel R_0 of the translation map on R. Refine the requirement of zero translation into its F_p coordinates. At each digit compute the orbit of zero under the resulting additive homomorphism, of size at most p, use Schreier generators for its kernel, and compress as in Section 1. Now, for each of the linear-part lifts, test whether its image of the target block differs from that target by an element of W. If so correct it by a translation and keep it. Those corrected lifts together with R_0 generate the stabilizer of the next block in K. Indeed any element fixing the block has one of these linear parts and differs from its chosen corrected lift by an element of R_0. Compress and continue.

After the last block the accumulated element is an actual transporter, and the remaining subgroup is the full H-stabilizer of B. The number of tensor blocks, field digits, and compression calls is polynomial. The only group traversals enumerate at most s³ linear parts, max(s,p) labels per compression stage, or p additive digits. Products, inversions, and Gaussian elimination in the explicitly presented field have polynomial bit cost. The matrices stored are in the original mode sizes. These bounds give the theorem.

## 3. Determinants and the scope of the result

If a transporter g was found, all transporters are h g with h in the computed stabilizer of B. Apply the determinant triple to each generator and to g. This gives the exact coset description for arbitrary presented F. At fixed F enumerate the generated subgroup of (F*)³, which has at most (q−1)³ elements.

For flags attached to A and B in different original coordinates, choose adapted bases and use the full block-upper-triangular groups. Generate them by within-block elementary row operations and nonzero scalings, and between-block elementary additions above the diagonal. Multiply the computed determinant triple by the per-mode ratios of target to source basis determinants. A prescribed triple then tests flag-preserving SL-TI. If the flags are computed equivariantly from A and B, every transporter necessarily matches them. In that case this solves the original instance whenever the widths meet the bound. This argument supplies no theorem that every concise local-centroid tensor has such flags.

The method uses classical orbit–stabilizer and Schreier generator computations. Related earlier work includes Eugene Luks's [Computing in Solvable Matrix Groups](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/solmatgrp.pdf). The bound here is phrased in terms of the orders of individual diagonal block images and does not assume those groups solvable. No publication priority is claimed.
