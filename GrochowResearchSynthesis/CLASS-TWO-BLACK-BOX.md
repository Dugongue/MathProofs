# Black-box search and counting for all finite class-two groups

**Theorem.** For finite nilpotent groups of class at most two given by Cayley tables, isomorphism search, exact isomorphism counting, and automorphism generation deterministically polynomial-time Turing reduce to unmarked class-two group isomorphism decision. If the source tables have order at most $N$, all oracle tables have order $N^{O(1)}$ with an absolute constant. For a $p$-group the oracle instances are $p$-groups for the same prime. This holds for $p=2$ and with arbitrary mixed cyclic orders and group exponents.

The proof combines the individualization method from the earlier alternating-space and Frattini-class-two results with the characteristic derived subgroup and power records. The new marker charges its auxiliary rank to the highest torsion layer. It then uses exact image size to remove even a nonunit admixture of any other marker. A power relation pins the common frame scalar. Part IV treats class two and exponent $p$. The earlier orbit algorithm for all class-two groups uses internal witnesses of the decision algorithm. Here each query is an ordinary unmarked group, and the decision procedure is a black box. ## 1. Characteristic central presentation

First consider a nontrivial class-two $p$-group $G$. Put $C=[G,G]$, $A=G/C$, $M=p^e=\exp A$, and $R=\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$. The alternating commutator bimap $\beta:A\times A\to C$, with convention $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$, has values generating $C$. In particular $MC=0$. For each divisor $m\mid M$, write
$$
q_m:A[m]\to C/mC,\qquad q_m(a)=\widetilde a^{\,m}\bmod mC
$$
in additive central coordinates. It is independent of the lift. Its cross-effect is $-\binom m2\beta$ modulo $mC$, and $q_m(ta)=tq_m(a)$ for every integer $t$.

Choose a cyclic basis $a_i$ of $A$ of orders $d_i$ and lifts $x_i$. Given invertible maps on $A,C$, they lift to a group isomorphism exactly when they preserve $\beta$ and the power records $q_{d_i}$ on the source basis. For sufficiency choose target lifts of the indicated images, then multiply each by a central element to correct its $d_i$-th power. The resulting images satisfy the central relations, the commutators, and the basis powers. These relations present $G$, since collecting gives at most $|A||C|$ normal forms and the original group has this order. Preservation of all $q_m$ is necessary. The map on the derived subgroup of any such pair is uniquely determined by its $A$ map, because commutators generate $C$. Over a valid $A$ map there are exactly $\prod_i |C[d_i]|$ group isomorphisms, from the independent corrections to the lifts. In particular the automorphisms trivial on $A$ are the central shifts $\operatorname{Hom}(A,C)$, with the explicit generators obtained by shifting each lift by generators of $C[d_i]$. [Power-record proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorUniversalityApplicationsII/CLASS-TWO-POWER-TENSORS.md)

All modules below are finite $R$-modules. Use $X^*=\operatorname{Hom}_R(X,R)$. The evaluation $X\to X^{**}$ is an isomorphism and $|X|=|X^*|$. On a cyclic summand of order $p^b$, this follows by identifying its dual with $p^{e-b}R$ and checking that evaluation has zero kernel. Finite direct sums give the assertions for all modules. In particular duals distinguish points also on nonfree modules.

## 2. Encode an ordered partial cyclic basis

We build a single oracle query for a marked comparison. The marks are an ordered initial segment of a cyclic basis of $A^*$ on either side, with corresponding orders. Write $f_1,\ldots,f_k$ for the marks. Adjoin two primal anchor coordinates $Ru,Rv$ and set $S=A\oplus Ru\oplus Rv$. In $S^*$ take the ordered partial cyclic basis
$$
(g_1,\ldots,g_t)=(u^*,v^*,f_1,\ldots,f_k),\qquad t=k+2 .
$$
Write $L_i=Rg_i$, $L_*=R\sum_i g_i$. Make a labelled list of slots $U_j\subset S^*$ with possible repetitions. If $2\le t\le6$, take $i$ copies of $L_i$ and $t+1$ copies of $L_*$. If $t\ge7$, take one copy of each $L_i$, two of $L_*$, and one $L_i+L_j$ per edge of the tree with center 1 and arms $2$, $3,4$, $5,6,\ldots,t$. Each slot is a direct summand. For the sum line use the full-order first anchor with coefficient 1, giving a retraction onto that line. Put
$$
m_j=\exp U_j,\qquad D=\bigoplus_j \mathbb Z/m_j\mathbb Z .
$$
Define a submodule $\mathcal K\subset\operatorname{Hom}_R(D,S^*)$ by allowing the image of the $j$-th coordinate generator to range independently over $U_j$. These maps give alternating cross forms on $S\oplus D$, by evaluation on $S$ and skew extension. The central output corresponding to the slots is $B_{\mathrm{frame}}=\bigoplus_j U_j^*$. Its commutator value on $s\in S$ and $z=(z_j)\in D$ is the tuple $(f\mapsto z_j f(s))_{j}$. Here $z_j$ is a scalar residue modulo $m_j$. Its product with $f(s)$ is well-defined since $m_j f=0$. Setting one $z_j=1$ and all others zero attains every value in that slot, since $U_j$ is a direct summand of $S^*$. Dualizing the central output realizes exactly $\mathcal K$.

We recover the labelled lines from $\mathcal K$ under block transport on $S^*,D$. For $z\in D$, the image $\{T(z):T\in\mathcal K\}$ is $\sum_j z_j U_j$. Its maximal nonzero cyclic possibilities under inclusion are exactly the recorded lines. Indeed the subgroups of a cyclic $p$-group are nested, so a cyclic contraction image equals one of its nonzero cyclic summands. Each cyclic summand from a plane slot lies in one of the plane's two basis lines, since the two coordinates form a direct sum. A line slot contributes a subgroup of its recorded line. Each recorded line is itself attained by a contraction at its slot, and the recorded lines are pairwise incomparable. The sum line has two full-order anchor components, so any nonzero element on it has both components nonzero and cannot lie in one coordinate line.

For any submodule $B\subset S^*$, the dimension of the image in $D/pD$ of
$$
\{z\in D : \mathcal K(z)\subseteq B\}
$$
is exactly the number of slots contained in $B$. The condition is independent on the slot coordinates. When $z_j$ is a unit modulo $p$, it acts invertibly on $U_j$, so it is allowed precisely when $U_j\subseteq B$. Other coefficients contribute zero to $D/pD$. On individual lines this statistic recovers the multiplicities and thus all labels for $t\le6$. For $t\ge7$ it detects the sum line, and on pairs of coordinate lines it counts two line slots plus the indicator of the indicated tree edge. The three distinct arm lengths and the unique degree-three vertex fix every vertex of that tree. This labels each coordinate line. In both formats, preserving the sum line means that the multiplier on every $g_i$ is the reduction of one common unit $\lambda\in R^\times$ to its cyclic order. This follows by comparing components of the transported sum generator, whose order is $M$ from the anchors.

The total logarithmic costs $\log_p|D|$ and $\log_p|B_{\mathrm{frame}}|$ are $O(\log_p|A|)$. For small $t$ the multiplicities are bounded. For large $t$ each factor appears in at most three tree edges, and the logarithm of an edge slot's exponent is at most the sum of its endpoint logarithms. Two sum slots cost $2e$. Also $|U_j|\le m_j^2$ for every slot, and $e\le\log_p|A|$.

## 3. A group query with recoverable core and power records

Use the primal core
$$
E_0=A\oplus Ru\oplus Rv\oplus D
$$
and the base central output $B_0=C\oplus Rc\oplus B_{\mathrm{frame}}$. Put the old bimap $\beta$ on the $A$ block, put $\beta(u,v)=c$ on the anchors, and use the cross forms in Section 2 for the frame blocks. All other base commutators vanish. The core's four blocks will be recognized using two markers each. For each marker $i=1,\ldots,8$ of primal block $V_i$ of the core, add a private copy $P_i=V_i^*$ to the primal space and a separate free even-rank heavy block $H_i=R^{w_i}$. Add a central coordinate $R b_i$. Its alternating component $F_i$ is the evaluation pairing between $V_i,P_i$, plus the standard nonsingular alternating form on $H_i$. Elsewhere it vanishes. In particular its contraction $P_i\to V_i^*$ and its opposite $V_i\to P_i^*$ are isomorphisms. Use
$$
r_e=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(p^{e-1}E_0),\qquad d=3r_e,\qquad
w_i=2(8+i)(d+1).
$$
Use $\widehat E=E_0\oplus\bigoplus_i (P_i\oplus H_i)$ and $\widehat B=B_0\oplus B_{\mathrm{heavy}}$, where $B_{\mathrm{heavy}}=\bigoplus_i R b_i$. This gives one alternating bimap $\widehat\beta:\widehat E^2\to\widehat B$. To see that its values generate $\widehat B$, take the $A$-by-$A$ pairs for $C$, the anchor pair for $Rc$, the $S$-by-$D$ pairs for the frame, and the private heavy block for each $b_i$.

Construct the class-two group $\widehat G$ with quotient $\widehat E$ and central subgroup $\widehat B$ as follows. Use the fixed cyclic coordinates on the source $A$, anchors, frame, and auxiliary modules. Keep the original powers $x_i^{d_i}$ for the old $A$ generators. Give the lift of $u$ its $M$-th power $c$, and give every remaining basis lift zero power at its respective cyclic order. For quotient basis $(e_i)$ of orders $d_i$ and specified central powers $s_i$, multiplication uses residue addition and the central correction
$$
\sum_i \left\lfloor\frac{a_i+b_i}{d_i}\right\rfloor s_i+
\sum_{j>i}a_j b_i\,\widehat\beta(e_j,e_i).
$$
Carries satisfy the cocycle equation by expanding the sum of three residues. The second sum is bilinear on the quotient since the coefficient for each pair is killed by both corresponding orders. Thus the product defines a group, with inverse obtained by taking the negative quotient coordinate and correcting the central coordinate. The basis commutators and powers are the prescribed ones. Since the commutators generate $\widehat B$, it is the derived subgroup, and $\widehat E$ is the abelianization. In particular an unmarked query isomorphism induces ordinary isomorphisms $a,b$ on these two specified abstract modules and preserves the central power records.

### Recovering the core under an arbitrary isomorphism

For a central covector, write its alternating form as
$$
F=Q+\sum_i \alpha_i F_i
$$
with $Q$ from $B_0^*$ and $\alpha_i\in R$. This coefficient description is injective since commutators generate $\widehat B$. A group isomorphism acts contragrediently on the central covectors and by congruence on their forms. For a form write $I(F)$ for the image of its contraction $\widehat E\to\widehat E^*$, and $r(F)=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}p^{e-1}I(F)$. Then
$$
r(F)\in\left[\sum_{\alpha_i\in R^\times}w_i, \sum_{\alpha_i\in R^\times}w_i+d\right].
$$
The new heavy blocks are disjoint and multiply by $\alpha_i$ times an invertible map. Only a unit coefficient survives on their highest torsion layer. On the core and its paired copies that layer has dimension $3r_e$, because the dual does not change cyclic orders. Thus the image of the source pure marker $F_i$ has exactly the $i$-th coefficient a unit. Indeed the eight singleton bands are disjoint, the zero band is below all of them, and $2w_1>w_8+d$.

Exact image cardinal gives a second rule. On the nonheavy part of the target, write the dual carrier as $V_i^*\oplus P_i^*\oplus W_i$. The private block $P_i$ occurs only in $F_i$. If $\alpha_i$ is a unit then the contraction's image projects onto $V_i^*\oplus P_i^*$. To obtain given outputs on these two summands, solve first for the $V_i$ input using the $P_i^*$ output and then for the $P_i$ input using the $V_i^*$ output. In the kernel of that projection within the image, the $V_i$ input is zero and the $P_i$ input cancels the $V_i^*$ output from the remaining input. The kernel is therefore precisely the image of the residual $W_i^*\to W_i$ block. Consequently
$$
|I(F)|=|V_i|^2\,|H_i|\,|\operatorname{im}(F_{W_iW_i})|\prod_{j\ne i}|\alpha_j H_j|.
$$
These are ordinary cardinalities of finite modules. The residual factor and every other heavy factor are at least 1. The source $F_i$ has image of size exactly $|V_i|^2|H_i|$, equal to the same base size in the target because the formats match. Equality forces $\alpha_j=0$ for all $j\ne i$, even for nonunits, and forces the residual block to be zero. This also shows that $I(F)$ intersects the whole dual core in exactly $V_i^*$ because the absence of a $P_i^*$ output makes the $V_i$ input zero, and the residual block is zero. Every element of $V_i^*$ is attained by varying the $P_i$ input.

The two markers for a given block have disjoint support outside the dual core, both before and after transport by the preceding calculation. Their image intersection is that block's $V_i^*$. This proves that the induced dual quotient map carries each of the four core blocks to its named counterpart. The base central covectors are now intrinsically the forms supported on the dual core, because every nonzero heavy coefficient has a nonzero contraction image on its private heavy block. Hence $B_{\mathrm{heavy}}$, the annihilator of the base central covectors, is preserved. Passing to the primal quotient by the annihilator of the dual core gives the block maps on $E_0$. Passing to the central quotient by $B_{\mathrm{heavy}}$ gives the map on $B_0$. The two particular central summands $C,Rc$ there are recovered as the spans of the $A$-by-$A$ and $u$-by-$v$ brackets. Intersection of the recovered base form space with the $S$-by-$D$ forms recovers exactly $\mathcal K$. This allows Section 2 to recover the labelled projective prefix.

### Recovering actual powers and fixing the common scalar

Modulo $B_{\mathrm{heavy}}$ the constructed group is the direct product of its core group and its auxiliary quotient module as an abelian group. The displayed collecting law proves this, since every basis power on the auxiliary coordinates is zero and their bracket values disappear. For $m\mid M$, a class in $\widehat E[m]$ contributes zero in its $m$-th power from its auxiliary component in this product. Thus the projected power record on $E_0[m]$ has values in $B_0/mB_0$, independently of choices of auxiliary components and lifts. The primal quotient map on $E_0$ and central quotient map on $B_0$ preserve these projected power records. On $A$ the records are exactly the original ones with $C/mC$ embedded in $B_0/mB_0$. Hence the recovered map on $A$ and the restriction on $C$ lift to an isomorphism of the original groups by Section 1.

The dual frame has common multiplier $\lambda$. In primal coordinates, the induced quotient maps on $u,v$ have a common multiplier $\mu=\lambda^{-1}$. In $B_0$, the image of $c$ from its bracket expression is $\mu^2 c'$. Its image from the $M$-th power of $u$ is $\mu c'$ because $MB_0=0$ and the power of a scalar multiple on a single cyclic line is homogeneous. The element $c'$ has full order $M$. Thus $\mu^2=\mu$ in $R$, and invertibility forces $\mu=1$. This recovers an exact marked isomorphism also at $p=2$.

Conversely a marked isomorphism of the original groups gives its quotient map on $A$, fixes the two anchor generators and the slot coordinates in $D$, and transports the frame slot submodules exactly. On each $U_j^*$ use the dual of the inverse transported slot map; on $C$ use the original map, and fix $c$ and the heavy coordinates. Extend the primal map to each $P_i$ by inverse duality and fix each $H_i$. This preserves the full commutator bimap. It also preserves every power record on each cyclic basis generator. For the original $A$ this follows from the starting group isomorphism. The anchors, $D$, and heavy bases are fixed. The paired copy $P_i$ has zero internal brackets and zero specified basis powers, hence zero $q_m$ on its $m$-torsion for every $m$. Taking the independent correct lifts as in Section 1 yields the query isomorphism. We have therefore reduced exact prefix feasibility to one ordinary class-two decision query.

## 4. Polynomial bounds and the reductions

The dual core has $\log_p|E_0|=O(\log_p|A|)$ by the slot bound. Its paired copies have twice its logarithmic size. All heavy blocks together cost $O(e(r_e+1))=O(\log_p|E_0|)$, since $r_e$ counts only summands of exact order $p^e$ and the anchors have this order. The heavy central coordinates cost $8e$, and $B_0$ costs $\log_p|C|+O(\log_p|A|)$. Thus $|\widehat G|=|\widehat E|\,|\widehat B|=|G|^{O(1)}$. Its table is generated directly by the collecting law and finite abelian coordinate arithmetic, in polynomial time in $|G|$. Each basis and each quotient needed for the original data is obtainable in polynomial time from the tables. One may compute cyclic coordinates by Smith normal form with transformations for the abelian presentation with a generator per element and table-product relations $e_x+e_y=e_{xy}$. This presents the abelian group in question since the relations reduce each formal sum to its table value. [Kannan–Bachem, Smith normal form with multipliers](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

Before querying, compare the two abelianization types and the two derived types. Reject a target frame prefix unless it extends to a cyclic basis of the same ordered cyclic orders as the source. There is a direct test. Use coordinates grouped by their cyclic exponents, and require each prescribed image to be killed by its source order. Modulo $p$, a homomorphism respecting these orders has block-triangular matrix, because entries in a target coordinate of higher order than the source must be divisible by $p$. For each exponent the prescribed columns projected to the corresponding diagonal block must be linearly independent. If they are, complete each diagonal block by choosing further columns, with all new off-diagonal blocks zero. This gives an endomorphism between the two matched abelian types invertible modulo $p$, hence invertible by induction down the $p$-power filtration. This proves that the test is exact.

For search, fix a full cyclic basis of $A_G^*$. After a successful empty-prefix query, try all $|A_H|$ possible images for each next covector, with the previous successful assignments unchanged. The prefix test guarantees an extension at each step. Taking the inverse dual gives the quotient map $A_G\to A_H$. Its action on brackets determines the central map. The required group lifts can be found by scanning for the central corrections, and the normal-form formula then returns the image of every table element. Per Sylow factor this takes at most $1+k|A_H|$ decision queries when the cyclic basis has length $k$.

For counting let $L\le\operatorname{Aut}(A_G^*)$ be the image of the group automorphisms. For each next cyclic basis generator, use exact prefix tests on $(G,G)$ to compute its orbit under the pointwise stabilizer of preceding basis generators. The product of these orbit sizes is $|L|$, since the final stabilizer is trivial. Multiplying by $\prod_i |C[d_i]|$ gives the automorphism count. The number of isomorphisms to any isomorphic second group is this same count, and is zero for a negative comparison. For generators, complete one representative per successful orbit point by the prefix search. It suffices to include representatives at every step of the stabilizer chain, since these together with generators of the terminal stabilizer generate $L$. Lift the representatives and adjoin the elementary central shifts from Section 1. The result generates the full automorphism group. This needs polynomially many queries and table operations.

For general finite class-two groups, use their characteristic Sylow direct factors. They are found by scanning for elements of prime-power order after factoring the table order, which can use trial division in polynomial time in the table size. Isomorphisms and automorphisms split over matching primes. Combine witnesses and generators factor by factor and multiply the counts. This includes the trivial and abelian cases.

Using the odd-order decision bound of Part V, the reductions give $N^{O((\log N)^{8/9})}$ algorithms for search, exact counting and generators on all odd-order class-two groups. The same black-box argument transfers the all-prime class-two decision bounds of Sun and the earlier tuned result, with no access to the decision algorithm's internal states. Since each table has order at most a fixed power of $N$, and there are polynomially many calls, all these displayed forms of the decision bound retain their exponents. The proof closes search and counting relative to decision for the full class-two family. The corresponding problem for unrestricted finite groups is outside this theorem. ## Verification

The reduction has a written proof. The accompanying Lean declarations cover the exact range of a contraction with a perfect private pair, its unit power anchor, and the two band inequalities. The finite replay checks 110 paired image cardinalities, 78 zero-residual intersections, 78,732 band cases, 1,979 frame contractions, 412 frame incidence cases, 29,109 mixed-order power records, and 45 unit anchors. The tests exercise the constituent algebra on small modules and do not enumerate the full large oracle tables. Historical priority remains open.
