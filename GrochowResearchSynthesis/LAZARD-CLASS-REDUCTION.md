# Polynomial-size class reduction and black-box search for Lazard \(p\)-groups

**Theorem.** Let \(G,H\) be finite \(p\)-groups of order \(N\) and nilpotency class less than \(p\), given by Cayley tables. Their isomorphism problem reduces deterministically in polynomial time to class-two \(p\)-group isomorphism on Cayley tables of order \(N^{O(1)}\), with absolute constants. For odd \(p\) the queries can preserve the common input exponent. There are also deterministic polynomial-time Turing reductions from isomorphism search, exact isomorphism counting, and automorphism generation for these \(G,H\) to the same class-two decision problem. The exponent of \(G,H\) can grow arbitrarily with \(N\).

The proof combines the fixed-signature approach to Grochow–Qiao Question 7.4 with the earlier size-sensitive class-two encodings. The construction here decodes the marked operations from the adjoint ring of a commutator, so each large module occurs only a constant number of times. Torsion-specific markings preserve the same bound during individualization. The published Cayley-table reduction in Part IV treats exponent \(p\). The search-and-counting extension in Remark 1.3 of the earlier paper is stated for that exponent setting. [Grochow–Qiao, Part IV Theorem 7.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S7.SS2) · [Grochow–Qiao, Remark 1.3](https://doi.org/10.1145/3625308)

## 1. Polynomial-time Lazard operations at arbitrary exponent

Write \(p^e\) for the group exponent and \(c<p\) for the class. These can be computed by element powers and the lower central series in polynomial time. The Lazard correspondence identifies the common underlying set of \(G\) and its Lie ring \(L\), preserves element orders, and identifies the two notions of isomorphism. The following word construction gives the addition and bracket tables uniformly in \(p,e,c\). [Lazard correspondence as stated in Part IV, Theorem 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S7.SS2)

In the tensor algebra on \(X,Y\) truncated above degree \(c\), work over \(\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z\). All denominators appearing in degree at most \(c\) of the truncated \(\exp\) and \(\log\) series are units. Construct a group expression \(w\) on \(\exp X,\exp Y\) with formal logarithm equal to \(X+Y\), and separately equal to \([X,Y]\). Start with the empty word and successively correct degrees \(k=1,\ldots,c\). If the current degree-\(k\) error, target minus current logarithm, is \(D_k=\sum_u d_u u\), then \(D_k\) is a homogeneous Lie polynomial. The Dynkin–Specht–Wever identity expresses it as
\[
D_k=k^{-1}\sum_{u=u_1\cdots u_k}d_u[\cdots[[u_1,u_2],u_3],\ldots,u_k],
\]
with the bracket of one letter defined to be that letter. This identity and the Lie-polynomial property of BCH hold over \(\mathbb Z[1/c!]\) in the degrees used here and hence over the stated residue ring. Equivalently, perform the formal induction over \(\mathbb Z[1/c!]\), with integer representatives of each correction coefficient, and compare each component after reduction. The multilinear Dynkin identity is integral, and its factor \(k\) is invertible. The standard universal BCH operations are evaluated in \(L\), so the same corrected group expression evaluates to the desired Lie operation in every group in the Lazard range.

To perform a correction, append the matching left-nested group commutator \([\cdots[g_1,g_2],\ldots,g_k]\), with \([g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}\), raised to the residue \(d_u/k\) as an integer exponent. Its leading logarithm is the displayed Lie commutator of degree \(k\). Multiplication adds this leading term, and the corrections change no lower degree. The statement that the degree error is Lie also holds directly over the localized coefficient ring: the logarithm of a group-like element is primitive through degree \(c\), and the Dynkin operator on a homogeneous primitive degree-\(k\) element acts by \(k\). The operator is the convolution of the antipode with the grading operator in the free associative Hopf algebra and, on words, gives the left-nested bracket. These two properties give the displayed formula.

There are \(2^{O(c)}\) monomials through degree \(c\). Naive truncated polynomial operations, rebuilding the logarithm as needed, cost \(2^{O(c)}\operatorname{poly}(\log N)\). The group expressions can be stored with shared subexpressions, and evaluating them for each pair of table entries costs the same bound. The class satisfies \(c\le \log_p N\), so the work is polynomial in \(N\) with an absolute exponent. The additive cyclic basis \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\) of \(L\) is also obtainable in polynomial time. For example, apply Smith normal form to the abelian presentation with a generator per element and relations \(u+v-w\) from its addition table, retaining generator coordinates. This presentation is exactly the additive group because the relations reduce any formal sum to its table sum, including inverse generators.

It suffices to test equal-order equal-prime inputs. Different exponents, or different additive cyclic types after this conversion, can be rejected before constructing paired queries. The trivial group and \(p=2\) cases in the theorem are elementary, since at \(p=2\) the class bound gives an abelian group.

## 2. A small associative ring containing the marked Lie-ring problem

Let \(L\) be any nonzero finite Lie ring with additive exponent \(p^e\), and put \(R=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z\). Construct an associative unital \(R\)-algebra \(A(L)\) using orthogonal vertices \(s_0,s_1,m_0,m_1,m_2,t_0,t_1\), each with diagonal corner \(R\). An arrow \(i\to j\) denotes the Peirce corner \(e_i A e_j\). Give each edge in the following table the indicated module, with distinct copies where it repeats.

| Module | Corners |
|---|---|
| \(L\) | \(s_0m_0,\ s_0m_1,\ m_0t_0,\ m_2t_0,\ s_0t_0,\ s_0t_1,\ s_1t_0\) |
| \(R\) | \(s_0m_2,\ s_1m_0,\ s_1m_2,\ m_0t_1,\ m_1t_0,\ m_1t_1\) |

Define the following products of pairs of off-diagonal corners, naming the composable triple of vertices.

| Triple | Product |
|---|---|
| \(s_0,m_0,t_0\) | \((x,y)\mapsto[x,y]\) |
| \(s_0,m_1,t_0\), \(s_0,m_0,t_1\), \(s_0,m_1,t_1\) | \((x,a)\mapsto ax\) |
| \(s_0,m_2,t_0\), \(s_1,m_0,t_0\), \(s_1,m_2,t_0\) | \((a,y)\mapsto ay\) |

All unlisted compositions of two off-diagonal corners are zero. Optional marks are an ordered list \(x_i\in L\) of nonzero elements of orders \(p^{a_i}\), representing their cyclic subgroups. For each mark add a vertex \(w_i\) with diagonal ring \(R_i=\mathbb Z/p^{a_i}\mathbb Z\), and corners \(s_0w_i,w_it_0\) each \(R_i\). The product from these two corners into \(s_0t_0\) is \((a,b)\mapsto abx_i\). Add a directed path \(w_i\to w_{i+1}\) with corner of order \(p^{\min(a_i,a_{i+1})}\). The pair compositions involving those path corners are zero.

To make all named vertices intrinsic, add a hub \(h\) with diagonal \(R\). Add a corner of module \(R\) from \(h\) to every original vertex, and one of module \(R_i\) from \(h\) to each \(w_i\). Number the seven original vertices from 1 to 7 in the displayed order and add \(10j\) pendant leaves to original vertex \(j\), with diagonal and incident corner \(\mathbb F_p\). Add \(2k+100\) such pendant leaves to the hub if there are \(k\) marks. All extra pair compositions are zero. Diagonal corners act on incident modules by the indicated scalar reductions. The product is associative. Indeed every listed off-diagonal product lands in an output corner \(s_it_j\), which has zero product with every off-diagonal corner on either side, and the two scalar actions of every listed composition are balanced. For example \(abx_i\) depends only on \(a,b\) modulo \(p^{a_i}\). The sum of the vertex idempotents is the unit.

The sum of the off-diagonal corners together with the \(p\)-multiples in the diagonals is the Jacobson radical \(J\): it is nilpotent and its quotient is \(\mathbb F_p\) to the power of the vertex count. Any isomorphism induces a permutation of the residue vertices and can be made to carry the chosen idempotents exactly by conjugating by a unit. For two lifts \(f_i,e_i\) of the same residue idempotents, \(u=\sum_i f_i e_i\) is a unit because \(u\equiv 1\bmod J\), and \(u e_i=f_i u\). The same argument works for an anti-isomorphism.

The Peirce nonzero pattern is a directed graph. Its hub has out-degree \(3k+107\) and every in-degree is at most \(k+9\). Consequently there is no anti-isomorphism between the two rings from matched inputs with the same number of marks. The hub is the unique vertex with maximum out-degree. Original vertex \(j\) has exactly \(10j\) outgoing neighbors of out-degree zero, all of which are its pendant leaves. The marked vertices are the remaining nonleaf vertices once the hub and the original vertices have been identified. The mark vertices are ordered by their induced directed path, and all seven original vertices and the hub are fixed. The center of the ring is \(R\). Commutation with all vertex idempotents makes a central element diagonal. Every corner out of the hub forces the diagonal scalars at its endpoints to be the hub scalar reduced to their local rings, and pendant corners do the same for the remaining vertices. These scalars indeed commute with every product.

Let \(X,Y,Z\) be the maps induced by an isomorphism on \(s_0m_0,m_0t_0,s_0t_0\). They are additive and hence \(R\)-linear. Multiplication by a generator on each scalar corner is transported to multiplication by a unit times that generator. From the three scalar triangles along the \(X\) path one obtains \(Z=aX\) for a unit \(a\in R^\times\); from the other three along the \(Y\) path one obtains \(Z=bY\) for a unit \(b\in R^\times\). Explicitly, if the scalar corner units on \(m_1t_0,m_0t_1,m_1t_1\) are \(u,v,w\), then \(a=uv/w\). If those on \(s_0m_2,s_1m_0,s_1m_2\) are \(u',v',w'\), then \(b=u'v'/w'\). Compatibility on \(s_0,m_0,t_0\) now reads
\[
Z[x,y]=(ab)^{-1}[Zx,Zy].
\]
Hence \(f=(ab)^{-1}Z\) is a Lie-ring isomorphism. The marked corners give \(Z(x_i)\in R_i^\times y_i\), so \(f\) sends each marked cyclic subgroup to its counterpart. Conversely a Lie-ring isomorphism sending the cyclic subgroups as indicated induces a ring isomorphism: use \(f\) on each \(L\) corner, preserve every diagonal and every unlabeled scalar arrow, and choose the unit on \(s_0w_i\) to match \(f(x_i)\) relative to \(y_i\). The unit exists since the element generates the target cyclic subgroup. No path or hub multiplication adds a condition on this choice.

The cardinality bound holds when \(\sum_i a_i\le \log_p|L|\). Each original \(L\) corner contributes \(\log_p|L|\), each core or hub \(R\) corner contributes \(e\le\log_p|L|\), and all marks, their hub corners and path corners together contribute \(O(\sum_i a_i)\). There are \(O(k+1)\) pendant leaves and arrows of length 1. In total, \(\log_p|A(L)|=O(\log_p|L|)\) with an absolute constant. This uses the actual additive orders of the marks.

## 3. A class-two group whose commutator recovers the associative ring

Use \(B=M_2(A)\). Its center is \(R\), a local ring. Write a group on \(B^3\) with product
\[
(x,y,z)(x',y',z')=(x+x',y+y',z+z'+xy').
\]
Bilinearity gives the cocycle identity. Its center and derived subgroup both equal the last copy of \(B\): taking \(y'=1\) and \(x'=1\) shows that the commutator bimap \(\beta((x,y),(x',y'))=xy'-x'y\) has zero radical and has all values in \(B\). Thus group isomorphism preserves this bimap up to additive changes of source and target. The order is \(|A|^{12}\). For odd \(p\), the power formula has central correction \(\binom m2 xy\), which is zero at \(m=p^e\). The central copy contains an element of order \(p^e\), so the output exponent is exactly \(p^e\).

Define the adjoint ring of \(\beta\) as pairs of additive endomorphisms \((S,T)\) with \(\beta(Su,v)=\beta(u,Tv)\), with product \((S,T)(S',T')=(SS',T'T)\). A bimap equivalence transports this ring by conjugation. Write \(L_a,R_a\) for left and right multiplications by \(a\in B\). Comparing the \(x,y'\) and \(y,x'\) terms and substituting 1 gives
\[
 S=\begin{pmatrix}R_a&Q\\U&L_b\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T=\begin{pmatrix}R_b&Q'\\U'&L_a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The \(y,y'\) identity has the form \(Q(y)y'=-Q'(y')y\). Substitution of 1 gives \(Q=L_d\), \(Q'=-L_d\), and \(d[y,y']=0\) for every \(y,y'\in B\) in the associative commutator convention. Likewise the \(x,x'\) identity gives \(U=R_{d'}\), \(U'=-R_{d'}\), and \([x,x']d'=0\). Because the matrix units \(E_{12},E_{21}\) are commutators in \(M_2(A)\), their joint left and right annihilators are zero. Thus all four off-diagonal endomorphisms vanish. Conversely all pairs given by the displayed diagonal parameters are adjoints. With the indicated composition the adjoint ring is \(B^{\mathrm{op}}\times B\).

Since the center of \(B\) is local, a ring isomorphism of adjoint rings induces either a ring isomorphism \(M_2(A)\cong M_2(A')\) or a ring anti-isomorphism between them. In either case one recovers the corresponding isomorphism or anti-isomorphism between \(A\) and \(A'\). In detail, the quotient by the radical of \(M_2(A)\) is a product of \(M_2(\mathbb F_p)\) factors. The idempotent \(E_{11}\) has rank one in every residual factor, as does its image under either kind of map. A unit in the residual product conjugates that image to the residue of \(E_{11}\); it lifts to a unit in the finite ring. After this conjugation, the idempotents have the same residue, so the two-idempotent version of the conjugating-unit argument in §2 aligns them exactly. The corners at \(E_{11}\) are the original rings. The graph in §2 excludes anti-isomorphism. In the forward direction any ring isomorphism \(A\cong A'\) induces a group isomorphism entrywise.

For Cayley tables the construction can enumerate the elements of \(A\), then of \(B^3\), and fill the table directly using the given addition and bracket tables and arithmetic in the indicated cyclic rings. This takes time polynomial in \(|L|\) when the marks satisfy the stated bound. The proof uses the Heisenberg and adjoint constructions for bilinear maps in their usual form, with the matrix amplification and the rigid Peirce pattern to resolve the opposite-ring ambiguity. [Wilson, generalized Heisenberg groups and adjoint rings](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.5466)

## 4. Search, counting and automorphism generators

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\) be an additive cyclic basis of \(L\), with generator orders \(p^{a_1},\ldots,p^{a_r}\). Then \(\sum_i a_i=\log_p|L|\). A query can mark any ordered initial segment of the cyclic subgroups \(\langle x_i\rangle\) and target subgroups of the same orders using one pair of the constructed class-two tables. This works also when guessing target subgroups which turn out to be incompatible. The cyclic subgroups of a given order can be enumerated by taking powers or multiples of all elements and deduplicating the resulting sets. There are at most \(N\) of them.

For search, extend a compatible marked prefix one step at a time, asking whether each candidate target subgroup preserves feasibility. The empty prefix first tests isomorphism. Once the basis subgroups are given, enumerate a generator of each target subgroup as the image of the basis generator. The number of combinations is at most \(\prod_i p^{a_i}=N\). Every combination defines a unique additive homomorphism, and its bijectivity and bracket preservation are directly testable from the tables. Feasibility of the full prefix guarantees successful search.

For counting let \(K_i\) be the subgroup of \(\operatorname{Aut}(L)\) stabilizing the first \(i\) cyclic subgroups individually as sets. For each coordinate, test every candidate target cyclic subgroup while keeping the earlier subgroups fixed. The number of accepted candidates is the size of the orbit under \(K_{i-1}\), and therefore equals \([K_{i-1}:K_i]\). The terminal group \(K_r\) is enumerable by the same at-most-\(N\) basis generator assignments. Multiply its size by the orbit sizes to obtain \(|\operatorname{Aut}(L)|\). The number of isomorphisms between two rings is this count when they are isomorphic, and zero otherwise. For generators, run the prefix search for each successful orbit candidate to recover one representative of each left coset at each step. Together with the enumerated terminal group these representatives generate the entire group by the stabilizer chain. Thus all reductions make polynomially many class-two decision queries of polynomial size and use polynomial additional time. The Lazard correspondence transports the witnesses, counts, and generators back to the groups.

As a corollary, the deterministic \(M^{O((\log M)^{8/9})}\) odd-order class-two decision algorithm in Grochow–Qiao Part V gives \(N^{O((\log N)^{8/9})}\) time for all four problems on this Lazard family, with no exponent parameter in the bound. More generally any class-two decision bound transfers with polynomial changes in its order parameter. Sylow factorization gives the same consequence for finite nilpotent groups whose Sylow \(p\)-subgroups have class less than \(p\), since every isomorphism and automorphism respects the Sylow factors. [Grochow–Qiao, Part V Theorem 1.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1)

The main argument is a written proof. The accompanying Lean statements audit the scalar normalization, matrix-unit annihilators, and the leading two-sided adjoint identity.
