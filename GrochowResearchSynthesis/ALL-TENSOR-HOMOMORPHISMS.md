# All tensor homomorphisms in arity three and in path algebras

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let the spaces of an ordinary order-$d$ tensor be finite free $R$-modules. A tensor homomorphism $T\to T'$ is a tuple of arbitrary linear maps $f_i:V_i\to V'_i$ with $(\bigotimes_i f_i)T=T'$. These include tensor restrictions with singular or rectangular maps. Fix $d\ge3$. Two encodings recover each homomorphism from its leaf maps and keep the maps on objects affine in the tensor coefficients. Both commute with arbitrary base change.

## 1. Balanced mixed trilinear records

**Theorem 1.** There is a fully faithful covariant functor from order-$d$ tensors with all tensor homomorphisms into a fixed finite linear signature with one constant 3-tensor and structural records of mixed variance of order three. There are fewer than $2d$ named spaces and $d$ records. If the input sides are at most $n$, each named space has dimension at most $n^{\lceil d/3\rceil}$.

Partition the ordered positions into three nonempty intervals whose sizes differ by at most one. Use a rooted binary tree for each interval, whose leaves are its input positions. For every node $I$ use the space $U_I=\bigotimes_{i\in I}V_i$, where $I$ denotes the positions of its descendant leaves. Each internal node with children $J,K$ has the fixed splitting map
\[
 \delta_I:U_I\longrightarrow U_J\otimes_R U_K.
\]
It is the canonical regrouping map. This is a structural record with one input and two outputs, equivalently in $U_I^*\otimes U_J\otimes U_K$. The original tensor regrouped into the three roots is the named constant.

A morphism of records preserves the constant by the tensor product of its root maps and satisfies
\[
 \delta'_I h_I=(h_J\otimes h_K)\delta_I
\]
for every internal node. Each $\delta'_I$ is invertible even when the $h_I$ are singular. Inducting from the leaves gives $h_I=\bigotimes_{i\in I}h_i$ under the canonical regroupings, so every record homomorphism is uniquely determined by its leaf maps. The root condition is then exactly the original tensor equation. Conversely these formulas extend every tensor homomorphism, and identity and composition agree with the leaf maps. The three trees have $2d-3$ nodes and $d-3$ internal nodes. Tensor products and canonical regrouping give the stated base-change and dimension properties. In basis coordinates the only input-dependent record is a reindexing of the input coefficients.

The reduction of mixed signatures to ordinary 3TI from *Universal tensor isomorphism for linear signatures* can be applied here on isomorphisms. The all-homomorphism assertion above uses the explicit one-input/two-output structural records. Their morphism equation involves a forward map on the input, so it remains meaningful for a singular map. This gives a balanced arity-three answer to the homomorphism question in the mixed category. [Grochow–Qiao, Open Questions 7.4, 7.5 and 7.8](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110).

## 2. A path algebra with marked endpoints and output

**Theorem 2.** Ordinary order-$d$ tensors with all tensor homomorphisms admit a fully faithful contravariant functor into finite free marked associative unital $R$-algebras with all unital algebra homomorphisms preserving the marks. The matrices of the underlying linear maps on proper path spaces are explicitly the indicated products of transposed leaf maps. The algebra dimension is at most $(d+1)+1+d^2 n^{d-1}$ when each input side is at most $n\ge1$.

To construct $A(T)$, use vertices $0,\ldots,d$ and write $W_i=V_i^*$. Include an idempotent $e_i$ for each vertex. For $0\le i<j\le d$ other than $(0,d)$ the corner $e_iA(T)e_j$ is the proper path space $W_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes W_j$. The corner $e_0A(T)e_d$ is instead the line $R\tau$, with a distinguished generator $\tau$. All reverse corners are zero. Products of composable proper paths concatenate, except that the full path product is evaluation of the multilinear form $T$ into $R\tau$. Multiplication by the $e_i$ gives the corresponding corner projections, and all other products of nonmatching paths vanish. The unit is $\sum e_i$.

Equivalently start with the tensor path algebra on the chain and add a direct arrow $\tau$ from $0$ to $d$. The chain's complete path space, together with this extra arrow, has no incoming or outgoing positive-length concatenations. Quotient that corner by the submodule of relations $u-T(u)\tau$ for full paths $u$. It is a two-sided ideal, and the complete corner of the quotient is freely generated by $\tau$. This proves associativity and the free basis claim over every $R$. The quotient sends the full path basis coordinates to the corresponding entries of $T$, so multiplication has the claimed affine and base-change properties.

Let $f:T\to T'$ be a tensor homomorphism. Its transposes $f_i^*:W'_i\to W_i$ define a unital algebra map $A(T')\to A(T)$ by tensor product on proper paths, and by the identity on every named idempotent and on $\tau$. Compatibility on a complete path is the pullback identity $T'(u)=T((\bigotimes_i f_i^*)u)$. Conversely, every map preserving the marks preserves the corners, so determines the maps $W'_i\to W_i$ on the single arrows. The proper path spaces are spanned by products of the arrows, which forces all their higher maps. The complete path products force exactly the pullback identity. Finite free double duality returns the unique $f$. This proves the functor and the Hom bijection, without any assumption of injectivity or surjectivity. Dually it gives a covariant functor into marked counital coassociative coalgebras.

The underlying unmarked algebras also detect ordinary tensor isomorphism over fields when all side dimensions are positive. The strictly upper-triangular corners are a nilpotent ideal and the quotient is the product of $d+1$ copies of the field, so this ideal is the Jacobson radical $J$. The directed graph of nonzero corners of $J/J^2$ on the simple factors is the chain, with a possible shortcut $0\to d$. The shortcut exists exactly when $T=0$, because its corner is generated by products of positive-length paths exactly when a full path has nonzero evaluation. Every edge of the chain is present and the order of the vertices is the reachability order. Any algebra isomorphism therefore fixes the vertex order in the quotients. Its images of the $e_i$ may be simultaneously conjugated to the named $e_i$ by the unit $u=\sum_i e_i^{\rm target}f(e_i^{\rm source})$, which is the identity modulo $J$ and satisfies $u f(e_i^{\rm source})=e_i^{\rm target}u$. After conjugation, the map on the corner $R\tau$ is multiplication by a nonzero scalar. The full path comparison gives the tensor pullback identity with this scalar. Rescaling the first leaf map absorbs it and yields an ordinary tensor isomorphism. The converse follows from the marked construction. This verifies the reduction as one to ordinary unmarked algebra isomorphism on the isomorphism groupoid, with a fully faithful refinement retaining the marked data for all homomorphisms. [Grochow–Qiao, Theorem A and §4](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110).

These are written proofs. The target categories and their homomorphisms are as specified above.
