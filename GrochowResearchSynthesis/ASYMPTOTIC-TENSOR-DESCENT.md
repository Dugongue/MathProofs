# Field descent for tied tensor restrictions and the prime-power subrank gap

Fix an order $d\ge 2$ and a partition $\Pi$ of the positions. Positions in each block $B$ use the same finite-dimensional space. A block-symmetric tensor is fixed by the permutations within each block. Write $S\preceq_\Pi T$ if $T$ restricts to $S$ by a single linear map per block, including singular and rectangular maps. We freely dualize each position and work with multilinear forms, so restrictions are pullbacks. Write $D_r$ for the unit diagonal tensor of size $r$, and $D(c_1,\ldots,c_r)$ for its weighted version with every $c_i\ne0$. The product below is the positionwise Kronecker product $\boxtimes$.

## 1. One multiplication tensor descends every use of an extension witness

**Theorem 1.** Let $S,T$ be block-symmetric tensors over $F$. If $S\preceq_\Pi T$ over any extension of $F$, some finite extension $L/F$ and an $F$-linear map $\lambda:L\to F$ with $\lambda(1)=1$ give a block-symmetric tensor
\[
 M(a_1,\ldots,a_d)=\lambda(a_1\cdots a_d)
\]
such that $S\boxtimes M\preceq_\Pi T\boxtimes M$ and $S\preceq_\Pi S\boxtimes M$ over $F$. The same $M$ works for every simultaneous power of the witness, and for every composition of witnesses defined over this $L$. For a given finite-extension witness, the multiplication tensor uses one space of dimension $[L:F]$ at each position.

For the finite-extension reduction, the matrix equations for a restriction have coefficients in $F$. If they have a solution in any field extension, their ideal is proper and their finitely generated coordinate algebra has a maximal ideal. Zariski's lemma makes the residue field there finite over $F$. Now regard each $L$-extended input space as an $F$-space. For an $F$-form $A$, composing its $L$-linear extension with $\lambda$ gives $A\boxtimes M$ over $F$, since on pure scalar-space inputs the value is $A(v_1,\ldots,v_d)\lambda(\prod_i a_i)$. The extension witness maps become $F$-linear maps on the enlarged spaces and still repeat within every tied block. Their equality after applying $\lambda$ is the asserted restriction. Repeating or composing witnesses over $L$ uses the same scalar multiplication and the same $\lambda$. Inserting $1\in L$ at every position restricts $M$ to $D_1$. The construction works for inseparable as well as separable extensions. The ordinary spectrum's field invariance is classical. The use of a multiplication tensor for ordinary scalar restriction also appears in recent work on analytic rank. [Strassen field invariance recalled in Remark 1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.07851) and [the multiplication tensor in §4 of Stability of ranks under field extensions](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04034v3#S4).

## 2. Exact field-size and scalar conditions for diagonal assistance

For $c(n)=2^{o(n)}$, say $S\preceq^{\mathrm{w}}_{\mathrm{a},\Pi}T$ if for every $n\ge 1$ there is a weighted diagonal of size at most $c(n)$ such that $S^{\boxtimes n}\preceq_\Pi T^{\boxtimes n}\boxtimes D(c_1,\ldots,c_r)$. Denote the corresponding relation with unit diagonals by $\preceq_{\mathrm{a},\Pi}$. Define weighted asymptotic rank by taking the $n$th roots of the minimum sizes of weighted diagonals restricting to $T^{\boxtimes n}$. Use rank infinity if no such diagonal exists. Define unweighted rank using unit diagonals. Products of decompositions give the usual submultiplicativity whenever these ranks are finite.

**Theorem 2.** If $F$ is infinite or $F=\mathbb F_q$ with $\max_B|B|\le q$, weighted assisted asymptotic restriction and weighted asymptotic rank of $F$-tensors are invariant under every field extension. This size condition is necessary for each assertion to hold for every extension. Put $g=\gcd\{|B|:B\in\Pi\}$ and let $A_g$ be the set of all finite sums of $g$th powers in $F$, including zero. If the size condition and $A_g=F$ both hold, the two invariances hold for unit diagonals as well. The two conditions together characterize invariance under every extension of unit-diagonal assisted asymptotic restriction. They also characterize finiteness of unit-diagonal rank for every block-symmetric tensor over $F$.

To see the finite-bound assertion, the vector space of block-symmetric tensors has a basis of within-block orbit sums of elementary tensors. The coefficients of a block-pure tensor in this basis are the distinct monomials of multidegree $(|B|)_{B\in\Pi}$ in the coordinates of its vectors. Over an infinite field these monomials are independent as functions. Over $\mathbb F_q$, homogeneous monomials of a fixed degree at most $q$ are independent as functions. For degree $q$, reducing by $X_i^q-X_i$ changes only the pure monomials, into the distinct degree-one monomials, whereas all other degree-$q$ monomials already have exponents less than $q$. Independence follows from the univariate root bound successively in each variable. For several blocks the reductions and the argument apply separately. Thus the pure block tensors span, and every block-symmetric tensor is a restriction of a weighted diagonal of finite size. The set of nonzero scalar factors available from rescaling the vectors of one pure term is $(F^\times)^g$. Indeed, the block sizes are multiples of $g$, and Bézout's identity gives every $g$th power as a product of block-size powers. Repetition of a pure term implements any finite sum of these factors. This proves the unweighted assertion when $A_g=F$. The latter holds with a singleton block and in characteristic $p$ if $g<p$. For the second claim, the difference between the $(g-1)$st forward differences of $X^g$ at $a$ and at $0$ is $g!a$. These differences are integer linear combinations of $g$th powers, negatives come from repeated addition in characteristic $p$, and $g!$ is invertible in the prime field. Over a finite field, $A_g$ is the subfield generated by $(F^\times)^g$, since the additive span of a multiplicative subgroup is closed under multiplication and is a finite domain.

For descent of the weighted relation, fix a powered comparison over an extension
\[
 S^{\boxtimes k}\preceq_\Pi T^{\boxtimes k}\boxtimes W,
\]
where $W$ is a weighted diagonal with $r$ terms. Include its weights in the polynomial witness equations, with one inverse variable per weight. Theorem 1 applies to the matrix maps over a finite $L/F$ supplied by these equations, with the following explicit adjustment because $W$ may have weights outside $F$. On each block of the direct sum, descending a scalar $c\in L^\times$ gives the multiplication form $M_c(a_1,\ldots,a_d)=\lambda(c\prod_i a_i)$. We need a bound on the number of weighted terms for these forms that is independent of $c$. The span proof gives a *fixed* basis of pure tensors on the $[L:F]$-dimensional spaces. Expanding $M_c$ in that basis uses at most $h$ terms, independent of $c$.

Repeat the extension witness $m$ times. Its auxiliary tensor still has the same $L$-multiplication form on each of the $r^m$ blocks, now with the product weights. Thus $S^{\boxtimes km}\preceq_\Pi T^{\boxtimes km}\boxtimes W_{F,m}$ for a weighted diagonal with at most $hr^m$ terms. For the unweighted relation start with unit diagonals, so every multiplier is the same $M_1$ and one fixed unit-diagonal decomposition suffices uniformly in $m$. The finitely many remainders $0\le b<k$ may be treated using their given comparisons over the extension, each with its own finite fixed cost, then tensoring with the powered comparison. For any $\epsilon>0$ choose $k$ with $k^{-1}\log_2 r<\epsilon/2$. The logarithmic cost per copy is then at most $\epsilon$ for all sufficiently large $n$. The least possible diagonal size at each $n$ therefore has subexponential growth. The reverse follows by scalar extension.

The rank argument is the same directly. An extension rank decomposition with $r$ terms of $T^{\boxtimes k}$ gives a decomposition of $T^{\boxtimes km}$ with at most $hr^m$ terms over $F$. Taking roots and then the infimum over $k$ proves the equality of asymptotic ranks under the corresponding hypotheses.

The finite field size condition is sharp even for weighted assistance. Let a block have size $s\ge q+1$, and take two labels with coordinates $X,Y$ on that block. The nonzero homogeneous polynomial $X^{s-q-1}(X^qY-XY^q)$ vanishes at every $\mathbb F_q$ point. The corresponding linear functional on orbit-sum coefficients vanishes on the span of all block-pure tensors. Choose a block-symmetric $U$ on two labels with functional value one, taking one-dimensional spaces in the other blocks. Set $T=U\oplus D_1$, so $T$ still lies outside the pure span, and $T$ has $D_1$ as a tied restriction. Each power of $T$ has $T$ as a tied restriction, so no power has finite weighted rank over $F$. Over the algebraic closure, $T$ has finite weighted rank and even finite unit-diagonal rank. In particular the assisted comparison with a fixed sufficiently large diagonal exists there and fails over $F$.

For the independent scalar condition in the unit-diagonal comparison, let $c\notin A_g$ be nonzero. Over an algebraic closure, $cD_1$ and $D_1$ mutually restrict. Over $F$, the scalar $(cD_1)^{\boxtimes n}$ cannot be obtained from a unit diagonal of any size when $n\equiv1\pmod g$. Otherwise $c^n\in A_g$, and rescaling by the $g$th power $c^{-(n-1)}$ would give $c\in A_g$. Multiplication of a finite sum of $g$th powers by another $g$th power preserves $A_g$. This gives infinitely many failed comparisons. For example an even fully tied block over $\mathbb R$ has $A_g=\mathbb R_{\ge0}$. This scalar issue concerns the all-powers preorder and individual ranks. A scalar obstruction can disappear on a subsequence of powers, so no equivalent statement about the infimum of unit-diagonal rank roots is implied by this example.

### Spectral consequence

Assume the size and scalar conditions of Theorem 2 over $F$. For an extension $E$, consider the subsemirings at both fields consisting of zero and tensors with a tied restriction to $D_1$, modulo mutual tied restriction. They are closed under sum and product. A visible nonzero $B$ has $D_1\preceq_\Pi B$, and every $A$ has $A\preceq_\Pi D_r$ by the finite-rank argument. Thus $A\preceq_\Pi B\boxtimes D_r$, which supplies Strassen's domination axiom. The other axioms follow from tensor sum and product and the flattening ranks of the $D_r$. The size and scalar hypotheses persist over $E$: this is automatic when $E$ is infinite only for the size condition, so for this consequence require $A_g(E)=E$ as well, or just assume finite unit-diagonal rank on the visible subsemiring over $E$. Also, $g<p$ in characteristic $p$ or $g=1$ gives the scalar condition uniformly over all extensions.

Pass to the mutual-equivalence quotient for the assisted preorder. Strassen duality gives the same spectral points there, since for $x^{\boxtimes n}$ the subexponential assistance contributes $2^{o(n)}$ to the monotonicity inequality. Tensor extension preserves visibility and embeds the resulting ordered semiring by Theorem 2. The subsemiring theorem makes restriction of spectral points from the visible semiring over $E$ onto the visible semiring over $F$ surjective. On the full semiring, every normalized spectral point sends any nonzero scalar tensor $cD_1$ to one because its $g$th power is tied-isomorphic to $D_1$. Consequently, even without the scalar condition, normalized spectral points on the full semiring respect the weighted assisted inequalities. The surjectivity argument here uses domination on the two visible semirings. [Wigderson–Zuiddam, Chapters 2 and 3](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/Wigderson_Zuiddam_Final_2025.pdf) and [Zuiddam's subsemiring theorem, Corollary 2.18](https://www.math.ias.edu/~jzuiddam/diss/diss_v2.pdf).

## 3. The exact prime-power gap at one and an exponent bound

**Theorem 3.** Let $F$ be a perfect field of characteristic $p$, let $d=p^a$ with $a\ge1$, and let $T$ be a fully symmetric order-$d$ tensor. Put $m=p^{a-1}$. Its deepest nontrivial Frobenius collapse is the symmetric order-$p$ tensor $C(T)$ with coefficients $T_{i_1^m,\ldots,i_p^m}$. Here $i^m$ means $m$ copies of the same index. Let $\ell$ be the linear functional given by $T(x,\ldots,x)=\ell(x)^d$.

1. If $\ell=0$, every power has tied subrank zero over every extension.
2. If $\ell\ne0$ and the one-position flattening of $C(T)$ has rank one, every power has tied subrank one over every extension.
3. If $\ell\ne0$ and this flattening has rank at least two, $T^{\boxtimes(d-1)}$ restricts to $D_2$ over an algebraic closure. For a base field with at least $d$ elements it already does so over a finite extension of degree at most $(d-1)^{d-1}$. For a smaller finite $F_q$, an initial extension of degree $f$ with $q^f\ge d$ followed by an extension of that bounded degree suffices. Thus over an algebraic closure the asymptotic tied subrank is at least $2^{1/(d-1)}$ exactly in the third case, and it is respectively zero and one in the first two cases.

To construct $\ell$, expand the pure diagonal. Every nonpure multinomial coefficient of degree $p^a$ vanishes modulo $p$, as follows from $(\sum x_i)^{p^a}=\sum x_i^{p^a}$. Perfectness supplies the roots of the pure coefficients. The same expansion for powers shows that the normal functional of $T^{\boxtimes n}$ is $\ell^{\otimes n}$. The collapse sends a tied map of matrix $A$ to the tied map of matrix $A^{[m]}$, its coordinatewise $m$th power. For each consecutive group of $m$ repeated output coordinates, expand and group input indices of the original tensor according to their multiplicity. Symmetry makes every term of a given type use the same coefficient, and the nonpure multinomial coefficients vanish. The pure terms are exactly the collapse and the entrywise Frobenius on the map. The collapse sends $D_r$ to $D_r$ and commutes with the Kronecker product directly on coefficients. This proves the first two conclusions, including attainment of one by a normal vector with $\ell(x)=1$.

For the third case, a rank-one symmetric tensor is a scalar multiple of a pure power: rank one gives a one-dimensional image of the first flattening, hence by symmetry the same line in each position. The pure coefficients of $C(T)$ are the same as those of $T$, so $C(T)$ differs from the collapse of $\ell^{\otimes d}$ by the assumed rank. For each $1\le j<d$ we prove that
\[
 P_j(x,y)=T(x^{d-j},y^j)
\]
is nonconstant on the product of affine hyperplanes $\ell(x)=\ell(y)=1$. If constant its value is one by taking $x=y$. Homogeneity would then give the polynomial identity $P_j(x,y)=\ell(x)^{d-j}\ell(y)^j$. To disprove this identity, choose labels of a coefficient on which the two collapsed tensors differ, with each of its $p$ original labels repeated $m$ times. Expand $d-j$ and $j$ in base $p$ and make a list of their digit chunks, each carrying its original color and size $p^u$. All sizes are at most $m$ and their total is $d$. Pack these chunks into $p$ bins of size $m$: starting at size one, group $p$ equally sized chunks and carry them up a level. The total is divisible by $m$, so there is no remainder at any smaller level, and the top level has exactly $p$ groups. Assign each bin one of the $p$ chosen labels. In each color and digit level, fewer than $p$ chunks existed, so the assigned counts of both colors are carry-free. The two multinomial coefficients of the resulting monomial in $P_j$ are therefore nonzero. Explicitly, factoring $(\sum z_i)^{b}$ according to the base-$p$ digits of $b$ gives a coefficient that is a product of multinomial coefficients of degrees less than $p$. The coefficient difference is the chosen nonzero difference times these two units. This proves the nonconstancy.

Over an algebraic closure, choose a zero pair $(x_j,y_j)$ of this polynomial on the two normal hyperplanes for each $j$. A nonconstant polynomial on affine space has such a zero, by specializing all but one variable while keeping its leading coefficient nonzero and taking a root of the remaining polynomial. The same specialization can be done over any base with at least $d$ elements because the individual degrees are at most $d-1$ and the leading coefficient is a nonzero polynomial in the remaining variables. Each root has degree at most $d-1$, which gives the extension bound by compositum. Let the rows of the final tied map be $\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d-1}x_j$ and $\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d-1}y_j$. The two pure evaluations are one, and a coefficient involving $j$ copies of the second row contains the zero factor $P_j(x_j,y_j)$. This gives $D_2$ exactly. This extends the order-$p$ construction in *The exact modular threshold for forgetting tensor symmetries* to all prime-power orders over algebraically closed fields. It proves the gap criterion and uniform witness bound. It leaves the full value of the unassisted rate above the gap unspecified for general $a$.

Over a perfect base field, the same zero/one/greater-than-one criterion holds for the **weighted assisted** rate when the field is infinite or $q\ge d$. This is the supremum of diagonal growth rates obtainable by adding subexponential weighted diagonal assistance to all sufficiently large powers of $T$. For the lower bound in the third case, descend the powered $D_2$ restriction via Theorem 1 using one fixed multiplication tensor. This yields $D_{2^k}$ from $T^{\boxtimes (d-1)k}$ with a fixed weighted diagonal. The intervening powers can use the restriction to $D_1$ on their remaining factors. In the first two cases, after any subexponential diagonal assistance the same pure or collapsed flattening gives respectively zero output or an output dimension bounded by the assistant size. For $d=p$, the order-$p$ field classification in the preceding note then gives equality of the weighted assisted rate and the ordinary asymptotic subrank over every perfect base whenever $\ell\ne0$. For this last application, the upper bound follows by forgetting the ties after extending to the algebraic closure. There a subexponential diagonal can be obtained from $o(n)$ extra copies of the source when it has a $D_2$ restriction in a fixed power. The simple case has rate one by flattening. To descend the lower bound, fix any power with a tied diagonal restriction over the algebraic closure, descend its iterations via one fixed scalar multiplication tensor, and take the supremum of the rates of those fixed-power witnesses. [Classical ordinary subrank descent, stated as Lemma 6.2](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/34472/34472.pdf).

## 4. Genuine unassisted subrank can depend on the field

For every even $d\ge4$, take the rational binary symmetric form $T$ with coefficient $1/(k+1)$ on any word of weight $k$. Over $\mathbb R$ this is the integral over $t\in[0,1]$ of the pure forms $(1,t)^{\otimes d}$. In the $n$th power, each nonzero dual row corresponds to a nonzero multilinear polynomial in $n$ independent real variables. For nonzero rows with polynomials $f$ and $h$, the mixed coefficient with two copies of the first and $d-2$ copies of the second is the integral of $f^2h^{d-2}$ on the unit cube. This is strictly positive. The polynomial product is nonzero and its complement of the zero locus meets the interior of the cube, giving a positive continuous integrand on a nonempty open set. Thus every power of $T$ has tied subrank one over $\mathbb R$ and over $\mathbb Q$, with the lower bound attained on the constant-one row. The first flattening has rank two, as witnessed by the minor from entries $1,1/2,1/3$. Over $\mathbb C$, the asymptotic symmetric subrank equals the ordinary one by Christandl–Fawzi–Ta–Zuiddam, and the ordinary growth gap for symmetric tensors of mode rank two makes it strictly greater than one. This example shows the distinction between weighted assisted descent and unassisted field invariance. [Symmetric Subrank of Tensors and Applications, Theorem 3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.01130v2#S3.SS2) and [the ordinary growth gap, Theorem 6.1](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/34472/34472.pdf).

## 5. Unassisted symmetric subrank in every characteristic $p>d$

**Theorem 4.** Over every field of characteristic $p>d$, ordinary and symmetric asymptotic subrank agree on symmetric order-$d$ tensors. Here both asymptotic subranks use the supremum of the $n$th roots of the subranks of powers. More precisely, let $T$ have first flattening rank at least two. For every ordinary restriction from $T^{\boxtimes n}$ to a symmetric tensor $S$, there is a tied restriction from $T^{\boxtimes(n+c)}$ to $S$, with $c\le p\,d!$. This removes the algebraically closed hypothesis for these assertions in positive characteristic above the tensor order. [Christandl–Fawzi–Ta–Zuiddam, Theorems 3.4 and 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.01130v2#S3.SS2).

First take the quotient of the dual space by the first-position radical of $T$. Symmetry makes it the radical in every position. We get a concise form on a space of dimension at least two, and restrictions of this form can be realized on the original space by lifting the rows. The $p$-fold power remains concise because the matrix of each position flattening is a Kronecker product of matrices of full row rank. Take independent vectors $x,y$ of the concise space. In that power's covector domain put
\[
 z=\sum_{i=1}^{p} x^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes y\otimes x^{\otimes(p-i)}.
\]
The summands are distinct basis words in a basis extending $x,y$, so $z\ne0$. On expanding the pure evaluation at $z$, the $d$ choices of summand positions are tuples in $\mathbb Z_p^d$. Simultaneously rotating all $p$ copies preserves each coefficient and gives free orbits of size $p$ on the tuples. Their sums vanish in $F$, making $z$ isotropic. This step works over every field of characteristic $p$.

Extend $z$ to a basis of the concise power. Some coefficient containing $z$ is nonzero by conciseness. Choose a nonzero type with maximal $z$-multiplicity $b$. Since the pure coefficient vanishes, $1\le b<d$. As in Lemma 3.9 of Christandl–Fawzi–Ta–Zuiddam, form the table of all distinct permutations of a word of this type and use its $d$ columns as the selected basis words in a further power, of exponent at most $d!$. The column words are distinct because the type has at least two different letters. Selecting all different columns gives the same nonzero product coefficient. If a column repeats and at least $b$ distinct columns occur, choose a table row placing $z$ on a repeated column and on $b-1$ further selected columns. If fewer than $b$ occur, use a row placing $z$ on every selected column. In both cases the resulting coefficient in that row has more than $b$ occurrences of $z$ and vanishes. Thus the restriction is a nonzero multiple of the permutation tensor $H_d$ on $d$ labels, the sum of the $d!$ words with all labels different. Rescale one label to normalize it because every word uses the label exactly once.

For an ordinary restriction $T^{\boxtimes n}\to S$, use the tag label $i$ in $H_d$ to choose the $i$th ordinary map, keeping the tag in an output tensor factor. One common block map makes these choices at every position. At the tag ordering $(1,\ldots,d)$ the output is $S$, and every other tag ordering permutes the positions of $S$, which agrees by symmetry. Hence the output is $S\boxtimes H_d$. The tensor $H_d$ restricts tied to $D_1$ by sending all labels to one and scaling one designated label by $1/d!$. This gives the stated constant overhead. Taking $S$ to be ordinary diagonals gives equality of asymptotic subranks for nonsimple $T$. The zero tensor has both rates zero. A nonzero simple symmetric tensor has form $a v^{\otimes d}$; its $d$th Kronecker power admits an exact tied restriction to $D_1$ by scaling one common row, so both rates are one. No roots in $F$ are needed for that case.
