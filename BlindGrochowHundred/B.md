## Graphs with group-valued edge labels

Fix a directed graph and choose a path from a root to every vertex, using an empty path for the root itself. For edge weights $A_e$, write $A_i$ for the product along the chosen path to $i$. The based edge labels are $A_i A_e A_j^{-1}$. For a second set of weights, use $B_i B_e B_j^{-1}$. A root value $z$ extends to a gauge $g_i A_e=B_e g_j$ exactly when $z$ simultaneously intertwines all the based labels. Its extension is unique and has $g_i=B_i^{-1}zA_i$. This applies to arbitrary groups and arbitrary directed edge types with the chosen reachability data. Flatness means the based labels are all units. For a multiplicative character $\chi$, the path formula gives the exact value of $\chi(g_i)$ from the root and the two path weights. Nonabelian gain-graph switching is a classical source for the conjugacy reduction. Here the statements retain a prescribed root gauge and track characters along directed paths. [Cavaleri and Donno](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12428)

## Matrix actions with supplied anchors

An invertible same-position slice reduces a left-right transport system to normalized conjugacy. With both changes constrained to a character kernel, the anchors must have the same character, and normalized conjugacy must admit a transporter in that kernel. A separate congruence theorem keeps one anchor congruence equation and replaces the remaining constraints by simultaneous conjugacy. The character-coset results describe exact feasibility relative to a known transporter using the simultaneous centralizer. A ring-level version allows singular slices outside the anchor. The broader context is the equivalence of systems of labelled matrices and systems of tensors under structured changes of basis. [Futorny, Grochow and Sergeichuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219)

A family of diagonal anchors gives an explicit coordinate support test. A transporter can move an entry between two indices only when their entire diagonal label vectors agree. The diagonal-conjugacy theorem then constructs all transporters in the jointly separating case from nonzero scalar gauges. A trace certificate adds a test for mismatched sizes of labelled blocks, provided the difference survives in the field. The certificate conclusion has this characteristic dependence.

## Polynomial Calculus identities

The ring identities express reverse transport, commutator transport, a scalar residual and a one-sided inverse defect as explicit combinations of residuals. When instantiated with constant input matrices and variables for the transporter and inverse, these give bounded-degree steps in the ordinary Polynomial Calculus presentation. The cubic reverse identity already appears in prior work on matrix proof complexity. It is given as the formal algebraic identity underlying a reusable calculation. The formal statements quantify over rings, and carry no general lower-bound claim. [Grochow and colleagues, §7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

## Reconstruction from linear probes or observations

Given a surjective map from a common probe space, all linear relations must survive to the target for a unique transport map to exist. Comparing two reachable realizations upgrades containment of probe kernels to equality and produces an equivalence. The dual criterion compares the ranges of two injective observation maps. For labelled operators lifted from a common probe space or observed in one ambient system, the recovered equivalences also intertwine every operator. Surjectivity together with injective observations yields a complete invariant by composing the observation with the probe. All results are stated over an arbitrary scalar ring. This extends the technique used in the earlier cyclic word basis result to externally given probes and observations without requiring either one to be a basis. The reachability and observability principle is classical. [Kalman](https://www.control.utoronto.ca/~broucke/ece557f/kalman.pdf)
