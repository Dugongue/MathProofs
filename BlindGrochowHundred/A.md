# Cycle periods, joint extension observations, and weighted decks

The statements work with finite cycle profiles, including Frobenius acting on the geometric points of a finite étale algebra. The arithmetic kernel is the elementary period formula $P_k(d)=d/\gcd(d,k)$ for a length $d>0$ and exponent $k$. Existing finite étale trace-tensor orbit recovery identifies these profiles with the corresponding tensor orbits over the chosen finite field. [Finite étale schemes and Galois sets](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04JI)

The two strongest conclusions combine arithmetic and tomography. If the images of an original cycle under power tests $k,\ell$ can be paired, then their least common multiple is $P_{\gcd(k,\ell)}(d)$. In particular a joint deck of paired lengths from coprime tests recovers every original cycle weight. Separate extension decks give marginals of the joint deck. They can coincide for two different profiles, as in the quadratic-cubic collision in dimension 12. For an isolated rectangle of four possible lengths, a collision is an alternating signed circulation of a single integer mass. Each edge-length divisor divides that mass, so its positive total is at least twice the least common multiple of the four lengths. The alternating circulation using precisely that least common multiple achieves the bound. The Lean results separately verify the general weighted rectangle witness and the exact lower bound in terms of a common divisor of the circulating mass.

Several related statements supply distinct recovery information.

- Power schedules compose by multiplication, preserve least common multiples of orbit periods, and factor through the divisors of a known common period bound. The fiber of the powered-period map has an explicit coprimality description. A fixed-point count at the full mass certifies that all occupied positive lengths divide the tested time. A partial count bounds the mass of every cycle type that the time misses.
- Weighted decks conserve total mass and compose under coarsening. Multiple decks recover signed weights if every potential disagreement set has a singleton fiber in an available test. This condition can use a specified set of retained tests after declared erasures.
- Two marginals recover the entries of a rank-one table over a domain when its known total mass is nonzero. With a fixed second factor, one marginal along that factor recovers the first as soon as the second factor has nonzero total.
