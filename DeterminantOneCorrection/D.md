# Supplied determinant-one correction over finite local rings

Let $R$ be a fixed finite commutative local ring with $R^\times\ne\{1\}$. Fix any nontrivial subgroup $\Gamma\le R^\times$. Ordinary three-tensor isomorphism **over $R$ itself** reduces in polynomial time to determinant-one correction on free unital associative $R$-algebras with center precisely $R$. Each output contains an explicit ordinary algebra isomorphism of the two multiplication tables. A successful correction yields an original ordinary transporter in polynomial time. The same construction reduces the decision to distinguishing $|R^\times|$ from $|R^\times|/|\Gamma|$ determinant-one orbits within one ordinary orbit. The two cases realize exactly $\{1\}$ and $\Gamma$ as automorphism determinant images.

This includes local rings of characteristic two with residue field $\mathbb F_2$, for example $\mathbb F_2[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$. The prescribed determinant in that example is the principal unit $1+\epsilon$. The construction uses the actual ring multiplication on input tensors. For $R=\mathbb F_2$, the supplied-witness decision always answers yes, since every invertible matrix already has determinant one. This is the only finite commutative local ring with trivial unit group.

## The determinant ratio over a ring

Suppose $(X,Y,Z)$ is an ordinary isotopy between two multiplication tables, with $Z(xy)=X(x)Y(y)$. Set $a=X(1)$ and $b=Y(1)$. The formulas $Z(x)=X(x)b=aY(x)$ show that left multiplication by $a$ and right multiplication by $b$ are bijective. They give inverses for $a,b$. For example if $ac=1$, then $a(ca-1)=0$ and injectivity gives $ca=1$. The map $\sigma=a^{-1}Z(-)b^{-1}$ is a unital algebra isomorphism, with
$$
(X,Y,Z)=(L_a\sigma,R_b\sigma,L_aR_b\sigma),
\qquad
\det\sigma=\det X\det Y/\det Z.
$$
The converse follows by substitution. Thus two true multiplication tables admit a three-leg determinant-one transporter exactly when their underlying algebras have a determinant-one isomorphism. On the stabilizer, the determinants of the two units and of the algebra automorphism independently give the three quotient coordinates. When the two unit images are full, the number of determinant-one orbits inside the ordinary orbit is $[R^\times:\det\operatorname{Aut}_R(A)]$. Indeed the three determinant maps from the free-module general linear groups are surjective, and each orbit of their kernel corresponds to a coset of the stabilizer image. ## Two algebra constructions over the same ring

Interpret a three-tensor $T$ as a bilinear map $T:U\times V\to W$ on free modules of positive ranks. Form $S(T)$ with three ordered diagonal copies of $R$ and Peirce spaces $U$ in position $(1,2)$, $V$ in $(2,3)$, and $W$ in $(1,3)$. The only product of two strictly upper blocks is $uv=T(u,v)$. The diagonal acts in the usual way. Associativity follows from bilinearity and the fact that every product of three strictly upper blocks is zero. Projection onto the first diagonal entry is an augmentation $\alpha:S(T)\to R$.

The radical is the strictly upper ideal plus the diagonal maximal ideals. This ideal is nilpotent, as its reduction modulo the maximal ideal of $R$ is strictly upper triangular. The semisimple quotient is three copies of the residue field. An isomorphism permutes these blocks. One may align the lifted block idempotents by conjugating by $u=\sum_i f_i e_{\pi(i)}$, where the $f_i$ are the transported idempotents and $e_{\pi(i)}$ their designated targets modulo the radical. Then $u\equiv1$ modulo the radical and $u e_{\pi(i)}=f_i u$. The nonzero directed Peirce corners recover the order of all three positions. Thus an algebra isomorphism gives invertible maps of $U,V,W$ carrying the bilinear products to each other. Conversely any such triple gives an algebra isomorphism and preserves the augmentation. This proves $S(T)\cong_R S(T')$ exactly when $T$ and $T'$ are ordinarily isomorphic over $R$.

Write $B=M_s(A)$ for a finite free $R$-algebra $A$. Each automorphism of $B$ is an inner conjugation followed by an entrywise automorphism of $A$. To see this, view $B$ as $\operatorname{End}_A(A^s)$ for the free right module. A transported system of matrix units splits $A^s$ into $s$ isomorphic summands $P$. By Krull–Schmidt for finite-length modules, $P^s\cong A^s$ gives $P\cong A$, so a basis change aligns the two matrix-unit systems. Restriction to the first corner now gives the claimed entrywise map. The same argument in the target module gives $M_s(A)\cong_R M_s(A')$ only if $A\cong_R A'$. Left and right multiplication in $M_s(A)$ repeat on its $s$ columns and rows respectively, so their determinants are $s$-th powers in $R^\times$. The determinant of an entrywise automorphism is an $s^2$-th power. In particular, when $s=|R^\times|$, every automorphism of $B$ has determinant 1. These arguments use R-linear bases and determinant identities over $R$.

## A translation whose determinant detects ordinary isomorphism

Take $s=|R^\times|$ and choose $\rho\in\Gamma\setminus\{1\}$. Both are constants for the fixed ring. Use a new algebra $E$ with a scalar source and sink, and a free rank-two space $H=R^2$ from source to sink. There are two kinds of intermediate blocks.

- For each $a\in\Gamma$, take $B_a=M_s(S(T_a))$, where $T_1=T$ and all other $T_a=T'$. Its based arrow spaces from the source and to the sink are a row $R^{1\times s}$ and a column $R^{s\times1}$. The middle algebra acts through its augmented matrix quotient $M_s(R)$, via $\alpha$. The row-column product is their ordinary scalar pairing times $w_a=(a,1)$ in $H$.
- Add $M_{2s}(R)$ and $M_{4s-2}(R)$ as two marked intermediate blocks, with the same row-column construction in their corresponding widths. Their pairing images in $H$ are the lines $R(1,0)$ and $R(0,1)$ respectively.

All other strictly upper outer-block products vanish. The pairing maps are balanced over the indicated middle rings, so the products are associative, including when the middle algebra is internally triangular. The algebra is free over $R$. The center is precisely the diagonal scalar $R$. Indeed a central element is outer-block diagonal, is central in each diagonal algebra, and agrees with the source scalar by acting on the rows. Each center of $S(T_i)$ is precisely $R$, since commuting with its three diagonal idempotents makes a central element diagonal and the nonzero free $U,V$ force equality of its three entries. This also proves the center assertion for its matrix algebra and gives faithfulness on central scalars.

Let $\phi$ be an automorphism of $E$. Refine the intermediate copies of $M_s(S(T_i))$ into their three internal diagonal matrix blocks. The full architecture is strictly upper triangular off its refined diagonal, and its diagonal entries are matrix algebras over $R$. The radical is the strictly upper ideal plus the diagonal maximal-ideal matrices. Thus the refined diagonal idempotents correspond to the split matrix factors of the semisimple quotient. Align the images of the idempotents as before using $u=\sum_i f_i e_{\pi(i)}$. In this case the diagonal entries of $u$ are exactly the identity matrices: an idempotent matrix congruent to 0 or 1 modulo the diagonal radical is equal to 0 or 1. Hence $u$ differs from 1 only in strictly upper blocks. Left multiplication by $u$ and right multiplication by $u^{-1}$ are triangular with diagonal identity on free Peirce blocks, by ordering respectively on the starting and ending vertices. This gives $\det\operatorname{Ad}_u=1$ **over $R$**.

After alignment, the scalar source and sink are the only width-one blocks and their directed nonzero corner distinguishes them. Widths distinguish the two marked blocks. On the width-$s$ idempotents alone, the undirected support of the nonzero Peirce corners has connected components precisely the three-vertex payloads. The two corners $U,V$ connect their triples. Therefore $\phi$ permutes entire payload blocks, including their associated row and column modules. Its invertible action on $H$ preserves the marked axes, so has matrix $\operatorname{diag}(x,y)$ with $x,y$ units. Preservation of the row-column products implies
$$
\operatorname{diag}(x,y)R(a,1)=R(\pi(a),1),
\qquad x/y=\pi(a)/a.
$$
These are identities over $R$. Equality of two such free lines follows by comparing the second coordinates of their displayed generators. In particular the lines remain pairwise distinct even when all their reductions modulo the maximal ideal coincide. Since the label $1$ must be sent to a label in $\Gamma$, every permutation of the positions is a common multiplication by an element of $\Gamma$.

If $T\not\cong T'$, matrix cancellation and the recovery of $T$ from $S(T)$ make position $1$ the unique payload of its isomorphism type. The permutation must be the identity, and $x=y=t$. The aligned automorphism contributes determinant 1 on each $B_a$, by matrix amplification, and on each marked diagonal matrix algebra, whose automorphisms are inner by the same argument with $A=R$. The latter determinant is 1 because left multiplication by a matrix on its columns and right multiplication by its inverse on its rows cancel. On each row-column pair of width $d$, the product of the two determinants is $t^d$. This follows by taking determinants of the equation expressing preservation of the scalar pairing with multiplier $t$. Thus the payloads contribute $t^s=1$ per pair. The remaining factors, from $H$ and the two marked pairs, contribute $t^{2+2s+(4s-2)}=t^{6s}=1$. Every automorphism has determinant 1.

If $T\cong T'$, choose augmentation-preserving identifications of all payload algebras with a single model. For any $h\in\Gamma$, send each payload $a$ to $ha$ using those identifications, and permute their row and column spaces in the standard bases. On $H$, use $\operatorname{diag}(h,1)$. On the marked pair at $(1,0)$ scale its row space by $h$, and on the other marked pair act as the identity. The products are preserved by construction. The determinants of the identifications cancel around each cycle. The permutation of the equally sized payloads and their row-column blocks contributes sign 1. If $s$ is odd, every element of $\Gamma$ has odd order, so its permutation cycles have odd length. If $s$ is even, the payload rank $s^2\operatorname{rank}_R S(T)$ and both arrow ranks $s$ are even. On the marked pair and $H$ the determinant is $h^{2s+1}=h$. The kernel of the action on the labelled lines has determinant 1 by the preceding block calculation, regardless of whether the payloads are isomorphic. Thus the determinant of any automorphism is its translation multiplier in $\Gamma$, and the exact image in the yes case is $\Gamma$.

## The promised decision and the exact orbit count

In the displayed basis of $E$, let $P$ scale the first coordinate of $H$ by $\rho^{-1}$ and fix every other basis vector. This fixes the identity of $E$, so the two products have the same unit vector. Output $\mu$ and $\mu'(x,y)=P\mu(P^{-1}x,P^{-1}y)$, and give the ordinary transporter $(P,P,P)$. The algebra isomorphisms between the two tables are $P\sigma$ for $\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_R(E)$. By the determinant ratio, a determinant-one transporter exists exactly when $\rho$ occurs as an automorphism determinant, which is exactly $T\cong T'$. For the orbit count, a scalar unit in the sink diagonal alone has its arbitrary scalar as left multiplication determinant. A scalar unit at the source works for right multiplication. The two unit determinant images are therefore full, so the claimed counts follow from the exact automorphism images.

To extract a witness, normalize a successful isotopy by the determinant ratio and compose with $P^{-1}$. The resulting automorphism of $E$ has determinant $\rho$, so it maps payload $1$ to payload $\rho$. Align its block idempotents by the displayed conjugator. In each of the three diagonal matrix blocks of payload $\rho$, conjugate the transported matrix units $F_{ab}$ to standard units. This is constructive over the local ring. Choose a column $v$ of $F_{11}$ that is nonzero modulo the maximal ideal, then the columns $F_{a1}v$ form a basis modulo that ideal and hence over $R$. This basis makes the transported units standard. After alignment restrict the three upper Peirce maps to the $(1,1)$ matrix slots, cut out by the now-fixed first matrix idempotents at the two ends of each slot. In these coordinates the products are precisely $T$ and $T'$. The restrictions give the desired three invertible maps. Conversely an ordinary input witness produces the translation by $\rho$, and composing with $P$ supplies a determinant-one witness.

For a fixed ring all amplification widths and the number of payloads are constants. Given two tensors of the same positive shape, the resulting free rank is linear in the sum of their three side lengths. Writing the full multiplication table and the diagonal change of basis takes polynomial time. Different input shapes can be mapped to the output of this construction on the scalar input tensors $T(u,v)=0$ and $T'(u,v)=uv$.
