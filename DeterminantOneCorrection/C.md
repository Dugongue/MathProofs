# Marked multiplication tensors realize binary codes and counting hardness

Let $A=\prod_{i=1}^f K_i$ be a finite étale algebra over $k=\mathbb F_q$, with $d_i=[K_i:k]$. Fix a labeled family of nonzero ideals $I_1,\ldots,I_r$, allowing overlaps, and write $M_{ji}=1$ if the factor $K_i$ belongs to $I_j$. Each ideal is a direct product of a subset of field factors. On each of the three copies of $I_j$ in the multiplication tensor choose a volume form, meaning a nonzero top exterior form. A transport of two such volume assignments consists of three linear bijections preserving the multiplication tensor and every $I_j$ on its leg, and pulling each target volume form back to its prescribed source form.

Put $m=q-1$. Regard a column of $M$ as both a vector over $\mathbb Z/m$ and a binary vector. Define $H=\operatorname{im}(M:(\mathbb Z/m)^f\to(\mathbb Z/m)^r)$. For odd $q$, let $C\subseteq\mathbb F_2^r$ be the span of the incidence columns belonging to the following factor groups.

- A group of even degree contributes its column.
- A group of odd degree contributes its column if there are at least two factors of that degree with that same incidence column.

Here a group means a class of factors with equal degree and equal incidence. For even $q$, take the sign subgroup to be trivial.

**Theorem.** The complete invariant of the volume assignments is the determinant-ratio class in $(k^\times)^r$ modulo the image of $C$ under $c\mapsto((-1)^{c_j})_j$, together with two classes in $(\mathbb Z/m)^r/H$. In particular, the number of equivalence classes is
$$
[ (k^\times)^r : (-1)^C ]\,[(\mathbb Z/m)^r : H]^2
=\frac{(q-1)^{3r}}{2^{\dim C}|H|^2}
$$
for odd $q$, with the sign factor omitted for even $q$. Every binary linear code is realized as $C$ by a split algebra of dimension $r+2\dim C$, with $H$ surjective. Over every fixed odd finite field, counting the subsets of at most $t$ output volumes whose signs must be reversed to reach equivalence is $\#\mathrm P$-complete under polynomial-time counting reductions, even on these split examples. The reduction from the permanent preserves the exact number of solutions.

## The two images of the stabilizer

A stabilizer of the multiplication map satisfies $Z(xy)=X(x)Y(y)$. Evaluating at 1 on both sides gives $Z(x)=X(x)Y(1)=X(1)Y(x)$. Since the three maps are invertible, left multiplication by $X(1)$ and right multiplication by $Y(1)$ are bijective, hence these elements are units. Set $a=X(1)$, $b=Y(1)$, and $\sigma(x)=a^{-1}Z(x)b^{-1}$. Substitution shows that $\sigma$ is an algebra automorphism. Conversely, any choice of $a,b,\sigma$ gives
$$
X=L_a\sigma,\qquad Y=R_b\sigma,\qquad Z=L_aR_b\sigma .
$$
Multiplication by a unit preserves every ideal and thus these three maps preserve each mark exactly when $\sigma$ does.

Let $\ell,r_0,\delta\in(k^\times)^r$ be the componentwise restrictions of the determinants of $L_a,R_b,\sigma$ to the marked ideals. The three determinant vectors are $(\ell\delta,r_0\delta,\ell r_0\delta)$. The invertible coordinate change
$$
(x,y,z)\longmapsto(xy/z,\ z/y,\ z/x)
$$
takes this triple to $(\delta,\ell,r_0)$. Every set of volume choices is a torsor for $(k^\times)^{3r}$, since the volume lines are independent decorations, including on overlapping ideals. Taking the quotient by the transported determinant triples therefore gives the claimed cosets.

On a factor $K_i$, multiplication by $a_i\ne0$ has determinant $\operatorname{Norm}_{K_i/k}(a_i)$. The norm is surjective for finite fields by cyclicity of their multiplicative groups. On $I_j$ one multiplies those norms over the factors it contains. Consequently the images of the two unit determinants both identify with $H$ after choosing a generator of $k^\times$. The two images vary independently, since $a,b$ can be chosen independently. If the integer Smith form of $M$ has nonzero diagonal $s_1,\ldots,s_\ell$, the unit index is $m^{r-\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\gcd(m,s_i)$.

An algebra automorphism can interchange factors exactly when both the field degree and the incidence column agree. It can also apply any field automorphism to one factor. Over an algebraic closure a Frobenius rotation on a degree-$d$ factor acts as a $d$-cycle on the geometric primitive idempotents, hence contributes sign $(-1)^{d-1}$ on each ideal containing that factor. A swap of two identical-profile factors, using some isomorphism and its inverse, acts as $d$ transpositions and contributes $(-1)^d$ on each ideal containing them. All mark-preserving automorphisms are generated by these rotations and swaps. This gives precisely the sign image stated above. For the single mark $I_1=A$, the two unit indices equal one and the sign image is the original $q-1$ versus $(q-1)/2$ case.

## Arbitrary codes via split factors

Take a basis $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ of a prescribed code $C\subseteq\mathbb F_2^r$. For each basis vector put two factors $k$ into exactly the ideals indicated by that vector. Also put one additional factor $k$ into just $I_j$ for each $j$. Equivalently, the incidence matrix has two copies of every column $g_i$, plus the standard coordinate columns. All ideals are nonzero and the unit image is full. The repeated columns span exactly $C$, including when a basis column also appears as a coordinate column. All factors have degree one so the sign formula says that this is the required code. The tensor is coordinatewise multiplication in a space of dimension $r+2s$.

The norm index can also be nontrivial for a split algebra with trivial sign image. If three marks contain all but one of three distinct factors, the incidence matrix is the $3$ by $3$ all-ones matrix minus the identity. Its Smith diagonal is $1,1,2$, so over an odd finite field the index is 2 in each of the two norm coordinates. This produces $4(q-1)^3$ classes. More generally the $d+1$ square all-ones-minus-identity block has Smith diagonal $1,\ldots,1,d$. One proof uses its presentation by generators $u_i$ with relations $\sum_{j\ne i}u_j=0$. Subtracting two relations makes all $u_i$ equal, after which a single relation says $d u_1=0$. Disjoint such blocks realize any finite abelian norm quotient of exponent dividing $q-1$. All incidence columns are distinct, so the sign image stays trivial.

## Counting repairs is the permanent

Work over any fixed odd finite field and restrict the edits to signs. The elements $1$ and $-1$ give two distinct choices in each marked volume line, including over $\mathbb F_3$. Use the split construction with full $H$ and let the two assignments have identical reference volumes except for a prescribed output sign vector $b$. Negating a subset of output volumes encoded by $e\in\mathbb F_2^r$ succeeds exactly when $e+b\in C$, by the complete invariant. Each subset is an edit of labeled volume forms, counted once regardless of the number of transports implementing it. Gaussian elimination checks this condition in polynomial time, so guessing $e$ with weight at most $t$ gives a $\#\mathrm P$ verifier. The input gives the coordinate factors and their marked subsets, so constructing the sign code requires only grouping identical columns. Even the decision variant with a weight bound includes binary syndrome decoding. [Berlekamp–McEliece–van Tilborg](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/aw9vs-ann16)

For hardness take a bipartite graph on $t$ vertices on each side, for which the permanent of its zero-one biadjacency matrix is the number of perfect matchings. Use one edit coordinate per edge. Let $B$ denote the binary vertex-edge incidence matrix, and set $C=\ker B$. Find $b$ with $Bb=\boldsymbol 1$ by Gaussian elimination. If none exists, there is no perfect matching, so output a fixed instance with zero repairs. Otherwise compute a basis of $C$, realize it by split factors as above, and impose a budget of $t$ volume changes.

The successful repairs are the $e$ with $Be=\boldsymbol 1$ and weight at most $t$. Any such subset of edges has positive odd degree at every vertex. Its total degree is at most $2t$, so all $2t$ degrees equal one. Conversely every perfect matching satisfies the constraints. Thus edits biject perfect matchings and the construction preserves the count. The split algebra has dimension at most three times the number of input edges, and the marked ideals and tensor entries can all be written in polynomial time. Valiant's hardness of the zero-one permanent gives the counting result. [Valiant](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~billey/colombia/references/valiant.permanent.1979pdf.pdf)

## Verification scope

The orbit classification, the realization theorem and the reductions have written proofs here. The Lean companion proves the exact budget-and-parity criterion for a perfect matching, stated for an arbitrary finite edge set in two equally sized vertex banks. The finite control directly enumerates marked factor permutations and unit choices for small split algebras, and independently compares syndrome edits with perfect matchings. It checked seven sign codes, six norm defects, 530 bipartite graphs, and 19,767 edit vectors. The coding decision and permanent hardness inputs are classical.
