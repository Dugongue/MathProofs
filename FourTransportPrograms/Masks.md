# Error-resilient masks for diagonal tensor calibration

Given a known tensor support and a candidate alignment of its modes, the remaining multiplicative calibration problem over a finite field has an exact measurement design and decoding theory. Prime-power torsion affects even noiseless identifiability. With one or two distinct primes in the field's multiplicative-group order, weighted matroid intersection gives both an optimal noiseless mask and a least-cost mask satisfying a constructive resilience guarantee. In contrast, finding the truly least-size mask resilient to even one erasure is NP-complete on matrix supports. Those same matrix supports admit polynomial-time decoding up to the full unique recovery radius.

## 1. The measurement model

Let $P$ be the explicit list of nonzero entries of a reference $d$-tensor $T$ over $\mathbb F_q$, where $d\geq2$. Fix a candidate coordinate alignment between the reference and a second array. Each observation at an entry $e=(i_1,\ldots,i_d)\in P$ is a ratio

$$
y_e=\frac{U_e}{T_e}=\prod_{j=1}^d g_{j,i_j}
$$

in the model, with unknown nonzero gains $g_{j,i}\in\mathbb F_q^\times$. Errors are arbitrary changes to individual observations. Erasures are flagged missing observations. The aim is to identify the ratio on all of $P$. Different gain tuples can yield the same full ratio and are allowed as alternative calibrations. The support and the coordinate alignment are specified in this theorem. For monomial isomorphism, one can apply the test to each candidate supplied by a separate support or coordinate recovery method.

The incidence row $v_e$ has one $1$ in each mode, at the coordinate of $e$, and zeros elsewhere. Write $M_A$ for these rows indexed by $A\subseteq P$. Set $m=q-1$. Under a choice of generator of $\mathbb F_q^\times$, feasible full ratio exponents are the code

$$
C_P=\{M_Px:x\in(\mathbb Z/m)^N\},
$$

where $N$ is the total number of used mode coordinates. The generator is used to prove the statements. The observation and decoding algorithms operate on field elements and integer powers and require no discrete logarithm. For $q=2$ or $P=\varnothing$ there is only one feasible ratio word, so assume $q>2$ and $P\ne\varnothing$ below. The matrix and Smith viewpoint has a close precedent in algebraic rank-one completion. The weighted resilient design and the distinction between exact design and decoding concern the present observation model. [Kahle, Kubjas, Kummer and Rosen, *The geometry of rank-one tensor completion*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01678)

## 2. A prime-power mask theorem

For each prime power $p^a\parallel m$, let $R_p=\mathbb Z/p^a$ and let $L_p\subseteq R_p^N$ be the row module generated by the full set of rows of $M_P$. Let $\mathcal M_p$ be the linear matroid on $P$ represented by the row classes in the $\mathbb F_p$-space $L_p/pL_p$.

**Theorem 1.** Observations on $E\subseteq P$ identify the full ratio on $P$ for every feasible data vector if and only if $E$ spans $\mathcal M_p$ for every $p\mid m$.

If $E$ spans $\mathcal M_p$, the span over $R_p$ of its rows, say $B_p$, satisfies $B_p+pL_p=L_p$. Substituting this identity repeatedly gives $B_p+p^aL_p=L_p$, hence $B_p=L_p$. Every desired row is therefore a linear combination of rows of $E$ over each $R_p$. The Chinese remainder theorem combines the identifying maps over the prime powers of $m$.

Conversely, when $E$ fails to span some $\mathcal M_p$, choose an $\mathbb F_p$-linear functional on $L_p/pL_p$ vanishing on all rows of $E$ and nonzero on at least one row of $P$. Composing with the quotient and with $\mathbb F_p\cong p^{a-1}R_p$ gives an $R_p$-linear functional on $L_p$. It extends to $R_p^N$. To see extension, use Smith coordinates so that $L_p$ is a direct sum of the coordinate submodules $p^{b_i}R_p$ for $0\leq b_i<a$. A linear functional on each summand has value on $p^{b_i}$ annihilated by $p^{a-b_i}$, so that value is $p^{b_i}$ times a scalar. Pairing with the vector of those scalars extends the functional. Use zero exponents in the other prime-power factors and combine by the Chinese remainder theorem. This produces a nontrivial full ratio agreeing with the identity on $E$.

Ambient row reduction modulo $p$ can give the wrong answer. For $q=5$, take $P=\{000,011,101,110\}$ and $E=P\setminus\{110\}$. The missing row is the sum of the three observed rows modulo $2$ in the ambient space. Nevertheless the diagonal gains $g_{1,0}=g_{2,0}=g_{3,0}=1$ and $(g_{1,1},g_{2,1},g_{3,1})=(2,2,3)$ yield the identity ratio on $E$ and ratio $4$ at $110$. The quotient $L_2/2L_2$ retains the class that ambient reduction loses.

To compute these matroids, diagonalize the full incidence matrix over each $R_p$ by choosing an entry of minimum $p$-valuation, scaling its unit part to $1$, and clearing its row and column. All coefficients to clear a pivot are obtained by exact division by its power of $p$ and unit inversion. Each pivot divides the remaining entries in its row and column. In the resulting column coordinates, the row module is the direct sum of its nonzero coordinate ideals. For an original row, divide each effective coordinate by its coordinate generator $p^{b_i}$ and reduce modulo $p$. This gives its vector in $L_p/pL_p$. The construction is polynomial in the incidence matrix size and bit lengths when the prime-power factorization of $q-1$ is given, including the fixed-field case.

When $q-1$ has at most two distinct prime divisors, the least-cost identifying mask containing an existing set $E_0$ can be computed in polynomial time. Contract $E_0$ in each matroid. A subset of the remaining entries spans a contracted matroid precisely when its complement is independent in the dual. Thus weighted matroid intersection maximizes the total omitted measurement cost subject to both spanning conditions. With one prime this is ordinary weighted matroid optimization. [Edmonds, *Matroid Intersection*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167506008708173)

## 3. Exact recovery radius and a constructible robust mask

Define

$$
D(E)=\min\{\operatorname{wt}(c|_E): c\in C_P,\ c\ne 0\}.
$$

If $E$ fails to identify, this value is zero. In all cases

$$
D(E)=\min_{p\mid m}\min\bigl\{|J|:J\subseteq E,\quad
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal M_p}(E\setminus J)
<\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal M_p}(P)\bigr\}.
$$

A nonzero full ratio exponent word can be hidden by deleting its observed support. Conversely, any deletion in the formula permits a nonzero word invisible on the remaining entries by Theorem 1. This proves the identity. In particular, for fixed $t,s$, the property $D(E)>2t+s$ can be tested by enumerating deletions of at most $2t+s$ entries and computing their matroid ranks.

**Theorem 2.** Uniform unique recovery with at most $t$ errors and $s$ erasures is possible exactly when $D(E)>2t+s$. After deleting the flagged positions, observed codeword distances drop by at most $s$, and radius-$t$ Hamming balls about distinct words are disjoint if the remaining distance exceeds $2t$. Conversely, take two words at distance $D(E)$ on $E$. Erase up to $s$ differences and partition the remaining at most $2t$ differences into two sets of size at most $t$. A common received word agreeing with each original word on one set is compatible with both.

Here is a sufficient condition that can be designed and decoded efficiently for nonconstant $t,s$. Set $k=2t+s+1$. Require that $E$ contain $k$ disjoint bases of $\mathcal M_p$ separately for each $p$. Collections for different primes may overlap. The $k$-fold union of a matroid has independent sets partitionable into $k$ independent sets of the original matroid. After checking that the full supply $P$ has union rank $k\,\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal M_p)$ for each prime, the requirement on $E$ is precisely spanning each of these union matroids. If $k>|P|$, reject the packing condition immediately since the matroid ranks are positive. When there are at most two primes, contract any already measured entries in the union matroids and again optimize the omitted cost in their duals. The union algorithm also produces the partitions into disjoint bases. [Edmonds and Fulkerson, *Transversals and matroid partition*](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/69B/jresv69Bn3p147_A1b.pdf)

For decoding, use the Chinese remainder exponent that is $1\bmod p^a$ and $0$ modulo every other prime-power factor to project measured elements to the $p$-primary subgroup. Compute modular linear combinations expressing each desired row in each basis of $\mathcal M_p$, whose lifted rows span $L_p$ by Theorem 1. Applying these combinations to field elements means multiplying the measured ratios to the indicated integer powers. Discard bases hit by an erasure. There are at least $2t+1$ bases left, and at most $t$ contain an error. The majority of the reconstructed values is correct at every entry of $P$. Repeat per prime and multiply the primary ratios to get the full result. Invalid zero observations may be treated as corrupted symbols. This decoder uses modular linear algebra and exact field arithmetic.

Disjoint bases are a sufficient condition. The four entries of a $2\times2$ matrix have row-matroid rank $3$ over any prime in this model. Any three identify the fourth, so taking all four tolerates one erasure. Two disjoint bases would require six entries.

## 4. Matrix supports and Cartesian supports

For a matrix support, put its row and column coordinates on the two sides of a bipartite graph. Invert the column gains so ratio exponents are differences of vertex potentials. The exact value $D(E)$ is the minimum edge-cut size in the measured graph restricted to any nontrivial connected component of the full support graph. For a disconnected measured restriction, the cut size is zero. To prove this, a nonconstant potential on a full support component has a proper nonempty level set. Every measured edge across its cut has a nonzero difference. Conversely, a potential taking one value on each side of a minimum cut, with the values different, attains that cut's size.

If $D(E)>2t+s$, each measured component after erasures has at least $2t+1$ edge-disjoint paths between a chosen root and any other vertex. Find these paths by max flow. Multiply ratios or their inverses along each path to estimate the same potential ratio. At most $t$ paths use a corrupted edge, so majority gives the exact ratio. Applying this at every vertex reconstructs the full ratio on $P$. This algorithm reaches the full unique recovery radius on arbitrary matrix supports.

In contrast, deciding whether a connected bipartite support admits a one-erasure-resilient mask of at most its number of vertices is NP-complete. The min-cut rule makes the requirement a spanning graph with no bridge and minimum degree at least two. A graph with these properties and at most as many edges as vertices is a single spanning cycle by the degree sum and connectivity. For completeness, directed Hamiltonian cycle reduces to the bipartite case by replacing vertex $v$ by the length-three path from $v_{\mathrm{in}}$ through two private degree-two vertices to $v_{\mathrm{out}}$, and replacing arc $v\to u$ by edge $v_{\mathrm{out}}u_{\mathrm{in}}$. Every spanning cycle uses each full private path and traverses the gadgets in a consistent direction, so it yields a directed spanning cycle. Disconnected instances can be replaced by any fixed connected no-instance. Verification uses polynomial-time connectivity tests. [Karp, *Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems*](https://www2.seas.gwu.edu/~simhaweb/champalg/tsp/papers/KarpOriginal.pdf)

Deciding whether a feasible fit has at most a given number of mismatches is also NP-complete on matrix supports even at $q=3$. Given an unweighted simple Max-Cut instance $G$, make one column $w_e$ per edge $e=uv$. Prescribe sign exponents $0$ and $1$ at $(u,w_e)$ and $(v,w_e)$. The two observations can both be matched precisely when the left vertex signs differ. Otherwise one can be matched. Thus the optimal number of mismatches is $|E(G)|-\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)$. This applies to unrestricted corrupted data and leaves the bounded-radius path decoder intact. [Garey, Johnson and Stockmeyer, Simple Max Cut](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2876%2990059-1)

For a full Cartesian support $P=\prod_{j=1}^d[n_j]$, using all of $P$ gives

$$
D(P)=\frac{|P|}{\max_j n_j}.
$$

If a mode's potentials vary, every line along that mode has at least one nonidentity ratio, giving the lower bound. If all potentials are constant per mode, any nonidentity word is nonidentity everywhere. A gain change in a single coordinate of a largest mode attains the formula. There is again a polynomial-time decoder at the exact radius. Compare each fixed mode coordinate to its baseline coordinate over all choices of the other modes, obtaining $|P|/n_j$ ratios for that pair. For that comparison each corrupted or erased entry affects at most one pair. Take majorities after dropping erased pairs, giving the relative gain within each mode. Divide each surviving observation by the reconstructed relative gains and take a final majority for the single common scalar. The strict inequality $2t+s<D(P)$ justifies every majority.

## 5. Monomial tensor isomorphism and the next theorem

For an already proposed family of mode permutations, align the supports first. Exact diagonal feasibility on the measured ratios can be checked directly by Smith normal form of the integer incidence matrix $M_E$, without a factorization of $q-1$. If $UM_EV$ is diagonal with entries $s_i$, apply $U$ to the observed ratios by products of powers. A transformed ratio $z_i$ is in the image of the $s_i$-th power map exactly if $z_i^{m/\gcd(s_i,m)}=1$. A zero row requires $z_i=1$. Invertibility of $U,V$ over the integers justifies both directions upon reducing exponents modulo $m$. With a spanning or robustly decodable mask, the full ratio is determined and can be checked on held-out observations. Recovery of an explicit gain tuple may additionally require root finding. A raw tensor with unknown invertible basis changes still needs a coordinate recovery step before these diagonal and monomial tests apply. Monomial code equivalence belongs to the established network of reductions to 3-tensor isomorphism. [Grochow and Qiao, Part I, Appendix B](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full)

A useful next question is whether the least-cost identifying mask remains polynomial-time computable for three distinct primes in $q-1$ when the rows come from a three-way support. General three-matroid intersection is insufficient to decide this specialized complexity. A second target is a polynomial-time decoder for arbitrary three-way masks at their exact promised radius, or a hardness result that respects this promise. The Max-Cut reduction above concerns optimization outside a certified radius. Both targets are falsifiable without changing the observation model.

These proofs are informal.
