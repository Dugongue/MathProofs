# Mixed supports and missing-cell tensor canonization

## 1. Exact compression for named primal and dual occurrences

**Theorem 1.** Fix a finite signature of labelled tensors in words of named spaces and their duals, over an arbitrary field $K$. Fixed-arity function records and relation subspaces specified by bases are allowed. For each name $V_s$, let $P_s\subseteq V_s$ be the sum of the supports of the primal occurrences and let $Q_s\subseteq V_s^*$ be the sum of the supports of the dual occurrences. Put $p_s=\dim P_s$, $q_s=\dim Q_s$, and $e_s=\operatorname{rank}(Q_s\times P_s\to K)$. The isomorphism problem in a fixed original format is equivalent to isomorphism after replacing the $s$-th space by one of dimension $p_s+q_s-e_s$. This is the minimum dimension admitting injective realizations of the two prescribed supports and their pairing.

There is also an ordinary 3-TI reduction. Choose for each nonzero coefficient tensor $T_a$ a forest of at most three full binary trees partitioning its ordered positions. At a node $J$, let $r_{a,J}$ be the rank of its flattening across $J$ and the remaining positions. Write
$
B=\sum_s(p_s+q_s)+\sum_{a,J}r_{a,J}.
$
An isomorphism-invariant choice of forests gives target total side length $O_\sigma(1+B)$ in a fixed original format, where the constant depends on the signature. The calculation and reduction take polynomial time in the explicit input size. Field operations are counted in the arbitrary-field model.

### Pairing extension

Let $P\subseteq V$, $Q\subseteq V^*$, with corresponding $P'\subseteq V'$, $Q'\subseteq (V')^*$ and $\dim V=\dim V'$. Suppose that isomorphisms $f:P\to P'$ and $h:Q\to Q'$ obey $h(u)(f(v))=u(v)$. Write $Z=Q^\perp$, $Z'=(Q')^\perp$, and identify $V/Z=Q^*$, $V'/Z'=(Q')^*$ by evaluation. Then $f(P\cap Z)=P'\cap Z'$, and the desired quotient map is $k=(h^{-1})^*:Q^*\to(Q')^*$. Its restriction to the image of $P$ is the quotient map induced by $f$. Extend $f|_{P\cap Z}$ to an isomorphism $g_Z:Z\to Z'$. Choose a complement $P_0$ of $P\cap Z$ in $P$. Extend the lifts $P_0\to V$ and $f(P_0)\to V'$ to sections of the two quotient maps, matching the quotient bases using $k$. The direct sum of $g_Z$ and $k$ in these splittings is an ambient $g$ extending $f$, with $g^{-T}|_Q=h$. Every step is a linear solve or basis extension.

For the minimum realization, let $Q_0=\ker(Q\to P^*)$. In any realization in $W$, the copy of $Q_0$ annihilates the copy of $P$, forcing $\dim W\ge\dim P+\dim Q_0$. For equality take $W=P\oplus Q_0^*$. Embed $Q$ into $W^*=P^*\oplus Q_0$ by $u\mapsto(u|_P,\pi(u))$ using any projection $\pi:Q\to Q_0$. This is injective and realizes the required pairing. The extension lemma applied in the original and minimal ambient dimensions shows that every isomorphism between the support records extends in both realizations.

### Assembly with cut supports

Each labelled coefficient tensor canonically lies in the tensor product of its singleton supports. The same holds for its intrinsic supports $E_{a,J}$ at the chosen roots. For disjoint children $J_1,J_2$, one has $E_{a,J_1\cup J_2}\subseteq E_{a,J_1}\otimes E_{a,J_2}$. To see this, choose complements in the two child products. The tensor lies separately in the corresponding child-supported subspaces, and their intersection supports it in both. Flatten across $J_1\cup J_2$.

Keep $P_s,Q_s$ as separately named sorts and record their evaluation, with output in a pinned scalar line. Represent each singleton support by its inclusion in the corresponding common named support. For each binary node retain its inclusion in the tensor product of its two children. Record the tensor at the at-most-three roots. All inclusions and evaluations have order at most three when written as map tensors. A pinned scalar sort is obtained from its multiplication, which forces its automorphism to be the identity. Lower-order records are padded with that scalar. The original tensor is recovered by applying the recorded child and singleton inclusions to the root tensor. Forward isomorphisms restrict to all these spaces. Conversely, preservation of the inclusions gives equality on the original leaf payloads by induction, and preservation of evaluation gives the required ambient witnesses by the extension lemma.

For a relation $R\subseteq W$ with a supplied basis, use its injective coordinate map $J:K^{\dim R}\to W$, with a freely changing extra basis sort. The map tensor uses the dual of that extra sort. A transporter of the inclusions is equivalent to carrying the relation subspaces to one another, since the extra basis map is then uniquely determined. This construction also accepts zero relations and zero functions. A function is encoded by its coefficient tensor, with its domain factors dualized. Labelled scalar data can be compared directly or recorded on the pinned line.

Choose a minimum-rank-sum forest with fixed tie-breaking on the ordered position sets. Its type and node dimensions, as well as the dimensions of the common supports and the zero pattern, are invariants. In pairwise decision, different descriptors immediately give NO. In an objectwise reduction at fixed original format, the descriptor marking of the earlier fractional-order construction keeps different descriptors in disjoint target orbits, with $O_\sigma(1+B)$ overhead. The number of tensor-system components and sort occurrences is bounded by the signature, so the linear-length ordinary 3-TI compiler applies. This extends the independent-slot support forest to repeated primal and dual occurrences. ## 2. Canonization with missing cells over fixed finite fields

**Theorem 2.** Fix a finite field $k=\mathbb F_q$ and a finite signature of labelled tensor records in signed words of named spaces. Each named space is equipped with a specified endomorphism $A_s$ of squarefree characteristic polynomial. Isomorphisms must conjugate the $A_s$ and transport all the records. Decompose the cyclic algebra $k[A_s]$ into its irreducible field components. In a record, a *cell* is the tensor product of a chosen field component at each position. Identify each dual field component with its field through the trace pairing. Assume every record cell is either zero or a multiplicative unit of its tensor-product algebra. This promise is invariant and decidable in polynomial time. Under it there is a deterministic polynomial-time canonical form, with reconstructible witnesses. At fixed format and with $L$ explicit coefficients including the endomorphism data, these canonical strings give an objectwise ordinary 3-TI reduction of total side length $O_\sigma(1+L^{1/3})$.

The theorem also applies when $A_s$ are supplied by a specified polynomial-time equivariant construction from the ordinary tensor input. When they are independent input data, the theorem is about marked isomorphism. For the ordinary signature with one tensor, this extends the previous all-cells-unit promise. The same argument handles maps by dualizing their input positions. It does not turn an arbitrary unmarked relation basis into squarefree covariant data.

### Coordinates and the promise

If $f_s$ is the squarefree characteristic polynomial of $A_s$, its cyclic algebra is $K_s=k[X]/(f_s)=\prod_j K_{s,j}$. Factor the polynomial in a fixed sorted order. Squarefree factorization over fixed $q$ can use the $q$-Frobenius fixed subalgebra and split the factors by the finitely many possible values of each fixed element. Choose a cyclic generator by summing a nonzero vector in each irreducible primary subspace. When two objects have identical $f_s$, their intertwining maps in cyclic coordinates are precisely multiplication by $a_s\in K_s^\times$. This follows by applying the map to the cyclic generator. For a dual occurrence, the trace pairing identifies the inverse-transpose action with multiplication by $a_s^{-1}$. The traces are nondegenerate because the irreducible finite extensions are separable.

Represent every cell in the tensor product of its field bases. If its entry is $x$, test $x=0$ or solve $xy=1$ by linear algebra over $k$. The product algebra itself need not be a field. Every local multiplier is a unit, so the live-cell pattern and this promise are invariant. There are no more cells than input coordinates, and their summed dimensions equal the explicit record lengths.

### Frobenius normalization

Call a pair $(s,j)$ active if its field appears in any position of a live cell. For each active pair choose the first such record, cell and position in the fixed orders. Write $E=K_{s,j}$ and $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$ for the sign of that position. If its live cell is $x$, apply the field Frobenius $\sigma:b\mapsto b^q$ in that one position only and form
$
\delta=\sigma(x)x^{-1}.
$
This is a unit in the cell algebra. Expand in the fixed $E$-basis from the other positions and let $c\in E^\times$ be its first nonzero coefficient. If the original data change by local units, $\delta$ and $c$ are multiplied by $\sigma(a_{s,j}^{\epsilon})/a_{s,j}^{\epsilon}$. All other occurrences are fixed by this Frobenius, including additional appearances of the same field name. Thus the location of this coefficient and its norm to $k$ are invariant.

Use a deterministic section of the norm $E^\times\to k^\times$ to choose $c'$ with the same norm. Solve
$
\sigma(b)=(c'/c)^\epsilon b
$
for nonzero $b\in E$. This is a linear system over $k$. A nonzero solution exists by finite-field Hilbert 90: in the cyclic group $E^\times$, the image of $b\mapsto b^{q-1}$ is exactly the kernel of the norm. Apply the selected multipliers at all active pairs. They normalize their chosen coefficients simultaneously since the other factor copies cancel in the ratio. Inactive pairs may use 1. Any transformation between two normalized arrays must use a Frobenius-fixed multiplier at every active pair, hence $a_{s,j}\in k^\times$, and these scalars preserve the normalization.

For completeness, the norm section uses only polynomial-time operations for fixed $q$. For each prime $\ell\mid q-1$ and $e=[E:k]$, let $m_\ell=\ell^{v_\ell(q^e-1)}$. The elementary lifting-the-exponent formulas give $m_\ell=O_q(e)$. For odd $\ell$ the exponent is $v_\ell(q-1)+v_\ell(e)$. For $\ell=2$ and odd $q$, it is $v_2(q-1)$ for odd $e$ and $v_2(q-1)+v_2(q+1)+v_2(e)-1$ for even $e$. Factor $X^{m_\ell}-1$ over $E$ by splitting the $q$-Frobenius fixed algebra of $E[X]/(X^{m_\ell}-1)$, viewed as a $k$-algebra. This algebra is a product of $m_\ell$ copies of $E$, and its fixed algebra is $k^{m_\ell}$. A root of exact order $m_\ell$ is identified by its $m_\ell/\ell$ power and its norm generates the $\ell$-primary subgroup of $k^\times$. Enumerating a constant number of powers of those norms realizes any base-field target. No discrete logarithm in $E^\times$ is needed. ### Residual scalars and canonical output

Fix a generator of the fixed-size cyclic group $k^\times$. For each live cell take the logarithm, in $\mathbb Z/(q-1)$, of its first nonzero $k$-basis coefficient. Let $v$ be the resulting vector. Define the signed incidence matrix $M$ with rows the live cells and columns the active pairs. Its entry is the sum of the signs of the occurrences of that pair in the cell. The residual action is exactly
$
v\longmapsto v+M u,\qquad u\in(\mathbb Z/(q-1))^{\{\text{active pairs}\}}.
$
Choose the lexicographically least vector in this coset by fixing one coordinate at a time and testing congruence feasibility by Smith normal form. Equivalently, solve modulo each prime power dividing the fixed modulus. A unit pivot permits row elimination and back-substitution. If no entry is a unit, the right side must be divisible by the prime, and division reduces the power by one. Combine the resulting solutions by the Chinese remainder theorem. Each coordinate has only $q-1$ candidates, and a final solve returns a realizing $u$. When $q=2$ the residual group is trivial. Apply $u$ to the entire array. Any choices of realizing $u$ differ by an element of the kernel of $M$ and thus multiply each live cell by exactly 1. Accordingly the canonical array itself is uniquely determined. Equality of canonical arrays and characteristic polynomials yields a transporter by composing the explicitly applied invertible cyclic-coordinate and normalization changes. This proves completeness.

The canonical string comprises the characteristic polynomials and the canonical coefficient arrays, with their fixed format. Pack it into a cube of side $\lceil L^{1/3}\rceil$, allowing constant-factor padding. On each of these three named cube spaces keep a fixed nilpotent shift and its first cyclic vector. A linear map commuting with the shift and fixing that vector fixes its entire basis. Compiling this fixed-type tensor system gives ordinary tensors whose isomorphism is equality of the canonical strings, at linear overhead in the side length. This final packing is the earlier fractional-order packing. The new step is canonization on the stated missing-cell promise, including the residual signed incidence case. The argument leaves cells that are nonzero nonunits outside the promise.

The statements are written proofs. The companion exact checks cover pairing extensions, signed residual normalization, and a finite quadratic-extension instance with holes in the cell pattern.
