# Polynomial Calculus for higher-order records and the FGS route in good positive characteristic

## Results

**Higher-order records, every field.** Fix a finite mixed tensor signature. Use one source linear witness and its two-sided inverse per named space, and provide a separate witness and its two-sided inverse for each relation frame. There is a deterministic polynomial-size encoding into ordinary three-tensors over the same field for which the source and target isomorphism equations admit Polynomial Calculus simulations in both directions with degree overhead depending only on the signature. In the direction from target refutations back to source refutations, the size overhead is polynomial even without a fixed proof-degree cutoff. In the other direction the size overhead is polynomial at any fixed proof-degree cutoff.

**The original FGS route, good positive characteristic.** Use the exact FGS augmentation with rank weights \(r_j=2^{j-1}(\min(n,m)+1)\) in each of its three partition erasures. The characteristic-zero reverse simulation extends to every positive characteristic prime satisfying the two explicit trace conditions in Section 3 at each erasure of the given pair. In particular it holds when the characteristic is bigger than the endomorphism-algebra dimensions and diagram vertex dimensions calculated there. The simulation-degree bound for a fixed signature is independent of that prime and the input dimensions. This gives a sufficient condition for the exact FGS route. Unrestricted small characteristic remains open here.

Use ordinary coordinate PC, with only action equations and the stated inverse equations. Refutations concern solvability over an algebraic closure in this presentation. Section 2 addresses the variant with finite-field equations. Both results are written proofs.

## 1. The fixed product maps are definitions in PC

For a signed word \(w\), let \(A_w\) be the ordered product of the witness factors over its leaves, using \(g_i\) in a positive occurrence of name \(i\) and \(h_i^{\mathsf T}\) in a dual occurrence, where \(h_i\) is the supplied inverse of \(g_i\). Thus \(A_w\) means the Kronecker product, with one factor per occurrence. Put \(B_w\) equal to the ordered Kronecker product of the corresponding inverse factors. Every individual entry of either is a single monomial, of degree \(|w|\), even when a name repeats. For an empty word both values are one. Inverse identities for \(A_w,B_w\) are bounded-degree consequences of the source identities: distribute each factor product, then telescope to the product of identities. There are finitely many factors fixed by the signature.

Use the typed basis-explicit compiler. Pin the scalar line by multiplication. Add an evaluation map coupling each named space to a separately named dual sort. Add the canonical product map \(W_u\times W_v\to W_{uv}\) on every internal node of a fixed product tree. Include the input tensor, map, and relation-frame equations at the resulting low-order ports. All tensors or linear maps at this stage have order at most three after scalar padding. Each witness has an inverse variable. For relation frames the source \(S_i\) is an independent change on the relation-basis space and the equation is \(A_wJ_i=J_i'S_i\). This gives equality of the represented subspaces, with arbitrary frame changes. For maps use \(A_vF=F'A_u\); replacing it by the vectorized tensor action or vice versa only multiplies by already supplied inverses, at bounded degree.

From the source equations, substitute \(A_w,B_w\) for every auxiliary word witness and inverse. Set the scalar witnesses to one. The evaluation equations are inverse identities. Every product equation follows entrywise from associativity of the fixed ordering of the factor products. The auxiliary inverse equations follow as above. The data axioms become the source equations, up to the stated bounded-degree changes of conventions. Entry substitutions are coordinate, constant, or monomial.

For the other direction, the multiplication equation on the scalar line and its inverse imply the scalar witnesses are one. The evaluation equation \(g_i^{\mathsf T}g_{i^\vee}=I\) gives \(g_{i^\vee}=h_i^{\mathsf T}\), and similarly for the inverse. The matrix equation for an internal product gives
\[
g_{uv}=g_u\otimes g_v
\]
entrywise by evaluating on pairs of basis elements. The corresponding inverse identity follows by substituting \(g_u^{-1},g_v^{-1}\) and multiplying by the given inverse of \(g_{uv}\). Iterate through the fixed tree, expanding products and replacing one factor at a time. This derives the original equations with the root witnesses read as their typed reference coordinates, and with no new variables. This argument works when a name occurs several times. It also works for a relation of rank zero, for which there are no relation-frame entries. Discard components with zero-dimensional slots, comparing common formats first.

For precise complexity, let \(a\) bound the arity and let \(C\) bound the finitely many degrees of all image-axiom derivations. A monomial substitution of degree at most \(a\) turns a refutation of degree \(D\) into one of degree \(O(\max(aD,C))\): a PC variable-multiplication becomes multiplication by the factors of a monomial. It increases sparse proof size by at most a signature-dependent constant factor plus polynomial preprocessing for the explicitly listed bounded-degree axioms. The readout in the other direction uses reference coordinates and the bounded-degree derivations above. Matrix-coordinate expansions have polynomially many terms at a fixed degree. All these statements follow directly from linear combinations and multiplication by individual variables, including when intermediate entries are zero.

Compose with the all-characteristic order-at-most-three PC compiler of [Intrinsic Isomorphism Crossovers](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IntrinsicIsomorphismCrossovers/proof-transfer.md). That construction provides the size bound in the direction from encoded refutations to typed refutations using coordinate or constant substitutions, and the converse at fixed degree. Composing its bounds with the just-proved product substitutions gives the first result. This choice of ordinary target is part of the theorem. The exact FGS output is addressed separately below.

## 2. Field equations for the definitional stage

If both systems are over \(\mathbb F_q\) with field equations, the defining substitutions also transport these axioms. For a monomial \(m=x_1\cdots x_s\), allowing repeats,
\[
m^q-m=\sum_{j=1}^s
   \Big(\prod_{i<j}x_i^q\Big)(x_j^q-x_j)\Big(\prod_{i>j}x_i\Big).
\]
Thus the extra derivation degree is at most \(sq\); a constant substitution over \(\mathbb F_q\) satisfies the field equation directly. More generally for \(f=\sum_\lambda c_\lambda m_\lambda\) of degree \(s\), Frobenius gives \(f^q-f=\sum_\lambda c_\lambda(m_\lambda^q-m_\lambda)\), with the same bound and an explicit sparse derivation. This verifies the extra axioms for any of the bounded-degree polynomial readouts in the cited low-order simulations, over a fixed finite field or with the degree bound carrying this dependence on \(q\).

## 3. Exact FGS cancellation in good positive characteristic

Use the notation of the [characteristic-zero proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FourRoutePolynomialCalculus/FGS-CANCELLATION.md). In a single augmentation the two diagrams are \(\mathcal A=A\oplus\bigoplus_j N_j^{r_j}\) and \(\mathcal B=B\oplus\bigoplus_j N_j^{r_j}\). Take indecomposable decompositions over an algebraic closure. For each side let \(E=\operatorname{End}(\mathcal A)\) or \(E=\operatorname{End}(\mathcal B)\). Its semisimple quotient is a product of matrix algebras, one factor per indecomposable diagram type.

Here are two sufficient conditions on the prime \(p\).

1. In the split regular left \(E\)-module, the composition multiplicity of each simple module is nonzero modulo \(p\), for both diagrams.
2. For each marker isotypic sum on one side and each nonmatching isotypic sum on the other, some coordinate of their vertex dimension vectors distinguishes that pair modulo \(p\). Also, if the corresponding marker sums have unequal multiplicities, some vertex dimension distinguishes them modulo \(p\). Impose the first distinction in both directions.

For a directly computable dimensional cutoff, it suffices that \(p\) be larger than each of \(\dim E_{\mathcal A},\dim E_{\mathcal B}\) and all dimensions of diagram vertices on both sides. Indeed the simple composition multiplicities are positive and bounded by the dimension of the regular module. The integer marker-dimension distinctions follow from the original bands: with \(r=\min(n,m)+1\), a marker \(N_j\) has total multiplicity in \([2^{j-1}r,2^{j-1}r+r-1]\). A type contributed only by data has less than \(r\) total dimension in at least one matrix direction. The first row vertex dimension of the marker is its total multiplicity. Consequently all required unequal integer vertex dimensions remain unequal modulo a prime above the cutoff. Endomorphism dimensions are ranks of explicit linear systems over the original field, hence computable by elimination and unchanged under extension.

To see that these hypotheses suffice, vertexwise conjugation by the transporter and its witnessed inverse produces a constant-degree algebra isomorphism of the two endomorphism algebras in PC. On a split matrix factor of \(E/J(E)\), the regular trace pairing is the ordinary matrix trace pairing times the composition multiplicity of its simple module. This follows by summing traces on a composition series. The radical acts by zero on all composition factors, and the ordinary matrix trace pairing is nondegenerate over every field by testing matrix units. Condition 1 therefore identifies the Jacobson radical with the trace kernel in exactly the same bounded-degree manner as in characteristic zero. Conjugating the matrices of left multiplication preserves this trace form using the existing inverse equations. The resulting quotient algebra maps preserve the centers.

The coordinates of the center map on primitive central idempotents form an idempotent matrix with row and column collisions zero and all row and column sums one. These identities use the multiplicative equations and the inverse center map. Preserving vertex traces gives
\[
(d'_{b,v}-d_{a,v})q_{ba}=0
\]
for the coefficient \(q_{ba}\) from source type \(a\) to target type \(b\) at every vertex \(v\). Vertex traces of radical elements vanish in every characteristic because they are nilpotent matrices. Condition 2 excludes every wrong marker destination and detects the case of different marker multiplicities. Now send a projector onto one source marker copy across the transporter and compress to its recovered isotypic sum. The multiplicity matrix has trace one at the first row vertex, since one copy has row dimension one. Slices supported outside the marker block yield the product of each off-block coefficient with each entry of this matrix equal to zero. Summing the diagonal entries gives the linear off-block equation. This recovers the slice partition and its inverse with no extraction of roots from nilpotent equations.

The subsequent cancellation uses the vanishing of every composition from a marker through the residual diagram back to a marker and the explicit inverse \(1+E_0\) for a defect \(1-E_0\) with \(E_0^2=0\). These identities are characteristic-free. The cancellation and the scalar-anchor links from the exact FGS construction therefore apply unchanged. Marker splittings for the final readout can be chosen over the original field by the rank pairing \(\operatorname{Hom}(N_j,M)\times\operatorname{Hom}(M,N_j)\to k\). Every derivation of fixed degree over the algebraic closure descends: the degree-bounded PC closure is obtained by spans, intersections with degree truncations, and variable multiplications. These operations commute with extension of scalars. Compose the three stages and the fixed linked-block stage as in the characteristic-zero proof.

For primes that fail either sufficient condition, the argument makes no PC simulation claim. In particular, the ordinary trace on a matrix factor loses its coefficient when \(p\) divides that composition multiplicity. The general original-gadget question in small characteristic is distinct from the all-field alternative construction in Section 1.
