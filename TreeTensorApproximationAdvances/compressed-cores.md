# Improved approximation directly from compressed tree cores

Suppose the input tensor is specified by dense rational local cores on a tree. Give target rank caps on that same tree. There is a deterministic algorithm polynomial in the total binary length of the supplied cores and caps that achieves the rational paired-cut bound
$$
\|A-X\|_F^2
\le\left(m-1-\sum_{\mathrm{pairs}}\frac{1}{2(r+3)}\right)E
$$
for $m\ge3$, where $E$ is the optimum squared error at the original target caps and $r$ is the first target cap of each pair. It returns rational cores. The physical coefficient tensor never needs to be expanded. Source and target ranks and vertex degrees are unrestricted. This combines the paired-cut approximation with compressed cut access and exact tree descent. ## 1. Exact rank and norm information

Across an edge of supplied width $D_e$, write its implicit unfolding as $M=L R^{\mathsf T}$, where the rows of $L,R$ are the two sides' open-edge vectors on physical assignments. Neither matrix is enumerated. The two Gram matrices $L^{\mathsf T}L$ and $R^{\mathsf T}R$ are computed by summing the doubled core network on the corresponding side, with two edge indices free. A doubled local contraction pairs two copies at their common physical labels. The double-network contractions use only polynomially many rational operations in the explicit sizes of the original cores, even at vertices of unbounded degree, since both copies' bond tuples can be enumerated. A pair of sweeps supplies this information for all edges; the full contraction gives $\|A\|_F^2$.

The rank of $M$ equals the rank of $(L^{\mathsf T}L)(R^{\mathsf T}R)$. Multiplying $M$ on the right by $R$ leaves its image unchanged because $\operatorname{im}(R^{\mathsf T} R)=\operatorname{im}(R^{\mathsf T})$. Applying $L^{\mathsf T}$ is then injective on that image, which lies in $\operatorname{im}L$. Thus exact rational rank tests detect the zero-error case. If all cuts fit, use the exact compressed descent algorithm with constant coefficients. It obtains simultaneous minimum-width rational cores by spanning each side's space of assignment messages and restricting nested bases to independent evaluation coordinates. This also handles $A=0$.

In the other case some cut of source width $D_e$ violates its cap $r_e$. Let $h=r_e+1\le D_e$. Choose a common denominator $D$ for the entries of the full implicit tensor as the product of the local cores' common denominators. Its bit length is polynomial. A nonzero minor of order $h$ in the violated unfolding has absolute value at least $D^{-h}$. If $B\ge1$ is a rational upper bound for $\|A\|_F$, the minor has magnitude at most $B^{h-1}\sigma_h$ by the singular-value bound for minors. Therefore $E_0=D^{-2h}B^{-2(h-1)}$ is a valid positive lower bound on $E$. Both $E_0$ and $B$ have polynomial bit length in the compressed input. This substitutes a bond-width exponent for an ambient dimension exponent.

## 2. Orthogonal truncations without physical expansion

Process the prescribed postorder. A processed child has its subtree replaced in current coordinates by a free physical coordinate of dimension equal to the width just kept. That physical coordinate is attached to the parent by incorporating the child's compressed core matrix. When processing a vertex $v$, the remaining subtree at this cut is therefore just its current physical coordinate (the original label and the compressed child labels). The unfolding is $HG^{\mathsf T}$, where $H$ is the current local core flattened to put its parent bond on the right, and $G$ is the rest-of-tree assignment matrix on that bond. Its left Gram matrix is the explicitly computable rational matrix $H(G^{\mathsf T}G)H^{\mathsf T}$. Form it using the same doubled-network contraction.

Each retained basis can have width at most the source width of its cut. If a target cap is at least that width, an exact projection of that width can retain the whole current tensor across the cut. In a pair with such a first cut this gives first loss zero and second loss at most the true two-cut optimum $E_2$, because the first projection of any comparison does not increase the second cut rank. If the second cut is nonbinding in this sense, its exact greedy loss is zero. For a binding cap use precisely the paired-cut trials. Clamping the first cap to the local physical dimension is harmless, with the case of complete retention treated in the same way.

Run the rational Jacobi rotations on the explicit left Gram matrices, at accuracy $\nu\le\xi^2/M$ where $M$ bounds all local left dimensions and $\xi\le\min(1,E_0/[200m(R+3)B])$, with $R$ any upper bound on the target caps. The Gram-to-unfolding comparison and the conceptual dummy padding of the paired-cut argument apply even though the right side is implicit. In the nonbinding case a top projection of source width loses at most $6B\xi$ by comparing with an exact zero-loss projection, and the projected two-cut comparison bounds the next exact loss by $E_2$. An approximate second cut adds at most $6B\xi$. Thus the same total additive error budget $15mB\xi$ applies across the tree, preserving the stated half-margin. For each candidate pair its actual squared loss is the before-minus-after squared norm, computed by doubled-network contraction. The algorithm keeps the least-loss pair.

To commit a projection, contract the transpose of its chosen rational orthonormal basis with $H$, then absorb the result into the parent and save the basis as the output local core. Each current local core has at most as many coordinates as its original supplied core, because compressed child dimensions are at most the source widths and unprocessed bonds retain their widths. At the root the compressed state supplies the remaining core. Orthogonal decomposition identifies its error with the sum of the local losses just as for dense data.

## 3. Intermediate sizes

The prescribed number of rotations and their dyadic parameter lengths are polynomial in the explicit local dimensions and $\log(B^2/\nu)$, by the same Jacobi analysis. Between truncations any current core is a contraction of original cores and one chosen basis per already processed vertex in the corresponding partial subtree. A common denominator has bit length bounded by the sum of the original and chosen-basis denominator lengths. The numerator is a sum over index choices whose count has logarithm bounded by the sum of the logarithms of the edge and local dimensions used. Product factors each come from an original core or one chosen basis. The squared-norm environment contractions obey the same bound with two copies. This bounds intermediate bit lengths polynomially and avoids dependence on the number of ambient physical coordinates.

The effective-cut bound also holds for compressed inputs. Initial activity is known from the exact Gram-product rank tests, each inactive cut can be processed with zero exact loss, and the error comparison for intervening inactive cuts uses only contractions. Its rational budget is unchanged. In particular the approximation may depend on the number of originally violated caps instead of the total number of edges. The comparison theorem and its implementation are paper proofs. This is a rounding theorem for input cores on the target tree.
