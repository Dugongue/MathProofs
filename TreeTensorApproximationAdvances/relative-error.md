# Deterministic relative-error approximation at the prescribed bond caps

For a supplied network graph with $m$ physical vertices and positive bond caps $r_e$, set $\rho_v=\prod_{e\ni v}r_e$ and $\rho=\max_v\rho_v$. Let $\mathcal S$ be the tensors realized by real cores with these caps, and let $E$ be the infimum squared Frobenius error of a dense rational input to $\mathcal S$. For $0<\varepsilon\le1$ and $E>0$, a deterministic algorithm returns rational cores with those same caps and error at most $(1+\varepsilon)E$. Its running time is $f(m,\rho,1/\varepsilon)L^{O(1)}$, where $L$ is the binary input size. It also decides whether $E=0$ and, in that case, whether exact real cores exist. For trees the zero case always has exact rational output. The algorithm uses a per-mode compression to $O(m\rho_v/\varepsilon)$ dimensions, followed by a bounded-dimensional real algebraic computation on the original graph. The same argument applies to fixed CP rank, with $\rho$ equal to the prescribed rank and the same border-error qualification. [Earlier relative-error algorithms for tensor decompositions](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2024.79)

## 1. Compressing a single physical index

Work with the Euclidean closure $\overline{\mathcal S}$. It is stable under arbitrary linear maps in each physical mode, with padding and slicing into the corresponding models when dimensions change. Mode-$v$ ranks are bounded by $\rho_v$ on the closure, since those rank constraints are closed and the local core factors the mode unfolding through the product of its incident indices. The Frobenius distance to the closure is attained and equals the infimum distance to $\mathcal S$.

Use $C,Y$ for the input and a best point in the closure, unfolded at mode $v$. Every matrix whose rows lie in the row space of $Y$ is a left linear image of $Y$. It is therefore feasible in the closure. Minimizing over that row space gives $Y=CQ$, where $Q$ is its orthogonal projector, and $(C-Y)Y^{\mathsf T}=0$.

Let $P$ project onto the first $k$ left singular directions of $C$. The pad-and-slice interpretation identifies $P$ with reduction of the physical dimension. Using orthogonality of the residual to all left linear images of $Y$, and $\operatorname{rank}Q\le\rho_v$, gives
$$
\|C-PY\|_F^2=\|C-Y\|_F^2+\|(I-P)Y\|_F^2,
\qquad
\|(I-P)Y\|_F^2 \le \rho_v\,\sigma_{k+1}(C)^2 .
$$
For the second bound use $Y=CQ$ and bound the squared Frobenius norm of a matrix of rank at most $\rho_v$ by $\rho_v$ times the squared operator norm of $(I-P)C$. By the best rank-$\rho_v$ matrix bound, when $k\ge\rho_v$ one has $(k-\rho_v+1)\sigma_{k+1}(C)^2\le\|C-Y\|_F^2$. Thus the projection loss plus the new best squared error is at most the previous best error times $1+\rho_v/(k-\rho_v+1)$. If $k$ reaches the dimension, the projection loses zero.

Process physical vertices in any order, using $k_v=\rho_v+\lceil4m\rho_v/\varepsilon\rceil$, capped at the current dimension. Write $E_i$ for the current best squared error, and $\tau_i$ for the loss from projection $i$. Contracting a feasible comparison shows $E_i\le E_{i-1}$. The preceding bound gives $\tau_i+E_i-E_{i-1}\le (\varepsilon/(4m))E_{i-1}\le(\varepsilon/(4m))E$. Summation telescopes. These physical projections preserve all graph bond caps, because each is a left linear change on a single physical core index.

## 2. Exact reduction when the physical ranks already fit

First test the rational mode ranks. If every rank is at most its corresponding $\rho_v$, choose rational bases $U_v$ of the original mode column spaces, say columns of the original unfoldings. The input lies in the tensor product of those spaces. Projecting to them cannot increase any candidate's error, and maps feasible tensors and their limits into the smaller format. Transfer the input by rational left inverses, and use metric $U_v^{\mathsf T}U_v$ in each reduced mode. The induced ambient squared norm is positive definite, rational, and agrees exactly with the original norm on the embedded tensors. This reduction preserves both the optimum and existence of exact real cores. Nonzero weights and basis coefficients have polynomial bit length by rational matrix elimination. The zero tensor is a direct exact case.

When a mode rank exceeds its bound, the actual optimum is positive. Multiplying the dense rational input by a common denominator $D$ gives an integer matrix at that mode. If the rank bound is $\rho_v$, a nonzero minor of order $h=\rho_v+1$ exists, and the minor for the rational matrix has absolute value at least $D^{-h}$. For any rational bound $B\ge\max(1,\|A\|_F)$, the product of the largest $h$ singular values dominates this minor and is at most $B^{h-1}\sigma_h$. It follows that $E_0=D^{-2h}B^{-2(h-1)}$ is a positive rational lower bound for $E$, with polynomial bit length.

## 3. Relative accuracy and output in the binary model

In the case with a violated mode rank, approximate each singular-vector basis by exact rational orthogonal Jacobi rotations using the construction for paired cuts. Obtain a Gram surrogate within $\xi\le\min(1,\varepsilon E_0/[100mB])$ in the tensor Frobenius norm. If a larger right dimension is needed, append a zero-padded dummy factor at a different physical vertex and allow its entire physical dimension during analysis. Embedding and slicing preserve model feasibility and exact optimal distance for zero-padded data. The surrogate therefore has the same one-mode estimate. The optimal loss restricted to a fixed left subspace, and the unrestricted optimal loss, are squared distances to feasible closures containing zero. Each changes by at most $3B\xi$ on replacing data by the surrogate. As the projection loss plus the new optimum equals the restricted loss, rational approximation adds at most $9B\xi$ per step. Orthogonality still gives $E_i\le E_{i-1}$. The accumulated loss plus optimal compressed error is at most $(1+\varepsilon/2)E$. Current dimensions never increase in the actual algorithm. The rotations and the compressed data have polynomial bit lengths by the common-denominator bound for orthogonal compressions. [Rational projection implementation](paired-cuts.md)

On the reduced instance the number of core variables, coordinate equations and their degrees depend only on $m,\rho,1/\varepsilon$. Contracting the cores gives a polynomial map, and squared error is a rational polynomial in the core entries. The rational metric from the exact mode reduction is included when needed. A fixed-dimensional real algebraic decision and sampling algorithm applied to the strict sublevel condition computes its infimum as a real algebraic endpoint. The binary running time has the asserted parameter dependence. In the case with a violated mode rank, choose a rational strict sublevel at most the compressed infimum plus $\varepsilon E_0/4$, and return an algebraic sample. Round each core coordinate rationally so that the squared error increases by at most $\varepsilon E_0/4$. A rational bound on the sample's coordinates and a bound on the gradient of the polynomial on the enclosing box give a dyadic precision of polynomial bit length in this fixed-dimensional setting. Core entry rounding preserves caps automatically. The total error is at most $(1+\varepsilon)E$.

In the exact mode reduction, if the computed algebraic endpoint is positive, choose a rational strict sublevel at most $(1+\varepsilon/2)$ times that endpoint, and reserve the other half of the relative budget for rounding. Univariate root isolation gives a positive rational lower bound of polynomial bit length in the parameterized input size. If the endpoint is zero, decide exact real membership by the polynomial equations with error zero. On a tree, equality of cut-rank constraints with representability gives exact rational cores by nested rational column bases and coordinate maps, as in the earlier tree proof. For a general graph the zero-error test distinguishes membership from membership only in the closure. [Renegar, decision and quantifier elimination over the reals](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747717110800057) · [Exact tree cores](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightEleventh/tree-tensor.md)

The CP case uses sums of a prescribed number of products of one-mode vectors as the polynomial core map. Such tensors have the same invariance under physical linear maps and the same bound on mode ranks. Everything above applies, including the infimum and exact-membership distinction.

## Verification

Paper proof with an independent Lean check of the supporting scalar projection identity. The real algebraic step uses parameter-dependent cost. The theorem makes no polynomial-time claim for unbounded parameters on general graphs.
