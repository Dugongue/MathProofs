# Oracle-free canonization of local slice tensors from small generator spaces

Fix a finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$. We give a deterministic canonization and transporter algorithm for raw concise tensors whose square slice space contains an invertible matrix and generates a split local ratio algebra $B$. Write $J$ for its radical, $d=\dim_F J/J^2$, and $e=\dim_F V/JV$ on the input matrix mode $V$. On raw input length $N$ the time is
$$
 q^{O(d^2+e^2)}\operatorname{poly}(N).
$$
If the input matrix mode is a free $B$-module, the time is $q^{O(d^2)}\operatorname{poly}(N)$, with no bound on its free rank. The algorithm uses no tensor-isomorphism oracle. It permits unbounded Loewy length and radical layers of unbounded dimension. The same method gives canonization of explicit split local associative algebras and of nilpotent Lie algebras over $F$ in time $q^{O(d^2)}\operatorname{poly}(N)$, where $d$ is the size of a minimal generating set of the radical or of the Lie algebra, respectively.

## 1. Intrinsic data from the raw tensor

Write an input as a linear injective slice map $M:U\to\operatorname{Hom}_F(V,W)$, where $\dim V=\dim W$. Fix an invertible slice $A$, put $L=A^{-1}M(U)$ and $B=F\langle L\rangle$, including the identity. The promise is that $B$ has a nilpotent ideal $J$ with $B/J=F$, the isomorphism sending the identity to one. The ideal is the Jacobson radical. Under the promise at least one slice from any basis is invertible, since the normalized slices are invertible exactly when their residues are nonzero and their span contains the identity. Find this slice by scanning the raw basis. A different invertible anchor $Au$ with $u\in L$ replaces $L$ by $u^{-1}L$ and leaves the generated algebra unchanged: in each direction the new anchor ratio and its inverse lie in the old algebra by Cayley–Hamilton. Compute $B$ by Gaussian elimination on products of generators. For each basis element $c$ of $B$, try each $\lambda\in F$ to find the unique possible residue such that $(c-\lambda I)^{\dim V}=0$. Extend these residues linearly and verify that the map is a unital homomorphism with nilpotent kernel. This recognizes the promise in polynomial time and recovers $J$. The radical filtration on $B$ and $V$ is then computed by products of subspaces.

An actual transporter $(s,g,h)$ satisfies $M'(sx)=hM(x)g^{-1}$. Let $A'$ be the target anchor, $\phi=\operatorname{Ad}(g)$ on $B$, and $u=(A')^{-1}hAg^{-1}$. Then
$$
 (A')^{-1}M'(sx)=u\phi(A^{-1}M(x)).
$$
Here $u$ is an invertible normalized target slice. The anchor argument gives $\phi(B)=B'$, and $g(cv)=\phi(c)g(v)$. On the associated graded of $B$, left multiplication by $u$ acts as its residue $\lambda\in F^\times$. On the input label space use the pullback of $L\cap J^i$ under $A^{-1}M$. On the output matrix mode use $A(J^iV)$. Both filtrations are independent of anchor and are carried by actual transporters. The induced actions on the three physical filtrations can therefore be compared using the graded algebra and module.

## 2. Exhaust only the top frames

Enumerate every ordered basis of $J/J^2$, every ordered basis of $V/JV$, and every $\eta\in F^\times$. For each such choice, the following rules determine ordered bases on every grade, with arbitrary lifts allowed at the end.

* In $J^i/J^{i+1}$, build the ordered word basis by multiplying each retained previous-grade word by each tangent basis vector, trying pairs in a fixed order, and retaining a product exactly when it is independent of those already retained on its grade. Start from the identity on $B/J$. Lift the tangent vectors arbitrarily. Errors from higher degrees do not change any grade. The products span because $J^i/J^{i+1}$ is generated by degree-one words. A discarded product is a linear combination of retained terms on its grade, so multiplying it only gives combinations of products already available at the next grade.
* In $J^iV/J^{i+1}V$, use the same rule with left multiplication by the tangent basis and start from the selected basis of $V/JV$. This spans each grade by the definition of $J^iV$.
* In the label grade, compute the image of $L\cap J^i$ in $J^i/J^{i+1}$. Use its row-reduced ordered basis in the grade coordinates just assigned to $B$, multiply by $\eta$, and lift to $U$ through the normalized slice map.
* In the output grade, apply $\eta A$ to the chosen input matrix-mode grade basis.

Order grades from deepest to shallowest and concatenate their chosen lifts into three adapted bases. Every step takes polynomial time for a fixed choice of top frames. In particular the algorithm keeps at most the grade dimension many words at a time, rather than generating all words of a given length.

Suppose an actual transporter maps one raw input to another. Send the selected tangent basis by $\phi$, the module top basis by $g$, and $\eta$ to $\lambda\eta$. The word rules are covariant because their product lists and all linear dependencies are carried by those maps. The image of the label grade as a subspace is carried by the graded $\phi$: the extra $\lambda$ does not change a linear subspace. The specified scaling by $\eta$ then makes the ordered bases agree under $s$. The output bases agree on grades because $h=A'ugA^{-1}$. Consequently the actual transporter in these two coordinate presentations is in the product of groups of block unitriangular matrices, with identity on each diagonal grade block. The converse needed for canonization is immediate from the tensor equation: an equivalence between any two of the coordinate presentations is an equivalence of the raw tensors after applying the recorded basis changes.

## 3. Canonize the remaining unitriangular action

For each top-frame choice, canonize the coordinate tensor under that product of block unitriangular groups. Its generators are elementary transvections in the strictly off-diagonal grade positions, with scalars from a prime-field basis of $F$. They generate by elementary elimination. If dual factors are used, order their coordinates in the opposite direction. The induced action on the full tensor coordinate space is unitriangular in a suitable lexicographic order of the factor basis indices. The order can be reversed overall when needed so that coordinate $i$ depends only on the prefix ending at $i$, with coefficient one on coordinate $i$. The vector space on which it acts has explicit dimension $\dim U\dim V\dim W$. Generator actions can also be evaluated directly on the tensor.

Here is one self-contained version of the coordinate stabilizer routine. Start from the tensor and the full group. Having fixed the first $i-1$ coordinates, maintain generators of the subgroup fixing that prefix and a current tensor with the chosen prefix. Its possible $i$-th coordinates form the orbit of a point of $F$ under this subgroup. The quotient action on a prefix is well-defined by the triangular form, so the orbit can be traversed on at most $q$ points. Take the least value in a fixed field order, move the current tensor there using the recorded representative, and compute generators for its stabilizer in the current subgroup by Schreier's lemma. Subsequent moves keep the entire prefix. Induction shows that the resulting full tensor is the lexicographically least tensor in its orbit. The recorded moves give a transporter to it.

To bound generator counts, compress each list within the faithful product of the matrix groups on the three modes. Order the strict triangular matrix entries by increasing distance from the diagonal, splitting each entry into digits in a fixed basis over the prime field $\mathbb F_p$. On matrices with all earlier digits zero, the next digit is additive under multiplication. Cross terms in that entry involve smaller distances. Process each generator by eliminating its leading digit with a previously retained matrix with the same leading position, using an appropriate power of that matrix. If the leading position is new, retain the residual matrix. If the residual is the identity, discard it. Each elimination changes the leading position to a later one and never changes the generated group of the processed list and the retained matrices. The result generates the same subgroup and has at most the total number of digit positions. Thus each Schreier step and each compression takes polynomial time, and there are only as many steps as tensor coordinates. The same routine can return generators of the final tensor stabilizer, by conjugating the final-prefix stabilizer back to the starting coordinates.

Use a serial encoding including the physical dimensions and the grade size lists, and take the least such encoded canonized tensor over all top-frame choices. Collecting the grade lists keeps the ordering interpretation fixed in the canonical output. If two inputs are isomorphic, the covariance argument makes their sets of branch outputs identical. If the least outputs agree, the recorded maps to that tensor give an explicit isomorphism. This proves canonization and witness search at the stated cost: there are $(q-1)|\mathrm{GL}_d(F)||\mathrm{GL}_e(F)|$ frame choices. Different raw anchors and arbitrary lift choices are covered by the same argument applied to the identity transporter. The finite-field orbit routine is included to make the algorithm explicit. It uses standard small-quotient stabilizer ideas and is closely related to existing unipotent orbit algorithms. [Luks, Computing in solvable matrix groups](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/solmatgrp.pdf) · [Magma, unipotent orbit canonical representatives](https://magma.maths.usyd.edu.au/magma/handbook/text/729)

## 4. Free modules and two algebra consequences

If $V$ is free, compute an explicit free basis by taking lifts of a basis of $V/JV$. They generate, by $V=\sum Bv_i+JV$ and nilpotence of $J$. The map $B^e\to V$ is an isomorphism when the dimensions agree, so freeness is testable by this step. Reduce the raw normalized tensor to the smaller model with the same subspace $L\subset B$ acting on $B$ by left multiplication and with output another copy of $B$. Include the original free rank $e$ in the canonical output. The label space and its map onto $L$ are retained.

To verify this reduction, every original transporter supplies $\phi:B\to B'$ and an invertible slice ratio $u$ with $L'=u\phi(L)$. On the reduced model use the vector-space map $\phi$ on the input regular module and $u\phi$ on the output regular module. Conversely a reduced-model transporter supplies the same kind of algebra map by conjugation on the faithfully represented ratio algebras, and a slice ratio $u$. Extend $\phi$ componentwise to the original free modules in their computed bases and use $A'ugA^{-1}$ for the output map. The label map is the one determined in the reduced model. Both constructions preserve the full tensor equation. The top of the regular module has dimension one, giving the claimed $q^{O(d^2)}$ time bound even when the raw free rank grows. A canonical output can either store this reduced tensor and rank or reconstruct its repetition on a standard free module.

For an explicit split local associative algebra, enumerate just the $J/J^2$ frame and build the ordered bases on its radical grades by the word rule. Keep the unit as the lift of the residue basis. In the resulting coordinates, isomorphic framed multiplications are related by a single block unitriangular change of basis acting simultaneously on the two inputs and the output of the multiplication tensor. Require this group also to fix the unit vector, which is an elementary block unitriangular subgroup. Apply the same canonicalizer to this simultaneous action. Equality of canonical tensors gives an actual algebra isomorphism. For a nilpotent Lie algebra use the lower central series in place of $J^i$ and iterated left brackets with the degree-one generators for the word rule, using $[\gamma_i,\gamma_j]\subseteq\gamma_{i+j}$. Every grade is spanned by these iterated brackets since $\gamma_{i+1}=[\mathfrak g,\gamma_i]$, and higher-degree errors raise the grade. The same simultaneous action canonizes its bracket tensor. In each case all dimensions except $d$ contribute only polynomially. For comparison with prior work on the graded setting, see [Brooksbank, O'Brien and Wilson, Testing isomorphism of graded algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08873).

## Scope

The raw frame extraction, the small-top graded propagation, and the unipotent canonizer address different parts of the algorithm. Previous determinant readouts for local slice algebras used ordinary TI queries, and previous small-diagonal flag routines took the flags and allowed maps as data. Here the raw tensor supplies both the flags and a frame enumeration whose size is independent of the large grade dimensions. For example, the method applies to the regular model of a truncated free algebra on two generators with one highest-degree word omitted from its slice space. The normalizing algebra has exponential grade width in the truncation length and the normalized slice space is not itself an algebra. It also applies to raw free-module tensors of arbitrary ranks over these algebras. There is no generic-randomness requirement. These are informal results for the stated classes. Publication priority for this combination is unverified.
