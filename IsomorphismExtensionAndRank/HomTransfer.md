# Tensor Isomorphism completeness for low-rank pencils with scalar adjoint

Fix a finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$ and an integer $k\ge 1$. Ordinary 3-tensor isomorphism remains TI-complete under deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions on a deterministically recognizable family of raw concise tensors with the following simultaneous properties.

- The two matrix modes both have dimension $n$, and the label dimension is $O(n^{1+1/k})$.
- The raw fixed-label pair-adjoint algebra is exactly the scalars for each of the three label choices, even after extension of the field.
- Every matrix slice along the stated square-pencil label mode, even over the algebraic closure, has rank $O(n^{1/k})$, yet the slices have zero common kernel and collectively span the output mode.

The family is closed under independent changes of basis in the three tensor modes. Its raw recognizer also reconstructs an alternating matrix space on $O(n^{1/k})$ coordinates that completely determines the isomorphism type. For each fixed odd prime $p$ and $k$, the construction also gives TI-completeness of class-two exponent-$p$ group isomorphism in the commutator representation on an explicit class with a unique maximum-order abelian subgroup of index at most $p^{O((\log_p |G|)^{1/(k+1)})}$.

## 1. The extension of an alternating matrix space

Take any nonzero alternating matrix space $S\subseteq\operatorname{Alt}(B)$ on a space $B$ of dimension $b\ge 2$. Here $\operatorname{Alt}(B)$ consists of bilinear forms $\omega$ with $\omega(v,v)=0$ for every $v$. This includes characteristic two. Let $A$ have dimension $a>2b$, put $V=A\oplus B$, and form the alternating matrix space
$$
 \mathcal P(S)=
 \left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&C\\-C^{\mathsf T}&D\end{pmatrix}
             :C\in F^{a\times b},\ D\in S\right\}.
$$
Its basis consists of all $ab$ elementary cross forms and a basis of $S$ in the bottom block. The tensor has these basis matrices as its slices.

Its label flattening is injective by construction. The elementary cross forms together have zero common kernel and their images span the whole dual $V^*$, which gives conciseness in the two matrix modes. Every matrix has image in $\operatorname{im}(C)\oplus B^*$, so the maximum rank is at most $2b$ after any field extension. Equality is attained when $C$ has column rank $b$. With $a=b^k+2b$ the matrix dimension is $n=b^k+3b$, and $2b\le 2n^{1/k}$. The label count is $ab+\dim S\le nb+b^2/2=O(n^{1+1/k})$.

## 2. Fixed-label Hom is scalar

The fixed-label endomorphism algebra of the underlying Kronecker representation consists of pairs $g,h$ of square matrices, possibly singular, such that $hM=Mg$ for all slices $M$. This is the kernel of the self Hom differential for the arrow tuple. Work first in the displayed coordinates.

For each elementary cross matrix $J_{ij}=E_{ij}-E_{ji}$, with $i$ from $A$ and $j$ from $B$, $h$ preserves the two-dimensional image $\langle e_i,e_j\rangle$. Intersecting the image planes for two different neighbors of a vertex leaves just its coordinate line. As $a,b\ge2$, $h$ is diagonal. Transposing the equations gives the same argument for $g^{\mathsf T}$, so $g$ is diagonal as well. The entries of $h$ on $A$ all coincide with the entries of $g$ on $B$, and the entries of $h$ on $B$ all coincide with those of $g$ on $A$. Any nonzero entry of one bottom slice $D$ forces these two common scalars to agree. Therefore $(g,h)=(\lambda I,\lambda I)$. The argument holds verbatim over any field extension. It also gives scalar fixed-label Hom for the other two choices of label mode. With one physical vector $v$ fixed as label, the slice is evaluation $M\mapsto Mv$. A pair $(f,h)$ in the corresponding adjoint algebra satisfies $(fM)v=hMv$ for all $v$, so $fM=hM$ as matrices. Since $fM$ remains alternating, $hM=Mh^{\mathsf T}$; the preceding scalar result forces $h$, and then $f$, to be the same scalar. Alternation interchanges the two physical vector modes, giving the last case.

This forces equation compatibility between the two moving matrix modes. If an ordinary tensor isomorphism takes one displayed pencil to another, their spaces satisfy $\mathcal P(S')=H\mathcal P(S)K^{\mathsf T}$ for invertible $H,K$. For each $M\in\mathcal P(S)$, alternation of $HMK^{\mathsf T}$ implies
$$
     K^{-1}H M=M H^{\mathsf T}K^{-\mathsf T}.
$$
By the just-proved fixed-label condition, $H=\lambda K$ for some nonzero scalar $\lambda$. Thus the two spaces are congruent. Conversely congruence of the spaces induces ordinary tensor isomorphism, since any invertible coordinate change on the slice space can be made in the label mode. The proof uses transposition with the signs valid in the field, including characteristic two.

## 3. Recover the source isometry type intrinsically

A subspace $L\le V$ is common totally isotropic if every form in $\mathcal P(S)$ vanishes on $L\times L$. For $v=(x,y), w=(x',y')$, all cross forms vanish on $(v,w)$ precisely when $x\otimes y'=x'\otimes y$. If $x,y\ne0$, this equation forces $w$ to be a scalar multiple of $v$. If $x\ne0,y=0$, it forces $y'=0$. The corresponding assertion holds for a nonzero vector in $B$. Consequently every common totally isotropic subspace of dimension at least two lies in $A$ or in $B$. Since $a>b$, $A$ is the unique one of maximum dimension.

The forms in $\mathcal P(S)$ annihilating $A$ in one argument are exactly the pullbacks of $S$ on $V/A$. An isometry of two enlarged spaces therefore takes their $A$'s to one another, and induces an isometry of the recovered quotient spaces $S,S'$. In the other direction an isometry of $S,S'$ extends using the identity on $A$, since all cross forms are present.

For a fixed $k$, the construction is polynomial in the explicit dimensions. Grochow and Qiao's reduction from 3-TI to alternating matrix-space isometry, with matrix side at most $3n_1+2n_2+2$ on an $n_1\times n_2\times n_3$ tensor, supplies the TI-hard input $S$. Take an independent basis of its image as a matrix space. Trivial inputs of dimension zero and inputs with incompatible dimensions can be decided directly and mapped to a fixed yes or no pair in the target family. One fixed no pair comes from two one-dimensional alternating spaces on a four-dimensional $B$, spanned by forms of ranks two and four, and the same $a$ in both extensions. On a pair in the target family, ordinary TI answers its isomorphism question directly. This establishes the claimed many-one completeness. [Grochow and Qiao, Part IV, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317v2#S2)

## 4. Recognize the whole raw isomorphism closure

The following procedure takes dense tensor coordinates with no given splitting. First check square matrix modes, slice independence, and that the fixed-label endomorphism system has dimension one. The identity pair belongs to that system. Solve the linear equations requiring $D M_i$ to be alternating for every raw basis matrix $M_i$, with $D$ an unknown matrix. In displayed coordinates a solution satisfies $D M_i=M_iD^{\mathsf T}$, so all solutions are scalar multiples of $I$. In arbitrary coordinates $M_i\mapsto H M_i G^{-1}$, the solution line is spanned by $G^{-\mathsf T}H^{-1}$. A label basis change just replaces one basis of the same space. Require a one-dimensional solution space with invertible generator and use that generator on the output to pass to alternating matrices. Call the resulting alternating space $\mathcal M$.

Over an extension $K$ of $F$ with more than $n$ elements, start with $M=0$ and improve its rank until its radical $\ker M$ is common totally isotropic for $\mathcal M_K$. To make one improvement, find a basis matrix $N$ and two vectors in $\ker M$ on which $N$ is nonzero. If the radical fails the test, two basis vectors of the radical and one basis matrix of $\mathcal M_K$ suffice, by bilinearity. Restrict $M+tN$ to a complement of $\ker M$ and those two radical vectors. The complement carries a nondegenerate restriction of $M$, as the form of $M$ descends nondegenerately to the quotient by its radical. If the present rank is $u$, the determinant of this restriction is a polynomial of degree at most $u+2$, with a nonzero coefficient of $t^2$. Indeed the two radical rows and columns have zero constant term, and the coefficient is the product of the determinant on the complement and the nonzero determinant of the alternating two-dimensional block from $N$. A cross-block term would use at least three factors of $t$. Hence some $t\in K$ yields rank at least $u+2$. Scan all $t$ and test by Gaussian elimination. There are at most $n/2$ improvements. For fixed $q$, one can construct a field $K$ of cardinality between $n+1$ and $q(n+1)$ by enumerating monic polynomials of a suitable logarithmic degree and testing irreducibility. These operations take deterministic polynomial time.

For a promised space of the displayed form, the radical found this way has dimension at least $a-b>b$ since the matrix rank is at most $2b$. By the isotropy argument it lies in $A_K$ and is nonzero. Its common perpendicular, taking perpendicularity with respect to every form in $\mathcal M_K$, equals $A_K$. In one direction all pairs from $A_K$ vanish. In the other direction any vector with a nonzero $B$-component is separated from any chosen nonzero member of the radical by some cross form. Compute this common perpendicular by linear equations. Its row-reduced basis descends to $F$ in the promised case, since $A$ is intrinsically defined over $F$ and the row-reduced basis is unique. Require this descent, a positive $b=n-a$ with $b\ge2$, $a=b^k+2b$, and that the candidate $A$ be common totally isotropic.

For arbitrary input a final exact verification certifies membership. Pick any complement for $A$ and write each basis form in blocks $\begin{pmatrix}0&C_i\\-C_i^{\mathsf T}&D_i\end{pmatrix}$ in this splitting. Require the cross projections $C_i$ to span the full $a\times b$ matrix space. Compute the bottom space $S$ arising from the kernel of this projection and require $S\ne0$. A different complement is the graph of a map $R:B\to A$ and replaces each bottom block by $D_i+R^{\mathsf T}C_i-C_i^{\mathsf T}R$. Solve the linear equations on $R$ requiring all these bottom blocks to lie in $S$, testing them in the quotient of the alternating space by $S$. If this system is solvable, the new matrix space is contained in the claimed template and has its exact dimension $ab+\dim S$, so they are equal. If the input arose from the construction, the transported original complement supplies a solution. All rejection and acceptance paths are therefore valid, and in an accepted instance the algorithm outputs $S$ up to its original isometry type.

## 5. A class-two group consequence

Take $F=\mathbb F_p$ for fixed odd prime $p$. Let $Z=\mathcal P(S)^*$ and let $\beta:V\times V\to Z$ evaluate each form of $\mathcal P(S)$ on its input pair. It is alternating, has zero common radical, and the associated linear map $\bigwedge^2 V\to Z$ is surjective by duality. Use the group law
$$
   (x,z)(y,w)=(x+y,z+w+\tfrac12\beta(x,y)).
$$
Bilinearity gives associativity, and the commutator is $(0,\beta(x,y))$. This is a class-two exponent-$p$ group with center and derived group both $Z$. A group isomorphism induces $\mathbb F_p$-linear bijections of the center and quotient preserving the commutator, and such maps conversely lift by the displayed multiplication. Preservation of the commutator in these two sorts is equivalent to the matrix-space congruence already analyzed. Thus these groups, in commutator coordinates, have the same TI-complete classification. Their adjoint algebra $\{(f,g):\beta(fx,y)=\beta(x,gy)\}$ is scalar by the fixed-label calculation, via $(g,f^{\mathsf T})$.

Every largest abelian subgroup contains the center, since adjoining the center preserves commutativity. Abelian overgroups of the center correspond to common totally isotropic subspaces of $V$. The unique largest one is the preimage of $A$, so it is characteristic and has index $p^b$. With $a=b^k+2b$, we have $\log_p|G|=a+b+ab+\dim S\ge b^{k+1}$. This proves the asserted index bound. The large abelian subgroup can be computed from the commutator coordinates by the same raw reconstruction. In fact the commutator map $y\mapsto\beta(x,y)$ has rank $b$ at each nonzero $x\in A$, and rank at least $a$ whenever $x\notin A$ because the cross forms already separate its images of $A$. Thus its preimage in the group has an intrinsic centralizer-index description.

The group completeness statement concerns succinct commutator coordinates. A full Cayley table can have exponential size in those coordinates. The standard tensor-to-class-two-group correspondence is used with this distinction. [Grochow and Qiao, Part I](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

## Scope and antecedents

The construction uses a complete family of cross forms to force the fixed-label Hom kernel to be one-dimensional. It then retains the hard instance on the quotient recovered by common isotropy. The full fixed-label Ext space can be large. The conclusion concerns maximum **slice** rank, which differs from tensor rank in [Grochow, Pitassi and She](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19320). The graph and matrix-space literature gives antecedents for encoding graph adjacency by elementary alternating forms and for using common isotropic subspaces in isomorphism and group theory. The construction and raw extraction above concern an arbitrary alternating matrix space, including spaces that are not graphical. Priority for this precise combination is unverified. [Li, Qiao, Wigderson, Wigderson and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04815)
