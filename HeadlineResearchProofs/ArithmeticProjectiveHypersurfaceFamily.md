# Rational-point universality for canonically polarized projective divisors

**Theorem.** Over every global field \(k\) of characteristic different from two, rational-point existence for arbitrary affine systems is uniformly computably many-one equivalent to rational-point existence on smooth geometrically integral canonically polarized ample divisors in \(\mathbb P^M_k\times C^r\), where the smooth projective curve \(C/k\) depends only on \(k\). The output can always be made everywhere locally soluble. For each number field it can always be made to have a nonempty étale–Brauer–Manin set. For a projective input the construction before adding a fixed locally soluble summand preserves its rational points bijectively.

**Fixed-family consequence.** Assume the Diophantine definition of \(\mathbb Z\) in \(\mathbb Q\) from the Bhargava–Burungale preprint. There are fixed \(M,r\), a fixed line bundle on \(\mathbb P^M\times C^r\), and one smooth projective family over a nonempty open of \(\mathbb A^1_{\mathbb Q}\) whose fibers are divisors in that line bundle. Every rational fiber has a nonempty étale–Brauer–Manin set. All complex fibers are diffeomorphic, and rational-point existence on the integer fibers is halting-complete. The reduction for arbitrary input systems is independent of this external arithmetic premise. [Bhargava and Burungale, A Diophantine definition of \(\mathbb Z\) in \(\mathbb Q\)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16130v2).

The divisor is a hypersurface in the displayed product. Poonen's question about smooth hypersurfaces in ordinary projective space remains open. [Poonen, Existence of rational points on smooth projective varieties, §12(iii)](https://ems.press/journals/jems/articles/1924).

## 1. A uniform compact encoding

Choose a finite place \(v_0\) of odd residue characteristic. Choose \(a,\pi\in k^\times\) with \(a\) a unit of nonsquare residue and \(\pi\) of valuation one at \(v_0\). The quadratic form

\[
q(z,l_1,l_2,l_3)=z^2-a l_1^2-\pi l_2^2+a\pi l_3^2
\]

is anisotropic over \(k_{v_0}\). Indeed, each of \(z^2-a l_1^2\) and \(l_2^2-a l_3^2\), when nonzero, is a norm from the unramified quadratic extension and has even valuation. Multiplication of the second by \(\pi\) makes its valuation odd. Equality therefore forces both norms to vanish and all coordinates to be zero. In particular \(q\) is anisotropic over \(k\). Write \(q'\) for its ternary part in the \(l_i\) and set \(b=-a\), the first coefficient of \(q'\).

There is an effective finite set \(S\) of places and neighborhoods \(U_v\) of zero for \(v\in S\) such that for all \(z\in k\) in those neighborhoods, \(q(z,l)=b\) has a solution \(l\in k^3\). To prove this, let \(S\) include \(v_0\), all archimedean places, all places above two, and all places at which a coefficient of \(q'\) is not a unit. Outside \(S\) the reduction of \(q'\) is nonsingular and isotropic over the finite residue field. A nonzero point exists because the three-variable quadratic has degree less than the number of variables, or by the elementary classification of quadratic forms over finite fields. Such a point lifts by Hensel's lemma. An isotropic nonsingular ternary form contains a hyperbolic plane and represents every scalar over the local field. At \(v\in S\), for sufficiently small \(z\), choose \(l_2=l_3=0\) and \(l_1^2=1-z^2/b\). The right side is locally a square, by Hensel's lemma or the archimedean square-root neighborhood of one. Take the neighborhoods small enough that the square-root condition holds uniformly. The Hasse–Minkowski theorem over global fields of characteristic different from two for \(q'(l)-(b-z^2)h^2\) now supplies a nonzero global zero. Its \(h\)-coordinate is nonzero because \(q'\) is anisotropic already at \(v_0\). Divide by \(h\). [Lam, Introduction to Quadratic Forms over Fields, local–global theorem](https://bookstore.ams.org/gsm-67).

By weak approximation, choose a fixed nonsquare \(\delta\in k^\times\) for which every \(v\in S\) has a square root of \(\delta\) in \(U_v\). At each of these places start with a small nonzero local square, require a nearby value, and at one extra finite place require an odd valuation of \(\delta\). The local root conditions and sufficiently small root bounds are effective. The selection is independent of the input equations.

Given input equations \(f_i(x)=0\), create coordinates \(w,s,y_1,\ldots,y_m\) with

\[
s=w^2-\delta,\qquad s^{\deg f_i}f_i(\boldsymbol y/s)=0\quad\text{for all }i.
\]

The second expression is interpreted as a polynomial. For each of the \(m+2\) displayed coordinates \(z\), introduce its own three slack coordinates \(l\) and require \(q(z,l)=b\). Any \(k\)-solution decodes by \(x_i=y_i/s\), since \(\delta\) is nonsquare. Given any \(k\)-solution \(x\) of the input, choose \(w\) by weak approximation as close as needed at all \(v\in S\) to the chosen small local root of \(\delta\). Then \(w,s\), and all \(y_i=s x_i\) are in their prescribed neighborhoods, hence all slack equations have \(k\)-solutions.

Homogenize using one common coordinate \(h\). At \(h=0\) each slack equation becomes \(q(z,l)=0\). Anisotropy forces all the homogeneous coordinates other than \(h\) to vanish, which is impossible for a projective point with \(h=0\). Thus the resulting projective scheme \(X\subset\mathbb P^{4(m+2)}\) has a \(k\)-point exactly when the input does. Its ambient dimension and the degrees of its equations depend only on the original number of variables and maximum degree. The construction is independent of the value of any input parameter occurring in the coefficients.

## 2. A smooth ample divisor retaining a prescribed rational slice

Apply Poonen's effective curve theorem to choose a fixed smooth projective geometrically integral \(C/k\) with just one \(k\)-point \(P\). The point can be found by enumeration. Given any projective input \(X\subseteq\mathbb P^M\), define one homogeneous form \(G(X_0,\ldots,X_M)\) cutting out precisely its \(k\)-points inside \(\mathbb P^M(k)\). First raise the homogeneous equations to positive powers to match degrees. Take the field norm of the sum of their multiples by a basis of an extension of degree the number of equations. A suitable separable extension is obtained by lifting an irreducible polynomial from a finite residue field. The norm is zero exactly when all equation values vanish. The same construction works for the empty set. If the input is the whole ambient projective space, first embed it as a hyperplane of a space of one higher dimension. We may always assume \(M\ge1\) and \(G\) a nonzero form of positive degree. [Poonen, Curves over every global field violating the local-global principle](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3965).

Set \(r=M+1\), \(B=C^r\), and \(p=(P,\ldots,P)\). Then \(B(k)=\{p\}\). Let \(L_0\) be the external product of a fixed very ample line bundle on each copy of \(C\), and choose \(a\) a sufficiently large multiple of \(\deg G\). Work with

\[
L=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^M}(a)\boxtimes L_0^a.
\]

Prescribe the restriction of a section of \(L\) on \(\mathbb P^M\times\{p\}\) to be \(G^{a/\deg G}\). In a local trivialization of \(L_0^a\) near \(p\), prescribe its first derivatives along a basis of \(T_pB\) to be the \(M+1\) forms \(X_0^a,\ldots,X_M^a\). These prescriptions can be met by the surjectivity of evaluation on the first infinitesimal neighborhood of \(p\) for large \(a\). One can see this directly using a very ample embedding of \(B\). Take one section nonzero at \(p\), and sections vanishing at \(p\) whose first differentials give a basis. Multiply them by the appropriate powers of the first section to get the specified first jets in degree \(a\).

Along the prescribed fiber, the divisor is smooth at every geometric point on it. Some \(X_i^a\) is nonzero there, so the derivative in a base direction is nonzero, even if the fiber polynomial itself is singular. Off the prescribed fiber, the allowable variations, which vanish to order two along it, give a basepoint-free system. Indeed, to reach a point whose base coordinate is \(p'\ne p\), use the square of a section of \(L_0\) vanishing at \(p\) and nonzero at \(p'\), then multiply by a section of the remaining degree nonzero at \(p'\) and by a spatial degree-\(a\) form nonzero at the point. This system also separates first jets at points off the fiber for \(a\ge3\). Fix one such square nonzero at the point. The remaining positive powers in the base and degree-\(a\) factors in the projective space are very ample and give arbitrary first jets; multiplication by the square, a unit locally, preserves first-jet surjectivity. It follows by the usual incidence argument that a general choice is smooth off the prescribed fiber in every characteristic. At any geometric candidate point the vanishing of the section and of its first derivatives is a collection of \(\dim(\mathbb P^M\times B)+1\) independent affine linear conditions on variations. The union over points therefore has dimension smaller than the parameter space. The field is infinite, so it contains an allowed choice.

For large \(a\) the divisor is geometrically connected. Over \(\bar k\), Serre duality and vanishing give \(H^1(\mathbb P^M\times B,L^{-1})=0\), since \(L\) grows as the \(a\)-th power of a fixed ample line bundle and the ambient dimension is at least two. The divisor sequence gives one-dimensional \(H^0(\mathcal O)\) for the divisor. Together with geometric smoothness this proves geometric integrality. Adjunction gives ample canonical bundle when \(a\) is large because the ambient canonical bundle tensored with \(L\) is then ample. All choices can be made effectively by dovetailing the search over exponents and sections and testing geometric smoothness and integrality by elimination. The positivity of the canonical class also follows explicitly from the factors by taking \(a>M+1\) and each curve degree large enough.

Call the output divisor \(D\). Every \(k\)-point of \(D\) lies above \(p\), where projecting to \(\mathbb P^M(k)\) gives the zeros of \(G\), exactly \(X(k)\). This correspondence is bijective for the projective input.

## 3. Local and étale–Brauer–Manin promises

Before the divisor construction, embed the compact encoding \(X\) and one fixed projective variety \(W\) with no \(k\)-points in disjoint coordinate subspaces of a larger \(\mathbb P^M\). Use their scheme union as the projective input. This does not change the rational point set and produces an inclusion \(W\hookrightarrow D\) in the prescribed fiber. An effective choice of \(W\) everywhere locally soluble comes from Poonen's same curve theorem with \(n=0\).

For any fixed number field instead take a smooth projective surface \(W\) with \(W(k)=\varnothing\) and \(W(\mathbb A_k)^{\mathrm{\acute et},\operatorname{Br}}\ne\varnothing\), supplied by Harpaz and Skorobogatov. The image of any element of that set in \(D(\mathbb A_k)\) survives étale–Brauer–Manin. Indeed, pull back each finite étale torsor and its twists from \(D\) along \(W\to D\). A Brauer-orthogonal adelic lift to the corresponding twist over \(W\) maps to a Brauer-orthogonal lift over \(D\), by functoriality of evaluation. This holds for each torsor. [Harpaz and Skorobogatov, Singular curves and the étale Brauer–Manin obstruction for surfaces](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.24033/asens.2226/).

The reverse decision reduction simply uses finitely many affine patches in the projective presentation. A finite disjunction of existential polynomial systems is expressible by polynomial equations over the field. Introduce idempotent selectors summing to one, require products of distinct selectors to vanish, and multiply each patch's equations by its selector. This proves the claimed many-one equivalences including the promises. The uniform assertion over global fields uses the unconditional locally soluble curve, and the stronger number-field assertion may fix the chosen surface.

## 4. One fixed deformation family

For a polynomial system \(f_i(T,x)=0\) with a distinguished parameter \(T\), the compact encoding gives one fixed formal dimension and one fixed list of maximum degrees. Homogenize to that list before specializing. The union with the fixed \(W\) likewise has a fixed list of homogeneous equations and a norm compression \(G(T,X)\) of fixed spatial degree. Over \(\mathbb Q(T)\) use the same jet construction and pick the allowed variations to have rational coefficients. Indeed, tuples of rational constants are Zariski dense in affine space over \(\mathbb Q(T)\), since a nonzero polynomial after clearing denominators has at least one nonzero coefficient as a polynomial in \(T\). The search can therefore test rational sections over \(\mathbb Q(T)\). The resulting divisor extends over the parameter line and is smooth projective with geometrically integral fibers after removing an effective finite closed subset. This follows by openness of these properties and properness, or by testing the relative equations by elimination. Adjunction is uniform. At every rational parameter in this open the divisor has the same prescribed rational slice, including its fixed copy of \(W\), so the conclusions around rational points and étale–Brauer–Manin specialize as stated.

The complex parameter open is connected. Ehresmann's theorem for the proper smooth map of complex manifolds then gives diffeomorphic complex fibers. Assuming the stated Diophantine definition of \(\mathbb Z\) in \(\mathbb Q\), the Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich theorem supplies fixed rational input equations encoding the halting predicate at nonnegative integer parameters. Use a padded predicate in which every instance has infinitely many parameter values with the same membership. An effective search for a padded parameter in the open terminates because the complement is finite. This proves halting-hardness for rational-point existence on the integer fibers. Enumeration of rational points gives computable enumerability. [Matiyasevich, Hilbert's tenth problem](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262132954/hilberts-tenth-problem/).
