# Marked subgroup transport under mixed-exponent splitting and height conditions

Let $G,G_*$ be finite class-two $p$-groups specified by Cayley tables, where $p$ may be 2. Let $C,C_*$ be specified central subgroups containing the respective derived subgroups. For instance, take $C=Z(G)$ and $C_*=Z(G_*)$. Set $A=G/C$, $A_*=G_*/C_*$, and write $\pi,\pi_*$ for the quotient maps. Each ordered actual subgroup mark $H_i\le G$ has quotient image $U_i=\pi(H_i)$ and central intersection $K_i=H_i\cap C$, and similarly on the target. The sought isomorphisms carry $C$ to $C_*$ and each $H_i$ to its specified counterpart. Taking the center or derived subgroup as $C$ adds no constraint to the subgroup transporter.

**Theorem.** Deterministic polynomial-time Turing reductions to ordinary class-two $p$-group isomorphism decision compute marked transporter existence, construct a transporter when it exists, count the transporters, and generate the source marked automorphism group in each of the following families. If the larger input group has order $N$, each oracle table has order $N^{O(1)}$. The constants may depend on the stated bounds on the number of marks or height levels.

1. There are a fixed number of marks. Their sum $S=\bigoplus_i U_i$ is internal in $A$, and $S\cap p^k A=p^k S$ at each cyclic-summand exponent $k$ of $C$. The respective conditions hold on the target. A direct summand $S$ always satisfies this condition. The $K_i$ and ambient groups may have mixed exponents, and no splitting of $K_i\le C$ is required.
2. There is one mark, and $K$ is a direct summand of $C$ on each side. Let $Q=C/K$ and let $p^e=\exp A$. The number of distinct positive cyclic-summand exponents $k<e$ of $Q$ is bounded. An exponent $k$ refers to a summand of order $p^k$, irrespective of its multiplicity. In particular a fixed bound on $\exp A$ suffices.
3. There is one mark. The ambient quotient admits a cyclic-coordinate decomposition $A=\bigoplus_j\langle e_j\rangle$, with $e_j$ of order $p^{a_j}$, in which $U=\bigoplus_j p^{s_j}\langle e_j\rangle$ for $0\le s_j\le a_j$. For each proper nonzero part $0<s_j<a_j$, the order $p^{a_j}$ kills $C$. The number of distinct proper heights $s_j$ is bounded, and the respective conditions hold on the target. The central intersection $K$ may be nonsplit. This includes arbitrary $A,C$ killed by $p^2$, and homocyclic $A$ of any exponent that kills $C$ with a bounded number of proper Smith heights.

In particular, if the derived subgroup of a class-two 2-group has exponent at most 2, the theorem handles one arbitrary actual subgroup by choosing $C=Z(G)$. Indeed $[g^2,x]=[g,x]^2=1$, so the quotient $A$ is elementary abelian and all of its subspaces split. The first family also applies to any fixed number of marks whose images in this quotient are independent. These statements allow arbitrary exponents of the original groups.

## 1. The central presentation and compatible maps

For a class-two extension with central subgroup $B$ and abelian quotient $X$, the commutator is an alternating biadditive map $\beta:X\times X\to B$. For $m$ dividing the exponent of $X$, the power record is

$$
 q_m(x)=\widetilde x^{\,m}\bmod mB\qquad (x\in X[m]).
$$

Multiplying the lift by a central element changes the power by an element of $mB$, so the record is well-defined. A pair of additive isomorphisms on $X,B$ lifts to a group isomorphism exactly when it preserves $\beta$ and the power records at the cyclic basis generators. Necessity follows by applying the group isomorphism to commutators and powers. For sufficiency, choose lifts of the desired images of a cyclic basis of $X$ and correct each lift by an element of the target $B$ to get its exact indicated power. These corrected lifts and the map on $B$ satisfy the central relations, the recorded commutators, and the basis powers. The relations present the given group. Collection gives at most $|X||B|$ normal forms, and the presented group surjects onto the given group of that size. Every pair passing this test is also compatible with every remaining $q_m$.

For the ambient extension write $(\alpha,\gamma):(A,C)\to(A_*,C_*)$ for such a pair. Assume that the pair also preserves every $U_i,K_i$. Fix any lift $f:G\to G_*$ of the pair. For $u\in U_i$, take $h\in H_i$ above $u$ and $h_*\in H_{i,*}$ above $\alpha(u)$, and define

$$
 \delta_i(u)=f(h)(h_*)^{-1}\bmod K_{i,*}\in C_*/K_{i,*}.
$$

Changing $h,h_*$ alters the value by $K_{i,*}$. Taking products in the two subgroups and using centrality of both discrepancies proves that $\delta_i$ is additive. All lifts of the same ambient pair are obtained by composing $f$ with a central shift $x\mapsto x\,s(\pi_*x)$, where $s\in\operatorname{Hom}(A_*,C_*)$. In fact any two lifts have central difference, trivial on $C$, and the difference is additive on the quotient. Thus the compatible pair has a marked lift if and only if the simultaneous linear system

$$
 s(\alpha u)\bmod K_{i,*}=-\delta_i(u)\qquad (u\in U_i,\ \text{all } i)
$$

is solvable. When this holds, the number of lifts is the cardinality of the kernel of the restriction map

$$
 \rho:\operatorname{Hom}(A,C)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_i\operatorname{Hom}(U_i,C/K_i),\qquad
 \rho(s)_i(u)=s(u)\bmod K_i.
$$

The source and target kernels are isomorphic via $(\alpha,\gamma)$. Working in cyclic coordinates, there is one unknown central element per basis element of $A$, subject to its basis order and one congruence per generator of a mark. Smith normal form with transformations yields a solution to a feasible system and a generator list and exact cardinality for the homogeneous kernel. Neither task enumerates the entire homomorphism group.

## 2. Independent quotient images and relative purity

Include the central presentation of $G$ on $(A,C)$ together with the central presentation of every $H_i$ on $(U_i,K_i)$. Include the homomorphisms $U_i\hookrightarrow A$ and $K_i\hookrightarrow C$. A compatible pair on the ambient extension and on every marked-subgroup extension is necessary for actual marked transport. Conversely take such a pair, lift it to $f:G\to G_*$, and lift each restricted pair independently to $f_i:H_i\to H_{i,*}$ by the power test. For $h\in H_i$ the central difference $f_i(h)f(h)^{-1}$ is trivial on $K_i$. Thus it defines an additive map $s_i:U_i\to C_*$. By independence these maps sum to an additive map on $S$. The following extension argument extends it to $A$. The resulting common shift makes $f$ agree with each $f_i$. The $K_i$ may be nonsplit because the subgroup power records give actual $C_*$-valued differences before extending from the quotient images.

For any finite abelian $p$-groups $S\le A$ and $B$, the restriction $\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)\to\operatorname{Hom}(S,B)$ is onto exactly when $S\cap p^k A=p^k S$ at every cyclic-summand exponent $k$ of $B$. Work componentwise in a cyclic decomposition of $B$. An additive map to $\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z$ extends from $S$ to $A$ if and only if it kills $S\cap p^k A$. Necessity follows because $p^k$ kills the target. For sufficiency, factor the map through the image of $S$ in $A/p^k A$, then embed the target as the $p^k$-torsion of $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$. A character extends from a subgroup by adding one generator at a time. If the new class has order $d$ modulo the current domain, choose a $d$-th root of the value of its $d$-fold multiple. This defines an extension, which lies in the $p^k$-torsion because the domain is killed by $p^k$. Finally the characters to $\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z$ separate $S/p^k S$, by cyclic decomposition, so all of them kill $S\cap p^k A$ exactly when $S\cap p^k A=p^k S$. Apply this argument with $B=C_*$, whose cyclic orders agree with those of $C$.

## 3. One mark and relative height records

Write $H,U,K$ for the single mark and its two images. For a positive integer $k$, put

$$
 W_k=U\cap p^k A,\qquad H_{W_k}=H\cap\pi^{-1}(W_k),\qquad
 N_k=(p^k C)H_{W_k}.
$$

The subgroup $N_k$ is normal in $G$. A commutator with any element projecting into $W_k\subset p^k A$ lies in $p^k C$ by biadditivity. The central subgroup $p^k C$ is normal and all conjugations of $H_{W_k}$ therefore preserve its product with $p^k C$. Its quotient image is $W_k$ and its central intersection is $p^k C+K$. Consequently $G_k=G/N_k$ is a class-two extension with named central subgroup $C_k=C/(p^k C+K)$ and abelian quotient $A_k=A/W_k$. These groups and maps are computed by table operations. An actual marked transporter preserves $W_k$ and $N_k$, hence gives compatible maps on the corresponding extension $G_k$.

Conversely suppose the ambient pair preserves $U,K$ and lifts, and the induced pair on $(A_k,C_k)$ lifts to $\phi_k:G_k\to G_{k,*}$. There are two maps from $G$ to $G_{k,*}$, given by $f$ followed by the target quotient and by source quotient followed by $\phi_k$. They agree on $C$ and on the quotient into $A_{k,*}$. Their central difference gives an additive map $A\to C_{k,*}$, since both maps are homomorphisms and their difference is central and kills $C$. The map kills $p^k A$, as $C_{k,*}$ is killed by $p^k$. On $W_k$ the second map kills every element of $H_{W_k}$. The value of the first map on such an element is the central discrepancy of its target against a target element of $H_{W_k,*}$. This proves

$$
 \delta(W_k)\subset p^k(C_*/K_*).
$$

### Split central intersection

In the second family, keep only the records whose positive $k<e$ occur as cyclic summand exponents of $Q=C/K$. Isomorphic $C,K$ as a pair with inclusion imply isomorphic $Q$, so the source and target schedules coincide on a positive comparison. Heights $k\ge e$ impose no condition because $W_k=0$. Project $\delta:U\to Q_*$ to each order-$p^k$ cyclic summand of $Q_*$. The projected map kills $U\cap p^kA$ by the displayed containment. Hence it defines a map from the image of $U$ in $A/p^k A$ to $\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z$. The character extension argument in Section 2 extends this map to $A/p^k A$.

Combining the extended maps gives an additive extension of $\delta$ from $U$ to $A$ with target $Q_*$. The splitting of $K_*\le C_*$ lifts this extension to $C_*$. Changing sign and transporting over $\alpha$ gives the common central shift in Section 1. The tests do not require a marked splitting.

### Diagonal cyclic coordinates

For the third family include the central presentation of $H$ itself on $(U,K)$, as well as the relative quotient records at the proper heights. Fix an ambient lift $f$ as in Section 1. The compatible $H$ presentation gives a lift $f_H:H\to H_*$ of the restricted pair. As in Section 2, the difference $f(h)f_H(h)^{-1}$ defines a $C_*$-valued additive map on $U$ whose reduction modulo $K_*$ is $\delta$. At $s_j=0$, take its value on $e_j$ for the assigned image of $e_j$ in $C_*$. Its order divides $p^{a_j}$ by additivity. For every proper coordinate $0<s_j<a_j$, one has $p^{s_j}e_j\in W_{s_j}$. The corresponding height record gives some $c_j\in C_*$ with $p^{s_j}c_j\bmod K_*=\delta(p^{s_j}e_j)$. The hypothesis on this coordinate makes $p^{a_j}c_j=0$. At $s_j=a_j$ choose zero. Thus these assignments give an additive map $A\to C_*$ inducing $\delta$ on $U$ modulo $K_*$. Changing sign gives the correcting shift.

The oracle procedure can recover which heights to use from the tables without knowing an adapted basis. For $k,r\ge0$ put $F(k,r)=\log_p |p^r(U\cap p^k A)|$. In a diagonal decomposition this equals $\sum_j\max(a_j-\max(s_j,k)-r,0)$. The difference $F(k,r)-F(k+1,r)$ counts the coordinates with $s_j\le k$ and $a_j>k+r$. Taking the difference of these counts at $r$ and $r+1$ gives the number with $s_j\le k$ and $a_j=k+r+1$. Thus for every $k<a_j$ the cumulative counts, and then their successive differences in $k$ at fixed $a_j$, recover the multiplicities of every nonzero type $(a_j,s_j)$. It suffices to run these computations for $k,r$ up to $\log_p\exp A$. Their equality across a positive comparison follows because the subgroup inclusion data are preserved. The correction itself is computed by the linear system of Section 1.

For homocyclic $A$ of exponent $p^e$, the needed diagonalization follows by applying integer Smith reduction to the preimage of $U$ in $\mathbb Z^n$ and reducing the ambient basis modulo $p^e$. The positive proper heights are the Smith valuations less than $e$. If $C$ is killed by $p^e$, the proper-coordinate order condition holds. In particular $C=[G,G]$ is killed by $\exp A$ because the values of the bracket generate $C$.

Suppose instead that $p^2A=p^2C=0$, with arbitrary mixed cyclic orders. If $u\in U$ has order $p^2$, at least one order-$p^2$ coordinate of $u$ is a unit. The projection onto such a coordinate, rescaled by that unit, is a map to $\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z$ taking $u$ to 1. Splitting off $\langle u\rangle$ with this map splits both $A$ and $U$. Repeat until the remaining $U$ is killed by $p$. On the remaining ambient group use a decomposition $A=(\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z)^b\oplus(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^d$, so $A[p]=pA\oplus(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^d$. Choose bases on $pA$ and the second summand to put $U\cap pA$ and the projection of $U$ to the second summand on coordinate subspaces. Basis changes on $pA$ lift to the order-$p^2$ summands by lifting a matrix invertible modulo $p$. After these changes, the remaining part of $U$ is a graph from its projected subspace in the second summand to $pA$, modulo $U\cap pA$. Subtract this graph by an additive shear from the second summand to $pA$, extending it from the projected subspace by zero on a complement. This gives the claimed adapted basis. Every proper coordinate now has $(a_j,s_j)=(2,1)$, so its order kills $C$, and a single quotient record at height 1 suffices even when $K$ is nonsplit.

### A fixed-pair check on the height conditions

Use the additive group $G=\langle a\rangle\oplus\langle z\rangle\oplus\langle w\rangle$ of cyclic orders $4,8,2$, respectively, with specified $C=\langle z,w\rangle$. Put $K=\langle2z+w\rangle$, $H=\langle2a,K\rangle$, and $H_*=\langle2a+w,K\rangle$. Both marks have image $\langle2a+C\rangle$ in $A=G/C$ and central intersection $K$. Their own presentations agree via the identity on $U,K$, since $2a$ and $2a+w$ each have order 2. The only possibly nontrivial height record has $k=1$. It is identical for the two marks because $w\in 2C+K$, so adding $2C$ to either mark gives the same subgroup. Yet no transporter induces the identity on both $A,C$. Any lift of that pair changes $2a$ by an element of $2C[4]=\langle4z\rangle$, contained in $K$, while $w\notin K$. This is a fixed-pair check with specified central subgroup. It exhibits the need for an extension hypothesis when using these height records alone.

## 4. Compile the tests into ordinary decision

All tests above compare a bounded number of central presentations together with a bounded diagram of finite abelian modules and additive arrows. For example, in Section 3 each quotient presentation is tied to the ambient one by the quotient arrows $A\to A_k$, $C\to C_k$, and the actual subgroup images are tied by the inclusions $U\hookrightarrow A$, $K\hookrightarrow C$. Thus the maps on $A_k,C_k$ have no independent freedom. Each named module or extension has size at most $N$. The named modules and coordinate operations are recoverable in polynomial time from the tables by abelian presentations and Smith normal form.

Here is a compilation using the paired marker and exact dual frame of the [class-two black-box reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowResearchSynthesis/CLASS-TWO-BLACK-BOX.md) and [central-shift normalizer reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ClassTwoOracleApplications/CENTRAL-SHIFT-NORMALIZERS.md). Let $R=\mathbb Z/p^b\mathbb Z$ kill all named modules, with $p^b\le N$. In the primal carrier $E$, use a separate block for each named module, including a copy of every module occurring as an extension's central output. Place each extension bracket on its quotient block with values in a dedicated central summand, storing its specified basis powers in that same central summand. Couple the extension's central summand to its named primal copy with an identity pairing $B_{\rm primal}\times R\to B_{\rm central}$ on a new free cyclic anchor. For an additive arrow $a:X\to Y$, add two more free cyclic anchors. In a fresh central copy of $Y$, record both the skew extension of $X\times R\to Y$, $(x,r)\mapsto ra(x)$, on the first anchor, and the skew extension of $Y\times R\to Y$, $(y,r)\mapsto ry$, on the second. These input supports distinguish the output copies by their bracket images. The identity pairings generate the intended central output even for extensions whose commutator values span only a proper subgroup of their given central subgroup.

Include two further named free cyclic anchors $u,v$ whose bracket is $c$ in a new free central summand and whose power relation is $u^{p^b}=c$. Apply the exact dual frame to the covectors of all anchors and any specified ordered prefixes of cyclic dual bases of $A,C$. The frame gives a common unit multiplier on all named covectors, and the bracket and power equations for $u,v$ pin it to 1. The identity and arrow pairings then impose exactly compatibility with every arrow. In particular the link to a primally named copy enforces the same central map on the extension output and in the marker diagram.

Use the paired private markers on each named block and on the frame carrier to recover each block as an intersection of contraction images. The rank bands on the top torsion layer and the exact image-cardinality argument of the cited reductions also identify the quotient onto the full core, including the frame carrier, and the base central output modulo auxiliary marker outputs. Modulo those auxiliary outputs, the added auxiliary carrier contributes zero in each power record on its $m$-torsion. The recorded central extensions are therefore recovered by restricting the projected power records to their corresponding named quotient blocks. Conversely, compatible diagram maps extend by the identity on the fixed anchors and frame slots, the inverse duals on frame outputs and private paired modules, and the identity on heavy marker modules. The extended maps preserve the bracket. The additional standalone blocks other than $u$ have zero intrinsic brackets and basis powers. On the original quotient blocks and the pinning anchor the chosen powers are preserved as residues by construction. Section 1 now gives an ordinary isomorphism of the oracle groups.

There are constantly many named blocks and ordinary arrow anchors. The exact dual frame uses logarithmic size $O(\log_p |E|)$ by the bounded-degree ordering of its basis slots. Write $E_0$ for the combined core with the frame carrier and $r_b=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(p^{b-1}E_0)$. The total free marker rank is $O(r_b+1)$ for a fixed number of blocks. Its cost is $O(b(r_b+1))=O(\log_p |E_0|)$, since each top cyclic summand contributes $b$ to $\log_p |E_0|$ and the anchors have order $p^b$. The central and primal carrier logarithms are each $O(\log_p N)$, so constructing the ordinary Cayley tables by the collecting law takes polynomial time and gives order $N^{O(1)}$. Nonmatching required module types, cyclic-basis orders, or Smith heights can be rejected before the oracle call. Trivial groups can be checked directly.

## 5. Search, count and generate

The compiler accepts optional exact dual-prefix marks on $A,C$. Starting with an empty prefix and keeping each accepted next image, use all possible target images of each next cyclic dual basis generator until a full pair of isomorphisms is recovered. The dual groups have the same sizes as $A,C$. A candidate prefix extends to a cyclic basis of the required type exactly when the orders are respected and the projected columns in each equal-order diagonal block modulo $p$ are linearly independent. Such columns complete to a basis modulo $p$, and an order-respecting map invertible modulo $p$ is invertible on the finite $p$-module. Thus the exact frame applies throughout the search. Upon recovering the pair, use the central power test and the linear shift system of Section 1 to produce an actual marked transporter.

On equal inputs the admissible pairs form a group, and their action on full dual cyclic bases of $A,C$ is faithful. Compute the orbit size of each successive basis generator under the subgroup fixing the previous generators by the same prefix tests. The product of the orbit sizes counts the admissible pairs. Completing one accepted prefix for each orbit point produces stabilizer-chain generators of that group. Multiply the admissible-pair count by $|\ker\rho|$ to count the actual marked automorphisms. Lift the pair generators by the common shift system and append a generating set of $\ker\rho$ to generate all marked automorphisms. When the inputs differ and a transporter exists, all transporters form a coset of the source marked automorphism group and have that same cardinality.
