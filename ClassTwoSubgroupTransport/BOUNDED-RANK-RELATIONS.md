# Subgroup transport from bounded-rank tensor relations

**Theorem.** Fix nonnegative integers $r$ and $b$. Let $G$ and $G'$ be finite groups of class at most two given by Cayley tables. Let $C\le Z(G)$ and $C'\le Z(G')$ contain the respective commutator subgroups. Suppose $A=G/C$, $C$, $A'=G'/C'$ and $C'$ are elementary abelian $2$-groups. The identity group is allowed. On each side, specify $r$ actual subgroups $H_i$ in order. Put $U_i=\pi(H_i)$, $K_i=H_i\cap C$, and $L_i=K_i^\perp\le C^*=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb F_2}(C,\mathbb F_2)$. Use primes for the analogous target data. Assume that on both sides the kernel
$$
\mathcal R=\ker\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^r U_i\otimes L_i
 \ \longrightarrow\ A\otimes C^*,\quad (T_i)_i\longmapsto\sum_i T_i\right)
$$
is generated by tuples with $\operatorname{rank}(T_i)\le b$ for every $i$. The hypothesis can be checked in deterministic polynomial time for the fixed $r,b$.

Decision, search, exact counting and stabilizer generation for isomorphisms carrying $C$ to $C'$ and each $H_i$ to $H_i'$ reduce to ordinary class-two $2$-group isomorphism decision in deterministic polynomial time. If both input groups have order at most $N$, all query groups have order $N^{O_{r,b}(1)}$. In fact the query groups have exponent dividing $4$. Taking the named central subgroup to be the derived subgroup or the center makes its preservation automatic. The rank hypothesis holds in particular if either subspace arrangement $(U_i)_i$ or $(K_i)_i$ splits simultaneously over a direct-sum decomposition with each ambient summand of dimension at most $b$.

The construction below is a written proof using the paired-marker and projective-frame compiler. The rank hypothesis concerns a vector space of relation tensors, which is never materialized as the underlying Cayley set of a query group. ## 1. The compatibility problem for the lifts

All modules through Section 4 are over $\mathbb F_2$. A group isomorphism preserving the named center induces maps $\alpha:A\to A'$ and $\gamma:C\to C'$. Its necessary and sufficient conditions are preservation of the commutator bimap $\beta:A^2\to C$ and the square map $q:A\to C$. In fact $q(a)=x^2$ for any lift $x$ of $a$, since $C$ is central of exponent two. The polar of $q$ is $\beta$. To lift $\alpha,\gamma$, choose any lifts of the prescribed images of a basis of $A$. They satisfy the original commutators and squares and so give the desired isomorphism. These relations present the source group by collecting to one basis-ordered word for each quotient coordinate and one central element. Any two lifts differ by a central shift $x\mapsto x\,s(\pi x)$ for $s\in\operatorname{Hom}(A,C)$. The same description using target coordinates applies to lifts to $G'$.

For each $H_i$, the subgroup $H_i/K_i$ gives a unique homomorphic section $s_i:U_i\to G/K_i$ of the quotient above $U_i$. Suppose $\alpha,\gamma$ preserve all the $U_i,K_i$ and start with any lift $f:G\to G'$ of the pair. At $u\in U_i$, define $\delta_i(u)\in C'/K_i'$ as the difference between the section transported by $f$ and $s_i'(\alpha u)$. It is linear because the two sections are homomorphisms with identical quotient map and their pointwise difference is central. The pair $\alpha,\gamma$ lifts to an isomorphism carrying all the actual subgroups precisely when $(\delta_i)_i$ is in the image of
$$
 \rho:\operatorname{Hom}(A,C')\longrightarrow\bigoplus_i\operatorname{Hom}(U_i,C'/K_i'),
 \qquad s\longmapsto (s|_{U_i}\bmod K_i')_i.
$$
A correction in this equation composes $f$ with one shift using source quotient coordinates. After pulling target covectors back along $\gamma$, the dual of $\rho$ is the displayed sum map whose kernel is $\mathcal R$. Here we use the perfect pairing between $\operatorname{Hom}(U,D)$ and $U\otimes D^*$, given on a decomposable tensor by evaluation. Thus the lift exists exactly when the discrepancy functional on relation tensors is zero:
$$
 \sum_{i,j} b_{ij}'(\delta_i(u_{ij}))=0
 \quad\text{whenever}\quad
 \sum_{i,j}u_{ij}\otimes b_{ij}=0,\qquad b_{ij}'=b_{ij}\gamma^{-1}\in(K_i')^\perp.
$$
This criterion uses a common shift for all marks.

## 2. Quadratic records for bounded-rank tests

Take $m=rb$ slots, with $b$ belonging to each mark. Write $i(j)$ for the mark of slot $j$. For every subspace $P\le\mathbb F_2^m$, make the two modules
$$
 X_P=\left\{(u_j)_j\in\bigoplus_j U_{i(j)}:
             \sum_j w_j u_j=0\ \forall w\in P\right\},
$$
$$
 Y_P=\left\{(b_j)_j\in\bigoplus_j L_{i(j)}:
             (b_j)_j\in P\otimes C^*\right\}.
$$
The tensor condition on $(b_j)$ uses the usual standard basis of $\mathbb F_2^m$. Fix a basis $w_1,\ldots,w_d$ of $P$, the same on both sides. For $b=(b_j)_j\in Y_P$, write $b_j=\sum_\ell (w_\ell)_j d_\ell$ with uniquely determined $d_\ell\in C^*$. For $u=(u_j)_j\in X_P$ choose arbitrary $h_j\in H_{i(j)}$ above $u_j$. Define
$$
 Q_P(u)(b)=\sum_{\ell=1}^d d_\ell\left(\prod_{j=1}^m h_j^{(w_\ell)_j}\right)
 \quad\in\mathbb F_2 .
$$
The products are in increasing slot order. Each product is in $C$ by the defining relation for $X_P$. Changing $h_j$ by $k_j\in K_{i(j)}$ changes the expression by $\sum_j b_j(k_j)=0$. The basis of $P$ is fixed as part of the construction, so no independence from that basis is needed. For fixed $u$, the expression is linear in $b$ and defines $Q_P(u)\in Y_P^*$.

For $u,v\in X_P$ one can use $h_j(u_j)h_j(v_j)$ as the lift for $u_j+v_j$. Gathering first the factors from $u$ and then the factors from $v$ adds only central commutators. The polar of $Q_P$ therefore has evaluation
$$
  (Q_P(u+v)-Q_P(u)-Q_P(v))(b)
     =\sum_{\ell=1}^d d_\ell\left(
       \sum_{j>k}(w_\ell)_j(w_\ell)_k\,\beta(u_j,v_k)\right).
$$
Either sign of the commutator is the same in $C$. This is bilinear in $u,v$. It is alternating because $Q_P(0)=0$ and its domain and output have exponent two. Thus $Q_P:X_P\to Y_P^*$ is an ordinary vector-valued quadratic map.

The projected maps on $U_i$ and $L_i$ induce maps on $X_P,Y_P$ by applying the same maps slot by slot. An arbitrary lift $f$ of an admissible $\alpha,\gamma$ changes $Q_P$ by the discrepancy functional
$$
 Q_P'( (\alpha u_j)_j )((b_j\gamma^{-1})_j)-Q_P(u)(b)
     =\sum_j (b_j\gamma^{-1})(\delta_{i(j)}(u_j)).
$$
Indeed, apply $f$ to each ordered product and replace its factors by chosen target section lifts. The central correction for the $j$-th factor is paired in aggregate with $b_j\gamma^{-1}$, which kills $K_{i(j)}'$. This gives the displayed identity independently of representatives.

For every $u\in X_P$ and $b\in Y_P$, the tuple $T_i=\sum_{j:i(j)=i}u_j\otimes b_j$ lies in $\mathcal R$ and has component ranks at most $b$. Conversely, write any tuple in $\mathcal R$ with these rank bounds in this form, padding with zero terms. Define $X\colon\mathbb F_2^m\to A$ by $e_j\mapsto u_j$ and $Y:C\to\mathbb F_2^m$ by $c\mapsto(b_j(c))_j$. The relation means $XY=0$. Taking $P=\ker X$ gives $u\in X_P,\ b\in Y_P$. By the rank hypothesis, preserving all the $Q_P$ now implies the vanishing of the full discrepancy functional, hence suffices for a common shift. A true subgroup transporter always preserves every $Q_P$.

## 3. Compiling the records to an ordinary group

Use one named primal block for each module in the finite diagram, including $A,C,C^*$, the $U_i,K_i,L_i$, the $X_P,Y_P$, and a primal copy of each $Y_P^*$. The named linear maps are the inclusions $U_i\hookrightarrow A,\ K_i\hookrightarrow C,\ L_i\hookrightarrow C^*$ and the individual coordinate maps from $X_P$ to the relevant $U_i$ and from $Y_P$ to the relevant $L_i$. Compatible block maps on $X_P,Y_P$ are uniquely determined by their coordinate maps, since the coordinate maps are jointly injective.

To realize a linear map $F:X\to Y$, put a central output copy $\overline Y$ for each relevant target block. Bracket $Y$ with a fresh pinned line to recover the identification $Y\simeq\overline Y$. Bracket $X$ with another fresh pinned line to realize $F:X\to\overline Y$. Recover the central summand as the image of the first bracket and both displayed maps from these two labelled pairs. An elementary scalar output line $Rc$, with $R=\mathbb F_2$, carries the perfect pairings between $C,C^*$ and between $Y_P,Y_P^*$. Once $c$ is fixed, these bracket records force the indicated maps to act by duality. Pair each primal $Y_P^*$ with another fresh pinned line to identify its action with an output summand for the quadratic record. Pair the primal $C$ with a pinned line to identify the central output for the original commutator and square records with the actual $C$ action. All these central outputs are generated by their anchoring brackets even when a polar map alone is degenerate.

Put the bracket given by the polar of $Q_P$ on $X_P$ with values in the copy of $Y_P^*$ in the center. Give each basis lift of $X_P$ square equal to $Q_P$ on that basis point. Use the original commutator and squares on $A$ with values in the central copy of $C$. All remaining new cyclic basis lifts have zero square except for the usual frame anchor $u^2=c$ with $[u,v]=c$. With elementary domain and elementary center, the projected $q_2$ is the actual square. A basis and the polar determine a quadratic map by repeated expansion. Therefore these power assignments, together with the recorded polars, preserve exactly the $Q_P$.

Use the paired markers of the class-two compiler to recover every named primal block. Use its projective cyclic frame for the anchor lines and any conditional prefixes of bases of $A^*,C^*$. In this elementary setting a projective line has exactly one nonzero vector. The power pin also realizes the usual general compiler convention. The compiler recovers the dual core by intersections of the images of paired private markers. Its projected center discards their private outputs. The discarded coordinates change no projected squares, since the other auxiliary basis lifts have square zero. Every isomorphism of the ordinary query groups thus induces matched $\alpha,\gamma$ preserving the old squares, all marked linear diagrams and all $Q_P$. Conversely such $\alpha,\gamma$ determine maps on the diagram, fixing the anchors and respecting all brackets and powers. These maps extend over the paired blocks by inverse duals and over the heavy blocks by the identity, yielding an ordinary query isomorphism.

There are finitely many $P$ depending only on $r,b$. Each of $X_P,Y_P$ has dimension at most $m(\dim A+\dim C)$. The one-dimensional anchors and the number of named blocks are bounded in terms of $r,b$. Each marker contributes $O_{r,b}(\dim A+\dim C+1)$ to the logarithm of the query order. The frame uses linear total size in its basis prefix and fixed anchors. Hence the oracle group has order $2^{O_{r,b}(\dim A+\dim C+1)}$, or $N^{O_{r,b}(1)}$ for nontrivial inputs. A comparison of two trivial groups needs no query. Cayley tables come from the basis collecting formula in the cited compiler. Pairwise comparisons can reject mismatched named module dimensions before invoking the compiler. All module arithmetic has polynomial cost in the input order.

The rank hypothesis is recognizable in the same fixed-parameter polynomial sense. Compute a basis of $\mathcal R$ as the nullspace of the explicit sum of tensor component spaces in matrix coordinates. Its dimension can be quadratic in $\log N$. Enumerate the vector pairs in $X_P\times Y_P$ for each $P$ and span their resulting relation tensors by binary linear elimination. Each product has size at most $2^{m(\dim A+\dim C)}$. Equality of the resulting span with $\mathcal R$ is the required test. Outside this scope a rank test alone can miss a genuine incompatibility, so a failed hypothesis test returns outside the scope of this reduction.

## 4. Witnesses, counts and generators

Run conditional decision with exact prescribed dual basis prefixes on $A^*,C^*$ to recover both induced maps, testing at most $|A|+|C|$ candidates per basis position. Once the full pair is known, construct one arbitrary lift via the basis powers and brackets, compute its discrepancy maps and solve the affine system for $\rho$ by Gaussian elimination. The resulting central correction gives an actual transporter.

For counting, the image of the decorated automorphism group on $A^*,C^*$ has order equal to the product of its prefix-stabilizer orbit sizes. The same decisions find those orbit sizes and allow one representative per orbit point to be completed. Over each admissible pair the lifts form a coset of $\ker\rho$, of size $2^{\dim\ker\rho}$. Thus the product of the image order and this factor is the exact count. The lifts of the chain representatives, together with elementary shifts from a basis of $\ker\rho$, generate the full stabilizer. A feasible transporter between two inputs is a coset of the source stabilizer, so its count and a representative follow by the same procedures.

If $A=\bigoplus_\ell A_\ell$ is a common splitting of the $U_i$ with $\dim A_\ell\le b$, projecting to $A_\ell$ decomposes every relation tuple into relation tuples of component rank at most $b$. If $C=\bigoplus_\ell C_\ell$ is a common splitting of the $K_i$, dualizing gives the corresponding common splitting of the $L_i$ and the same argument on the second tensor factor. Neither splitting needs to be supplied to the algorithm.

## 5. Four marks can require unbounded relation rank

For every $d\ge2$, take $A=X\oplus X$ and $C^*=X\oplus X$ for $X=\mathbb F_2^d$. Let $M$ be the companion matrix of an irreducible degree-$d$ polynomial over $\mathbb F_2$. For the four $U_i$ take the horizontal and vertical summands and the graphs of $I$ and $M$. For the four $L_i$ use the same summands and the graphs of $I$ and $M^\mathsf T$. Use $K_i=L_i^\perp$ in $C$. Each isomorphic copy of $X\otimes X$ has $d$-by-$d$ matrix coordinates $T_i$. The two off-diagonal blocks of the relation equation force
$$
 T_3=MT_4=T_4M .
$$
The diagonal blocks then give $T_1=T_3+T_4$, $T_2=T_3+MT_4M$. Thus the relation space is parametrized by matrices commuting with $M$. Every such matrix is multiplication by an element of $\mathbb F_2[M]\cong\mathbb F_{2^d}$, because it is determined by its action on a cyclic vector and then on the basis of its powers under $M$. Every nonzero $T_4$ is therefore invertible. All nonzero relation tuples have fourth component rank $d$.

The missing relation rank can affect actual subgroups. In the elementary abelian group $G=A\oplus C$, give one input the zero-section marks $U_i\oplus K_i$. In the second input replace only the first mark by the graph modulo $K_1$ of a linear map $U_1\to C/K_1$. Choose the map to pair nontrivially with the first component of the relation $T_4=I$. That component is $M+I\ne0$, so the perfect pairing permits the choice. Any transporter would carry all the graphs by one common shift after composing with its induced base maps. Every induced base pair preserving the projected marks produces zero discrepancy for the source zero sections, so the target functional must vanish on the target relation space. It does not. There is no transporter, although every rank test with $b<d$ is zero. This example delimits the rank hypothesis of this construction.
