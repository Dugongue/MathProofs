# Optimal actions for scalar-center coprime extensions

Fix an odd prime \(p\) and let \(P\) be a normal Sylow \(p\)-subgroup of a finite group \(G\), of class at most two and exponent \(p\). Suppose that conjugation by a complement \(C\) acts on \(Z(P)\) by scalars. From the Cayley table, a minimum faithful permutation action can be constructed in deterministic polynomial time whenever all subgroups of \(C\) are generated by at most a fixed number \(r\). This includes all cyclic complements, even when their action on \(P/Z(P)\) is nonsplit. The result also holds for abelian \(C\) of exponent dividing \(p-1\), without a rank bound. This is a nonnilpotent Cayley-table case of the problem in Kantor–Luks §13. [Source problem](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/quotient.pdf#page=9) · [Earlier abelian Hall-kernel result](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightEleventh/groups.md#2-minimum-faithful-actions-for-abelian-by-abelian-hall-extensions)

## Separate the kernel of the complement action

The normal Sylow factor is recovered by testing element orders. A complement is constructive: split \(G/[P,P]\) by averaging the factor cocycle in the abelian kernel \(P/[P,P]\), then split the preimage of the complement over the abelian kernel \([P,P]\) by the same procedure. The inverses of the complement order exist on both kernels. Every subgroup of \(G=P\rtimes C\) is \(P\)-conjugate to \(W\rtimes B\) for \(B\le C\) and \(B\)-invariant \(W\le P\), by applying existence of a complement to that subgroup and conjugacy of complements in \(P\rtimes B\). [Schur–Zassenhaus, including conjugacy for a solvable kernel](https://web.mat.bham.ac.uk/D.A.Craven/docs/lectures/finitegroups2012.pdf)

Put \(K=\ker(C\to\operatorname{Aut}(P))\), normal in \(G\). A normal action kernel disjoint from \(P\) centralizes \(P\), because its commutators with \(P\) belong to both. Also \(C_G(P)=Z(P)\times K\): an automorphism from \(C\) that is inner on \(P\) has order both prime to \(p\) and a power of \(p\). Taking powers in the coprime direct product shows that any normal action kernel disjoint from \(P\) lies in \(K\).

The minimum degree decomposes exactly as
\[
\mu(G)=\mu(G/K)+\mu(C;K),
\]
where the second term denotes the smallest degree of a \(C\)-action faithful on \(K\), with the empty action allowed when \(K=1\). To prove this, consider an orbit with stabilizer \(W\rtimes B\), \(W<P\). If \(K\not\le B\), replace it by stabilizers \(W\rtimes BK\) and \(P\rtimes B\). The first has the same trace on \(P\) and is valid since \(K\) centralizes \(P\). The second retains the old core trace on \(K\), namely \(K\cap\operatorname{core}_C B\). If \(w=[P:W]\ge2\) and \(b=[BK:B]\ge2\), the degree comparison is \(w+b\le wb\) after removing the factor \([C:BK]\). Orbits with \(W=P\) already factor through \(C\). The new orbits detecting \(P\) factor through the effective quotient and their kernel there is trivial, by its effective action and the centralizer argument. The quotient orbits and the relative \(C\)-orbits give the required lower inequality, and their disjoint union gives the upper inequality.

The same additive identity holds for any split coprime extension with a solvable normal kernel. Schur–Zassenhaus gives the same stabilizer normal form, and the coprime-order argument still forces the centralizer of the kernel to be its center times the ineffective complement. The orbit replacement uses only these two facts.

For the bounded subgroup-rank case enumerate all candidate \(B\) from \(r\)-tuples. For the relative \(C\)-action, keep the intersection of \(K\) with the cores of the selected subgroups as a shortest-path state. These are subgroups of \(C\), so the state set is polynomial. Adding \(B\) costs \([C:B]\) and intersects the state with \(\operatorname{core}_C B\). For abelian \(C\), substitute the elementary cyclic-character algorithm: split each stabilizer annihilator into cyclic prime-power factors without increasing the cost, then choose minimum-weight bases of restrictions to \(K[q]\) for every prime \(q\), weighting a character by its order. This uses at most \(|C|\) characters. A quotient of a group of subgroup-generator rank at most \(r\) still has that rank bound.

## A symplectic problem for the effective quotient

It suffices to work with \(C\) acting faithfully on \(P\). Every nontrivial kernel now meets \(Z=Z(P)\), by the centralizer argument and the class equation for \(P\). Because \(C\) acts by scalars, every subgroup of \(Z\) is normal in \(G\). The central-hyperplane splitting proof works unchanged, using these normal subgroups, and a minimum action is a minimum-weight basis of nonzero functionals \(\lambda\) on \(Z\). There are at most \(|P|\) such marks.

Identify \(P\) with its class-two Lie algebra over \(\mathbb F_p\), using the product corrected by half its central commutator as vector addition. Its subgroups are exactly the bracket-closed vector subspaces, and automorphisms act linearly. Choose a \(C\)-invariant complement \(V\) to \(Z\) by averaging a projection. For fixed \(\lambda\) and \(B\le C\), a largest \(B\)-invariant subgroup with central trace \(\ker\lambda\) is obtained by taking that hyperplane and a largest \(B\)-invariant totally isotropic subspace of \(V\) for
\[
\beta_\lambda(u,v)=\lambda([u,v]).
\]
Indeed the projection of any such subgroup is isotropic, and the direct sum with the hyperplane achieves every admissible projection. The radical of the form is \(B\)-invariant because \(B\) acts by similitudes. Include it and pass to the nondegenerate quotient.

In a nondegenerate alternating similitude module the following greedy step is optimal. If a nonzero simple invariant isotropic subspace \(S\) exists, some maximum-dimensional invariant isotropic \(U\) contains \(S\). When \(U\cap S=0\), pairing with \(S\) gives a map from \(U\) to the twisted dual of \(S\), which is simple. The map is nonzero as otherwise \(U+S\) is a larger isotropic space. It is therefore onto. The new subspace \((U\cap S^\perp)+S\) is invariant and isotropic, and it has the same dimension as \(U\). After choosing \(S\) recurse on \(S^\perp/S\) and lift. One finds a simple \(S\) by enumerating nonzero vectors and taking a nonzero isotropic orbit span of smallest dimension. If no such span exists there is no nonzero invariant isotropic space. Each vector space has at most \(|P|\) elements, so the algorithm is polynomial in the Cayley-table length.

The resulting orbit cost is \([C:B]p^{1+\dim V-\dim U}\). Minimize over the listed \(B\)'s and take a minimum-weight basis of central marks. If \(C\) is abelian of exponent dividing \(p-1\), simultaneous eigenspaces are defined over \(\mathbb F_p\). A nondegenerate alternating similitude module has an invariant Lagrangian: distinct character spaces that pair are dual in pairs and one whole side can be chosen. On a self-paired character space, the action is scalar and any ordinary Lagrangian works. Thus in this abelian split case \(B=C\) always attains the cheapest cost, and the effective action has minimum degree \(\mu(P)\) without subgroup enumeration.

For a nonsplit example take the order-27 Heisenberg group, with a cyclic group of order four fixing its center and acting through an irreducible order-four matrix of determinant one on \(\mathbb F_3^2\). The full complement has no invariant isotropic line, giving cost 27. Its index-two subgroup acts as the scalar \(-1\), giving cost 18. The trivial subgroup gives cost 36. Thus the minimum degree is 18, versus 9 for the normal Sylow group alone.
