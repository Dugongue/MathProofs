# A sharp small-orbit threshold for minimum faithful transitive degree

Write \(\tau(G)\) for the least degree of a faithful transitive action. For every fixed prime \(p\), deciding \(\tau(G)\le T\) for a binary threshold \(T\) is NP-complete in the permutation-generator model, even for class-two groups supplied on an already minimum-degree faithful action with all orbits of size \(p^2\). The hard groups have exponent \(p\) at odd primes and exponent four at \(p=2\). A two-sided multiplicative approximation of \(\tau\) within \(p^{c_p D}\) is NP-hard for some constant \(c_p>0\), where \(D\) is the supplied permutation degree. At supplied orbit cap \(p\), the exact transitive degree is polynomial-time computable for every permutation \(p\)-group. This gives a sharp threshold for the transitive version of the optimization. [Related unconstrained problem and earlier small-orbit thresholds](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/MinimumDegreeCrossovers)

## Realizing a graph in a group with a minimum supplied action

Fix a simple graph \(\Gamma\) without isolated vertices, with \(v\) vertices and \(e\) edges. If starting with a general graph, delete its isolated vertices and subtract their known contribution from the target independence number. Use one copy of the order-\(p^3\) exponent-\(p\) Heisenberg group per edge at odd \(p\), and one copy of \(D_8\) per edge at \(p=2\). In either case use standard generators \(a,b\) of order \(p\), with their commutator \(z\) central of order \(p\). Use the faithful degree-\(p^2\) coset action on each factor, by the subgroup generated by \(a\).

Orient each edge once. For every vertex \(i\), give a global generator with entry \(a\) at its outgoing edge coordinates, entry \(b\) at its incoming edge coordinates, and identity elsewhere. Let \(G_\Gamma\) be the group these permutations generate on the disjoint edge orbits. The commutator of the two endpoint generators of an edge yields its independent central element, trivial on all other factors. The images in the product modulo those edge centers are independent, since each vertex occurs on some edge and the two endpoint coordinates are independent in that factor. Thus \(|G_\Gamma|=p^{v+e}\) and its derived subgroup is the product of the \(e\) edge centers. The common radical of the commutator forms on the \(v\) vertex coordinates is zero, because any nonzero coordinate can be paired with a neighbor. So the derived subgroup is also the whole center.

The supplied degree \(D=p^2e\) equals \(\mu(G_\Gamma)\). For the lower bound, refine a minimum faithful action to central hyperplanes and a basis of their marks. Such a basis has \(e\) marks. Each marked orbit has index at least \(p^2\) because every index-\(p\) subgroup of a \(p\)-group contains the derived subgroup. Faithfulness and the matching upper bound are supplied by the explicit action above. Each projection is all of its edge factor, so every supplied orbit has size exactly \(p^2\). [Central hyperplane refinement](commutator-parameter.md)

## The exact transitive degree

A subgroup is core-free exactly when it meets the center trivially, as every nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite \(p\)-group meets its center. Here such a subgroup injects into the vertex space \(\mathbb F_p^v\), and the image has zero commutator on every edge. For a given subspace of dimension \(k\), form a matrix whose rows are a basis. Choose \(k\) independent columns. No pair of their vertex indices is an edge: the two-column projection on an edge has rank at most one by the vanishing of that edge's alternating commutator. Hence the dimension is at most \(\alpha(\Gamma)\). Conversely the generators on an independent set commute, have order \(p\), and freely span the elementary abelian subgroup that projects onto that coordinate space. It meets the center trivially. This also handles the binary square-map constraint because those generators are commuting involutions. Therefore
\[
\tau(G_\Gamma)=p^{v+e-\alpha(\Gamma)}.
\]
The underlying alternating-space independence argument is due to Bei–Chen–Guan–Qiao–Sun. The reduction here uses it to separate the two action objectives on an already optimal small-orbit input. [Graphical alternating spaces, Theorem 3](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol151-itcs2020/LIPIcs.ITCS.2020.8/LIPIcs.ITCS.2020.8.pdf)

The threshold reduction sets \(T=p^{v+e-k}\) to test \(\alpha(\Gamma)\ge k\). All group generators are polynomial-size explicit permutations for fixed \(p\). Membership in NP under the class-two promise uses a compact generating set for the proposed stabilizer \(H\). Check membership of those generators, its order and \([G:H]\le T\) by standard permutation-group algorithms. Check \(H\cap Z(G)=1\) by the homomorphism taking \(h\) to its commutators with the supplied generators of \(G\). In class two it factors through \(H/[H,H]\) into a product of abelian derived groups. The kernel over the abelian section is computable by linear equations, and \([H,H]\) itself lies in the kernel, so triviality can be checked in polynomial time using the same abelian section calculations as in the earlier class-two algorithm. [Compact section calculations](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowGroupFrontier/MinimumAction.md#5-assemble-and-bound-the-algorithm)

For the approximation claim, the Berman–Karpinski degree-three gap has independence numbers separated by \(\Omega(v)\) and \(e\le3v/2\), with any deleted isolated-vertex count known. The two transitive degrees differ by a factor \(p^{\Omega(v)}=p^{\Omega(D)}\). A two-sided estimator of error below the square root of this gap distinguishes the cases. The exponent scale is attained by a trivial upper estimator on permutation \(p\)-groups, since the regular action gives degree at most \(|G|\le p^{D/(p-1)}\). [Berman–Karpinski, Theorem 3](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/TOPICS/pcp/BK99.pdf)

Finally, when every supplied orbit has at most \(p\) points, each nontrivial constituent is cyclic of order \(p\). The displayed faithful action embeds \(G\) into their product, so \(G\) is elementary abelian. Its only core-free subgroup is the identity, and hence \(\tau(G)=|G|\), computable in polynomial time.
