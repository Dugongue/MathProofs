# A sharp initial segment of Chebyshev tests for permutation conjugacy

Given a permutation on a known number $N$ of points, label each point of least period $d$ with the real number $x_d=\cos(\pi/d)$. For each $k$ from 1 through $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$, count the points whose label satisfies $|T_k(x_d)|=1$, where $T_k$ is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. **Two permutations have identical counts on this prefix exactly when they are conjugate.** For every $N\ge2$, dropping the last test can identify two different conjugacy classes. The bound concerns initial segments of consecutive positive indices.

**Proof.** The cosine identity gives $T_k(x_d)=\cos(k\pi/d)$. Its absolute value is one precisely when $d$ divides $k$, which is precisely when the point returns under the $k$th iterate. This first equivalence applies to periodic points of any self-map and was independently checked in Lean. [Lean proof](Return.lean)

If $a_j$ counts cycles of length $j$, the measured count is $F(k)=\sum_{j\mid k}j a_j$. In increasing order of $k$ the earlier proper divisors are already known, so subtraction and division by $k$ recover $a_k$. Once all lengths at most $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ are accounted for, any remaining points form one cycle of the remaining length: two remaining cycles would exceed $N$. For sharpness put $m=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ and compare the partitions $(N)$ and $(m,N-m)$. They yield all zero counts before $m$ and distinct counts at $m$. This proves the stated equivalence and sharpness. Fixed-point inversion is classical. The Chebyshev formulation gives a translation between return times and tests on real numbers. Its use presupposes the period labels or equivalent fixed-point observations.
