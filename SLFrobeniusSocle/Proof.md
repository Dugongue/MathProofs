# Reading algebra-isomorphism determinants from the Frobenius socle

Informal proof. Fix a finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$, and consider unital associative Frobenius algebras $A,B$ over $F$ given by multiplication tables in specified bases. A Frobenius functional $\lambda$ means that the bilinear form $\lambda(xy)$ is nondegenerate. For an $F$-algebra isomorphism $\theta:A\to B$, write $\det\theta$ for its determinant in the given bases.

**Theorem.** Suppose $A/\operatorname{rad}A$ has at most a fixed number of simple factors, each isomorphic to a matrix algebra $\operatorname{Mat}_{d_i}(E_i)$ over a finite extension of $F$. Write $b=\max_i d_i$. Specifying $(\det\theta)^2$ reduces deterministically to one ordinary TI instance over $F$, in time polynomial in the dense input length and $2^b$. If $q$ is even, this decides any prescribed $\det\theta$ because squaring on $F^\times$ is bijective.

Over any fixed finite field, the same bound decides the exact determinant if the radical grading has perfect complementary pairings at an odd top degree. This means that for $J=\operatorname{rad}A$, its largest nonzero power $J^s$ has odd $s$, and the forms $\lambda(xy)$ on $J^i/J^{i+1}\times J^{s-i}/J^{s-i+1}$ are nondegenerate. There is also an exact even-top-degree case when the semisimple quotient is commutative and the middle pairing is alternating and nondegenerate. The two-filtration criterion in Section 5 also gives an exact determinant test without a graded Frobenius assumption. The degrees $[E_i:F]$ and radical dimensions have no fixed bound. The ordinary reductions retain the full multiplication equation.

These sufficient classes do not exhaust the triangular algebras of Step 17. The source of the determinant readout is the Frobenius pairing of the whole regular module, with a coordinate change on the small simple parts of its socle. [Frobenius bilinear forms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.6486)

## 1. Calibrate the regular pairing on the socle

Compute the Jacobson radical $J$ and explicit simple factors of $S=A/J$ by deterministic finite-algebra algorithms. For example the decomposition theorem quoted by Ivanyos and Qiao is deterministic in time polynomial in matrix dimension, $\log q$ and the characteristic. View $A^*$ as a left module with $(a\lambda)(x)=\lambda(xa)$. A left cyclic vector is exactly a Frobenius functional, because its regular-module map has equal source and target dimensions. Compute one in polynomial time by Nakayama's lemma. In $A^*/JA^*$, write each component as copies of the natural simple module $E_i^{d_i}$. It is cyclic precisely when the number of copies is at most $d_i$. Pick a vector whose column in each copy is a corresponding distinct coordinate vector and lift. The radical filtration then verifies cyclicity of any such lift. This also detects failure of the target's Frobenius promise. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03495)

Write $K=\{x:Jx=0\}$. In a Frobenius algebra it also equals $\{x:xJ=0\}$. In fact these annihilators are the left and right orthogonal spaces to $J$ under $\lambda(xy)$, by its nondegeneracy and the ideal property of $J$. The identity $\lambda(xy)=\lambda(y\nu(x))$ defines an invertible linear map $\nu$, and rotating two factors shows $\nu(xx')=\nu(x)\nu(x')$. Hence $\nu$ is a unital algebra automorphism preserving $J$ and $K$, so the two orthogonal spaces coincide. It follows that $K$ is a right $S$-module. The map $K\to S^*$ given by $x\mapsto[a\mapsto\lambda(xa)]$ is an isomorphism, first by nondegeneracy and then by dimensions. Under the nondegenerate pairing $(C,D)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{E_i/F}\operatorname{tr}(CD)$, identify $S^*$ with $S$ as a right module. Write $\Psi_A:K_A\to S_A$ for this identification and do the same on $B$.

Fix the two Frobenius functionals $\lambda_A,\lambda_B$. For any actual $\theta$ there is a unique unit $u\in A^\times$ with $\lambda_B\theta(x)=\lambda_A(xu)$. Existence and uniqueness follow from nondegeneracy. Right multiplication $R_u$ is invertible since $\lambda_A(xyu)$ is again a Frobenius pairing. Write $\overline\theta:S_A\to S_B$ and $\overline u\in S_A^\times$ for the induced quotient data. There is an exact identity on the socle:
$$
 \overline\theta^{-1}\Psi_B(\theta x)=\overline u\,\Psi_A(x)
 \qquad (x\in K_A).
$$
Indeed evaluate both at $a\in S_A$ using the trace pairing. The left evaluates to $\lambda_B\theta(xa)$ since matrix and field traces are preserved by the quotient isomorphism. The right evaluates to $\lambda_A(xau)$ by cyclicity of matrix trace. These agree by the definition of $u$. Quotient lifts in the formula are immaterial because $K_AJ=0$.

Let $G_A,G_B$ be the full pairing matrices in the prescribed bases. The matrix equation for $\theta$ gives
$$
 (\det\theta)^2=\frac{\det G_A}{\det G_B}\det_F R_u .
$$
Right multiplication preserves the radical filtration $J^p$. On a natural right simple summand of type $i$ its determinant is $\mathrm N_{E_i/F}(\det_{E_i}\overline u_i)$, so $\det_F R_u$ is the product of these values with exponents equal to their total multiplicities in the radical layers. These multiplicities are computed from the graded modules by matrix units. Thus the exact square character only needs the socle data above.

## 2. Fix the quotient action without losing determinant values

Enumerate the pairings of the simple factors, and the isomorphisms of paired center fields. This is polynomial for a fixed number of factors, since degree-$e$ finite fields have $e$ possible isomorphisms. One way to list them constructively is to decompose the tensor product of the two equal-degree fields over $F$, and use each factor's two inclusions. Each factor of this tensor product is a field of degree $e$.

Every isomorphism between two paired matrix algebras over the centers has the form $\operatorname{Ad}(P)\sigma$, where $\sigma$ denotes its action on entries, and $P$ is an invertible target matrix. Over the center, conjugacy of matrix-unit systems follows by choosing a nonzero vector in the first diagonal image and applying the first-column matrix units. Enumerate just $\mathrm N(\det P)\in F^\times$, and choose a representative $Q=\operatorname{diag}(t,1,\ldots,1)$ with the same norm-determinant. The quotient unit $QP^{-1}$ then has norm-determinant one. Combining such units on every factor and lifting to $B^\times$, compose $\theta$ with conjugation by this lift. On any radical layer, both its left and right actions have determinant one by the same simple-module norm formula. The inner conjugation on $B$ therefore has determinant one. The quotient isomorphism of the corrected $\theta$ is exactly the enumerated $\operatorname{Ad}(Q)\sigma$. Hence these polynomially many branches cover all determinant values. Each branch prescribes its full quotient map, not just its factor pairing.

The norm representatives $t$ can be found deterministically in polynomial time in the center degree at fixed $F$. For each prime $\ell\mid q-1$, find a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity in $E$ and successively take $\ell$-th roots until its order has the largest possible $\ell$-power dividing $|E^\times|$. Each extraction is factoring a separable polynomial of constant degree over $E$. To do this at fixed characteristic, use the algebra defined by the polynomial, find the fixed space of the prime-field Frobenius by linear equations, and split factors by the idempotents obtained from fixed elements outside the diagonal scalar line. An element $x$ of this fixed space has entries in $\mathbb F_p$ in every factor and $1-(x-a)^{p-1}$ is the projection to the entries equal to $a$. The norm of the resulting maximal-order root has the full $\ell$-primary order in $F^\times$. Multiplying these roots yields an element whose norm generates $F^\times$. Enumerate its powers to provide a representative of every value.

## 3. Encode the determinant through ordinary TI

In a branch keep the full multiplication tensor as a bilinear map $A\times A\to A$ with the same sort for all three occurrences. Attach the semisimple quotient map, its prescribed isomorphism and the socle inclusion. For each simple factor take the subspace of $K_A$ that $\Psi_A$ identifies with matrices supported in the first column of that factor. It is an $E_i$-space of dimension $d_i$. Choose the target subspace by using the branch's fixed quotient isomorphism and then $\Psi_B^{-1}$. The socle identity of Section 1 forces the actual isomorphism to map these subspaces and shows in the paired coordinates that its action is left multiplication by $\overline u_i$. Its determinant over $E_i$ is $\det_{E_i}\overline u_i$.

Attach the exterior algebra over $E_i$ of this simple subspace, with its degree-one inclusion and exterior multiplication. A top-line scalar in the chosen volume coordinates is this determinant. The volume vectors are not fixed individually. Record its norm by successive line multiplications to exponent $(q^{[E_i:F]}-1)/(q-1)$. For this operation, two one-dimensional $E_i$-spaces multiply to another such space by the bilinear map defined using their chosen coordinates. An isomorphism scales the output by the product of the scales on the input lines. Repeated squaring computes the exponent with polynomially many lines. The output scalar lies in $F^\times$, so the $F$-subline through the chosen coordinate has the same scale. Multiply these norm lines to the required exponents from Section 1, and pin a vector only in the final product line, calibrated by the known pairing determinants. This implements the prescribed square determinant.

All data are linear and bilinear maps and a fixed number of primary sorts. Ordered coordinate algebras tag multiple occurrences, so the norm steps, exterior grades, subspace inclusions and projection maps of each kind can be packed into one block map, with identity-linked copies when needed. The field structures and bases are linked to the branch calibration. The induced maps on the auxiliary lines and exterior algebras are forced by their multiplications. Conversely every allowed $\theta$ extends to those induced maps. The fixed-size typed tensor-system compiler therefore gives one ordinary TI instance for this branch. Polynomially many branches can be joined by a balanced binary OR, encoded by the multiset identity $\{(a,c),(b,d)\}=\{(a,d),(b,c)\}$ for ordinary isomorphism types. It holds exactly when $a=b$ or $c=d$. Unfolding this balanced expression gives polynomially many colored copies of the leaf systems. A single typed system encodes it by coordinate labels for nodes and incidences, with leaf mode spaces tagged separately. Thus it compiles once to one ordinary TI instance. The exterior algebra has dimension $[E_i:F]2^{d_i}$ over $F$, which accounts for the stated bound. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

## 4. Exact determinant from complementary grades

Let $V_i=J^i/J^{i+1}$, with $J^0=A$. Define the paired form $\beta_i$ on $V_i\times V_{s-i}$ by $\lambda(xy)$, which is well-defined because $J^{s+1}=0$. The perfect-pairing condition is independent of the choice of Frobenius functional: every other such functional has the form $\lambda(xu)$ with $u$ a unit, and the extra $R_u$ on a grade is invertible. Thus any actual isomorphic target inherits the condition. Test it by computing the matrix ranks.

In chosen graded bases each distinct complementary pair satisfies
$$
 \det(\theta_i)\det(\theta_{s-i})=
 \frac{\det \beta_{A,i}}{\det\beta_{B,i}}\,
      \det(R_u|_{V_{s-i}}).
$$
This is the same Gram calculation between two separate spaces, using the socle relation only to read $\overline u$. When $s$ is odd, multiply this identity for $i=0,\ldots,(s-1)/2$. This reads the exact determinant of the graded map and therefore the determinant of $\theta$, with the known ratios from lifting the chosen graded bases into the original bases. The norm exponents now count the simple multiplicities only in the upper half of the grading. The branch argument and ordinary TI compilation stay valid when some weights vanish modulo $q-1$.

If $s$ is even, these pairs leave the middle grade. Suppose $S$ is commutative and the middle pairing is nondegenerate alternating for some Frobenius functional. One can choose such a functional deterministically on both sides. Alternation is a linear constraint on the functional: for basis elements $x_j$ of the middle grade use $\lambda(x_j^2)=0$ and $\lambda(x_jx_k+x_kx_j)=0$. Compute a basis of the space satisfying these equations. Relative to any already chosen Frobenius functional, the possible new functionals correspond by linear isomorphism to elements $u$ of $A$, and the new functional is Frobenius exactly when all components of $\overline u\in S$ are nonzero. If such a vector exists in the computed linear space, there is one supported on at most the number of simple factors in its chosen basis. Indeed for each factor choose a linear coordinate nonzero at a witness. The product of these coordinates is a polynomial of at most that degree, nonzero as a function. Reduce exponents by $X^q=X$, choose a surviving monomial and set variables outside its support to zero. The restricted reduced polynomial is still nonzero, hence is nonzero somewhere on the finite grid. Enumerating all basis combinations of this constant support size therefore finds a valid functional, or rejects. If an isomorphism exists it transports an alternating choice to the target.

Write $\beta$ for the chosen source middle form. An actual isomorphism gives the middle Gram equation with right multiplication by $\overline u$, so $R_{\overline u}$ is self-adjoint for $\beta$, because both middle forms are alternating. Compute the algebra
$$
 D=\{a\in S:R_a\text{ on the middle grade is self-adjoint for }\beta\}.
$$
This is a unital subalgebra by commutativity of the right actions. It is reduced since it lies in a product of fields. Write $D=\prod_j D_j$ as fields, with middle summands $W_j$ given by their idempotents. These summands are $\beta$-orthogonal since the idempotents are self-adjoint. Each carries the trace to $F$ of a perfect alternating $D_j$-bilinear form, by the trace pairing and the self-adjointness of all scalar multiplications. For alternation before taking search-independent traces, one needs $\beta(a x,x)=0$ for all $a\in D_j$. This follows by skew symmetry in odd characteristic, and in characteristic two by writing $a=c^2$ and using $\beta(c^2 x,x)=\beta(cx,cx)=0$. In particular $e_j=\dim_{D_j}W_j$ is even. Over the algebraic closure, the corresponding orthogonal decomposition by field embeddings shows
$$
 \frac{\operatorname{Pf}(\beta R_{\overline u})}{\operatorname{Pf}(\beta)}
     =\prod_j\mathrm N_{D_j/F}(\overline u_j)^{e_j/2}.
$$
Here scaling a nondegenerate alternating matrix of size $e_j$ scales its Pfaffian by the $e_j/2$-th power. The basis factors cancel in the ratio. Pfaffian congruence of the middle Gram equation now gives the exact middle determinant with the known source and target Pfaffian calibration. Alternating elimination computes the two Pfaffians in polynomial time.

To attach these extra norms to the actual tensor map, choose for each $D_j$ one field factor $E_i$ of $S$ into which it projects nontrivially. The projection embeds $D_j$ as a subfield. Under $\Psi_A$, the $i$-th socle block is the one-dimensional $E_i$-space $E_i$ with distinguished coordinate $1$. In that block take its selected subfield $D_j$, and take the corresponding subfield on the target in its prescribed quotient coordinates. The actual action is calibrated by the quotient as multiplication by $\overline u_i$, which belongs to this subfield. Attach the inclusions and use the corresponding $D_j$-line norm just as in Section 3. Thus even an unbounded middle grade contributes determinants via field lines. This finishes the exact even-top-degree case. The theorem gives sufficient terminals for Frobenius algebras under the stated conditions, with publication priority unestablished.

## 5. A two-filtration version without perfect complementary radical grades

The exact determinant theorem also applies to a larger class of Frobenius algebras, even if the radical grading has no perfect top-degree pairing. In this version use the radical powers and their annihilators together. Set $F_i=J^i$ for $0\le i\le s+1$, and let $K_i=\{x:J^i x=0\}$. The argument of Section 1 works with $J^i$ in place of $J$. It shows $K_i=\{x:xJ^i=0\}$, equal to either orthogonal complement of $F_i$ for $\beta(x,y)=\lambda(xy)$. These are two-sided ideals, intrinsic under isomorphism. For $0\le i,j\le s$ put
$$
 C_{ij}=
 \frac{F_i\cap K_{j+1}}
 {(F_{i+1}\cap K_{j+1})+(F_i\cap K_j)}.
$$
These are the successive quotients from refining $F_i/F_{i+1}$ by the images of $F_i\cap K_{j+1}$. Consequently $\det\theta$ in adapted bases is the product of its determinants on all the cells. Each cell is a right $S$-module, since right multiplication by $J$ sends $F_i$ into $F_{i+1}$ and preserves all $K$'s.

The pairing induced by $\beta$ on $C_{ij}\times C_{ji}$ is perfect. The denominator terms pair to zero by definition of $K_i$ and $K_j$. The left orthogonal of $F_j\cap K_{i+1}$ is $K_j+F_{i+1}$. If $x\in F_i\cap K_{j+1}$ lies in this sum, write $x=k+f$ with $k\in K_j$ and $f\in F_{i+1}$. Since $F_{i+1}\subseteq F_i$, $k\in F_i\cap K_j$, and since $K_j\subseteq K_{j+1}$, $f\in F_{i+1}\cap K_{j+1}$. Thus $x$ lies in the denominator. The same argument interchanging $i,j$ proves perfectness. The right relative multiplication $R_u$ preserves both filtrations, so the induced Gram identity
$$
 \det(\theta_{ij})\det(\theta_{ji})
   =\frac{\det\beta_{A,ij}}{\det\beta_{B,ij}}\,
     \det(R_u|_{C_{ji}})
$$
holds for each $i\ne j$. Multiply it once per unordered pair of distinct indices. The right hand sides are read from the same socle norms, with exponents counted from the selected cells. Only the diagonal cells $C_{ii}$ remain.

A diagonal cell of logarithmic $F$-dimension costs only its own polynomial-size exterior algebra, attached directly to the actual flag quotient. A diagonal cell with an alternating form and commutative $S$ costs only subfield norms by the last part of Section 4. The Frobenius form used on each such cell can be selected independently by the same constant-support test with the alternation equations restricted to that cell. Its relative unit is calibrated on the same intrinsic first socle, using the functional chosen for that term. All cells, Gram calibrations, radicals and filtrations are computed by polynomial-size linear algebra, and their number is polynomial. Thus for a fixed number of simple factors the many-one result holds with a width bound given by the largest necessary matrix-factor dimension and the largest nonalternating diagonal cell that is extracted directly. This makes the determinant terminal apply even in the absence of a graded Frobenius condition. It remains an informal sufficient theorem for a restricted class of algebras.
