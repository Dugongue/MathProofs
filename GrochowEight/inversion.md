# GP153 and GP154. The published inversion rule collapses proof-line degree

GP153 records Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She's Conjecture 1.7, which asks for a linear-degree lower bound for cubic-format tensor isomorphism in PC+INV. GP154 records their proposed modular counting test. The rule in Section 2.3 allows arbitrary polynomial substitutions for the entries of the two matrices. Section 2.1 measures degree on the proof polynomials, and Section 1.2 keeps that convention for PC+INV. With these definitions the exact worst-case degree in GP153 is **3**. The GP154 systems have degree at most **2**. [Published paper, Sections 1.2, 2.1 and 2.3](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2023.4) · [Grochow Open Problems catalog, GP153–GP154](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowOpenProblems/COMPLEXITY.md#gp153)

## One INV step expresses ideal membership

Let \(R=F[z_1,\ldots,z_s]\) for any field \(F\), and let \(f_1,\ldots,f_m\in R\) be proof lines. Suppose \(h=\sum_i g_i f_i\) with \(g_i\in R\). Use matrices of size \(m+2\) and put
\[
 E=e_{m+1}(g_1,\ldots,g_m,0,0),\qquad
 C=(f_1,\ldots,f_m,0,0)^\top e_{m+2}^\top.
\]
Then \(E^2=CE=0\). Set \(A=I-E\) and \(B=(I+E)(I+C)\). The following are polynomial identities:
\[
 AB-I=C,\qquad BA-I=C+EC,\qquad
 EC=h\,e_{m+1}e_{m+2}^\top.
\]
Every entry of the premise \(AB-I\) is either one of the given \(f_i\) or zero. The \((m+1,m+2)\)-entry of the consequent is \(h\). Thus one INV application proves \(h\) using lines of degree at most
\[
 \max\bigl(\deg h,\ \max_i\deg f_i\bigr).
\]
A zero premise is obtainable by a zero linear combination of an available line. All remaining conclusion entries are input polynomials or zero as well.

The converse ideal-membership statement also holds. Reduce \(A,B\) modulo the ideal generated by the previously derived lines. In the resulting commutative ring \(AB=I\), so \(\det(A)\det(B)=1\). The adjugate identity makes \(A\) invertible there, and \(BA=I\). Hence the rule preserves ideal membership, as do both ordinary polynomial-calculus rules.

When the original equations have no solution over \(\bar F\), the Nullstellensatz gives \(h=1\) in the extended ideal. The witnessing coefficient polynomials have finite degree. Solving for their coefficients at that same bound is a consistent finite linear system over \(F\), so witnesses already exist over \(F\). Every infeasible system consequently has a one-INV refutation of degree at most its input degree.

## GP153. Exact tensor-isomorphism bound

The standard tensor-isomorphism equations have degree at most 3. Every instance infeasible over the algebraic closure therefore has a degree-at-most-3 refutation, for every field and every tensor dimension. This disproves the stated linear lower-bound conjecture. The bound 3 is sharp for this encoding. Take a nonzero rank-one source tensor and a zero target tensor of the same shape. Every nonzero action equation is homogeneous cubic. The inverse equations alone have a common solution, so soundness rules out a degree-2 refutation.

If degree-\(q\) field equations are added over \(\mathbb F_q\), the same argument gives degree at most \(\max(3,q)\) for every instance infeasible over \(\mathbb F_q\). The algebraic-closure convention for the unaugmented encoding remains in force. In particular, this argument does not turn a tensor pair that is isomorphic over the closure into an unaugmented refutation. Adrian She's 2024 thesis reproduces the same conjecture and rule in Chapter 5, Conjecture 2 and Section 5.1.3. [University of Toronto thesis](https://www.mathematics.utoronto.ca/sites/www.mathematics.utoronto.ca/files/Algebraic%20Methods%20in%20Query%20and%20Proof%20Complexity.pdf)

## GP154. Modular counting bound

Fix primes \(p\ne q\), and \(n\) with \(q\nmid n\). For each \(q\)-subset \(S\) of \([n]\), let \(x_S\) be its block indicator. The specified system consists of \(x_S^2-x_S=0\), the equations \(x_Sx_T=0\) for distinct intersecting blocks, and \(\sum_{S\ni i}x_S=1\) for every \(i\in[n]\). In any putative solution over \(\overline{\mathbb F_p}\), each idempotent is \(0\) or \(1\). The overlap equations give at most one selected block through each element. The coverage equation gives at least one. Thus the selected blocks partition \([n]\) into \(q\)-sets, contradicting \(q\nmid n\) by counting in the integers. The universal theorem then gives a degree-at-most-2 refutation, uniformly in \(n\), and disproves the proposed growing degree bound for these systems. The same proof works for any field and any family of allowed subsets with no exact cover, with the corresponding Boolean, overlap and coverage equations.

For n at least q+1 with q not dividing n, degree two is sharp for this encoding. The incidence columns of the q-subsets span the field vector space on the n elements. Differences of two columns sharing a common q−1 subset supply every difference of coordinate vectors, and any one column has nonzero coordinate sum q modulo p. The coverage equations alone therefore have a solution. The degree-two axioms cannot occur in a degree-one proof.

## Scope of the degree bound

The polynomial entries used in the inversion substitution can have high degree and require large descriptions. The theorem bounds the total degrees of the expanded premise and conclusion polynomials, as the published rule specifies. It gives no polynomial-time proof search or polynomial bound for an encoding that charges for the rule-instantiation data. A version that bounds the degrees of the substitution entries poses a separate lower-bound question. The ordinary PC lower bounds are unaffected.
