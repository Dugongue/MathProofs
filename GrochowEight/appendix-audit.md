# Appendix A degree raising and a covariant algebra functor

## Exact behavior of the unguarded degree raise

In Appendix A following Proposition A.1, Grochow and Qiao propose extending the transformation \(f\mapsto z^{d-e}f\) from cubics to all homogeneous degree-\(e\) forms. Here form equivalence means exact equality after an invertible linear substitution on the displayed variables. [Grochow–Qiao, Part I, Appendix A](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full)

**Theorem.** Let \(K\) be any field, \(n\ge1\), \(d>e\ge1\), \(k=d-e\), and \(f,g\) degree-\(e\) homogeneous forms in \(n\) variables. Then
\[
 z^k f \cong z^k g
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 f\cong\lambda^k g\text{ for some }\lambda\in K^\times .
\]
In particular the proposed map is a reduction for every pair over \(K\) exactly when \((K^\times)^k\subseteq(K^\times)^e\). It works over algebraically closed fields for all these degrees. For example, over \(\mathbb R\) with \(e=4,d=5\), the two forms \(x^4\) and \(-x^4\) are inequivalent, while the two outputs are equivalent by negating \(z\).

For a polynomial \(a\) in the symmetric algebra of a space of linear forms \(V\), write \(E(a)\) for the smallest linear subspace \(W\) such that \(a\in\operatorname{Sym}(W)\). Such a space exists because \(\operatorname{Sym}(U)\cap\operatorname{Sym}(W)=\operatorname{Sym}(U\cap W)\), by choosing a basis adapted to the intersection and the two subspaces. A product of two nonzero polynomials belongs to \(\operatorname{Sym}(W)\) only if both factors do. To see this, take a basis of \(W\), extend it to \(V\), and use additivity of degree under multiplication in each extraneous variable over the domain formed from all other variables. Hence \(E(a b)=E(a)+E(b)\) for nonzero \(a,b\), and essential spaces commute with invertible linear changes.

If one of \(f,g\) is zero, the equivalence is immediate. Otherwise suppose \(z^k f=\ell^k g'\) under an invertible pullback, where \(\ell\) is the image of the target marker and \(g'\) is the image of \(g\). The line \(K\ell\) is independent of \(E(g')\). If \(\ell=\lambda z\), cancel \(z^k\) and restrict \(z=0\). The pullback has invertible induced map on the original quotient by the marker, giving \(f=\lambda^k g\circ A\) for some \(A\in\mathrm{GL}_n(K)\).

Otherwise unique factorization gives \(z^k f=(z\ell)^k H\). As \(E(f)=K\ell+E(H)\) is independent of \(Kz\), and \(E(g')=Kz+E(H)\) is independent of \(K\ell\), the sum \(Kz+K\ell+E(H)\) is direct. Extend the swap of \(z\) and \(\ell\) fixing \(E(H)\) to an invertible linear map. It fixes \((z\ell)^k H\), so composing the pullback with it reduces to the preceding marker-aligned case. Conversely any witness for \(f=\lambda^k g\circ A\) extends using \(z\mapsto\lambda z\). If all \(k\)-th powers are \(e\)-th powers, an \(e\)-th root of \(\lambda^k\) can be incorporated into \(A\). If the inclusion fails, choose \(\lambda^k\) with no \(e\)-th root and compare \(x_1^e\) with \(\lambda^k x_1^e\). Equivalence of these forms would map their single intrinsic line to itself and produce precisely that root.

## Guarded degree raising over every field for \(e\ge4\)

A separate construction gives a reduction for every \(d>e\ge4\) over an arbitrary field. Put \(h(y)=y_1y_2y_3+y_1^2+y_1+y_2+y_3\). For nonzero \(f\), form
\[
 P_f(x,y,z)=z^{d-e}\bigl(f(x)+z^e h(y/z)\bigr),
\]
where the bracket denotes the expanded polynomial, and send a zero form to zero in the same format. An original equivalence extends by the identity on \(y,z\).

For the converse, the bracket has no linear factor. A factor depending on \(y\) has highest-\(y\) part dividing \(y_1y_2y_3\) over \(K(x,z)\), so it may be written \(y_i+\ell(x,z)\) after multiplying by a nonzero scalar. Substituting \(y_i=-\ell(x,z)\), the coefficient of the product of the two other \(y\)'s is \(-z^{e-3}\ell\), forcing \(\ell=0\). A remaining linear \(y\)-term then survives. A factor independent of \(y\) must divide the cubic \(y\)-coefficient \(z^{e-3}\), but cannot be \(z\) because \(f(x)\ne0\). Thus any equivalence between nonzero outputs preserves the \(z\)-line, say \(z'=\lambda z\).

Write the other coordinates of the linear substitution as \(A(x,y)+b z\), with \(A\) invertible. Cancel \(z^{d-e}\) and restrict \(z=0\) to obtain \(f=\lambda^{d-e}g\circ A\) on the full \(x,y\) space, since \(e\ge4\). In particular the pullback carries \(E(g)\) onto \(E(f)\). Choose coordinates on \(E(f)\) and extend them to a basis of the original \(x\)-space, keeping the \(y\) coordinates distinct. Dehomogenize at \(z=1\) and work over the rational function field in the coordinates on \(E(f)\). The translated pullback of \(g\) is constant in the remaining coordinates because its linear dependence is only through \(E(g)\) and the pullback of that space lies in \(E(f)\). Consequently the equation in the other variables has the form
\[
 h(L(y,r)+c)=s h(y)+c_0,\qquad s=\lambda^{-d}.
\]
Here \(r\) records the remaining original coordinates and \(c,c_0\) are constants over the displayed rational function field. The product of the three linear components of \(L\) equals \(s y_1y_2y_3\) by cubic coefficient comparison. Unique factorization implies the components are rescaled distinct \(y\)'s, so no \(r\) occurs. The three mixed quadratic coefficients force the three entries of \(c\) to vanish. Matching the sole square term fixes index 1 under the resulting permutation. The linear coefficients give all three scales \(s\), and the square gives \(s^2=s\), hence \(s=1\). This proof works unchanged in characteristics two and three.

It follows that \(\lambda^d=1\) and \(f=g\circ(\lambda^{-1}A)\). The pullback \(\lambda^{-1}A\) restricts to an isomorphism of the essential spaces, which extends to a linear isomorphism on the original \(n\)-dimensional spaces by adding complements. Hence \(f\cong g\) in the original format. This is a polynomial-size reduction in dense coefficients for fixed \(e,d\). The separate unguarded theorem above classifies the exact formula proposed in Appendix A, including when that simpler map suffices.

## Tensor-to-algebra reduction on all covariant homomorphisms

Grochow and Qiao's Part I Open Question 7.5 asks which of their reductions can be made functorial on all homomorphisms and asks additional questions about asymptotic spectra. Section 2.3 defines covariant tensor morphisms by factorwise linear maps carrying one tensor to the other, and asserts functoriality of their reductions on the isomorphism subcategories. Theorem A uses a path algebra with a direct output-arrow space and inverse transpose on one factor when lifting tensor isomorphisms. The following variation realizes the specified covariant category, including noninvertible morphisms. [Grochow–Qiao, Part I, §2.3, Theorem A, and Open Question 7.5](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full)

**Theorem.** Fix \(d\ge3\) and a field \(K\). There is a polynomial-size objectwise reduction from ordinary \(d\)-tensor isomorphism in positive formats to unmarked unital associative \(K\)-algebra isomorphism, which extends to a faithful functor on all covariant tensor morphisms. If the target category keeps the indicated labeled idempotents and the two specified arrow vectors and requires morphisms to preserve them, the functor is fully faithful. Its structure-constant map is a polynomial-size projection, and the same multiplication table handles the zero tensor.

Let \(T\in V_1\otimes\cdots\otimes V_d\). Form the path algebra of the chain \(0\to1\to\cdots\to d\), with arrow space \(V_i\) in position \(i-1\to i\), and add a separate short branch \(0\to *\to d\) with one-dimensional arrow spaces, containing distinguished vectors \(a,b\) in that order. Identify the branch path \(ab\) with the element \(T\) of the full chain path space \(P=V_1\otimes\cdots\otimes V_d\). More explicitly, quotient the path algebra by the two-sided ideal generated by \(ab-T\) in the terminal Peirce corner \(0,d\). Here paths multiply in their traversal order. The relation spans a one-dimensional ideal because \(0\) is a source, \(d\) is a sink, and multiplication by idempotents at its two ends is scalar. It eliminates the \(ab\) basis path and retains the entire basis of \(P\) regardless of \(T\). The other paths and the diagonal idempotents retain their basis. Structure constants are zeroes, ones, or entries of \(T\) in the product \(ab\). The dimension is \(O(d^2\prod_i\dim V_i)\) in positive formats.

For a covariant morphism \(g=(g_1,\ldots,g_d)\colon T\to T'\), map the \(i\)-th chain arrow space by \(g_i\), fix the labeled idempotents, and map each branch generator to its namesake. The product of the chain maps sends \(T\) to \(T'\), so the source relation goes to zero and the induced map is a unital algebra homomorphism. This works for noninvertible \(g_i\). The identity and composition rules hold on generators. Every target homomorphism preserving the displayed marks is determined by its restrictions to the individual arrow spaces, since each arrow corner has no other path. On the full path corner it consequently acts as the tensor product of the chain maps, and preservation of \(ab=T\) forces these maps to be a source tensor morphism. This proves full faithfulness in the marked category.

For unmarked isomorphism reflection, the radical \(J\) is the strictly upper path ideal, since it is nilpotent with semisimple quotient \(K^{d+2}\). The arrows are recovered as the nonzero Peirce blocks of \(J/J^2\), with their multiplicities, and this graph is independent of \(T\). Indeed the relation concerns only paths of length at least two, and every full chain path remains a product of positive-length paths. The unique source and sink are fixed, and the short and long paths between them have respective lengths \(2\) and \(d\ge3\), so all vertices are intrinsically ordered. Thus an unmarked isomorphism induces the prescribed matching on the quotient idempotents.

Align their lifts by an inner conjugation. For two complete orthogonal idempotent systems \((e_i),(f_i)\) with identical reductions modulo \(J\), set \(u=\sum_i f_i e_i\). Then \(u\equiv1\bmod J\), hence \(u\) is a unit, and \(u e_i=f_i u\) for every \(i\). After alignment the isomorphism is blockwise on the Peirce corners. Let its invertible maps on the chain arrows be \(g_i\) and its scalars on the two branch arrows be \(\alpha,\beta\). Its map on \(P\) is \(\bigotimes_i g_i\), and the relation gives \((\bigotimes_i g_i)T=\alpha\beta T'\). Multiplying \(g_1\) by \((\alpha\beta)^{-1}\) gives an ordinary tensor isomorphism. The forward implication is the functor already constructed.

The same extraction has an algebraic section on transporter maps. Conjugation by \(1+J\) is the identity on \(J/J^2\), so \(g_i,\alpha,\beta\) can be read directly from the intrinsically distinguished arrow blocks of the induced quotient map, before aligning any lifts. Reading these blocks and dividing the first map by the product of the two invertible scalar entries gives a rational map that respects composition and is a left inverse to the functor on isomorphisms. This answers the all-homomorphism clause for the tensor-to-associative-algebra reduction in the exact covariant convention of §2.3. It leaves the functoriality of the other reductions and the asymptotic spectra clauses open.

These are informal proofs. The path construction does not assert an asymptotic-semiring map or a linear output-dimension bound.
