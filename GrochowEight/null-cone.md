# Exponential null-cone degree for one tensor and one cubic

Over \(\mathbb C\), the null cone of \(\mathrm{SL}_n^3\) on one \(n\times n\times n\) tensor requires invariant equations of degree at least \(n2^{n-1}\). The null cone of \(\mathrm{SL}_n\) on one cubic in \(n\) variables requires invariant equations of degree at least \(n2^{n-1}/3\). This proves exponential lower bounds for the two single-object catalog questions GP066 and GP067. We give explicit points with coefficients zero or one in the indicated bases. [Catalog GP066–067](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowOpenProblems/INVARIANTS.md#gp066) · [Derksen–Makam on tuple lower bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06838)

## The cubic

Take the cubic \(f_n=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_i^2x_{i+1}+x_n^3\), with \(n\ge2\). A positive-degree invariant which is nonzero on this support must have a surviving coordinate monomial of its degree with zero weight for the special-linear diagonal torus. Indeed the torus acts diagonally on coordinate monomials. If the multiplicities of the \(n\) supported terms are \(a_i\), zero torus weight means that the total exponent at each variable is the same integer \(c\). Thus
\[
 2a_1=c,\qquad a_{i-1}+2a_i=c\ (2\le i<n),\qquad a_{n-1}+3a_n=c.
\]
For \(i<n\) the solution is \(a_i/c=(2^i-(-1)^i)/(3\cdot 2^i)\). The numerator is odd and divisible by 3, so \(2^{n-1}\) divides \(c\). For degree \(D\) the total exponent is \(3D=nc\). This proves vanishing for all positive-degree homogeneous invariants below \(n2^{n-1}/3\). Taking homogeneous parts also covers nonhomogeneous equations in the invariant ring which vanish at the origin.

To see that \(f_n\) itself is outside the null cone, first treat the same recurrence over the reals with \(c=1\). All \(a_i\) are positive, including \(a_n=(1-a_{n-1})/3\). Give each supported monomial squared norm proportional to \(a_i\), using an orthonormal weight basis for \(\mathrm{Sym}^3\). Different support exponents never differ by a root \(e_i-e_j\): nonadjacent terms have disjoint variables, adjacent pairs have a difference involving three coordinates, and the final pair has difference \(2e_{n-1}-2e_n\). Thus all off-diagonal entries of the moment matrix vanish. The diagonal entries are equal by the recurrence, so the special-linear moment is zero. The resulting nonzero point is outside the null cone by Kempf–Ness. [Kempf–Ness and scaling for these representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06652)

The coefficient-one point is a nonzero scalar multiple of a diagonal special-linear translate of the chosen point. In fact the matrix whose columns are the \(n\) support exponents is triangular with nonzero diagonal, and its column sums are 3. Hence every choice of positive coefficients is achieved from \(f_n\) by positive diagonal scaling. Divide the scaling matrix by the positive \(n\)-th root of its determinant to get a special-linear matrix, changing all cubic coefficients by one common scalar. A nonzero scalar does not change membership in the null cone.

## The tensor

Use all three distinct placements of \((i,i,i+1)\) in the three modes for \(1\le i<n\), and the entry \((n,n,n)\). Let \(T_n\) have a one in each of these positions. No two different entries of its support agree in two modes. In a single triple of placements, moving the unique \(i+1\) changes two modes. Entries from adjacent groups can share only one coordinate because their only common label occurs once in the earlier group. Entries from nonadjacent groups have no shared label, and the final diagonal entry shares just one coordinate with each entry in the last group.

Take a positive-degree invariant nonzero on this support. Its surviving torus-weight-zero coordinate monomial has equal integer degree \(c\) at each basis vector in each mode, with total degree \(D=nc\). Sum multiplicities over the three placements of each class. Summing the three marginal constraints for each label gives the cubic recurrence above with right side \(3c\). Hence \(2^{n-1}\mid c\), and \(D\ge n2^{n-1}\).

For semistability, distribute the cubic weight \(a_i\), computed with \(c=1\), equally across its three placements, and use \(a_n\) on the diagonal term. The marginals in each mode are uniform. No agreement in two modes means that the three reduced density matrices are diagonal. The tensor with square roots of these positive weights as coefficients has zero moment for \(\mathrm{SL}_n^3\) and is outside the null cone. A common positive diagonal matrix applied in all three modes can scale \(T_n\) to exactly these coefficients because its effect on each class is given by the same invertible exponent matrix as above. Normalizing that matrix to determinant one changes all coefficients by a common scalar only.

## Relation to earlier supports and arbitrary order

Kravtsov's free support, reproduced by Franks–Reichenbach in Lemma 2.5, already supplies a different tensor support with a positive uniform solution and a coordinate of exponentially small dyadic size. Coupling that support with the zero-weight monomial argument yields the same exponential obstruction. The cyclic-placement support above makes the tensor fully symmetric and gives a common argument with the cubic. The polynomial chain shape is also classical in the study of invertible polynomials. [Franks–Reichenbach, Lemma 2.5](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.13) · [Chain-type invertible polynomials](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-023-03258-x)

The same proof works for any fixed degree or tensor order \(r\ge3\). Use the monomials \(x_i^{r-1}x_{i+1}\) and \(x_n^r\), or all \(r\) placements of the unique \(i+1\) together with the final diagonal entry. The recurrence replaces 2 by \(r-1\) and 3 by \(r\). Its fraction at position \(i<n\) has reduced denominator \((r-1)^i\): the numerator before cancellation is \(((r-1)^i-(-1)^i)/r\), coprime to \(r-1\). The same free-support and positive-scaling arguments yield the lower bounds \(n(r-1)^{n-1}/r\) for one form and \(n(r-1)^{n-1}\) for one tensor.

These lower bounds concern set-theoretic invariant equations, so they also apply to invariant generating and separating degree bounds for these actions. They do not compute the minimum nonvanishing degree of either witness, give matching general upper bounds, or imply arithmetic circuit lower bounds for a particular invariant. All proofs here are at the paper level.
