# Further cases of the determinantal normal-module codepth conjecture

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Set $q=t-r\ge1$, $n=t+c-1$, $I_A=I_{q+1}(\mathcal A)$, $I_B=I_{q+1}(\mathcal B)$ for a $t\times n$ matrix and its last-column deletion. Write $A=R/I_A$, $B=R/I_B$, $J=I_A/I_B$, $N_A=\operatorname{Hom}_R(I_A,A)$, $N_B=\operatorname{Hom}_R(I_B,B)$, $H_1=\operatorname{Hom}_R(I_B,J)$, and $H_2=\operatorname{Hom}_R(I_B,A)$. Put $\ell=r(r+c-1)$, the codimension of $A$, and measure codepth using dimension of support and homogeneous depth.

**Theorem.** For $1\le r<t$, general matrices of every allowed positive homogeneous degree pattern with $\dim A\ge3$ satisfy the normal-module codepth formulas of Conjecture 10.7(i), with codepth zero for the square maximal-minor hypersurface $(r,c)=(1,1)$. They satisfy all formulas of Conjecture 10.7(ii), retaining the stated exclusion $(r,c)=(1,2)$. The same formulas hold for independent entries. For $r=t$, individual-entry ideals instead have the regular-sequence formulas given below.

The square-to-rectangular case $c=2,\ r\ge2$ uses the universal rank-drop calculation in the companion proof. For the other formats we use Proposition 7.18 and Remark 7.19 where they give exact universal projective dimensions or bounds, and establish the missing equalities below. [Kleppe–Miró-Roig, Conjectures 7.15 and 10.7, Proposition 7.18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12119)

## Passing universal depths to general homogeneous entries

Give the universal entries $x_{ij}$ the positive weights prescribed by the degree pattern and let $\mathcal A_T=(f_{ij})$ have independent generic coefficients over their fraction field. Write $T=K[y_1,\ldots,y_N]$, $\mathfrak m=(y_1,\ldots,y_N)$. On each chart where $y_j$ is invertible, the universal map is flat. Before inverting coefficient polynomials it is a polynomial and Laurent polynomial extension after eliminating the coefficient of $y_j^{\deg f_{ab}}$ independently in each entry $f_{ab}$. Inverting all nonzero polynomials in the coefficients preserves flatness.

If a universal module has a minimal graded free resolution of length $s\le N-1$, its tensor with $T$ is a resolution of the substituted module. The higher homology is supported at $\mathfrak m$ by flatness on the charts. To see that it vanishes, localize at $\mathfrak m$ and let $i>0$ be the greatest index of nonzero homology. The module of boundaries $B_i$ is zero or has a free resolution of length at most $s-i-1$, resolving it by the exact higher tail. In the latter case its depth is at least two. The cycles $Z_i$ are a submodule of a free module and have no $\mathfrak m$-torsion. In $0\to B_i\to Z_i\to H_i\to0$, the local cohomology exact sequence gives $H_i=0$ in both cases. Positivity then preserves minimality, and the Hilbert numerator of the resolution shows that codimension of support is unchanged.

Apply this first to the universal determinantal rings $A,B$, which are Cohen–Macaulay of codimension $\ell,\ell-r$ for the proper flags here. It follows that their generic substitutions have the same codimensions and are Cohen–Macaulay. The ideals and their quotient $J$ commute with substitution: vanishing of $\operatorname{Tor}_1^R(A,T),\operatorname{Tor}_1^R(B,T)$ gives this by the ideal and quotient exact sequences. Apply the argument also to a universal Hom module $L$ equal to $N_A$ or $H_1$. Its comparison map to the actual Hom of the substituted modules agrees on each chart by flatness and finite presentation. The substituted $L$ has depth $N-s$. The actual Hom has depth at least two when $\dim A\ge3$. Indeed, $A_T$ has depth at least three, and the sequence $0\to J_T\to B_T\to A_T\to0$ shows that $J_T$ has depth at least two. Hom into either target is a kernel of a map of sums of the target, whose image has depth at least one whenever nonzero, by a target-regular element. Thus if $N-s\ge2$, the comparison map identifies both modules with their sections on the punctured spectrum and is an isomorphism. The same open-chart argument transports the expected codimension of lower-rank strata, using the resolutions of the associated determinantal rings whenever the codimension is smaller than $N$. Where codimension is at least $N$, the rank stratum is absent projectively for general coefficients: the incidence in coefficient space times $\mathbb P^{N-1}$ has codimension equal to the universal rank stratum in each coefficient fiber of the projection to $\mathbb P^{N-1}$, because evaluation is surjective.

All claims over the fraction field of the coefficients hold on a nonempty open set of coefficient values. Work over the polynomial parameter ring and clear denominators of finite presentations and complexes. Higher homology and the kernel and cokernel of an isomorphism vanish after inverting a single more nonzero parameter polynomial, since they are finite modules. Generic freeness ensures that taking parameter fibers preserves the short exact sequences defining the complexes, the ideals and their quotients, and the kernels used to compute Hom. Positive degrees in every fiber keep the resolutions minimal.

For $N_A$, the universal projective dimensions from Proposition 7.18(i) are $\ell$ for $c=0,2$, $\ell+1$ for $c\notin\{0,1,2\}$, and $\ell+2$ for square matrices with $r\ge2$. The above transfers all these values whenever the resulting depth is at least two. For $H_1$, we use this transfer for $s=\ell$ when $c\ge2$ or $s=\ell+1$ when $c\le1$, always with $\dim A\ge3$.

## Square matrices of dimension three

It remains to treat $c=1$ and $\dim A=3$ for $r\ge2$. Choose general forms of the given degrees in $N=\ell+4$ variables. The resulting ring $A_4$ has dimension four and its normal module $L_4$ has depth two by the previous section. The lower-rank stratum has ambient codimension $(r+1)^2=\ell+2r+1$, greater than $N-1$; it is therefore absent away from the vertex for general coefficients. On this complement the rank is exactly $q$. In a pivot chart the equations for $A_4$ reduce to its $\ell$ Schur complement entries. Because the ideal has height $\ell$ and the ambient scheme is regular, these form a regular sequence wherever $A_4$ is supported on the chart. Thus $L_4$ is locally free there over $A_4$, and $\operatorname{Ext}_T^{N-2}(L_4,T)$ is supported at the vertex. Graded local duality now gives that $H^2_{\mathfrak m}(L_4)$ is nonzero of finite length.

Restrict to the hyperplane $h=y_N=0$. The coefficients can be chosen so that the restriction is also general, since restriction is a surjective linear map on coefficient spaces. The ring $A_4/(h)$ has the expected dimension three, so $h$ is $A_4$-regular by Cohen–Macaulayness and is $L_4$-regular since $L_4$ embeds into a finite sum of $A_4$. Write $M=L_4/hL_4$; it has depth one. In the same pivot charts off the vertex, ordinary base change identifies $M$ with the actual normal module $L_3$ of $A_4/(h)$. Indeed the local generators of the ideal and their reductions are regular sequences of length $\ell$, and their classes form the two corresponding conormal bases. The actual $L_3$ has depth at least two by the kernel argument above, hence is the module of sections of $M$ over the puncture. Consequently $M\to L_3$ has zero kernel, finite-length cokernel, and induces an isomorphism on $H^2_{\mathfrak m}$.

From the exact sequence for multiplication by $h$ on $L_4$, the local cohomology of $M$ receives an injection
$$
H^2_{\mathfrak m}(L_4)/hH^2_{\mathfrak m}(L_4)\ \hookrightarrow\ H^2_{\mathfrak m}(M).
$$
The quotient is nonzero by Nakayama's lemma. Thus $L_3$ has depth precisely two and codepth one, as predicted. The dimension-four theorem and avoiding the lower-rank stratum are open conditions on the lifted coefficients. Their intersection with the preimage of the expected-dimension locus downstairs is nonempty open. Its image contains a nonempty open under the surjective linear coefficient map, proving the assertion for general forms in dimension three.

## A Segre calculation for $4-r\le c\le-1$

First take independent entries and work on a $(q-1)$-minor pivot chart entirely within $\mathcal B$, with the convention that $q=1$ needs no pivot. Gaussian elimination replaces the two ideals by the $2\times2$ minors of a generic $m\times n_0$ residual matrix and of its last-column deletion, where
$$
m=r+1,\qquad n_0=r+c,\qquad m\ge n_0+2,\qquad n_0\ge4.
$$
All pivot coordinates and upper and left off-pivot blocks are extra polynomial or invertible variables, by the inverse Schur-coordinate change. The expected codimension $\ell=(m-1)(n_0-1)$ is preserved. A projective dimension on the residual model at its vertex also occurs at the prime generated by its entries in this pivot chart, after extension to the field of fractions of the extra variables. Hom and the flag modules commute with this polynomial extension and localization.

For the rank-one residual model $A$, write $X=\mathbb P^{m-1}\times\mathbb P^{n_0-1}$. Its coordinate ring is the Segre ring with polarization $\mathcal O(1,1)$. Write $Q_x=\operatorname{coker}(\mathcal O(-1)\to\mathcal O^m)$ on the first factor. On the second factor put
$$
Q'=\operatorname{coker}\left(\mathcal O(-1)\xrightarrow{(y_1,\ldots,y_{n_0-1})}\mathcal O^{n_0-1}\right).
$$
Off the fiber $y=[0:\cdots:0:1]$, the pulled-back normal sheaf of the last-column deletion is the pullback of $Q_x\boxtimes Q'$ to the punctured cone. To see this, at a nonzero rank-one point $x\otimes y'$ the tangent subspace to the rank-one locus in the space of truncated matrices is $x\otimes k^{n_0-1}+k^m\otimes y'$. The quotient of the ambient tangent space is the tensor of the respective quotient spaces. Constant ambient derivatives give this identification, with grading normalized so those derivatives are in degree zero; ordinary grading can differ by an overall shift.

The ring $A$ is a normal domain and $\operatorname{Hom}_A(I_B\otimes_R A,A)=H_2$ is reflexive as a dual over it. The rank-zero deletion stratum has codimension $n_0-1\ge3$ on the cone. At the missing point on the second projective factor, $Q'$ has a locally free resolution of length one and depth at least two, so it is the reflexive extension across that point. Its torsionfreeness also follows directly: a fraction multiple of the vector of the $y_i$ that belongs to the ambient free module must be a ring multiple, since the coordinates have no common prime divisor. The hull on the product and its pullback to the punctured cone are therefore $Q_x\boxtimes Q'$ and its pullback. Hartogs across the vertex then identifies $H_2$ with the associated section module. Its local cohomology in degree $i+1$, for $i\ge1$, contains every
$$
H^i\big(X,Q_x(a)\boxtimes Q'(a)\big),\qquad a\in\mathbb Z.
$$

Set $a=-(m-1)$. The quotient sequence of $Q_x$ gives $H^{m-2}(Q_x(a))\cong k$, since $\mathcal O(a-1)=\mathcal O(-m)$ has top cohomology and $\mathcal O(a)$ is acyclic. The quotient sequence for $Q'$ gives $H^{n_0-2}(Q'(a))$ as the kernel of the map on top cohomology induced by $y_1,\ldots,y_{n_0-1}$. By Serre duality the dual of this kernel is the degree $-a+1-n_0$ quotient of the homogeneous polynomial ring by $(y_1,\ldots,y_{n_0-1})$. It is nonzero by the pure power of $y_{n_0}$, since $-a+1-n_0=m-n_0\ge2$. Künneth produces nonzero local cohomology of $H_2$ in degree $m+n_0-3$, two below the dimension $m+n_0-1$ of the Segre cone. Auslander–Buchsbaum and the chart reduction show $\operatorname{pd}H_2\ge\ell+2$ universally.

In the range $c\ge4-r$, the exact sequences (7.4) and (7.6) and the negative row of Proposition 7.18 give the upper bound $\operatorname{pd}H_2\le\ell+2$ and $\operatorname{pd}N_B=\ell-r+1$. As $H_1$ is the kernel of the surjection $N_B\to H_2$, the top nonzero Tor gives $\operatorname{pd}H_1=\ell+1$. Its support is all of $B$, as $J$ is a nonzero ideal in the universal domain $B$ and the conormal of $B$ has positive rank at the generic point. Its codepth is $r+1$. The specialization proved above preserves this result for general forms.

## Boundary $c=3-r$: rank one and the general lower bound

The same $(q-1)$-pivot reduction gives a residual $m\times3$ matrix with deletion $m\times2$, $m=r+1\ge3$. For $q=1$ work in this rank-one model directly. The construction of $H_2$ by the Segre normal sheaf continues to apply. Here $(Q')^{**}\cong\mathcal O(1)$ on $\mathbb P^2$: the map $(-y_2,y_1)$ identifies $Q'$ with $(y_1,y_2)\mathcal O(1)$. Hence $H_2$ is the diagonal section module of $Q_x\boxtimes\mathcal O(1)$. The deletion ring $B$ before freely adjoining the $m$ last-column entries is the Segre ring for $\mathbb P^{m-1}\times\mathbb P^1$, with normal module the diagonal sections of $Q_x\boxtimes\mathcal O(1)$ on that product as well. In each case the normal directions and grading normalization are those in the previous section.

The depth of $H_2$ is at least $m+1$. Indeed the quotient sequence for $Q_x(a)$ places all potential cohomology in degrees $0,m-2,m-1$, with degree zero requiring $a\ge0$, and the positive degrees requiring $a<0$. On $\mathbb P^2$, $\mathcal O(a+1)$ has either degree zero for $a\ge-1$ or degree two for $a\le-4$. In the latter range the $x$ factor cannot contribute $H^0$; Künneth therefore gives no positive cohomology of the product below degree $m$. For the $\mathbb P^1$ factor in the deletion, the same argument shows depth at least $m$ before adjoining the $m$ free variables, so $\operatorname{depth}N_B\ge2m$.

Let $G$ be the image of $N_B\to H_2$, so $H_1$ is its kernel. Constant coordinate derivatives generate $N_B$. This follows in each degree $a\ge0$ from the surjection $k^m\otimes H^0(\mathcal O(a))\to H^0(Q_x(a))$ and ordinary polynomial multiplication on the second factor, whose $\mathcal O(1)$ sections are generated by the two coordinate functions. Negative degrees have no sections. Freely adjoining the last column preserves the assertion. On $H_2$ the two coordinate generators of $\mathcal O(1)$ are sent to $-y_2,y_1$ on $\mathbb P^2$ by the identification of $(Q')^{**}$ above. Thus
$$
G_a=H^0(\mathbb P^{m-1},Q_x(a))\otimes(y_1,y_2)_{a+1}
$$
inside $H_{2,a}$, where $(y_1,y_2)_{a+1}$ means the degree $a+1$ part of this ideal of $k[y_1,y_2,y_3]$. In particular $D=H_2/G$ is the section module of $Q_x$ over the polynomial ring $k[z_1,\ldots,z_m]$ of last-column variables, with all other entries acting by zero. It has resolution $0\to k[z](-1)\xrightarrow{z} k[z]^m\to D\to0$ with projective dimension one over $k[z]$, so its depth is $m-1$. The local cohomology exact sequence of $0\to G\to H_2\to D\to0$ gives depth $G=m$. Since $\operatorname{depth}N_B\ge2m>m+1$, the sequence $0\to H_1\to N_B\to G\to0$ then gives depth $H_1=m+1$. Its support is all of $B$ of dimension $2m+1$, hence codepth $m=r+1$. This proves the universal rank-one boundary case and gives the lower bound for all $q$ via the pivot chart, $\operatorname{pd}H_1\ge 2m-1=\ell+1$.

## Boundary upper bound by the kernel incidence resolution

Continue with the universal matrix and $c=3-r$, allowing any $q\ge1$. Write $F=k^{q+2}$, $V=k^{q+r}$, $F'\subset F$ for the first $q+1$ coordinates, and $\lambda\in F^*$ for the final coordinate covector. On $\mathrm{Gr}(2,F)$ let $K$ be the tautological rank-two subbundle and $U=F/K$ the rank-$q$ quotient. Let
$$
Z=\operatorname{Tot}(\operatorname{Hom}(U,V)).
$$
Its points are a plane $K\subset F$ and a matrix killing that plane. The projection $Z\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is proper and is an isomorphism where the matrix has rank $q$. All bundles on the Grassmannian below are pulled back to $Z$. Write $C$ for the cokernel of the tautological map $U\to V\otimes\mathcal O_Z$; this map is an injection of sheaves because it is generically injective and the bundles are torsion-free.

The bundles $\mathcal O_Z,U,U^*,\operatorname{End}(U)$ have vanishing higher cohomology on $Z$, and their global-section modules are maximal Cohen–Macaulay over $A$. This is the direct-summand consequence of Propositions 3.1 and 3.4 of Buchweitz–Leuschke–Van den Bergh. To match their hypotheses, view our incidence as the Springer resolution for the transposed matrix $V^*\to F^*$, with the $q$-plane in the smaller space $F^*$ being the annihilator of $K$. Their bundle $Q$ is the dual of that $q$-plane, hence is $U$. Their tilting bundle contains the structure sheaf and $U$ (partitions $0,(1)$ in the $q$-by-$2$ box), and $q+2\le q+r$ since $r\ge2$. Apply their results to the four corresponding Hom summands of its endomorphism bundle. [Buchweitz–Leuschke–Van den Bergh, Theorem C and Propositions 3.1, 3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.1833)

Set $D_0=\dim A$. Apply global sections to the dual tautological sequence $0\to U^*\to F^*\otimes\mathcal O_Z\to K^*\to0$ and to its tensor with $U$. The preceding vanishings give exact sequences of sections and no higher cohomology for $K^*$ and $U\otimes K^*$. Their modules of sections have depth at least $D_0-1$ by the depth lemma. Apply the same reasoning to $0\to U\to V\otimes\mathcal O_Z\to C\to0$ and its $K^*$-tensor. It gives no higher cohomology for $C$ or $C\otimes K^*$, and
$$
\operatorname{depth}\Gamma(Z,C)\ge D_0-1,\qquad
\operatorname{depth}\Gamma(Z,C\otimes K^*)\ge D_0-2 .
$$
The exact sequences give a surjection of $A$-modules $V\otimes F^*\otimes A\to\Gamma(Z,C\otimes K^*)$.

The restriction $\lambda|_K$ is a section of $K^*$ with regular zero scheme $\mathrm{Gr}(2,F')$ of codimension two. This holds scheme-theoretically by the tautological condition $K\subset F'$, or by writing the two components of $\lambda|_K$ in Grassmannian graph charts. Write $\mathcal I$ for its pulled-back ideal on $Z$. The sheaf $C$ is flat over the Grassmannian: on each open trivializing $U$ the total space and tautological map are obtained from an independent generic $V$-by-$q$ matrix over $k$ by tensoring with the base coordinate ring. Thus the Koszul sequence of the section stays exact after tensoring by $C$; explicitly,
$$
0\longrightarrow C \xrightarrow{\,s\mapsto s\otimes\lambda|_K\,}
C\otimes K^*
\xrightarrow{\,s\otimes\alpha\mapsto s\otimes(\alpha\wedge\lambda|_K)\,}
\mathcal I\,C\otimes\det K^*
\longrightarrow0 .
$$
The image here is a subsheaf of $C\otimes\det K^*$, again by base flatness, and $\Gamma(Z,\mathcal I\,C\otimes\det K^*)$ is the cokernel on global sections because $H^1(Z,C)=0$. Write $G_Z$ for this module. The depth lemma gives $\operatorname{depth}G_Z\ge D_0-2$, and the displayed map shows that $G_Z$ is generated over $A$ by the images of constant $V\otimes F^*$, with the $\lambda$ component sent to zero.

It remains to identify $G_Z$ with the earlier module $G=\operatorname{im}(N_B\to H_2)$. On the open set where both matrices have rank $q$, the line $L=K\cap F'$ is the kernel of $\mathcal B$ and the truncated matrix has the same image as the full one. The normal space of the truncated rank locus is $\operatorname{Hom}(L,C)$: in a rank-$q$ pivot chart, restricting a matrix perturbation to $L$ and passing to the cokernel is exactly its lower off-pivot Schur corner, which is the normal direction. The sequence $0\to L\to K\xrightarrow{\lambda}\mathcal O\to0$ identifies $\det K$ with $L$. If $(u,v)$ is a basis of $K$ with $u\in L$ and $\lambda(v)=1$, then $(\alpha\wedge\lambda)(u\wedge v)=\alpha(u)$. Consequently the generator $w\otimes\alpha$, with $w\in V$, maps precisely to the truncated matrix perturbation restricting to $u\mapsto\alpha(u)[w]$ in $\operatorname{Hom}(L,C)$. Every constant perturbation in $\operatorname{Hom}(F',V)$ has a lift to $V\otimes F^*$, and changing this lift by a multiple of $\lambda$ leaves its image unchanged.

Constant coordinate perturbations generate $N_B$ over $B$ by the universal rigidity sequence (5.1) of Kleppe–Miró-Roig, applied to the transposed $(q+1)\times(q+r)$ maximal-minor matrix, then adjoining the independent final-column variables. It follows that $G$ and $G_Z$ have the same generators in the common generic normal space. Both modules are torsion-free over the domain $A$, so the equality of the generated submodules in that space identifies them. For $G$ this follows because $H_2$ is a dual over $A$. For $G_Z$, the sheaf $C$ on the integral smooth variety $Z$ is torsion-free: the locus of rank less than $q$ in a generic $V$-by-$q$ fiber has codimension $r+1$, while $C$ has a locally free resolution of length one. It is locally free in codimension one and has positive depth at all primes of height at least two by Auslander–Buchsbaum, excluding any torsion associated prime. The subsheaf $\mathcal I\,C\otimes\det K^*$ is therefore torsion-free, and taking sections embeds $G_Z$ in its generic fiber. [Kleppe–Miró-Roig, equation (5.1) and Remark 7.19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12119)

The transposed maximal-minor ring for $B$ has normal codepth at most one by Remark 7.19 with parameter $c=r\ge2$. Thus $\operatorname{depth}N_B\ge\dim B-1=D_0+r-1\ge D_0+1$. The exact sequence $0\to H_1\to N_B\to G\to0$ now gives $\operatorname{depth}H_1\ge D_0-1$, or $\operatorname{pd}H_1\le\ell+1$. This matches the pivot lower bound. The full boundary $c=3-r$ consequently has codepth $r+1$ for independent entries and by specialization for general positive forms with $\dim A\ge3$.

## Maximal minors with $r=1$

For $r=1,c=2$, the normal module $N_A$ has codepth zero by Remark 7.19. For $r=1,c\ge3$, that remark gives universal upper bounds of one for both $N_A$ and $H_1$. We show both are exact via a $(q-1)$-pivot contained in $\mathcal B$. The residual $A$ has rank one, size $2\times n_0$ with $n_0=c+1\ge4$. Here $N_A$ is the section module of $\mathcal O(1)\boxtimes Q_y$ on $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{n_0-1}$, where $Q_y$ is the quotient using all $n_0$ coordinates. This follows from the same normal-space computation and reflexivity. At twist $a=-(n_0-1)$, $H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(1+a))\ne0$ and $H^{n_0-2}(Q_y(a))\ne0$ by the Euler sequence. Künneth gives nonzero local cohomology of $N_A$ in degree $n_0=\dim A-1$ on this chart, giving the sharp universal lower bound.

For $H_1$ in the same residual model, $H_2$ is the section module of $\mathcal O(1)\boxtimes Q'$, with $Q'$ omitting the last coordinate. At the same twist, $H^{n_0-2}(Q'(a))\ne0$ by the missing-coordinate computation above, so $H^{n_0}_{\mathfrak m}(H_2)\ne0$. Constant coordinate derivatives generate $H_2$: in each degree $a\ge0$, the Euler sequence and $H^1(\mathcal O(a-1))=0$ on $\mathbb P^{n_0-1}$ give generation of $H^0(Q'(a))$ by its constant $n_0-1$ coordinate sections. The $\mathcal O(a+1)$ factor is generated by its two sections in normalized degree zero. In negative degrees $Q'(a)$ has no sections. The derivative generators lift to $N_B$, hence $N_B\to H_2$ is surjective in the residual model. Remark 7.19 gives $\operatorname{depth}N_B\ge\dim B-1=\dim A=n_0+1$. The long exact local cohomology sequence therefore injects $H^{n_0}_{\mathfrak m}(H_2)$ into $H^{n_0+1}_{\mathfrak m}(H_1)$. Since $\dim B=n_0+2$, the same pivot and Auslander–Buchsbaum argument yields the universal lower bound one on codepth. Both computations transfer to general forms as above. The remaining maximal-minor hypersurface $r=1,c=1$ and excluded flag $r=1,c=2$ have the scopes stated here and below. [Kleppe–Miró-Roig, Remark 7.19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12119)

## Literal degenerate cases

For $r=1,c=1$, the determinant is a hypersurface equation and $N_A$ is free of rank one over $A$, of codepth zero. Thus the literal square assertion in Conjecture 10.7(i) requires its non-hypersurface scope $r\ge2$. For $r=t$ the minors are individual entries. With positive-degree regular-sequence entries, $N_A$ is free over $A$, of codepth zero. In a proper flag the conormal of $I_B$ is free over $B$ and $J$ is a regular-sequence ideal of length $r$. Then $H_1$ is a nonzero direct sum of copies of $J$; the sequence $0\to J\to B\to A\to0$ gives $\operatorname{depth}J=\dim A+1$ for $r\ge2$. Thus its codepth is $r-1$ in the one-minor boundary. Independent indeterminates already exhibit these phenomena if one-minor cases are read literally into the conjecture.
