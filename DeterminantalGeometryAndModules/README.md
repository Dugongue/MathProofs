# Four proofs in determinantal geometry and module isomorphism

1. **Exact uniform determinantal size.** For a linear family of quadrics containing the constant polynomial, let $e$ be the largest dimension of a common vanishing linear subspace of the quadratic parts. The exact minimum is $n-e+1$ over any field, and the symmetric minimum is $2(n-e)+1$ in characteristic different from two. The full family gives $N^*(n,2)=n+1$, proving the four-variable conjecture. The same family admits optimal representations whose matrix coefficient substitutions fail, refuting the speculation in §6. The proof also classifies universal cokernels and gives NP-completeness over each fixed finite field. [Proof](UniformQuadraticSystems.md) · [Lean identities](UniformCorners.lean) · [Boralevi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1085656)

2. **Codepth of determinantal normal and flag modules.** A pivot chart computes the square-to-rectangular restriction module and its Fitting ideal. Specialization transfers universal resolutions to general positive-degree forms, including in small ambient dimension. The companion proof gives both parts of Conjecture 10.7 for minors of size at least two, with the square maximal-minor hypersurface corrected to codepth zero. The remaining format ranges use a Segre calculation, an incidence resolution and a hyperplane section. [Square-to-rectangular proof](DeterminantalCodepth.md) · [Codepth extensions and exact scope](CodepthExtensions.md) · [Kleppe–Miró-Roig, Conjecture 10.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12119)

3. **Prescribed nodal coranks and ordinary local–global failure.** For a geometrically rational nodal plane curve over a perfect field, the normalization and closed-node data determine exactly which corank-two loci occur. The finite-field classification has one exceptional pattern over $\mathbb F_2$. For any fixed anisotropic conic over a number field, there are infinitely many inequivalent nodal counterexamples to local–global existence in each degree $d\equiv2\pmod4$, $d\ge6$. Failure persists over all odd-degree extensions, and degree six is sharp in this class. [Proof](NodalCorankLoci.md)

4. **Arithmetic of symmetric and integral representations.** In every degree $d\ge5$, a smooth plane curve with local symmetric representations everywhere and none globally exists over a suitable finite extension of any number field. This answers Ishitsuka–Ito Problem 1.6(2). Over Dedekind domains, Picard-group torsion classifies the integral matrices in a fixed generic equivalence class. A single smooth quartic yields unbounded numbers of inequivalent symmetric matrices that are locally projectively congruent. [Proofs](ArithmeticOfRepresentations.md) · [Ishitsuka–Ito, Problem 1.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.8336)

The four results have written proofs. Lean 4.33.0 independently checked the two determinant identities in the first proof. Literature priority is provisional.
