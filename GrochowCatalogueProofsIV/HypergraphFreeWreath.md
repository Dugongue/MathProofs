# A free wreath product from directed hypergraph substitution

## GP-0959

Let $F$ be a finite directed hypergraph on a nonempty vertex set $V$ of size $n$. Assume its undirected source-or-range incidence graph is connected and no edge has both source and range equal to $V$. Let $X$ be a finite simple loopless directed graph. Form $H$ as follows. Put a copy $F_i$ of $F$ at every vertex $i$ of $X$. For each arc $ij$ of $X$ add one hyperedge with source the entire vertex set of $F_i$ and range the entire vertex set of $F_j$. Then, for Faroß's quantum incidence automorphism group,
$$
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{inc}}^+(H)
\ \cong\
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{inc}}^+(F)\wr_*
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{Bic}}^+(X).
$$
The formula includes arbitrary directed bases and genuinely hypergraphic connected fibers. It specifies an explicit directed hypergraph product for which the free wreath formula holds. [Faroß's incidence group and Question 6.4](https://doi.org/10.4153/S2976859426100046) · [Bichon's free wreath product](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0107029)

### Extracting the block permutation

Write $S,R$ for the source and range incidence matrices of any hypergraph, with vertices as rows and edges as columns. The incidence quantum group is generated by vertex and edge magic unitaries $U,W$ with $SW=US$ and $RW=UR$. Orthogonality of the two magic matrices gives the corresponding transpose intertwiners. The edge magic matrix consequently fixes each of the two edge-size vectors $S^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1,R^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1$. For a magic matrix fixing a real vector, its entries vanish between indices having distinct vector values, by multiplying the vector equation by one row projection. Thus $W$ preserves the cross-edge set $W_{\mathrm{cross}}$, whose arity pair is $(n,n)$, and the internal edge set.

Combine the vertex and internal-edge magic matrices into a direct sum $M$ with two types. It intertwines the weighted undirected incidence adjacency with the sum of the two internal incidence matrices as its off-diagonal block. The connected components are exactly the blocks consisting of each fiber's vertices and internal edges. For the adjacency $A$, the entries of $I+A+\cdots+A^L$ with $L$ at least the total number of vertices and internal edges are positive exactly between positions in the same component. If a magic $M$ commutes with a scalar matrix $T$, then
$$
(T_{xy}-T_{ab})M_{xa}M_{yb}=0.
$$
This follows by sandwiching the appropriate entry of $MTM^*=T$ between the two displayed projections. Summing on $a,y$ shows that $M$ also commutes with the zero/nonzero pattern of $T$, since the products vanish when the two pattern values differ. Apply this to the displayed polynomial in $A$. It follows that $M$ commutes with the equivalence matrix having entry one exactly for positions in the same fiber.

For any $x$ in block $i$ and $y$ in block $j$, that commutation says
$$
p_{ij}:=\sum_{y'\in F_j}M_{x y'}=\sum_{x'\in F_i}M_{x'y}.
$$
The shared expression is independent of $x,y$. Since it is a sum of orthogonal projections in a row or a column, the $p_{ij}$ make a magic unitary $P$. Edge and vertex type projections are already preserved by $M$, so the block sum is computed using only the type of the chosen row or column. A single coefficient $M_{(i,a),(j,b)}$ is dominated by $p_{ij}$ on both sides.

### Extracting the fiber and base actions

For every source block $i$, put
$$
z^{(i)}_{ab}=\sum_j M_{(i,a),(j,b)}.
$$
Here $a,b$ range over positions of $F$, namely the disjoint union of its edge and vertex sets. The sums are projections because the supports $p_{ij}$ are orthogonal as $j$ varies. Their row sums are one, and their column sums are one by the column-block formula for $p_{ij}$. They preserve the types and the two incidence matrices of the fiber. For the latter, sum the intertwining equations of $M$ over destination blocks. The matrix $z^{(i)}$ therefore gives the $i$th copy of the fiber quantum group. It commutes entrywise with each $p_{ij}$, and
$$
M_{(i,a),(j,b)}=z^{(i)}_{ab}p_{ij}=p_{ij}z^{(i)}_{ab}.
$$

Let $Y$ be the cross-edge magic matrix, indexed by arcs of $X$. For either source or range cross incidence, a row at any vertex in block $i$ is the corresponding base incidence row, repeated on all $n$ vertices of that block. Hence its intertwining with $Y$ is precisely the base incidence intertwining with $P,Y$. The block row sums of $U$ are $P$. It follows that $P,Y$ constitute the base graph incidence quantum group. On a directed graph without parallel edges that group is Bichon's group, and its $Y$ is determined by $P$, by Faroß's graph specialization. One can also see the determination directly by applying both source and range projections to the relevant edge column sum of $Y$. [Graph specialization](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09894#S4)

### Inverse construction

In the defining presentation of the free wreath product, take the base magic matrix $P$ and one copy $z^{(i)}$ of the fiber group for each base vertex. Require the entries of each copy to commute with the corresponding row entries $p_{ij}$, as usual. Use the base Bichon edge matrix $Y$ on the cross edges, and use $z^{(i)}_{ab}p_{ij}$ on the internal positions as above. The internal parts are magic. Fiber row sums and block row sums give the full row sum, and fiber column sums followed by block column sums give the full column sum. The internal incidence equations follow entrywise from the fiber equations since the scalar incidence maps use identical copies of $F$. The cross equations hold because row sums on each vertex block equal $P$, which intertwines base incidences with $Y$. These substitutions therefore give the quantum incidence action on $H$.

Extracting the block sums and the fiber sums recovers the input free wreath generators, and reconstructing afterward recovers each original coefficient. The fundamental coefficient formula is the free wreath corepresentation, so the isomorphisms of presentations also intertwine comultiplication. This proves the claimed formula.
