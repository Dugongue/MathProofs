# Bounded-degree quantum graph isomorphism

## GP-0972

There are connected finite simple graphs of maximum degree at most $15$ that are isomorphic in the commuting-operator model and have no finite-dimensional quantum isomorphism. Commuting-operator graph isomorphism is also undecidable on that same bounded-degree connected class.

### The operator constraints

Use binary linear systems in the sign convention. Each variable is a self-adjoint involution in a unital algebra. A parity equation requires its variables to commute within the equation and their product to equal the given sign times the identity. We use this model both in complex matrix algebras and in unital $C^*$-algebras with faithful tracial state. These operator solutions are equivalent to perfect strategies of the corresponding binary constraint-system game in the finite-dimensional and commuting-operator models, respectively. One direct verification uses joint spectral projections for each constraint. In the other direction, sum the measurement projections for assignments that give $+1$ to an indicated variable. Consistency on a shared variable means that the two opposite marginal projections are orthogonal. If $p_i,p_j$ are the two $+1$ marginals, then $p_i(1-p_j)=0$ and $(1-p_i)p_j=0$, so they agree. The resulting observable $2p_i-1$ is independent of the equation and the joint measurements give the required products and commutation. Faithfulness turns prohibited zero traces of products of projections into zero products. [Binary constraint-system game and graph reductions](https://upcommons.upc.edu/bitstream/handle/2117/127696/Quantum_Isomorphisms-pre-proofs-version.pdf)

The system can be normalized in polynomial time so all equations have exactly two or three variables with no repeated variable in an equation, every variable occurs in at most three equations and the variable–equation incidence graph is connected. All these transformations preserve both kinds of operator solvability.

First split an arbitrary long equation into a chain of equations of arity three using private prefix-product variables. For example, $x_1x_2x_3x_4=\epsilon$ is replaced by $x_1x_2y=1,\ yx_3x_4=\epsilon$. Before replacement, or as a separate simultaneous step, impose the commutation of every original pair $x_i,x_j$ in each original equation by adding an equation $x_ix_j z=1$ with fresh $z$. This equation extends a commuting pair by setting $z=x_ix_j$. Conversely it explicitly requires commutation of the pair in every solution. With these auxiliary equations, forward extension assigns commuting prefix products, and backward multiplication of the chain recovers the original product and the original commutations. For a unary equation $x=\epsilon$, use $xyz=\epsilon$ and $yz=1$ with fresh $y,z$. In a forward extension they can both be the identity. A vacuous zero-arity equation can be dropped. An inconsistent zero-arity equation is an immediately detectable negative instance.

After dropping variables unused by any equation, connect the incidence graph by choosing one variable $x_i$ from each component. Add a shared new variable $w$ and one equation $x_i w v_i=1$ with private $v_i$ for each component. A solution extends by $w=1$ and $v_i=x_i$, and the new equations can be forgotten in the reverse direction. A system with no equations is an immediately detectable positive instance.

Finally replace each variable by one new copy per occurrence, and join its copies in a path of binary equations $x^{(j)}x^{(j+1)}=1$. These equations identify the operators. Each copy has one original occurrence and at most two equality occurrences. The incidence graph remains connected because each former variable is replaced by a connected path and each former occurrence retains its own incidence. The total size remains polynomial, even with the quadratic number of commutation constraints in an original long equation. No transformation changes the containing algebra or the trace when extending a solution.

### The graphs

Apply the graph construction of Atserias, Mančinska, Roberson, Šámal, Severini and Varvitsiotis. For each equation create one vertex for each satisfying local Boolean assignment. Connect two distinct vertices exactly when they assign opposite values to a shared variable. Compare this graph with the one obtained by replacing every right-hand side by $+1$, keeping the same transformed incidence pattern. Their Theorems 6.3 and 7.1 identify finite-dimensional or commuting-operator solvability with the respective graph isomorphism relation for this pair. Their Theorems 7.2 and 7.3 quote Slofstra's separation of the strategy models and undecidability in the commuting model. [Graph reductions and Slofstra applications](https://upcommons.upc.edu/bitstream/handle/2117/127696/Quantum_Isomorphisms-pre-proofs-version.pdf) · [Slofstra, linear-system games](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03140)

Within one equation the vertices form a clique of order at most four. A vertex belongs to an assignment on at most three variables. Each variable has at most two other equations containing it, and an equation of arity at most three has at most two satisfying assignments that oppose a specified value on that variable, since that value fixes one coordinate and the parity equation imposes another binary condition. The degree is therefore at most $3+3\cdot2\cdot2=15$. Every equation of arity at least two admits both values of each of its variables among its satisfying local assignments. Thus any two equations sharing a variable have some edges between their cliques. Incidence connectivity implies graph connectivity for both graphs. Equal single-vertex graphs handle an immediately detected positive system. The connected pair $P_3,K_3$ handles an immediately detected negative one, since the degree vectors rule out even a fractional isomorphism. The construction is effective and works for every system in Slofstra's reduction. Applying it to his separation and undecidability families proves both claims.
