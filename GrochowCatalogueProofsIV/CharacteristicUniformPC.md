# One graph-isomorphism lower bound in every finite characteristic

## GP-0916

There is one explicit sequence of nonisomorphic pairs of simple graphs on $N$ vertices for which the standard graph-isomorphism equations require polynomial-calculus refutation degree $\Theta(N)$ over every fixed finite field. The graph pair is independent of the field. An optional coloring with bounded-size color classes also gives monomial-size lower bound $2^{\Omega(N)}$. The upper degree bound and the linear vertex overhead below hold uniformly.

### Input pairs and equations

Use a polynomial-time explicit family of connected cubic expanders, for example one of Morgenstern's cubic Ramanujan families. For each base use the Grädel–Grohe–Pago–Pakusa construction twice. Let $(A,B)$ be their zero-twist and single-nonzero-twist CFI graph pair for the prime $2$. Let $(C,D)$ be the corresponding pair for prime $3$. Within each pair the orders and ordered color block sizes agree. The simple uncolored versions are already nonisomorphic by the inverse interpretation of their Lemma 5.3. Their proofs of Theorems 6.2 and 6.3 give linear PC degree, and exponential monomial size in the bounded-color equations, for each pair when the coefficient characteristic differs from its CFI prime. Each graph has order and edge count linear in the base order. [Explicit cubic expanders](https://doi.org/10.1006/jctb.1994.1054) · [CFI polynomial-calculus bounds](https://lmcs.episciences.org/5119/pdf)

The standard equations use a variable $x_{vw}$ for a putative source-to-target match. Each row and column sums to one. Each variable is Boolean. Products of inconsistent matches, which reuse a vertex on only one side or disagree on adjacency, vanish. In a colored instance only matching colors have variables.

### Constructing an OR of two graph-isomorphism instances

For two graphs $P,Q$ of the respective input sizes, form a connected graph $J(P,Q)$ with two hubs joined by an edge. The first hub is adjacent to all vertices of $P$, and the second to all vertices of $Q$. Attach sets of private leaves to the two hubs. If $s$ is the sum of the two input orders plus two, use $2s$ private leaves on the first hub and $4s$ on the second. For large bases the original graph degrees are bounded while the two hub degrees lie in disjoint intervals. Consequently an isomorphism recognizes the hub types. Every input vertex also receives one private leaf. This makes each input vertex have degree at least two after joining it to its hub. The remaining neighbors of degree at least two are therefore exactly the corresponding input graph vertices. Thus $J(P,Q)$ determines the two factors separately. For the optional coloring use a separate color for each hub type and each indexed hub leaf, reused across both components. Use the disjoint tagged palettes of the input factors and a second tagged copy of those palettes for their private marked leaves.

The output is
$$
G=J(A,C)\sqcup J(B,D),\qquad
H=J(A,D)\sqcup J(B,C).
$$
An isomorphism must pair the two connected components. If it keeps their listed order it requires $C\cong D$, and if it interchanges them it requires $A\cong B$. In either situation that one input isomorphism suffices, using its inverse on the other component. In particular these $G,H$ are nonisomorphic.

### Preservation under polynomial-calculus substitution

To carry hardness from $(A,B)$, force the two output components to interchange. The copies of $C$ and of $D$ then match identically, while the active maps are $A$ to $B$ and $B$ to $A$. Substitute the input variable of $(A,B)$ in the first active block and its transpose in the reverse block. Map all other permitted vertices by the fixed matching, which uses the identity for hubs, indexed hub leaves and inactive vertices. The variables of the private marked leaves copy exactly the matches of their owners. Set every incompatible match to zero. To carry hardness from $(C,D)$, keep the two output components in place and use the analogous substitution, now leaving the $A$ and $B$ copies fixed.

Each row and column equation becomes a row or column equation of the active input pair or zero. Boolean equations become input Boolean equations or zero. If the image of a local inconsistency contains two active variables in the same role block, it is an input inconsistency or zero. In distinct blocks, adjacency and nonadjacency are determined by the roles and chosen component permits, so incompatible source and target pairs cannot both have nonzero images. For a private marked leaf, equality of owners yields the only additional nonconstant adjacency test. An inconsistent owner match pulls back to one of the input row or column collision monomials. Consequently every initial equation pulls back to zero or an input equation. Multiplication by a variable pulls back to multiplication by a variable, identity or zero. Thus degree and expanded monomial size cannot increase. This is the same support-preserving substitution principle used in the earlier catalogue's TI lower bounds. [Prior sparse TI substitution](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/d7366c4348bc03f0e93cb8674db191de32ba58bf/GrochowCatalogueProofsII/SparseTensorPC.md)

The construction itself is independent of the coefficient field. In characteristic $2$ use the prime-$3$ input, and in every other characteristic use the prime-$2$ input. Since $N$ is linear in the base order, the PC lower bound is $\Omega(N)$. For the optional coloring the sizes of color classes remain bounded, and the substitution gives the exponential monomial-size lower bound. A degree-$O(N)$ upper bound is available over every field. Multiply consecutive row-sum equations through the monomials for compatible partial assignments to expand the constant $1$ as a sum over that row prefix. Discard each incompatible product by a multiple of its quadratic collision or adjacency axiom. After all rows the sum is empty, because there is no isomorphism. This argument never exceeds degree $N+2$. The theorem concerns ordinary PC over the fixed coefficient field and does not require field equations beyond the Boolean equations for match variables.
