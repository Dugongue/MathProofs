# Canonization of unordered families of compact permutation groups

## GP-0513

Given any multiset of permutation subgroups on one $n$-point domain, with each subgroup specified by generators, one can canonize the entire family under simultaneous point relabeling and arbitrary family reordering in $2^{O(n)}\operatorname{poly}(L)$ time, where $L$ is the total input length. The algorithm yields a canonical representative and a coset of relabeling witnesses. With a common ordered point coloring of maximum color class size $k$, it has the explicit bound $\binom{2n}{k}^{O(1)}\operatorname{poly}(L)$. This is a consequence of the two different canonization algorithms in Wiebking, rather than a new algorithm for either atom type.

Fix the full labeling coset, or in the colored version the labeling coset whose members place each ordered color class in a prescribed consecutive block. For each subgroup $H$, apply Wiebking's single-group canonical labeling procedure to this coset. Its output $\Lambda_H$ consists of the labelings taking $H$ to one canonical subgroup $K_H$ on the fixed ordered domain. The labeling coset is represented by a permutation group and one representative, and $K_H$ is represented by conjugated generators. Both can be manipulated without enumerating group elements.

Partition the original multiset by equal $K_H$, sorting the groups by the polynomial-time comparison for compact ordered group atoms, and then by multiplicity if desired. For every part replace each distinct subgroup $H$ by the labeling-coset atom $\Lambda_H$, retaining multiplicity by making one labeled part per multiplicity. Equal $K_H$ is critical. In that part the original subgroup is uniquely recovered from its atom by choosing any $\lambda\in\Lambda_H$ and pulling $K_H$ back under $\lambda$. This recovery is independent of the choice of $\lambda$. The replacement is equivariant under all allowed point relabelings since the individual labeling algorithms are equivariant and the common $K_H$ is canonical. Hence the automorphisms and isomorphisms of the replacement object are exactly those of the original family. This is the object-replacement argument, with the parts ordered by their invariant group and multiplicity labels.

The resulting object is a tuple of sets of compact labeling cosets, with no group cosets needing expansion. Apply the object canonizer to it with the original labeling coset. Wiebking's Theorem 5 charges $2^{O(k)}$ times a polynomial in the resulting hereditary object size when the largest initial labeling-group orbit has size $k$. Its proviso about explicitly given group cosets is vacuous for this replacement object: labels themselves remain compact labeling cosets. The single-group calls cost $\binom{2n}{k}^{O(1)}\operatorname{poly}(L)$ by Theorem 9. The unrestricted claim takes $k=n$. To output the original family in canonical coordinates, conjugate all original generators by any returned common labeling and use the canonical ordering on compact subgroup atoms. Two equivalent inputs receive the same labeled family, even if their supplied generator lists differ, because the compact ordered-subgroup comparator can additionally convert each labeled group to its canonical strong generators using a fixed base and lexicographically least transversals. One can compute each such transversal by greedily fixing images subject to membership in a coset of the relevant stabilizer, using the usual Schreier–Sims orbit tests.

This uses Wiebking's group canonizer to supply the individual atoms and the earlier set canonizer to preserve the common relabeling. It also explains why taking a sorted list of individual canonical groups alone would lose the family constraint. [Wiebking, Lemma 3 and Theorems 5 and 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.10454)
