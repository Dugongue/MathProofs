# Faithful complex and rational dimensions of compact p-groups

## GP-0401 · Minimum complex dimension is NP-complete

Fix a prime $p$. Deciding whether a class-two permutation-generator $p$-group has a faithful complex representation of dimension at most a supplied binary integer is NP-complete. At odd $p$ the hard groups have exponent $p$; at $p=2$ they have exponent at most four. The witnesses in the reduction are permutations on polynomially many points, with no reliance on a succinct group table or an unexpanded coset action.

### Character costs

For a class-two $p$-group $G$ and a character $\lambda$ of $Z(G)$, define $R_\lambda=\{g\colon \lambda([g,G])=1\}$. An irreducible complex representation with central character $\lambda$ has dimension $d_\lambda=\sqrt{[G:R_\lambda]}$. Such an irreducible exists by induction of $\lambda$ from the center and taking an irreducible constituent. An element of $R_\lambda$ acts by a scalar by Schur's lemma. For an element outside $R_\lambda$, conjugating by a suitable group element multiplies its character value by a nontrivial scalar, so its trace is zero. Character orthogonality now gives the degree formula.

A collection of irreducibles is faithful if and only if the restrictions of its central characters to $Z(G)[p]$ span the dual of that elementary abelian group. Indeed every nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite $p$-group meets the center and then contains a central element of order $p$. A minimum solution can thus be taken as a basis of central marks, using one irreducible for each basis member.

This also gives membership in NP with arbitrary exponent. The derived group is generated by the basic commutators of the input generators, since the group has class at most two. Build additive cyclic coordinates of the abelian quotient by the derived group by successively adjoining generator cosets. Their relative orders are powers of $p$, found by group powering and constructive membership, and elements of the previous subgroup are expressed as straight-line words by constructive Schreier–Sims. Interpreting the resulting power relations additively and using integer Smith normal form yields the invariant coordinates. The center is the preimage of the kernel of the linear map from this quotient to a product of copies of the derived group, taking brackets with each input generator. The same abelian-coordinate computation applies within the center and the derived group. A guessed central character is given by its values on cyclic generators as exponents of a primitive root of unity of order dividing the group exponent. A second application of the commutator kernel calculation yields $|R_\lambda|$. Test the basis condition on $Z(G)[p]$ and add the resulting integer degrees. All coordinate lengths are polynomial in the supplied permutation degree. [Polynomial constructive presentations from permutation generators](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.08656#S8.SS2)

### A rank-one problem with a cheap explicit basis

Convert 3-SAT to satisfiability of a Boolean circuit with AND and NOT gates, treating every gate output as a wire variable. Remove AND gates with two equal inputs and share duplicate AND gates on the same unordered pair, by processing the circuit in topological order. This retains satisfiability, and the resulting AND gates use distinct unordered pairs.

Let $W$ be the space of rectangular matrices over $\mathbb F_p$ with rows indexed by $0$ and all wires, and columns indexed by $0$, all wires, and an extra index $*$. Impose the following linear constraints, with $\ell(M)=M_{00}$.
- $M_{0*}=M_{00}$, and $M_{i0}=M_{0i}$ for every wire $i$.
- $M_{ii}=M_{i*}$ for every wire $i$.
- For AND gate $k=i\wedge j$, impose $M_{ij}=M_{k*}$, choosing the fixed orientation $i<j$.
- For NOT gate $k=\neg i$, impose $M_{k0}+M_{i0}=M_{00}$.
- If $o$ is the output wire, impose $M_{o0}=M_{00}$.

For a rank-one $M$ with $M_{00}\ne0$, normalize $M_{00}=1$ and write $M_{ij}=a_i b_j$ with $a_0=b_0=1$. Then $b_*=1$, $a_i=b_i$, and the second group of constraints says $a_i^2=a_i$ for all wires. The remaining equations are exactly the Boolean circuit constraints. Conversely a satisfying Boolean assignment constructs such a rank-one matrix.

There is always an explicit matrix $M_0$ with $\ell(M_0)=1$ and rank at most two. Use only row zero and column zero and choose the wire entries there to satisfy the NOT relations and the one output condition. The directed NOT-only dependency graph has no directed cycles and indegree at most one, so its underlying graph is a forest. Assign each component independently, starting with the designated output value in its component and propagating complements. All entries away from row and column zero may then be zero. A basis of $\ker\ell$ with rank at most three per element is equally explicit. Choose a linear basis for the homogeneous row and column zero constraints, whose elements all have rank at most two. Away from that row and column, changing an independent marker $M_{i*}$ changes at most three entries, since each wire has at most one gate definition and each retained AND pair occurs once. The other free entries can be changed singly. These give the desired basis by independent parameter coordinates.

### A polynomial-degree compact group

Let $d=\dim W$. Form the space $\mathcal W$ of $r$-block diagonal matrices with blocks in $W$ whose $\ell$-coordinates agree. Choose $r\ge2$ with $p^r>rp^3(d-1)+2$. Such an $r=O(\log(d+2))$ can be found by increasing $r$ one at a time. The common-anchor kernel has a basis of $r(d-1)$ matrices of rank at most three. The diagonal tuple of $M_0$'s has rank at most $2r$ and anchor one. A nonzero-anchor matrix has rank at least $r$ in the satisfiable case and at least $2r$ in the unsatisfiable case. In the positive case rank $r$ is attained by repeating a satisfying matrix.

Let $U,V$ be the row and column vector spaces of the block matrices, divided by their respective common radicals, and put $C=\mathcal W^*$. Let $B(u,v)\in C$ be evaluation at the bilinear pairing $u^\mathsf{T} Mv$ for $M\in\mathcal W$. It spans $C$ since no nonzero matrix vanishes on every pair of basis vectors. Define
$$
  (u,v,z)(u',v',z')=(u+u',v+v',z+z'+B(u,v')).
$$
Bilinearity gives associativity. The center is exactly $C$ after removal of the common radicals, and it equals the derived group because the commutator values span it. The group has the stated exponent, by the power formula with correction $\binom{j}{2}B(u,v)$. For a central mark represented by $M$, the scalar commutator has alternating rank $2\operatorname{rank} M$, so the complex character cost is $p^{\operatorname{rank} M}$.

Here is an explicit faithful permutation representation. For each basis mark $M$, including the chosen $M_0$ block diagonal and the cheap kernel basis, use the subgroup
$$
 H_M=\{(u,v,z): Mv=0,\ \lambda_M(z)=0\}.
$$
Its index is $p^{1+\operatorname{rank}M}$. The left cosets have coordinates $(v\bmod\ker M,\lambda_M(z))$, since right multiplication by an element of $H_M$ leaves these two coordinates fixed. For a group generator $(u',v',z')$, left multiplication adds the two corresponding coordinates and the extra scalar $\lambda_M B(u',v)$ to the last coordinate. This term depends only on the displayed coset coordinate. Enumerate the polynomially many cosets and compute these permutations on each orbit. The union is faithful because the intersection of its action kernels meets $C$ trivially. Use coordinate basis generators for $U,V,C$. The number of cosets is polynomial since $\operatorname{rank}M\le2r$ or $3$, with $r=O(\log(d+2))$.

Every basis of $C^*$ contains a mark with nonzero common anchor. An unsatisfiable instance therefore has minimum cost at least $p^{2r}$. A satisfiable instance has a basis comprising an anchor mark of rank $r$ and the cheap kernel basis, with cost at most $p^r+rp^3(d-1)<p^{2r}$. The smaller value gives the binary threshold, proving NP-hardness. This extends the previous catalogue's Boolean rank-one epimorphism reduction by making both a low-rank basis and the promised short faithful action explicit. ## GP-0402 · Rational dimension and exact field-cost range

For a nontrivial finite $p$-group of exponent $p^e$, write $\delta_{\mathbb C}$ and $\delta_{\mathbb Q}$ for its minimum faithful dimensions. Then
$$
  p-1\ \le\ \delta_{\mathbb Q}/\delta_{\mathbb C}\ \le\ \varphi(p^e).
$$
Both endpoints are attained for every $p,e\ge 1$ (with $p$ prime) and every positive center socle rank. Determining a rational faithful-dimension threshold is NP-complete for permutation-generator $p$-groups at every fixed $p$, with arbitrary nilpotency class and exponent in the decision problem. Hardness already holds for the class-two groups constructed above.

For the lower bound, constituents of a rational representation occur in full Galois orbits. One such orbit with nontrivial central mark has at least $p-1$ elements, since the order-$p$ central values include a primitive $p$-th root. Their restrictions to the center socle are nonzero scalar multiples of one mark over $\mathbb F_p$. Choosing one constituent per such orbit preserves faithfulness on the center socle and has total complex dimension at most the rational dimension divided by $p-1$. For the upper bound, finite $p$-groups are supersolvable and their irreducible characters are monomial. Induced linear characters give realizations over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{p^e})$; restricting scalars multiplies dimensions by $\varphi(p^e)$. In particular equality with factor $p-1$ holds for every exponent-$p$ group, which transfers both the upper verification and lower reduction in GP-0401 on that class.

For upper endpoint at center rank $s$, take $s$ copies of $C_{p^e}$. A faithful complex character basis has dimension $s$. A rational Galois orbit with a nonzero mark on the order-$p$ socle necessarily contains a character of order $p^e$, and costs $\varphi(p^e)$; $s$ independent marks are necessary and sufficient.

For lower endpoint at $e>1$, put $n=p^{e-1}$ and take the regular wreath product $H=C_p\wr C_n$. Its center is the constant diagonal in its elementary abelian base, and its exponent is $p^e$. Restrict an irreducible representation to the abelian base. Its weights form a single orbit under the cyclic shift, by irreducibility. A base weight fixed by a proper nonzero shift has period a proper divisor of $n$ and sums to zero on the diagonal, because that period is repeated a multiple of $p$ times. An irreducible with nontrivial center mark thus has dimension at least $n$. Inducing a one-coordinate nontrivial linear character of the base attains $n$; its weights are distinct and the transitive shift on their one-dimensional spaces proves irreducibility. It is realized over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)$. For $H^s$, a simple constituent with any nonzero center mark has dimension at least $n$, by the tensor product description of irreducibles of a direct product. At least $s$ such constituents are needed for faithfulness. The direct sums of the displayed $n$-dimensional representations give equality $sn$, and restriction of scalars gives rational dimension $(p-1)sn$. At $e=1$ both endpoint statements already follow from cyclic products. [Character theory of supersolvable groups](https://doi.org/10.1090/gsm/189)

### Rational decision in the arbitrary compact model

For odd $p$, the exponent-$p$ equality transfers the NP-hardness construction above. At $p=2$, every mark in the constructed group attains its complex cost already over $\mathbb Q$. The subgroup $\widetilde H_M=\{(u,v,z):Mv=0\}$ contains the center and supports the sign character $(u,v,z)\mapsto(-1)^{\lambda_M(z)}$. This is multiplicative because the scalar of $B(u,v')$ vanishes whenever $Mv'=0$. Its induction is a rational signed-permutation representation of dimension $2^{\operatorname{rank}M}$ with central mark $M$. A basis of these marks gives a faithful rational representation of exactly the complex optimum. This proves the binary NP-hardness as well.

For the upper bound, use rational genetic induction: every irreducible rational representation of a finite $p$-group is induced from the inflation of the faithful irreducible rational representation of a Roquette section $T/S$, with $S\triangleleft T\le G$. The Roquette groups are cyclic, and at $p=2$ include dihedral, semidihedral and generalized quaternion types. This is the rational case of the genetic induction theorem. [Bouc, rational genetic basis](https://www.lamfa.u-picardie.fr/bouc/genome-rev.pdf) · [Barker, genetic induction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10957)

The unique faithful rational irreducible dimension $d_R$ of each Roquette type $R$ has an elementary computation. For a cyclic group of order $t$ it is $\varphi(t)$, by the primitive cyclotomic module. For the other types take $t$ to be the order of the standard index-two cyclic subgroup generated by $r$. Any faithful rational representation restricted to this cyclic subgroup has a primitive character and therefore has dimension at least $\varphi(t)$. For dihedral and semidihedral groups this dimension is attained on $\mathbb Q(\zeta_t)$: let $r$ act by multiplication by $\zeta_t$ and the other generator by the appropriate field automorphism from the defining conjugation relation. The automorphism has order two, and the representation is faithful since the power of $r$ in any possible kernel element is detected by evaluating at $1$. For the quaternion type, the second generator $s$ instead satisfies $s^2=r^{t/2}$ and inverts $r$. On any primitive eigenspace, complex conjugation followed by $s$ acts antilinearly with square $-I$. Writing this map as $A$ times entrywise conjugation gives $|\det A|^2=(-1)^b$ for an eigenspace of dimension $b$, so $b$ is even. All primitive eigenspaces have equal multiplicity in a rational representation, giving a lower bound $2\varphi(t)$. Inducing the primitive rational cyclic representation attains this bound. These minimal faithful dimensions give $d_R$, since the faithful irreducible exists uniquely and a representation detecting the unique central order-two subgroup has a faithful constituent in each of these groups. The cyclic formula also covers the trivial section with $\varphi(1)=1$.

Let $n$ be the input permutation degree. A threshold at least $n$ has a positive answer by the defining permutation representation. At a smaller threshold, a witness is a list of genetic sections for the irreducible constituents of some faithful rational representation. Each section has cost $[G:T]d_R$. A $p$-group of permutation degree $n$ has exponent at most $n$, since the order of every permutation in it is the largest of its power-of-$p$ cycle lengths. A Roquette section consequently has order at most $2n$, as its cyclic subgroup of index at most two has order at most the group exponent. Also $[G:T]\le n$ for every witness section. Thus $[G:S]\le 2n^2$.

The verifier checks the subgroup relations, normality and indices using permutation-group algorithms. It constructs the at most $2n$ quotient cosets in $T/S$ by breadth-first search using the generators and membership in $S$. It can recognize the displayed Roquette type by testing candidate generators and defining relations in this explicit quotient. For each verified section the induced inflated representation of degree $[G:T]d_R$ exists, regardless of whether the supplied section is genetic. Its kernel is $\operatorname{Core}_G(S)$: an element acting trivially must fix every induced summand indexed by $G/T$ and act trivially on each summand. This is equivalent to membership in each conjugate of $S$. The verifier therefore constructs instead the at most $2n^2$ cosets in $G/S$ and tests faithfulness of the disjoint union of these actions by comparing the order of its image with $|G|$ via Schreier–Sims. The resulting kernel is the same as the intersection of the representation kernels. Check the sum of the costs against the threshold. The witness and all computations have polynomial size. Necessity of some certificate follows by decomposing a minimum faithful representation into irreducibles and applying the genetic induction theorem to each.
