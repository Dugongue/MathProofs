# A Banica-compatible action for directed hypergraphs

## GP-0958

Every finite directed hypergraph has a compact matrix quantum group defined uniformly from its incidence maps and hypergraph $C^*$-algebra. It acts on that algebra by quantum permutations of the vertices and a linear action on the edge-generator space. For a directed graph with no parallel edges it is exactly Banica's quantum automorphism group with its usual graph-algebra action. Its classical points are exactly the classical hypergraph automorphisms, including in the presence of parallel hyperedges. This gives a positive answer to Faroß's Question 6.3. [Question and the hypergraph algebra relations](https://doi.org/10.4153/S2976859426100046)

### A universal presentation

Let $E,V$ be the edge and vertex sets. A directed hyperedge has source $S(e)\subseteq V$ and range $R(e)\subseteq V$. Let $U=(u_{va})$ be the vertex magic unitary and let $B=(b_{fe})$ be an edge matrix such that both $B$ and its entrywise adjoint matrix $\overline B=(b_{fe}^*)$ are unitary. Impose $B\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1$, so unitarity gives $\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}B=\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}$ as well.

Use the ordered tensor products of representations. On $\mathbb C^V\otimes\mathbb C^E$ let $P_S$ be the diagonal projection onto the flags $(v,e)$ with $v\in S(e)$. On $\mathbb C^E\otimes\mathbb C^V$ let $P_R$ be the diagonal projection onto the flags $(e,v)$ with $v\in R(e)$. Impose that $P_S$ commutes with $U\otimes B$ and that $P_R$ commutes with $B\otimes U$. These are respectively the entrywise relations
$$
u_{va}b_{fe}=0\quad\text{if }[v\in S(f)]\ne[a\in S(e)],\qquad
b_{fe}u_{va}=0\quad\text{if }[v\in R(f)]\ne[a\in R(e)].
$$
The different orders are essential. Here tensor products of representations have coefficients formed by multiplying entries in the written order, in their common coefficient algebra.

On $\mathbb C^E\otimes\mathbb C^E$ also impose that the projection onto pairs of distinct parallel edges commutes with $B\otimes B$. Edges are parallel if both their source and range sets are identical. This relation is vacuous on a hypergraph with no parallel edges.

Finally, require the assignments
$$
p_a\longmapsto \sum_v p_v\otimes u_{va},\qquad s_e\longmapsto \sum_f s_f\otimes b_{fe}
$$
to preserve the defining relations of $C^*(\Gamma)$ in the spatial tensor product. This is a universal bounded $C^*$-presentation. More explicitly start from the free product of the vertex quantum permutation algebra and the edge free unitary algebra. Impose the matrix relations above and quotient by the closed ideal generated by slices of the differences in the evaluated defining $C^*$-relations. The first tensor leg is the already defined algebra $C^*(\Gamma)$. One can write each order relation between projections as a polynomial equality. Bounded linear functionals on that fixed first leg separate the corresponding spatial tensor product, so the resulting universal quotient is exactly the one requiring preservation of all displayed relations. The identity coefficients act and satisfy every relation.

The standard coproduct on each of $U,B$ descends. The product corepresentations satisfy the projection-intertwining constraints under composition of coefficient matrices in separate tensor legs. The new edge matrix fixes $\mathbf 1$ since both copies do so. Composing the two $*$-homomorphisms on the hypergraph algebra verifies the defining relations for the composed coefficients, and its coefficients are the standard coproduct of $U,B$. Thus $U\oplus B$ satisfies the compact matrix quantum group axioms. The two substitutions then give a coaction, since their coefficients are those of corepresentations. The counit is the identity on these generating subspaces. Equivalently the usual density property follows from invertibility of the coefficient matrices and their induced coefficient matrices on products of generators and their adjoints. In case a zero range yields a zero edge generator the action can have a kernel. The presentation includes all edges in order to retain the underlying hypergraph information.

### Exact graph specialization

Suppose the edges are singletons of the form $e=(a,b)$ with no parallel edges. Fix vertex indices $x,y$ and such an edge $e$. From the column sum of $B$,
$$
u_{xa}u_{yb}=\sum_f u_{xa}b_{fe}u_{yb}.
$$
By the left and right incidence constraints, a term survives only on an edge $f=(x,y)$. If such $f$ exists, each of the two vertex projections acts as identity on the corresponding side of $b_{fe}$. For example one sums the left zero constraints over possible $x$ in the column indexed by $a$. Thus the expression equals $b_{(x,y),(a,b)}$ on an edge and zero on a nonedge. Conversely fix $f=(x,y)$ an edge and use its row sum to expand the same product. The corresponding argument gives zero if $(a,b)$ is a nonedge. The edge/nonedge zero product conditions are equivalent to commuting with the adjacency matrix, by multiplication of the magic-unitary equations. Thus $U$ satisfies the Banica relations, and $B$ is determined by $U$.

Conversely, Banica's $U$ preserves the edge subspace of the tensor-square vertex representation and also its orthogonal complement, by the same zero conditions. Its restriction has coefficients $b_{(x,y),(a,b)}=u_{xa}u_{yb}$. The restriction is unitary. The entrywise adjoint of the full tensor square equals its conjugate by tensor-factor reversal, hence is unitary too, and the edge projection also commutes with the entrywise adjoint because it is a real scalar diagonal projection. Thus $\overline B$ is unitary. The edge row and column sums are one by summing over all ordered vertex pairs and deleting the zero nonedge terms. The source and range constraints follow from orthogonality of the two factors against their respective vertex coefficients, in the displayed orders. The parallel-edge projection is zero. Finally the Schmidt–Weber action for finite directed graphs is precisely the two substitutions above, so all $C^*$-relations are preserved. The two universal presentations are inverse. [Banica graph-algebra action](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08833)

### Classical points

On a scalar character $U$ is a permutation $\sigma$. If $b_{fe}\ne0$, the incidence conditions then imply $S(f)=\sigma S(e)$ and $R(f)=\sigma R(e)$. Thus all nonzero coefficients of a fixed row $f$ occur within a single parallel class on the input side. If $e\ne e'$ are in that class, the distinct-parallel relation forces $b_{fe}b_{fe'}=0$, since the output pair $(f,f)$ is outside the projection. There is therefore at most one nonzero entry in the row. Its sum is one, and unitarity gives a permutation matrix. These permutations are exactly the compatible vertex and edge permutations, since every classical automorphism preserves the hypergraph algebra substitutions. This also covers empty incidence sets and arbitrary parallel multiplicities. The construction is invariant under relabeling of vertices and edges.
