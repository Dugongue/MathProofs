# Minimum genus of a homomorphism separator

## GP-0923

For a pair of nonisomorphic simple graphs let $g_*(G,H)$ be the least Euler genus of a simple graph $F$ satisfying $\operatorname{hom}(F,G)\ne\operatorname{hom}(F,H)$. This integer function is uncomputable, even on connected input pairs of maximum degree at most $15$. For $n$-vertex inputs it always lies in $O(n\log(n+1))$, and explicit input pairs attain $\Omega(n)$. Among pairs with $g_*=0$, the order of a smallest planar separator has no computable uniform bound in the input order, even for the same connected bounded-degree inputs.

### Exact noncomputability

A planar separator exists exactly when the inputs fail homomorphism indistinguishability over the planar class. Mančinska and Roberson identify the latter equivalence with commuting-operator quantum graph isomorphism. On the connected degree-$15$ class its undecidability follows from the binary-constraint reduction in GP-0972. If an algorithm computing $g_*$ is only promised nonisomorphic inputs, one first decides ordinary isomorphism by finite enumeration and calls the proposed algorithm on the remaining cases. Thus the promise does not evade undecidability. A computable bound on smallest planar separator order would similarly give a decision algorithm by enumerating planar graphs up to the bound and computing their homomorphism counts directly. The bound would only need to apply when a planar separator actually exists. [Planar homomorphisms and quantum isomorphism](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06958) · [Undecidability in the commuting model](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09837)

### Effective numerical upper bound

Apply GP-0886 from the earlier catalogue to the Boolean edge coordinates of $G,H$. It gives an orbit-sum pattern with at most $d=1+\lfloor\log_2(n!)\rfloor$ edges whose injective homomorphism counts differ. The partition-lattice identity expresses each injective count as an integer linear combination of ordinary homomorphism counts from vertex quotients of the pattern. A quotient with a loop contributes zero on simple targets, and duplicate edges can be merged without changing the count. Some simple quotient of at most $d$ edges is therefore a separator. A graph with $m$ edges embeds in an orientable surface of genus at most $m$, by taking an embedding of a spanning forest and adding at most one handle per remaining edge. Its Euler genus is at most $2m$. Thus $g_*(G,H)\le2d$. Removing isolated vertices from an equal-order comparison only divides the counts by a common factor, and some connected test separates whenever a disconnected one does. [GP-0886 and partition inversion](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/d7366c4348bc03f0e93cb8674db191de32ba58bf/GrochowCatalogueProofsII/HomomorphismSeparation.md)

### Explicit linear lower bound

For each $g\ge1$ let $K_g$ be a path of $g$ vertex-disjoint copies of $K_5$ joined by bridges between consecutive copies. It has maximum degree at most six. It has Euler genus $g$: each $K_5$ has Euler genus one and genus is additive over blocks. Alternatively the lower bound follows from the $g$ vertex-disjoint Kuratowski subgraphs and the upper bound by putting the copies in disjoint crosscap neighborhoods and joining along the bridges.

The class $\mathcal P^{(g)}$ of graphs excluding every disjoint union of $g$ Kuratowski graphs as a minor contains the graphs of Euler genus at most $g-1$. It is closed under weak oddomorphisms by Neuen and Seppelt's bounded-genus argument. The graph $K_g$ is outside this class. Roberson's CFI homomorphism criterion says that a graph distinguishes the even and odd CFI graphs over $K_g$ precisely when it admits a weak oddomorphism to $K_g$. By closure, no graph of Euler genus at most $g-1$ can distinguish them. The identity of $K_g$ is itself an oddomorphism, so $K_g$ distinguishes them at Euler genus $g$. This is the block construction in Neuen and Seppelt's proof with the joining graph chosen as a path. Bounded base degree keeps the two CFI graphs of order $O(g)$, since each base vertex contributes a number of local states bounded by $2^6$. Thus the numerical lower bound is linear in the target order along an explicit family. [Neuen–Seppelt, bounded-genus hierarchy and weak oddomorphisms](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18602#S6.SS1)
