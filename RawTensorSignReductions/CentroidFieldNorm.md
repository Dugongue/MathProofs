# Remove one determinant on low-rank centroid-field modules

Work over \(F=\mathbb F_3\). Suppose two explicit concise tensors each have a local centroid, with residue field of degree \(e\) over \(F\). Let \(W\) be the designated mode and put \(d=\dim_F W/e\).

**Theorem.** A deterministic ordinary tensor-system mark enforces determinant one on \(W\), on the same transporter as the original tensor, at cost polynomial in the dense input length \(L\) and \(2^d\). This yields a single two-sign instance for SL-TI when \(d=O(\log L)\), regardless of the residue field degree. Neither the field action nor the field identification between inputs is supplied.

## Recover the intrinsic coefficient field

Compute the commutative centroid \(C\) by its linear equations. If \(3^r\ge\dim_F C\), the kernel of the linear map \(x\mapsto x^{3^r}\) is its nilpotent radical \(J\). In fact nilpotent elements have nilpotence exponent at most \(\dim_F C\), and the kernel consists of nilpotents. Test locality by checking that the cube-fixed algebra has dimension one, since it has one \(F\) factor per residue field. Every element of a residue field of degree \(e=\dim_F C/J\) lifts uniquely to an element of \(K=\{x:x^{3^e}=x\}\): injectivity follows from nilpotence, and surjectivity by repeatedly cubing an arbitrary lift whose difference \(x^{3^e}-x\) is nilpotent. This gives a covariant coefficient field \(K\), and its action makes \(W\) a \(K\)-module. Mismatch of the two field degrees excludes ordinary equivalence. These fixed spaces and actions are computed by polynomial-time linear algebra.

Choose compatible \(F\)-bases in the two coefficient fields using one reference field isomorphism. To find it without enumerating the field, compute the primitive idempotents of \(K_T\otimes_F K_S\) by the cube-fixed procedure from the centroid-block construction. Each factor is a field and its degree is \(e\), since each of the \(e\) embeddings splits the minimal polynomial of a generator of either field in the other. In any one factor the two maps induced by the factor projection are field isomorphisms. Compose one with the inverse of the other. Use any basis in the source field and its image in the target. If \(e\) is even, also choose a basis of the intrinsic quadratic subfield \(\{x:x^9=x\}\) and its image under that same reference isomorphism.

## The determinant and its two factors

Choose ordered \(K\)-bases of \(W\). In the \(F\)-basis of \(W\) assembled from these \(K\)-bases and the reference field frames, any original transporter on the designated mode is \(g(x)=A\sigma(x)\), where \(\sigma:K_T\to K_S\) is the induced field isomorphism. The determinant in these frames is
\[
  \mathrm{N}_{K_S/F}(\det_{K_S}A)(\det_F\sigma)^d .
\]
The first factor is the restriction-of-scalars determinant of \(A\), obtained by splitting the field over an algebraic closure and multiplying over embeddings. The second factor comes from applying \(\sigma\) to \(d\) coordinates. With the common reference frames, \(\det_F\sigma\) is the determinant of a power of Frobenius on a single abstract field. Over an algebraic closure it acts on the \(e\) embeddings by the corresponding power of an \(e\)-cycle. Thus for odd \(e\) its determinant is one. For even \(e\), its determinant equals the determinant of its restriction to the quadratic subfield in the chosen compatible quadratic bases. Only when \(d\) is odd and \(e\) is even is a second line needed.

## Implement the factors as ordinary tensor equations

Include the original tensor, the field multiplication, \(K\otimes W\to W\), the \(K\)-exterior algebra \(H=\Lambda_K W\), its multiplication and field action, and the inclusion \(W\to H\). Include its top \(K\)-line \(D=\Lambda_K^dW\) with field action and inclusion into \(H\). The faithful field action fixes \(\sigma\) as the one induced by the original map. The inclusion and products fix the map on \(D\) as the semilinear exterior map. In the top \(K\)-basis its multiplier is \(\mu=\det_K A\).

In the chosen top frame attach copies of the \(K\)-line \(D\), using the \(F\)-linear maps \(x\mapsto x^{3^j}\) into them, for \(0\le j<e\). The action on the \(j\)-th copy has multiplier \(\mu^{3^j}\) along the same \(\sigma\), since \(\sigma\) commutes with Frobenius. Multiply these copies in a sequence of bilinear product maps in their chosen coefficient frames. Such a product is surjective to the next \(K\)-line, forcing its multiplier as the product of the incoming multipliers. The last multiplier equals \(\mathrm{N}_{K_S/F}(\mu)\), so the map restricts to the \(F\)-span of its coordinate \(1\) with exactly that multiplier. Include that \(F\)-line by a linear inclusion. This is the norm-line construction from [intrinsic coefficient-field pencils](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ObjectwiseTensorOrientation/FieldPencils.md), applied here to an exterior line without a pencil.

When \(d\) is odd and \(e\) is even, include the quadratic subfield in \(K\), its exterior algebra over the base field with degree-one inclusion, and its top \(F\)-line. Its multiplier is \(\det_F(\sigma|_{\mathbb F_9})\). Form the product of this line and the norm line with a surjective bilinear map to the final line. In the remaining cases use the norm line alone. The final multiplier \(m\) is exactly the determinant in the constructed \(F\)-basis of \(W\).

Let \(C_T,C_S\) be the ordered constructed \(F\)-basis matrices in raw coordinates, separately for the two tensors. The raw determinant is \(m\det C_S/\det C_T\). Mark the final line vector with coefficient \((\det C_T)^{-1}\) on the source and \((\det C_S)^{-1}\) on the target. Its preservation is equivalent to raw determinant one. Conversely every input transporter with that determinant extends by the specified field, exterior, and product actions. All equations refer to this same transporter.

There are \(O(e)\) auxiliary \(K\)-lines and \(\dim_F H=e2^d\). The ladder can use distinct named roles, or be bundled into fixed-signature sums with coordinate label algebra \(F^e\). In the bundled version a marked first idempotent and the shift to successive idempotents fix the label order, and one aggregate bilinear map encodes all consecutive products. The ladder uses explicit field arithmetic and polynomial-size data. The [retained-two-sign compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignChain/RetainedSigns.md) consequently retains the other two raw signs in one two-sign instance. The ordinary or one-sign compiler gives the corresponding lower-tier reductions when fewer other signs are required.

## Scope

The construction works even for a single centroid block with arbitrarily large absolute mode dimensions. For example take the multiplication tensor of the full matrix algebra \(M_2(K)\) as an \(F\)-tensor. Its centroid is its center \(K\), by substituting the unit in the three centroid equations. Its relative rank is four and its absolute mode dimension is \(4e\). The theorem applies to all recognized local-centroid tensors of small relative rank, and does not assume algebra multiplication structure. The organizing idea is to make each exact step depend on the same underlying change of coordinates, as in the [pinned AgentAlphaXiv step-verification study](https://huggingface.co/datasets/alphaXiv/AgentAlphaXiv/resolve/a63f46066ac16fcb470f41933415e175825b6bcc/papers/01a1b00f-3a81-791c-98fd-c5daefe85ff8.pdf). The theorem is informal and does not settle the chain without this hypothesis.
