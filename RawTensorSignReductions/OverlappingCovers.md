# Removing a tensor determinant using an overlapping subspace cover

Work over $\mathbb F_3$ with explicitly given three-tensors. An orientation constraint in a mode means that the corresponding map has determinant one in the input coordinate frames. This note gives an informal deterministic single-instance reduction wherever a covariant tree of subspaces is recoverable as specified below. It applies to the one-to-zero, two-to-one and three-to-two sign reductions.

## The exact cover criterion

Let $V$ be the mode whose sign is to be removed. Suppose a polynomial-time procedure recovers a rooted tree $\mathcal T$ with vertices labelled by subspaces $W_v\subseteq V$. Covariance means that every ordinary tensor transporter carries the entire labelled tree to the tree recovered from the other tensor, by a rooted tree isomorphism and its actual linear map on $V$. A tree whose labels are distinct before rooting specifies the vertex action automatically. Conditions on dimensions and span below can all be checked by linear algebra. Write $I_v=W_v\cap W_{\operatorname{par}(v)}$ for a nonroot vertex and $I_r=0$ at the root. Require

\[
 \sum_v W_v=V,\qquad \sum_v\dim W_v-\sum_v\dim I_v=\dim V.
\]

For fixed constants, require that the number of vertices is polynomial in the dense input length $L$, the maximum unordered fan-out is $O(\log(2+L))$, and each quotient $Q_v=W_v/I_v$ has dimension $O(\log(2+L))$. Two endpoints are recognized independently. If one passes and the other fails, covariance rules out ordinary equivalence. When both pass, the following construction removes the designated sign and retains any other requested signs.

## Two exact sequences on the same carrier

Put $X=\bigoplus_v W_v$, $Y=\bigoplus_v I_v$ and $Q=\bigoplus_v Q_v$. Let $p:X\to V$ add the subspace inclusions. There are two injections $B,B_0:Y\to X$. For $v$ nonroot and $x\in I_v$, the map $B$ sends $x$ to its copy in $W_v$ and its negative in $W_{\operatorname{par}(v)}$. The map $B_0$ sends $x$ just to its copy in $W_v$. The boundary $B$ is injective by inspecting a leaf's coordinate and deleting that leaf repeatedly. Since $pB=0$, surjectivity and the dimension identity make $0\to Y\xrightarrow{B}X\xrightarrow{p} V\to0$ exact. Also $0\to Y\xrightarrow{B_0}X\to Q\to0$ is exact by the quotient definitions. Both sequences are covariant under the same rooted tree isomorphism. No arbitrary choice of an edge orientation enters this statement.

In raw bases of $Y,X,V,Q$, let $d_V$ be the determinant in $X$ of the column list consisting of $B$ applied to the basis of $Y$ followed by any lifts of the basis of $V$. Define $d_Q$ with $B_0$ and lifts of the basis of $Q$. Both are nonzero, and changing the lifts adds combinations of earlier columns. Put $\gamma=d_V/d_Q$. A transporter from $T$ to $S$ induces maps $g_X,g_Y,g_Q$ and its original map $g_V$. Block triangular determinants give
\[
 (\det g_X)d_V(T)=d_V(S)\det g_Y\det g_V,\qquad
 (\det g_X)d_Q(T)=d_Q(S)\det g_Y\det g_Q.
\]
Taking the ratio yields $\det g_V=(\gamma_T/\gamma_S)\det g_Q$. Thus an orientation mark $\gamma$ in the top determinant line of $Q$ enforces exactly $\det g_V=1$.

## Assemble the root line at polynomial size

Write $D(v)$ for the descendants of $v$, including $v$. Form a family of vertex subsets by taking, at each vertex, the union of $D(c)$ for any subset of its children, optionally with $\{v\}$ added. Keep the nonempty sets, remove duplicates, and include all full $D(v)$ and singletons. This covariant family $\mathcal F$ has at most $|\mathcal T|2^{1+b}+2|\mathcal T|$ members when $b$ bounds the fan-out. Each nonsingleton used in the recursive assembly admits a split into two disjoint members of $\mathcal F$ until each branch reaches a singleton. Each split simply separates one child tree or the root of its own tree. For $I\in\mathcal F$ take a one-dimensional space
\[
 L_I=\det\left(\bigoplus_{v\in I}Q_v\right).
\]
A basis for $L_I$ is computed from the ordered local bases; no exterior algebra of the whole direct sum is built. For disjoint $I,J$ with $I\cup J\in\mathcal F$ include the determinant product $L_I\otimes L_J\to L_{I\cup J}$. Its scalar in chosen frames is the sign of the shuffle of quotient basis vectors. This formula handles block permutations with odd or even local dimensions, and is natural for every linear isomorphism that transports the labelled subsets.

Here is an explicit bounded-order tensor system for the construction. Use the coordinate algebra $F^{\mathcal T}$ with its unit, adjacency as a bilinear form on its coordinate idempotents, and a marked root idempotent. The aggregate spaces $X,Y,Q$ are modules over this algebra by componentwise multiplication. Include the maps $Y\xrightarrow{B_0}X\to Q$ and the sum map $X\to V$. A map on $X$ respecting each $W_v$ and the sum map is forced by $g_V$ and the vertex permutation, since the sum map is injective on each vertex. The inclusions and projections force the maps on $Y$ and $Q$. Along with the original tensor, these marks suffice to preserve the two sequences above.

The direct sum of the local exterior algebras $\bigoplus_v\Lambda Q_v$ has total dimension at most $|\mathcal T|2^{\max_v\dim Q_v}$. Include its multiplication, the embedding of $Q$ in degree one, and its unit map from the vertex coordinate algebra. These data force each local top-line map. The direct sum of $L_I$ is a module over the coordinate algebra $F^{\mathcal F}$. The linear map from the latter algebra to $F^{\mathcal T}$ taking $e_I$ to $\sum_{v\in I}e_v$ forces its index permutation to be the transported subset permutation. Include the map from the sum of $L_I$ into the local exterior-algebra sum which inserts each singleton into the corresponding local top line and is zero on the other components. Include the determinant products in one bilinear map on the sum of $L_I$, zero for pairs whose disjoint union is not in the family. By induction on the split expression, a map on the full-set line agrees with $\det g_Q$. Finally mark $\gamma$ times its chosen top-line basis vector. The orientation calibration then tests the desired sign.

Conversely any input transporter passing the removed sign, as well as any retained signs, induces the maps on every marked carrier. The determinant product covariance ensures all the aggregate products are preserved. Thus the whole system implements the lock on precisely the original transporter. All spaces, dense arrays and time bounds are polynomial in $L$, and there is a fixed set of role types using tensor order at most three. Apply the ordinary compiler for zero retained signs or the earlier retention compiler for one or two. ## Recover an overlapping cover from the raw slices

There is a concrete polynomial recognition test. View the raw tensor as a linear matrix pencil $M(z):U^*\to V$ with contraction space $Z$. Assume $\dim Z\le c\log(2+L)$ for a fixed $c$. For each projective linear form $[\ell]\in\mathbb P(Z^*)$, compute
\[
 W_\ell=\bigcap_{\ell(z)\ne0}\operatorname{im}M(z).
\]
The list of distinct $W_\ell$ of dimension at least three is covariant under all tensor transporters. Derive a graph on that list by joining two vertices exactly when their intersection is nonzero. If it is a tree, choose its center as root. If its center is an edge, subdivide that edge and give the new root the intersection subspace of its endpoints. This adds one vertex of that dimension and replaces the edge intersection with two copies of it, preserving the dimension identity. The additional root is distinguished even if its subspace repeats an original label. This supplies a canonical choice under all tree permutations. Check the exactness and width conditions on the resulting tree. Enumeration of forms and slice values together, their intersections and the tests all take polynomial time.

For an explicit family with all three mode dimensions growing, let $m=3^e$. In the $\mathbb F_3$ span of the vectors $(1,a,a^2,a^3)$ over $\mathbb F_{3^e}$, as $a$ ranges over the field, denote these $m$ vectors by $\ell_i$. View them as forms on the dual $Z$ of that span. Any four different vectors are independent over the extension by Vandermonde and therefore over $\mathbb F_3$. The dimension of $Z$ is at least $e$ by projection to the second coordinate and at most $1+3e$. Choose a path on the $m$ labels. Use the private pencil blocks $\ell_i(z)I_4$ at vertices. For each edge $e=ij$ fix an order of its endpoints, use a copy of $\mathbb F_9$ in both matrix modes, and use the block given by multiplication by $\ell_i(z)+\iota\ell_j(z)$, where $\iota^2=-1$. That block is invertible exactly when $(\ell_i(z),\ell_j(z))\ne(0,0)$. The matrix modes both have dimension $4m+2(m-1)$, are concise, and the span choice gives conciseness in the label mode.

A private four-space contributes to $W_\ell$ exactly when $\ell$ is proportional to its form $\ell_i$. An edge two-space contributes exactly when $\ell$ belongs to the span of its endpoint forms, by the inclusion test on kernels. The independence of up to four forms means that a nonvertex projective form belongs to at most one such edge span. Consequently the threshold of three selects just the vertex spaces, each equal to its private four-space plus the incident edge two-spaces. They recover the chosen path, satisfy the exact dimension identity, and have each local quotient dimension at most eight.

This example gives a nontrivial two-sign comparison in the two large matrix modes. Write an ordinary automorphism as $M(Cz)B=A M(z)$, with $A$ on the output mode and $B$ on the input. The recovered vertex labels force $\ell_{\pi(i)}(Cz)=\lambda_i\ell_i(z)$ where $\pi$ is a path automorphism and $\lambda_i=\pm1$. The intersections of adjacent recovered spaces are exactly the edge blocks. Recover the same configuration in the input dual by transposing the pencil. Thus $A$ maps the edge subspaces to their counterparts, and $B$ induces the corresponding maps on the edge quotients of the input. For the remaining pieces, $A$ induces the private vertex maps modulo the sum of the edges, and $B$ restricts to the private subspace annihilating the edge duals. The quotients of the recovered vertex spaces by their incident edge spaces distinguish these private summands, in the output and in the input dual. All block permutations have positive determinant because all blocks are even-dimensional.

On a private block the pencil equality gives $A_i=\lambda_i B_i$, by looking at its diagonal block modulo the edge outputs and restricting to the private input. Hence its two determinants have product one. On an edge look at its column and the matched edge output row. The equality gives $A_e L_\mu=L_{\mu'} B_e$ where $L$ denotes multiplication in $\mathbb F_9$, and $\mu'$ is obtained from $\mu$ by a signed permutation of the two coordinates. In particular, at $\mu=1$ the multiplier $\mu'$ is one of $\pm1,\pm\iota$, whose multiplication map has determinant one. So $\det A_e=\det B_e$. Multiplying on the two filtrations proves $\det A\det B=1$ for every automorphism. Now reflect only the $A$ mode to obtain an ordinarily isomorphic pair whose transporter determinant product is always minus one. It has a negative two-sign verdict. The two large tested modes here are linear in $m$, even though the third label mode is logarithmic in the written input length.

## Scope

The local pieces and their overlaps are found by invariant tests on the original tensor. The determinant quotient amounts to eliminating a duplicated state using two exact transitions, an application of the verification discipline illustrated by the pinned AgentAlphaXiv step-decomposition study. The study supplies no mathematical premise. The subspace criterion extends the determinant-line method for a single flattening and the aggregate determinant-line method for direct sums. Arbitrary tensors need not pass the cover test, so the unrestricted reverse reductions are still open.
