# Finite-ring Tensor Isomorphism within one rational six-manifold type

**Theorem.** Let $R$ be a nonzero finite commutative unital ring supplied by invariant-factor coordinates for its additive group and by the structure constants of multiplication on its additive generators. Set $m=\operatorname{char} R$. For three explicitly presented finite $R$-modules with fixed free-cover ranks, encode a tensor by an array of coefficients in the free covers. There is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time map from this data to a triangulated closed, simply connected, stably parallelizable smooth six-manifold $M_T$ with torsion-free homology and $b_3=0$ such that the following hold for inputs over the same ring and with the same free-cover ranks.

1. The tensors are isomorphic if and only if the resulting manifolds are orientation-preservingly diffeomorphic. Equivalently, they are orientation-preservingly homotopy equivalent, or one admits a degree-one map to the other.
2. All outputs for this ring and these ranks have the same rational homotopy type. Their oriented cohomology rings are isomorphic over $\mathbb Z[1/m]$.
3. For every prime $p$, an oriented cohomology-ring isomorphism over $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ exists if and only if the $p$-primary scalar extensions of the input tensors are isomorphic. Consequently, for this constructed family the integral classification is equivalent to the simultaneous prime-local cohomology classification. This statement concerns the localized rings.
4. For every input format the double suspensions of all outputs are homotopy equivalent to
   $$
   \bigvee_{j=1}^{b_2(M_T)}(S^4\vee S^6)\vee S^8.
   $$

The same double-suspension restriction already suffices for the full integral Tensor Isomorphism-completeness of the triangulated manifold problem. The finite-ring theorem concerns compactly specified rings. For example, it applies to $\mathbb F_{p^e}$ given over $\mathbb F_p$ by an irreducible degree-$e$ polynomial, with $p$ in binary. The case $R=\mathbb Z/m$ with free modes already reduces to integral Tensor Isomorphism by the previous presentation gadget. The new parts are the uniform arbitrary finite-ring lift and the localization and suspension conclusions after geometric realization. [Earlier presentation gadget](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/f98f6bdf13cb7ba606f020ef0ea07512028cc9d1/TensorUniversalityApplications/PRESENTED-MODULE-TENSOR-ISOMORPHISM.md) · [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Part V](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1)

## Quotient tensors and their lifts

Let $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ be the given additive generators of $R$. Expand the presentations over $\mathbb Z$. For each named $R$-module write $P_i=\mathbb Z^{s r_i}$ for the cover obtained by taking those $s$ coordinates for each of the $r_i$ module generators. The kernel is generated by the additive relations in those copies of $R$ and the columns of the module presentation multiplied by the $g_j$. The multiplication tables also give integer matrices lifting each $g_j$ action to $P_i$. Stabilize each cover to $F_i=P_i\oplus P_i$ with the second summand mapped to zero. Denote the new kernel by $K_i$, and write $D_{ij}:F_i\to F_i$ for the chosen lift of the $g_j$ action, extended by zero on the added summand.

Every additive isomorphism of the quotients lifts to an isomorphism of the stabilized covers. Here is the formula, valid over any commutative coefficient ring. If $a:P_i\to P_i'$ and $b:P_i'\to P_i$ lift a quotient isomorphism and its inverse, use
$$
H(x,y)=(ax+y-ab y,\ x-by).
$$
The inverse sends $(z,w)$ to $(w+b(z-aw),z-aw)$. Projecting the first output coordinate to the target quotient gives precisely the original isomorphism on the source quotient. This also lifts isomorphisms on images of marked linear maps. For a fixed input format the two copies already have the same dimensions for every instance. When a comparison is supplied with different free-cover ranks, stabilize by the other side's cover, giving the same pairwise reduction with twice the general marked data. This pairwise version is sufficient for polynomial many-one reduction without a fixed-format promise.

Put $V=(F_1\otimes_{\mathbb Z} F_2)\otimes_{\mathbb Z} F_3$. Let $L\subset V$ be the subgroup generated by the images of $K_i$ in the corresponding factors, and by the relations
$$
(D_{1j}u)\otimes v\otimes w-u\otimes (D_{2j}v)\otimes w,\qquad
u\otimes (D_{2j}v)\otimes w-u\otimes v\otimes (D_{3j}w).
$$
It suffices to take all $j$ and basis vectors $u,v,w$. The quotient $V/L$ is the tensor product of the three original modules over $R$. Indeed killing the first relations passes to their underlying additive groups. Killing the displayed relations imposes precisely $R$-balancing, since the $g_j$ generate the additive group of $R$. This characterizes the quotient by the universal property. The array lifts to an integer vector $\widetilde T\in V$ by expressing its coefficients and the designated tensor generators in the covers.

## A fixed list of free tensors

Use a free sort $G$ of rank $s$ with basis $e_1,\ldots,e_s$. In the data include the vector $e_1$ and the endomorphism sending $e_j$ to $e_{j+1}$ for $j<s$ and $e_s$ to zero. An automorphism preserving these two records fixes $G$ pointwise, over the integers and after all scalar extensions. For each $i$ add the free sort $U_i=G\otimes F_i$ and the canonical bilinear product map, so its transformation is $I_G\otimes A_i$ if the change of basis on $F_i$ is $A_i$. Let $D_i:U_i\to F_i$ be determined by $D_i(e_j\otimes x)=D_{ij}x$. Use the marked subgroup
$$
\Gamma_i=\{(u,D_i u+k)\mid u\in U_i,\ k\in K_i\}\subset U_i\oplus F_i.
$$
The auxiliary direct-sum sort has its two canonical inclusion maps. Mark $K_i$ in $F_i$ as well. Preserving $K_i$ and $\Gamma_i$ onto their target counterparts is equivalent to requiring that $A_i$ descend to an $R$-linear isomorphism. In fact preservation of the graph is precisely
$$
A_iD_i-D_i'(I_G\otimes A_i)\ \text{has image in }K_i'.
$$
This is the collection of equations expressing that the quotient map intertwines each $g_j$.

Add $F_1\otimes F_2$ and $V$ as free sorts with the two canonical bilinear maps. Their changes of basis are thereby determined, and the change of basis on $V$ is $A=\bigotimes_i A_i$. For the last marked data let $E$ be a rank-one sort with a fixed generator $e$, and use the sort $V^+=V\oplus E$ with its canonical inclusion of $V$ and projection onto $E$. Mark the subgroup $L\oplus E\subset V^+$. Also mark the vector $\widetilde T+e$. An isomorphism of this data on $V^+$ must act by
$$
(x,z)\longmapsto(Ax+zs,z),\qquad s\in L'.
$$
The quotient map on $E$ is the identity because the specified generator is fixed. The induced $A$ sends $L$ onto $L'$ automatically, since the $R$-module isomorphisms induce an isomorphism of their tensor products and the maps from the covers commute. Conversely any such $A$ and $s\in L'$ preserve the mark. Carrying the specified vector to its counterpart is exactly $A\widetilde T+s=\widetilde T'$. Thus the system is isomorphic precisely when the input tensors are isomorphic. The cover lift supplies every quotient automorphism needed in this assertion.

All marked subgroups are full-rank lattices in their ambient free groups because the module quotients are finite, and $L\supset mV$. For each mark, extract a lattice basis by integer Hermite normal form and record its inclusion matrix with a duplicate domain summand mapping to zero. All ranks now depend only on the free-cover ranks and the additive ring format. Stabilization of the mark domains permits arbitrary lifts of isomorphisms on the images if the basis matrices lose injectivity after base change. The other records are named vectors, maps and bilinear maps, and there are a constant number of records. Computing the kernel generators, products, graph generators and Hermite normal forms takes polynomial bit time. The largest sort rank is $O(1+s\sum_i\dim F_i+\prod_i\dim F_i)$.

The fixed-type free-data compiler in the earlier presentation proof converts this list to a single ordinary free integral three-tensor. That compiler works over any commutative ring and commutes with scalar extension entry by entry. Applying the same argument above after extending scalars to $S$ proves exact correspondence for $S\otimes_{\mathbb Z}R$ and the scalar-extended input modules. In particular the quotient by the image of $L$ still implements exactly the scalar-extended balanced tensor product, by right exactness. The coevaluation mechanism of the earlier proof has been replaced here by graph marks and a shear in one full tensor-product sort. [Fixed-type free-data compiler, Section 3](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/f98f6bdf13cb7ba606f020ef0ea07512028cc9d1/TensorUniversalityApplications/PRESENTED-MODULE-TENSOR-ISOMORPHISM.md#3-erase-the-marks-over-the-original-ring)

## Passage to manifolds and localization

Apply the symmetric-tensor branch of Part V, Theorem 3.7, to the resulting free three-tensor, using the same block placements and identity gadgets over the integers and localizations. All these rings are domains, hence satisfy the paper's diagonal-full property. The output gives an integral symmetric trilinear form $\mu_T$. Switching between a symmetric array and the covariant trilinear form uses the dual action, which preserves orbit equivalence. The framed triangulation construction realizes the form $6\mu_T$ on $H^2(M_T;\mathbb Z)$ with $b_3=0$ and torsion-free homology. Common scaling by six preserves and reflects equivalence over the integers and their localizations. This last construction is polynomial in binary coefficient lengths. [Part V, Theorem 3.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S3.SS3) · [Binary framed realization](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/f98f6bdf13cb7ba606f020ef0ea07512028cc9d1/ManinGrochowCrossovers/IntegralTensorManifolds.md)

The equivalence with integral diffeomorphism and with degree-one domination is the same Wall and Poincaré-duality argument as in the integral result. Over $S=\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ or $S=\mathbb Z[1/m]$, the free cohomology ring of each output is determined by its oriented trilinear pairing: $H^4$ is dual to $H^2$, and the degree-two product is evaluated by the cubic pairing. Thus the base change of the construction computes comparison of the oriented localized rings exactly. In $\mathbb Z[1/m]$ all input modules vanish and the oriented cup rings agree. Over $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, the modules are precisely their $p$-primary parts and the tensor equivalence is the corresponding primary equivalence. The primary decomposition of a finite ring is a product of its primary factors, and its modules and balanced products decompose accordingly. Thus simultaneous primary equivalence is equivalent to the original tensor equivalence. Finally the simply connected six-manifolds are formal, so the equality of rational cohomology rings gives the claimed rational homotopy agreement. [Wall](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01389738) · [Miller](https://mathscinet.ams.org/mathscinet-getitem?mr=528561)

## The common double suspension

Let $N$ be a closed, simply connected, stably parallelizable smooth six-manifold with torsion-free homology, $b_3=0$, and even integral triple cup tensor. Poincaré duality implies that the mod-two cup square on $H^2(N;\mathbb F_2)$ vanishes, since all its pairings with $H^2$ vanish. By the torsion-free, $c=0$ case of the double-suspension decomposition, $\Sigma^2 N$ is homotopy equivalent to $B\cup_\alpha e^8$ with $B=\bigvee_{j=1}^{b_2(N)}(S^4\vee S^6)$. If $b_2=0$, $N$ is a simply connected integral homology sphere and the result is immediate. [Suspension homotopy of six-manifolds, Theorem 1.1 and Lemmas 4.1–4.2](https://msp.org/agt/2023/23-1/agt-v23-n1-p12-p.pdf)

Stable parallelizability gives a stable fundamental class of degree one by Pontryagin–Thom: embed with stably trivial normal bundle, add trivial dimensions to frame the normal bundle, and collapse to its Thom space. This gives a stable section of the degree-eight quotient $B\cup_\alpha e^8\to S^8$. In the stable cofibration, the connecting map therefore vanishes. Equivalently, $\alpha$ is stably zero.

In degree seven the Hilton–Milnor splitting of $\pi_7(B)$ has contributions from the $\pi_7(S^4)$ and $\pi_7(S^6)$ projections, and one integral Whitehead coefficient for each pair of bottom $S^4$ spheres. Higher length factors and mixed $S^4,S^6$ pairs have bottom sphere dimension greater than seven. The $\pi_7(S^6)$ components are already stable and so vanish. For each bottom sphere the kernel of stabilization on $\pi_7(S^4)$ is generated by the Whitehead square. This is the EHP exact sequence in that degree, followed by Freudenthal. The Hopf invariant sends the Whitehead square to $\pm2$, and on the projected attaching map it is the corresponding cup-square coefficient. Every cup product on $\Sigma^2 N$ vanishes. Hence each Whitehead-square component is zero. The mixed bottom-sphere Whitehead coefficients are the off-diagonal cup products in the cone, so they vanish too. Therefore $\alpha=0$, proving the wedge formula for $N$.

Every output in the binary framed construction has even triple cup tensor, so its entire double-suspension type is determined by the input format. This part of the result also applies to the earlier full integral TI-complete family.

## One finite coefficient ring suffices on the constructed family

In the unpadded construction, the oriented cup rings over $\mathbb Z/(6m)$ are already a complete comparison test for the resulting manifolds. Let $p$ divide $m$, put $a=v_p(m)>0$, and put $c=v_p(6)$. The graph, balancing-lattice, and mark-domain proof applies over $\mathbb Z/p^a$ by scalar extension. Part V's same symmetric block construction applies there because this coefficient ring is a local principal ideal ring and has the diagonal-full property. The resulting symmetric tensors represent the same primary tensor-orbit problem as over $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, since the primary ring and module data are annihilated by $p^a$. An isomorphism of $6\mu_T$ and $6\mu_{T'}$ over $\mathbb Z/p^{a+c}$ reduces to an isomorphism of $\mu_T,\mu_{T'}$ over $\mathbb Z/p^a$, by cancelling the common factor $p^c$ in those congruences. Conversely, any matrix giving an isomorphism at depth $a$ can be lifted to depth $a+c$, with determinant still a unit because its reduction modulo $p$ is invertible. That lift matches $6\mu_T,6\mu_{T'}$ at depth $a+c$. At a prime of $6$ absent from $m$ the extra depth is $c$ and both pairings vanish. The Chinese remainder theorem now gives the comparison over the single ring $\mathbb Z/(6m)$. The prime factorization of $m$ is used only in the proof. The universal coefficient theorem and Poincaré duality identify oriented cup-ring comparison with comparison of these pairings over the stated ring. [Part V, Proposition 3.6 and Theorem 3.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S3)

## Degree-one domination inside bidirectional nonzero-degree domination

For the finite-ring construction, each pair of output manifolds in the same format admits nonzero-degree maps in both directions. Their common rational cohomology ring is a positive-weight model because the manifolds are formal. Fedor Manin's positive-weight theorem produces actual maps that become equivalences after rationalization. Existence of a degree-one map still classifies the original finite-ring tensors. [Manin, Theorem B](https://doi.org/10.1090/proc/15978)

The same conclusion can be imposed with the additional restriction that **every nonzero mapping degree among the outputs is an integer cube**. Before realizing the cup forms, append $n+1$ mutually orthogonal unit cubic axes to the $n$-variable form. For an invertible rational similitude of the padded forms, the $n+1$ axes have linearly independent images, each with rank-one contraction. At least one image has a nonzero padding coordinate. Its remaining padding coordinates vanish, and its old component is in the contraction radical. Evaluating the cube on it gives the common similitude multiplier as a rational cube. An integer that is a rational cube is an integer cube.

For degree one the similitude is an integral isometry, by the split cohomology injection and the common Betti numbers. Cancelling a padded unit axis preserves the original integral isometry problem. In detail, if the image $v$ of a unit axis has a nonzero coordinate on the chosen target unit axis $e$, rank-one contraction forces this coordinate to be the only nonzero padding coordinate and forces the remaining component to have zero contraction. The coordinate is $1$, so a shear by the remaining component makes $v=e$. If the coordinate on $e$ is zero, split its complement into $\mathbb Z v\oplus\ker(\mu(v,v,-))$, using $\mu(v,v,v)=1$. These summands are orthogonal for cubic products because the contraction has rank one. Swapping $v$ and $e$ is then an integral isometry. In both cases cancel the matched axes by restricting to their complements, and iterate. Padding preserves all the remaining common-type assertions by taking the same direct sum everywhere, and the realized pairing still has even coefficients.
