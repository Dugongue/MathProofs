# Tensor Isomorphism and four-dimensional homotopy equivalence

## Dimension thresholds

For every fixed dimension bound $d\ge 4$, rational homotopy equivalence of finite simply connected simplicial complexes of dimension at most $d$ is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb Q$. At dimension four the hardness construction already has torsion-free homology and $H_3=0$. With a dimension bound of at most three, rational Betti numbers decide equivalence in deterministic polynomial time. Thus completeness below dimension four would imply a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for rational TI.

For finite simply connected simplicial complexes of dimension at most four with torsion-free integral homology, ordinary homotopy equivalence is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb Z$. Hardness holds with $H_3=0$. With a dimension bound of at most three and the same homology promise, integral Betti numbers decide equivalence in polynomial time.

The common construction takes a binary integral symmetric bilinear cup table and returns a finite simplicial complex of dimension at most four that realizes the table exactly. The output size is polynomial even when the attaching maps need exponentially large integer coordinates in their smallest obvious CW presentations. Whitehead's realization and classification give the underlying geometric relationship. The claims here concern polynomial bit complexity on simplicial input and the exact dimension threshold for applying the two tensor reductions. [Whitehead's classification](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02568048) · [Rational tensor reduction, Corollary 7.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S7) · [Integral tensor reduction, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S1.SS3)

## A simplicial cone construction with no dimension overhead

Let $f:X\to Y$ be a simplicial map, with $X$ a simplicial $r$-sphere. Give the vertices of $X$ a total order and use a disjoint copy of its vertex set. For every simplex $\{v_0<\cdots<v_k\}$ of $X$, include the simplices
$$
\{f(v_0),\ldots,f(v_i),v_i,\ldots,v_k\},\qquad 0\le i\le k
$$
and their faces, along with $X$ and $Y$. Repeated image vertices are included only once. Denote the resulting abstract simplicial complex by $C_f$. It contains $X$ and $Y$ as subcomplexes, and has dimension at most $\max(\dim Y,r+1)$.

The map $C_f\to Y$ sending $v\mapsto f(v)$ and fixing $Y$ is a homotopy equivalence relative to $Y$. Replace vertices from $X$ by their images in global increasing order. On each displayed staircase simplex, the union of the image before and after any one replacement is again contained in a staircase simplex. Its cut index is the position of the replaced vertex when that vertex is present. If no vertex of the simplex is replaced, the two images coincide. Indeed, all source vertices in that simplex prior to the cut have already been sent to their image vertices. The remaining source vertices are in the suffix. These successive simplicial maps are contiguous and the straight-line homotopies fix $Y$. The retraction restricts to exactly $f$ on $X$.

Attach the ordinary simplicial cone on the disjoint source copy $X$ inside $C_f$. There is a map of pairs to the usual attachment $Y\cup_f D^{r+1}$, obtained from the retraction and the identity on the cone. On the lower spaces it is a homotopy equivalence. On relative homology groups it is an isomorphism, since both quotients by the lower space are $CX/X$. Consequently the map is a homology equivalence. For $r\ge2$ and simply connected $Y$, both attached complexes are simply connected by van Kampen, so the map is a homotopy equivalence. The same construction makes arbitrarily many attachments simultaneously, with disjoint source copies and cone vertices. The maps of pairs and relative summands then give the same proof. In each fixed dimension there are at most a constant number of new simplices per source simplex, and the output has dimension at most $\max(\dim Y,r+1)$.

## Binary cup-table realization

Take a symmetric bilinear map $\mu:\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb Z^n\to\mathbb Z^m$ with entries $m_{ikj}=m_{kij}$. We construct a simply connected complex $P_\mu$ with $H^2=\mathbb Z^n$, $H^4=\mathbb Z^m$, vanishing $H^3$, and cup multiplication $\mu$.

Start with a wedge of $n$ based two-spheres. For an original sphere $\iota_i$ and any needed signed binary integer $a$, compress the class $a\iota_i$ as follows. Give each binary prefix of $|a|$ its own temporary two-sphere, except for the leading prefix which already represents $\iota_i$. If $u$ is the previous prefix and $v$ is the newly defined sphere, the next bit $\epsilon$ is encoded by a 3-cell with boundary class $v-2u-\epsilon\iota_i$. If a negative sign is needed, a final 3-cell with relation $v+u$ gives its signed representative. Zero terms need no sphere. Each relation has at most four signed summands counted with multiplicity.

Do this for all needed constants and attach all these 3-cells simultaneously. In the CW attachment the boundary matrix on temporary generators is triangular with diagonal $1$. The reduced homology is concentrated in degree two and the old wedge identifies with a free basis. The old wedge maps to the new simply connected space by a homology equivalence, hence by a homotopy equivalence. In the simplicial version use the preceding cone construction. Each degree-bounded 3-cell relation is one of finitely many fixed simplicial maps from $S^2$ to a wedge of at most four two-spheres, followed by assignment of the target spheres. These maps may be fixed once and for all by simplicial approximation to the pinch maps and signed degree-one maps. The new space again contains the old and temporary spheres as actual simplicial subcomplexes. The number of temporary spheres and relations is linear in the total binary lengths of the required nonzero coefficients.

For $i=k$, write $m_{iij}=2q_{ij}+\epsilon_{ij}$ with $\epsilon_{ij}\in\{0,1\}$. For $i<k$, use a temporary representative of $m_{ikj}\iota_i$, and for diagonal entries use one of $q_{ij}\iota_i$. For each basis vector $j$ of the target of $\mu$, attach a 4-cell by the sum
$$
 \sum_{i<k}[m_{ikj}\iota_i,\iota_k]
 +\sum_i\bigl([q_{ij}\iota_i,\iota_i]+\epsilon_{ij}\eta_i\bigr).
$$
Here the first entries in brackets are the temporary signed spheres, and $\eta_i$ is the Hopf map to the original sphere $i$. The bracket convention is the attaching map of the top cell of the oriented product $S^2\times S^2$. With that convention $[\iota_i,\iota_i]=2\eta_i$ and the diagonal Hopf invariant of $\eta_i$ is $1$. The sign of a Hopf generator is chosen accordingly. Over the original wedge, the cellular coefficient of a cup product is evaluated on the attachment by its Hopf or cross-Whitehead coefficient. For distinct $i,k$, the single bracket is the product attachment and evaluates to $1$ on the corresponding cross product. Folding both factors onto one sphere gives the diagonal coefficient $2$; the Hopf attachment has diagonal coefficient $1$. These evaluations are additive in the attaching class, by naturality under the pinch that expresses a sum of attaching maps. This verifies each entry $m_{ikj}$. [Whitehead products and the basis of $\pi_3(\bigvee S^2)$](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT-doublepage.pdf)

To keep the actual simplicial input polynomial, fix bounded-size simplicial templates for the Whitehead and Hopf maps to a wedge of two two-spheres and to one two-sphere, respectively. The Whitehead map comes from the quotient of $\partial(D^2\times D^2)$ to the wedge, and the Hopf template can be a fixed simplicial approximation of the Hopf fibration. Each source template has two disjoint marked tetrahedra mapped to the base vertex. Such marked templates exist by first precomposing with a degree-one map of $S^3$ that is constant on a ball containing the two tetrahedra, then using relative simplicial approximation. All the resulting finite complexes and maps are fixed, so they can be included in the reduction as constant data.

For each 4-cell, make the connected sum of the indicated copies of the templates along marked tetrahedra, with compatible orientations and with all glued boundaries mapping to the base vertex. The marked tetrahedra in a template have disjoint vertex sets, so the chain of gluings gives a triangulated $S^3$ with a number of simplices linear in the number of summands. Collapsing its separating spheres represents the usual pinch sum of the summand maps. Assigning the target spheres, including repetitions of the same sphere, gives the stated attaching map. An empty sum uses a constant sphere map. Apply the simplicial cone construction with $r=3$. The result is homotopy equivalent to the wedge of the $n$ original two-spheres with the $m$ indicated 4-cells attached, since replacing the intermediate target by the old wedge is a homotopy equivalence and homotopy cofibers are invariant under that replacement. This CW model exhibits the claimed homology and the cup table. The simplex counts are polynomial in the binary table length, and the output has dimension at most four.

## Rational classification and reductions

Every simply connected finite CW complex of dimension at most four is formal over $\mathbb Q$. To see this, take its minimal Sullivan model $M=(\Lambda V,d)$, with $V^1=0$. The degree-two generators are closed and represent all of $H^2(M)$. Map them to their corresponding cohomology classes in $(H^*(M),0)$. The closed degree-three generators give $H^3(M)$; map these to their classes and choose any complementary degree-three generators to map to zero. A degree-three differential has degree four and is a product of degree-two generators. Since it is a boundary in $M$, it maps to zero in cohomology.

In degree four the only decomposables are products of degree-two generators, and they are closed. Thus the degree-four cocycles are the direct sum of these decomposables and the closed degree-four generators. Map the latter to their own classes and choose any complementary degree-four generators to map to zero. All generator differentials of degree at least five vanish after mapping to cohomology, since the space has dimension at most four. Send every higher generator to zero. The assignment gives a differential graded algebra map inducing the identity on cohomology in degrees at most four, and both cohomologies vanish in higher degrees. It is a quasi-isomorphism.

It follows that in dimension at most four, rational homotopy equivalence is determined by the Betti numbers and the symmetric product $H^2\times H^2\to H^4$. Below dimension four, this product vanishes. At dimension four or below, compute the rational cohomology and its product by polynomial-time exact linear algebra on simplicial cochains. Compare the Betti numbers and use the symmetric two-slot action of $\operatorname{GL}(H^2)$ and the independent output action of $\operatorname{GL}(H^4)$ on the product to reduce to ordinary rational TI. For a fixed dimension bound greater than four, use the finite rational minimal model and tensor-system reduction in the preceding fixed-dimension result. [Fixed-dimensional upper reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/f98f6bdf13cb7ba606f020ef0ea07512028cc9d1/ManinGrochowCrossovers/FixedDimensionRationalHomotopy.md) · [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk, tensor-system reduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219)

For hardness, Grochow–Qiao's symmetric matrix-space isometry reduction transforms every rational TI instance to a pair of vector-valued symmetric products under exactly these basis changes. Equivalently the frontal slices record the scalar-valued coordinate forms, and the free third slot changes their basis or, for tuples with redundant slices, the basis of their full coefficient space. Clear denominators by a common nonzero factor for the pair. The cup realization returns a pair of actual four-dimensional finite simplicial complexes with this product and with no third homology. Their rational homotopy types agree exactly when the original tensors are isomorphic. This supplies hardness for every fixed bound at least four. [Grochow–Qiao, Corollary 7.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S7)

## Integral classification and reductions

Suppose $X$ is simply connected, of dimension at most four, with torsion-free homology. Choose a map $W_2=\bigvee S^2\to X$ representing an integral basis of $H_2(X)$, possible by Hurewicz. Use its mapping cylinder to regard $W_2$ as a subspace of a space homotopy equivalent to $X$. This pair is 2-connected, since $W_2\to X$ is an isomorphism on $\pi_2$. Relative Hurewicz identifies $\pi_3(X,W_2)$ with $H_3(X,W_2)=H_3(X)$. The long exact homotopy sequence shows that each such relative class comes from $\pi_3(X)$, because $\pi_2(W_2)\to\pi_2(X)$ is an isomorphism. Thus add a wedge of three-spheres $W_3$ representing an integral basis of $H_3(X)$.

Now take $W=W_2\vee W_3\to X$, replacing the map by its mapping cylinder again. The pair is 2-connected and has zero relative third homology. Relative Hurewicz first gives $\pi_3(X,W)=0$, then $\pi_4(X,W)\cong H_4(X,W)\cong H_4(X)$. Attach to $W$ a 4-cell for each chosen relative representative of a basis of $H_4(X)$, and map these cells to $X$. Their attaching maps have zero image in $H_3(W)$ since its map to $H_3(X)$ is an isomorphism. The wedge decomposition of $\pi_3(W)$ has $\pi_3(W_2)$ and the integral basis classes of $W_3$, with no mixed terms in degree three, so the attaching maps are homotopic to maps into $W_2$. The resulting map from the attached CW complex to $X$ is a homology equivalence in all degrees, hence a homotopy equivalence.

For a wedge of two-spheres, $\pi_3(W_2)$ is free on the individual Hopf classes and the cross Whitehead products. Each attaching class therefore corresponds bijectively and naturally to the integral symmetric bilinear form it evaluates on $H^2$ under the cup pairing. A change of basis of $H_2$ is realized by a self-homotopy equivalence of the wedge. If two cup tables are isomorphic by integral basis changes in degrees two and four, use the dual changes in homology. After applying the selected map on wedges, the attaching classes then match via the integral change of basis in $H_4$. To extend over a source 4-cell, choose the relative $\pi_4$ class of the requisite linear combination of target 4-cells. Relative Hurewicz identifies that class by its cellular homology coefficients. Its boundary agrees with the source attachment after applying the wedge map, and a boundary homotopy adjusts the representative to have the exact prescribed boundary. With the selected basis map for the three-sphere wedge, this gives a homology equivalence and hence a homotopy equivalence. Necessity of the cup-table isomorphism follows from functoriality of cohomology. This proves the torsion-free classification used here, including cases with nonzero $H_3$.

Compute integral cohomology bases and cup products from simplicial input by integer normal forms and the simplicial cup formula in polynomial bit time. As in the rational case, compare Betti numbers and reduce the resulting symmetric two-slot action to ordinary integral three-tensor isomorphism. Conversely, Theorem 1.4 of Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun reduces ordinary integral three-tensor isomorphism to the two-slot symmetric action, and the exact four-dimensional cup realization applies. In dimension at most three the same relative construction stops at the wedge of two- and three-spheres, which gives the stated polynomial algorithm. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S1.SS3)
