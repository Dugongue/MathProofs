# Tensor isomorphism for diagrams on presented modules

## Theorem

Let $R$ be a commutative unital ring. Fix a finite linear signature of arbitrary arity. Allow finitely many named finitely presented $R$-modules. A signature can specify a tensor word in their names, a marked element or a finitely generated submodule of a word, or an $R$-linear map between two words. On free named modules, dual occurrences are allowed with the contragredient action. Input coefficients are lifts to the supplied free coordinate covers. Input maps are promised to descend to the presented quotients. Isomorphism uses one common module isomorphism for each named module, and respects every structure record through the induced maps on the words.

This problem has a deterministic polynomial many-one reduction to ordinary free three-tensor isomorphism over the same ring. The reduction is valid without a principal ideal or Noetherian hypothesis. It also works with the signature as part of the input when words are explicitly listed and each ambient word rank is charged in the input length. Over $\mathbb Z$, and uniformly over $\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$ for binary $m$, the reduction is polynomial in bit time. The same output pair tests the base-changed diagrams after every unital scalar extension $R\to S$, including nonflat extensions. Submodules after base change mean their images in the scalar-extended ambient modules, and the allowed free duals extend as ordinary free duals.

Over fields this contains the basis-explicit models of Grochow and Qiao, including relations given by arbitrary lists of spanning vectors. Over $\mathbb Z$ it adds mixed diagrams on modules that may contain both torsion and free summands. The prior same-ring transfer for three presented modes and one tensor treats an important special case. The graph construction below replaces its three-sector coevaluation by constraints for arbitrary structure maps and for each step of a tensor word. ## Stabilizing coordinate covers

Let $p:P\to M$ and $p':P'\to M'$ be the supplied epimorphisms from free modules. Use $E=P\oplus P'$ with cover $(x,y)\mapsto p(x)$, and $E'=P'\oplus P$ with $(z,w)\mapsto p'(z)$. Each kernel has an explicit generating list formed from the original relation columns and the added basis. Given an isomorphism $g:M\to M'$, choose lifts $a:P\to P'$, $b:P'\to P$ of $g$ and $g^{-1}$. The block map
$$
(x,y)\longmapsto (ax+y-aby,\ x-by)
$$
is invertible, with inverse $(z,w)\mapsto (w+b(z-aw),z-aw)$, and induces $g$. Thus any quotient isomorphism lifts to transport the stabilized kernels, with no rank or flatness assumption. The same observation serves for the free cover of any finitely generated submodule that must be marked.

In a typed system of free modules, mark a submodule by a linear map whose columns generate it and give the generator domain its own change of basis. Include the other input's number of zero columns on each side. The preceding lift shows that any ambient isomorphism preserving the submodule then extends to this map record. Conversely an isomorphism of the records preserves the image. Each mark has its own independent generator domain, so these choices do not impose extra compatibility. Linear maps are order-two records; tensoring with a pinned scalar $1$ gives order three when needed. A named free scalar sort is pinned by recording its multiplication.

For a freely presented name whose dual is used, keep its original free cover in both diagrams and use the ordinary evaluation pairing with a free formal dual. The paired modules must have equal free rank to be isomorphic. After comparison of ranks no stabilization is required for such freely based names. Equivariance of evaluation forces inverse-transpose transport in the dual. This also ties repeated occurrences to the same change of basis.

## Graph marks enforce equality on quotients

For a presented map $f:M\to N$, let $E,F$ be the stabilized free covers with kernels $K,L$, and choose a lift $B:E\to F$. A choice of lift is part of the given coefficient data, with the new padding coordinates sent to zero. The inverse image of its graph in $E\oplus F$ is generated by the columns $(e_i,Be_i)$ and $(0,\ell_j)$, where $\ell_j$ generates $L$. To prove this, a pair $(x,y)$ is in the inverse image exactly when $y-Bx\in L$. In particular the generating formula stays valid under arbitrary scalar extension. For an element of $M$, use the map $R\to M$ sending $1$ to that element with the pinned scalar base. For a submodule of $M$, mark its preimage, generated by the lifts of its specified generators together with the kernel list of $E\to M$.

When a free direct-sum sort is used as a marked ambient space, include its standard injections from its summands as typed linear maps. They force any transporter on the sum to be the exact sum of the induced maps on the named summands. Thus a transporter preserving the base kernels sends one lifted graph to the other exactly when its quotient maps intertwine the two original maps. This follows by passing to the quotient and comparing the graphs, and also by taking preimages in the converse direction. There is no requirement to preserve a chosen representative modulo relations.

## Tensor words and polynomial size

Assign a separate presented module to each internal node of a binary expression for a word. Before stabilization, its free cover is the ordinary tensor product of the original free covers in its subtree. Its relation matrix lists the generators obtained by putting one original relation in a factor and basis elements in the other factors. This presents the actual tensor product by its universal property. A unital scalar extension commutes with this presentation and with each product map. If a word contains zero-rank covers, its zero-rank leaf on either side may be put first, or both zero-rank leaves may be combined first when they occur in different positions, using the same canonical reordering on both sides and permuting the data coordinates accordingly. This avoids constructing large inactive prefix spaces. All original cover ranks are polynomial in the supplied ranks and the fixed arity, and in the variable-arity case the positive prefix ranks are bounded by the full ambient word rank on that side.

Stabilize the free cover of each prefix module separately. Mark the stabilized kernel of every named quotient, including the prefix modules. For an internal node $W=U\otimes_R V$, write $E_U,E_V,E_W$ for these covers. Include the free sort $E_U\otimes_R E_V$ and the fixed canonical bilinear product into it. Transporters on this auxiliary free sort are thereby forced to be the tensor product of the transporters on the two factors. Mark the inverse-image graph of the induced map
$$
E_U\otimes_R E_V\longrightarrow W
$$
inside $(E_U\otimes_R E_V)\oplus E_W$, using the graph construction. On the original cover summands use the canonical product on basis vectors, and on the added padding summands use zero. Through the quotient, the relation enforces that the independently chosen transporter on $W$ is the actual product of the two quotient maps. Equality on decomposable tensors implies equality on the whole module. Iteration recovers the precise change of basis of every word.

Importantly, only original covers are multiplied over long words. Multiplying stabilized ranks is confined to one graph record, hence to pairs whose ranks are already individually bounded by the sum of the two original ambient ranks. This avoids a factor exponential in the word length even if every original mode has dimension one. Generator lists consist of relations crossed with explicit basis elements, with numbers polynomial in the explicit sizes. The direct sums, injections, evaluation, graph records and binary product records are all typed tensor data of order at most three.

A diagram isomorphism lifts independently to the stabilizations of all named base and prefix modules. Canonical free tensor product and direct sum maps determine their auxiliary transports. Since the quotient diagrams commute, every mark is carried to its counterpart; lift each generator-domain mark independently. Conversely preservation of the typed records gives base quotient maps, forces their designated actions on words, and recovers every original relation and function. Apply the arbitrary-ring free typed compiler in the companion proof to produce one ordinary free three-tensor per input, using common bookkeeping for the pair. Only addition, copying coefficients, and insertion of fixed constants are required. The input and both output diagrams can be interpreted over any test ring $S$. All proofs use images of the scalar-extended relation lists and direct sums of free modules, and so verify the simultaneous equivalence over each $S$, even when tensoring the old kernel destroys injectivity.

## Consequences for the two homotopy classifications

For free names with integral structure coefficients this is a single tensor comparison that distinguishes integral transport from transport after passage to $\mathbb Q$, using the same underlying integer arrays. For general integral presentations the same statement compares the original diagrams and their rationalizations, interpreting arbitrary marked submodules by their images. The distinction between these two decisions is retained by the two four-dimensional homotopy reductions: use the fixed integral-to-symmetric two-input tensor construction of Part V, which is correct over $\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb Q$ on the same coefficient arrays, then realize the symmetric cup table by the four-dimensional construction. The resulting pair is integrally homotopy equivalent exactly on the integral YES instances, and rationally homotopy equivalent exactly on the rationalized YES instances. The topology has torsion-free homology even when the input diagrams do not.
