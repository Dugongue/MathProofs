# Uniform universality of Tensor Isomorphism over commutative rings

## The answer to the published question

Grochow–Qiao's Open Question 10.4 asks whether order-$d$ Tensor Isomorphism is universal for basis-explicit linear structures of order $d$. In the SIAM version it is Question 7.4. Grochow–Ivanyos–Qiao–Sun restated the full arbitrary-degree question in footnote 5 of their FOCS 2026 paper. The answer is affirmative. The construction below also works over every commutative unital ring, with explicit free modules, and takes a variable number of labelled tensors. It gives a side bound linear in the total slot ranks and number of labels. An earlier MathProofs argument already gives the qualitative field answer. The new contribution is one direct, quantitative ring construction that can also process arbitrary explicitly generated relation submodules. [Part I, §10.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S10.SS1) · [FOCS 2026, footnote 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00874v1#S1.SS1) · [Earlier field proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/f98f6bdf13cb7ba606f020ef0ea07512028cc9d1/LinearStructureUniversality/UNIFORM-SIGNATURE.md)

**Theorem.** For any commutative unital ring $R$, consider a list of labelled finite free $R$-modules with standard bases, and a list of explicitly represented tensors on ordered words in those modules and their duals. Multiple occurrences of the same module, arbitrarily many labels, and variable arity are allowed. The isomorphism problem under one change of basis per named module reduces to ordinary free three-tensor isomorphism over $R$. Explicitly generated relation submodules of word spaces can be included. The construction is deterministic and uses polynomially many coefficient copies and structural entries $0,1$ in the dense input model, with word lists explicit. The same comparison holds after every scalar extension. In particular, for all $d\ge3$ this gives universality of ordinary $d$-TI as asked, by appending $d-3$ rank-one modes to the three-tensor.

For an order-at-most-three network, let $L\ge1$ bound the number of named modules, labelled records and individual slot occurrences together. Let $S$ be one plus $L$, the sum of the named module ranks, and the sum of all slot ranks with multiplicity. Each output mode has dimension $O(S)$, with an absolute implicit constant. For higher order, apply the same bound to the auxiliary network described below. The coefficient construction on a fixed format is a projection.

For $R=0$ all comparisons are affirmative, so assume $1\ne0$.

## 1. Reducing arbitrary arity and marking relation images

Duals of free modules use the inverse transposes of the primal maps. A word $U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_k$ in primal and dual modules can be constructed by binary product markers. Each marker is the canonical isomorphism $U\otimes V\to W$ on the product basis, expressed as a mixed order-three tensor. Its preservation forces $g_W=g_U\otimes g_V$. For an individual tensor of order at least four, partition its slots into three nonempty ordered groups, construct each product by binary markers, and store the original entries as one order-three tensor on those products. For a multilinear map, use its dual input slots and primal output slot. For a relation, construct the whole word space and use a generator map into it; the next paragraph explains how its image is all that is marked. Pairing an empty tensor word with a scalar line marked by its unit handles constants.

After deleting zero-domain or zero-target records in a public format, every active product rank is at most the size of the corresponding dense input record, apart from a zero image supplied with no generators. Omit a relation only when both lists are empty in the chosen format. Otherwise the nonempty list charges for the full ambient rank. In a nonzero relation the list of generators includes at least one column whose length is the full word rank. Every binary product marker has at most the square of its output rank many dense entries. The number of product sorts and markers is bounded by the input word-list lengths. Hence all steps take polynomial time in the explicit input. For equal rank $n$ in an isolated $k$-slot tensor the three groups can be chosen with at most $\lceil k/3\rceil$ slots each.

To encode a generated relation $K\subseteq W$, let $p:P\to K$ and $q:Q\to K'$ be the two generator maps viewed as surjections onto their images. The input maps in the comparison are $(p,0):P\oplus Q\to W$ and $(q,0):Q\oplus P\to W'$. Suppose an ambient isomorphism carries $K$ onto $K'$, inducing $u:K\to K'$. Because the covers are free, lift $u,u^{-1}$ to maps $a:P\to Q,b:Q\to P$. The map between the padded generator modules
$$
(x,y)\mapsto (ax+y-aby,\ x-by)
$$
is invertible and satisfies $q(ax+y-aby)=up(x)$. Its inverse sends $(z,w)$ to $(w+b(z-aw),z-aw)$. Conversely, an isomorphism of the padded generator maps necessarily preserves the relation images. The same padding can be fixed in advance when the input column counts are public. This construction uses no independent-column test or division. It works over each scalar-extended ring, even when tensoring a submodule inclusion is not injective, because the marked object there is the image of the extended generator matrix.

## 2. One bilinear map retaining designated summands

Convert each record into a typed bilinear map by dualizing the two input slots as needed. Add a shared scalar sort and pin its unit using its multiplication; unary and binary constraints are padded with pins from that sort. Give each primal or dual reference sort an index, and each record occurrence a private index of the same rank as its reference. For each index $i$, create copies $X_i,Y_i,Z_i$ on the three sides of one bilinear map $B:X\times Y\to Z$. Include scalar copies $X_0,Y_0,Z_0$ and one extra scalar summand $s_i$ in $Y$ for each private index $i$. All these summands are initially designated.

The nonzero structural blocks of $B$ are multiplication on $X_0,Y_0$, the two scalar-copy maps $X_i\times Y_0\to Z_i$ and $X_0\times Y_i\to Z_i$, evaluation of each dual reference against its primal reference in the first two slots to $Z_0$, and, for each private index $i$ with reference $r$, the two blocks $X_0\times s_i\to Z_0$ and $X_i\times s_i\to Z_r$ given by scalar multiplication. Put each data map in its private block. Each of these blocks uses a distinct pair of input summands. The scalar sort used to pad the original network is one ordinary reference label with its own pinned multiplication data, separate from $X_0,Y_0,Z_0$.

An isomorphism preserving the summands of $X,Y$ also preserves each $Z_i$, since each is the image of a scalar-copy map. If the $X_0,Y_0$ transformations are units $a,b$, the three maps on the copies of a label are $ag_i,bg_i,abg_i$. This follows directly from the two copy maps. The first $s_i$ block forces its scalar to be $b$. The second forces $g_i=g_r$. Evaluation forces inverse-transpose on dual reference sorts, even over a ring, by applying to every basis vector and covector. The remaining blocks impose the original equations after cancelling the units $a,b$. Conversely every input isomorphism extends by taking $a=b=1$ and the stated copy and scalar maps. The ranks of $X,Y,Z$ are $O(S)$.

## 3. Erasing all labels in linear dimension

Let the designated summands of $X$ be $X_i$. Use the projections $X\to X_i$ and their kernels $K_i=\bigoplus_{j\ne i}X_j$. The intersection of the $K_j$ with $j\ne i$ is exactly $X_i$, with the empty intersection interpreted as $X$. Thus preserving all these kernels is equivalent to preserving every summand. The sum of the ranks of the projection targets is just the rank of $X$. Apply the same construction to the $Y_j$.

Define an associative unital algebra with three main idempotents $e_0,e_1,e_2$, copies of $R$ on diagonal corners, and off-diagonal corners $X=e_0Ae_1,\ Y=e_1Ae_2,\ Z=e_0Ae_2$. Their composition is $B$. For the projection onto $X_i$, add a fresh vertex $s_i$ with $e_1Ae_{s_i}=R$, $e_0Ae_{s_i}=X_i$. Their composition $X\times R\to X_i$ is the scalar multiple of the projection. The left annihilator in $X$ of $e_1Ae_{s_i}$ is $K_i$. For the projection onto $Y_j$, add a fresh vertex $t_j$ with $e_{t_j}Ae_1=R$, $e_{t_j}Ae_2=Y_j$. The analogous composition marks the projection kernel by its right annihilator in $Y$. Choose an order $v_0,\ldots,v_{k-1}$ of all the vertices introduced so far, including the three main vertices.

Add distinct vertices $q,p_0,\ldots,p_{k-1}$. Add a sentinel corner $q\to p_0$ of free rank $M$, strictly greater than two and every other existing corner rank. Add one corner $p_i\to p_{i+1}$ of rank two for $i<k-1$, and one corner $p_i\to v_i$ of rank one for every $i$. Give every new vertex its diagonal copy of $R$. All off-diagonal products involving a new corner vanish, and all unspecified off-diagonal products vanish. Outputs of the specified off-diagonal compositions are annihilated by every off-diagonal factor on either side. This verifies associativity on triples of off-diagonal elements. Diagonal scalars act on their incident source and target, so associativity also holds on all remaining triples.

Write $J$ for the sum of off-diagonal corners. It is the commutator ideal: the quotient is commutative, and any off-diagonal element $x$ with source $i$ satisfies $x=[e_i,x]$. We have $J^3=0$, and $A/J$ is a product of copies of $R$ indexed by the vertices. An algebra isomorphism induces an isomorphism of $A/J$ and of its bimodule $J/J^2$. Over every residue field the quotient automorphism permutes the coordinate idempotents, preserving the dimensions of the corresponding bimodule corners. Each new corner survives in full in $J/J^2$, since it is outside every product output. Each other corner of the bimodule is a quotient of its original free corner, hence has dimension less than $M$ after passage to the residue field. Thus the sentinel corner fixes both $q$ and $p_0$. From a fixed $p_i$, its unique outgoing corner of rank one identifies $v_i$ and its unique outgoing corner of rank two, when present, identifies $p_{i+1}$. Induction fixes every vertex over every residue field. An idempotent in every maximal ideal is zero since its complement is a unit. Applying this to the coordinates of each quotient idempotent and its complement shows that the vertex idempotents are fixed in $A/J$ over $R$ itself.

If the isomorphism sends $e_i$ to $f_i$ in the target, set $u=\sum_i f_i e_i$. Then $u\equiv1\bmod J$, hence $u$ is invertible, and $f_i u=u e_i$. Conjugation by $u^{-1}$ gives an isomorphism preserving every actual vertex. It preserves the annihilator marks and therefore all the designated summands, so reconstructs the original typed comparison. Conversely a summand-preserving isomorphism of the bilinear maps induces the required actions on the projection targets, extends by the identity on the one-dimensional projection arguments, and extends by the identity on all sentinel, path and tag corners.

There are $O(L)$ vertices and marker corners. The total rank of the projection targets is the sum of ranks of $X$ and $Y$; the projection arguments, path and tag corners contribute $O(L)$; and the sentinel contributes $M=O(S)$. Thus the full algebra rank is $O(S)$. The construction retains $B$ as a block of the multiplication table and uses only zeros, ones and coordinate copies.

## 4. From the multiplication table to ordinary TI

The multiplication tensor of a free associative unital algebra records multiplication as a usual three-tensor. An ordinary tensor isomorphism gives invertible linear maps $P,Q,H$ with $H(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$. Put $a=P(1)$ and $b=Q(1)$. The identities $H(x)=P(x)b=aQ(x)$ make right multiplication by $b$ and left multiplication by $a$ bijective. Thus both are units. For example if $ac=1$, then $a(ca-1)=0$ implies $ca=1$ by injectivity. Now
$$
H(xy)=H(x)(ab)^{-1}H(y).
$$
It follows that $x\mapsto (ab)^{-1}H(x)$ is a unital algebra isomorphism. Conversely an algebra isomorphism supplies an isomorphism of the multiplication tensors. This proves the side bound and all comparisons. A base change preserves every displayed structure constant; over the new ring the same proof applies.

The earlier tensor ladder and fixed-type triangular algebra compiler supply the two starting constructions. The projection-target marks and directed path give the linear side bound with arbitrary label counts. Its special case over fields answers the published question for the stated basis-explicit inputs. Over $\mathbb Q$, composing the three-tensor output with four-dimensional cup-table realization gives one polynomial comparison of actual rational homotopy types. Over $\mathbb Z$, the same is true for integral homotopy equivalence of the four-dimensional torsion-free realization family, or for oriented diffeomorphism of the six-dimensional stably parallelizable realization family. The universal reduction therefore goes from all explicitly supplied higher-order structures to actual spaces, through a single three-tensor per object in a common format.
