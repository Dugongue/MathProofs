# Exact proof-degree transfer to longest simple timetable rides

A timetable lists directed rides between physical stations, each with an integer departure and arrival time. A ride sequence here starts at a prescribed station at time zero, ends at a prescribed station by a given time, waits for zero time between hops, and never visits a physical station twice.

**Theorem.** Given a finite simple graph \(G\), one can construct a timetable with \(O(1+|V(G)|+|E(G)|)\) events and services and integer horizon \(T\) such that proper three-colourings correspond bijectively to rides of \(T\) hops. Every scheduled hop has duration one. There are at most three station events per time, each physical station has at most two appearances across all times, the physical station graph has maximum degree at most six, and the graph on station-time events is series-parallel. The literal indicator equation systems below have isomorphic coordinate rings over every commutative base ring. Over any field, their minimum polynomial-calculus refutation degrees agree after both are raised to at least two. Each system polynomial-calculus simulates the other with no degree increase above two and at most \(O(1+|V(G)|+|E(G)|)\) additive expanded monomial size.

In particular, there are such negative timetable instances with \(N\) events whose full-length ride impossibility needs degree \(\Omega(N)\) and expanded size \(2^{\Omega(N)}\) over every field. The exact degree transfer applies to the indicators and checks defined below. ## Timetable

Order the vertices of \(G\) and the edges incident at each vertex. Create one segment for each vertex \(v\), sharing its exit event with the entrance event of the next segment. All entrance and exit events have distinct physical station labels, subject only to these identifications of single events. In segment \(v\), put three internally disjoint paths from its entrance to its exit, one per colour \(c\), each of \(\max\{1,\deg(v)\}+1\) hops. Each level of internal events has one event per colour, matching a designated incident edge if \(v\) is nonisolated. In that edge’s position give the colour-\(c\) event the station label \((e,c)\). For isolated vertices use fresh labels. Assign times starting at zero with each hop moving to the next time. These are all scheduled rides. For an empty graph the timetable consists of one event at time zero serving as both endpoints, with a unique zero-hop ride.

A no-wait sequence of \(T\) hops, with \(T\) the sum of the segment hop counts, takes exactly one monochromatic branch in each segment. Its sole possible duplicated labels are the two appearances of \((e,c)\) when the endpoints of \(e\) both choose \(c\). This proves the bijection. The number of station-time events and of services is \(O(|V(G)|+|E(G)|)\). Internal event nodes have two neighbours in the undirected event graph, and each is identified with at most one other event into a physical station label. Hence such physical stations have degree at most four. Entrance and exit nodes have at most three neighbours in each segment they meet, giving the degree-six bound. Each segment is a parallel composition of three directed paths, and the full event graph is their series composition.

## Algebraic equality

In the timetable system, use one variable \(z_a\) per station-time event \(a\). Add the following polynomials as axioms set equal to zero.

- For every time, the sum of its variables minus one, each Boolean polynomial \(z_a^2-z_a\), and products of distinct variables at that time.
- For consecutive times, the product \(z_a z_b\) for every pair with no scheduled service from \(a\) to \(b\).
- For every two different events at the same physical station, the product of their variables.

At a singleton time the first axiom just sets its variable to one. Over a field these are exactly the stated ride constraints, since a family of pairwise orthogonal Boolean elements summing to one consists of one \(1\) and several \(0\)s. Write \(C_G\) for the usual graph-colouring system with variables \(x_{v,c}\), one-hot equations at vertices (the Boolean equations, pairwise products, and sum minus one), and \(x_{v,c}x_{w,c}=0\) for every edge and colour.

Map every internal event variable in colour branch \(c\) of segment \(v\) to \(x_{v,c}\), and each singleton-time variable to one. Every timetable axiom becomes zero or a graph-colouring axiom, and every repeated station axiom becomes exactly its edge-colour product. Conversely choose the first level of variables in each segment as the graph-colouring variables. To derive the graph edge axiom using that level, first identify every later internal \(c\)-variable with the first. For two consecutive internal levels of the segment, write the colour variables as \(a_c,b_c\). From the second level sum equation, multiplication by \(a_c\), and the forbidden transition axioms \(a_c b_{c'}=0\) for \(c'\ne c\), obtain \(a_c b_c-a_c=0\). The first level sum and symmetric calculation give \(a_c b_c-b_c=0\). Thus \(a_c-b_c=0\). Taking linear combinations along levels identifies each later variable with its first variable. At a repeated station, the product of its two event variables is an axiom. Replacing each factor by its first-level counterpart uses this equality multiplied by one variable, twice, to derive the corresponding graph edge product.

These maps are inverse on the two quotient coordinate rings, and the proof uses only integer coefficients, so it holds after any base change. All the required axiom derivations have degree at most two. In the timetable-to-colouring direction, each variable is replaced by a single variable or a constant, so replaying arbitrary polynomial-calculus proofs does not increase degree or expanded size. The reverse direction uses a variable renaming and the degree-two preparation just given. For the size bound, the identifications with the first level are propagated once per internal event by summing the previous identification and the consecutive-level equality. Every repeated station product then requires only constantly many monomials after this preparation. The number of layers, forbidden products and repeated stations is linear in the construction size, since the layer width is at most three. This proves both exact degree comparisons and additive size bounds.

For the lower bound, apply the construction to the fixed-degree graph family of Conneryd, de Rezende, Nordström, Pang and Risse. Their all-field linear degree lower bound applies to the same colouring polynomials. There are \(N=\Theta(|V(G)|)\) events for that family, and all timetable axioms have degree at most two. The polynomial-calculus size–degree theorem then gives exponential expanded size. The comparison concerns the timetable rather than the specific transit datasets, whose schedules and parameters are separate empirical inputs.

Decision hardness for related temporal paths with waiting-time restrictions, including hardness at physical degree six in a timestamp model, was already known. The statement above gives a parsimonious coordinate-ring and two-way proof transfer for a width-three station-event timetable with unit travel durations and station multiplicity at most two.
