# A size lower bound for determinant gadgets that lift every special-linear map

Let F be the field with three elements. A typed tensor system consists of finitely many labelled vector spaces W_j over F and a list of fixed tensors in products of the W_j and their duals. Its automorphism group G is the subgroup of the product of their general linear groups fixing every listed tensor. Linear maps, bilinear maps, multiplications and vectors are examples of such tensor data. Let r bound the number of slots of every listed tensor. A mode may occur more than once in a tensor. The list itself may have any length.

Assume a distinguished invariant subquotient V of one W_j has dimension n and carries the natural coordinates for the determinant comparison. Taking V to be one of the labelled spaces is a special case. Let π:G→GL(V) be its action. Write M=Σ_j dim W_j.

**Theorem.** Suppose n≥5 and π(G) contains SL(V). Let d≥1 satisfy 2d<n and rd<n. If

    3M < 3^(n−1)−1,       max_j dim W_j < ½ binom(n,d+1),

then π(G)=GL(V). In particular a bounded-order system whose size is polynomial in n cannot have exact projected automorphism group SL(V) for arbitrarily large n. For a fixed positive r, taking d=⌊(n−1)/max(3,r)⌋ gives an exponential lower bound on the total explicit vector-space dimension of any such system with exact projection SL(V).

The result applies whether or not the automorphism extensions split, and allows arbitrary auxiliary linear actions on the spaces. The proof has two parts. The small dimension bound first yields a subgroup surjecting onto SL(V) with odd kernel. The binomial bound then forces a lift of a reflection. No assumptions on the original kernel are needed.

## A small cross-characteristic representation is trivial

For n≥3, every linear representation of SL_n(F) over a field of characteristic two of dimension smaller than 3^(n−1)−1 is trivial. Extend scalars to split the third roots of unity. In the group SL_n(F), use the elementary abelian subgroup U made of matrices I+Σ_(j≥2) a_j E_1j, with a_j∈F. Its elements have order dividing three. The restriction of the representation to U is a direct sum of character spaces. The block copy of SL_(n−1)(F) normalizes U and is transitive on the nontrivial characters. Indeed those characters are naturally all the nonzero linear functionals on the row parameter space over F, and determinant-one changes of a space of dimension at least two act transitively on nonzero vectors in its dual. If any nontrivial character occurs, all 3^(n−1)−1 occur. Below this dimension U acts trivially. Its conjugates contain every elementary transvection, which generate SL_n(F), proving the claim.

## Extract an odd-kernel lift subgroup

Take the preimage of SL(V) in G and choose in it a minimal subgroup H that still maps onto SL(V). Such a subgroup exists because G is finite. Put K=ker(π|_H). Every maximal subgroup B of H contains K. Otherwise BK=H by maximality and normality of K. Then B maps onto SL(V), contrary to the minimal choice of H. Therefore K is in the Frattini subgroup Φ(H), the intersection of the maximal subgroups.

The finite group Φ(H) is nilpotent. To see this directly, let P_0 be one of its Sylow subgroups. The Frattini argument gives H=Φ(H)N_H(P_0), since Φ(H) is normal and its Sylow conjugacy accounts for every conjugate of P_0. If the normalizer were proper, a maximal subgroup containing it would already contain Φ(H), and could not contain their product H. Thus every Sylow subgroup of Φ(H) is normal, which proves nilpotence. Its subgroup K is also nilpotent. Write K=P×O for its Sylow two-subgroup P and its odd-order complement O, both characteristic in K and hence normal in H.

Set H̄=H/O, with kernel P for its projection onto SL(V). The conjugation action on P/Φ(P) is a linear representation over the two-element field, since this quotient is elementary abelian. The action factors through SL(V) because conjugation by P acts trivially on that abelian quotient. Its dimension is at most log₂|P|.

For any positive integer a, the two-valuation of |GL_a(F)| is Σ_(i=1)^a ν₂(3^i−1). For odd i a summand is one. For even i it equals 2+ν₂(i), by repeated factorization of 3^(2i)−1 and the fact that 3^(2i)+1 has exactly one factor of two. Thus the sum is at most 2a+ν₂(a!), which is at most 3a. Since H is a subgroup of the product of the GL(W_j), and P is a two-subgroup of K≤H before taking the odd quotient, log₂|P|≤3M. The small cross-characteristic bound now makes its representation on P/Φ(P) trivial.

The kernel of Aut(P)→Aut(P/Φ(P)) is a two-group. For a short proof, fix a basis of the quotient and consider all ordered generating tuples lifting that basis. There are |Φ(P)|^s tuples for basis length s. Each tuple generates, since any proper generated subgroup lies in a maximal subgroup containing Φ(P). The automorphism kernel acts freely on the tuples, so its order divides a power of two. It follows that the centralizer C of P in H̄ has two-power index. Its image in SL(V) is a normal subgroup with two-group quotient. This quotient is trivial because SL(V) is generated by elements of order three. Hence C surjects onto SL(V), and C∩P=Z(P) is central in C.

Use central closedness of SL_n(F) for n≥5. Every central extension of it splits, by the classical finite-field computation of the Steinberg extension. Thus C contains a subgroup mapping isomorphically onto SL(V). Pull that subgroup back to H. We obtain a subgroup H₁ of the original stabilizer surjecting onto SL(V) with odd-order kernel O. [Steinberg, Lectures on Chevalley Groups, Corollary 2](https://math.soimeme.org/~arunram/Resources/LOCGS7CentralExtensions.html)

## Use the diagonal signs to lift a reflection

Inside SL(V), take the diagonal subgroup D₀ of signs with product one, in the chosen natural basis of V. A Sylow two-subgroup D̃ in its preimage in H₁ maps isomorphically onto D₀, since the kernel is odd and the quotient is a two-group. The signed permutation matrices of determinant one induce the full symmetric group on the n diagonal positions. For any such signed permutation, choose a lift in H₁. It conjugates D̃ to another Sylow subgroup of the same preimage. Correct the lift by an element of that preimage to normalize D̃, by Sylow conjugacy. The correction projects to D₀, whose conjugation on D₀ is trivial. Thus the induced action of the normalizer on diagonal characters includes all permutations of their positions.

Each W_j now splits into simultaneous sign eigenspaces for D̃. A character of D₀ is indexed by a subset A of the n coordinates, modulo identifying a subset with its complement. If its shortest representative has size s<n/2, its orbit under coordinate permutations has size binom(n,s). If s=n/2 the size is ½ binom(n,s). All those weights occur in the same W_j once one does, by the normalizing lifts just constructed. The bound dim W_j<½ binom(n,d+1) therefore forces each occurring weight to have a unique representative A of size at most d. Duals have the same sign characters.

Define a new invertible linear map on each W_j by multiplication by −1 on a weight space if position 1 belongs to its chosen representative A, and by 1 otherwise. To see that these maps fix a specified tensor, decompose that tensor into products of weight spaces. Every component which can occur is invariant under D̃, since D̃ was already a subgroup of its stabilizer. Thus the symmetric difference of the chosen representatives in that product is either empty or the full set of n coordinates. A tensor with at most r slots has representative union size at most rd<n, so the full set is impossible. The empty symmetric difference means that the product of our new signs is positive. The same scalar rule acts on duals because signs are their own inverses. Hence the new tuple belongs to G.

The construction preserves any D̃-invariant subspace because it is scalar on each weight space. It acts on V by diagonal reflection in its first coordinate, including if V is only an invariant subquotient of a labelled space. Indeed the natural coordinate weights have the singleton representatives, and those are the chosen shortest representatives. This reflection and SL(V) generate GL(V) over F. This completes the proof.

## Applicability to the one-sign problem

A universal determinant marker attached independently of the input tensors is one natural route to a single ordinary TI comparison. To use such a marker by exact extension of transporters, every determinant-one change of the designated space must extend to an automorphism of the fixed added system, and a reflection must fail to extend. The theorem rules this out at polynomial dimension and bounded tensor order for typed systems. Merely adding hidden linear spaces and permitting arbitrary coupling through their kernel does not avoid the size bound. A full exterior volume construction falls outside the bound by using high order or exponentially large intermediate spaces.

The bound concerns a fixed universal system with a visible natural subquotient and complete special-linear lifting. It does not rule out a construction depending on the two compared tensors, a construction that retains only a verdict-preserving collection of transporters, a high-order succinct input model, or a reduction with a different action encoding. This distinction matters for padded or restricted input families. A marker that is intended only to calibrate a known class of transporters, or a recovered covariant tensor feature, need not lift every special-linear map. The alternating-form and operator methods work through exactly this kind of input-dependent restriction. [Single-comparison sufficient tests](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/OneSignTensorComparison/AlternatingForms.md)

This is an informal lower bound for a construction model. It settles neither unrestricted one-sign TI nor the impossibility of a polynomial-time many-one reduction to ordinary TI. Priority for the precise theorem is unverified.
## Even polynomially many exact marked comparisons cannot cover all witnesses

A proper subgroup of SL_n(F) has index at least 3^(n−1). Indeed its action on cosets is nontrivial. In the complex permutation module the same elementary abelian group U argument yields each of the 3^(n−1)−1 nontrivial characters once any is present. The constant vector adds a trivial character. If no nontrivial character occurs, every elementary transvection acts trivially and the coset action is trivial, a contradiction.

Consider instead a comparison of two bounded-order marked systems, as long as the mode maps are recovered on a distinguished invariant subquotient. If it has a transporter, its projected maps are a coset of the source automorphism image. Suppose every projected map has positive determinant. Then that image is contained in SL_n(F), and by the theorem it is a proper subgroup whenever the marked systems meet the size bounds. Thus a branch can retain at most a 3^(1−n) fraction of all positive maps. Even a polynomial number of such branches cannot cover every positive map of the standard pairing tensor to itself, whose positive first-mode image is the entire SL_n(F). This conclusion applies to pair-dependent branches as well. A decision reduction may preserve the existence of a witness by retaining a much smaller set of them, for which the counting argument gives no contradiction.
