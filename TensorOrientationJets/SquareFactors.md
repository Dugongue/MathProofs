# Discarding even Pfaffian multiplicities

**Theorem.** Let a raw tensor T over the field F of three elements determine a polynomial-time computable space B(T) of alternating forms on its tested mode. Assume covariance under pullback by every tensor transporter. Write P_T for the Pfaffian polynomial on B(T), in the original tested-mode coordinates. Suppose P_T is nonzero. Its irreducible factors of even multiplicity can be removed, and each odd multiplicity can be reduced to one, while retaining exactly the physical determinant sign. If the resulting polynomial has polynomially many possible monomials and can be extracted in polynomial time, one ordinary TI comparison enforces the sign. Using straight-line-program factorization gives a bounded-error randomized reduction from the raw input on this promised class. When P_T is a scalar times a square, there is an exact Las Vegas reduction from the raw input under that promise, with no factorization.

This improves the sufficient dimension condition for pencil evaluations. The pencil itself can have linear dimension and the Pfaffian can have high total degree. The relevant size is that of the polynomial after removal of squares.

## Canonical signed part

In fixed coordinates on B(T), define H_T by the unique factorization

    P_T = H_T R_T²,

where H_T has squarefree irreducible factorization, and R_T is normalized to have leading coefficient one in a fixed monomial order. Because the only nonzero square scalar in F is one, the polynomial H_T is independent of the signs of representatives for the square root. Equivalently, write the irreducibles as monic in that order and multiply the odd ones by the leading coefficient of P_T.

If g transports T to S, let h:B(S)→B(T) be pullback in their pencil coordinates. The Pfaffian congruence identity for the raw tested-mode matrix of g gives

    P_T(hx) = det(g) P_S(x).

Linear substitution of variables preserves irreducibility and multiplicity. The monic normalization of the new square factor may multiply it by a sign, so its square stays identical. It follows that

    H_T(hx) = det(g) H_S(x).

Thus preservation of the one nonzero polynomial H is equivalent to positive determinant. This is a statement about the actual transporter, with arbitrary separate choices of pencil bases at the two ends.

## Encode the polynomial with one ordinary query

For d=dim B(T), let k=deg H_T. Give the tensor system the exterior-independent data B(T), its evaluation on the original tested mode by alternating forms, and the truncated symmetric algebra on B(T)* through degree k. Its dimension is binom(d+k,k). The degree-one inclusion and multiplication enforce the usual symmetric action on every piece, because the images of degree-one products span. Mark the vector H_T itself. Include actions on B(T) via its injective form evaluation, so the reading on the auxiliary sort is pullback, with inverse and dual conventions calibrated by the evaluation map. An ordinary transporter extends to these marks exactly when it has positive sign in the tested mode. All components have bounded order. Uniform packing and the fixed-component ordinary tensor-system compiler give an objectwise reduction. Fixed tags keep original raw formats distinct. The exact algebra construction is polynomial in the raw length and binom(d+k,k). [Uniform tensor-system packing](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SLAdjointMultiplicities/PRIOR-UNIFORM-PACKING.md) · [Ordinary TI compiler](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

For raw algorithmic extraction, compute a polynomial-size division-free Pfaffian circuit in the pencil coordinates. The randomized factorization algorithm for straight-line programs produces irreducible factor circuits, with their multiplicities, with high probability. In characteristic three its output can include the appropriate Frobenius powers of inseparable factors. This suffices even here. For a circuit for f^(3^s), take the inverse Frobenius of its evaluation in a finite extension to obtain a black-box evaluation of f at the same point. Its monic normalization can be computed once the small odd factors are interpolated. Factors whose original multiplicities are even need no coefficient interpolation. One can extract the scalar of the full product by evaluating P and the normalized odd factors at a random nonzero point of an odd-degree extension and taking the quadratic character of their ratio. The omitted even powers are squares and an odd-degree extension preserves the two square classes of F. A large enough odd-degree extension has polynomial bit size and makes a nonzero point easy to find. With the stated size promise, ordinary bounded-total-degree interpolation recovers the odd factors using polynomially many evaluations. For instance evaluate on the simplex of tuples of coordinate indices of sum at most k, using k+1 distinct scalars per variable. Products of the successive univariate Newton factors form a triangular interpolation basis on these tuples. Degree above the promised bound or invalid factorizations can be treated as failure. This is a bounded-error randomized reduction because factor correctness uses the factorization algorithm's success guarantee. The algebra encoding after correct extraction is exact. [Division-free Pfaffian circuit](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2003.12.001) · [Kaltofen, Factorization of Polynomials Given by Straight-Line Programs](https://users.cs.duke.edu/~elk27/bibliography/89/Ka89_slpfac.pdf)

## An exact case and a large raw family

When P_T=cR² and P_S=c'R'², the polynomials H_T,H_S are the constants c,c'. By sampling a point of a large odd-degree extension until Pfaffian is nonzero, compute c as the quadratic character of that evaluation. The check that an evaluation is nonzero is exact, and the expected number of trials is bounded by the polynomial zero bound. This is Las Vegas on the promise. If the constants agree, the original ordinary comparison decides the signed question. If they differ, return a fixed ordinary no pair. Alternatively attach the computed constant as an object mark.

For a raw example use the quotient of the tensor product of two equal vector spaces by the annihilator of B inside their hidden exterior square. The earlier linear-recovery argument in the symbolic pencil construction applies for any dimension d≤n−2. Split the tested space into r four-dimensional blocks, and give the pencil an independent coordinate x_i multiplying a fixed symplectic form on block i. The Pfaffian is, up to a nonzero scalar, the product of the x_i². Here d=r can grow linearly in the mode dimension and the exact square test applies. All stabilizer signs are positive by the constant transformation law. A reflection of one raw endpoint flips the computed constant. Add one two-dimensional block with an independent coordinate y to get a Pfaffian with the same square part times y. The signed part is now linear. A sign-minus scaling of one coordinate in the two-dimensional block lifts to a pencil-preserving autotopism of negative sign, so this family includes flexible tensors as well. Both families have more than polynomially many pencil elements. [Raw pencil recovery](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/OneSignTensorComparison/SymbolicPencils.md)

The determinant character survives removal of squares for every nonzero covariant Pfaffian polynomial. Polynomial-size extraction and representation of its signed part are the sufficient resource bounds. The generic full alternating space need not meet those bounds. This is an informal proof, and priority for the application has not been established.
