# Constructive logspace membership for finitely presented subgroups of residually free groups

**General theorem.** Fix any finitely generated residually free group $G$ and any finitely presented subgroup $H$. On input a word on fixed generators of $G$, a deterministic logspace transducer rejects precisely the elements outside $H$ and returns a canonical word on fixed generators of $H$ for every element of $H$. In particular every finitely presented residually free group has an intrinsic logspace normal form. We first give the proof for free factors and then prove the general theorem.

**Free-factor case.** Fix a finite direct product of finite-rank free groups and a finitely presented subgroup $H$ with a fixed finite generating alphabet. There is a deterministic logarithmic-workspace transducer which, given arbitrary words in the ambient factors, rejects precisely the tuples outside $H$. For each tuple in $H$ it outputs a word in the prescribed generators of $H$. Two successful inputs have identical output if and only if they represent the same ambient tuple. All output words have polynomial length in the total input length. In particular, $H$ has an intrinsic logspace normal form.

Here and below the finitely presented subgroup, its embedding and the number of factors are fixed. We construct the data-complexity machine using fixed choices attached to this subgroup. The theorem does not assert a logarithmic-space preprocessing algorithm for a varying list of subgroup generators. Normal forms need not be geodesics. This is an informal proof.

## Producing a proof of a nilpotent identity in logspace

For a fixed finitely generated nilpotent group $Q$ and any fixed finite generating alphabet $X$, there is a finite relator list and a logspace transducer that writes a polynomial-length list of conjugates of signed relators for every null word. The product it writes equals the input **in the free group on $X$**. This last equality permits substitution of lifts which may live in a different group.

Refine a central series of $Q$ into cyclic central factors, including finite factors. Induct on the length. For the trivial group, use the one-letter relators. For one induction step let $\langle z\rangle$ be the bottom central cyclic subgroup, and choose a fixed word $z$ on $X$ for its generator. Apply the quotient algorithm on the same alphabet to a null word. For every signed quotient relator $r$, fix $e_r$ such that $r=z^{e_r}$ in $Q$. Put $r z^{-e_r}$ on the new relator list, together with $[a,z^{e_r}]$ for every signed generator $a$, and $z^m$ if $z$ has finite order $m$. Use the commutator convention $[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$.

For each emitted quotient factor $h r h^{-1}$, use the free identity

$$
h r h^{-1}
=\bigl(h(rz^{-e_r})h^{-1}\bigr)[h,z^{e_r}]\,z^{e_r}.
$$

Expand the commutator using $[u v,b]=u[v,b]u^{-1}[u,b]$. This yields a sequence of conjugates with conjugating prefixes of $h$, in decreasing letter order. If preceding blocks have accumulated $z^E$, conjugate each new true relator factor by $z^E$, then update $E$ to $E+e_r$. The final $E$ is zero when $z$ has infinite order, and is a multiple of $m$ otherwise. In the latter case use signed copies of $z^m$ to finish. This gives the claimed free equality and a finite presentation on $X$.

The quotient certificate has polynomial length by induction. Each $e_r$ is fixed, so the signed accumulator and all prefix indices fit in logarithmic space. Regenerate the indexed quotient output when a letter of a prefix or its inverse is needed. The number of central factors is fixed. Each induction step composes a fixed number of logspace subroutines and enlarges length by at most a polynomial. This proves the certificate claim, including torsion.

## Full subdirect products with virtually nilpotent factor quotients

Suppose $S\leq P_1\times\cdots\times P_d$ projects onto each factor, with $P_i$ fixed free groups and $L_i=S\cap P_i$. Each $L_i$ is normal in $P_i$ because every conjugator has a lift in $S$. Assume finite-index normal subgroups $K_i\leq P_i$ contain $L_i$ and have nilpotent quotients $K_i/L_i$. Let $K=\prod K_i$ and $S_0=S\cap K$.

Use the finite quotient $(\prod P_i)/K$ to reject unrepresented cosets and choose a fixed lift $\sigma\in S$ for each represented coset. Reduce the input $g$ to $g\sigma^{-1}\in K$. Set $B_i=\pi_i(S_0)$. This subgroup has finite index in $P_i$, since $S_0$ has finite index in the subdirect group $S$. Also $L_i\le B_i$ and $B_i/L_i$ is a subgroup of the fixed nilpotent group $K_i/L_i$. Membership in $B_i$ and conversion to its fixed Schreier basis require a finite-state coset scan. Reject any failed coordinate. The resulting words describe $x\in\prod B_i$.

Let $N=\prod_i(B_i/L_i)$ and let $A\le N$ be the image of $S_0$. Since $\prod_i L_i\le S_0$, one has $x\in S_0$ exactly when $\bar x\in A$. Use the known nilpotent constructive membership algorithm on $N$. In particular, one can use its unique representation in a fixed full-form sequence for $A$, then substitute fixed lifts in $S_0$ to get an explicit word $\ell$ with the required image. Mal'cev exponents have polynomial magnitude on ordinary word inputs for a fixed nilpotent group, and hence can be expanded. [Macdonald, Myasnikov, Nikolaev and Vassileva, Theorem 3.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03888)

For each $i$, write the $i$-th coordinate of $x\ell^{-1}$ as a word $w_i$ on the free basis of $B_i$. Its image in $B_i/L_i$ is trivial. Apply the certificate transducer above with that basis for $X$. For each relator fix a word in $S_0$ representing the corresponding **pure** $i$-coordinate element. Such words exist by the definition of $L_i$. For each basis generator fix an arbitrary lift to $S_0$, possible by the definition of $B_i$. Replace each certificate factor by the lift of its conjugator, the pure lift of its signed relator, and the inverse of the lifted conjugator. Other coordinates cancel in every conjugation, and the free equality certifies the exact $i$-th coordinate. The resulting subgroup word represents just the $i$-th residual. Concatenate all residual words in factor order, followed by $\ell$ and $\sigma$, with fixed substitutions into the desired generators of $S$. This recovers $g$. All intermediate tapes can be regenerated by standard closure of logarithmic-space functions under fixed composition.

## Removing the subdirect and intersection hypotheses

For each projection of $H$, fix its finite folded subgroup graph and spanning tree. First freely reduce each input coordinate in logspace. Test whether it labels a based loop in this finite graph; for successes record the non-tree edge generators along the loop to write the word in a free basis of the projection. Thus the computation from this point depends only on the tuple itself. Free reduction is available in deterministic logspace. [Elder, Elston and Ostheimer, Proposition 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.4363)

Replace the factors by these projections. If $H$ intersects a coordinate factor trivially, forgetting that coordinate embeds $H$ isomorphically into the remaining product. Repeat on the projected group until all remaining intersections are nontrivial. This is a finite series of fixed preprocessing choices. Each surviving projection is still onto, and the projected $H$ is still finitely presented. A single surviving factor is the whole projected group and requires only fixed substitutions. Zero factors give the trivial group.

With at least two factors, Theorem 4.7 of Bridson and Miller supplies the required $K_i$, with $\gamma_{d-1}(K_i)\le L_i\le K_i$. To avoid any convention excluding cyclic free factors from that theorem, cyclic factors may first be split off as follows. Their nontrivial pure intersections $L_i$ have finite index. Restrict each cyclic coordinate to $L_i$ using the finite quotient and a fixed coset representative in $H$, as above. The restricted subgroup splits as its projection to the noncyclic coordinates times the pure cyclic factors $L_i$. The projected subgroup is finitely presented as a retract of a finitely presented group, has full intersections, and is subdirect onto its new projection factors. Each new projection has finite index in the previous noncyclic projection, hence is a nonabelian free group. This gives the asserted construction with any number of cyclic factors. [Bridson and Miller, Theorems 4.7 and 5.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.4331)

Reconstruct a word on the original generators by fixed preimages of the projected generators. Check its value in every original ambient factor, including every forgotten coordinate, before returning it. If the projected tuple is outside the projected group, no word exists. Otherwise injectivity on $H$ makes the reconstruction unique as a group element, and this last check decides whether the original tuple equals it. Check an intermediate output before emitting it by suppressing the output tape on one pass and replaying on success. The initial free reductions and all fixed choices make the output string canonical for the ambient tuple. Substituting ambient expansions of subgroup generators on input gives the intrinsic normal form.

## General residually free groups

A limit group is a finitely generated fully residually free group. Every finitely generated residually free group embeds in a finite direct product of limit groups. Fix such an embedding of $G$. The coordinates of an input word in $G$ are obtained by substituting the fixed images of its generators. For every projected subgroup $P_i$ of $H$, finite generation implies it is itself a limit group, including the free abelian case. We give the two additional algorithms needed to reuse the proof. [Bridson and Wilton, Theorems 3 and 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.4247)

1. **Canonical words in limit groups.** Every fixed limit group has a finite-index subgroup which is a virtual retract of a finitely generated right-angled Artin group, by Wise's virtual compact specialness theorem and Haglund and Wise's canonical completion and retraction. Right-angled Artin groups have deterministic logspace normal forms. The property passes to fixed finite-index subgroups and finite extensions by Schreier rewriting. It also passes to retracts: normalize the word in the ambient group, then replace each letter by its image under the retraction. The resulting word in the retract has the prescribed value and depends only on that value. Applying these three transfers gives intrinsic logspace normal forms in every fixed limit group. [Wise, *The Structure of Groups with a Quasiconvex Hierarchy*](https://doi.org/10.1515/9780691213507) · [Antolín, Minasyan and Sisto, discussion of virtual retracts before Theorem 1.1](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/29799) · [Diekert, Kausch and Lohrey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.3174) · [Elder, Elston and Ostheimer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.4363)
2. **Entering fixed subgroup projections.** Wilton's Hall theorem supplies a finite-index subgroup $U_i$ in the ambient limit factor, containing $P_i$, and a retraction $\rho_i:U_i\to P_i$. Normalize the ambient coordinate first. Reject outside $U_i$ using its finite coset action. Schreier rewriting and substitution of fixed retraction words give $\rho_i(x)$ on the generators of $P_i$. Compare it to $x$ in the limit factor using its word problem. Equality is equivalent to $x\in P_i$. On success normalize in $P_i$ itself. This replaces the folded free-group graph. [Wilton, *Hall's Theorem for limit groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0605546)

Delete coordinates with trivial pure intersection as before. For the resulting finitely presented full subdirect product of limit groups, each $P_i/L_i$ is virtually nilpotent, including when a factor is abelian. This is exactly Bridson and Wilton's Theorem 8, which records the general form of the theorem of Bridson, Howie, Miller and Short. Take $K_i$ to be the inverse image of a finite-index normal nilpotent subgroup of $P_i/L_i$; a finite-index nilpotent subgroup can be replaced by its core. Continue with the finite-index projections $B_i$ and the nilpotent quotient algorithm above. Replace each free Schreier basis by a fixed finite Schreier generating alphabet. No freeness was used when decoding the residual: the certificate multiplies correctly in the free group on that alphabet, so evaluating it in $B_i$ gives exactly the required residual even when $B_i$ has relations. To restore erased factors, compare coordinates using their limit-group normal forms. The embedding of $G$ into the product is injective, so the final test detects equality in $G$. All component choices are fixed independently of the input word. [Bridson and Wilton, Theorem 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.4247) · [Bridson, Howie, Miller and Short](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2009/170-3/p11)

Taking $G=H$ proves the intrinsic normal-form conclusion. Bridson and Wilton's Corollary 11 already gives a uniform decision algorithm for finitely presented ambient presentations under a subgroup finite-presentability promise. The resource bound here is for each fixed embedded pair, and produces an explicit canonical subgroup word. It gives no uniform complexity bound on constructing the fixed data. [Bridson and Wilton, Corollary 11](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.4247)

## What changes relative to the published algorithms

Bridson and Miller already prove decidability of membership and supply the structural quotient theorem. Their proof of Theorem 5.6 uses enumeration when restoring erased factors and in the virtually nilpotent quotient. Here the subgroup is fixed and reconstruction has a deterministic logarithmic-workspace bound, including emission of the actual subgroup word. A normal form for the ambient free product factors alone gives no such word. The closest sources checked establish the structural and arithmetic ingredients separately. Publication priority for the combined bound remains unverified.
