# Uniform surjection counting onto finite linear inverse monoids

Let \(I(V)\) be the monoid of all partial linear isomorphisms between subspaces of a finite vector space \(V=\mathbb F_q^n\), with partial composition. The zero is the map on the zero subspace. Given explicit finite operation tables for \(S,B\), one can recognize when a distinguished multiplication on \(B\) is a linear inverse monoid and then count all surjective homomorphisms \(S\to B\) in polynomial table time, uniformly in \(q,n\). Arbitrary additional basic operations may be present and the source multiplication need not be associative. This combines the earlier matrix entries construction with cancellation in inverse semigroup corners. In particular it covers targets with arbitrarily large nonabelian maximal subgroups, while the reconstruction uses only one cyclic scalar group.

The multiplication monoid is generated by a constant number of elements. For \(n\ge2\) choose the cyclic coordinate permutation, a diagonal matrix scaling one coordinate by a primitive field element, a transvection, and the partial identity on one hyperplane. The three units generate the full general linear group by conjugation of transvections around the coordinate cycle, scaling their coefficients, commutators along directed simple paths, and elementary elimination. Conjugating the partial identity by units gives every hyperplane identity. Their products give all subspace identities by intersection. Every partial linear isomorphism is obtained by composing the relevant domain identity with a unit extending the map, by independent basis completions. For \(n=1\), a primitive scalar and zero suffice, and the singleton case is direct.

One can recognize the standard multiplication model from the raw table. For each potential \(n,q\), require \(q^n\le |B|\) and \(|\operatorname{GL}_n(q)|\le |B|\), and enumerate only prime powers \(q\le |B|\) and \(n\le\log_2|B|\) outside the singleton case. For \(n=1\), \(q^n=|B|\) on promised inputs, and for \(n>1\) the units already give \(q^n\le |B|\). The inequality \(q^{n^2}\le4|\operatorname{GL}_n(q)|\) follows from the product formula, using \(\prod_{i\ge1}(1-2^{-i})>1/4\), for example by retaining the first three factors and using the sum bound on the tail. Thus all matrices can be inspected within polynomial table time. List subspaces by the successive initial column spans of all invertible matrices, deduplicated by reduced row echelon form. Enumerate all restrictions of invertible maps to these subspaces. Deduplicate by the domain basis and its images, and abandon a candidate once the number of distinct maps exceeds \(|B|\). This lists the model in polynomial time, since the number of visited choices is at most \((n+1)|B|^2\) before deduplication. Test each model of the right size against the raw table by enumerating images of its bounded generating tuple, propagating multiplication and validating bijectivity and the equations. Keep product circuits for all target values by the same propagation.

Fix a line \(L=\mathbb F_q e\). For each nonzero \(v,w\in V\), use the partial map \(a_v\) from \(L\) to \(\mathbb F_qv\) sending \(e\) to \(v\), and the partial map \(b_w\) from \(\mathbb F_qw\) to \(L\) sending \(w\) to \(e\). The probe \(b_w x a_v\) takes values in the single scalar corner \(\{0\}\cup\mathbb F_q^\times\). It records \(\lambda\) if \(v\in\operatorname{dom}(x)\) and \(x(v)=\lambda w\), and zero otherwise. All probes together determine both the domain and every value of \(x\).

Enumerate the preimages of the bounded generator tuple of the target. Interpret the recorded product circuits on \(S\) using the chosen pins, thereby supplying source lifts of the probes. Close the source probe outputs under multiplication to obtain \(D\subseteq S\). Any compatible homomorphism sends \(D\) into the scalar corner. List its possible restrictions to \(D\) by the cyclic-corner procedure from full matrix monoids. Explicitly, the set of nonzero outputs is empty or a one-seed meet fiber, because the fiber contains a product of all its points and is recovered from that seed by the backward product implications. Enumerate and deduplicate the fibers. Within a nonempty fiber, force associativity by congruence closure, then impose both cancellations iteratively, merging whenever a left or right translation identifies two distinct classes. Every merge is forced for maps to a group. The resulting finite associative cancellative structure is a group, since translations are bijective and a finite semigroup contains an idempotent, which cancellation makes an identity. All maps from it to the cyclic units factor through its abelianization. Smith form lists these maps, with cardinality at most \(|D|\) per fiber. Extend by zero outside the fiber and check multiplication.

Each resulting candidate corner map determines the proposed global images by the probes. Verify consistency on \(D\), the pinned values, every original operation, and surjectivity. Retain only the least preimages of the fixed generators in a prescribed source order. Each actual surjection then occurs exactly once. The algorithm uses polynomially many pins, probes, and corner maps, with polynomial work per candidate. A positive count supplies a witness. This proof is informal.
