# Rational best rank-one approximation at a known optimum

**Theorem.** Assume that the integers admit an existential Diophantine definition in the rationals. There is a fixed format of fully specified rational order-three tensors of real spectral norm exactly one in which the following two problems are halting-complete, in the sense of computable many-one reductions.

1. Does a rational rank-one tensor attain the optimal real Frobenius error?
2. Given an explicit rational rank-one minimizer, is there a different rational rank-one minimizer?

For the second family the known minimizer is the same coordinate tensor for every input. There is either exactly one or there are infinitely many rational minimizers. Both families consist of orthogonal projections under a specified square flattening. Within each family, the difference of ANY two of these flattenings has rank at most two. The optimal squared error is an explicitly computable integer. Dimensions and the tensor order are fixed, while input coefficient heights can grow. Rationality refers to the entries of the approximating tensor. Normalize vectors only when maximizing over the reals.

**Rational point input.** Encode the halting set using one fixed arithmetic circuit of addition and multiplication over the rationals, with inputs a nonnegative integer code n and a tuple of existential rational variables. Integer DPRM followed by the assumed definition gives such a circuit. Introduce gate variables and keep their affine quadratic equations E_i(x)=0 fixed, using one coordinate x₀ for the input. Supply x₀=n separately. The next construction is the project's earlier projective quadrics construction. [Invariant planes and projective quadrics](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/RationalDescentAndCalibration/InvariantPlanes.md)

Introduce homogeneous coordinates w,s,h,z and four slack coordinates v_j. Impose the quadrics

    s² E_i(z/s) = 0,       w z₀ = n w s,       w s = 2h² − w²,
    Σ z_i² + s² + h² + Σ v_j² = 4 w².

The first expression denotes polynomial homogenization, also evaluated at s=0. Any rational projective solution has w nonzero, and after setting w=1 has s nonzero by irrationality of the square root of 1/2. Hence z/s solves the gate system. For any rational gate solution x take rational h arbitrarily close to the positive square root of 1/2. Put s=2h²−1 and z=sx. The slack 4−Σ z_i²−s²−h² is positive and rational, so four rational squares represent it by Lagrange's four-square theorem and clearing denominators. Infinitely many distinct rational projective solutions result. For every n there is also a real solution with w=1, h=1/√2, z=s=0 and v=(√(7/2),0,0,0). This verifies the real-point promise even on negative inputs. Call the resulting projective zero set Z. For the known-minimizer variant adjoin a coordinate p and impose p y=0 for every old coordinate y. The new zero set is the disjoint union of Z in p=0 and the fixed rational coordinate point [e_p].

**From quadrics to closest tensors.** Write d for the resulting number of coordinates. In the space of d by d matrices take the rational linear subspace L consisting of symmetric matrices satisfying the linearization of every quadric. A monomial x_i x_j becomes the matrix entry X_ij. Vectorize matrices with the ordinary Frobenius inner product. Compute the orthogonal projection P onto L over the rationals as B(BᵗB)⁻¹Bᵗ for any rational basis matrix B of L. Form the d by d by d² tensor A for the bilinear map (x,y) ↦ P(x⊗y). Its spectral norm is at most one, since an orthogonal projection is contractive. Every real projective solution supplies a unit x with P(x⊗x)=x⊗x, so equality holds.

If unit x,y attain equality they have x⊗y in L. Symmetry forces y=±x. The third maximizing direction is then determined up to sign by the projected vector. For a unit simple tensor u the optimal scalar on that line is the inner product of A with u, by minimizing the squared distance on the line. Combining these facts, all the distinct minimizing tensors are exactly

    B_x = x ⊗ x ⊗ (x ⊗ x) / (xᵗx)²,       [x] ∈ Z(ℝ),

using the enlarged Z in the marked case. The displayed tensor determines the line [x]. If x_j ≠ 0, the ratio of its (i,j,(j,j)) entry to its (j,j,(j,j)) entry equals x_i/x_j. Thus it has rational entries precisely when the line is rational. Every rational simple tensor has a factorization over the rationals by taking nonzero slices. Density shows that the infimum over rational factors always equals the real optimum, even when no rational approximant attains it. The squared error equals ‖P‖²_F−1 = rank(P)−1. The only varying equation cuts a single hyperplane (possibly redundant) from a fixed subspace. For two inputs their subspaces share a common intersection of codimension at most one in each, so the difference of the projections has rank at most two.

**Computability and scope.** Enumerating rational factors and checking the squared error against the known optimum recognizes each affirmative answer. The reductions above show halting-hardness. In particular there is no computable uniform height bound for rational witnesses in the affirmative instances as a function of the input length. For variable formats the reduction from arbitrary rational polynomial feasibility to these two promised tests is unconditional. One can replace an input polynomial circuit by its gate quadrics. The construction has polynomial bit cost for circuit input, since exact rational nullspace and projection matrices have polynomial bit size by determinant bounds. In the reverse direction, asking for rational factors achieving a supplied promised optimal squared error is existential rational polynomial feasibility. Distinctness from a given minimizer is enforced by requiring the squared Frobenius distance from it to have a multiplicative inverse. Both promise tests on the constructed projector inputs are therefore equivalent, under polynomial transformations, to rational polynomial feasibility with circuit input.

**Sharp contrast with matrices.** For a nonzero rational matrix M the right directions of all real best rank-one approximations are the top eigenspace of MᵗM. A direction [v] gives the minimizing matrix (Mv)vᵗ/(vᵗv), which is rational exactly when [v] is rational, as its nonzero rows determine that line. The top eigenvalue can be located exactly by univariate root isolation. A rational eigenvector forces a rational eigenvalue, and a rational eigenvalue gives an eigenspace defined over the rationals. Its dimension now decides whether there is one rational minimizer or infinitely many. The zero matrix gives zero for the convention of rank at most one. This decides both matrix analogues.

**Literature.** Tensor spectral norm and best rank-one approximation already have strong hardness results. Here the value, and in the second test a minimizer, are given exactly. The reduction concerns rational attainment and multiplicity. The proofs are informal. No claim of publication priority is made.
