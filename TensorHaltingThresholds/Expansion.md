# Exact rational subspace expansion has a two-slice to three-slice threshold

This is an informal proof. Tensor dimensions refer to explicit arrays. The arithmetic premise in the fixed-format halting result is that the integers are Diophantine in the rationals. The variable-format equivalence with Hilbert's tenth problem over the rationals holds unconditionally.

For a tuple of square matrices over a field $K$, write
$$e_k(A_1,\ldots,A_r;K)=\min_{\dim_K X=k}\dim_K\Big(\sum_i A_iX\Big)-k.$$
This quantity is invariant under invertible changes of basis in each of the three tensor modes. A change in the slice basis preserves the slice span, a left multiplier preserves dimensions, and a right multiplier permutes the candidate subspaces. When the span contains an invertible slice, the quantity is nonnegative. Its vanishing is equivalent to the tensor having an identically zero restriction to $Y\times X$ across all slices for some column $k$-plane $X$ and dual row $(N-k)$-plane $Y$.

**Theorem.** For two rational square slices whose span contains an invertible slice, the exact value of $e_k$ belongs to $\{0,1\}$ and is computable in deterministic polynomial bit time. For three rational slices including the identity, deciding $e_k=0$ is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to rational polynomial feasibility, even restricted to outputs satisfying $e_k\in\{0,1\}$. Here dimensions are variable. Under the arithmetic premise, fixed integers $N,k$ and three integral slices $(I,D,B_0+nE)$ suffice for a halting-complete zero test. The matrix $D$ is fixed positive diagonal, $E$ has one nonzero entry, and all $B_0+nE$ are rationally conjugate to the same finite-order signed permutation matrix. On this fixed family, $e_k$ is always zero at every completion of the rationals, while its rational value is zero or one according to whether machine $n$ halts.

### Two slices

For a regular pencil $(P,Q)$, find an invertible $P+tQ$ by trying $t=0,\ldots,N$. Its determinant is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $N$. Left multiplication by its inverse and a change of slice basis give $(I,A)$. Here $e_k=0$ exactly if $A$ has an invariant rational $k$-plane. Factor the characteristic polynomial as $\prod_i p_i^{m_i}$ over the rationals. The attainable dimensions are precisely
$$\sum_i h_i\deg p_i,\qquad 0\le h_i\le m_i.$$
Indeed the primary projectors preserve every invariant subspace, and each simple composition factor in the $p_i$-primary module has dimension $\deg p_i$. A composition series there contains a submodule of every length from zero to $m_i$. Rational polynomial factorization and a bounded subset-sum table up to $N$ decide the condition in polynomial bit time. To see that all other cases have value one, decompose the rational module into cyclic companion blocks. Fill whole blocks until the desired dimension is reached or a final partial block is needed. In the latter block the span of the first $t$ Krylov basis vectors gains at most one dimension after adding its $A$-image.

### From rational feasibility to projective quadrics

Reduce an arbitrary polynomial equation over the rationals to an affine system of degree at most two by introducing variables for arithmetic gates. Binary-encoded powers use repeated squaring. A finite system permits the same construction. In the fixed-format halting specialization, use the Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich theorem for a fixed enumeration of machines and replace each integer-variable predicate with the fixed existential rational definition of the integers. This gives a fixed gate system $E_i(x)=0$ with one distinguished input $x_0=n$. The extra integer restriction on the machine code is already met for the supplied nonnegative integer $n$.

Homogenize each gate to $s^2E_i(z/s)=0$ and add homogeneous quadrics
$$ws=2h^2-w^2,\qquad \sum_j z_j^2+s^2+h^2+\sum_{i=1}^{4}v_i^2=4w^2.$$
In the fixed-format specialization also impose $wz_0-nws=0$. At every rational projective solution, the sum of squares forces $w\ne0$. Normalize $w=1$. Then $s\ne0$ since $2h^2=1$ is impossible over the rationals. This yields the affine solution $x=z/s$. Conversely take a rational affine solution $x$, choose rational $h$ sufficiently close to $1/\sqrt2$, put $s=2h^2-1$ and $z=sx$. The remaining four-square target is positive rational. For a positive fraction $a/b$ with positive integer numerator and denominator, represent $ab$ as four integer squares and divide the four integers by $b$.

To make every local instance positive, add three pairs of coordinates $(a_d,b_d)$, one for each $d=13,17,221$, with equations $a_d^2=d b_d^2$. Impose vanishing of all coordinate products between different blocks, treating all original coordinates as one block and each new pair as one block. Over a field exactly one block can be nonzero at a projective point. The new blocks add no rational points. Over the reals all three have points. In $\mathbb Q_2$, the unit $17$ is $1$ modulo $8$ and is a square. At $13$, the unit $17$ reduces to $4$; at $17$, the unit $13$ reduces to $8^2$. Both lift by Hensel's lemma. At every other odd prime the three units have Legendre symbols $u,v,uv$, at least one of which is $+1$. The corresponding square also lifts. Thus every completion admits a point regardless of the machine or affine input.

### Encode the quadrics with three slices

If the projective system has $d$ coordinates, take $U$ to be the symmetric $d$-square matrices and $V=K^d\oplus K$. Use one arrow $U\to V$ for each column, landing in the first summand. For each quadric use one more arrow, landing in the last summand, that replaces each monomial $x_i x_j$ in the collected polynomial by $X_{ij}$. Compatible pairs of lines $L\subset U,M\subset V$ are equivalent to projective solutions. A nonzero $X$ in $L$ has a nonzero column, so $M$ lies in the first summand. Every column lies on this line, and symmetry implies $X=cxx^t$ for a vector spanning the column line and a nonzero scalar $c$. The remaining arrows vanish exactly when the quadrics do. Conversely each projective solution gives the pair $Kxx^t,K(x,0)$. This works over the rationals and all their completions.

Choose $m>\dim U$ at least as large as the arrow count; append zero arrows to length $m$. On $U\oplus V^{\oplus m}$ take $D$ scalar with a different positive integer on each displayed summand. Let $C$ be the cyclic shift of the $V$ copies with one minus sign on wraparound and put
$$B=\begin{pmatrix} I&0\\ (A_i)_{i=1}^m&C\end{pmatrix}.$$
Here $C^m=-I$, so $I-C$ is invertible and a rational lower block shear conjugates $B$ to $\operatorname{diag}(I,C)$. In the fixed family only the single $X_{ws}$ coefficient of one arrow varies with $n$.

A $D$-invariant subspace splits over its displayed eigenspaces. If it is also $B$-invariant, the cyclic shift forces all its $V$-subspaces to coincide, and the remaining invariance requirement is exactly the $m$ arrow inclusions from its $U$-subspace into that common subspace. Choose $k=m+1$. The dimension equation $a+mb=1+m$ with $0\le a\le\dim U<m$ forces $a=b=1$. Thus $(I,D,B)$ has zero excess exactly when the quadrics have a projective point.

Append a block of dimension $k+1$. Extend $D$ there by $k+1$ new, pairwise distinct positive eigenvalues, and extend $B$ by a permutation cycling the new coordinate vectors. The spectral projectors split the new block from the previous space. Within the new block every simultaneous invariant subspace is a coordinate subspace stable under a transitive cycle, hence is zero or the whole block. A common invariant $k$-plane must therefore lie entirely in the old part. The span of any $k$ consecutive new coordinates has its image span under the three slices of dimension $k+1$. This gives the required binary excess and preserves the fixed conjugacy class assertion.

### Complexity and attribution

All dimensions, bit lengths and explicit output sizes grow polynomially in the variable-format arithmetic input. In reverse, use unknown $N$ by $k$ rational frames $X,Y$, left inverses enforcing full rank, and $k$ by $k$ matrices $C_i$. The equations $A_iX=YC_i$ encode excess zero for an identity-containing tuple. A sum of squares combines the system into a single rational polynomial equation. On the fixed one-entry family the machine code is mapped directly to the integer input $n$, and enumeration of rational frames semidecides a positive instance. The halting equivalence concerns this prescribed-dimension invariant of a tensor. It gives no reduction to ordinary tensor isomorphism.

The construction adapts the repository's invariant-plane encoding by omitting its marked rational point, adding local witnesses, and forcing a one-dimensional upper bound on excess. The two-slice calculation uses the classical rational module structure theorem and polynomial-time rational factorization. Dimension expansion and projective universality for quiver Grassmannians have extensive prior literature. This note does not establish priority for the threshold.
