# Compact isomorphism for tensor supports with a small relation space

Consider real or complex tensors with rational coordinates (rational real and imaginary parts in the complex case). Suppose the nonzero positions are nonempty and no two differ in exactly one mode. Form the incidence matrix \(A\) whose column for a nonzero position has one 1 for its index in each mode. Assume \(Ap=u\) has a strictly positive solution, where the entries of \(u\) in mode \(j\) are all \(1/n_j\) for that mode dimension. Fix constants \(k,C\). Suppose the real kernel of \(A\) has dimension at most \(k\) and admits an integer basis over \(\mathbb R\) with coordinate absolute values at most \(m^C\), where \(m\) is the number of nonzero positions.

There is a deterministic polynomial-time reduction from ordinary tensor isomorphism for pairs in this class to positive-definite orthogonal isomorphism in the real case and unitary isomorphism in the complex case, with exactly represented algebraic coordinates in the output. The two sides need not have the same support. This combines the support tests and Gram balancing from the earlier compact-isomorphism work with low-dimensional algebraic solving. It gives a partial case of the compact direction of Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang Question 1.8.

## The unique balanced positive magnitudes

For each occupied position \(i\), let \(c_i=|a_i|^2>0\) be the squared absolute value of its input coefficient. The feasible nonnegative vectors \(Ap=u\) form a compact polytope because their coordinates sum to 1. Minimize
\[
\sum_i p_i(\log p_i-\log c_i)
\]
on it, with the summand extended continuously at zero. At a point with a zero coordinate, moving towards the promised strictly positive vector has right derivative negative infinity. Thus the minimizer is interior. It is unique by strict convexity. The directional conditions on the kernel of \(A\) are
\[
\sum_i \eta_i\log(p_i/c_i)=0 \qquad(\eta\in\ker A).
\]
The additional constant from differentiating cancels because \(\sum_i\eta_i=0\). Equivalently, the vector \(\log(p_i/c_i)\) belongs to the range of \(A^{\mathsf T}\). Therefore positive diagonal changes in each mode achieve the new squared magnitudes \(p_i\) while retaining every phase. This proves the new tensor lies in the original ordinary orbit.

Parametrize the affine solution space by at most \(k\) variables through rational elimination. The reduced-echelon kernel basis can be cleared to short integer vectors under the hypothesis. Indeed select the free-coordinate rows of any promised short basis matrix; their square submatrix must be invertible. Multiplying its inverse by its determinant gives integer transforms producing the echelon basis up to one scalar. The determinant and adjugate bounds are polynomial for fixed \(k,C\). Thus the directly computed integral kernel vectors also have polynomially bounded entries. For each such vector, exponentiate the logarithmic equation and clear negative powers. This gives the polynomial equation
\[
\prod_{\eta_i>0}p_i^{\eta_i}\prod_{\eta_i<0}c_i^{-\eta_i}
=
\prod_{\eta_i<0}p_i^{-\eta_i}\prod_{\eta_i>0}c_i^{\eta_i}.
\]
After affine substitution the equations and the positivity inequalities have polynomial degree and coefficient bit length, with a fixed number of variables. Fixed-dimensional real algebraic solving produces the unique feasible vector in exact real algebraic representation in polynomial time. The coefficients of the new tensor are \(a_i\sqrt{p_i/c_i}\). These have polynomial-size algebraic coordinate descriptions, since extracting a positive square root of one such coordinate has only a constant-degree overhead. A bound on the degree of the field generated by all output entries together is unnecessary in this model.

## Why the compact group suffices after balancing

For the new tensor every one-mode Gram matrix is diagonal, since a nonzero off-diagonal term would require two occupied positions differing only in that mode. The diagonal entries are the uniform marginals. The tensor norm is 1.

Suppose two such balanced unit tensors are ordinarily isomorphic. Separate each mode map into its orthogonal or unitary part and a positive part, and separate the latter into a positive scalar and the exponential of a traceless self-adjoint operator \(H_j\). Absorb the compact parts into one balanced tensor. Writing \(X=\sum_j H_j^{(j)}\) for the total infinitesimal action, the remaining map is a scalar times \(e^X\). The derivative at 0 of \(\|e^{sX}T\|^2\) vanishes by the uniform Gram matrices and the zero traces. The derivative at 1 also vanishes because the image is a scalar multiple of the other balanced tensor. In an eigenbasis for \(X\) this function is a sum \(\sum_\lambda \|T_\lambda\|^2 e^{2s\lambda}\), with nonnegative second derivative. If any nonzero \(\lambda\) has a nonzero component, the derivative is strictly increasing, contrary to the endpoint equalities. Thus \(e^XT=T\). Unit norms force the remaining positive scalar to equal 1. The compact parts therefore give an isomorphism, and the reverse implication is immediate.

The statements use exact algebraic outputs or an exact real model. They concern a support and feasibility class within the unrestricted compact-group problem. This proof is informal. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Question 1.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

## Balanced block generalization

The same bound holds if the raw scalar coordinates are replaced by a supplied orthogonal block decomposition in each mode, nonzero blocks differ in at least two indices, and each nonzero block tensor has scalar Grams on its own mode spaces. Use one incidence column per occupied block, \(c_i\) its squared norm, and \(u_{j,\alpha}=\dim V_{j,\alpha}/\dim V_j\) for the marginal on block \(\alpha\) of mode \(j\). The entropy equations, effective algebraic solving and compact-group conclusion are unchanged. Block-scalar operators achieve the new squared norms. Their off-block Gram terms vanish by support, and the diagonal restrictions are scalar with the required value by local balance and the new marginal. The bit bound holds for coordinate block partitions and rational input coefficients, or explicit rational orthonormal frames of the supplied blocks. The output rescales each whole block by the same real algebraic square root.
