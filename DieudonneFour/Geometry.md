# Tensor isomorphism in subgroup schemes of additive groups of height two

Let $k$ be a perfect field of characteristic $p>0$. Every projective $k$-scheme $T$, including a nonreduced one, occurs as the inverse Frobenius twist of the scheme-theoretic image of the relative Frobenius of a projective scheme $Y_T$. Here $Y_T$ is a parameter scheme for finite locally free closed subgroups of an explicit product of $\alpha_p$ and $\alpha_{p^2}$. The presentation of the group marks a direct-sum decomposition of its primitive generators. The subgroups parameterized by $Y_T$ have defining ideals generated by one line from each marked summand. The subgroup description works over arbitrary $k$-algebras.

Over every fixed finite field there is a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction from ordinary three-tensor isomorphism to abstract isomorphism of the resulting parameter schemes. Isomorphisms may forget the subgroup presentation and projective embedding. The construction also identifies a class of embedded flat degenerations exactly, through a closed immersion of Hilbert schemes.

## Ordinary incidence

Choose a quadratic presentation $T\subseteq\mathbb P(E)$, where projectivization parameterizes lines, $E=k^b$, $b\ge 2$, and $T$ is cut out scheme-theoretically by a space $Q$ of homogeneous quadrics. Such a presentation exists for every projective scheme. Indeed, for a homogeneous ideal $I$ in a polynomial ring $S$, choose $d$ at least the maximum degree of a set of homogeneous generators. For $q\ge1$, monomial factorization in the multiples of those generators gives $I_{qd}=S_{(q-1)d} I_d$. The $d$-uple Veronese presentation therefore uses linear equations from $I_d$ inside the Veronese scheme. Its own equations are the quadratic binomials exchanging one variable between two monomial factors of degree $d$. These exchanges connect all factorizations with the same total monomial. One can put the first factor into any desired form by taking missing variables from the other factors, then repeat for each subsequent factor. This proves that the quadratic binomials generate the kernel of the monomial presentation. Multiplying each linear equation by all the projective coordinates gives quadratic equations with the same ideal sheaf, since one coordinate is invertible on each affine chart. Padding via a linear embedding gives $b\ge 2$ if required. [Reineke, projective universality of quiver Grassmannians](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.5730) · [Hille, quadratic incidence presentations for quiver Grassmannians](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.06008)

Let $M\subseteq\operatorname{SymMat}_b(k)$ be the linear subspace given by replacing each monomial $x_i x_j$, $i\le j$, of the equations in $Q$ by the matrix coordinate $A_{ij}$. There is no division by $2$. Let $a_j:M\to E$ be the $j$th column map and set $P=\mathbb P(M)\times\mathbb P(E)$. Inside $P$ impose
$$
                  a_j(U)\subseteq L \qquad(1\le j\le b),
                  \tag{1}
$$
where $U,L$ are the tautological lines. The resulting closed subscheme $X$ is canonically isomorphic to $T$ by projection to $\mathbb P(E)$.

To see the scheme assertion, work on a test ring locally where a generator $w$ of $L$ has unit coordinate $w_i$. If $A$ generates $U$, the column constraints give $A=w v^{\mathsf T}$ for a vector $v$. Symmetry gives $w_i v_j=w_j v_i$ and hence $A=c w w^{\mathsf T}$. The element $c$ is a unit locally, because $A$ is unimodular in $M$ and $M$ is a direct summand of the full symmetric matrix space. Thus the unique line is generated by $w w^{\mathsf T}$. It belongs to $M$ exactly when the quadratic equations of $Q$ hold on $w$. Conversely that matrix has unit $ii$ entry on the chosen chart, so it generates a direct-summand line of $M$. The local rules agree under rescaling of $w$. This proves the assertion on all test rings. If $T$ is empty one may use $b=2$ and $M=kI_2$ to realize empty incidence and hence an empty $Y_T$.

Under a linear map $\phi:E\to E'$ carrying the chosen quadratic presentation to another, the corresponding map on symmetric matrices is $A\mapsto\phi A\phi^{\mathsf T}$. It carries $M$ to $M'$ since its effect on $w w^{\mathsf T}$ gives the ordinary change of variables on quadrics. These rank-one symmetric matrices span the full symmetric matrix space in every characteristic, using the coordinate vectors and their pairwise sums. The product isomorphism takes the graph $X$ to $X'$.

## Finite group and its subgroup functor

For a finite-dimensional space $D$ with a Frobenius-semilinear endomorphism $\Phi$, write
$$
  G(D,\Phi)=\operatorname{Spec}
  \frac{\operatorname{Sym}_k(D)}
       {(x^p-\Phi(x):x\in D)} .
  \tag{2}
$$
Use primitive comultiplication on $D$, so that each relation defines a Hopf ideal generator. The notation uses the primitive-coordinate convention for additive Cartier–Dieudonné modules. The dimension of $D$ is the number of primitive generators. Locally over any test algebra $R$, with a basis $y_i$ of $D_R=R\otimes_k D$, the monic relations are $y_i^p-\Phi(y_i)$, where $\Phi$ acts on $R$-coefficients by $p$th power. Reduction of total degree and reduction of the pairwise ambiguities give a free $R$-basis of monomials whose exponents are all below $p$. Explicitly, at the only possible overlap of two rules, the two first replacements in the product $y_i^p y_j^p$ both reduce to $\Phi(y_i)\Phi(y_j)$. These generators define a finite locally free closed subgroup of the additive vector group. [Poguntke, §3 and Theorem 3.13, additive group schemes and their Cartier–Dieudonné modules](https://smf.emath.fr/system/files/2017-08/smf_bull_145_345-380.pdf)

Take copies $E_0,\ldots,E_b$ of $E$, called the sink summands, and use the ordered decomposition
$$
 D=M\oplus E\oplus E_0\oplus\cdots\oplus E_b .
$$
Fix bases. On $M$, the $E_j$-component of $\Phi$ for $j\ge1$ is the semilinear map with matrix $a_j^{[p]}$, obtained by powering every coefficient of $a_j$. The $E_0$-component there is zero. On $E$, the map is coordinatewise $p$th power to each $E_j$, and on the sink summands it is zero. Thus $\Phi^2=0$. The coefficient matrix on $M\oplus E$ has rank $r=\dim M+b$. Its component in $E_0$ first detects $E$, and the columns then detect $M$. In a sink basis beginning with the images under $\Phi$ of a basis of $M\oplus E$, the relations for those $r$ generators have the form $x_i^p=z_i$, $z_i^p=0$. All remaining generators satisfy $u_j^p=0$. Consequently the unmarked group is
$$
 G(D,\Phi)\ \cong\ \alpha_{p^2}^{\,r}
                \times \alpha_p^{\,b(b+1)-r},
 \qquad
 \alpha_{p^s}=\ker(F^s_{\mathbb G_a/k}).
 \tag{3}
$$
In particular it is killed by $p$, has zero Verschiebung, and has Frobenius height two. For zero Verschiebung, the additive vector group has zero Verschiebung by faithful flatness of its Frobenius and the identity $V F=[p]=0$ there. Naturality and the closed immersion from (2) give the assertion for the subgroup.

For a $k$-algebra $R$, choose one direct-summand line in each marked summand of $D_R$ and let $W$ be their direct sum. Consider closed subgroups of $G(D,\Phi)_R$ whose ideal is generated by $W$ and whose map from primitive generators has kernel $W$. Such a subgroup exists precisely when $\Phi(W)\subseteq W$. If the condition holds, the coordinate quotient is (2) on $D_R/W$, and the monomial basis proves both finite local freeness and the claimed kernel. Conversely, in the coordinate quotient each $x\in W$ has $x^p=0$. Thus $\Phi(x)$ belongs to the primitive kernel, giving the condition. The subgroup is determined by $W$ and has rank $p^{\dim D-b-3}$. This description specifies the parameter scheme using only finite locally free subgroup quotients over the chosen test rings.

Let $L_j$ be the line in $E_j\otimes R$ and $U,L$ the lines in the two source summands. The $E$-arrow forces $L_j=L^{[p]}$ in the chosen coordinates. Indeed a local unimodular generator of $L$ remains unimodular after coordinatewise powering, and its containment in $L_j$ makes it generate that line. Eliminating all $L_j$, the $M$-arrows require $a_j^{[p]}(u^{[p]})\in L^{[p]}$. In output coordinates this is given by the $p$th powers of the minors defining (1). This is where powering the coefficients of the arrow matrices matters over $k\supsetneq\mathbb F_p$.

Write $Z^{(p)}$ for base change of a $k$-scheme $Z$ along Frobenius of $k$ and $F_{Z/k}:Z\to Z^{(p)}$ for relative Frobenius. The marked subgroup functor is therefore represented, after eliminating the sink coordinates, by
$$
                    Y_T= P\times_{P^{(p)}} X^{(p)}.
                    \tag{4}
$$
The map to $X^{(p)}$ is finite faithfully flat of rank $p^{\dim P}$ when $P$ is nonempty, and radicial. Indeed on the standard affine charts of $P$ the ambient Frobenius is given by coordinate $p$th powers and has a free monomial basis with exponents below $p$. These statements persist under the base change in (4). [Stacks Project, relative Frobenius](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CC6)

## Abstract recovery and tensor isomorphism

The ideal of $Y_T$ in $P$ is locally generated by the $p$th powers of the ideal of $X$. Hence $X\hookrightarrow Y_T$. By naturality, the relative Frobenius of $Y_T$ factors as
$$
              Y_T\longrightarrow X^{(p)}
                         \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow Y_T^{(p)},
                    \tag{5}
$$
where the first map is the faithfully flat map in (4). Since a faithfully flat morphism injects the target structure sheaf into the pushforward of the source structure sheaf, the scheme-theoretic image in (5) is precisely $X^{(p)}$. Inverting the twist over the perfect field $k$ recovers $T$ from the abstract scheme $Y_T$, functorially with respect to isomorphisms. An ambient linear equivalence carrying the chosen quadratic presentation to another extends in the forward direction by functoriality of the product and relative Frobenius.

For a nonreduced example, take $T=\{x_2^2=0\}$ in $\mathbb P^1$. Then $M$ has coordinates $(A_{11},A_{12})$ with $A_{22}=0$. On the chart $x_1=A_{11}=1$, with $u=x_2$ and $v=A_{12}$, the equations for $X$ are $v-u=0$ and $u^2=0$. The support of $Y_T$ lies in this chart, and its algebra is
$$
                  k[u,v]/((v-u)^p,u^{2p}).
$$
The image of relative Frobenius has coordinate algebra generated by $1,u^p$ with $(u^p)^2=0$. Thus the intrinsic image retains the whole double point.

For the computational corollary, use the smooth projective variety $T_t$ already constructed from a three-tensor $t$ in a common product of projective spaces. Its abstract $k$-isomorphism type is exactly the ordinary tensor orbit. A tensor transporter supplies a product linear map carrying its bilinear section equations to those of $T_{t'}$. After the Segre embedding take $Q_t$ to be the span of its rank-one minors and products of the section's linear equations with each projective coordinate. These equations preserve the scheme and their span is carried to $Q_{t'}$ by the induced Segre linear map. Hence a tensor transporter gives $Y_{T_t}\cong_k Y_{T_{t'}}$. The reverse implication follows from (5) and abstract recovery for $T_t$. At any fixed parameter in the prior construction, the dimensions of $E,M,D$ are polynomial in the dense tensor input size. Linear algebra computes the equations, $M$, column maps, and marked presentation in polynomial time over a fixed finite field. The output encoding is the marked matrix of $\Phi$ or the equivalent equations, with the expanded coordinate algebra in (2) used only to specify its meaning. The output is an abstract scheme-isomorphism instance for those parameter schemes. The unmarked group itself has the split type (3). [Projective tensor-isomorphism construction, family and recovery](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SliceSimilarityFour/Geometry.md) · [Grochow and Qiao, tensor-isomorphism completeness and reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16317)

## Embedded flat degenerations

Fix a product $P=\mathbb P(M)\times\mathbb P(E)$ and the polarization $\mathcal O(1,1)$. Pullback along $F_{P/k}$ gives a closed immersion from every fixed-Hilbert-polynomial scheme $\operatorname{Hilb}_{\pi}(P^{(p)})$ into the disjoint union $\operatorname{Hilb}(P)$. Over any test base $B$, a family in $P^{(p)}\times B$ pulls back to a finite locally free scheme over that family. It is flat over $B$ by composition of flat maps, so pullback gives a morphism of the Hilbert functors. Faithfully flat extension and contraction of ideals recover the original family from its inverse image. The morphism is therefore a monomorphism on schemes. The domain is projective over $k$ and the target is separated over $k$. The graph is then closed, and the domain projection after base change shows the morphism is proper. A proper monomorphism of schemes is a closed immersion. [Grothendieck, construction of Hilbert schemes](https://numdam.org/book-part/SB_1960-1961__6__249_0/) · [Stacks Project, proper monomorphisms](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04XV)

Let $G=\operatorname{GL}(M)\times\operatorname{GL}(E)$ act on $P$. Given $g\in G$, the relative Frobenius transforms the action of $g$ on $P$ to the action of $F_{G/k}(g)$ on $P^{(p)}$. On algebraically closed points $F_{G/k}$ is surjective by taking entrywise $p$th roots. The pullback map therefore carries each geometric $G^{(p)}$-orbit of a Hilbert point onto the geometric $G$-orbit of its pullback. The closed immersion identifies the reduced closures of these orbits, since it identifies the orbit point sets and their closures. In particular, for $X,X'$ embedded in the common $P$ with the same Hilbert polynomial, $X'^{(p)}$ lies in the geometric Hilbert-orbit closure of $X^{(p)}$ exactly when the pullback of $X'^{(p)}$ lies in the equivalent closure of the pullback of $X^{(p)}$. This statement concerns flat embedded families and the specified product action.

## Relation to prior work

Quiver Grassmannians supply classical projective incidence universality, and Shimada studies geometric Frobenius incidence for subspaces over finite fields. Poguntke supplies the underlying primitive-coordinate description of zero-Verschiebung groups. Here the arrow construction identifies a marked functor of finite locally free subgroup quotients with the full Frobenius pullback of arbitrary quadratic incidence. Intrinsic recovery via the scheme-theoretic image retains the input nilpotents. The Hilbert closed immersion specifies which embedded degeneration relation is preserved. This is a prose proof. Publication priority and Lean verification are unestablished.
