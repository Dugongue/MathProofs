# A transporter-counting compiler for finite Dieudonné modules

**Theorem.** Let $k=\mathbb F_{p^f}$, $R=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$, and $W=W_e(k)$. Give two finite Dieudonné modules killed by $p^e$ by their $W$-module presentations and the columns of their Frobenius and Verschiebung operators. There is a deterministic polynomial-bit construction of one ordinary three-tensor isomorphism instance over the **same** ring $R$, on three free modules. On matched additive module types its output is the multiplication tensor of a free unital associative algebra of rank $O(n+1)$, where $n=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}M/pM$. The construction is uniform in $p,e,f$ with the coefficient representation specified below.

Every ordinary output transporter yields a Dieudonné module transporter in polynomial time, and every source transporter has a computable lift. The number of output transporters above each source transporter is a known, explicitly computable constant independent of the source transporter. Thus an exact ordinary TI counting query, followed by exact integer division, counts the original transporters. This includes nonfree modules of mixed exponents. Through Cartier–Dieudonné duality it applies to finite commutative group schemes of $p$-power order over $k$ **given in Dieudonné coordinates**.

The construction uses a one-point extension of a fixed incidence algebra to enforce a common transporter on all the required operator graphs. The nilpotent ideal of that algebra gives an exact count of the extra choices.

## 1. Coordinates and the three operator graphs

Use a monogenic unramified presentation of $W$ over $R$, for example $R[X]/(g)$ with $g$ monic and $\bar g$ irreducible of degree $f$, with the named identification of $k$. A lift $\theta$ of a residue field generator generates $W$ over $R$. Its first $f$ powers reduce to a basis and so are a basis over $R$. Witt Frobenius $\sigma$ fixes $R$. Frobenius $F$ acts $\sigma$-semilinearly and Verschiebung $V$ acts $\sigma^{-1}$-semilinearly. Both are $R$-linear. Their defining identities include $FV=VF=p$. The construction uses the actual operators, including when their reductions alone have lost information. [Waterhouse, *Abelian varieties over finite fields*, §1](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_1969_4_2_4_521_0.pdf).

Write $C$ for multiplication by $\theta$. An additive $R$-linear isomorphism is a Dieudonné isomorphism exactly when it intertwines the three ordered $R$-endomorphisms $C,F,V$. Intertwining $C$ implies $W$-linearity because $W=R[\theta]$. The other two conditions retain their respective roles. Ordinary $R$-matrices for the three operators are computable in monogenic coordinates. In a cyclic $W$-basis, for example, the columns for $\theta^j x_i$ are obtained from the columns at $x_i$ using $\theta^j,\sigma(\theta)^j,\sigma^{-1}(\theta)^j$, respectively. Frobenius on the coefficient ring can be computed by lifting the simple root $\bar\theta^p$ of $g$ digit by digit. Its inverse is obtained by inverting the resulting $R$-linear matrix. These are polynomial-bit computations with monic modular polynomial arithmetic. The input model uses this effective finite algebra presentation, rather than charging unit cost for conversion from an arbitrary Witt coordinate encoding.

Use Smith elimination over the chain ring to put the additive $R$-module in the form
$$
M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^n R/(p^{a_i}),\qquad 1\le a_i\le e .
$$
An $R$-presentation can also be obtained from a $W$-presentation by expanding a basis of $W/R$. Smith elimination chooses a least-valuation nonzero entry, uses its unit multiple as the pivot and clears using division by its power of $p$. It yields the change of generators and the new operator columns. Distinct additive types reject before the construction by outputting a fixed ordinary no-instance. Henceforth use matched types. Put $P=R^n$, with kernel $K=\bigoplus_i p^{a_i} R e_i$ for the standard surjection $P\to M$. This is a minimal free cover and $K\subseteq pP$.

For $T=C,F,V$, define the full graph preimage
$$
G_T=\{(x,y)\in P\oplus P\colon\bar y=T\bar x\}.
$$
Here bars indicate the quotient by $K$. If $\widehat T$ is any matrix of representatives of the operator columns, then $G_T$ is generated by the $n$ vectors $(e_i,\widehat T e_i)$ and the nonzero relation columns $(0,p^{a_i}e_i)$. The shear $(x,y)\mapsto (x,y-\widehat T x)$ identifies $G_T$ with $P\oplus K$. Put $k_0=\#\{i:a_i<e\}$. The displayed generators give a minimal free cover $D_T:Q_T\to G_T\subseteq P\oplus P$ of rank $n+k_0$. Its kernel has size
$$
\kappa=p^{\,\sum_{a_i<e}a_i}
\tag{1}
$$
because the first components force the first $n$ coordinates of a kernel vector to vanish and the other coordinates lie in $p^{e-a_i}R$. This size is independent of $T$.

The ordered linear diagram now has two target copies $P_1,P_2$ of $P$, one source $Q_T$ for each of the three operators with its two projection maps $D_{T,1},D_{T,2}$, and a source $Q_0=P$ with identity maps onto both targets. Its transporter is a change of basis in every named object that fits all the arrows. The two identities force both target maps and the map on $Q_0$ to be one common $g$. Thus each graph image is carried by $g\oplus g$. Its intersection with $0\oplus P_2$ is $0\oplus K$, so $g$ descends to a quotient isomorphism and all three graph equations force it to intertwine $C,F,V$.

Conversely any source transporter lifts to a matrix $g$ by choosing each column in the target free cover. There are $|K|^n$ choices. All are invertible because their residue map is the isomorphism on $M/pM$. The map $g\oplus g$ then gives the required graph isomorphisms. Each lifts to the free source by lifting all its prescribed generator images. These choices number $\kappa^{n+k_0}$, and all are invertible by minimality of the graph covers. The identity source map is forced to be $g$. The number of diagram transporters above any given Dieudonné transporter is consequently
$$
C_{\mathrm{cov}}=|K|^n\,\kappa^{3(n+k_0)} .
\tag{2}
$$
Forward choices are found by modular linear systems, and backward recovery is by reading the common quotient map. This step uses graph preimages so no equation asks two arbitrary operator-matrix lifts to conjugate exactly on the free cover.

## 2. One free algebra containing the diagram

Let $B$ be the incidence algebra over $R$ of a fixed seven-element poset. It has a least element $b$, the four diagram sources $s_0,s_C,s_F,s_V$, and the two targets $t_1,t_2$. Each source is below both targets, and there are no other strict comparisons except those from $b$. Write $e_{ij}$ for its rank-one incidence corner when $i\le j$, with products $e_{ij}e_{jk}=e_{ik}$ and zero for mismatched indices. There are fourteen strict incidence corners.

Regard the diagram as a right $B$-module $N$. The component $N_i$ is the corresponding diagram object, and $N_b=0$. Incidence multiplication by $e_{s_j,t_a}$ is the given arrow. The comparison maps from $b$ are zero, so compositions of incidence arrows satisfy the module equations. The one-point extension has the seven vertices of $B$ and a new vertex $o$, with corner $o\to i$ equal to $N_i$. In block notation it is
$$
\mathcal A_0=
\begin{pmatrix}R&N\\0&B\end{pmatrix},
$$
with products on the new row equal to the indicated module action. This multiplication is associative.

At each vertex $i$ attach a free square-zero loop summand of rank $l_i$. The diagonal idempotent acts as a unit on its own loop summand. Products of a loop with any loop or strict off-diagonal element are zero. This is a split square-zero extension acted on via the diagonal scalar maps, so it remains associative. Choose eight distinct $l_i\ge0$ with $l_i+1$ prime to $p$. One can take them from the first sixteen nonnegative integers, uniformly in $p$. Denote the resulting free algebra by $\mathcal A$. Its strict off-diagonal rank is
$$
d_{\rm off}=14+6n+3k_0.
$$
The fourteen incidence lines contribute fourteen, the targets and identity source contribute $3n$, and the three graphs contribute $3(n+k_0)$. Its total dimension is $d_{\rm off}+\sum_i(l_i+1)$. No rank marker depends on the cardinality of $R$.

The Jacobson radical $\mathfrak J$ consists of the strict off-diagonal corners, the loop spaces, and $pR$ in the eight diagonal scalar coordinates. This is a nilpotent ideal. The strict off-diagonal part is triangular, the loops square to zero and annihilate it, and $pR$ is nilpotent. The quotient is $\mathbb F_p^8$, proving the assertion about the radical.

### Aligning the idempotents

An algebra isomorphism permutes the eight primitive residue idempotents. Let $f_i$ be the images of the displayed source idempotents under the isomorphism, and let $e'_{\pi(i)}$ be the target residues they lift. Define
$$
u=\sum_i e'_{\pi(i)} f_i .
$$
It is $1$ modulo $\mathfrak J$ and satisfies $u f_i=e'_{\pi(i)}u$. After conjugating by $u$, the isomorphism carries each idempotent to the indicated target one. The ranks $l_i+1$ of the diagonal corners are distinct, forcing $\pi$ to be the identity. This alignment is effective from the transporter matrices.

In fact the set of possible ordered idempotent tuples has cardinality $|R|^{d_{\rm off}}$. Let $I_{\rm off}$ be the ideal of strict off-diagonal entries. In every diagonal corner the only idempotents are zero and the unit, since that corner is local. The diagonal entries of $f_i$ are thus exactly those of $e_i'$. The displayed aligning unit belongs to $1+I_{\rm off}$. This group acts transitively on the tuples, and its stabilizer of the standard tuple is trivial because an element commuting with every $e_i$ is block diagonal. Consequently each tuple has a unique aligning unit in this group. Conjugating by it identifies transporters inducing that tuple with the aligned transporters.

### Exact transporters after alignment

An aligned isomorphism preserves the loop space in each diagonal corner. Indeed left multiplication by $r e_i+j$ there has trace $(l_i+1)r$, a unit multiple of $r$. The loop space is the intrinsic trace kernel. Each allowed loop action is an arbitrary independent element of $\mathrm{GL}_{l_i}(R)$ and affects no other product.

On an incidence line $i<j$ an aligned isomorphism acts by a unit $\lambda_{ij}$. Multiplication through the least vertex gives
$$
 \lambda_{ij}=\lambda_{b,j}/\lambda_{b,i}
$$
with $\lambda_{b,b}=1$. Conversely arbitrary units $\lambda_{b,i}$ for the six vertices other than $b$ define all incidence scalings and preserve their products. On the new row let $g_i:N_i\to N'_i$ be the aligned maps. Intertwining the row multiplication says
$$
g_j D_{ij}=\lambda_{ij}D'_{ij}g_i .
$$
Writing $h_i=\lambda_{b,i}^{-1}g_i$ turns this into the exact diagram equations. Thus every aligned algebra isomorphism gives a unique diagram transporter and arbitrary independent choices of those six units and the loop maps. Conversely any such choices extend to an aligned algebra isomorphism, as follows directly from the displayed equations. The vertices marked by loop ranks keep the two semilinear operators ordered.

## 3. Ordinary tensor transport and the exact count

Use the multiplication tensor of $\mathcal A$ on three free modules. An arbitrary tensor transporter gives invertible maps $f,g,h$ with $h(xy)=f(x)g(y)$. Evaluating at the two units shows that left multiplication by $a=f(1)$ and right multiplication by $b_1=g(1)$ are bijective. They make $a,b_1$ units. Indeed a one-sided inverse obtained by surjectivity is two-sided by injectivity of the same multiplication. Then
$$
\varphi(x)=a^{-1}h(x)b_1^{-1}
$$
is a unital algebra isomorphism, and $f=a\varphi,\ g=\varphi b_1,\ h=a\varphi b_1$. Conversely the three maps from any algebra isomorphism and two arbitrary target units satisfy the tensor equation. The correspondence is unique in both directions. This proves that ordinary output transporters have the required reverse map, without a partition promise on their modes. [Albert, *Non-associative algebras I*](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968960).

Let
$$
C_{\mathrm{all}}
 =|\mathcal A^\times|^2
  |R|^{d_{\rm off}}
  |R^\times|^6
  \left(\prod_i|\mathrm{GL}_{l_i}(R)|\right)
  |K|^n\kappa^{3(n+k_0)} .
\tag{3}
$$
This is the exact number of output transporters above each source transporter. The factors, in order, are the two units in the isotopy, the lifted idempotent tuple, the six incidence scalars, the loop maps, and the free-cover lifts. The unique aligning unit in $1+I_{\rm off}$ is determined by the idempotent tuple, so each choice in the count gives a distinct transporter and the displayed recovery returns the same prescribed source map.

Recovery respects composition. For isotopies the normalized algebra maps compose, as follows by multiplying the two displayed unit factors on the appropriate left and right. For algebra maps write each one as the composition of an aligned map and an inner map from $1+I_{\rm off}$. An aligned map carries that off-diagonal group to its counterpart, so the aligned part of a composition is the composition of the aligned parts by uniqueness. The six scalars multiply under composition, and the normalized diagram maps and quotient maps then compose. In particular the induced homomorphism from the ordinary tensor stabilizer onto the Dieudonné automorphism group is surjective and has kernel order (3).

All factors in (3) are computable exactly. In particular,
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal A^\times|
 &= (p-1)^8\,p^{8(e-1)}\, |R|^{\,d_{\rm off}+\sum_i l_i},\\
|\mathrm{GL}_l(R)|
 &=p^{(e-1)l^2}\prod_{j=0}^{l-1}(p^l-p^j),\\
|K|&=p^{\sum_i(e-a_i)} .
\end{aligned}
$$
The first formula follows from the quotient $\mathbb F_p^8$ and the radical coordinates. The second follows from reduction to the field and its congruence kernel. All count sizes have polynomial bit length. The construction writes $O((n+1)^3)$ dense ring entries. It uses modular linear algebra and operations in the effective unramified coefficient ring, polynomial in the explicit input and the bit length of $p^e$. The zero module uses the same fixed incidence and loop algebra with the vector row zero.

Cartier–Dieudonné duality is contravariant. In the group-scheme formulation one inverts a recovered module isomorphism if necessary to obtain the prescribed group-scheme direction. The correspondence is bijective on isomorphisms, giving the same source count. This use of the classical equivalence is on the supplied finite Dieudonné presentations.

The same proof applies to any explicitly ordered nonempty list of $s$ endomorphisms of a finite $R$-module. Use $s+1$ sources including the identity source and choose distinct trace-unit loop lengths at the resulting $s+5$ vertices. There are $s+3$ free incidence scalars and $s$ graph factors in the count. The side length is $O(s^2+(s+1)(n+1))$. For an empty list, first insert the zero endomorphism to retain the relations of the free cover. Thus extra prescribed linear operators can be included without changing the ordinary target action.

## Scope and comparisons

Grochow, Qiao, Stange and Sun treat free and general tensor modules over finite local principal ideal rings and establish ring tensor-isomorphism reductions. Earlier presented-module constructions use free covers and block markers. The compiler here applies to a single ordered semilinear module on potentially unequal cyclic exponents. The fixed incidence extension is the step that produces an unmarked free tensor with uniform size and a computable witness multiplicity. Brooksbank and Wilson provide direct module-isomorphism algorithms over cyclic rings, so the decision reduction is compatible with existing tractability results. The claim is the explicit tensor encoding with search recovery and exact counting fibers, with no hardness claim for the original module problem. The proof is informal and publication priority for the exact compiler remains unverified. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Part V](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1) · [Earlier presented-module compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowCatalogueProofsV/PresentedModuleTensorReduction.md) · [Brooksbank–Wilson, *The module isomorphism problem reconsidered*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021869314004918).

Finite controls checked the mixed module $\mathbb Z/4\oplus\mathbb Z/2$ with $F=1,V=2$. All eight source automorphisms had four cover lifts, and all 96 ordered graph instances had eight graph-cover lifts. For the Frobenius on $\mathbb F_4$ at level one, the scalar generator reduced the additive Frobenius centralizer from two invertible maps to the single Dieudonné automorphism. In a three-vertex upper triangular control over $\mathbb Z/4$, the 64 off-diagonal units gave 64 distinct lifted idempotent tuples, with the displayed aligning unit in each case.
