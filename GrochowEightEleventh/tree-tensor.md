# A strict approximation improvement for every tree tensor format

**Theorem.** Fix any tree tensor format with $m\ge3$ nodes and positive edge rank caps. Give the tensor as a dense array. Choose any leaf edge as the first cut and let $r$ be its rank cap, clamped to the left unfolding dimension. Write $E$ for the best squared Frobenius error in the format. There is a polynomial-time approximation algorithm with
$$
 \|A-X\|_F^2\le \left(m-1-\frac{1}{96r}\right)E
$$
with exact singular-vector projections. For rational input there is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time algorithm with rational cores and factor $m-1-1/(192r)$. Both return the input exactly when $E=0$. For $E>0$ this gives the strict improvement requested in Workshop Problem 6.1 for every tree, including tensor trains, Tucker trees and hierarchical Tucker trees. With a single edge the problem is ordinary matrix approximation, for which factor one is optimal. [Simons Workshop Problem 6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05394v3#S6.SS1)

## Tree truncation after a prescribed first projection

Root the tree so the selected leaf is nonroot. Process the oriented edge subtrees in an order with children before parents, starting with that leaf. At a cut, compress its current left unfolding by an orthogonal projection of rank at most the prescribed cap, recording an orthonormal basis of its image. Replace that subtree by the corresponding bond index. All previous subtrees are contained in or disjoint from any later subtree. Consequently applying the previous compressions to a reference tensor $Y$ of the prescribed ranks cannot increase its rank across a remaining cut. This follows by writing the previously applied map, across the current unfolding, as the tensor product of a left and a right map.

Each compression is an orthogonal decomposition into a retained tensor and a discarded tensor. Re-expanding the retained coordinates by the stored isometries shows that the total squared error is the sum of the squared discarded norms. The stored bases give a tensor network on the original tree because each new basis is recorded on the physical coordinate of its node and the already compressed children. In particular the cut-rank bounds characterize this tree format. Necessity follows by contracting each side separately, and sufficiency follows by applying the same construction without any discarded component to a tensor satisfying all cuts. The rank conditions define a closed set, so a best approximation exists by restricting to a bounded ball.

Let $A,Y$ now also denote the two matrices in the first cut, where $Y$ is any comparison tensor in the format and $R=A-Y$, $E=\|R\|_F^2$. Let $P$ be any first-cut rank-$r$ orthogonal projector. If all later projections are greedy singular-value truncations, the total error is bounded above by
$$
 \mathcal E(P)=\|(I-P)A\|_F^2+(m-2)\|PR\|_F^2.                 \tag{1}
$$
Indeed every later cut has the previously compressed $Y$ as a valid rank competitor. Its current error is at most $\|PR\|_F^2$, since subsequent compressions are contractions. There are $m-2$ later cuts.

## A single swap suffices

Compute a full set of left singular vectors $u_i$ at the first cut, with singular values in decreasing order and padded by zeros. Consider the projection $P_*$ onto the first $r$ such vectors. If all left directions are retained, using $P_*$ in (1) already saves $E$ from the usual error bound. Otherwise form $r$ extra candidates. Candidate $P_i$ replaces $u_i$ in $P_*$, for $i\le r$, by $u_{r+1}$. Run the tree truncation for all candidates and keep the least-error output. Its squared error is known from the retained projection norms or by direct evaluation.

Put $t=\|(I-P_*)A\|_F^2$, $s=E-t\ge0$, $S=AA^{\mathsf T}$, and $\lambda_i=\sigma_i^2$. Let $P_Y$ be the orthogonal projection onto the column space of the first unfolding of $Y$, and $Q_Y=I-P_Y$. Define $\Delta_i=\lambda_i-\lambda_{r+1}$ for $i\le r$. Orthogonal error splitting and the fact that $\operatorname{rank}P_Y\le r$ give
$$
 \|P_Y A-Y\|_F^2\le s,\qquad
 \sum_{i\le r}\Delta_i\|Q_Yu_i\|^2\le s.                    \tag{2}
$$
For the second claim, expand $\|Q_YA\|_F^2-t$ in the singular basis. The subtraction of tail terms is at most $\lambda_{r+1}\sum_{i\le r}\|Q_Yu_i\|^2$, because $\operatorname{tr}P_Y\le r$. The excess $\|Q_YA\|_F^2-t$ is at most $s$.

Assume $E>0$, let $\beta=E/(4r)$, and write $H$ for the projection onto those first $r$ vectors with $\Delta_i>\beta$. The following estimate controls the residual energy in those directions:
$$
 \|HR\|_F^2\le (6+4E/\beta)s\le22rs.                       \tag{3}
$$
To see this, write $R=Q_YA+(P_YA-Y)$. The squared norm of $HQ_Y A$ is $\sum_j \lambda_j\|H Q_Y u_j\|^2$. On the high indices, $\lambda_i\le(1+E/\beta)\Delta_i$, since $\lambda_{r+1}\le t\le E$. Apply (2) there. On all other indices $\lambda_j\le E+\beta$, while $\|H Q_Y\|_F^2=\sum_{i\in H}\|Q_Y u_i\|^2\le s/\beta$. These give $\|HQ_YA\|_F^2\le2(1+E/\beta)s$. The squared norm of a sum is at most twice the sum of the squared norms, so (2) proves (3).

If $s\ge E/(96r)$, candidate $P_*$ saves at least $E/(96r)$ in (1) just from its first drop. If $\|(I-P_*)R\|_F^2\ge E/(8r)$, the remaining terms of (1) give the saving. In the remaining case (3) gives high-index residual energy less than $E/4$. Therefore at least $E/2$ of the total residual lies in the first $r$ vectors with $\Delta_i\le\beta$. Some such $u_i$ carries at least $E/(2r)$. Candidate $P_i$ adds $\Delta_i\le E/(4r)$ to the first drop and removes at least $E/(2r)$ of the residual. The incoming direction $u_{r+1}$ adds less than $E/(8r)$ of residual, since it lies in the orthogonal complement of $P_*$. The net decrease in residual before all $m-2$ later cuts is at least $3E/(8r)$. Since $m-2\ge1$, the saving is at least $E/(8r)$ in (1). The uniform factor follows on choosing an optimal $Y$. The algorithm does not need to know $Y,E,\beta$, or the case used in the proof.

## Polynomial-bit implementation for rational data

The exact-rank case can be detected by fraction-free rank tests of every unfolding. When all caps are satisfied, output $A$ and, if desired, obtain rational cores by taking column bases and coordinate maps of the nested unfoldings. A child column span contains the corresponding factor of its parent's span because every parent fiber is built from child fibers. Thus the compressed coordinate maps exist and are found by linear equations. For each cut choose a column basis of the original unfolding and a set of evaluation rows giving independent coordinates on that basis. Normalize the basis by inverting its square evaluation submatrix, so the chosen rows are an identity matrix. The coordinates of each normalized parent basis in the child bases and its own physical coordinate are obtained by restriction to the Cartesian product of the chosen child rows and that physical coordinate. The root uses the corresponding restriction of the original tensor. This avoids repeated growing inversions, and each stored coefficient has polynomial bit length by the determinant bounds for rational linear systems. For the nonexact case there is a positive rational lower bound $E_0\le E$ of polynomial bit length depending only on the input. Indeed multiply the tensor by a common denominator $D$ to get an integer tensor $M$. Some violated cut has a nonzero minor of order one more than its cap. Its determinant has absolute value at least one and is at most the product of that many largest singular values. For a tensor with $N$ entries we can use
$$
 E_0=D^{-2}\left(1+\sum |M_{i_1,\ldots,i_d}|\right)^{-2N},
$$
by the matrix best-rank lower bound at that cut.

Here is a rational orthogonal projection procedure that handles repeated singular values. For a rational matrix $C$, apply approximate Jacobi diagonalization to $S_C=CC^{\mathsf T}$. At each step choose a largest off-diagonal entry and approximately diagonalize its two-by-two principal block using an exactly orthogonal rational rotation. Such a rotation has entries $(1-t^2)/(1+t^2),2t/(1+t^2)$, with rational $t$ chosen to approximate the tangent of the half angle. The plane rotation can be approximated by rational square-root bisection, using the angle of magnitude at most $\pi/4$. In the two-by-two block with diagonal $a,d$ and off-diagonal $b$, the magnitude of its tangent is $2|b|/(|a-d|+\sqrt{(a-d)^2+4b^2})$. Its denominator is at least $2|b|$; before stopping this is at least $2\nu/d_C$. Use dyadic half-angle parameters of precision polynomial in $\log(2+d_C B^2/\nu)$. A one-dimensional left space needs no rotation. Reduce the selected off-diagonal entry in absolute value by at least one half. The sum of squares of the other entries involving just one of these indices is unchanged, by orthogonality. Hence each step reduces squared off-diagonal Frobenius norm by a fraction at least $3/[4d_C(d_C-1)]$ for left dimension $d_C$. Stop when the off-diagonal Frobenius norm is at most $\nu$. Before stopping the selected entry has magnitude at least $\nu/d_C$, so rational rotation parameters need only polynomially many bits in the matrix dimension and in the logarithm of the starting norm divided by $\nu$. Both the number of rotations and the bit lengths of their products are polynomial. Keep a common denominator for each compressed tensor: each rotation has a single common denominator, and orthogonal compression increases the bit length of the tensor denominator by at most the denominator bit length of the new basis. Forming a Gram matrix squares the current denominator only inside a compression. Obtain a rational orthogonal matrix $U$, sort the diagonal entries of $U^{\mathsf T}S_C U$ by a permutation, and use the columns of $U$ as the candidate left basis.

For analysis only, there is a matrix $C^\sharp$ with these exact left singular vectors and $\|C^\sharp-C\|_F\le\sqrt{d_C\nu}$; this assertion allows zero column padding of $C$. To see this, let $S^\sharp=U\operatorname{diag}(U^{\mathsf T}S_C U)U^{\mathsf T}$. It is positive semidefinite and differs from $S_C$ by at most $\nu$ in operator norm. With enough zero padding, write $C=S_C^{1/2}V$ using a row co-isometry $V$, and put $C^\sharp=(S^\sharp)^{1/2}V$. Operator monotonicity of the positive square root and the scalar inequality $\sqrt{x+\nu}-\sqrt x\le\sqrt\nu$ give $\|(S^\sharp)^{1/2}-S_C^{1/2}\|_2\le\sqrt\nu$. For example operator monotonicity follows by integrating the inverse-order inequality in the usual representation of the square root as a positive integral of $S_C(S_C+tI)^{-1}$. The Frobenius bound follows. No padded columns are formed by the algorithm, and all left projections act on the original tensor.

For a rational norm bound $B\ge\max(1,\|A\|_F)$, set $\xi=E_0/(4096rm B)$, reduced if needed so $\xi\le1$, and take $\nu\le\xi^2/N$ at every cut. Each surrogate difference is at most $\xi$. At the first cut, run the candidates in the exact rational basis $U$ and apply the single-swap proof to the surrogate and the zero-padded reference $Y$. Since $\|A-Y\|_F\le B$ for an optimal reference, the squared norms in (1) and the reference error change by at most $3B\xi$ per term between the surrogate and unperturbed matrices. The first-candidate bound is consequently within $6(m-1)B\xi$ of $(m-1-1/(96r))E$ for the unperturbed expression (1). At every later cut a greedy projection for the local surrogate loses at most the true optimum plus $6B\xi$, by the same comparison of squared norms in both directions. The use of exactly orthogonal rational projections preserves (1)'s rank and contraction arguments. The combined additive budget is at most $12mB\xi\le E/(192r)$, proving the claimed bit-time factor. The number of branches is at most $N+1$, every branch has $m-1$ compressions, and all prescribed accuracies have polynomial bit length. The collected rational orthonormal cores give a feasible tree tensor.
