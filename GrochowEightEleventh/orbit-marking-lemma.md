# Orbit-marking lemma used for affine conjugacy

This supporting argument appeared in the preceding packet and is reproduced here for completeness. It reduces prescribed matching of distinguished point orbits to ordinary conjugacy for arbitrary permutation groups.

### Encoding distinguished point orbits

Let $G,H\leq S_n$ be given by generators. Distinguish pairwise distinct point orbits $O_1,\ldots,O_t$ of $G$ and corresponding pairwise distinct point orbits $D_1,\ldots,D_t$ of $H$. Choose distinct primes $q_i>n$. These can be found in polynomial time by trial division in a polynomial range, using $p_m=O(m\log(m+2))$ for the $m$-th prime and $t\leq n$. Construct the action of $\widetilde G=G\times\prod_i C_{q_i}$ on the original domain together with the disjoint sets $O_i\times C_{q_i}$. On each such set, $G$ acts as on $O_i$, the $i$-th cyclic factor acts by translation in the second coordinate, and all other added factors act trivially. On the original domain all added factors are trivial. Form $\widetilde H$ analogously. All generator arrays have polynomial length. If the two expanded degrees differ, return a fixed no instance of ordinary conjugacy.

A conjugator mapping each $O_i$ onto $D_i$ extends coordinatewise to a conjugator of the expanded groups. Conversely, in either expanded action the original domain is the union of the orbits of sizes at most $n$. Among the other orbits, the one whose size is divisible by $q_i$ is exactly the added orbit for label $i$. The factor $C_{q_i}$ is the unique Sylow $q_i$-subgroup, since all primes dividing a subgroup of $S_n$ are at most $n$. Thus any expanded conjugator induces a bijection from the $C_{q_i}$-orbits in $O_i\times C_{q_i}$ to those in $D_i\times C_{q_i}$. This is an equivariant bijection $O_i\to D_i$ for the same group isomorphism $\phi:G\to H$ induced on the original action. The equivariance on the original domain follows because the quotient by the product of the added cyclic factors is exactly the original group.

The expanded conjugator's restriction already gives some $\phi$-equivariant bijection of the original domains. For each isomorphism type of transitive $G$-set, consider the $H$-orbits as $G$-sets via $\phi$. The total multiplicities of each type agree, and every prescribed pair matches the same type. Assign the prescribed pairs first using their equivariant bijections, then match the remaining orbits of each type arbitrarily. Their union is a conjugator of the original groups with all specified orbit matches. This proves the many-one reduction. By successively trying the target orbits for each source orbit and retaining a choice allowed by this decision test, one can find a complete orbit matching that extends to some conjugator. There are at most $n^2$ tests.
