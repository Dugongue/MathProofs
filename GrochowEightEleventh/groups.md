# Two algorithms for finite-group actions

## 1. Conjugator search with abelian local complements

**Source question.** Allender, Grochow, van Melkebeek, Moore and Morgan ask whether finding a conjugator for permutation groups reduces to deciding conjugacy. A previous reduction covered mixed-prime orbit sizes when each local complement acts by integer scalars. [Allender et al., Open Problems](https://arxiv.org/html/1710.09806#S1.SS4) · [Scalar-affine case](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightTenth/groups.md)

**Theorem.** Suppose that on every nontrivial point orbit of each input permutation group the faithful local constituent has a regular abelian normal Sylow subgroup and abelian quotient by that Sylow subgroup. Then finding a conjugator has a deterministic polynomial-time Turing reduction to ordinary permutation-group conjugacy decision. The additive groups and the primes can vary across orbits. The quotient can act by arbitrary automorphisms of the local Sylow group, and the global extension can be nonsplit.

**Local coordinates.** Apply the prime-marking reduction to get a full orbit matching compatible with some conjugator or determine nonconjugacy. On orbit $i$ write $V_i$ for the regular abelian Sylow $p_i$-subgroup and $C_i$ for its quotient. A chosen origin identifies the local group with $V_i\rtimes C_i$, since the point stabilizer maps isomorphically onto the quotient. The stabilizer embeds into $\operatorname{Aut}(V_i)$. A permutation centralizing the translations and fixing the origin fixes every point. The kernel of reduction from $\operatorname{Aut}(V_i)$ to automorphisms of $V_i/p_iV_i$ is a $p_i$-group. Indeed, its elements are $1+f$ with $f(V_i)\subseteq p_iV_i$, an additive group of nilpotent endomorphisms of $p_i$-power cardinality. Thus the $p_i'$-group $C_i$ embeds into the automorphisms of the top. Over $\mathbb F_{p_i}$ an abelian semisimple action decomposes into simple summands of dimensions $d_j$, on each of which the group acts by units in a field of size $p_i^{d_j}$. Consequently $|C_i|\le\prod_j p_i^{d_j}\le |V_i|$. Both local groups can be enumerated in polynomial time. The Sylow group is the set of local elements of $p_i$-power order, and each local generator is expressed in the affine coordinates. [Prime-marking reduction](orbit-marking-lemma.md#encoding-distinguished-point-orbits)

**Marking an element of a local quotient.** Consider matched local quotients $C_i,C'_i$ and elements $b,b'$ of the same order $m>1$. There is an ordinary decision query, with the orbit matching and any existing polynomial-size list of such element conditions included, that tests whether a compatible conjugator sends $b$ to $b'$ on this quotient. To construct one condition, choose a cyclic group $L$ and a unit $u$ of order $m$ acting on it. For each odd $r^a\parallel m$, take a cyclic factor of order $r^{a+1}$ with unit $1+r$. For $2^a\parallel m$ take order $2^{a+2}$ and unit $5$. These units have the desired prime-power orders by the binomial valuation of their powers, and their product has order $m$. The cyclic group $L$ has order at most $4m^2$. Form $D=L\rtimes C_m$ with generator $1\in C_m$ acting as $u$. Any automorphism $\delta$ of $D$ preserving $L$ induces identity on $C_m$. Its restriction to the cyclic $L$ commutes with $u$. Comparing the actions of a quotient generator and its image therefore gives $u=u^k$, which forces $k\equiv1\pmod m$.

Extend the source and target groups by an independent direct factor $D$ for each condition. Keep the original point actions. For each added factor, include three marked transitive orbits. The first is the regular $D$-orbit, and the second is the regular $D/L$-orbit. The third is the coset action of $C_i\times C_m$ on the cosets of $\langle(b,1)\rangle$, via the quotient on the matched original local constituent and the quotient of the added factor. On the target use $b'$. The original pairings and all additional orbits are marked using the prime-marking reduction. The expanded degree and generators are polynomial because each regular $D$-orbit has size $O(m^3)$, each coset orbit has size $|C_i|$, and each condition has $m\le |C_i|$.

A marked conjugator induces an isomorphism of the expanded groups. The combined action on the original orbits has kernel consisting of all auxiliary factors. Each regular auxiliary orbit has kernel the product of all factors other than its own. Preserving these kernels forces the isomorphism to factor as an original-group isomorphism and one automorphism of each auxiliary factor. Preserving the marked orbit of $D/L$ forces that auxiliary automorphism to preserve $L$ and therefore fix $C_m$ elementwise. The stabilizer in the abelian quotient of the third orbit must be transported to the target graph. Thus the local quotient isomorphism sends $b$ to $b'$, because the graph has a unique element with second entry $1$. Conversely, an original conjugator meeting the condition induces an isomorphism of the extended groups that is identity on the auxiliaries. Each of the three marked representations admits an equivariant bijection under this isomorphism by the graph condition. This proves the exact query equivalence.

Greedily pin the images of generators of every $C_i$. For each generator, try all target elements of the same order and query with all previous conditions included. Keep one satisfiable condition at each step. The number and size of queries are polynomial, and repetition yields fixed local quotient isomorphisms $\alpha_i:C_i\to C'_i$ consistent with one original conjugator.

**Recovering a witness.** Use the fixed $\alpha_i$'s to identify projected groups in the product of local quotients, and let $B$ be the common projection. Its representation on the union of the regular $C_i$-sets has polynomial degree. Constructive membership gives, for each original source generator $(a_j,b_j)$, a target lift $(a'_j,\alpha(b_j))$. Adjoin these auxiliary quotient points to the original actions and take their pointwise stabilizers to obtain the translation kernels $A,A'$. Here $A\le V=\prod_iV_i$ and $A'\le V'=\prod_iV'_i$. All groups and maps used additively have compact cyclic presentations, obtained by integer normal forms. [Babai–Luks–Seress](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539794229417) · [Kannan–Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

At each prime $p$ use the primary parts of $A,V,A',V'$ and their designated local factors. Let $R_0=\mathbb Z/p^e$ where $p^e$ is the largest local Sylow exponent at this prime. The additive diagram has local vertices $V_i$, a vertex $Q=(V/A)\oplus R_0$, and a terminal vertex $R_0$. Its arrows are the maps from local coordinates into the first summand of $Q$ and the last-coordinate arrow from $Q$ to the terminal. At $V_i$ include the endomorphism given by the local action of each $b_j$, with the target action of $\alpha_i(b_{j,i})$. At $Q$ include the maps

$$
 (x,s)\longmapsto (b_jx+s\bar a_j,s),
$$

and in the target the corresponding maps using $\alpha(b_j)$ and $a'_j$. The actions preserve the translation kernels, so these maps are well defined. Place corresponding vertex idempotents, arrows and endomorphisms together on the direct sum of both additive diagrams. The unital subring they generate is computable in compact form by repeatedly closing the additive span under products of generator pairs. Each strict enlargement at least doubles its size, and the log of the ambient endomorphism-group order is polynomial in the presentation size. Constructive finite-ring module isomorphism now returns an isomorphism of diagrams. [Ciocănea–Teodorescu, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08365)

The terminal isomorphism is multiplication by a unit of $R_0$. Multiply all vertex maps by its inverse to make the terminal map identity. The local maps give an additive isomorphism $U=\prod_i U_i$ intertwining the paired local actions, and the coordinate arrows force $U(A)=A'$. The map on $Q$ then has the form $(x,s)\mapsto(\bar U x+s\bar t,s)$. Lifting $\bar t$ to $t\in V'$ and commuting with the endomorphisms on $Q$ yields

$$
 U a_j+(1-\alpha(b_j))t-a'_j\in A'.
$$

An actual conjugator with the pinned orbit and quotient maps conversely gives such a diagram isomorphism at every prime. Thus all the module queries are positive. Combine the affine maps $x\mapsto U_i x+t_i$ over the different primes. The generator equations give inclusion of the conjugated source in the target. Equality follows from equality of their translation kernels and projected groups. This produces the required point permutation. The non-scalar case already includes the group $\mathbb F_4^+\rtimes\mathbb F_4^\times$ in its natural action, where the order-three action cannot come from integer scalars on the additive group.

## 2. Minimum faithful actions for abelian-by-abelian Hall extensions

**Source question.** Kantor and Luks ask for a polynomial-time algorithm for minimum faithful permutation degree in the permutation model. Das and Thakkar give a general quasipolynomial-time algorithm in the Cayley-table model. A previous Cayley-table theorem covered abelian normal Hall kernels when the quotient had fixed subgroup-generator rank. [Kantor–Luks, §13](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/quotient.pdf) · [Das–Thakkar](https://doi.org/10.1137/24M1685353) · [Bounded subgroup-rank case](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightTenth/groups.md)

**Theorem.** Given the Cayley table of a finite group $G$ with an abelian normal Hall subgroup $N$ and abelian quotient $C=G/N$, one can construct a minimum-degree faithful permutation action in deterministic polynomial time. The rank and exponent of each factor are unrestricted. The input need not specify $N$.

**Separating the ineffective complement.** Enumerate the at most $|G|$ subsets of primes dividing $|G|$. For each, take the set of elements with orders supported on those primes and test whether it is an abelian normal Hall subgroup with abelian quotient. Every normal Hall subgroup is recovered from its prime support, since the quotient has coprime order. For one passing candidate, choose a complement so that $G=N\rtimes C$. A section of the quotient gives a factor cocycle in the abelian $N$, and averaging over $C$ makes that cocycle a coboundary and constructs the complement. Explicitly, for factor set $u(c,d)$, the correction cochain $|C|^{-1}\sum_d u(c,d)$ has coboundary $u$. The inverse is available on $N$ by coprimality.

Use the character group $M=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$ with the contragredient $C$-action. Up to conjugation by $N$, any orbit stabilizer is $W\rtimes B$ for $B\le C$ and $B$-stable $W\le N$. In fact, after factoring out its intersection $W$ with $N$, a stabilizer gives a graph of a cocycle into $N/W$ over the projected subgroup $B$. Averaging over $B$ makes it principal and gives the conjugation. Write $D=W^\perp\le M$, so orbit cost is $|D|[C:B]$. The kernel meets $N$ trivially exactly when the $C$-spans of all the $D$'s together generate $M$, by finite abelian duality. Set $K=\ker(C\to\operatorname{Aut}(N))$. Any normal kernel disjoint from $N$ centralizes $N$ by taking commutators, and is in $N\times K$. Coprime powers show it is contained in $K$. Here $K$ is central because $C$ is abelian, so the remaining kernel test is that the intersection of $K\cap B$ over all the orbits is trivial.

If an orbit with $D\ne0$ has $K\not\le B$, replace $(D,B)$ by the two orbits $(D,BK)$ and $(0,B)$. The first stabilizer is valid because $K$ acts trivially on $M$. The two original kernel tests are preserved. With $w=|D|\ge2$ and $k=[BK:B]\ge2$, the sum of their costs is $(w+k)[C:BK]\le wk[C:BK]$. The resulting nonzero-annihilator orbits factor through $G/K$ and together act faithfully there. The zero-annihilator orbits factor through $C$ and together act faithfully on $K$. Conversely, an optimum action in each of those roles gives a faithful action of $G$ by disjoint union. Thus the optimum is the sum of the two optima, using the empty action in a trivial role. Handle the trivial input separately if positive degree is required.

Find the relative optimum for $C$ on $K$ as follows. A stabilizer $B$ gives an annihilator $B^\perp$ in $C^\vee$. Split it into cyclic prime-power factors and use their individual annihilator orbits. The product of the nontrivial orders is at least their sum, and the kernel on $K$ is preserved. For each prime $q$ of $|K|$, it therefore suffices to choose cyclic $q$-power-order characters of $C$ whose restrictions to the elementary subgroup $K[q]$ span its dual. Detecting all $K[q]$'s detects all of $K$. Enumerate the $|C|$ characters, use the character order as weight, and use the greedy minimum-weight basis algorithm on each restriction space. A character of weight $w$ gives the coset action of its kernel with degree $w$.

**Orbit types for the effective quotient.** Replace $C$ by $C/K$ for the remaining task, so that its action on $N$ is faithful. For a prime $p\mid |N|$ put $U_p=M_p/pM_p$ and filter this top by $F_{\le e}=(M_p[p^e]+pM_p)/pM_p$. Choose a simple decomposition compatible with it, with $n_{p,\sigma,h}$ copies of type $\sigma$ entering at level $h$. We use the following filtered lifting facts. [Previous Hall case](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightTenth/groups.md#1-minimum-faithful-permutation-actions-for-abelian-hall-extensions)

For a finite abelian $p$-group with a $p'$-group action of $B$, every equivariant filtered map on tops lifts. Lift on cyclic generators using their order bounds and average conjugates over $B$. Every such group splits into $B$-stable homocyclic factors with simple tops. Choose a simple projection compatible with the filtration and lift it. The ideal reducing to zero maps into $p$ times the underlying group and is nilpotent. Iteration of $f\mapsto f^2(3-2f)$ squares the idempotent error up to a polynomial factor, yielding an idempotent with that reduction. Split its image and kernel and repeat. A simple top at one level forces a homocyclic group. A simple $B$-module has a homocyclic lift of exponent $p^e$ by lifting its projection in the regular module from $\mathbb F_p[B]$ to $(\mathbb Z/p^e)[B]$ in the same way. All these operations use additive presentations of polynomial bit length.

Split each nonzero annihilator $D$ into such homocyclic factors over each prime and replace its orbit by the corresponding smaller orbits. This preserves faithfulness and cannot increase cost. A factor with simple top $\tau$ and exponent $p^e$ has cost $p^{e\dim\tau}[C:B]$. Its $C$-span in $U_p$ is the image of a map from $\operatorname{Ind}_B^C\tau$ to $F_{\le e}U_p$. Generating these tops is equivalent to generating $M$. Each simple $\sigma$ for the abelian $C$ is a field $E_\sigma$ with character $\chi_\sigma:C\to E_\sigma^\times$. Its restriction to $B$ is a direct sum of copies of a single simple type, given by the subfield generated by $\chi_\sigma(B)$. The multiplicity of $\sigma$ in $\operatorname{Ind}_B^C\tau$ is one if that type is $\tau$, and is zero otherwise. In the first case the dimension of $\operatorname{Hom}_B(\tau,\sigma)$ is $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\sigma$, by the field model, and Frobenius reciprocity divides this by $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\operatorname{End}_C(\sigma)=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\sigma$.

**Constructing polynomially many stabilizer options.** For each prime and exponent level $e$, enumerate a nonempty subset $\Sigma$ of simple types, with one target level $h\le e$ chosen for each type. Fix a reference $\sigma_0\in\Sigma$. For each other $\sigma$ choose one embedding $\iota_\sigma:E_\sigma\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, taking the reference embedding as fixed. For each positive divisor $t$ of $|\chi_{\sigma_0}(C)|$ compute the subgroup

$$
 B^*=\{c\in C\mid \iota_\sigma\chi_\sigma(c)=\chi_{\sigma_0}(c)\text{ for all }\sigma\in\Sigma\setminus\{\sigma_0\},\quad \chi_{\sigma_0}(c)^t=1\}.
$$

Enumerate the elements of $C$ to compute it. All selected types restrict to copies of the same $B^*$-simple type, whose dimension $d$ is the degree of the subfield generated by $\chi_{\sigma_0}(B^*)$. Store the option of cost $p^{ed}[C:B^*]$, covering one copy of each selected type at its chosen level.

The options dominate every possible nonempty coverage of a split orbit. If its subgroup is $B$, its selected types restrict to copies of a common simple $\tau$, so suitable embeddings make all restricted characters equal. The equivalence of restrictions identifies their image subfields, and an embedding of a subfield of a finite field extends to its whole field. Choose $t=|\chi_{\sigma_0}(B)|$. Then $B\le B^*$, and the reference image orders agree because the image of $B^*$ lies in the unique subgroup of order $t$. Thus $d=\dim\tau$ and the new cost is at most the original. Every option can realize its offered summands. By the multiplicity computation and Maschke's theorem, there is a map from the induced module onto any selected copies at the stated levels. Find it by solving a linear system for a nonzero map to each simple target, splitting by averaging and recurring on the kernel. Recover the associated $B^*$-map from the simple top by Frobenius reciprocity and lift from a homocyclic source of exponent $p^e$. Its image in $M_p$ is an annihilator of order at most $p^{ed}$.

**Complexity and minimization.** The simple decomposition is constructive by enumerating the at most $|N|$ top vectors, choosing a nonzero orbit span of least dimension, splitting by averaging, and repeating within successive filtration quotients. Isomorphic simple types can be identified by linear equations. The field attached to a simple $\sigma$ is the image of the group algebra, computed by taking the matrix span of the group elements. It has size $p^{d_\sigma}\le |N|$ where $d_\sigma=\dim\sigma$. A field generator and minimal polynomial can therefore be found by enumeration. To compute all the relative embeddings used in the character comparisons with the reference field, factor the minimal polynomial of a generator of $E_\sigma$ over $E_{\sigma_0}$. For each factor use the corresponding quotient field and the conjugates of its root under powers of the $|E_{\sigma_0}|$-Frobenius. This yields all $d_\sigma$ root comparisons with the reference without enumerating the quotient fields. Berlekamp factorization runs in polynomial time here because the reference field has size at most $|N|$. The number of embedding choices for fixed types is at most $\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma}d_\sigma\le 2^{\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}d_\sigma}\le |N|$. The number of subsets with level choices is bounded by $\prod_{p,\sigma,h}(n_{p,\sigma,h}+1)\le |N|$. The tested exponent levels and divisors are polynomial in $|G|$. [Berlekamp](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1967.tb03174.x)

Use a shortest-path graph on covered copy counts in all the $(p,\sigma,h)$ positions. Each transition adds the counts of one option, within the remaining deficits. There are at most $|N|$ states by the same product bound. For a given faithful action, split it as above. On each $\sigma$-multiplicity space, the combined induced maps from the contributing orbits are onto. Choose a full-rank square minor and a nonzero term of its determinant over $E_\sigma$. This assigns each target copy to a different source column with nonzero coefficient in its row. Each orbit has at most one column for that $\sigma$, and a nonzero row has level at most the exponent bound for that orbit. The assignments group by orbit into exactly the coverage patterns used to form options. Hence the shortest path costs at most the optimum. Conversely, along a path allocate fresh target copies and construct each annihilator as above. The annihilators span all the tops and the corresponding coset actions are faithful on $N$, hence faithful on the effective group. Their total cost is at most the path cost. This proves optimality and constructs the action. Combining with the relative complement action yields explicit permutations of minimum total degree for the original group.

## Verification status

Both results are paper proofs for the stated input classes. The general input versions of the source questions remain open in this treatment.
