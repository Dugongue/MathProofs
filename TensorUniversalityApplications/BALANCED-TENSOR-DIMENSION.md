# Balanced reduction to ordinary 3-tensor isomorphism

**Theorem.** Over every field, $d$-tensor isomorphism on formats with positive mode lengths reduces to ordinary 3-tensor isomorphism by a deterministic polynomial-time kernel reduction and a coordinate projection with constants. For $d\geq 3$ and maximum source side length $n$, all three output sides are $O(d^7 n^{\lceil d/3\rceil})$. The lift of source isomorphisms and recovery from target isomorphisms preserve composition. Recovery is a left inverse to the lift. On each fixed format the coordinate map is affine, the lift is a homomorphism of algebraic groups, and recovery extends to an algebraic homomorphism on a subgroup containing every target isomorphism between encodings.

This gives the requested exponent in Grochow–Qiao's Open Question 7.8 when the relative $o(1)$ is taken as $d\to\infty$, uniformly in the side lengths. Their published comparison bound is $O(d^2n^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor})$ for $d\ge5$. Here the bound is $O(d^7 n^{d/3})$ for every $d$ divisible by three. For other fixed $d$ the exponent of $n$ is $\lceil d/3\rceil$, and Section 4 proves this rounding is unavoidable under algebraic equivariance. The functorial statement is on the isomorphism categories used for the results of §2.3. [Grochow–Qiao, Part I, §§2.3 and 7.2](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110) · [Preprint §§6.2 and 10.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S10.SS2)

## 1. Balanced product trees as one partitioned tensor

Partition the $d$ modes into three nonempty consecutive chunks differing in size by at most one. Choose a rooted full binary tree on each chunk in the original mode order. At a leaf $v$ use the corresponding source space $W_v$. At an internal node $v$ with children $a,b$ use $W_v=W_a\otimes W_b$, with the ordered product basis. The three root spaces are $W_{r_1},W_{r_2},W_{r_3}$, and the natural regrouping places the source tensor $T$ in their tensor product.

In each of the three new modes take one copy of every $W_v$, one copy of every $W_v^*$, and one scalar line $K e_j$ for that mode $j$. Regard these summands as an ordered partition of that mode. They are distinct labeled summands even when they have equal dimensions. Build a tensor $U(T)$ with the following components in the direct-sum tensor blocks.

- In the block of the three scalar lines, put $e_1\otimes e_2\otimes e_3$.
- For each node $v$ and each ordered pair of distinct modes $i,j$, put the coevaluation of $W_v$ in the blocks $W_v$ in mode $i$, $W_v^*$ in mode $j$, and the scalar line in the remaining mode. The coevaluation is $\sum_q w_q\otimes w_q^*$ in the chosen basis.
- For each internal node $v=(a,b)$, put the structural tensor of the identity on $W_a\otimes W_b$ in $W_a\otimes W_b\otimes W_v^*$, in modes one, two and three. Its coordinates are $\sum_{q,t} a_q\otimes b_t\otimes(a_q\otimes b_t)^*$, where the final factor denotes the corresponding dual basis vector.
- In the three primal root blocks, put the regrouping of $T$.

The indicated block triples are pairwise distinct. Each coefficient is zero, one, or one source coefficient. All three mode supports of $U(T)$ are full for every $T$, including the zero tensor. For the scalar line, contract against the other two scalar dual basis vectors. For any primal or dual block choose its opposite-sign copy in a different mode, contract with appropriate basis covectors there, and contract with the scalar covector in the remaining mode. With those two blocks fixed there is exactly one possible coevaluation component, and the contractions give a basis of the desired block. A 3-tensor with full support in each mode is called concise here.

Consider a partition-preserving isomorphism $H=(H_1,H_2,H_3)$ from $U(T)$ to $U(T')$. If its scalar line maps are multiplication by $s_1,s_2,s_3$, the scalar component gives $s_1s_2s_3=1$. Multiplying the whole mode maps by $s_i^{-1}$ gives another isomorphism in which all scalar maps are the identity. This normalization preserves composition, since the scalar factors multiply under composition and are central in the mode groups.

For a fixed node write $A_i$ and $B_i$ for the normalized maps on the primal and dual copies in mode $i$. The ordered pair components give $A_i B_j^{\mathsf T}=I$ whenever $i\ne j$. For any two different $i,i'$ use the third $j$ to see $A_i=A_{i'}$. Write the common map as $g_v$. Then $B_j=g_v^{-\mathsf T}$ for each $j$. The structural tensor of $v=(a,b)$ is the identity under $W_a\otimes W_b\otimes W_v^*\cong\operatorname{Hom}(W_v,W_a\otimes W_b)$. Its image is $(g_a\otimes g_b)I g_v^{-1}$, so preservation is equivalent to $g_v=g_a\otimes g_b$. Induction recovers every $g_v$ as the product of the source leaf maps. The root block equation is exactly $(\bigotimes_{i=1}^d g_i)T=T'$.

Conversely, a source isomorphism gives such a partitioned isomorphism by putting its product maps and their inverse transposes on the indicated node blocks, and putting the identity on scalar lines. The lift preserves identity and composition. The recovery is read from the normalized leaf maps and is a left inverse to the lift. It is defined for all encodings of the source format, with formulas given by block restrictions and the invertible scalars $s_i^{-1}$.

## 2. One step erasing a partition

Let $A\in X\otimes Y\otimes Z$ be a concise 3-tensor, with $X=\bigoplus_{i=1}^k X_i$ an ordered, nonempty partition with $k\ge2$, and with any desired partitions on $Y,Z$. Write $a=\dim X,b=\dim Y,c=\dim Z,D=a+b$. For $1\le i\le k$ put $w_i=(k+i+1)(D+1)$. Let $R_i,S_i$ be private spaces of dimension $w_i$ in modes one and two, respectively. Use fresh spaces $L_i\cong Y^*$ in mode one and $M_i\cong X_i^*$ in mode two. Augment the modes to
$$
\begin{aligned}
 X'&=X\oplus\bigoplus_i(L_i\oplus R_i),\\
 Y'&=Y\oplus\bigoplus_i(M_i\oplus S_i),\\
 Z'&=Z\oplus\bigoplus_i K z_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
The old $X$ partition is removed. In $Y',Z'$, append the added spaces to any one designated old block. In particular if a mode was already unpartitioned, it stays so. The new tensor is
$$
 E(A)= A+\sum_i C_i\otimes z_i,
$$
where $C_i\in X'\otimes Y'$ is the sum of the coevaluations in $X_i\otimes M_i$ and $L_i\otimes Y$, and the identity tensor on the chosen paired bases of $R_i,S_i$. All these additions use disjoint coordinate blocks and constants zero or one.

For a covector $f\in (Z')^*$, let $\rho_A(f)$ be the rank of its contraction with $E(A)$ as a map $(Y')^*\to X'$. Write $f=(u,\alpha)$ with $u\in Z^*$ and $\alpha=(\alpha_i)$. The private identity blocks are disjoint from everything else in modes one and two, so
$$
 \rho_A(f)=\sum_{\alpha_i\ne 0}w_i+e(f),\qquad 0\le e(f)\le D.
$$
For the upper bound, the matrix of the remaining part has nonzero rows only in $X\oplus\bigoplus_i L_i$. Its restriction to columns in $\bigoplus_i M_i$ lands in $X$ and has rank at most $a$. The columns in $Y$ contribute at most $b$. Rank is invariant under changes of basis in either of these two augmented modes.

All rank values at most $D$ occur only on $Z^*$, because the least marker rank is $w_1>D$. Conversely every covector in $Z^*$ has rank at most $D$, so $Z^*$ is intrinsically the subspace defined by that threshold. A single nonzero coefficient $\alpha_i$ gives rank in $[w_i,w_i+D]$. These intervals are disjoint, since successive $w_i$ differ by $D+1$. Two or more coefficients give rank at least $2w_1>w_k+D$. Thus the inverse image of each punctured marker line modulo $Z^*$ is precisely its rank band. This argument uses only the nonzero support of $\alpha$, and applies in every characteristic.

Suppose $H=(P,Q,J)$ is an isomorphism from $E(A)$ to $E(B)$, respecting the retained partitions. The induced pullback $J^*$ of slice covectors preserves the threshold and marker bands just found, since the paired contractions differ by $P,Q$. In particular $J$ preserves the annihilator of $Z^*$, namely the added span of the $z_i$. Thus it induces an invertible map $J_0$ on its quotient, the old $Z$, respecting its partition. The joint row and column supports of the slices contracted against $Z^*$ are precisely $X,Y$ by conciseness of $A,B$. Hence $P,Q$ preserve $X,Y$, and their restrictions $P_0,Q_0$ are invertible maps. The restriction $Q_0$ respects the old partition since intersections with the retained augmented blocks give exactly the old blocks of $Y$. The induced maps $P_0,Q_0,J_0$ send $A$ to $B$, by projecting $E(A)$ and $E(B)$ modulo the marker span in $Z'$ and restricting to the recovered old modes.

To recover the old $X$ partitions, take a covector in the $i$-th rank band. Its contraction has precisely one nonzero marker coefficient, say $\alpha_i=\lambda$. The image of the $M_i^*$ columns is $X_i$ and the image of the $S_i^*$ columns is $R_i$. The $Y^*$ columns have image of the form ${L(y)+\lambda y^{(i)}:y\in Y^*\}$, where $L(y)\in X$ and $y^{(i)}\in L_i$ is the fixed corresponding vector in that private copy. Since $X,L_i,R_i$ are independent and $y\mapsto y^{(i)}$ is an isomorphism, the full column image intersects $X$ exactly in $X_i$. This description is independent of which covector in the band was used. Therefore $P_0$ respects the old partition. All maps in recovery are restrictions or induced quotient maps, so recovery preserves identity and composition.

In the forward direction, given a partitioned isomorphism $(P_0,Q_0,J_0)$, lift it on $L_i$ as $Q_0^{-\mathsf T}$, on $M_i$ as $(P_0|_{X_i})^{-\mathsf T}$, and by the identity on $R_i,S_i$ and each $Kz_i$. Use the old maps on $X,Y,Z$. This preserves each $C_i$, respects the remaining partitions, and has the original maps as recovery. It too preserves identity and composition. In coordinates all lifts are algebraic maps on the relevant general linear groups.

Finally $E(A)$ is concise. Slicing against $Z^*$ spans old row and column supports, and slicing against the separate marker covectors adds all the private row and column supports. The map from slice covectors to matrices is injective, since a vanishing contraction has all marker coefficients zero by rank, then its old component vanishes by conciseness of $A$. This is full support in the last mode.

## 3. Iteration, accounting and the reduction properties

Apply the erasure step successively to modes one, two and three, cyclically rotating the roles $X,Y,Z$ at each step. It applies every time because conciseness is preserved. In modes not yet erased keep the original number of blocks, appending new space to the designated scalar block if needed. The resulting $\Phi(T)$ is an ordinary 3-tensor, with no partitions. Lifting and recovery compose with the maps of Sections 1 and 2, then give the exact isomorphism equivalence and the claimed left-inverse identities.

For different source formats of the same $d$ the construction is also a kernel reduction. Each stage uses a number $k\ge2$ depending only on $d$. Writing $S=\sum_i w_i$, its ordinary output lengths in the stage's own mode order are $a+kb+S,a+b+S,c+k$, and $S=\kappa_k(a+b+1)$ for a positive integer $\kappa_k$ depending only on $k$. The second length therefore determines $a+b$. The difference between the first and second is $(k-1)b$, and the third determines $c$. Thus equality of final ordinary formats determines equality of all earlier ordinary formats. The threshold and band argument then recovers each erased ordered partition intrinsically even if its block sizes in the two sources had differed. At the end of recovery the ordered partitioned formats of $U(T),U(T')$ agree. The leaf block sizes are the original input mode sizes, so these must agree as well.

The three product trees have $2d-3$ nodes in all. Each node has at most $\lceil d/3\rceil$ leaves. Thus each mode of $U(T)$ has dimension $O(d n^{\lceil d/3\rceil})$ and has $4d-5$ ordered blocks. Every erasure grows the maximum side by at most $O(k^2)$, since each $w_i=O(k(D+1))$ and there are $k$ markers. Three erasures give $O(d^7 n^{\lceil d/3\rceil})$. For the relative bound, $3\lceil d/3\rceil/d=1+O(1/d)$. This is a bound for the **unmarked output**, including all identity padding.

To see polynomial-time computability uniformly even for unbalanced formats, let $m=\prod_i \dim W_i$, the number of source coordinates. All product-tree node lengths are at most $m$, since the mode lengths are positive. The construction dimensions are $O(d^7 m)$. The output may be produced as a dense array in polynomial time in $d+m$, with no arithmetic on the input coefficients beyond copying. Each variable coordinate of $T$ appears in one root position, which travels by inclusions through erasure. Every other output coefficient is a constant zero or one. This gives an affine coordinate projection with constants over any field. Source sides of size one require no special rule. Comparisons with zero-dimensional source modes can be decided directly from the formats, since all such tensors vanish.

On every fixed format all source dimensions and summands are fixed. The forward maps use products, inverse transposes, identity blocks and direct sums, and hence give homomorphisms of algebraic groups. At any erasure stage consider the subgroup preserving the fixed old first-mode blocks, the old second-mode space, the last-mode marker span, and the retained partitions. On this subgroup restriction and quotient give an algebraic homomorphism into the preceding mode groups, and the recovery proof places every actual transporter in this subgroup. At the last erasure take the inverse images of the corresponding subgroups from the preceding stages under these homomorphisms. At the product-tree stage require that the maps preserve its ordered blocks. The result is an algebraic subgroup of the final mode groups containing every actual transporter and every lifted source transformation. On this subgroup the normalized leaf restrictions are regular group homomorphisms, since the normalizing scalars are characters with nonzero values. Their composite with the restriction and quotient maps is the asserted algebraic recovery, and it is a left inverse to the lifted action. This gives the isomorphism functor and witness recovery from §2.3 of Part I.

## 4. The rounded exponent is optimal under algebraic equivariance

Suppose all input sides equal $n\ge2$. Over an infinite field, any affine kernel reduction equivariant under a homomorphism of algebraic groups from $G=\mathrm{GL}_n^d$ to the product of the three output general linear groups must have an output side of dimension at least $n^{\lceil d/3\rceil}$. The same statement holds over any field if the equivariance identity holds as an identity of algebraic maps.

Write the affine map as $F(v)=F(0)+L(v)$. Equivariance makes $F(0)$ fixed, so $L$ is equivariant. The zero orbit cannot be identified with a nonzero orbit, so some tensor basis vector $v=e_{j_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_d}$ has nonzero image under $L$. Restrict the lifted action to the split diagonal torus of $G$. Its characters are integer tuples in $\mathbb Z^{dn}$, organized into $d$ consecutive blocks each of length $n$. Each output mode decomposes into character spaces. The weight of $v$ is the tuple whose block $i$ is the $j_i$-th standard unit vector, hence is nonconstant in every block.

The character decomposition follows directly by writing the representation matrix on the split torus as $\rho(t)=\sum_\lambda t^\lambda E_\lambda$. Its entries are Laurent polynomials. Comparing the coefficients in $\rho(tt')=\rho(t)\rho(t')$ shows that the $E_\lambda$ are mutually orthogonal idempotents, and $\rho(1)=I$ shows that they sum to the identity.

The weight of $v$ occurs in the target tensor representation since $L(v)\ne0$. The tensor product of the three weight decompositions shows that this weight is a sum of three weights, one from each mode. In every block at least one summand is nonconstant. Accordingly at least one of the three chosen mode weights is nonconstant on at least $\lceil d/3\rceil$ blocks.

Permutation matrices of each source factor normalize the split torus and permute the $n$ entries in its block. A nonconstant tuple of $n$ entries has at least $n$ distinct permutations. In fact, one value has multiplicity $t$ with $1\le t<n$, and the positions of that value give at least $\binom nt\ge n$ permutations. The source factors act independently. The target mode under consideration therefore contains at least $n^{\lceil d/3\rceil}$ distinct weights, each with a nonzero weight space. This proves the dimension lower bound.

Together with the construction, the lower bound determines the exponent of $n$ in the largest output side for every fixed $d$ within this affine equivariant class. In particular a fixed-$d$ interpretation of the question's $o(1)$ as $n\to\infty$ cannot hold in this class when three does not divide $d$. As $d\to\infty$, the construction has relative error $O(1/d)$ uniformly in $n$.

Part IV also asks about side lengths $n^{4/3}$ and $n^{5/3}$ for $4$-TI and $5$-TI. The lower bound above shows that an affine reduction equivariant under the full lifted algebraic action requires side length at least $n^2$ in both cases. The Part IV question permits broader reductions outside this class. [STOC 2025 version, §1.5](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/189643/2/3717823.3718282.pdf)

## 5. Polynomial dependence on the number of partition blocks

**Corollary.** For partitioned 3-tensor isomorphism with an ordered partition on one axis into $k$ independent blocks and side lengths $a,b,c>0$, there is a coordinate projection to ordinary 3-tensor isomorphism with output sides $O(k^2(a+b+c))$ and split witness recovery. With ordered partitions in all three modes with $k_X,k_Y,k_Z$ independent blocks respectively, the output sides are $O((k_X k_Y k_Z)^2(a+b+c))$. These statements hold over every field and for all input tensors, including degenerate and zero tensors.

The number of parts and their ordering are part of the partition data. The reduction is a kernel reduction on formats with fixed numbers of blocks on each axis. In the decision problem arbitrary block counts are allowed, with incompatible counts rejected by comparing the format headers. This concerns independent block changes of basis, the group in the Part IV definition of Partitioned Tensor Isomorphism. In the STOC version the authors explicitly asked whether the exponential dependence on the number of parts can be avoided. [Grochow–Qiao, Part IV, §1.4 and Theorem 1.14](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/189643/2/3717823.3718282.pdf)

Use the layout of Section 2 with two separate markers for each first-mode block. The two markers for $X_i$ both have a coevaluation into $X_i$ and each has a coevaluation with the base second mode $Y$. All auxiliary row and column spaces are private to an individual marker. Use the rank weights from Section 2 with marker count $2k$, assigning each marker its own index. The same rank formula holds with residual rank at most $a+b$. Hence the low slices still identify the old $Z^*$ and each singleton band identifies its marker individually. This part applies without conciseness.

For a singleton slice corresponding to marker $h$ assigned to $X_i$, the first-mode support is the direct sum of $X_i$, $R_h$, and the graph in $X\oplus L_h$ of a linear map from $L_h$ to $X$. Indeed, the old $Y^*$ columns have a component in $X$ and project isomorphically onto $L_h$, while the columns in $M_h^*,S_h^*$ give $X_i,R_h$. For the two markers assigned to $i$, these graphs and private supports have independent auxiliary coordinates. The intersection of the two first-mode supports is precisely $X_i$. The second-mode support of a singleton slice for $h$ is $Y\oplus M_h\oplus S_h$. To see this, the $L_h$ rows give all of $Y$, the $X_i$ rows give $M_h$ up to terms in $Y$, and the $R_h$ rows give $S_h$. Thus the intersection of the second-mode supports for two different markers is exactly $Y$. Take both slices for any one block. This recovers $Y$ and each $X_i$ without needing the unpadded tensor to have full mode support.

The rest of the lifting and quotient argument is unchanged. One restricts the first and second mode maps to the recovered $X,Y$ and quotients the last by the marker span. Lifts use the inverse transposes on the newly attached dual copies for both markers. In particular these maps preserve identity and composition and recovery is a left inverse to the lift. Every erasure therefore works even if the prior augmented tensor has a deficient mode support. Erasing the relevant axes in succession gives the claimed product of quadratic factors.

For completeness, recovery also distinguishes the old formats across tensors with different block sizes and the same block counts. In one doubled step the dimensions are $a+2kb+S,b+2a+S,c+2k$, where $S=\kappa(a+b+1)$ for the known positive integer $\kappa$ from the $2k$ marker weights. The first two output dimensions determine $a,b$ because the coefficient matrix has determinant $1-2k\kappa-4k\ne0$. The third gives $c$. Thus the rank bands are intrinsic within each step even across source formats, and the recovered subspaces determine the ordered block sizes.

## Source and verification

The construction applies the balanced forcing step from the earlier universality proof for Open Question 7.4 to independent source modes. Its partition erasure uses the layout of Grochow–Qiao's Part IV §5. The extra requirement here is uniform polynomial growth in the number of parts. The small disjoint rank bands suffice because the recovery only needs to identify each single-marker line and distinguish it from multi-marker mixtures. Conciseness supplied by the tree encoding then recovers the old modes intrinsically. [Part IV, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S5)

The supporting Lean source checks the rank threshold and singleton band equivalences for arbitrary finite marker supports and any residual rank up to $D$. The independent axiom audit records the exact source hash. The Python check uses sparse matrices over small prime fields to replay the contraction ranks and image intersections in the single-marker construction in Section 2. The constructions, their recovery, and the lower bound have written proofs above.
