# Same-field polynomial identity testing from Boolean IPS over finite fields

The finite-field part of Grochow's Open Question 7.4 has an affirmative answer. For each fixed finite field $F$, the Ideal Proof System over $F$ for Boolean UNSAT is polynomially simulated by a Cook–Reckhow proof system if and only if formal polynomial identity testing over $F$ lies in NP. The implication also holds uniformly for explicitly presented input finite fields when the simulation has one polynomial bound. The rational-input strengthening with only an $\mathrm{IPS}_{\mathbb Q}$ assumption remains open. [Grochow, Theorem 6.1 and Open Question 7.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.02184#S7.SS2)

## 1. Descending a circuit certificate

Let $F\subseteq K$ be a field extension of finite degree $e$, and use a basis $b_0=1,b_1,\ldots,b_{e-1}$ with multiplication constants $\mu_{ij\ell}\in F$, so $b_i b_j=\sum_\ell\mu_{ij\ell}b_\ell$. Let $P_1(x),\ldots,P_m(x),Q(x)$ have coefficients in $F$. Suppose $C_K(x,y)$ is a size-$s$ circuit over $K$ satisfying the formal polynomial identities
$$
C_K(x,0)=0,\qquad C_K(x,P_1(x),\ldots,P_m(x))=Q(x).
$$
There is an $F$-circuit with $O(e^3s+e^3)$ gates witnessing these same identities.

At every gate keep the $e$ coordinate circuits of the polynomial in the fixed basis. A variable lies in coordinate zero. For a constant use its $F$-coordinates. Addition and subtraction are pointwise, and coordinate $\ell$ of a product of vectors $u,v$ is
$$
\sum_{i,j}\mu_{ij\ell}u_i v_j .
$$
Inductive correctness is over the formal polynomial ring, and each input gate is simulated only once. Take coordinate zero of the output. Since both substitutions for $y$ use only coefficients in $F$, they commute with taking coordinates. The zero coordinate of the polynomial $Q$ is $Q$ itself. Thus this is an IPS certificate with the original placeholders, including if the starting certificate is nonlinear in the placeholders. Ordinary Hilbert-like linearity is preserved when present.

For finite fields in polynomial-basis representations the coordinate descriptions and multiplication constants, including for $K=F[T]/h(T)$, have length polynomial in the field presentation. Thus encoding the new gates and constants has polynomial bit overhead in the extension degree, presentations and circuit bit size. For a fixed number field over $\mathbb Q$ the rational structure constants have fixed bit length and the same statement holds. The argument uses a coordinate functional fixing $1$; the field trace could vanish on $1$ in finite characteristic.

If the circuit model permits inversions of syntactically constant subcircuits, over a finite field evaluate those subcircuits and replace their inversion outputs by field constants in polynomial bit time. One can also simulate such an inverse without evaluating a large rational constant when descending from a number field. Take the matrix $M$ for multiplication by its input element in the fixed basis. Its entries are linear forms in the coordinate circuits. The requested inverse has coordinate vector $M^{-1}e_0$, obtained by division-free determinant circuits and one legal syntactically constant inversion of $\det M$. The latter is nonzero since the original inversion was legal. This gives a polynomial gate overhead in the model admitting these gates.

## 2. The formal PIT to Boolean UNSAT construction

Take a circuit $U$ over the finite field $F$, of bit size $n$, with addition, subtraction, multiplication, constants and variables. After normalizing fan-in its formal degree is at most $2^{O(n)}$. Choose an extension $K/F$ of degree $e=O(n+1)$ with $|K|>\deg U$, using the known syntactic upper bound and padding the degree by a fixed constant factor if necessary. Every nonzero polynomial of total degree less than $|K|$ has a nonzero evaluation on $K$. Consequently the following equations, over $K$, have a solution exactly when $U\ne0$:
- introduce a variable $g_v$ for each gate and enforce the gate's value using its corresponding equation $A_v(x,g)=0$;
- require $1-zg_{\rm out}=0$.

For $U=0$, these equations have a polynomial-bit-size $\mathrm{IPS}_K$ certificate. For example, retain the sharing of $U$ and construct a derivation $D_v$ of $g_v-U_v(x)$ for every gate, using placeholders for the gate equations. At a multiplication gate the correction to the gate equation is
$$
(g_i-U_i)g_j+U_i(g_j-U_j),
$$
so the two already constructed derivations can be reused. Addition and subtraction only require the corresponding linear combinations. Multiplying $D_{\rm out}$ by $z$ and adding the placeholder for $1-zg_{\rm out}$ yields $1-zU(x)$, which is formally $1$ on a yes-instance of PIT. This is the gate-circuit construction of Grochow's Lemma 3.1. [Grochow, Lemma 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.02184#S3)

Use the Boolean translation from the proof of Theorem 6.1. Its input is the described $K$ and the gate-equation system. Write each $K$ coordinate in a basis over $\mathbb F_p$. Expand the gate equations into prime-field coordinate equations. Encode field values in bits and require, by a polynomial-size Boolean circuit and its Tseitin CNF, that the bit representations of these equations evaluate to zero. One may allow all bit strings as inputs and take their residues modulo $p$; every field coordinate has at least one valid encoding. This gives a polynomial-size CNF $\Phi_{U,K}$, satisfiable exactly when the equations have a solution over $K$. The source's Lemmas 2.5, 5.2 and 5.12 give the precise syntactic property needed here. Modulo-$p$ binary value derivations turn the CNF's Boolean gate requirements into the prime-field coordinate equations under the value substitutions. Expansion in the $K$-basis then turns these into the original equations under the same substitutions. Composition with the preceding certificate yields an $\mathrm{IPS}_K$ refutation of $\Phi_{U,K}$ of polynomial bit size when $U=0$. Once the field presentation is supplied, all the underlying coordinate circuits and the CNF construction are deterministic and polynomial in their encoded input sizes. [Grochow, Lemma 2.5, Lemma 5.2, Lemma 5.12 and proof of Theorem 6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.02184#S6)

The polynomials encoding a Boolean CNF and its Boolean axioms have coefficients in the prime field. Apply Section 1 to the composed certificate of $\Phi_{U,K}$. This gives a polynomial-bit-size $\mathrm{IPS}_F$ refutation. This descent is performed on the final certificate *after* the Boolean translation. The extension still lets the Boolean circuit detect a nonzero evaluation; the simulation hypothesis only has to cover the ground field.

## 3. Uniform NP verification

Assume a fixed Cook–Reckhow proof system $\mathcal P$ for Boolean UNSAT polynomially simulates $\mathrm{IPS}_F$ with its bit-size measure. Guess an irreducible monic polynomial $h\in F[T]$ of the specified degree $e$, and a $\mathcal P$-proof of $\Phi_{U,K}$ for $K=F[T]/h$. A degree-$e$ irreducible exists over every finite field. Irreducibility is checked in polynomial bit time by Frobenius powering and gcds: $T^{q^e}-T$ must vanish modulo $h$, and for each prime divisor $r$ of $e$, $\gcd(h,T^{q^{e/r}}-T)=1$, where $q=|F|$. Trial division suffices for the small integer $e$. The forward implication uses no deterministic construction of an irreducible polynomial. For a varying-field version, the supplied base field presentation can be checked in the same way, including ordinary deterministic primality testing for its characteristic if valid field presentations are not promised.

If $U=0$, the descended IPS refutation gives a short $\mathcal P$-proof. If $U\ne0$, every guessed valid extension of the prescribed degree has a nonzero evaluation, so its CNF is satisfiable and no guessed $\mathcal P$-proof verifies. This proves the uniform NP upper bound from the ground-field simulation. The converse is the direction from Theorem 6.1: guess the IPS circuit and NP witnesses for its two certificate identities, using the assumed PIT verifier over $F$.

## 4. Consequences for the field range

For Boolean UNSAT, any two fixed finite fields of the same characteristic give polynomially equivalent IPS proof systems. Extend prime-field certificates by scalar inclusion and descend certificates from each extension using Section 1. More generally, if the extension degree and presentations are polynomially bounded in a proof family, the same proof-size translation applies. For fixed algebraic number fields, the comparable reference field in characteristic zero is $\mathbb Q$. This establishes the finite-extension comparison discussed in Grochow's Open Question 7.5 for unrestricted arithmetic-circuit IPS. The question across distinct characteristics remains open. [Grochow, Open Question 7.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.02184#S7.SS2)

Ordinary formal PIT over any two fixed finite fields of the same characteristic is also equivalent under deterministic polynomial-time reductions. To descend, expand the circuit into coordinate circuits $U_0(x),\ldots,U_{e-1}(x)$, using the original variables in the unit coordinate; output the single polynomial $\sum_{j=0}^{e-1}t^j U_j(x)$ over the smaller field with fresh indeterminate $t$. This is a formal zero precisely when all coordinates are zero, even if the smaller field has very few elements. Scalar inclusion gives the other direction. The same comparison holds between an algebraic number field and $\mathbb Q$. Consequently the same-field question over a fixed number field is equivalent to the rational same-field question. For finite fields and varying degree, this comparison is uniform when the presentations are explicit; both outputs of the reductions have polynomial encoding length.

## 5. A bounded-degree characteristic-zero consequence

A separate consequence uses prime-field simulations to test rational circuits of bounded *syntactic* degree. Suppose a single Cook–Reckhow system simulates Boolean IPS over the explicitly presented prime fields, with a single polynomial in the bit size. Then rational PIT for division-free circuits of polynomial syntactic degree is in NP. The same holds for such circuits over fixed algebraic number fields by the coordinate reduction just given.

Here is the height argument that makes the choice of a guessed prime sound. At a product gate syntactic degrees add, at a sum or scalar linear combination they take the maximum, and inputs have syntactic degree one. Let $D$ bound the syntactic degree, and let $n$ be the bit size of the normalized circuit. Let $A$ be the product of the denominators of all listed scalar coefficients, including the rational inputs. Its bit size is $O(n)$. Expand at multiplication gates into both branches and select just one branch at each scalar linear combination. Each term of this formal expansion has at most $D$ leaves and depth at most $n$, hence at most $D(n+1)$ nodes. Each term has $O(D(n+1))$ scalar factors, whose denominators divide $A$, and the number of terms is at most $n^{O(D(n+1))}$. Clearing a common power of $A$ therefore gives an integer numerator for each coefficient with bit size bounded by an explicit polynomial in $n,D$. The power and the bit bound are computed without listing terms or coefficients. For example, with the usual binary circuit description, $B=2^{10(n+1)^2(D+1)}$ exceeds these numerator magnitudes, $A$, and $D$. If $D$ is polynomially bounded, $B$ has polynomial bit length. Guess any prime $p$ strictly between $B$ and $2B$, with deterministic primality verification. For every such guess reduction modulo $p$ preserves formal zero and formal nonzero. The latter also has a nonzero evaluation, as $p>D$. Now use the finite-field Boolean construction over $\mathbb F_p$ and verify the simulated proof. Completeness follows from Bertrand's postulate and soundness from the height bound.

The explicit height bound matters because circuit constants can grow inside the circuit. For example, $1+1$ has syntactic degree one and integer coefficient $2$. It reduces to zero at the prime $2>1$, contrary to the stated constant-free specialization rule. Iterating $u\mapsto u+u$ preserves syntactic degree one and grows the coefficient bit length. Thus the coefficient-height estimate in Observation 2.3 of the arXiv v1 cannot justify choosing an arbitrary polynomial-bit prime for unrestricted rational circuits. The finite-field conclusion in Sections 1–3 uses no characteristic-zero specialization. The bounded-syntactic-degree theorem here uses simulations in positive characteristic, and the rational-input strengthening with only an $\mathrm{IPS}_{\mathbb Q}$ assumption remains unresolved. [Grochow, arXiv v1, Observation 2.3 and Lemma 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.02184v1#S4)
