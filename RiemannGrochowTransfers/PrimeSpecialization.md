# Boolean spectra of prime reductions of rational tensors

**Theorem.** Fix a nonempty 3-CNF formula $F$ on $r\ge1$ variables that occur or are explicitly listed in the input, with no empty clause, and a nonzero integer $B$. One can construct, in time polynomial in the binary input length, two explicit integer three-tensors $T_0,T_1$ of the same format and an even integer
$$
D=2|B|\prod_{i=1}^r\ell_i
$$
where $\ell_i$ are the first $r$ odd primes. The tensors are isomorphic over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ and are inequivalent over $\mathbb Q$. For every prime $p\nmid D$ with $p\equiv3\bmod4$, their reductions are ordinarily isomorphic over $\mathbb F_p$ if and only if the truth assignment $a_i(p)=1$ exactly when $(\ell_i/p)=1$ satisfies $F$.

Writing $t=\#\{a\in\{0,1\}^r:F(a)=1\}$, the qualifying successful primes have natural relative density $t/2^{r+1}$ among all primes. This assertion is unconditional. Assuming Dirichlet GRH, there is an absolute effective constant $C$ such that every real $X\ge C4^r\log^2(2D)/t$ has a qualifying successful prime in $(X/2,X)$ when $t>0$. Each prescribed assignment occurs at a qualifying prime in this interval for every $X\ge C4^r\log^2(2D)$ independently of whether that assignment satisfies the formula. The finite prime-existence problem with a binary upper bound is NP-complete on the explicitly recognizable family described below. This NP-completeness also holds unconditionally with a weaker certificate bound obtained by Linnik's theorem. Exact computation of the scaled density $2^{r+1}\operatorname{dens}$ is #P-hard on this family, unconditionally. A tautological-clause variant even gives #P-hardness for the density of all successful primes without a congruence restriction.

Here ordinary tensor isomorphism uses three independent invertible linear maps. The GRH assumption is only for Dirichlet $L$-functions.

## Literal atoms

For a field $K$ of characteristic different from two and $a\in K^\times$, compare the multiplication tensors of $K[u]/(u^2-a)$ and $K[u]/(u^2-1)$. In the displayed basis $(1,u)$ their entries are in $\{0,1,a\}$ and $\{0,1\}$. These are concise because their multiplication has a unit. Any multiplication-tensor isomorphism of unital associative algebras $A_0,A_1$ has invertible maps $P,Q,U$ with $U(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$. If $a_0=P(1)$ and $b_0=Q(1)$, left multiplication by $a_0$ and right multiplication by $b_0$ are bijective by evaluation at the unit. Both are units. Hence
$$
U(xy)=U(x)\,U(1)^{-1}U(y).
$$
The invertible map $x\mapsto U(1)^{-1}U(x)$ is a unital algebra isomorphism. The converse uses three copies of an algebra isomorphism. The literal atom thus holds precisely when $a$ is a square, because its two algebras are split exactly when $u^2-a$ has a root. Use $a=\ell_i$ for a positive literal and $a=-\ell_i$ for a negative literal. At the qualifying primes, $(-1/p)=-1$, so their square tests are complements. Every atom is true in $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ and false in $\mathbb Q$.

## Integral ordinary tensor gates

Write a concise tensor as a matrix pencil $M(w):V\to U$ with labels $w\in W$. Choose an integer $b>\dim U$. Append a two-dimensional label $c=(x,y)$, input summands $E=U\otimes (K^2)^*$ and $K^{3b}$, and the output summand $K^{3b}$. The new pencil is
$$
\widehat M(w,c)=
\begin{pmatrix}M(w)&\mathrm{ev}_c&0\\0&0&\operatorname{diag}(xI_b,yI_b,(x+y)I_b)\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The label subspace $W$ is intrinsic, since the slices on $W$ have rank at most $\dim U$ while all others have rank at least $2b$. The sum of images and intersection of kernels on $W$ recover $U$ and the quotient $V$. Consequently this wrapper preserves and reflects equivalence of the original tensors. A transporter of the original extends by the identity on $c$ and the appended diagonal blocks, and by its $U$-map tensored with the identity on $(K^2)^*$ on $E$. The wrapper remains concise.

Its centroid consists of scalar triples. Indeed take a centroid triple $(L,R,H)$ satisfying $H\widehat M(w,c)=\widehat M(L(w,c))=\widehat M(w,c)R$. Ranks show $L$ preserves $W$. On the slices there, the image and kernel descriptions give that $H$ preserves $U$ and $R$ preserves $E\oplus K^{3b}$. Applying the centroid equations on $E$ at all $c$ further shows $R(E)\subseteq E$. The evaluation block gives $H|_U(v)f(c)=v f(\overline Lc)$ for $v\in U$ and $f\in(K^2)^*$. The two relevant maps are therefore one common scalar. Subtract this scalar centroid triple. Now $H$ kills $U$ and $\overline L=0$. The equations on $W$ make $L|_W=0$ by conciseness. Projecting the equations to the new output summand makes the projected $H$ vanish, since the diagonal-block images span that summand. The remaining $H\widehat M(w,c)$ and $\widehat M(L(w,c))$ are supported on disjoint input summands, respectively $K^{3b}$ and $V$. So both vanish. Conciseness makes all of $H,L,R$ zero.

For direct sums of concise tensors the centroid is the product of the individual centroids, by restricting the centroid identities to labels supported on one summand and using conciseness in all three modes. For sums of the wrappers, primitive centroid idempotents intrinsically separate the summands. The sum compares exactly by a permutation of isomorphic summands. To compare an ordered pair, use two wrapped summands with companion parameters $b=1+L_0$ and $b=2(1+L_0)$, with $L_0$ a common bound for all original sides. Their output sizes distinguish the two positions. This implements conjunction of two comparisons. For disjunction of $T_0\simeq T_1$ and $S_0\simeq S_1$, take the two unordered sums of wrapped ordered pairs
$$
\{(T_0,S_0),(T_1,S_1)\},\qquad
\{(T_0,S_1),(T_1,S_0)\}.
$$
Each ordered pair uses the two distinct parameters. Each outer wrapper uses the same common parameter on the four candidate pairs. The straight matching succeeds exactly when $S_0\simeq S_1$, and the crossed matching exactly when $T_0\simeq T_1$.

All tensor entries are formed by copying coefficients and adding the displayed $0,1$ blocks. There is no pivot-dependent preprocessing: the literal multiplication tensors and every wrapper or direct sum are already concise. Reduction commutes with each gate. Every binary gate increases side lengths by a bounded constant factor plus a constant. Build each three-literal disjunction with two gates and build a balanced conjunction of clauses. Its depth is $O(\log |F|)$, giving polynomial side length and polynomial bit length. Over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ every literal holds, and over $\mathbb Q$ no literal holds. The two assertions for the finished comparison follow because $F$ has a nonempty clause list and every clause contains a literal. The wrapper argument is the same field-uniform scalar-centroid gate used in the prior MathProofs rational extension-degree result, applied here with integral quadratic algebra atoms in place of pure radical extension-degree atoms. ## Simultaneous primes and the density

On primes outside $D$, let
$$
s(p)=\bigl((-1/p),(\ell_1/p),\ldots,(\ell_r/p)\bigr)\in\{\pm1\}^{r+1}.
$$
The corresponding quadratic Dirichlet characters are independent. Indeed every nonempty product comes from a nonsquare squareclass in $\mathbb Q^\times/\mathbb Q^{\times2}$, since the odd valuations at the distinct $\ell_i$ recover all their exponents and the sign recovers the remaining factor $-1$. The conductors divide $4\prod_i\ell_i$, owing to the discriminant formula for quadratic fields. Thus the map from the unit group of that modulus onto $\{\pm1\}^{r+1}$ is surjective. Applying the prime number theorem in fixed arithmetic progressions to its equally sized fibers proves the density claim. Removing the finitely many divisors of $B$ changes no density.

For the quantitative assertion use a fixed nonnegative $C^2$ weight $w$ supported on $[1/2,1]$, positive on $(1/2,1)$, and write $I=\int w(t)\,dt>0$. Under Dirichlet GRH, uniformly for every product character $\chi$ above,
$$
\sum_{p\nmid D}\chi(p)\log p\,w(p/X)
  = {\bf1}_{\chi=1} I X + O_w(\sqrt X\log(2D)).
$$
To obtain this estimate, apply the smoothed explicit formula to the primitive character inducing $\chi$. The Mellin transform is $O_w((1+|\operatorname{Im}s|)^{-2})$ on the critical line, and the unit-interval zero count is $O(\log(2q_\chi(2+|\operatorname{Im}s|)))$ at conductor $q_\chi$. The zero sum is $O_w(\sqrt X\log(2q_\chi))$ and the principal pole contributes $IX$. The trivial zeros contribute $O_w(1)$, higher prime powers $O_w(\sqrt X)$, and dropping prime divisors of $D$ costs $O_w(\log(2D))$. Here each $q_\chi$ divides $4\prod_i\ell_i$. The implicit constants are independent of $r,F,B$.

Let $h$ be the indicator in the sign cube that the first sign is $-1$ and that the other signs satisfy $F$. Its Fourier expansion has constant coefficient $t/2^{r+1}$. By Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz the sum of the absolute values of all Fourier coefficients is at most $\sqrt t$. Applying the estimate character by character gives
$$
\sum_{p\nmid D} h(s(p))\log p\,w(p/X)
 = \frac{tIX}{2^{r+1}}+O_w(\sqrt{tX}\log(2D)).
$$
For sufficiently large effective absolute $C$ the main term is strictly greater than the error at every $X\ge C4^r\log^2(2D)/t$. Its positivity implies the asserted prime window. Applying the same argument to a single prescribed sign vector is the case $t=1$. Prescribing all $2^r$ assignments requires at least $2^r$ distinct primes, so an upper cutoff polynomial in $r$ and $\log D$ cannot guarantee every possible assignment for $B=1$. This counting observation does not assert an optimal exponent in the upper bound.

## Raw family and computational consequences

Take a deterministic binary encoding of the input 3-CNF formula. In both tensors include a separate identical header block of shape $1\times1\times (m+1)$, whose entries are $1$ followed by the $m$ encoding bits. Wrap the main tensor once more. The header has support dimensions $(1,1,1)$ over every field; after restricting the total tensor to its intrinsic supports, it is a single centroid block whose dimensions differ from those of the wrapped main block. Thus it adds no isomorphism condition. Carry the binary prime bound and the nonzero excluded integer as separate input fields. Recognize the raw family by reading the header, verifying the encoded formula and dimensions, regenerating the prescribed tensors, and checking their arrays. A claimed qualifying prime can be checked in polynomial bit time with deterministic primality testing, Euclid's algorithm, repeated squaring for the Legendre symbols, and Boolean evaluation from the validated header. Therefore the binary-bounded existence problem on this family is in NP.

From a nonempty 3-CNF instance with $r\ge1$, use $B=1$ and a binary bound dominating $C4^r\log^2(2D)$. It has $O(r+\log\log(2D))$ bits. The effective GRH estimate puts a qualifying successful prime below this bound whenever the formula is satisfiable, and the literal and gate correctness rules out every qualifying prime when it is not. This proves conditional NP-hardness at the sharper bound. Unconditionally, for any assignment the character independence gives a reduced residue class modulo $q=4\prod_i\ell_i$ yielding that assignment and $p\equiv3\bmod4$. Linnik's theorem bounds its least prime by $C_0 q^L$ for absolute effective constants $C_0,L$; this bound still has polynomial bit length. This gives the unconditional NP-hardness claim with $B=1$. Finally, a procedure computing the exact rational density of qualifying successful primes and multiplying by $2^{r+1}$ gives the number of satisfying assignments. The construction is a counting reduction. To remove the progression restriction from the density problem, before constructing the same tensors append the tautological clause $x_i\lor\neg x_i$ for every $i$. Width-two clauses are allowed and can also be padded by repeating a literal. At $p\equiv3\bmod4$ the appended clauses hold identically. At $p\equiv1\bmod4$, the two tests for each variable become identical, so the appended clauses force all Legendre symbols to be positive. For that one sign vector every literal of the original formula is true. The density over all good primes is therefore exactly $(t+1)/2^{r+1}$, and one subtraction recovers $t$.

The new combination is the literal-to-prime correspondence for one ordinary-tensor comparison, including the exact prime density, same-prime pattern guarantee with arbitrary finite exclusions, and binary-bounded search problem. The quadratic recoding, tensor gates, prime distribution theorem, and Fourier estimate are existing ingredients. The earlier MathProofs result encodes least extension degree over one fixed characteristic or over number fields. The present parameter is the characteristic of reduction. No publication-priority claim is made. The proof is a written argument, and no Lean formalization or kernel audit was run.

Primary references for analytic and algorithmic ingredients are [Bach and Sorenson, character-weighted prime sums](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1996-65-216/S0025-5718-96-00763-6/S0025-5718-96-00763-6.pdf), [Xylouris, Linnik's constant](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.2749), [Agrawal, Kayal and Saxena, PRIMES is in P](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781), and [Grochow and Qiao, tensor isomorphism completeness and change of fields](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110).

## Additional sufficient result for linked monomial tensors

Let $A\in\mathbb Z^{m\times n}$ have full row rank. Consider the torus equations $\prod_j x_j^{A_{ij}}=a_i$, with $a_i\in\mathbb Q^\times$. Suppose its positive Smith invariants are $2^{s_i}$ and $s=\max_i s_i$, and let $D$ be any even positive integer divisible by all primes dividing the reduced numerators and denominators of the $a_i$. Use the linked tensor construction below. For any $i$ with $s_i\ge3$, require $2^{s_i}\mid (U(v_2(a_1),\ldots,v_2(a_m))^\top)_i$, where $UAV$ is a Smith form. Under these valuation conditions and Dirichlet GRH, the rational tensors are equivalent if their reductions are equivalent at every prime $p\nmid D$ below $C4^s\log^2(2^{s+1}D)$. Rational equivalence always implies all the valuation conditions and all these modular equivalences. In particular, when all constants are odd rational units or all $s_i\le2$, the modular probes alone suffice.

For the arithmetic, Smith row and column operations become monomial group automorphisms and preserve feasibility over every field and reduction away from $D$. For each resulting equation $y_i^{2^{s_i}}=b_i$, if it fails over $\mathbb Q$, choose the largest $k<s_i$ for which $b_i=c^{2^k}$ with $c\in\mathbb Q^\times$. For $k\ge1$ take $c>0$. The quadratic character attached to $c$ is nontrivial on the class $1\bmod2^{k+1}$, in the sense that its restriction to the corresponding subgroup of units is nontrivial. For $k=0$ this follows from $c$ being a nonsquare. For $k=1$, a positive nonsquare can have no quadratic conductor dividing $4$. For $k\ge2$, the valuation assumption gives even $v_2(c)$, and the positive nonsquare $c$ then has an odd prime in its squarefree part, so its conductor is divisible by an odd prime. For each such failed row impose $p\equiv1\bmod2^{k+1}$ and $\chi_c(p)=-1$. The indicator has density $1/(2\varphi(2^{k+1}))$, a Dirichlet-character expansion of sum of absolute coefficients at most one, and primitive conductors dividing $2^{k+3}D$. The same smoothed estimate gives such a prime outside $D$ at $p\le C4^k\log^2(2^{k+1}D)$, after increasing the absolute constant. Now $c$ has odd exponent relative to a generator of $\mathbb F_p^\times$, so $b_i=c^{2^k}$ has exponent exactly divisible by $2^k$ in the sense of its exponent modulo $2^{k+1}$. It cannot be a $2^{s_i}$-th power. Thus the modular system also fails at this prime.

For the linked tensor version, allocate a coordinate to each original unit variable, its formal inverse when used, and each assignment or multiplication in square-and-multiply evaluation of the monomials. Use one split coordinate algebra with the nilpotent shift sending each basis idempotent to its successor and the final idempotent to zero, one module with a coordinate for each allocated variable, and the coordinate action. Any isomorphism fixes the coordinate algebra because it commutes with the shift. It scales each module coordinate separately. Use one bilinear map on the module whose output coordinates encode multiplications, so preserving the map forces each output multiplier to be the product of its two input multipliers. Each inverse pair gets a distinct multiplication-output coordinate, pinned to multiplier one. Introduce a pinned unit coordinate, and append a distinct terminal multiplication by that unit for every equation. A single map from the coordinate algebra to the module marks unit, inverse-check and terminal coordinates. It maps an associated basis idempotent to the basis vector on the source, and to the vector times the intended rational constant on the target, or zero when unmarked. Because the coordinate algebra is fixed, the marked map forces precisely the desired multipliers. Conversely a solution supplies every multiplier and the isomorphism. The signature is independent of $m$ and $n$. The earlier triangular tensor compiler applies to this fixed list of multilinear maps over each field. Its structure constants are the same after reduction at good primes, as in the prior pure-radical construction. The statement can also be used directly for simultaneous isomorphism of the listed maps. The polynomial side bound comes from binary addition chains. The prime schedule by enumeration is polynomial time whenever the largest Smith invariant has polynomial numerical size in the input bit length. The theorem concerns the supplied monomial description, and already admits a direct rational decision by Smith operations and power tests on gcd-free factorizations.

The valuation test is essential to a general theorem with only good odd primes. The equation $y^8=16$ is insoluble over $\mathbb Q$ by its $2$-adic valuation $4$. For every odd $p$, if $v_2(p-1)\le2$ then $16=2^4$ is already in the subgroup of eighth powers. Otherwise $p\equiv1\bmod8$, so $2$ is a square in $\mathbb F_p$ and $16$ is an eighth power there as well. This is the classical Wang example. Earlier MathProofs unitary descent considered analogous obstructions over norm-one tori. The present result gives a quantitative schedule with the restricted Dirichlet GRH input for split monomial systems over $\mathbb Q$; it does not address nonabelian Kummer constraints.
