# Anonymous joint prime counts for abelian multiplication tensors

Assume the Riemann hypothesis for primitive Dirichlet $L$-functions, including the primitive principal character of conductor one. Let $A$ and $B$ be nonzero finite étale $\mathbb Q$-algebras given by rational multiplication tables. Every field factor is assumed abelian over $\mathbb Q$. The tensor is the bilinear map of multiplication, with an independent invertible linear map permitted in each of the three roles.

Choose full-rank orders $\mathcal O_A,\mathcal O_B$ and put $D=|\operatorname{disc}\mathcal O_A\,\operatorname{disc}\mathcal O_B|$. For an input $A$ and a finite set $S$ of primes avoiding $D$, use the integer
$$
M_A(S)=\sum_{E\text{ a field factor of }A}[E: \mathbb Q]\prod_{p\in S}\mathbf 1_{\{p\text{ splits completely in }E\}}.
$$
Repeated field factors count with multiplicity. In particular $M_A(\varnothing)=\dim_{\mathbb Q}A$. Each answer is one integer and carries no component identifier.

**Theorem.** There is an absolute effective constant $C$. Write $\mathcal P=\{p\le C\log^2(2D):p\nmid D\}$. Suppose the Galois group of every field factor of $A$ and $B$ is generated by at most $r$ elements. Then their multiplication tensors are isomorphic over $\mathbb Q$ if and only if $M_A(S)=M_B(S)$ for every $S\subseteq\mathcal P$ with $|S|\le r$. There is also a deterministic adaptive comparison using only the integer answers $M_A,M_B$, at most $O(1+r|\mathcal P|N^2)$ queries of order at most $2r$, where $N=\dim A+\dim B$. The number of queries and all bit costs are polynomial, including when $r=\lfloor\log_2\max(\dim A,\dim B)\rfloor$ is chosen to cover every input under the abelian promise.

The required joint order is sharp. For each $r\ge1$ there are two nonisomorphic products of abelian degree-$2^r$ fields, all with Galois group $(C_2)^r$, with the same joint counts at every set of at most $r-1$ primes unramified in their common compositum.

## 1. The joint counts recover the underlying algebra

Tensor isomorphism of unital associative multiplication tables is equivalent to unital algebra isomorphism. For a transporter $R(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$, with $a=P(1)$ and $b=Q(1)$, one has $R(x)=P(x)b=aQ(x)$. Because all three maps are bijective, left multiplication by $a$ and right multiplication by $b$ are bijective, so both elements are units. The map $x\mapsto a^{-1}R(x)b^{-1}$ is multiplicative, preserves the unit and is bijective. The converse uses one algebra isomorphism in each role.

Put all field factors inside one algebraic closure and let $K$ be their compositum and $G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)$. An abelian field factor $E$ is determined within $K$ by its rational isomorphism type, since its normality forces any rational embedding of $E$ to have image $E$. Write $H_E=\operatorname{Gal}(K/E)$, $d_E=[E: \mathbb Q]$, and $U_E=H_E^\perp\le \widehat G$. Here $U_E$ consists of the complex characters trivial on $H_E$. It is the dual group of $G/H_E$, so it has order $d_E$ and can be generated by $r$ elements.

For a list of $r$ elements of $G$ define the difference of abstract joint counts
$$
F(g_1,\ldots,g_r)=\sum_E c_Ed_E\prod_{j=1}^r\mathbf1_{H_E}(g_j),
$$
where $c_E$ is multiplicity in $A$ minus multiplicity in $B$. Fourier expansion of a subgroup indicator gives $\mathbf1_{H_E}=d_E^{-1}\sum_{\chi\in U_E}\chi$. Consequently the coefficient of $(\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_r)$ in the expansion on $G^r$ is
$$
\sum_{E: \langle\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_r\rangle\le U_E} c_Ed_E^{1-r}.
$$
If some $c_E\ne0$, choose a maximal $U_E$ by inclusion among the nonzero differences. A generating tuple for this subgroup, padded by trivial characters, isolates the nonzero summand $c_Ed_E^{1-r}$. Thus $F$ is nonzero. This is a restricted version of the fixed-point information in the Burnside table of marks. The bound uses the generator ranks of the field factors rather than the rank of the compositum. [Bouc on Burnside marks](https://www.lamfa.u-picardie.fr/bouc/burnamscorr.pdf)

If $r=0$, all field factors are $\mathbb Q$ and the dimension alone suffices. In particular, for arbitrary products of cyclic fields, $r=1$. In that case $M_A(\{p\})$ is the number of degree-one factors of the single unmarked reduction $A\bmod p$. One-prime unmarked comparisons therefore suffice on this locus even if the common compositum is noncyclic.

## 2. Realizing a distinguishing tuple by small primes

Fix a nonnegative smooth function $w$ supported in $(1/2,1)$ with $I=\int_0^\infty w(t)\,dt>0$. The smoothed explicit formula gives the following estimate for every character $\eta$ of $G$, with primitive conductor $q_\eta$, and $x\ge4$:
$$
\sum_{p\nmid D}\eta(\operatorname{Frob}_p)\log p\,w(p/x)
=\mathbf1_{\{\eta=1\}}Ix+
O_w\bigl(\sqrt x\log(2q_\eta)+\log(2D)\bigr).
\tag{1}
$$
Indeed Kronecker–Weber realizes the character by a primitive Dirichlet character. Apply the explicit formula to its $\Lambda(n)$-weighted sum. The Mellin transform of $w$ decays at least quadratically on the critical line. The number of zeros in a unit ordinate interval is $O(\log(2q_\eta(2+|t|)))$, and each one contributes $x^{1/2}$ times its Mellin factor by Dirichlet GRH. The principal pole gives $Ix$. The trivial zeros have total $O_w(1)$ by the support $(1/2,1)$. Higher prime powers contribute $O_w(\sqrt x)$ by the Chebyshev bound. Dropping primes dividing $D$ costs at most $\|w\|_\infty\log D$. [Bach and Sorenson on weighted prime estimates](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-96-00763-6)

Fix all but one of the variables of $F$. The resulting section $h$ is a difference of the permutation characters of two subcollections of the original field factors. Thus
$$
h(g)=\sum_\chi a_\chi\chi(g),\qquad a_\chi\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
W_0:=\sum_{\chi\ne1}|a_\chi|\log q_\chi\le \log D.
\tag{2}
$$
For the last inequality use conductor–discriminant on each field factor, then the index-square formula for the given orders. The maximal-order factor discriminants multiply, and their product divides the order discriminant. Section selection simply drops factors, so the same original $D$ suffices for every section.

Let $L=\sum|a_\chi|$ and $M=\sum|a_\chi|^2$. By (1) applied to $\chi\overline\psi$ and $q_{\chi\overline\psi}\le q_\chi q_\psi$,
$$
\sum_{p\nmid D}|h(\operatorname{Frob}_p)|^2\log p\,w(p/x)
=IxM+O_w\bigl(\sqrt x(L^2+LW_0)+L^2\log(2D)\bigr).
\tag{3}
$$
One can bound the error relative to $M$ even if a section has very many trivial summands. Let $b=\#\{\chi\ne1:a_\chi\ne0\}$. Each nontrivial primitive character has conductor at least 3, so $b\le W_0/\log3$. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives $L^2\le(b+1)M$, and integrality gives $L\le M$. The relative error is therefore
$$
O_w\bigl(\sqrt x\log(2D)+\log^2(2D)\bigr).
$$
If $h$ is nonzero, choosing $x=C\log^2(2D)$ makes (3) positive for an absolute effective $C$. Some good prime below $x$ has $h(\operatorname{Frob}_p)\ne0$.

Start at any tuple where $F\ne0$, and replace the entries one at a time by Frobenius elements of good primes at most $x$. Each current one-variable section is nonzero, so (3) allows the next replacement to retain a nonzero value. After $r$ steps there is a tuple of actual primes with unequal counts. Repeated primes in the tuple can be dropped because each indicator is idempotent. This proves the stated cutoff and both directions of the equivalence.

## 3. Adaptive comparison with integer queries

For the proof of the query bound, let $\Omega$ be the disjoint union of the rational field-factor occurrences of both inputs. On functions on $\Omega$, use the positive definite inner product $\langle u,v\rangle=\sum_{E\in\Omega}d_Eu(E)v(E)$. Set $f_S(E)=\prod_{p\in S}\mathbf1_{\{p\text{ splits completely in }E\}}$. The algorithm stores a function $f_S$ by its set $S$, and recovers its inner products without seeing any component:
$$
\langle f_S,f_T\rangle=M_A(S\cup T)+M_B(S\cup T).
$$

Start with $f_\varnothing$. From a basis for the span of the $f_S$ of degree at most $j-1$, generate candidates by adjoining one prime to each basis set, then retain basis rows using Gram matrix rank. Adjoining a fixed prime multiplies by $f_{\{p\}}$, a linear map on the space of functions. Hence the candidates and the previous basis span all degrees at most $j$. Equality of $M_A$ and $M_B$ on the final basis forces equality on its whole span, since the difference is the linear functional that gives $E$ weight $d_E$ on the first input and weight $-d_E$ on the second. Every Gram query is of order at most $2r$. At most $N$ basis rows survive, and there are at most $rN|\mathcal P|$ candidates. Each independence test uses at most $N+1$ inner products and exact rational linear algebra on integer entries bounded by $N$. The algorithm may stop at any round whose span does not grow, since that span is then invariant under adjoining each prime.

For a multiplication-table implementation, construct the orders by clearing denominators, adjoining $1$, and taking Hermite normal form. The trace discriminants have polynomial bit length. Compute the primitive rational idempotents using rational étale decomposition. At $p\nmid D$ the idempotents are integral and remain nonzero and distinct, since passing to the product of projected orders has index prime to $p$. In each component test whether Frobenius $z\mapsto z^p$ is the identity on a basis of its reduction. The reduction is finite étale and Frobenius is the identity exactly for a product of copies of $\mathbb F_p$. This computes each aggregate answer without finite-field factorization. A group generated by a minimal list of $r$ elements has order at least $2^r$, by the chain of successively generated subgroups, so the permitted choice of $r$ is $O(\log N)$. The algorithm does not construct $K$, $G$, or their characters. [Lenstra and Silverberg on rational algebra decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.08843)

## 4. Sharpness with field factors of identical degree

Take a multiquadratic extension $K/\mathbb Q$ generated by square roots of $2r$ different rational primes, so $\widehat G\simeq\mathbb F_2^{2r}$. Write $\widehat G=X\oplus X$ with $\dim_{\mathbb F_2}X=r$. Each graph $U_L=\{(x,Lx):x\in X\}$ of an endomorphism $L$ of $X$ is an $r$-dimensional subgroup. Attach the field $E_L=K^{U_L^\perp}$, of degree $2^r$, where the orthogonal subgroup is taken under the character pairing. Let $A$ be the product of the fields for which $\operatorname{tr}L=0$, and $B$ the product for which $\operatorname{tr}L=1$. Both products are nonempty, and their constituent fields are different since different graphs have different annihilators.

For every subgroup $W\le X\oplus X$ of dimension at most $r-1$, the numbers of graphs from the two families containing $W$ agree. If the first projection is not injective on $W$, both numbers are zero. Otherwise $W$ is the graph of a map on a proper subspace $X_0<X$. Choose a nonzero functional $\ell$ vanishing on $X_0$ and a vector $u$ satisfying $\ell(u)=1$. The map $L\mapsto L+u\ell$ preserves the prescribed restriction to $X_0$ and switches the trace, since $\operatorname{tr}(u\ell)=\ell(u)=1$. This supplies the bijection. Fourier expansion in any $j\le r-1$ variables now gives equal coefficients in every position. The subgroup generated by $j$ characters has dimension at most $r-1$, and all constituent degrees, hence all the weights $d_E^{1-j}$, are equal. The joint counts agree for every tuple of group elements and therefore for every set of at most $r-1$ good primes. Distinct normal subfields are nonisomorphic, so the two products and their tensors are nonisomorphic. A distinguishing tuple of order $r$ in $G$ consists of the elements of $G$ dual to a basis of the second copy of $X$. The only graph annihilated by every one of them is the graph of the zero map, which lies in the trace-zero family.

Exact finite calculations checked the two trace families for $r=1,2,3$ against respectively 1, 17, and 2,081 sets of group elements below the cutoff order. The first distinguishing tuple at order $r$ had unweighted counts 1 and 0 in all three cases. Exact Gram-based comparison independently found a discrepancy at order $r$ for $r=1,2$.

## Scope and verification

The theorem concerns multiplication tensors of abelian rational étale algebras. For singleton prime sets the output is ordinary unmarked modular fixed-point data. Higher joint counts correlate different prime reductions inside each input and return one scalar for that correlation. The algorithms for rational étale decomposition and Galois descent are established ingredients. The contribution here is the generator-rank criterion for anonymous joint counts, the matching sharpness examples, and the polynomial aggregate-query comparison. The small-prime bound uses the Dirichlet GRH consequence for primitive character sums. The Fourier reconstruction, query argument and sharpness do not require that hypothesis. The proof is in writing.
