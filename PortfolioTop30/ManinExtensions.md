# Four extensions of Manin’s bilinear undecidability theorem

These are four written proofs. Throughout, a **raw system** for a fixed bilinear map $B:U\times V\to W$ consists solely of equations $\sum_{ij}a_{ij}B(u_i,v_j)=c$. The unknowns are elements of the two input modules, the coefficients are base-ring scalars, and the right sides are specified target elements. A finite presentation and effective encodings are fixed for each ring and module. Over the integers the starting theorem supplies integral frames and a compiler for arithmetic in the adjoint center. [Starting proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/97cab48ab2ca92c0ba830a5573041cde985fef27/BilinearDiophantineUndecidability/PROOF.md) · [Manin, §§7–8](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.10632#S7)

## 1. The finite-image dichotomy

**Theorem.** Let $R$ be any commutative unital algebra finitely generated over $\mathbb Z$, let $U,V,W$ be finitely generated $R$-modules, and fix an $R$-bilinear $B$. Put $I=\langle B(U,V)\rangle_R$. Raw solvability is decidable exactly when $I$ is a finite set. The criterion includes finite characteristic, modules supported entirely on a proper closed subset, and nonreduced base rings. It is a classification of the decision problem for each fixed map.

**Finite case.** Testing a left input against a fixed generating tuple of $V$ distinguishes its class modulo the left radical, embedding the quotient set into a finite power of $I$. The right quotient is finite by the same argument. Fix representatives and the finite pairing table. A decision algorithm enumerates assignments in these finite sets and compares the computed right sides in $W$. Word equality is effective in finitely presented modules over finite-type commutative $\mathbb Z$-algebras, by polynomial-module membership over $\mathbb Z$. The Noetherian hypothesis supplies finite presentations.

**Domain case.** First let $R$ be an infinite domain, $W$ torsion-free and $B\ne0$. Quotient both sources by their radicals, which preserves the solution predicate by lifting representatives. The quotient sources are torsion-free and the map over $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ has zero radicals. Choose module generating lists $e_i,f_j$, allowing relations. Impose the frame equations $B(a_i,b_j)=B(e_i,f_j)$.

Flatten this array by rows over $K$. Its rank as a list of vectors in $(W_K)^{\#j}$ is $\dim U_K$, by nondegeneracy and generation. The same list is obtained by evaluating $a_i$ against the $b_j$, so the $a_i$ span $U_K$. The column argument gives the corresponding spanning result for $b_j$. Any relation on the $e_i$ gives the same relation on the $a_i$ by testing against all $b_j$, and conversely for the right inputs. Thus the frames define endomorphisms $A,D$ of the actual modules, invertible over $K$, preserving $B$.

Write $\Phi(u)=B(u,-)$. Choose a $K$-linear left inverse $L$ of $\Phi_K$. The module $\operatorname{Hom}_R(V,W)$ is finitely generated because $R$ is Noetherian and $V,W$ are finitely generated. Clear denominators on a finite generating list to obtain a single $c\ne0$ with $cL(\operatorname{Hom}_R(V,W))\subset U$. For every $k\ge0$, $\Phi(A^{-k}u)=\Phi(u)\circ D^k$. Consequently $U\subset A^{-1}U\subset A^{-2}U\subset\cdots\subset c^{-1}U$. The containing module is Noetherian, so successive terms agree for some $k$. Applying a power of $A$ gives $A^{-1}U=U$. The symmetric argument works for $D$. All frames can therefore be normalized simultaneously inside the original modules.

The adjoints are the pairs $(P,Q)$ satisfying $B(Pu,v)=B(u,Qv)$, with reversed multiplication in the second coordinate. Both projections are injective. The adjoint algebra and its center $C$ are finite $R$-modules as submodules of finite endomorphism modules. Scalar multiplication embeds $R$ in $C$ because a nonzero torsion-free source is faithful. Thus $C$ is an infinite commutative ring finitely generated over $\mathbb Z$, and Koymans–Pagano, Theorem 1.1, applies. To compile a center variable, store the images of the generators of both source modules. Test adjoint membership on all pairs of generators. These tests force all module relations on the proposed images, by nondegeneracy. Test commutation against an $R$-generating list of the adjoints, using its fixed columns in the frame. More precisely the tests for a generator $(E,F)$ read $B(Pe_i,Ff_j)=B(Ee_i,Qf_j)$. For multiplication $XY=Z$ use $B(P_Ye_i,Q_Xf_j)=B(P_Ze_i,f_j)$. Addition and constants use linear combinations of columns. Bilinearity expands every test into raw syntax. Coefficients of a polynomial instance over $C$ can be expressed in fixed module generators, or compiled from a fixed ring generating list. A normalized solution decodes back to a center solution. [Koymans–Pagano, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01768v3#S1.SS2)

**Reduction to the domain case.** Suppose $I$ is infinite. Some minimal prime $\mathfrak p$ of its support has $R/\mathfrak p$ infinite. Otherwise all minimal primes in the support are maximal with finite residue fields, and a finite prime filtration of $I$ has only finite successive factors. Now $I_{\mathfrak p}$ is nonzero of finite length. Take the largest $k$ such that $(\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p})^k I_{\mathfrak p}\ne0$. Some $u\in\mathfrak p^k$ acts nontrivially there. The finite module $\mathfrak p uI$ vanishes at $\mathfrak p$, so some $s\notin\mathfrak p$ kills it. Hence $M=suI$ is a module over $R/\mathfrak p$ with nonzero generic fiber. Kill its torsion submodule by multiplying by one $s'\notin\mathfrak p$, possible by finite generation. The image $tI=s'M$, with $t=s'su$, is nonzero and torsion-free over $R/\mathfrak p$. Indeed, if a nonzero scalar kills $s'm$, then $m$ is torsion and $s'm=0$.

The nonzero pairing $tB$ descends to $U/\mathfrak p U$ and $V/\mathfrak p V$ with values in $tI$. The domain case gives undecidability. Lift coefficients of that system to $R$, multiply each by $t$, and use the given targets in $tI\subset W$. Surjectivity on both source quotients gives equivalence. All choices are fixed finite data for the fixed $B$. For example this detects the infinite image in $W=\mathbb F_p[x,y]/(x)$ even though $W$ is a torsion module over $\mathbb F_p[x,y]$.

**Prior work.** Garreta–Miasnikov–Ovchinnikov interpret a commutative algebra in an arbitrary bilinear map in a multisorted module language and derive broad Diophantine consequences. The additional assertion proved here concerns the coefficient-only language itself. The prime-support reduction handles images that vanish upon passage to the generic fiber of the original ring. [Prior Theorem 3.10 and Lemma 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.02573#page=18)

## A common arithmetic input for Theorems 2–4

Fix a nonzero integral bilinear $B$ on finite free lattices. Use the radical quotients and the normalized frames of the starting proof. Write $C$ for the center of the integral adjoints. It is a nonzero finite free abelian ring. In particular $C_{\mathbb Q}$ is a finite-dimensional commutative algebra. Choose any residue field $K$ of this rational algebra. The image of $C$ in $K$ is an order, since its elements are integral and its rational span is $K$.

There is a fixed existential conjunction of polynomial equations over $C$ that recognizes any prescribed computably enumerable integer relation on the residue-field images of chosen center inputs. To see this, regard the number ring $\mathcal O_K$ as a finite $C$-module through $C\to\mathcal O_K$. A $\mathbb Z$-basis and $1$ generate it as a $C$-module. Present it as $C^m/L$ with $1$ first on the list. The relation module $L$ is finitely generated. Equality of representatives is expressed by existential linear combination of these relations. Addition is coordinatewise and multiplication uses a fixed table on generators. An input $T\in C$ represents its image via $(T,0,\ldots,0)$. Koymans–Pagano, Theorem 1.2, defines the ordinary integers existentially in $\mathcal O_K$. Apply this definition to all integer variables of a DPRM representation of the chosen relation, using four squares for nonnegativity. This produces the asserted fixed conjunction and ensures the recognized inputs themselves have the required nonnegative integer images. Only the fixed predicate is compiled, regardless of the size of the integer parameter. [Koymans–Pagano, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01768v3#S1.SS2) · [Jones, Universal Diophantine Equation](https://doi.org/10.2307/2273588)

## 2. Every computably enumerable set as boundary extension degrees

**Theorem.** For every fixed simply connected finite complex $Y$ outside rational H-space type there is one fixed finite CW pair $(X,A)$, with $\dim X=d+1$, where $d$ is the first failing Postnikov degree. Two distinguished $d$-sphere summands of $A$, one infinite-order class $\gamma\in\pi_d(Y)$, and one fixed map on the remaining summands suffice. For $e,n\ge0$, give the distinguished spheres classes $e\gamma,n\gamma$. This boundary map extends to $X$ exactly when $n\in W_e$, for a standard universal enumeration of computably enumerable sets.

**Read an integer through one right side.** Take Manin’s specific nonzero $B$ from his Theorem 8.1. Choose a minimal nonzero ideal of $C_{\mathbb Q}$ and a nonzero element $s$ in it, clearing denominators so $s\in C$. Its annihilator in $C_{\mathbb Q}$ is maximal, since the ideal generated by $s$ is a simple module. Use the associated residue field in the common arithmetic construction. For $T\in C$, its field image equals the integer $j$ exactly when $Ts=js$.

Take center inputs $T_1,T_2$ and apply the common predicate for the universal relation. It forces their images to be integers $e',n'$ satisfying $n'\in W_{e'}$. Add fixed product gates for $Z_i=T_i s$. Faithfulness of the left adjoint action and zero radicals give some frame indices $a,b$ with $\delta=B(P_s e_a,f_b)\ne0$. Impose just one further equation per parameter, $B(P_{Z_1}e_a,f_b)=e\delta$ and $B(P_{Z_2}e_a,f_b)=n\delta$. Since $Z_1=e's$ and $Z_2=n's$, torsion-freeness gives $e=e'$ and $n=n'$. Conversely, use the integer scalars themselves as inputs when $n\in W_e$. Thus both coefficient arrays, the number of equations, and every other right side are fixed.

**Fix the pair.** In Manin’s explicit construction $A$ is a wedge with one $d$-sphere for each equation, together with the fixed lower-dimensional sphere factors. The attaching maps for $X$ depend on the coefficient arrays. Each right side is carried solely by the map on its corresponding $d$-sphere. The fixed targets here are integral combinations of pairings and hence have integral homotopy lifts by Manin’s construction. Choose one lift $\gamma$ of $\delta$. Its rational class is nonzero. Use degree maps followed by its fixed representative on the two varying spheres. Manin’s fixed torsion exponent makes the top-cell extension equations independent of lift choices. This proves the equivalence and fixes the underlying pair for every $e,n$. The degree maps are effective, with subdivisions allowed when using simplicial encodings. The cofibration property preserves extendability when a representative is changed by a homotopy. [Manin’s attaching maps, §8](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.10632#S8)

**Consequence.** With $e$ fixed at an index for a complete set, one varying sphere suffices for halting completeness. Equality of two degree sets is $\Pi^0_2$-complete, as is deciding whether one degree set is all of $\mathbb N$. For the upper bounds express inclusion using entry stages in the two enumerations. For hardness, uniformly enumerate $n$ when the first $n+1$ computations of a given machine all halt. Fullness of that set is totality, and equality to the fixed full section is equivalent to fullness. Earlier fixed-source and fixed-target undecidability results precede this work. The scope here is the universal enumeration on two degree coordinates for every target in Manin’s negative class. [Čadek et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.2370)

## 3. Two pairing terms on a path suffice

**Theorem.** For each fixed nonzero integral $B$ there are two fixed templates for the binary digits, fixed end templates, and a fixed finite set of right sides, such that a digit chain has an integral solution exactly when the machine with that binary index halts. Every equation uses at most two pairing terms with coefficients $\pm1$. Variable occurrence and the pathwidth of the primal graph are bounded by constants depending only on $B$ and the chosen universal machine. The number of variables and equations is linear in the length of the binary representation. For scalar multiplication with right sides $0,1$, the one-term problem is decidable in linear time in the explicit graph representation.

**Eliminate addition gates.** In any commutative unital ring put
$$
P_z(x,y):\quad (zx+1)(zy+1)=z^2(xy+1)+1.
$$
The difference of the two sides is $z(x+y-z)$. Imposing also $P_{z+1}(x+1,y)$ gives $(z+1)(x+y-z)=0$. Subtraction forces $x+y=z$ even with zero divisors. The converse follows by the same identities. Hence all addition in the fixed center circuits can be implemented by multiplication, successor, and equality. Using intermediate variables, each of these gates costs at most two raw pairing terms per equation. For successor compare the left columns of its output and input against each right frame vector, setting the difference equal to $B(e_i,f_j)$. For multiplication use the two-term equation of Theorem 1. Equality, adjoint membership, and commutation use two terms as well. Any fixed integer linear combination of frame vectors required in a test can be named using successive basis increments or decrements checked against the opposite frame, with the orientation chosen so that the right side is $0$ or $B(e_i,f_j)$. Keep separate copies linked by equality whenever two formal terms would coincide. This controls the literal coefficient on each pairing. The classical Tarski–Robinson identity motivates the first test. The second test removes the need to cancel a possibly singular element. [Restricted successor and product arithmetic](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.3826)

**The chain.** A template has its own pair of frames, normalized by the fixed array $c_{ij}=B(e_i,f_j)$. Between successive templates impose $B(a_i^h,b_j^{h+1})=c_{ij}$. In the radical quotients, the left frame in cell $h$ spans. Subtracting its own frame equations forces the adjacent right frames to agree, and then the adjacent left frames agree by testing against that common right frame. Thus one global normalization decodes every cell. Each interface carries the generator columns of a center variable. Equate left columns in adjacent cells by testing against the common opposite frame. Initialize at zero. In the tile for digit $b$ compute $T_{\rm out}=T_{\rm in}+T_{\rm in}+b$ by the fixed gates above. The terminal template evaluates the fixed halting predicate of the common arithmetic construction on the final state. This gives both directions, since the terminal center parameter is the actual integer scalar determined by the chain. Lifts from radical quotients to the original lattices exist independently for every variable. The only target values are zero and the finitely many $c_{ij}$.

For the primal graph take bags consisting of two adjacent complete templates, including the end caps as templates. All equations are internal to one bag and the bags containing a given variable are consecutive. The finite templates also bound the number of occurrences of every variable. For the lower boundary take $B(x,y)=xy$ with the unit term coefficients allowed above. A nonzero edge forces both endpoints to be signs and prescribes a parity relation. Check consistency on each component of these edges. A zero edge with two forced endpoints is impossible, and all remaining unforced vertices may be assigned zero. This proves the claimed sharpness for a uniform bound applying to every fixed nonzero $B$.

## 4. Integral halting completeness with two supplied rational solutions

**Theorem.** For each fixed nonzero integral $B$, one fixed array of coefficients with right sides affine in $t$ yields systems integrally solvable exactly when machine $t$ halts. For every $t$ the reduction also outputs a solution in $(\tfrac12\mathbb Z)^N$ and another in $(\tfrac13\mathbb Z)^N$ in the original scalar coordinates. In particular every instance has real points, integral points over every $\mathbb Z_p$, and points modulo every positive integer. Here $N$ is fixed. Supplying both witnesses as part of the input preserves halting completeness on this family.

**Proof.** Write the common fixed predicate as $f_j(T,X)=0$ over the center $C$. Set $T=t1_C$ using column tests against the frame with right sides $t c_{ij}$. Introduce $u,v\in C$. For every $j$ replace the required vanishing at the output by
$$
(2u-1)(3v-1) f_j(T,X)=0.
$$
Multiplication by $ku-1$ on the finite free abelian group $C$ has matrix $kM_u-I$, with determinant congruent to $(-1)^{\operatorname{rank} C}$ modulo $k$. For $k=2,3$ that determinant is nonzero, so both factors are non-zero-divisors at every integral assignment. The masked system has precisely the original integral existence predicate. Compile polynomial evaluations and final multiplications as fixed arithmetic circuits via the starting proof.

Select the two rational assignments $(X,u,v)=(0,1/2,0)$ and $(0,0,1/3)$, where scalars act by the identity. On either assignment the mask vanishes. To preserve the claimed denominators in every gate, compute the integer multiples by successive additions, subtract one, multiply the two resulting integral factors, and only then multiply by the already evaluated residual. Each original residual evaluation uses integer center elements for the chosen $T,X$. Intermediate multiples of the selected fractional scalar have denominator dividing the same 2 or 3. Use the original addition compiler for this theorem. With the standard integral frames, every gate is encoded by its adjoint columns, and integral splittings of the free radical quotients preserve those common denominators upon lifting. The two displayed choices compute all coordinates by fixed polynomials in $t$ with the indicated common denominators. They work over $\mathbb Z[1/2]$ and $\mathbb Z[1/3]$ as polynomial sections. For a prime $p$ choose a denominator prime to $p$. For composite moduli combine the prime-power solutions by the Chinese remainder theorem. Enumeration of the integral vectors gives the computably enumerable upper bound. The project's earlier local-promise construction concerns rational matrix completion and uses an intersective polynomial. Here a pair of explicit rational sections with fixed coprime denominators works for every fixed integral pairing. [Prior completion construction, §5](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/97cab48ab2ca92c0ba830a5573041cde985fef27/RankTwoGeometricUniversality/PROOF.md)

## Verification and attribution

The four main theorems have written arguments. Their novelty assessments concern the precise restrictions and quantifiers proved above. Priority remains provisional. None of the arguments uses Dirichlet GRH.

Lean 4.33.0 checked the reverse implication in the two-shift identity used in Theorem 3. Its only reported axiom is `propext`. The exact checked source follows.

```lean
import Mathlib.Tactic.LinearCombination

theorem twoShiftAddition {R : Type*} [CommRing R] (x y z : R)
    (h1 : (z*x+1)*(z*y+1) = z^2*(x*y+1)+1)
    (h2 : ((z+1)*(x+1)+1)*((z+1)*y+1) = (z+1)^2*((x+1)*y+1)+1) : x+y=z := by
    linear_combination h2 - h1
```
