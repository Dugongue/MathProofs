# Proof and novelty audit for the MathProofs top 10

The scope is the ten entries of the previous ranking at [MathProofs commit 97cab48](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/97cab48ab2ca92c0ba830a5573041cde985fef27). ## Manin’s bilinear conjecture

**Verdict.** The written proof of Conjecture 7.2 holds up. For each fixed nonzero bilinear map of finite free abelian groups, it gives a many-one reduction from a fixed infinite commutative ring’s Diophantine problem to Manin’s coefficient-only bilinear systems.

- **Integral normalization.** The two radicals are kernels of maps to torsion-free groups, so passing to the quotient preserves free lattices and existence of solutions. Frame equations force A and D to be invertible over the rationals. For the injection Φ from the left lattice into the group of linear maps on the right lattice, Φ(A⁻ᵏu) is Φ(u) composed with Dᵏ. A single rational left inverse to Φ supplies a denominator bound independent of k. Since the determinant of an integral matrix is integral, this forces |det A| = 1. Interchanging sides proves |det D| = 1. The normalizing inverses therefore preserve the integer search domain.
- **Ring and gates.** The zero radicals make both projections of the adjoint ring injective. Commuting against an additive basis tests membership in its center. Pairing constraints recover both that test and the multiplication of left operators in the correct order, with every fixed input column supplied by an integral frame combination. The center is unital and finitely generated over the integers, and its identity has infinite additive order. These are the hypotheses of the unconditional Koymans–Pagano theorem.
- **Credit and formal boundary.** Manin already gave scalar-frame normalization and the topological reduction. Garreta–Miasnikov–Ovchinnikov already developed ring interpretations in a richer multisorted bilinear language. The contribution here is the all-output-ranks integral normalization and explicit simulation in the exact coefficient-only language. Applying Manin’s Theorem 8.1 gives the negative side of the fixed simply connected finite-target extension dichotomy. An existing local kernel replay records only the standard axioms for the Lean reduction with its number-ring undecidability premise. The log has no source hash. The separate provisional corollary uses a visible `sorry`. No earlier proof of the same conjecture was located in the targeted search. ## The missing complex Hanner inequality

**Verdict.** The written proof holds up for independent uniform complex phases, every finite family, and $1<p<2$. The audit also checked the $p=1$ endpoint.

Write $a_i=x_i^{1/p}$, $S=\sum_i a_i\xi_i$, $T=\varepsilon+|S|^2$, $v_i=\operatorname{Im}(\overline S\xi_i)$, and $C_{ij}=\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\xi_i}\xi_j)$. If $D=\operatorname{diag}(a_i^{p-1})$, the recomputed identity is
$$
D\nabla^2\mathbb E[T^{p/2}]D
=(2-p)\mathbb E\!\left[T^{(p-4)/2}
\left(vv^\mathsf{T}+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}C\right)\right].
$$
The angular derivatives have signs $\partial_{\theta_j}T=-2a_jv_j$ and $\partial_{\theta_j}v_i=-a_j C_{ij}$ for $i\ne j$. The diagonal uses $\partial_{\theta_i}v_i=\operatorname{Re}(\overline S\xi_i)-a_i$, which supplies the derivative of the amplitude factor as well. The two sides yield the common coefficient $1-(p-1)^2=p(2-p)$ before division by $p$. Both $vv^\mathsf{T}$ and the phase Gram matrix $C$ are positive semidefinite. Differentiation takes place at $\varepsilon>0$, where there is no singularity. Pointwise limits preserve convexity, continuity adds zero coefficients, and phase invariance followed by Jensen gives the asserted integral inequality.

Jenkins–Tkocz already gave the convexity/Jensen reduction and settled the Steinhaus direction for $p\ge2$. Their published §3.2 explicitly asks for the reverse direction in $1<p<2$. The new step is the regularized positive matrix identity, which groups the angular terms together. A search of the published article, its authors’ bibliographies, and related follow-up preprints found no earlier proof of this range. The evidence is a written derivation. ## The published Inversion Rule

The block-matrix calculation checks directly. With $fg=1$, where $f$ is the row of axioms, take $A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\-g&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&f\\g&I+gf\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Then $AB-I$ contains only the premises and zero, and the top-left entry of $BA-I$ is $-1$. A certificate found over the algebraic closure descends to the coefficient field by solving its finite coefficient system over that field. Sections 2.1 and 2.3 of the published paper allow arbitrary polynomial substitutions, a single reverse entry, and measure the degree of proof lines. This gives the advertised counterexample under the literal convention. The certificate can be arbitrarily costly in an encoded rule annotation. ## Smooth affine hypersurfaces and rational-point decision

The Faltings argument checks. The square-class choice excludes every nonzero rational first coordinate of the fixed genus-two curve. The auxiliary nonsquare norm equation preserves the zero predicate and makes the map to that curve nonconstant. Both irreducibility and smoothness are geometric. A factorization of the monic quadratic would produce a nonconstant rational map from affine space to a positive-genus curve, which restricts nontrivially to a general line and contradicts Riemann–Hurwitz. The singular values of the scaling parameter form a finite set because a polynomial in characteristic zero is constant on each component of its critical locus. Effective Jacobian testing finds a smooth choice. The field-dependent square class is hardcoded for the per-field many-one statement. The uniform Turing variant obtains it by asking the promised oracle about smooth square-class curves. The separate rational degree-eight construction uses the elliptic curve $y^2=z^3+z$, with its rational-point input justified by Fermat’s descent.

Poonen’s 2009 question explicitly distinguishes smooth affine hypersurfaces from the already equivalent classes of arbitrary affine hypersurfaces and smooth projective varieties. The new result is the smooth affine hypersurface equivalence, with the degree-eight restriction over $\mathbb Q$. ## Exact entanglement in a fixed commuting matrix-product family

The reduction checks as written. The shifted integer square makes the sign test strict and permits a uniform exponentially small perturbation of the separable boundary. The two-qubit blocks are full rank and have a negative partial transpose exactly on witness words. Conditioning on a flag string is local to the opposite side of the first-qubit cut, so a bad block certifies entanglement of the full state. Averaging over one erased independent Pauli key completely depolarizes the right qubit. This proves all proper marginals are maximally mixed. The monomial-translation matrices commute, and their direct sum with the commuting key shift reproduces the block coefficients while only the input boundary varies.

Earlier work establishes undecidable unbounded-length positivity and consequences for uniform purification. Here every state is already positive definite, every proper marginal is maximally mixed, and the undecidable property is exact entanglement at some finite length. The fixed commuting bulk with a varying boundary is the strongest distinction. ## Binary rank-two matrix completion

The scheme-level argument checks. A fixed identity block identifies the rank-two determinantal equations with $D=AB$ over arbitrary commutative coefficient algebras. Each tagged scalar is made a unit by an explicit inverse pairing. The multiplication and addition gadgets have unique auxiliary coordinates after those inversions. The split variables and shifted partial sums contribute only free coordinates and localization. This preserves nilpotents, and the projection is smooth and surjective because the finitely many forbidden values in each lift can be avoided over every characteristic-zero field. Rational constants may be compiled with binary doubling and unit inversion.

Shitov’s earlier results give different matrix universality statements, including nonnegative-factorization spaces and ordinary rank-three completion feasibility. The new combination is binary prescribed entries, ordinary rank two, and an open-product description as schemes. ## Equiangular tight frames at every marginal purity

The construction checks. With $\omega$ primitive of order $d^2+1$, the $j$-th frame vector has $(a,b)$-entry $d^{-1}e^{2\pi i t ab/d}\omega^{(da+b)j}$. The truncated Fourier columns give unit norm, overlap $-1/d^2$ for distinct columns, and the claimed tight frame operator. Varying $j$ acts by local diagonal unitaries, so both marginal purities are independent of $j$. The endpoints of the continuous seed family are a rank-one seed and a scaled unitary Fourier matrix. Continuity supplies every marginal purity in $[1/d,1]$.

The public Lean blob matches the SHA-256 of the exact source in an existing Lean 4.33.0 audit of both roots, with only the three standard axioms. The source was hashed against that receipt during this review. The known inputs are harmonic simplex frames and phase invariance. Wei–Cobucci–Tavakoli left existence of the maximally entangled $d^2+1$-outcome frame open and described qubit entanglement tuning numerically. The explicit seed path and all-dimensional theorem are the additional contribution. ## Determinant partition rank, promoted from rank 11

The Pfaffian expansion supplies $n-1$ terms in every even size $n\ge 4$, including characteristic two by the universal integral polynomial identity. A short decomposition would give a nowhere-vanishing section of a direct sum of line bundles on the ordered column-frame variety. The flag-bundle computation identifies its integral Chow ring with the type-A coinvariant ring. Substituting the $n$-th roots of unity times $t$ into that ring modulo $t^n$ tests the resulting Chern product. The one-swap count bounds the probability that an index subset gives a vanishing linear factor. A union bound keeps every factor nonzero for fewer than $n-1$ terms in even size and fewer than $n$ terms in odd size. Thus the exact parity formula follows in every characteristic.

Lampert–Moshkovitz proved the four-column case, and Chen–Zhao proved the largest-prime lower bound by an intersection-theoretic approach. The column-wise Chern obstruction and the argument that makes it sharp in all sizes are the additional contribution. ## Exact matrix and diagram proof degrees

The main written arguments survived checks of the conjugacy multiplicity formula, the odd- and characteristic-two congruence flags, the stable-range degree lower bound, and the finite-field algorithms on unsplit center sectors. One-sided rank-gap-one projection systems have degree $r+2$ and sparse size $n^{\Theta(n)}$. With both inverse directions, the same conjugacy and congruence instances have degree two and three, respectively, and polynomial sparse size. ## Arbitrary Cayley-table groups in deterministic logspace

The decision and search arguments survived focused checks of the chief series, small-field rank, crown enumeration, and workspace composition. The leading rank coefficient in the determinant test survives in every characteristic because the row and column matroids each pick a unique minimum-weight basis. In the nonabelian case, the first nontrivial chief image and faithfulness on the monolith identify the crown-coordinate kernels. Lucchini–Thakkar’s Lemma 5 supports the fixed-suffix lifting count. The component and pair tests distinguish a full product from diagonal subproducts. The quotient recursion retains residual generator ordinals, and the logarithms of the successive kernel sizes sum to at most the logarithm of the input group order. One compressed step in the solvable-radical write-up recovers a basis coefficient by scanning candidate scalars and testing the residual in the span of the other basis columns. The companion solvable proof spells out this scan.

The advance is deterministic logarithmic workspace for the minimum number and the minimum witness in the arbitrary-group Cayley-table model. Lucchini–Thakkar gave polynomial time. Collins–Grochow–Levet–Weiß give prior low-depth bounds and ask for deterministic logspace in their Question 8.1. Lean evidence concerns greedy generator replay. This audit gives qualified support to the much longer written minimum-search proof. ## Finite-field orthogonal and unitary tensor algorithms

The Lean root is a fixed coin-machine statement with positive dimensions, input representation and field quantifiers, a deterministic invariant good-source recognizer, correctness for every finite halting run, and an expected polynomial-time bound. The public source exports the root. The repository reports an October 4 kernel replay of 35,192 constants using the standard axioms. This audit inspected the statement and receipt.

The version 1 paper asked for both orthogonal and unitary algorithms. Version 2, posted November 3, adds an odd-characteristic cubic orthogonal theorem and renumbers Problem 1.8. Its Theorem 5.6 has a stronger success-rate guarantee on the overlapping cubic case, polynomial worst-case time with a safe failure option, and lists all isomorphisms on success. The distinct scope here is the unitary case, the characteristic-two standard bilinear dot-product case, arbitrary rectangular formats, and deterministic invariant good-input certification. The dot-product theorem covers the stated standard form, including one orthogonal type in odd characteristic and even dimension. The repository replay date is evidence of a reported internal run. Public priority in the overlap is unverified.
