# OEIS A065255 has density one tenth

Write $b(k)$ for the increasing enumeration of the positive integers $m$ satisfying $\sigma(m)\equiv m\pmod{10}$. Then $b(k)/k\to10$. This is a readable proof of the density argument accompanying the formal work. [Sequence statement](https://oeis.org/A065255).

If a prime $p\equiv9\pmod{10}$ has valuation exactly one in $m$, the multiplicativity of $\sigma$ gives $p+1\mid\sigma(m)$, so $10\mid\sigma(m)$.

For any finite set $S$ of such primes, the Chinese remainder theorem gives natural density $\prod_{p\in S}(1-1/p+1/p^2)$ for the integers $m$ with no valuation-one prime in $S$. Indeed for each $p$ the valuation-one residue classes modulo $p^2$ number $p-1$. The reciprocals of primes in the reduced progression $9\bmod10$ diverge, so these finite products tend to zero as $S$ exhausts the progression. Thus the exceptional $m$ have upper density zero. Outside the exceptional set, the defining condition for $b$ is equivalent to $10\mid m$. Its counting function is therefore asymptotic to $x/10$, and inversion along the increasing sequence yields the claim.
