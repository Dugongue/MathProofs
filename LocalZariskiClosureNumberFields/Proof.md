# Local Zariski closures and simultaneous recurrence of rational points

This note gives informal deductions for number-field curves and closed subvarieties of abelian varieties. The local Zariski-closure question is Mazur’s Question I, reproduced for one place as Pasten’s Question 5.7 and for finitely many places as Poonen–Shlapentokh’s Question 1.4. The diophantine-approximation statement concerns the curve-source case of Pasten’s Conjecture 5.24. [Mazur’s chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/galois-representations-in-arithmetic-algebraic-geometry/open-problems-regarding-rational-points-on-curves-and-varieties/B5A1A706B111DA866DB025147BACA06E), [Pasten, §§5.2 and 5.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.14054), [Poonen–Shlapentokh, §1](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/bigsubrings.pdf).

Let $K$ be a number field. For any nonempty set $S$ of places, give $X_S=\prod_{v\in S} X(K_v)$ the product topology and embed $X(K)$ diagonally. Write
$$
W_S(x)=\bigcap_{N\ni x}\overline{X(K)\cap N}^{\,\mathrm{Zar}},
$$
where $N$ ranges over open neighborhoods in $X_S$ and Zariski closures are over $K$.

## 1. Curves

**Theorem.** For any projective $K$-variety $C$ of dimension at most one, the map $x\mapsto W_S(x)$ has finite range for every $S$. If an integral smooth projective curve has infinitely many $K$-points, then every nonempty relatively open subset of the diagonal $C(K)$ in $C_S$ is Zariski dense in $C$.

First suppose $C$ is smooth, projective and geometrically integral with infinitely many points. Faltings’s curve theorem gives $g(C)\le1$. If $g=0$, a rational point gives $C\simeq \mathbf P^1_K$. Weak approximation then gives infinitely many rational points in every basic open neighborhood meeting the diagonal. For $g=1$, choose an origin in $C(K)$. The product $\prod_{v\in S} C(K_v)$ is a compact Hausdorff group. Every nonempty relatively open subset of an infinite subgroup of a compact group is infinite. Indeed, the subgroup closure is an infinite compact group and has no isolated point, since a compact discrete group is finite. A relatively open subset of a dense subgroup in a Hausdorff space without isolated points is infinite. On an integral curve, every infinite set of rational points is Zariski dense. The curve theorem is available as the exact statement `Faltings.finite_setOf_comp_eq_id_of_two_le_genus` on the intake’s pinned Axiom source. [Axiom Mordell, curve theorem](https://github.com/AxiomMath/Mordell/blob/82a30cb7857f6e3cfdb1be91f5533c0ccb57d493/Mordell/MordellConjecture/Curve.lean#L798).

For an arbitrary $C$, remove a finite closed subset $F$ containing zero-dimensional components, component intersections and nonsmooth loci of the integral one-dimensional components. Components whose smooth locus has no $K$-point contribute no points outside $F(K)$. The other components have geometrically integral smooth projective normalizations, since each smooth normalization has a $K$-point and its geometric components are disjoint and Galois transitively permuted. Their normalizations identify with the corresponding components away from finitely many points. Enlarge $F$ to include the images of the rational points of all those normalizations with finitely many $K$-points. Thus $C(K)$ is a union of a fixed finite set and the pieces $C_i(K)\setminus F(K)$ indexed by components with infinitely many smooth rational points. Every nonempty relatively open subset of such a piece is infinite, by the smooth case and the identification off $F$.

For any $x\in C_S$, a piece contributes its full component to $W_S(x)$ if $x$ lies in its product-topological closure. Otherwise one neighborhood excludes the piece. A point of the finite set contributes exactly its singleton if $x$ equals that diagonal point. Intersecting the finitely many excluding neighborhoods proves that $W_S(x)$ is the union of the indicated components and singletons. This gives its precise possible constituents independently of $S$.

## 2. Subvarieties of abelian varieties

**Theorem.** Let $X\subseteq A$ be a closed $K$-subvariety of an abelian variety over $K$. There are finitely many cosets $Y_i=a_i+B_i\subseteq X$ with $a_i\in A(K)$, $B_i/K$ abelian and $B_i(K)$ Zariski dense in $B_i$, such that for every $S$ and $x\in X_S$,
$$
W_S(x)=\bigcup_{i\mid x\in\overline{Y_i(K)}^{\,X_S}}Y_i.
$$

Classical Mordell–Lang implies that the geometric irreducible components of the Zariski closure of $X(K)$ are translates of abelian subvarieties. In each component the complement of the others is a nonempty relatively open subset of the whole closure, so it contains a point $a_i$ of $X(K)$ and its rational points are Zariski dense there. Galois fixes $a_i$ and permutes the components, so it fixes the unique component $Y_i$ containing $a_i$. Then $B_i=Y_i-a_i$ is defined over $K$, and $X(K)=\bigcup_i Y_i(K)$. [Faltings, Diophantine approximation on abelian varieties](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1991/133-3/p04), [Mazur, formulation of Mordell–Lang, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2](https://library2.msri.org/books/Book39/files/mazur.pdf).

For an abelian variety $B$, let $\Gamma=B(K)$ be Zariski dense and $H$ its topological closure in the compact group $\prod_{v\in S} B(K_v)$. Any identity neighborhood $U$ in $H$ has finitely many translates by elements of $\Gamma$ covering $H$. For each $h\in H$, density gives an element of $\Gamma$ in the open set $h-U$, and compactness gives the finite subcover. Consequently $\Gamma$ is a finite union of translates of $\Gamma\cap U$. If the latter had proper Zariski closure in $B$, the finite union of its translated closures would contradict irreducibility and the density of $\Gamma$. In particular, any neighborhood of a point of $H$ contains a translated neighborhood of the identity centered at an element of $\Gamma$, so its intersection with $\Gamma$ is also Zariski dense in $B$. Apply this to each $Y_i(K)$. Components with $x$ in their topological closure occur in every neighborhood’s Zariski closure. The rest can all be excluded in one neighborhood because there are finitely many. This proves the formula.

The finite-range consequence in Sections 1 and 2 concerns the exact local Zariski-closure question. Classical sources discuss the related real connected-components conjecture for curves and abelian varieties. The proofs here use the established finiteness and coset-decomposition theorems and state the result for the simultaneous product topology. [Mazur, 1995 update](https://numdam.org/item/AST_1995__228__165_0.pdf), [Poonen, discussion of the known real curve case](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/h10_notices.pdf).

## 3. Diophantine approximation and dimension-one presentations

**Theorem.** Suppose $X$ is a smooth projective geometrically integral curve or an abelian variety over $K$, with $X(K)$ Zariski dense. Let $U$ be a nonempty open of $X$ and $f:U\to Y$ a dominant morphism to a positive-dimensional integral projective $K$-variety. For every $P\in U(K)$ there exist a single effective Cartier divisor $D$ on $Y$ containing $f(P)$ and a sequence $P_j\in U(K)$ such that $f(P_j)$ are distinct, lie outside $\operatorname{supp}(D)$, and converge to $f(P)$ in every $Y(K_v)$.

Embed $Y$ in projective space and choose a $K$-hyperplane through $f(P)$ that does not contain $Y$. Its section is an effective Cartier divisor since $Y$ is integral. Take a countable descending product-neighborhood basis of the diagonal point $P$, available because the set of places is countable and each local point space is metrizable. For each neighborhood the intersection with $X(K)$ is Zariski dense by the curve proof or the compact-group argument. It therefore contains a point in $U(K)$ outside $f^{-1}(\operatorname{supp}D)$ and all fibers above the finitely many earlier image points. These are proper closed subsets of $U$ by dominance and positive target dimension. The resulting $P_j$ converge to $P$ at all places, and continuity gives the required image convergence. The divisor and sequence satisfy the curve-source case of Pasten’s approximation conjecture over number fields, simultaneously for every place. For a singular integral projective curve with dense rational points, choose $P$ in the common smooth open of the normalization and perform the same proof there. [Pasten, Conjecture 5.24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.14054#page=27).

**Corollary.** If a $K$-diophantine set $T\subseteq K$ has a presentation $T=f(V(K))$ with $V$ affine of dimension at most one, there is a single finite set $T_0\subseteq T$ such that every $t\in T\setminus T_0$ is approached by a sequence of distinct points of $T\setminus\{t\}$ at all places simultaneously.

For each irreducible component of $V$ apply the curve argument to a smooth projective normal model of a dense open that has a rational point. Zero-dimensional components, exceptional loci, components with finitely many rational points and components on which $f$ is constant contribute only finitely many values. These make up $T_0$. At any rational witness for a remaining $t$, the curve argument with target $\mathbf P^1_K$ and divisor $\{t\}$ produces the sequence. Consequently an infinite subset of $K$ that is locally discrete at even one place, including $\mathbf Z$ at the real place of $\mathbf Q$, has no presentation of dimension at most one. A bounded infinite diophantine set with a unique transcendental accumulation point at a place also requires a witness variety of dimension at least two. Both conclusions apply to the presentation and require no hypothesis about the existential definability of the given set.

**Real closure.** For $K\subseteq\mathbf R$ with its displayed real place, the real closure of such a $T$ is a finite union of closed intervals with endpoints real algebraic or infinite, allowing singleton intervals and the empty union. For a genus-zero component use density of $K$ in $\mathbf R$. For a genus-one component $E$ with infinitely many rational points, the compact real Lie group $E(\mathbf R)$ has finitely many components and identity component a circle. The subgroup $E(K)$ meets the identity component in an infinite subgroup, which is dense in the circle. Its real closure is therefore a union of connected components of $E(\mathbf R)$. These are semialgebraic over the real algebraic numbers. Intersection with the open on which $f$ is regular preserves semialgebraicity and the density of the rational points in the local closure on that open. The images under $f$ of those open sets and their closures are semialgebraic over the real algebraic numbers by quantifier elimination. Adding finitely many exceptional rational values and taking the finite union proves the claim. In particular, a real number whose lower rational cut has a dimension-one diophantine presentation over $\mathbf Q$ is algebraic. [Pasten, Proposition 5.6 and Conjecture 5.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.14054#page=23).

## 4. Exact transfer to the Manin formalization

The arithmetic intake contains a finite-generation theorem that can supply hypotheses of the checked quadratic rank-stability lemma. In Axiom Mordell, `Faltings.group_fg_points_of_numberField` proves finite generation for the group of $K$-valued points of an abelian scheme over a subfield $k$. The weak Mordell–Weil file proves finite index of the subgroup of $m$-th powers for $m\ne0$. The Kummer directory provides the Galois character and unramified-extension infrastructure used in that proof. These are useful for constructing the finite-generation and Kummer stages of an elliptic-curve arithmetic formalization. [Strong Mordell–Weil](https://github.com/AxiomMath/Mordell/blob/82a30cb7857f6e3cfdb1be91f5533c0ccb57d493/Mordell/MordellWeil/Main.lean#L238), [Weak Mordell–Weil](https://github.com/AxiomMath/Mordell/blob/82a30cb7857f6e3cfdb1be91f5533c0ccb57d493/Mordell/MordellWeil/Weak.lean).

The checked `elliptic_rank_stable_of_nonsquare_twist_rank_zero` in `ManinKPTwistRank.lean` assumes $\mathbf Z$-module finite generation of the Mathlib affine point types over the quadratic field and on the base-field twist. To transport the new theorem, construct the smooth projective Weierstrass cubic as an abelian group scheme, and identify its scheme points over each relevant field with the affine Weierstrass point group plus the infinity point, carrying the group law to the chord-and-tangent law. A natural additive equivalence suffices. Finite generation transports through it, converting the scheme-side multiplicative `Group.FG` to `AddGroup.FG` and then `Module.Finite`. Axiom’s Jacobian proof already demonstrates the last two conversions. The pinned Stacks source supplies smoothness, projectivity and dimension of the Weierstrass cubic in `WeierstrassCurveExists.lean`, together with point-to-Picard lemmas under its genus-one modules. The exact affine-point group adapter is still required in the Lean interface. [Pinned Stacks, Weierstrass curve](https://github.com/AxiomMath/Stacks/blob/c496df0d9beea9deaeabcea8492605f9a0a2313b/StacksFrontier/AlgebraicGeometry/Curves/GenusOne/WeierstrassCurveExists.lean), [Axiom Mordell, finite generation conversion](https://github.com/AxiomMath/Mordell/blob/82a30cb7857f6e3cfdb1be91f5533c0ccb57d493/Mordell/NeronTate/JacobianLattice.lean#L180).
