# Exact cancellation of every finite-dimensional algebra catalyst for sparse tensor powers

Let \(A_\pm=(A_m^\pm)^{\otimes r}\) be the \(r\)-fold powers of the sparse Möbius-telescope pair, for \(m,r\ge1\). Let \(\operatorname{Caps}_k(S,T)\) denote the feasible three-tuples of nonnegative forward entry pole caps for Laurent degeneration of the multiplication tensor of \(S\) to that of \(T\). The maps in the three regrouped modes are arbitrary and their inverses have no cap. [Sparse family and the integer cap criterion](PowerSeed.md)

**Theorem.** Over any field \(k\), for every nonzero finite-dimensional associative unital \(k\)-algebra \(B\),
\[
 \operatorname{Caps}_k(A_+\otimes_k B,A_-\otimes_k B)
   =\operatorname{Caps}_k(A_+,A_-).
\]
No modification of the sparse pair is needed. In particular, the minimum polynomial-matrix presentation order remains \(\lceil r\lambda_m\rceil\), with \(\lambda_m\) the base-graph constant of the exact power-order theorem. This includes nonsemisimple catalysts and catalysts containing arbitrary products of the sparse algebras themselves. [Exact power orders](PowerOrderSeed.md)

The proof uses the following properties of the original sparse pair. The convex reachability order of its support is a chain of size \(n\), with a one-dimensional corner on each supported pair (including the vertex idempotents on the diagonal). The two pairs have the same support and the same based radical grading. Their multiplication tables agree except at one triangle \(\sigma\), whose source product is nonzero and whose target product is zero. Every other originally nonzero product has coefficient \(1\) on both sides. Tensor powers have the product reachability order and preserve this common radical grading.

## 1. Removing the internal radical of each strongly connected component

Extend any alleged witness to \(F=\overline k\) and set \(R=F[[t]], K=F((t))\). Put \(U_\pm=(A_m^\pm)^{\otimes r}\otimes_k F\) and \(D=B\otimes_k F\). The two slices at the target unit give the integral normalization from the power proof. It provides a free associative unital law \(\Lambda\) over \(R\), special fiber \(U_-\otimes_F D\), generic transporter \(H:(U_+\otimes_F D)_K\to\Lambda_K\), and three same-cap maps
\[
 F_A=L_{ab}R_{b^{-1}}H^{-1},\qquad
 F_B=L_bH^{-1},\qquad F_C=H L_{(ab)^{-1}}, \tag{1}
\]
where \(a,b\) are source units over \(K\) and \(L,R\) with a unit subscript mean left and right multiplication. The first two maps go from the generic target space to the generic source space, and the third goes in the reverse direction. Their action on the source multiplication law equals the normalized law. [Unital normalization of tensor degenerations](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.19)

We recall some lattice facts, with the proofs included to specify their use. The Jacobson radical of an endpoint \(U_\pm\otimes_F D\) is the sum \(J_\pm\otimes D+U_\pm\otimes J_D\), where \(J_\pm,J_D\) are the two radicals. This sum is nilpotent, and the quotient is a product of full matrix algebras because \(U_\pm/J_\pm=F^{[n]^r}\) and \(D/J_D\) splits. This formula holds also over \(K\), and the generic and special radical dimensions agree. The integral radical lattice \(J_R=\Lambda\cap\operatorname{Jac}(\Lambda_K)\) is saturated in \(\Lambda\) and thus free with free quotient over the DVR \(R\). Its special image is nilpotent of the full special radical dimension, and hence is the entire special radical. These facts will also apply after replacing \(D\) by a common quotient of the catalyst.

Index the matrix factors of \(D/J_D\) by \(b\) and choose a complete system of orthogonal block idempotents \(f_b\) in \(D\) lifting their identities. In \(\Lambda\), lift the special system \(e_x\otimes f_b\), \(x\in[n]^r\), to a system \(u_{x,b}\). Idempotent lifting over a complete \(t\)-adic algebra follows by iterating \(v\mapsto v-(2v-1)^{-1}(v^2-v)\), which squares the \(t\)-adic error, and lifting successively in complementary corners. The same formula works in a nilpotent ideal and provides the lifts in \(D\). For any pair of these idempotents the corner is the image of the projection \(z\mapsto u_{x,b}zu_{y,c}\), so ranks and corner support specialize exactly. Applied in \(\Lambda/J_R\), off-diagonal corners vanish and each diagonal idempotent has nonzero reduction. The generic quotient has just as many simple blocks. Thus the generic images of these idempotents are the primitive central identities of the quotient. Complete idempotent systems \((g_i),(h_i)\) agreeing modulo a nilpotent ideal are conjugate by the unit \(\sum_i h_i g_i\), which is \(1\) modulo the ideal. It follows that the block permutation induced by \(H\) preserves all endpoint corner dimensions and support.

In the block support digraph put an arc \(b\to c\) for a nonzero \(f_bDf_c\). Product corners of \(U_\pm\otimes D\) have product support and all diagonal corners are nonzero. Hence the strongly connected components of its support have the form \(\{x\}\times C\), where \(C\) is a strongly connected component for \(D\), since the support of \(U_\pm\) is acyclic. The support permutation induced by \(H\) matches these components. In \(\Lambda\) sum the \(u_{x,b}\) in each component to an idempotent \(g\) and take the two-sided ideal generated by the corners \(gJ_Rg\). Define \(I\) in \(D\) to be the ideal generated by \(f_C J_D f_C\), where \(f_C=\sum_{b\in C}f_b\). In the special algebra the generated ideal is \(U_-\otimes I\): the diagonal \(x\)-corner of \(U_-\) is \(F e_x\) and contains no radical. The generic ideal is the image under \(H\) of \(U_+\otimes I\), since matched idempotent systems are conjugate modulo the generic radical and two-sided ideals are invariant under inner automorphisms. To see that the integral generated ideal is a direct summand, express it as the image of the multiplication map formed by the finite list \(v(gjg)w\). The generic and special dimensions of that image are the same. A full-rank minor modulo \(t\) is a unit and elimination splits off the image, since the remaining block has rank zero generically.

The multipliers in (1) preserve these ideals and \(H\) transports them. In the three scaled lattice inclusions \(t^{p_i}F_i(L_{\rm in})\subseteq L_{\rm out}\) we may therefore pass to quotients, with the same caps. We have obtained a Laurent witness for \(U_+\otimes D'\) to \(U_-\otimes D'\), where \(D'=D/I\) is nonzero because \(I\subseteq J_D\). No off-diagonal block corner within an old strongly connected component survives, since every such corner was in \(J_D\). All remaining strict arcs go forward among the old components. Thus the simple-block support of \(D'\) is acyclic. Its diagonal block corners are \(M_{d_b}(F)\), the full matrix blocks of its semisimple quotient, because their entire diagonal radicals were killed. This reduction made no distinction between the original sparse directions and the catalyst directions. [Earlier radical-lattice and support specialization arguments](CatalystSeed.md)

## 2. Path coordinates that can meet an outer sparse factor

Apply the integral radical construction to the quotient witness and denote its normalized law again by \(\Lambda\). Let \(E_\pm=U_\pm\otimes D'\) and \(P\) be the induced permutation between the endpoint labels of simple blocks, identified using their common support. The block reachability poset is \([n]^r\times Q\), where \(Q\) is the reachability poset of \(D'\). In each connected Hasse component use the Cartesian prime decomposition of its underlying undirected graph. The \(r\) outer factors are paths on \(n\) vertices, hence prime: a product of two nontrivial connected graphs contains a square. A poset support isomorphism preserves the prime edge classes and their factor layers. It is coordinatewise up to a factor permutation, since varying any of the other source coordinates leaves a given target factor coordinate unchanged. This continues to hold when a component is mapped to a different component. [Canonical Cartesian prime edge classes](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/jf/FHS-DAM85.pdf)

In the finite set of prime-factor positions over all components, call a position active if its \(P\)-orbit contains any outer sparse position. Every active position is a path on \(n\) vertices. Label its path by the order \(1,\ldots,n\) transported to it by an iterate that matches it to an outer sparse path. This label is independent of the choice of iterate: between two outer positions the poset isomorphism preserves their increasing order. Thus \(P\) preserves these ranks as it permutes active positions. Call the remaining positions inactive. The set of all inactive spectator tuples, each equipped with its component label, has a finite induced permutation \(p\) under \(P\). Fixing such a tuple \(q\) leaves an active grid, on which \(P\) acts by matching path coordinates.

Write \(w(v)\) for the matrix degree of the quotient block at a vertex \(v\) temporarily. It is preserved by \(P\). On every active grid the degree is constant: for a single active coordinate edge of the Hasse graph, apply an iterate of \(P\) taking its direction to an outer factor. There, the two vertices have the same \(D'\) block, so their degrees agree. The edges of the active grid connect it, and equality follows. Write the common degree as \(d\) when the grid is fixed.

Let \(\Gamma\) be the sparse corner support, including diagonals. For two vertices in an active grid, their corner has dimension \(d^2\) if each changed coordinate is a supported corner of \(\Gamma\), and dimension zero otherwise. For a single changed coordinate this is immediate by taking it to an outer factor with a \(D'\) diagonal matrix block. For several changed coordinates, take one to an outer factor. In those labels the corner is the tensor product of the outer's corner and the corner with only the remaining changes, for which induction applies. The \(d^2\) dimension and zero statuses are invariant under \(P\). This proves the claim in every active grid even if arbitrary other corners of \(D'\) have large dimension.

The matrices of \(J_R^h\) as multilinear images have matching generic and special ranks. The dimension equality follows from the tensor-product radical formula
\[
 J(E_\pm)^h=\sum_{i+j=h}J(U_\pm)^i\otimes J(D')^j,
\]
using bases adapted to the radical filtrations. This also shows that the associated graded of \(E_\pm\) is \(U_\pm\otimes\operatorname{gr}D'\), since \(U_\pm\) have the indicated radical grading. Unit-minor elimination therefore gives base change for all these powers, and \(\operatorname{gr}_{J_R}\Lambda\) is free in every degree. After the SCC reduction each endpoint diagonal block itself is \(M_d(F)\) at a degree-\(d\) vertex. A \(d^2\)-dimensional active corner is a single regular matrix bimodule for its endpoints. Its subspaces coming from radical powers are bimodules for those diagonal blocks, so there is only one nonzero graded piece. This statement also holds on the diagonal in degree zero. The particular graded degree of each active corner is preserved by \(P\), by the graded corner projections and their matching ranks.

## 3. Mixed products and the count of genuine losses

For an ordered triple of vertices in one active grid, let \(\chi_\pm(x,y,z)\) be \(1\) if the product of the \((x,y)\) and \((y,z)\) corners into \((x,z)\) is nonzero in \(\operatorname{gr}E_\pm\), and \(0\) otherwise. The graded corner projection construction and the generic isomorphism give
\[
 \chi_-(P x,P y,P z)\le \chi_+(x,y,z). \tag{2}
\]
A generic zero product is zero on the corresponding free integral graded corners. At fixed labels the indicators differ only when an outer coordinate performs the omitted triangle \(\sigma\). This follows from \(\operatorname{gr}E_\pm=U_\pm\otimes\operatorname{gr}D'\) and the original multiplication tables.

Suppose a composable pair of supported edges in an active grid moves a single coordinate in one step and a disjoint set of coordinates in the other step. This type of pair persists under \(P\), and no coordinate performs \(\sigma\). Thus \(\chi_+=\chi_-\) at every iterate. Equation (2) implies that the indicators are constant around the finite orbit. Take the singleton coordinate to an outer position by an iterate. In that factor the product is an edge with an identity, and in the other factors the singleton step acts by the identity at their common endpoint. The product of the two graded corners is therefore nonzero. We conclude that every such mixed product is nonzero on both endpoints. In particular both orders of two disjoint singleton moves give nonzero multiples of their common composite corner.

For nonzero active corners, graded multiplication has the form \((X,Y)\mapsto cXY\) in regular matrix-bimodule coordinates, by equivariance over the outer matrices and balancing over the middle matrix block. This can also be checked on the three sets of matrix units. Thus associativity identities about nonzero products can be read in one-dimensional corners by using the \(11\)-unit in each regular matrix corner. Every simultaneous supported edge can be represented, up to a nonzero scalar in these coordinates, by a sequence of singleton edges. This follows by induction, using the nonzero product of a singleton and a disjoint composite step just proved. Adjacent singleton moves in distinct coordinates can be commuted up to a nonzero scalar by the same mixed product rule.

Consider a single-coordinate triangle in position \(i\), specified by ranks \(u,v,w\). Its nonzero status does not depend on the other active spectator coordinates. To see this, change one such spectator \(j\ne i\) by one Hasse step. With its increasing orientation, multiply the two \(i\)-steps followed by this \(j\)-step. By the nonzero mixed products, move the \(j\)-step to the beginning and use associativity. On the first side the \(i\)-triangle lies at the old spectator value, and on the second side it lies at the new value. Multiplication of either \(i\)-chord by its adjacent \(j\)-step has nonzero coefficient. The associativity relation therefore identifies the two triangle coefficients up to multiplication by nonzero scalars. All spectator changes are connected by these steps. This proof works separately in both graded endpoint algebras.

For any retained triangle of the original sparse pair, each active position has nonzero product at that triangle. Indeed, at every iterate the source and special indicators at those labels agree, because that triple can never perform \(\sigma\) in an outer coordinate. They are therefore constant by (2). An iterate along an outer factor evaluates the original retained product, which is nonzero. The same argument controls any original zero product other than the omitted triangle, with constant indicator zero.

For a fixed inactive spectator tuple \(q\) and active position \(i\), let \(s_i(q)\) and \(t_i(q)\) be the source and special indicators for the triangle \(\sigma\) in that position. The previous independence result makes them well-defined. Let \(S(q)=\sum_i s_i(q)\) and \(T(q)=\sum_i t_i(q)\), summing the active positions of its component. At the same labels, every outer position contributes \(1\) to \(S(q)-T(q)\), and every other position contributes \(0\), since fixing all outer coordinates leaves the same \(D'\) product. Hence \(S(q)-T(q)=r\).

Under \(P\), (2) says that every \(s_i(q)\) is at least the matched \(t_{P(i)}(p(q))\). Thus the number of \(1\)-to-\(0\) matches at \(q\) is \(S(q)-T(p(q))\). Summing around any finite orbit of \(p\) gives average exactly \(r\). Choose \(q\) with at least \(r\) such positions, choose \(r\) of them, and fix the other active coordinates at arbitrary ranks. The resulting source subgrid is matched by \(P\) to the corresponding target subgrid. On every chosen axis its source and target local product statuses coincide with \(A_m^+\) and \(A_m^-\), respectively. The independence result ensures this for all settings of the other chosen coordinates.

On these subgrids the corner supports and all product statuses are exactly those of \(U_+\) and \(U_-\), ignoring the common full matrix coordinate in each corner. To verify the product statement, expand each of its two supported edges into singleton moves. Commute moves in distinct coordinates until the moves of each single coordinate are consecutive. These replacements preserve vanishing by associativity and the nonzero mixed squares. If a local product vanishes the whole product vanishes. If every local product survives, each local segment can be replaced by its chord and the resulting disjoint sequence has nonzero product. This proves the assertion even for products with several simultaneous occurrences of \(\sigma\).

## 4. Keeping each cap in the recovered sparse subgrid

We record the scalar extraction, since the three forward caps on the original tensor witness are needed individually. The free semisimple quotient \(\Lambda/J_R\) is a product of full matrix algebras over \(R\). To obtain integral matrix units, lift their diagonal idempotents, lift \(E_{i1},E_{1i}\) in their rank-one corners, and normalize their product in the first diagonal rank-one corner to its identity. Their opposite product is then an idempotent reducing to the identity of its rank-one corner, so is the identity there. The products of the adjusted row and column lifts give the matrix units. On each active graded corner choose a generator of its \((11,11)\) corner reducing to the prescribed generator. The action of the lifted matrix units gives an integral basis, since its reduction is a basis. This gives compatible adapted frames for the source and target graded modules.

Index the selected target subgrid by its matching under \(P\). For a source vertex \(x\), the induced quotient block map of \(H\) is \(X\mapsto S_x X S_x^{-1}\), by Skolem–Noether. On any active corner of the chosen source subgrid the induced map is \(X\mapsto \eta_{xy} S_x X S_y^{-1}\) for some \(\eta_{xy}\in K^\times\), since a regular matrix bimodule has only scalar bimodule endomorphisms after removing the two conjugating matrices. Put \(\eta_{xx}=1\). Write \(a_x,b_x\) for the quotient-block values of the units in (1) and \(v(M)\) for the least valuation of the entries of a matrix. Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 s_x&=v(S_x),\qquad u_x=v(b_xS_x^{-1}),\qquad q_x=v(a_xb_xS_x^{-1}),\\
 w_{xy}&=v(\eta_{xy})+s_x-s_y,\qquad
 \beta_x=u_x+s_x,\qquad \alpha_x=q_x-u_x .
 \end{split}
\]
For a source nonzero product, the valuation of the matching integral graded product coefficient is \(w_{xz}-w_{xy}-w_{yz}\), by multiplying the three transported corner frames. The source coefficient is a nonzero constant and hence has valuation zero. The coefficient in the graded target law has valuation zero if retained and strictly positive if deleted. The vertex corrections cancel on every triangle. Hence the integer defects are those of the unamplified \(r\)-fold sparse comparison.

On the selected graded corner the maps (1) have the respective formulas
\[
 \begin{split}
 X&\mapsto \eta_{xy}^{-1}(a_xb_xS_x^{-1})X(S_yb_y^{-1}),\\
 X&\mapsto \eta_{xy}^{-1}(b_xS_x^{-1})X S_y,\\
 X&\mapsto \eta_{xy}\bigl(S_x(a_xb_x)^{-1}\bigr)X S_y^{-1}.
 \end{split}
\]
The three entry bounds hold in these frames because the maps (1) preserve the corresponding filtrations and the chosen adapted bases are integral with integral inverses. The minimum entry valuation for \(X\mapsto MXN\) is \(v(M)+v(N)\), by taking minima independently over the two coefficient pairs. Also \(v(M)+v(M^{-1})\le0\) for an invertible matrix \(M\). Therefore the three expressions
\[
 \alpha_x+\beta_x-\beta_y-w_{xy},\qquad
 \beta_x-w_{xy},\qquad
 w_{xy}-\alpha_x-\beta_x
\]
are at least the respective map valuations. In fact they simplify to \(q_x-u_y-v(\eta_{xy})\), \(u_x+s_y-v(\eta_{xy})\), and \(v(\eta_{xy})-q_x-s_y\), where the second is exact and the other bounds follow from the inverse inequality. They satisfy the three original bounds \(-p_A,-p_B,-p_C\). The same calculation on \(x=y\) gives \(\alpha_x\ge-p_A\), \(\beta_x\ge-p_B\), \(\alpha_x+\beta_x\le p_C\).

These weights now give a witness on the sparse power itself. Take the diagonal map \(H_0\) of weight \(w_{xy}\) on its indicated basis corner and take units \(a_0=\sum_x t^{\alpha_x}e_x\), \(b_0=\sum_x t^{\beta_x}e_x\). The three maps (1) with \(H_0,a_0,b_0\) have the stated cap bounds. Their tensor action is the law transported by \(H_0\), whose coefficients are \(1\) on retained products, positive powers of \(t\) on deleted products, and zero on the original zero products. The weight data are integers and this construction is over \(k((t))\) itself. Conversely tensor any unamplified witness with the identity maps on \(B\). This proves the entire cap-region equality. [Integer extraction in the original matrix case](PowerSeed.md)

The multiplication tensors are concise by unitality. Clearing the three caps and truncating each resulting power-series matrix above degree \(p_A+p_B+p_C\) gives a polynomial-matrix presentation of that order. Conversely a polynomial presentation with this target as leading coefficient has generically invertible mode matrices by conciseness. Dividing one matrix by the leading power of \(t\) gives a Laurent witness of total cap at most that order. This identifies the minimum polynomial order with the least feasible cap sum, and the exact power-order formula gives the numerical claim.

This is a written proof for multiplication tensors of the fixed sparse source and target. The key comparison uses the fact that those base tables differ in one triangular product.
