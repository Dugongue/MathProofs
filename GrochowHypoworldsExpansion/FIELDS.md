# Field transfer and involutive-algebra norms

## Ordered isometry over a finite extension reduces to the prime field

Let \(E=\mathbb F_{p^e}\) be explicitly presented with an irreducible defining polynomial. Ordered matrix-tuple isometry over \(E\) reduces to ordered matrix-tuple isometry over \(\mathbb F_p\) in deterministic polynomial time in the entry encodings and \(e\). If all the input forms are alternating, the output forms are alternating. In characteristic two even a mixture of alternating bilinear forms and Hermitian forms for a quadratic subfield can be encoded by an all-alternating tuple over \(\mathbb F_2\). This improves the field scope of the equivalence between T10's binary and fixed finite characteristic-two versions. It leaves the polynomial-time decision question itself open. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Part V, §1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S1.SS4)

Regard a mode \(V=E^n\) as a prime-field space of dimension \(ne\). Take four copies \(V_1,V_2,V_3,V_4\). In both output tuples put a separate alternating identity-pairing marker on each edge of the cycle \(12,23,34,41\). On the two incident copies it has block matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-I&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
and it is zero on the other copies. Its radical is the sum of the two nonincident blocks. Intersections of these radicals recover each individual block. Thus an isometry of the markers is block diagonal. Adjacent markers force inverse transpose on adjacent blocks, giving \(\operatorname{diag}(A,A^{-\mathsf T},A,A^{-\mathsf T})\), and every invertible \(A\) extends this way.

Let \(L\) be the matrix of multiplication by the field generator on the first copy of \(V\). Add another alternating marker on \(12\) with upper-right block \(L^{\mathsf T}\) and lower-left block \(-L\). Preservation of it means \(A^{\mathsf T}L^{\mathsf T}A^{-\mathsf T}=L^{\mathsf T}\), or \(AL=LA\). Consequently \(A\) is \(E\)-linear. Finally put the trace of each given form on copy 1 alone. For an \(E\)-linear \(A\), preservation of the trace form is equivalent to preservation of the original form: multiply its first, linear argument by an arbitrary scalar of \(E\) and use the nondegenerate trace pairing. The trace of an alternating form is alternating. For a Hermitian form in characteristic two, its diagonal values lie in the involution's fixed field, whose elements have zero trace from the full field to the fixed field. Its prime-field trace is therefore alternating as well. The same use of a linear first argument recovers the Hermitian equation. All matrices in the construction have polynomial coordinate size.

## Effective modular transfer for geometric solvability

Given a finite list of existential or universal one-block sentences over \(\mathbb Q(t_1,\ldots,t_\ell)\), with Boolean combinations of polynomial equalities and inequalities, there is an unconditional randomized polynomial-time reduction to the same questions over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\) for one sampled prime of polynomial bit length, with one common evaluation of the \(t\)'s in \(\mathbb F_p\). The input coefficients may be given by arithmetic circuits for valid rational functions, and the output can retain arithmetic circuits with ordinary finite-field scalar labels. All listed truth values agree with probability at least \(1-2^{-k}\), when the accuracy parameter \(k\) is charged to the running time. This follows from effective Nullstellensatz bounds and rank witnesses. In particular it applies to geometric tensor isomorphism, which is the existence of invertible transporter matrices over the algebraic closure. Treating the same formulas as tests for rational points or for prime-field points would be a different statement. [Kollár, effective Nullstellensatz](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9805091) · [Related parametric Nullstellensatz work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13027)

For a universal sentence, negate its Boolean predicate and test the resulting existential sentence, then complement the result. For each existential predicate use its disjunction of conjunctions. Each conjunction turns into an equation system by requiring the product of the inequated polynomials to be invertible. Clear parameter denominators and retain their nonvanishing separately. There can be exponentially many branches, but every branch's degree and logarithm of coefficient absolute sum are at most \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(s)}\) for input length \(s\), by the standard arithmetic-circuit bounds.

For each system, the effective weak Nullstellensatz in arbitrary characteristic bounds the degrees needed to certify 1 in the ideal by \(D^{O(n+1)}\) for degree bound \(D\ge3\) and \(n\) quantified variables. Thus emptiness over the algebraic closure is equality of ranks of a Macaulay coefficient matrix and its augmentation by the constant column. Use a fixed row and column bound adequate also if some polynomials lose degree after specialization. Matrices have size at most \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(s)}\), and all entries are integer polynomials in \(t\) with the bounds above. Choose one maximal-rank minor of each matrix that is nonzero over \(\mathbb Q(t)\), interpreting a rank-zero minor as 1. Every larger minor of that matrix vanishes identically over the integers. It therefore stays zero after any specialization. Preserving just the selected nonzero witnesses suffices to preserve both ranks for every branch.

Let \(P(t)\) be the product of those selected witnesses and denominator polynomials. It is a nonzero integer polynomial. Its degree and logarithm of coefficient absolute sum are at most \(B=2^{\operatorname{poly}(s)}\). For example a minor of size \(r\) of entries with norm at most \(M\) has norm at most \(r!M^r\), and there are only exponentially many selected witnesses, even though the number of possible minors can be much larger. The product is used in the analysis, and need not be constructed. A chosen nonzero integer coefficient of \(P\) has at most \(B\) prime divisors. Sample a prime uniformly from sufficiently large \([X,2X]\), with \(\log X\) polynomial in \(s+k\) and enough primes that a set of \(B\) can occupy at most a \(2^{-k-2}\) fraction. Uniform integer sampling and a deterministic polynomial-time primality test give bounded randomized time with arbitrarily small extra failure probability. For a prime avoiding the chosen coefficient, a uniform assignment to \(t\) makes \(P(t)\) vanish with probability at most \(B/p\). Choose \(X\) large enough to make this at most \(2^{-k-2}\). Evaluate the coefficient circuits in \(\mathbb F_p\), retaining the original size of each formula. This proves simultaneous agreement at the specified accuracy.

Consequently uniform polynomial-time solvability of the prime-characteristic geometric TI problem, with the prime part of the input, would imply BPP for the characteristic-zero geometric problem on rational input. The theorem gives a transfer to varying sampled primes and says nothing about fixed prime-field TI in T3 or rational-point TI in T4. Both arguments in this file are informal.

## A polynomial-time case of binary matrix-tuple isometry

There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for ordered symmetric tuples over a fixed characteristic-two field when, after quotienting the common radical, the comparison adjoint algebra is commutative. This includes alternating tuples and permits nilpotent elements in the adjoint algebra. No nonsingular member of the tuple is required.

Compare dimensions of the common radicals and pass to the quotients. Any quotient isometry lifts by choosing complements and an isomorphism of the common radicals. Use fixed-field module isomorphism to find an invertible left-right equivalence \(X^{\mathsf T}B_iY=C_i\) or reject. Set \(\widetilde B_i=Y^{\mathsf T}B_iY\) and \(h=Y^{-1}X\). Then \(h^{\mathsf T}\widetilde B_i=\widetilde B_i h=C_i\). The adjoint algebra \(A\) is given by the simultaneous linear equations \(T^{\mathsf T}\widetilde B_i=\widetilde B_i T^*\). The common radical is now zero, so the adjoint is unique. Symmetry gives an involution, and \(h\) is a self-adjoint unit. A tuple isometry is equivalent to \(T\in A^\times\) with \(T^*T=h\), since the equation \(T^{\mathsf T}\widetilde B_iT=C_i\) is precisely the adjoint equation with partner \(hT^{-1}\). Compute a basis of \(A\) and check the commutativity promise by basis products.

In the commutative algebra \(A\), the Jacobson radical \(J\) is the kernel of a sufficiently high iterated squaring map, computable by binary linear algebra. The quotient is a product of finite fields. Its unique square root of \(\bar h\) is fixed by the involution, so it is a norm root. Lift it to a unit \(T_0\) and reduce the equation to norm membership of \(h/(T_0^*T_0)\) in \(1+J\). There is no loss in choosing that quotient root. Indeed \(A^\times\) is finite abelian, its radical kernel \(1+J\) has two-power order, and \((A/J)^\times\) has odd order. The odd part of \(A^\times\) maps isomorphically onto that quotient, and the isomorphism respects the involution. Every quotient unitary element thus has a genuinely unitary lift, so any norm solution can be adjusted to the chosen quotient root.

To test the remaining equation, use generators \(1+b\), where \(b\) ranges over basis lifts of all \(J^j/J^{j+1}\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). Their norms generate the image because the norm is a homomorphism in a commutative algebra. At step \(j\) map the current group to the elementary additive layer \(J^j/J^{j+1}\). Gaussian elimination tests the desired target layer and provides a correction from the pivot generators. The kernel is generated by squares of pivot generators and the nonpivot generators adjusted by pivots to have zero layer image. To see this, first express any kernel element by these adjusted nonpivots and the pivots. The remaining pivot exponents have even parity by independence. Thus the number of generators never grows across a step. Iterating until the radical power vanishes tests membership and recovers the norm preimage by tracking the same products and squares in the original generator list. All operations have polynomial size. [Module isomorphism background](https://doi.org/10.1145/258726.258751) · [Characteristic-two isometry question](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S1.SS4)

## Radical congruence in a noncommutative algebra

Let \(A\) be a finite-dimensional algebra of characteristic two with involution, with its radical \(J\) supplied. Given \(a,b\in A\), there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm to find \(u\in1+J\) with \(u^*au=b\), or reject. The statement applies to any supplied involution-stable nilpotent ideal \(J\).

Start by rejecting if \(a\ne b\bmod J\), and otherwise start with \(u=1\). At stage \(j\) maintain \(u^*au=b\bmod J^j\) and generators for \(H_j=\{g\in1+J:g^*ag=a\bmod J^j\}\). The map \(\delta(g)=g^*ag-a\bmod J^{j+1}\) on \(H_j\) is an additive homomorphism into \(J^j/J^{j+1}\). Multiplying a member of \(J^j\) on either side by any member of \(1+J\) leaves its class unchanged. This proves additivity and gives \((gu)^*a(gu)=u^*au+\delta(g)\bmod J^{j+1}\). Every existing solution modulo \(J^j\) has the form \(gu\). Gaussian elimination on the images of the generators tests for a correction and constructs one. The next group is the kernel of \(\delta\).

The initial group has generators \(1+v\) for basis lifts of all successive layers. Refine these layers into binary codimension-one steps using nested subspaces. The steps are central in their respective quotients since \([1+J,1+J^j]\subseteq1+J^{j+1}\). Keep at most one subgroup generator per leading position in this chain. Sift a generator by multiplying away its leading position with the stored pivot there, installing a new pivot when needed. Close the pivot list under sifting squares and pair commutators until all sift to the identity. Each successful addition occupies a previously vacant position. To see correctness, induct backwards through the positions. The ordered products of later pivots form a subgroup, and a pivot at the current position normalizes it by the commutator checks and finiteness. The pivot's square lies in that subgroup. Thus the ordered products through the current position form a subgroup too. Sifting preserves the generated subgroup. For each binary coordinate of \(\delta\), compute the index-two kernel using Schreier generators with representatives \(1\) and a generator whose value is one, or keep the group if the coordinate vanishes. At most twice the prior generator count is produced before recompression. This is polynomial in the binary dimension and field-operation cost. At the last stage the next radical power vanishes, giving an exact answer.

In particular, if \(A/J\) is a product of finite fields with identity involution, the quotient norm equation has the unique square-root solution. Lift it to any unit \(u_0\) and use the algorithm on \(a=u_0^*u_0\), \(b=h\). Every lift is \(u_0u\) with \(u\in1+J\). This solves the norm problem for arbitrary radicals in this case.

## Binary alternating tuple isometry and involutive norms are polynomial-time equivalent

Given any explicitly presented finite-dimensional involutive algebra \(A\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) and any invertible self-adjoint \(h\), build an ordered alternating tuple on four copies of the underlying vector space of \(A\). Use the same four cycle pairing markers as in the field transfer. An adjoint pair \(T,S\) satisfies \(T^{\mathsf T}B_i=B_iS\) on every marker. Both preserve every radical, by applying the equation and its transpose. Block equations give \(T=\operatorname{diag}(P,Q,P,Q)\) and \(S=\operatorname{diag}(Q^{\mathsf T},P^{\mathsf T},Q^{\mathsf T},P^{\mathsf T})\).

On edge \(12\) add forms with off-diagonal blocks \(R_b^{\mathsf T}\) and \(-R_b\) for every basis element \(b\), with \(R_b\) right multiplication by \(b\). The equations force \(P\) and \(Q^{\mathsf T}\) to commute with right multiplication. Each such operator is left multiplication by its value on 1, giving \(P=L_a\) and \(Q^{\mathsf T}=L_c\). On edge \(13\) add forms with off-diagonal blocks representing \((x,y)\mapsto \ell(x^*y)\) and its negative transpose, for every dual basis functional \(\ell\). The two resulting equations are \(\ell(x^*a^*y)=\ell(x^*cy)\) and \(\ell(y^*ax)=\ell(y^*c^*x)\). Taking \(x=y=1\) forces \(c=a^*\), which also suffices. Thus the adjoint algebra is precisely \(A\), under \(a\mapsto T_a=\operatorname{diag}(L_a,L_{a^*}^{\mathsf T},L_a,L_{a^*}^{\mathsf T})\), and the adjoint on this algebra is the given involution.

Compare the tuple \((B_i)\) to \((B_iT_h)\). The latter matrices are symmetric by self-adjointness and have zero diagonal because every marker uses distinct blocks. Hence the targets are alternating. A congruence \(X^{\mathsf T}B_iX=B_iT_h\) yields the adjoint partner \(T_hX^{-1}\), so \(X=T_a\) and \(a^*a=h\). Every norm solution gives that congruence. All dimensions and the number of forms are polynomial. The reverse polynomial-time reduction follows from the left-right equivalence construction above. This identifies exactly the norm problem whose polynomial-time solution would settle the binary alternating case.

The same cycle reduces general ordered tuples to alternating tuples over any field. Encode each original matrix as the \(13\) block of a new marker, with its negative transpose in the \(31\) block. The four pairing markers force blocks 1 and 3 to carry the same map \(A\), so congruence on this pair of blocks is precisely the original equation \(A^{\mathsf T}M_i A=M'_i\). Thus the general and alternating binary cases have the same polynomial-time status. In combination with field transfer, every fixed finite characteristic-two field in T10 has the same status as arbitrary binary involutive-algebra norm membership.

## Ordered quadratic tuples have the same complexity

Represent each quadratic form \(q_i\) by \(x^{\mathsf T}B_i x\) with \(B_i\) upper triangular, adding the zero form \(i=0\). On \(V\oplus\bigoplus_j Z_j\), with each \(Z_j\) another copy of \(V\), encode it by the bilinear form
\[
F_i((x,z),(y,w))=x^{\mathsf T}B_i y+x^{\mathsf T}w_i-z_i^{\mathsf T}y.
\]
Both radicals are \(\bigoplus_{j\ne i}Z_j\). Their intersections distinguish each \(Z_j\), so any tuple isometry acts by a common invertible \(A\) on the main quotient \(V\), invertible \(D_j\) on \(Z_j\), and blocks \(R_j\) from \(V\) to \(Z_j\). The cross pairing forces \(D_j=A^{-\mathsf T}\). The main block equation is \(A^{\mathsf T}B_i A+A^{\mathsf T}R_i-R_i^{\mathsf T}A=B'_i\). A suitable \(R_i\) exists exactly when the discrepancy has skew transpose and zero diagonal, since every such matrix can be written as \(S-S^{\mathsf T}\) by choosing its strict upper triangle. This is precisely the condition that \(A\) transports the quadratic form. Apply the bilinear-to-alternating construction and prime-field transfer to obtain a binary alternating tuple.

In the reverse direction, use quadratic markers \(x_i^{\mathsf T}x_j\) on the four cycle edges. Their polars are the pairing markers in characteristic two, so they enforce the same pattern of a block and its inverse transpose. For each original bilinear matrix \(M_i\) add \(x_1^{\mathsf T} M_i x_3\), whose preservation gives the original ordered congruence. Both constructions have polynomial size. Empty tuples and zero-dimensional inputs are decided directly. This gives the equivalence of the polynomial-time questions.

## Consequence for counting group epimorphisms

If binary ordered tuple isometry is in P, exact Cayley-table epimorphism counting into targets of class at most two and exponent dividing four is in P for each fixed bound on their Frattini rank. First quotient the source by fourth powers and third commutators. This gives a bijection on homomorphisms to such targets. For each of the resulting groups \(G\), \(W_G=G^2[G,G]\) is central elementary abelian. Class two and exponent four give commutators of exponent at most two by expanding \((xy)^4\), and hence squares are central. Write \(V_G=G/W_G\) and let \(q_G:V_G\to W_G\) take squares. Its polarization is the commutator.

Write \(S,H\) for the source quotient and target. Enumerate onto linear maps \(b:W_S\to W_H\), at most \(|S|^h\) for \(h=\dim W_H\). For each \(b\) count onto linear \(A:V_S\to V_H\) satisfying \(q_H A=b q_S\). Set \(q'_S=bq_S\). For either quadratic map let \(R\) be the intersection of its polar radical and its zero locus. This is a subspace. An onto transporter pulls back \(R_H\) exactly to \(R_S\). Hence the induced quotient map is an isometry of ordered quadratic tuples. If their dimensions agree and there are \(I\) such isometries, the number of onto linear transporters is
\[
I\,2^{u r_H}\prod_{j=0}^{r_H-1}(2^{r_S}-2^j),
\]
where \(u\) is the common quotient dimension and \(r_S,r_H\) are the radical dimensions. This counts quotient isometries, arbitrary maps from a quotient complement to \(R_H\), and onto maps between radicals. If \(r_S<r_H\) the count is zero.

The quadratic-to-bilinear gadget handles vector marks. On the dummy \(Z_0\) block add a rank-one matrix \(vv^{\mathsf T}\) for each marked vector \(v\). The action there is \(A^{-\mathsf T}\), so over \(\mathbb F_2\) equality of the two rank-one forms enforces exact vector transport, in the inverse direction on labels. One decision checks whether a quotient isometry exists. If so, \(I\) is the size of the target isometry group on the quotient. Count it by stabilizing a basis one vector at a time and multiplying orbit sizes. Find each orbit by enumerating all vectors and using marked decisions while fixing earlier basis vectors. Enumeration is polynomial in the Cayley-table size.

Each compatible pair \(A,b\) lifts to exactly \(2^{h\dim V_S}\) epimorphisms by independently choosing in \(H\) lifts of images of an ordered basis of \(V_S\). The presentation by these basis lifts and the central \(W_S\) uses their actual square, commutator and central order relations. Collection gives at most \(2^{\dim V_S+\dim W_S}\) words, already realized by distinct elements in \(S\). The quadratic equation and its polar ensure every lift respects the relations. Surjectivity follows on both the quotient and central subgroup. Sum the counts over \(b\). This implication concerns targets of bounded Frattini rank and is conditional on binary tuple isometry.

## A quotient unitary element need not lift

Over \(\mathbb F_4\) let \(\sigma(a)=a^2\), and define a four-dimensional binary algebra on pairs \((a,j)\) by
\[
(a,j)(b,k)=(ab,ak+j\sigma(b)),\qquad
(a,j)^*=(\sigma(a),j+a+\sigma(a)).
\]
The product is associative by expanding the two products of three pairs. The additive map \(d(a)=a+\sigma(a)\) satisfies \(d(ab)=\sigma(b)d(a)+a d(b)\) and \(d(\sigma(a))=d(a)\). These identities give the anti-involution laws. The radical consists of \((0,j)\) and has square zero. The norm of \((a,j)\) equals \((\sigma(a)a,\sigma(a)d(a))\). For \(\mathbb F_4^\times\) the first coordinate is always 1. Only \(a=1\) gives second coordinate zero. Consequently the quotient has three unitary elements and only the identity has a unitary lift. A norm target arising from a primitive \(a\) cannot be reached by any lift of the quotient root 1. The alternating-tuple construction realizes this algebra and target explicitly. The example refutes the rule of choosing an arbitrary quotient root and searching solely within its radical coset.
