# Rational power-oracle elimination and new degree comparisons

## Exact power-oracle elimination with divisions

Over a field of characteristic zero, consider a rational circuit computing a polynomial \(H(X)\). Its oracle calls may be adaptive and take rational functions as arguments. Suppose each call returns an \(a\)-th power of its underlying rational function, using one common exponent \(a\). For fixed circuit size, input arity and \(\deg H\), there is a uniform bound on \(a\) above which all oracle calls can be replaced by scalar constants. This preserves the rational circuit size and depth. All divisions are understood to be legal rational-function operations.

Cut the oracle outputs into formal variables \(Y\). By the usual fraction construction the ordinary output is \(R(X,Y)/S(X,Y)\), with polynomial numerator and denominator and logarithmic degree bounded by a polynomial in the original circuit size. The actual values of the oracle variables are \(G_j(X)^a\). For oracles identically zero assign their variables zero. Expanding \(R-HS\) after the other substitutions gives a relation \(\sum_i p_i(X)g_i(X)^a=0\), with rational bases \(g_i\) equal to products of the nonzero \(G_j\). Combine bases that differ by scalar factors, absorbing their \(a\)-th powers into the coefficients. The number of surviving terms and their coefficient degrees are bounded by the outer skeleton size and \(\deg H\), independently of the actual oracle-query degrees.

For a contradiction suppose there is a nonzero relation after this grouping. Restrict to a generic affine line that preserves nonzero coefficients and ensures all distinct base ratios remain nonconstant. This is possible over an infinite field since nonproportional rational functions take different ratios at a suitable pair of points. Clear one common denominator from all bases and remove their common polynomial gcd. Choose a minimal linear dependence among the remaining polynomial terms \(p_i g_i^a\), of length \(r\), and again remove a common base gcd for this sublist. Every Wronskian of \(r-1\) terms obtained by omitting one column is nonzero, and these Wronskians differ by nonzero scalar factors. This follows from minimal dependence and the characteristic-zero Wronskian criterion, which can be seen by changing an independent polynomial basis to one with distinct degrees and taking its leading Vandermonde determinant.

At any root of a base, omit a column whose base does not vanish there, possible because the gcd is 1. The derivative product rule then shows that the common Wronskian is divisible by every local factor of \(\prod_i g_i^{a-r+2}\), and hence by the whole product. For an upper degree bound omit instead a column whose base has maximal degree \(D>0\). Comparing the lower and upper degrees gives
\[
aD\le(r-2)\sum_i\deg g_i+(r-1)\max_i\deg p_i.
\]
This is impossible once \(a>r(r-2)+(r-1)\max_i\deg p_i\). Thus every projective group of original bases has identically vanishing combined coefficient.

Choose one scalar point \(X_0\) where the original rational gates are defined and every nonzero base is nonzero. Replace each oracle by its value at \(X_0\). The grouped cancellations still hold because evaluation preserves each scalar proportionality within a projective group. The stripped circuit still has denominator nonzero, since at \(X_0\) its values reproduce all the original gate values. Therefore it computes \(H\).

For unrestricted depth, an ordinary rational circuit for a degree-bounded polynomial converts to a division-free circuit with polynomial overhead by truncated division after a scalar shift. In constant depth with polynomial wire size, there is a constant-depth version of this operation. Construct the numerator and denominator circuits via fractions, keeping polynomial degree and polynomial size at constant depth. After a shift write the denominator as its nonzero constant term times \(1-E\), with \(E(0)=0\). Replace its inverse by the geometric sum through the target degree, using parallel products. Substitute a marker \(tX\) in the resulting rational-free expression and extract the required low homogeneous components by interpolation of a polynomial of polynomial degree in \(t\). This uses polynomially many scalar nodes and adds constant depth. Thus the previous exact determinant-extraction counterexample \(f_n=\det_n^{\,2^{2^n}}\) still works when divisions and rational nested queries are allowed. The same argument applies to high powers of the Pfaffian. A Pfaffian of polynomially growing size specializes up to sign to a comparably sized determinant by using the skew off-diagonal block matrix. The ordinary characteristic-zero constant-depth determinant lower bound therefore applies. The division-allowed black-box factor strengthening with matching-index oracle likewise fails by choosing sufficiently high powers of multilinear polynomials of superpolynomial arithmetic complexity. [Prior determinant conjecture](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol362-itcs2026/html/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.49/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.49.html) · [Determinant lower bound](https://pure.itu.dk/files/92949831/column.pdf) · [Factor conjecture variants](https://eatcs.org/beatcs/index.php/beatcs/article/download/620/633)

## Power elimination with queries at several indices

Use the rational multilinear hard family \(h_j\) from the next section and let \(L_j=2^{2^j}\), \(a_j=2^{L_j}\). Suppose circuits of size at most \(n^c\) use power oracles \(h_j^{a_j}\) at indices of polynomially bounded arity to recover \(h_n\). Choose a constant \(K\) sufficiently large in terms of the circuit-size and arity bounds and let \(J\) be the largest index with \(L_J\le n^K\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), such a \(J\) exists and \(L_J>n^{K/2}\) because \(L_{J+1}=L_J^2\).

Inline all oracles at indices at most \(J\) using their monomial expansions and repeated squaring. This uses polynomial size since \(2^J=\log_2 L_J\) and the number of squarings is at most \(L_J\). To bound the degree of the fraction representing the resulting outer skeleton, cut all higher-index calls and treat a lower-index call as a single polynomial operation of total degree at most \(J2^{L_J}\). Substitution of rational functions of fraction degree at most \(M\) into a polynomial operation of degree \(D\) and arity \(r\) yields fraction degree at most \(rDM\): use the product of their denominators to the power \(D\). The other gates increase fraction degree by at most a polynomial factor in their fan-in. The resulting logarithmic fraction degree is bounded by \(n^{O(1)}(1+L_J)\), where the polynomial exponent depends on \(c\) and the arity bound and is independent of \(K\). The same bound holds for the logarithm of the term count in the power-elimination argument, since the formal variables are just the inputs and the cut calls.

All remaining oracle exponents are integer multiples of \(a_{J+1}\). Treat each actual high-index answer as a power with this common exponent by raising its base to the corresponding quotient first. The logarithm \(L_J^2\) of the common exponent dominates the logarithmic Wronskian threshold for \(K\) large enough. Elimination removes the remaining oracles. The resulting polynomial-size rational circuit can be made division-free since \(h_n\) has degree at most \(n\). This contradicts the choice of \(h_n\). The common exponent hypothesis matters because adjacent powers \(h^a,h^{a+1}\) recover nonzero \(h\) by a single division.

## An exact partial order inside a principal ideal

Use matching-index exact oracle circuits and allow unrestricted oracle degree. Choose rational multilinear \(h_n\) in \(n\) variables with superpolynomial arithmetic circuit size by coefficient-space dimension counting. Put \(a_n=2^{2^n}\). In the principal ideal \((h_n)\) with one extra variable \(z\), encode any subset \(A\) of the positive integers by
\[
F^A_n(X,z)=h_n(X)^{a_n}+{\bf1}_{n\in A}\,z h_n(X).
\]
Then \(F^A\) reduces to \(F^B\) exactly when \(A\setminus B\) is finite. In the forward construction for almost inclusion, on indices shared by the two sets reuse the oracle, on indices not in \(A\) set \(z=0\), and hardwire the finitely many exceptions. Conversely at any index in \(A\setminus B\) a purported reduction would recover \(h_n\) by evaluating its output at \(z=1\) and \(z=0\) and subtracting. The input oracle at that index has no \(z\) dependence and is only the high power. Power elimination and ordinary division removal would then give small arithmetic circuits for \(h_n\). The contradiction holds along any infinite sequence of such indices because the \(h_n\) can be chosen with the same exponential lower bound at all sufficiently large \(n\).

This realizes inclusion modulo finite differences exactly among degrees in one principal ideal family. It contains a copy of every countable partial order: assign disjoint infinite sets of integers to its elements and map an element to the union of the sets assigned to all elements below it. An almost-disjoint family indexed by the infinite binary sequences also gives a continuum of incomparable degrees, by taking the coded initial segments of each sequence. These are claims about this degree-unrestricted matching-index order. The displayed family does not assert that such a principal ideal has continuum many *minimal* degrees, nor does it concern the degree-bounded-only order. [Grochow, ideal circuit degrees](https://eatcs.org/beatcs/index.php/beatcs/article/download/620/633)

## Continuum many minimal degrees with polynomial degree

The degree bound can be retained in a two-generated prime ideal over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\). At stage \(n\) choose two multilinear polynomials \(A_n(Y),B_n(Y)\) in \(n\) variables with all coefficients jointly algebraically independent, including across stages. Use the prime ideal generated by \(u-A_n(Y),v-B_n(Y)\). For each set of stages choose the first or second generator according to membership in the set. Two choices that differ infinitely often are incomparable under exact polynomial-size oracle reduction, even if queries at other polynomially bounded indices are allowed. On a differing stage the \(2^n\) coefficients of the required block are independent over the field of all coefficients of the queried generator family. Polynomially many additional circuit constants cannot supply them. Choices differing only finitely often are equivalent by hardwiring.

To verify minimality, suppose an eventually nonzero polynomially bounded ideal family reduces to one selected generator family. At a stage when that family selected \(u-A_n\), substitute \(u=A_n(Y)\) in the lower family member. The resulting polynomial lies over the field of all selected-block coefficients together with polynomially many additional scalars, and vanishes at \(v=B_n(Y)\). If it were nonzero, choose a rational two-point grid in each \(Y\)-coordinate so that at all \(2^n\) grid points it remains nonzero as a polynomial in \(v\). Such a grid exists because the product of the selected nonzero coefficient evaluations and the pairwise differences is a nonzero polynomial in the grid choices. The values of \(B_n\) on this grid are independent over the selected-block field, by invertible multilinear interpolation. The equation would make every one of the \(2^n\) values algebraic over a field extension of polynomial transcendence degree, a contradiction. Thus \(u-A_n\) divides the lower family member, and the symmetric argument works on the other stages.

The selected generator reduces back to that lower family because the latter has polynomial degree. For the monic factor \(u-A_n(Y)\), specialize unused output coordinates at scalars preserving its multiplicity. Use polynomial-degree interpolation to take one fewer \(u\)-derivative than that multiplicity. The resulting polynomial has simple root \(A_n(Y)\). Choose a scalar center where this root is simple, hardwire its value there, and lift in a directional parameter through order \(n\). Each coefficient is the current residual coefficient divided by the fixed nonzero derivative at the center. Polynomial-degree interpolation extracts the residual coefficients, sharing prior stages. Setting the directional parameter to 1 recovers \(A_n\) and thus its selected generator. This proves minimality. There are continuum many classes of stage choices modulo finite change, yielding continuum many incomparable minimal degrees in one two-generated prime ideal family. This applies to the polynomially bounded, eventually nonzero order in the earlier ideal-degree work. [Earlier finite and countable cases](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/IdealCircuitDegrees)

## A strict IPS degree-size tradeoff for polynomial systems

There is an explicit linear versus logarithmic separation for general circuits, over every field, when the inputs are unsatisfiable polynomial systems rather than ordinary deduplicated CNFs. Use \(N\) variables and equations
\[
P=x_1^D,\qquad R=1-x_1,\qquad Q_i=x_i-x_{i+1}\ (1\le i<N),\qquad Q_N=x_N,
\]
with \(D\) a power of two between \(N^2\) and \(2N^2\). With equation placeholders \(y\), the degree-one certificate \(y_R+\sum_i y_{Q_i}\) has size \(O(N)\). The degree-\(D\) certificate \(y_P+y_R(1+x_1+\cdots+x_1^{D-1})\) has size \(O(\log N)\) by the doubling geometric-series identity.

For Geometric IPS, which uses only placeholders, the same low-degree certificate works and the high-degree certificate is \(1-(1-y_R)^D+y_P\). After substituting the equations its value is 1. The lower bound below also applies to this placeholder-only model.

Every certificate of total degree less than \(D\) uses each \(y_{Q_j}\). Otherwise set the first \(j\) variables equal to one free variable \(x\) and the rest to zero. Every used \(Q_i\) vanishes, so the result is a certificate involving only \(x^D\) and \(1-x\). The part involving the \(P\)-placeholder vanishes modulo \(x^D\) when the equations are substituted. The other part, after substituting \(1-x\), has degree less than \(D\). It must therefore equal 1 identically. At \(x=1\) it is zero by the IPS requirement at zero placeholders, a contradiction. Thus every degree-at-most-\(N\) certificate has size at least \(N\) under input-node counting or bounded-fan-in wire counting. This is a polynomial-input nontrivial size separation for the general polynomial version of the Grochow–Pitassi question. It leaves the ordinary CNF and superpolynomial firm lower-bound regimes separate. These are informal proofs. [Grochow–Pitassi, Question 7.2](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~toni/Papers/jacm-gp.pdf#page=40)
