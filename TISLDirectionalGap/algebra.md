# Directional signs, complement closure, and a witnessed subgroup test

Work over \(F=\mathbb F_3\) and write determinant signs additively in \(V=(\mathbb F_2)^3\), with \(0\) meaning three positive determinants. Write \(A_v(T,U)\) for feasibility of determinant vector \(v\), \(A=A_0\), and \(B\) for ordinary tensor isomorphism. Then \(H\) is \(A\le_m^P B\). A target frame reflection \(r_v\) shifts the profile by \(v\). The promise for
\[
D_1=A_{e_1}\wedge \neg A_0
\]
is \(B\). The complementary rail on this promise is \(\neg D_1\). Let \(H_{\rm w}\) denote one ordinary many-one query for \(A\) when the input includes one verified ordinary transporter, and let \(S_1(T)\) ask whether \(e_1\) occurs among the determinants of ordinary self-transporters of \(T\).

**Results.** A single deterministic polynomial-time ordinary TI compilation of either directional rail on the ordinary-YES promise is equivalent to \(H\) **and** \(\overline B\le_m^P B\). When the promise additionally supplies a verified ordinary witness, the exact condition is \(H_{\rm w}\) **and** \(\overline B\le_m^P B\). The positive subgroup test \(S_1\le_m^P B\) is equivalent to \(H_{\rm w}\) with no complement assumption. These are equivalences of reduction statements, so the directional compiler would fail in the strict branch. There is also an unconditional single-query solver on a matrix-multiplicity subclass of raw multiplication isotopes, and a complement-GI gate inside the square-zero-radical subclass. All proofs are informal. [Original SL-TI question and tensor-system compiler](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2) · [Classical tensor and algebra comparisons](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

## 1. Two exact sign encodings

Use the atlas's weighted symmetric cube-zero character compiler in *CubeZeroAndExtensions.md*. Given a tensor \(T\) and binary character \(\chi:V\to\mathbb F_2\), it produces a commutative unital scalar-residue local algebra \(C_\chi(T)\). All relative algebra determinants on this pair are precisely \((-1)^{\chi(v)}\), for the ordinary transporter vectors \(v\) of \(T,U\). The empty profile is also preserved. Every actual ordinary tensor transporter has an explicit blockwise algebra lift. The construction comes in both even- and odd-dimensional versions, denoted \(C_\chi^+\) and \(C_\chi^-\) below, where the superscripts refer to dimension parity. In the weighted construction with main-role dimension \(D\), main output dimension \(Q\), and trace-cycle marker count \(b\), choose the parity of \(D\) and use \(b\equiv D\bmod 2\), \(Q\) even. The normalized pin contribution and common marker contribution are then both \(+1\), as are the even-dimensional trace block contributions. The algebra dimension \(1+2D+Q+b+\sum 4r_i\) has the opposite parity to \(D\). An extra pure pin and the reserve products adjust \(D,Q\), and unused markers adjust \(b\). The reflection and exact lifts in the weighted carrier are unchanged. This odd-dimensional variant was proved in *CyclicSlices.md*, Section 3.

For multiplication tensors of unital associative algebras, evaluating an isotopy on each unit gives
\[
(P,Q,R)=(L_a\phi,R_b\phi,L_aR_b\phi)
\]
for units \(a,b\) in the target algebra and an algebra isomorphism \(\phi\). Conversely all such choices give isotopies. The product of their three determinants is \(\det\phi\), as the left and right unit determinants cancel and \((\det\phi)^2=1\). For a scalar-residue local algebra \(C\), a unit of residue \(s\) has determinant \(s^{\dim C}\), by the radical filtration. Define \(d=(1,1,1)\) and \(E_0=\{v:v_1+v_2+v_3=0\}\) in sign-bit notation.

* For even-dimensional \(C_\chi^+\), the sign profile of the normalized multiplication pair is exactly the set of \(\chi(v)d\). Both left and right unit determinant signs are necessarily \(+1\).
* For odd-dimensional \(C_\chi^-\), the signs of left and right units are independently arbitrary. They generate \(E_0\); the product character of the three signs is the algebra sign. Thus on a self-pair, \(e_1\) occurs if and only if an odd algebra automorphism exists, which holds exactly when \(\chi\) is nonzero on the original stabilizer spectrum.

The formulas are invariant under usual bimap versus tensor inverse conventions because all signs are self-inverse.

## 2. Directional test necessity, including the positive rail without complement closure

Fix \(\chi\) and replace \(T,U\) by the two normalized multiplication tensors of \(C_\chi^+(T),C_\chi^+(U)\). Reflect the first target mode. Every determinant vector now lies in \(e_1+\{0,d\}\), which misses \(0\). Therefore \(D_1\) is precisely feasibility of the prescribed character \(\chi=0\) (as a bit) on the original tensor pair. If the original pair is ordinary YES, so is this pair. If an ordinary witness is supplied, the prescribed algebra lift and reflection construct an explicit ordinary witness for the query.

For each of the seven nonzero characters make that single query. On an ordinary YES, the sign set is a coset \(c+S\), and \(0\in c+S\) exactly when every character takes value zero somewhere on it. If \(0\notin c+S\), a character of \(V/S\) is nonzero on its nonzero coset. Thus a one-query compilation of the promised \(D_1\), followed by ordinary AND with an ordinary guard for \(B\), implies \(H\); the guard rejects any off-promise behavior on a NO. In the witness-supplied model the same proof implies \(H_{\rm w}\), using a witness check instead of the ordinary guard when desired. This strengthens the necessity direction of the earlier atlas C43 by removing any assumption of ordinary complement closure. [Fixed-arity ordinary tensor systems](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2)

## 3. Two separate complement gates with explicit ordinary witnesses

Let \(T,U\) be any input to ordinary TI. Preprocess mismatched or zero formats, whose ordinary answers are immediate, by replacing them with a fixed ordinary YES or NO example of matching positive dimensions. The zero character in the odd-dimensional carrier produces sign-rigid commutative local algebras \(C_0^-(T),C_0^-(U)\). They are ordinarily isomorphic exactly when \(T,U\) are ordinarily isomorphic. Every factor isomorphism is even. Put
\[
 P=C_0^-(T)\times C_0^-(U).
\]
Its central primitive idempotents are exactly the two local factors. A factor-preserving automorphism is even. A swap can occur precisely when the factors are isomorphic, and then a swap with any isomorphism and its inverse has sign \(-1\) because the blocks are odd-dimensional. Thus \(P\) has an odd algebra automorphism exactly when the ordinary input is YES. Both regular unit signs on \(P\) are surjective, since negating one of the odd factors gives a unit of determinant \(-1\). These statements concern actual maps, including all possible central component permutations.

For the **positive rail** use \((\mu_P,r_{e_1}\mu_P)\) with the displayed ordinary reflection witness. The determinant group before reflection is \(E_0\) when the original ordinary input is NO and all of \(V\) when it is YES. Thus \(A_{e_1}\) always holds after reflection, while \(A_0\) holds exactly in the YES case. Hence this map sends \(\overline B\) exactly to the positive rail.

For the **negative rail**, feed the single tensor \(\mu_P\) through the even-dimensional weighted character carrier for \(\chi(v)=v_1+v_2+v_3\). Its encoded normalized multiplication tensor \(M\) has self-spectrum \(\{0\}\) when the ordinary input is NO, and \(\{0,d\}\) when YES. This follows from cancellation of the units in the product character. The output is \((M,r_{e_1+d}M)\), with its explicit reflection witness. Its profile never contains \(0\), and contains \(e_1\) exactly in the original YES case. Thus \(D_1\) there is exactly the ordinary answer, and \(\neg D_1\) is exactly \(\overline B\). No extra typed-system character compiler is required in this step.

Consequently an ordinary compilation of **either** promised rail implies \(\overline B\le_m^P B\), even with the stronger input convention requiring an explicit ordinary witness. Once complement closure is available, a compiler for either rail gives one for the other just by complementing its ordinary query's answer through that reduction. Section 2 then supplies \(H\) or \(H_{\rm w}\) in the respective models. Conversely, under the two stated premises, implement \(A_{e_1}\) and \(A_0\) on the pair and its reflected pair, and use complement closure and ordinary AND or OR. Witnesses for the reflected queries are obtained by applying the same fixed reflections to the given ordinary witness. This proves both directions of the stated equivalences.

The positive subgroup test on the two-factor product gate is simply the original ordinary isomorphism comparison. On arbitrary displayed products of sign-rigid commutative local factors, an odd algebra automorphism is present exactly if a pair of ordinarily isomorphic odd-dimensional factors can swap. This is a positive polynomial-size OR of ordinary factor comparisons, yielding one ordinary TI test for subgroup membership whenever the right unit sign allows the tested direction. The co-\(B\) gate thus persists on the same instances for which the positive subgroup test already has one ordinary compilation.

## 4. The exact witnessed subgroup theorem

For a verified ordinary witness \(g_0:T\to U\), let \(v\) be its determinant vector and let \(S\le V\) be the source self-spectrum. The full relative sign profile is \(v+S\), so the signed answer is \(v\in S\). The condition \(v\in S\) is equivalent to: for every \(\chi\) with \(\chi(v)=1\), some element of \(S\) has \(\chi\)-value \(1\). This is annihilator duality in \(V\): if \(v\notin S\), a separating character is zero on \(S\) and is one at \(v\).

For each relevant \(\chi\), form the odd-dimensional \(C_\chi^-(T)\) and query \(S_1\) on its normalized multiplication tensor. Section 1 identifies the resulting positive answer precisely with \(\chi(S)\ne0\). A fixed positive AND therefore converts a compiler for \(S_1\) into \(H_{\rm w}\). If \(v=0\) the sign witness already works. Conversely \(S_1(T)\) asks for signed isomorphism of \((T,r_{e_1}T)\) with the explicit reflection witness, hence follows from \(H_{\rm w}\). There is no complement premise. A full ordinary-YES promise by itself does not supply \(g_0\); this equivalence concerns the witness-augmented problem. Also \(B\le_m^P S_1\) by the product swap in Section 3, and \(S_1\le_m^P A\) by the reflected self-pair.

A positive spectrum predicate such as rank at least \(r\) is a finite positive disjunction of conjunctions of prescribed subgroup memberships, since \(V\) has dimension three. Once the witnessed subgroup compiler exists, direction separation above expresses every such rank threshold as one ordinary query. In contrast, *exact* rank zero on the diagonalized swap or exact rank two on the full-unit swap both encode \(\overline B\). This distinguishes access to positive subgroup membership from access to exclusion even on highly structured local factors.

## 5. Two unconditional structured reductions

### Raw multiplication isotopes with even common residue size

For arbitrary raw associative multiplication isotopes, take normalization maps to common multiplication frames, with raw relative calibration signs \(a=\det K_1/\det K'_1\), \(b=\det K_2/\det K'_2\). Let \(C_L,C_R\) be the left and right unit determinant images of the target, and \(R_{\rm iso}\) its possible relative algebra determinants. The attainable raw signs are \((aur,bvr,uvr)\) for \(u\in C_L,v\in C_R,r\in R_{\rm iso}\). Solving the two sign targets gives
\[
 D_1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 b\in C_L,\quad -a\in C_R,\quad -ab\in R_{\rm iso},\quad
 \big(a\notin C_R\ \lor\ ab\notin R_{\rm iso}\big).
\]
In particular with \(C_R=\{1\}\) the directional test on the eligible calibration only uses a **positive** relative sign test. With \(C_R=\{\pm1\}\) it requires the odd relative sign and missing opposite sign, rather than just the existence of an odd witness. The unit determinants and raw normalizations are computed by the standard regular module and finite-algebra algorithms, as in entries 82–87 of the 234 atlas. [Brooksbank–Luks, constructive module isomorphism](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf) · [Ivanyos–Qiao, finite-algebra decomposition](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

If the common divisor of the residue matrix sizes of the normalized algebras is even, align lifted matrix units and pass to a first full matrix corner. Every aligned algebra isomorphism is entrywise on a corner, so its determinant before frame calibration is an even power and equals \(+1\). Undoing alignment multiplies by an actual inner determinant, whose image is computed from the left/right multiplicity differences. Therefore, on ordinary YES, \(R_{\rm iso}\) is exactly a computed sign \(\kappa\) times that inner image. Evaluate the displayed numerical tests and, if they pass, output one ordinary TI comparison of the corner multiplication tensors. If a test fails, output fixed NO. An ordinary NO remains NO by corner reflection. The same numerical evaluation is possible whenever a computed subgroup of actual algebra automorphism determinants is full, in which case \(R_{\rm iso}\) on ordinary YES is both signs. This is an unconditional one-query regime with unrestricted radical length.

### A complement-GI gate with square-zero radical

For a finite simple graph \(G\), construct a split associative algebra \(D(G)\) with \(J^2=0\). Use a unique hub factor of matrix size one, one matrix size two factor per vertex, and one matrix size four factor per edge. Include two copies of the natural ordered bimodule from each edge factor to each of its two vertex factors, and two copies from the hub to each vertex factor. The radical product is zero. A primitive central idempotent of the full algebra must select a union of components of the incidence pattern: commuting with the nonzero corners forces equality at their endpoints. The hub attaches all vertex and edge factors, so the algebra is centrally indecomposable. The graph can be recovered from the type-preserving residue permutations and the bimodule multiplicities. Conversely an incidence isomorphism gives an algebra isomorphism by basis transport on all displayed factors. Ordinary comparison therefore exactly reflects graph isomorphism.

This algebra has odd dimension, since only the uniquely fixed hub factor is odd-dimensional and every radical block is even-dimensional. All automorphisms are even. The non-hub simple factors and the radical blocks permute with no sign. Bimodule automorphism determinants on each block are raised to the product of its endpoint matrix sizes, which is even. All quotient inner determinants are even because the differences of natural left/right multiplicities are even, by the doubled incidences. Radical unit conjugation is unipotent. These exhaust the automorphisms by aligning the residue section, which is the usual split square-zero bimodule classification. Both left and right unit signs are full by scaling the hub: it has odd left and right regular multiplicities, again by the even incidence multiplicities.

For a graph comparison use \(D(G)\times D(G')\) in the positive-rail gate of Section 3, with the product's square-zero radical. Central blocks swap exactly when the graphs are ordinarily isomorphic, supplying an odd sign precisely in that case. Thus \(\overline{\rm GI}\) many-one reduces to directional comparison of one-reflection self-pairs of multiplication tensors with square-zero radical and an explicit ordinary witness. On this exact subfamily the reverse is the displayed graph comparison. This is a complement-GI gate in the radical-square-zero setting, distinct from the co-\(B\) gate, which uses cube-zero local factors. [Graph-to-tensor comparison context](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

## 6. A verifier consequence for either rail

Either promised rail is in PromiseNP if and only if \(A\in\mathrm{coNP}\). For sufficiency, each rail is a fixed AND or OR of a signed YES and a signed NO test. For necessity, both complement gates give \(\overline B\in\mathrm{NP}\). On an ordinary YES signed-NO input, some character \(\chi\) never takes zero. With the negative rail this has a certificate by the diagonal shift in Section 2 and an ordinary witness ensuring its promise. With the positive rail, guess a relative transporter with \(\chi\)-value one, and certify that \(\chi\) vanishes on the source stabilizer by the positive directional test on the one-reflection self-pair of \(\mu_{C_\chi^-}\). Section 1 makes this last gap precisely the absence of an odd algebra automorphism. The reflected self-pair is always ordinary YES. The guessed relative sign and this certificate enforce that \(\chi\) takes one everywhere on the original transporter coset. Ordinary NO inputs are handled by the separate \(\overline B\) certificate. Thus in both cases \(\overline A\in\mathrm{NP}\).

These proofs separate the complement and witness assumptions precisely. They give no algorithm establishing \(H\), \(\overline B\le_m B\), or \(H_{\rm w}\).
