# Directional TI and deterministic small-mode reductions

Work over \(F=\mathbf F_3\), with based, dense three-tensors and independent changes of basis in their modes. Write determinant signs additively in \(V=(\mathbf F_2)^3\). For a pair \(x\), let \(C(x)\subseteq V\) be its exact ordinary transporter sign set, \(B(x)=[C(x)\ne\varnothing]\), and \(A(x)=[0\in C(x)]\). Let \(r_1x\) reflect one coordinate of the target in mode 1, so \(C(r_1x)=C(x)+e_1\). The directional promise problem is \(D_1(x)=[e_1\in C(x),\,0\notin C(x)]\) on \(B(x)=1\). The compilers below use the standard positive-format encoding. Incompatible or degenerate zero-mode formats can be handled by their immediate decision cases. These are written proofs.

## Exact characterization of a directional compiler

**Theorem.** A single deterministic ordinary TI compiler for \(D_1\) on \(B\)-positive inputs exists if and only if both \(A\le_m^P B\) and \(\overline B\le_m^P B\). The first implication uses only concise equal-mode tensors of even dimension with profile contained in the fixed translate \(e_1+\langle(1,1,1)\rangle\). The second uses concise pairs \((S,r_1S)\) with a supplied ordinary reflector and with profile either full or one affine coordinate hyperplane.

### Previously established ingredients

The weighted cube-zero construction in the earlier atlas applies to a constant signature of typed linear and bilinear maps. For each typed space choose a weight in \(\mathbf F_2\). It constructs, in polynomial time, a based scalar-residue commutative local algebra \(R=F1\oplus X\oplus Z\) of even dimension with \(J^3=0\), preserving the exact set of values of the weighted determinant character on typed isomorphisms. Here \(J=X\oplus Z\). Distinct bilinear blocks have distinct unordered input-role pairs, and repeated-input blocks are symmetric. A non-symmetric repeated-input block can instead use an independent role tied to the original by a typed identity. In particular, this construction applies to a raw three-mode tensor, with any prescribed character \(\chi:V\to\mathbf F_2\), and to the switch signature below. The proof normalizes all variable-role maps by one common pin scalar and assigns one or two output copies according to the sign weight. In the unmarked construction it adds marker blocks to recover every role, weight-cancels their determinants and those of the role duals, and adjusts the algebra dimension to be even. [Earlier atlas, CubeZeroAndExtensions, §§2–4](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/TISLAtlas200)

For such an \(R\), its multiplication tensor is concise. Its ordinary isotopisms are \((L_a\phi,R_b\phi,L_aR_b\phi)\) in bimap notation, with \(\phi\) a unital algebra isomorphism and \(a,b\) units of the target algebra. Indeed \(a=f(1)\), \(b=g(1)\) satisfy \(h(x)=f(x)b=a g(x)\) for an isotopism \((f,g,h)\), so both multipliers are units and \(a^{-1}f(x)=g(x)b^{-1}\) gives \(\phi\). Each unit is \(\lambda 1+j\) with \(j\in J\). Its regular multiplier has determinant \(\lambda^{\dim R}=1\), since the nilpotent summand contributes a unipotent factor. Thus all three tensor determinants equal \(\det\phi\), including in the covariant tensor convention because inverses and inverse transposes preserve signs. A finite conjunction of ordinary TI comparisons is a single ordinary comparison by gathering distinct named factors into a tensor system and compiling the constant signature. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2)

### A directional compiler implies the full signed compiler

For each of the seven nonzero characters \(\chi\in V^*\), use the weighted construction on a raw comparison \(x\), and let \(X_\chi\) denote the resulting pair of multiplication tensors. By the previous observations,
\[
 C(X_\chi)=\{(\chi(v),\chi(v),\chi(v)):v\in C(x)\}.
\]
A diagonal profile never contains \(e_1\), so \(D_1(r_1 X_\chi)=[0\in C(X_\chi)]\). If \(x\) is ordinary YES, all seven calls are on the directional promise because the weighted construction preserves emptiness. A nonempty transporter profile is a coset \(v_0+U\) of the stabilizer image \(U\). It contains 0 exactly when every nonzero \(\chi\) takes value 0 somewhere on the coset: if \(v_0\notin U\), extend a basis of \(U\) by \(v_0\) to construct \(\chi\) vanishing on \(U\) and satisfying \(\chi(v_0)=1\). Compile the seven directional calls and conjoin their ordinary outputs with the original \(B(x)\) query. When \(B(x)=0\), this guard makes the result NO regardless of the behavior of the promised compiler on all off-promise images. Hence \(A\le_m^P B\).

### A directional compiler also implies an ordinary complement compiler

For an ordinary comparison \(q=(T,U)\), first use the triangular algebra \(Q(T)\). It has three scalar diagonal idempotents, and off-diagonal spaces \(X,Y,Z\) in the positions \(01,12,02\). The only product of two off-diagonal elements is \(X\times Y\to Z\) given by \(T\). Its center is \(F\) because commuting with the diagonal idempotents kills off-diagonal entries and the nonzero adjacent corner spaces equate the three diagonal scalars.

The algebra isomorphisms \(Q(T)\to Q(U)\) recover exactly the ordinary tensor equivalences on the off-diagonal corners after conjugating the lifted diagonal idempotents. The off-diagonal ideal is the radical, with quotient \(F^3\). If \(f_i\) and \(e_i\) are two ordered complete lifts of the same quotient idempotents, \(\sum_i f_i e_i\) is \(1\) modulo the radical, hence a unit, and intertwines the two systems. The directed corner pattern \(0\to1\to2\) then forces the permutation of vertices to be the reference one. Conversely, any ordinary tensor equivalence extends to an algebra isomorphism by fixing each diagonal idempotent. This works with vanishing bilinear map or nonconcise tensors as long as the three declared mode dimensions are positive.

Let \(P=Q(T)\times Q(U)\). In a typed system include \(P\), a right-input copy \(P_R\) tied to \(P\) by an identity, and the multiplication \(P\times P_R\to P\). Include a separate role \(E=F^2\) with coordinatewise multiplication, and the linear link \(E\to P\) carrying the two basis idempotents to the identities of the two factors. A typed automorphism acts on \(E\) as either identity or the swap. The swap extends exactly when \(Q(T)\cong Q(U)\), since the two factor identities are the primitive central idempotents, and in the isomorphic case any chosen isomorphism and its inverse supply the required swap. Give \(E\) sign weight one and both copies of \(P\) weight zero in the weighted construction. Apply it to this system compared with itself. The resulting even-dimensional local algebra has determinant image \(\{0\}\) if \(B(q)=0\), and \(\mathbf F_2\) if \(B(q)=1\). Write \(M\) for its multiplication tensor. Its self-profile is the corresponding diagonal subgroup. [Earlier two-factor switch, LinearSwitchCompiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/TISLAtlas200)

There are two ways to change this to a pure mode-1 toggle. One can pad by one null coordinate in each of modes 2 and 3 only. The intrinsic mode supports require block-upper-triangular matrices in adapted frames, so those two signs can be corrected independently on the null coordinates while the first sign reflects exactly from the original tensor. The following alternative keeps all modes concise.

Define a fixed bimap \(K:F^2\times F^{11}\to F^{11}\). Relative to a decomposition of \(F^{11}\) into four blocks of sizes \(1,2,3,5\), the pencil \(K(x,\cdot)\) acts by the four distinct projective linear forms in \(x\in F^2\) times the four identities. It is concise: the forms span \((F^2)^*\), every block acts invertibly for some \(x\), and the common left and right radicals vanish. Its four distinct projective rank-drop values force the first-mode map of every automorphism to fix all projective points. This map is scalar and has determinant one. Scalar sign changes on modes 1 and 2, or on modes 1 and 3, supply \(e_2,e_3\) because the dimensions are \(2,11,11\). Thus the sign image of \(K\) is exactly \(\langle e_2,e_3\rangle\).

The centroid of \(K\) is \(F\). In a centroid triple, the equality \(K(c_1x,y)=K(x,c_2y)=c_3K(x,y)\) first forces block preservation of \(c_2,c_3\): take \(y\) in one block and compare output components while varying \(x\). Within each block it forces \(f_i\circ c_1=a_i f_i\) and the other maps to be the scalar \(a_i\) there. Three distinct lines \(Ff_i\) already force \(c_1\) scalar, so all \(a_i\) coincide. The centroid of \(M\) is the original commutative local algebra, by evaluating the same centroid identities at the unit. Centroids of concise direct sums split as products: at \(x,y\) from distinct summands, the two centroid expressions lie in disjoint output summands and vanish. Zero radicals eliminate all cross-maps on input modes and spanning products eliminate cross-maps on the output. It follows that the two primitive central projections of \(S=M\oplus K\) are preserved by every automorphism. A swap is impossible because the first-mode ranks of these projections are \(\dim M>2\) and 2. Therefore the self-profile of \(S\) is \(\langle e_2,e_3\rangle\) if \(B(q)=0\) and \(V\) if \(B(q)=1\).

Now set \(y=(S,r_1S)\), where the notation on the right means reflection of the second tensor only. This pair is always ordinary YES with the explicit coordinate reflector as a witness. Its profile is \(e_1+C(S,S)\). In particular \(e_1\) is always attained, and 0 is attained exactly when \(B(q)=1\). Thus \(D_1(y)=\neg B(q)\). One call to the promised compiler yields \(\overline B\le_m^P B\).

### Converse and complexity variants

Given \(A\le_m^P B\) and \(\overline B\le_m^P B\), map \(A(r_1x)\) to ordinary TI, map \(A(x)\) to ordinary TI and apply the complement reduction to that output, then conjoin. This works on every input. In particular a promised directional compiler would put both signed and ordinary TI in \(\mathrm{NP}\cap\mathrm{coNP}\). If ordinary TI were NP-complete under many-one reductions, the promised compiler would therefore be equivalent to \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\), since in that case \(A\le_m^P B\) already holds and the complement reduction is equivalent to ordinary TI lying in coNP.

The same characterization holds separately for polynomial-size advice circuits and for uniform bounded-error randomized one-output reductions. For advice circuits use circuits for all polynomially bounded intermediate lengths and then the uniform ordinary conjunction compiler. For randomized reductions amplify each call to arbitrarily small constant error by compiling an ordinary majority of independent outputs. On promised YES ordinary inputs in the seven-character construction, a union bound makes all mapped answers correct simultaneously. On ordinary NO inputs the original guard makes the output unconditionally NO. The switch for the complement always lands on the promise. For the converse amplify and compose the two randomized compilers, using the fact that each invocation of the complement compiler succeeds with its error guarantee for every possible preceding query string. Majority is a polynomial-size uniformly constructible positive formula, via unfolding a logarithmic-depth sorting network. These arguments are for two-sided error; they do not assert that every composition preserves a particular one-sided error form. [Ajtai–Komlós–Szemerédi sorting network](https://doi.org/10.1145/800061.808726)

For deterministic Cook reducibility, a promise \(D_1\) algorithm with ordinary oracle access exists exactly when \(A\in\mathrm P^B\). The forward proof still uses the seven-character construction with the initial ordinary guard, and the reverse directly asks the two signed membership questions through that algorithm. An ordinary oracle provides its own complement by flipping an answer, so there is no separate complement-degree requirement at this strength.

## Deterministic exact profiles with one small mode or an explicit candidate list

**Theorem.** If a ternary comparison has one intrinsic mode support of dimension \(r\), its complete signature profile can be computed deterministically in time \(3^{r^2}\operatorname{poly}(L)\), where \(L\) is the dense input bit length. In particular a promise \(r=O(\sqrt{\log L})\) gives an unconditional polynomial-time one-query ordinary compiler for \(D_1\), and for \(A\). More generally, a given polynomial-size list containing every realized candidate for one supported mode map gives a polynomial algorithm. For each such map the strict left–right profile is computed by the following deterministic construction; no construction of a negative-norm field element is needed.

### Reduce to supports

The intrinsic support of each mode is the image of its flattening. Unequal source and target support dimensions give ordinary NO. If both tensors are zero their profile is the whole sign group in positive formats. Otherwise, compute framed bases of the supports and complete them to bases of the ambient spaces. Any ambient transporter maps supports to supports, and its determinant in adapted frames is the determinant on the support times the determinant on a complementary block. Every support transporter extends independently in each complement. Thus the sign of a deficient mode is freely correctable, while a full mode has the fixed frame calibration given by the determinant ratio of the target and source basis changes. It suffices to compute all support signs.

### Fix one mode

After any required mode permutation, enumerate the invertible \(r\times r\) matrices on its support or use the supplied candidate list. For a fixed candidate, apply it to the source and obtain two lists of slices \(X_j,Y_j:V_0\to W_0\) in the other modes. The remaining transporter equations can be written
\[
 aX_j=Y_j b
\]
for invertible \(a,b\), because inverse transposition of a mode matrix preserves its determinant sign. Encode this as simultaneous conjugacy on \(W_0\oplus V_0\) using the projection onto \(W_0\) and one upper-right block endomorphism for each slice. The projection forces a block-diagonal intertwiner. Construct one actual left–right solution, or certify its absence, by the deterministic generalized conjugacy/module-isomorphism algorithm. [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, Theorem 2 and Corollary 3](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf)

### Determine the strict stabilizer sign image

Construct the finite-dimensional unital algebra
\[
 R=\{(a,b)\in\operatorname{End}(W_0)\times\operatorname{End}(V_0):aX_j=X_jb\ \forall j\}
\]
by linear algebra. The strict stabilizer is \(R^\times\). Compute its Jacobson radical \(J\) by the deterministic finite-field radical algorithm, and let \(S_0=R/J\). The representations on the two spaces admit the respective \(J\)-power filtrations. The quotient action of \(S_0\) on each grade is well-defined. An element of \(1+J\) acts trivially on all grades, hence has positive determinant on both spaces. The map \(R^\times\to S_0^\times\) is surjective: lifts of a unit and its inverse multiply to units in \(1+J\), giving an invertible lift. [Ivanyos, radical algorithms and constructive module isomorphism](https://real-d.mtak.hu/260/1/Ivanyos_Gabor-dr.pdf)

Find the primitive central idempotents of \(S_0\) explicitly without finding a Wedderburn complement. Compute \(C=Z(S_0)\) by commutator equations. This is a product of finite fields. The \(\mathbf F_3\)-linear condition \(z^3=z\) gives \(C^{\mathrm{Frob}}=\prod_i\mathbf F_3\). Multiplication by a basis of this fixed algebra can be split iteratively into its three possible eigenspaces on \(C\); the nonempty simultaneous eigenspaces are the individual field factors, since the basis separates distinct coordinates in \(C^{\mathrm{Frob}}\). The projection of \(1\) to each summand is its idempotent \(e_i\). Set \(d_i=\dim_F e_i C\) and \(a_i=\sqrt{\dim_F(e_i S_0)/d_i}\), so the simple factor is abstractly \(M_{a_i}(\mathbf F_{3^{d_i}})\).

For either mode \(L_0=W_0,V_0\), compute
\[
 \mu_{i,L_0}=\frac{\sum_k \dim_F e_i(J^k L_0/J^{k+1}L_0)}{a_i d_i}.
\]
These are the total multiplicities of the natural simple module in the graded space. To compute the dimensions, any vector-space lift of \(e_i\) acts on each grade and its rank there is obtained by ordinary Gaussian elimination. On a copy of the simple module the determinant of a matrix \(g\) is \(N_{\mathbf F_{3^{d_i}}/F}(\det g)\) over \(F\). This follows by factoring \(g\) into elementary transvections and one-coordinate scalings: the transvections have determinant one over both fields and the scaling has determinant equal to its norm. Both the determinant on the full matrix factor and the field norm are surjective. It follows that the exact strict stabilizer sign image is
\[
 \operatorname{span}_{\mathbf F_2}\{(\mu_{i,W_0}\bmod 2,\mu_{i,V_0}\bmod 2):i\}.
\]
Thus a successful fixed mode candidate determines its full two-sign coset from the signs of the ordinary witness already constructed. Include the fixed first sign, union over the candidates, then apply the support calibrations and free complement signs. All steps after fixing a candidate are polynomial in \(L\). Enumerating at most \(3^{r^2}\) candidates proves the bound, and reading the two indicated profile bits gives the desired \(D_1\) decision.

Two elementary unconditional zero cases are worth recording. If mode 1 has a nonzero unused complement, a reflection there gives \(e_1\) in the source stabilizer, so no directed mode-1 boundary exists. Also, when mode 1 has odd declared dimension and at least one other mode has even dimension, applying \(-I\) in those two modes fixes the tensor and has determinant vector \(e_1\), with the same conclusion. The small-mode theorem does not require either condition.
