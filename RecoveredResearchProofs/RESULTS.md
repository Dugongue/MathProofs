# Recovered proofs from the research archive

## Published conjectures answered

- **Kaspers–Pott’s design-isomorphism conjecture.** The square-subgroup designs over the Galois ring and finite field are nonisomorphic for every odd prime power $q\ge 7$. At $q=5$ both developments give the complete design of pairs, so the unrestricted claim as printed is false. The note proves the corrected classification by separating unit blocks from ideal blocks through carry polynomials and using the classification of primitive groups with a regular cyclic subgroup for the prime case. The isolated $q=9$ case has a reproducible exact check. [Proof](KaspersPottDesigns.md) · [Kaspers–Pott, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.05848v2#S5)
- **Fang’s collapse-pattern conjectures.** Complements of two-factor subgraphs in a complete bipartite graph refute Conjectures 2.5, 2.7, 2.11, and 2.13. The first two witnesses were recovered from the archive. The edge and directed-edge refutations extend the same construction. The existing Lean certificate proves Conjecture 2.9 false with the same ten-vertex graph used for 2.7. This note gives the rooted calculation, the comparison graph for 2.5, and the edge and directed-edge arguments. [Proof](FangCollapsePatterns.md) · [Fang, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.09230#page=8)

## Additional recovered results

- **Designs with identical intersection distributions.** Two explicit nonisomorphic $2$-$(256,4,31)$ designs have the same number of block pairs at each intersection size. The construction answers the request for further examples in Kaspers–Pott §5 for short developments and ordinary developments with repetition. Its base blocks overlap. [Proof](ShortDifferenceFamilies.md) · [Kaspers–Pott, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.05848v2#S5)
- **Perfect sequences of prime-square length.** Cyclotomic descent and discrete Fourier inversion classify every perfect $p$-phase sequence of length $p^2$. The answer is exactly $p!\,p^p$ indexed sequences, each determined by a permutation of the affine slopes and an arbitrary offset in each column. This extends the archived $p=5$ computation. Mow’s maximum-length conjecture for general alphabets concerns a separate problem. [Proof](PrimeSquarePerfectSequences.md)
- **A six-job scheduling lower bound.** For deterministic, nonpreemptive makespan scheduling on two identical machines with prediction error at most $21/20$, the exact game certificate gives a worst-case ratio of at least $31527/29600>841/800$. Immediate revelation of each assigned job’s length and free choice of machine make the game easier for the scheduler. The lower bound thus also applies to schedulers that idle and learn durations only at completion. The full optimal curve remains open. [Proof](SixJobScheduling.md) · [Chen–Tan, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10945v1#S2)
- **A base-two approximation for unit-ball TSP with neighborhoods.** The archive’s use of direct spherical coverings improves the earlier exponential base of approximately 7.73 to 2. A separate construction for short tours gives a multiplicative $O(d^{3/2}\log(d+1)2^d)$ guarantee for every instance, in polynomial time for fixed $d$. A nonexponential dependence on dimension remains open. [Proof](UnitBallTour.md) · [Dumitrescu–Tóth, Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.6659)

The proofs are informal. The Kaspers–Pott residual case and the scheduling game include exact Python checks. The notes credit known antecedents where relevant. Publication priority has not been established.
