# Parallel normal centralizers and socles in quotient permutation groups

**Theorem 1.** Let $P\leq\operatorname{Sym}(n)$ be specified by generating permutations, and let a linear representation $\rho:P\to\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb F_r)$ be specified by the images of those generators, where $r$ is prime and $r\leq n$. There is a deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$ algorithm returning generators of $\ker\rho$. The complexity is in the explicit total input length. The same result over an explicitly presented finite extension of $\mathbb F_r$ follows by restriction of scalars.

**Theorem 2.** Given $K\leq N$ normal in $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(n)$, by lists of generating permutations, the preimage of $C_{G/K}(N/K)$ can be computed in deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$. In the same model the preimage of $\operatorname{Soc}(G/K)$ can be computed in deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$, for arbitrary $G/K$. This answers Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar Problem 12.2 and the unrestricted quotient question following Problem 12.4. [Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar, §12](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039v4#S12)

## Parallel primitives and quotient coordinates

We use the published parallel algorithms for permutation-group order, membership, constructive membership, normal intersections, normal closure, kernels of explicit permutation actions, and lift of a homomorphism to an explicitly given quotient. Constructive membership provides polylogarithmic-depth straight-line programs evaluated in parallel. Evaluating the same program on supplied generator images computes the image of any subsequently produced source generator. Throughout, subgroups of a quotient are encoded by full preimages specified by permutation generators. [Babai–Luks–Seress, Theorems 1.1 and 7.1](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf) · [Kantor–Luks–Mark, §§2.2 and 3.2](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf)

An elementary abelian quotient $A/L$ of exponent $p$ has direct parallel coordinates. From a list $a_1,\dots,a_s$ generating $A$, retain $a_j$ exactly if it is outside $\langle L,a_1,\dots,a_{j-1}\rangle$, testing all prefixes in parallel. The retained cosets $b_1L,\dots,b_tL$ are a basis over $\mathbb F_p$. Given $a\in A$, its coordinate at $b_j$ is the unique $c\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}$ with
$$
 a b_j^{-c}\in\langle L,b_1,\dots,b_{j-1},b_{j+1},\dots,b_t\rangle.
$$
Try all $p$ choices in parallel. Here $p\leq n$ whenever the quotient is nontrivial because $p$ divides the order of a subgroup of $\operatorname{Sym}(n)$. Evaluating a product of powers converts coordinates back to a representative. The additive group of any explicitly computed $t$-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$-space can also be represented by translations on the disjoint union of $t$ copies of $\mathbb F_p$, of degree $tp$.

We also use parallel linear algebra over fields of prime order at most $n$, in particular rank, basis selection, kernels, and solving linear equations. To compute the algebra generated by an explicit list of matrices, take the span including the identity and repeatedly replace it by the span of all pairwise products of basis vectors. After $j$ iterations the span includes all words of length at most $2^j$, and words of length at most the algebra's ambient matrix-space dimension suffice. The same argument works for nonunital algebras by taking the span of the current list and its products at each round. [Mulmuley, *A fast parallel algorithm to compute the rank of a matrix over an arbitrary field*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579205) · [Kantor–Luks–Mark, §3.4](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf)

## Linear representation kernels

If $d=0$, return the generators of $P$. Otherwise, for each prime $\ell\leq n$ dividing $|P|$, find a Sylow $\ell$-subgroup $D\leq P$ in parallel. If $U=\ker\rho$, then $D\cap U$ is a Sylow $\ell$-subgroup of $U$, since $U$ is normal. The subgroup generated by these intersections over all primes lies in $U$ and has order divisible by every prime power in $|U|$, so equals $U$. It suffices to compute the kernel on each $\ell$-group $D$. This reduction to Sylow domains is also used in the sequential algorithm of Luks and Miyazaki. [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Proposition 5.2](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf) · [Luks–Miyazaki, Problem 16 and Lemma 3.16](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/polytimenormalizers.pdf#page=14)

### Defining characteristic

Suppose $\ell=r$. The matrices of $D$ admit a common invariant flag on which all one-dimensional quotients are trivial. Indeed, on any nonzero module, the number of fixed vectors under a finite $r$-group is congruent to the number of vectors modulo $r$ by orbit counting. This yields a nonzero fixed vector because zero is fixed, and induction on the quotient gives the flag. Therefore the nonunital algebra $J$ spanned by positive words in the generator differences $\rho(g)-I$ is nilpotent with $J^d=0$. All of $\rho(D)$ lies in $1+J$, since the generators do and their inverses are finite geometric sums in the nilpotent differences. Compute bases of $J,J^2,J^4,\dots$ by repeated spans of products. On the subgroup currently known to map into $1+J^t$, the map $g\mapsto\rho(g)-I\pmod{J^{2t}}$ is an additive homomorphism. Compute its kernel using LIFT into the explicitly represented additive group $J^t/J^{2t}$. After $O(\log d)$ such steps the target condition is $\rho(g)=I$.

### Coprime characteristic

Suppose $\ell\neq r$. A recursive step has an explicit $D$-module $W$ and an explicit scalar field $C$, embedded as operators on $W$ that commute with $D$, with $\mathbb F_r\subseteq C$. Initially $C=\mathbb F_r$. The algebra $R=C[\rho(D)]$ in $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb F_r}(W)$ is generated by the supplied operator bases and matrices. It is semisimple because it is an image of the semisimple finite group algebra $C[D]$ by Maschke's theorem. Its center is obtained by linear equations in a basis of $R$. It is a product of finite fields, and its primitive idempotents give a direct sum of isotypic blocks. The kernel on $W$ is the intersection of the kernels on those blocks, all normal subgroups of $D$.

For clarity, primitive idempotents in any commutative semisimple $\mathbb F_r$-algebra $U$ are computable in deterministic $\mathsf{NC}$ when $r\leq n$. The linear map $u\mapsto u^r$ is computed on basis elements by binary powering. Its fixed subalgebra $U_0$ is the product of one copy of $\mathbb F_r$ for each field factor of $U$. A basis of $U_0$ is obtained by a kernel computation. Multiplication on $U_0$ by any of its basis elements has eigenspaces over $\mathbb F_r$. List them by trying every scalar in $\mathbb F_r$. Merge these lists with a balanced tree, taking all pairwise intersections in each merge and retaining only nonzero intersections. Each collection at an intermediate node is a direct sum of simultaneous eigenspaces and has at most $\dim U_0$ nonzero spaces. The final spaces are precisely the one-dimensional factor lines, since the basis separates every pair of coordinates in the field product. For each such line, a nonzero $u$ satisfies $u^2=\mu u$ for some nonzero $\mu\in\mathbb F_r$, and $u/\mu$ is its unique nonzero idempotent. This computes all required idempotents without factoring polynomials over fields of large cardinality.

In one isotypic block replace $W$ by that block and $R$ by the associated simple component acting on it. Let $F=Z(R)$, a field of operators extending the carried field $C$. If $R$ is commutative then $\rho(D)$ lies in the multiplicative group of a finite field and is a cyclic $\ell$-group. One of the supplied generator images has order $|\rho(D)|$. Each $\ell$-element of $\operatorname{Sym}(n)$ has order equal to its largest cycle length, so its order, and the order of its image, is at most $n$. Enumerate powers up to $n$ to find a maximal-order image and obtain the exponent of every other image in this cyclic group. Compute the kernel of the resulting explicit cyclic permutation action by LIFT.

If $R$ is noncommutative, compute a central composition series $1=D_0<\cdots<D_s=D$ of the source $\ell$-group by Luks and McKenzie. The series may have polynomial length, but it is computed in parallel, and all terms are tested in parallel to find the first term whose image is outside $F$. Choose a generating permutation $a$ of that term whose image is outside $F$. The previous subgroup has image in $F^\times$. Because the series is central, for every $g\in D$ the element $gag^{-1}$ agrees with $a$ modulo the previous subgroup. Hence it takes $\rho(a)$ to a scalar multiple by an element of $F^\times$. The algebra $B=F[\rho(a)]\subseteq R$ is consequently normalized by $D$, strictly contains $F$, and is a product of fields. The latter follows because the minimal polynomial of $\rho(a)$ over $F$ divides the separable polynomial $X^{|a|}-1$. [Luks–McKenzie, Theorem 5.8 and following remark](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(88)90044-X)

If $B$ has at least two primitive idempotents, then $D$ acts transitively on them. If there were more than one orbit, the sum of the idempotents from one orbit would be a nontrivial idempotent commuting with $D$ and with $C$. It would then be a nontrivial idempotent in the center $F$ of $R$, which is impossible. The induced permutation action on the idempotents is computed by conjugating them by every input generator and matching them in the computed list. Let $D_0'$ be the kernel of this action, found by LIFT. It preserves every idempotent image $W_j$, all of equal dimension at most $\dim W/2$. Recurse on $D_0'$ on each $W_j$ carrying the restriction of $F$ as scalar field. An element acts trivially on $W$ exactly when it belongs to $D_0'$ and acts trivially on every $W_j$.

If $B$ is a field, conjugation acts on it by $\mathbb F_r$-automorphisms. Compute Frobenius on a basis of $B$, then use binary powers of its linear matrix to obtain its first $\dim_{\mathbb F_r}B$ powers. Match the conjugation action of each generator to one of these Frobenius powers, which are exactly the finite field automorphisms. The actions give an explicit cyclic permutation representation of degree at most $\dim W$, with kernel $D_0'$ obtained by LIFT. Every element acting trivially on $W$ belongs to $D_0'$. The group $D_0'$ commutes with $B$, so recurse on the same $W$ carrying the enlarged scalar field $B$.

In either branch, the measure $\dim_{\mathbb F_r} W/\dim_{\mathbb F_r}C$ at least halves when a further recursive call is made. At the preliminary isotypic split, both the passage to a subspace and the enlargement from $C$ to $F$ can only decrease this measure. After the additional split the numerator at least halves, and after scalar enlargement the denominator at least doubles. There are $O(\log d)$ recursive levels. The sum of the dimensions of active blocks at each level is at most $d$, because each nontrivial branching is a direct sum decomposition. Store all nodes of a level in a padded array and execute their local computations in parallel. Combine the kernel lists bottom-up with balanced intersections of normal subgroups in the common domain at each split. The subgroup operations, the linear algebra, and the polylogarithmic depth and polynomial total width give deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$. This completes Theorem 1. For DLOGTIME uniformity throughout, a logspace-uniform wiring predicate can be evaluated in DLOGTIME-uniform $\mathsf{NC}^2$ via the polynomial-size configuration graph. Evaluate those predicates for all potential circuit wire pairs in parallel, then evaluate all gates simultaneously for a number of rounds equal to the circuit depth. This preserves polynomial size and polylogarithmic depth.

## Marked conjugation actions on semisimple layers

Let $A=M/L$ be a direct product of nonabelian simple groups, where $M,L$ are normal in an explicitly given permutation group $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(n)$. Assume the simple factor preimages and their faithful permutation representations of degree polynomial in the input length are given, as they are for each nonabelian layer of the good series. We construct in $\mathsf{NC}$ a polynomial-degree permutation action of $G$ with exactly the kernel of conjugation on $A$.

For each simple factor $S$ with a supplied faithful representation of degree $b$, take marks as follows. If $|S|\leq b$, take each individual element, represented by a lift modulo $L$. List the represented group by repeated pairwise products with deduplication, which takes $O(\log b)$ iterations for a group of order at most $b$, and obtain a preimage of each element by LIFT. Otherwise choose a moved point and let $T<S$ be its point stabilizer. Its index is in $[2,b]$, so $T$ is proper and nontrivial. As a mark use its preimage over $L$, computed by lifting a generating list for the represented point stabilizer and including the generators of $L$. Conjugation can send individual factor marks to only isomorphic factors. The full automorphism orbit of $T$ inside one factor has size at most $[S:N_S(T)]|\operatorname{Out}(S)|\leq 2b\log_2|S|$, by the outer-order bound and the size of the inner-conjugacy class. There are at most $\log_2(n!)$ factors, so all marked $G$-orbits have polynomial size. [Kohl, Theorem 1](https://stefan-kohl.github.io/preprints/outbound.pdf)

To enumerate the orbit of any mark, its generated Schreier graph admits paired ports for each generator of $G$ and its inverse. The reverse port depends only on the original port, including at repeated edges and loops. Reingold's theorem for consistently labeled graphs constructs a universal traversal word in logspace for each given graph size and port pairing. Use the words for all sizes from one through the known polynomial orbit-size bound. In parallel evaluate their prefixes as permutation products and conjugate the mark. The correct size's prefix list visits every orbit point. Each candidate belongs to the orbit regardless of size. Deduplicate cosets by membership of the representative difference in $L$, and subgroups by testing mutual membership of all supplied generators. The action of any input permutation on this list is another conjugation and matching operation. The words have polynomial length and can be wired by a logspace uniformity machine, and the products have balanced trees. The disjoint union of all the resulting orbit actions is a polynomial-degree permutation representation. [Reingold, Theorem 5.4](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/sl.pdf#page=15)

Any automorphism fixing every mark preserves each simple factor, since the factors have trivial mutual intersection and each has nontrivial marks. Its action is trivial on a factor marked by all elements. For a factor marked by a nontrivial proper $T$, the action of $S$ on the inner-conjugates of $T$ is faithful, since its kernel is normal and its action is nontrivial by simplicity. If $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)$ fixes all those conjugates, that faithful action assigns the same permutation to $s$ and $\alpha(s)$, by compatibility of conjugation with $\alpha$. Thus $\alpha(s)=s$ for all $s$. This proves equality of the two kernels. Apply the ordinary parallel permutation-action kernel algorithm to compute their generators. The same statement holds with a trivial group of factors, using the trivial action.

## Normal centralizers in the quotient

Use the good-series algorithm to compute a polylogarithmic series of $G$-normal subgroups $K=N_0\leq\cdots\leq N_s=N$, whose layers are abelian or direct products of nonabelian simple groups. For each nonabelian layer it provides the simple factor preimages and their faithful permutation representations of polynomial degree. Put $H_i=\{g\in G:[g,N]\subseteq N_i\}$. Start with $H_s=G$ and compute $H_{i-1}$ from $H_i$. These are normal subgroups of $G$. The desired output is $H_0$. [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Lemma 3.14](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf)

In a nonabelian layer $A=N_i/N_{i-1}$, use the parallel marked conjugation action from the quotient radical proof to compute the kernel of the action of $G$ on $A$. This action enumerates polynomial orbits of small-factor elements or large-factor point stabilizers under conjugation, using universal traversal words and membership tests on the corresponding lifted subgroups. Intersect its kernel with $H_i$. If $g$ belongs to this intersection, $[g,x]$ has image in $A$ for every $x\in N$. The actions of $g$ and $x$ on $A$ commute because that of $g$ is trivial. Therefore $[g,x]$ acts trivially on $A$, so its image belongs to $Z(A)=1$. Thus this intersection is exactly $H_{i-1}$.

In an abelian layer $A=N_i/N_{i-1}$, first compute the kernel of the action of $H_i$ on $A$. The following cases use parallel computations for each prime $p\leq n$ for which $A/A^p\neq 1$, where $A^{p^j}$ denotes the subgroup of $p^j$-th powers. All $p$-power subgroups are characteristic, and their preimages are generated by the denominator together with the indicated powers of the supplied generators of $N_i$. Form the representation of $H_i$ on the elementary abelian quotient $A/A^p$ using the direct quotient coordinates above. Theorem 1 yields the kernel $H_{i,p,1}$ of the action on $A/A^p$.

Inductively let $H_{i,p,j}$ be the kernel of the action on $A/A^{p^j}$ for $j\geq1$. For $g$ in this subgroup, write $\delta_g(a)=gag^{-1}a^{-1}$ in the quotient $A$, so $\delta_g(a)\in A^{p^j}$. Also $g$ acts trivially on $A^{p^j}/A^{p^{j+1}}$, since $\delta_g(a^{p^j})=\delta_g(a)^{p^j}\in A^{p^{2j}}\subseteq A^{p^{j+1}}$. Hence $g\mapsto(\delta_g(a_u))_u\bmod A^{p^{j+1}}$, with $a_u$ ranging through the original generating list of $A$, is an additive homomorphism into a direct power of the elementary abelian group $A^{p^j}/A^{p^{j+1}}$. Its kernel, computed by LIFT, is $H_{i,p,j+1}$. Each subgroup of this filtration is normal in $H_i$. Every element of $A$ has $p$-part of its order at most $n$, because the order divides that of an ambient permutation and the maximal $p$-part of a permutation order is the largest $p$-part of one of its cycle lengths. Thus $O(\log n)$ refinements per prime suffice to reach the kernel of the action on the entire $p$-primary factor of $A$. Intersect these kernels in a balanced tree. The result $H'$ is the kernel of the action on $A$.

For $g\in H'$, the displacements $g x g^{-1}x^{-1}\bmod N_{i-1}$, for the supplied generators $x$ of $N$, belong to $A$. Because $H'$ centralizes $A$, the resulting tuple is a homomorphism from $H'$ to the abelian group $A^u$, where $u$ is the number of generators of $N$. Use LIFT with target the quotient $N_i^u/N_{i-1}^u$, represented on the disjoint union of $u$ copies of the input permutation domain. The kernel is exactly $H_{i-1}$, since the supplied elements generate $N$. This covers the case $A=1$ as well. There are only polylogarithmically many successive layers and all intermediate subgroup lists can be compressed to polynomial length by the usual parallel strong generating set algorithm. The computations are uniform and establish the centralizer theorem.

## The abelian and full socle

Write $Q=G/K$. Use a good series $1=Q_0\leq\cdots\leq Q_m=Q$, and compute $C_i=C_Q(Q_{i-1})\cap Q_i$ using the centralizer theorem just proved and normal intersections on the lifted groups. Compute $Z(C_i)$ by the same centralizer theorem with the two input groups equal to the preimage of $C_i$. Then $A=\prod_i Z(C_i)$ is normal and abelian. For $i<j$, this is because $Z(C_i)\leq Q_{j-1}$, which $C_j$ centralizes. An abelian minimal normal subgroup $M$ first contained in $Q_i$ meets $Q_{i-1}$ trivially and therefore centralizes it, so $M\leq C_i$. Its image in $Q_i/Q_{i-1}$ is normal and abelian, thus central since the layer is abelian or a direct product of nonabelian simple groups. Hence $[M,C_i]\leq M\cap Q_{i-1}=1$, and $M$ lies in $A$.

For each prime $p\leq n$, compute $B=A[p]$, the kernel of the power-$p$ homomorphism from $A$ to $A$, by LIFT. If $B=1$, this prime contributes only the identity. Otherwise use the explicit coordinate construction for the elementary abelian quotient $B$ and compute the conjugation matrices of $Q$ from the generators of $G$. Let $R$ be the unital $\mathbb F_p$-algebra generated by these matrices and $J$ its Jacobson radical. The sum of its minimal nonzero submodules on $B$ is $\operatorname{Ann}_B(J)$. Indeed, every simple is annihilated by $J$, while this annihilator is a module over the semisimple algebra $R/J$, hence is a sum of simples. The simple submodules of $B$ are exactly the $Q$-minimal normal subgroups it contains. Convert a basis for the annihilator back to lifted permutations by the coordinate maps. The union of the results over all primes, together with the nonabelian part constructed below and the input generators of $K$, generates the full preimage of $\operatorname{Soc}(Q)$. Including those denominator generators covers the case $Q=1$ as well.

For the radical computation, the parallelized Cohen–Ivanyos–Wales recurrence starts at $I_0=R$ and takes $I_{h+1}$ to be the left kernel on $I_h$ of the coefficient pairing $(x,y)\mapsto[t^{p^h}]\det(I+txy)$. Over an algebraic closure, let $m_j$ be the positive multiplicity of each simple factor of the split quotient algebra on the faithful matrix module. At stage $h$, the ideal $I_h$ retains exactly the factors with multiplicities divisible by $p^h$, together with the radical. The coefficient in question is the sum over $j$ of valuation $v_p(m_j)=h$ of the ordinary matrix trace pairing on factor $j$, raised to $p^h$ and multiplied by the nonzero scalar $m_j/p^h$ modulo $p$. Factoring the determinant along a composition series gives this identity. Nondegeneracy of the matrix trace pairing eliminates exactly those factors. Frobenius semilinearity over the algebraic closure becomes bilinearity over $\mathbb F_p$, and extension of the computed kernels agrees with the geometric elimination because Frobenius fixes the base field. The multiplicities are bounded by the representation dimension, so $O(\log \dim B)$ rounds compute $J$. All coefficients come from the parallel characteristic-polynomial algorithm. This is the same radical computation as in the companion ordinary permutation socle proof. [Cohen–Ivanyos–Wales, Algorithm 1](https://research.tue.nl/nl/publications/finding-the-radical-of-an-algebra-of-linear-transformations/) · [Berkowitz](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(84)90018-8)

The nonabelian part follows from the same groups $C_i$. A nonabelian minimal normal subgroup first contained in $Q_i$ injects into the layer and lies in $C_i$. For each nonabelian simple direct factor $S$ of the layer, take its preimage $T\leq Q_i$, form $E=C_i\cap T$, and keep $E'$ exactly when $|E'|=|S|$. The projection of $E$ onto $S$ is trivial or onto, and its kernel lies in $Z(C_i)$. In the former case $E'$ is trivial. In the latter case $E'$ surjects to the perfect group $S$ and is perfect since $E$ is the product of $E'$ and a central subgroup. The order test says that $E'$ maps isomorphically to $S$. If a lift $S'\cong S$ already exists in $E$, then $E=S'\ker(E\to S)$ and $E'=S'$, so every factor of every minimal normal of this type is retained. Distinct retained groups in a layer commute, since their commutators are central and each group is perfect. The retained groups are permuted by $Q$, and their products over orbits give minimal normal subgroups, by normal-subgroup structure of a direct product of nonabelian simple groups. For the order test, if $\widetilde E$ is the preimage of $E$, the full preimage of $E'$ is $K[\widetilde E,\widetilde E]$, and its order divided by $|K|$ is the order of $E'$. All operations are uniform $\mathsf{NC}$, proving Theorem 2.

## Parallel p-cores and the Fitting subgroup

**Theorem 3.** Given $Q=G/K$ in the same model, the full preimages of all its largest normal $p$-subgroups $O_p(Q)$ and its Fitting subgroup are computable in deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$.

First, the solvable radical $R$ of $Q$ is the intersection of the conjugation-action kernels on the nonabelian layers of a good normal series of $Q$. Any solvable normal subgroup has trivial image where it meets a nonabelian layer, because a direct product of nonabelian simple groups has no nontrivial solvable normal subgroup. Taking commutators shows it acts trivially on that layer. Conversely, if $U$ is the intersection of these kernels, its intersection with the normal series has trivial image at every nonabelian layer since the image is central there. Its remaining layers are abelian. Hence $U$ is solvable and normal, proving the formula. The marked action construction computes these kernels in parallel.

Take the derived series of $R$ and refine its abelian layers into primary layers. It is normal in $Q$, as the derived subgroups of $R$ and the partial products of primary components in abelian quotients are characteristic in their respective groups. These refinements are parallel constructions. In an abelian layer, the $q$-primary component is generated by the powers of the given generators with exponent the prime-to-$q$ part of the layer order. A lifted subgroup for each partial product is obtained by adjoining the denominator to the combined lists. For every $p\leq n$ let $H_p$ be the subgroup of $R$ acting trivially on all $q$-primary layers with $q\neq p$. Compute it using the abelian-layer action kernels from the centralizer algorithm. All the required kernels can be computed in parallel and intersected in a balanced tree. In particular, it is unnecessary to descend the whole refined series sequentially.

Every normal $p$-subgroup of $Q$ lies in $R$ and acts trivially on all the indicated layers, by normality and coprime orders. Thus $O_p(Q)\subseteq H_p$. The group $H_p$ has a normal Sylow $p$-subgroup. To see this, intersect the refined series with $H_p$, so that each non-$p$ layer is central in the corresponding quotient of $H_p$. Induct along this property by quotienting out the first nontrivial term $L$. By induction the quotient has a normal Sylow $p$-subgroup, with a normal full preimage $V$ in $H_p$. If $L$ is a $p$-group then $V$ is itself a normal Sylow $p$-subgroup. Otherwise $L$ has order prime to $p$ and is central, and $V=L\times P$ for any Sylow $p$-subgroup $P\leq V$, because the orders are coprime and $V=LP$. Thus $P$ is characteristic in $V$ and normal in $H_p$. The induction is complete. The unique Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H_p$ is characteristic there, and $H_p$ is normal in $Q$ as a kernel intersection in its normal subgroup $R$. Therefore that Sylow subgroup is $O_p(Q)$. Compute its full preimage by the parallel Sylow algorithm in the quotient model. The product over primes gives the Fitting subgroup, since a finite nilpotent normal subgroup is the product of its characteristic Sylow subgroups, and distinct normal primary subgroups commute. Include the input generators of $K$ in the lifted product, also when there are no nontrivial primary factors. [Babai–Luks–Seress, Theorem 7.1](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf) · [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Proposition 5.2](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf)

## Scope and priority

The proofs are informal. The nonabelian-layer step is the marked conjugation action from our quotient radical proof, reproduced above. The linear-kernel step reuses the Sylow reduction of Luks and Miyazaki and the parallel central series of Luks and McKenzie. The balanced splitting or scalar enlargement gives a new parallel bound for the linear-kernel subroutine in the small-characteristic scope needed here. The sequential treatment in Kantor and Luks uses Sylow and group-intersection methods and already identifies the abelian socle with the sum of simple modules in a suitable normal elementary abelian subgroup. A targeted source search found these antecedents and the open parallel quotient formulations stated above. [Kantor–Luks, §§6 and 9](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/quotient.pdf) · [Luks–Miyazaki, Lemma 3.16](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/polytimenormalizers.pdf#page=14)
