# Determinant readout from overlapping adjoint blocks of square-zero pencils

Fix \(F=\mathbb F_3\). Let \(D=F\Omega\oplus J\) be an anchored square-zero alternating pencil with common radical \(R\) of \(J\) Lagrangian, as in the ternary one-sign normal form. The requested sign is the determinant of the action on the based space \(J\). Put \(U=W/R\), view \(J\) as a subspace of \(\bigwedge^2 U^*\), and let
\[
\beta:U\times U\longrightarrow L=J^*,\qquad \beta(x,y)(B)=B(x,y).
\]
The induced linear map from \(\bigwedge^2 U\) onto \(L\) is surjective, and the common radical of \(\beta\) is zero. An isomorphism of these two descended alternating maps, with maps \(p\) on \(U\) and \(t\) on \(L\), lifts to a pencil congruence using \(p\oplus p^{-\mathsf T}\) on the two hyperbolic splittings with the anchors fixed. Conversely every pencil congruence descends to such maps. The determinant on \(J\) is the inverse of that on \(L\) in the dual bases, which is the same sign over \(F\). For the raw ambient-times-label problem with odd \(\dim U\), retain the normal form's Pfaffian and basis calibration. [Square-zero normal form](SquareZeroNormalForm.md)

## The recognizable criterion

Compute the adjoint algebra
\[
\mathcal A=\{(P,Q):\beta(Px,y)=\beta(x,Qy)\ \text{for all }x,y\}
\]
with multiplication \((P,Q)(P',Q')=(PP',Q'Q)\) and involution \((P,Q)^*=(Q,P)\). Identify it with its first-coordinate algebra on \(U\), which is faithful by the zero common radical. Let \(e_i\) be the central primitive idempotents and \(U_i=e_iU\). The involution permutes the \(e_i\). For each involution orbit \(v\), define \(K_v\subseteq L\) to be the span of \(\beta(U_i,U_{i^*})\) for one \(i\in v\). The choice of \(i\) in a two-element orbit does not matter by alternation. Write \(G\) for the graph on the orbits with an edge \(vw\) exactly when \(K_v\cap K_w\ne0\). Write \(K_e=K_v\cap K_w\) for an edge.

**Theorem.** Suppose \(G\) is a tree and
\[
 \sum_v\dim K_v-\dim L=\sum_e\dim K_e. \tag{1}
\]
Let \(d=\max_v\deg_G(v)\). In the definition of \(b\), take the maximum of all overlap dimensions and of the dimensions \(\dim K_v\) except at a single self-adjoint block \(U_i\) of odd dimension whose map \(\bigwedge^2 U_i\to K_v\) is injective. Use maximum zero if no dimensions remain. A test for the prescribed sign is one ordinary three-tensor isomorphism instance over \(F\), with construction time and side lengths polynomial in the dense input size, \(2^b\), and \(2^d\). In particular the reduction is polynomial for logarithmic \(b,d\), with arbitrarily many mutually interchangeable labels. The graph and its exactness test are computed from the raw square-zero pencil. The \(K_v\) may overlap, and the construction retains the action on those overlaps.

## 1. Recovering the arrangement

The defining equation for \(\mathcal A\) is linear. If the first component is zero, then the second has image in the common radical, so it is zero. Closure under multiplication follows by moving the first-coordinate operators to the other variable in reverse order. Alternation gives the involution. A congruence conjugates the first-coordinate algebra and transports the involution.

Compute its center \(C\) by linear equations. The subalgebra fixed by \(c\mapsto c^3\) is a product of copies of \(F\), one for each local factor of \(C\). Indeed the residue of a fixed element in a local factor lies in \(F\), and after subtracting that scalar the remaining fixed nilpotent vanishes by repeated cubing. The fixed subalgebra and its primitive idempotents are found by linear algebra, splitting common eigenspaces of multiplication and projecting the unit. These are the central primitive idempotents of \(\mathcal A\). No quotient lift is needed. The involution restricts to an algebra automorphism of \(C\) and of its fixed subalgebra. Its invariant subalgebra there is the coordinate algebra \(E=F^{\{v\}}\) of the involution orbits, with idempotents the sums over each orbit. All these constructions are respected by every actual congruence. [Wilson, adjoints of bilinear maps](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.4329)

Because \(\beta(e_i x,y)=\beta(x,e_i^*y)\), its value on \(U_i\times U_j\) vanishes unless \(j=i^*\). The \(K_v\) therefore span \(L\). Compute their bases by the corresponding products of input bases, and all pairwise intersections by elimination. Ordinary transport carries the entire arrangement and graph to their counterparts, using the \(t\) induced by the same map \(p\). The common-radical, graph and dimension tests require polynomial time, even when they fail.

## 2. The exact incidence sequence and its central edge

Let \(V=\bigoplus_v K_v\) be an external direct sum and let \(s:V\to L\) be the sum of the inclusions. Give each edge of \(G\) an orientation for the moment, and let \(B=\bigoplus_e K_e\). Define \(\partial:B\to V\) by mapping an edge's component \(x\in K_e\) to \(x\) on its initial vertex and \(-x\) on its terminal vertex. This is injective for a tree. In any vector in its kernel the leaf component forces zero on the single incident edge, and successively removing leaves proves the claim. The image lies in \(\ker s\). Since \(s\) is surjective, (1) gives the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow B\xrightarrow{\;\partial\;} V\xrightarrow{\;s\;}L\longrightarrow0. \tag{2}
\]

Root \(G\) at its unique center if it has one. If it has two centers, they are adjacent, and subdivide their edge with a new vertex as root. The root and this subdivision are intrinsic, by the description of the center as the final set from repeated leaf deletion. Place the original \(K_v\)'s on their own vertices of the rooted tree, with \(V\)-component zero at a new root. For \(B\), index all the nonsubdivided edges by their child vertices, orienting them away from the root, and place the overlap for a subdivided central edge at the new root. At a true root the \(B\)-component is zero. Choose either direction for the original central edge when defining \(\partial\). A genuine congruence carrying the central endpoints in the reverse order acts on this \(B\)-component by the negative of its restriction to \(K_e\). If the endpoints are preserved, its map there is the restriction itself. This sign is exactly what makes the incidence map commute. All other overlap components have the usual transported restriction, since their rooted orientation is respected.

Keep \(\beta\) in the comparison, along with the first-coordinate adjoint algebra action and the inclusion of \(E\) into that algebra. Faithfulness ensures that their maps are the ones induced by \(p\). The rooted tree has a coordinate algebra of its vertices. Include its multiplication, the parent map, and the inclusion of \(E\) from the original vertices, which preserves the link even when there is an artificial root. Give \(V\) and \(B\) the projection actions of this rooted coordinate algebra and include \(s\) and \(\partial\) as actual linear records. Since \(s\) is injective on each \(K_v\)-component, its intertwining forces the specified restrictions of \(t\) on those components. Since \(\partial\) is injective, its intertwining forces the overlap action, including the central reversal factor. Conversely every genuine \((p,t)\) extends to these maps, with the same enlarged vertex permutation.

## 3. Determinant lines for a permutable rooted tree

Here is the determinant attachment for either \(V\) or \(B\). Write \(X=\bigoplus_v X_v\) for the associated framed blocks on the rooted tree. Choose the order of a node's children independently on the two inputs, using the order only to define coordinates. Frame each subtree by the basis of its local block followed by the framed subtrees of the children in that order.

Attach the componentwise exterior algebra \(\bigoplus_v\bigwedge X_v\) with its multiplication, projection action of the vertex algebra, and degree-one inclusion of \(X\). Its top local lines \(P_v\) are attached by inclusions with their projection action. Multiplication and the unit of each component force the actual exterior action and the local determinant on \(P_v\), also for the zero-dimensional block.

Place one line \(A_v\) at each node for the determinant of the entire subtree, and one line \(Q_{v,S}\) for every subset \(S\) of its children \(C_v\). Write \(b_i\) for the full subtree dimension at a child \(i\). The coordinate algebra of the subset labels is linked to the vertex algebra by the linear maps sending \((v,S)\) to \(e_v\) and to \(\sum_{i\in S}e_i\). These determine the subset label under every allowed vertex permutation. Use these algebras' actions by projection on the direct sums of the corresponding lines. Retain these records in the chosen line bases.

- A linear map sends \(e_v\) to the basis of \(Q_{v,\varnothing}\).
- A linear map sends each nonroot line \(A_i\) to its singleton line \(Q_{\operatorname{parent}(i),\{i\}}\). It vanishes on the root.
- For disjoint subsets \(S,T\) of \(C_v\), a bilinear map sends the ordered line bases of \(Q_{v,S},Q_{v,T}\) to the basis of \(Q_{v,S\cup T}\) times
\[
 (-1)^{\sum_{i\in S,j\in T,\ i>j} b_i b_j}.
\]
Here \(i>j\) means their order among the children of \(v\). Other pairs have product zero.
- A bilinear map sends the basis of \(P_v\) and the basis of \(Q_{v,C_v}\) to that of \(A_v\), and vanishes on the other pairs.

For a fixed map on \(X\) and a vertex permutation, the scalars on this data are forced from the leaves toward the root, taking subset products by increasing cardinality. The scalar on \(Q_{v,S}\) is the determinant of the map of the corresponding sum of child subtrees, in the chosen frames. To verify the assertion for a disjoint product, concatenating the ordered frames for \(S,T\) and moving them to the frame for \(S\cup T\) gives the displayed sign, since interchanging blocks of dimensions \(b_i,b_j\) has determinant \((-1)^{b_i b_j}\). Apply this frame identity in both inputs. It proves that the actual child-subset determinants satisfy every product record, including products in the reversed order, and proves the recursion by multiplying by singleton lines. Including the local block gives the subtree determinant on \(A_v\). Conversely those determinants define invertible maps on all new roles, and satisfy the kept records by the same identity. A line included into \(A_*\) at the root reads the full determinant. The auxiliary dimensions are \(O(\sum_v(2^{\dim X_v}+2^{|C_v|}))\) and the number of records is constant. This uses the same graded sign as the earlier rooted direct-sum construction, here applied twice to the exact overlap sequence. [Prior rooted orientation construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/OQ19DeterminantTransfers/OrientationTrees.md) · [Knudsen–Mumford, determinant lines](https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/mumford/alg_geom/papers/1976b--DetDiv%2BKnudsenSequel-GT.pdf)

**Local-line interface.** The recursion needs only the local determinant lines and the actual subtree dimensions. A local exterior algebra can be replaced by any linked record that (i) extends the same given map on its \(X_v\), and (ii) forces the multiplier on its line to be \(\det(X_v\to X'_{\pi(v)})\) in the same chosen frames. The replacement records must be compatible with the permitted vertex permutations. The exact sequence (2) and all root-line conclusions then hold with the size of the supplied local records in place of the omitted exterior algebras.

At a vertex covered by the exception in the theorem, frame \(K_v\) by the images of the basis wedges of \(U_i\). The actual \(K_v\)-map is the exterior square of the restricted \(p\)-map. Its determinant is \((\det(p|_{U_i}))^{\dim U_i-1}=1\). Pin its local determinant line by the linear map from the vertex algebra sending \(e_v\) to its basis, zero at unpinned vertices. The exception is covariant and polynomially recognizable by the dimension and rank tests. For the unpinned vertices keep the componentwise exterior algebra and projection records above, restricting from the original direct sum by a linear inclusion of its unpinned components. This is a fixed-signature mixed attachment.

## 4. One ordinary TI instance

Use Section 3 on both \(V\) and \(B\), with the actions from Section 2. In the framed basis of \(V\), compute the determinant \(\eta\) of the basis given by the images of the framed \(B\)-basis under \(\partial\), followed by lifts of the supplied \(L\)-basis under \(s\). If \(\eta'\) is the target value, the commuting exact sequence gives
\[
 \det(t)=\frac{\eta}{\eta'}\,
             \det(V\to V')\,\det(B\to B')^{-1}.
\]
In fact the map in the adapted bases is block triangular with the actual \(B,L\) maps on the diagonal. Link the two terminal lines by a bilinear multiplication record, using the inverse of the \(B\)-line scalar via a dual pairing if desired. Over \(F\) its inverse is the same scalar. Pin the product to the required sign divided by the frame ratio. The two readouts use the same map \(t\), since Section 2 forces both module actions from the original congruence. Conversely any sign-compatible congruence extends through every record, then respects the scalar mark. Incompatible role dimensions or a failing necessary matching condition can produce a fixed negative instance.

The rooted tree has bounded branching by \(d\), adding at most a degree-two artificial root. Each \(B\)-block has dimension at most \(b\), as does each \(V\)-block whose determinant line is unpinned before the tree assembly. All maps have tensor order at most three, and the number of types and records is constant since each collection of vertex, edge and subset roles is a single direct sum. The fixed-signature tensor-system theorem compiles these data to one ordinary three-tensor comparison in the claimed time and size. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

## 5. A sign obstruction with many interchangeable labels

For each \(k\ge1\) define a colored rooted tree \(T_k\) recursively, and at its vertices use spaces of dimension either \(3\) or \(7\). Let \(T_0\) be a single \(3\)-dimensional vertex. The root of \(T_{k+1}\) has dimension \(3\) and two identical children of dimension \(3\). Each of those children has two children of its own, a \(7\)-dimensional leaf and a copy of \(T_k\). Thus every leaf has even depth, every odd-depth vertex is internal, and the odd-depth vertex's two children are distinguished by dimension. For \(k\ge1\), the root is the unique degree-two vertex and is fixed by every graph automorphism.

At each vertex \(v\) let \(U_v=F^{n_v}\) with the stated \(n_v\) and let \(\widetilde K_v=\bigwedge^2 U_v\). Choose a distinct basis wedge for each incident edge. At odd-depth vertices choose the three wedges in the same order relative to parent, \(3\)-dimensional child, and \(7\)-dimensional child. At a \(7\)-dimensional leaf use its first wedge \(e_1\wedge e_2\). Let \(L\) be the quotient of \(\bigoplus_v\widetilde K_v\) identifying the two chosen wedge generators along each edge. Because the marked lines at a vertex are independent, these identifications take place on disjoint pairs of basis directions. The \(\widetilde K_v\) embed, and two images meet in the shared line exactly at adjacent vertices. The quotient therefore satisfies (1).

On \(U=\bigoplus_v U_v\) use the direct sum of the wedge maps followed by the quotient to \(L\), zero between distinct vertex spaces. Its common radical is zero since each local full wedge maps injectively into \(L\), and its values span \(L\). Its adjoint algebra is exactly the product of vertex scalars. For distinct vertices, the adjoint equation has its two sides in different \(K_v\)'s, whose intersection has dimension at most one. A nonzero vector wedged with all vectors of its own local space has image of dimension at least two, so the off-diagonal blocks of both adjoint maps vanish. Within a local wedge space, \(Px\wedge y=x\wedge Qy\) implies \(Px\) is in every plane spanned by \(x\) and an independent \(y\), by taking a further wedge with \(x\). Hence \(P\) is scalar, and the adjoint equation makes \(Q\) the same scalar. This proves that the arrangement is recovered by the actual adjoint algorithm, including after arbitrary rebasing.

Every color-preserving tree automorphism lifts to a tensor automorphism. On each incident edge prescribe a common scalar \(\pm1\) for the image of its marked generator. At a \(3\)-dimensional vertex of degree three, the product of these scalars must be the sign of its permutation of the ordered incident wedges so that the local map on \(\widetilde K_v\) has determinant one. Solve these equations upward from the leaves, taking the parent edge as the free scalar at each degree-three vertex. The degree-two root and the \(3\)-dimensional leaves can choose scalars on unused basis wedges to make their local determinant one. Every determinant-one map on \(\bigwedge^2 F^3\) arises as an exterior-square map, since in the basis dual to a volume \(\bigwedge^2 g=(\det g)g^{-\mathsf T}\). A \(7\)-dimensional leaf can realize either scalar on its single marked wedge by a diagonal map. These local maps agree on each overlap, hence induce the required automorphism of the quotient. In particular swapping the two odd child subtrees at each disjoint bottom copy of \(T_1\) gives at least \(2^{2^{k-1}}\) actual independent choices of label permutations.

Nevertheless **every** automorphism has determinant one on \(L\). Each local map on \(\widetilde K_v\) has determinant \((\det g_v)^{n_v-1}=1\). All the dimensions \(\binom{n_v}{2}=3,21\) are odd, so the permutation factor for \(V\) is the sign of the vertex permutation. The permutation factor for \(B\) is the same sign, since each edge corresponds to its nonroot child and each overlap is one-dimensional. There is no orientation reversal, since the root is fixed. Formula (2) therefore leaves only the product of overlap scalars. At each odd-depth vertex, the parent and the two child types fix the incidence order. Its local determinant one says that its three incident overlap scalars have product one. Every edge has exactly one odd-depth endpoint. Multiplying those local equations gives product one for all overlaps.

The vertex count is \(6\cdot2^k-5\), which is odd. Thus the ambient descended dimension is odd as required by the hard normal form. Both \(\dim L\) and the codimension of the dual form space \(J\) in \(\bigwedge^2 U^*\) grow. The overlap graph has degree at most three. All vertex lines use the full odd-dimensional wedge exception, so \(b=1\) comes from the edge overlaps. Reversing one basis vector of the target \(J\) leaves the underlying form space unchanged but changes the attainable raw sign from positive to negative. This example has a genuine sign restriction and exponentially many permutable labels even after requiring the original bimap equations.

One can also replace the two special \(7\)-dimensional leaves adjacent to the root's children by \(N\)-dimensional leaves with \(N\equiv3\pmod 4\) and \(N\) larger than the vertex count. Their marked overlap remains \(F(e_1\wedge e_2)\). All preceding arguments apply: the leaves still differ in dimension from their siblings, the local determinant is one, and \(\binom N2\) is odd. The two large leaves give \(\dim J=\Theta(N^2)\), while the absent cross wedges between their underlying spaces give codimension \(\Theta(N^2)\) in \(\bigwedge^2 U^*\). The ambient dimension is \(\Theta(N)\), the parameter \(b\) stays one, and the independent subtree permutations are retained.

## Scope

This is a written sufficient result for the square-zero sign problem. It assembles overlapping adjoint components through an exact incidence sequence, rather than requiring a direct-sum decomposition of the label space or enumerating a permutation action. The earlier ordered adjoint-grade reduction and rooted determinant lines are ingredients. The general square-zero problem can have a single adjoint block or a different arrangement, so the unrestricted reduction in Open Question 1.9 remains open. [Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)
