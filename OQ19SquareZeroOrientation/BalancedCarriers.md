# Balanced carriers for one ternary TI instance

Let \(F=\mathbb F_3\), and let \(\beta:\Lambda^2_F U\twoheadrightarrow Z\) be a based alternating bimap with zero common radical. For the anchored square-zero question take \(Z=J^*\), where \(J\) is the singular radical of the pencil. An actual congruence \(p\) on \(U\) induces the unique \(t\) on \(Z\) with \(t\beta=\beta'(p,p)\). Its action on \(J\) is dual inverse to \(t\), so their determinants agree over \(F\).

**Theorem.** Fix \(c>0\) and let \(L\ge2\) be the dense input length. Either of the following recognizable conditions suffices for a deterministic polynomial many-one reduction for the prescribed label determinant to one ordinary TI instance over \(F\).

1. Group the balanced carriers below by their descriptors and order the descriptor classes. For each successive label grade, either its dimension is at most \(c\log_2 L\), or its class has even-dimensional determinant-neutral carriers and the map from the class carrier onto the grade has kernel of dimension at most \(c\log_2 L\). In particular, exchanged simple adjoint factors of arbitrary degree over any finite extension meet the carrier requirement when the two multiplicities are distinct and even. There can be arbitrarily many copies of one descriptor.
2. The graph on the balanced carrier images passes the tree, logarithmic degree and incidence exactness tests of [OverlapTrees.md](OverlapTrees.md). Each overlap has dimension at most \(c\log_2 L\). Each local label image has either dimension at most \(c\log_2 L\) or a determinant-neutral carrier with kernel of dimension at most \(c\log_2 L\). Here a local neutral carrier can be odd-dimensional, since the tree accounts for exchanges.

These constructions compose with star stripping and centroid decomposition as specified below. In all cases the attached maps use the same raw congruence.

## Align the adjoint complements by an isometry

Recover \(A=\{a\in\operatorname{End}_F(U):\exists b,\ \beta(ax,y)=\beta(x,by)\}\) by linear equations. The mate \(a^*=b\) is unique by the zero common radical. Alternation makes the mate operation involutive. Moving two maps across the equation in succession gives \((aa')^*=(a')^*a^*\). Thus \(A\) is a unital algebra with involution. A congruence induces an isomorphism with involution on these algebras by conjugation. If \(u^*u=1\) then \(\beta(ux,uy)=\beta(x,y)\), so \(u\) is an actual isometry.

Write \(I=\operatorname{rad}A\), invariant under the involution. Compute the radical and matrix factors of \(A/I\) by the deterministic finite-field algebra algorithm. Compute a unital \(*\)-stable complement by lifting sections from \(A/I^m\) to \(A/I^{2m}\), starting at \(m=1\). Given the old section, a linear lift can be corrected to a multiplicative unital lift \(s\) by linear equations, since the error lies in the square-zero ideal \(I^m/I^{2m}\). Solvability follows from Wedderburn–Malcev existence and conjugacy over the perfect field. Indeed, conjugate any section in the larger quotient using a lift of a correcting unit from the smaller quotient. Equality of images in the smaller quotient implies equality of sections, since both induce the identity on \(A/I\). Now \(s^\#(x)=s(x^*)^*\) is another lift. The difference \(s^\#-s\) is an \(s\)-derivation into the square-zero ideal. Therefore \((s+s^\#)/2\) is multiplicative and is \(*\)-stable. Double until the radical power vanishes. [Ivanyos–Qiao, finite-field algebra algorithms, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18) · [Involutory Wedderburn–Malcev complements](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#S3.SS6.SSS1)

Any two such complements \(S_1,S_2\) are conjugate by an isometry in \(1+I\). Choose a Wedderburn–Malcev conjugating unit \(v\in1+I\). Since the sections preserve the involution,
\[
(v x v^{-1})^*=v x^*v^{-1}\qquad(x\in S_1).
\]
Hence \(a=v^*v\) centralizes \(S_1\). The element \(a-1\) is nilpotent. Form
\[
w=\sum_{i\ge0}(-1)^i\binom{2i}{i}(a-1)^i/4^i,
\]
truncating where the powers vanish. The formal binomial identity over \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\) gives \(w^2a=1\) over \(F\). Also \(w^*=w\). Thus \(u=vw\) is unitary, belongs to \(1+I\), and conjugates the same complements. Its raw determinant is one since \(u-1\) is nilpotent, and it fixes \(Z\). Every transporter can therefore be made to carry the chosen complements by an isometry on the target acting on both input slots through the same map. The construction never needs to find a transporter or its correcting unit.

## Balanced carriers and their characters

For a chosen complement \(S\), group simple factors into orbits \(O\) of the involution on central idempotents. For the self-adjoint orbit idempotent \(e_O\), put \(U_O=e_OU\). Distinct blocks contract to zero by balancing against those idempotents. Define
\[
 C_O=\Lambda^2_F U_O\big/\langle (ax)\wedge y-x\wedge(a^*y):a\in S,\ x,y\in U_O\rangle_F .
\]
There is a surjection \(\psi_O:C_O\to W_O\), the span of the values of \(\beta\) on \(U_O\times U_O\). Compute it by evaluating on wedge bases. In simultaneous records, use a coordinate algebra on the orbit set, with its idempotents acting by projection onto the indicated summands. Link the inclusion of orbit idempotents into \(S\), the faithful action of \(S\) on \(U\), multiplication and involution on \(S\), the wedge-quotient maps, and the sum of the maps \(\psi_O\) to \(Z\). The faithful action forces the induced algebra map, and surjectivity of the wedge maps forces the carrier maps. Every aligned transporter extends, using the indicated induced maps.

Descriptors can be taken to be orbit type, center degree, simple matrix degree, sorted multiplicities on the raw modules, and, for fixed factors, whether the involution fixes the center and the dimension of its fixed subspace. All are computed by linear algebra and the simple algebra algorithm. Mark whole descriptor classes and allow their internal permutation. Isometric complement alignment fixes \(Z\), so the \(W_O\) are intrinsic when indexed by quotient orbits, and hence so are the sorted image flags and overlap graph.

For an exchanged pair, identify its first factor with \(M_d(E)\) and the other with its opposite by the involution. Write \(P=E^d\), \(U_-=P\otimes_E R\), and \(U_+=P^\vee\otimes_E T\). The equal-side wedges vanish in the quotient by applying the central balancing equation. Diagonal matrix units kill unequal evaluation coordinates, other matrix units identify the equal-coordinate wedges, and central scalars give \(E\)-balancing. Consequently the carrier identifies with \(R\otimes_E T\) by
\[
(p\otimes x)\wedge(f\otimes y)\longmapsto f(p)x\otimes y.
\]
The inverse uses the first coordinate of \(P\) paired with its dual coordinate. If \(r=\dim_E R\) and \(s=\dim_E T\) differ, order the two sides by multiplicity. An aligned isomorphism on \(M_d(E)\) is \(\operatorname{Ad}(M)\sigma\), with \(\sigma\) its center action, as follows by putting the images of matrix units into standard form. After factoring off \(M\sigma\) and its linked semilinear dual on the two raw sides, intertwining matrix units leaves multiplicity maps. Use common explicit field models for the fixed degrees. The simple-module action cancels from evaluation. The carrier map is therefore \((H_R\otimes H_T)\sigma\) in these model frames, with determinant
\[
N_{E/F}(\det_E H_R)^s\,N_{E/F}(\det_E H_T)^r\,(\det_F\sigma)^{rs}.
\]
For even \(r,s\) the determinant is one in the model frames, and the dimension \([E:F]rs\) is even. Any permutation of these even-dimensional blocks has determinant one in the concatenated model frames. The balanced quotient is intrinsic after alignment, so no chosen embedded maximal field or independent field automorphism has to be recorded.

For a fixed field factor \(E\) with trivial involution, the carrier is \(\Lambda^2_E U_O\). With \(r=\dim_E U_O\), the carrier determinant is
\[
N_{E/F}(\det_E H)^{r-1}(\det_F\sigma)^{r(r-1)/2}.
\]
It is one for odd \(r\) if \([E:F]\) is odd or \(r\equiv1\bmod4\), since Frobenius acts by a cycle in a normal basis. Use the carrier in an even neutral class if its dimension is even, or in the overlap tree at either parity.

More generally, on a fixed factor \(M_d(E)\) with \([E:F],d,r\) all odd the involution is orthogonal. Its simple-module form can be scaled to square discriminant, leaving the involution unchanged, and put in a common diagonal model over \(E\). The calculation in [SimpleAdjoint.md](SimpleAdjoint.md) identifies the carrier with \(\Lambda^2_E R\) for its multiplicity space. The realizing semilinear similitude has square multiplier in the common model, by taking determinants in degree \(d\). Hence the carrier action is a square scalar times \((\Lambda^2_E H)\sigma\), of determinant one since \(r-1\) is even and \([E:F]\) is odd. Equivalently use the explicit calibration there between differing discriminants.


If the two multiplicities are equal, choose an order on each pair just for the model frames. In these frames a side exchange acts on the balanced carrier by negative matrix transposition followed by a side-preserving map. Indeed the wedge of the two sides changes sign and its multiplicity factors reverse. For \(r=s\), the sign of negative transposition over \(F\) is \((-1)^{[E:F]r(r+1)/2}\). Thus equal even multiplicities give another neutral even-dimensional class when \([E:F]\) is even or \(4\mid r\). In the remaining even case, a local line carrying the carrier determinant is the anti-diagonal in the two-side coordinate algebra. Use it as a local line in the overlap construction. Marking the side-algebra action and its map to \(S\) recovers the actual exchange without imposing either chosen order.

## Thin kernels and large grades

For each descriptor \(D\), form \(C_D=\bigoplus_{O\in D}C_O\), the flag \(Z_{\le D}=\sum_{O\text{ in classes }\le D}W_O\), and the surjection \(f_D:C_D\twoheadrightarrow G_D=Z_{\le D}/Z_{<D}\). The flag ends at \(Z\) by surjectivity of \(\beta\). Include its spaces, inclusions and quotients and the maps \(f_D\). The same raw maps then force the grade actions. For a small grade, record its exterior algebra and top line. Otherwise apply the determinant identity for the exact sequence of \(f_D\). In carrier coordinates let \(d_D\) be the determinant of the frame consisting of a basis of \(\ker f_D\) followed by lifts of the grade basis. In primed notation at the target,
\[
 \det(t|_{G_D})=(d_D/d_D')\det(C_D\to C_D')\det(\ker f_D\to\ker f_D')^{-1}.
\]
This follows by taking the determinant of the block triangular matrix in the adapted frames. The carrier factor is one in the neutral concatenated frames. The last factor is forced by the exterior algebra on the small kernel, with the kernel inclusion to the actual carrier. Use a dual kernel line and rescale it by the frame calibration. The local version for \(C_O\twoheadrightarrow W_O\) is identical. It allows a neutral carrier of odd dimension, or another available carrier line such as the two-side line above. The source and target frame calibrations are encoded by the linear maps to rescaled output lines, also when the orbit is permuted.

For a single carrier with a recovered field \(E\) and \(E\)-stable kernel, one can replace the bound on kernel dimension over \(F\) by the same bound over \(E\). Record the field-module action on the kernel. Form its exterior algebra over \(E\) using that action. For the resulting semilinear action on the top line, the field norm is read by linking its Frobenius-conjugate copies and multiplying their scalars in \(E\), ending with the \(F\cdot1\) line. A quadratic trace-zero line reads the determinant of the common Frobenius when the extension degree is even. Raise that sign to the dimension of the kernel over \(E\). These links and the norm construction are detailed in [MaximalSubfieldDescent.md](PreviousMaximalSubfieldDescent.md).

Multiply the grade lines and calibrate from the adapted flag frames to the given bases of \(Z\). Pin only the resulting product to the prescribed sign. For the overlap construction, use the same local readout on \(W_O\), and attach exterior top lines on the small overlaps. The incidence exact sequence of [OverlapTrees.md](OverlapTrees.md) gives the quotient determinant by dividing the vertex determinant by the edge determinant. The common orbit algebra forces all permutations to agree with the original \(p\), even for a reversal of a central edge.

These constructions use polynomially many spaces and linear and bilinear records of polynomial total dimension. Pack fixed roles by distinct multiplicity markers on the idempotents of a coordinate algebra. A permutable collection is itself one role organized by its own projection algebra. Take map-slot copies linked to their original roles by incidence maps, and use a fixed scalar role to pad array slots. This gives a constant number of arrays of tensor order at most three. The tensor-system reduction yields one ordinary three-tensor isomorphism instance over \(F\). Every matching restricts to the actual bimap transporter of the prescribed sign. Every true witness first aligns complements, preserving both raw determinants, and then extends with its induced maps and lines. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

## A free label sign with no defect bound

There is an additional one-instance branch using only the images \(W_O\), without a width condition on the carriers, kernels or overlaps. Call an orbit sign-scalable if its semisimple orbit algebra has a unit \(a\) with \(a^*a=-e_O\). Acting by \(a\) on \(U_O\) multiplies the label contraction by \(-1\). This property is recognizable from the simple involution type. In an exchanged pair, choose \(-1\) on one side and \(1\) on the other. In second kind use a central scalar of relative norm \(-1\). In first kind with alternating simple-module form, use \(1\) on one Lagrangian and \(-1\) on the other. In first kind with symmetric form over \(E\), \(a\) exists exactly when that form and its negative have the same discriminant class. This is equivalent to \(d\) even or \(-1\) a square in \(E\), by classification of nondegenerate symmetric forms over finite fields of odd characteristic. The reduction only needs to decide the type and condition. [Ivanyos–Qiao, involution and finite-field form classification](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#S3.SS5.SSS6)

Write \(B_0=\ker(\bigoplus_O W_O\to Z)\), with the arrow given by summing the actual inclusions. Compute the split algebra
\[
D=\{(\lambda_O)\in F^{\{O\}}:\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_O) B_0\subseteq B_0\}.
\]
This is a unital subalgebra obtained by linear equations, since the diagonal operator is linear in its coefficients. Partition orbit indices by equality of their evaluations on an \(F\)-basis of \(D\). Then \(D\) consists of all scalars constant on the resulting classes. For each class, its indicator is in \(D\), since an element of \(D\) separates its evaluation from any given other class, and applying \(x\mapsto1-(x-a)^2\) over \(F\) selects the coordinates with value \(a\). Multiplying these selections for all other classes gives exactly the class indicator. Moreover the sum of images \(Z_Q=\sum_{O\in Q}W_O\) over each class \(Q\) is a direct summand of \(Z\), since every indicator descends from the external direct sum via \(B_0\).

Suppose some class \(Q\) consists solely of sign-scalable orbits and has \(\dim_F Z_Q\) odd. Use the indicated unit to rescale \(\beta|_{U_O}\) by \(-1\) on those orbits and the identity elsewhere. Distinct orbits contract to zero, so this defines a genuine bimap automorphism acting by \(-1\) on \(Z_Q\) and \(1\) on its complement. Its label determinant is \(-1\). Thus every ordinary transporter can be corrected to either prescribed label determinant. For this class use one ordinary bimap comparison compiled to TI, even if every carrier defect is large and the overlap graph is arbitrary. The criterion is covariant under complement alignment, so it can be tested on either input. No one-sign search or isometry search is used. This is a separate branch for the label sign, which can also be called after stripping when the retained ambient exponent is zero.

## Composition with star stripping and centroid splitting

The full-star and support operations in [StarStripping.md](StarStripping.md) can be applied first. Carry the exact character \((\det p)^a(\det t)^b\), with exponents in \(\{0,1\}\), and calibrate each target sign as in that proof. If a free exponent is odd, the ordinary core comparison already decides the sign condition. Otherwise apply the label construction when \(b=1\) and link an ambient determinant line when \(a=1\). An ambient line is available for logarithmic dimension, or by the same ordered-class or intrinsic-tree assembly on the blocks \(U_O\) when their ambient lines are available. For example, exchanged even multiplicities give neutral ambient lines when the simple degree \(d\) is also even. The multiplicity determinants occur to the even power \(d\), the simple-module contributions cancel modulo squares, and permuting the even-dimensional raw blocks has determinant one. For equal even multiplicities a side swap on the raw blocks is also even when \(d\) is even. This allows the ambient line in these classes regardless of rank.

One can split the raw bimap by the primitive idempotents of its centroid, as in [CentroidCosets.md](PreviousCentroidCosets.md). Its two input projections agree: if \(\beta(au,v)=c\beta(u,v)=\beta(u,bv)\) then alternation and the zero radical show \(a=b\). The centroid is commutative by commuting the two placements and using the faithful action. Primitive idempotents thus give orthogonal decompositions on \(U\) and \(Z\), with disjoint label summands. Keep their actions on the raw spaces as records, and attach local lines inside the indicated blocks. Order component classes by the tuple of source and label dimensions and the sorted multiset of their adjoint orbit descriptors. For each tied class of size at most \(c\log_2 L\), use one artificial root with one child per component, and apply the signed subset products of the tree construction. This accepts every component permutation within the class and uses polynomial space. In the combined line for powers \(a,b\), the swap of two child subtrees has factor \((-1)^{a\dim U_x\dim U_y+b\dim Z_x\dim Z_y}\). This is the product of the two direct-sum determinant signs. Repeated components may also be grouped when their tested lines and their permutation contributions are neutral. Pin the resulting character only after assembly. In particular none of the local comparisons is solved first or replaced by a Boolean output.

The reduction applies to anchored square-zero pencils with these conditions and preserves the raw sign when changing complements, choosing field and module frames, passing to quotients, and combining components. An unrestricted reduction would suffice for the ternary endpoint after the seven-character assembly. That square-zero case remains open. [Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

## Example with a cyclic overlap and repeated noncommutative factors

Take at least three exchange blocks. Each has \(U_j=(F^d\otimes R)\oplus((F^d)^*\otimes T)\), where \(d\ge2\) and the dimensions \(r,s\) of \(R,T\) are large, distinct and even. On \(U_j\) take the alternating bimap whose mixed contraction is \(\sum_{a=1}^d x_a\otimes y_a\) in \(C_j=R\otimes T\). Mixed contractions between different blocks are zero. Add one three-dimensional block with its full exterior square as label. This makes the total ambient dimension odd.

In each of the first three exchange blocks, choose two matrices of rank \(4d+2\) on disjoint row and column sets, using the two matrices for the two incident edges of a triangle. This is possible for \(r,s\ge8d+4\). For each triangle edge quotient the total label sum by the relation identifying its two endpoint matrices. These three relations are independent. On each block, every nonzero projected relation has matrix rank at least \(4d+2\).

The quotient keeps the same adjoints as the unquotiented bimap. Indeed every projected difference of two adjoint-equation contractions has rank at most \(4d\). If it lies in the relation space, all of its exchange projections vanish by the preceding rank bound. Its three-dimensional exterior label also vanishes since that block has no relation. Thus every quotient adjoint satisfies the original equations. The same argument with one contraction shows that the common radical stays zero.

For the unquotiented bimap, different blocks have disjoint labels and zero common radicals, which rules out off-block adjoints by testing their two inputs on different blocks. On an exchange block, write the two input sides as \(X=F^d\otimes R\) and \(Y=(F^d)^*\otimes T\). Test an adjoint equation on vectors from \(X\) supported in independent \(R\)-lines. The two contributions from side-changing parts then belong to disjoint \(R\)-line tensor summands. Varying the vector in \(F^d\) forces those parts to vanish. Testing on \(Y\) gives the other direction. For mixed inputs supported in a single \(R\)-line and a single \(T\)-line, projecting the equation off either line forces each side-preserving map to preserve every such line summand. Additivity on sums of independent vectors implies that it is the same \(F^d\) endomorphism on every multiplicity coordinate. The mixed equation pairs that endomorphism with its transpose. Thus the adjoint is \(M_d(F)\times M_d(F)^{\mathrm{op}}\) on each exchange block, with the two factors exchanged by the involution. On the three-dimensional full-wedge block the adjoint is scalar: the equation \(ax\wedge y=x\wedge by\) for all \(y\) forces \(ax\) to belong to every plane spanned by \(x\) and \(y\), hence to \(Fx\). Linearity and the corresponding mate equation give scalar maps on both slots.

The descriptor class of exchange carriers has a three-dimensional kernel onto its label grade. Pairwise overlaps of the three glued labels give a triangle since their edge vectors have disjoint supports in each carrier. Further unglued exchange blocks of the same descriptor can be permuted freely. The flag construction therefore gives one query here, with no overlap tree and with both the label and its exterior codimension growing quadratically in \(r,s\) for fixed \(d\) and fixed block count.
