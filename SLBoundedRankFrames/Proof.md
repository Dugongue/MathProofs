# Determinant profiles from bounded-rank Peirce frames

Informal mathematics. Fix $F=\mathbb F_q$ and constants $r,c$. Consider a square matrix pencil $M:U\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)$, injective, with $\dim X=\dim Y$. Use the bilinear convention
$$
M'(fv)=hM(v)g^{-1}.
$$
The theorem computes the full possible triples $(\det f,\det g,\det h)$, in the raw coordinate bases. It therefore decides each determinant character on the corresponding explicit truncated triangular multiplication algebras whenever the residue pencil satisfies the hypotheses below.

Assume there exists a subspace $H_0\subset U$ of codimension at most $c$ with a basis $u_i$ such that the image spaces $Y_i=\operatorname{im}M(u_i)$ are in direct sum, the row spaces of $M(u_i)$ are in direct sum in $X^*$, and the ranks $r_i$ satisfy
$$
1\le r_i\le r,\qquad r_i+r_j>r\quad(i\ne j).
$$
Write $K=\bigcap_i\ker M(u_i)$ and $Y_0=\bigoplus_i Y_i$. Suppose every nonzero class in $U/H_0$ induces a map $K\to Y/Y_0$ of rank greater than $r$. Suppose also that at least one of these maps is invertible. Then there is a deterministic polynomial-time ordinary-TI Turing reduction computing the exact profile. If the complement dimension $\dim K$ is bounded by a fixed constant, colored graph isomorphism alone suffices as the oracle. If, for some fixed list-length bound, the construction below finds a list containing an invertible slice and generating the full matrix algebra after normalization, the profile is computable in deterministic polynomial time without an oracle.

The bases, subspaces, and marked slice lists are recovered from raw input. In particular rank one is not required, the ranks $r_i$ can differ, and the characteristic may divide $r_i$.

## Recover the frame in raw coordinates

Let $D\ge\max(q,c(q-1)+r+1,r+1+2(q-2))$. Form the span of all monomial multiples through total degree $D$ of the $(r+1)$-minors of $M$ and the field equations $x_j^q-x_j$. Its intersection with homogeneous linear forms annihilates $H_0$, since all its elements vanish at the frame vectors. In the other direction, write $z$ for hidden coordinates of $U/H_0$. For each nonzero $a\in F^{\dim U/H_0}$, one $(r+1)$-minor of the compressed pencil is nonzero at $a$. It is a linear combination of the original minors after constant row and column maps. Multiply it by the finite-field indicator $I_a(z)=\prod_j(1-(z_j-a_j)^{q-1})$. Reduce modulo the field equations in the quotient coordinates to get a nonzero multiple of $I_a$. Every reduction has total degree at most $D$, and the field equations of hidden linear coordinates are linear combinations of the raw ones. Interpolating $z_j=\sum_{a\ne0}a_j I_a(z)$ proves that the annihilator is exact. Thus Gaussian elimination recovers $H_0$.

Now restrict the minors to $H_0$ and similarly compute the homogeneous quadratic consequences with field equations through degree $D$. In hidden frame coordinates $x_i$ the pencil is diagonal by blocks of sizes $r_i$. For $i\ne j$, taking all $r_i$ rows and columns of block $i$ and $r+1-r_i$ rows and columns of block $j$ gives a nonzero multiple of $x_i^{r_i}x_j^{r+1-r_i}$, using invertible constant row and column basis changes. The second exponent lies in $[1,r_j]$. Multiply by powers of $x_i,x_j$ of exponent at most $q-2$ each to make both exponents congruent to one modulo $q-1$. Reduction by the field equations gives $x_i x_j$. Conversely a quadratic consequence vanishes on every frame vector, so it is in the span of these $x_i x_j$. Covariance of the bounded-degree calculations follows from Cauchy–Binet and the covariance of the field equations under $F$-linear substitution.

In any computed $H_0$ basis pair quadratic coefficients with upper-triangular entries of symmetric matrices, so $ww^{\mathsf T}$ represents evaluation at $w$. The annihilator is the span of the $u_i u_i^{\mathsf T}$. If $H_0=0$, use the empty frame. Otherwise recover the $F$-rational lines $Fu_i$ by simultaneous diagonalization. If $D_1,\ldots,D_s$ is a basis of this symmetric space, scan $D(t)=\sum_j t^{j-1}D_j$ over an extension of polynomial cardinality for an invertible $D(t_0)$. The hidden coefficient polynomials have degree at most $s-1$, are nonzero, and are pairwise nonproportional. Scan again until $D(t_1)D(t_0)^{-1}$ has simple spectrum. There are at most $s(s-1)$ forbidden values at the first scan and $\binom{s}{2}(s-1)$ at the second. Its eigenlines are the frame lines: writing the hidden basis matrix $U_0$, this product is $U_0\operatorname{diag}(d_i(t_1)/d_i(t_0))U_0^{-1}$. Normalize each eigenvector by its first nonzero coordinate to read its line over $F$. Computing and scanning an extension of sufficiently large polynomial cardinality is polynomial at fixed $q$, such as by trial of irreducible polynomials of logarithmic degree followed by evaluation in the extension. Check the recovered lines, the direct-sum ranks, and the quotient rank promise by enumerating the $q^c$ quotient vectors. This gives a recognized class. Every transporter maps the computed frame onto the mate, since each step used spaces or finite sets defined by intrinsic equations.

## Split into matrix blocks without guessing all frame matches

For any projective direction $\bar b$ of $U/H_0$ with invertible compression, let
$$
X_0=\{x:M(b)x\in Y_0\},\qquad Y_1=M(b)K.
$$
These depend only on the direction because $M(H_0)K=0$ and $\operatorname{im}M(H_0)\subseteq Y_0$. Invertibility gives $X=X_0\oplus K$ and $Y=Y_0\oplus Y_1$. Recover the subspaces $X_i\subset X_0$ as the kernels of all $M(u_j)$ there with $j\ne i$. The restriction $M_i:X_i\to Y_i$ of $M(u_i)$ is invertible and $X_0=\bigoplus_i X_i$. These facts follow because the simultaneous map $X_0\to \bigoplus_i Y_i$ given by all $M(u_i)$ has zero kernel and its domain and codomain have equal dimension. Write $\ast$ for the complement block on each side. A transporter carrying $\bar b$ to $\bar b'$ permutes corresponding $X_i,Y_i$ by the same label map, and maps the complement spaces to the corresponding complements.

## Bounded complements permit a finite relational reduction

Fix one source direction with invertible compression. Enumerate every quotient isomorphism $\bar f:U/H_0\to U'/H'_0$, retaining only those carrying that direction to one with invertible compression. There are constantly many choices. For this branch construct both matrix block decompositions. Choose source lifts $v_j$ of a quotient basis and target lifts $v'_j$ of its images. Set $B_j=M(v_j)$, $B'_j=M'(v'_j)$.

Make a finite relational structure on the vector sets of each $X_i,Y_i$, and also the two complement blocks. Keep the block labels, identify which $X,Y$ blocks have the same index, and designate the complement by a separate color. Encode addition and multiplication by every element of $F$ on each vector set. This forces each vector map to be an actual $F$-linear isomorphism. Attach the nonzero maps in $F M_i$ as records with their graph-of-evaluation relation on $X_i\times Y_i$. For $B_j$ keep its name $j$ fixed. Encode each off-diagonal block, including the complement incidences, by its graph of evaluation. For its diagonal block $(i,i)$ at a frame index keep the evaluation graphs of the $q$ maps in $B_{j,ii}+F M_i$ as an unordered family, with one record per map. At the complement diagonal encode its one exact block map. A relational isomorphism induces matrices $g,h$ and a frame permutation $\sigma$. Its action on the frame line of maps determines the unique nonzero $s_i$ in
$$
hM(u_i)g^{-1}=s_i M'(u'_{\sigma(i)}).
$$
It also satisfies $hB_jg^{-1}-B'_j\in M'(H'_0)$ by the off-diagonal and diagonal conditions. Conversely these identities imply the relational isomorphism by carrying the evaluation records to their maps.

Recover $f$ by taking $f(u_i)=s_i u'_{\sigma(i)}$, and
$$
f(v_j)=v'_j+(M'|_{H'_0})^{-1}(hB_jg^{-1}-B'_j).
$$
This is invertible and gives an original transporter with quotient map $\bar f$. The added terms in $H'_0$ do not affect $\det f$. This also proves that any original transporter in this branch has a lift to the relational structure. With bounded complement and frame dimensions each block vector set has bounded size at fixed $q$, so the total relational description is polynomial in the raw input. Ordinary colored GI encodes it by the standard incidence vertices with distinct coordinate-position colors.

Use the colored GI decision oracle to recover a relative isomorphism by pinning vertices in succession. Generate the source automorphism group by the same query with fixed source and target structure. For the $j$-th vertex in a fixed ordering and each possible target image, test whether the prefix stabilizer reaches it. A yes query followed by further pins gives a transporter for that orbit item. These representatives over the entire chain generate the group by successively matching the next vertex. There are polynomially many queries. The relative determinant triple multiplied by the subgroup generated by those of the source automorphisms gives exactly the branch's full determinant coset, because the correspondence just proved takes graph isomorphisms to original transporters with the fixed quotient map and respects composition in the self-branch. Unite the profiles over the constant quotient branches. Colored GI reduces to ordinary TI over the same field, giving the claimed Turing reduction. [Grochow–Qiao, Tensor Isomorphism-Completeness](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

## Arbitrary complements are handled as modules after polynomial marking

Use the same quotient branches, block decompositions, and lifts as above. Within one branch an ordinary TI query can fix the quotient map and prescribe any matched list of vectors in $X_0$ and $Y_0$. Keep the original bilinear tensor, the quotient projection $U\to U/H_0$, and maps whose columns are the marked vectors in each sort. Pin their coordinate spaces by a shift and its cyclic vector, and pin the scalar sort by field multiplication. Mark a basis of $U/H_0$ and its prescribed image. A fixed number of tensors of order at most three describe all these marks, because each mark list occupies one matrix. Evaluation pairings tie duals to their primal actions. The fixed-signature ordinary-TI compiler therefore gives polynomial-size queries with precisely the stated feasibility conditions. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4.Thmtheorem1)

Choose a basis in every $X_i,Y_i$. For one such vector its candidates are the union of vector sets of corresponding target blocks. Each set has at most $q^r$ vectors and there are only linearly many blocks. The marked quotient branch forces the relevant splits, and the recovered frame guarantees that a real transporter sends each basis vector into its candidate set. Greedy feasibility queries therefore recover a realized pair of full top maps $g_0:X_0\to X'_0$, $h_0:Y_0\to Y'_0$, whenever the quotient branch is feasible.

The label map is now determined. Its frame-line scalars come from $h_0 M_i g_0^{-1}$, and the top-top equation for each slice lift reads off the unique shear in $H'_0$. The top-top block matters here in full, including off-diagonal entries. The other three blocks contain no unknown shear, because $M'(H'_0)$ is supported in the top-top block. They are precisely the equations
$$
 h_0 B_{j,0\ast}=B'_{j,0\ast}g_\ast,\qquad
 h_\ast B_{j,\ast0}=B'_{j,\ast0}g_0,\qquad
 h_\ast B_{j,\ast\ast}=B'_{j,\ast\ast}g_\ast.
$$
Here $0$ aggregates the frame blocks and $\ast$ denotes the complement. The equations describe isomorphism of a pair of four-vertex quiver representations with the two top maps prescribed. Align the two target top spaces by $g_0,h_0$ and adjoin a single one-dimensional selector vertex, with labeled arrows to all top basis vectors. An isomorphism of the resulting quivers acts on both top spaces and the selector by one common scalar. Dividing all its vertex maps by that scalar makes the two top maps the required identities in the aligned bases. Construct this isomorphism by the deterministic module isomorphism algorithm on the direct sums of vertex spaces, using the vertex projections and arrows as operators. This obtains a genuine complete transporter, and its determinants are evaluated in the original bases. [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, Polynomial Time Algorithms for Modules over Finite Dimensional Algebras](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf)

For the source stabilizer fixing the quotient pointwise, use the same marked queries to find the consecutive stabilizer transversals on the chosen top basis vectors. For every allowed image that passes the prefix test, greedily complete the remaining top marks and construct the module isomorphism as above. The resulting stabilizer elements, together with the subgroup fixing all top vectors, generate the pointwise-quotient stabilizer. Indeed one can successively divide any element by a chosen lift of the current image until it fixes every top vector.

In the last subgroup the label map is the identity, since the quotient, frame lines with their scalars, and all lift shears are fixed. Compute the endomorphism algebra $E$ of the source quiver with the selector. Its radical $J$ and its semisimple quotient $\prod_t\operatorname{Mat}_{d_t}(F_t)$ are computable in deterministic polynomial time at fixed characteristic. On each vertex space $V$, let $\mu_{V,t}$ be the multiplicity of the $t$-th simple module. Compute it by filtering by $J^jV$, applying the simple central idempotent to each resulting grade, and dividing its dimension by $d_t[F_t:F]$. For a unit with simple components $a_t$, its determinant on $V$ is
$$
 \prod_t \mathrm N_{F_t/F}(\det_{F_t}a_t)^{\mu_{V,t}}.
$$
This follows from determinants on the filtration. Units lift modulo $J$, and each norm of a simple determinant independently ranges over $F^\times$, since finite-field norms are surjective. Thus the joint image on $(K,Y_1,\text{selector})$ is generated by the exponent triples $(\mu_{K,t},\mu_{Y_1,t},\mu_{\mathrm{selector},t})$ applied to a generator of $F^\times$. Intersect this joint image with selector coordinate one. This reads the exact determinant triples of the last subgroup, because the selector arrows force both top maps to be the identity on that fiber. Together with the recorded stabilizer lifts, this produces the full pointwise-quotient determinant subgroup. Multiply it by a relative representative for each feasible quotient branch and take their union. The computation uses polynomially many queries and module operations, even when $K$ grows with the input. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

## Short canonical lists remove the oracle on another branch

For each projective direction $\bar b$ with invertible compression build the block decomposition above. For an actual vector $\bar v\in U/H_0$ and any lift $v$, the $(i,i)$ block of $M(v)$ is an affine line of possible matrices up to adding a multiple of $M_i$. Score a candidate matrix $D$ in that affine line by the sorted list of characteristic polynomials of $(\lambda M_i)^{-1}D$ for all $\lambda\in F^\times$, with the score of zero placed first. This is invariant under the independent choices of frame-line representatives, simultaneous block matching, and isomorphism. If all the blockwise least scores are attained uniquely for that particular $\bar v$, the resulting choices recover a unique lift of $\bar v$. Each block can be evaluated independently, since the frame maps have disjoint supports. If a least score ties, omit that vector from this construction. In particular if a block affine line contains zero, zero is the unique preferred matrix: its other members are nonzero multiples of $M_i$ and have nonzero eigenvalue in the normalized matrices. This step introduces no linearity requirement on the section. Enumerate bases of the quotient, using only vectors with unique lifts, and append a bounded number of frame vectors in all possible ways. All lists are intrinsic and there are polynomially many.

Retain lists with an invertible first slice $B$ whose normalized other slices generate the full matrix algebra. Choose one retained source list and try every retained target list. For each pair of lists the normalized anchor equations are linear simultaneous intertwiner equations $gN_i=N'_i g$. A nonzero solution is invertible since its kernel is invariant under the full source matrix algebra. All solutions differ by scalars after a nonzero one is chosen. For a solution $g_0$, test if it conjugates the entire source normalized pencil subspace onto the target one. If so, injectivity recovers the uniquely induced label map, and the original first slice determines $h$. Scaling $g_0$ and $h$ by the same base-field scalar runs through the entire marked branch. The lists are intrinsic, so testing the mates of one qualifying source list recovers the whole determinant profile. All operations are polynomial. This refines the prior rank-one anchor method and also works when the characteristic divides the frame ranks.

## Growing scalar-stabilizer examples with no rank-one slices

Fix $r\ge2$. Choose a prime $d>r$ and a degree $d$ irreducible over $F$ of nonzero trace and nonzero constant term. Such a field element exists since the number with nonzero trace in $F_{q^d}$ is $(q-1)q^{d-1}$, larger than $q$, and $d$ is prime. Let $J$ be its companion matrix. Take $m\ge3$ blocks of size $r$ followed by a bottom block of size $d$, and put $n=rm+d$. Let $P$ shift the top blocks cyclically, taking each internal coordinate unchanged. Put $B_1=\operatorname{diag}(P,I_d)$ and $B_2=B_1N$, where $N e_j=e_{j+1}$ for $j<n$ and $N e_n=e_1+\sum_{\ell=1}^d c_\ell e_{rm+\ell}$, using the last-column coefficients of $J$. Use $m$ block identities $D_i$, and $B_1,B_2$, as the $m+2$ slices. The pencil is concise and the two quotient generators compress to $I_d,J$. Every nonzero quotient combination is invertible over $F$ on the complement. The rank-$\le r$ slice lines are exactly the $F D_i$, with all nonzero ranks exactly $r$.

The $B_1$-split is the displayed split, and $B_1,B_2$ have zero top diagonal blocks because the top contributions move a block forward by one or two. Therefore their quotient basis has unique preferred lifts. Take also $D_2$; its normalized operator is $P^{-1}D_2$ supported from block two to block one. Multiplying this operator on the left by $N^r$ gives the coordinate projection $E$ onto the second block. The conjugate $NEN^{-1}$ projects onto the shifted coordinate interval, since the exceptional last column of $N$ has no coordinate in that interval. The inverse of $N$ belongs to the generated algebra by Cayley–Hamilton. Then $E(I-NEN^{-1})$ projects onto the first coordinate of block two. The row and column of that coordinate are cyclic for $N$: the columns follow the invertible companion chain, and the rows iterate backward to $e_1^{\mathsf T}$, then $e_n^{\mathsf T}$, and down the remaining chain by subtracting known multiples of $e_n^{\mathsf T}$. All powers $N^a E(I-NEN^{-1})N^b$ therefore span full matrix space. This verifies the constructive anchor test.

To compute the stabilizer, consider the intrinsic condition that a direction with invertible compression has at most one nonzero off-diagonal top block in every block column, in its own split. In the original coordinates for a quotient direction $bB_1+tB_2$, the matrix in this split is the Schur complement
$$
 bP+tP L-t^2U(bI_d+tJ)^{-1}V ,
$$
where $L$ is the top truncated companion shift and the cross maps $U,V$ of $B_2$ are supported from the last bottom coordinate to the first coordinate of top block two, and from the last top coordinate to the first bottom coordinate, respectively. This is obtained by identifying the split complements with their top and bottom coordinates and taking the Schur complement. The last term has source in the last top block, so it does not affect first block column. For $t\ne0$, that first block column has nonzero entries to both top blocks two and three, since they are $bI_r+tJ_r(0)$ and a nonzero rank-one boundary arrow. These are off-diagonal entries, unaffected by the choice of lift. For $t=0$ the top matrix is $bP$, which has just one nonzero block in each block column. The direction $F\bar B_1$ is consequently intrinsic. Its split and the unique zero-diagonal lifts force $B_1$ to be scaled and $B_2$ to be mapped into $\operatorname{span}(B_1,B_2)$. The normalized domain map consequently conjugates $N$ to $aN+bI$. Every top diagonal block of $N$ has sole eigenvalue zero. Since the map permutes top blocks, comparison forces $b=0$. The nonzero arrows among distinct top blocks of $N$ form a directed path, so the top permutation is trivial. Thus $g e_1$ lies in the first block and $gN=aNg$. The requirement $g e_r$ lie in that same block forces $g e_1$ to be a multiple of $e_1$, by taking $r-1$ steps of the shift and looking at the spill into block two. Consequently the coordinate scalars of $g$ are $g_1a^{j-1}$. Comparing the $e_n$-coefficient in the final companion equation gives $a=1$ because $c_d\ne0$. Hence both matrix-mode maps are scalar, and the label map is the corresponding scalar. This gives the full scalar stabilizer at arbitrary size.

The two matrix modes have scalar pair adjoint because the normalized slices generate full matrix algebra. For either remaining pair adjoint, the corresponding left or right idealizer preserves the slice space. Applied to $D_i$ its value must be zero or a multiple of that same $D_i$, by rank and the support in just the input block, or just the output block for the right idealizer. For the left idealizer the matrix is therefore top-block scalar diagonal with arbitrary top-right and bottom diagonal blocks. Applying it to $B_1$ gives zero bottom-left block, ruling out any $B_2$ coefficient. Comparing the remaining blocks forces the top-right block to vanish and the top and bottom diagonal blocks to be one common scalar. For the right idealizer use the top-right block to rule out the $B_2$ coefficient, with the remaining comparison giving the same conclusion.

## Effect on the normalized multiplication class

Take two multiplication tensors promised to come from the same truncated-corner parameters and padding. The split algebra before padding has three ordered diagonal rings $F[t]/(t^{M_p})$. The three nonzero corners $01,12,02$ are free modules over $F[t]/(t^{k_i})$, with truncation for the two diagonal actions. The only product between distinct corners is the residue tensor extended by the coefficient ring. The determinant formula of the normalization is
$$
  \det\theta=\prod_i (\det a_i)^{k_i}
$$
in adapted bases, where $a_i$ are the three residue maps. The formula follows by taking determinants on the corner and diagonal filtrations. Before cancellation the uniform parameter change $\lambda$ contributes the power $\sum_p\binom{M_p}{2}+\sum_i\dim(X_i^{\mathrm{res}})\binom{k_i}{2}$, which is zero modulo $q-1$ by the chosen normalization. The padding fields have odd and pairwise distinct degrees, so they add determinant one. Every residue transporter lifts to an algebra isomorphism in the displayed models by acting coefficientwise. In arbitrary recovered bases its effect is related by the known adapted-basis determinant ratio.

These data can be recovered when the tensors are given in arbitrary multiplication-isotope bases. First normalize an input $T:X\times Y\to V$ using the left and right adjoint pairs. In a multiplication isotope they are copies of the left and right regular actions. A regular module cyclic vector can be computed by finding a generator modulo the radical and lifting it, so take such vectors $x_0,y_0$ for the two actions. The maps $R(x)=T(x,y_0)$, $L(y)=T(x_0,y)$ are invertible, and $z*z'=T(R^{-1}z,L^{-1}z')$ is unital associative, isomorphic to the original algebra. This normalizes both sides with explicit linear maps. Testing associativity and computing the finite module decompositions use polynomial time.

Compute the center by linear equations. Its primitive idempotents separate the padding factors, with the triangular summand recognized by its nonzero radical. Radical computations and the semisimple quotient of the triangular summand recover its three residue idempotents. In characteristic $p$, an arbitrary lift $x$ of one residue idempotent has $x^2-x$ nilpotent. Raising $x$ to a sufficiently large $p$-power gives an exact idempotent lift because $x^2$ and $x$ commute. Lift the successive idempotents in the complementary corners, giving an orthogonal system. The three nonzero off-diagonal corners order the vertices intrinsically. Changing a lift system by a radical inner automorphism has determinant one, so this choice introduces no unknown determinant factor.

Choose a generator $t_0$ of the diagonal radical at vertex zero, by choosing any element nonzero modulo its square. Solve linear equations for $t_1,t_2$ in the other diagonal radicals so that all three corner identities $t_p x=x t_{p'}$ hold. These equations have a solution because in the displayed model the same univariate polynomial in the standard parameter acts on each corner. Each chosen $t_p$ is first-order nonzero because every $k_i\ge2$. Divide each corner by the action of its parameter, choose a raw basis of the residue, and lift that basis to elements of the corner. Multiply each lift by the successive parameter powers to obtain an adapted basis, together with the diagonal powers and arbitrary bases on the padding fields. Change-of-basis determinants are now explicit. The induced multiplication of the recovered $01$ and $12$ residues into $02$ is the original pencil in recovered coordinates. Modeling each tensor by its promised displayed algebra proves that its computed residue tensor is isomorphic to the displayed residue tensor. Thus the lifting argument applies to all residue transporters even when these computable lifts of the residue basis have higher-degree terms.

Apply the determinant-profile algorithm to the residue pencil whenever it satisfies the raw frame hypotheses. For each possible residue determinant triple, use the displayed exponent formula and the computed adapted-basis determinants. This gives the exact algebra-isomorphism determinant coset for the recovered algebras. For multiplication-tensor isotopy, every transporter in the bilinear convention has maps $L_a\theta,R_b\theta,L_aR_b\theta$, with $a,b$ independent target units and $\theta$ an algebra isomorphism. Compute the possible left and right unit determinants separately from the same semisimple-quotient norm formula applied to the two regular modules. The inverse duals in the first two covariant modes and the known maps $R,L$ convert the triple to the original input coordinates. In the normalized multiplication bases its product is $(\det\theta)^{-1}$. For the same-format literal multiplication tensors in the displayed bases, a determinant-one $\theta$ gives all three determinants one. Thus the result is an ordinary-TI Turing reduction for this structured subclass, with arbitrary complement dimension, raw tensor bases, and no supplied frames. Universal coverage of the triangular multiplication class remains open.

The normalizations and determinant-filtration calculations reuse the truncated-corner character construction and the active radical generator argument. The bounded-degree raw frame extraction and the reconstruction across unrestricted complements give the sufficient condition here.
