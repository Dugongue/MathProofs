# Group epimorphisms by action-kernel extension classes

## Results

Consider Cayley-table groups with compatible decompositions
\[
G=A\rtimes K,\qquad H=B\rtimes L.
\]
Here \(A,B\) are abelian normal Hall subgroups on the same set of primes, allowing primes absent from one group. Put \(E=\ker(L\to\operatorname{Aut}(B))\) and \(D=L/E\). The following are deterministic polynomial table-time results for deciding, constructing, and exactly counting epimorphisms \(G\twoheadrightarrow H\).

1. If \(K,L\) are abelian, it suffices that the abelian extension \(0\to E\to L\to D\to0\) be supported on a bounded-generator direct summand of \(E\). In particular the target action kernel can be nonsplit cyclic of unbounded order. There is no exponent or rank bound on the two Hall modules or on the action quotients. The same method may instead use a bounded-support source extension, as specified below.
2. Allow \(K\) to be arbitrary. Suppose \(E\le Z(L)\), \(D\) is abelian, and both \(d(E)\), the number of generators of \(E\), and \(|L/Z(L)|\) are bounded by constants. The same conclusions hold. In particular the target complement can be nonabelian.

An extension is *supported on a direct summand* \(E_0\le E\) if its pushout along \(E\to E/E_0\) splits. The constants above are fixed. The algorithms compute extension-class orbits rather than enumerating complement isomorphisms. The split-target case and fixed-map module counting are established precedents. The nonsplit class-orbit step and the indicated nonabelian case are the new results here. [Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar, Open Question 3(4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7) · [Earlier split-action-kernel theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ConstrainedEpimorphismCampaign/Groups.md)

## 1. Common group reduction and counting

An epimorphism sends \(A\) onto \(B\) and sends \(K\) to a complement of \(B\). In fact the image of \(A\) is a normal subgroup on the Hall primes, and its quotient in \(H\) is a quotient of \(K\), of order prime to those primes. The image of \(K\) then has the order of a complement. Complements are conjugate by \(B\), with stabilizer \(B^L\). Normalize a map by taking the image complement to be \(L\). The normalized maps are exactly the pairs consisting of epimorphisms \(A\to B\) and \(\beta:K\to L\) such that the first map intertwines the actions through \(\beta\). Each fixed image complement has the same number of maps by conjugation. Denote their number of choices by \(\kappa=|B|/|B^L|\).

List one representative \(B'\) of each possible \(K\)-module quotient of \(A\), up to \(K\)-module isomorphism. There are at most \(|A|\) types and the list is computable in polynomial table time. More explicitly, at a prime \(p\) dividing \(|A|\), coprime module structure decomposes the primary part into homocyclic lifts of simple \(\mathbb F_p K\)-modules, each with a simple residue type and a length. A type of length \(u\) is free over \(\mathbb Z/p^u\mathbb Z\). Lifts of a fixed simple at a given length are isomorphic, and quotienting such a lift by \(p^v\) gives the lift at length \(v\), omitting \(v=0\). Equal-length lift isomorphism follows by lifting a residue isomorphism and averaging the lifted map over \(K\); the reduced map is invertible. Maps between different simple types vanish, since their composition factors are different. Within one type, the quotient lists of lengths are exactly the descending lists \(\mu_i\) with \(0\le\mu_i\le\lambda_i\), where \(\lambda_i\) is the descending source list and zeros are appended. Necessity follows from surjectivity on every \(p^{j-1}\)-layer modulo \(p^j\). Sufficiency comes from taking the respective reductions. The number of lists over all types and primes is bounded by
\[
\prod_i(\lambda_i+1)\le 2^{\sum_i\lambda_i}\le |A|.
\]
One table representative per list is obtained by quotienting the displayed summands.

For the effective decomposition, use that coprime primary indecomposables are homocyclic with simple head and generated by one element as a group-ring module. Scan elements of the current primary module for a proper nonzero cyclic \(K\)-submodule that is a direct summand. Additive splitting is tested by solving the linear congruences for a retraction, and an additive retraction can be averaged over \(K\) to become equivariant. Continue on the two summands when a split succeeds. If none succeeds a cyclic indecomposable summand of a proposed nontrivial decomposition would contradict the scan. Heads and residue module isomorphisms are computed by linear algebra. All relevant subgroups and action tables have at most \(|G|\) entries per element index. [Coprime module structure and decomposition method, Lemmas 1–2 and §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S4) · [Finite-ring splitting algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08365)

For each \(B'\), put \(C=\operatorname{im}(K\to\operatorname{Aut}(B'))\), with quotient map \(\rho:K\to C\). In both theorems \(D\) is abelian, so reject this profile if \(C\) is nonabelian. Compare
\[
X=B'\rtimes C,\qquad Y=B\rtimes D.
\]
Both have abelian Sylow towers. For example each Sylow subgroup of \(B'\) is normal, and their successive removal leaves the abelian complement \(C\). Babai–Qiao give the isomorphism coset \(\mathcal I=\operatorname{Iso}(X,Y)\) in polynomial table time. Their output includes generators of \(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\) and one representative if nonempty. The Hall modules are characteristic relative to the fixed prime set, so each \(\gamma\in\mathcal I\) induces an action intertwiner \(g:B'\to B\) and a quotient isomorphism \(\alpha:C\to D\). Each such pair \((g,\alpha)\) occurs for exactly \(\kappa\) choices of \(\gamma\): normalizing its image complement by \(B\) gives the unique isomorphism with those data sending \(C\) into \(D\), and there are \(\kappa\) choices of image complement. Here \(B^L=B^D\). [Babai–Qiao, Theorem 1.1](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2012.453)

Write
\[
b(\alpha)=\#\{\beta:K\twoheadrightarrow L\mid\pi\beta=\alpha\rho\},
\qquad \pi:L\to D.
\]
For a fixed \(K\)-equivariant quotient map \(u:A\twoheadrightarrow B'\), the data \((u,\gamma,\beta)\) give the normalized epimorphism with lower map \(gu\) and complement map \(\beta\). Conversely a normalized epimorphism of quotient type \(B'\) arises in this way. Its possible identifications \(g\) of \(B'\) with the pulled-back \(K\)-module \(B\) are a torsor for \(\operatorname{Aut}_K(B')\). Each \(g\) determines \(u\), and its induced \(\alpha\) is forced by \(\beta\) and the faithful actions. There are then \(\kappa\) choices of \(\gamma\). Dividing the triple count by this multiplicity and restoring the \(\kappa\) image complements gives the contribution of \(B'\):
\[
\frac{|\operatorname{Epi}_K(A,B')|}{|\operatorname{Aut}_K(B')|}
    \sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal I} b(\alpha_\gamma).
\tag{1}
\]
Each group epimorphism has a unique quotient type on \(A\), so these contributions are disjoint. The module factors are computed below. An individual positive branch gives an epimorphism explicitly. When \(A,B,K,L\) are supplied by their decompositions the construction is direct. For a supplied common Hall prime set, recover the normal Hall subgroups and complements by the polynomial table algorithms for normal Hall subgroups of solvable groups, or the classical abelian Hall splitting linear system. For the nonabelian case with \(K\) not promised solvable, the decomposition and an abelian normal Hall subgroup of \(G\) are part of the scope. No prime-set guessing or decomposition multiplicity enters (1).

## 2. Counting the required module maps

We use the following fixed-map count. The source \(U\), target \(V\) and common quotient \(W\) are finite abelian groups, with prescribed epimorphisms \(\eta:U\to W\) and \(\theta:V\to W\). A coprime action on these groups may be given, and all maps are then required to intertwine that action. We need the count of epimorphisms \(s:U\to V\) with \(\theta s=\eta\). The cases of trivial \(W\) and trivial action suffice for the respective applications of (1).

Work one prime and one simple residue type at a time, with the coprime decompositions above. If the action is trivial, the simple type is just \(\mathbb F_p\). On the source, each homocyclic simple lift \(U_i\) of length \(t_i\) is projective over \((\mathbb Z/p^{t_i}\mathbb Z)[K]\): its underlying free scalar module lifts linearly through any surjection, and averaging over the coprime acting group makes the lift equivariant. The allowed maps \(U_i\to V\) subject to the mark equation form an independently specified affine congruence system. Solve it using the cyclic generator images as variables, including generator orders, equivariance and the given mark. Smith form computes one solution and the sizes and images of its homogeneous kernel. [Integer Smith form](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040) · [Prior fixed-map count](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ConstrainedEpimorphismCampaign/Groups.md)

In the multiplicity space of the target head \(V/pV\), one head column varies over an affine space \(v_i+W_{t_i}\). The coefficient field is the finite endomorphism field of the simple residue module. The space \(W_t\) corresponds to the image of \((\ker\theta)[p^t]\) in the target head, hence these spaces are nested. Indeed every variation takes values in that subgroup. Conversely each map of the simple head into its image lifts by projectivity over length \(t\). Every allowed column has the same number of lifts, computed by the zero-head homogeneous congruences. All field coordinates can be obtained by taking the linear commutant of the simple module.

Order the columns by increasing \(t_i\). Track the rank \(r\) of the span of chosen columns. At step \(i\), the image of the preceding span modulo \(W_{t_i}\) is the span of the preceding offsets modulo that subspace, since all previous variations lie in it. Let the rank of that span be \(d\). If \(v_i\) projects outside it, every choice increases the rank. Otherwise exactly \(|\mathbb D|^{r-d}\) points of \(v_i+W_{t_i}\) lie in the preceding span, where \(\mathbb D\) is the endomorphism field. These choices keep the rank, and all remaining points increase it. This gives the exact rank distribution with only one state per rank. Count the full-rank outcomes, multiply by the constant lift factors, and combine the independent simple types and primes. Surjectivity of each head map is equivalent to surjectivity on the original primary part by iterating powers of \(p\).

A witness follows by choosing column rank transitions with positive suffix count and solving the corresponding affine intersection, or taking a point outside it in the rank-increasing case. The existing congruence solver then lifts the chosen columns. The number of variables and all count bit lengths are polynomial in the table sizes. With trivial mark this computes the two module factors of (1), since any surjective endomorphism of \(B'\) is an automorphism. With trivial action it computes \(b(\alpha)\) when both complements are abelian.

## 3. Abelian extension coordinates and orbit counts

For a marked abelian extension \(0\to E\to L\xrightarrow{\pi}D\to0\), fix one prime \(q\) and write \(E_q=\bigoplus_j E_j\), where \(E_j\) is cyclic of order \(q^{s_j}\). On the indicated torsion subgroup define
\[
\delta_j:D_q[q^{s_j}]\longrightarrow E_j,\qquad
\delta_j(d)=\operatorname{pr}_j(q^{s_j}\widetilde d).
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(\widetilde d\in L_q\) is any lift of \(d\). Its multiple lies in \(E_q\). Changing the lift adds an element killed after taking this multiple and projecting to \(E_j\), so the formula is well-defined and additive.

These maps classify the extension up to isomorphism fixing both the kernel and the quotient. For an explicit proof, write \(D_q\) in cyclic generators \(d_i\) of orders \(q^{t_i}\), choose lifts \(x_i\), and let \(c_i=q^{t_i}x_i\in E_q\). Together with the additive relations of \(E_q\), the relations \(q^{t_i}x_i=c_i\) present the extension: reduction to a cyclic quotient normal form gives at most \(|D_q||E_q|\) elements, and the specified extension attains this order. Changing \(x_i\) changes \(c_i\) by an element of \(q^{t_i}E_q\), and this is the only ambiguity for isomorphism fixing the two endpoints. If \(t_i\leq s_j\), the value on \(d_i\) in (2) is \(q^{s_j-t_i}\operatorname{pr}_j(c_i)\), which determines the latter modulo \(q^{t_i}E_j\). If \(t_i>s_j\), the value on \(q^{t_i-s_j}d_i\) is \(\operatorname{pr}_j(c_i)\). Thus all extension residues are recovered. The maps (2) commute with pullback along a quotient isomorphism by direct substitution of lifts.

For each \(j\) the number of possible characters is \(|D_q[q^{s_j}]|\), as is seen by decomposing the domain into cyclic groups whose orders divide \(q^{s_j}\). In particular it is at most \(|D_q|\). If the extension is supported on an \(s\)-generated direct summand of \(E\), choose a decomposition consistent with that summand to bound the number of possible pullback classes. There are at most \(s\) nonzero components (2) at each prime since the complementary extension splits. Thus the class orbit after transporting the quotient has size at most \(\prod_q |D_q|^s=|D|^s\). The orbit and its size are independent of the choice of cyclic coordinates. The algorithm can compute (2) in any decomposition and enumerate the actual orbit, without finding a minimal support summand.

Choose \(\gamma_0\in\mathcal I\) and its quotient isomorphism \(\alpha_0:C\to D\). Pull back the target extension to \(C\) along \(\alpha_0\), and enumerate the orbit of this class under the quotient maps induced by the generators of \(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\). The action is precomposition on the maps (2). Breadth-first traversal keeps one automorphism word or permutation for each orbit state, giving one actual \(\gamma\in\mathcal I\) per state. This uses polynomial work per state on the tables of the characters. Two isomorphisms whose states agree induce isomorphic extensions \(L\to C\) when the quotient maps are marked by \(\alpha^{-1}\pi\). Composing with this extension isomorphism is a bijection on the complement epimorphisms over \(\rho\), so \(b(\alpha)\) is constant on each state. Every state has the same number \(|\mathcal I|/o\) of preimages in the isomorphism torsor, where \(o\) denotes the orbit size. This is the orbit-stabilizer theorem applied to the induced action of \(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\). Compute \(|\mathcal I|\) as the order of its permutation automorphism group. The sum in (1) is consequently \((|\mathcal I|/o)\sum_i b(\alpha_i)\), and each term \(b(\alpha_i)\) is the fixed marked count from Section 2.

There is a symmetric option. Put \(F=\ker\rho\). When \(K\) is abelian, view its extension \(0\to F\to K\to C\to0\) over \(D\) by the quotient map \(\alpha\rho\). Its class is the pullback by \(\alpha^{-1}\). Enumerate this orbit using \(\operatorname{Aut}(Y)\) instead. Equal classes give isomorphic marked source groups over \(D\), hence the same count by precomposition on \(\beta\). State sizes are again uniform by orbit-stabilizer. For each \(B'\) the algorithm can expand both searches in alternation and use the first completed orbit. Thus the running time is polynomial in the tables times a polynomial in the smaller actual orbit size for each profile. The bounded-support hypothesis is sufficient on either side, for each nonempty faithful comparison. It is not required that both extensions have small support.


## 4. Central action kernels and nonabelian complements

Suppose \(E\le Z(L)\) and \(D=L/E\) is abelian. Choose a section of \(\pi:L\to D\) carrying \(0\) to \(1\). It determines a normalized central additive \(2\)-cocycle \(z:D\times D\to E\). Two such marked extensions are isomorphic fixing both endpoints exactly when their cocycles differ by the coboundary of a function \(D\to E\) vanishing at \(0\). Indeed changing the section by \(f\) changes the cocycle by \(f(x)+f(y)-f(x+y)\). The converse follows by writing every element uniquely as a kernel element times a section value and defining the isomorphism using \(f\). Test this equivalence by linear congruences in the cyclic coordinates of \(E\). The same orbit search and uniform multiplicities from Section 3 now operate on these central extension classes. Pullback just changes arguments of the cocycle.

The alternating commutator form of the extension is
\[
b_z(x,y)=z(x,y)-z(y,x)=[\widetilde x,\widetilde y]\in E,
\]
using the convention \([g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}\). The form is biadditive because commutators are central, and its radical \(R_0\) is \(\pi(Z(L))\). Its index in \(D\) is \(|L/Z(L)|\). All pulled-back forms have radical index at most the constant \(r_0\) in the hypotheses. There are only polynomially many possible radicals. In fact for each abelian quotient group \(Q\) of order at most \(r_0\), there are at most \(|Q|^{d(D)}\) homomorphisms \(D\to Q\), where \(d(D)\le\log_2|D|\). Every possible radical is the kernel of a map to one of the finitely many such \(Q\). For each radical the form descends to a function on a quotient of size at most \(r_0\), giving at most \(|E|^{r_0^2}\) functions. For each radical the reachable forms come from identifying its quotient with the fixed original quotient equipped with its form. There are at most \(r_0!\) such identifications.

Over a fixed form take one representative cocycle \(z_0\). Any other cocycle \(z\) with that form has \(z-z_0\) symmetric. The latter defines a marked abelian extension of \(D\) by \(E\). Two such differences determine the same marked abelian extension class exactly when the original cocycles differ by a coboundary. Applying (2) to all cyclic kernel factors bounds this fiber by \(|D|^{d(E)}\), where \(d(E)\) is bounded by assumption. Thus a class orbit has polynomial size in \(|D|\). Pairwise equality testing by linear congruences is sufficient for breadth-first enumeration.

To compute \(b(\alpha)\) for a fixed \(\alpha:C\to D\), put \(\phi=\alpha\rho\) and \(F=\ker\rho\). With the chosen section \(s\), any map lifting \(\phi\) has the form \(\beta(k)=f(k)s(\phi(k))\) for one function \(f:K\to E\). The condition for a homomorphism is the affine system
\[
f(kk')=f(k)+f(k')+z\bigl(\phi(k),\phi(k')\bigr)\qquad(k,k'\in K).
\]
The system forces \(f(1)=0\). Because \(\phi\) is onto, \(\beta\) is onto exactly when \(\beta(F)=E\), which means \(f(F)=E\). On \(F\), \(f\) is itself a homomorphism. Its image in the primary factor \(E_q\) generates \(E_q\) precisely when it spans \(E_q/qE_q\), by iterating powers of \(q\). Surjectivity onto every primary factor implies surjectivity onto \(E\): any subgroup of a finite abelian group is the direct sum of its primary parts.

Split the affine equations by the coefficient prime \(q\). The head \(E_q/qE_q\) has dimension at most \(d(E)\). For every subspace \(W\) of that head count the solutions \(f_q\) with \(f_q(k)\bmod qE_q\in W\) for all \(k\in F\). Membership is given by linear equations in field coordinates, and Smith form in cyclic coordinates counts solutions to the resulting congruences. To see the polynomial bound, enumerate subspaces by taking row spans of all square matrices of the head dimension over \(\mathbb F_q\) and deduplicate in reduced row echelon form. There are at most \(q^{d(E)^2}\le |E|^{d(E)^2}\) matrices. In order of increasing dimension, subtract from the count for \(W\) the counts of solutions of exact head image \(U\) over all proper subspaces \(U<W\). This is Möbius inversion on the subspace lattice and yields the exact number of solutions with head image equal to the full head. Multiply the full-head counts across the primes. For a witness when the count is positive, fix one unknown \(f(k)\) at a time to a value with a positive count conditioned on the chosen values. The extra linear equalities leave the same counting algorithm valid, with at most \(|K||E|\) trials. Substitution in (1) completes the second theorem. All bounds are polynomial in the Cayley table sizes at fixed \(d(E)\) and fixed \(|L/Z(L)|\), even for unbounded \(|E|\) and nonabelian \(K\).

The fixed-quotient lift equations are classical. The polynomial reduction here groups the faithful comparison coset by extension-class orbits before counting the lifts. [Matei–Suciu, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0405122)
## Scope and examples

For the first theorem, take \(L=(C_8)^m\times(C_4)^m\) and \(E\) the order-two subgroup of the first cyclic factor. Then \(E\subseteq 2L\), so the extension at \(E\) is nonsplit. The quotient \(D\) has a faithful diagonal action on a finite abelian \(17\)-group \(B\) with arbitrarily many factors of each of the orders \(17\) and \(17^2\). To construct it, choose characters separating the factors of \(D\), using the cyclic group of units of order \(16\) over \(\mathbb F_{17}\) and its lifts of order \(16\) to the units modulo \(17^2\). Let \(L\) act through these characters. The Hall module can have arbitrarily many cyclic summands at both lengths while \(L\) contains arbitrarily many cyclic factors of orders four and eight.

For the second theorem, take \(L=Q_8\times(C_2)^m\), with \(E\) the order-two center of the quaternion factor. Then \(D\) is elementary abelian and \(|L/Z(L)|=4\) for all \(m\). The elementary abelian quotient has a faithful diagonal action by sign changes on a finite elementary abelian \(3\)-group. The resulting target has a nonabelian complement and a nonsplit action-kernel extension.

These statements are scoped polynomial algorithms for the indicated cases of the solvable-group epimorphism question. The second algorithm also allows a nonsolvable source complement if the compatible decomposition is given. The verification here is a written proof, with finite independent checks in the companion program.
