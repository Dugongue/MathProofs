# Brauer obstructions to a rational generic tensor representative

The generic orbit of a tensor action can lack a representative over the field generated by its rational invariants. We compute the obstruction’s index and period for every partition of the modes. This gives an exact test for a rational generic representative and, in a specified range, the exact minimum extension degree that makes one possible.

## Statement

Let \(k\) be a field, let \(n_1,\ldots,n_r\) be positive integers, and let
\[
W=\bigotimes_{i=1}^r k^{n_i},\qquad
H=\left(\prod_{i=1}^r\operatorname{GL}_{n_i}\right)/
\{(\lambda_i I):\ \textstyle\prod_i\lambda_i=1\}.
\]
Take any generically free linear representation of \(H\) and let \(K\) be its rational invariant field over \(k\). The hypothesis is generic freeness for the stabilizer group scheme. In particular the theorem applies to the diagonal action on \(\dim W\) labelled copies of \(W\), because a basis of \(W\) has trivial effective stabilizer. It also applies whenever the effective action on a single tensor is known to be generically free.

Let \(P\) be the generic orbit, an \(H\)-torsor over \(K\), and let \(\alpha_i\in\operatorname{Br}(K)\) be the degree-\(n_i\) central simple algebra classes obtained by pushing out to the projective mode groups. For a subset \(I\) of modes write \(\beta_I=\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_i\) and \(I^c\) for its complement. Then
\[
\begin{split}
\operatorname{ind}(\beta_I)
&=\gcd\left(\prod_{i\in I}n_i,\ \prod_{j\in I^c}n_j\right),\\
\operatorname{per}(\beta_I)
&=\gcd\left(\operatorname{lcm}_{i\in I}n_i,\,
            \operatorname{lcm}_{j\in I^c}n_j\right).
\end{split}
\]
An empty product or least common multiple is one. The generic orbit has a \(K\)-point, equivalently the generic quotient map has a rational section, precisely when the \(n_i\) are pairwise coprime.

For a prime \(p\), write \(e_{i,p}=v_p(n_i)\), \(b_p=\sum_i e_{i,p}-\max_i e_{i,p}\) and \(B=\prod_p p^{b_p}\). There is a finite splitting extension of the generic orbit of degree dividing \(B\). If every prime satisfies \(\max_i e_{i,p}\ge b_p\), every finite splitting extension has degree divisible by \(B\). In that range \(B\) is the exact minimum degree. The result holds in every characteristic.

## The mode torsors and the quotient field

The effective group has the central extension
\[
1\longrightarrow\mathbb G_m\longrightarrow H
\longrightarrow\prod_i\operatorname{PGL}_{n_i}\longrightarrow1 .
\]
It is the pushout of the product of the standard general-linear central extensions along multiplication of the scalar coordinates. The connecting obstruction is consequently the sum of the mode Brauer classes. Hilbert 90 for the central \(\mathbb G_m\) gives injectivity on \(H^1\) into the product of the projective-linear cohomology sets. This identifies \(H\)-torsors over every extension field \(F/k\) with tuples of central simple \(F\)-algebras of degrees \(n_i\) whose Brauer classes sum to zero. The degree and the class determine each member of the tuple. The kernel of the tensor-product representation is exactly the scalar subgroup in the definition of $H$, even on test rings. A tensor product acting as the identity commutes with every endomorphism applied in one slot. The single-slot endomorphism inclusion is faithful, so each mode matrix commutes with all elementary matrices and is scalar. The product of these scalars is one. This cohomology calculation is established in greater generality by Nguyen, and was used for the essential dimension of these tuple functors by Cernele, Reichstein and Nguyen. [Theorem A.1 and §6](https://arxiv.org/html/1406.2953)

A generically free linear action admits a dense invariant open that is a torsor over a geometric quotient, with generic base field $K$. [Versality and generic quotient torsors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.6093) For the framed tensor case one can instead take the homogeneous space \(\operatorname{GL}(W)/H\) of the open of ordered bases. The torsor construction gives a family of Azumaya algebras \(\mathcal A_i\) of degrees \(n_i\) on the quotient by the action of each projective mode group on its matrix algebra. Their generic Brauer classes are \(\alpha_i\).

We use two properties of the generic torsor.

- **Versal specialization.** Given an \(H\)-torsor \(Q\) over an infinite extension field \(F/k\), twist the representation by \(Q\). It becomes an \(F\)-vector space, and the twist of any chosen invariant dense open used for the quotient is a nonempty open of that vector space. An \(F\)-rational point in it gives an equivariant map from \(Q\) to the original open over \(F\). It identifies \(Q\) with the quotient fiber over an \(F\)-point because an equivariant map between torsors is an isomorphism. The same argument works after restricting to any nonempty open in the quotient, by replacing the open in the representation with its preimage.
- **Specialization divisibility.** For an Azumaya algebra \(\mathcal B\), if its generic index is \(q\), choose a right ideal of reduced dimension \(q\) over the generic field. It gives a rational section of the corresponding relative generalized Severi–Brauer variety, which extends to a section on a nonempty open. On that open every specialized index divides \(q\). Likewise if the generic period is \(a\), a splitting of the \(a\)-fold tensor power spreads to an open by spreading its matrix-algebra isomorphism. The period of every fiber there divides \(a\).

It follows that any index or period achieved by an \(H\)-torsor over an infinite extension field divides the corresponding generic index or period. The use of general versal torsors to maximize indices appears already in the indicated essential-dimension work. [§2 and Proposition 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1406.2953)

## Independent cyclic division algebras in any characteristic

We need a family of cyclic algebras whose tensor product has the product of their indices, also when the cyclic degrees are divisible by the characteristic. Given positive integers \(m_1,\ldots,m_q\), let \(L\) be a rational function field over \(k\) with a separate group of \(m_j\) variables for each \(j\). Let \(\Gamma=\prod_j C_{m_j}\) act by cyclic permutation of the variables in the corresponding group. Put \(F_0=L^\Gamma\). The extension \(L/F_0\) is Galois with group \(\Gamma\) by the fixed-field theorem for finite groups of field automorphisms. Let \(K_j\) be the intermediate cyclic extension of degree \(m_j\) fixed by the product of the other factors. These extensions are linearly disjoint and have compositum \(L\).

Over \(F=F_0((t_1))\cdots((t_q))\) form the cyclic crossed product \(D_j=(K_jF/F,\sigma_j,t_j)\). The compositum of the coefficient fields is \(L((t_1))\cdots((t_q))\), as can be seen by expanding each Laurent coefficient in a finite \(F_0\)-basis of \(L\). Use the iterated Laurent valuation with group \(\mathbb Z^q\), where \(t_j\) has the \(j\)-th coordinate valuation. Each \(\sigma_j\) preserves that valuation. In the tensor product of the crossed products, write elements uniquely as sums of \(c_{\boldsymbol i}u_1^{i_1}\cdots u_q^{i_q}\) with \(c_{\boldsymbol i}\) in the compositum and \(0\le i_j<m_j\). The \(u_j\) are their cyclic generators with \(u_j^{m_j}=t_j\) and commute for distinct \(j\). Give that term the valuation
\[
v(c_{\boldsymbol i})+(i_1/m_1,\ldots,i_q/m_q).
\]
Different terms have different values modulo \(\mathbb Z^q\), hence a nonzero element has a unique least term in the order of the Laurent valuation. Multiplication of two least terms produces the unique least term of the product and has nonzero coefficient, using the Galois action on the coefficients. Thus the tensor product has no zero divisors. It is finite-dimensional, hence division of degree \(\prod_jm_j\). The same argument with one factor proves that each is division of degree \(m_j\). Its class has period \(m_j\) by the cyclic norm criterion: a norm from \(K_jF\) has \(j\)-th coordinate valuation divisible by \(m_j\), whereas \(t_j^a\) has that valuation \(a\). This construction uses separable cyclic extensions, including when \(m_j\) is a power of the characteristic, with no root-of-unity assumption.

## Computing the period and index

For the upper bounds, each \(\alpha_i\) has index dividing \(n_i\) and period dividing \(n_i\). The index of a sum divides the product of the indices, by tensoring division representatives. The period of a sum divides the least common multiple. Since \(\beta_I=-\beta_{I^c}\), combining both sides gives the stated upper bounds.

Fix a prime \(p\). For the period lower bound, take \(e\) to be the smaller of the largest \(p\)-valuations on the two sides of the partition. If \(e>0\), choose one mode on each side having valuation at least \(e\). Put a cyclic division class of period \(p^e\) in the first mode and its negative in the second. Put split algebras in the other modes. Realize the two classes in the indicated degrees by passing to matrix algebras over their division representatives. The classes sum to zero, so an \(H\)-torsor realizes this tuple. The partition class has period \(p^e\). Versal specialization therefore forces the full \(p^e\) factor in the generic period.

For the index lower bound, give a mode \(i\) capacity \(e_{i,p}\). Let \(q_p\) be the smaller of the total capacities on the two sides. Route \(q_p\) units from the modes in \(I\) to the modes in \(I^c\), staying within each mode’s capacity. One construction greedily matches a remaining unit on one side with a remaining unit on the other until the smaller supply is exhausted. Let \(r_{ij}\) count the units assigned to \((i,j)\). For each positive \(r_{ij}\) take one of the independent cyclic division algebras above of degree \(p^{r_{ij}}\). In mode \(i\in I\) put the sum of its outgoing classes. In mode \(j\in I^c\) put the negatives of its incoming classes. Each resulting index divides the product of its incident degrees and thus divides \(n_i\) or \(n_j\) as appropriate. Again it has a representative of the prescribed degree and the tuple is an \(H\)-torsor. Its class \(\beta_I\) is the full tensor product of the independent division factors and has index \(p^{q_p}\). Specialization forces this divisibility in the generic index. Applying the argument at each prime separately gives the claimed formula.

For instance a framed format \((4,2,2)\) has a generic first-mode class of period two and index four. Thus period two does not guarantee a quadratic descent field.

## Canonical representatives and splitting degrees

Every splitting field for \(P\) splits all its mode classes by the classification. Conversely a field splitting all the mode classes splits \(P\). A rational section exists precisely when the generic torsor has a \(K\)-point. If some prime divides two mode dimensions, the period formula applied to a single one of these modes gives a nonzero class. If the dimensions are pairwise coprime, each single-mode class is zero by the same formula. This proves the section criterion. In the coprime case the invariant field is stably rational over \(k\). The generic fiber is then the split rational group \(H_K\), which is rational by the product of the general-linear big cells and the split diagonal torus quotient. The function field of the representation is therefore purely transcendental over its invariant field.

To split any tuple, work one prime at a time. For \(p\), choose one mode of largest \(p\)-valuation and split the \(p\)-primary components in every other mode via a tower of maximal-subfield extensions of their division representatives over the successive fields. The extension degree of each step is a power of \(p\) dividing the corresponding \(p^{e_{i,p}}\). The class of the omitted mode then vanishes in the \(p\)-primary part because the total sum is zero. Splitting all the primes in this way gives a finite extension of degree dividing \(B\).

If the largest \(p\)-valuation is at least the sum of the remaining ones, the index formula for that largest mode has \(p\)-valuation exactly \(b_p\). The index of a Brauer class divides the degree of every finite field extension splitting it. Thus every splitting degree for the generic torsor is divisible by \(p^{b_p}\) whenever that prime satisfies the stated hypothesis. This gives the exact bound \(B\) when all primes do. It applies, in particular, when every prime appears in at most two mode dimensions. For the framed format \((4,2,2)\), the exact joint splitting degree is four. Outside the stated range the subset formula still gives a lower bound for all splitting degrees, and the tower still gives the upper bound.

## Single-tensor examples and scope

Over \(\mathbb C\), Sauerwein, Wallach, Gour and Kraus prove generic triviality of the effective local invertible stabilizer for three equal modes of dimension \(4,5,6\), and for at least four equal modes of dimension greater than two. The algebraic free locus follows from their result because the locus of nontrivial stabilizers is constructible and has no dense open subset, and characteristic zero stabilizers are smooth. Thus a generic single \(4\times4\times4\) tensor has a first-mode obstruction of period and index four, and no rational generic representative. For a single \(n\times n\times n\times n\) tensor with \(n\ge3\), the two-mode versus two-mode class has index \(n^2\), so every field supplying a representative over the generic invariant field has degree divisible by \(n^2\). [Generic trivial stabilizer, Theorem 1 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.11056)

The result concerns a representative from rational invariants on a generically free geometric quotient. It does not obstruct a canonical representative using nonrational operations, and it does not compare the closures of two affine ordinary tensor orbits. The cohomological description and the underlying versality mechanism are prior work. The computations here concern partition-index and period formulas and their application to a generic tensor quotient. This is an informal proof.
