# Parallel normal forms for principal congruence groups

## GP-0421

Fix a prime $p$. Given structure constants for a finite-dimensional associative algebra over $\mathbb F_p$ and an adapted basis for the supplied powers of its nilpotent ideal $J$, the group $1+J$ admits constructive normal forms and a short power-conjugation presentation in deterministic uniform NC. The nilpotence length may grow with the input. This covers a supplied Jacobson radical filtration over a fixed prime field.

Let the basis elements $b_i$ be ordered by increasing filtration degree, with a prescribed order within a layer, and put $g_i=1+b_i$. Refine the descending series of powers $1+J^k$ by the coordinate flags of $J^k/J^{k+1}$. Each step is a central factor of order $p$. Indeed multiplication in one layer reduces to addition and $[1+J,1+J^k]\subseteq1+J^{k+1}$, as follows by calculating modulo $J^{k+1}$. Each $g_i$ maps to the generator of its corresponding factor. Thus the ordered products $\prod_i g_i^{c_i}$ with $0\le c_i<p$ are unique normal forms. Ordinary one-layer collection would take as many sequential stages as the filtration length.

For a parallel extraction, suppose the current residual belongs to $1+J^k$. In the quotient modulo $J^{2k}$ the product rule is addition on the whole subspace $J^k/J^{2k}$, because the product of two elements of $J^k$ lies in $J^{2k}$. Read, simultaneously, the residual's coefficients in every layer of degree $k$ through $2k-1$. These are exactly the next block of normal form exponents. Construct the corresponding ordered prefix using a balanced product tree and left-divide it from the residual. The new residual is in $1+J^{2k}$. Iterate $k=1,2,4,\ldots$ until the ideal is zero. The doubling blocks are contiguous in the specified order, so the result is the fixed normal form.

All ring products have uniform logarithmic-depth circuits over the fixed field by evaluating their structure constants and summing in a tree. Products of polynomially many factors, and inverses of $1+x$ for $x\in J$, have uniform polylogarithmic depth. For inverses use the finite geometric series, with all powers computed using binary powering and a final parallel sum. For a $d$-dimensional nilpotent ideal, $J^{d+1}=0$ since its nonzero powers decrease strictly in dimension. Exponents $c_i$ lie in a fixed alphabet. There are $O(\log(d+1))$ extraction stages, so composition still has polylogarithmic depth and polynomial size. The gate layouts use only fixed-field arithmetic, balanced trees and the supplied basis index ordering, and are logspace-uniform.

In parallel compute the normal forms of all $g_i^p$ and $g_i g_jg_i^{-1}$ for $i<j$. The former use only later generators, and the latter lie in the subgroup generated by $g_j$ and later generators. Use these words as relations. They present $1+J$: by descending induction the subgroup of later generators in the abstract presentation has at most the expected order and is finite. The next generator conjugates it into itself by the displayed relations and hence onto itself, since conjugation is injective on a finite subgroup. Its $p$th power lies in that subgroup, so the index grows by at most $p$. The natural map onto $1+J$ has target order $p^{\dim J}$, forcing equality. This yields both the claimed short presentation and parallel exact word coordinates for every input unit of the form $1+x$. [Finite-ring computational context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15497)
