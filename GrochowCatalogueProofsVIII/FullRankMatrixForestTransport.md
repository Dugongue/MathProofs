# Exact classical tensor transport on invertible matrix forests

## GP-0286

Consider rational real three-tensors with the positive-definite product orthogonal action, or Gaussian-rational complex three-tensors with the product unitary action. Assume all three Gram spectra split over the rationals. Match the two sets of eigenvalues mode by mode and use the induced matching of the eigenspace blocks. The following support class has a deterministic polynomial-bit-time all-target equivalence test.

- Each nonzero block tensor involves exactly two eigenspaces of dimension at least two and a one-dimensional eigenspace.
- After choosing a generator of the one-dimensional space, the occupied block tensor is an invertible square matrix on the two larger spaces.
- The multigraph on the larger eigenspaces with one edge per occupied block tensor is a forest.

There is no bound on the number of edges in a component or on the eigenspace dimensions. A one-dimensional eigenspace may occur on multiple edges. Eigenspaces, invertibility, and the forest condition can be verified in polynomial time given the rational spectra. The theorem concerns this matrix-block subclass of the bounded-treewidth question. [Spectral-block context](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27128v1)

### Propagating the edge equations

Reject a target with different eigenvalue multiplicities, different occupied blocks, or a rank-deficient matrix at an occupied block, since all these data are invariant under product isometries. Compute rational or Gaussian-rational bases for all the eigenspaces and their positive-definite Gram matrices. Choose a unit vector on each singleton, using its chosen basis vector divided by the square root of its squared length. Contracting the corresponding tensor block with this unit vector multiplies its coefficient matrix in the other two modes by that positive square root.

Bipartition each forest component. In the unitary case replace the spaces on one side by their conjugate spaces, and replace their unknown unitary maps by the conjugates. In the orthogonal case no conjugation is needed. A two-mode tensor then specifies a map from one side to the other using the metric to identify the conjugate space with the dual, since the two actions in matrix notation are $g_u M g_v^{\mathsf T}=g_u M\bar g_v^{-1}$ in orthonormal coordinates. In the displayed nonorthonormal frames this identification just inserts the known Gram matrix of the input space. Orient or reverse any edge as needed, replacing its invertible map by its inverse when reversed.

The unknown singleton actions contribute unit scalars, or signs over the reals. They can be gauged away. In each tree choose one larger-space vertex as root, multiply its transporter by one, and recursively multiply the other transporters by scalars canceling the edge phase. Every new vertex has exactly one parent, so these adjustments are consistent and preserve isometry. They make every edge equation hold with its singleton action equal to the identity. Thus repetition of singleton labels across edges introduces no additional condition.

For a vertex $v$, let $P_v$ be the product of source edge maps along the path from the root, and let $P_v'$ be its target counterpart. For the identity singleton actions the entire edge system requires
$$
g_v=P'_v g_r P_v^{-1}.
$$
Write $H_v,H'_v$ for the source and target metric matrices. Then $g_v$ is an isometry precisely when
$$
g_r^*(P'_v)^*H'_vP'_v g_r=P_v^*H_vP_v .
$$
The equation at the root supplies the root metric isometry as well. Each edge map is a matrix over the original exact field multiplied by a positive square root, or its inverse when traversed in reverse. In each path metric those factors are squared, so all displayed metric matrices lie in the original exact field. Their bit lengths grow polynomially by rational matrix product and inversion bounds.

### Deciding the root metric equations

Let $G_v=P_v^*H_vP_v$, $G'_v=(P'_v)^*H'_vP'_v$, and put $A_v=H_r^{-1}G_v$, $A'_v=(H'_r)^{-1}G'_v$. The root equations are equivalent to simultaneous conjugacy of the tuples $(A_v)$, $(A'_v)$ by a root isometry. These operators are self-adjoint for their respective root metrics. Complexify if necessary and use the positive-definite metrics to identify them abstractly with Hermitian matrices. These abstract orthonormalizations are used only in the proof.

Form the common word algebra generated by the pairs $(A_v,A'_v)$ in the direct sum of two matrix algebras. A basis consisting of words is found by beginning with the identity pair and repeatedly multiplying retained words on the right by each generator, adding independent words to the basis. The dimension is at most twice the square of the root dimension, which also bounds the lengths needed. Compute the difference of the two traces on every basis word. The algebra over the complexes is closed under the adjoint defined by the root metrics, since each generator is self-adjoint and the adjoint reverses words. It is therefore semisimple. The two coordinate actions are unitary representations of this finite-dimensional adjoint-closed algebra. Equality of traces on the algebra gives equal multiplicities of every simple representation, by applying the traces to its minimal central idempotents. Hence they admit a unitary intertwiner. Conversely such an intertwiner forces every tested trace difference to vanish.

For real data a complex invertible intertwiner gives a real invertible one. The determinant of the real part plus $t$ times the imaginary part is a polynomial that is nonzero at $t=i$, so it is nonzero at some real $t$. The polar factor of this real intertwiner, relative to the two real root metrics, still intertwines the tuples, because both tuples are self-adjoint in those metrics. This supplies a real orthogonal root isometry. Thus the trace test works in both cases. All traces and basis-independence tests use exact matrices over the base field and polynomial-length words, hence polynomial bit time. Independent components are treated separately and the recovered root conditions imply every original block equation.
