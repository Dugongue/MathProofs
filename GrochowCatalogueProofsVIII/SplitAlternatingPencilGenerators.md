# Pseudo-isometry generators of split regular alternating pencils

## GP-0243

Let an alternating pencil over an explicitly represented finite field $k=\mathbb F_q$ be given by an ordered basis $B_0,B_1$, with $B_0$ nonsingular. Suppose $L=B_0^{-1}B_1$ has split characteristic polynomial and its distinct roots are supplied. Given also a generator of $k^\times$, the entire pseudo-isometry group of the pencil has a deterministically computable polynomial-size generating set. The running time is polynomial in the matrix dimension and $\log q$. Repeated primary factors and arbitrary Jordan lengths are permitted, as is characteristic two. The input restriction includes the given split roots and the multiplicative generator. [Genus-two context](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.03033)

### Canonical local symplectic pairs

Write $B=B_0$, viewed as a bilinear form. The operator $L$ is self-adjoint for $B$. Its different primary spaces are orthogonal, by applying relatively prime annihilating polynomials on opposite sides of the pairing. Fix a primary space for $\lambda$, let $N=L-\lambda I$ on that space, and write $R=k[t]$ acting by $t=N$. Every cyclic $R$-submodule is isotropic. Indeed $B(x,N^{2i}x)=B(N^i x,N^i x)=0$. For an odd power use that $B(-,N-)=B_1-\lambda B_0$ is alternating, so $B(x,N^{2i+1}x)=0$ as well. These identities require no division by two.

Choose $x$ with maximal Jordan length $j$. Nondegeneracy gives a vector $y$ with $B(N^{j-1}x,y)\ne0$, so $y$ also has length $j$. The matrix of cross-pairings of the two cyclic chains has nonzero anti-diagonal and zeros above total exponent $j-1$, so it is nonsingular. In particular the two chains span a nondegenerate direct sum. Replace $y$ by a unit polynomial in $N$ applied to it to make $B(x,N^i y)$ equal to one for $i=j-1$ and zero for $i<j-1$. The triangular system has the anti-diagonal entry $B(x,N^{j-1}y)\ne0$. Split off this pair and repeat on its orthogonal complement, which is $N$-stable. This constructs a sum of paired $k[t]/t^j$ modules with the standard alternating form read at the top coefficient of $t^{j-1}$. Consequently the root labels and multiplicities $2m_j$ of Jordan lengths determine simultaneous ordered-pair isometry, and matching these computed bases gives a witness.

### Which basis changes lift

An invertible change of basis of the pencil replaces $B,L$ by
$$
B'=B(aI+bL),\qquad L'=(aI+bL)^{-1}(cI+dL).
$$
If it lifts to an isometry of the original represented pencil, $a+b\lambda\ne0$ at every root. The new root is $(c+d\lambda)/(a+b\lambda)$ and the Jordan lengths are unchanged. Indeed subtracting that scalar from $L'$ factors the result as a unit polynomial in $L$ times $L-\lambda I$, since $ad-bc\ne0$. Conversely a projective transformation preserving the multiset of roots labeled by their Jordan multiplicities preserves the ordered-pair normal form, so every representative in $\mathrm{GL}_2(k)$ lifts by the preceding construction. This includes scalar changes.

With at least three roots, fix three distinct points and enumerate their images among the roots. A projective linear transformation of the line is fixed by a triple, so these polynomially many trials give the entire projective image. With one root, conjugate it to infinity and use the affine group there, generated by translations for a prime-field basis of $k$ and multiplication by the supplied field generator. With two roots, conjugate them to zero and infinity. Use the scaling subgroup and also inversion exactly when the two Jordan labels coincide. Add one scalar matrix corresponding to the field generator. The resulting pencil basis changes generate the full image in $\mathrm{GL}_2(k)$, and each can be lifted constructively.

### Generators in the ordered-pair kernel

The ordered-pair isometries on a primary space are the units $g$ in $\mathcal E=\operatorname{End}_R(M)$ satisfying $g^*g=1$, where $*$ is adjoint for $B$. In the paired coordinates above, reduce the diagonal blocks of each chain length modulo $t$. This gives an algebra quotient
$$
\mathcal E/J \simeq \prod_j\operatorname{Mat}_{2m_j}(k)
$$
with the usual symplectic adjoints. To see that reduction is multiplicative, a composite through a different length contributes a factor of $t$ in a diagonal block. Indeed a map from length $i$ to length $j>i$ has all coefficients divisible by $t^{j-i}$. The kernel $J$ is nilpotent. In a long product of its block entries, an increase in length or a step of equal length supplies a factor of $t$. A descending run has length bounded by the maximum chain length. Thus any sufficiently long path through lengths supplies at least that many factors of $t$ and gives zero. This also gives a polynomial bound on the powers needed. The adjoint preserves $J$, as follows by the paired form and its coefficient pairing on each length. Each element of the symplectic quotient lifts by taking constant coefficient matrices on the module of each length. Thus generators of the quotient lift directly, using the elementary symplectic root matrices with parameters from a prime-field basis of $k$. Root matrices at all parameters follow from additivity and generate by symplectic elimination.

It remains to generate $G=\{g\in1+J:g^*g=1\}$. The group $1+J$ has explicitly computable central factors obtained by taking the powers $J^i$ and then a prime-field coordinate flag in each additive quotient $J^i/J^{i+1}$. The commutator relation $[1+J,1+J^i]\subseteq1+J^{i+1}$ follows by multiplying modulo $J^{i+1}$. It allows collection and membership by sifting on the first nonzero coordinate of an element. Here are the needed compact operations, including when $p=\operatorname{char} k$ is large.

For a proposed generating list keep at most one pivot at each coordinate. Sift by a power of the current pivot to cancel the leading entry, or insert the element at an empty pivot. Close the pivot list under sifted $p$-th powers and conjugations of later pivots by earlier ones. At most the central series length many new pivots can be inserted. With those tests complete, descending induction shows that products of pivots in order with exponents in $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$ form the generated subgroup. At each induction step the later subgroup is normalized by the earlier pivot and contains its $p$-th power. Normalization follows because conjugation by one pivot preserves the later subgroup by the tests, and inclusion is equality for a finite subgroup. This proves both the representation and the membership algorithm. Exponentiation by a field integer uses repeated squaring.

Similarly one can generate the kernel of a character from a subgroup to $\mathbb F_p$ without listing $p$ cosets. Choose a generator $x$ of character one by rescaling a power. Cancel the character of each original generator using a power of $x$, adjoin $x^p$, and close the resulting subgroup under conjugation by $x$ using sifting. Stop when conjugating every current generator stays inside. Every addition strictly increases the number of pivots, since each pivot contributes a factor $p$ to subgroup order. The resulting subgroup is normalized by $x$ and by the adjusted generators, and the quotient is cyclic of order $p$. It is the required kernel. Iterate on the coordinate characters to handle a vector-valued homomorphism.

Start from $1+J$, generated by lifts of additive bases in the displayed layers. For $i\ge1$, on the subgroup with $g^*g-1\in J^i$, the function
$$
g\longmapsto g^*g-1 \pmod {J^{i+1}}
$$
is an additive homomorphism to $J^i/J^{i+1}$. This follows by expanding $(gh)^*gh$ and noting that $h\in1+J$ conjugates the error trivially modulo $J^{i+1}$, even without assuming $h$ is already isometric. Compute its kernel with the compact procedure above and advance to the next power. When the power vanishes this gives $G$ exactly. Every step uses explicit matrices and polynomial-size lists. Finally concatenate the primary-space kernel generators and the lifts of the pencil image generators. This generates all pseudo-isometries, with polynomial bit complexity throughout.
