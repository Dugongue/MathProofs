# Uniform separating invariants for binary weights of fixed torus rank

## GP-0753

For a diagonal complex torus action on $\mathbb C^n$ given by binary integer weights $a_1,\ldots,a_n\in\mathbb Z^r$, one can explicitly construct a single separating family of invariant monomials of size $n^{O(r)}$. Each uses at most $2r+1$ coordinates and has polynomial-bit exponents. At fixed $r$, the family and arithmetic circuits evaluating it are computable in deterministic polynomial time. Separation is simultaneous for all pairs of orbit closures, including points with zero coordinates. This gives succinct separating invariants for the fixed-rank torus case of the reductive-action question. [Algorithms for torus actions](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/LIPIcs-CCC-2020-12.pdf)

Write $W$ for the matrix of weights. An invariant monomial has exponent vector in $\ker_{\mathbb Z}W\cap\mathbb N^n$. For a point $x$, let $S$ be its support, and let $T\subseteq S$ consist of indices which occur positively in some nonnegative integer relation supported in $S$. Any invariant monomial using an index outside $T$ vanishes at $x$. An index $i\in S$ belongs to $T$ exactly when a nonnegative relation of support at most $r+1$ supported in $S$ has positive coefficient at $i$. For the nontrivial implication, express $-a_i$ in the cone of the other weights from a given relation. Conic Carathéodory reduces the expression to at most $r$ linearly independent weights. The resulting coefficients are rational by the integer matrix equations, and clearing denominators yields the relation. Thus one can enumerate such monomials for all $i$ and all subsets of at most $r$ other weights, solving a small rational system for each subset. Include them all in the family. The union of the supports of those with nonzero values recovers $T$.

Recall that a primitive integral circuit of $W$ is a nonzero kernel vector with minimal support and relatively prime coordinates. Each has support at most $r+1$. The integral kernel on any set of columns is generated by the primitive circuits on those columns. For a proof, retain independent rows and, at a prime $\ell$, choose a nonsingular maximal column minor whose $\ell$-adic determinant valuation is minimum. Every nonbasis column is a linear combination of the basis columns with coefficients in $\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}$, by Cramer's rule and the minimum choice. The associated kernel vectors with nonbasis coordinate one generate the localized kernel. Each is a localized unit times its primitive circuit, since clearing denominators need not introduce a factor $\ell$. Thus circuits generate after localization at every prime. The quotient of the integral kernel by the circuit span is finitely generated, and vanishes since every such localization vanishes. The argument also covers a zero-weight column, whose individual unit vector is a circuit. [Circuit generation of saturated lattices](https://eisenbud.github.io/papers/pdfs/1996-002.pdf)

Enumerate all primitive signed circuits $z$ and add the following monomial tests. If $z\ge0$, include $x^z$. Otherwise let $N_z=\{i:z_i<0\}$. For every set $J$ of at most $r$ independent weights, test whether $-\sum_{i\in N_z}a_i$ has a nonnegative rational expansion using $J$. Include the empty set for the zero vector. If it does, clear denominators in the relation formed by the unit contributions on $N_z$ and the expansion on $J$. Multiply the resulting nonnegative integral relation by a large enough integer that its exponent vector $u$ satisfies $u+z\ge0$. Add both invariant monomials $x^u,x^{u+z}$. Their supports lie in $\operatorname{supp}(z)\cup J$, which has size at most $2r+1$.

Suppose all tests agree on $x,y$. The first tests show that their sets $T$ coincide. Sum one nonnegative witness relation per index of $T$ to get a relation positive on every index of $T$. Hence the cone on those columns is their full linear span, since $-a_i$ lies in the cone for every $i\in T$. For each signed circuit supported on $T$ the needed nonnegative expansion can therefore use a subset $J\subseteq T$ of at most $r$ independent columns. For that test $x^u$ and $y^u$ are nonzero. Dividing the equal monomial values shows that the Laurent character $x^z$ agrees with $y^z$ on these nonzero coordinates. Primitive circuits generate the integral kernel on $T$, so all Laurent invariant characters there agree. All other nonconstant invariant monomials vanish at both points. Every polynomial invariant is a linear combination of invariant monomials, so standard torus orbit-closure separation gives the claim.

Enumeration uses at most $n^{2r+O(1)}$ candidate sets, and solving the small linear systems and normalizing exponents have polynomial bit cost. Repeated squaring evaluates every chosen monomial by an arithmetic circuit of size polynomial in the input bit size. The result concerns arithmetic circuits for the invariants, even when their ordinary polynomial degrees are large.
