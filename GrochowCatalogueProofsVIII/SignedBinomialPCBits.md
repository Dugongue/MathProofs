# Polynomial-bit bounded-degree PC for signed binomial systems

## GP-0719

Over $\mathbb Q$, suppose every axiom is a monomial or a binomial $m-\sigma m'$, where $\sigma\in\{1,-1\}$, and nonzero rational scaling of an entire axiom is allowed. Let $d$ be a degree bound and put $N=\binom{n+d}{d}$ for $n$ variables. Every polynomial with a degree-$d$ Polynomial Calculus derivation has a degree-$d$ derivation whose length is polynomial in $N,n$ and the input size and whose coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in the input bit lengths and $N$. Such a derivation can be found within the same polynomial bit bound, or the absence of a degree-$d$ derivation can be decided. This covers every fixed Boolean parity constraint language in its signed-variable encoding, including inconsistent instances, without increasing the degree. It gives a bit-complexity theorem for that class of fixed languages. [Bounded-degree PC ideal membership](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28243v1)

### Subspaces with signed relations

Make one vertex for each monomial of degree at most $d$. An edge from $m$ to $m'$ of sign $\sigma$ represents the relation $m-\sigma m'$, and a zero mark on $m$ represents the monomial $m$. In each connected component choose one root and paths to it. Summing signed path relations derives $m-\sigma_m m_0$ for every vertex $m$, where the sign $\sigma_m$ is the product along its path. Each step uses only coefficients $1,-1$ and yields a signed binomial or zero. If a component has a zero mark, the paths derive every monomial of that component. The same happens if a cycle has negative sign: two inconsistent paths yield $2m_0$, and a multiplication by the scalar $1/2$ yields $m_0$. We call such a component zero. In every remaining component the signs are consistent, and its spanned subspace is precisely the kernel of the linear functional mapping $m$ to $\sigma_m$.

The intersection of this relation span with the linear span of any subset $U$ of vertices has the same type. In a zero component take the individual retained monomials. In a nonzero component with a retained vertex $u_0$, use $m-\sigma_m\sigma_{u_0}u_0$ for every other retained vertex. There is no generator from an empty component. The componentwise functional description proves that this gives the entire intersection. The required relations have signed path derivations using the original graph, even when some path vertices are not in $U$.

### Saturating the PC multiplication rule

Start with the normalized input axioms of degree at most $d$ and their signed graph. Given its current span $S$, take $U$ to be the monomials of degree at most $d-1$. Produce the stated signed generators of $S\cap\operatorname{span}(U)$, multiply each by each variable, and add the resulting monomials or binomials to the graph. Every new axiom line is a valid PC inference of degree at most $d$. If a pass does not enlarge $S$, this operation has reached the least subspace that contains the eligible axioms and is closed under all degree-respecting PC multiplication steps. Indeed a polynomial of degree $d$ cannot be multiplied by a variable within a degree-$d$ derivation, and the lower-degree polynomials are exactly the intersection used in the pass. Linear combinations are already allowed by taking spans. It follows by induction on derivations that the final span is exactly the set of degree-$d$ consequences.

There can be at most $N$ strict passes. The span dimension is read from the signed graph, and its generators and their paths can be computed in polynomial time. Only add a new edge or mark if its inclusion enlarges the span, recording the earlier path steps and the multiplication. This records a polynomial-size derivation of a basis. Every recorded relation can be written with coefficients $1,-1,2$, with the scalar $1/2$ used for inconsistent cycles. Asserted rational scalings of input axioms require only the input bit size. Finally express the target in this basis by its known componentwise coefficients. In a nonzero component the functional being zero gives the needed coefficient on the root, and in a zero component each monomial is already available. The last combination can be built by successive sums, whose numerator and denominator bit lengths are bounded by a polynomial in the number and bit lengths of the input target coefficients. This gives the claimed bit bound for all lines and rules.

For signed Boolean parity constraints the domain equations are $z_i^2-1=0$, and an arity-$k$ parity constraint has equation $\prod_{i\in I}z_i-\sigma=0$. They have exactly the required form. The affine change $z_i=2x_i-1$ transfers the conclusion to their corresponding expanded equations in Boolean coordinates without changing the degree. A multiplication by $z_i$ is then a multiplication by $x_i$ followed by a linear combination with the previous line. Expansion of degree-$d$ polynomials under that affine change has at most $N$ monomials and polynomial bit growth.
