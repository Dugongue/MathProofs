# Exact partial-deck thresholds for cluster graphs

## GP-0878

A cluster graph is a disjoint union of complete graphs. There is a deterministic polynomial-time formula for its adversary reconstruction number, with competing reconstructions allowed to be arbitrary simple graphs. There is also a closed formula for its ally reconstruction number. In particular, at every even order $n\ge4$ the largest ally number within the class is $n/2+2$.

Let $a_s$ be the number of cliques of order $s$ in the graph $G$, and assume $n\ge3$. Write $e_s$ for the corresponding unit vector, put $e_0=0$, and let $d_s=e_s-e_{s-1}$. The card of type $s$ has count vector $a-d_s$ and appears $sa_s$ times. For each $s$ with $a_s>0$, form each alternative cluster graph candidate by adding a singleton or growing one component of this card. Thus choose $t\ne s$ with $t=1$ or with an available $(t-1)$-component, and put $b=a-d_s+d_t$. Define $M$ as the maximum of $\min(sa_s,tb_t)$ over these choices, or zero if the choice list is empty. Put $\eta=3$ if $a_1\ge2$ and $a_2\ge1$, and $\eta=0$ otherwise. Then the adversary reconstruction number is
$$
1+\max(2,M,\eta).
$$

To prove the formula, two distinct cluster graphs that share a card obey $b-a=d_t-d_s$. The $d_s$ are linearly independent, so for distinct graphs the ordered pair of deletion types is unique. All their common card occurrences are therefore of exactly that type, with multiplicity the smaller of the two type multiplicities, as used in $M$. The polynomial number of candidates includes all possibilities.

A non-cluster graph contains an induced three-vertex path. Any deletion yielding a cluster card must delete a vertex of this path. Thus it has at most three such deletions. If there are three, all induced three-vertex paths must use that same triple. Every outside vertex then has either all or none of the path's vertices as neighbors, because disagreement along either path edge forms another induced three-vertex path. It cannot be adjacent to all of them, because the two endpoints are nonadjacent. The other vertices form a cluster graph, so the competitor is $P_3\sqcup C$ for a cluster graph $C$. Its three good cards consist of two copies of $K_2\sqcup C$ and one of $2K_1\sqcup C$. For $G$ to share both types, their difference $d_2-d_1$ forces the deletion types $1$ and $2$, respectively. Their three occurrences are all shared exactly when $G=C\sqcup K_2\sqcup K_1$ with at least two singletons in $G$. This is equivalent to the condition in $\eta$ for some competitor. At least two cards can always be shared with a distinct graph, by toggling the edge between two deleted vertices. Taking one plus the largest possible overlap proves the formula.

For the ally number, if there are at least two component sizes, choose three cards containing two different types. A cluster competitor cannot have both types. A non-cluster competitor would need all three of its good cards. If there are three component sizes, choose one of each, which makes that impossible. If the two sizes are other than $1,2$, their types cannot be the pair in the three-card non-cluster case. If the two sizes are $1,2$, choose two deletions from cliques of size two and one from a singleton, reversing the competitor's multiplicity pattern. Thus the ally number is three whenever component sizes differ.

When all components have the same size, the card type is unique, so the ally and adversary numbers coincide. For $b\ge2$ copies of $K_s$ with $s\ge2$, the maximum overlap is $s+1$: after one deletion, growing a surviving $K_s$ gives a competitor with $s+1$ good deletions, while adding a singleton contributes at most two and every non-cluster competitor at most three. The answer is $s+2$. The edgeless graph has maximum overlap two, since any two deletions yielding edgeless cards force all edges to lie on that pair. By complementation the complete graph has the same value. These formulas give the even-order extremum by taking two equal cliques. The ally numbers here agree with known disconnected-graph results; the adversary formula computes exact multiset overlaps for arbitrary mixtures of clique sizes.
