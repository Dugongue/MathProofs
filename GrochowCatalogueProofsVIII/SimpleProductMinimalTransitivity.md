# Minimal transitivity in Cartesian products of simple actions

## GP-0505

For a Cartesian product of transitive actions of nonabelian finite simple groups, there is an exact criterion for minimal transitivity. It gives a constructive fixed-parameter algorithm when the factor actions are supplied, with parameter the largest point stabilizer order of a factor. The same criterion and algorithm allow repeated isomorphic simple groups and arbitrary transitive factor actions.

Let $G=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t$ act coordinatewise on $X_1\times\cdots\times X_t$, with $A_i\le S_i$ a point stabilizer. Then $G$ is minimally transitive if and only if both of the following hold.

- Every factor action is minimally transitive.
- No isomorphic pair $S_i,S_j$ admits an isomorphism $\alpha:S_i\to S_j$ such that $\alpha(A_i)A_j=S_j$.

Failure of the first condition gives a proper transitive product by replacing one factor. If the second fails, the graph of $\alpha$ acts transitively on $X_i\times X_j$: any desired first coordinate can be reached by one element, after which its stabilizer is $\alpha(A_i)$ on the second coordinate. Transitivity there is exactly the displayed product equality. Combining this diagonal with the remaining factors gives a proper transitive subgroup.

Conversely, suppose a proper subgroup acts transitively and the factor actions are minimal. Its projections are all onto. A subdirect product of nonabelian simple factors is a product of full diagonals on a partition into isomorphic blocks. This follows inductively from Goursat's lemma: when including a new factor the projection onto the previous ones is a product of diagonal factors. A proper subdirect product with the new simple group identifies it with a quotient of that preceding product. Such a surjection is an isomorphism on exactly one simple factor, because the images of the factors are commuting normal subgroups. Otherwise the new factor joins independently. The resulting partition must have a block containing two indices if the subgroup is proper. Its projection to that pair is a diagonal and transitivity of the whole action forces transitivity there. This violates the second condition.

The criterion is effective from factor generator permutations. If $|A_i|\le b$, the whole factor has order at most $b|X_i|$, so enumerate its table by multiplying the given generators. Test each factor for transitivity of a proper subgroup as follows. Any transitive subgroup has index at most $|A_i|$ by the orbit-stabilizer formula. For each index $2\le j\le b$, enumerate all transitive permutation representations of $S_i$ on $j$ labeled points by trying the images of a generating pair. A pair exists by the finite simple two-generation theorem and can be found by enumeration in the table. Propagate the two generator images across table elements, checking the resulting homomorphism, and test each point preimage stabilizer in the original action. Every subgroup of index $j$ occurs from its coset action. This costs at most a function of $b$ times polynomial table time. If no factor exhibits a proper transitive subgroup, enumerate the isomorphisms between each pair of simple factors by the same generating-pair method, now with images in the target table. Compute the product of the two displayed stabilizers for every such isomorphism. The failed condition supplies generators of a proper transitive subgroup, and the two tests run in $f(b)\operatorname{poly}(L)$ on the supplied factor input.
