# Epimorphisms onto dyadic groups with one-dimensional Frattini subgroup

## GP-0359

Given two Cayley-table groups of class at most two and exponent dividing four, when the target has Frattini subgroup of order two there is a deterministic polynomial-time test and witness algorithm for an epimorphism. One may prescribe its entire induced linear map on the source Frattini subgroup or the image of a marked subgroup inside that Frattini subgroup. The square map is included throughout, including targets with a nonzero square on the commutator radical.

For such a group its commutators have order at most two by the class-two fourth-power formula. Squares are central, and commutators are products of squares. Thus $\Phi(G)=G^2$ is central elementary abelian and $V=G/\Phi(G)$ is elementary abelian. Write $W=\Phi(G)$ and let $q:V\to W$ take a lift to its square. Changing the lift by $W$ does not affect the square. The polarization of $q$ is the commutator, and the ordered coordinate lift, with the displayed squares and swaps, classifies the group with its $V,W$ data. In detail, choose bases and lift the basis of $V$, writing every group element uniquely as a product of the lift powers with exponents zero or one and a central element of $W$. All multiplication is obtained by swaps and squares. Thus a pair of linear isomorphisms matching $q$ gives a group isomorphism, with explicit images for chosen generators.

Enumerate all nonzero maps $\lambda:W\to\mathbb F_2$, with any prescribed constraints, at most $|W|$ choices. Any possible epimorphism first kills $\ker\lambda$, and in the resulting group it is injective on the order-two Frattini subgroup. The remaining kernel must be central elementary abelian: commutators and squares of its elements lie in the intersection with the Frattini subgroup and so are trivial. Let $b$ be the polarization of the scalar form $\lambda q$, put $R=\operatorname{rad}(b)$ and $L=\ker((\lambda q)|_R)$. The projected kernel is exactly some subspace $U\le L$. Conversely every such subspace lifts to a possible kernel disjoint from the Frattini subgroup. The preimage of $U$ is central elementary abelian, so choose any vector-space complement to the Frattini line inside that preimage. The quotient square form is the original scalar form on $V/U$.

For clarity, the isometry type of a scalar quadratic form in characteristic two is determined by the rank of its polar form, the dimension of its singular radical $L$, and the following core data. If the form vanishes on $R$, the core is the nondegenerate quadratic form on a symplectic complement, distinguished by its Arf invariant. One computes this invariant from the products of the two square values on each hyperbolic pair of the polar form. If the form does not vanish on $R$, choose a radical vector on which it is one. Adding that vector to each chosen symplectic basis element whose square value is one makes every one of those values zero. In this case there is a unique core at each polar rank, with the extra nonsingular square on the radical vector. The zero-square vectors of $R$ supply a zero-form summand in both cases. This also follows constructively by ordinary quadratic Witt reduction in the first case. [Quadratic-form classification and Arf invariant](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-16.4.232)

Quotienting by $U$ leaves the core unchanged and decreases the singular radical dimension by $\dim U$. For each $\lambda$, compare the two cores and check whether the target singular radical dimension is at most the source's. These tests are necessary and sufficient. A basis of the removed subspace, a central lift complement and the quadratic coordinate isometry give the actual two-step quotient map, then a table witness by normal-form evaluation. All vector spaces are obtained by subgroup generation from the given tables. The number of $\lambda$ and the size of every intermediate quotient are bounded by the source table size, so the algorithm is polynomial.
