# Audit of the finite-ring tensor-isomorphism reduction

**Verdict.** Four informal reviews found the reduction valid for explicit tensors and fixed nonzero finite commutative coefficient rings. The proof gives deterministic polynomial-time many-one equivalence of free and general three-module tensor isomorphism over any two such rings whose characteristics have the same prime divisors. Publication priority for the ring comparison remains unestablished.

## Proof checks

1. **Truncation and reflection.** The coproduct of a monomial of degree $d$ has total augmentation degree at least $d$. This makes the target total-degree quotient essential to well-definedness. For a normalized group-like element, the first nonzero homogeneous term is primitive in the graded truncated polynomial algebra. For each mixed monomial there is a surviving mixed split unique to that monomial. For each pure monomial whose exponent is other than a power of $p$, a suitable base-$p$ digit gives a surviving nonzero binomial split. The primitive terms are therefore combinations of $x_i^{p^r}$. Multiplication by inverses of actual images of $p^r e_i$ cancels one leading degree at a time. The retained degree $p^{e-1}$ also detects the highest base-$p$ digit, giving injectivity. Together these facts show that the only normalized solutions are the original module elements.
2. **Maps and tensors.** Linear extension of a biadditive map kills the augmentation ideal power in each argument separately, by fixing a group basis element in the other argument. The marked field line fixes the image of the input tensor. In the arbitrary-module case the two pure-tensor maps force the recovered auxiliary module maps by additive generation. This covers nonfree modes and zero tensors. Over a general fixed ring the unary scalar-action markers force exactly the required module linearity.
3. **Bit size and plain tensor output.** Cyclic decomposition and the two tensor-product presentations are computable from explicit generators and relations in polynomial time. The two truncated spaces have polynomially many monomials for fixed $p,e$. Basis-explicit evaluation needs at most $2^{p^{e-1}}$ group symbols per monomial. A fixed number of subspace, linear and bilinear markers is within the published compiler's scope over any field, including the binary field. The compiler removes all markers into one ordinary three-tensor pair. In the input model for arbitrary modules the generator lists count toward the input length. The proof treats empty free arrays given by dimension headers separately. These bounds do not give a uniform polynomial in the bit length of a varying modulus. [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219), [Grochow–Qiao, Theorem 10.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S10.SS1)
4. **Reverse and product assembly.** A minimal nonzero ideal is a copy of one residue field as a module. Matrix action on tensors with entries in that ideal factors through that field. Every invertible matrix over the residue field lifts on the corresponding local factor and can be assigned identities on the other factors. The symmetric-power marker forces a putative transporter over the residue field to have prime-field entries. Across different primary factors the Chinese remainder idempotents split the modules, the tensors, and the orbit test. Padding the field pairs is exact because an isomorphism induces isomorphisms on their mode supports, which extend to invertible maps on the original formats. Padding takes place over the fields before the ideal embeddings.

The [binary check](CARRY_CHECK.py) was rerun and passed all 1,023 augmentation-one candidates and 5,797 ordered addition pairs across the ten small module types. This is a test of the degree-two model. The general reduction was audited mathematically.

## Relation to published questions and work

Grochow–Qiao's Open Question 7.6 asks for comparisons among fields. Its three listed comparisons are a prime field versus an extension of the same characteristic, finite fields of coprime characteristics, and a prime field versus the rationals. The ring theorem contains the first clause. The project's earlier Frobenius-marker proof already established that clause. The comparisons across distinct primes and with the rationals remain outside this result. [Journal version, Section 7.2](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full), [arXiv Open Question 10.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S10.SS2), [earlier fixed-characteristic proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/FixedCharacteristicTensorIsomorphism)

Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun's STOC 2025 paper defines the two ring TI classes. Its Theorem 3.6 (Theorem 1.6 in the longer version) establishes equivalence of five tensor actions over a common ring $\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$. The audit found no published problem explicitly asking for the larger ring comparison, and no source stating the exact reduction. The literature search does not settle priority. [STOC paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3717823.3718286), [long version, Theorem 1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S1.Thmtheorem6)

The augmentation quotient is classical in Passi's work. Rüping–Stephan give the closely related dual filtration by weights on Jennings coordinate functions. Their Section 5 supplies the weighted monomial description, and Example 6.8 explicitly exhibits the carry for $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$. This is prior work for the carry mechanism. The distinct contribution here is to encode all the required biadditive maps and tensor data in such truncated structures, prove that their isomorphisms reflect the original tensor orbit, and compile the result at polynomial cost. Work on recovering certain finite groups from truncated modular group algebras is also nearby. [Passi](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/BFb0067186), [Rüping–Stephan, Sections 5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.03091#S5), [Tuzzolo, *Short Jennings series and the modular isomorphism problem*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40868)

## Complexity consequence

For a fixed nonzero finite commutative ring $R$, write $P(R)$ for the set of prime divisors of its characteristic. A canonical representative for its polynomial-time many-one degree is ordinary TI over the squarefree ring $\prod_{p\in P(R)}\mathbb F_p$. Equivalently, a decision reduces to the conjunction of one prime-field TI instance per prime in $P(R)$. In particular the theorem proves a one-way reduction whenever $P(R)\subseteq P(S)$. In the proof, assign matching zero tensors to the extra primary factors of $S$ and leave the other components as constructed. There is no separation established between degrees for different prime sets.

For singleton support, this proves that the entire fixed-ring problem, including arbitrary finite modules, has the same polynomial-time many-one degree as prime-field TI. Every polynomial-time method for the field case therefore gives one for the ring case. This is a structural result in isomorphism complexity. The reduction has a polynomial increase in dimensions and establishes no improved running time for general TI.
