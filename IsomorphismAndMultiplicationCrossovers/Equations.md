# Product subspaces and equations of the matrix multiplication orbit closure

**Exact threshold over every field.** Let $M_n$ denote multiplication of two $n\times n$ matrices, and let $O_{k,r}$ be the bilinear map $(x,y)\mapsto x\otimes y$ from $F^k\times F^r$ to $F^{kr}$. Then $O_{k,r}$ is a restriction of $M_n$ exactly when $kr\le n^2$. The same threshold holds for border restriction. The output flattening of $O_{k,r}$ has rank $kr$, whereas every image or limit of $M_n$ has output rank at most $n^2$.

For sufficiency, transpose the products and exchange the two inputs if necessary so $k\le r$, giving $k\le n$. Over a field of size at least $n$, choose a diagonal $D$ with $n$ distinct diagonal entries. Take $A_i=D^{i-1}$ for $1\le i\le k$. Read the $n^2$ matrix units column by column, and partition the first $kr$ units into $r$ consecutive groups of $k$. In each group the row indices are distinct, even across a column boundary. Let $B_j$ be the sum in group $j$. The $k$ products $A_iB_j$ are independent by the Vandermonde determinant. Different groups have disjoint support. An output map can therefore send the products to the $kr$ coordinate vectors.

For a small finite field $F_q$, take a $k$-dimensional subspace $U$ of the regular matrix representation of $F_{q^n}$. Every nonzero member is invertible. If $t<r$ right factors already span a product space $W$ of dimension $kt$, an unsuitable next factor lies in $\bigcup_{[u]\in\mathbf P(U)}u^{-1}W$. Its size is at most $(q^k-1)q^{kt}/(q-1)<q^{k(t+1)}\le q^{n^2}$. Choose a factor outside it. This induction supplies the same independent product family over the original field.

**Coordinate-ring consequence.** Put $m=n^2$ and take the complex orbit closure $X_n$ of $M_n$ in the ordinary $m$ by $m$ by $m$ tensor space. Composing the construction with an arbitrary output map realizes every tensor supported in a box of format $k\times r\times m$ when $kr\le m$. The three choices of output are equivalent by cyclic symmetry of the trilinear matrix trace and reversal by transpose.

For a triple of polynomial partitions of the same degree, each of length at most $m$, write $a\le b\le c$ for their sorted lengths and use the usual dual convention for the coordinate functions. When $ab\le m$, the multiplicity of this type in the coordinate ring of $X_n$ equals its **full ambient Kronecker coefficient**. Indeed, any irreducible copy in the homogeneous ideal has a nonzero highest-weight polynomial. In bases adapted to the three highest weights, weights in each slot outside its partition length are zero. Since each variable contributes a nonnegative multidegree in each slot, that polynomial only uses entries within the $a\times b\times c$ box. It is nonzero somewhere on this box, which is impossible for an equation. Complete reducibility in characteristic zero now gives the full multiplicity. This conclusion also applies to zero ambient multiplicities. The construction does not imply loss of multiplicity outside this range.

**Circuit consequence.** Suppose a nonzero degree-$D$ equation $f$ of $X_n$ is nonzero at $T_0$ with first support dimension at most $k$ and $3\le k<n$. Write $h=\lfloor m/k\rfloor$. For a generic $m$-square matrix $Y$, the polynomial $g(Y)=f((I\otimes Y\otimes I)T_0)$ is nonzero at $Y=I$. It vanishes at every matrix of rank at most $h$, since the first two support dimensions of the transformed tensor have product at most $kh\le m$. The classical determinantal ideal theorem puts $g$ in the ideal of the $(h+1)$-minors. In particular $D\ge h+1$. Invoking the repository's informal full-minor extraction theorem now gives $\det_{h+1}$ at constant depth with exact oracle-circuit size polynomial in $n,D$, or border size polynomial in $n$, over $\mathbb C$. This is a nonuniform circuit theorem; $T_0$ can be hardwired. The multiplicity result above is independent of extraction. For $n=64,k=3$ this gives order $1366$; the earlier unconditional extraction gives order $65$. The existing special two-slice criterion gives a stronger result when it applies.

The hypothesis for $k=3$ can hold when two-slice tests in all three modes vanish. For $n\ge4$, take the existing two-slice discriminant equation and multiply by a $3$-minor of each flattening. In a fixed $3\times m\times m$ box containing the two tested slices, the discriminant is nonzero at some pencil and each minor can be chosen individually nonzero as a polynomial on the box. Their product is nonzero there since its coordinate ring is a domain. All tensors of support at most two in any mode annihilate the product.
