# Tensor isomorphism within the representation class of the flip

**Theorem.** Over each fixed finite field, ordinary three-tensor isomorphism reduces in deterministic polynomial time to local equivalence of involutive Yang–Baxter operators. The target instances can all be chosen so that, for every strand number, their representations are conjugate to ordinary permutations of tensor factors. The converse reduction holds even on arbitrary input operators. Local equivalence means conjugation by the same invertible matrix in both slots.

**Encoding.** Strip the three unused supports by row reduction and record their dimensions. For input pairs, first compare the original and compressed dimensions, and decide zero cases directly. Thus a nonzero input is a linearly independent list of slices $M_i:B\to A$ for $1\le i\le t$, with images spanning $A$ and common kernel zero. Its isomorphism class is the left-right equivalence class of $S=\langle M_i\rangle$. Put $C=F^t$, let $z$ span another line, and put $H=A\oplus B\oplus C\oplus Fz$. Extend all $M_i$ by zero. Put $U_i=z e_i^*:C\to Fz$, extended by zero, and form
$$s=\sum_iM_i\otimes U_i,\qquad r=s-s_{21},\qquad B_S=P(I+r),$$
where $P$ is the flip. The factors of $s$ lie in the matrix subspace of maps from $B\oplus C$ into $A\oplus Fz$ extended by zero there. Any two such maps multiply to zero. The construction uses polynomially many field operations even with dense output.

**All strand numbers.** In $H^{\otimes k}$ put $J=\prod_{i<j}(I+s_{ij})$. Distinct factors commute because disjoint placements commute and overlapping products vanish in either order. Each factor is invertible. The adjacent flip $P_i$ permutes the factors except for replacing $s_{i,i+1}$ by its reverse. Hence
$$J^{-1}P_iJ=(I-r_{i,i+1})P_i=P_i(I+r_{i,i+1}).$$
This is simultaneous for all adjacent generators. It proves the braid relations and involutivity, including characteristic two. The conjugating operator is allowed to depend on $k$.

**Local equivalence reflects TI.** A transporter of the slice spaces acts on $A,B$ by the given left and right bases. Extend it on $C$ by the induced map on slice-coordinate vectors, so that the dual basis $U_i$ transforms contragrediently. This carries $s$ and hence $B_S$ to their counterparts. Conversely $P$ commutes with $g\otimes g$, so a local equivalence conjugates $r$ by $\operatorname{Ad}(g)\otimes\operatorname{Ad}(g)$. The image of contracting one tensor leg of $r$ is exactly
$$E=S\oplus\operatorname{Hom}(C,Fz),$$
by independence of both lists and disjoint supports. Its total image and common kernel are both $A\oplus Fz$. Taking this intrinsic image as codomain and the quotient by this kernel as domain shows that conjugation of $E$ induces left-right equivalence of its support-compressed matrix spaces. Their ordered three-tensors are $T_S\oplus T_{\rm ev}$, with the same evaluation block $T_{\rm ev}$ on both sides.

For clarity, direct-sum cancellation here is valid for concise tensors with three ordered modes. Use the algebra of triples of slot endomorphisms whose actions on the tensor coincide. Endomorphisms in different slots commute. Moving two actions through three distinct slots interchanges their order, so concision implies that their endomorphisms in each slot commute as well. This is a finite-dimensional commutative algebra. Its families of orthogonal idempotents summing to one are precisely the direct-sum decompositions: in the induced decompositions of the three modes all components with mismatching idempotent labels vanish. Primitive idempotents therefore give a unique multiset of indecomposable summands, transported by every isomorphism. Cancel the multiset of the fixed evaluation tensor. This recovers $T_S\cong T_{S'}$. For early decisions a fixed yes pair consists of two identical examples; a fixed no pair can use flip and a nonzero example of equal dimension, since every local basis change fixes flip.

For the upper reduction encode $K=H\otimes H$, its universal bilinear map $H\times H\to K$, and the operator on $K$ as a fixed system of tensors with repeated roles. An isomorphism on these data must be $g$ on $H$ and $g\otimes g$ on $K$. Apply the published fixed-system reduction to ordinary TI. [Futorny, Grochow and Sergeichuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219)

**Multiplicity of indistinguishable examples.** For $d\times d\times d$ tensors, a union bound over nonzero covectors shows that the nonconcise fraction is at most $3q^{d-d^2}$. Each isomorphism orbit has at most $q^{3d^2}$ elements. Thus in dimension $3d+1$ there are at least $(1-3q^{d-d^2})q^{d^3-3d^2}$ inequivalent local classes so constructed. Scaling $A\oplus Fz$ by a common parameter scales $r$ quadratically, so each geometric orbit closure also contains the flip. All polynomial invariants for the algebraic group take the flip value; finite rational group invariants have a different meaning.

**Connections and scope.** The square-zero matrix block comes from the repository's pencil construction. Its balanced reduction further gives local dimension $O(n^{\lceil d/3\rceil})$ for fixed-order $d$ source tensors of side at most $n$. Earlier literature studies representation equivalence, in particular for unitary involutive operators. The operators here are algebraic and in general lack Hilbert-space unitarity. The contribution claimed in this note is the explicit reduction inside one representation class. Publication priority is unverified.
