# Cancellation of matrix algebra actions in saturated tensor restrictions

Work over $\mathbb C$. Write $M(a,b,c)$ for multiplying an $a\times b$ matrix by a $b\times c$ matrix. Let $V$ be a nonzero finite-dimensional vector space and $B\subseteq\operatorname{End}(V)$ a unital associative matrix algebra. Write $C_B$ for the three-tensor **whose output-dual slices are the matrices of $B$ acting on $V$**. Fixing this orientation is essential. Tensoring by $C_B$ means the modewise tensor product of three-tensors. A border restriction permits arbitrary linear maps on the three enlarged spaces and takes their closure.

**Single product, arbitrary matrix algebra.** Fix positive $a,b,u,v$ such that $ab=uv$. Then
$$M(a,b,a)\otimes C_B\ \text{border-restricts to}\ M(u,v,u)\otimes C_B
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u\mid a.$$
Every positive instance already has an exact restriction before adding the catalyst. The catalyst dimension, number of inequivalent simple modules, and radical length are unrestricted.

**Batches, one simple module type.** Let $S=\bigoplus_j M(a_j,b_j,c_j)$ and $T=\bigoplus_i M(u_i,v_i,w_i)$. Suppose the input dimensions match on both sides:
$$\sum_j a_jb_j=\sum_i u_iv_i,\qquad \sum_j b_jc_j=\sum_i v_iw_i.$$
Suppose all target outer ratios $u_i/w_i=p/q$ agree, in lowest terms. If $B/\operatorname{rad}B$ is a full matrix algebra, the following are equivalent:
* $S\otimes C_B$ border-restricts to $T\otimes C_B$.
* $S$ restricts exactly to $T$.
* Nonnegative integers $k_{ji}$ satisfy $a_j=\sum_i k_{ji}u_i$, $c_j=\sum_i k_{ji}w_i$, $v_i=\sum_j b_jk_{ji}$.

**Factorization lemma.** Write $B/\operatorname{rad}B=\prod_{\alpha}M_{d_\alpha}(\mathbb C)$. On a composition series of $V$, let $\ell_\alpha$ be the multiplicity of the natural module for component $\alpha$. Each component occurs, since the kernel of the representation on the associated graded maps every filtration stage into the next and is a nilpotent ideal; faithfulness identifies that ideal with the radical. An $h\times h$ generic matrix with independent entries in $B$ acts on $V^h$. In a basis respecting the filtration its determinant is
$$\prod_\alpha \det(Z_\alpha)^{\ell_\alpha},$$
where $Z_\alpha$ is a generic square matrix of size $h d_\alpha$. Indeed project each entry through the quotient; surjectivity makes the entries independent coordinates after a linear coordinate change. The remaining radical coordinates disappear in the determinant. Arbitrary linear substitutions preserve this product expression, although a substituted factor can vanish or split.

**Single-product necessity.** Take the determinant of the generic output slice of the target. Its factors are distinct generic determinants of degrees $u d_\beta$, repeated $v\ell_\beta$ times. For the source, before the output substitution the corresponding factors have degrees $a d_\alpha$ and exponents $b\ell_\alpha$. Saturation makes both input transformations square even after tensoring by $C_B$. They therefore multiply this polynomial by a scalar, possibly zero. The cone of zero together with products of fixed homogeneous degrees and exponents is closed: its projectivization is the image of the multiplication map on a product of projective spaces. Thus the target determinant admits the source factor pattern, even for a border restriction. Unique factorization forces each $a d_\alpha$ to be a nonnegative integral combination of the target degrees $u d_\beta$. Put $d=\gcd_\beta d_\beta$. Then $ud$ divides every $a d_\alpha$, hence divides their gcd $ad$, and $u\mid a$. Conversely, group the source rows and columns into $a/u$ matching blocks and sum their partial products, giving inner dimension $b(a/u)=v$. Tensor with the identity on $C_B$.

**Batch necessity.** Replace each output coordinate of a slice by its own generic $q\times p$ matrix, as in the repository's saturated restriction proof. The resulting matrix is square. On target summand $i$, after permuting indices the block is a generic $(qu_i)\times(pw_i)$ matrix with entries in $B$, repeated $v_i$ times. Put $d=d_1$ and $\ell=\ell_1$ for the unique simple type. Its determinant is $\Delta_i^{\ell v_i}$ with $\Delta_i$ a generic determinant of degree $dqu_i$. Variables for distinct $i$ are disjoint and the target determinant is nonzero. Each source summand has rectangular blocks of dimensions $qa_j\dim V$ by $pc_j\dim V$. If any is nonsquare, their total rank is smaller than the common overall dimension (sum the smaller of the two dimensions over all blocks). This makes the determinant identically zero on all restrictions and their closure. Thus $qa_j=pc_j$ for every $j$. For each $j$ the factorization lemma now gives one factor of degree $dqa_j$ with exponent $\ell b_j$. The same closed-cone argument followed by unique factorization gives $dqa_j=\sum_i k_{ji}dqu_i$ and $\ell v_i=\sum_j k_{ji}\ell b_j$. Cancel the positive constants and use the aspect ratios to obtain all three stated equations. Conversely these equations give exact block routing on the unamplified inputs: split each source's row and column sets into the prescribed target groups and sum matching output blocks over the partitions of each target's inner index.

**Example.** Even after tensoring both sides with the regular action of $\mathbb C[t]/(t^L)$ for any $L\ge1$, $M(3,2,3)$ cannot degenerate to $M(2,3,2)$. The single-product result also holds for a product of arbitrarily many matrix algebras acting with arbitrary positive multiplicities.

The two ingredients are the fixed-degree factor patterns from saturated tensor restrictions and the determinant on module filtration quotients. Related literature studies catalytic changes of tensor restrictions and stable embeddings of semisimple algebras. These theorems concern a single finite conversion with equal input dimensions and catalysts of the specified form. They give no bound on the matrix-multiplication exponent.
