# Density and sampling of mixed generating witnesses

**Theorem.** Let $G$ be a finite group, $R$ its solvable radical, and $G/R$ a direct product of nonabelian simple groups (the empty product is allowed). Prescribe subgroups $H_1,\ldots,H_c$ and at least one free element slot, $r\ge1$. If some $g_i,x_j$ satisfy
$$G=\langle g_1H_1g_1^{-1},\ldots,g_cH_cg_c^{-1},x_1,\ldots,x_r\rangle,$$
then independent uniform choices of all $c+r$ elements succeed with probability at least $1/|G|$. The count is of ordered tuples of elements, including multiplicities of conjugators realizing the same subgroup.

**Simple coordinates.** First take $Q=G/R$. Use the images of the original prescribed subgroups. Choose generator lists for them, retaining the labels of the original generators in each coordinate, including any identities. In a simple coordinate $S$ the profile is the concatenation of these projected lists after independently conjugating each entire list, followed by the $r$ free entries. The probability of generating $S$ is at least $3/|S|$ in any feasible instance. If a marked list contains a nonidentity element $a$, fix the conjugators and use the last uniform free element. By Guralnick–Kantor there is a mate $b$ generating $S$ with $a$. Every element of $\langle a\rangle b\langle a\rangle$ is also a mate. If $|a|\ge4$ this supplies at least four mates; for order two or three the two conjugate prime-order subgroups intersect trivially since a mate cannot normalize $\langle a\rangle$. This again supplies at least four. When all marked entries project trivially, feasibility implies $r\ge2$; expose the penultimate entry first and apply the same bound when it is nonidentity. Since $4(1-1/|S|)\ge3$, both cases suffice. [Guralnick and Kantor](https://doi.org/10.1006/jabr.2000.8357)

**Repeated simple factors.** A tuple generates the whole direct product exactly when it surjects on each coordinate and no two isomorphic coordinates have profiles related by one automorphism. To see this, the labelled entries define homomorphisms from one free group. A subdirect subgroup of a product of nonabelian simple groups is a product of diagonals. Inductively, appending a simple factor to a previously full product either gives the full enlarged product or, by Goursat's lemma, identifies a simple quotient of the old product with the new factor. Any surjection of a direct product onto a nonabelian simple group factors through one coordinate, since the factor images are commuting normal subgroups. This proves the criterion.

For isomorphic coordinates group their templates into types: two templates match if a single automorphism takes each prescribed marked list's inner-conjugacy class to its counterpart. Free slots have no restrictions. Different types cannot collide. Within a type, align the templates by automorphisms. Each allowed profile then has equal probability, because the fibers of each conjugation map have equal cardinality. Conditional on coordinate generation, the possible orbit labels under the template-preserving automorphisms are uniform: the action is free on generating profiles. Two allowed profiles related by any automorphism are already related by one preserving the template. Coordinates are independent because uniform elements of a direct product have independent components, including the components of each conjugator. For $k$ identical types and $m$ available orbit labels, feasibility implies $m\ge k$. The no-collision probability is
$$\frac{(m)_k}{m^k}\ge\frac{k!}{k^k}\ge e^{-k}.$$
Multiply this with the simple-coordinate bounds over all types. Writing $K$ for the number of simple factors, the resulting probability is at least $(3/e)^K/|Q|\ge1/|Q|$.

**Solvable radical.** Lift along a chief series inside $R$. At each step the kernel $A$ is an elementary abelian minimal normal subgroup. For any fixed generating choices downstairs the bad lifts either form the empty set or are an affine subspace of the vector space of $|A|^{c+r}$ possible lifts (in the latter notation the cardinality, rather than dimension, is displayed). The number of bad lifts is independent of which successful downstairs tuple was chosen. Here is why. A proper generated subgroup surjecting onto the quotient must meet $A$ trivially by minimal normality; thus it must be a complement. In a split extension, complements are graphs of cocycles. Requiring a complement to contain all prescribed conjugated subgroups and free lifts imposes joint affine equations on the cocycle and the lifts, hence the bad affine projection. If a prescribed subgroup meets $A$ nontrivially or the extension is nonsplit, bad lifts are impossible. For the independence assertion, an eligible cocycle must restrict to each original marked subgroup's image in a fixed cohomology class, independent of the chosen quotient conjugators. Each eligible cocycle determines the free lifts and allows a fixed number of conjugator lifts, equal to the product of the corresponding fixed-space sizes. No two cocycles share a lift tuple since the quotient entries generate. The full equations appear in the source proof. [Mixed lifting, §1](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FurtherPortfolioIsomorphismCrossovers/Groups.md)

Feasibility therefore makes the bad affine subspace proper whenever nonempty. A uniformly random lift succeeds conditionally with probability at least $1-1/p\ge1/|A|$. Multiply up the series to lose at most the factor $|R|$. This proves the theorem, including $Q=1$.

**Algorithms.** With a Cayley table, test any sampled tuple by subgroup closure. On positive instances, rejection sampling returns the exactly uniform distribution on witnesses in at most $|G|$ expected trials. For a total randomized relative counting algorithm, use $O(|G|\varepsilon^{-2}\log(2/\delta))$ independent trials and multiply the observed success fraction by $|G|^{c+r}$. The multiplicative Chernoff bound gives relative error $\varepsilon$ with probability at least $1-\delta$ on positive inputs and the estimate is always zero on negative inputs. The runtime is polynomial in the table size, the listed subgroup data, the number of slots, and the accuracy parameters. Neither algorithm needs a radical or simple decomposition as input.

The repository already has decision and lifting results; the additional conclusion is a quantitative guarantee for prescribed subgroups and one or more free slots, including repeated simple factors of unbounded order. Related work on random generating tuples without prescribed subgroups is extensive. The full semigroup minimum-generation question is outside this conclusion.
