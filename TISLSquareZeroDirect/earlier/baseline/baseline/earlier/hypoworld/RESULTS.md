# The special-linear tensor-isomorphism hypoworld

Let $H$ be the assertion that special-linear three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb F_3$ reduces to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb F_3$ by a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction. Ordinary TI reduces in the other direction by unused mode lines, so $H$ is the degree equality $\mathsf{TI}=\mathsf{TI}_{\rm SL}$ at this fixed field. The statements below concern dense tensors and the specified compact presentations. No field-uniform determinant-one collapse is assumed. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

## Ten statements equivalent to the collapse

| # | Equivalent assertion | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **One-mode orientation.** Requiring determinant one only on the output mode reduces to ordinary TI. | Weighted algebra corners program any determinant character. Unit determinant images are killed so isotopies carry the same output sign. [Proof](UniformNorms.md#four-equivalent-assertions) |
| 2 | **Uniform finite extension norms.** Independent determinant norms can be prescribed on the three modes over any explicitly represented $\mathbb F_{3^e}$, with a single uniform ordinary $\mathbb F_3$-TI reduction. | A four-line cross-ratio anchor forces coefficient-field linearity in the weighted algebra, even when $e$ grows. [Proof](UniformNorms.md#a-weighted-multiplication-table-that-fixes-the-coefficient-field) |
| 3 | **Unbounded-order multilinear equivalence.** An arbitrary weighted product of determinant norms can be prescribed when the tensor order is part of the input. | A triangular chain uses tensor products on proper intervals and the given multilinear map on the full interval. Even truncation lengths discard all unwanted interior determinants. [Proof](UnboundedTensorOrder.md) |
| 4 | **Coupled affine sign systems.** Independent comparisons across a list of fields can satisfy a single specified binary affine system of rank $O(\log L)$ on their combined signs, by one ordinary TI test. | Dual characters separate cosets. Balanced positive formulas combine their attainable values without assuming individual sign choices are unique. [Proof](UniformNorms.md#combining-character-and-sign-choices) |
| 5 | **Finite-state sign policies.** For every fixed automaton on the eight determinant patterns, acceptance of a sequence of independently realizable signs reduces to ordinary TI. | Boolean transition matrices over attainable signs multiply along the input list. [Proof](UniformNorms.md#combining-character-and-sign-choices) |
| 6 | **Even isomorphism of commutative local rings.** Drop the determinant constraint by a many-one reduction for even-dimensional $\mathbb F_3$ residue-scalar rings with $J^9=0$. | Two marker towers program determinant weights and intrinsically retain the original input roles. [Proof](LocalRingsAndQuandles.md#weighted-orientation-in-a-commutative-local-ring) |
| 7 | **Even isomorphism of compact connected involutory quandles.** Drop the evenness requirement for the specified unpointed quandle family. | The operation recovers a regular displacement group. Its translations are even, and the involution eliminates the central shear after normalizing the image of zero. [Proof](LocalRingsAndQuandles.md#an-unpointed-involutory-quandle) |
| 8 | **Alternating conjugacy of compact regular groups.** Reduce $A_N$ to $S_N$ conjugacy for left-regular special class-two exponent-three groups written as ordered commutator coefficients. | The Baer correspondence identifies parity with additive determinant. The explicit bimap construction preserves weighted characters through isomorphism. [Proof](RegularGroupConjugacy.md) |
| 9 | **Reduced determinantal hypersurface equivalence.** The specified family of reduced projective zero sets of homogeneous-entry pencils belongs to ordinary TI for projective changes defined over the base field. | Distinct irreducible marker factors recover coordinates without factor multiplicities and enforce determinant-one actions by omitted-column anchors. [Proof](DeterminantalHypersurfaces.md#the-computational-equivalence) |
| 10 | **Abstract isomorphism of irreducible determinantal hypersurfaces.** The specified absolutely irreducible projective hypersurfaces with affine-linear determinantal presentations have abstract base-field isomorphism in ordinary TI. | A suspension makes the variety irreducible and recovers the original reduced hypersurface from the span of its singular locus. Its Picard group forces abstract isomorphisms to preserve the projective embedding. [Proof](DeterminantalHypersurfaces.md#absolutely-irreducible-projective-hypersurfaces-from-affine-linear-pencils) |

The first five use one common determinant-character mechanism but impose different requirements on the reduction. The last five change the source structures. Counts of entries exclude the baseline hypothesis and do not count the equivalent formulations within a single row a second time. For the batch and character entries, the reverse implication uses the one-pair instance or a fixed conjunction of three-mode character tests. For the geometric entries, it uses the specified presentation family, including its reconstruction promise.

## Eighteen further consequences under the collapse

1. **Arbitrary allowed signs.** Any prescribed subset of the eight determinant triples can be tested with one ordinary TI instance. [Proof](ComplexityTransfers.md#one-ordinary-instance-for-arbitrary-allowed-signs)
2. **Orientation diversity thresholds.** One ordinary comparison can test whether at least $k$ distinct determinant triples are attainable, for any $k\le8$. [Proof](FurtherTransfers.md#positive-tests-on-feasibility-tables)
3. **Orbit splitting.** Eight ordinary decision calls give the whole attainable sign profile, the number of SL orbits inside the ordinary orbit, and their relative sizes and entropy gap. [Proof](ComplexityTransfers.md#the-entire-determinant-pattern-and-the-orbit-index)
4. **All individual norms at unbounded arity.** Fully prescribed norm signs on dense multilinear maps reduce to one ordinary comparison, even with arbitrarily many one-dimensional modes. [Proof](UnboundedTensorOrder.md#further-uniformly-transferable-problems)
5. **Semilinear orientations.** Allowing simultaneous Frobenius across the modes over an extension preserves the one-query reduction after calibrating its explicit frame determinants. [Proof](UniformNorms.md#combining-character-and-sign-choices)
6. **High-order coupled constraints.** The logarithmic-rank sign system test also applies to lists of independent higher-order maps whose orders are part of the input. [Proof](UnboundedTensorOrder.md#further-uniformly-transferable-problems)
7. **Rigid profiles with arbitrary constraint rank.** If each nonempty local profile is a singleton, arbitrary-rank parity equations reduce to one ordinary TI comparison. Logarithmic-depth Boolean circuits with negations also work on those rigid sign bits. [Proof](FurtherTransfers.md#unbounded-rank-conditions-on-rigid-independent-sign-profiles)
8. **Mixed algebraic batches.** Logarithmic-depth positive Boolean tests can combine oriented rings, groups, and tensor instances into a single ordinary TI comparison. This includes fixed exception budgets. [Proof](FurtherTransfers.md#positive-tests-on-feasibility-tables)
9. **Exact tensor counts.** At most nine nonadaptive queries to exact ordinary counting compute an exact signed count. At most eight signed count queries give the unconditional reverse. [Proof](ComplexityTransfers.md#exact-counts-and-their-decision-reductions)
10. **Modular tensor counts.** One ordinary count modulo $8M$ and the sign feasibility table give a signed count modulo arbitrary $M$. For odd moduli the factor of eight is unnecessary. [Proof](ComplexityTransfers.md#exact-counts-and-their-decision-reductions)
11. **Approximate counting.** Fully polynomial randomized approximation schemes exist for ordinary counts exactly when they exist for signed tensor counts, conditional on $H$. [Proof](ComplexityTransfers.md#exact-counts-and-their-decision-reductions)
12. **Counting-to-decision.** Polynomial-time counting-to-decision in the dense ordinary model is equivalent to counting-to-decision in the dense signed model under $H$. [Proof](ComplexityTransfers.md#exact-counts-and-their-decision-reductions)
13. **Full-coset search-to-decision.** With output consisting of one map and stabilizer generators, the two search-to-decision conjectures are equivalent under $H$. [Proof](ComplexityTransfers.md#search-complete-cosets-and-sampling)
14. **Uniform signed sampling.** With an exact ordinary uniform transporter sampler, nonempty specified sign fibers can be sampled exactly with at most eight expected trials, and $H$ supplies the emptiness test as ordinary decision. [Proof](ComplexityTransfers.md#search-complete-cosets-and-sampling)
15. **Representative canonization.** Ordinary and special-linear representative canonization have the same deterministic polynomial-time Turing degree under $H$. [Proof](ComplexityTransfers.md#canonical-representatives)
16. **Native counts beyond tensors.** One ordinary count and two ordinary decision queries recover the even count in the compact group, commutative local ring, or quandle model. [Proof](FurtherTransfers.md#counts-for-rings-compact-groups-and-compact-quandles)
17. **External targets.** For every fixed comparison language, deterministic many-one reductions to or from ordinary TI transfer to or from signed TI under $H$. This includes GI or circuit-minimization languages whenever the corresponding ordinary reduction is proved. [Proof](ComplexityTransfers.md#what-the-hypothetical-reduction-changes)
18. **Worst-case algorithm transfer with a size bound.** A polynomial-time or quasipolynomial-time ordinary decision algorithm transfers to the signed problem under $H$. More precise time bounds transfer with the actual blow-up of the reduction, and membership in classes closed under deterministic many-one reductions transfers as well. [Proof](ComplexityTransfers.md#what-the-hypothetical-reduction-changes)

## Boundaries and unconditional results

The equality remains open here. The catalog gives scoped equivalences and consequences, rather than a claim of equivalence with $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, a solution to general symbolic determinant identity testing, or polynomial-time classification of all groups or varieties. A general reduction of determinantal hypersurface equivalence would suffice, but the proven converse uses the explicit families. Finite-field norm one on $\mathbb F_{3^e}$ only tests the norm character of the extension-field determinant.

Independently of $H$, ordinary and signed full transporter cosets already reduce to each other with polynomial postprocessing and at most eight signed coset calls. The published ordinary matrix-space coset algorithm also yields a $3^{\widetilde O(n^{3/2})}$ upper bound for signed decision, full-coset search, and exact count when $n$ is the maximum tensor side length. The formulas relating compact group and quandle counts via central shears and translations are unconditional.
