# Equivalent orientation questions for tensors, local rings, and quandles over $\mathbb F_3$

All reductions in this note are deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions unless a fixed conjunction is stated explicitly. Tensor and ring inputs are dense structure constants in ordered bases. Groups and quandles are given by compact commutator coefficients in ordered bases, so the input does not enumerate the underlying set. A map of a framed $\mathbb F_3$-space is called even if its determinant is $1$.

Let $\mathsf{C}$ be ordinary isomorphism of even-dimensional commutative unital local $\mathbb F_3$-algebras with scalar residue and $J^9=0$. Let $\mathsf{C}^{+}$ ask for an even isomorphism on the same class. Let $\mathsf{Q}$ be isomorphism of the compact connected involutory quandles defined in Section 2, and let $\mathsf{Q}^{+}$ ask for a quandle isomorphism of even permutation sign on the coordinate-ordered underlying sets.

**Theorem.** The following statements are equivalent.

1. Special-linear three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb F_3$ reduces to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb F_3$.
2. $\mathsf{C}^{+}$ reduces to $\mathsf{C}$. Equivalently, on the stated local multiplication tables, imposing determinant one in the output mode reduces to ordinary tensor isomorphism.
3. $\mathsf{Q}^{+}$ reduces to $\mathsf{Q}$.
4. Even isomorphism of compact special class-two exponent-three groups reduces to ordinary isomorphism of those groups.

The first statement is equivalently equality of the ordinary and special-linear many-one degrees, since ordinary TI reduces to SL-TI by adding a zero direction in every mode and using those directions to correct the mode determinants. Conversely padded isomorphisms restrict to the intrinsic contraction supports and hence give ordinary isomorphisms of the original tensors. The ring and quandle constructions below give the new transfers. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

## 1. Weighted orientation in a commutative local ring

Work with a bilinear tensor $T:U\times V\to W$. A transporter is $g_W T(x,y)=T'(g_Ux,g_Vy)$. Over $\mathbb F_3$, taking the dual of a mode does not change its determinant sign. A character of the determinant triple is a product of any subset of its coordinates. Any nontrivial character can be written after permuting modes as
$$
 (\det g_U)^{\varepsilon_U}(\det g_V)^{\varepsilon_V}\det g_W ,
 \qquad \varepsilon_U,\varepsilon_V\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{1}
$$

First consider a nonzero concise bimap, with zero kernel in each input and output spanned by its values. Choose $k_U,k_V\in\{4,5\}$ of parities $\varepsilon_U,\varepsilon_V$. Use a unit and the radical vector space
$$
 J=Fa\oplus Fa^2\oplus\cdots\oplus Fa^{M_a-1}
   \oplus Fb\oplus Fb^2\oplus\cdots\oplus Fb^{M_b-1}
   \oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k_U-1}U_j
   \oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k_V-1}V_j
   \oplus W ,
$$
where $U_j,V_j$ are copies of the original input spaces. Set $M_a=5$ and $M_b\in\{8,9\}$. Multiply the two scalar branches internally as truncated polynomial ideals. Let $a^i$ multiply $U_j$ by shifting it to $U_{i+j}$, with out-of-range coordinates zero, and let $b^i$ act likewise on $V_j$. The only other nonzero product of two radical summands is $U_0\times V_0\to W$ via $T$ and its symmetric counterpart. All products involving $W$ and a radical element vanish. Add these products bilinearly, with $1$ acting as the unit.

The result $A(T)$ is associative and commutative. Products internal to a scalar branch act on its tower by the truncated module law. Any product involving $T$ vanishes after a third radical factor, and moving a positive marker power across a $T$-product gives zero on either side. All other mixed triple products are zero. The radical is nilpotent, with scalar quotient, so it is the Jacobson radical. Degree $1$ consists of $a,b,U_0,V_0$. Give $a^j,b^j$ degree $j$, give $U_j,V_j$ degree $j+1$, and give $W$ degree $2$. This is a grading generated in degree one, including $W$ by conciseness. The grading therefore identifies the graded algebra with the associated radical grading. Its highest nonzero degree is at most eight, so $J^9=0$. Choose $M_b$ so that the total dimension is even. The choice depends only on the format and the character, so the two endpoints use the same lengths. One can also prescribe odd total dimension when needed below.

### Recovering the input roles

For degree-one elements the cubing map into degree three has kernel $E=U_0\oplus V_0$. Indeed the ring is commutative in characteristic three, so the cube of $\alpha a+\gamma b+u+v$ is exactly $\alpha^3a^3+\gamma^3b^3$, and the two powers are independent. This makes $E$ intrinsic to the graded algebra. The ideal generated by $E$ contains all input-tower spaces and $W=E^2$. The quotient by that ideal consists of the two scalar branches and the unit. Its degree-one axes are intrinsic and distinguished. Their squares are independent and their mutual product is zero, so a graded isomorphism on their span must be monomial. Their distinct nilpotence lengths then forbid swapping them. Thus an algebra isomorphism acts on the two distinguished quotient lines by scalars $\alpha,\gamma$.

The product of $a$ with $E$, followed by the quotient of grade two by $W$, has kernel $V_0$. This condition is independent of the lift of the $a$-line to degree one, since replacing the lift adds a product with $E$ lying in $W$. The product of $b$ similarly recovers $U_0$. Therefore every algebra isomorphism induces maps $g_U,g_V,g_W$ on the two input roles and on $W=E^2$, and they satisfy the original tensor equation. Conversely each tensor transporter extends by using $g_U,g_V$ on each corresponding tower copy, $g_W$ on $W$, and the identity on the scalar branches.

### Exact determinant

On each grade $j\ge2$, the part generated by $E$ is invariant. Its quotient consists of the visible scalar powers $a^j,b^j$, scaled by $\alpha^j,\gamma^j$ respectively. In degree two, its subspace $W=E^2$ is invariant, and the two tower spaces modulo that subspace are scaled copies $\alpha g_U,\gamma g_V$. This follows by replacing $a$ or $b$ by its scalar lift plus an element of $E$. In degree $j\ge3$, the tower maps on the nonzero copies are $\alpha^{j-1}g_U,\gamma^{j-1}g_V$. For example expand the product of $j-1$ copies of the induced $\alpha a+e$ and one copy of $g_U(u)$, with $e\in E$. Any extra term contains at least two $E$ factors, whose product is in $W$ and is killed by every further radical factor. The tower action is exactly the pure marker term. All these actions are on associated grades, so higher filtration changes from arbitrary algebra automorphisms do not affect the determinant.

It follows that every algebra isomorphism has determinant
$$
 (\det g_U)^{k_U}(\det g_V)^{k_V}\det g_W \cdot
 \alpha^{\binom{M_a}{2}+(\dim U)\binom{k_U}{2}}
 \gamma^{\binom{M_b}{2}+(\dim V)\binom{k_V}{2}} .
$$
All four binomial numbers occurring in the scalar exponents are even: the lengths are $4,5,8,9$. Thus the scalar factors are $1$, and the determinant is exactly (1). The coefficientwise lift realizes every value in the original transporter character set. This proves exact reflection for all maps, including isomorphisms that do not respect the displayed splitting.

### Raw support and the trivial character

Contraction supports are intrinsic and computable by linear algebra. A mismatch of support dimensions gives a fixed no-instance. Compact the two sides to their supports for ordinary comparison. In modes of full support, use their raw bases, retaining their determinant constraints. If an untested mode is compacted, its determinant is irrelevant. If a tested mode has an unused direction, then a reflection on that complement changes only its determinant sign and stabilizes the tensor. The character test on that branch is exactly ordinary TI. Zero tensors in matching formats can be decided directly. The trivial character is also ordinary TI.

For an entirely ordinary branch, use $A(T)$ on the concise compaction with any nontrivial choice of weights, starting with odd total dimension. Add one extra radical direction $Fc$, with $cJ=c^2=0$ and with the unit acting identically on $c$. Its new determinant can be either sign by scaling $c$, and the algebra now has even dimension. To check reflection, the original algebra's radical annihilator lies in $J^2$. Indeed, multiplying a potential annihilator by $a$ detects the coefficient of $a$ and the $U_0$ part independently in degree two. Multiplication by $b$ likewise detects the coefficient of $b$ and the $V_0$ part. In the enlarged algebra, the quotient of the radical annihilator by its intersection with $J^2$ is exactly one-dimensional, represented by $c$. Every complement of that intersection in the annihilator can be carried to $Fc$ by a shear fixing the original algebra, since both $c$ and its added correction kill the radical. Hence after an isomorphism one can quotient by the identified line and recover ordinary isomorphism of the original $A(T)$ tables. Conversely every ordinary isomorphism extends, and its sign can be corrected on the extra direction.

### Equivalence of collapses

The construction is linear in side lengths, with bounded towers and bounded radical length. It produces valid inputs in $\mathsf C$. The class is polynomial-time recognizable on arithmetic tables: check multiplication and the unit, compute the nilradical as the stable kernel of the $\mathbb F_3$-linear cube map, test the one-dimensional residue condition, and compute $J^9$. A unital associative ring isomorphism here is automatically $\mathbb F_3$-linear.

Ordinary isomorphism on these rings is the same as ordinary isomorphism of their multiplication tensors. Indeed an isotopy $C(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$ has the form $(L_u\phi,R_v\phi,L_uR_v\phi)$, with $\phi$ an algebra isomorphism and $u,v$ units, by substituting the two identities into the isotopy equation. In a scalar-residue local algebra of even dimension, multiplication by a unit has determinant $1$ on either side. A unit acts on each radical grade by its scalar residue, and its total exponent is the even dimension. Hence every multiplication-tensor determinant triple is exactly $(\det\phi,\det\phi,\det\phi)$. This proves both $\mathsf C\le_m\mathsf{TI}$ and $\mathsf C^+\le_m\mathsf{TI}_{\rm SL}$, as well as the output-mode assertion. The construction with the orientation requirement ignored gives $\mathsf{TI}\le_m\mathsf C$.

The raw relative determinant triples of any tensor comparison form an empty set or a coset of a subgroup of $\{\pm1\}^3$. The identity is in a nonempty coset exactly when every character image contains $1$: if the coset misses the identity, linear separation over $\mathbb F_2$ gives a character trivial on the subgroup and nontrivial on the coset. The trivial character detects the empty case. Thus eight independent character comparisons from the same input suffice, and each becomes an instance of $\mathsf C^+$ by the construction. If $\mathsf C^+\le_m\mathsf C$, turn each of these eight into ordinary TI and pack the resulting ordinary conjunction into one ordinary instance via the constant-signature tensor-system compiler. Conversely, if $\mathsf{TI}_{\rm SL}\le_m\mathsf{TI}$, compose the reductions from $\mathsf C^+$ through TI to $\mathsf C$. The conjunction here is on ordinary TI instances. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

The longstanding ordinary TI-completeness of commutative associative algebra isomorphism supplies context. The two different scalar marker branches and determinant-calibrated towers give the orientation transfer in the present fixed local class. [Grochow and Qiao, Tensor Isomorphism-Completeness, Corollary 6.5](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

## 2. An unpointed involutory quandle

An alternating bracket $\beta:X\times X\to Z$ is assumed to have spanning image and zero common radical. Its compact quandle has underlying set $X\oplus Z$ and binary operation
$$
 (x,a)\star(y,b)=(-x-y,\ a+\beta(x,y)).
\tag{2}
$$
Coordinates are supplied for computing operations and ordering elements. The zero element is not a marked point for isomorphism. An operation defined by (2) is idempotent and every right translation $R_y$ is an involution, since $\beta(-x-y,y)=-\beta(x,y)$. It is right self-distributive. On $X$ both sides of the identity have value $x+y-z$. In the central coordinate, applying $R_z$ after $(x,a)\star(y,b)$ adds $\beta(x,y)-\beta(x,z)-\beta(y,z)$. Applying $R_z$ first to both arguments adds $\beta(x,y)+2\beta(x,z)-\beta(y,z)$, the same in characteristic three.

On the same set define the group
$$
 (x,a)\cdot(y,b)=(x+y,a+b+\beta(x,y)).
$$
It has exponent three, is of class two, and is special for the stated rank conditions. Its commutator is $2\beta$, which spans $Z$. Let $\tau_g$ denote right multiplication by $g$. In (2), $R_0(x,a)=(-x,a)$, and $R_0R_y=\tau_{(y,0)}$. Thus the displacement group, generated by the products $R_yR_z^{-1}$, equals the group of all right translations of this group. In one direction every such product is a composition of the translations $R_0R_y$ and their inverses. In the other direction, their commutators give every central translation, and the entries $(y,0)$ give every first coordinate. The displacement group acts regularly. This proves connectedness and identifies the group action intrinsically from the single unpointed quandle operation.

Any quandle isomorphism can be composed with a displacement translation of the target to send zero to zero. Such a translation is even, since all its cycles have length one or three. A zero-preserving quandle isomorphism conjugates $\tau_g$ to $\tau_{\psi(g)}$, because these are the unique displacement maps sending zero to the indicated elements. It is therefore a group isomorphism. It also conjugates $R_0$ to $R_0'$. The Baer addition makes the group isomorphism linear on $X\oplus Z$. In the displayed splitting a group isomorphism has matrix of the form $(x,a)\mapsto(gx,ha+bx)$, where $g,h$ preserve the bracket. Commuting with the map that negates $X$ while fixing $Z$ gives $b=0$. Conversely each pair $g,h$ preserving the bracket defines a quandle isomorphism by the block-diagonal map, as (2) verifies directly. Therefore the unpointed quandle comparison has an isomorphism of either prescribed sign exactly when the original compact special-group comparison has one of that sign. A central shear on the group side has determinant one.

For a linear map on $\mathbb F_3^D$, its permutation sign is its determinant as a sign. Transvections act by cycles of odd length, and negating a single coordinate exchanges $3^{D-1}$ pairs. These transformations generate the general linear group. This justifies the sign statement for the compact group and for the affine quandle maps, with the coordinate orders on both endpoints. The regular displacement translations add no further sign choices. Consequently the ordinary and even quandle problems are respectively many-one equivalent to the ordinary and even special-group problems, simply by rewriting the compact formula. The parameterization of the constructed quandles uses the stipulated bracket data; this proves no assertion about recovering such parameters from arbitrary operation tables or deciding quandle membership from arbitrary presentations. [Hulpke, Stanovský and Vojtěchovský, Connected quandles and transitive groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2249)

The proof in [Oriented compact groups](OrientedGroups.md) gives statement 4 equivalent to statement 1. Its ordinary comparison is TI-complete by the alternating form/Baer correspondence. For the oriented direction a bracket gives the local algebra $F1\oplus X\oplus Z$ whose only radical product is $\beta$; the algebra determinant is $\det g\det h$. In the other direction the weighted algebra construction supplies one oriented algebra query per determinant character, and the four-marker alternating bimap with two unit-line pairings transports the algebra determinant exactly to the corresponding compact special group. This use of a finite list concerns only the final ordinary-TI conjunction for the proposed collapse. [Baer, Groups with Abelian Central Quotient Group](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1938-1501972-1) · [Grochow and Qiao, Tensor Isomorphism-Completeness](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

The displayed equivalences concern specific compact presentation models over $\mathbb F_3$. In particular, the size of a quandle or group here may be exponential in the number of structure constants. No implication to tractability of unrestricted group or quandle isomorphism is used. The proofs are informal. Publication priority for the new transfers is unverified.

Finite controls checked 72 local ring tables, 2,955,536 basis associativity triples, and 83,152 multiplication identities for explicit weighted lifts. They also verified all 19,683 self-distributivity triples on the 27-element quandle and found its 27 regular displacement permutations, all even. These tests supplement the written proofs.
