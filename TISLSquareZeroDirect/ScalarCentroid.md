# The unresolved sign already has scalar centroid

We work over \(F=\mathbb F_3\). Let \(H\) denote a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction from determinant-one to ordinary dense tensor isomorphism over \(F\).

**Theorem.** In the square-zero comparison of the preceding run, it suffices to solve the determinant-one promise problem for algebras whose radical bimap has centroid **exactly \(F\)**. The usual even first layer and odd second layer, zero common radical, and spanned output all remain in force. The bimap may also be required to contain a split nonsingular scalar slice. There is a deterministic polynomial-time exact-sign reduction to this class from the weighted character test on arbitrary three-tensors. Thus this scalar-centroid promise gives an equivalent missing lemma for \(H\).

The proof makes a small change to the earlier weighted symmetric cube-zero construction and verifies that it preserves all fixes and determinant calculations. ## 1. Generate the output by cross-role products

Recall the earlier roles of the main space \(M\): the variables, a pin \(e\), and pins \(f_0,\ldots,f_{s-1}\). The symmetric map \(\eta:M\times M\to W\) realizes the source bimap, its weighted copies and any linear pins. The copy maps for a variable role are its products with another pin. There are special output coordinates \(z_*,z_j\), where
\[
 \eta(e,e)=\eta(e,f_j)=z_*,\qquad
 \eta(f_j,f_j)=z_j .
\]
Choose \(s\ge5\) in the required parity class for the old dimension \(D=\dim M\), and add the products
\[
      \eta(f_{j+1},f_{j+2})=z_j,\qquad 0\le j<s ,
\]
with indices modulo \(s\) and symmetric partners. The unordered pin pairs are distinct and have not been used for copy or linear role maps. Each pair omits \(f_j\). If needed for the output dimension adjustment, use one of the remaining nonadjacent pin pairs for a new output coordinate. As before choose \(D\) odd and \(\dim W\) even. The prescribed parity of \(s\) and the presence of an unused pair supply both conditions.

Each output coordinate is now generated by cross-role products. The copy outputs use one variable and one pin. The special \(z_*\) uses \(e,f_j\), and the new products generate the \(z_j\). This property is in addition to the original zero common radical. Indeed pairing a radical vector with \(e\) again first kills its variable-role coordinates using the distinct degree-one copies. Then the \(z_j\)-coordinate of pairing with \(f_j\) detects its coefficient at \(f_j\), since the new \(z_j\) cross pair omits \(f_j\). The remaining \(e\)-coordinate is detected by the \(z_*\)-pairing with \(e\).

The original pin equalities still force one scalar \(\tau\) on all pins for any role-preserving isomorphism. Each new relation compares one output of scale \(\tau^2\) with a product whose scale is also \(\tau^2\). Every actual variable-role transporter still lifts with \(\tau=1\). Thus the exact twisted determinant-image calculation on \(W\) remains unchanged. In particular the modification neither introduces spurious variable transporters nor eliminates any of the old lifts.

## 2. Recover the same roles and the same determinant

Use the old marker extension \(X=M\oplus M^*\oplus\bigoplus S_{ac}\) with output \(Z=W\oplus Z_0\), where the marked edge spaces \(S_{ac}\) form a connected cycle of two-coordinate trace forms. Choose the edge multiplicities superincreasing and even. The rank separation works exactly as before: low-rank scalar forms are the annihilator of \(Z_0\), their common radical gives \(M^*\oplus\bigoplus S_{ac}\), and the marker forms there recover \(M^*\) and each marked edge by rank. Evaluations against \(M^*\) recover the original roles in the \(M\) quotient. All these steps either use the nondegenerate marker forms or use only the zero common radical of \(\eta\), which was preserved.

The earlier proof then forces the marker-line scalars to coincide along the cycle and forces each edge transporter to have regular determinant one. The induced action on \(M^*\) is the inverse dual action on \(M\) times the common marker scalar. Their determinants cancel up to that scalar to the power \(\dim M\), and the marker scalar on \(Z_0\) cancels the remaining sign by the original parity choice. Thus the total \(X\oplus Z\) sign recovers the weighted variable sign exactly. Increasing the number of pins by a constant and taking even multiples of the rank weights keeps \(\dim X+\dim Z\) linear in the source side lengths. The square-zero matrix space derived from the unital table inherits the same exact sign. For a split nonsingular scalar slice take the functional zero on \(W\) and one on every marker. Evaluation on \(M\oplus M^*\) is hyperbolic. On each trace block the sum of its two marked forms is nonsingular, and pairing identical copies in an even multiplicity makes it split: its determinant is square and its dimension divisible by four.

## 3. Centroid computation

Let \((P,Q)\) be a centroid element of the modified bimap \(\beta:X\times X\to Z\). The spaces \(M\oplus M^*\) and the individual edge spaces are pairwise orthogonal and each has zero common radical on its own output. Thus \(P\) preserves each. Indeed if \(u,v\) are in distinct members of the decomposition, then \(\beta(Pu,v)=Q\beta(u,v)=0\). Varying \(v\) rules out the component of \(Pu\) in that member. As the product on an edge spans its two marker lines, \(Q\) preserves each edge plane. Intersecting adjacent edge planes shows that it preserves every individual marker line. Let its two scalars on a fixed edge be \(\alpha_a,\alpha_c\). Nondegeneracy of the first trace form forces \(P\) on that edge to be \(\alpha_a I\), and the second form implies \(\alpha_c=\alpha_a\). The cycle forces a single \(\alpha\) on \(Z_0\) and on all edge spaces.

Subtract \((\alpha I,\alpha I)\). The new \(Q\) kills \(Z_0\) and the new \(P\) kills the edges. Because \(\beta(M^*,M)\subseteq Z_0\), the centroid identities show that \(P\) kills \(M^*\): for \(p\in M^*\) pair \(Pp\) with every element of \(M\oplus M^*\) and use zero common radical there. Similarly \(\beta(Pm,p)=0\) for all \(p\in M^*\), so \(P(M)\subseteq M^*\). On \(M\times M\) the equality is now
\[
       Q\eta(m,m')=\beta(Pm,m')\in Z_0 .
\]
Write \(Q\) on \(W\) in its marker coordinates. The coordinate at the marker for role \(M_i\) is a functional \(\lambda_i\) on \(W\). The right-hand side at that marker is the evaluation of the \(M_i^*\)-component of \(Pm\) against \(m'_i\). It vanishes when \(m'_i=0\). The left-hand side is symmetric, so it also vanishes when \(m_i=0\). In particular \(\lambda_i\eta\) vanishes on every cross-role pair. These pairs span \(W\) by the modification, so every \(\lambda_i=0\). Unused markers have zero coordinates on the right from the outset. Therefore \(Q=0\), and zero common radical now gives \(P=0\).

It follows that the original centroid is precisely the common scalar field \(F\). The proof computes and certifies the property in the actual dense representation. There is one primitive factor, and its relative dimensions equal the absolute dimensions. In particular an improvement that only handles a large number of small centroid factors cannot close all these instances.

To finish the degree implication, apply the construction to each of the seven nontrivial ternary determinant characters and conjoin their ordinary outputs under the hypothetical scalar-centroid square-zero lemma. The old character separation argument works unchanged, including empty ordinary transporters. The converse is the earlier exact multiplication-tensor conversion on this narrower class. All conclusions are deterministic many-one reductions with one ordinary output where stated.

## A boundary for normalized trace-commutator tests

For any nonsingular scalar slice \(B_0\) of this constructed carrier, write \(S_\lambda=B_0^{-1}B_\lambda\) for the operators of all scalar slices. On \(M\oplus M^*\), the matrix of \(B_\lambda\) has the form
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}H_\lambda&D_\lambda\\D_\lambda&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
where \(D_\lambda\) is scalar on each role. The block \(D_0\) is invertible, since a nonzero vector in its kernel on the dual side would be a radical vector. Solving the two block equations shows that \(S_\lambda\) is lower block triangular, with both diagonal blocks equal to \(D_0^{-1}D_\lambda\). These diagonal blocks commute for different \(\lambda\). On the trace edge spaces the \(S_\lambda\) are multiplications by field elements and commute. Therefore every commutator of two normalized slices is strictly lower on \(M\oplus M^*\) and zero on all edges. The product of any two such commutators is zero.

It follows that the trace pairing \((\lambda,\mu)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}([S_{\nu_1},S_{\nu_2}][S_\lambda,S_\mu])\) is identically zero on the entire output dual, for any two probes \(\nu_1,\nu_2\). The ambient alternating form \(B_0[S_{\nu_1},S_{\nu_2}]\) also has the full dual side and every edge space in its radical. Thus using these probe forms to force logarithmic radical widths cannot cover the exact scalar-centroid carrier above, even though it can give one-query reductions on other inputs.
