# Rural postman tours with prescribed holonomy

A weighted undirected Rural Postman instance can constrain the total binary voltage of its tour. If the required edges form $r$ connected regions with trivial interior holonomy and the quotient cycle voltages have rank $\rho$, a local metric certificate guarantees an optimum with at most $2r-2+\rho$ boundary crossings. When the metric inequalities have slack, the same certificate bounds the extra cost of a compressed tour. Once the crossings are fixed, minimum-weight $T$-joins solve the remaining routing inside the regions.

## Model and connection with Grochow

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a connected loopless undirected multigraph with nonnegative rational edge weights and voltages $g_e\in\mathbb F_2^k$. The objective is a minimum-cost closed walk that traverses every required edge and has prescribed total voltage $\tau\in\mathbb F_2^k$. A walk adds the voltage of every edge occurrence, in either direction. Its lift starting at sheet $0$ ends at sheet $\tau$.

Partition $V$ into $r\ge 2$ nonempty parts $P_1,\ldots,P_r$. All required edges lie within the parts, and the required edges in each part contain a spanning tree of that part. For a singleton this tree is empty. The walk must visit every part, including such singletons. Assume the voltages sum to zero on every cycle inside a part.

Choose an interior spanning tree and root in each part and let $b_v$ be the voltage of the tree path from its root to $v$. Replacing $g_{uv}$ by $g_{uv}+b_u+b_v$ preserves the voltage of any closed walk. Check that the new voltage is zero on every interior edge. This is equivalent to the interior cycle condition and places all residual voltages on cross edges. Grochow and Tucker-Foltz use graph covers to interpret Unique Games as maximum sections and relate principal covers to cohomology localization. Here their cover viewpoint puts the holonomy constraint on the crossings of a single route. Gross and Tucker developed the earlier voltage-graph and cycle-basis machinery. [Grochow and Tucker-Foltz](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06800), [Gross and Tucker](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-6632.1979.tb32796.x).

## Crossing theorem

A boundary vertex is incident to a cross edge. Give each boundary vertex a nonnegative price $p_v$ such that for every pair $u,v$ in the same part, their interior shortest-path distance is at most $p_u+p_v$. The distance to an interior anchor is one choice. Half the diameter of the boundary metric is another uniform choice. For a designated set $H$ of cross edges to protect from deletion, put $\delta_e=\max(0,p_u+p_v-w_e)$ for every cross edge $e=uv$ outside $H$.

In the quotient multigraph on the parts, a cross edge between $P_i$ and $P_j$ has incidence column $a_e=e_i+e_j\in\mathbb F_2^r$ and voltage column $g_e$ after the change of coordinates. Write $A$ and $L$ for the matrices of incidence and voltage columns. Define $\rho=\operatorname{rank}(L|_{\ker A})$, the rank of the voltage map on the quotient cycle space. The stacked matrix has rank $r-1+\rho$. Indeed, projection of its column span onto the incidence coordinates has image $\operatorname{im} A$ of dimension $r-1$ by connectivity, and kernel $\{0\}\times L(\ker A)$. In particular $\rho\le k$.

**Theorem.** If a feasible tour exists, there is a feasible tour with at most $B=2r-2+\rho+2|H|$ cross-edge occurrences and cost at most $\mathrm{OPT}+2\sum_{e\notin H}\delta_e$, where the sum is over cross edges. If every unprotected crossing edge has $w_e\ge p_u+p_v$, an optimum satisfies the crossing bound.

**Proof.** A feasible tour gives a connected spanning multigraph of edge occurrences in which every degree is even. Whenever one edge has at least three copies, remove two. A copy remains, so support and required traversals are preserved. Degree parities and total voltage are unchanged, and cost cannot increase. There are finitely many tours as occurrence multisets after this normalization, so an optimum exists whenever the instance is feasible. Start with a normalized optimum.

Reserve one occurrence for each edge of a spanning tree of its used quotient. Reserve all occurrences of edges in $H$ as well. This reserves at most $r-1+2|H|$ crossings. Take the joint columns $(a_e,g_e)$ for all unreserved crossing occurrences. Choose an occurrence basis for their span, with at most $r-1+\rho$ columns. Express the sum of all unreserved columns as a sum of a subset of the basis, keep that subset, and delete all other unreserved occurrences. The sum of the deleted columns is zero. The incidence coordinates imply that each part loses an even number of crossing endpoints, including repeated endpoints. The voltage coordinates imply that the deleted occurrences have total voltage zero.

Pair the deleted endpoints within each part and add a shortest interior path for each pair. Pairing a repeated endpoint to itself can use the empty path. The new paths restore every physical vertex's former degree parity and contribute zero voltage. The mandatory trees connect the parts internally and the reserved quotient tree connects them to one another. Thus an Euler tour of the new support visits every part and satisfies the traversal and voltage requirements. The total path cost is at most the sum of prices of the deleted endpoints. Since each unprotected edge initially had at most two occurrences, the increase is at most $2\sum_{e\notin H}\delta_e$. The retained crossings number at most $(r-1+2|H|)+(r-1+\rho)$.

## Exact completion and enumeration

For a candidate crossing multiset, start with it and one copy of every required edge. Check that the candidate connects the quotient and that its total voltage is $\tau$. Let $T_i$ be the vertices with odd current degree in $P_i$. If $|T_i|$ is odd, no completion is possible. Otherwise add a minimum-weight $T_i$-join using the interior edges. This can be found by minimum-weight perfect matching of the odd vertices at shortest-path distances, followed by expanding matched pairs into paths. Every valid completion corrects these degree parities, and every interior addition contributes zero voltage. Connectivity already follows from the mandatory trees and the selected quotient edges. Consequently these independent $T_i$-joins give the least-cost completion, and an Euler traversal produces the tour. [Edmonds and Johnson](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01580113).

With $m$ physical cross edges, enumerate multisets of at most $B$ crossings with every physical edge multiplicity at most two. The proof's compressed tour can be normalized again if necessary. The total time is at most $(m+1)^B$ times a polynomial in input length, including weight bit lengths. This gives an exact deterministic XP algorithm in $r+\rho+|H|$ under zero unprotected slack. More generally, the best completed tour has cost at most $\mathrm{OPT}+2\sum_{e\notin H}\delta_e$. For a fixed $H$, a linear program minimizes the certified additive term using the same-part distance inequalities and $\delta_e\ge p_u+p_v-w_e$, with nonnegative variables.

A further parameter gives FPT. Let $p$ be the number of distinct physical boundary vertices. Use a quotient spanning tree to change coordinates once more, adding the same vector to every vertex in a given part. The tree voltages become zero, and every other cross voltage is the voltage of its fundamental cycle. Thus at most $2^\rho$ different voltages appear. For every exact unordered endpoint pair and resulting voltage keep only the cheapest cross edge. Substitution preserves weight or improves it while keeping the same boundary parity, voltage, and crossing count. Renormalize if a replacement produces more than two copies. At most $\binom p2 2^\rho$ cross edges remain, so enumeration with the bound established before preprocessing is fixed-parameter tractable in $p+\rho+|H|+r$.

Exact endpoint identities matter to this reduction. Take two unit-weight required edges $ab$ and $cd$, cross edges $ac$ of cost $1$ and $bd$ of cost $1.1$, all voltages zero, and price $1/2$ at each vertex. All cross edges satisfy the metric certificate. Using both crossings gives optimum $4.1$. Keeping only the cheapest edge for their common voltage and price forces a tour of cost $6$, since both required edges must be doubled.

## Controls and relation to prior work

- **The metric hypothesis matters.** Take two three-leaf stars with every spoke required and of weight $2$. Join corresponding vertices by four cross edges of weight $1$, with all voltages zero. One copy of each spoke and all four crossings gives optimum $16$. A limit of two crossings gives optimum $18$, because two crossings can cancel at most two of the four odd vertices in each star and the remaining repair costs at least $2$ per star. Using two leaf-to-leaf crossings attains that value.
- **Rank dependence is necessary.** Take parts consisting of one unit-weight required edge each. Connect one endpoint of each with five parallel edges of cost $3$, carrying voltages $0,e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4$. Prices of $1/2$ satisfy every crossing inequality. To attain target $e_1+e_2+e_3+e_4$, a route must use each basis crossing an odd number of times. Four crossings and twice each required edge give optimum $16$. The analogous even-dimensional family forces a number of crossings linear in $\rho$.
- **Finite checks.** A separate enumeration checked normalized edge multiplicities on 192 small weighted instances with two to four two-vertex parts, seven physical cross edges, and up to three voltage coordinates. All 533 feasible zero-slack target-instance pairs had equal full and restricted optima, and the 49 positive-slack pairs satisfied the additive bound. Complete enumerations also checked both explicit controls.

For a high-treewidth family with positive weights, use two arbitrarily large unit-weight cliques, with zero interior voltages and a required spanning tree in each. Join the cliques by unit-weight cross edges. Every boundary vertex can use price $1/2$. The crossing bound depends on the voltage rank even as the original treewidth grows.

Ordinary Rural Postman already has a randomized $O^*(2^r)$ algorithm for polynomially bounded integer budgets. Approximate data reduction for the ordinary problem uses odd vertices and required components and has been tested on snow-plowing networks. On surface graphs, binary homology localization has algorithms parameterized by genus. The contribution here is the combination of joint incidence and voltage dependence with local cost control for a connected tour traversing designated edges, on a graph with no surface embedding assumed. The crossing bound concerns a tour's support and multiplicities. The enumeration may still depend on all physical crossings. Priority for the combined statement remains open. The theorem has a mathematical proof and finite controls. Lean formalization has not been done.
