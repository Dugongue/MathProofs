# Near-exact metric TSP requires exponential-size shared linear formulations

A fixed LP relaxation containing every tour needs exponentially many inequalities to approximate all $N$-city metrics with distances in $\{1,2\}$ within $1+c/N$ for any fixed $c>0$ and sufficiently large $N$. For error $\varepsilon_N\to0$, the lower bound is $2^{\Omega(\min\{N,1/\varepsilon_N\})}$. The same argument gives an inverse-linear accuracy lower bound for a family of Euclidean instances and, using a separate preprint theorem about real PSD rank, for semidefinite formulations. The reduction uses metric objectives to expose *positively shifted* perfect-matching slacks. This keeps the lower bound under nearly exact approximation.

A sufficient construction links this result to Grochow's work. Affine guards and compact minimum-of-affine leaves, including assignment leaves, compile to one LP relaxation on a bounded polyhedral cost domain. The construction uses the full affine data of each leaf. It only requires approximation on the bounded domain. Grochow's original transfer starts with a monotone projection from the permanent and uses the Birkhoff polytope to extend a Newton polytope. Here the projected assignment data are combined with the polar of each guard region. [Grochow, Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 4.2](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v013a018/)

## A formulation from guarded value certificates on a bounded domain

Let $P$ be a nonempty polytope, and let $\operatorname{opt}(w)=\min_{x\in P}w\cdot x$. Let $W\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be a nonempty polytope of objective inputs. Suppose a finite binary tree compares affine functions of $w$ and, on a reachable leaf $\ell$, returns

$$
g_\ell(w)=\min_{(a,b)\in S_\ell}(w\cdot a+b),
$$

where $S_\ell\subseteq\mathbb R^{d+1}$ is a nonempty polytope with a formulation of $s_\ell$ inequalities. Along leaf $\ell$ write the restrictions comprising its branch and the domain $W$ as $B_\ell w\le d_\ell$, interpreting selected rows strictly. Let $r_\ell$ count these rows. Assume that for all $w\in W$, the value $g(w)$ returned by the tree satisfies

$$
\operatorname{opt}(w)\le g(w)\le\alpha\operatorname{opt}(w),\qquad \alpha\ge1.
$$

**Theorem.** One can construct a polyhedron $R\supseteq P$ with an extended formulation using at most $\sum_\ell(s_\ell+r_\ell+1)$ inequalities such that, for all $w\in W$,

$$
g(w)/\alpha\ \le\ \min_{x\in R} w\cdot x\ \le\ \operatorname{opt}(w).
$$

In particular, any certified feasible tour cost satisfying the factor guarantee can supply the value $g(w)$ if it has the stated leaf representation. Equalities in the lift are free.

**Proof.** For the region $W_\ell$ of leaf $\ell$, homogenize its closure. The resulting closed cone is

$$
K_\ell=\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{cone}\{(w,1):w\in W_\ell\}
      =\{(w,t):B_\ell w\le t d_\ell,\ t\ge0\}.
$$

For positive $t$, divide by $t$. The weak inequalities describe the closure because mixing any weakly feasible point with a point in the actual nonempty region makes every designated strict inequality strict. At $t=0$, the constraints force $w=0$ because the rows for the bounded nonempty polytope $W$ admit no nonzero recession direction. Define the positive dual by $K_\ell^*=\{v \mid \langle k,v\rangle\ge0\text{ for all }k\in K_\ell\}$ and form

$$
R=\{x:(x,0)\in S_\ell/\alpha+K_\ell^*\text{ for all reachable }\ell\}.
$$

The sum $S_\ell/\alpha+K_\ell^*$ is closed because the first summand is compact. Its linear minimization over the sum has finite value for objectives in $K_\ell$, where the value is $\min_{u\in S_\ell/\alpha}\langle k,u\rangle$. The value is unbounded below in every direction outside $K_\ell$ by the bipolar theorem. Thus separation characterizes the sum by exactly the lower supporting inequalities in $K_\ell$.

For $x\in P$ and $w\in W_\ell$, we have $w\cdot x\ge\operatorname{opt}(w)\ge g_\ell(w)/\alpha$. By positive homogeneity and continuity the lower supporting inequalities also hold on $K_\ell$. Hence $P\subseteq R$. For each $w\in W$, using the leaf reached gives $w\cdot x\ge g(w)/\alpha$ for all $x\in R$. The minimum is finite and attained by polyhedrality and the existence of points of $P$. It is at most $\operatorname{opt}(w)$ by containment.

By Farkas' lemma $K_\ell^*$ is generated by the negatives of the rows defining $K_\ell$. Use one nonnegative multiplier per row, a scaled copy of the formulation of $S_\ell$, and an equality for $(x,0)$ as the sum of the two terms. Share $x$ across all leaves. This proves the inequality count. $\square$

For a leaf minimizing over a bipartite assignment polytope $F_\ell$ on $m_\ell$ jobs and positions with affine costs $A_\ell w+b_\ell$, the lift of the coefficient-intercept polytope has at most $m_\ell^2$ inequalities. It is the affine image $X\mapsto(A_\ell^{\mathsf T}X,\langle b_\ell,X\rangle)$ of $F_\ell$. An affine output correction translates this image. Forbidding pairs selects a face by equality constraints. The same construction accepts any leaf with a supplied compact formulation for $S_\ell$. In particular, min-plus circuits with affine inputs can use the support-function formulation of the circuit after reversing the signs of its inputs and output. Apply it to the augmented objective $(w,t)$, so offsets enter as coefficients of $t$. [Grochow, proof of Theorem 4.2](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v013a018/v013a018.pdf) · [Hertrich–Loho, support functions and extended formulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03006)

## An explicit family of hard metric objectives

**Theorem.** Fix $0<\rho<1$ and $0<\kappa\le1$. For every sufficiently large integer $N$, there is a family of metrics with distances in $\{1,1+\kappa\}$ for which the following holds. If $Q$ contains the $N$-city tour polytope and approximates each of those objectives in the sense that

$$
\min_{x\in Q}c\cdot x\ \ge\ \operatorname{opt}(c)\,\frac{N+\kappa(1-\rho)}{N+\kappa},
$$

then any LP formulation of $Q$ has $2^{\Omega(N)}$ inequalities. The complexity claim allows arbitrary real coefficients and arbitrary extra variables. In particular $\kappa=1$ already proves the result for every fixed $0<c<1$, by choosing a fixed $\rho$ with $c<\rho<1$ and taking $N$ sufficiently large.

**Host graph.** Let $n=2\lfloor N/6\rfloor$, so $n$ is even and $0\le N-3n\le5$. On $3n$ cities use left and right cliques $L=\{L_i\}$ and $R=\{R_i\}$, and middle vertices $D_i$ joined only to $L_i$ and $R_i$. If necessary, subdivide $L_1D_1$ with $N-3n$ new cities. Call the resulting graph $H$. Every tour of $H$ uses the forced paths through the middle and subdivision vertices. Each of $L$ and $R$ therefore has exactly one clique edge at each label in the tour, giving a left and a right perfect matching. Conversely any left perfect matching $M$ extends to a tour $T_M$ of $H$. List and orient its pairs $(a_1,b_1),\ldots,(a_{n/2},b_{n/2})$. In the right clique join $R_{b_j}$ to $R_{a_{j+1}}$ cyclically. The matching edges and forced paths now form one tour. Fix this extension for each $M$ once, independently of the objective. This is Yannakakis's face construction with subdivision for padding. [Yannakakis, proof of Theorem 2](https://www.tcs.tifr.res.in/~prahladh/teaching/2011-12/comm/papers/Yannakakis1991.pdf) · [Rothvoß, Corollary 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2369)

**Costs.** For each odd subset $U\subseteq[n]$, charge $1+\kappa$ on every edge absent from $H$ and every left clique edge in the cut $\delta_L(U)$. All other edges cost $1$. These positive symmetric distances satisfy the triangle inequality because $1+\kappa\le2$. A tour using an edge outside $H$ costs at least $N+\kappa$. A tour of $H$ also costs at least $N+\kappa$ because a perfect matching crosses any odd cut an odd number of times. A matching with exactly one crossing exists by pairing vertices within each side after removing one vertex per side. Its extension attains $N+\kappa$. For every column $M$ the cost is exactly

$$
c_U\cdot\chi^{T_M}=N+\kappa\,|M\cap\delta(U)|.
$$

**Rank transfer.** For each $U$, the inequality $c_U\cdot x\ge N+\kappa(1-\rho)$ is valid on $Q$ by the guarantee. On the fixed columns $\chi^{T_M}$ its slack is

$$
\kappa\bigl(|M\cap\delta(U)|-1+\rho\bigr).
$$

If an LP with $r$ inequalities projects to $Q$, choose a feasible lift of each fixed tour column. By LP duality any of these valid lower bounds is a nonnegative combination of formulation slacks plus a nonnegative constant. Hence the displayed matrix has a nonnegative factorization of rank at most $r+1$. The constant corresponds to one extra factor. Braun and Pokutta's shifted matching bound gives nonnegative rank $2^{\Omega(n)}$ for the displayed matrix, since positive scaling leaves rank unchanged. As $n=\Theta(N)$, the LP lower bound follows. This uses a fixed common projected relaxation across all objectives. [Braun–Pokutta, Section 2.2.3 and Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.6710) · [Braun–Pokutta–Roy, approximation-preserving formulation reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.04932)

**Guarded assignment consequence.** Every vector in $W=[1,2]^{\binom N2}$ is a metric cost vector. Apply the first theorem to any affine-guarded tree of assignment or other admissible leaves with an upper approximation guarantee on that box. The assembled relaxation $R$ contains every tour and has lower guarantee $\min_R w\cdot x\ge \operatorname{opt}(w)/\alpha$. For $\alpha\le(N+1)/(N+1-\rho)$ its inequality count, and therefore the total expanded formulation budget of the tree, is exponential. This counts all reachable leaves. A comparison bound on a single execution measures a different resource. The accuracy here is inverse-linear in $N$, while the deterministic metric algorithm of Karlin, Klein, and Oveis Gharan gives a constant approximation factor just below $3/2$. [Karlin–Klein–Oveis Gharan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06296)

## Amplifying the hard slack on the same two distances

Let $k\ge1$ and let $n\ge2$ be even with $3kn\le N$. Use $k$ left cliques $L_b$, each with $n$ labels, and $k$ sets of middle vertices $D_b$. Use a single right clique on all $kn$ right vertices. The paths from left through middle to right are defined as before within each block $b$. If $N>3kn$, add the remaining cities by subdividing one path in block $1$. Use the same odd set $U\subseteq[n]$ in each block. Charge $2$ for forbidden edges and for left clique edges crossing $U$ within their block, and $1$ elsewhere.

For any tour, let $t$ be the number of edges outside the host. Write $S_b$ for the union of the left and middle vertices of block $b$ and any subdivision vertices of that block. Let $t_b$ count forbidden incidences at $S_b$, counting an edge with both endpoints there twice. The only allowed edges across the boundary of $S_b$ are the $n$ final path edges to the right clique. If $t_b=0$, all forced paths are used, and the tour induces a perfect matching in $L_b$.

If $t_b=1$, the unique forbidden incidence is an edge crossing the boundary of $S_b$. A tour meets that boundary an even number of times. Since $n$ is even, at least one of the $n$ final path edges is omitted. Its endpoint in $D_b$ has a forbidden incidence, which must be the unique one in $S_b$. That middle vertex uses its other allowed edge, and every other middle or subdivision vertex uses both allowed edges. All left vertices still have their forced path edge, so the left clique again has a perfect matching. In either case the tour pays for at least one left crossing in block $b$.

The remaining blocks have $t_b\ge2$. As $\sum_b t_b\le2t$, at most $t$ blocks lack the guarantee. The tour has at least $k-t$ left crossings and costs at least $N+t+(k-t)=N+k$. This bound also holds when $t>k$. Conversely, prescribe a perfect matching with one crossing in each left clique. Across all blocks, list and orient the $kn/2$ pairs, then join consecutive pairs cyclically by right edges. This gives a host tour of cost $N+k$.

For the rank calculation, fix one tour column for each matching $M$ on $[n]$ by putting a copy of $M$ in every left clique and choosing a right matching by that construction. The row cost of the column is $N+k|M\cap\delta(U)|$. For fixed $0<\rho<1$, an approximation factor at most $(N+k)/(N+k(1-\rho))$ makes $c_U\cdot x\ge N+k(1-\rho)$ valid. Its slack is $k(|M\cap\delta(U)|-1+\rho)$, giving the same $2^{\Omega(n)}$ lower bound.

For an error sequence $\varepsilon_N>0$ with $\varepsilon_N\to0$, fix $\rho=1/2$. The factor is $1+k/(2N+k)$. Take

$$
k=\max\left(1,\left\lceil\frac{2\varepsilon_NN}{1-\varepsilon_N}\right\rceil\right),
\qquad n=2\left\lfloor\frac{N}{6k}\right\rfloor .
$$

Then $1+\varepsilon_N$ is within the factor, and $k\le1+4\varepsilon_NN$ eventually. Since $k=o(N)$, $n\ge N/(6k)$ eventually, which is $\Omega(\min\{N,1/\varepsilon_N\})$. The fixed-shift rank estimate forces at least $2^{\Omega(\min\{N,1/\varepsilon_N\})}$ inequalities. This proves the exponential bound on $\{1,2\}$ metrics for **every fixed $c>0$** at factor $1+c/N$, and applies to the total expanded budget of the bounded-box guarded optimizer. It also gives a superpolynomial lower bound whenever $1/\varepsilon_N$ grows faster than $\log N$.

Braun, Fiorini, Pokutta and Steurer already use the same definition of admissible metric objectives and give the Held–Karp relaxation as a compact approximation example. The present argument combines fixed-shift matching rank with the replicated two-distance host to obtain a bound in the near-exact regime. [Braun–Fiorini–Pokutta–Steurer, Examples 1 and 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.0957)


## Euclidean distances with the same inverse-linear scale

For $N=3n$ and even $n$, keep the same unsubdivided host. Let $e_i$ be the $i$-th unit vector in $\mathbb R^n$. For each odd $U$, choose $\sigma_i=1$ in $U$ and $\sigma_i=-1$ outside $U$. Place the cities at

$$
 L_i=(e_i,-1,\sigma_i/4),\qquad
 R_i=(e_i,1,0),\qquad
 D_i=(2e_i,0,0)
$$

in $\mathbb R^{n+2}$. The following entries are squared distances, with the condition on distinct vertices understood.

| Edge | Condition | Squared distance |
|---|---|---:|
| $L_iL_j$ | $i\ne j$, same sign | $2$ |
| $L_iL_j$ | $i\ne j$, opposite signs | $9/4$ |
| $R_iR_j$ | $i\ne j$ | $2$ |
| $D_iD_j$ | $i\ne j$ | $8$ |
| $L_iR_j$ | $i=j$ | $65/16$ |
| $L_iR_j$ | $i\ne j$ | $97/16$ |
| $L_iD_j$ | $i=j$ | $33/16$ |
| $L_iD_j$ | $i\ne j$ | $97/16$ |
| $R_iD_j$ | $i=j$ | $2$ |
| $R_iD_j$ | $i\ne j$ | $6$ |

Put $b=\sqrt2$, $\eta=\sqrt{33}/4-b$, $\gamma=3/2-b$, and $B=Nb+n\eta$. A host tour with left matching $M$ costs $B+\gamma |M\cap\delta(U)|$. An arbitrary tour with $k$ forbidden edges omits at most $2k$ edges $L_iD_i$. Each omission forces a forbidden incidence at $D_i$, and each forbidden edge provides at most two such incidences. Every forbidden edge costs at least $2$. Every other edge costs at least $b$, and each used $L_iD_i$ contributes another $\eta$. Hence the tour costs at least

$$
B+k(2-b-2\eta).
$$

For $k\ge1$ this is greater than $B+\gamma$. In fact $b<3/2$, $\eta=(1/16)/(\sqrt{33}/4+b)<1/32$, and $\gamma=(1/4)/(3/2+b)<1/8$. The optimum is exactly $B+\gamma$, achieved by a host tour extending a matching with one crossing. At tolerance $\gamma\rho$ the valid inequalities $c_U\cdot x\ge B+\gamma(1-\rho)$ have slack $\gamma(|M\cap\delta(U)|-1+\rho)$. The same rank transfer proves exponential LP size for the Euclidean factor $(B+\gamma)/(B+\gamma(1-\rho))=1+\Theta(1/N)$ on this sequence of city counts. The dimension grows with $N$. Arora's Euclidean approximation scheme concerns fixed-dimensional inputs. [Arora](https://doi.org/10.1145/290179.290180)

## Real semidefinite consequence and proof status

The basic, amplified, and Euclidean transfers also apply to real semidefinite lifts, using the claimed exponential rank theorem of the October 2026 preprint on positively shifted matching matrices as an input. If $Q$ is an affine image of an affine slice of the real PSD cone of order $r$, restrict the slice to the span of the ranges of all feasible matrices. A finite convex combination attains full rank on that span, giving strict feasibility in the compressed cone of order at most $r$. Each valid objective inequality has finite infimum. SDP strong duality gives its slack on a feasible lift as $\langle A_U,Y_M\rangle+\delta_U$ with $A_U,Y_M$ positive semidefinite and $\delta_U\ge0$. A scalar block factors the constant, so PSD rank of the shifted matrix is at most $r+1$. The cited preprint's fixed-$\rho$ theorem then gives $r=2^{\Omega(n)}$ with the same matching parameter as in the corresponding LP proof. This semidefinite corollary depends on the informal exponential result in that preprint. The associated Lean scope covers weaker statements. [Pinned shifted matching preprint](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Exponential-PSD-rank-of-positively-shifted-matching-matrices-October-5-2026/shifted-matching-psd.pdf) · [Lean scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/docs/126.md)

The closest exact semidefinite transfer is Kriebel's October 2026 note using the classical matching face. The new deductions here use *metric objective rows*, which retain the slack after a positive shift and therefore apply to approximation guarantees without taking a face of the relaxation. The guarded bounded-domain construction is independent of the rank results. The LP claims use proved published matching bounds and the informal reductions above. The literature comparison establishes scope, with publication priority unresolved. [Kriebel's exact TSP preprint](https://aleckriebel.com/research/exact-semidefinite-tsp/) · [Lee–Raghavendra–Steurer, exact SDP lower bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6317)

For a finite arithmetic check, take four matching labels, twelve cities, and all eight odd sets. The metric optimum was verified by the exact subset recurrence over the eleven cities other than a fixed starting city. Using edge costs reduced by $1$, initialize $D(\varnothing,\mathrm{start})=0$, update $D(S\cup\{j\},j)$ with $D(S,i)+c_{ij}-1$ for each unvisited $j$, and add the reduced cost of closing the tour. The minimum reduced cost was $1$ for each odd set. The three perfect matchings extended to tours and gave the claimed crossing costs in all 24 row-column pairs. Multiplying the Euclidean coordinates by $4$ before taking squared distances checked all 528 row-edge pairs with integer arithmetic, including the forbidden-edge lower bound in all 368 forbidden pairs. A further integer-cost check used two blocks with two labels each and one subdivision city. The optimum was $15$ on the $13$ cities. When left crossings were forbidden in one block, minimizing forbidden incidences at that block gave $2$, for either choice of block. These controls check the finite constructions. The asymptotic results follow from the proofs and cited rank bounds.
