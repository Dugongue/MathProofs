# Undecidable local–global R-equivalence on one smooth projective variety

Assume that the embedded integers are Diophantine in the rationals, as in [Glazer's manuscript](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q).

**Fixed-variety theorem.** There exists a single smooth projective geometrically integral variety $Y/\mathbb Q$ for which deciding R-equivalence of two given rational points is halting-complete. The reduction supplies pairs that are directly R-equivalent over every completion of $\mathbb Q$. On positive instances one rational curve over $\mathbb Q$ joins the two points. On negative instances no chain of rational curves joins them.

**Family theorem.** For every computably enumerable set $S\subseteq\mathbb Q$ there are a nonempty Zariski-open $U\subseteq\mathbb A^1_{\mathbb Q}$, a smooth projective morphism $X\to U$ with geometrically integral fibers, and two disjoint rational sections $P_+,P_-$. The following hold.

- All real fibers over $U(\mathbb R)$ are diffeomorphic to the same sphere.
- For every $t\in U(\mathbb Q)$ and every completion $K$ of $\mathbb Q$, a morphism $\mathbb P^1_K\to X_t$ passes through $P_+(t)$ and $P_-(t)$.
- If $t\notin S$, the two marked points are the only rational points of $X_t$ and are not R-equivalent over $\mathbb Q$.
- If $t\in S$, the fiber has infinitely many rational points. Every rational point lies on the image of a morphism $\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Q}\to X_t$ through both marked points. In particular $X_t(\mathbb Q)/R$ has exactly one class.

Thus there is one such fixed family on which deciding R-equivalence of the two given sections is halting-complete, even though their equivalence over every completion is guaranteed. The same hard instances distinguish two from infinitely many rational points, or two from one R-equivalence class.

Two points over a field $K$ are directly R-equivalent if they lie on the image of a $K$-morphism from $\mathbb P^1_K$ at $K$-rational source points. R-equivalence is the equivalence relation generated by direct R-equivalence.

## 1. A bounded witness system with a branch over every completion

Fix a program enumerating $S$. The integer pairs $(a,d)$ with $d\ne0$ and $a/d\in S$ form a computably enumerable set. By Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich and the assumed Diophantine definition, this set, viewed as a subset of $\mathbb Q^2$, is the projection of a fixed system of polynomial equations over $\mathbb Q$. In detail, start with an integer Diophantine representation and impose the Diophantine integer predicate on its input and integer witness variables. The witnesses internal to that predicate range over $\mathbb Q$. Introduce arithmetic gate variables to make all equations quadratic. Denote this fixed system by
$$
Q_j(x)+L_j(x)+c_j=0,
$$
where $Q_j,L_j$ are homogeneous of degrees two and one. Include $a$ and $d$ as coordinates of $x$.

Use the fixed polynomial
$$
H(s)=(s^2-13)(s^2-17)(s^2-221).
$$
It has no rational root and has a root in every completion. For odd primes other than $13$ and $17$, at least one of $13,17,221$ is a quadratic residue because the third is the product of the first two. Its nonzero square root lifts. At $13$ use $17$, at $17$ use $13$, and at $2$ use $17\equiv1\bmod 8$. The real root $\sqrt{13}$ suffices at the infinite place.

Define a system $q_j(t,W)=0$ in $W=(z,b,s)$ consisting of
$$
Q_j(z)+bL_j(z)+c_j b^2=0,\qquad b-H(s)=0,\qquad
\frac{z_a-tz_d}{1+t^2}=0.
$$
Here $z_a,z_d$ name the corresponding coordinates of $z$. The coefficients are regular on $U_0=\mathbb A^1_{\mathbb Q}\setminus V(1+t^2)$ and uniformly bounded for real $t$.

Over $\mathbb Q$ the scale $b$ is nonzero, so dividing the quadratic equations by $b^2$ and the last numerator by $b$ gives an original witness with $a=td$. Conversely, if $t\in S$, choose a rational witness $x$ and choose rational $s$ close enough to $\sqrt{13}$ that $b=H(s)$ and every coordinate of $z=bx$ has absolute value at most one, with $|s|<4$. There is consequently a fixed bound on the Euclidean norm of at least one witness on every positive rational fiber. Over any completion take $s$ to be a local root of $H$ and put $b=z=0$. This solves the displayed system for every rational $t$. These particular local witnesses have $\sum_i W_i^2=s^2\in\{13,17,221\}$.

## 2. An affine model with two marked poles

Choose a positive integer $\rho$ with $\rho^2>1+221$ and $\rho^2>1+\sum_i W_i^2$ for all the bounded rational witnesses just constructed. Introduce coordinates $V=(\theta,h,Y)$ where $Y$ has the same length as $W$. Choose one degree $d\ge6$ at least the degree of every $q_j$ in $W$. Form the polynomial residuals $h^d q_j(t,Y/h)$. Each vanishes at $(h,Y)=(0,0)$ by homogeneity. Include the additional residual $\theta^2/\rho^2+h^2-1$. Let $F_t(V)$ be the sum of the squares of these residuals.

The coefficient of $\theta^4$ in $F_t$ is $\rho^{-4}$, so $F_t$ is nonconstant. Its coefficients remain uniformly bounded on the real parameter line. Fix a small nonzero rational $c$ to be specified below. Define $Z_t$ in affine coordinates $(V,u,r)$, where $r$ has four coordinates, by
$$
u^3+u=c^2 F_t(V)^2,\qquad
\theta^2+h^2+\sum_i Y_i^2+u^2+\sum_{i=1}^4 r_i^2=\rho^2.
$$
The two marked points are $\theta=\pm\rho$ with every other coordinate zero.

### Rational points

The affine elliptic curve $y^2=u^3+u$ has just one rational point, $(0,0)$. Negative $u$ gives a negative right side. For positive $u=A/B$ in coprime positive integers, the reduced fraction $A(A^2+B^2)/B^3$ being a square implies that $A,B,A^2+B^2$ are all squares. This contradicts Fermat's theorem that a sum of two nonzero fourth powers cannot be a square. One proof of that theorem uses descent. In a primitive solution $\alpha^4+\beta^4=\gamma^2$ take $\beta$ even. Parametrize the Pythagorean triple with legs $\alpha^2,\beta^2$ as $m^2-n^2,2mn$, where $m,n$ are coprime and $m$ odd. Since $\alpha$ is odd, reduction modulo four forces $n$ even. The square $2mn$ then implies $m=e^2,n=2f^2$. The primitive triple with legs $\alpha,n$ and hypotenuse $m$ gives $n=2pq,m=p^2+q^2$ with $p,q$ coprime. Hence $pq=f^2$ implies $p=i^2,q=j^2$, giving $i^4+j^4=e^2$ with smaller positive hypotenuse. [Fermat descent](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/ugradnumthy/descent.pdf)

Apply the curve calculation with $y=c F_t(V)$. Every rational point has $u=0$, so $F_t(V)=0$ and every residual vanishes. If $h=0$, the circle residual gives $\theta=\pm\rho$. The sphere equation then forces $Y=r=0$. If $h\ne0$, the residuals give $q_j(t,Y/h)=0$ and hence $t\in S$. This proves the exact negative-fiber statement in the affine chart.

For a positive rational parameter choose a bounded witness $W$. By the rational four-square theorem there is $r_0\in\mathbb Q^4$ with $\sum r_{0,i}^2=\rho^2-1-\sum W_i^2$. The rational four-square theorem here follows by applying Lagrange's integer theorem to $AD$ for a positive fraction $A/D$ and dividing the squares by $D^2$. The affine circle $A_0^2+B_0^2=1$ maps into $Z_t$ by
$$
\theta=\rho A_0,\qquad h=B_0,\qquad Y=B_0 W,\qquad
r=B_0 r_0,\qquad u=0.
$$
Its points $(\pm1,0)$ map to the poles. Moreover, at any given rational point off the poles we can take $W=Y/h$ and $r_0=r/h$. The two equations of $Z_t$ show that these choices satisfy the same conditions, so that point lies on a circle through both poles.

Over a completion, take the special witness from Section 1 with $b=z=0$ and $s^2\in\{13,17,221\}$. The required squared slack $\rho^2-1-s^2$ is a positive rational number. The same rational four-square argument therefore supplies $r_0$ over $\mathbb Q$ itself, regardless of the completion. The displayed circle construction then gives a local curve through both poles for every rational parameter.

## 3. Generic smoothness and geometric integrality

Work first over $k=\overline{\mathbb Q(t)}$. Let $D$ be the smooth affine sphere quadric in the second equation. On $D$ write $A=u^3+u$ and $B=F_t(V)^2$. At every base point of the pencil $A-c^2 B=0$ we have $u=0,i,-i$, so $dA=(3u^2+1)du$ and $dB=0$. On the tangent space of $D$, $du$ can vanish identically only when all the other coordinates are zero, which would give $u^2=\rho^2$. Hence the pencil members are smooth at the base points. Off the base locus, a point of a member with $c\ne0$ has $B\ne0$, and a singularity can occur only at a critical point of the regular function $A/B$ on this open part of $D$.

The critical locus has finitely many irreducible components. In characteristic zero a regular function whose differential vanishes on such a component is constant on it. Indeed its differential vanishes in the component's function field, where the kernel of the universal differential consists of constants over the algebraically closed base. Thus only finitely many values of $c^2$ can cause a generic singularity. A positive rational $c$ can be chosen arbitrarily small outside those values.

For geometric integrality, first use the cubic $u^3+u-c^2 F_t(V)^2$ over $k(V)$. If it factored, it would have a root in $k(V)$, hence in $k[V]$ by monicity and integral closedness. This gives a map $V\mapsto (u(V),c F_t(V))$ from affine space to the elliptic curve $y^2=u^3+u$. Since $F_t$ is nonconstant, some affine line restricts it to a nonconstant map. The map extends to the projective line by properness of the elliptic curve, contradicting Riemann–Hurwitz. So the cubic coordinate ring $R$ is a domain.

Adjoining the four slack variables amounts to imposing $\sum r_i^2=\rho^2-\sum V_i^2-u^2$. Over the fraction field of $R$, this is geometrically irreducible. A product of two affine linear forms has quadratic part of rank at most two, whereas the quadratic part here has rank four. The resulting algebra over $R$ injects into its localization over the fraction field. In fact the equation is monic in $r_4$, so the algebra is free over $R[r_1,r_2,r_3]$ with basis $1,r_4$. This proves geometric integrality of the generic affine model.

## 4. Smooth projective fibers with spherical real loci

Over $\mathbb Q(t)$ take the closure of the affine model in the projective sphere quadric and take a projective resolution that is an isomorphism over the smooth affine model. The projective resolution is smooth and geometrically integral. Spread the projective embedding, the map to the quadric, and the isomorphism over the affine chart to a nonempty Zariski-open subset $U\subseteq U_0$. This uses finite presentation, by clearing finitely many denominators for the morphisms and their inverse identities. Shrink $U$ further to obtain smooth geometrically integral fibers. Smoothness can be imposed using properness to remove the image of the nonsmooth locus. After shrinking to flatness, geometric integrality holds on an open neighborhood of the generic point. The poles then give disjoint sections on all of $U$. [Resolution in characteristic zero](https://arxiv.org/abs/alg-geom/9508005) · [Geometrically integral fibers](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0559)

The projective sphere quadric at infinity has equation $\sum V_i^2+u^2+\sum r_i^2=0$ in homogeneous coordinates, and hence has no real points there. Every real or rational point of the resolved variety maps to the affine chart. Since the resolution was spread together with its affine-chart isomorphism, the affine calculations determine all rational and real points of every retained fiber. Local curves constructed in the affine chart remain valid even though the argument does not exclude additional local points at infinity. The circle used above is a smooth conic after projective closure and has a rational point over the relevant field, so it is isomorphic to $\mathbb P^1$ over that field. Its map to the resolved projective fiber extends from the affine circle by properness. Consequently the two poles are directly R-equivalent over every completion. Over $\mathbb Q$ on a negative fiber any morphism from $\mathbb P^1$ is constant, since a nonconstant morphism has finite point fibers and $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q)$ is infinite. On a positive fiber the same conic construction joins every rational point to both poles.

For the real topology, solve $u^3+u=c^2F_t(V)^2$ for $u=g_t(V)$. The inverse is smooth since $3u^2+1\ge1$. On the radius-$\rho$ ball in $V$, both $g_t$ and its gradient tend uniformly to zero as $c\to0$, uniformly in real $t$. This follows from the uniform coefficient bounds and the formulas
$$
|g_t(V)|\le c^2|F_t(V)|^2,\qquad
\nabla g_t=\frac{2c^2 F_t\nabla F_t}{3g_t^2+1}.
$$
Choose the earlier nonsingular $c$ small enough that $g_t^2<\rho^2/4$ and the absolute radial derivative of $g_t^2$ on the ball is less than $\rho/2$. In the $(V,r)$ coordinates, along a unit ray the squared norm equation becomes
$$
\lambda^2+g_t(\lambda V_0)^2=\rho^2
$$
where $(V_0,r_0)$ is a unit vector. There is no solution at $\lambda\le\rho/2$. Between $\rho/2$ and $\rho$ the left side is strictly increasing, and at $\rho$ it is at least $\rho^2$. The unique radial solution varies smoothly with the ray by the implicit function theorem. Radial projection is therefore a diffeomorphism to the unit sphere in the $(V,r)$ space. This works for every real parameter in $U$.

## 5. One fixed variety suffices

Let $E$ be the smooth projective elliptic curve $y^2=x^3-2$ and let $P=(3,5)$. It has infinite order by Nagell–Lutz, since $25$ does not divide $108=|4\cdot0^3+27(-2)^2|$. For positive $n$, the values $x(nP)$ are finite, computable rationals and are pairwise distinct. Indeed, equal $x$ coordinates on this Weierstrass curve mean the two points are equal or negatives of one another, both impossible for distinct positive multiples of $P$. [Nagell–Lutz theorem](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~ebelmont/ec-notes.pdf)

Apply the family theorem to
$
S=\{x((\langle e,j\rangle+1)P): e\text{ halts},\ j\ge0\}.
$
This set is computably enumerable by group-law arithmetic and enumeration of halting programs. Pull the family back along the $x$ coordinate on $E^\circ=x^{-1}(U)\subset E$, using $U\subset\mathbb A^1\subset\mathbb P^1$. The pullback is projective and smooth over $E^\circ$ and has geometrically integral fibers. Its total space is smooth and geometrically integral. For example, after extension to an algebraic closure each irreducible component of the smooth total space is open and dominates the base by smoothness, and the geometric generic fiber is integral. Take its closure in $E\times\mathbb P^N$ using the fixed relative projective embedding, and resolve projectively, preserving the smooth open pullback. This yields one smooth projective geometrically integral variety $Y$ equipped with a morphism $Y\to E$. Over $E^\circ$ this morphism is the original pullback, since it was already projective and closed in the relative embedding there.

On input $e$, try successive $j$ until $T=(\langle e,j\rangle+1)P$ belongs to $E^\circ$. This terminates because the excluded $x$ coordinates form a finite set. Output the two marked points of the fiber over $T$, viewed as rational points of the fixed $Y$. Their coordinates can be computed using the section maps and the fixed open identification with $Y$. For any characteristic-zero field, a morphism from $\mathbb P^1$ to $E$ is constant by Riemann–Hurwitz. Thus every rational curve in a chain joining points of the fiber over $T$ stays in that fiber, over $\mathbb Q$ and over each completion. The two points are therefore R-equivalent in $Y(\mathbb Q)$ exactly when $e$ halts, and directly R-equivalent over every completion on every input.

R-equivalence on this fixed projective variety is computably enumerable even on arbitrary pairs of its rational points. Enumerate finite chains of morphisms $\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Q}\to Y$ and rational source parameters. In a fixed projective embedding each morphism is given by homogeneous binary forms of the same degree without a common geometric zero. The base-point condition and the defining equations of $Y$ are decidable by polynomial arithmetic. Evaluate at the given source parameters to test the chain's endpoints and consecutive joins. This establishes halting-completeness and also applies to direct R-equivalence by restricting the enumeration to single morphisms.

## 6. The fixed family and relation to earlier work

For the halting-complete family take $S$ to consist of the rational integers $\langle e,j\rangle$ for which program $e$ halts, using any standard computable pairing of nonnegative integers. The complement of $U$ has finitely many rational parameters. Given $e$, test successive $j$ until $\langle e,j\rangle\in U$, using the fixed polynomial defining the complement. This is an effective many-one reduction and preserves both answers. In the other direction the positive outcome can be semidecided by enumerating affine rational solutions and stopping at a point distinct from the two poles. This test is valid on the fixed family for each of the three decision questions in the theorem. In particular, the fixed-family proof needs no algorithm for recognizing negative rational-point instances and no numerical bound on witness heights.

Poonen transfers the existence problem to smooth projective geometrically integral varieties and asks about deciding finiteness versus infinitude of rational points. The unrestricted finite-solution problem has long been related to Hilbert's tenth problem over the rationals. Ait El Manssour and coauthors already use the unrestricted infinitude problem as a hardness source. The result here supplies undecidability on one fixed smooth projective variety and a fixed smooth projective family with two marked rational points, a common real sphere topology, and guaranteed direct local R-equivalence. Its global R-equivalence question realizes arbitrary computably enumerable rational parameter sets after removal of finitely many parameters. A targeted search found no earlier theorem with this combination, and publication priority remains unestablished.
