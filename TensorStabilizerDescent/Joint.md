# Arbitrary-order jets with nonsplit graphical residue

**Combined theorem.** Fix a finite field k. Let R=k[t]/(t^s) with any positive s, presented by coefficient lists. On raw free three-tensors whose residues belong to a six-layer graph GL orbit over the algebraic closure, SL equivalence is polynomial-time equivalent to GI. All higher coefficients are arbitrary. A decorated graph match reconstructs the unique corresponding special-linear transporter. The construction rejects residues outside that family.

Apply the [geometric decoder](Descent.md) to each residue. Choose the fixed core cell last for the difference basis in the [jet proof](Jets.md), recording any column reorderings in volume calculations. In model coordinates the residue is now the standard tensor over the algebraic closure. The descent datum is the Frobenius permutation f on the graph and its associated permutations on the three modes. Concretely the maps F from the model coordinates to the supplied axes satisfy Frobenius(F)=F P_f. The model spaces with this semilinear descent are rational vector spaces. The distinguished core basis elements used by the normalization are all fixed. Changing to the fixed-core difference convention preserves the determinant comparison for two isomorphic models, since the conventions are related by the same ordering rule (or one can compute both volumes directly in the fixed-core-last convention).

Pull the full input back by these constant maps. The linear left inverse to the derivative from the jet proof commutes with graph isomorphisms, hence with twisted Frobenius. The determinant fixes in that proof use only fixed core coordinates. Therefore the full order-by-order normalization preserves this descent condition. The coefficient on an index tuple I and that on f(I) are Frobenius conjugates. By the uniqueness part of the jet proof, two normalized arrays match exactly when one graph map respecting f carries all these coefficients to their counterparts, and the residual volumes agree. In the forward direction the actual residue witness gives such a graph map after its two scalar factors are removed; a central triple of product one acts trivially on an entire tensor over R. In the converse direction the volume equality gives a rational scalar correction, and correcting the constant matrices by these three scalars commutes with normalization. The resulting transporter is rational and determinant one. Uniqueness follows from absence of a nonidentity principal stabilizer together with the uniqueness of the residual SL lift for that graph witness.

**Complexity of descending the computation.** Each geometric model mode is a permutation module in the chosen basis. For an orbit of length d, a descended vector has coordinates y,y^q,...,y^(q^(d−1)) for one element y of K_d. Use a k-basis of this small field to give a k-basis of the corresponding descended subspace. With these bases the actual F matrices are rational linear maps: applying a whole orbit of columns to an equivariant input uses a field trace. Inversion, multiplication and the determinant corrections can therefore be performed over k. The model tensor space, the space of endomorphisms of each mode, and the space of linear maps between any two such spaces have the same description on index tuples. Each tuple needed has bounded arity, so its orbit length is bounded by a fixed power of the total number of model indices. Tables for the equivariant left inverse can either be assembled directly from its explicit graph formulas or by applying it to each descended basis element. For sums over a variable index in those formulas, group it into cycles under the stabilizing Frobenius power of the output tuple; each cycle sum is a relative trace back to the output field. The tree propagation step adds along the fixed core and one attached edge per moving vertex, with at most one additional incidence at a dangling edge, so no unbounded-degree compositum arises along such a path. Alternatively perform the potential solve for that tree directly over k in descended coordinates. This explains how every bounded-arity operation and each matrix inversion runs in polynomial time with the coherent small fields of the geometric decoder.

Make a colored graph with the same Frobenius relation as before. For each coefficient index (row, edge, nonbase incidence), attach its tuple of all normalized higher coefficient values in the *common* K_d representation, using the tuple's orbit length d as part of its color. A Frobenius-intertwining graph map preserves that orbit length. Use typed positions for all three incidence slots. There are polynomially many such colors and tuples in the explicit input, and equality of these colors is exact. The ordinary GI lower bound follows by constant lifts of the original graph tensors. The claim is an informal proof and places no bound on the common splitting degree.

**Geometric consequence.** Write G₀ for the product of the three SL groups, M for the ambient tensor space, and N for the kernel of the linear left inverse at the graph tensor T. Over any algebraically closed field the action map G₀ × (T+N) → M has invertible differential at (1,T) because the tangent representation splits as the orbit directions and N. Both sides are smooth and have the same dimension. The map is étale in a neighborhood of that point and its image contains a nonempty Zariski-open subset of these tensor formats. This gives a coordinate chart in the étale topology; it does not make the global intersections of an orbit with T+N unique. In particular, substituting t=1 in a truncated power series is not a passage to an arbitrary field-valued input.
