# Reading nonsplit graph tensors over a fixed finite field

Fix k of order q. Write M(G) for the six-layer integral three-tensor in the [earlier construction](Signed.md). The three mode dimensions are r,e,c with c=2e−1; they are pairwise coprime. In graph coordinates its third flattening identifies the dual third space with the coefficient-sum hyperplane in the 2e incidence cells of a rigidly padded simple graph J(G). Ordered graph witnesses on these tensors have determinant one in every mode. The only additional GL witnesses are scalars (u,w,(uw)⁻¹). These are precisely the properties of the earlier construction used below.

**Theorem.** There is a deterministic polynomial-time recognition and reduction to graph isomorphism for SL equivalence of all tensors over k which become GL-isomorphic to some M(G) over the algebraic closure. From the raw input we compute a graph G, a permutation f of its vertices preserving its edges, and a nonzero scalar μ in k. For matching formats two inputs are SL-equivalent precisely when their scalars agree and there is a graph isomorphism intertwining their two permutations. No splitting field or transport matrix is supplied. In particular the restricted decision language, rejecting tensors outside this family, is GI-complete under polynomial many-one reductions for each fixed k. Unrestricted tensors can be tested for membership in this family. No claim of coverage outside it is needed.

## Find the geometric lines in separate fields

From the third flattening image S inside A⊗B, use exactly the quadratic extraction of the split construction. Regroup S⊗S, intersect with the tensor product of the row swap-fixed space and the alternating column space, and contract the two column factors. Alternating here means the span of v⊗w−w⊗v, including in characteristic two. On the image the result over the algebraic closure is the span of all row squares: a mixed row term would force a rectangle in simple incidence, and two independent allowable vectors in each row give its square. The left multipliers on A of this square space are consequently the full geometric diagonal algebra. All computations before this observation are linear over k and commute with field extension.

Compute this multiplier algebra in raw coordinates. Require it to be a commutative étale k-algebra acting as its regular module. This is testable by linear algebra: require it to be unital and closed, reduced (q-power is injective), of dimension dim A, and after decomposition each field factor to act on a subspace of its field degree. The fixed subspace of q-power splits it into factors by testing its multiplication operators against the q scalars. Under a factor of degree d, compute the common eigenlines on its d-dimensional summand over a field K_d of degree d. This can be done by factoring the characteristic polynomials of a basis of its multiplication operators there and intersecting eigenspaces. Require all d resulting lines. Frobenius permutes them cyclically. Testing all elements of K_d would be exponential; polynomial factoring in fixed characteristic suffices. [Shoup's finite-field algorithms](https://shoup.net/papers/)

For two rows, intersect their row-projection spaces in B to find an edge line or zero. A projection onto a given row is obtained using its primitive eigenprojector over its individual field. For rows of degrees d,d' their comparison uses only degree lcm(d,d') ≤ dd'. Normalize line generators by making the first nonzero raw coordinate one. An edge line conjugates with its endpoints; determine its orbit length from the edge permutation. Its normalized coordinates lie in the corresponding subfield. Check that different edges yield distinct independent column axes spanning B. This last independence can use the determinant procedure below. Trace the previous graph scaffold backwards, verifying the anchor graph, layers, duplicated adjacency and matchings; compare the rebuilt graph with the recovered one using the recovered incidence identification. We obtain G and its Frobenius automorphism f. Choose any ordering of the original vertices of G and use the model's prescribed ordering on the enlarged graph. Frobenius fixes the identifiable anchors.

Here and below comparisons in small auxiliary fields must use *coherent* embeddings. A concrete implementation fixes one field tower for each prime l dividing the required orbit degrees. In an extension of degree the largest required power of l, extract the nested subfields as fixed spaces of powers of Frobenius. For each needed degree d use the tensor product over k of the corresponding prime-power subfields. They are linearly disjoint by coprimality of their degrees. Two such models embed into the one for their least common multiple by those tower inclusions. The largest prime powers needed are bounded by the largest individual orbit degrees (and two). Only individual fields and composita of a bounded number of them are materialized. Deterministic irreducible-polynomial construction and factorization here take polynomial time for fixed k. For example splitting a polynomial already known to split in an auxiliary field can use fixed-prime Berlekamp algebra linear equations, separating factors by elements fixed by the prime-field Frobenius and testing prime-field scalars. The mathematical common algebraic closure serves to specify identifications; the algorithm never builds the compositum of all orbits. [Shoup](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

## Normalize the incidences by one linear system

Let a_v and b_h be the normalized geometric axis generators. They are Frobenius-equivariant. Check that S has codimension one in the incidence span U and is contained in it. One way works entirely in rational coordinates: on each orbit of incidence cells, expand one generator a_v⊗b_h in a basis of its orbit field. The coefficients in the raw coordinate space span the rational descent of the conjugate cell span by separability; concatenate and row-reduce these coefficient vectors. Compute a k-linear functional on A⊗B vanishing on S with nonzero restriction to U. Write d_vh for its value on each incidence. Reject if any value vanishes.

The equations x_v d_vh=y_h on incidences are linear. Give each row or edge orbit one unknown in its individual field, use its Frobenius powers for the other unknowns, and expand the equations over k in the pairwise composita. Set x=1 at one fixed anchor. On the geometric image a solution exists over the algebraic closure by the scalar-factor form of the incidence functional. Connectedness gives a unique solution with that normalization, so Frobenius fixes it in the specified equivariant sense. Reject inconsistent equations or zero coordinates. Rescale a_v by x_v and b_h by y_h⁻¹. Now each incidence vector z_i has functional value one. A fixed anchor-to-anchor cell z_* is rational. These checks ensure that the maps with columns a_v and b_h carry the model hyperplane onto S; the third map is forced by the flattenings. The recognition checks are sufficient since they construct an isomorphism over the algebraic closure. They are all necessary by the preceding argument.

## Compute the volume without constructing the splitting field

Let φ be the actual injective third flattening. Let D_A,D_B denote the determinants with columns the normalized row and edge generators in model order and displayed ambient coordinates. Define D_I as the determinant of the matrix of 2e columns
$$
 \big(\phi^{-1}(z_i-z_*),\,1\big).
$$
The inverse on S is computed by solving the rational flattening using pivots. Differences of the columns against the last one, followed by that last column, are a determinant-one change of basis. Therefore the determinant of the third-mode map from model to input is D_I⁻¹ in the earlier difference-dual convention. This is independent of using the fixed cell z_* instead of the last one for translations.

Here is a general way to compute each displayed determinant. Reorder the columns by Frobenius cycles, recording the permutation sign. For an orbit of length d write its initial column in a k-basis β_i of K_d. Its orbit matrix factors as a matrix of rational coordinate columns times the square matrix with entries β_i raised to q^j, for 0 ≤ j < d. The latter has nonzero determinant by separability, and Frobenius multiplies it by (−1)^(d−1), a cyclic column permutation sign. Its determinant therefore lies in k or in the degree-two field, and can be computed locally in K_d and identified in the common degree-two model. Concatenate all rational coordinate columns and take a single determinant over k. Multiply it by the orbit determinants and the reordering sign. This computes the desired determinant in polynomial time even when the least common multiple of all orbit lengths is superpolynomial. Each of the complete vertex, edge and incidence permutations has sign one in the six-layer model, so each of D_A,D_B,D_I belongs to k. Require all three nonzero.

Compute
$$
 \mu=D_A^{ec}D_B^{rc}D_I^{-re}.
$$
The character χ(a,b,d)=a^(ec)b^(rc)d^(re) kills all allowed scalar lifts and all model graph permutations have determinant one. Thus μ is independent of ordering and normalization. For the converse, if π:G_X→G_Y intertwines Frobenius, let P be its model tensor lift. The comparison F_Y P F_X⁻¹ using the normalized axis maps and the forced third maps is Frobenius fixed and therefore rational. More explicitly the Frobenius of each F_X is F_X P_(f_X) since its columns and its flattening identification are equivariant. Its comparison has determinant character μ(Y)/μ(X). When this is one its actual determinants can be removed over k by scalar lifts: the cross product of (r,0,−c) and (0,e,−c) is (ec,rc,re), primitive by coprimality. Adjoining a Bézout column makes a unimodular change of the three scalar coordinates, with inverse given by Laurent monomials over k. Conversely an SL match descends to a Frobenius-intertwining graph match and forces equal characters. Encode G with the distinguished directed relation v ↦ f(v) as one colored graph, then replace the finitely many colors and edge roles with standard polynomial graph gadgets. GI-hardness follows from the split models with identity Frobenius. The constructions and comparisons are polynomial for fixed k, with no claim of small practical constants.

## Why both pieces are needed

On the split geometric SL orbit itself μ is one. Distinct arithmetic forms still occur. For example take an edgeless input with an automorphism having one cycle of each length 1 through m. The corresponding twist exists by Lang's theorem for products of SL groups. Its hidden axes first split over degree lcm(1,...,m), superpolynomial in the displayed input length as m increases. The previous split decoder only covered an axis frame defined over k. The algorithm here covers these twists and arbitrary changes of volume. This is an informal proof; the general equality of TI and SL-TI remains open.
