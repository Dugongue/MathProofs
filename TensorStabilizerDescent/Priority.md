# Priority audit and descent of the integral graph construction

## What the nearest papers already prove

- Frucht realized any finite group as the full automorphism group of a finite graph. This supplies the last graph-theoretic step of the realization argument. [Frucht, 1939](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1939__6__239_0/)
- Costoya, Ligouras, Tocino and Viruel give a faithful functor from finite simple graphs with injective edge-preserving maps to regular evolution algebras over any field. Theorem 3.1 identifies the automorphism groups and Theorem 1.1 realizes every finite group. Their January 2021 version explicitly proves realization of a constant finite group **scheme** as Theorem 1.3. The December 2021 v3 PDF retains the graph and abstract group theorems but omits that numbered scheme assertion. Elduque and Labra's exact sequence for the automorphism scheme also explains the scheme computation. The common integral structure constants allow the tied-action realization itself to be extended integrally by the same fiber argument used in the graph proof. The latter extension is an inference from their construction. [CLTV v2, Theorems 1.3 and 3.1](https://www.math.uci.edu/~brusso/Autounivfinite.pdf) · [CLTV v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.03338v3) · [Elduque and Labra](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02191)
- Kaygorodov, Martín González and Viruel's October 2026 manuscript obtains every finite group as the automorphism group of a *simple* evolution algebra over every field. Milne gives a broader scheme-realization theorem for nonassociative algebras over fields with at least eight elements, and studies stabilizers of collections of mixed tensors over Dedekind domains. These are genuinely strong realization results with a change of basis shared across the algebra inputs and output. [Kaygorodov, Martín González and Viruel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22248) · [Milne, Theorem 1 and Proposition 8](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/AUTO.pdf)
- Li, Qiao, A. Wigderson, Y. Wigderson and Zhang recover graph isomorphisms from graphical matrix spaces. For instance Corollary 6.4 extracts one from the support of a congruence witness. Futorny, Grochow and Sergeichuk compile systems of tensors of order at most three into ordinary three-tensor equivalence. These results treat other actions or preserve the existence of transformations; an exact finite stabilizer scheme does not follow from the existence equivalence. [Graphical matrix spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04815) · [Wildness for tensors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219)

There is a direct mathematical check of the distinction. In a regular evolution algebra of dimension d, the natural basis vectors b_i multiply to zero in distinct pairs and their squares form another basis. With an independent choice of the output basis, its multiplication tensor therefore becomes the unit tensor of size d. For d at least two its three-mode SL stabilizer contains a split torus of dimension 2(d−1): pick diagonal determinant-one changes in the first and second modes and compensate coordinate by coordinate in the third. Thus the evolution algebra proofs cannot yield the claimed finite independent-mode stabilizer just by reinterpreting the same array.

The priority candidate is the combination of a polynomial graph-to-tensor construction, a **single integral concise tensor**, three **independent** special-linear factors, and equality of the entire scheme of transporters between two inputs, including in small characteristics. The comparison found no earlier proof of this package. It does not establish publication priority. The GI inclusion is suggested in the discussion following Open Question 1.8 of Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang. The opposite direction on arbitrary tensors is their Open Question 1.9. [Classical groups, §1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135#S1.SS5)

## One ordinary TI test on all forms in the geometric SL orbits

Let k be any field with algebraic closure K. For each finite simple graph G use the unsigned, six-layer integral tensor M(G). Its mode sizes are (r,e,c), with r a power of two, e odd and c=2e−1, hence pairwise coprime. The promise on each raw input is that over K it is SL-equivalent to M(G) for some G. The graph, an isomorphism, a field extension and a basis over that extension are all absent from the input. On this union of geometric orbits the identity map on tensor pairs is a many-one reduction from SL-TI to ordinary TI over k.

Indeed let f be an ordinary k-transporter between X and Y. Over K choose special-linear maps from standard models to X and Y. Conjugating f by these maps gives an ordinary isomorphism of the two models. The centered-incidence theorem forces a graph isomorphism times two scalars u,w, with determinant triple

$$
(u^r,w^e,(uw)^{-c}).
$$

The graph lift has determinant one in all modes. The displayed entries are already in k since they are the three determinants of f. Both u^r and u^{ec} are in k, the latter by taking ((uw)^c)^e and dividing by (w^e)^c. Bézout then gives u in k. It follows that w^c and w^e are in k, so w is in k as well. Composing f with the inverse of the scalar automorphism (uI,wI,(uw)^{-1}I) gives a k-special-linear witness. The converse follows by inclusion. This proof works also over imperfect fields, without using a separability or smoothness assumption.

The same sufficient lemma works for any pair of geometrically SL-equivalent tensors with pairwise-coprime mode dimensions whose geometric automorphism determinant image consists exactly of the central scalar triples displayed above. This promises geometric SL-equivalence; independently deciding it on arbitrary raw inputs is a separate matter. For the graph-model union the promise on each object suffices because graph equivalence over K is recovered by a GL comparison and every graph isomorphism lifts specially. Choosing unrelated arbitrary GL orientations could destroy that property.

## These forms can require very large splitting degrees

Over a finite field k, within the geometric SL orbit of M(G), the rational SL orbits are indexed by conjugacy classes of H=Aut(G). In fact the integral theorem makes its stabilizer the constant finite group scheme H. An orbit point obtained by applying b in the ambient product of special-linear groups to M(G) is rational exactly when b^{-1}F(b) belongs to H, where F is Frobenius. Lang's theorem solves this equation over the algebraic closure for every h in H. Altering b within the same rational orbit and by a lift in H makes exactly the usual conjugation identification. Over a degree-j extension the label changes from h to h^j. Thus the least splitting degree of the twist labeled h is its order. [Lang, Algebraic groups over finite fields](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Lang-Algebraic_Groups_Over_Finite_Fields.pdf)

For a concrete family, take the edgeless graph on n=m(m+1)/2 vertices. Choose h with one cycle of each length from 1 through m. The degree of its first splitting extension is lcm(1,...,m), at least 2^m/(m+1). Indeed every binomial coefficient in row m divides this lcm: for each prime p the factorial valuation formula bounds its valuation by the number of positive prime powers p^j at most m. At least one coefficient is at least the average 2^m/(m+1). Since the dense tensor length is polynomial in n for fixed k, this splitting degree is superpolynomial in that length. The same degree is needed to reach the standard model by an ordinary k-extension-field isomorphism, by the preceding descent argument. The identity reduction bypasses the entire splitting extension. This is an existence statement for such twists, not a polynomial-time construction via a field of that degree.

These are informal mathematical arguments. No claim of an unrestricted reverse reduction or an efficiently recognizable union of all these geometric orbits is needed.
