# The integral graph transporter and realization of finite groups

**Theorem.** The six-layer construction of [the graph theorem](Signed.md) is defined over the integers. For each pair of graphs of matching output dimensions, its special-linear tensor transporter scheme over the integers is the constant finite scheme on their graph isomorphisms. In particular, every finite group occurs as the full stabilizer group scheme of one integral three-tensor, concise over every field, under the product of three independent special-linear groups. Given a Cayley table, an example can be built in polynomial time in its length. The tensor entries belong to {0,1,−1}.

A concrete interpretation is that over every nonzero connected commutative ring, including the integers modulo any prime power and the dual numbers over any field, the actual matrices taking one constructed tensor to another are exactly the integral graph lifts reduced to that ring. Over a general ring, graph isomorphisms can be chosen separately on a finite partition of its spectrum into open-and-closed subsets. Composition respects these descriptions.

### Eliminate infinitesimal symmetries

Use the graph, incidence space and coefficient-sum kernel S of the six-layer construction. Let k be any algebraically closed field. There are already exactly the required lifts on k-points. This leaves a genuine issue: in positive characteristic, a group scheme can have only the identity as a field point and still admit nonidentity matrices over the dual numbers. We check its tangent space.

Write a first-order stabilizer as identity plus ε times (X,Y,Z), with ε²=0. The operator X⊗1+1⊗Y must send S into S because the third map preserves its flattening image. For distinct row vertices u,w, choose an edge f at u missing w, and another distinct edge g at u. Such choices exist by minimum degree and simplicity. Test the difference of the incidence cells (u,f) and (u,g). At the forbidden cell (w,f) the derivative has coefficient X_wu. It must vanish. Thus X is diagonal. For distinct edge columns f,h choose an endpoint u of f missing h. Take the difference of (u,f) and a cell on another row. At the forbidden cell (u,h) the derivative now has coefficient Y_hf. Hence Y is diagonal as well.

Preserving the coefficient-sum kernel requires the sums X_vv+Y_ff to agree on every incidence (v,f). Connectedness forces X=αI and Y=βI. The third map is then uniquely Z=−(α+β)I. If the mode dimensions are r,e,c, the three determinant-one equations impose

$$r\alpha=0,\qquad e\beta=0,\qquad c(\alpha+\beta)=0.$$

Here r is a power of two, e is odd, and c=2e−1, making the three dimensions pairwise coprime. In each positive characteristic at most one coefficient vanishes, and the other two equations give α=β=0; in characteristic zero the same conclusion follows immediately. Translation by any graph lift gives zero tangent space at every point of each nonempty transporter.

### Recover the integral scheme

The determinant and tensor-preservation equations define an affine scheme of finite type over the integers with coordinate ring B. In each geometric fiber there are only the known graph lifts, by the field theorem. At any of them zero tangent space means the maximal ideal of its Noetherian local ring has zero quotient by its square; Nakayama's lemma makes the ideal zero. A finite-type algebra over an algebraically closed field with finitely many closed points is zero-dimensional by the Nullstellensatz. Thus the entire fiber is a disjoint union of reduced points, including in characteristics dividing the graph automorphism group order. A fiber with no points is empty.

Let H be the finite set of graph isomorphisms. Evaluation at their integral lifts gives B → ℤ^H, and this map is surjective. Indeed in the first mode the lifts are distinct permutation matrices. The product of the matrix entries along the permutation of a given lift evaluates to one there and zero at every other lift. The target is free over ℤ, so the surjection splits as a map of abelian groups. Consequently its kernel I tensored with any residue field embeds as the kernel after base change. The geometric-fiber calculation makes that kernel zero, also over the residue field itself by faithful field extension. The same holds at the generic point. At each prime of B lying over p>0 the finitely generated ideal I consequently satisfies I=pI after localization; Nakayama gives zero. Localizations over characteristic zero vanish by the generic calculation. Hence I=0 and B=ℤ^H, with the group law for equal graphs induced by composition. If H is empty, all fibers are empty and B is the zero ring. This argument deals explicitly with possible integral torsion.

### Polynomial construction from a Cayley table

One can invoke Frucht's graph realization theorem for existence. For the polynomial claim here is a direct construction. For a group K of order m start with m vertices colored as group elements of one common color. For each x and each nonidentity h put a private three-edge path from x to xh. Give its first and second internal vertices distinct colors T_h and H_h, respectively. These colors are fixed, regardless of how a group element is spelled in the table. A color-preserving automorphism of the resulting simple graph must satisfy π(xh)=π(x)h for every x,h, and is precisely a left translation; all path maps are forced.

To remove the colors, attach three private pendant paths to every vertex of this colored graph. Use positive lengths at least two, all different within a triple, with different triples for different colors. The lengths may be chosen at most 3(2m+1)+2. The old vertices are exactly those of degree at least three, and the induced graph on them is the original colored construction with the color recoverable from each attached triple. Every color-preserving automorphism extends uniquely. The construction also works for the trivial group. There are O(m³) vertices before the polynomial six-layer encoding. Composing gives the claimed integral tensor directly from the table. The proof constructs the tensor without enumerating its automorphisms.

Frucht's graph realizations and realizations of group schemes as automorphism groups of algebras are prior work. The setting here uses one concise tensor, three *independent* special-linear actions, a polynomial construction, an entire transporter between distinct objects, and a single integral array valid at all primes. Priority for that package is unverified. This argument is informal. [Frucht](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1949-033-6) · [Milne](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05708)
