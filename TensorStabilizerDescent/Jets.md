# A polynomial normal form for graphical tensor deformations

**Theorem.** Fix a finite field $k$. Let $R=k[t]/(t^s)$, with $s\geq1$ variable and ring elements given by their coefficient lists. Consider dense three-tensors over $R$ whose reduction modulo $t$, in its supplied bases, belongs to the general-linear orbit of a six-layer graph tensor from the integral construction. All the other coefficients of the input tensors are arbitrary. The special-linear isomorphism problem on this recognizable family is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to graph isomorphism. One graph comparison describes the whole set of special-linear transporters, with a polynomial-time bijection on witnesses. This applies even if the characteristic divides the order of the graph automorphism group.

Here and below, graph residue means an actual rational general-linear isomorphism to a six-layer graph tensor. The direct construction uses only the finite field and polynomial-size linear algebra. For non-split residue forms, a separate descent argument would be required. The theorem is informal.

## The graphical frame

Recall the parts of the six-layer construction needed in the proof. Its simple graph $J(G)$ has a rigid connected core, with individually recognizable anchor vertices. Each other vertex is in a distinguished layer and adjacent to four designated anchors. Each core vertex has at least three adjacent core vertices. Every edge has a direction respected by graph isomorphisms, given by layer and anchor roles; edges within the fixed core can be oriented by core order. The graph isomorphisms of $G$ extend uniquely to $J(G)$.

Put $A=k^{V(J)}, B=k^{E(J)}$ and denote by $c_{uf}$ the cell indexed by a vertex $u$ and an incident edge $f$. Their span in $A\otimes B$ is $U$. Let $S$ be the kernel on $U$ of coefficient sum. The model $T_G$ is the inclusion $\iota:S\to A\otimes B$, viewed in $M=A\otimes B\otimes S^*$. The dimensions $r,e,c$ of its three modes are pairwise coprime. Every graph isomorphism lifts to a determinant-one tensor transporter with permutation actions on $A,B$; all determinant-one transporters arise this way. Every general-linear transporter is a graph lift times $(uI,wI,(uw)^{-1}I)$. These properties, the hidden-coordinate recovery algorithm over fixed fields, and the polynomial dimensions are proved in the [integral graph packet](Signed.md). They are the inputs from that construction.

Choose an incidence $c_0$ within the fixed core and use the basis $(c_i-c_0)_{i\ne0}$ of $S$. This simply re-expresses the known model; it can equivalently be used as the original convention by ordering $c_0$ last. Use a consistent core and layer ordering on both model outputs. Graph transport acts by an actual permutation in this basis and its dual, including between distinct isomorphic graphs. Fix another core incidence, so that each of the three modes has a distinguished basis element fixed by all graph automorphisms.

## A graph-equivariant inverse to the derivative

In the matrix description $M=\operatorname{Hom}(S,A\otimes B)$, the derivative of the group action is
$$
 d(X,Y,Z)=(X\otimes1+1\otimes Y)\iota+\iota Z^{\mathsf T}.
$$
We construct a linear map
$$
 L_G:M\longrightarrow\mathfrak{sl}(A)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(B)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(S^*)
 \quad\hbox{with}\quad L_G d=1
$$
on these trace-zero inputs. Its construction commutes with every graph isomorphism. Choosing an arbitrary left inverse of a matrix by pivoting would not ensure that property.

1. For distinct row vertices $w,u$, pick an edge $f$ from $u$ to a core anchor other than $w$, and a second distinct edge $g$ from $u$ to a core anchor. The fixed anchor order determines both. For any $\Delta\in M$, take the coefficient at $(w,f)$ of $\Delta(c_{uf}-c_{ug})$ as $X_{wu}$. This is a forbidden cell outside $U$. In a genuine derivative the third-mode term therefore vanishes there, the $Y$-term stays on row $u$, and the indicated coefficient is precisely $X_{wu}$. Put zero on the diagonal for now, then subtract the derivative of the recovered off-diagonal $X$.
2. For distinct edges $h,f$, choose an endpoint $u$ of $f$ outside $h$, breaking ties by the intrinsic orientation of $f$. Pick a core row $v\ne u$ and a core incidence $c_{vg}$ using fixed core positions. Read the coefficient at $(u,h)$ of the residual map on $c_{uf}-c_{vg}$. On a genuine derivative this is $Y_{hf}$, because $(u,h)$ is forbidden, the balancing cell has a different row, and the remaining first-mode matrix is diagonal. Subtract the derivative of this off-diagonal $Y$.
3. Let $\ell:A\otimes B\to k$ sum the incident-cell coefficients and ignore the forbidden cells. For each incidence $i=(u,f)$, set $q_i=\ell(\Delta_{\rm rem}(c_i-c_0))$, taking $q_0=0$. On a derivative, the unknown diagonal entries $x_u,y_f$ satisfy $q_i=x_u+y_f-(x_{u_0}+y_{f_0})$. Choose a graph-invariant spanning tree of the bipartite vertex-edge incidence graph as follows. Choose once a tree on the ordered core, and attach each moving vertex to its first designated anchor. Replace the edges of this vertex tree by the two incidences of each, then hang each remaining edge node from its intrinsically first endpoint. Fix the potential at a core root to zero and propagate $x'_u+y'_f=q_i$ on the tree. A genuine derivative gives consistent equations on all incidences, and the entries obtained differ from its $x_u,y_f$ only by separate global constants. This follows since a difference satisfying a constant sum on a connected bipartite tree is constant on each part. For a general $\Delta$, tree propagation still defines a linear map.
4. Subtract the actions of the recovered diagonal entries. Project the residual to $S$ by the map $c_i\mapsto c_i-c_0$ on incidences and zero on other cells. It is the identity on $S$. This gives $Z^{\mathsf T}$. The resulting triple recovers any general-linear derivative up to an additive triple $(aI,bI,-(a+b)I)$; the constants lost in the previous step are exactly compensated in $Z$.
5. Adding such a triple changes the traces by $(ra,eb,-c(a+b))$. Some two of the three equations have an invertible coefficient matrix in $k$, because $r,e,c$ are pairwise coprime. Pick the first such pair in mode order and solve to set its two traces to zero. For a genuine trace-zero derivative, this unique pair of scalar corrections recovers the exact original triple, thus making the third trace zero as well. On a general input, subtract the remaining trace times the projection onto the distinguished basis element of that mode along its other basis elements. All three outputs now have trace zero.

Every choice used only fixed anchors, layer roles, invariant edge directions or mode dimensions. Local readouts, propagation, projection and trace correction commute with the graph maps. This proves the claimed equivariance and the left inverse. In particular
$$
 M=d(\mathfrak{sl}(A)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(B)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(S^*))\ \oplus\ \ker L_G .
$$
The splitting is obtained without dividing by a group order.

## Arbitrarily high powers of the parameter

First put the reduction of the tensor literally in the model $T_G$. Normalize its coefficients in order $j=1,\ldots,s-1$. If the present coefficient of $t^j$ is $\Delta_j$, let $(X,Y,Z)=L_G(\Delta_j)$. Apply $I-t^jX,I-t^jY,I-t^jZ$ in their respective modes. These matrices are invertible. Their determinants equal one modulo $t^{j+1}$ because all three traces vanish. Multiply each matrix by the diagonal map with its inverse determinant in the distinguished basis position and ones elsewhere. This makes the step special-linear over $R$ and does not affect coefficients through degree $j$. Hence the resulting coefficient is $\Delta_j-dL_G(\Delta_j)\in\ker L_G$, while all earlier coefficients remain as normalized. Store the normalizing matrices as they are multiplied. Each operation is polynomial in the listed coefficients, including when $s$ grows. These instructions commute with graph transports.

Suppose two normalized tensors are related by a special-linear triple congruent to the identity. If the triple is nonidentity, let $j$ be the first degree at which a component differs. Its leading coefficient lies in the three trace-zero algebras. The two normalized tensors have degree-$j$ difference equal to its derivative $d(X,Y,Z)$. That difference is also in $\ker L_G$; applying the left inverse forces $X=Y=Z=0$, a contradiction. Therefore equality is the complete comparison under this congruence group, and a congruence transporter between two equivalent unnormalized tensors is unique. This proves the result at every order at once. Vanishing of infinitesimal stabilizers by itself would give uniqueness without this explicit efficient comparison.

For general model residues the transporter reduces to a graph isomorphism $\pi$ with its unique special-linear lift. Comparing normalized tensors then amounts to testing whether $\pi$ also permutes the full arrays of normalized coefficient values into each other. Represent these values as a colored relation on an ordered row vertex, edge and incidence other than $c_0$. For instance add a relation vertex colored by the full coefficient list of degrees $1,\ldots,s-1$, attach it to those three objects through distinct slot types, and keep all incidence, layer and base-graph data. Index edges by their endpoints and incidences by their edge and vertex. Unique relation and slot vertices make every lift of the underlying permutation unique. Color names can be encoded with rigid type markers or jointly sorted before an ordinary colored-GI comparison. There is one polynomial-size graph comparison, and a graph witness gives the tensor witness by composing with the stored normalizers. The converse and exact counts follow by uniqueness.

Finally, on a raw tensor in arbitrary bases whose reduction is on the general-linear model image, use its graph decoder to get $F_X:T_{G_X}\to\bar X$ and $F_Y:T_{G_Y}\to\bar Y$. These matrices extend as constant matrices over $R$. A residual special-linear comparison exists only if the displayed determinant triples of $F_X,F_Y$ can be made equal by precomposing one with the two model scalars; check this over the fixed field. Abort on dimension mismatch or incompatible determinants. When compatible, make that adjustment, apply the two inverse matrices to the actual input arrays, and proceed with the model comparison. Because the adjusted triples have the same determinant in each corresponding mode, an isomorphism in model coordinates is special-linear exactly when it is in the input coordinates. The field model recognizer can reject residues outside the family. Conversely constant graph tensors over $R$ are equivalent exactly when their graphs are isomorphic, by reduction and the integral graph lifts. This establishes both many-one bounds.

The application is to arbitrary nilpotent perturbations of this particular residue class. An arbitrary tensor over $\mathbb F_3$ need not have such a residue, and this theorem supplies no unrestricted signed-to-ordinary reduction in that field.
