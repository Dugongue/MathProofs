# Complementary exterior degrees admit an ordinary TI determinant test

**Theorem.** Let F be a fixed finite field of odd characteristic and let a,b,c be positive integers with sum m. For tensors over F promised to be independently rebased copies of the trilinear form

    (x,y,z) ↦ vol(x ∧ y ∧ z)

on Λ^a F^m, Λ^b F^m, and Λ^c F^m, there is a Las Vegas polynomial-time Turing reduction computing the exact determinant profile using ordinary TI decision over F. Polynomial time is measured in the explicit arrays. The degrees need not be equal. In particular this covers cases where 3 to the power m is superpolynomial in the array length. The algorithm needs no exterior coordinates and no invariant projective lists. This is a promise algorithm. Given the degrees, ordinary TI can check the promise by comparison with the explicit standard tensor.

## Derivations determine exterior charts

Use factor maps acting on the inputs of the trilinear form. An infinitesimal stabilizer is a triple (A,B,C) with the sum of the three single-slot evaluations equal to zero. Solve these equations on the raw array. Let ρ_d be the differential of the natural action on the d-th exterior power. Every triple in standard coordinates has the form

    (ρ_a(X)+λ₁I, ρ_b(X)+λ₂I, ρ_c(X)+λ₃I),    tr X + λ₁+λ₂+λ₃=0.

The forward identity follows by differentiating the top exterior multiplication law. For the converse, index the standard bases by subsets. An off-diagonal entry taking I to I' with at least two replacements must vanish. Partition the complement of I' into the other two slot subsets, placing one element of I minus I' in each. In the derivation equation at those three inputs, the other two terms vanish from a repeated index and the first term detects precisely the selected entry.

For single replacements i→j, compare I={i}∪R and J={i}∪S for disjoint sets R,S excluding i,j, of sizes a−1,b−1. Use the rest of the ground set outside R,S,i,j as the third input. The two replacement terms cancel. Divide each coefficient by the sign of the corresponding coefficient of ρ(E_ji), which itself has cancellation. This equates the normalized single-replacement coefficients across the first two slots. As R varies by one-element swaps, any consecutive R,R' admit a common disjoint S. In fact the available set has size m−2 and the union of consecutive R,R' has size at most a. The inequality m−2−a≥b−1 follows from c≥1. The Johnson graph on such R is connected. This proves constancy throughout the first slot, and every S has an R disjoint from it. Use the same argument on the other pairs. Thus the off-diagonals are one common exterior action.

After subtracting it, evaluate the remaining diagonal identity on partitions. Swapping i,j between two parts shows that the diagonal increment from replacing i by j is common across the first two slots for disjoint R,S. The same connectivity argument makes the increment independent of the subset and common to all slots. To add successive increments, choose a single subset outside any three selected indices, possible since every slot has dimension degree at most m−2. The increments therefore have the form t_j−t_i. Summing t_i on each subset accounts for the diagonal up to one constant per slot. The partition equation gives the trace relation. This argument works in all characteristics, including when an exterior differential kills scalar matrices.

## Find matching cyclic types with polynomial expected sampling

Work over a safe prime degree extension K of F, of size Q>4m. One can efficiently choose a prime ℓ avoiding both the characteristic and the order of the product of the three general linear groups over F, and use a power ℓ^r as the degree if needed. The order of each corresponding group over K agrees modulo ℓ with its base order by Fermat's congruence. For a norm determinant tuple, the set of K-transporters satisfying it is empty or a coset of a subgroup of that product, so its size is prime to ℓ. It is stable under base Frobenius. All its non-singleton Frobenius orbits have size divisible by ℓ, so a nonempty set has a base-fixed transporter. At that transporter the determinant norm is the base determinant to the extension degree. Invert this power to recover the exact base profile. The prime choice and explicit field construction have polynomial bit complexity by the earlier safe extension argument. [Safe extension theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignSearchSpace/Extensions.md)

Preconstruct E of degree m over K. This second field is only for coordinates. In every query require the physical mode maps to be K-linear maps over K itself, as detailed below. Sample a uniformly random derivation triple over K. The displayed classification makes its law the pushforward of a uniform X and two independent uniform scalars. For a normal element α of E over K, its m Frobenius conjugates form a basis and are distinct. Almost every α is normal for large Q. Indeed the determinant of those conjugate column vectors is a nonzero polynomial of degree m in the K-coordinates, by the normal basis theorem, and Schwartz–Zippel bounds its zero probability by m/Q. There are therefore at least (1−m/Q)Q^m/m irreducible degree-m polynomials with normal roots. Each corresponds to one GL_m(K) conjugacy class with centralizer E×. The resulting fraction of all matrices X is Ω(1/m), using |GL_m(K)|/Q^(m²) bounded below by a positive constant. An X of this type has distinct subset sums of any fixed size and distinct nonzero ordered root differences, by independence of the conjugates. Ordered differences are distinct because the characteristic is odd.

Hereafter take odd characteristic for the stated constructive root test. Require that all three projected matrices have simple spectra splitting in E. In the first mode form the adjoint operator, the commutator with the projected matrix on the first projection of the derivation algebra. Require exactly m(m−1) nonzero eigenvalues, each with multiplicity one and each in E. Require that none of these eigenvalues be fixed by the Q^r-Frobenius for any proper divisor r of m. Linear algebra and finite-field factoring test all conditions in polynomial bit time. A normal root guarantees all of them, because a nontrivial power of an m-cycle fixes no ordered pair of endpoints. Conversely simplicity forces the natural X to be diagonalizable with distinct eigenvalues. If it has a nonzero nilpotent part, its image under ρ is a nonzero nilpotent commuting with its semisimple part. The kernel of ρ is contained in scalars, as is seen by its single-replacement entries and by swapping diagonal entries in subsets. So no such nilpotent part is compatible with simple spectrum. A repeated natural eigenvalue would also give a repeated subset sum. The nonzero adjoint roots are then precisely the ordered differences, distinct by the count condition. Their splitting in E implies that the natural Frobenius permutation has order dividing m, since a permutation fixing all ordered pairs fixes all vertices. Any cycle of length 2≤r<m would fix an ordered pair at its r-th power, and two singleton cycles would fix one already. Hence it is an m-cycle. When m≥3 this also rules out a mixture of only singleton cycles. The acceptance test terminates in expected O(m) trials on each promised input.

## Mark the eigenspaces while retaining every determinant tuple

In each mode take the centralizer of the sampled A in that mode's projected derivation algebra. It equals the image of the natural diagonal matrices in an eigenbasis of X, together with scalars. Indeed off-diagonal matrix units have nonzero commutator eigenvalues, and the exterior differential is injective on their span. Its joint lines are exactly the simple eigenlines already computed over E. Add the three centralizer spaces as markers using their evaluation maps on the corresponding modes. An ordinary marked transporter must preserve those spaces by conjugation and therefore permute their joint lines.

Every attainable K-determinant tuple is still attained by a marked transporter. Any unmarked transporter maps the derivations on the source to the target. Its transported natural eigenline chart in the target and the chosen target chart both have the cyclic Frobenius type. In the target's hidden natural coordinates, choose a nonzero eigenvector in E on one line in each cycle. Its successive conjugates form bases and the map between the two resulting bases is K-rational. Multiply that map by an element of the centralizer E× to adjust its determinant to one, using surjectivity of the norm E×→K×. The resulting natural SL_m(K) element acts simultaneously on all three exterior modes, matches the three centralizers, and has determinant one in each mode by the exterior determinant identity. It preserves the tensor by its top exterior action. Composing the original transporter with this correction preserves the preset determinant tuple. This works also for automorphisms of a single input.

## Run ordinary TI queries on the marked pair

Extend the tensors to E and restrict coordinates to K so all spaces remain explicit. Mark scalar multiplication by one extension generator on each E-linear space, forcing E-linearity. Mark the physical Frobenius operator relative to the original K-coordinates in each of the three modes. An E-linear map commuting with these Frobenius operators is exactly a map defined over K. Use the evaluation maps to tie the centralizer markers to the physical modes. Prescribing a joint line image is an additional inclusion mark. The ordinary mixed-system compiler with uniform packing reduces these queries to ordinary TI. One may further restrict K to F and mark K-linearity. A K-rational marked transporter extends to the auxiliary maps in these queries. The auxiliary line maps need not have their own base Frobenius constraint. [Uniform packing and flags](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IntrinsicTensorSubspaces/Flags.md) · [Ordinary tensor-system compiler](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

Use greedy matching of the finite lists of individual eigenlines and stabilizer-chain coset representatives, as in the previous determinant-profile algorithm. Each mode has exactly as many lines as its dimension. Once a full matching has been selected, the maps are diagonal scalings between the matched frames. Conjugating their centralizer spaces then depends only on the selected permutations, which can be checked directly. The form-preservation equations compare tensor coefficients through products of scalars. Physical Frobenius sends each chosen line basis vector to a known multiplier times another basis vector. Commuting with it gives monomial equations on the unknown scalars, with the Q-power on the source of the semilinear operation. Norm constraints on the K-determinants are monomial equations too, with exponent (Q−1)/(|F|−1) and explicit frame ratios lying in E. Equations in these E-valued scalars are solved for feasibility by integer Smith form and power residue tests in E×. Computing feasible norm tuples needs no discrete logarithms.

One full matching gives a reference norm tuple. To compute the norm image of the marked stabilizer, use each chosen coset representative's feasible norm tuple and the full norm image of the kernel of the action on lines. This returns the entire profile under the marked group. Every unmarked determinant tuple survived the markings by the correction proved above. Apply safe norm descent to finish. Reversing conventions between trilinear forms and triple products inverts each relevant determinant coordinate and does not affect determinant-one testing.

This establishes the theorem in odd characteristic. Over the two-element field ordinary and special-linear coordinates already coincide. The proof uses labelled charts that can be selected independently, because a determinant-neutral subgroup moves them into agreement. It makes no claim that arbitrary tensors have such a subgroup. All proofs are informal.
