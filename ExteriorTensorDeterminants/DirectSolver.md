# Recovering an exterior multiplication chart from raw tensors

**Theorem.** Over the field with three elements, there is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time algorithm which computes the full determinant-sign profile for raw tensors promised to be isomorphic to
$$
T_0(x,y,z)=\operatorname{vol}(x\wedge y\wedge z),\qquad x,y,z\in\bigwedge^k F^{3k},
$$
where $k\geq2$ and the three coordinate changes can be unrelated. There is no requirement to supply an exterior basis, a decomposable point, or a canonical list. The algorithm needs no tensor-isomorphism oracle. It gives the signed verdict on the exterior-product family from the preceding packet, including the examples with trivial sign image where every invariant spanning projective list is superpolynomial. Section 7 also recognizes the orbit from arbitrary raw arrays. Sections 1–5 also give a self-contained Las Vegas variant using a direct spectral search. Section 6 replaces that search and proves the deterministic bound. The proof and complexity analysis are informal.

## 1. The full space of infinitesimal stabilizers

Write $m=3k$ and $N=\binom{m}{k}$. Let $e_I$ be the increasing wedge basis indexed by $k$-subsets of $[m]$. For an $m$-square matrix $M$ let $\rho(M)$ be the infinitesimal action on $\bigwedge^k F^m$, obtained by applying $M$ to each factor of a pure wedge in turn and summing. The space of tensor derivations consists of triples $(A_1,A_2,A_3)$ such that
$$
T_0(A_1x,y,z)+T_0(x,A_2y,z)+T_0(x,y,A_3z)=0.
$$
This space is precisely the triples
$$
A_i=\rho(M)+\lambda_i I\quad (1\leq i\leq3),\qquad
\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=-\operatorname{tr}M.
$$
Every displayed triple is a derivation by the Leibniz rule in the exterior algebra and the trace action on its top degree. We prove the converse directly, so the description applies even when the characteristic divides $m$ or $k$.

An off-diagonal coefficient with column $I$ and row $I'$ vanishes if $|I\setminus I'|\geq2$. Partition the complement of $I'$ into two $k$-sets, each meeting $I\setminus I'$. Evaluating the derivation relation at $I$ and those two sets leaves exactly that coefficient up to sign. The other two terms each contain a repeated index in unchanged positions. The same holds in each mode.

For a replacement of $i$ by $j$, compare inputs indexed by $I=I_0\cup\{i\}$ and $J=J_0\cup\{i\}$ with $I_0,J_0$ disjoint $(k-1)$-sets avoiding $i,j$. In the third mode use the complement of $I_0\cup J_0\cup\{i,j\}$. The corresponding replacement coefficients in the first two modes cancel in the derivation relation. Normalize each by the sign of its entry in $\rho(E_{ji})$. The normalized coefficients must agree, since the known simultaneous action of $\rho(E_{ji})$ makes the same two terms cancel with nonzero coefficients. For any two choices of $I_0$ a common disjoint choice of $J_0$ exists, as $3k-2-2(k-1)=k$. This proves independence of the $(k-1)$-set and equality across the two modes. Compare the first and third modes in the same way. Subtracting the single common off-diagonal matrix action leaves three diagonal maps.

For the diagonal maps the relation says that the three entries sum to zero on each partition into $k$-sets. Start with $I_0\cup\{i\}$ and $J_0\cup\{j\}$ in two modes, then swap $i,j$ while keeping the third set fixed. Subtracting the two equations makes the increment for replacing $i$ by $j$ in one mode equal to the same increment in the other mode. The common-disjoint-set calculation again shows that this increment depends only on $i,j$, across all modes. Using a fixed $(k-1)$-set avoiding $i,j,l$ shows that the increment from $i$ to $l$ is the sum of those from $i$ to $j$ and $j$ to $l$. Thus there are scalars $t_i$ such that the increment is $t_j-t_i$. The graph on all $k$-sets whose edges are single replacements is connected, so in mode $r$ the diagonal entries are $\sum_{i\in I}t_i+\lambda_r$. The relation on partitions gives the asserted trace equation.

The derivation space in raw coordinates is obtained by solving the displayed linear equations there. Under the hidden basis changes its projections are conjugate to the three displayed maps. Uniformly sampling this linear space is equivalent in distribution to using uniform $M,\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ and solving for $\lambda_3$, since the parametrization is a surjective linear map with equal fiber sizes.

## 2. Find a simple exterior spectrum over an odd extension

Take $L=\mathbb F_{3^e}$ with odd $e=O(\log N)$ and $Q=|L|\geq8N^2$. Let $r$ be the largest odd integer at most $m$, and take $E=\mathbb F_{Q^r}$ containing $L$. Both extension degrees are polynomial, and the absolute degree of $E$ is odd. Construct the extension fields by fixed-characteristic polynomial-time finite-field algorithms. Sample derivation triples over $L$ and keep one for which each of the three projected matrices has $N$ distinct eigenvalues in $E$. Eigenvalues and their bases are computable in polynomial bit time. For example, to split a known separable split polynomial over $E$, use gcd with the equations for the traces to the prime field after multiplication by each element of a field basis. Nondegeneracy of the trace pairing separates all roots, and the trace polynomials are computed by modular powering. Split separability can first be tested by divisibility of the characteristic polynomial by $X^{|E|}-X$. [Fixed-characteristic extension construction](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

The probability of success in a trial is $\Omega(1/m)$. If $m$ is odd, consider $M$ with irreducible characteristic polynomial of degree $m$. If $m$ is even, consider $M$ with characteristic polynomial the product of a degree $m-1$ irreducible and a linear factor. For either type and fixed polynomial the orbit under conjugation has size $|\mathrm{GL}_m(L)|/(Q^r-1)$ in the odd case, with an additional divisor $Q-1$ in the even case, by the multiplicative groups of the two irreducible modules. There are at least $Q^r/(2r)$ monic irreducibles of degree $r$, since fewer than half of the elements of $\mathbb F_{Q^r}$ are in proper intermediate fields. In the even case there are also $Q$ choices of linear factor. Since $|\mathrm{GL}_m(L)|/Q^{m^2}\geq1/2$, the type event has probability at least $1/(4r)$ in either case.

Conditioned on that type, the degree-$r$ irreducible can be sampled by a uniform element $\alpha$ of degree $r$ over $L$ and taking its minimal polynomial. Its eigenvalues are the $Q$-power conjugates of $\alpha$. In the even case an independent uniform $b\in L$ supplies the last eigenvalue. A difference between two exterior $k$-sums has the form
$$
\sum_{j=0}^{r-1} c_j\alpha^{Q^j}+c b,
$$
where each coefficient is $0,1$ or $-1$, and they are not all zero. Omit the term involving $b$ in the odd case. If $c\ne0$, uniform $b$ supplies at most probability $1/Q$ of zero. Otherwise this is a nonzero polynomial in $\alpha$ of degree at most $Q^{r-1}$. Its vanishing probability conditioned on degree $r$ is at most $2/Q$. Taking the union bound over at most $\binom N2$ differences gives conditional probability of success at least $3/4$. Adding the scalar shifts $\lambda_i$ never changes the simplicity or splitness here.

Every accepted triple is valid for the reconstruction. Indeed over the algebraic closure the eigenvalue multiset of $\rho(M)$ consists of the $k$-sums of the eigenvalues of $M$ with their multiplicities (triangularize $M$). If $M$ had two equal eigenvalues, two distinct $k$-subsets using one or the other would yield equal sums. So $M$ has distinct eigenvalues. Its Frobenius over $E$ permutes the eigenvalues, and hence their $k$-subsets. The corresponding distinct sums belong to $E$, so that permutation fixes every $k$-subset. The action on these subsets is faithful, so all eigenvalues of $M$ are in $E$. Consequently the raw eigenbasis lines in each mode are precisely the coordinate $k$-wedges of a common hidden eigenbasis of the natural $m$-dimensional space, transported into raw frames.

## 3. Recover and align the hidden coordinates

For the raw eigenline bases in any two modes form a bipartite graph by joining a pair when its contraction in the third mode is nonzero. This is disjointness of $k$-sets. Two vertices in one mode with underlying intersection size $s$ have exactly $\binom{k+s}{k}$ common neighbors in the other mode. Since this count is strictly increasing in $s$, the ordinary Johnson graph on each mode is recovered by taking intersection size $k-1$.

Here is a polynomial-time reconstruction of the ground set from a Johnson graph on $t$-subsets of $[m]$, with $2\leq t\leq k$. For any adjacent pair $P+i,P+j$ with common $(t-1)$-set $P$, their common neighbors split into two disjoint cliques with no edges between them. One contains the other $t$-sets containing $P$. The other contains the $t$-sets lying in $P\cup\{i,j\}$ other than the two endpoints. Their sizes are $m-t-1$ and $t-1$ respectively, so the larger identifies the first clique. Adjoin the endpoints and deduplicate these larger cliques over all edges. They are exactly the stars belonging to $(t-1)$-sets. Two distinct stars have nonempty intersection precisely when the two underlying $(t-1)$-sets are adjacent in the Johnson graph at level $t-1$. Repeating gives the ground set at $t=1$. Reversing the incidence data identifies every original vertex with its $k$-subset. At every level the vertex count is at most $N$.

Match the ground sets between modes by comparing their recovered stars of $k$-sets through the bipartite disjointness graph. The two stars for the same ground element have no disjoint pair. Stars for different ground elements have a disjoint pair since $m\geq2k$. This determines the matching between the three ground sets without any eigenvalue correspondence or canonical naming choice. Give the common ground set any order and compare it to the standard order.

## 4. Compute the determinant norms of a normalization

Let $P_i$ be the invertible matrix of the computed eigenvectors in raw mode $i$, ordered by their $k$-subsets. An ordinary isomorphism from the standard form to the raw form exists among the maps $P_i\operatorname{diag}(s_{i,I})$ with $s_{i,I}\ne0$. In fact the eigenline bases are hidden exterior basis vectors up to independent nonzero scalars. Once the common natural ground order is fixed, one global volume change is absorbed in one mode. Thus the required coefficients in the new frame are obtained by a monomial equation for every partition $(I,J,K)$:
$$
s_{1,I}s_{2,J}s_{3,K}\,T(P_1e_I,P_2e_J,P_3e_K)=T_0(e_I,e_J,e_K).
$$
All other entries already vanish by the reconstructed support. A desired norm determinant sign $\delta_i\in\{1,-1\}$ gives one further monomial equation with exponent $(|E|-1)/2$ on each $s_{i,I}$, using
$$
\operatorname{Norm}_{E/F}\det(P_i\operatorname{diag}(s_i))
=\operatorname{Norm}_{E/F}\det P_i\cdot \left(\prod_I s_{i,I}\right)^{(|E|-1)/2}.
$$
This accounts for the actual raw frame determinant. Test the eight sign tuples to obtain one feasible normalization tuple. One always exists under the promise.

The monomial system is polynomial-time decidable without discrete logarithms. Apply integer Smith normal form with polynomial-bit-size unimodular basis changes to its exponent matrix. Row changes replace constants by the corresponding monomials, and column changes give bijective monomial substitutions over $E^\times$. For a diagonal exponent $a$ and constant $b$ the equation $x^a=b$ is solvable precisely when $b^{(|E|-1)/\gcd(a,|E|-1)}=1$. Zero rows require constant one. This uses only polynomial-bit-size arithmetic and field powering. [Smith normal form with transformation matrices](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

## 5. Pass back to the base field and compare the pair

The full stabilizer of the standard form over any finite field consists of $(u_i\bigwedge^k g)_{i=1}^3$ with common $g\in\mathrm{GL}_m$ and $\prod_i u_i\det g=1$. The proof identifies the decomposable locus by the minimum nonzero slice rank, uses Chow's Grassmannian automorphism theorem, and forces the three induced projectivities to agree by preservation of incidence of $k$-planes. This was proved for the exterior family in the preceding packet, with geometric and finite-field descent checked explicitly. [Exterior-product stabilizer](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IntrinsicTensorSubspaces/MatrixObstruction.md) · [Grassmannian automorphisms over finite fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.5547)

Put $b=\binom{m-1}{k-1}$. The determinant tuple of such a stabilizer element is $(u_i^N d^b)_{i=1}^3$, where $d=\det g$. Thus its image over the base field is the explicit subgroup $H$ obtained by enumerating $u_1,u_2,d\in F^\times$ and setting $u_3=(u_1u_2d)^{-1}$. The norm of the extension determinant image is exactly $H$. One inclusion follows by applying the norm to $u_i,d$ and their relation. The reverse follows by choosing arbitrary norm lifts of $u_1,u_2,d$ from the base and then defining $u_3$ by the relation. A matrix in $\mathrm{GL}_m(E)$ with determinant $d$ always exists.

Every raw tensor in the promise has a base-field normalization, and any extension normalization differs from one by an extension stabilizer. On a base-field sign the norm through the odd-degree extension is the identity, so any computed normalization norm tuple represents exactly the genuine base-field transporter coset modulo $H$. Do this separately for the two raw inputs and take the second tuple times the inverse of the first, then multiply by $H$. This is their entire determinant profile over $F$. The determinant-one condition tests whether the tuple $(1,1,1)$ belongs to that coset. The contravariant versus covariant convention does not change determinant-one conditions. For the obstruction sequence $k=3\cdot2^s$ both exponents are even, so $H$ is trivial. In that case each input has a unique computed normalization sign tuple.

All matrices, basis reconstructions and monomial systems have polynomial size in the explicit tensor format. Acceptance checks are exact and the spectral search takes an expected $O(m)$ trials. Neither the promise nor verification of the signed answer depends on an unproved distributional assumption. The theorem covers raw tensors promised to be in this standard exterior multiplication orbit for each $k$, rather than recognizing all tensors that admit such a chart.


## 6. Deterministic extraction by Cartan and Frobenius descent

This section upgrades the theorem from Las Vegas to deterministic polynomial bit time. Keep the same large odd-degree field $L/F$ but replace the random search and its specific splitting field as follows.

Use the algorithm of de Graaf, Ivanyos and Rónyai to find a locally regular element of the adjoint action of the tensor derivation Lie algebra over $L$. In de Graaf's full account, the algorithm and Proposition 3.7 in Section 3.2 use the finite-field non-nilpotence routine of Section 3.3. Proposition 3.13 in Section 3.4 shows that the Fitting nullspace of the resulting adjoint map is a Cartan subalgebra. The stated size requirement is at least one more than the Lie algebra dimension. Our field is larger than this dimension by Section 1 and $Q\geq8N^2$. The computation uses polynomially many field operations and linear equation solutions, so fixed-characteristic extension arithmetic implements it in polynomial bit time. [De Graaf–Ivanyos–Rónyai](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/computing-cartan-subalgebras-of-lie-algebras/) · [Full proof and algorithms, Sections 3.2–3.4](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/files/1475343/495936.pdf)

Here this Cartan is the image of a full diagonal Lie algebra in the hidden natural space, together with the independent scalar shifts. To prove it, Section 1 identifies the Lie algebra as the image of $\mathfrak{gl}_m\oplus L^2$, with exactly a central kernel. In fact $\rho(M)$ can be scalar only if the off-diagonal entries of $M$ vanish and all its diagonal entries coincide. This follows by comparing exterior basis entries for a single replacement. The kernel therefore consists of scalar natural matrices and their compensating scalar shifts. Lift the computed element whose adjoint gives the Cartan to a matrix $M$ and scalar shifts. Over the algebraic closure, the Fitting zero space of the adjoint action on $\mathfrak{gl}_m$ is the block-diagonal space preserving each generalized eigenspace of $M$. On a block between distinct eigenvalues, the adjoint is a nonzero scalar plus a nilpotent and hence invertible. Within a block it is nilpotent. Adding the scalar-shift directions and taking a quotient by the central kernel gives the same Fitting calculation for the tensor derivations. If any generalized eigenspace had dimension at least two, the Cartan would contain the images of a diagonal matrix $D$ and an elementary matrix $E_{12}$ within that block satisfying $[D,E_{12}]=E_{12}$. The image of $E_{12}$ is nonzero because the kernel is central and $E_{12}$ is not scalar. Its iterated commutators with $D$ never vanish, contradicting nilpotence of a Cartan subalgebra. Hence all the natural generalized eigenspaces have dimension one. The Cartan is as claimed.

Take a basis of this Cartan over $L$. Combine its elements greedily, retaining at each stage a combination whose first-mode projection has largest minimal-polynomial degree among $\binom N2+1$ choices of the next scalar. In the common hidden diagonal basis all these matrices are semisimple, so the degree counts distinct eigenvalues. A pair of diagonal coordinates separated by the previous combination or by the next basis element can fail to be separated by at most one scalar choice. Pairs separated by neither remain equal. Therefore maximizing the number of distinct values produces exactly the common refinement of the two partitions. Iterate over the basis. The full diagonal natural algebra distinguishes every pair of $k$-subsets, so the final combination has $N$ different eigenvalues. The other two modes differ in hidden coordinates by scalar shifts and have the same separation property.

Determine the splitting degree $R$ over $L$ of the first-mode characteristic polynomial using distinct-degree factorization. This only needs successive gcd tests against $X^{Q^j}-X$ for $j\leq N$, followed by the least common multiple of the appearing degrees. Frobenius permutes the natural eigenvalues and therefore permutes the distinct exterior sums by its induced permutation of the $k$-subsets. This action is faithful, so $R$ is the order of a permutation of $m$ points, and in particular $R\leq2^m$. Since $m=O(\log N)$, constructing and using $E=\mathbb F_{Q^R}$ is still polynomial in the explicit input size. Construct the inclusion of $L$ by locating a root of its defining polynomial in this field. The same split-polynomial procedure from Section 2 computes the eigenbases. Section 3 labels and aligns them exactly as before.

Here $R$ may be even. To retain signs, descend chart transporters to $L$ before reading their determinants. Let $\sigma$ denote the $Q$-power Frobenius of $E/L$. It permutes the eigenlines within each mode by the same permutation induced from a permutation $\pi$ of the recovered ground set. Compute $\pi$ by applying $\sigma$ to the eigenvectors, matching their lines, and reading its action on the recovered stars. If $b_{i,I}$ are the chosen eigenvectors in mode $i$, record the nonzero scalars $\eta_{i,I}$ from
$$
\sigma(b_{i,I})=\eta_{i,I}b_{i,\pi(I)} .
$$

In the standard natural space tensored to $E$, construct a reference basis $u_j$ satisfying $\sigma(u_j)=u_{\pi(j)}$. For each cycle of length $d$ allocate a separate $d$-dimensional coordinate block. Choose an element $\alpha$ of degree $d$ over $L$ in the degree-$d$ subfield of $E$. Vandermonde columns $(1,\alpha^{Q^j},(\alpha^{Q^j})^2,\ldots,(\alpha^{Q^j})^{d-1})^\top$ give the vectors on that cycle in cyclic order. For $d=1$ take the single coordinate vector. To find $\alpha$ deterministically, compute a basis $v_0,\ldots,v_{d-1}$ of the subfield as the fixed space of $\sigma^d$. Test $\sum_{j=0}^{d-1} t^jv_j$ for membership in the maximal proper subfields of degrees $d/p$ for primes $p\mid d$. Each proper subfield lies in the kernel of some nonzero linear functional, which excludes at most $d-1$ values of $t$. Testing $1+(d-1)\omega(d)$ distinct values of $t$ therefore suffices, where $\omega(d)$ is the number of distinct prime divisors. Membership is checked by powering. Every degree and trial count here is polynomial, and $Q\geq8N^2$ supplies sufficiently many scalars.

Let $a_I$ be the increasing $k$-wedge of the $u_j$. Then $\sigma(a_I)=\epsilon_I a_{\pi(I)}$, where $\epsilon_I$ is the sign needed to reorder the factors. Seek maps $g_i a_I=s_{i,I}b_{i,I}$. Impose the coefficient equations of Section 4 with standard coefficients now evaluated on the $a_I$. Append the monomial equations
$$
\eta_{i,I}s_{i,I}^Q=\epsilon_I s_{i,\pi(I)} .
$$
These are exactly the condition that $g_i$ commute with Frobenius. They therefore force the maps to be linear over $L$ in the standard and raw coordinate spaces. The diagonal system has an $L$-linear transporter under the promise. Indeed, hidden natural eigenvectors of the selected natural matrix can also be chosen as exact Frobenius cycles. Choose one eigenspace vector over the degree-$d$ field of the corresponding eigenvalue and apply Frobenius to obtain the others. The natural change of basis from the reference $u_j$ to those hidden vectors therefore belongs to $\mathrm{GL}_m(L)$, and it induces a standard tensor automorphism after absorbing its volume multiplier in one mode. Composing with the promised normalization to raw coordinates gives an $L$-linear transporter of the stipulated diagonal chart form. This argument only proves existence. The algorithm uses the monomial equations to compute determinant signs.

For each possible sign tuple impose the norm equations for the actual matrix $g_i=P_i\operatorname{diag}(s_i)A^{-1}$, where $A$ is the reference exterior basis matrix and $P_i$ is the raw eigenbasis matrix. Those equations have exponent $(Q-1)/2$ on each $s_{i,I}$, since the determinant of $g_i$ belongs to $L$ and its norm to $F$ is its $(Q-1)/2$-th power. The constants incorporate both known basis determinants. All exponents have polynomial bit size, so the Smith-form procedure still decides feasibility in deterministic polynomial time. Finally use the norm-image argument of Section 5 for the odd extension $L/F$. This gives the exact base determinant profiles and the claimed deterministic algorithm. The intermediate extension $E/L$ served only to construct the charts. The Frobenius equations ensure the sign test takes place over $L$.


## 7. Recognize the exterior family from arbitrary arrays

The deterministic branch also recognizes membership in the specified exterior multiplication orbit without a promise. Reject dimensions that are not of the required form. Compute the derivation Lie algebra and the Cartan algorithm over $L$ for any raw tensor with the correct dimensions. The Lie algebra has dimension at most $3N^2$, so the field-size requirement still holds. Use the same bounded greedy schedule and reject if it fails to provide $N$ distinct eigenvalues in each mode and a common splitting field of degree at most $2^m$. One may check the splitting degree of each mode separately against the proposed degree from the first mode. Only construct the field once its degree has passed the bound.

Check every graph reconstruction against the expected binomial counts. During the star construction enumerate at most all edges in a graph on at most $N$ vertices, and terminate with rejection if more distinct stars than the required $\binom{m}{t-1}$ appear, or if any common-neighbor clique calculation gives the wrong structure. Also check that the final labels actually describe every $k$-set once in each mode, that the labels identify the observed disjointness relations, and that each Frobenius action on the labels is the claimed common induced permutation. Reject when any of these tests fail. These guards keep the computation polynomial even off the promised family. All actual exterior tensors pass, by the preceding proofs.

In the final coefficient system check every coefficient, including that the zeros coincide with the zeros of the standard form in the reference frame. On nonzero coefficients require the exact multiplicative equalities of Section 4, with the Frobenius equations of Section 6. Acceptance for any norm tuple then gives an actual $L$-linear isomorphism from the standard tensor to the input. Thus an off-promise input can be accepted only if it belongs to that orbit over $L$.

An $L$-isomorphism here always implies an $F$-isomorphism. The reduced geometric stabilizer of the standard form is connected. Indeed its description in Section 5 gives a surjective morphism on geometric points from $\mathrm{GL}_m\times\mathbb G_m^2$, by choosing $g,u_1,u_2$ and setting $u_3=(u_1u_2\det g)^{-1}$. This domain is connected, and its image contains every geometric point of the reduced group. Over a finite field the reduced algebraic group is smooth, so Lang's theorem applies to it. If an isomorphism $a$ exists over the algebraic closure, its Frobenius discrepancy $a^{-1}\operatorname{Frob}(a)$ lies in this stabilizer. Lang's theorem supplies a stabilizer correction that makes the isomorphism Frobenius-fixed, hence defined over $F$. This argument involves the reduced group of geometric points, irrespective of any infinitesimal scheme structure. Once an input passes recognition, the norm and determinant-profile argument of Section 5 applies. [Lang, Algebraic Groups Over Finite Fields](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Lang-Algebraic_Groups_Over_Finite_Fields.pdf)
