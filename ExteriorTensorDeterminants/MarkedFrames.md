# Recovering wedge-tensor determinant profiles by marking Cartan subalgebras

**Theorem.** Fix the base field F = F₃. Put m = 3k with k ≥ 2 and let N = binom(m,k). On three copies of the space ΛᵏFᵐ let the trilinear form be T(x,y,z) = vol(x∧y∧z). Given two raw arrays promised to be ordinary rebasings of this form over F, the full determinant profile is computable in Las Vegas polynomial time with ordinary 3-TI decision queries over F. No exterior basis or projective lines are supplied. The argument applies for every k, including the subfamily with trivial determinant image in its stabilizer. The algorithm uses a finite extension to expose eigenvectors and explicitly imposes descent on the original transporter. It therefore works even when the extension degree is even.

Here a transporter of forms is a triple (g₁,g₂,g₃) with S(g₁x,g₂y,g₃z)=T(x,y,z). To convert to the triple tensor product convention, invert the determinant coordinates. Over F₃ inversion fixes both determinant signs.

## The derivation algebra in raw coordinates

Compute the linear space D of all triples (A₁,A₂,A₃) with

    T(A₁x,y,z) + T(x,A₂y,z) + T(x,y,A₃z) = 0.

If ρ is the action of GL_m on ΛᵏFᵐ, write dρ for the additive exterior action of m by m matrices, defined on a wedge by applying the matrix to each factor in turn and adding. In exterior coordinates D consists exactly of triples

    A_i = dρ(X) + λ_i I,
    tr(X) + λ₁ + λ₂ + λ₃ = 0.

The displayed triples do satisfy the equation by applying X to the top exterior degree. For the converse index exterior basis vectors by k-subsets. If I and I′ differ in at least two positions, choose J,K partitioning the complement of I′ and put one member of I without I′ in each. Evaluate the derivation equation at (I,J,K). The contributions from modes two and three vanish due to repetitions. The first reads the (I′,I) entry of A₁ with a nonzero sign, so that entry vanishes. The same argument works in each mode.

For distinct a,b, the possible remaining off-diagonal entries for the replacement a by b have source a∪R and target b∪R, with R avoiding a,b. Normalize them by the signs of the corresponding entries of dρ(E_ba). For disjoint R,S, evaluate at I=a∪R and J=a∪S, with K the complement of (b∪R)∪J. The first and second contributions are the only ones, and the known derivation dρ(E_ba) shows their normalized entries agree. Any two sets R,R′ of size k−1 have a common disjoint S in the complement of a,b, since that complement has size 3k−2. Thus all normalized entries for this replacement agree in modes one and two. Exchanging the roles of modes gives the agreement with mode three. Subtract the common off-diagonal exterior action.

For the remaining diagonal entries a_I,b_J,c_K the equation is a_I+b_J+c_K=0 whenever I,J,K partition the m indices. Swap a and b between I=a∪R and J=b∪S to obtain an equality of the two corresponding first differences. The same common-disjoint-set argument makes each difference depend only on a,b, and makes it the same across the three modes. A single support R avoiding a,b,c shows that the differences add along a,b,c. Hence they have the form α_a−α_b. All k-subsets are connected by replacements. Therefore every diagonal entry in mode i is λ_i plus the sum of the α entries on its subset. The partition equation now gives the stated trace condition.

Write P_i for the image of D in mode i. It is dρ(gl_m)+F I. The only X with dρ(X) scalar are scalar matrices X, by the off-diagonal entries and the single-replacement comparison on its diagonal. Consequently P_i identifies with gl_m if k is nonzero in F, and with pgl_m plus an independent scalar line if k is zero in F. In either case it is a Lie algebra of dimension m². The characteristic polynomial of ad_(dρ(X)+λI) on P_i has eigenvalues α_a−α_b over an algebraic closure, counting multiplicities, where α_a are the eigenvalues of X. To see this first compute these eigenvalues on gl_m by triangularizing X and using the action on a tensor product with its dual. In the case k=0 in F, passage to pgl_m removes a single zero and the independent scalar line restores it.

## Pick one rational field Cartan with a certified type

Choose a uniform A in P₁. Factor the characteristic polynomial of ad_A on P₁ over F. Accept A when the zero eigenvalue has multiplicity exactly m and all other irreducible factors have degree m and multiplicity one. This criterion uses only linear algebra and finite-field polynomial factorization in the input dimension. The multiplicity of zero forces all m eigenvalues of the underlying X to be distinct. Simplicity of all nonzero eigenvalues forces their ordered differences to be distinct.

Frobenius permutes the m eigenvalues of X, and by distinctness of differences it permutes the nonzero adjoint eigenvalues exactly as it permutes the ordered pairs of distinct indices. Each ordered pair has orbit length m by the factor degrees. If the underlying permutation has a cycle of length c between 2 and m−1, a pair of distinct indices within that cycle has orbit length c. If every cycle is a singleton, every pair is fixed. Thus the underlying permutation must be one m-cycle. In particular X has irreducible characteristic polynomial, and its centralizer in gl_m is the embedded field F_(3^m). With distinct eigenvalues, centralization modulo scalars gives the same field after projection. Indeed in an eigenbasis a commutator with X has zero diagonal, so if it is scalar it is zero.

An accepted sample exists with inverse polynomial probability in N. Uniform X in gl_m together with an independent uniform scalar projects to uniform A by linear surjectivity. Take X whose eigenvalues are the Frobenius conjugates of a normal element of E=F_(3^m). They are linearly independent over F, so all nonzero ordered differences are distinct, and Frobenius is an m-cycle on them in the sense of the criterion. There are at least 2^m such normal elements. In a normal basis each element is a polynomial in Frobenius applied to a fixed normal element, and is normal exactly when that polynomial is a unit modulo x^m−1. The fraction of units equals the product of 1−3^(−deg f) over the distinct irreducible factors f of x^m−1, which is at least (2/3)^m. Each normal irreducible characteristic polynomial corresponds to one similarity class of size |GL_m(F)|/(3^m−1). As |GL_m(F)|/3^(m²) ≥ 1/2, the success probability is at least (2/3)^m/(2m). Also N≥3^k, by choosing one index from each of k disjoint triples. Hence 3^m≤N³, so finding a certified sample is Las Vegas polynomial time. The explicit field E, polynomial factoring in it, and even enumeration of its elements take polynomial time in N.

For an accepted A, mark its Lie centralizer C₁ inside P₁ as a subspace of operators on the first mode. Inside D take triples whose first component belongs to C₁ and project to C₂,C₃. The derivation description shows that these are the projected exterior actions of the common field Cartan in all three modes, plus the scalar operators. Over E the field Cartan has a diagonal m-dimensional algebra of underlying coordinate actions. Distinct exterior k-subsets give distinct additive weights on this diagonal algebra even in characteristic three. Consequently the joint eigenspaces of each C_i over E are exactly N lines and span the physical mode. Compute the joint eigenspaces and their dual lines by simultaneous diagonalization in the explicit field E.

## Marking the two Cartans loses no determinant tuples

In one raw wedge tensor, any two selected C₁ subspaces correspond in a hidden exterior basis to embeddings of the same abstract field E into M_m(F). Each embedding makes Fᵐ a one-dimensional E-module. An E-module identification gives a conjugating matrix in GL_m(F). The centralizer of the source embedding consists of the multiplications by E units, whose F determinants are the surjective field norm to F×. Multiplying the conjugator by a commuting unit therefore makes its determinant one without changing its action on the field. The resulting SL_m matrix acts simultaneously on the three exterior spaces and fixes T. Its physical determinants are one because det Λᵏ(g)=(det g)^binom(m−1,k−1).

For any transporter of the raw pair, conjugation sends the full derivation space to its counterpart, and it sends the chosen source Cartan to a Cartan of the same certified type in the target. Compose with one of the determinant-one automorphisms just proved to match the target Cartan. The determinant tuple does not change. This applies also to an automorphism of a single input. It proves that the marked transporter profile equals the original one, even though the chosen Cartan is highly noncanonical. All transporters respecting this mark carry the extracted line sets to each other in every mode.

## Ordinary TI queries over the base using the split lines

Use the prior linear marking of subspace correspondences. A mark for C₁ is its evaluation C₁×U₁→U₁ with a free map on C₁. A mark for a covector line in another constraint is the quotient evaluation U_i→L* for that line L, with a free action on L*. The commuting squares impose exactly the stated correspondences. Work initially with the three raw E-extended mode spaces, C₁, and the auxiliary mark spaces. Restrict everything to F. On each originally E-linear variable add its multiplication by a field generator as a distinguished endomorphism. The action must intertwine it, which enforces E-linearity. On the three original modes also mark the F-linear map σ given by entrywise x↦x³ in the raw base coordinates. Then an E-linear mode map commutes with σ exactly when its matrix entries lie in F. No Frobenius condition is imposed on an arbitrary auxiliary line mark. A base transporter respecting the marks extends to all auxiliary variables, and every satisfying tuple restricts to an actual base transporter.

There are polynomially many components in these mixed tensor systems, each with order at most three. Use the prior uniform packing into a constant number of bounded-order tensors by occurrence copies, fixed label idempotents and a link map. The published constant-system compiler then yields ordinary 3-TI over F. The output format is polynomial in N and [E:F]. [Uniform packing](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SLAdjointMultiplicities/PRIOR-UNIFORM-PACKING.md) · [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

Use the earlier two-list algorithm to find a reference matching and stabilizer coset representatives by greedy line-marked queries for the first two modes. Its only extra step here is that the terminal linear-congruence check must enforce base rationality. After choosing projective images of both covector bases, the maps on the dual primal bases are diagonal changes with unknown nonzero multipliers. Regard the trilinear form as bilinear into the dual of its third space. Select a third basis from the products of pairs of those input basis vectors, which span by conciseness. The third map is diagonal in the respective product bases as well. Check coefficient support and equate the other coefficients. Each equation among nonzero coefficients is monomial in the unknown scalars.

For each of the three mode maps, let (s_ij) and (t_ij) give the matrices of the source and target semilinear Frobenius on the chosen bases. The equation for a diagonal matching with coefficients a_i is s_ij a_i = t_ij a_j³. A support mismatch rejects this matching. Otherwise these are again monomial equations. Explicit determinants are products of the diagonal scalars times the known change-of-frame factors. They can be required to equal the desired base values directly inside E. Over F₃ no norm is taken, so an even extension degree cannot erase a negative sign. The fully marked first-mode line bijection also determines how C₁ is conjugated. The scalar choices have no effect on this conjugation, since C₁ is diagonal in the line bases. The ordinary query certifies that this bijection matches C₁. Discrete logarithms in E may be tabulated by enumeration since |E|≤N³, and solve the resulting linear congruences. Over a larger explicit field the same solvability check can use integer Smith normal form of the exponent matrix and fast power-residue tests. Dualizing the third mode reciprocates the determinant and leaves its F₃ sign unchanged.

After greedy matching the finite list marks, record a feasible reference sign. Work down the pointwise stabilizer chain of the two chosen eigenbases. At each level try all listed possible images of the next line and complete each feasible match greedily. A terminal monomial check supplies one feasible sign for each stabilizer coset representative. The same check enumerates the signs of all automorphisms fixing both line bases projectively. These values generate the entire image of the marked stabilizer, since the lifts together with the final pointwise kernel generate it. Multiply by the reference sign to obtain the full profile. Only polynomially many oracle calls are made, with no enumeration of a Cartan orbit. [Earlier two-list algorithm](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignSearchSpace/ProjectiveLists.md)

## A sufficient generalization to large blocks

Suppose raw tensors admit a polynomial-time procedure producing an auxiliary mark taken from an intrinsic class of marks. Require that each stabilizer's determinant-one subgroup acts transitively on the class. Require also that the stabilizer of a mark yields two enumerable spanning projective lists over an explicit extension of polynomial degree, with polynomial list sizes. A transporter automatically sends a source intrinsic class to its target counterpart. The preceding correction then makes it respect the chosen marks without altering any determinant. Consequently the same ordinary TI algorithm applies whenever marks and line correspondences are represented by polynomial-size bounded-order tensor diagrams. One can use subspace marks and the filtered determinant-width criterion in place of projective lists. The key sufficient condition is symmetry under the determinant-one subgroup, and there is no requirement to enumerate the full intrinsic class.

This gives an informal sufficient theorem for large blocks that carry determinant-neutral changes of frame. For the wedge family the conditions and the extraction algorithm were proved explicitly. Arbitrary tensor stabilizers need not have the exhibited Cartan action, so the unrestricted determinant problem remains open. The document makes no claim of priority for the combination. [Derivation-algebra methods for tensor isomorphism](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04046)

## Recover the profile without an oracle by using the roots of the Cartan

The Cartan proof also gives a direct Las Vegas polynomial-time algorithm with no TI queries. It applies to arbitrary positive complementary degrees a,b,c with sum m over any fixed finite field F of odd characteristic. Use the exact derivation description and cyclic field Cartans constructed over a safe large K in [the complementary-degrees proof](ComplementaryDegrees.md). Write Q=|K| and E=F_(Q^m). The base F₃ sampling above is another option for equal degrees. Everything below works for comparison over K, followed by the norm descent to F.

For the chosen Cartan C₁⊂P₁, compute the nonzero common eigenlines for its action on P₁ by brackets. Each eigenline comes with its root, a K-linear functional on C₁ valued in E. Equivalently, diagonalize ad_A for the certified sample A, then get the root functional by evaluating commutators on a basis of C₁. In hidden natural coordinates over E these lines are indexed by ordered pairs (i,j), i≠j, and the functional is r_ij=λ_i−λ_j, where λ_i is the i-th coordinate functional on the underlying diagonal natural matrices. The functional on C₁ is well defined despite scalar ambiguity because these differences annihilate scalars. Distinct root differences on the certified A give one-dimensional root eigenspaces. Compute the corresponding root lines in the full triple derivation algebra by taking the matching nonzero eigenspace of the adjoint of a sampled lift of A. Over the split field its nonzero eigenlines are exactly the common exterior actions of the individual off-diagonal natural matrix units. Projection to P₁ identifies them with the computed lines. The roots are intrinsic to the Cartan subspace, independent of the sampled element used for computing them.

Make a graph on the root lines. Join distinct, nonopposite roots r,s if r−s is another root. This joins exactly the ordered pairs that share their first coordinate or share their second coordinate. Indeed the root functionals pull back to the coordinate differences on all natural diagonal matrices, so equality of differences is equality of their coefficient vectors there. Two roots with disjoint endpoints have four nonzero entries in their difference. If they share exactly one endpoint in opposite positions, the difference has three nonzero entries, since 2 is nonzero. The opposite-root case is excluded explicitly because it can satisfy the test in characteristic three. For an adjacent pair, the pair together with its common neighbors is exactly the star of all directed pairs having that common first or common second coordinate. Deduplicate these stars. Their intersection graph is K_(m,m) with one perfect matching removed, where the missing matching pairs the outgoing and incoming stars at each underlying vertex. For m≥3 this graph is connected, so its bipartition and its missing matching are intrinsic up to exchanging the two parts. All these constructions have polynomial size.

Frobenius acts on both parts by the same m-cycle on the underlying vertices. Thus between the two inputs there are at most 2m isomorphisms of their star graphs commuting with Frobenius. The image of one chosen star determines the image of its entire part by iterating Frobenius, and the unique missing neighbor in the other part determines all remaining images. Each choice determines the correspondence of all root lines. Every genuine marked transporter induces such a correspondence because conjugation preserves root subtraction and commutes with Frobenius.

For each physical joint eigenline compute the list of root lines that act nontrivially on it, using the projection of the full-triple root line to the given mode. In the hidden exterior basis, the projected natural matrix unit E_ij acts nontrivially on the wedge line of I exactly when j∈I and i∉I. These crossing patterns distinguish all subsets of size a, b, or c in the respective modes, since every subset is nonempty and proper. Therefore each candidate root correspondence determines all physical line correspondences, or fails because a crossing pattern has no matching line. This step needs only matrix-vector multiplication and comparison of finite sets. It works also when the star map reverses both parts. Such a candidate is discarded if its implied line correspondences do not exist.

Now write T,S in the three matched primal line bases. A candidate map scales each line by a nonzero variable. The form-preservation equations on matched nonzero coefficients are monomial equations, and zero support disagreements reject. The Frobenius equations for each mode are again s_ij a_i=t_ij a_j^Q in the line bases, with s,t the two semilinear Frobenius matrices. The determinant in each raw coordinate frame is its explicit frame ratio times the product of the scales. Prescribe a norm value to F by raising this determinant to (Q−1)/(|F|−1). Once the Frobenius equations hold the determinant lies in K, so this is exactly the norm. Solve the monomial equations by Smith normal form and power-residue tests in E×. For equal degrees over base F₃ with K=F₃, prescribe the determinant directly instead. Every positive solve represents a genuine K-transporter of the raw forms. Conversely every K-determinant tuple can be represented by a transporter matching the sampled Cartans by the determinant-neutral correction, hence appears in one of the at most 2m matchings. Enumerate the constant number of desired base tuples, with the inverse power adjustment in the safe norm descent. This computes the whole base profile without any TI queries under the raw wedge promise.

The earlier oracle algorithm remains useful for the abstract marked-frame criterion, because other tensor families may supply the frames while lacking a root graph with only polynomially many Frobenius-compatible matchings.
