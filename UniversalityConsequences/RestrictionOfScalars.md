# Restriction of scalars and four-dimensional homotopy genera

## Main theorem

Let $R$ be a commutative unital ring and $S$ a commutative unital $R$-algebra with an explicit free $R$-basis of rank $d$ and a multiplication table. Ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $S$, with mode ranks $n_1,n_2,n_3$, reduces uniformly to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $R$ with every side of dimension $O(d(1+n_1+n_2+n_3))$. One pair of output tensors works after every commutative scalar extension $R\to R'$: the output test over $R'$ is exactly the original test over $S\otimes_R R'$. There are no separability, trace-pairing, or reducedness hypotheses. The same bound holds for comparing tensors over two $R$-algebras of rank $d$ with the scalar isomorphism also unknown.

The arithmetic application uses the same coefficient-marking and linear-size compilation. For any associative unital algebra $A$ that is finite free over $\mathbb Z$, an $A$-lattice here is a left $A$-module $L$ with underlying abelian group $\mathbb Z^r$, given by action matrices for an explicit basis of $A$. There is a uniform polynomial-bit-time construction $L\mapsto X_L$ of finite, simply connected, at most four-dimensional simplicial complexes with torsion-free homology, supported in degrees $0,2,4$. Their suspensions are homotopy equivalent to wedges of three- and five-spheres, and their Betti numbers depend only on $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}A$ and $r$. For all multiplicative subsets $D\subseteq \mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, writing $B=D^{-1}\mathbb Z$ and $X_B$ for localization of a space at $B$, one has
$$
 (X_L)_B\simeq (X_M)_B
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 B\otimes_\mathbb Z L\simeq B\otimes_\mathbb Z M
   \text{ as }(B\otimes_\mathbb Z A)\text{-modules}.
$$
This includes the integral test, the rational test, and all prime-local tests on the same two complexes. Consequently any lattice genus injects into one actual Mislin genus of four-dimensional simply connected complexes.

For an order $S$ in a number field $K$, invertible fractional $S$-ideals give an injection of the full set $\operatorname{Pic}(S)$ into a single such genus. The case of comparing an ideal $I$ with $S$ gives a polynomial reduction from ideal principality, with both output spaces always in that genus. The coefficient algebra stays pinned pointwise, which keeps classes distinct even when a field automorphism interchanges them. The Betti bounds for these ideal examples depend only on $[K: \mathbb Q]$, independently of the order discriminant. Dimension four is minimal for such nontrivial genera under the torsion-free and simply connected hypotheses.

## The scalar-algebra marker

Fix an $R$-basis $s_1,\ldots,s_d$ of $S$. Use one reference sort $E$ of rank $d$. Mark a vector $s_1$ and an endomorphism $J$ with $J s_i=s_{i+1}$ for $i<d$ and $J s_d=0$. An invertible map preserving both records fixes the entire basis, since $s_i=J^{i-1}s_1$. This works over every test ring, even in residue characteristic dividing $d$. These two records have total occurrence rank $3d$, and the same number of records serves every $d$.

Make three further $R$-free sorts $V_1,V_2,V_3$, the restrictions of scalars of $S^{n_1 *},S^{n_2 *},S^{n_3}$, respectively. Include, for each $i$, the structural bilinear record $E\times V_i\to V_i$ for multiplication by $S$. Because $E$ is fixed and the records specify the actions of all $s_i$, an isomorphism is exactly $S$-linear on each $V_i$. Conversely every $S$-linear isomorphism preserves the action record.

Use one data record $V_1\times V_2\to V_3$, the restriction of scalars of the $S$-bilinear map corresponding to the three-tensor. The free dual basis of the first two $S$-modes gives this correspondence over any commutative $S$; the first two changes of basis on the source tensor become the inverse dual changes on $V_1,V_2$. The condition of preserving the record says precisely that the original tensors match. There is no need to extract an $S$-valued form from a trace recombination. In particular the construction works for inseparable field extensions, nonreduced finite free algebras, and ramified orders.

The mixed order-three input now has a constant number of records and total occurrence rank $O(d(1+\sum_i n_i))$. Apply the linear mixed-network compiler of the arbitrary-degree universality theorem to obtain one ordinary tensor on each side. Every structure constant of the network comes from fixed zero-one data and the supplied coefficients of $S$ and $T$ by at most two successive uses of the multiplication table. Thus the construction is deterministic polynomial bit time for binary integer or rational tables, uniformly in $d,n_i$. These record identities and the compiler work over each $R'$, in the corresponding extended bases. This proves the simultaneous comparison.

For two different algebras $S,S'$ with unknown scalar isomorphism, replace the fixed vector and shift by the multiplication and unit records $E\times E\to E$ and $R\to E$, with the scalar $R$-line pinned by its multiplication. The unknown map on $E$ is then exactly an $R$-algebra isomorphism. On the other sorts the same intertwining equations mean semilinearity relative to that isomorphism, and preserve the bimap exactly. The total occurrence rank is unchanged up to an absolute factor. If one instead gives a named $S$-algebra with an $R$-module presentation whose underlying module is not free, the same records can be used with the presented-diagram compiler, without the linear rank estimate. Tensor compatibility follows from its graph and lifted-product construction. [Earlier presented-diagram compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/b3bde958f05b17ea912394edb1edd18852b9c85d/HigherOrderUniversality/PresentedDiagramUniversality.md)

The finite-field restriction-of-scalars direction in the literature already follows from multiplication markers and a trace or pairing argument. The additional assertion here is the single finite-free ring reduction with a linear side bound, its simultaneous behavior at all base changes, and the semilinear variant with the scalar algebra itself supplied. [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219) · [Grochow–Qiao, §10.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S10.SS2) · [Earlier fixed-characteristic transfer](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/f98f6bdf13cb7ba606f020ef0ea07512028cc9d1/FixedCharacteristicTensorIsomorphism/Proof.md) · [Linear ring compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/b3bde958f05b17ea912394edb1edd18852b9c85d/HigherOrderUniversality/RingUniversality.md)

## Compiling lattices to integral cup tables

Use the same fixed vector and shift to pin an explicit $\mathbb Z$-basis of $A$. Use one additional free sort for $L$ and the bilinear action $A\times L\to L$ as the data record. An isomorphism of these records is exactly an $A$-linear isomorphism. The argument also applies over every $B=D^{-1}\mathbb Z$, with the same integer records. The multiplication of $A$ need not be commutative here, since $A$ itself is simply the pinned sort and the scalar ring of the compiler is $\mathbb Z$.

Compile to one ordinary free integral three-tensor per lattice, of side $O(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}A+r)$. Apply the symmetric two-slot branch of Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun Part V, Theorem 1.4 and its explicit gadgets in §3.3.3, to obtain an integral cup table
$$
 \mu_L: \mathbb Z^{n}\times\mathbb Z^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{m},
 \qquad \mu_L(x,y)=\mu_L(y,x).
$$
The third mode is the independent output variable in the symmetric action. The construction is valid over $\mathbb Z$ and each localization $B$: those rings are domains with the diagonal-full property, and the same fixed block placement and structural coefficient arrays are used over all of them. Therefore $\mu_L,\mu_M$ are isomorphic under $\mathrm{GL}_n(B)\times\mathrm{GL}_m(B)$ precisely when the two $B$-lattices are isomorphic. The ranks $n,m$ are bounded functions of the ranks of $A,L$ and may be fixed once for that input format. [Part V, Theorem 1.4 and §3.3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1)

Use the binary four-dimensional cup-table realization of the earlier homotopy theorem to construct $X_L$ with the table $2\mu_L$. It has the homotopy type of a wedge of $n$ two-spheres with $m$ four-cells attached. The earlier realization builds polynomially many simplices in the bit length of the table by encoding large coefficients in primitive binary-addition relations, rather than by repeating attaching maps proportional to the magnitudes of their coefficients. Its table and Betti ranks are exactly $2\mu_L,n,m$. [Four-dimensional cup-table realization](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/b3bde958f05b17ea912394edb1edd18852b9c85d/HigherOrderUniversality/SharpFourDimensionalHomotopy.md)

The common factor of two preserves the isomorphism comparisons, since multiplication by two is injective in $\mathbb Z$ and its localizations. Every attaching class of the resulting model therefore has even coefficients in the Hopf and Whitehead basis. Suspension sends each Whitehead product to zero and each Hopf generator to an element of order two in $\pi_4(S^3)$. Thus each suspended attaching class is zero. Taking the homotopy cofiber of the suspended attaching map gives
$$
 \Sigma X_L\simeq \bigvee_{i=1}^{n} S^3\ \vee\ \bigvee_{j=1}^{m} S^5.
$$
This proves the common ordinary suspension type without passing to any localization. [Suspension and Whitehead products](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT-doublepage.pdf)

## Local homotopy equivalence from local cup tables

For these spaces, we prove that an isomorphism of cup tables over $B$ suffices for a $B$-local homotopy equivalence. Let $X,Y$ be two wedge-and-four-cell models. Their integral attaching descriptions give maps
$$
 \alpha_X:H_4(X;\mathbb Z)\to\pi_3(\bigvee S^2_X),
 \qquad
 \alpha_Y:H_4(Y;\mathbb Z)\to\pi_3(\bigvee S^2_Y).
$$
For a wedge of two-spheres, the individual Hopf classes and pairwise Whitehead products give a free basis of $\pi_3$. Their cup coefficients are the corresponding diagonal and off-diagonal entries of an integral symmetric bilinear table. Thus the $\alpha$ map and the cup table determine one another, naturally with respect to a wedge map in degree two. This is also the identification $\pi_3(\bigvee S^2)=\Gamma(H_2)$ with Whitehead's universal quadratic functor. [Whitehead, On simply connected 4-dimensional polyhedra](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02568048)

In particular, every pair of integral maps $U:H^2(Y;\mathbb Z)\to H^2(X;\mathbb Z)$ and $V:H^4(Y;\mathbb Z)\to H^4(X;\mathbb Z)$ preserving the cup product is realized by a continuous $f:X\to Y$. Indeed, dualize $U,V$ to $a:H_2(X)\to H_2(Y)$ and $b:H_4(X)\to H_4(Y)$. Realize $a$ on the wedge of two-spheres. Since the pair of $Y$ with its wedge is 3-connected, relative Hurewicz identifies its relative $\pi_4$ with $H_4(Y)$. Choose a representative for the element corresponding to the image under $b$ of each source cell. The cup-product condition says $\Gamma(a)\alpha_X=\alpha_Y b$, so the boundaries of those representatives are homotopic to the images of the prescribed attaching maps. Use those boundary homotopies to extend the wedge map over every four-cell. This realizes the chosen duals in both degrees, and needs no invertibility assumption.

Let $U,V$ instead be an isomorphism over $B$. Choose $m\in D$ clearing the denominators of both matrices, and replace them by $mU,m^2 V$. These have integral entries. They still commute with the cup product: both sides of the product equation acquire the factor $m^2$. Consequently they are induced by an actual continuous map. Since $m$ is a unit in $B$, the map induces a $B$-homology isomorphism. The localized spaces are simply connected, their integral homology groups are the original groups tensored with $B$, and the homological Whitehead theorem gives the desired equivalence after localization. Conversely a local homotopy equivalence gives an isomorphism of the cup tables over $B$, by naturality of cohomology and of localization. This proves the displayed comparison for every $B$. [Hilton–Mislin–Roitberg, localization of nilpotent spaces](https://books.google.com/books/about/Localization_of_Nilpotent_Groups_and_Spa.html?id=IihfDAAAQBAJ)

## Ideal classes in one genus

If $S$ is an order in a number field, an invertible fractional $S$-ideal is a projective rank-one $S$-module, free of rank $[K: \mathbb Q]$ over $\mathbb Z$. Its multiplication matrices relative to a supplied $\mathbb Z$-basis can be obtained with rational linear algebra, and are integral by the ideal property. Two invertible fractional ideals are $S$-linearly isomorphic precisely if they differ by multiplication by $a\in K^\times$. To see the only-if direction, tensor any $S$-linear isomorphism with $K$; it acts on the rank-one $K$-space by a scalar, which already describes its action on the original lattice.

For every rational prime $p$, $S\otimes\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ is semilocal because it is finite over the local ring $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$. An invertible module over this semilocal ring is free of rank one. For example, choose a generator modulo each maximal ideal and combine them using the Chinese remainder theorem for modules; the resulting vector generates by local Nakayama and gives an isomorphism from the ring. Thus $I_{(p)}\simeq S_{(p)}$ for every invertible $I$, and $X_I$ is in the Mislin genus of $X_S$. Integral homotopy equivalence recovers the ideal class exactly by the theorem. This needs no identification by conjugation.

Genus sizes are unbounded even with fixed Betti bounds. For example take $S_j=\mathbb Z+3^j\mathbb Z[i]$, $j\ge1$. The standard ideal class exact sequence for the conductor identifies $\operatorname{Pic}(S_j)$ with the quotient of $(\mathbb Z[i]/3^j\mathbb Z[i])^\times$ by the images of $(\mathbb Z/3^j\mathbb Z)^\times$ and $\mathbb Z[i]^\times$, since $\mathbb Z[i]$ has class number one. The first group has order $8\cdot3^{2j-2}$ because $3$ is inert. The subgroup in the denominator has order $4\cdot 3^{j-1}$: the intersection of the two unit images consists exactly of $1,-1$. The genus constructed for $S_j$ therefore has at least $2\cdot3^{j-1}$ pairwise inequivalent spaces at the same bounded Betti ranks. In dimensions at most three, a simply connected complex with torsion-free integral homology is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two- and three-spheres according to its Betti numbers. Its prime-local type fixes those numbers. This proves minimality of dimension four under the stated hypotheses.

Class group phenomena in Mislin genera have prior examples for nilpotent groups, including the quadratic orders of Casacuberta–Cassidy–Scevenels. The construction here is for actual simply connected finite simplicial complexes and treats all finite-free arithmetic lattice modules at once. It distinguishes the full ideal group even when scalar conjugation permutes its classes. No classification of the genus outside the constructed family is asserted.
