# Four consequences of the higher-order tensor universality theorem

The underlying compiler takes explicitly labelled linear-algebraic structures to one ordinary tensor. These four results determine what else it preserves and give arithmetic applications. All four have written proofs and provisional publication priority. Lean independently checks the determinant identity in the first proof. [Compiler and previous Grochow ranking](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/b3bde958f05b17ea912394edb1edd18852b9c85d/HigherOrderUniversality/README.md)

1. **Polynomial Calculus degree and circuit IPS.** The literal order-three network and the bare ordinary tensor equations have bounded-degree simulations in both directions over every field. Sparse proofs transfer from output to source with polynomial overhead at arbitrary degree. Polynomial-size circuit IPS, including Hilbert-like IPS, transfers in both directions. Adding field equations gives the base-field version for each fixed finite field. [Proof](ProofComplexity.md) · [Lean lemma](WeightedRankTwoCorner.lean)

2. **Exact transporter schemes and forms.** The output transporter is the source transporter times explicit affine, torus, and general-linear factors, compatibly with arbitrary base change. The source stabilizer is a split quotient. The theorem computes finite-ring witness counts, retains infinitesimal data, and gives a formula for fppf forms over any affine coefficient ring. Over a local ring the induced map on forms is a bijection. [Proof](ExactTransporterSchemes.md)

3. **Restriction of scalars and homotopy genera.** A finite free commutative extension of rank $d$ gives an ordinary tensor comparison over the base whose sides are linear in $d$ times the sum of the original ranks. One output works after all scalar extensions, including inseparable and nonreduced cases. Integral lattice genera then embed in actual four-dimensional homotopy genera, with exact localization at each prime and a common suspension type. For a number-field order this retains every ideal class. [Proof](RestrictionOfScalars.md)

4. **Tensor comparisons under groups specified by equations.** An explicit group scheme with a supplied monic degree-compatible Gröbner basis gives one ordinary tensor comparison valid over all base changes. The added side length is linear in the dimension of the bounded-degree polynomial window. Finite free Hopf tables give a direct linear-side comparison for their torsors. [Proof](EquationDefinedGroups.md)
