# Ordinary tensor compilers with rank-one labels

A chain of rank-one algebra corners suffices to erase the labels of any finite free triangular algebra with no directed cycles in its strict corners. Its ordinary multiplication tensor retains the labelled witness scheme up to explicit affine-space and split-torus factors, over every commutative base ring. The construction replaces larger general linear marker groups in the existing higher-order compiler and gives a form-classification statement over any affine base.

## 1. The labelling construction

Let $R$ be a commutative ring. A split triangular algebra here is an associative unital $R$-algebra $A$ with specified orthogonal idempotents $e_1,\ldots,e_v$, summing to $1$, such that $e_iAe_i=Re_i$, all strict corners $e_jAe_i$ for $i\ne j$ are finite free, and the following directed graph is acyclic. It has vertices $1,\ldots,v$ and an edge $i\to j$ whenever $e_jAe_i$ has positive rank. Consider two such algebras $A,B$ with the same corner ranks and a common bound $h$ on the number of edges of a directed path in either strict-corner graph. One can always use $h=v-1$. Write $I_{\rm str}(A,B)$ for the functor of algebra isomorphisms preserving all specified idempotents, on commutative $R$-algebras.

Set $\ell=v+h+1$. Adjoin vertices $p_0,\ldots,p_\ell$, arrows $p_i\to p_{i+1}$ for $0\le i<\ell$, and arrows $p_{i-1}\to i$ for $1\le i\le v$. Each new vertex has scalar diagonal corner and each new arrow a free rank-one corner. All products of a new arrow with a strict corner on either side are zero. Diagonal elements act at the endpoints and all original products in $A$ are retained. This defines an algebra $C(A)$. Indeed it is the square-zero extension of $A\times R^{\ell+1}$ by the module of new arrows with diagonal endpoint actions. The endpoint actions factor through the algebra quotient to the product of diagonal copies of $R$, which proves associativity. Use the same construction for $B$.

Write
$$
 w=v+\ell+1,\qquad m=\ell+v,\qquad
 j=\sum_{i\ne k}\operatorname{rank}_R(e_kAe_i)+m.
$$
Thus $w$ is the new total number of vertices, $m$ is the number of new arrows, and $j$ is the rank of the direct sum of all strict corners in $C(A)$. Let $T_A,T_B$ be the ordinary multiplication tensors of $C(A),C(B)$.

**Theorem 1.** There is a natural isomorphism of transporter schemes
$$
 I_{\rm TI}(T_A,T_B)\simeq
 I_{\rm str}(A,B)\times\mathbb A_R^{3j}\times\mathbb G_{m,R}^{m+2w}.
$$
The projection and the identity-factor section preserve composition. In particular a tuple of ordinary invertible matrices transporting $T_A$ to $T_B$ exists over a commutative $R$-algebra $S$ exactly when a labelled algebra isomorphism exists there. The addition to tensor side length is $3v+2h+3\le 5v+1$. In a basis containing the specified diagonal idempotents, the added nonzero multiplication coefficients are $\ell+1+2m$ entries equal to $1$. Over a finite $S$, the witness count is multiplied by $|S|^{3j}|S^\times|^{m+2w}$.

*Proof.* The ideal $J$ of strict corners in a decorated algebra is the two-sided ideal generated by commutators. Every commutator has zero diagonal because the quotient by $J$ is the commutative algebra $S^w$. Conversely an element $x$ in the strict corner $e_b C e_a$ equals $[e_b,x]$. The ideal is nilpotent by acyclicity, including after any base change. Moreover products containing a new arrow vanish as soon as there is another strict factor, so $J^r$ equals the old strict ideal power for all $r\ge2$.

First work over a field. An isomorphism induces a permutation of the quotient idempotents in $S^w$. After inner conjugation by a unit in $1+J$, it sends the original diagonal idempotents to the permuted diagonal idempotents. Explicitly, if $f_i$ are the images and $e'_i$ the corresponding permuted idempotents, then $u=\sum_i f_i e'_i$ is in $1+J$ and satisfies $u e'_i=f_i u$. The permuted idempotents identify the directed strict-corner graphs because each corner goes to its corresponding corner. In this graph the chain $p_0\to\cdots\to p_\ell$ is the unique longest directed path. An old-only path has length at most $h$, and any path that leaves the chain for an old vertex has length at most $(v-1)+1+h=\ell-1$. Paths that stay in the chain attain length $\ell$ only by using the entire chain. Consequently every $p_i$ is fixed. The arrow from $p_{i-1}$ to old vertex $i$ then fixes $i$, since its only other outgoing arrow leads to the already fixed $p_i$. This recovery uses just the presence or absence of a strict corner.

Now work over any commutative $R$-algebra $S$. Reduce the isomorphism modulo every maximal ideal. The field proof shows that every quotient idempotent is fixed in every residue field. Its coordinates in $S^w$ are idempotents. An idempotent contained in every maximal ideal is zero since its complement is a unit. Applying this fact to each coordinate or its complement shows that the quotient idempotents are fixed over $S$. Thus the same formula for $u$, this time with the given ordering, yields a strictly idempotent-preserving isomorphism $\phi=\operatorname{Ad}(u^{-1})F$. The conjugator $u$ is unique in $1+J$, because the centralizer of the diagonal idempotents has zero intersection with the strict ideal. A strictly idempotent-preserving map restricts to one point of $I_{\rm str}(A,B)$ on the old corners. On every added rank-one arrow it is independently multiplication by a unit. Conversely these data define such a map, since the only nonzero products involving a new corner involve an endpoint idempotent. The strict maps and the conjugators are natural under arbitrary base change. Under composition, $\operatorname{Ad}(u')\phi'\circ\operatorname{Ad}(u)\phi=\operatorname{Ad}(u'\phi'(u))\phi'\phi$, and uniqueness identifies the factors.

For the passage to ordinary tensor witnesses, an isotopy satisfies $P(x)Q(y)=W(xy)$. Let $a=P(1)$, $b=Q(1)$. Evaluating at either argument $1$ shows that left multiplication by $a$ and right multiplication by $b$ are invertible. Hence $a,b$ are units: each invertible one-sided multiplication map supplies a one-sided inverse of its multiplier and its injectivity then gives the other inverse equation. Associativity now gives the algebra isomorphism $\phi(x)=a^{-1}W(x)b^{-1}$. Conversely all isotopies have the form $P=a\phi$, $Q=\phi b$, and $W=a\phi b$. The normalized map $\phi$ composes under composition. Each unit of $C(B)\otimes_R S$ is uniquely a diagonal unit times a unit in $1+J$. Diagonal units are $(S^\times)^w$, and $1+J$ is naturally the affine space of dimension $j$ as a scheme. Thus the two isotopy units and the idempotent conjugator give the three affine-space factors, and the two diagonal-unit terms and all arrow scales give precisely $m+2w$ split-torus coordinates. All expressions are polynomial or inversions of specified units, so this is an isomorphism of transporter schemes. The section extends by the identities on new arrows and diagonal vertices and uses isotopy units $1,1$. The size formula counts the new diagonal and arrow basis vectors. Only their squares and endpoint products contribute new structure constants. $\square$ [Earlier exact associative isotopy](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/AssociativeIsotopyDegeneration/PROOF.md)

## 2. Fppf forms over affine bases

**Theorem 2.** Put $H=\operatorname{Aut}_{\rm str}(A)$ and $G=\operatorname{Aut}_{\rm TI}(T_A)$. For every commutative $R$-algebra $S$, the previous projection and section give a natural bijection of pointed sets
$$
 H^1_{\rm fppf}(S,G)\ simeq H^1_{\rm fppf}(S,H)\times \operatorname{Pic}(S)^{m+2w}.
$$
In particular every affine base with trivial Picard group has exactly the original fppf forms. These forms allow finite locally free underlying modules.

*Proof.* Decomposing each isotopy unit into its diagonal residue and its principal unit, the formulas of Theorem 1 express the automorphism group as a split extension of
$$
       H\times \mathbb G_m^{m+2w}
$$
by $N=((1+J)\times(1+J)^{\rm op})\rtimes(1+J)$, where the last factor consists of the idempotent conjugators. A section takes the diagonal units, the strictly idempotent-preserving old automorphism, and the arrow scales. The strict lift fixes diagonal units and is independent of the arrow scales. Thus these factors commute, and the indicated section is a group homomorphism. The action on each principal-unit subgroup is by maps preserving the strict-corner filtration given next.

Choose a topological ordering $\tau$ of the decorated strict-corner graph. Let $F^pJ$ be the direct sum of the corners $e_b C e_a$ with $\tau(b)-\tau(a)\ge p$. These are free summands in the fixed format, with $F^pJ F^qJ\subseteq F^{p+q}J$. Diagonal conjugation and every strictly idempotent-preserving map preserve all of them. In particular every successive quotient of $1+F^pJ$ by $1+F^{p+1}J$ is the additive group of the corresponding free corner module. This filtration works even when taking the actual powers of $J$ would give nonfree successive quotients.

Twist by any torsor of $H\times\mathbb G_m^{m+2w}$. The vector-group quotients descend to additive groups of finite locally free modules. They have trivial first fppf cohomology on the affine base. Successively reducing a torsor through the filtration proves $H^1(S,(1+J)^\xi)=1$ for every twisted principal-unit factor. Applying the same argument to the normal product of the first two factors and the last-factor quotient gives $H^1(S,N^\xi)=1$. The section lifts every quotient torsor. Two lifts can be compared using a choice of identification of their quotient torsors and then a torsor under $N^\xi$, which is trivial. Hence each quotient torsor has exactly one lift up to isomorphism. Finally $H^1(S,\mathbb G_m)=\operatorname{Pic}(S)$. $\square$ [Faithfully flat descent for modules](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/023R) · [Torsor cohomology](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03AG)

## 3. Which twisted tensors have free underlying modules

**Theorem 3.** Suppose the affine base is the spectrum of a Dedekind domain $S$. Fix an old labelled form $\xi\in H^1_{\rm fppf}(S,H)$, and write $\delta(\xi)$ for the determinant class of its underlying old algebra module in $\operatorname{Pic}(S)$. Let $\mu_e$ be the classes for the $m$ new arrow scales, and $\alpha_i,\beta_i$ the classes of the two diagonal unit groups at the $w$ vertices. Put
$$
 D=\delta(\xi)+\sum_e\mu_e,\qquad
 A_1=\sum_i\operatorname{rank}(e_iC)\,\alpha_i,\qquad
 A_2=\sum_i\operatorname{rank}(Ce_i)\,\beta_i.
$$
The corresponding tensor form is on three free $S$-modules exactly when $D=A_1=A_2=0$. In particular every old labelled form, including ones on nonfree modules, admits a free ordinary tensor form. After choosing the pivot classes specified below, the free lifts of a fixed old form are parametrized by $\operatorname{Pic}(S)^{m+2w-3}$.

*Proof.* Under the strict section from Theorem 2, the underlying algebra map acts on the old part by its old transporter and on each marker by its scalar. Its determinant class is $D$. Left multiplication by the diagonal units acts by the $i$-th scalar on $e_iC$, while the right multiplication acts by the $i$-th scalar on $Ce_i$. Their determinant classes are $A_1,A_2$. Principal units have determinant one: strict multiplication raises corner distance in the filtration of Theorem 2, so adding it to the identity gives a triangular matrix with diagonal ones. The same is true of their conjugations. Thus the determinant classes in the two input-map conventions and the output are $D+A_1,D+A_2,D+A_1+A_2$. Passing to the covariant tensor convention dualizes the first two underlying modules, which does not affect their being free. By Steinitz, the three modules are free exactly when the three displayed classes are zero. Subtracting the equations gives $A_1=A_2=D=0$.

At the chain source $p_0$, $e_{p_0}C$ has rank one, since no arrow enters it. At the chain sink $p_\ell$, $Ce_{p_\ell}$ has rank one, since no arrow leaves it. The $\alpha_{p_0}$ and $\beta_{p_\ell}$ coordinates are therefore coefficient-one pivots. Any arrow scale is a coefficient-one pivot for $D$. Given all other classes, each pivot has a unique value solving its equation. The split parameterization in Theorem 2 identifies these with the claimed tensor forms. $\square$

## 4. Consequence for arbitrary labelled tensor networks

In the existing linear-side compiler for a labelled finite tensor network, remove the path and sentinel used solely for distinguishing the diagonal vertices. The remaining strict corners are the core bilinear table, the projections and the scalar-copy records. The core algebra has split scalar diagonal, finite free corners, and acyclic strict support. Its strictly idempotent-preserving isomorphisms give the network transporter and only the independent scalar factors from the two scaling maps and the projections. This is the strict-witness part of the earlier construction, before any of its marker factors. The binary product markers for higher-order tensors have their maps forced by the named source maps. Applying Theorem 1 to that core yields one ordinary three-tensor with the same labelled witness scheme up to affine spaces and split tori only. The side length remains linear in the earlier compiled format, including the order-three format obtained by the binary product reduction. [Linear-side compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/HigherOrderUniversality/RingUniversality.md) · [Strict-witness calculation](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/UniversalityConsequences/ExactTransporterSchemes.md)

This improves the previous form theorem by eliminating its auxiliary general linear factors. To apply Theorem 2, include the independent core scales in the displayed split torus. Core scales preserve every corner, so the same filtration and twisting proof applies. Thus over any affine base of trivial Picard group, the original network and its unlabelled ordinary tensor have exactly the same fppf forms, with underlying modules allowed to be finite locally free. In positive characteristic every marker coefficient is still $1$, and recovery uses directed support with corner rank one on the added arrows. The result is an exact transporter theorem. It makes no reverse Polynomial Calculus bound. The distinction matters because the FGS marker construction has a fixed-characteristic reverse-simulation obstruction in the earlier portfolio, whereas its field-level equivalence is valid.
