# Raw projective line extraction from the center of tensor derivations

Theorem. Let T:U × V → W be a bilinear map over a finite field of fixed characteristic, specified by its raw coefficients. Define its derivation algebra to consist of all triples (A,B,C) of endomorphisms satisfying

    C T(u,v) = T(Au,v) + T(u,Bv) for all u,v.

Take its Lie center Z using the coordinatewise commutator bracket. In each of U and V take the common eigenspace decomposition for the corresponding projection of Z. If both decompositions split over the input field and each joint eigenspace has dimension one, the unordered covector lines dual to the decompositions are computable in deterministic polynomial bit time and are invariant under every ordinary transporter. This applies in particular to the graph tensors specified below, of unbounded dimensions and with scalar pair adjoints. Combined with the existing two-list algorithm, the result yields a polynomial-time Turing reduction from their SL-TI problem to ordinary TI.

## Raw computation and covariance

The derivation space is the kernel of explicit linear equations in the three endomorphisms. It is a Lie algebra, because expressing the identity as annihilation of T in the tensor representation makes the commutator of two annihilating operators annihilate T too. With a computed basis D_1,…,D_m, its center is obtained by solving the linear equations [D,D_j]=0. The projections of central triples onto the two modes are commuting families. A transporter conjugates the derivation Lie algebra componentwise and therefore conjugates its center and the unordered joint eigenspace decompositions. Each extracted covector line is the annihilator of the sum of all other eigenspaces. This definition eliminates dependence on a choice of primal basis or eigenvector normalization.

For the complexity claim, use the projection of each vector of a basis of Z as one endomorphism to process. Test if its minimal polynomial divides X^Q−X, where Q is the order of the input field. Compute the minimal polynomial by linear dependence of the powers and test divisibility by modular exponentiation. This condition is exactly split diagonalizability. If it fails then there is no common eigenbasis. For split minimal polynomials one can recover all roots deterministically in bit time polynomial in the degree and log Q at fixed characteristic p. Use a basis b_j of the field over its prime field and split the polynomial by gcd with Tr(b_j X)−a for each basis element and each a in the prime field. Compute the formal trace polynomial modulo the current factor by p-power iterations. Each root has a distinct tuple of trace values because the finite-field trace pairing is nondegenerate. Thus the gcd procedure finishes with linear factors. At each step there are at most the starting degree many factors. Now refine the current invariant decomposition by the eigenspaces of the next endomorphism, using linear equations. Commutation preserves the current spaces. There are at most dim(U) or dim(V) nonzero spaces throughout, and the final dimension-one test gives exactly the desired condition. This procedure also works over an explicit polynomial-degree extension without enumerating its elements.

Apply the two-projective-list oracle algorithm when W is spanned by the products. If W has unused coordinates, first restrict to the product span and allow independent determinant correction on the unused quotient. The computed lists need no comparison by an external labeling. They are intrinsic before any TI query. [Two-projective-list reduction](../TensorSignSearchSpace/ProjectiveLists.md)

## A raw family beyond scalar pair adjoints

Let G be a connected graph with incomparable closed neighborhoods. Thus no N[k] is contained in N[i] for distinct vertices i and k, where the closed neighborhood includes its own vertex. Cycles of length at least four give an unbounded family. On two n-dimensional spaces with bases e_i and f_i, take an output basis h_ij labeled by the unordered diagonal pairs and edges of G. Define the tensor by

    T(e_i,f_j)=h_ij if i=j or {i,j} is an edge, and 0 otherwise,

with h_ij=h_ji. It is concise in all modes. Squares h_ii detect any nonzero vector in either input by pairing it with the corresponding basis vector, and the displayed outputs span W.

To see that the input pair adjoint is scalar, suppose T(Au,v)=T(u,Bv). For i≠j, comparison at u=e_i and v=f_j gives A_ji=0 from the h_jj coefficient and B_ij=0 from the h_ii coefficient. Hence both maps are diagonal. Their diagonal entries agree at each vertex by h_ii and along each edge by h_ij, so connectedness makes both scalar. For a pair involving the output, equality T(Ae_i,f_j)=C T(e_i,f_j) and the symmetry in the indices imply T(Ae_i,f_j)=T(e_i,Af_j) under the basis identifications. The input adjoint argument gives scalar A, and spanning yields scalar C. The other output pair is identical upon interchanging inputs.

For a derivation triple, comparing the equations at the index pairs (i,j) and (j,i) implies that A−B, under the same identifications, is an input pair adjoint to itself. Therefore B=A−λI. If k≠i, incomparability of the closed neighborhoods gives j in N[k] outside N[i]. The input (i,j) is a nonedge and its tensor output is zero. The coefficient of h_kj in the right-hand side of the derivation equation equals A_ki. The term T(e_i,Bf_j) cannot contribute because i differs from both k and j. Hence A_ki=0, proving A and B diagonal. Conversely every diagonal A and every λ are admissible by letting B=A−λI and C act on h_ij by the scalar A_ii+A_jj−λ. The derivations are all diagonal and commute, so the Lie center projects onto the full diagonal algebra on both input modes. This forces one-dimensional eigenspaces. The raw algorithm therefore recovers the two line lists after arbitrary independent rebasings, without recognizing G or choosing any tensor slice.

The crucial difference from computing pair adjoints is that all three modes move together in the derivation equations. The graph example exhibits the distinction on raw inputs with only scalar pair adjoints. This argument is an informal deduction and makes no publication priority claim.

## What a safe extension does to mode eigenlines

Let the base field have order q and let n be the largest mode dimension. A prime ℓ avoiding the characteristic and the order of the ambient product of general linear groups satisfies ℓ>n+1. Indeed, if ℓ≤n+1, the order of q modulo ℓ is at most ℓ−1≤n, so some factor q^j−1 in the order of the largest GL factor is divisible by ℓ. Every eigenvalue of a base-field n by n matrix has minimal polynomial degree at most n. Such an eigenvalue belongs to the extension of degree ℓ^k only if its minimal polynomial degree divides ℓ^k. It must then have degree one. This applies simultaneously to every projection of the base-field Lie center because the derivation and center equations commute with extending the field. Thus the eigenline extraction criterion above holds over a safe prime-power extension exactly when it holds over the base. This calculation applies more generally to a base-defined commuting family of mode endomorphisms. Safe extensions remain available for methods that construct new intrinsic data over the larger field. ## Intrinsic primary blocks with no split-spectrum requirement

Over a fixed base field, use the whole unital commutative associative algebra M generated by one projection of Z rather than only the joint eigenspaces. It is a subalgebra of the mode endomorphism algebra. Compute a vector basis by adding products until the span stabilizes. The dimension never exceeds the square of the mode dimension. The canonical primary subspaces are the images on the mode of the primitive idempotents of M. Finite commutative algebras are products of local Artinian algebras, with finite separable residue fields. These primary subspaces therefore sum directly to the entire mode and are permuted by every transporter. Their duals can be obtained as annihilators of the sums of the other blocks.

Here is one explicit extraction using only matrix powers, polynomial factorization over the fixed field, and Gaussian elimination. For each element A_j of the vector basis of M factor its minimal polynomial. For each irreducible factor f compute the generalized primary space ker(f(A_j)^n), where n is the mode dimension. Refine the decomposition by these kernels for each successive A_j. Only nonzero spaces are kept, and their number is always at most n. In a fixed local factor the residue of A_j has one irreducible minimal polynomial f. Thus f(A_j) acts nilpotently there, and any other irreducible polynomial in A_j acts invertibly there. The procedure groups local factors whose residues have the same minimal polynomial for all the basis elements.

To check that it does not group distinct local factors, let their residue degrees be a and b. Embed both residue fields in the extension of degree c=lcm(a,b). If their residues on every A_j had the same irreducible minimal polynomial, the two traces from the common degree-c extension to the base field would agree on each A_j. They would then agree on all of M by linearity. But the residue values of arbitrary elements of M range independently over the two residue fields, since M is the product of its local factors. On those two fields the traces are c/a and c/b times the respective field traces. At least one of c/a and c/b is nonzero in the characteristic, by the definition of the least common multiple, and the corresponding finite-field trace is not identically zero. This contradicts equality. Thus the output of the refinement is exactly the intrinsic primary decomposition. Over the fixed base field all polynomial arithmetic and factorization here are polynomial bit time.

There are two ways to use these blocks. Over the fixed base field, if their maximum dimension b in the selected two modes is logarithmic in the dense input length, list all projective covector lines inside the primary dual blocks. The number of lines is at most n (q^b−1)/(q−1) in a mode of dimension n. This is polynomial and yields the two projective lists even when the central algebra is nonsemisimple or nonsplit. Alternatively return the primary dual blocks themselves, with no enumeration. The subspace-list algorithm handles the pair whenever its width is logarithmic, including when the product of the two maximum block sizes is logarithmic or its filtered product maps have small rank or corank. There are at most n blocks per mode. This second route may also be combined with a safe extension. The local residue fields have degree at most n because each factor contributes a nonzero module, so this extension does not split any residue field. The base blocks extend to exactly the primary blocks upstairs. Their widths and the ranks and coranks of the filtered product maps are unchanged. [Proof of the subspace-list algorithm](Flags.md)
