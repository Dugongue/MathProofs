# Intrinsic subspaces and the limits of projective lists

The universal spanning-list hypothesis fails, even for tensors whose ordinary orbit splits into distinct special-linear orbits over the field of three elements. Two substitute algorithms use invariant subspaces of bounded width or recover a semisimple multiplication algebra from the raw tensor. All proofs here are informal.

| Result | Exact scope |
|---|---|
| Large projective orbits | Even-size full matrix multiplication has scalar centroid, trivial stabilizer sign image over the base field, and exponentially large projective orbits in every mode over every extension. An exterior multiplication family gives superpolynomial orbits even when all pair-adjoint algebras are scalar and the full sign image is trivial. [Proof](MatrixObstruction.md) |
| Two lists of subspaces suffice | Match intrinsic subspaces in two modes with ordinary TI queries. Mark their cumulative flags, and induce an output flag using the tensor. Compute all determinant norms through exterior algebras on small quotients or kernels. The cost is polynomial in the input, list lengths and 2 to the width of the largest determinant computation. [Proof](Flags.md) |
| Extract the lists from the raw tensor | Solve the full derivation equations and compute the center of that Lie algebra. The primary blocks of its commuting associative envelope are intrinsic and computable over a fixed field. Small joint eigenspaces or small primary blocks supply the previous algorithms. The worked graph family has scalar pair adjoints, so this uses information absent from those adjoints. [Proof](Derivations.md) |
| Solve the semisimple multiplication family directly | For raw isotopes of a product of matrix algebras over finite fields, a Las Vegas polynomial-time algorithm computes the exact determinant profile. A safe extension makes unit contractions easy to find. Explicit Wedderburn factors give one transporter and their unit norms, Frobenius maps and block permutations give the full stabilizer correction group. [Proof](SemisimpleMultiplication.md) |

The subspace reduction requires each list to span its mode. If the two input widths are b and c, the induced output increment has width at most bc. When that increment is large, a small kernel can still determine its determinant. Thus polynomial-size lists with bc logarithmic in the input size suffice. Derivation primary blocks are one explicit way to obtain such data.

A safe prime degree avoiding the characteristic satisfies ℓ>n+1, with n the largest mode dimension. Consequently no irreducible factor of a base-field mode endomorphism newly splits after a safe prime-power extension. Large fields still help with polynomial nonvanishing tests such as the search for unit contractions. They cannot produce small invariant projective lists on the counterexample families.

The narrowest positive result that applies to arbitrary input would require a different invariant decomposition with small induced determinant computations, or a direct test on the remaining large blocks. Unrestricted Open Question 1.9 remains open. The polynomial-time oracle algorithms here cover the specified classes. Priority for the precise tensor statements has not been established.
