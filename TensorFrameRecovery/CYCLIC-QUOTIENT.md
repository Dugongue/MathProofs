# Compact exact invariants for arbitrary cyclic covariants

Informal theorem. Fix a finite field F_q. Suppose each source mode V_i admits an explicitly computable conjugation-covariant endomorphism A_i(T), and each A_i is cyclic. Let N=prod_i dim V_i. Then all such input tensors have an objectwise deterministic polynomial-time reduction to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism of size O_q(N^(1/3)), for a fixed source format with all mode dimensions at least two. The tensor may have any support and the characteristic polynomials may have repeated factors. This is a conditional source class. Universal availability of cyclic covariants remains open.

## Cyclic reduction

Let f_i be the characteristic polynomial and K_i=F_q[x]/(f_i). Cyclicity means that the minimal polynomial equals f_i. Both are computed by linear algebra. Factor f_i by deterministic fixed-field polynomial factoring. Project onto its primary factors by CRT. On a primary factor h^a, some projected standard basis vector survives h(A_i)^(a-1), by the minimal polynomial criterion. Its annihilator is h^a, so choosing one such vector on each primary part and adding gives a cyclic vector. Use its A_i-power basis to identify V_i with K_i. With K=⊗_i K_i, the payload becomes t in K. For equal f_i, all possible local source transporters intertwining A_i are precisely multiplications by local units after these identifications. Conjugation covariance implies that every source isomorphism intertwines the A_i. Thus the remaining problem is exactly t'=(prod_i u_i)t with u_i in K_i^×. The identification ambiguity is also local units.

## A canonical generator of the ambient ideal

Compute I=Kt in its fixed coordinate basis and decompose K into local factors. Here and below a finite commutative F_q algebra splits into local factors. Its q-Frobenius-fixed subalgebra is a product of copies of F_q, because a Frobenius-fixed nilpotent is zero and every residue scalar in F_q has its base-field lift. Gaussian elimination computes this fixed subalgebra. For its basis elements a and scalars lambda in F_q, split with multiplication eigenspaces for a-lambda. These compute the primitive idempotents in polynomial time for fixed q. The radical J of any factor is the kernel of x↦x^(q^b) for q^b≥dim K, since that power kills nilpotents and is injective on residue fields. This map is linear over F_q.

In each nonzero local component I_s choose the first vector of its deterministic RREF basis outside J_s I_s. Such a vector exists by Nakayama and generates I_s, since I_s is cyclic and its quotient modulo J_s I_s has dimension one over the residue field. Put g_I equal to the sum of those chosen vectors, with zero in missing components. This is a canonical generator of I. Let R=K/Ann(g_I), using a deterministic quotient basis. Multiplication by g_I identifies the vector space R with I. Write t=g_I u uniquely for u in R. Since t itself generates I, u is a unit in R. For fixed I, multiplication by local units on t is exactly multiplication by their images on u. The case t=0 has a separate all-zero marker.

Thus g_I, written in N coefficients, records the ideal, all coordinates of R, and all maps K_i→R used below. Every step depends only on f_i and I except the value u.

## Splitting local unit groups

In each finite local factor C with residue field degree e over F_q, the fixed subring of x↦x^(q^e) maps isomorphically onto its residue field. In fact for any lift x, x^(q^e)-x is nilpotent, so sufficiently many iterations of q^e-power stabilize, giving a fixed lift. Uniqueness follows because a fixed nilpotent is zero. This fixed subring E is found by linear algebra and gives C^×=E^××(1+J_C). Homomorphisms of local factors preserve these two parts. For the field part this follows by taking the fixed lift and using the induced residue field embedding. The maps K_i→R therefore split into maps on products of finite field units and maps on abelian principal-unit p-groups, where p=char F_q. The two image subgroups are independent because source units split independently.

## Compact quotient of products of field units

Here the target group is G=prod_s E_s^×, where E_s=F_(q^e_s). The acting subgroup H is the image of a product of finite fields L_j^×, each mapping on a given target slot either by a field embedding or by the trivial unit. This describes our local-factor maps: each target factor selects one source factor for each i. Put m_s=q^e_s-1 and m_j=q^[L_j:F_q]-1.

We construct compatible field models without constructing an extension of huge degree. Factor the integer degrees by trial division, polynomial in the ambient dimension. For each degree prime ell, choose one displayed field among all participating fields with maximal ell-adic degree valuation. Its nested kernels of Frobenius^(ell^a)-1 are the subfields of degrees ell^a. For any required degree e, let F(e) be the tensor product of these prime-power subfields at the valuations of e, in fixed prime order. They are linearly disjoint because their degrees are pairwise coprime, so F(e) is a field of degree e. For e|f, tensoring the nested inclusions defines a field embedding F(e)→F(f). These maps compose exactly. Intersections in a common model are the gcd-degree fields. Every required constructed model has degree at most the original ambient dimension. The common model of degree lcm of ALL degrees is used only in the proof and is never constructed.

Each displayed field of degree e is identified with F(e) deterministically. To find an algebra generator, avoid its maximal proper subfields of degrees e/ell. Greedily fix coordinates in a fixed basis, keeping positive count of elements outside their union in the current affine prefix. Count by inclusion-exclusion over at most 2^omega(e)≤e subsets, with intersections and affine counts computed by linear algebra. The union is proper because a generator of the finite multiplicative group lies outside it. Find the generator's minimal polynomial by power-basis elimination. Split that polynomial over F(e) by prime-field Frobenius decomposition of the explicit étale algebra F(e)[X]/(f), and select a deterministic root. This yields an explicit isomorphism. Factoring through the prime-field fixed-space method is polynomial for fixed characteristic and all the algebras here have polynomial dimension. Each actual field embedding of degree e into degree f, in the models, is the canonical embedding followed on its domain by one of e q-Frobenius powers. Check all those powers on an algebra generator to recover its shift k.

In the hypothetical common field pick an abstract primitive root. Its powers give compatible primitive roots of all model fields. No multiplicative primitive root or discrete logarithm is computed. The abstract log-coordinate matrix A for the image of source units then has entries A_sj=(m_s/m_j) q^k when that field embeds with shift k and zero otherwise. Even though the log coordinates are unknown, the matrix is explicit. For x in G let l be its abstract log vector. Define the lattice L=diag(m_s) Z^S + A Z^J. Compute a Smith transform U with U L=diag(d_j) Z^S, using polynomial-time integer Hermite and Smith normal form with multipliers. Thus the residues (U l)_j mod d_j exactly determine the coset of x modulo H. The input integer orders and exponents have polynomial bit lengths. If a square lattice basis is required, start with diag(m_s) and add each column a. In the current basis B write B^-1 a=p/D. A unimodular gcd transform W sends p to (g,0,...), yielding the new basis B W^-1 diag(gcd(g,D)/D,1,...). Renormalize by Hermite form each time, whose coefficients are bounded by the determinant, itself at most prod_s m_s.

We now pack these quotient residues into the existing field slots, avoiding any new extension degree. Compute a gcd-free basis of the positive integers m_s,d_j, ignoring 1. Repeatedly replace two distinct atoms a,b with gcd g>1 by g,a/g,b/g, discarding 1 and merging equal atoms. The product of distinct atoms decreases by at least a factor 2, so there are polynomially many steps. Keep the factorizations as atom powers. The resulting atoms are pairwise coprime and need not be prime. For each atom g, sort its exponents in the d_j and in the m_s, in descending order with fixed tie rules. The sorted d_j exponents are componentwise bounded by the sorted m_s exponents. To see this choose any prime dividing g. The quotient map from G onto G/H surjects on every successive p-primary layer p^(k-1)G_p/p^k G_p, so the number of invariant factors with valuation at least k cannot increase. For this prime each valuation is v_p(g) times the g-atom exponent. This proves the required sorted domination without computing that prime. Assign the atom power D=g^a occurring in d_j to its matched slot s, so D|m_s. For one atom each slot receives at most one such assignment.

To encode (U l)_j mod D in that slot s, for every i compute the power x_i^(m_i U_ji / D) in F(e_i). This exponent is an integer because m_i e_i^std belongs to L, so d_j divides m_i U_ji. Negative powers are ordinary unit inverses and the exponent can be reduced modulo m_i. In the hypothetical common field the result is the D-th root of unity with exponent U_ji l_i. It lies in the intersection field of degrees e_i and e_s because its order divides both m_i and D|m_s. Transfer it through the gcd-degree model by inverting its subfield inclusion by linear algebra, then embed in F(e_s). Multiplying these transferred terms gives the element of F(e_s) whose abstract log is (m_s/D)(U l)_j. Multiply the encodings assigned to the same slot across all atoms. Their orders are pairwise coprime, so equality of the product is equivalent to equality of each encoded residue. This follows by raising the product to the product of the other orders and inverting the resulting exponent modulo D. Taking all slots therefore encodes the entire quotient exactly in sum_s e_s F_q coefficients, using only actual field powers and field maps.

## Compact quotient of principal units

Write J for the radical of R. In each successive J^k/J^(k+1), select an F_p basis with fixed lifted representatives b_j. Order all representatives by filtration depth, and put z_j=1+b_j. Every unit in 1+J has a unique word prod_j z_j^a_j with a_j in {0,...,p-1}. To compute it, peel off coefficients in the current quotient and multiply by their inverse powers. This works because multiplication is addition in each filtration quotient. The same construction gives generators for each source principal-unit group.

The abelian presentation has one integer generator per z_j and relations p z_j = word(z_j^p), using additive word notation. The right hand side involves only deeper filtration because z_j^p=1+b_j^p and pk>k. These relations present the actual group. They reduce every exponent to a base-p digit in order of depth, hence give at most p^(dim_Fp J) elements, and the actual group has exactly that many and is a quotient. Add the computed words of the images of source principal-unit generators as relations. Smith form gives quotient residue coordinates. Their moduli are powers of p, and the sum of their base-p digit lengths is at most dim_Fp J, the logarithm of the original group size. Write the residues as these digits, using F_p⊂F_q. Fixed q makes this O_q(N) coefficients. All operations use integer linear algebra or explicit ring arithmetic on polynomial-dimensional algebras.

## Objectwise encoding

The canonical record consists of f_i, the full generator g_I, the packed field-unit quotient record, and the principal-unit quotient digits, with fixed-length padding. Equality of g_I fixes all subsidiary ring models, generators, and integer matrices. The two quotient records agree exactly when the corresponding u values differ by images of local units. Therefore record equality is exactly original tensor isomorphism on the stated class. The record has O_q(N) field coefficients. Put it in a cube of side O_q(N^(1/3)). Pin each cube coordinate space pointwise by a Jordan shift and a cyclic basis vector. The existing linear-size mixed-system compiler makes the resulting equality instance an ordinary three-tensor instance with the same dimension scale. The proof is informal and does not assert universal cyclic covariance.
