# Raw Coordinate Recovery for Weighted Four-Tensors

Informal proofs. Let $q$ be fixed. All reductions are deterministic, act on one input tensor at a time, and output ordinary three-tensors over the original field $\mathbb F_q$. The size measure is total mode dimension. In particular the input is given in arbitrary bases.

The first note on [fractional tensor order reduction](../FractionalTensorOrder/PROOFS.md) gave an infinite family outside its sufficient guarantees. Its four-way array has entries 1 at $(a,b,a+b,a+2b)$ for $a,b\in\mathbb Z/n$, $n$ odd, and zero elsewhere. The construction below covers this family, its versions with independent nonzero weights on all $n^2$ support positions, and more general abelian supports. The modular case, where the characteristic divides $n$, is included. Unrestricted Grochow–Qiao Question 7.8 remains open.

## Theorems

**Weighted abelian supports.** Fix $k$. Let $G$ be an abelian group of order $n$, generated by at most $k$ elements. Consider tensors with nonzero weights on the graph of four homomorphisms $\ell_i:G^2\to G$ such that every ordered pair $\ell_i,\ell_j$ gives a bijection $G^2\to G^2$. Use arbitrary bases in the four modes. From the raw tensor, with neither $G$ nor the four bases given, one can compute a canonical representative in $n^{O(k+1)}$ time. One ordinary three-tensor of total dimension $O((n^2\log(n+1))^{1/3})$ suffices for exact comparison. The algorithm applies on a larger checkable class described below.

**Primary groups of unbounded rank.** If $G$ is an abelian $p$-group with $p=\operatorname{char}\mathbb F_q$, the same weighted support class admits an objectwise polynomial-time reduction to ordinary three-tensors of dimension $O(n)$, without a generator bound. The algorithm applies whenever the intrinsic axes extracted below are unique by the identity-component criterion and exhibit a support of strength 2 and index 1, and $\gcd(n,q-1)=1$.

## 1. Build an intrinsic algebra from an invertible flattening

Fix one mode $i$, put it on the input side of an ordered two-versus-two flattening $M:V_i^*\otimes V_j^*\longrightarrow V_u\otimes V_v$, and choose the other indices deterministically. On the theorem's support this is an invertible matrix. For $X\in\operatorname{End}(V_i^*)$, expand $M(X\otimes I)M^{-1}$ in $\operatorname{End}(V_u)\otimes\operatorname{End}(V_v)$. Realign it as a matrix $B(X)$ from the dual of the second endomorphism space to the first. Compute the algebra
$$
 \mathcal A=\{(L,R): L B(X)=B(X)R\text{ for every }X\}.
$$
All these equations are linear in $L,R$ and it suffices to use a basis of matrices $X$. The algebra is closed under componentwise multiplication. Compute its center by another linear solve. These operations are polynomial in $n$. Under source isomorphism, the family $B$ undergoes left and right invertible changes and its parameter $X$ undergoes conjugation on $V_i^*$. Consequently the center, its primitive idempotents, and the following subspaces of $\operatorname{End}(V_i^*)$ are carried to their counterparts:
$$
 S_e=\{X:e_L B(X)=B(X)\},\qquad e=(e_L,e_R).
$$
This assertion uses the action on the two matrix spaces and requires no choice of an invertible member of the realigned matrix space.

For a finite commutative algebra over $\mathbb F_q$, its $q$-Frobenius fixed space is $\mathbb F_q^s$, where $s$ is the number of local factors. In a local factor the fixed residue lies in $\mathbb F_q$, and subtracting it gives a Frobenius-fixed nilpotent, necessarily zero. Find the fixed space by linear algebra. Split its primitive idempotents using the spectral projectors $1-(a-\lambda)^{q-1}$ for basis elements $a$ of that space and $\lambda\in\mathbb F_q$. The resulting idempotents are also the primitive idempotents of the full center. This is deterministic polynomial time for fixed $q$, including when the center has nilpotents.

## 2. Exact computation on weighted abelian supports

Use the template coordinates only to analyze the raw algorithm. Take the first two coordinates of the chosen flattening as parameters $a,b\in G$. The output coordinates are
$$
 c=Aa+Bb,\qquad d=Ca+Db,
$$
where $A,B,C,D$ are automorphisms of $G$ and the full map $(a,b)\mapsto(c,d)$ is invertible. All four entries of the inverse map are automorphisms too. For example, fixing $d$, the map from $c$ to $a$ is a bijection because $(a,d)$ is one of the allowed parameter pairs. Let $w_{a,b}\ne0$ be the weight on this support position.

A matrix entry $X_{a+s,a}$ in the first input factor, after conjugating by $M$, connects the output $(c,d)$ to $(c+As,d+Cs)$, with coefficient
$$
 X_{a+s,a}\,w_{a+s,b}/w_{a,b}.
$$
Realignment gives a block diagonal matrix space, with blocks indexed by $s\in G$ after relabeling row and column differences by $A$ and $C$. The variables belonging to different blocks are disjoint. For a fixed $s$, let $f_s(a)=X_{a+s,a}$. Fixing one $a_0$ and setting $f_s$ to its indicator gives a weighted permutation matrix in that block, since $a$ is a sum of automorphic images of $c,d$. Each block therefore contains an invertible matrix on its own row and column spaces. Using such a matrix supported in only one block in the equations for $\mathcal A$ forces the off-block entries of both $L$ and $R$ to vanish. Hence $\mathcal A$ is a product of independent block algebras, and the block projectors are central.

In the $s=0$ block the weight ratio is 1. After fixed row and column relabelings the matrices are exactly the regular convolution matrices of $\mathbb F_q[G]$, with function $f_0$ as coefficient vector. This block contains the identity and all translations, so its intertwiner algebra has $L=R$ and equals the centralizer of the regular representation. That centralizer is the commutative algebra $\mathbb F_q[G]$: a commuting endomorphism is determined by the image of the group identity basis vector and acts by multiplication by that image. Thus the corresponding subspaces $S_e$ are exactly the diagonal matrices whose coefficient functions lie in the primary ideals of $\mathbb F_q[G]$.

Write $G=P\times H$, where $P$ is the $p$-part and $H$ the prime-to-$p$ part. The augmentation ideal of $\mathbb F_q[P]$ is nilpotent, since it is generated by $g-1$ for finitely many $p$-power order generators and each such generator is nilpotent. Its quotient is $\mathbb F_q$. Over a splitting extension for $H$, the remaining group algebra is the product of its one-dimensional character factors, by the character orthogonality relations with $|H|$ invertible. The primary subspace for a single character consists of coefficient functions
$$
 (x,h)\longmapsto u(x)\chi(h),\qquad x\in P,\ h\in H,
$$
up to replacing $\chi$ by its inverse according to the convolution convention. Over the original field, the corresponding block collects a Frobenius orbit of characters. These claims are used for the proof only. The algorithm computes all central idempotents directly over the original field.

The matrix $I$ belongs to exactly one subspace $S_e$. Indeed its coefficient function is constant 1 and lies entirely in the trivial character component in the shift-zero block. Write $S_0$ for this intrinsic subspace. In the template it is the diagonal algebra of functions on $P$, repeated along $H$. If $H$ is trivial, $S_0$ is already the full diagonal algebra in the raw coordinates.

In general the character group of $H$ has at most $k$ generators. Select a rational primitive block containing each of a generating set of characters. Together with $S_0$, the resulting subspaces generate the full diagonal algebra: after extension, the characters and the functions on $P$ separate every pair of coordinates, and their products span the diagonal algebra. Their generated algebra over the original field must then have dimension $n$ and is the full diagonal algebra there. Consequently one finds the true diagonal algebra in an intrinsic polynomial list. Enumerate at most $k$ primitive blocks, generate the matrix algebra of their $S_e$ and $S_0$, and keep it if it is a split commutative algebra of dimension $n$ with primitive idempotents of rank one. If there is no unique $S_0$, declare that this extractor supplies no frame for that mode. The split test and the idempotents use the same fixed-space method. The one-dimensional images give candidate axes on $V_i^*$; the associated basis lines on $V_i$ are the dual coordinate lines, determined by annihilating the other axes. There are $n^{O(k+1)}$ choices, since the center has polynomial dimension.

## 3. Canonical labels and residual coefficients

Choose one candidate axis system in each mode. Determine the support using any nonzero vector on each axis. Retain choices for which projection onto every pair of modes gives a bijection between support and the pairs of coordinate lines. Such a support has $n^2$ positions, with the third and fourth coordinates functions $c(a,b),d(a,b)$ of the first two.

For each choice of origins $a_0,b_0$, identify the first axis set with the third by $a\mapsto c(a,b_0)$ and the second by $b\mapsto c(a_0,b)$. Transport $c(a,b)$ to a binary operation on the third axis set. Check that this operation is an abelian group law, and enumerate all ordered generating tuples of length at most $k$. For each such tuple, use deterministic breadth-first enumeration starting at the identity, multiplying by the generators in tuple order. This labels the third axes, and hence the first two via the identifications. Label the fourth by the bijection $a\mapsto d(a,b_0)$. These choices all commute with isomorphism, in the sense that a source isomorphism carries the whole set of choices bijectively to that for the other tensor. For the true template axes, the operation is the original abelian law up to translation by the chosen origin and automorphisms. Indeed $c$ has the form $Aa+Bb$, so the transported operation in the third coordinates is $x+y-c(a_0,b_0)$. This ensures a nonempty list on the theorem's entire input class. More generally the algorithm works on the invariant promise that the described tests produce at least one labelling.

For a labelled support, fix a generator of $\mathbb F_q^\times$ and replace each nonzero weight by its log modulo $q-1$. This lookup has constant cost for fixed $q$. Changes of chosen vectors on the axes add vertex potentials to edge logs, with one contribution from each coordinate. If $D$ is the edge-by-vertex incidence matrix, the orbit is exactly the class modulo the lattice
$$
 (q-1)\mathbb Z^{n^2} + D\mathbb Z^{4n}.
$$
Compute a canonical residue by integer Hermite normal form and triangular reduction. The diagonal entries of this lattice basis divide $q-1$ because the lattice contains $(q-1)\mathbb Z^{n^2}$, so each reduced coordinate is representable in constant space. For $q=2$ all logs and residues are zero. This gauge step is invariant under changing the vectors on the labelled axes.

Take the lexicographically least pair of labelled support and normalized weights over the full list. Isomorphic inputs have the same list of normalized outcomes. Each outcome represents an actual basis change and local scalings of the input. Therefore equality of minima also implies tensor isomorphism. Store the two output coordinate indices and one normalized weight for each $(a,b)$. This uses $O(n^2\log(n+1))$ field symbols. As in the balanced compiler, place the string in a three-way cube and force each cube-space change of basis to be the identity by attaching a fixed nilpotent shift and its cyclic vector. The labelled mixed compiler converts this to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism at linear cost in the cube side. This proves the stated dimension bound. The polynomial exponent depends on the fixed $k$ and field. None of the arithmetic here depends on coprimality of $n$ with the characteristic.

## 4. Linear-size output without a generator bound

When $G$ is a $p$-group, the unique identity component $S_0$ gives the four full axis systems immediately. More generally one can use this branch whenever each $S_0$ is a split diagonal algebra of dimension $n$ with rank-one primitive projectors and the extracted support has the pair-bijection property. These are intrinsic, polynomial checks. It remains to encode arbitrary weights while allowing any permutations of the axes.

Let $R=\mathbb Z/(q-1)$. Write $f$ for the edge weight logs, $P_i$ for conditional averaging on the fibers of coordinate $i$, and $P_*$ for the global average. These operators exist when $\gcd(n,q-1)=1$, in particular for $p$-groups in the stated characteristic. Each fiber has $n$ edges. Given distinct coordinates $i,j$, every pair of values occurs once, so $P_iP_j=P_*$, and $P_iP_*=P_*$ for all $i$. Therefore
$$
 P=P_1+P_2+P_3+P_4-3P_*
$$
maps into the sum of the four vertex-potential spaces and restricts to the identity on each such space. The residual $f-Pf$ is invariant under local scalings and its equality is sufficient for two arrays on the same support to differ by local scalings. It commutes with all support relabelings. For $q=2$, take the residual identically zero.

Represent each axis set by the coordinatewise multiplication algebra on $\mathbb F_q^n$. Its algebra automorphisms are exactly the permutations of the primitive idempotents. Use one named space for each axis set in the mixed compiler. Encode the two support functions $c,d$ as their arity-three tensors using the first two axis sets and the appropriate output set. Encode the residual log for each $(a,b)$ as a matrix, using an injective encoding of $R$ in $\mathbb F_q$. The multiplication tensors, the two support maps, and the residual matrix have a constant number of bounded-arity occurrences on four spaces of dimension $n$. The mixed compiler produces one ordinary three-tensor of total dimension $O(n)$. Its equality up to ordinary isomorphism exactly permits common axis permutations preserving support and residual, which lift to a source isomorphism by the scaling criterion. This proves the second theorem.

## 5. Arbitrary orthogonal mates and nonabelian primary groups

Both constructions allow an arbitrary fourth support function. Suppose the first three coordinates are the group table $(a,b,ab)$, and the fourth is $L(a,b)$, where every pair of the four coordinates is a bijective parameterization. This is an orthogonal Latin mate of the group table. The four input bases may be arbitrary, and all weights on the support may be independently nonzero.

To recover one of the first three modes, put the fourth mode on the other input side of the flattening in Section 1. Use the remaining two group-table modes as outputs. When $X$ is diagonal in the template frame, $B(X)$ is supported on the block whose row and column differences are both zero. For a diagonal coefficient function $f$, the entries of this block evaluate $f$ at the group coordinate determined by the two output coordinates. After permuting rows or columns and possibly transposing, these are the regular convolution matrices of the group algebra. For example $f(ab)$ becomes $f(ab^{-1})$ after column inversion. The other two forms follow by solving $c=ab$ for $a$ or $b$.

When $X$ is an off-diagonal matrix unit, the other input label remains fixed in each matrix entry of the conjugated operator. Changing the first input label changes both output labels, because either output label together with the other input label determines the row. Thus $B(X)$ has only off-diagonal row and column indices. It is a weighted partial permutation: as the other input label varies, the old and new output coordinates each vary bijectively, so there are no repeated rows or columns among its nonzero entries. For every off-diagonal row index there is such a matrix with a nonzero entry in that row, since one can fix the other input label and use the two indicated output labels to find old and new first input labels. The same holds for every off-diagonal column index. These matrices therefore have jointly spanning images and trivial common kernel on the off-diagonal block.

The diagonal block contains an invertible convolution matrix. The equations defining $\mathcal A$ on that matrix first force $L_{\mathrm{off},\mathrm{diag}}=0$ and $R_{\mathrm{diag},\mathrm{off}}=0$. Applying the equations to the matrices supported on the off-diagonal block forces $L_{\mathrm{diag},\mathrm{off}}=0$ and $R_{\mathrm{off},\mathrm{diag}}=0$ by spanning images and trivial common kernel. Hence the diagonal block is an intrinsic direct algebra factor in the template analysis. Its intertwiner algebra is the commutant of the regular group algebra, which is the opposite group algebra or the group algebra depending on convention.

For an abelian group the diagonal factor now yields exactly the same character components and candidate axis systems for the first three modes. For each candidate triple of axes, contract along the corresponding dual axes of the first three modes and collect nonzero output lines in the fourth mode. Keep the triple when the result consists of exactly $n$ independent lines. In the true axes this recovers all fourth-mode axes, since every occupied row contributes its single fourth axis. The support and labeling tests and gauge canonicalization from Section 3 finish the reduction for bounded-generator abelian group tables with arbitrary orthogonal mates. No description of the group or mate is needed as input.

In characteristic $p$, the group algebra of any finite $p$-group is local. For a proof, induct on its order using a central element $z$ of order $p$, whose existence follows from the class equation and Cauchy's theorem. The ideal generated by $z-1$ is nilpotent since the generator is central and $(z-1)^p=0$. The quotient is the group algebra of $G/\langle z\rangle$. Induction proves that the original augmentation ideal is nilpotent, and the augmentation quotient is the base field. Thus the diagonal intertwiner block has exactly one primitive central idempotent, and $S_0$ is the full diagonal axis algebra in all three recovered modes. Use the same contraction recovery in the fourth mode and the linear-size compiler of Section 4. The result is $O(n)$ for arbitrary finite $p$-groups with an orthogonal mate, including nonabelian groups and groups of unbounded generator rank. In odd order the formula $L(a,b)=ab^2$ is always an orthogonal mate, since the squaring map is bijective and $(ab)^{-1}(ab^2)=b$.

For the original fixed cyclic support $(a,b,a+b,a+2b)$, the first algorithm can retain only labelings whose support is that fixed template. Its canonical record then has exactly $n^2$ weight entries. The rigid packing costs $O(n^{2/3})$. For fixed $q>2$ this exponent is sharp on that weighted template promise. For any given input in this family, its possible isomorphic weighted template representatives are accounted for by the polynomially many intrinsic candidate axes and cyclic labelings, followed by at most $(q-1)^{4n}$ local scalar choices. Indeed any isomorphism transports the true template axes into the candidate lists, and any fixed cyclic template labeling is obtained from a choice of origins and cyclic generator. Thus the $(q-1)^{n^2}$ possible weight arrays contain at least $(q-1)^{n^2-4n}/n^{O(1)}$ isomorphism classes. The number of three-tensors with maximum side at most $m$ is bounded by $(m+1)^3q^{m^3}$, which forces $m=\Omega_q(n^{2/3})$.

## 6. The principal-block extension

The same $O(n)$ result holds for any finite group $G$ with no nontrivial normal subgroup of order prime to $p$, given a support with an orthogonal mate and with $\gcd(n,q-1)=1$. The raw tensor checks still require neither the group nor its mate as input. In the algorithm for this branch, generate the matrix algebra of $S_0$ before testing for the full split diagonal algebra.

In the convolution block, the constant coefficient vector is $\sum_{g\in G}g$. Multiplication by an element of the group algebra scales this vector by the element's augmentation. Thus the unique primitive central block containing the vector is the principal block $B_0=e_0\mathbb F_q[G]$, and $S_0$ consists of the diagonal matrices whose coefficient functions belong to this block, up to the fixed coordinate conventions. The pairing taking the coefficient of the identity in a product is nondegenerate on the group algebra, and remains nondegenerate on $B_0$ because distinct central blocks are orthogonal. Accordingly, evaluation at two group elements $a,b$ is identical on $B_0$ precisely when $e_0(a^{-1}-b^{-1})=0$. The coefficient functions of $B_0$ separate all group elements if the action of $G$ on $B_0$ by multiplication is faithful.

The kernel of that action is exactly the largest normal subgroup of order prime to $p$. Such a subgroup has a central averaging idempotent of augmentation 1, which contains $e_0$ as a summand and hence acts trivially on $B_0$. In the other direction, $B_0$ is a nonzero projective summand of the regular representation. Its restriction to a Sylow $p$-subgroup remains projective because the regular representation restricts to a free module. This projective module is free over the Sylow group algebra, which is local by Section 5. A nonzero free module has faithful group action, so the kernel intersects the Sylow subgroup trivially and its order is prime to $p$. This is the standard principal-block kernel identity, also described in the [CTBlocks documentation](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/homes/Thomas.Breuer/ctblocks/doc/chap3.html).

A subalgebra of $\mathbb F_q^G$ separating points is the full product algebra. For each target point, multiply normalized differences of functions that distinguish it from each other point to obtain its indicator. Thus the algebra generated by $S_0$ gives the full axes in the first three modes. Recover the fourth by contraction and apply the weight projection and mixed compiler from Section 4.

## Scope and provenance

The weighted input family has all pair flattenings invertible. Its ordinary centroid is scalar, by canceling an invertible pair flattening in the equality of the two same-side mode actions. In particular the all-one cyclic member has a nonscalar simultaneous translation stabilizer. The constructions here therefore cover the preceding note's explicit missed family. A complementary route is developed in [Compact exact invariants for arbitrary cyclic covariants](CYCLIC-QUOTIENT.md). It keeps the counting exponent while allowing both nonunit payloads and repeated characteristic roots. The new coordinate-recovery condition concerns intertwiners of the realigned conjugation family, which live on endomorphism spaces. Arbitrary full-rank tensors need not have the recovered diagonal subalgebras.

The output compiler is the labelled mixed compiler in [BalancedOrdinaryReduction.lean](../BalancedOrdinaryReduction.lean) and the linear-length approach of [Grochow and Qiao, Part IV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16317). The Frobenius idempotent computation reuses the method in [Determinant Coset Assembly for Tensor Direct Sums](../SLDeterminantAssembly/PROOF.md). The motivating general question is [Grochow–Qiao, published Question 7.8](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full). Orthogonal arrays and their relation to four-party perfect tensors are discussed in [Goyeneche et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.08857). For polynomial-time integer normal forms see [Kannan and Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040). These proofs are informal.
