# A many-one reduction for multiplication isotopes via matrix corners

Fix a finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$. All tensors and algebras are given in ordered bases. The result is an unconditional deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over the same field. It applies to associative multiplication isotopes with possibly nonzero Jacobson radical and different sizes of residue matrix factors. It decides any prescribed triple of nonzero mode determinants.

We use the bilinear convention $U T(x,y)=T'(Px,Qy)$, with $P,Q,U$ invertible. The determinant-one question is invariant under conversion to the trilinear convention. For other prescribed determinants, dualizing a mode inverts that coordinate of the prescribed triple.

## The class recognized by the reduction

First normalize the two isotopes to explicit algebras $A$ and $A'$. The procedure in Section 1 recognizes the associative unital multiplication-isotope promise.

For either normalized algebra write $J=\operatorname{rad} A$ and $A/J=\prod_i M_{n_i}(E_i)$, with $E_i/F$ a finite field. Use lifted matrix units $e^i_{ab}$ and $e_i=e^i_{11}$. Define
$$
B_{ij}=e_i A e_j,\qquad B_{ij,k}=e_i J^k e_j,\qquad J^0=A.
$$
These are vector spaces. Through a semisimple section, every nonzero grade $B_{ij,k}/B_{ij,k+1}$ is a module over $E_i$ on the left and $E_j$ on the right. Denote its rank over the larger of the two fields by $d_{ij,k}$, choosing the left field in a tie.

The correction index $g$ is the index in $F^\times$ of the subgroup $C$ defined in Section 2. It incorporates all inner automorphism determinants and determinants from a detachable part of the radical. For a candidate comparison use the target index for both inputs. Call an ordered pair $(i,j)$ **visible** if $B_{ij}\ne0$ and $g\nmid n_i n_j$. Call a vertex visible if it occurs in such a pair.

The reduction has polynomial size whenever the following bounds hold, for fixed constants $c,b$ and total dense input bit length $L\ge2$.

1. On both inputs every visible grade has $d_{ij,k}\le c\log_2 L$.
2. The possible matching bijections of the visible vertices, respecting the descriptors below, number at most $L^b$.

A usable descriptor of $i$ is $(n_i,[E_i:F])$ together with the sorted multiset of tuples consisting of $(n_j,[E_j:F])$ and the two graded dimension sequences for $B_{ij}$ and $B_{ji}$ as $j$ varies. Any additional computable isomorphism-invariant descriptor may refine these classes. The possible bijections are generated by matching only equal descriptors, so their count is a product of factorials of visible descriptor multiplicities. A mismatch of the visible descriptor multisets means no isomorphism. The class allows an arbitrary number of residue factors and arbitrary radical nilpotence length. The width and matching bounds concern visible grades and vertices.

## 1. Normalization of an arbitrary associative isotope

For $T:V_1\times V_2\to W$, compute the projections to $\operatorname{End}(V_1)$ and $\operatorname{End}(V_2)$ of the linear solution space of $T(Xx,y)=T(x,Yy)$. These are unital algebras. If $T(Px,Qy)=U(xy)$ for multiplication in a unital associative algebra $A_0$, evaluation at $1$ in the two arguments identifies these projected algebras with right and left multiplication in $A_0$, respectively. Their evaluation modules are isomorphic to their regular modules. In the first case the regular algebra is the opposite algebra. A constructive module-isomorphism algorithm returns cyclic vectors $\alpha=P(a)$ and $\beta=Q(b)$, where $a,b$ are units. In fact cyclicity says $aA_0=A_0$ or $A_0b=A_0$, and a one-sided inverse in a finite-dimensional algebra is two-sided. [Brooksbank and Luks on module isomorphism](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf).

Put $J_T=T(-,\beta)$ and $L_T=T(\alpha,-)$, and transport to $W$ by $x\star y=T(J_T^{-1}x,L_T^{-1}y)$. In the hidden coordinates $J_T(Px)=U(xb)$ and $L_T(Qy)=U(ay)$. The map $z\mapsto U(azb)$ is an algebra isomorphism into this new product, since the two inverse slice maps send $U(azb),U(awb)$ to $P(az),Q(wb)$. Conversely invertible slices and a verified associative unital product witness the isotope promise. Unequal dimensions, failed module isomorphism, singular required slices or a failed product check reject. All checks and constructions are polynomial over fixed $F$. [Earlier semisimple isotope normalization](../AlpogeTISL/SemisimpleMultiplicationIsotopes.md).

For multiplication tables every transporter has the form
$$
P=L_a\phi,\qquad Q=R_b\phi,\qquad U=L_aR_b\phi
$$
for units $a,b\in A'^\times$ and a unital algebra isomorphism $\phi:A\to A'$. Indeed $a=P(1),b=Q(1)$ are units by invertibility and the equations $U(x)=P(x)b=aQ(x)$. Substitution shows that $\phi(x)=a^{-1}U(x)b^{-1}$ is multiplicative and unital. The converse follows by substitution. With $\lambda_1=\det J_T/\det J_{T'}$ and $\lambda_2=\det L_T/\det L_{T'}$, the raw determinant triple has the form
$$
(\lambda_1 u r,\lambda_2 v r,uvr),
\quad r=\det\phi,\quad
u=\det L_a,\quad v=\det R_b .
$$
Thus for a desired triple $(s,t,w)$ we require
$$
r_*=\frac{st}{\lambda_1\lambda_2 w},\qquad
u_*=\frac{s}{\lambda_1r_*},\qquad
v_*=\frac{t}{\lambda_2r_*}.
\tag{1}
$$

## 2. Actual determinant corrections

Let $\ell_i$ and $r_i$ be the multiplicities of the natural simple modules for factor $i$ in the left and right regular modules restricted to a semisimple section. They equal $\dim_F(e_iA)/[E_i:F]$ and $\dim_F(Ae_i)/[E_i:F]$. For a unit $u$ with residue components $u_i$, the two determinant characters are
$$
\det L_u=\prod_i N_{E_i/F}(\det_{E_i}u_i)^{\ell_i},
\qquad
\det R_u=\prod_i N_{E_i/F}(\det_{E_i}u_i)^{r_i}.
$$
The kernel $1+J$ has determinant one for both actions by the radical filtration. A lift in the section acts on copies of natural simples on each side, giving the formulas. Finite-field norms and matrix determinants are surjective. Hence the two unit feasibility tests in (1) are membership in $(F^\times)^{\gcd(q-1,\ell_i:i)}$ and $(F^\times)^{\gcd(q-1,r_i:i)}$, respectively. The determinant subgroup of inner automorphisms is exactly
$$
C_I=(F^\times)^{\gcd(q-1,\ell_i-r_i:i)} .
$$
Membership in any group $(F^\times)^d$ with $d\mid q-1$ is the power check $x^{(q-1)/d}=1$.

There is also a correction group from the radical itself. Put
$$
K=\{x\in J:xJ=Jx=0\},\qquad W=K/(K\cap J^2).
$$
These are semisimple bimodule data for the residue algebra $S=A/J$. Over the finite field $S$ is separable, so its enveloping algebra is semisimple. Choose a section acting on $J$ and a bimodule complement $U$ to $K\cap J^2$ in $K$. Split $J=U\oplus V$ with $J^2\subseteq V$. Any $S$-bimodule automorphism of $U$ extends to an algebra automorphism fixing the section and $V$. The products with $U$ inside $J$ vanish, and all products inside $J$ lie in $V$. This proves the extension assertion directly. The simple factors of the $(i,j)$ part of the enveloping algebra are matrix algebras of size $n_i n_j$ over finite fields, from the field factors of $E_i\otimes_F E_j$. On each nonzero simple multiplicity block the determinant image is exactly $(F^\times)^{n_i n_j}$, by the norm of the multiplicity-matrix determinant raised to the simple module rank. Hence these automorphisms contribute
$$
C_W=(F^\times)^{\gcd(q-1,\{n_i n_j: \epsilon_iW\epsilon_j\ne0\})},
$$
where the $\epsilon_i$ are the central residue idempotents. An empty exponent list gives the trivial subgroup. Define $C=C_I C_W=(F^\times)^g$ and $h=(q-1)/g$. Verified additional automorphisms of $A'$ may be used as input to enlarge $C$ by their determinants, without changing the argument. The determinant subgroup used for correction consists of actual automorphism determinants in all cases. The fuller radical analysis and constructive finite-algebra details are in [matrix stabilization and radical corrections](RadicalMultiplication.md).

## 3. Aligning the residue section and reading the determinant

The semisimple quotient and lifted structure can be computed by deterministic finite-algebra algorithms over fixed $F$. For clarity, lift a complete family of orthogonal diagonal idempotents by successive idempotent lifting in the complementary corners. Lift the $(a,1)$ and $(1,a)$ matrix units in their prescribed corners. If their product differs from the corresponding diagonal idempotent by a radical element, invert it in that corner and correct one lift. The reverse product is then an idempotent congruent to that corner's unit, hence equal to the unit. Products through column $1$ give the full system. To lift each center field, choose a primitive element in the explicit residue field, lift it into its first diagonal corner, and apply Newton lifting for its separable minimal polynomial. All corrections lie in the commutative algebra generated by the lift. Its derivative value is a unit, and each iteration squares the ideal containing the error. The finite nilpotence length bounds the number of steps. Matrix units carry the lifted field to the entire factor, giving the chosen complement. [Finite-algebra decomposition in Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18).

Any $\phi:A\to A'$ sends the chosen section to a section. Wedderburn–Malcev conjugacy aligns it with the target section by a unit in $1+J'$. Within the latter section conjugate by a unit to align the matrix units up to the induced permutation of the factors. In a matrix factor this amounts to choosing a nonzero vector in the first diagonal image and applying the first-column matrix units to obtain a basis. These inner changes multiply the determinant only by an element of $C_I$. This proves completeness of the alignment without having to compute $\phi$ in the reduction.

For each $(i,j)$ the $n_i n_j$ maps
$$
B_{ij}\longrightarrow e^i_{aa}A e^j_{bb},
\qquad z\longmapsto e^i_{a1}z e^j_{1b},
$$
are separate coordinate identifications, with inverses using $e^i_{1a}, e^j_{b1}$. The total direct sum over $i,j,a,b$ is $A$. Order the $(i,j)$ pairs, then the copies inside each pair. Choose a basis in each corner adapted to $B_{ij,k}$. Concatenating the copies gives a frame of $A$, and similarly choose the target frame. Across a factor permutation $\pi$, an aligned isomorphism acts by $n_i n_j$ identical copies of its map on $B_{ij}$, from the $(i,j)$ block to the $(\pi(i),\pi(j))$ block. Triangularity inside the radical filtration gives the exact determinant
$$
\det\phi=\kappa\,\varepsilon(\pi)
       \prod_{i,j,k}\det_F(\overline\phi_{ij,k})^{n_i n_j}.
\tag{2}
$$
Here $\kappa$ is the ratio of target to source frame determinants and $\varepsilon(\pi)$ is the sign from reordering the whole Peirce blocks of sizes $n_i n_j\dim_F B_{ij}$. The sign comes from swaps, one for each product of the two block sizes. The order inside each set of $n_i n_j$ copies is preserved.

Raise (2) to $h$. A corner with $g\mid n_i n_j$ has exponent divisible by $q-1$ after powering, so it makes no contribution. If $(-1)^h=1$, the block sign also disappears. Otherwise $h$ is odd and $g$ is even. Every odd-sized Peirce block then has $n_i n_j$ odd, so both its endpoints are visible. Only the relative order of odd blocks affects the sign, and their entire matching is known after matching the visible vertices. Thus every unpaired vertex can still move freely without affecting the computable sign in the powered equation.

## 4. Compiling one ordinary comparison

Reject unequal algebra dimensions or residue-type multisets. Discard any candidate pairing of visible vertices that gives different visibility or graded dimensions to some paired ordered corner. These checks ensure that the following systems have matching formats. For each remaining pairing build a typed tensor system with the following data.

- Keep the full algebra multiplication and unit on the same sort. Register the chosen section by inclusion. Use coordinate algebras of the residue factors and of the triples $(i,a,b)$ to mark all matrix units. The incidences forget to the residue label and to the two matrix indices. The matrix-index label sorts are ordered. The component label sort permits any matching extending the chosen pairing of visible vertices. The linear map sending a unit-label idempotent to its matrix unit forces the shared algebra map to transport that specific system. Fix the visible labels by marking their idempotents. The section inclusion and the centers of its factors ensure the induced field maps are the same in every grade using that field.
- For each visible grade include its two filtration spaces by inclusion and its quotient projection. Register the action of the chosen endpoint field. For each visible vertex use one field role with its multiplication, unit and inclusion into the corresponding first corner of the section. Every grade action and trace-zero inclusion for this field uses that same role. Basis choices calibrate determinants without fixing field basis vectors as points. Identify the corresponding endpoint fields on the two sides in a common presentation, using the deterministic finite-field isomorphism algorithm over fixed $F$. In the chosen grade bases the map is $P\tau$, with $P$ linear over the endpoint field $E$ and $\tau$ its induced field automorphism. The field action is part of the diagram, so the exponent of Frobenius is not chosen separately for different grade occurrences. [Lenstra, finite-field isomorphisms](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf).
- Link the grade to its full exterior algebra over $E$ by wedge maps. This forces the determinant scalar $\lambda=\det_E P$ on the top line, together with $\tau$. The exterior space has $F$-dimension $[E:F]\,2^{d_{ij,k}}$. To extract the norm, link Frobenius-twisted copies of the top line using $x\mapsto x^{q^j}$ in the selected coordinates, then multiply the lines. The final semilinear map has linear scale $N_{E/F}(\lambda)$. Link a free one-dimensional $F$-role by the inclusion of the scalar subline to obtain that scale. The inclusion is a linked map and its nonzero vector is free to scale. If $[E:F]$ is even and the characteristic is odd, $\tau$ acts on the trace-zero line in the quadratic subfield by the sign of its determinant in a normal basis. Link this sign by the inclusion of another free one-dimensional role. Raise that sign to $d_{ij,k}$ and multiply by the norm. In other cases the semilinear sign is one. The resulting line scale is exactly $\det_F(P\tau)$. This is the [previous endomorphism-of-a-field norm and sign construction](../AlpogeTISL/CentroidNormLines.md), here applied to nonsemisimple radical grades.
- Multiply the visible determinant scales with exponents $h n_i n_j$ using binary multiplication of one-dimensional lines. In this final line give the source the marked vector $1$ and the target the scalar $r_*^h/\bigl(\kappa^h\varepsilon(\pi)^h\bigr)$, with the powered permutation sign known from the branch. This imposes exactly $(\det\phi)^h=r_*^h$.

Products, wedge products, field actions, inclusions, quotient projections and point marks all have bounded tensor arity. The variable number of occurrences is packed by role. Give each role the direct sum of its spaces with a coordinate-algebra projection action. For each kind of map use separate argument-occurrence copies with their own projections and incidence to the original role. The identifications of an argument with its original component are linear maps. Combine the maps of one kind componentwise on those copies, zero across nonincident indices. Thus the number of sorts and kinds of map is fixed. Pin indices through a map from an ordered label algebra to the relevant coordinate-algebra sort. Use coordinatewise multiplication on the ordered algebra, a point mark on its first coordinate idempotent, and the linear shift taking each coordinate idempotent to the next and the last to zero. Preserving the mark and shift fixes every coordinate. Map its successive idempotents to the labels being pinned, with the same order on the two sides. Any compatible original map induces the auxiliary maps by restriction, quotient, wedge or multiplication. Conversely, the algebra map of any system isomorphism obeys every displayed equation on the same map. The fixed-signature tensor-system compiler gives an ordinary three-tensor query. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4).

Every actual transporter of the desired determinants has $\det\phi=r_*$ by (1). Align its section and matrix units with inner automorphisms, so its determinant remains in $r_* C$. Its visible matching is among the branches, and it induces a transporter of that branch's diagram. Conversely a branch isomorphism gives an algebra map with determinant in $r_* C$. By the definition of $C$ an actual target automorphism brings the determinant exactly to $r_*$. Independently choose left and right units of determinants $u_*,v_*$, possible precisely when the two power membership tests pass. This gives the required raw transporter.

Each diagram has polynomial dense size by the visible rank bound, and the number of branches is polynomial. A single ordinary TI query suffices by a balanced positive OR of the branch queries using linear-side-growth TI gadgets. For two isomorphism comparisons $a\cong b$ and $c\cong d$, compare the unordered pairs of tagged ordered objects $\{(a,c),(b,d)\}$ and $\{(a,d),(b,c)\}$. Matching straight gives the second comparison and matching crossed gives the first. Fixed-size direct-sum wrappers and an intrinsic scalar-centroid tag realize this with linear side growth. Balancing gives logarithmic depth, hence polynomial dense size. [Field-uniform ordinary TI Boolean gates](../FourIsomorphismResults/RationalExtensionDegree.md), [Grochow and Qiao, linear-length tensor gadgets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16317).

If there are **no visible vertices**, the required isomorphism determinant coset is just the frame ratio modulo $C$. If the membership and unit tests pass, the remaining query can be the plain unmarked ordinary comparison of the normalized multiplication tensors. No bound on the number of residue factors or their widths enters that case.

## 5. A polynomial TI oracle reduction without the matching bound

Keep the logarithmic rank bound for visible grades, with no bound on the number of visible matchings. Over fixed $F$ there is a deterministic polynomial-time Turing reduction to ordinary TI. It computes the exact coset of possible algebra-isomorphism determinants, and together with the unit characters in (1) determines the full raw transporter determinant profile.

Use the section and matrix-unit diagram of Section 4 with the determinant lines omitted. In this diagram one can pin any list of distinct visible labels to prescribed distinct images by the ordered label algebra. The ordinary TI comparison decides whether such a partial matching extends to an aligned algebra isomorphism. Thus a greedy search that tries each possible image of the next unpinned visible label either finds one full matching $\pi_0$ for the source and target or proves there is no isomorphism. Each successful pin is kept, so the final matching is realizable by construction. When the visible set is empty, use the unpinned comparison.

For a single prescribed full matching, the determinant diagram of Section 4 has polynomial size even when other matchings would be numerous. Test the powered determinant equation for every value in $(F^\times)^h$, using one ordinary TI call per value. This set has at most $q-1$ elements. This computes the set of powered determinants for the particular matching, without producing a matrix representative. Select any value $a_0$ found for $\pi_0$.

On the target alone let $G$ be the group of algebra automorphisms that take the chosen section to itself and carry the chosen matrix units by a permutation of components. Let $P$ be its permutation image on the visible labels and $K$ the kernel of that action. Apply the same pinned comparisons to target self-isomorphisms. At each position in an ordering of the visible labels, fix all earlier positions and try each permitted image of that position. For each successful image, extend greedily to one full visible permutation. Those permutations generate $P$: at a given position they supply a transversal of the next point stabilizer in the current stabilizer, so an induction over the ordered positions generates the whole group. The number of trials and representatives is polynomial in the number of labels.

The character $\chi:G\to F^\times$ sends $\alpha$ to $(\det_F\alpha)^h$. Compute $\chi(K)$ by the full-matching test for the identity. For every permutation representative above, use the same test and keep any one character value. These values and $\chi(K)$ generate $\chi(G)$, because chosen lifts of generators of $P$ generate $G$ together with $K$. The character of the full target automorphism group has exactly the same image: any automorphism can be aligned by inner automorphisms whose determinants lie in $C=\ker(x\mapsto x^h)$. Thus the isomorphism determinants modulo $C$ are exactly $a_0\chi(G)$. All determinants in the preimage of this coset are achieved since every element of $C$ is the determinant of a genuine target automorphism. The two unit determinants remain independently selectable. Since $q$ is fixed, the subgroup calculations and the number of character values tested per full matching have constant size. All other work is polynomial by the rank bound and the fixed-signature compiler.

## A nonsemisimple family with unbounded corner widths

For fixed odd $q$, let
$$
A=F[t]/(t^N)\ \times\ M_{q-1}(B),
$$
where $N\ge3$ and $B$ is any finite-dimensional local associative $F$-algebra with residue field $F$. No bound is placed on the radical width or nilpotence length of $B$. The two residue matrix sizes $1$ and $q-1$ differ. The second corner is invisible because every correction index divides $q-1$. The first corner has one-dimensional radical grades and a unique residue descriptor, so it is handled even when its total dimension $N$ is unbounded. Different endpoint presentations and arbitrary raw isotopy twists are allowed. This family illustrates why one can use a heterogeneous corner expansion without requiring a common matrix divisor of the two residue sizes.

The reduction keeps the hypotheses explicit. Its algebraic character and correction formulas use neither classical RH nor Dirichlet GRH. The earlier Moore and trace orientation perspective extends here through residue fields and the radical filtration. It applies to the stated family of associative multiplication isotopes within the reverse direction of [Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2).

## A recognizable TI-complete class with cube-zero radical

For each fixed $q$, set $d=q-1$. Consider all raw multiplication isotopes of algebras with $J^3=0$ and every residue matrix size divisible by $d$. The preceding reduction recognizes this class and reduces special-linear isomorphism on it to ordinary TI. All weights $n_i n_j$ are multiples of $q-1$, so the no-visible-vertices case applies. This restricted special-linear problem is also ordinary-TI-hard by a deterministic same-field many-one map.

For a tensor $T:X\times Y\to Z$, form the triangular algebra $B_T$ on $F^3\oplus X\oplus Y\oplus Z$. Its diagonal idempotents are the three vertex labels. Place $X,Y,Z$ in corners $(1,2),(2,3),(1,3)$, and make $T$ the sole radical multiplication $X\times Y\to Z$. The radical is $X\oplus Y\oplus Z$, its square is $\operatorname{im}T$, and its cube is zero. For positive input dimensions the arrows in the radical modulo its square contain $1\to2$ and $2\to3$, with possibly $1\to3$. This forces every quotient permutation to preserve the vertex positions. Conjugating the lifted idempotents back by a radical unit identifies the corner maps, and preservation of their only product is precisely an ordinary transporter for $T$. Conversely an ordinary transporter gives an algebra isomorphism on these corners. Zero-dimensional inputs, if permitted, have a zero tensor and can be settled directly. [Earlier triangular tensor-to-algebra encoding](../FourIsomorphismResults/RationalExtensionDegree.md).

Now output the multiplication tensor of $M_d(B_T)$ in the ordered entrywise basis. Every ordinary transporter of $T$ gives an entrywise algebra isomorphism with determinant $(\det\phi_{B})^{d^2}=1$. It therefore supplies three special-linear mode maps. Conversely any transporter of the output multiplication tensors yields an algebra isomorphism of the matrix algebras by Section 1. The complete matrix-unit system in the source can be aligned with that in the target: align modulo the radical within each residue factor, then conjugate by a radical unit to align the lifts. This is the same argument as Section 3 with the chosen $d$-by-$d$ matrix units. Restricting to the $(1,1)$ corner gives $B_T\cong B_{T'}$, and hence the original ordinary transporter. The factor $d$ is constant and the output has cube-zero radical. Thus this gives both polynomial many-one directions for the stated, recognizable nonsemisimple class.

For $q>2$ the raw class still contains ordinarily equivalent pairs with no special-linear transporter. For example, take the multiplication tensor of $M_d(B_T)$ and apply an output-only basis twist of determinant different from $1$. All three unit and algebra-automorphism determinants of the matrix algebra are $1$. For the left and right unit characters the multiplicities in Section 2 are divisible by $d$, since the regular actions on a matrix algebra repeat across its $d$ columns or rows. For algebra automorphisms use the same matrix-unit alignment, whose inner determinant is trivial, and the entrywise determinant formula. Therefore the output determinant of every raw transporter equals the chosen twist, so the pair remains inequivalent under the three special-linear groups.
