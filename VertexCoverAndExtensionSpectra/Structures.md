# Exact field-degree spectra for alternating spaces, Lie algebras, cubic forms and groups

Informal proofs. Fix a finite base field $F=\mathbb F_q$. The finite étale multiplication-table construction of the preceding field-degree result associates to a Vertex Cover instance a pair $A,B$ of finite étale $F$-algebras and distinct primes $p_i$ such that

$$
A_{\mathbb F_{q^m}}\simeq B_{\mathbb F_{q^m}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\{i:p_i\mid m\}\text{ covers the graph}.
$$

This note transfers the entire spectrum, including the threshold and approximation hardness, using concrete structures. Several constructions work over arbitrary base fields. For every fixed $q$, testing a specified binary degree is polynomial time on the represented families. Alternating spaces, Lie algebras, and cubic forms have polynomial constructions on the explicit coordinate inputs. The group and graph results use succinct families indexed by $m$. [Grochow–Qiao on isomorphism problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309) · [Karp on Vertex Cover](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf)

## 1. A Heisenberg construction that commutes with scalar extension

For a nonzero finite-dimensional commutative unital $F$-algebra $A$, set $V_A=A^2$, $W_A=A$, and define the alternating bilinear map

$$
\beta_A((x,y),(x',y'))=xy'-x'y\in W_A.
$$

It has zero radical and spans all of $W_A$, by testing the two coordinate vectors with a unit coordinate. It is alternating in characteristic two as well. The alternating matrix space is the image of $W_A^*$ in the alternating forms on $V_A$ under $\beta_A^*$. An isometry of the matrix spaces is equivalent to a pair of invertible maps $(P,R)$ with $\beta_B(Pu,Pv)=R\beta_A(u,v)$, since $\beta_A^*,\beta_B^*$ identify the form spaces with the dual output spaces. The isomorphism in the output mode is uniquely determined by $P$.

Define the two-step Lie algebra $\mathfrak h(A)=V_A\oplus W_A$ with bracket $[(u,z),(v,z')]=(0,\beta_A(u,v))$. Its derived algebra equals its center and is $W_A$. Its isomorphisms therefore induce exactly the same transporter pairs. Conversely any pair lifts to Lie isomorphisms, by independently choosing an arbitrary linear map $V_A\to W_B$ in the lower-left block. All these constructions are given by multiplication constants and commute with any scalar extension.

To prove they reflect algebra isomorphism, compute the centroid. For a bilinear map $\beta$, these are pairs $(T,S)$ on its input and output satisfying

$$
\beta(Tu,v)=\beta(u,Tv)=S\beta(u,v)\qquad\text{for all }u,v.
$$

For $\beta_A$, using two vectors on the first coordinate axis forces the lower-left block of $T$ to be zero. The second axis forces the upper-right block to be zero. Taking $u=(x,0),v=(0,y)$ gives $T_{11}(x)y=xT_{22}(y)=S(xy)$. Setting $x=1$ and $y=1$ gives $T_{11}=T_{22}=S$, and $S$ is multiplication by $S(1)$. The centroid is exactly $A$ as a unital algebra. A pseudoisometry conjugates the centroids, so it induces an algebra isomorphism $\phi:A\to B$. Conversely $(\phi\oplus\phi,\phi)$ is a pseudoisometry, and this proves the equivalence over every field extension. The centroid reconstruction for Heisenberg groups has published precedents. The proof here includes arbitrary commutative unital coefficients and gives the precise functor needed for the spectrum. [Lewis–Wilson](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.5466) · [Grochow–Qiao on alternating spaces and two-step Lie algebras](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol200-ccc2021/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.16/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.16.pdf)

**Exact determinant transport.** Centroid conjugation gives $P(au)=\phi(a)P(u)$ and $R(az)=\phi(a)R(z)$. Thus all pairs are

$$
P=Q(\phi\oplus\phi),\qquad R=m_{\det_B Q}\phi,\qquad Q\in\operatorname{GL}_2(B).
$$

The expression for $R$ follows by computing the determinant of two columns over the commutative ring. Over a finite étale algebra in the displayed bases, put $\delta=\det_F\phi$ and $t=\operatorname{N}_{B/F}(\det_B Q)$. After extending to a splitting field, each block of $Q$ has size two and each block of $m_{\det_B Q}$ has size one, which proves

$$
\det P=t\delta^2,\qquad \det R=t\delta,qquad \det\mathfrak h(P,R)=t^2\delta^3.
$$

The Lie determinant is independent of the extra linear map into the center. Both quotient-and-center determinants equal one precisely when there is a determinant-one algebra isomorphism, since their ratio is $\delta$ and $Q=I$ suffices in the reverse direction. On the matrix-space side the induced determinant on dual form coordinates is the reciprocal of $\det R$ under the corresponding push-forward action, so determinant one has the same condition. For just the determinant on the whole Lie algebra over a finite extension, the equation becomes $t^2\delta^3=1$. Norms from finite étale algebras to finite fields are surjective on units, since each constituent finite field has a surjective norm, so the condition is equivalent to $\delta$ being a square in the extension field. In particular the normalized Vertex Cover instances preserve the exact ordinary spectrum also for determinant one in the Lie algebra's ambient space.

The raw-coordinate version supports recognition and pointwise ordinary and prescribed-determinant queries. For a raw alternating space form its bimap by assigning coordinates to a basis of the space. For a raw two-step Lie algebra compute the center, derived subspace, and quotient. Require that center and derived coincide, and take the induced alternating bimap. Require a zero common radical and a spanning image, then compute the centroid by linear equations. Products of centroid pairs again belong to the centroid: the two orders of acting on different inputs show that their output maps commute, by the spanning-image condition, and the zero radical then implies commutation on inputs. Require the centroid to be finite étale using the multiplication table and invertibility of Frobenius. For every centroid field factor, check that the input module has rank two and the output module has rank one, using the factor idempotents. The balanced bimap on each factor is then a nonzero scalar times the two-dimensional wedge product, and rescaling an output basis puts it in the displayed form. These tests recognize the family up to isomorphism and recover an explicit adapter. They use fixed-base-field linear algebra and finite étale decomposition.

For adapters chosen in the supplied bases, first convert any requested determinants to the normal-form bases by dividing by the corresponding adapter determinant ratios. Ordinary pointwise decision compares the powered Frobenius profiles. For separate input and output determinant targets, the ratio of normalized targets is the required $\delta$, and a suitable norm $t$ always exists. Use the trace-form ratio $D_A/D_B$, whose square roots are the only possible $\delta$. In odd characteristic both signs occur if the common powered profile has an even length or a repeated odd length. Otherwise the powered profile has distinct odd lengths, so no cycle split and $m$ is coprime to the joint permutation order. Any such isomorphism already exists over the base, where its determinant can be computed by matching equal-degree factors. For the single Lie determinant, use the same candidate $\delta$ values and test whether the normalized determinant target divided by $\delta^3$ is a square in the extension. If a candidate needs a quadratic field, only that small field is constructed. For a nonzero $z$ in a small subfield $\mathbb F_{q^d}$, in odd characteristic it becomes a square in $\mathbb F_{q^m}$ precisely when it is already a square in the subfield or $m/d$ is even. In characteristic two square roots are unique. This gives polynomial pointwise decision without constructing the degree-$m$ field. [Lenstra on finite-field isomorphisms](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

An additional ordinary invariant is the rank enumerator of the alternating matrix space. Over a finite extension $E$ of size $Q_0$, write the étale decomposition of $A_E$ as a product of field factors of degrees $d_j$. Identify the form indexed by $c\in A_E$ with $(u,v)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(c\beta_A(u,v))$. Its rank is $2\sum_{j:c_j\ne0} d_j$, since the trace pairing on each field factor is nondegenerate. Thus the enumerator is

$$
\prod_j\bigl(1+(Q_0^{d_j}-1)z^{2d_j}\bigr).
$$

Its lowest positive-degree term identifies the smallest degree and its multiplicity, after which the corresponding factors can be canceled and the argument repeated. Thus equality of alternating-space rank distributions has precisely the same extension spectrum on these examples. The integer coefficients can be very large for a binary degree, but the profile verifier evaluates equality without expanding them.

## 2. Uncolored cubic form equivalence in every characteristic

For finite étale $A$ take the homogeneous cubic on the full unmarked space $A^3$

$$
f_A(x,y,z)=\operatorname{Tr}_{A/F}(xyz).
$$

Over a splitting field it is $\sum_i x_i y_i z_i$ in the primitive-idempotent coordinates. At a nonzero point of a single three-dimensional block its Hessian has rank at least two, since some principal $2\times2$ minor is the negative square of a nonzero coordinate. Its rank is at most three. At a point with nonzero coordinates in two blocks the Hessian rank is at least four, since the Hessian is block diagonal. Thus the geometric projective locus of Hessian rank at most three is exactly the disjoint union of the projective planes of the primitive blocks. Its maximal irreducible components are those planes. This argument is valid in characteristics two and three.

Any equivalence over an extension field sends the planes to the planes, since linear substitutions give the congruence transformation on Hessians. This holds even if equivalence allows a global nonzero scalar multiplying the cubic. The induced bijection of primitive blocks is Galois-equivariant, so it descends to an isomorphism of the two finite étale algebras there. Conversely applying any algebra isomorphism to all three coordinates preserves the trace cubic. The uncolored cubic equivalence spectrum and the projective equivalence spectrum are therefore both exactly the algebra spectrum. [Grochow–Qiao–Tang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.01085) · [Finite étale algebras and Galois sets](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04JI)

In the exact cubic equivalence problem, any automorphism is a permutation of the geometric blocks followed in each block by a monomial map with product of its diagonal factors equal to one. This follows because the coordinate planes have been recovered and each restricted cubic is a product of three independent linear forms. Over the splitting field its determinant is therefore a sign. In odd characteristic both determinant signs occur over every finite base of definition. If a Frobenius cycle of blocks has odd length, swap two coordinates in all its blocks. If all cycles have even length, rotate one even cycle using the canonical coordinate identifications in the split product. Both constructions commute with Frobenius and have determinant $-1$, whereas the identity has determinant one. In characteristic two every automorphism has determinant one. With three copies of the algebra bases, a transporter induced by $\phi$ has determinant $\delta^3$, where $\delta^2=D_A/D_B$ is the ratio of the trace-form determinants. For a normalized determinant target $d$, the signed spectrum is therefore exactly the ordinary spectrum if $d^2=(D_A/D_B)^3$, and is empty otherwise. The normalized Vertex Cover instances satisfy this for $d=1$.

**Recognition from a cubic coordinate array.** The cubic form is supplied by its coefficients as a formal polynomial. Use its complete trilinear polarization obtained by taking the coefficient of $t_1t_2t_3$ in $f(t_1u+t_2v+t_3w)$. No division by six is needed. The polarization of $xyz$ is concise and has scalar centroid. Indeed, testing $S(a u,e_j,e_j)=S(u,a e_j,e_j)$ makes the off-diagonal entries of $a$ zero, and the three distinct basis vectors then force the diagonal entries to agree. For a direct sum of such polarizations, test vectors in different blocks prohibit any off-diagonal block of $a$, by conciseness of each summand. Consequently the centroid over a splitting field is a product of scalar algebras indexed by the primitive blocks, and the centroid before splitting is exactly $A$.

For raw input compute the polarization centroid by linear equations, requiring concise polarization and a finite étale centroid. Recover the field idempotents, and require the vector space component over each field $K_i$ to have rank three. In bases of these modules, solve the linear equations for ternary $K_i$-cubics $g_i$ such that the original cubic polynomial equals $\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}_{K_i/F}(g_i)$. This trace assembly is injective on the local coefficient spaces: after scalar extension to a splitting field the distinct embeddings have disjoint blocks of variables. It checks the entire raw cubic, which matters when polarization loses information. Accept precisely when each recovered $g_i$ factors into three independent linear forms over $K_i$. Such a factorization supplies an explicit adapter to $f_A$. Conversely the preceding centroid analysis shows that every cubic of the intended family passes.

The factor test is deterministic polynomial time over fixed characteristic. For $|K_i|>3$, find a vector $a$ with $g_i(a)\ne0$ by testing a grid of four elements in each coordinate, using the individual degree bound. For the three coordinate vectors $e_j$, factor the monic cubic $g_i(a t+e_j)/g_i(a)$. When a linear-factor decomposition exists, the roots are the negatives of the $j$-th coefficients of the factors normalized to evaluate to one at $a$. Enumerate the constant many ways to match these root lists across the three coordinates, and check multiplication and linear independence directly. The tiny fields may be handled by enumeration. To extract distinct roots from the univariate cubics, first take the gcd with $t^{|K_i|}-t$ using modular powering, then split the resulting squarefree polynomial via fixed-prime Frobenius fixed spaces and gcds. Repeated divisions recover multiplicities, and any leftover factor rules out complete splitting. This check gives pointwise polynomial-time decision for the recognized raw cubic family, with the adapter ratios accounting for signed determinants in the supplied bases. [Shoup on deterministic finite-field techniques](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

## 3. Commutative local algebras with radical cube zero

For a finite étale algebra $A$ form the commutative unital algebra

$$
R(A)=F\oplus A\epsilon\oplus A\epsilon^2,
\qquad (x\epsilon)(y\epsilon)=xy\epsilon^2,
$$

where all other products within the radical involving $\epsilon^2$ vanish, and the first factor is the scalar unit. The displayed radical is nilpotent of index three and the quotient is $F$, so the algebra remains local under every extension. Its radical modulo its square and its radical square carry the multiplication bimap of $A$. Any algebra isomorphism induces $f,g$ on these two layers with $g(xy)=f(x)f(y)$. Here $u=f(1)$ is a unit, since $g(x)=uf(x)$ and both maps are invertible. Then $u^{-1}f$ is a unital algebra isomorphism of the coefficient algebras. Conversely every coefficient isomorphism extends degreewise. Thus even the extension-degree problem between these local algebras has the full étale spectrum. This is the unit normalization for associative isotopy applied to a characteristic layer. [Albert](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968960)

The same calculation gives the local algebra's determinant spectrum. All pairs are $(m_u\phi,m_{u^2}\phi)$, and every such pair can be lifted by choosing an arbitrary linear map from the first radical layer to the second. The quotient unit is fixed. Writing $t=N(u)$ and $\delta=\det\phi$, the two layer determinants are $t\delta,t^2\delta$, and the whole determinant is $t^3\delta^2$. Thus requiring one on both layers gives the signed algebra spectrum, while a prescribed whole determinant $d$ is available at an ordinary successful degree precisely when $d/(D_A/D_B)$ is a cube in the extension field in the displayed bases. This is polynomial to test for binary $m$: for a base scalar one needs the exponent $(q^m-1)/\gcd(3,q^m-1)$ only modulo $q-1$, which is obtained by powering modulo $3(q-1)$. In particular the normalized Vertex Cover pairs also preserve the whole determinant-one spectrum for these local algebras.

## 4. Abstract class-two group and uncolored graph families

For any finite extension $E/F$, take the actual abstract group $\mathrm{UT}_3(A_E)$, the upper unitriangular $3\times3$ group. In coordinates the group law is

$$
(x,y,z)(x',y',z')=(x+x',y+y',z+z'+xy').
$$

The center equals the derived subgroup. Both the center and the quotient by it are elementary abelian, and the commutator bimap on them is $\beta_{A_E}$. An isomorphism of abstract groups therefore induces a pseudoisometry over $\mathbb F_p$, where $p=\operatorname{char} F$. The same axis computation as in Section 1 recovers $A_E$ as a ring. For finite étale algebras over finite $E$, a ring isomorphism implies the existence of an $E$-algebra isomorphism, since the absolute field degrees determine the relative degrees by dividing by $[E : \mathbb F_p]$, and each extension of a specified degree over a finite field is unique up to base-linear isomorphism. Conversely an algebra isomorphism gives a group isomorphism by applying it to entries. The argument works for exponent $p$ in odd characteristic and exponent four in characteristic two. It does not assume that an arbitrary commutator pseudoisometry preserves the square map in characteristic two. The output specification is an explicit base algebra table and the instruction to use this group law after degree $m$ scalar extension. The group itself has order $q^{3nm}$ for algebra dimension $n$. Thus this is a succinct indexed group family result. It gives no lower bound on ordinary Cayley-table group isomorphism. [Lewis–Wilson](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.5466) · [Grochow–Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

For a graph version let $\pi$ be the base Frobenius permutation on the finite geometric set $X$, given by an explicit disjoint-cycle representation. The family at degree $m$ is the uncolored graph on $X\times\{0,1,2\}$ with edges $(x,0)--(x,1)--(x,2)--(\pi^m(x),0)$. A $d$-cycle of $\pi^m$ gives one simple cycle of length $3d$, including a triangle when $d=1$. Thus all graphs in the family are simple 2-regular, and isomorphism is exactly equality of powered cycle profiles. Any binary $m$ permits construction of these explicit graphs in time polynomial in the graph order and the bit length of $m$. The threshold optimization over the family remains NP-complete. This describes the choice of a numeric parameter across graph pairs, with uniform polynomial decision for each specified pair.

There is also a graph construction using the scalar-extended ring directly. Give the additive group of $A_E$ edges $x\sim y$ when $x-y$ is a unit. If the field factors have sizes $k_1,\ldots,k_s$, the graph is the categorical product of complete graphs of those orders. Its adjacency eigenvalues are the products of the eigenvalues $k_i-1,-1$ from each factor, with multiplicities $1,k_i-1$, respectively. This spectrum already determines the field sizes. In detail, the largest eigenvalue modulus is $D=\prod_i(k_i-1)$, with absolute-value multiplicity $2^r$, where $r$ counts the factors of size two. Divide all absolute-value multiplicities by $2^r$. For every occurring absolute eigenvalue $h$, the remaining multiplicity divided by $D/h$ counts the subsets of the remaining factors whose $k_i-1$ multiply to $D/h$. Recover these factors inductively by taking the smallest subset product greater than one and its multiplicity, then canceling the corresponding subset-product factor. More explicitly, the free abelian group on formal symbols $[a]$ for positive integers, with multiplication $[a][b]=[ab]$, contains the finite enumerator $\prod(1+[k_i-1])$. The coefficient at the smallest nontrivial index is the number of copies of that factor. Removing those copies is unique and can be performed in increasing index order because their constant coefficient at index one is one. This graph family has the same threshold spectrum for either graph isomorphism or adjacency cospectrality. It is succinct, since the expanded graph has $q^{nm}$ vertices.

The categorical product description and the forward adjacency spectrum are established for unitary Cayley graphs of finite commutative rings. [Akhtar et al.](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v16i1r117) · [Liu–Zhou](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.5932)

## 5. Complexity consequences and scope

All ordinary reductions preserve the entire set of successful degrees, as do the signed clauses for the normalized Vertex Cover instances. These sets can have exponentially many divisibility-minimal members and can require a superpolynomial least degree compared with the algebra dimension. The hard graph gadget uses primes $p_i\in[P,Q]$ for a ratio $Q/P\le1+1/v$ with $P$ growing polynomially in the vertex count $v$. Such primes are found by sieving an interval $[X,2X)$ starting with $X=v^3$, splitting it into $v$ consecutive parts, and taking a part containing at least $v$ primes. The prime number theorem guarantees success at the initial scale for all sufficiently large $v$, and finitely many smaller sizes can be handled by doubling $X$ until success. For an edge $ij$, the difference of Frobenius fixed counts at power $t$ is

$$
p_i p_j(1-\mathbf1_{p_i\mid t})(1-\mathbf1_{p_j\mid t}).
$$

This is realized by the four cycle lengths $1,p_i,p_j,p_ip_j$, with signs $+,-,-,+$, using multiplicities to give each term the same coefficient. Summing over the edges gives the exact cover spectrum. Taking $K=Q^k$ separates covers of size at most $k$ from larger covers, since $P^{k+1}>Q^k$. The gap $P^{k+1}/Q^k\ge P/\mathrm e$ tends to infinity, giving hardness of approximation to within any fixed factor. The coordinate algebras have polynomial dimension. Since all cycle lengths in these instances are odd, their trace-form determinants are squares. A base change makes them equal. The two input profiles differ, so at every successful degree at least one cycle split and the common powered profile affords a determinant sign correction when needed. In characteristic two equal trace determinants already force determinant one. These are the normalized instances used in the signed clauses above.

For fixed $q$, the threshold problem is in NP on the explicitly represented families, since a binary degree of length at most that of the threshold suffices and a powered-profile test verifies it. The same membership holds for the raw recognizable alternating, Lie and cubic classes above by doing that recognition first. The proof establishes NP-completeness on these classes and NP-hardness for unrestricted input versions containing them. It does not assert NP membership for arbitrary tensor or algebra instances with a binary extension degree. Centroids, Heisenberg constructions, trace-form descent, and Hessian covariance are established ingredients. The exact spectra and hardness are the outcomes of combining them with the Vertex Cover construction.
