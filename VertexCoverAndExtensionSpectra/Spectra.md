# Cover constraints for arbitrary finite étale tensor pairs

Fix a finite field of size q. A finite étale algebra is a finite product of finite extension fields. Its ordinary multiplication-tensor transporters exist precisely when an algebra isomorphism exists. Indeed, if h(xy)=f(x)g(y), evaluation at the two units shows that f(1) and g(1) are units and that h divided by their product is an algebra isomorphism. For determinant-one transport the normalized isomorphism must also have determinant one, since its determinant is det(f)det(g)/det(h). The converse in both cases uses the same algebra isomorphism in all three places. We use multiplication tables in coherent algebra bases throughout. The associated permutation of geometric primitive idempotents is Frobenius. Its cycles of length d are exactly the degree-d factors. [Finite étale descent](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04JI) · [Algebra isotopy](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968960)

## 1. A polynomial-size conjunction of divisibility clauses

Let x_d be the number of degree-d factors on the first side minus the number on the second. Assume equal dimension n. Compute for each k from 2 to n

$$b_k=\sum_{k\mid d}(d/k)x_d.$$

Include a clause for every k with nonzero coefficient. Its literals are all the maximal prime powers dividing k, one per prime. A positive extension degree m works exactly when each included clause has at least one literal dividing m. For example, one degree-12 field and six degree-2 fields on one side, versus two degree-6 and three degree-4 fields on the other, encode precisely “4 divides m or 3 divides m.”

**Proof.** Let e_d denote the formal class of a d-cycle. Powering by m sends it to gcd(m,d) cycles of length d/gcd(m,d). On coprime lengths Cartesian products multiply the lengths. In the free abelian group on cycles put v_1=e_1, put v_{p^a}=e_{p^a}−p e_{p^{a−1}}, and take the Cartesian product of these expressions for the distinct prime powers in k to define v_k. The change of basis is triangular, with inverse

$$e_d=\sum_{k\mid d}(d/k)v_k.$$

Thus b_k is exactly the coefficient on v_k, and the coefficient at 1 vanishes by equality of dimensions. Powering by p kills v_p, sends v_{p^a} to p times v_{p^{a−1}} for a at least two, and acts as the identity on the other prime factors. Consequently powering by m kills v_k precisely when k's clause holds. On the surviving k the new prime support is unchanged, and each positive exponent is reduced by the corresponding exponent of m. This is injective on surviving indices and each scale multiplier is a positive integer. There can be no cancellation between them. Hence the powered cycle profiles agree exactly under the stated condition. The coefficient arithmetic here takes place over the integers irrespective of the characteristic of the tensors. The underlying formal arithmetic of cyclic sets is classical. [Dress and Siebeneicher](https://doi.org/10.1016/0001-8708(89)90027-3)

All clauses are computable in polynomial time on explicit tables. For clarity, the fixed subspace dimension of the t-th power of the q-Frobenius on one algebra is the sum of gcd(t,d) over its cycle lengths d, by the subfield formula. For t up to n, use gcd(t,d) = sum of Euler phi(j) for j dividing both t and d. Invert the divisor sum on t to obtain the number of cycles of lengths divisible by each j, then invert the multiples sum to find every cycle count. Powering a linear map and computing its fixed dimension are polynomial operations over the fixed field. All factorizations subsequently needed are of integers at most n. Equivalently one can use factor tables directly if supplied.

**Converse and encoding cost.** For any explicit list of distinct clause indices k, form the signed cycle profile equal to the sum of their v_k. Separate its positive and negative cycle multiplicities. These are equal-dimension étale algebras, with exactly the conjunction specified. Their common dimension is at most the sum over k of k times two to the power omega(k)−1, where omega counts distinct prime divisors. For the empty list take two identical algebras. This is polynomial in the sum of the numerical indices, a unary size measure. It need not be polynomial in the binary size of an unrestricted list of prime literals.

**Comparison over every extension is polynomial.** For each clause of a proposed consequent, construct the largest divisor of the joint period that falsifies that clause. In the period use the maximum prime exponents occurring on either side. For each literal p^a in the tested clause, cap the exponent at a−1, leaving all other exponents at their maxima. Every divisor falsifying the clause divides this one. Thus the antecedent implies the clause exactly when its formula is false at this extremal divisor. Repeat for all clauses, and in both directions for equality. Every integer projects to the divisor given by its gcd with the joint period without changing any truth value. This decides inclusion and equality of ordinary finite étale extension spectra without enumerating divisors. Removing redundant clauses is also polynomial. The maximal boxes of failed exponents give a unique irredundant description relative to the joint period.

## 2. A size separation between ordinary and determinant-one encodings

Use an odd finite field and distinct odd primes p_1 through p_k. Take the product A of the k corresponding degree-p_i fields. Give B a copy of A with one basis vector negated. Ordinary isomorphism holds at every extension. A determinant-one algebra isomorphism exists over degree m exactly when some p_i divides m. To see this, the reference isomorphism has determinant minus one, so we require an automorphism of odd permutation sign on the geometric idempotents. Before any cycle splits, the powered permutation has distinct odd lengths, and its centralizer consists entirely of even rotations. As soon as a length-p_i cycle splits, at least two resulting length-one factors can be exchanged. This gives the requisite odd automorphism. The same criterion applies to special-linear tensor transport by the unit argument.

For ordinary finite étale encodings of that same disjunction, the **minimum possible common dimension** is

$$2^{k-1}\prod_{i=1}^k p_i.$$

Here is a proof allowing arbitrary auxiliary cycles. The difference vector x is nonzero at degree one and killed separately by powering with each designated prime p. The cycle formula then gives x_{pr}=−x_r/p when p does not divide r, and every coefficient with index divisible by p squared vanishes. Writing N for the product of the primes, take any nonzero block with indices ud where u is coprime to N and d divides N. The coefficient at ud must equal the Möbius function of d times x_u/d. In particular x_u is a nonzero multiple of N. In that block alone the total dimension of the positive coefficients is at least 2^{k−1}uN. Conversely the positive and negative parts of v_N attain equality, by Section 1. This reproduces the one-clause minimal-dimension theorem in the preceding research packet and applies it to a small signed input.

**Consequences.** The signed pair has common dimension only the sum of the primes. Choosing the first k odd primes gives a superpolynomial size separation, including when the input is a dense tensor table. In the other direction, the ordinary spectrum of any pair of dimension n can be realized by a signed pair of dimension 2n. Double both algebras by direct product with themselves. Their trace determinants are now squares, so rescale one basis vector to make the two trace determinants equal. Ordinary isomorphism on the doubled pair is equivalent to the original profile equality. Every algebra isomorphism on that pair now has determinant plus or minus one, by the trace form. At every successful degree an odd automorphism is available: rotate an even cycle, or interchange two identical odd cycles in the doubled profile. Thus either orientation can be realized. In characteristic two equal nonzero discriminants force determinant one directly.

More precisely, over a fixed odd finite field the maximum, over signed inputs of dimension at most n with nonempty spectrum, of the least dimension of an ordinary étale pair realizing the same spectrum has logarithm

$$ (1+o(1))\sqrt{n\log n}. $$

Indeed unequal trace determinants force an empty signed spectrum. With equal trace determinants, any successful ordinary degree where orientation cannot be corrected has distinct odd cycle lengths after powering. This can happen only if no original cycle split, as splitting creates duplicate lengths. The unsplit powered profile therefore agrees already at the base field, and its conjugating bijections are those for the original Frobenius because the exponent is invertible modulo the joint period. Thus either the signed and ordinary spectra agree everywhere, or the base profiles agree, the base orientation fails, and the signed spectrum consists precisely of degrees divisible by any prime of the common permutation order. An order one case has empty spectrum and was excluded. The preceding sharp dimension formula applies to this disjunction. The radical of a permutation order on n letters is at most Landau's maximal order g(n). The number of distinct prime divisors is O(sqrt(n/log n)), since the sum of these primes is at most n, as one sees by assigning each prime to one cycle divisible by it. Hence the upper estimate follows from Landau's asymptotic. For the matching lower estimate take all odd primes up to (1−epsilon)sqrt(n log n), whose sum is asymptotic to x squared divided by 2 log x at cutoff x. Their product has logarithm asymptotic to x. Let epsilon tend to zero. [Landau's function](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.2160)

The resource being separated is the dimension of **one pair of finite étale multiplication tensors reproducing the answers over all extensions of the base field**. A reduction allowed to output arbitrary tensors, or required to answer only over the specified field, has a different contract. This is a restricted succinctness result relevant to the classical-group question. [Grochow and coauthors, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2)

## 3. Exact counting and algebraic complexity

Give each non-isolated graph vertex a distinct polynomial-sized prime, and encode its edges as the indices p_i p_j in Section 1. Take R to be their squarefree product, including isolated vertices as optional designated primes when needed. Good divisors of R correspond bijectively to vertex covers. This preserves vertex membership, inclusion, size as number of prime factors, and weights assigned to selected primes. The construction is polynomial in graph size in the dense tensor model. The optional primes can alternatively be made visible in the joint permutation order by appending the same prime-length cycles to both algebras.

* Counting successful divisors of the supplied squarefree period is #P-complete under polynomial-time counting reductions, even on bipartite graph images. The certificate selects the prime subset and tests the powered profiles. Counting divisibility-minimal successful degrees is also #P-complete under counting reductions. To see hardness for that variant, attach one private leaf to every vertex of a graph G. Every maximal independent set of this enlarged graph picks exactly one of each vertex-leaf pair, and its original vertices form any independent set of G. Complements are minimal covers. For membership in #P one tests each single-prime deletion from a candidate and uses upward closure. [Vadhan on counting covers and independent sets](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539797321602)
* Over any coefficient field of characteristic different from two, there are polynomial-size finite étale tensor pairs for which the polynomial that sums the squarefree monomials of successful divisors is **VNP-complete under polynomial-size projections**. It is literally the vertex-weighted cover polynomial on the Briquel–Koiran bipartite family. The arithmetic coefficient field for this polynomial is independent of the finite field defining the tensor tables. This classifies the generating polynomial, not the complexity of testing one pair at one degree. [Briquel and Koiran, Theorem 4](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/pascal.koiran/Publis/mfcs09.dichotomy_theorem.pdf)
* Its Newton polytope on a graph image is the cover polytope, affinely equivalent to the stable-set polytope. For the hard graphs of Göös–Jain–Watson it requires extension complexity exponential in v/log v on v vertices. Grochow's Newton-polytope method therefore precludes realizing this family by polynomial-size **monotone projections of the permanent** over the nonnegative reals. Indeed such a projection would exhibit the Newton polytope as a projection of a face of the corresponding Birkhoff polytope, which has a quadratic-size description in the permanent's side length. These hard graphs and the bipartite VNP-completeness examples are separate families. [Göös–Jain–Watson](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.07062) · [Grochow](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.08417)

The graph-image hardness statements also hold for signed tables after the dimension-doubling construction. The cycle calculus, counting hardness of graph problems, and polyhedral and algebraic-complexity theorems above are established ingredients. Priority for these tensor-spectrum applications remains unverified.
