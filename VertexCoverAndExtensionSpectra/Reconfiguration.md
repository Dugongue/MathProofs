# Reconfiguring tensor-isomorphism extension degrees

Informal proofs. Fix a finite field $\mathbb F_q$. The input comprises two finite étale multiplication tensors of common dimension $n$, a positive integer cap $K$ in binary, and two given successful extension degrees $u,v\le K$. A move multiplies or divides a degree by one prime. Every intermediate degree must be at most $K$ and admit a transporter. We give a hardness result, an exact reverse reduction to a weighted vertex-cover model, and parameterized algorithms.

## Profile representation

Let $c_d(A),c_d(B)$ be the multiplicities of the two algebras' field factors of degree $d$, and let $N$ be the least common multiple of all lengths appearing on either side. Over extension degree $m$, a length $d$ produces $\gcd(d,m)$ cycles of length $d/\gcd(d,m)$. The algebras are isomorphic exactly when these powered profiles agree. For a multiplication-tensor transporter written $h(xy)=f(x)g(y)$, substituting $1$ gives $f=m_a\phi,\ g=m_b\phi,\ h=m_{ab}\phi$, where $a,b$ are units and $\phi$ is an algebra isomorphism. Conversely these triples are transporters. The determinant-one variants agree as well since $\det h=\det f\det g/\det\phi$. The finite étale classification follows by passing to the primitive idempotents in a splitting field. [Stacks Project, Tag 04JI](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04JI).

For completeness the profiles themselves are recoverable in polynomial time from multiplication tables. Compute the linear $q$-Frobenius matrix $M$ from the $q$-th powers of the input basis elements. Write $b_t=\dim\ker(M^t-I)=\sum_d\gcd(d,t)c_d$. For $t=1,\ldots,n$, divisor inversion of the identity $\gcd(d,t)=\sum_{j\mid d,\ j\mid t}\varphi(j)$ gives $s_j=\sum_{j\mid d}c_d$. Descending subtraction then gives each $c_d$. Factor the lengths at most $n$ to obtain $N$ and all its prime exponents. A pointwise test for $m$ given in binary uses this data without constructing the field extension.

The threshold formula for ordinary success has one positive clause for every index $k$ with
$$
\sum_{d : \,k\mid d}(d/k)\bigl(c_d(A)-c_d(B)\bigr)\ne 0.
$$
The clause requires $v_p(m)\ge a$ for at least one $p^a\parallel k$. To see the formula, write $e_d$ for one cycle of length $d$, put $z_1=e_1$ and $z_{p^a}=e_{p^a}-p e_{p^{a-1}}$, and multiply across coprime cycle lengths. Expanding $e_{p^a}=\sum_{j=0}^a p^{a-j}z_{p^j}$ in every prime proves the coefficient formula. Powering by $m$ kills $z_{p^a}$ when $v_p(m)\ge a$; if $v_p(m)=r<a$ it gives $p^r z_{p^{a-r}}$. The products that survive keep distinct prime-power indices, so their coefficients cannot cancel.

All nonempty determinant-one spectra also have this monotone threshold form, with the same $N$. Indeed the square of the determinant of an isomorphism is the ratio of trace-form determinants. Unequal trace determinants therefore give an empty signed spectrum. In characteristic two, equal trace determinants make ordinary and signed success identical. In odd characteristic with equal trace determinants, an ordinary successful extension gives both signs if the powered cycle profile contains an even length or repeated odd length. Rotation of an even cycle and exchange of two equal odd cycles give the required odd automorphisms, respectively. In the remaining case all cycles have distinct odd lengths, no original cycle split, and therefore $\gcd(m,N)=1$. Because $m$ is invertible modulo $N$, transporters on the idempotents after powering are already defined over the base field. If the base profiles differ, signed success therefore equals ordinary success. If the base profiles agree, a determinant-one base isomorphism gives all degrees, and a base isomorphism with only the negative sign gives exactly the extra positive clause $\bigvee_{p\mid N}(v_p(m)\ge1)$. Polynomial-time construction of a base isomorphism for the orientation test follows by splitting the Frobenius-fixed product algebra into primitive idempotents by interpolation over fixed $\mathbb F_q$ and matching the resulting finite field factors by deterministic field isomorphism. Each splitting step chooses a nonscalar element in a current factor of the fixed algebra and takes its nonzero spectral projectors. [Lenstra, finite field isomorphisms](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf).

## Exact transfer from vertex-cover reconfiguration

**Theorem.** Fix $q$. Bounded extension-degree reconfiguration for finite étale multiplication tensors is PSPACE-complete, in both the ordinary and determinant-one settings. Hardness holds when the graph making the prime variables of each threshold clause a clique has bounded bandwidth, and every nontrivial clause has two literals with threshold $1$. The reduction preserves the exact number of moves between the constructed endpoints.

Start with vertex-cover reconfiguration on a simple graph $G$ with at least one edge, given covers $S,T$ of size at most $k$, and moves that add or remove one vertex with the size never exceeding $k$. This source problem is PSPACE-complete on bounded-bandwidth graphs. In Wrochna's Proposition 5, complement maximum independent sets to get minimum covers of size $\tau(G)$. Let the cover cap be $\tau(G)+1$ and simulate a jump by adding and then removing. Every cover path under that cap alternates between sizes $\tau(G)$ and $\tau(G)+1$, so each two steps induce one jump or a stutter. For the source restriction a graph with no edges belongs to an elementary always-connected family. [Wrochna, Proposition 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0847), [Mouawad–Nishimura–Raman, TAR convention](https://arxiv.org/html/1402.4926).

For the tensor reduction, assign distinct odd primes $p_i$ to vertices, all in an interval $[P,Q]$ with $Q/P\le1+1/|V(G)|$ and $P>e$. These primes have polynomial size and can be found by sieving. For $v=|V(G)|$, split $[v^3,2v^3)$ into $v$ equal intervals and look for a block with at least $v$ primes. The prime number theorem ensures a block for all sufficiently large $v$, since the whole interval eventually has more than $v^2$ primes. For small $v$ one can double the search scale until a block is found, a bounded overhead over the finitely many exceptions.

For an edge $ij$, put $M=p_ip_j$ and take $M$ base-field factors and one degree-$M$ factor on the $A$ side. Take $p_j$ factors of degree $p_i$ and $p_i$ of degree $p_j$ on the $B$ side. Their cycle-profile difference is $z_{p_i}z_{p_j}$, so the clause is precisely $p_i\mid m$ or $p_j\mid m$. Distinct edges give distinct basis indices. Take the direct products over all edges and include on both sides a common degree-$p_i$ field factor for every vertex, including isolated vertices. This last operation puts all marked primes in $N$ and preserves the profile difference. Construct each finite field factor by a deterministic fixed-characteristic irreducible-polynomial algorithm and expand the blockwise multiplication to tensor coordinates. All dimensions are polynomial in $v$. [Shoup, deterministic construction of irreducible polynomials](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf).

Set $K=Q^k$ and construct endpoints $u=\prod_{i\in S}p_i$, $v'=\prod_{i\in T}p_i$. A degree of at most $K$ cannot have $k+1$ marked prime divisors, since $P^{k+1}>Q^k$. Thus every admissible degree projects to a cover of size at most $k$ by taking its marked prime support. Conversely every cover of size at most $k$ lifts to the corresponding squarefree prime product. One prime move projects to one cover move or a stutter. Every cover move lifts to one prime move. At the chosen endpoints this proves exact equality of the two distances, with infinity for different components. The primal graph of the clauses is the original $G$.

To make the same hard instances determinant-one, every degree in the profiles is odd. In a splitting field the basis evaluation matrix $V_A$ satisfies $M_{\operatorname{Tr},A}=V_A^{\mathsf T}V_A$, and Frobenius permutes its rows evenly. Hence its determinant belongs to $\mathbb F_q^\times$, and the trace determinant is a nonzero base-field square. The same holds on the other side. Change the scale of one basis vector if needed to make the trace determinants equal. The base profiles differ because the graph has an edge, so the signed and ordinary spectra coincide by the profile classification.

For PSPACE membership, write $\pi(m)=\gcd(m,N)$. Success is preserved under this projection and so is the cap. One prime move projects to one prime move or a stutter. Any endpoint is connected to its projection by removing the extra primes one at a time, preserving its projection and only lowering its degree. Thus it suffices to decide reachability between projected endpoints in the divisor graph of $N$. A nondeterministic search stores one divisor and a counter up to the number of divisors, which is at most $N$, and tests each new state by the polynomial-time pointwise criterion. The encoding of $N$ is polynomial in the input length. This gives PSPACE membership.

## Reverse reduction for arbitrary profiles

**Theorem.** For every pair of finite étale multiplication tensors with feasible endpoints, bounded prime-move reachability has a polynomial-time many-one reduction to token-jump reachability on minimum-cardinality covers subject to one multiplicative weight cap. It also reduces to token sliding under the same cap. At divisor endpoints the reduction preserves exact distances after assigning the explicit move costs below.

By the endpoint projection just proved it suffices to use divisors $m$ of $N$. Write $A_p=v_p(N)$ and $a_p=v_p(m)$. Construct a graph having one clique $\{x_{p,a}:0\le a\le A_p\}$ for each prime and one clique $\{y_{J,(p,b)}:(p,b)\text{ is a literal of }J\}$ for each threshold clause $J$. A clause vertex $y_{J,(p,b)}$ is joined to $x_{p,a}$ exactly when $a<b$. These and the clique edges are all edges. The number of vertices and edges is polynomial in the profile description.

Let $r$ be the number of cliques. An independent set of size $r$ chooses one level for each prime and one valid witness per clause. Since valid endpoints exist, $r$ is the independence number. Consequently the complementary covers are precisely the minimum-cardinality covers. Give $x_{p,a}$ weight $p^{A_p-a}$ and each clause vertex weight $1$. Let $W$ be the product of all vertex weights, which is divisible by $N$ since it includes the options at level zero. The independent set at degree $m$ has weight product $N/m$, so its cover has weight product $Wm/N$. Set the cover product cap to $(W/N)K$. All weights and their products have polynomial bit length.

Every fixed degree fiber is connected by changing witnesses one at a time. An increasing unit prime step preserves all current witnesses. Before decreasing a prime by one level, replace any witness that would become invalid by a literal true after the step. Such a literal is true before the step because all clauses are monotone. Thus every prime-step path lifts to a graph path, and arbitrary feasible graph endpoint witnesses can be used. Conversely any token jump on these maximum independent sets, or equivalently on their complementary minimum covers, changes a single option within a single clique. A witness move projects to a stutter. A move from level $a$ to $a'$ can be subdivided into $|a-a'|$ prime steps. Every intermediate degree is at most the larger endpoint degree and is a multiple of the smaller endpoint degree. Hence every intermediate state satisfies the cap and all clauses. The jump's two exchanged vertices are adjacent in the clique, giving the same assertion for token sliding. If a level jump costs $|a-a'|$ and a witness jump costs zero, both directions prove equality of the weighted graph distance and the prime-step distance between projected divisor endpoints.

The cap in this reverse reduction is a bound on the product of positive integer weights. The theorem gives an exact simulation of the arbitrary-profile problem in this weighted graph model. The forward result uses ordinary cardinality-capped vertex covers.

## Parameters and distances

Let $t$ be the number of distinct prime divisors of $N$. The explicit projected graph has at most $(1+\log_2 n)^t$ vertices and at most $2t$ incident moves per vertex. This gives fixed-parameter tractability in $t$, since for every real $x>0$, $x^t \le e^x(t/e)^t$ when $t>0$, and one can set $x$ to a constant multiple of $1+\log n$. Thus the resulting bound is $2^{O(t\log(t+2))}$ times a polynomial in input length. Breadth-first search also gives exact distances between divisor endpoints. No factorization of an arbitrary binary endpoint is needed for the reachability algorithm.

There is an unconditional subexponential bound in the tensor dimension. For each prime let $A^A_p,A^B_p$ be its maximum exponent among the cycle lengths on the two sides, using zero for a missing prime. If these maxima differ, every good degree $m$ has $v_p(m)\ge A_p=\max(A^A_p,A^B_p)$. Indeed the two powered profiles have maximum exponents $\max(0,A^A_p-v_p(m))$ and $\max(0,A^B_p-v_p(m))$, which cannot coincide below $A_p$. Fix these forced coordinates before enumerating graph states.

For each remaining prime, the maximal power $p^{A_p}$ occurs as a divisor of a cycle length on each side. Assign it to one supporting cycle on the $A$ side. The sum of the prime powers assigned to a given cycle is at most their product, which divides its length. Thus the remaining maximal prime powers have total at most $n$. At most $(2+o(1))\sqrt{n/\log n}$ primes can have total at most $n$. This follows from the prime number theorem by comparing their sum to the sum $(1/2+o(1))s^2\log s$ of the first $s$ primes. For remaining primes above $\sqrt n$, the level has only two options because $A_p=1$. The smaller primes contribute at most $O(\sqrt n\log\log n/\log n)=o(\sqrt{n/\log n})$ to the base-two logarithm of the number of assignments, since each has at most $1+\log_2 n$ options. Here $\log n$ denotes the natural logarithm. The projected graph can therefore be enumerated in
$$
2^{(2+o(1))\sqrt{n/\log n}}\operatorname{poly}(\text{input length})
$$
time, including binary caps and projected endpoints. This is also the sharp leading exponent for maximum projected state count. Take identical profiles with one cycle of each prime length up to $x=(1-\epsilon)\sqrt{n\log n}$, pad with degree-one factors, and take $K=N$. The sum of the primes up to $x$ is asymptotic to $x^2/(2\log x)<n$ for fixed $\epsilon>0$ and sufficiently large $n$. Every divisor of $N$ is feasible, giving $2^{(2-2\epsilon+o(1))\sqrt{n/\log n}}$ states. Let $\epsilon$ decrease to zero. This lower bound concerns the number of states in the projected graph.

If $\operatorname{lcm}(u,v)\le K$, a shortest path between any successful $u,v$ first inserts missing factors through their least common multiple and then removes excess factors. The first segment consists of multiples of $u$, and the second consists of multiples of $v$. All states are feasible by monotonicity and the cap. The length $\sum_p |v_p(u)-v_p(v)|$ is a lower bound for every prime-step path, so this route is shortest. This applies in particular without a cap.

There is a more general exact formula for arbitrary endpoints. Put $h(x)=x/\pi(x)$. For each $\gamma\mid\gcd(h(u),h(v))$, define
$$
F_\gamma=\prod_{\substack{p\mid\gamma\\p\mid N}}p^{A_p}.
$$
Let $d_\gamma$ be the projected divisor-graph distance between $\pi(u)$ and $\pi(v)$ restricted to vertices divisible by $F_\gamma$ and at most $\lfloor K/\gamma\rfloor$. Writing $\Omega$ for prime factors counted with multiplicity, the original distance is
$$
\min_{\gamma\mid\gcd(h(u),h(v))}
\left[d_\gamma+\Omega\!\left(\frac{h(u)}{\gamma}\right)
+\Omega\!\left(\frac{h(v)}{\gamma}\right)\right].
$$
For a lower bound, take any original path and choose $\gamma$ with each exponent the minimum of that excess exponent along the path. Its projected states are all at most $K/\gamma$. Every retained excess prime belonging to $N$ forces its projected exponent to stay saturated. Each step changes either a projected exponent or an excess exponent, and the number of excess steps is at least the displayed sum of two $\Omega$ terms. For the upper bound, remove excess factors from $u$ until it becomes $\pi(u)\gamma$, lift the restricted projected path by multiplying every vertex by $\gamma$, and restore the excess factors of $v$. The saturation condition ensures every lifted vertex has the correct projection, and the cap condition holds on all three segments. This formula is combinatorial. Evaluating it with an exhaustive search over excess divisors can require factorization of arbitrary binary inputs.

Lastly, a supplied width-$w$ tree decomposition of the clause-primal graph gives a fixed-parameter algorithm for the minimum good degree. Each threshold clause has scope a clique and therefore lies in a bag by the subtree intersection property of tree decompositions. Assign each clause to such a bag and each prime to one bag containing it. A rooted decomposition dynamic program lists assignments of levels to each bag. It enforces the clauses assigned to that bag, multiplies the weights $p^{a_p}$ of the primes assigned there, and optimizes over compatible assignments in each child. Assignments in different children that match the parent agree on all shared variables by connectedness of occurrences. Products stay at most $N$. There are at most $(1+\log_2 n)^{w+1}$ states per bag, yielding $2^{O(w\log(w+2))}$ times a polynomial in the input length, even with a quadratic pairwise compatibility scan. Thus the static optimization is polynomial on the same bounded-width profile class supporting PSPACE-complete reconfiguration.
