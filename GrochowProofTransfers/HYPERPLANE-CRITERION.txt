Recovering a split linear matrix family from its hyperplane ranks

Theorem
Let K be any field, let V be a finite-dimensional K-vector space, and let
A: V → Mat_n(K) be linear, with n ≥ 1. Suppose there is e ∈ V with A(e)
invertible. Let ℓ_1,…,ℓ_r be distinct linear forms normalized by ℓ_i(e)=1.
Let m_1,…,m_r be positive integers summing to n. Assume the polynomial identity

    det A(x) = det A(e) · ∏_i ℓ_i(x)^{m_i}.

Write H_i = ker ℓ_i. On a linear subspace U, "generic nullity" is the
nullity of A restricted to U over the fraction field of its coordinate
ring. This meaning also applies to the zero subspace, with fraction field
K. The following conditions are equivalent.

1. There are constant invertible K-matrices P,Q with
   P A(x) Q = ⊕_i ℓ_i(x) I_{m_i}.
2. At the generic point of each H_i and each H_i ∩ H_j (i ≠ j), the
   nullity is ∑_{k: U ⊆ H_k} m_k.

The right-hand sum includes all hyperplanes containing the tested
intersection. Pairwise intersection spaces need only be checked once.

Proof
Set B(x) = A(e)^{-1} A(x), so that B(e)=I. Then

    det(TI − B(x)) = ∏_i (T − ℓ_i(x))^{m_i},

by applying the determinant hypothesis to x−Te. The map
x ↦ x − ℓ_i(x)e is a surjection from V to H_i. It sends the generic
point of V to a generic point of H_i in the sense of extension of its
rational function field. Therefore over K(V) the eigenspace of B
for ℓ_i has dimension m_i. The eigenspaces sum directly since their
eigenvalues are distinct. Their dimensions sum to n.

Let R = K[V], a polynomial ring. The projector onto the i-th generic
eigenspace is

    E_i = ∏_{j≠i} (B − ℓ_j I)/(ℓ_i − ℓ_j)  ∈ Mat_n(Frac R).

All factors here commute. The potential poles lie on collision
hyperplanes D defined by a linear form h = ℓ_i−ℓ_j. We now show that
each E_i has entries in R_(h) for every such h.

Fix h and write O = R_(h), a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer
h and residue field K(D). Partition the indices into classes S by
equality of ℓ_i modulo h. The monic polynomials
f_S(T) = ∏_{i∈S}(T−ℓ_i) have pairwise unit resultants in O, because
each difference between distinct classes is a unit. Their product
annihilates B over Frac R and hence over O. By Bézout and the Chinese
remainder theorem the lattice O^n decomposes into B-stable direct
summands L_S, one for each f_S. Explicitly the identity in the S-th
factor of O[T]/(∏_S f_S) and zero in the other factors evaluates at B
to the corresponding projection. The summands are free. Their ranks
are M_S = ∑_{i∈S} m_i by passing to Frac R.

If S is a singleton, that summand is already the integral image of
the corresponding E_i. Otherwise select i,j∈S with i≠j. The linear
map D → H_i∩H_j given by x ↦ x−ℓ_i(x)e is onto. Indeed its image
lies in the intersection, and it is the identity there. A form
ℓ_k vanishes on the whole intersection precisely when ℓ_k−ℓ_i
vanishes on D, so precisely when k∈S. The rank hypothesis at the
generic point of the intersection yields

    dim_{K(D)} ker( B mod h − (ℓ_i mod h) I ) = M_S.

On all other L_{S'} modulo h, this operator is invertible, since
there it differs by a nonzero scalar from a nilpotent operator.
On L_S modulo h the dimension of the kernel equals the full
dimension M_S. Thus (B−ℓ_i I)|_{L_S} is divisible by h as an
endomorphism of the free O-module L_S.

The integral endomorphism (B−ℓ_i I)|_{L_S}/h has, over Frac R,
eigenvalues (ℓ_k−ℓ_i)/h for k∈S. These are constants in K and
are pairwise distinct. Lagrange interpolation now gives all
its individual eigenprojectors over O and these, extended by zero
on the other summands, equal the E_k. This removes the pole at h.

The denominators of E_i are products of the linear collision forms.
Unique factorization of R makes each entry a polynomial, since its
valuation at every one of those prime factors is nonnegative. Its
numerator and denominator are homogeneous of the same degree, so
the resulting polynomial is constant. The constant projections
are orthogonal, sum to I, and B(x)=∑_i ℓ_i(x) E_i. Their images
have dimensions m_i. A basis adapted to that direct sum gives
the stated diagonal form after undoing A(e)^{-1}. The converse
follows by evaluating the diagonal form on the same strata.

Consequences for 3-tensor isomorphism and tensor rank
View A as a 3-tensor with each slice in Mat_n. For pencils satisfying
the theorem, 3-tensor isomorphism is exactly projective equivalence
of the hyperplanes with their multiplicities. Indeed a left-right
transformation changes the determinant by a nonzero scalar and a
change of slice coordinates pulls back its factors. Conversely, a
linear isomorphism matching the weighted arrangement matches the
diagonal forms after rescaling individual diagonal coordinates and
permuting them. Combining the two diagonalizations gives actual
left, right and slice witnesses, once the hyperplane matching is
given. The tensor rank is exactly n because the diagonal form gives
n rank-one summands and the invertible slice requires at least n.

Finite exact acceptance and witness recovery
The following criterion accepts exactly the stated normal form with
the supplied ℓ_i,m_i and does not require a promised determinant
factorization. Work in an extension L of K containing a vector v
with all ℓ_i(v) distinct. For finite K one may choose L of size
strictly larger than max(r, binom(r,2)). There are at most binom(r,2)
proper linear hyperplanes to avoid, each with |L|^{dim V−1} points.
This size also supplies enough distinct points on the testing line.
For infinite K one can choose v over K itself.

For each vector w in a K-basis of V, form the projective test line
[s:t] ↦ sv+tw. Include its point [1:0] and every collision point,
meaning a point where ℓ_i(sv+tw) = ℓ_j(sv+tw) for i≠j. Add other
points until there are at least r+1 distinct projective points.
The total is at most max(r+1, binom(r,2)+1) per basis vector.
At each tested point x=sv+tw, and for every distinct label α among
the ℓ_i(x), check

    nullity(B(x) − α I) = ∑_{i: ℓ_i(x)=α} m_i.

These are ordinary rank computations over L. Since ℓ_i(v) are
distinct, every pair collides at one finite point in the chart
w+tv and never at v. A test line contained geometrically in a
determinant hyperplane is allowed, since a zero label is treated
in the same way as each other eigenvalue. A basis vector
proportional to v also causes no issue. The interpolation below is
on parameter pairs [s:t], even if their images are dependent.

To prove soundness, note that the required eigenspaces on every
sample are direct and their dimensions sum to n. Thus at each
sample the product ∏_i (B(sv+tw)−ℓ_i(sv+tw)I) vanishes, including
at a collision. Each entry of this product is homogeneous of
degree r in s,t, so r+1 distinct projective zeros force the
product to vanish identically. Over the rational function field
of the line the labels are distinct, giving the usual generic
spectral projectors. Near [1:0] they have unit denominators in a
local chart and specialize to the spectral projectors of B(v).
Their generic ranks are therefore m_i. This rank statement
follows because the image and kernel of each integral idempotent
are complementary free modules over that local discrete
valuation ring.

At a finite collision, exactly the preceding local lattice
argument works, now with the linear factor t−t_0 as uniformizer
and the tested equality providing the full nullity for each
colliding class. Every finite pole disappears. Directly on
w+tv, each projector has numerator of degree at most r−1 and
denominator of degree exactly r−1 since the slopes are distinct.
It is therefore constant on the line and equals the corresponding
projector at v. These conclusions apply to every basis vector w,
so all B(x) over L have the same direct-sum eigenspaces. Conversely
a pencil in the stated diagonal form passes every check.

For descent, form, for each label ℓ_i, the intersection over all
K-basis vectors w of ker(B(w)−ℓ_i(w)I) over K. This is a single
homogeneous K-linear system for each i. Extending to L preserves
its rank, and its extended solution space is the common
eigenspace for that label with dimension m_i. The natural direct
sum map of the K-solution spaces into K^n becomes an isomorphism
after extending scalars, so it is an isomorphism over K. Bases
obtained by Gaussian elimination over K supply the normal form
and the left-right witnesses over the original field. The accepted
form in turn gives the determinant identity for the supplied labels
and multiplicities. This certificate addresses verification of a
supplied arrangement. Finding a matching between arrangements
is a separate orbit problem.

Checks and scope
The exact 2×2 replay compares the finite test with direct projector
recovery for every normalized pencil with determinant xy over F_3.
It is an implementation check of a bounded case. The general proof
above is an informal proof. The accompanying Lean source formalizes
the two-factor algebraic core with a scalar collision hyperplane
and a square-free annihilator at a separating vector. Its theorem
is GrochowTransfer.twoFactors_projector. Specializing the target
algebra to endomorphisms makes the image and kernel of the
constant idempotent the common summands. The general rank
recognition and tensor-isomorphism corollaries have no Lean audit.

Sharpness of the list of intersection tests
Fix any nonzero intersection J=H_i∩H_j in the input arrangement.
Start from the diagonal form and choose one diagonal coordinate in
the i-block and one in the j-block. Add the single off-diagonal
entry g(x) from the j-coordinate to the i-coordinate, where g(e)=0
and g is nonzero on J. Such a g exists because e does not belong to
J. The modification preserves the determinant and the value at e.

The affected 2×2 block has diagonal entries ℓ_i,ℓ_j and off-diagonal
entry g. If either diagonal entry is nonzero, its rank equals the
number of its nonzero diagonal entries. If both are zero, its rank
is one exactly when g is nonzero. Thus no individual hyperplane
test changes. Among the pairwise intersection spaces, only J sees
both diagonal entries vanish identically, because the distinct
spaces all have codimension two and containment in J implies
equality. The generic nullity on J drops by exactly one, whereas
every other tested generic nullity stays as on the diagonal form.
Every nonzero pairwise intersection space is therefore essential
to this list of tests over the unrestricted class of pencils with
that determinant. The zero intersection, when present, has automatic
nullity n. A minimal example is A(x,y,z) = [[x,z],[0,y]], whose
determinant is xy and whose generic nullity drops on x=y=0.

Related work and transfer
The method borrows the fixed-hyperplane viewpoint of Davis and
GPT-6.2 Astra's Lonely Runner work. Fix each possible relation
hyperplane, retain the exact conditional behavior there, then
combine the local information into a global statement. Here the
relation is equality of spectral labels and the exact conditional
invariant is generic rank. The source theorem itself is about
loneliness and is not a premise of the argument.

The isomorphism problem is the left-right plus slice-change
action in Grochow and Qiao's 3-tensor framework. Friedland's
property-L results already treat pointwise diagonalizable
complex two-variable pencils. Kerner and Vinnikov study
maximal generation and local-to-global decomposition of
determinantal representations. The contribution here is this
explicit arbitrary-field hyperplane/intersection test, its
finite rank certificate and base-field witness recovery.
Priority for this exact formulation has not been established.
