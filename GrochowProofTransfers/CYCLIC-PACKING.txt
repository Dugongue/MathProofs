Maximum isotropic spaces from cyclic four-point packings

Let H be a finite abelian group and B a nonempty subset of H. Form the graph
on H in which distinct x,y are adjacent exactly when (x+B) ∩ (y+B) ≠ ∅.
Over a field F, the associated alternating map β has one coordinate for
each edge, equal to u_x v_y − u_y v_x after choosing an orientation.
A subspace U ≤ F^H is isotropic if β vanishes on U×U.

Theorem 1. Suppose B tiles H by translations. Let k=|H|/|B| and let K be
the subgroup of translations preserving B. The maximum isotropic dimension
is k. All the maximum-dimensional isotropic spaces are given bijectively
by the following data.

  • A tiling complement to the image of B in H/K.
  • One arbitrary line in F^{π^{-1}(c)} for each selected quotient center c.

The subspace is the direct sum of the chosen lines in their distinct
coordinate fibers. If F has q elements, the number of maximum-dimensional
isotropic spaces is the number of quotient tiling complements multiplied
by ((q^{|K|}−1)/(q−1))^k. If K={0}, this counts the ordinary tiling
complements themselves and every maximum-dimensional isotropic subspace
is a coordinate space.

Theorem 2. Let H=Z/N, let gcd(N,a,b)=1, and suppose B={0,a,b,a+b} has
four distinct elements in H. Then B tiles H if and only if 4 divides N,
the steps have opposite parity, and 2h divides N, where h=gcd(N,e)
for the even step e. In that case all tiling complements are obtained
by choosing a parity and, for each residue s of that parity modulo h,
choosing exactly one of s or s+h modulo 2h. The selected classes are
taken in full. There are 2^{1+h/2} complements.

When these conditions hold, the maximum isotropic dimension is N/4.
The number of maximum-dimensional isotropic spaces over F_q is

  • 2^{1+h/2} if N>4 and e≠N/2 in H.
  • 2(q+1)^{N/4} if N>4 and e=N/2 in H.
  • 1+q+q²+q³ if N=4.

Theorem 3. Let 0<a<b be odd coprime integers and put P=ab+b−a. If P
divides N and N>2(a+b), the maximum isotropic dimension for the same
four-point set in Z/N is N(P−1)/(4P). There are exactly P maximum-
dimensional isotropic spaces over every field. They are coordinate
spaces on the P translates of the periodic packing constructed by
Davis and Astra. Its distinct hole classes modulo P distinguish the
P subspaces.

For each statement over a finite field of odd order, the same count
gives the number of maximum-order abelian subgroups of the graph's
class-two group. Their index is q^{|H|−k} for maximum dimension k.

Theorem 4. Under the hypotheses of Theorem 3, every pseudo-isometry
of β acts monomially on the input coordinates, with the sole exception
(N,a,b)=(10,1,3). Thus its group of pseudo-isometries is
(F×)^N ⋊ Aut(G), where G is the conflict graph. In the exception it is
GL₂(F)^5 ⋊ S₅. These statements hold over any field.

A pseudo-isometry means a pair of invertible linear maps (S,T) on the
input and output spaces such that β(Su,Sv)=Tβ(u,v) for all u,v.
The output map is unique for every allowable S because the edge
coordinates of β span the output.
Proof of the shared coordinate argument

For any U, let φ_x be restriction to U of the x-th coordinate functional.
These restrictions span U*, since their common kernel is zero. Choose
a basis (φ_c) indexed by I⊆H. If (x+B)∩(y+B) is nonempty, isotropy says
φ_x∧φ_y=0, so adjoining nonzero restrictions are proportional. To see
this directly, choose u with φ_y(u) ≠ 0 and solve the vanishing two-by-two
determinant for φ_x(v) for every v∈U. In particular distinct basis indices
have disjoint translates. This proves that dim U is at most the maximum
packing size. The coordinate span of any packing's centers is isotropic,
giving equality.

Now let U have maximum dimension and use such an I. The coordinate-basis
dual in U gives vectors w_c, with w_c(d)=1 for c=d and 0 for c≠d, for
c,d∈I. If φ_x≠0 and x+B meets both c+B and d+B, then φ_x is a nonzero
multiple of both φ_c and φ_d. This forces c=d. Thus any active coordinate
can meet at most one tile of the packing chosen by the basis.

Proof of Theorem 1

A tile has a packing of size k, and a disjoint family has size at most k
by counting sites. Every maximum packing is therefore a tiling. Each
active x must meet at least one tile, and the argument above shows its
translate is contained in that one tile. Equal sizes give x+B=c+B,
equivalently x−c∈K. It follows that w_c is supported in the fiber c+K.
Its span is the corresponding summand of U. Conversely, a choice of
one line per fiber over a quotient tiling gives an isotropic space.
Translates from distinct selected fibers are disjoint, and coordinate
functionals in each selected fiber all factor through its single line.

Each translate of B is the full inverse image of a translate of its image
in H/K. A tiling in H projects to a quotient tiling and may choose any
one representative from each selected fiber. The chosen quotient
centers in the subspace construction are intrinsic to U, since they
are precisely the fibers where its projection is nonzero. Each line is
also recovered as that projection. The number of lines in a fiber over
F_q is (q^{|K|}−1)/(q−1), as desired.

A finite Fourier observation

Write S_d f(x)=f(x−d) on Z/N. For real functions, the kernel of
(1+S_a)(1+S_b) is the sum of the kernels of the two factors.
Indeed the characters simultaneously diagonalize the shifts, and the
product eigenvalue vanishes exactly when either factor vanishes.
The Fourier modes in each kernel are preserved by conjugation, so a
real vector has a decomposition with real summands. If d has odd order
in Z/N, then ker(1+S_d)=0, since the sign change along its odd cycle
forces every value to be zero. Otherwise, if g=gcd(N,d), a function in
that kernel changes sign under addition of g. To see this, d/g is
invertible modulo the even order N/g, and its inverse is odd.
Consequently an odd multiple of d equals g in Z/N. The converse follows
from d/g being odd.

Proof of Theorem 2

A tiling complement has indicator f satisfying
(1+S_a)(1+S_b) f=1, and counting shows 4 divides N.
By the Fourier observation, f=1/4+u+v with u and v in the respective
sign-change kernels. If both steps are odd, set g=gcd(N,a), h=gcd(N,b).
The odd step gh changes the sign of both u and v, so f(x)+f(x+gh)=1/2.
This is impossible for an indicator. Both steps even contradict
gcd(N,a,b)=1.

Relabel the odd step a and even step b. The odd step has even order.
If the even step has odd order, its kernel vanishes and
f(x)+f(x+a)=1/2, again impossible. Thus h=gcd(N,b) is even and 2h
divides N. As g=gcd(N,a) is odd and coprime to h, g and 2h are coprime
and 2gh divides N. Write u(x)=(-1)^x U(x mod g), and write v(x) in
terms of V(s)=v(s) for 0≤s<h, with v(s+h)=−V(s). For every r mod g
and s mod h, CRT gives both combinations of r with s and with s+h
modulo 2h. The two corresponding values of f are

    1/4 + (-1)^s U(r) + V(s)
    1/4 + (-1)^s U(r) − V(s).

Their sum is an integer in {0,1,2}. Comparing its value for even and odd
s, the two sums add to 1. Hence U(r)=±1/4 and exactly one parity has
pair sum 1. If two residues r gave opposite signs for U(r), the same
s would have pair sum 0 on one residue and 1 on the other. The first
would force V(s)=0, while the second forces V(s)=±1/2. Thus U(r) is
constant. This proves that f has the claimed form.

Conversely, an indicator of that form has f+S_b f equal to the indicator
of the chosen parity. In fact b/h is odd, so shifting by b exchanges s
with s+h mod 2h. The shift by a exchanges the two parities. The cover
equation follows, producing every listed tiling. There are h/2 choices of
residue-pair bits independently for each of the two possible parities.

A nontrivial period subgroup has order dividing 4 and therefore contains
N/2. Since 0 lies in B and N/2 is even, the only possible such element
of B is the even step, so a period occurs exactly when e=N/2. If the
subgroup has order 4, it equals B and contains both steps. The primitive
hypothesis then gives N=4. In the remaining period case |K|=2. Each
quotient tiling complement has exactly 2^{N/4} lifts, and the preceding
tiling formula with h=N/2 gives two quotient complements. The three
isotropic counts follow from Theorem 1.

Proof of Theorem 3

Use the sharp local packing bound of Davis and Astra. For the odd
coprime steps in question every P consecutive sites of an integer
packing have a hole. They also construct a P-periodic packing with
one hole per period. The cyclic packing problem lifts to a periodic
integer packing because disjointness modulo N rules out overlap of
distinct lifts, and N>a+b rules out overlap of a tile with its own
nontrivial N-shift. Conversely their P-periodic construction descends
because P divides N. The local bound and the construction give the
maximum packing size N(P−1)/(4P).

In any optimal cyclic packing, the N/P holes have forward gaps at
most P and those gaps sum to N, so all the gaps equal P. Fix the
hole alignment. Let f₀ be the indicator of the corresponding translate
of the cited P-periodic construction, and f another optimal packing
indicator. Their occupation functions agree because the hole classes
agree. Thus f−f₀ lies in the kernel of (1+S_a)(1+S_b). If N is odd,
the Fourier observation gives a zero kernel. If N is even, let
g=gcd(N,a), h=gcd(N,b) and d=Pgh. All three factors of d are odd.
The shift d fixes f₀ and changes the sign of each kernel summand of
f−f₀, since d/g and d/h are odd. Therefore

    f(x)+f(x+d)=2f₀(x).

Since f and f₀ are indicators, both terms on the left have to equal
f₀(x). This proves uniqueness for each of the P hole alignments.

For any maximum-dimensional isotropic U, choose a coordinate basis I
as above. Each translate of B meets at most one hole since its offsets
range over a+b<P, and holes are evenly spaced by P. A pair of distinct
translates of B overlap in at most two sites. Indeed N>2(a+b) keeps
distinct integer differences distinct modulo N. In the positive
difference multiset of B, the differences are a and b of multiplicity
two and b−a and a+b of multiplicity one. There is no collision among
these values since the only possible one under 0<a<b is b=2a, excluded
by parity. The same bound holds for negative differences. For x∉I,
at least three sites of x+B are occupied, so it meets at least two
distinct center tiles. The shared coordinate argument proves φ_x=0.
Consequently U is exactly the coordinate space on I. There are
exactly P such spaces.

Proof of Theorem 4

Write V_r for the coordinate fiber on the vertices x congruent to r
modulo P. The family of all maximum isotropic spaces intrinsically
determines the unordered decomposition into V_r as follows. Their
coordinate supports are all shifts of a set C modulo P. Each residue
belongs to exactly |C| shifted supports, and distinct residues have
distinct membership profiles. Indeed identical profiles would make
their difference a period of C. Such a translation would preserve the
unique uncovered site modulo P, so that difference would be zero.
Consequently the intersection of all maximum spaces whose supports
include r is exactly V_r. Equivalently these fibers are the minimal
nonzero intersections of maximum isotropic spaces. Every
pseudo-isometry must permute them.

The allowed edge displacements are ±a, ±b, ±(b−a), and ±(a+b).
Between any two fibers with at least one displacement there is a
perfect matching, obtained by adding that displacement. For u∈V_r
and a neighboring fiber V_s, the rank of β(u,−) restricted to V_s
equals the number of neighbors in V_s of the support of u. In fact
the images of distinct coordinate vectors e_y for y∈V_s have
disjoint supports in the edge output, and such an image is nonzero
exactly when a coordinate in the support of u is adjacent to y.
The rank is always at least the support size of u because of the
perfect matching.

If a≥3, then P−2(a+b)=a(b−3)−b>0, so no two allowed displacements
are congruent modulo P. If a=1 and b≥5, the allowed displacement
+1 alone represents its residue modulo P=2b−1. Thus for every
fiber there is a neighboring fiber whose entire edge relation is
one matching. Minimize the restricted rank over all neighboring
fibers. It equals the support size, by the matching lower bound and
the existence of a neighbor attaining it. Since this minimum is
defined by the intrinsic collection of fibers and the map β, the
set of coordinate lines in each fiber is preserved by S.

If a=1 and b=3, P=5 and between every two distinct fibers the edge
relation is two matchings. Their displacements differ by P, hence
the two neighbors of a vertex are consecutive in the natural cyclic
order on the other fiber. If N/P≥3, distinct vertices have distinct
two-element neighborhoods there. A single support vertex gives
rank 2 and any larger support gives rank at least 3, for every
neighboring fiber. The coordinate lines are again preserved by S.
The only other admissible N is 10. In that case the graph joins all
vertices from distinct fibers and no vertices within a fiber. The
intrinsic decomposition is into five planes. Every map permuting
the planes and acting by arbitrary invertible linear maps on them
supports a pseudo-isometry, since the edge output is the direct sum
of V_r⊗V_s over unordered pairs of distinct fibers. Thus precisely
the displayed wreath product occurs.

In all the monomial cases, the input permutation must preserve the
edge relation, as read from whether β(e_x,e_y) vanishes. Conversely,
any such permutation and arbitrary nonzero coordinate scalings give
a pseudo-isometry by applying the induced permutation, signs and
scalings to the edge output. This proves the group formula.
Group consequence

For odd q put V=F_q^H and W=F_q^E, where E is the edge set. Define the
group by (u,z)(v,w)=(u+v,z+w+β(u,v)/2). Its commutator is (0,β(u,v)).
A maximum-order abelian subgroup contains the central W, since
adjoining it preserves commutativity. The image in V is additive and
β vanishes on pairs in that image. Its F_q-span is also isotropic, so by
maximal order the image equals this span, even when q is a proper prime
power. Its full inverse image has order q^{|E|+k} for maximum isotropic
dimension k. This establishes the stated bijection and indices.

Scope and provenance

The prior graphical independence-dimension correspondence and Qiao's
isotropic polynomials supply the setting. The local odd-window bound
and its explicit witness are the pinned Davis–Astra inputs. The results
above specialize to an explicit normal form and exact subgroup count.
The claims concern the displayed graphical class-two groups. Priority
for the specialization remains provisional.
