# Special-linear canonization from raw ternary tensor entries

**Theorem.** Over the field of three elements there is a deterministic polynomial-time recognition and SL canonization algorithm for the entire general-linear orbit family of weighted, properly three-edge-coloured simple cubic graphs without four-cycles. It accepts arbitrary nonzero weights, disconnected graphs, repeated isomorphic components, triangles, and independent arbitrary input bases. The modes and the three edge colours are ordered. No graph, decomposition, or ordinary transporter is supplied. Equality of the resulting keys is equivalent to SL isomorphism. The key below also specifies an actual representative in the input's SL orbit.

In particular, if one input is in the family, its signed comparison with an arbitrary second input reduces to a single ordinary TI comparison. Test the second input for membership, compare keys, and output the ordinary scalar pair $(1,1)$ or $(1,0)$ according to the decision. The principal unrestricted target is the other-direction containment in Open Question 1.9, in the deterministic same-field many-one model. The argument below leaves it open. The earlier cubic-graph determinant algorithm starts from the coloured graphs in specified edge bases. Here the central step recovers those bases from the raw tensor; the normalization also handles component permutations. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, §1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

## An intrinsic space of quadrics

Write $d$ for the number of edges of each colour. A graph vertex has three incident edges, one per colour, and contributes its weight to the corresponding entry of a $d\times d\times d$ tensor. Consider contraction in the dual of the first mode. In hidden coordinates $x_i$ indexed by the edges of the first colour, the resulting linear matrix has exactly two entries labelled by each $x_i$, one for each endpoint, each multiplied by a nonzero constant. The two entries are in distinct rows and columns. The same description holds in each mode.

Let $I$ be the homogeneous ideal of the three-rowed minors of this linear matrix, in the formal polynomial ring over $\mathbb F_3$. Compute

$$
Q=\{f\in \mathbb F_3[x]_2:f^3\in I_6\}.
$$

Only degree-six linear algebra is needed. Span the cubic minors, multiply successive homogeneous spans by the individual variables to obtain $I_6$, and take the inverse image under the linear map sending $x_i x_j$ to $x_i^3x_j^3$. The Frobenius identity makes this an exact calculation for arbitrary quadrics. Each variable change induces the matching change in $Q$, and row and column changes preserve the minors' ideal by Cauchy–Binet and invertibility. All polynomial spaces used have polynomial dimension, at most $\binom{d+5}{6}$ in degrees through six for $d\ge 1$.

**Claim.** In the hidden coordinates, $Q$ is precisely the span of $x_i x_j$ for $i\ne j$.

For the nontrivial inclusion fix two first-colour edges $i,j$. Their four endpoints are distinct. Adjacencies of the other two colours between these two pairs all share one endpoint if there are any: two disjoint cross-adjacencies together with $i,j$ would form a four-cycle. Thus we can select both endpoints of one edge and one endpoint of the other so that none of the three selected vertices share an edge of either remaining colour. In the matrix this gives a three-entry matching and a three-rowed minor.

The full support of the contraction matrix is a disjoint union of even cycles. Its nodes are the edges of the other two colours, and its matrix entries correspond to graph vertices. There is no four-cycle in this support graph: such a cycle would give a four-cycle of alternating colours in the original graph. A second perfect matching on our selected three rows and three columns would therefore require a six-cycle, which must be a whole support component because every node has degree two. The three selected entries would alternate around it. In the original graph these are three alternating vertices around a six-cycle of the two remaining colours.

In this exceptional case switch the singleton selection to the other endpoint of its first-colour edge. This endpoint must be outside the six-cycle. The two cycle neighbours of the original singleton are already joined to it by edges of other colours, so simplicity excludes both as its first-colour partner. An opposite vertex as partner would give a four-cycle. The two vertices at distance two are the two selected endpoints of the doubled first-colour edge, whose partners are already fixed. Thus the replacement lies on a different two-colour component. It conflicts with neither retained entry and the three entries can no longer belong to a six-cycle component. The new minor has a unique determinant term. In either case we have a nonzero constant times $x_i^2x_j$ or $x_i x_j^2$ in $I$. Multiplying yields $(x_i x_j)^3\in I_6$.

Conversely, on each coordinate axis the contraction has rank at most two. If $f^3\in I_6$, evaluation at its coordinate unit vector shows that the coefficient of $x_i^2$ in $f$ vanishes. This proves the claim. The companion Lean lemma verifies the ideal multiplication step for every commutative ring. [MixedCube.lean](MixedCube.lean)

## Recovering the lines and recognizing the family

Find all matrices $D$ such that the linear derivation sending $x_i$ to $\sum_jD_{ij}x_j$ preserves $Q$. These are linear equations. In the hidden basis the matrices are exactly diagonal: applying the derivation to $x_ix_j$ exposes $D_{ij}$ as the coefficient of $x_j^2$ and $D_{ji}$ as the coefficient of $x_i^2$. Every diagonal matrix preserves $Q$. The matrices act on contraction points by $z\mapsto Dz$, since the derivative of $f$ at $z$ is evaluated in direction $Dz$. Their common eigenspaces are exactly the hidden coordinate lines in the contraction space. Successively intersect with the kernels of $D-\lambda I$ for a basis of the algebra and $\lambda=0,1,2$. Diagonal matrices separate each pair of coordinates. Only nonzero spaces are retained, and their total dimension never increases.

For membership, reject dimensions outside the format, or failure of the expected dimensions or splitting tests. In each mode choose one covector on each recovered line, form the matrix of covector rows, and use it to express the tensor in the recovered coordinates. Check that every coordinate occurs in precisely two support triples and that joining these triples by their shared coordinate gives a simple four-cycle-free graph. This last check supplies a witness to membership on any accepted input. Every input from the family passes by the preceding proof. In these bases, every transporter is monomial because its dual permutes the recovered lines. Conversely, a support permutation respecting colours and edge scalars matching all weights gives a transporter. This establishes recognition and recovery without solving general MinRank.

## Canonization with the orientation retained

Use bits for the two nonzero field elements, with one denoting minus one. Keep the determinant bits of all three initial basis changes. Within a connected component, try each possible root vertex. Number its vertices by breadth-first traversal in fixed colour order and order its edges of each colour by the first endpoint in that numbering. A rooted colour-preserving isomorphism is forced by its root image. Record the three lists of edge endpoint pairs. A sign change on one edge adds its two-vertex incidence column to the vertex weight bits and flips the corresponding mode determinant bit.

For each root, perform binary elimination on these augmented columns, putting the three mode bits below all vertex bits. Reduction of the weights to the least binary residue gives a canonical weight vector for this rooted graph; the rows with only determinant bits give exactly the freedom in the signs for reaching it. Include the signs of the three edge permutations. Take the least pair of endpoint lists and weight residue over roots and unite the attainable sign sets over tied roots. The resulting component code is a complete ordinary invariant: equality explicitly provides a graph map and compatible edge scalings. The attained sign set records every map to its canonical labelled model because pulling back the canonical root gives one of the roots tried.

Sort the components by their codes and concatenate their bases in each mode. Add the signs of the permutations grouping the incoming bases and the three initial frame signs. Take all sums of local sign choices; this needs at most eight possibilities at every step. Equal component codes admit arbitrary permutations of their copies. A transposition of two copies contributes the parity of their block dimension in each mode, as seen by exchanging the two ordered equal models; choices of the maps within copies have already been included. Such transpositions generate every remaining permutation, so their bit vectors give exactly the extra sign freedom. This argument includes all maps from the original tensor to the same ordinary canonical model $C$.

The key consists of the ordered component codes and the least attainable determinant triple $s$ for transport to $C$. The attainable set is a coset of the determinant image of the stabilizer of $C$. Two inputs with the same ordinary model are SL-equivalent precisely when these cosets coincide, equivalently when their least triples coincide. To turn the invariant into a representative, apply a diagonal change with determinant $s_i$ in each mode of $C$ (in multiplicative notation). Composing with the map to $C$ of those same signs has determinant one in each mode. Every step is polynomial in the written tensor size. [Frame extraction](frame.py) · [Canonization](canon.py)

## Beyond cubic supports

The same determinant tracking gives a second sufficient result. Suppose a polynomial-time equivariant procedure recovers an unordered basis of lines in each mode of a ternary tensor. The computed splitting test above is one such procedure on any tensor where it returns full line frames: its intrinsic derivation space must be a full diagonal algebra in those frames, so its common eigenlines are unique. For each fixed bound on the number of nonzero entries meeting one recovered coordinate, signed isomorphism of such tensors is decidable in deterministic polynomial time. With arbitrary occurrences it is decidable in deterministic quasipolynomial time.

After recording frame determinants, build a coloured graph with one centre for each coordinate, its two sign vertices in one fibre, and four vertices per nonzero tensor entry. Each of the latter encodes one triple of sign bits of parity equal to the entry's weight bit. Join it to the indicated three sign vertices. Colour centres and sign vertices by mode and role, and give the entry vertices their own colour. Every graph isomorphism permutes the coordinates by mode and swaps some sign pairs. It preserves exactly the supported triples with their required weight changes. Conversely any permitted monomial map extends uniquely, since a triple of sign vertices determines the corresponding entry vertex. With at most $\Delta$ occurrences per coordinate, the degree is at most $\max(3,1+2\Delta)$.

Find a graph isomorphism and generators of the automorphism group using coloured graph isomorphism under individualization. To recover generators, traverse point stabilizers, find the possible images of each next point and one transporter per image by the same individualization procedure. These coset representatives generate the full group. Unique colours implement all markings without raising degrees. Read each map's three determinant bits from its coordinate permutations and sign swaps, and include the frame corrections. An affine span in three bits gives the entire signed transporter profile. The time bounds follow from Luks's bounded-valence algorithm and Babai's general algorithm. This generalization is a written proof; the implementation specializes to the cubic family. [Luks](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/iso.pdf) · [Babai](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03547)

## The unrestricted case

There are concise ternary tensors for which even the existence of an equivariant ternary line frame fails. Consider the trilinear form $\mathrm{tr}(XYZ)$ on three copies of $M_3(\mathbb F_3)$. The trace pairing is nondegenerate, and taking one matrix to be the identity gives conciseness. The stabilizer includes $(X,Y,Z)\mapsto(PX,Y,ZP^{-1})$ for every invertible $P$. Multiplication by an element of order 13 in $\mathbb F_{27}^{\times}$ gives such a $P$ on its three-dimensional underlying vector space. Left multiplication by $P$ on the first nine-dimensional matrix space still has order 13, as does its contragredient. A permutation of nine basis lines with ternary scalings belongs to a group of order $2^9 9!$, coprime to 13. Thus no equivariant first-mode basis of lines exists on this input. The example limits the proposed frame mechanism; the general decision reduction could use other information. Directly adjoining the volume tensor via all exterior powers would entail a middle space of dimension $\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}$. This run supplies no polynomial replacement on arbitrary inputs.

The proofs of frame recovery, canonization, and the complexity bounds are informal. The Python replay constructs ternary tensors from the truncated tetrahedron and the Heawood graph, hides their bases, checks minor certificates, and compares signed orbit decisions with independent exhaustive edge-sign controls. It also checks repeated odd-size component swaps and a diagonal tensor on which the cubic test declines. [Replay](check.py)
