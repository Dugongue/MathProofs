# Four isolation transfers from Hoza to Grochow's research program

| Result | What the proof establishes |
| --- | --- |
| [Smaller class-two group power orbits](ClassTwoPowerOrbits.md) | Sun's power-recovery search can retain just the cyclic-basis digits that central translations cannot change. The state bound drops from \(p^{\phi\zeta}\) to \(p^r\). On a supplied matched branch, a deterministic search outputs an isomorphism in \(O(\log L+r\log p)\) space. Hoza's result gives the first shortest positive generator word in the same space. |
| [Additive isolation for exterior and symmetric powers](AdditiveIsolation.md) | One short seed of additive diagonal weights preserves the zero pattern of every prescribed minor and symmetric-power coefficient of every subinterval of a transfer product. The first nonzero coefficient is one product of local transition coefficients. The construction works in every characteristic. |
| [Shared selectors for matrix multiplication](SharedSelectors.md) | A logarithmic-height contraction tree admits one seed that simultaneously isolates its feasible sections. Three independently routed operand pairs give an approximative restriction to masked matrix multiplication and an exact restriction over a bounded extension. A projective family attains the logarithmic extension threshold in the shared-selector model. |
| [Determinant-constrained tensor transporters](DeterminantTransporters.md) | A layered graph counts every determinant fiber of a labelled block transporter over finite commutative rings. Hoza's generator isolates all nonempty fibers with one seed. It gives unambiguous logarithmic-space witness output when the active block matrices across a cut fit in logarithmic space. |

## Connections to the open problems

The determinant-fiber theorem is a case of the determinant constraints in Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang Open Question 1.9. The group theorem refines the power-recovery step in Sun's algorithm, complementing Grochow and Qiao's search-to-decision work. The selector theorem addresses a specific exact-versus-border restriction question and applies to masked matrix multiplication. The additive theorem gives a structured PIT result for compound transfer products, in the direction of the field and model comparisons in Grochow's PIT agenda. These connections use the stated models. The unrestricted questions about TI, group isomorphism, PIT and the matrix multiplication exponent remain open here. ## Proof and source status

The four arguments are written proofs. The additive transfer, the shared-selector argument and the deterministic group orbit search have their own proofs independent of Hoza's complexity-class equality. The stated unambiguous search bounds use his report. The Lean companion and axiom audit cover the minimizer increment bound, the geometric tail and leading-bit comparison, the lowest-coefficient identity, erasure of zero coordinates, and the sharp linear-independence threshold for a shared selector.
