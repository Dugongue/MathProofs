# Short additive isolating weights for exterior and symmetric powers of matrix products

Let \(K\) be a field, \(w,n,r,T\) be positive integers, and
\[
\mathcal A=\{\alpha\in\mathbb N^w:|\alpha|\le r\},
\qquad W=|\mathcal A|=\binom{w+r}{r}.
\]
At layer \(i\), let \(X_i=(x_{i1},\ldots,x_{iw})\) be a block of independent variables. A product has a set \(S_i\subseteq\mathcal A\) of states at each layer, matrices \(A_i\in K^{S_{i-1}\times S_i}\), and diagonal matrices \(D_i=\operatorname{diag}(X_i^\alpha)_{\alpha\in S_i}\). Its interval from layer \(a\) to layer \(b>a\) is the matrix
\[
M_{a,b}(X)=A_{a+1}D_{a+1}A_{a+2}\cdots D_{b-1}A_b.
\]
All instances use the same variables and may have different state sets and matrices.

## Isolation theorem

There is a deterministically constructible family of substitutions
\[
x_{ij}=z^{\rho_\sigma(i,j)}
\]
with \(O(\log((n+1)WT))\) seed bits, nonnegative integer exponents, and output degrees at most
\[
D=((n+1)WT)^{O(\log(r+2))}.
\]
For every list of at most \(T\) products as above, some one seed \(\sigma\) isolates every nonzero entry of every interval of every product simultaneously. More precisely, in each such entry there is a unique contributing state sequence of least weight, where the weight is the sum of the substituted state exponents at its internal layers. The resulting univariate entry has valuation exactly that weight. Its leading coefficient at zero is the product of the transition coefficients along that sequence. Every identically zero entry remains zero.

The family depends only on \(w,n,r,T\), and the preprocessing to construct its seed-to-exponent map takes time polynomial in \(W,n,T\). For fixed \(r\) and bounded \(T\), the seed count, exponent bound and preprocessing are polynomial in \(w,n\). The field \(K\) has arbitrary characteristic. The auxiliary integers used to construct the weights are independent of \(K\).

If there are at most \(Q\) entries under consideration, any grid with \(QD+1\) elements in \(K\) or an extension gives a point family by evaluating each substitution at the grid elements. For each list there is one point preserving the zero/nonzero pattern of all \(Q\) entries. The product of the nonzero univariate entries for a good seed has degree at most \(QD\), so some grid value avoids its roots. Testing one entry only requires \(D+1\) grid values.

## Proof

### Additive features for monomial labels

Put \(q=2^{\lceil\log_2(r+1)\rceil}\), so \(r<q\le 2r\). Choose
\[
\delta=\left\lceil\log_2\bigl(T(n+1)^2W^4\bigr)\right\rceil+2,\qquad
L=4\bigl(\delta+\lceil\log_2(W^2+1)\rceil\bigr).
\]
There exist features \(c(j,h)\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\), for \(j\in[w]\) and \(h\in[L]\), such that for every pair \(\alpha\ne\beta\) in \(\mathcal A\) the integer sums
\[
u_\alpha(h)=\sum_j \alpha_jc(j,h)
\]
differ modulo \(q\) from \(u_\beta(h)\) on at least \(\delta\) coordinates \(h\).

For a proof and construction, start with independent uniform features modulo \(q\). For each pair let \(H_{\alpha,\beta}\) count coordinates at which the sums differ. For each \(h\), the dot product \((\alpha-\beta)\cdot c(\cdot,h)\) modulo \(q\) is uniform on a subgroup of size at least two, since some nonzero difference lies in \([-r,r]\) and \(q>r\). Hence
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}2^{-H_{\alpha,\beta}}\right]
   \le W^2(3/4)^L<2^{-\delta}.
\]
Fix the features one entry at a time, minimizing the conditional expectation. It can be computed exactly. In a partly fixed column the difference dot product is uniform on a coset of the subgroup of \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) generated by the unfixed differences. Its size is determined by the gcd of \(q\) and those differences. The fixed contribution tells whether the coset contains zero. Thus the expected factor for the column is \((1+\Pr[\text{difference}=0])/2\). Multiply these factors over the columns for each pair and sum. The number of pairs is at most \(W^2\), the denominators have bit length \(O(L\log q)\), and there are only \(q\) choices per entry. Both \(w\) and \(q\) are bounded by a polynomial in \(W\). At the end the potential is below \(2^{-\delta}\), so every pair has the required distance.

### Isolation with additive weights

Let \(U=r(q-1)\), \(R=16(U+1)\), and \(\sigma\in\{0,1\}^L\). Use the weights
\[
\rho_\sigma(i,j)=\sum_{h=1}^L R^{\nu_2(i)+L-h}\,c(j,h)\sigma_h
\]
at layers \(1\le i<n\), where \(\nu_2(i)\) is the exponent of two in \(i\). The weight of state \(\alpha\) is the actual exponent of \(X_i^\alpha\) after substitution, namely the same expression with \(u_\alpha(h)\) in place of \(c(j,h)\). Its digits are integers in \([0,U]\). Its size is at most \((U+1)R^{L+\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil}\). Multiplying by \(n\) bounds the degree of every substituted entry and gives the stated \(D\).

For each product make a layered graph with an edge from state \(\alpha\) to state \(\beta\) exactly when the relevant matrix entry is nonzero. For fixed endpoints every path contributes a distinct monomial, since it chooses a unique exponent vector in each internal layer and the variable blocks of the layers are independent. Its coefficient is the product of its nonzero edge entries. Consequently every nonzero entry is represented by a nonempty path family.

Fix reachable endpoints in layers \(a<b\) of one graph, with \(b-a\ge2\). Let \(i_*\) be the index of maximal \(\nu_2\) among the internal layers. It is unique. If \(m=\nu_2(i_*)\), there are at most \(2^t\) internal layers of valuation \(m-t\), for \(t\ge1\). Indeed the interval between the two nearest multiples of \(2^{m+1}\) around \(i_*\) contains exactly \(2^t\) indices of valuation \(m-t\) when \(t\le m\), and the internal layers form a subinterval of it. For \(t>m\) the count is zero.

For a reachable pivot state \(\alpha\) at \(i_*\), let \(F_\alpha(\sigma)\) be the least weight of a path through \(\alpha\) with the given endpoints. Flipping seed bit \(h\) from zero to one increases the weight of each such path by
\[
B_h u_\alpha(h)+\text{a number in }[0,\epsilon B_h],\qquad
B_h=R^{m+L-h},\quad
\epsilon=U\sum_{t\ge1}2^t R^{-t}=\frac{2U}{R-2}<\tfrac18.
\]
The same bounds hold for the change in \(F_\alpha\), by comparing a minimizer before and a minimizer after the flip. This is the step that makes the argument independent of which path attains the minimum for each seed.

For two distinct pivot states \(\alpha,\beta\), let \(J\) be the coordinates on which their sums differ modulo \(q\). Keep the seed coordinates outside \(J\) fixed. At most one assignment to \(J\) makes \(F_\alpha=F_\beta\). To see this, start from a seed where equality holds and transform it into any different seed on \(J\), flipping the differing bits in order from left to right. At the first changed coordinate \(h\), the difference \(F_\alpha-F_\beta\) changes by at least \((1-\epsilon)B_h\) in magnitude since the two integer sums differ by at least one. The total possible magnitude of the changes at all later coordinates is bounded by
\[
(U+\epsilon)\sum_{j>h}B_j < \frac{U+\epsilon}{R-1}B_h<\tfrac18 B_h .
\]
The final difference cannot be zero. Thus the tie has probability at most \(2^{-|J|}\le2^{-\delta}\) over a uniform seed. There are at most \(T(n+1)^2W^4\) choices of product, endpoints and pivot pair. The union bound leaves a seed with no such ties.

For that seed, the minimizing path is unique for every reachable endpoint pair, by induction on \(b-a\). At distance one there is at most one edge. At larger distances there is exactly one minimizing pivot state since the pivot costs are distinct. Each of its two subpaths minimizes independently and is unique by the induction hypothesis. Hence the term of lowest degree has exactly one nonzero contribution, giving the valuation and leading coefficient. This extends Hoza's discrete seed-derivative argument by controlling the integer sum of features in a monomial label. [Hoza, Theorem 1.3 and §2.2](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/323/)

## All minors and symmetric powers of diagonally perturbed products

Let \(A_i\) instead be ordinary transfer matrices between spaces of dimensions at most \(w\), padding the internal indexing sets as needed. Insert the simple diagonal \(D_i^{(1)}=\operatorname{diag}(x_{i1},\ldots,x_{iw_i})\) at each internal layer. For each \(1\le k\le r\), the \(k\)-th exterior power of the interval product has entries equal to the \(k\times k\) minors. By Cauchy–Binet, its transition entries are the \(k\times k\) minors of the individual \(A_i\), and the diagonal entry for a \(k\)-subset is the product of its \(k\) coordinate variables. If a layer has dimension less than \(k\), its \(k\)-th exterior power is zero and contributes no paths. Take the list of the \(r\) exterior-power products. One seed simultaneously preserves all their nonzero minors over \(K(z)\), and the same point family gives one evaluation that preserves the entire zero/nonzero minor pattern up to size \(r\). In particular, it preserves each interval's generic rank capped at \(r\).

One can include the symmetric powers \(\operatorname{Sym}^k\) of these products in the list, using \(T=2r\). The monomial basis of \(\operatorname{Sym}^k K^{w_i}\) is indexed by exponent vectors \(\alpha\) of total degree \(k\). The action of a diagonal matrix uses exactly \(X_i^\alpha\), including repetitions. Matrix multiplication is functorial for both \(\operatorname{Sym}^k\) and \(\bigwedge^k\), in every characteristic. Thus the same seed simultaneously preserves the zero/nonzero coefficient pattern in the monomial bases of every selected symmetric power. No factorial inverses are used.

This construction respects the original diagonal parameters. Giving the exterior-power states unrelated weights would not in general give a substitution on the original coordinates, since the weight at a subset must be the sum of the weights of its members. The additive feature construction enforces this sum and still isolates all nonzero entries simultaneously.

For example over \(\mathbb F_2\), take
\[
A_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The size-two minor through the internal layer \(\operatorname{diag}(x_1,x_2,x_3)\) is \(x_1x_3+x_2x_3\). The transition through subset \(\{1,2\}\) has coefficient zero because the corresponding local minor of \(A_1\) vanishes, despite its nonzero entries. At \(x_1=x_2=x_3=1\), the two remaining paths cancel. For an isolating seed the two terms have different weights and the lowest coefficient is \(1\). The theorem accounts for local determinant cancellation by working with the actual nonzero compound matrix entries.

## Context and proof status

Forbes and Shpilka's work on Noether normalization for simultaneous matrix conjugation relates invariant separation to black-box PIT for broader algebraic branching programs. Basis isolation by Agrawal, Gurjar, Korwar and Saxena is a related way to control coefficient dependencies in general read-once oblivious ABPs. The result here is a simultaneous leading-term statement for diagonal transfer products and their exterior and symmetric powers, where labels identify contributing state sequences. It applies to determinant and rank tests on serial tensor contractions with independently marked intermediate coordinates. The treatment of higher powers uses additive weights on the original coordinates and needs no test for general ABP identities or unrestricted tensor isomorphism. [Forbes–Shpilka](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.0084) · [Agrawal–Gurjar–Korwar–Saxena](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.7535)

The proof is written mathematics. It extends the argument in Hoza's ECCC report TR26-323, which discloses the use of GPT-6.2 Astra in discovering the proof. The cited report is the source for the layered vertex-isolation method. This argument proves its own additive generalization. Priority for the diagonal-product application remains unestablished.
