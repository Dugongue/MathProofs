# Minimum generating sets of arbitrary finite groups in deterministic logspace

**Theorem.** Given the Cayley table of a finite group $G$, a deterministic logarithmic-space transducer outputs an actual smallest generating set of $G$. In particular the threshold decision problem and minimum-set search both have deterministic logspace algorithms. This answers Question 8.1 of Collins, Grochow, Levet, and Weiß on either reading of minimum generation. The proof is informal. [Question 8.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00133#S8) · [Definitions](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00133#S2.SS1)

Write $N=|G|$. A Cayley-table input has bit length polynomial in $N$, so $O(\log(N+1))$ bits is logarithmic workspace in the input length. The output tape is write-only. The three construction lemmas are the [quotient tower](QuotientTower.md), the [solvable radical lift](SolvableRadicalLift.md), and the [nonabelian semisimple lift](SemisimpleLift.md). The number $d=d(G)$ is computed once using the accompanying [minimum generator number algorithm](MinimumGeneratorNumber.md). All tuple queries use this length, with identity entries allowed in proper quotients. If valid Cayley tables are treated as a language promise, the promise can also be checked by scanning associativity, identity, and inverses with a fixed number of table indices.

## Alternating quotient tower

Set $K_0=1$ in $G$. In each round, quotient the current group $G/K_i$ by its solvable radical. If the result $E$ is nontrivial, quotient it by $A=\operatorname{Soc}(E)$ and begin another round. All the normal subgroups here are specified canonically. A radical-free $E$ has a socle that is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups, because its minimal normal subgroups have that structure, distinct minimal normals commute, and their product has trivial center. It acts faithfully on $A$ by conjugation: the normal centralizer of $A$ intersects $A$ in $Z(A)=1$, whereas any nontrivial normal subgroup of $E$ contains a minimal normal subgroup. Thus $|E|\le |\operatorname{Aut}(A)|\le |A|^{\log_2|A|}$, using an at most $\log_2|A|$-element generating list to encode each automorphism.

At the start of a round put $h=\log_2|G/K_i|$. If the radical removal reduces $h$ by at least $h/2$, then $\sqrt h$ decreases by at least $(1-1/\sqrt 2)\sqrt h$. Otherwise the logarithm of the radical-free quotient is at least $h/2$, and the semisimple removal decreases $h$ by at least $\sqrt{h/2}$ by the automorphism bound. In the latter case $\sqrt h$ drops by at least $1/(2\sqrt 2)$. Any nonterminal group has $h\ge1$, so the total is $O(\sqrt{\log(N+1)})$ rounds. A round with an entire solvable group terminates after the radical removal. This argument allows quotients that shrink slowly in logarithmic order.

The quotient-tower lemma gives direct $O(\log(N+1))$-space membership tests for the preimage $K_i$ of each layer, by replaying a stack of small generator indices on the original table. It therefore gives a single table interface for every $G/K_i$. Use least original labels for cosets, or rank those labels when a compact table is needed. Scans for these operations use no tuple subroutine.

## Witness construction

Compute one coordinate at a time, as follows. At the terminal trivial group, every coordinate equals the identity. At a radical removal, use the solvable radical lift to recover a coordinate in the parent from coordinate queries to the tuple in the child. At a nonabelian socle removal, use the semisimple lift to do the same. Both lifts apply at length $d$, since every quotient of $G$ is at most $d$-generated. They allow repeated quotient entries and identity padding. In a radical step, if the internal algorithm names a quotient element by a compact rank, translate its returned label using table scans after the recursive tuple query finishes. These translations do not invoke any further tuple operation.

Each layer's coordinate subroutine chooses the corresponding entry of a single deterministic tuple specified by the lift procedure. Repeated queries therefore use compatible answers. At every return the tuple described by those coordinates generates the parent group, by the relevant lemma. The tuple described at the original group generates $G$. Since its length is minimal, it has no repeated entries or identity entries. Emit its coordinates in order.

## Total workspace

There are two kinds of active recursive work. When a lift suspends to query a coordinate in a smaller quotient, keep just its indices and its kernel-sized state. In a semisimple layer of kernel order $a$, this is $O(\log(a+1)+(\log\log(N+4))^{C})$ bits for an absolute $C$. The radical lift applies the same accounting to each abelian sublayer of the radical, with no suspended full-table labels. Each radical is handled by its own Fitting and Frattini tower of depth $O(\log\log(N+4))$. The number of active sublayers across the whole recursion is thus $O(\sqrt{\log(N+1)}\log\log(N+4))$, and the product of their nontrivial kernel orders is at most $N$. The sum of their kernel logarithms is at most $\log N$, and the remaining polyloglog terms sum to $O(\log(N+1))$. A top-level layer identifier or queried generator position also costs only $O(\log\log(N+4))$ bits per frame.

At a leaf of this active stack, a lift can do table arithmetic and independent rank or subgroup computations using one additional $O(\log(N+1))$ scratch area. The direct quotient-tower interface itself is a fixed logspace subroutine with no calls to witnesses. Within a radical sublayer's local table construction, use its internal geometric quotient stack over the current radical, which also takes $O(\log(N+1))$ total space and makes no witness queries. Thus any full-size scratch used by a layer is released before calling for a child tuple coordinate. Evaluating a coordinate uses only the sum of these bounds. All scans halt and recursive calls descend. The resulting transducer is deterministic with logarithmic work space, which also bounds its running time by a polynomial in the input length. This proves the theorem.
