# Constructive minimum group generation with a compressed chief-series transcript

**Theorem.** Given the Cayley table of a finite group $G$ of order $N$, a deterministic transducer outputs an actual minimum generating set in polynomial time and $O(\log(N+2)\log\log(N+4))$ work space. If $d(G)$ is bounded by a constant, its space is $O(\log(N+2))$.

The group-theoretic search at an individual chief factor is due to Lucchini–Thakkar. The refinement is to store only the changes made at each factor and replay those changes to answer individual generator queries. [Lucchini–Thakkar, Proposition 9, Lemma 12 and Corollary 13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07633) · [Collins–Grochow–Levet–Weiß, §7.1 and Question 8.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00133#S7.SS1)

## Quotient interfaces

For each table element $x$, write $N_x$ for its normal closure. Membership in $N_x$ is undirected reachability using the conjugates of $x$ and their inverses. Order the elements by $(|N_x|,\text{table label}(x))$. For the prefix subgroup $K$ generated by all normal closures earlier than $N_x$, test membership by another undirected Cayley reachability query over those earlier conjugates. This uses a fixed nesting of logarithmic-space computations. If $KN_x>K$, then $KN_x/K$ is a minimal normal subgroup of $G/K$. Indeed, for normal $K<L<KN_x$, write an element of $L\setminus K$ as $kb$ with $k\in K$, $b\in N_x$. Then $b\in L\setminus K$ and $N_b\le L\cap N_x<N_x$, so $b$ was earlier and is in $K$, a contradiction. The strict prefix changes thus give a chief series, and any stage is located by an ordinary scan. [Reingold, Undirected Connectivity in Log-Space](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/sl.pdf)

Write this series as $1=K_0<\cdots<K_h=G$. At a descending step let $E=G/K_{i-1}$, $A=K_i/K_{i-1}$ and $F=G/K_i$. Use least table labels to name cosets. Quotient multiplication, comparison, and ranking of $A$ elements by least label in $O(\log N)$ space require only the chief prefix membership tests and table scans. Quotient and kernel interfaces always refer directly to the original group, with fixed nesting depth. In each test of a candidate generating tuple in $E$, Reingold's algorithm checks reachability from the identity to every element of $E$ in the undirected graph of right multiplication by the generator entries and their inverses. Entries will be supplied by transcript replay.

## Search at one chief factor

Begin with the empty tuple for the trivial quotient. Suppose $g_1,\ldots,g_d$ represent a minimum generating tuple of $F$. The same original table labels are lifts to $E$.

If $A$ is abelian, Lucchini–Thakkar's Proposition 9 says that either the displayed tuple already generates, one coordinate can be right-multiplied by a single element of $A$ to generate, or $d(E)=d+1$ and appending any nonidentity element of $A$ succeeds. Test the unchanged tuple, then all pairs consisting of a coordinate and an element of $A$, and append a least nonidentity element only if these tests fail. For $d=0$ this makes precisely the cyclic prime-order choice. The record stores a no-change flag or an index and one $A$ rank, together with whether the length increased. This takes $O(1+\log(d+1)+\log |A|)$ bits.

If $A$ is nonabelian, put $b=|A|$, $m=\max(1,\lceil\log_2 N\rceil)$, $u=\max(d,2)$, and $T=\lceil 8/5+\log_b m\rceil$. The integer $T$ is computable as the least integer $k\ge2$ with $b^{5k-8}\ge m^5$, using logarithmic-space arithmetic with capped powers, or with powers bounded by $b^5m^5$ at the first successful trial. Pad the quotient tuple by identities to length $u$ and test every tuple obtained by corrections in its first $t=\min(u,T)$ positions. The crown multiplicity $\delta_E(A)$ is at most $m$. If $u\ge T$, Lemma 12 therefore guarantees success, even when the actual threshold is smaller, since unused correction positions may be assigned the identity. If $u<T$, these tests include every lift of the padded tuple. Gaschütz's lifting lemma says that they succeed whenever $d(E)\le u$. A nonabelian $E$ requires at least two generators and surjects onto $F$, so a success has minimum possible length $u$. If they fail, the bound $d(E)\le\max(2,d+1)$ used in Corollary 13 gives $d(E)=u+1$ and $u=d$. Pad by one more identity, then test every correction in all $u+1$ slots. Gaschütz guarantees a successful candidate, and $u+1\le T$ in this case. The record stores the case flag and the ranks of at most $T$ consecutive corrections within $A$.

In the nonabelian step $b^T\le b^{13/5}m$, so both the first search and any second search have polynomially many candidates. The temporary trial code has $O(T\log b)=O(\log b+\log m)$ bits and fits in logarithmic space on its own. The abelian search has at most $1+d|A|$ candidates plus the append. Both searches are deterministic, using the first successful candidate in the specified order.

## Replay, final output and the bounds

The transcript is a packed variable-width array of the chosen records. Store the trial separately until it succeeds. To recover one generator slot at any stage, start with the original identity label and scan the previous records from top to bottom. For that slot, multiply the current original group element by the least original representative of the recorded kernel element when the record modifies that slot. The same procedure inserts a new slot by beginning with identity. Every earlier correction is retained even when passed through a finer quotient. Induction on the number of records shows that the resulting original label projects to exactly the tuple entry used in the current quotient. The final generator at each slot is obtained by a complete replay. It requires only one original table element register, a slot counter, a stage counter and transcript offsets in addition to the quotient-interface scratch. Flags and current tuple length determine all record widths during the scan. No list of earlier tuple labels or recursive stack is required. The membership testers use a read-only view of the existing transcript and the temporary trial.

Since the kernels' orders multiply to $N$, the sum of their binary logarithms is $\log_2 N$, and there are at most $\log_2 N$ strict stages. At an abelian stage, $d\le\log_2 N$, and the index accounts for the extra $O(\log\log(N+4))$ bits. At a nonabelian stage, $T\log b\le(13/5)\log b+\log m$. Summing transcript widths gives $O(\log(N+2)\log\log(N+4))$ bits. For bounded $d(G)$, all quotient tuple lengths and all chosen numbers of nonabelian corrections are bounded, so the sum is $O(\log(N+2))$. Replay and arithmetic have polynomial running time, and the chief, trial, reachability and replay loops nest to a fixed depth. Thus polynomial time still holds with the larger transcript cap. For $N=1$ output the empty set. Otherwise the last tuple has minimum length and generates $G$. Its entries are distinct and nonidentity, because any repeated entry or identity could be removed without changing the generated subgroup. Hence the emitted tuple represents the required set.

## A limit of a separate quotient-size strategy

The order of an arbitrary group need not be bounded by a fixed power of the order of any fixed initial segment of its generalized Fitting series. To see this, take $S_s$ in its natural action, for $s\ge5$. Iteratively take faithful imprimitive permutational wreath products $G_j=C_{p_j}\wr G_{j-1}$, alternating $p_j=2,3$, with $G_0=S_s$. For a stage with base $V=C_{p_j}^{\Omega}$, the quotient acts faithfully on its permuted coordinates, so $C_{G_j}(V)=V$. A normal $q$-subgroup for $q\ne p_j$ commutes with $V$ and is trivial. Components also commute with the Fitting subgroup and hence with $V$, so they are absent. The normal $p_j$-core consists of the preimage of the quotient's normal $p_j$-core, which is trivial since the preceding layer has the other prime, and $S_s$ has no nontrivial normal prime-power subgroup. Thus each generalized Fitting step removes precisely one base. The logarithm of the product of the first $h$ base orders is $O(3^h s)$, whereas $\log|S_s|=\Theta(s\log s)$. Taking $h=\lfloor (\log_3\log s)/2\rfloor$ gives unboundedly many successive layers whose combined log size is $o(\log |G_h|)$. This only limits the fixed-depth shrink argument. The transcript theorem above applies to all these groups.
