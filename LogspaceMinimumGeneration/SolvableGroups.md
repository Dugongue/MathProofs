# Constructive minimum generation of finite solvable groups in logspace

**Theorem.** There is a deterministic logarithmic-space transducer that outputs a generating set of minimum cardinality from the Cayley table of any finite solvable group. The same procedure produces a generating tuple of any given length $d\ge d(G)$ with $d\le |G|$, allowing padding by the identity. This is an informal proof. The method uses the preceding logspace computation of $d(G)$ on Cayley tables to select the optimal length.

Write $N=|G|$. All table entries, counters of polynomial magnitude in $N$, and group-element labels use $O(\log(N+1))$ bits. In a table of order $n$, this statement holds with $n$ in place of $N$, including for virtual tables with compact labels. For a logspace-decidable list of generators, membership and witness words use undirected Cayley graph reachability in logspace. A walk can be streamed by retaining its consecutive endpoints and matching each edge with the least available generator or its inverse. The greedy generating list is computable by testing each group element against all earlier unfiltered elements. Its length is at most $\log_2 n$. We use the chief-series scan and the implicit narrow-matrix rank lemma of the preceding proof. [Reingold, *Undirected Connectivity in Log-Space*](https://doi.org/10.1145/1391289.1391291)

## Frattini subgroup

For a solvable group $E$ and a chief series $1=K_0<\dots<K_s=E$, a maximal subgroup $M$ first misses a chief term $K_i$. Since $K_i$ is normal, $M K_i=E$. The subgroup $M\cap K_i$ is normalized by $M$. Its image in $K_i/K_{i-1}$ is normalized also by $K_i$ because this chief factor is abelian and $K_{i-1}\le M$. The image is therefore normal under $E$ and trivial by minimality. Thus $M/K_{i-1}$ is a complement to $K_i/K_{i-1}$ in $E/K_{i-1}$. Conversely every such complement lifts to a maximal subgroup. Indeed any subgroup strictly between it and $E/K_{i-1}$ would have a nontrivial proper quotient-invariant intersection with the chief factor.

For each step put $X=E/K_{i-1}$, $B=K_i/K_{i-1}$, and $Q=X/B$. Stream the affine system of the preceding proof for sections $\sigma:Q\to X$, parametrized by corrections in $B$ to the lifts of a greedy generating list for $Q$. For a given $x\in X$, membership in every complement holds if the system is inconsistent or if every solution satisfies $\sigma(xB)=x$. Each coordinate of the latter condition is an affine equation, and consistency combined with rank detects implication of that equation. The matrices have polynomially many rows and polylogarithmically many columns with individual entries available in logspace. Thus we can test membership in the intersection of all the maximal subgroups, $\Phi(E)$, in logspace. A tuple generates $E$ exactly when its image generates $E/\Phi(E)$. If the tuple fails, its subgroup lies in a maximal subgroup, which contains $\Phi(E)$ and has proper image.

## The Fitting subgroup and quotient sizes

Suppose now $\Phi(E)=1$ and $E$ is solvable. Its Fitting subgroup $F$ is the largest nilpotent normal subgroup. A normal $p$-subgroup $P$ of $E$ has $\Phi(P)\le \Phi(E)$. If some maximal $M$ failed to contain $\Phi(P)$, then normality would give $M\Phi(P)=E$, so $(M\cap P)\Phi(P)=P$, which forces $M\cap P=P$, a contradiction. Apply this to each characteristic Sylow subgroup $P$ of $F$. These Sylow subgroups have trivial Frattini subgroup and are elementary abelian. In particular $F$ is abelian.

For each maximal subgroup $M$ missing $P$, $P\cap M$ is normal under $E=PM$ and $P/(P\cap M)$ is a simple $E$-module by maximality. The intersection of these kernels is $1$ because $\Phi(E)=1$. Thus $P$ embeds into a product of simple modules and is semisimple. It follows that $F$ is the subgroup generated by all the minimal normal subgroups, since each is abelian and hence lies in $F$. Also $C_E(F)=F$. Otherwise a minimal nontrivial $E/F$-normal subgroup $U/F$ inside $C_E(F)/F$ is abelian by solvability. Then $F$ is central in $U$ and $U/F$ is abelian, making $U$ nilpotent of class at most two, contrary to the definition of $F$.

Enumerate minimal normal subgroups by their least principal-normal-closure labels. Minimality of $N_x=\langle x^E\rangle$ is tested by scanning for a strictly smaller nontrivial $N_y\le N_x$. Inclusion follows from membership of $y$ in $N_x$, and cardinalities or ordinary inclusion scans test strictness. Membership in the product of any logspace-listed selection of normal subgroups is Cayley reachability using the union as generators. This computes $F$ in logspace. It also computes an internal simple-module direct decomposition: greedily select a minimal normal subgroup exactly when it is outside the product of all earlier ones. Its intersection with that product is trivial by minimality. The unfiltered prefix product equals the product of selected predecessors, so the selection test has fixed subroutine depth. Projection onto any selected factor is computable by testing which element of that factor differs from the given element by the product of all the other selected factors.

Set $Q=E/F$. Its action on $F$ is faithful and its action on each Sylow subgroup $P$ is completely reducible. Write $Q_p$ for the image in $\operatorname{GL}(P)$. The solvable completely reducible linear-group order bound gives $|Q_p|\le |P|^3$. Thus $|Q|\le |F|^3$, and $\log |Q|\le\tfrac34\log |E|$. In the algorithm we first pass from a solvable group $H$ to $E=H/\Phi(H)$, then from $E$ to $Q=E/F$ and recurse only on $Q$. One can treat $H=1$ separately. For a nontrivial $H$, its Frattini-free quotient has nontrivial Fitting subgroup. Consequently the logarithms of the orders of successive recursive inputs sum to at most $4\log |G|$. [Pálfy–Wolf bound, in Halasi and Maróti, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5454)

## One lifting step across a semisimple abelian normal subgroup

Let $F$ be an abelian semisimple normal subgroup with the accessible internal direct decomposition above. Let $t=(t_1,\dots,t_d)$ generate $Q=E/F$, and assume $d\ge d(E)$. Access to the coordinates of $t$ is by queries. For this step also assume $d$ is at most polylogarithmic in $|E|$, or use the length reduction at the end of this section. Pick the least base lift $x_i$ of $t_i$ in $E$. For every $q\in Q$ fix a canonical streamed word $w_q$ on the generator indices of $t$ representing $q$, with $w_1$ empty. For an arbitrary lift tuple $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_d)$, form the list in $F$ of evaluations of the words

$$
w_qw_rw_{qr}^{-1}\quad(q,r\in Q),\qquad X_iw_{t_i}^{-1}\quad(1\le i\le d).
$$

The $Q$-submodule of $F$ they generate is exactly $\langle y\rangle\cap F$. First $\langle y\rangle$ surjects onto $Q$, so its intersection with abelian $F$ is $Q$-invariant and contains all values. Conversely quotient by the $Q$-submodule generated by the values. In that quotient the word representatives define a section of $Q$ and each lift belongs to that section by the additional relations. The generated subgroup then has trivial intersection with the image of $F$. The additional relations also cover repeated quotient entries and identity padding.

Identify an isotypic direct product with $S^m$ for a simple abelian $Q$-module $S$ of characteristic $p$. All requisite equivariant maps between simple factors can be enumerated in logspace. Choose a nonzero $a$ in the domain. Its $Q$-orbit generates the additive group. For each candidate image $b$ scan every domain element and evaluate one fixed streamed additive Cayley word on the conjugates of $a$, replacing each conjugate by the same conjugate of $b$. This yields the only possible equivariant additive map taking $a$ to $b$. Verify additivity and $Q$-equivariance by table scans. Each nonzero map of simple modules is an isomorphism. This identifies isotypic classes, gives the least isomorphisms for a coordinate identification, and enumerates the finite division ring $D=\operatorname{End}_Q(S)$. Addition and composition use evaluation on $a$. The ring is a field by Wedderburn's little theorem. A greedy $\mathbb F_p$-basis of this additive group is enumerable by the unfiltered prefix test. Elements of $D$ have labels of $O(\log |S|)$ bits since each is determined by its value at $a$.

Let $R$ be the number of displayed relators and identify each simple coordinate with $S$. The $j$th coordinate of the relator list for the base tuple gives $r_j\in S^R$. Corrections to the $j$th coordinates of the $d$ generators are an arbitrary vector in $S^d$. They change $r_j$ by the image of a common linear map $L:S^d\to S^R$. Indeed, in a word the change from each multiplicative correction is a signed conjugate under the fixed quotient action, independent of all other corrections. The same action on isomorphic simple coordinates gives the same map $L$, and $Q$-equivariance of the $D$ action gives $D$-linearity. Individual entries of a baseline column or a column produced by a basis correction are streamed by evaluating the relator twice in $E$ and taking the simple projection of the difference. Store one running group element and the counters identifying the row, coordinate and correction. The quotient tuple and its witness words are accessed by queries throughout.

A subgroup lifting $t$ generates $E$ exactly when for every isotypic class the resulting columns in $S^R$ are linearly independent over $D$. A proper submodule of a semisimple module is killed by a nonzero map to a simple module. Every such map on the chosen isotypic component has the form $(v_1,\ldots,v_m)\mapsto \sum_j a_j(v_j)$ for $(a_j)\in D^m$. It kills the $Q$-submodule generated by the relators exactly when this combination of the columns vanishes. There are no nonzero intertwiners between different isotypic classes, so their requirements are independent.

Put $V=\operatorname{im} L$. Greedily select $r_i$ independent modulo $V$, using prefix ranks. Number the unselected indices in order. For the $k$th unselected $j$, compute the unique coefficients expressing its residue via the selected residues, say $v_j=r_j-\sum_i a_i r_i\in V$. Let $b_k$ be the $k$th vector of a greedy basis of $V$, together with the original preimage basis vector for $L$ that produced it. Correct the $j$th column by $b_k-v_j$. Leave selected columns unchanged. There are enough basis vectors of $V$: Gaschütz's lemma gives a generating lift of $t$ as $d\ge d(E)$, and any independent choice from the translates $r_j+V$ requires $m\le \dim_D V+\dim_D\langle r_j+V\rangle$. The constructed columns are independent because their residues force a relation to be a combination of the relations $r_j-\sum_i a_i r_i$ for unselected $j$, and after correction these become distinct basis vectors $b_k$. Use the unique coordinates of $b_k-v_j$ in the $V$ basis and the associated stored-by-query preimage basis vectors to obtain a correction tuple in $S^d$. Multiplying the independently determined simple coordinates gives the correction in $F$ for each slot. [Gaschütz lifting theorem as stated by Lucchini–Thakkar, Lemma 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.07633#page=2)

Every rank or coordinate computation in this construction uses the preceding narrow-matrix rank lemma. To see the explicit reduction, write vectors in $\mathbb F_p$-coordinates using a greedy additive basis of $S$. To take a $D$-span of a list, replace each listed column by all its multiples by an $\mathbb F_p$-basis of $D$. Test containment by appending the queried vector to that enlarged list and comparing ranks over $\mathbb F_p$. For a basis over $D$, recover the coefficient of a given basis column by enumerating $D$ until the corresponding residual lies in the $D$-span of all the other basis columns. In the quotient calculation include $V$ in that span. The basis of $V$ is chosen from images of the ordinary $\mathbb F_p$ standard basis vectors of $S^d$ by the same unfiltered prefix test over $D$. There are only $O(d\log |E|)$ candidate images and at most $\log |E|$ simple direct factors, and the expansion by a scalar basis multiplies the column count by at most $\log |E|$. All the individual entries are computed with fixed depth of local rank subroutines and streamed arithmetic. Thus the space outside the tuple queries is $O(\log(|E|+1))$ for $d\le\log_2 |E|$.

To handle varying lengths in the recursive construction, let the target length at a group $E$ be at most $\lfloor\log_2|E|\rfloor$. Obtain a quotient tuple of length $\min(d,\lfloor\log_2|Q|\rfloor)$ and pad it with identities to length $d$ before lifting. This request is feasible because $d(Q)\le d(E)$ and $d(Q)\le\lfloor\log_2|Q|\rfloor$. A Frattini quotient is handled by the same cap and identity padding, followed by arbitrary lifting. For a trivial group return the empty tuple and use its identity whenever a caller pads. If the requested output at the root has length greater than $\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor$, produce a tuple capped at that bound and append the remaining identities. For the minimum-length theorem there is no need to pad at the root.

## Space of the composed transducer

At each recursive level the table is virtual. Give each quotient the compact labels equal to ranks of the least representatives of its cosets in its parent table's ordering. Translation between ranks and representatives uses scans, membership in the normal subgroup, and a count. A query for the multiplication or other entry of the virtual table calls only the parental table and uses $O(\log(n+1))$ bits locally when the parent has order $n$. This quotient-access recursion has no calls to the generator-tuple computation. Keep the sizes at each active level and use the statically specified Frattini and Fitting kernels of its ancestors for membership tests. Their computations use only those ancestor tables and earlier ancestors. There are at most two adjacent quotient steps per level, with the parent orders bounded by the recursive order at that level.

The generator-tuple recursion goes toward smaller groups. To output one coordinate, use the lifting procedure and recompute requested coordinates of the quotient tuple. Each active level contributes $O(\log(n+1))$ bits for $n$ its recursive order, excluding the recursive queries. At any instant there is at most one stack of virtual table accesses, with the same geometric bound, in addition to the tuple recursion. A table query has no reason to retain the stack for an earlier completed query. Free local storage on return. Thus both stacks together take $O(\log(N+1))$ bits. Termination follows from finite scans and strict decrease of recursive orders. There is a single deterministic transducer with write-only output, so the space bound also bounds pre-output and between-output runs by a polynomial number of work configurations. Scanning the $d(G)$ output coordinates yields a generating set of size $d(G)$, since a repeated element or redundant identity would contradict minimality.
