# Minimum generation with a bounded radical index

Let $G$ be a finite group of order $N$ given by its Cayley table. Let $R(G)$ denote its solvable radical and put $B=\max(2,[G:R(G)])$. There is a deterministic transducer that outputs a minimum generating set of $G$ in polynomial time using

$$
O\bigl(\log(N+1)+\log(B+1)\log\log(B+2)\bigr)
$$

bits of workspace. In particular, for every fixed $c$, the construction uses logarithmic space on the class with $\log B\log\log(B+2)\le c\log(N+1)$. This includes groups whose radical index is polylogarithmic in their order and, more generally, those with index $\exp(O(\log N/\log\log N))$. The proof is informal. It uses the Cayley-table chief-series scan, the narrow rank procedure, and the simultaneous abelian lifting algorithm from the companion proofs.

## Computing the Frattini subgroup in arbitrary groups

Normal closure membership in a table group is undirected Cayley reachability using all conjugates of the given element as generators. This gives a logspace table for a normal closure, with compact labels obtained by scanning its elements. An arbitrary subgroup given by such a table is solvable exactly when the factors in its chief series are all abelian. The chief-series scan uses the sizes of principal normal closures, and abelianness of each factor is a table test. All of these scans and calls have fixed nesting depth. Hence $x\in R(G)$ is in logspace because the condition is equivalent to solvability of the normal closure of $x$.

For any finite group $E$, $\Phi(E)$ is nilpotent. In fact if $P$ is any Sylow subgroup of the normal subgroup $\Phi(E)$, Sylow conjugacy gives $E=\Phi(E)N_E(P)$. A subgroup generating $E$ together with $\Phi(E)$ already equals $E$, since every maximal subgroup contains $\Phi(E)$. Thus $N_E(P)=E$ for each such $P$, and all Sylow subgroups of $\Phi(E)$ are normal. In particular $\Phi(E)\le R(E)$.

To test $x\in\Phi(E)$, first reject if $x\notin R(E)$. Generate a prefix of an $E$-chief series ending at $R(E)$ by sorting the principal normal closures of $x\in R(E)$ by size and then by the label of $x$. The usual chief-series proof applies because every element of $\langle x^E\rangle$ also lies in $R(E)$. More explicitly, if $K$ is the prefix normal subgroup before $x$ and $K<L<K\langle x^E\rangle$ are normal in $E$, choose $b\in (L\cap\langle x^E\rangle)\setminus K$. Then $\langle b^E\rangle$ is a proper subgroup of $\langle x^E\rangle$ and its label $b$ was processed earlier, a contradiction. The prefix reaches $R(E)$ and every chief factor in it is abelian.

If a maximal subgroup $M$ avoids $x\in R(E)$, it first fails to contain some term $K_i$ of this chief prefix. It contains $K_{i-1}$ and satisfies $MK_i=E$. Since $K_i/K_{i-1}$ is abelian, $(M\cap K_i)/K_{i-1}$ is normal under both images of $M$ and $K_i$, hence trivial by chief minimality. Thus $M/K_{i-1}$ is a complement to $K_i/K_{i-1}$ in $E/K_{i-1}$. Conversely each such complement lifts to a maximal subgroup by the same minimality argument. Test the existence of a complement avoiding $x$ by the implicit narrow affine system for homomorphic sections. The extra condition is that the section evaluated at $xK_i$ differs from $xK_{i-1}$. Scan a coordinate of the difference and a nonzero field value, then compare coefficient and augmented ranks. This decides arbitrary Frattini membership in logarithmic space.

## Geometric reduction above the radical scale

Pass to the Frattini-free quotient $E$ of the current table group $H$. As usual, the Frattini subgroup of $H/\Phi(H)$ is trivial by maximal-subgroup correspondence. Let $F=F(E)$ be its Fitting subgroup. The proof that $F$ is abelian and semisimple as an $E$-module works for all finite $E$ with $\Phi(E)=1$. For each normal $p$-subgroup $P$ of $E$, $\Phi(P)\le\Phi(E)$ by the maximal-subgroup supplement argument, so each Sylow subgroup $P$ of $F$ is elementary abelian. For each maximal subgroup $M$ missing $P$, the kernel $P\cap M$ is an $E$-submodule and $P/(P\cap M)$ is a simple $E$-module by maximality. The intersection of all such kernels is zero by $\Phi(E)=1$, so $P$ is a submodule of a direct sum of simple modules and is semisimple. Consequently $F$ is the product of the abelian minimal normal subgroups of $E$. Find its simple internal direct decomposition by the greedy scan of all abelian minimal normal subgroups. Each candidate is a principal normal closure, minimality is a smaller-closure test, and the unfiltered prefix product supports logspace membership.

Write $R=R(E)$. Normality and characteristic invariance give $F(R)=F(E)=F$. Indeed $F(R)$ is normal nilpotent in $E$, and $F(E)$ is normal nilpotent in $R$. Since $R$ is solvable, $C_R(F)=F$. The proof is elementary here. A hypothetical minimal nontrivial $R/F$-normal subgroup $U/F$ inside $C_R(F)/F$ would be abelian. Then $U$ would be nilpotent of class at most two, which contradicts the definition of $F(R)$. Each simple $E$-module in the decomposition of a Sylow subgroup of $F$ restricts to a semisimple $R$-module: take a simple $R$-submodule and sum its $E$-conjugates. This sum is nonzero and $E$-stable, so it is the whole simple $E$-module. It is a sum of simple $R$-submodules because $R$ is normal. The resulting faithful action of $R/F$ on $F$ satisfies the Pálfy–Wolf bound, prime component by prime component, giving $|R/F|\le |F|^3$. [Halasi and Maróti, *The minimal base size for a $p$-solvable linear group*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5454)

Every group encountered by the recursion is a quotient of $G$. The image of $R(G)$ is a solvable normal subgroup in each such quotient, so the radical index of each quotient is bounded by the original $B$. In particular, $[E:R]\le B$, and

$$
\log|E/F|=\log[E:R]+\log|R/F|\le \tfrac34\log|E|+\tfrac14\log B.
$$

If $|E|>B^2$, this bounds $\log|E/F|$ by $\tfrac78\log|E|$. Recurse on $Q=E/F$. The companion simultaneous abelian lifting algorithm lifts an optimal quotient tuple, padded as needed, to a tuple of length $d(H)=d(E)$ in $E$. It applies to semisimple abelian normal $F$ in an arbitrary finite group, since its relator argument uses no assumption that $Q$ is solvable. Lift arbitrarily from $E$ to $H$; the Frattini property gives generation. At the first quotient $E$ of order at most $B^2$, use the procedure below to construct the minimum set outright. The original counter $B$ is shared by all frames. Its square can likewise be stored once, using $O(\log(N+1))$ bits.

## A compact record of chief-factor corrections

For an arbitrary Cayley-table group of order $n$, the next procedure constructs a minimum generating set in polynomial time and $O(\log(n+1)\log\log(n+2))$ bits. Fix the implicit chief series, choose the least lifts at every step, and reconstruct the evolving tuple coordinatewise by retaining only a record of corrections. Work from the trivial quotient downward, letting $A$ be the current chief factor, $b=|A|$, and $d$ the length of the optimal tuple already obtained in the preceding quotient. Slots are indexed in a fixed order, and appended slots take the next available index. An identity is used for any new slot before adding its correction.

For an abelian factor, Proposition 9 of Lucchini and Thakkar implies that if a lift of length $d$ generates, one may use the base lifts or correct just one slot. Otherwise append any nonidentity element of $A$. Test candidates by undirected Cayley reachability. The record is a slot index, an element of $A$ in compact labels, and the length increment. [Lucchini and Thakkar, Proposition 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.07633#page=7)

For a nonabelian factor put $m=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$, $u=\max(2,d)$, and $r=2+\lceil\log_b m\rceil$. Pad the base lifts to length $u$ with identities. The threshold in Lucchini and Thakkar's Lemma 12 is at most $r$, because the crown multiplicity is bounded by the chief length, at most $m$. If $u\ge r$, changing only the first $r$ slots suffices. If $u<r$, enumerate all changes in length $u$. If these all fail, the optimum is $u+1$ by Gaschütz's lemma and the bound $d(E)\le\max(2,d+1)$ used in their Corollary 13. Enumerate all changes in that length, which fits in $r$ slots. Testing an arbitrary lift of length $u$ is safe when $d<2$, since a group with a nonabelian chief factor cannot be cyclic. The record stores the length change and at most $r$ correction labels. The number of candidates is polynomial in $n$ since $b^r\le b^3m$. [Lucchini and Thakkar, Lemmas 1 and 12 and Corollary 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.07633)

The accounting uses at most $O(\log b+\log\log(n+2))$ bits of record per chief factor, because $r\log b\le3\log b+\log m$. The sum of $\log b$ is $\log n$ and there are at most $\log_2 n$ factors. Store the record in consecutive fields whose bit widths are derived from the implicit chief scan. Thus there is no need to store all chief-subgroup endpoints. For any requested slot, stream one pass through the existing record, recomputing the chief steps in order. Maintain a single current group element, take its least lift in the next quotient, and apply the recorded factor element if the slot is changed. At intermediate stages the factor and quotient labels use only a fixed-depth implicit table interface back to the given table. The candidate currently being tested occupies one more record field. Each step has polynomially many candidates, and every generation test and query of the partially constructed tuple takes polynomial time by the fixed-depth table scans. The entire record and current candidate fit the stated space bound. Output the final tuple one coordinate at a time by scanning the final record. Identity and repeated elements cannot occur in a tuple of minimum length.

## Combined resources

Every nonterminal recursive call decreases the logarithm of the group order by a factor at most $7/8$, measured in the ambient current table group $H$ before its Frattini quotient. The sum of the logarithms of all current table orders, including the intermediate quotients, is $O(\log(N+1))$. The active transducer and virtual-table frames therefore use $O(\log(N+1))$ bits outside the terminal record. In the terminal table $|E|\le B^2$, so its record uses $O(\log(B+1)\log\log(B+2))$ bits. This establishes the claimed workspace.

For time, view a local computation at order $h$ with unit-cost tuple and table queries. Each lifting or quotient-table routine uses at most $h^{O(1)}$ time in that model by the local logspace bound, with fixed uniform exponents. The terminal record routine also takes $h^{O(1)}$ time by its explicit scan bound. Across calls to smaller groups the products of the $h^{O(1)}$ bounds remain polynomial in $N$ because the sum of their logarithms is $O(\log(N+1))$. A virtual-table query follows the chain in the opposite direction only through table callbacks, without tuple calls, so its entire cost has another such polynomial bound. This proves polynomial time even when the terminal record exceeds logarithmic space.
