# The minimum generator number of arbitrary Cayley-table groups in deterministic logspace

**Theorem.** A deterministic logarithmic-space transducer computes $d(G)$, the minimum number of generators of any finite group $G$ given by its Cayley table. In particular the decision problem $d(G)\le k$ is in $\mathsf L$, answering the decision version of Question 8.1 of Collins–Grochow–Levet–Weiß. The proof gives a transducer for the number $d(G)$. [Question 8.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00133#S8)

Write $N=|G|$. A table element or counter of polynomial magnitude uses $O(\log N)$ bits. Given a logspace-enumerable generating set, subgroup membership is undirected reachability from the identity in the Cayley graph with generators and their inverses. Here and below, nested algorithms have fixed composition depth. This allows logspace transducers to supply on-demand table and graph entries to one another. Cosets of a logspace-decidable subgroup are named by their least element, so one can scan quotient elements and evaluate their multiplication in logspace. From Reingold's search algorithm one can also stream a word over the generators and inverses giving any element in the generated subgroup. An edge in the reported walk can be converted to a generator label by scanning the available generators. [Reingold, *Undirected Connectivity in Log-Space*](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/sl.pdf)

Two general scans will be used.

- Order the elements of a group $F$ by label, and choose an element precisely when it is outside the subgroup generated by all earlier elements. The chosen elements generate and number at most $\lfloor\log_2|F|\rfloor$. Each test uses the unfiltered earlier prefix as its implicitly supplied generating set. Thus this sequence is logspace-enumerable, including in a quotient with a logspace multiplication interface.
- For each $x\in G$ let $N_x$ be its normal closure, generated by all $gxg^{-1}$. Test membership by the preceding reachability operation. The cardinality $|N_x|$ is computable by scanning the table. Scan $x$ in increasing order of the pair $(|N_x|,\text{label}(x))$. Before processing $x$, the prefix subgroup $K$ is generated by all conjugates of elements preceding $x$, and the next subgroup is $K N_x$. Both are normal and support logspace membership, using a fixed nesting of two reachability operations. If $K N_x>K$, this is a chief step. Indeed, if $K<L<K N_x$ were normal, choose $y\in L\setminus K$ and write $y=kb$ with $k\in K$ and $b\in N_x$. Then $b\in L\setminus K$ and $N_b\le L\cap N_x<N_x$. Hence $b$ was scanned earlier and is already in $K$, a contradiction. This yields a chief series whose individual levels can be recomputed from the endpoints of their defining prefixes.

Process this chief series from top to bottom. In one step write the current group as $E=G/K$ and $A=(KN_x)/K$, where $A$ is the minimal normal subgroup for this step. The previous value $d(F)$ for $F=E/A$ is stored as one counter. Only the endpoints of the current prefix are needed for all group operations. The remaining chief factors of $E$ are computed from the corresponding suffix of the original chief series. This permits a scan within any step without storing the sequence of quotient tables.

## 2. Computing the abelian increment in logspace

Let $A$ be abelian, of order $p^s$. Any proper subgroup mapping onto $F$ is a complement. For any fixed generating $d$-tuple of $F$, a complement contains exactly one lift of this tuple, and distinct complements give distinct lifts, since the lifted tuple generates that complement. There are $|A|^d$ total lifts and therefore the step increases $d=d(F)$ precisely when the number of complements is $|A|^d$. In that case one additional generator suffices. Add a nonidentity element of $A$ to any lifts of the quotient tuple, and the subgroup produced intersects $A$ nontrivially and maps onto $F$, so it equals $E$ by minimal normality. The computation needs only $d$, without an explicit generating $d$-tuple.

Compute the number of complements using a system on a short generating list of the quotient. Let $g_1,\dots,g_\ell$ be the greedily chosen generating tuple of $F$ from Section 1. Choose the least lift of each $g_i$. Fix for each $q\in F$ the streamed word $w_q$ supplied by undirected reachability in the Cayley graph of this tuple. Use the empty word for the identity and the one-letter word for a selected generator. A candidate section is specified by $\ell$ unknown elements of $A$, one correcting the chosen lift of each $g_i$. Evaluate $w_q$ at the corrected lifts to define a map $\widehat q$ from $F$ to $E$. The equations
$$
\widehat q\,\widehat r=\widehat{qr}\qquad(q,r\in F)
$$
give a complement precisely when they all hold. Every complement arises uniquely, since a section is determined by its generator lifts. In additive $A$-coordinates these are affine-linear equations in at most $s\ell=O((\log N)^2)$ scalar variables. To see linearity, write elements using a fixed quotient section and an additive correction in the abelian kernel. In those coordinates multiplication has the form $(a,q)(b,r)=(a+q\mathbin{\cdot}b+c(q,r),qr)$ for a fixed cocycle $c$. Apply this identity at each letter of a word. The quotient coordinate at each prefix is fixed, and both multiplication and inversion with fixed quotient coordinates are affine-linear in the variables recording the corrections. Equivalently one may express each equation as the $A$-element $\widehat q\widehat r(\widehat{qr})^{-1}$ and demand that it be zero.

Every scalar coefficient of the resulting affine matrix can be computed in logarithmic space. Choose a basis for the elementary abelian group $A$ by the same prefix reachability test from Section 1, applied inside $A$. Its coordinate words can be enumerated by base-$p$ counters from $0$ to $|A|-1$, and a coordinate of any supplied element is obtained by scanning those words and comparing group elements. The prime $p$ is the order of any nonidentity element of $A$. For the constant term of a requested matrix row evaluate the corresponding word products with all lift corrections zero. For a variable coefficient, repeat with one generator changed by one basis element of $A$ and subtract the constant coordinate. Each word is evaluated by holding its current product in $E$ as one group-element label while streaming its letters. This uses only fixed compositions of logspace scans.

The following linear-algebra observation completes the space estimate. Suppose a matrix $M$ over $\mathbb F_p$ has $R\le N^{O(1)}$ rows and $D\le(\log N)^{O(1)}$ columns, $p\le N^{O(1)}$, and logspace-computable individual entries. Its rank is computable in $O(\log N)$ space. For $t,u,z$ indeterminates put
$$
P(t,u,z)=\det\bigl(I_D+zD_D(u)M^{\mathsf T}D_R(t)M\bigr),
\qquad
D_a(v)=\operatorname{diag}(v,v^2,\dots,v^a).
$$
By the expansion in principal minors and Cauchy–Binet, the coefficient of $z^j$ is
$$
\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq[R],\,J\subseteq[D]\\ |I|=|J|=j}}
\det(M[I,J])^2\,t^{\sum_{i\in I}i}\,u^{\sum_{j'\in J}j'}.
$$
Take $j=\operatorname{rank}(M)$. Among nonzero terms, the lexicographically least pair of exponents in $(t,u)$ occurs exactly once. The feasible row subsets $I$ are the bases of the row matroid and have a unique minimum weight basis for distinct index weights $i$. For that fixed $I$, the feasible column subsets are the bases of the column matroid of $M[I,*]$ and have a unique minimum for the distinct weights $j'$. Uniqueness follows from the greedy basis theorem, or by exchanging the lightest element of the symmetric difference into any other minimum basis and removing a heavier element. The nonzero squared determinant of the least term survives in every characteristic. All $z^j$ coefficients for larger $j$ vanish. Hence the desired rank is the maximum degree in $z$ obtained by specializing $t,u$ over any extension field of size greater than $\max\{RD,D^2,p\}$. The degree bounds in $t$ and $u$ and the univariate root bound in succession ensure some grid point preserves the top coefficient.

Take the least extension degree $a$ with $p^a>\max\{RD,D^2,p\}$. Its order is polynomial in $N$. In logarithmic space one can construct an irreducible monic polynomial of degree $a$ by scanning monic candidates and testing divisibility by every monic polynomial of smaller degree at most $\lfloor a/2\rfloor$. Each polynomial uses $O(a\log p)=O(\log N)$ bits, and the scans have polynomial length. For each $(t,u)$ in the extension grid a single matrix entry of $D_D(u)M^{\mathsf T}D_R(t)M$ is a sum of $R$ products and can be streamed, with the accumulator and table methods taking logarithmic space. For this small $D\times D$ matrix, use the uniform Berkowitz circuit to compute the coefficients of $\det(I_D+zB)$, which are signed reindexings of the characteristic coefficients of $B$. The Boolean circuit for this computation has size polynomial in $\log N$ and depth polynomial in $\log\log N$. Indeed Berkowitz uses a polynomial-size, polylogarithmic-depth fan-in-two field-operation circuit. Operations in the polynomial-basis field have uniform Boolean circuits of polylogarithmic depth in their own input length. Multiply the coefficient polynomials by convolution, and reduce modulo the monic polynomial by reversal and truncated power-series inversion. If the reversed divisor is $1+h$, use $1-h+\cdots+(-h)^{a-1}$ modulo $t^a$ at precision $a$. The truncated powers are computed by repeated squaring and balanced products, and the result is reversed to obtain the quotient and remainder. This uses only convolutions of degrees polynomial in the input length. Addition, multiplication and modular reduction on prime-field coefficients have uniform bounded-fan-in polylogarithmic-depth circuits by the standard uniform integer arithmetic results. Use the small Boolean circuit, so recursive evaluation keeps bits and gate identifiers only. The evaluation stack costing $(\log\log N)^{O(1)}=O(\log N)$ is combined with the streaming computation of one input bit at a time. No matrix of logarithmically many field elements is stored. The outer $(t,u)$ scan keeps the largest degree encountered. This proves the rank bound. [Berkowitz, *On computing the determinant in small parallel time using a small number of processors*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(84)90018-8)

Apply this rank algorithm to the coefficient and augmented matrices of the implicit affine system. If their ranks differ, there is no complement and every lift succeeds. If they agree and the common coefficient rank is $r$, the proportion test uses exactly $p^{s\ell-r}$ complements. Compare exponents $s\ell-r$ and $sd$, both at most $O((\log N)^2)$, by counters. Thus the abelian chief step is in deterministic logspace.

## 3. Computing the nonabelian increment in logspace

We use Lucchini–Thakkar's chief-factor lift bound and the crown generation criterion. Their Lemma 12 implies that for a nonabelian minimal normal subgroup $A$ of $E$, writing $b=|A|$, $\delta=\delta_E(A)$ for the crown multiplicity, and $d=d(E/A)$, the increment is zero whenever $d\ge2$ and
$$
d\ge\left\lceil\frac85+\log_b \delta\right\rceil.
$$
Their Corollary 13 and the generation bound it cites give $d(E)\le\max\{2,d+1\}$. Therefore for $d=0,1$ the answer is 2, since $E$ has a nonabelian subgroup. For the remaining case $d\ge2$, let $m=\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil$, an upper bound on $\delta$. If $b^2\ge m^5$, then $\log_b\delta\le2/5$ and the increment is zero. If $d\ge2+\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$, the increment is also zero, since $b\ge2$. These tests use polynomial-sized integer counters. Every case still to be considered therefore has $b\le(\log N)^{O(1)}$ and $d=O(\log\log N)$. [Lucchini–Thakkar, §6, Lemma 12 and Corollary 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.07633#page=8)

In the remaining case, compute the exact $\delta$ by scanning the chief factors of $E$. It counts non-Frattini chief factors $E$-equivalent to $A$, and every nonabelian chief factor is non-Frattini. Filter for order $b$ and nonabelian factors. An isomorphism $f:A\to B$ between such factors is specified by the images of the $O(\log b)$ greedily chosen generators of $A$, with the elements of $B$ stored using their ranks in its enumerated table. Test such a code by evaluating on logspace words, checking all products, and checking bijectivity with table scans. The code uses $O((\log b)^2)$ bits. This exhaustively tests all isomorphisms in logarithmic space in $N$ under the small-$b$ bound. [Crown multiplicity and chief factors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.05400)

For a candidate $f$, let $\alpha_g$ and $\beta_g$ be the automorphisms of $A$ and $B$ induced by each $g\in E$. The factors are $E$-equivalent exactly when there is an $f$ for which
$$
f\alpha_g f^{-1}=c_{v_g}\beta_g\qquad(g\in E)
$$
for some $v_g\in B$, with $c_v$ denoting conjugation by $v$. This criterion is tested by enumerating $v_g$ separately for each $g$ and comparing the two maps on generators. Since $B$ is centerless, $v_g$ is unique whenever it exists. Taking products then gives $v_{gh}=v_g\beta_g(v_h)$. Therefore $(a,g)\mapsto(f(a)v_g,g)$ defines the requisite isomorphism of semidirect products commuting with projection onto $E$. Conversely any isomorphism commuting with that projection restricts to an $f$ and has this form on $(1,g)$, so the criterion is exact.

Let $L$ be the conjugation image of $E$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(A)$. The embedded copy of $A$ is $\operatorname{Inn}(A)$. An element of $L$ can be encoded by its images on the $O(\log b)$ generators of $A$, each using $O(\log b)$ bits. One can test a code and recover the least representative $g\in E$ inducing that action just by scanning the table. Products and equivalence modulo $\operatorname{Inn}(A)$ are also decidable by table scans. In this encoding a $d$-tuple uses $O((\log\log N)^3)$ bits. Such tuples can be enumerated explicitly, testing generation of $L$ or $L/A$ by Cayley graph reachability with on-demand adjacency. To construct a fixed generating $d$-tuple of $L/A$, scan the $d$-tuples in $L$ until finding one generating modulo $A$. Such a tuple exists because $L/A$ is a quotient of $E/A$.

Let $\Gamma$ be the automorphisms of $L$ inducing the identity on $L/A$. Restriction to $A$ is injective: an automorphism fixing $A$ pointwise must fix the conjugation action of every element of $L$, and $L$ is embedded faithfully in $\operatorname{Aut}(A)$. A candidate $f\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)$ gives such an automorphism of $L$ exactly when $f\ell f^{-1}\ell^{-1}\in\operatorname{Inn}(A)$ for every $\ell\in L$. This condition implies that conjugation by $f$ normalizes $L$ and gives the required trivial action on the quotient. Conversely an automorphism of $L$ with restriction $f$ must act by that conjugation, from its effect on $A$. Enumerate these $f$ using the same short generator-image codes as above.

Among the generating $d$-tuples of $L$ lifting the fixed quotient tuple, count the $\Gamma$-orbits, stopping at $\delta$. This is done by scanning tuples, accepting only the lex least code among all their images under the enumerated $\Gamma$. The tuple codes are unique because generator images determine the action. Each group scan and its counters still fit in logarithmic space. By the crown criterion, the crown-based power $L_\delta$ is $d$-generated exactly when the count reaches $\delta$. Indeed, to generate the crown one needs generating tuples in every coordinate with the same quotient tuple and pairwise distinct $\Gamma$-orbits. If a generating tuple exists over another quotient tuple, the ordinary Gaschütz lifting lemma synchronizes it with the fixed one. [Lucchini–Thakkar, §3, Lemmas 1 and 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.07633#page=2)

Finally $d(E)=\max\{d(E/A),d(L_\delta)\}$. For completeness let $R=R_E(A)$ be the crown kernel, with $E/R\simeq L_\delta$. The lower bound is immediate from the two quotients. For the upper bound, pad a generating tuple for $E/A$ to the larger of the two generator numbers. Its image generates $E/(AR)$. Gaschütz's lemma chooses a generating tuple for $E/R$ of that same length with this prescribed image in $E/(AR)$. The pair of tuples lifts coordinatewise to $E$, since the natural map to the fiber product of $E/A$ and $E/R$ over $E/(AR)$ is onto. This surjectivity is the elementary compatibility of two normal-subgroup coset equations. The subgroup generated by the joint lift surjects to both quotients, so Lucchini–Thakkar's Lemma 7 forces it to be $E$. Thus the count of $\Gamma$-orbits decides whether the current $d$ suffices. If it does not, $d+1$ is the answer by the cited bound. This completes the nonabelian step and the whole logspace computation. [Lucchini–Thakkar, §4, Lemmas 7 and 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.07633#page=7)

The logarithmic-space result combines the chief-factor generation theorem, implicit Cayley reachability, and determinant coefficients of narrow matrices. All bounds concern explicit Cayley tables. This proof is informal. Publication priority is unestablished.
