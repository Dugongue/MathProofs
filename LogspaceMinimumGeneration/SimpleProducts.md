# Minimum generating sets in logspace for products of nonabelian simple groups

**Theorem.** For an explicit Cayley-table group $Q$ that is a direct product of nonabelian finite simple groups, a deterministic logarithmic-space transducer outputs a minimum generating set. For a prescribed $d\ge d(Q)$ it can also output a generating $d$-tuple in $O(\log(|Q|+2)+\log(d+1))$ space.

## Factor and isomorphism access

An element's normal closure is computable by Cayley-graph reachability on its conjugates. Among nontrivial principal normal closures, the inclusion-minimal ones are all the minimal normal subgroups, because any nontrivial normal subgroup contains a nontrivial principal normal closure. In a direct product of nonabelian simple groups, these are precisely the direct factors, and they commute. To see the first assertion, a normal subgroup projecting nontrivially to a factor $S$ contains a commutator with an element supported at $S$, since $S$ is centerless. This yields nontrivial intersection with $S$, hence contains $S$ by simplicity. Enumerate each minimal normal by its first normal-closure witness in table order. Element ranks local to a factor can be translated to and from table labels by scanning membership. These procedures use a fixed nesting of deterministic logarithmic-space scans and undirected reachability. [Reingold](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/sl.pdf)

Every nonabelian finite simple group has a generating pair. Choose the first pair in the table order that passes the ordinary reachability generation test. For two factors $S,T$ of equal order, an isomorphism is specified by the two images of that generating pair. For a proposed image pair, evaluate each $x\in S$ using a word in the original pair, obtained by search in the undirected Cayley graph. The induced map is a real isomorphism if and only if it is bijective and preserves every table product. Those checks are logspace scans, and the word is evaluated by retaining just its current target-group element. Equal orders plus injectivity also suffice once all products hold. Thus we can enumerate the isomorphism types, name each by its first factor, count its multiplicity, and choose a fixed isomorphism from that first factor to each copy using logarithmic space. The factor count and recognition bits may be streamed. The same procedure enumerates every automorphism of any factor without duplicates. [Aschbacher and Guralnick, two-generation of finite simple groups](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(84)90183-2)

## Local search and assembly

In a product of nonabelian simple groups, a tuple generates exactly when each factor projection generates and no two component tuples are related by an isomorphism. For a proof, a subgroup with all projections onto is subdirect. Induct on the number of factors, assuming all previous factors already have full product projection. The kernel of this projection is normal in the last factor, since the subgroup surjects onto that factor, and is thus either trivial or the whole factor. If it is the whole factor we have the full product. If it is trivial, the subgroup is the graph of a surjective homomorphism from the earlier product to the last factor. The images of the earlier factors are normal and commute, so exactly one map is an isomorphism onto the last nonabelian simple factor and the rest are trivial. This produces an identified pair. Conversely such a pair excludes full generation.

Consider an isomorphism type with representative $S$ and multiplicity $m$. For any generating pair $(a,b)$ of $S$, the tuples $(a,b,z_1,\ldots,z_k)$ lie in distinct $\operatorname{Aut}(S)$-orbits as the suffix varies. An automorphism fixing both $a$ and $b$ is the identity. Taking $k=\lceil \log_{|S|}m\rceil$ therefore gives an elementary upper bound $d(S^m)\le 2+k$. For a requested tuple length $d\ge d(Q)$ define $\ell=\min(d,2+k)$. We have $\ell\ge d(S^m)$. A candidate length-$\ell$ tuple uses at most $O(\ell\log |S|)=O(\log |S|+\log m)=O(\log |Q|)$ bits. Test generation with reachability and retain only the lexicographically first tuple in each automorphism orbit by testing every automorphism as above. There are at least $m$ orbits of generating tuples. Neither a list of these orbits nor the resulting component tuples is stored. To query position $j$ in a given factor, recompute the first $m$ orbit representatives by a single scan with a rank counter, take the representative indexed by the factor's index within its type, use its $j$th coordinate or the identity when $j>\ell$, and apply the chosen isomorphism onto that factor. Computing $k$ uses a capped-power loop and is logarithmic-space.

For each slot $j$, multiply the queried component elements across all factors, retaining only the accumulated product in $Q$. The criterion above proves that the streamed length-$d$ tuple generates $Q$. One may compute the minimum $d$ without the general value algorithm: scan $d$ from zero through $\lfloor\log_2|Q|\rfloor$ and use the same orbit scans to test whether all factors have enough orbit representatives, truncating the count at $m$. This test remains in logarithmic space for all trial $d$, including zero. Generation of the trivial product has minimum size zero. For nontrivial products, a minimum-length generating tuple cannot contain an identity or duplicate. Thus the output is a minimum set. [Earlier polynomial-time simple-product algorithm, Das–Thakkar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08405)

## With a solvable radical

The product theorem can be used with a relative logspace lifting theorem for solvable normal subgroups. In an arbitrary Cayley table, the solvable radical is logspace-accessible. An element is in the radical if and only if its normal closure is solvable. Test solvability by constructing the chief series inside the normal closure using the order-sorted principal-normal-closure scan and checking that all factors are abelian. The normal closure's own table entries are logspace-computable, so these routines have fixed composition depth. When $G/\operatorname{Rad}(G)$ is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups, compute $d(G)$ using the generator-number algorithm, apply the product algorithm at length $d(G)$, then use the relative lifting theorem. The resulting tuple in $G$ gives an actual minimum generating set.
