# Logspace lifting through the solvable radical

**Theorem.** Let $G$ be a finite group given by its Cayley table, and let $R$ be its solvable radical. Given a generating tuple for $G/R$ of length $d\ge d(G)$, with $d=O(\log N)$ and $N\ge |G|$, a deterministic transducer using $O(\log(N+1))$ workspace constructs a generating tuple for $G$ of the same length. This permits minimum-generator search for arbitrary Cayley-table groups using minimum-generator search on groups with trivial solvable radical. The final algorithm takes $N=|G|$ and pads an optimal quotient tuple to $d(G)$ before lifting. The length $d(G)$ is available from the accompanying minimum-generator-number algorithm. All proofs here are informal.

We use the chief-series and tall narrow rank operations of [Minimum generator number](MinimumGeneratorNumber.md), and the computation of the Frattini subgroup of solvable groups and the Fitting quotient size bound in [Solvable groups](SolvableGroups.md). Space always refers to workspace with a read-only input and a write-only output. Quotient elements supplied by a subroutine are queried by coordinate.

## A lift with small suspended workspace

Let $A$ be an abelian normal subgroup of $E$, with each Sylow subgroup elementary abelian. Put $Q=E/A$. We show that, to compute one coordinate of a generating lift of $q=(q_1,\ldots,q_d)$ with $d\ge d(E)$ and $d=O(\log N)$, each call requesting a coordinate of $q$ suspends only $O(\log(|A|+1)+(\log\log(N+4))^C)$ bits for an absolute constant $C$. Between such calls the computation may use $O(\log(N+1))$ workspace. All groups in the construction have order at most $N$.

For each primary part of $A$ take its module radical, the intersection of its maximal $Q$-submodules. Their product $J$ has the property that every subgroup $H\le E$ with $HA=E$ and $HJ=E$ equals $E$. In fact $H\cap A$ is $Q$-invariant and, if proper, lies in a maximal $Q$-submodule of $A$. That submodule contains $J$, so $(H\cap A)J=A$ is impossible. We can work in $E/J$ and lift the resulting tuple arbitrarily. To compute $J$, use the dual of each elementary abelian primary part. Enumerate its linear functionals by their values on an implicit additive basis. Test each nonzero functional for generating a simple module by taking the span of its conjugates and checking that the span of the conjugates of each nonzero vector in it is the whole same span. Additive spans are implicit Cayley reachability tests, and the dual action is computed by evaluation on a basis. The common annihilator of all these simple modules is precisely $J$, since annihilators exchange simple dual submodules and simple quotients of the original module. Thus $J$ membership and the quotient table are available in logarithmic space without requesting a coordinate of $q$.

Now suppose $A$ is semisimple. Choose the canonical direct product decomposition into minimal modules and the identifications of each isotypic part $S^m$ from [the solvable proof](SolvableGroups.md). These operations apply to any acting group. Write $D=\operatorname{End}_Q(S)$, a finite field, and call a $Q$-linear map $\lambda:A\to S$ split if the extension induced along $\lambda$ has a section. Choose a fixed arbitrary set section $s:Q\to E$, with $s(1)=1$, independent of $q$. With left additive coordinates on $A$, its cocycle $c(u,v)=s(u)s(v)s(uv)^{-1}$ gives the induced extension $S\times Q$ with law

$$
(a,u)(b,v)=(a+u b+\lambda(c(u,v)),uv).
$$

The split maps form a $D$-subspace of $\operatorname{Hom}_Q(A,S)$, since the condition that $\lambda c$ be a coboundary is $D$-linear. The zero map is included. A nonzero $\lambda$ is onto and the induced extension is $E/\ker\lambda$. Enumerate the maps by their $D^m$ coefficients, using at most $\log|A|$ bits each. Splitting is tested using the affine section equations on a fixed greedy generating tuple of $Q$. A candidate section chooses only its values on that tuple. Streamed words and the pairs $(u,v)$ give the affine equations for these choices, using only polynomially many rows and polylogarithmically many columns. Rank tests decide splitting. A prefix-span scan over the split maps gives a $D$-basis $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r$. Both enumeration and the scan are independent of $q$.

For each $\lambda_i$, choose a section $(b_i(u),u)$ using a canonical solution in generator coordinates. Choose a greedy column basis of the coefficient matrix, set the off-basis variables to zero, and obtain each basis coefficient by testing membership of the residual in the span of all the other basis columns. This supplies one solution coordinate at a time without storing earlier choices. Evaluate a section at an arbitrary $u$ by its fixed streamed word. Use the same construction to obtain an implicit basis of $Z^1(Q,S)$, the derivations from $Q$ to $S$. For each nonbasis column of its homogeneous equation matrix, set that variable to one and the other nonbasis variables to zero, and solve for the basis coordinates. These elementary solutions form an $\mathbb F_p$-basis of the solution space. As with the sections, this uses the fixed greedy tuple of $Q$ and makes no query to $q$ until an argument $u=q_j$ is needed for evaluation.

The failure test for a lift $(a_j,q_j)_{j=1}^d$ is now local to each isotypic part. The subgroup generated by a lift surjects onto $Q$. If it is proper, its intersection with $A$ lies in the kernel of a nonzero $Q$-map $\lambda:A\to S$. Its projection into $E/\ker\lambda$ is then a complement, so $\lambda$ is split. Write $\lambda=\sum_i z_i\lambda_i$ with $z_i\in D$. A section of this induced extension differs from the section with coordinates $\sum_i z_i b_i(u)$ by a derivation. Therefore the lift generates if and only if, for every component $S$, the $r$ vectors

$$
\bigl(\lambda_i(a_j)-b_i(q_j)\bigr)_{j=1}^d\in S^d
$$

are independent over $D$ modulo the evaluations of $Z^1(Q,S)$ at $q$. The converse failure direction follows by using that nonzero linear combination and its derivation to exhibit a complement containing the projected tuple. The required quotient dimension is at least $r$, since Gaschütz's lemma gives a generating lift of the prescribed $q$ at length $d$. [Gaschütz's lemma](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.07633#page=2)

Choose $r$ ordinary $\mathbb F_p$ coordinate unit vectors in $S^d$ by greedy prefix independence over $D$ modulo evaluated derivations. Rank tests use the narrow evaluation matrix whose columns are the implicit derivation basis, together with $D$-multiples of prefix coordinate vectors as in the solvable proof. The matrices at this stage have **both** dimensions polylogarithmic in $|G|$. For each slot $j$, choose the least $a_j$ giving the prescribed values under every $\lambda_i$, namely $b_i(q_j)$ plus the selected coordinate-unit entry. Such an $a_j$ exists by independence of the split-map basis over $D$ and directness of the distinct isotypic parts. Enumerate $a_j$ in compact rank order inside $A$. To emit the actual lift, compute the correction first, then query $q_j$ and multiply that correction by the chosen representative. The section, the bases of split maps and derivations, and the actual lift representatives are all recomputed on demand.

Here is the workspace distinction on calls to $q$. Evaluating $b_i(q_j)$ or an individual derivation at $q_j$ first requests $q_j$, keeping only local indices and coefficients. Full-size ambient labels are obtained and used after that call returns. During a rank test using evaluations at $q$, view the Vandermonde-weighted determinant test of the minimum-generator-number algorithm as a Boolean circuit on the bits of its matrix. The matrix has polylogarithmic dimensions, and a field element has at most $O(\log |G|)$ bits. The extension degree can be chosen using the polynomial-in-the-matrix-dimensions grid bound in that argument. Its elements use $O(\log |A|+\log\log(|G|+4))$ bits. The Boolean circuits for the weighted matrix entries and determinant have polylogarithmic size and depth polynomial in $\log\log(|G|+4)$. A recursive gate evaluation keeps only a stack of gate identifiers and bits, and suspends at an input-bit query before computing its ambient group data. The grid scan and stored correction use only the field-size bound above and polylogarithmic matrix indices. Thus a suspended frame has the stated small size. Setup computations such as basis extraction for derivations use the tall narrow rank test with full local workspace but no $q$ queries. This separation applies also in $E/J$, using compact correction labels and lifting the final answer afterward.

## Composition through the radical

Membership in $R$ is computable in logarithmic space. An element lies in $R$ exactly when its principal normal closure is solvable. Test solvability by the abelian chief factors of that closure, using the chief-series algorithm on its implicit table. Thus all computations on $R$ can start from a compact virtual table. Build its tower by alternating removal of its Frattini subgroup and its Fitting subgroup after this Frattini quotient. The Fitting kernels at that point are abelian with elementary abelian primary parts. These subgroups are canonical, so each kernel in the original radical is characteristic in $R$ and normal in $G$. The logarithms of the orders at recursive stages in the tower of $R$ sum to $O(\log |R|)$ by the solvable proof. Nontrivial recursive stages therefore number $O(\log\log(|R|+4))$.

Use each kernel in the tower to form the corresponding ambient quotient of $G$. Ambient group arithmetic does not recursively go from one large ambient table to the next. For each quotient, take least representatives directly from $G$ and test equality modulo its kernel using the **radical's** compact quotient tower. Specifically, test membership in $R$, convert to a compact radical label, and evaluate the element's image in the corresponding radical quotient by the geometric stack of radical table queries. This uses $O(\log |G|)$ workspace, and it does not call any generating-tuple subroutine.

In the upward lifting of a generating tuple from $G/R$, each Frattini removal is lifted by choosing arbitrary representatives. For a normal subgroup $U$ of a finite group $H$, $\Phi(U)\le\Phi(H)$: otherwise some maximal subgroup $M$ of $H$ satisfies $M\Phi(U)=H$, and then $(M\cap U)\Phi(U)=U$, a contradiction. Each Fitting removal is lifted by the abelian procedure just proved. During each active recursive tuple call keep only the small suspended state described above. The logarithms of the kernel orders add to at most $\log |R|$, and the polyloglog overhead across the $O(\log\log(|G|+4))$ frames is $O(\log(|G|+1))$. At the bottom, query the supplied tuple on $G/R$, or call a logspace transducer for that quotient if one is available. At any one time at most one active frame needs the full additional workspace for table arithmetic and the independent rank calls. The whole stack is thus logarithmic. Every lift preserves length $d$, and the final tuple generates $G$. At the minimum length it contains no redundant entries. This proves the claimed reduction.
