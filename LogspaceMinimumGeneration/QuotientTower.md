# Logspace access to the alternating radical and socle tower

Let $G$ be a finite group of order $N$ given by its Cayley table. Starting at $K_0=1$, take in succession the full preimage of the solvable radical of $G/K_0$, the full preimage of the socle of the resulting radical-free quotient, and repeat. This gives a characteristic normal series. A level counter is sufficient to query any table $G/K_i$ in logarithmic workspace. The tables are accessed directly from the original table of $G$. The proof below does not recursively access a long sequence of large quotient tables.

## Two residual computations in the original table

For a subgroup $H$ presented by a compact virtual Cayley table, define its solvable residual $H_{\mathrm{sol}}$ to be the final term in the derived series. It is perfect, characteristic, and the smallest normal subgroup with solvable quotient. We compute it without constructing the derived series. Every finite perfect group $P$ is normally generated by one element. Indeed, take all maximal proper normal subgroups of $P$. Their quotients are nonabelian simple because $P$ is perfect. The diagonal map into their product has subdirect image, which is a direct product of nonabelian simples embedded by possibly repeating isomorphic factors diagonally. In this image choose a tuple nonidentity in every coordinate, which exists by the structure of the subdirect product. Lift it to $P$. Its normal closure cannot lie in a maximal proper normal subgroup, hence is all of $P$. The trivial case takes the identity. The subdirect product assertion follows by induction from the two-factor Goursat lemma. In the induction a quotient of an already-decomposed product can map onto a single simple factor only through one isomorphic simple component, since the images of the components are commuting normals in the simple target.

Apply this to $P=H_{\mathrm{sol}}$. A normal generator of $P$ under conjugation by $P$ has the same normal closure $P$ in $H$, since $P$ is normal in $H$. Conversely every perfect normal subgroup of $H$ maps to a perfect subgroup of the solvable quotient $H/H_{\mathrm{sol}}$, so it lies in $H_{\mathrm{sol}}$. Thus among all $H$-principal normal closures that are perfect, one of largest order is exactly $H_{\mathrm{sol}}$. For each candidate element, its normal closure $J$ is accessible in logspace by Cayley reachability with the conjugates as generators. Test perfection of $J$ by generation of $J$ by its commutators, another implicit Cayley reachability calculation. The compact candidates, the size counter and the fixed nested table interfaces take logarithmic space. This provides either membership in $H_{\mathrm{sol}}$ or a canonical normal generator of it.

Define $H_{\mathrm{ss}}$ as the intersection of the kernels of all epimorphisms from $H$ to nonabelian simple groups, with empty intersection $H$. Then $H/T$ is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups, including the empty product, if and only if $H_{\mathrm{ss}}\le T$ for $T\trianglelefteq H$. In one direction the component projections have kernels whose intersection is $T$. In the other direction $H/H_{\mathrm{ss}}$ is semisimple by the subdirect product assertion, and every quotient of a direct product of nonabelian simples is another such product.

There is a logarithmic-space description of the intersected kernels for $H_{\mathrm{ss}}$. Given an epimorphism to a simple group, the first nontrivial image of a term of an $H$-chief series is all of the target. The previous image is trivial, so the corresponding chief factor $D$ maps onto the target. The kernel of the restricted map is $H$-invariant and is trivial by chief minimality. Thus $D$ is nonabelian simple and conjugation on it is precisely conjugation by the images of $H$ under the epimorphism, transported by this isomorphism. Conversely, if a nonabelian chief factor $D$ is simple and all $H$ actions on it are inner, conjugation defines an epimorphism to $D$ itself, since all inner actions occur from the numerator of the chief factor and $D$ is centerless. Its kernel is the centralizer of this chief factor in $H$. To enumerate the kernels, scan the chief series. A factor is simple if it has no proper nontrivial principal normal closure in its own table. Test that every $h\in H$ acts by some inner automorphism by scanning factor labels and checking on each factor element. Test membership in the kernel by comparing conjugation with the identity. This gives membership in $H_{\mathrm{ss}}$ by a scan with fixed nesting depth. Chief series, principal normal closures and compact labels here are all computed on $H$ alone, without the tower.

For each of the two residual subgroups use its canonical greedy ordinary generating list. Enumerate elements in input label order, select an element when it is outside the subgroup generated by all earlier labels of the residual, and use undirected reachability on the unfiltered earlier labels. The list has length at most $\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor$, and a requested generator by ordinal or a test that the ordinal exceeds the length is a logspace computation. We will always use this residual algorithm on $H=\langle x^G\rangle$ for some explicit source element $x$. This $H$ has a compact virtual table accessed directly from $G$ via principal-normal-closure membership. Thus the residual-transition operation given $x$, a choice of residual, and an ordinal uses $O(\log(N+1))$ bits independently of the tower level.

## The membership recursion

For every $K\trianglelefteq G$, the normal closure of the image of $x$ in $G/K$ is the image of $H=\langle x^G\rangle$. This image is solvable exactly when $H_{\mathrm{sol}}\le K$. Since the image is normal, this is equivalent to membership of $xK$ in the solvable radical of $G/K$.

If $G/K$ is radical-free, its socle is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups. An element is in this socle exactly when its normal closure is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups. In one direction the normal closure is normal in the socle and is therefore such a product. In the other direction the ambient action permutes the simple components of the normal closure, and each orbit product is an ambient minimal normal subgroup. Thus for a socle step membership of $xK$ is equivalent to $H_{\mathrm{ss}}\le K$.

Consequently a query $x\in K_i$ reduces to testing $y\in K_{i-1}$ for every generator $y$ in a residual list determined by $i$ and $x$. The base query $K_0$ is equality with the identity. To implement a depth-first evaluation, store the original query element $x_0$ once. Each active frame stores only which child ordinal it is currently testing. The type of residual at a depth is fixed by the parity of the level counter. Whenever an ambient element label is required at one of the frames, replay the sequence of earlier residual-transition ordinals starting from $x_0$, replacing the current label by the resulting child at each transition. Each transition uses one local logspace computation independent of the tower. No previous ambient labels are retained. A frame performs the usual depth-first control with termination when its final child has passed or a failed child returns. If it is the base level, it uses the replayed label directly. This computes membership in $O(\log(N+1)+i\log\log(N+4))$ workspace.

## Number of levels and ambient table queries

For the bound on $i$, put $h=\log_2 |E|$ at the start of a round on the current quotient $E$. If its radical removal removes at least $h/2$ of log order, this round reduces $h$ to at most $h/2$. Otherwise the radical-free quotient $U$ has log order at least $h/2$. Its socle $S$ is self-centralizing. Indeed, the centralizer is normal with trivial intersection with the centerless socle. If the centralizer were nontrivial, it would contain a minimal normal subgroup of $U$, which is contained in the socle, a contradiction. Thus $U$ embeds by conjugation in $\operatorname{Aut}(S)$. A finite group of order $|S|$ has a generating set of size at most $\log_2 |S|$, so $|\operatorname{Aut}(S)|\le |S|^{\log_2|S|}$. Therefore $\log_2 |S|\ge\sqrt{h/2}$, and the socle removal decreases $h$ by at least this amount. For $h\ge1$, either kind of round decreases $\sqrt h$ by an absolute positive constant. There are $O(\sqrt{\log(N+1)})$ rounds and twice as many levels, including possibly equal consecutive subgroups. The ordinal stack is $O(\sqrt{\log(N+1)}\log\log(N+4))=O(\log(N+1))$. The empty group stage is recognized by testing that every label belongs to its kernel, in a table scan. All computations terminate by strict descent of the query level or by a finite scan.

For a table of $G/K_i$, name a coset by the least source label, or by the rank of that label among coset representatives. Test equality of cosets using membership of an element quotient in $K_i$. Translations between ranks and least labels, subgroup size, the table product and inverse, and membership of a subsequent level kernel are ordinary source-table scans composed with the membership test. Every such operation uses only the original table and the membership test. The running auxiliary label or counter outside the membership test uses another $O(\log(N+1))$ bits. Thus each table query, kernel query, or level search is in deterministic logarithmic space. This also implies polynomial time, since these are terminating fixed machines with read-only input and logarithmic workspace.

The same source-rank interface works for intermediate quotients of an individual radical layer used in the simultaneous abelian lifting proof. Use the just-established $G/K_i$ table to create the radical table of that level. Compute its own Frattini/Fitting tower on compact radical labels with geometric bounds. Membership of an ambient label in an intermediate kernel is then a single query on that radical tower after mapping the label into $G/K_i$ and compactly ranking its image in the radical. This uses a geometric radical-quotient label stack, the upper tower ordinal stack, and a fixed number of ambient source labels. None of this requires suspending a full ambient label per upper-tower level.
