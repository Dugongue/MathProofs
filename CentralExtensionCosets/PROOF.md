# Group isomorphism through central extension cosets

**Status.** AI-assisted informal proof, not Lean-verified or independently peer-reviewed. The contribution is a combination of finite-ring unit orbits, subgroup transport in finite abelian groups, and central extension cohomology. Publication priority is not established.

## Main results

**Theorem A.** Fix a constant $d$. Let $G,H$ be finite Cayley-table groups with supplied central subgroups $A,A'$, and let $\alpha:G/A\to H/A'$ be a quotient isomorphism. Supply permutation generators for $P\le\operatorname{Aut}(G/A)$, whose nonabelian composition factors embed in $S_d$. In deterministic polynomial time one can compute the entire coset of isomorphisms $G\to H$ carrying $A$ to $A'$ and inducing a quotient map in $\alpha P$, or decide it is empty. The output includes a witness, generators for the source automorphism group of the coset, and its exact size.

In particular $P$ may be any solvable permutation group, however large its order. Only its generating set is supplied; its elements are not listed. All polynomial bounds are in the explicit group-table input size, with $d$ fixed.

This replaces the supplied *single* quotient map in the earlier [abelian-extension encoding](AbelianExtensionLinearization.md) with an entire permitted coset, for central kernels.

**Theorem B.** Let $G,H$ be arbitrary finite groups of nilpotency class at most two, given by Cayley tables, of common order $n$. After rejecting unequal central quotient types, write
$$
Q=G/Z(G)\cong\bigoplus_{p,e}(C_{p^e})^{m_{p,e}}.
$$
There is a deterministic isomorphism algorithm, including witnesses, full cosets and exact counts, with running time
$$
n^{O(1)}\,\prod_{p,e}[\operatorname{GL}_{m_{p,e}}(\mathbb F_p):B_{m_{p,e}}(\mathbb F_p)]
\ \le\ n^{O(1)}\prod_{p,e}p^{\binom{m_{p,e}}2},
\tag{1}
$$
where $B_m$ is the invertible upper-triangular group. No quotient map, flag, generating tuple, or decomposition is supplied as auxiliary data.

Consequently the algorithm is polynomial-time for every fixed bound on the multiplicities $m_{p,e}$, with no bound on the number of distinct exponents, the total generator rank, the center, or the primes. More generally it is polynomial whenever
$$
\sum_{p,e}\binom{m_{p,e}}2\log_2 p=O(\log n)
\tag{2}
$$
with a fixed implied constant. The exponent values $e$ do not appear in the exponential factor in (1).

Theorem B is an input-sensitive bound for all class-two groups, not a polynomial algorithm for the entire class. It is not asserted to improve the best unrestricted worst-case bound. The paper *Faster Isomorphism for Groups of Class 2*, submitted in November 2026, gives a different general bound; that result must not be presented as superseded by (1). [Recent class-two paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05920).

## 1. Eliminating the coefficient automorphism group

We first use a finite-ring fact.

**Lemma 1.** For a finite unital ring $R$, a left $R$-module $M$, and $x,y\in M$,
$$
Rx=Ry\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y=ux\text{ for some }u\in R^\times.
\tag{3}
$$
The module itself need not be finite for this statement.

The reverse implication is immediate. For the forward implication choose $a,b\in R$ with $y=ax$ and $x=by$. Put $c=1-ba$. Then $cx=0$ and $ba+c=1$. We show there is $t\in R$ such that $u=a+tc$ is a unit. This is the stable-range-one property of a finite ring; the following proof specifies the required side of multiplication.

Modulo the Jacobson radical, a finite ring is a product of full matrix rings over finite fields. In one block let $A,C$ be the images of $a,c$. The equation $BA+C=1$ implies $\ker A\cap\ker C=0$. Choose a complement $U$ to $\ker C$, and a complement $U'$ to $A(\ker C)$ of the same dimension as $U$. Choose an isomorphism $j:U\to U'$. Define $T$ on $C(U)$ by $TCv=j(v)-Av$, and extend it linearly. Then $A+TC$ is invertible, mapping $\ker C$ and $U$ isomorphically onto complementary spaces. Do this in every block and lift $T$ to $t\in R$. Units lift across the Jacobson radical. Finally $ux=ax=y$ because $cx=0$. This proves (3).

For a central extension $1\to A\to G\to Q\to1$, write $c_G\in M=H^2(Q,A)$ for its class, with trivial action on $A$. Set
$$
R=\operatorname{End}(A),\qquad X_G=R c_G\le M.
\tag{4}
$$
This is an additive subgroup generated by the images of $c_G$ under additive generators of $R$. Lemma 1 shows that $X_G=X_H$ exactly when a coefficient automorphism carries one class to the other. Thus (4) remembers the full coefficient-automorphism orbit, including mixed prime-power torsion.

The action of $\operatorname{Aut}(Q)$ on cohomology by inverse pullback commutes with $R$. Therefore, after identifying kernels and quotient groups,
$$
\exists u\in\operatorname{Aut}(A):u\,\beta(c_G)=c_H
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\beta(X_G)=X_H.
\tag{5}
$$
This identity applies to every quotient automorphism, without a solvability assumption. The bounded-composition-factor assumption is used only to compute the subgroup transporter efficiently.

The cohomology group and (4) are computed by linear congruences. Normalized 2-cochains have at most $|Q|^2$ kernel coordinates. Impose the cocycle equations on triples, then quotient by the image of the coboundary map on normalized 1-cochains. Smith normal form gives cyclic coordinates and maps. Endomorphisms of $A$ are specified by their images on cyclic generators. Applying them to the cocycle and reducing to cohomology coordinates gives generators for $X_G$. Quotient automorphism generators act by reindexing cochains. All sizes are polynomial in the Cayley-table input, although $|H^2(Q,A)|$ itself may be exponentially large.

## 2. Subgroup transport in finite abelian groups

**Lemma 2.** Let $P\le\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ belong to the fixed class $\Gamma_d$ of groups whose nonabelian composition factors embed in $S_d$. Suppose a homomorphism $P\to\operatorname{Aut}(B)$ is supplied on generators, where $B$ is a finite abelian group in cyclic coordinates. Given subgroups $U,V\le B$ by generators, their transporter under $P$ can be computed in time polynomial in the coordinate input length, $|\Omega|$, and the largest prime dividing $|B|$.

The action on $B$ need not be faithful; the supplied permutation representation is faithful. Output generators remain permutations on $\Omega$, with their induced additive actions tracked by straight-line programs (shared multiplication circuits, not expanded words). The field-case ingredient is the vector and subspace transporter algorithm of Luks–Miyazaki, Proposition 3.17. [Luks–Miyazaki](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/531/download).

It suffices to prove the lemma for an abelian $p$-group $B$, and process the characteristic primary components consecutively. At each stage a successful transporter set is a coset, so one representative normalizes the next problem and the remaining group is a subgroup of $P$.

Use induction on the exponent of $B$. Set $K=pB$. First compute the transporter of the images $\overline U,\overline V$ in the vector space $B/K$, using Luks–Miyazaki. If empty, reject. Otherwise transform $U$ by a returned representative and restrict to the group preserving the now common image $D$. Next recursively transport $U\cap K$ to $V\cap K$ inside $K$. If empty, reject. Transform $U$ again and restrict the acting group to preserve both $D$ and the now common intersection $W$.

These two tests alone are not sufficient. The exact remaining comparison is as follows. Let $C$ be the inverse image of $D$ in $B$. In
$$
0\longrightarrow K/W\longrightarrow C/W\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow0,
\tag{6}
$$
the subgroups $U/W$ and $V/W$ are complements. Indeed they meet $K/W$ trivially, project bijectively onto $D$, and generate $C/W$ together with $K/W$. Choose their homomorphic sections $s_U,s_V:D\to C/W$.

Every complement of (6) has a unique section $s_U+h$, where
$$
h\in L=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb F_p}\bigl(D,(K/W)[p]\bigr).
\tag{7}
$$
This uses that $C/W$ is abelian. The difference of two group-homomorphic sections is an additive homomorphism into $K/W$, and its image is killed by $p$ because $pD=0$.

The remaining acting group preserves (6). Its action on the affine space of sections is
$$
h\longmapsto g_K h g_D^{-1}+b_g,
\qquad b_g=g s_U g_D^{-1}-s_U.
\tag{8}
$$
Here $g_K$ is the induced action on $(K/W)[p]$ and $g_D$ that on $D$. These matrices and vectors are computed from cyclic subgroup and quotient coordinates. Equation (8) is a genuine group action because it is induced by composition of sections. Homogenizing it gives a linear representation on $L\oplus\mathbb F_p$.

Use the vector transporter algorithm on $(0,1)$ and $(s_V-s_U,1)$. Its answer is exactly the remaining subgroup transporter, including a representative and stabilizer generators. Composing the representatives from the three stages gives the full original transporter coset. There is no search over complements.

The induction has one recursive call on $pB$, not one call per possible top image. At each stage it performs polynomial-size subgroup arithmetic and two finite-field transporter operations. There are at most $\log_2|B|$ stages across all primary components. All acting groups remain subgroups of $P$, hence in $\Gamma_d$. Compress generating sets by permutation stabilizer chains after each call, and evaluate their straight-line programs in the original action on $B$. Retain these explicit action matrices. Thus each next call has polynomial input size in the original input, without nested word expansion. Every vector-space dimension is at most $(\log_2|B|)^2+1$, and all cyclic moduli divide the original exponent. These uniform bounds make the sum of the costs of the calls polynomial; one is not iterating a polynomial size blowup. Prime factors needed for the primary decomposition can be found by trial division with cost polynomial in the largest prime divisor and the bit length. The case $B=0$ is immediate. This proves the stated bound.

## 3. Whole central-extension cosets

To prove Theorem A, first compare $A,A'$ as abelian groups and identify them, or reject. Transport the target extension along the supplied quotient isomorphism $\alpha$, so that the allowed maps become $P$ on a common $Q$.

Compute $M=H^2(Q,A)$ and the two subgroups (4). Apply Lemma 2 to their transporter under $P$. The largest prime in $|M|$ divides $|A|$, and is therefore at most the group-table size. Thus the lemma is polynomial-time here. By (5), the resulting coset is precisely the set of liftable quotient maps.

For a returned map, use the fixed-quotient abelian-extension algorithm in the companion proof to find an actual group isomorphism. The existence of the needed coefficient unit follows from Lemma 1, while the companion module algorithm constructs the isomorphism directly.

To describe the entire solution coset, compute the subgroup $P_0\le P$ of source quotient automorphisms preserving $X_G$. Lift its generators separately by the fixed-map algorithm. Compute the automorphism kernel over the quotient identity by the marked-search algorithm in the companion proof. Arbitrary choices of lifts of the generators suffice: together with the full kernel they generate the complete inverse image of $P_0$. Its order is computed by permutation stabilizer chains on the elements of $G$. A particular isomorphism and this subgroup describe exactly all solutions. This proves Theorem A.

## 4. A solvable subgroup of the abelian quotient automorphism group

Let $Q_p=\bigoplus_e(C_{p^e})^{m_e}$, with $m_e=m_{p,e}$. In $E_p=\operatorname{End}(Q_p)$, reduction modulo $p$ on each equal-exponent diagonal block gives a surjective ring homomorphism
$$
\theta:E_p\longrightarrow\prod_e M_{m_e}(\mathbb F_p).
\tag{9}
$$
Cross products through a different exponent vanish in these diagonal blocks modulo $p$: a homomorphism from a smaller cyclic $p$-power group to a larger one has the required divisibility by the exponent difference.

The kernel $J$ of (9) is nilpotent. To see this, order the exponents. On $Q_p/pQ_p$ its elements have strictly block-triangular matrices, since the equal-exponent diagonal blocks vanish. If $r$ exponent types occur, a product of $r$ such elements maps $Q_p$ into $pQ_p$. Endomorphisms commute with multiplication by $p$, so a product of $r e_{\max}$ elements maps $Q_p$ into $p^{e_{\max}}Q_p=0$. Thus $J^{r e_{\max}}=0$.

Consequently (9) restricts to a surjection
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(Q_p)\longrightarrow\prod_e\operatorname{GL}_{m_e}(\mathbb F_p)
\tag{10}
$$
with kernel $1+J$. Invertible diagonal blocks have invertible lifts on their homocyclic summands, and a unit modulo a nilpotent ideal is a unit. The group $1+J$ is a $p$-group because $x\mapsto1+x$ is a bijection from the additive $p$-group $J$.

Let $P_p$ be the inverse image of the product of upper-triangular groups under (10). It is solvable, being an extension of solvable groups. Set $P=\prod_pP_p\le\operatorname{Aut}(Q)$. Then
$$
[\operatorname{Aut}(Q):P]
=\prod_{p,e}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{p,e}}\frac{p^j-1}{p-1}.
\tag{11}
$$

All groups and coset representatives in this construction are obtained explicitly within the required time. Here are the details preventing a hidden automorphism-group enumeration.

Compute additive generators of $J,J^2,\ldots$ by composition and subgroup closure in cyclic coordinates. For every such generator $x$, include $1+x$. These generate $1+J$: on $(1+J^k)/(1+J^{k+1})$, multiplication agrees with addition in $J^k/J^{k+1}$, and induction down the nilpotent filtration proves generation. Lift generators of each upper-triangular block using elementary upper-triangular matrices and diagonal nonzero elements of $\mathbb F_p$. Listing the latter costs at most $p$, which is polynomial in the table size. This produces generators of $P$ as explicit permutations of $Q$.

A coset $tB_m$ in $\operatorname{GL}_m(\mathbb F_p)$ corresponds to a complete flag. Enumerate these flags by choosing the first line, then a complete flag in its quotient. Canonical bases and complements give a representative matrix for every flag in polynomial time per output, with the number of outputs given by its factor in (11). Lift these matrices to their respective homocyclic summands, taking their entries as integers modulo $p^e$. Their determinants remain units. Products across all blocks give exactly a transversal of cosets $tP$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(Q)$.

## 5. Removing the supplied quotient map for class-two groups

Compute $Z(G),Z(H)$, their quotient tables, and cyclic decompositions. Reject unequal center types or quotient types. Otherwise choose any abelian-group isomorphism $\alpha:Q\to Q'$ by matching cyclic coordinates.

Construct $P$ and its transversal from Section 4. For each representative $t$, apply Theorem A to the coset $\alpha tP$. These cosets partition every possible quotient isomorphism. Because the full centers are characteristic, every isomorphism of the original groups appears in exactly one of the resulting solution sets. No input map or flag has been supplied.

For witnesses accept any nonempty coset. Sum the coset cardinalities for exact counting. For the automorphism group, run the procedure on $G$ against itself, retain one witness from each nonempty quotient coset, and adjoin generators for the automorphisms inducing an element of $P$. Every automorphism differs from a retained witness by an element of this latter group. Thus the output also gives the complete isomorphism coset.

Each call and all preparatory work take $n^{O(1)}$ time. The transversal has size (11). Since
$$
\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{p^j-1}{p-1}
\le p^{\binom m2}\left(\frac p{p-1}\right)^m
\le 2^m p^{\binom m2}
$$
and $2^{\sum_{p,e}m_{p,e}}\le |Q|\le n$, the excess factor is absorbed into $n^{O(1)}$. This proves (1).

If $m_{p,e}\le b$, then $\binom{m_{p,e}}2\le (b-1)m_{p,e}/2$, giving an exponential factor at most $|Q|^{(b-1)/2}$. This proves the bounded-multiplicity corollary and (2).

For example, if $Q\cong(C_{p^e})^r$, the bound is $p^{r(r-1)/2}n^{O(1)}$, independent of $e$ in its exponential factor. It is polynomial when $r=O(e)$ because $\log_p n\ge er$. If $Q$ has at most two cyclic factors of each exact prime-power order, the factor is at most $|Q|^{1/2}$. These statements apply to all class-two groups of the indicated central-quotient types, without assumptions about decomposability or the commutator map.

## 6. Attribution and significance

The action/cohomology framework and the published questions come from Grochow–Qiao. Their general abelian-radical question is not closed here. The earlier fixed-map encoding is included as a dependency. The finite-ring orbit identity is a consequence of classical stable range one; its proof is given above. The essential algorithm for vector and subspace transport is Luks–Miyazaki. The structure of automorphisms of finite abelian groups and complete-flag enumeration are classical. [Grochow–Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.1776), [Luks–Miyazaki](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/531/download).

The combined result eliminates all coefficient automorphisms at once, transports the resulting subgroups without losing their prime-power lifting data, and searches solvable quotient cosets in bulk. Theorem B therefore concerns actual unmarked groups given only by their multiplication tables. Its scope is stronger in this direction than a theorem that requires a quotient isomorphism as input.

It does not give polynomial time for unrestricted class-two groups, arbitrary abelian-radical groups, or TI versus SL-TI. In particular an elementary abelian central quotient of large rank still gives a large factor in (1). The recent general class-two bound and older composition-series methods are important comparison points. A targeted search did not establish that the exact combined parameter bound is new; it should be submitted as an informal result for expert review, rather than advertised as a new unrestricted record.
