# Size and orbit bounds for compressing independent determinant signs

Work over the field F of three elements. The k-sign comparison requires determinant one in k specified physical modes of a three-tensor, for 0≤k≤3. The case k=0 is ordinary tensor isomorphism. There are two distinct bounds below. The first treats encodings that retain the actual mode maps of the input. The second concerns object maps without any requirement to recover those mode maps.

## Exact lifting across a gap in the number of signs

**Theorem.** Fix k>j, with k≤3. There is a family of concise balanced tensors with arbitrarily large even mode dimensions on which the following construction would require exponentially large total vector-space dimension at bounded tensor order. Encode a comparison by a pair of typed tensor systems. Recover the original three mode maps on distinguished invariant subquotients. Require every valid k-sign transporter to lift to an output transporter satisfying j prescribed determinant-one conditions, and require every such output transporter to project to a valid k-sign transporter. The lower bound holds even if the encoding of both output objects depends on the input pair, and even if there are arbitrarily many fixed tensor relations of bounded order.

More precisely, the example below has mode dimensions 3n+2 with n≥6 even. Let r bound the number of slots in each fixed tensor of the output system, let W_l be the labelled auxiliary and physical spaces, and let M=Σ_l dim W_l. If for some integer d≥1

    2d<n,    rd<n,    max_l dim W_l < ½ binom(n,d+1),    3M<3^(n−1)−1,

then any such construction lifting all allowed input maps admits at least 2^(k−j) input sign tuples while satisfying its j output sign tests. Only one of these tuples is valid for the k tested input signs. In the automorphism version the j tests may be arbitrary group homomorphisms to the two-element multiplicative group. For example, at fixed r one may take d=⌊(n−1)/max(3,r)⌋ for sufficiently large n. The binomial bound and the bound on M then both give exponential lower bounds.

A typed tensor system here consists of separately labelled finite-dimensional spaces and fixed tensors with slots drawn from those spaces and their duals. A transporter acts by its linear mode maps and must preserve the fixed tensors. Linear map relations, bilinear multiplications and labelled vectors can all be written this way. The original visible subquotients may be among the named spaces or obtained from invariant subspaces of named spaces. For paired systems assume each output transporter induces the corresponding invertible map on these subquotients, compatibly with composition.

### An explicit family with independent sign directions

For i=1,2,3, choose U_i of dimension n and R_i of dimension two. In slots i,i+1,i+2, using cyclic slot indices, place the tensor

    T^(i) = Σ_(a=1)^n Σ_(b=1)^2 u_a ⊗ (u^a ⊗ r_b) ⊗ r^b,

where the indexed vectors are bases of U_i and R_i and superscripts denote their dual bases. Thus the three spaces for this summand, in cyclic order, are U_i, U_i*⊗R_i and R_i*. Take their tensor direct sum T, with independent summand spaces in each mode. Each summand spans each of its spaces under contractions. Direct sum blocks have disjoint supports, so T is concise. Its format is (3n+2,3n+2,3n+2).

Independently for each summand, choose A_i∈GL(U_i). Its actions A_i, A_i^(−t)⊗I and I in the three cyclic slots preserve T^(i), and thus extend to one automorphism of T. In the first of those slots the determinant is det A_i. In the second the determinant is (det A_i)^(−2)=1. The third is unchanged as well. Hence these three independent copies of GL_n(F) contribute exactly their three independent determinants in the corresponding physical modes of T. For the theorem vary only the k factors associated to tested input modes and use the identity on the others. Write ρ for this embedding of GL_n(F)^k into the raw input stabilizer.

Apply the proposed construction to (T,T). A lift of the original identity satisfying the output signs identifies the two output systems, with visible actions identified by the original identity too. Composition with this lift and its inverse identifies the relative output transporter problem with the output automorphism problem and preserves positivity of the j tested output determinants. It is therefore enough to prove the asserted leakage for automorphisms of a single typed system.

### Find a diagonal-sign lift inside an arbitrary extension kernel

Let G be the output automorphism group. Use its action on the visible subquotients and take the preimage of ρ(SL_n(F)^k). By the lift requirement this preimage maps onto SL_n(F)^k. Take a minimal subgroup H of it that still surjects, and put K=ker(H→SL_n(F)^k). Every maximal subgroup B of H contains K. Otherwise BK=H and B would still surject, contrary to minimality. Thus K lies in the finite Frattini subgroup Φ(H), which is nilpotent. Indeed each Sylow subgroup P_0 of Φ(H) satisfies H=Φ(H)N_H(P_0) by Sylow conjugacy and normality of Φ(H). A proper normalizer would lie in a maximal subgroup that already contains Φ(H), a contradiction. All Sylow subgroups of Φ(H) are therefore normal. This also proves that its subgroup K is nilpotent.

Write K=P×O for its Sylow two-subgroup P and odd-order part O. Both are characteristic in K. In the group H̄=H/O the projection to SL_n(F)^k has kernel P. Conjugation on the elementary abelian quotient P/Φ(P) factors through SL_n(F)^k, as inner conjugation by P is trivial on that quotient. For any a the two-valuation of |GL_a(F)| is at most 3a. Indeed its valuation is Σ_(i≤a) ν₂(3^i−1), and these summands are one for odd i and 2+ν₂(i) for even i. The latter formula follows inductively by factoring powers with even exponent. Bounding the sum by 2a+ν₂(a!) gives the stated estimate. Consequently dim_(F₂)(P/Φ(P))≤log₂|P|≤3M.

Any nontrivial linear representation of SL_n(F) in characteristic two has dimension at least 3^(n−1)−1. For the short argument, extend scalars to contain the third roots of unity. The elementary abelian three-subgroup I+Σ_(b>1) c_b E_(1,b) has one character per possible linear functional on its row of parameters. The block diagonal copy of SL_(n−1)(F) normalizes it and transitively permutes its nontrivial characters. If any such character occurs, every one occurs and takes a separate eigenspace. Otherwise the three-subgroup and its conjugates act trivially. Those conjugates contain all elementary transvections and generate SL_n(F). This proves the bound. Apply it to each of the k factors of the representation on P/Φ(P). The inequality on M makes conjugation on that quotient trivial.

The kernel of Aut(P)→Aut(P/Φ(P)) is a two-group. Its action on ordered generating tuples lifting one fixed quotient basis is free. Every lift generates, since a proper generated subgroup would lie in a maximal subgroup containing Φ(P). There are |Φ(P)| to the power of the quotient rank many such tuples, a power of two. Thus the centralizer C_H̄(P) has two-power index in H̄. Its image in SL_n(F)^k is normal, with two-group quotient. Since each special-linear factor is generated by transvections of order three, this image is the entire product. The kernel of this projection on the centralizer is Z(P), which is central.

For each n≥5, SL_n(F) is centrally closed, so its central extensions split. The direct product is centrally closed too. In a central extension of the product, split the preimage of each factor. The commutators between two factor sections lie in the central kernel. Holding either section element fixed makes these commutators a homomorphism from the other perfect factor to an abelian group. Each factor is perfect since its elementary transvections are commutators of elementary transvections in three distinct coordinates. Hence the commutators across factor sections vanish. The sections multiply to a section of the product. Apply this to C_H̄(P) and pull the section back to H. We obtain a subgroup H₁ surjecting onto SL_n(F)^k with odd-order kernel O. [Steinberg, Lectures on Chevalley Groups, Corollary 2](https://math.soimeme.org/~arunram/Resources/LOCGS7CentralExtensions.html) · [Single-factor lift argument](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorOrientationJets/UniversalLifts.md)

In each input factor let D_(0,i) be its diagonal sign subgroup with coordinate product one. A Sylow two-subgroup D̃ of its product's preimage in H₁ projects isomorphically onto the product D₀=∏_i D_(0,i) because the kernel is odd. Determinant-one signed permutation matrices in each factor normalize D₀ and induce the full product of symmetric groups on the n coordinates of the factors. A lift of any one of these signed permutation matrices can be corrected in the preimage of D₀ to normalize D̃, using Sylow conjugacy there. The correction projects to D₀ and therefore has trivial conjugation action on D₀ itself. Thus the normalizer of D̃ permutes the coordinates of each factor independently.

### Extend the diagonal signs and count the sign directions lost

Every W_l is a direct sum of D̃-character spaces, since D̃ is an elementary abelian two-group over a field of odd characteristic. In each factor, characters are subsets of its n coordinates modulo complement. A shortest representative of size s<n/2 has orbit size binom(n,s) under coordinate permutation, and at s=n/2 its orbit has half that size. Each such orbit must occur in the same W_l, by the normalizer. Varying any one factor independently and applying the dimension bound therefore gives a unique representative of size at most d in every factor for each occurring character.

Now take arbitrary coordinate signs in all k factors with no constraint on their products. On every W_l-character space define their action to be the scalar obtained by evaluating the signs on its chosen short representatives in each factor. This gives a homomorphic action of the full product of diagonal sign groups. To prove it preserves each fixed tensor, expand that tensor in character-space components. Every nonzero component is fixed by the lifted D̃, so the symmetric difference of the representative subsets in each factor is either empty or the entire coordinate set there. Since there are at most r slots, the combined subset has size at most rd<n. It is therefore empty. This makes the new scalar one on the component. A dual space has the same sign as its original character, as all character values are ±1. Thus every new diagonal tuple belongs to G.

Such a tuple preserves the visible subquotients because it acts by scalars on whole character spaces. The prescribed visible action agrees with ρ on the lifted subgroup D̃. Its weights on V_i are therefore precisely the natural coordinate, dual coordinate and trivial weights coming from the summands described above. Their short representatives are singletons in the corresponding factor or empty. Thus the full diagonal action just constructed projects on the visible modes exactly as ρ does.

Use now one coordinate reflection from each of the k input factors. Their lifts give a subgroup with k independent bits, and the corresponding source sign tuples realize all those bits. Restricting the j output sign tests to these lifts gives a binary linear map with rank at most j. Its kernel has size at least 2^(k−j). The lifts in this kernel pass all output tests and have pairwise distinct source sign tuples. At least 2^(k−j)−1 of these are forbidden by the source k-sign requirement. This proves the theorem.

### Polynomially many exact branches cannot preserve every witness either

A proper subgroup B of SL_n(F)^k has index at least 3^(n−1). The coset permutation action is nontrivial, so some factor acts nontrivially. Apply the same elementary abelian character argument over the complex numbers to the permutation module. All 3^(n−1)−1 nontrivial characters occur, and the constant vector supplies a trivial character. Thus the size of the coset set is at least 3^(n−1).

Consider a nonempty j-sign branch on the fixed input pair (T,T), including its visible decoding. Allowed output transporters are a coset of a subgroup given by its j target tests. Intersect their visible images with the positive input symmetry subgroup ρ(SL_n(F)^k). When nonempty this intersection is a coset of a subgroup of ρ(SL_n(F)^k). If it contained that entire subgroup, the lift argument would apply to the branch and contradict its soundness. Thus each sound branch of the stated size covers at most a 3^(1−n) fraction of these explicit positive maps. Polynomially many such branches cannot cover them all. A decision algorithm can still choose an exponentially small fraction that contains a witness whenever the verdict is positive.

## A separate count for objectwise comparisons

**Theorem.** Let f be an objectwise kernel reduction from k-sign TI to j-sign TI, with k>j. On any input ordinary orbit that splits into the full 2^k k-sign orbits, the image of f meets at least 2^(k−j) ordinary output orbits. Such input orbits exist in arbitrarily large formats. In particular no objectwise reduction across one of these gaps can preserve ordinary equivalence on every pair.

For an acting product Γ of the three general linear groups in a fixed format, the k-sign subgroup is normal with index 2^k. On the ordinary orbit of T, its orbit classes are indexed by cosets of the stabilizer's k-bit image in the k-bit group. Indeed transporters from T differ by its stabilizer, and multiplying by a k-sign map preserves the recorded tuple. Conversely two transports whose sign tuples differ by the stabilizer image can be made to have the same tuple, and then differ by a k-sign map on the left. Thus an ordinary orbit splits into at most 2^k k-sign orbits and reaches that maximum when the stabilizer has trivial k-bit image.

For explicit large examples, use the multiplication tensor of M_m(F) for even m. For an autotopism F_1(X)F_2(Y)=F_3(XY), put a=F_1(I) and b=F_2(I). Evaluation at I gives F_3=R_b F_1=L_a F_2, with a,b units. Thus φ(X)=a^(−1)F_3(X)b^(−1) is an algebra automorphism, and the three maps are L_a φ, R_b φ and L_a R_b φ. Such φ is inner by Skolem–Noether, so has determinant one. Left and right multiplication by a matrix have determinant its fixed-field determinant to the power m, by grouping the matrix space by columns or by rows. Both are one for m even. Hence every autotopism has all three determinants one. Dualizing the two inputs to use a covariant tensor convention leaves the signs unchanged. This proves the claimed maximal splitting for each k. [Matrix multiplication determinant computation](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IntrinsicTensorSubspaces/MatrixObstruction.md)

Take one representative from each of the 2^k signed classes in such an orbit. The kernel property makes their images pairwise inequivalent in the j-sign relation. An ordinary output orbit can contain at most 2^j such classes. At least 2^(k−j) ordinary output orbits are therefore needed, establishing the count.

These bounds concern informal construction models with exact hypotheses. They do not separate unrestricted polynomial decision reductions between the four tiers. The first bound addresses an exact lifting mechanism, and the second gives a required property of any objectwise collapse. Publication priority for these extensions has not been established.
