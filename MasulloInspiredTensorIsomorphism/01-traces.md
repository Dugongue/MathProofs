# Short determinantal certificates for simultaneous conjugacy

Masullo's proposed trace formula separates an operator calculation into orbital pieces, then uses Mellin transforms of test functions to read off spectral multiplicities. The orbital basis is the proposed bridge between the two calculations. The finite construction here uses central idempotents to isolate simple matrix-algebra factors and reads their multiplicities from determinant coefficients. [Masullo, *The Music of the Primes*, §§1.4.8–1.4.9 and the concluding argument of §1.4](https://math216.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/the-music-of-the-primes.pdf)

## Theorem

Let $A=(A_1,\ldots,A_h)$ and $B=(B_1,\ldots,B_h)$ be marked tuples of $n\times n$ matrices over any field $F$, with $n\geq 1$. Let $H$ be the unital algebra generated by $A_i\oplus B_i$, let $d=\dim_F H$, and let $L$ be the shortest length for which the joint word evaluations of length at most $L$ span $H$. For a noncommutative polynomial $P$ over $F$, put

$$
c_j(P;A)=[t^j]\det(I_n+tP(A)).
$$

Write $A^{\mathrm{ss}}$ and $B^{\mathrm{ss}}$ for the semisimplifications of the two modules over the free algebra on the marked generators.

Then $A^{\mathrm{ss}}\cong B^{\mathrm{ss}}$ if and only if $c_j(P;A)=c_j(P;B)$ for all $P,j$ in the following ranges:

- $P$ is a linear combination of at most $d$ words of length at most $L$.
- $j=1$ in characteristic zero, and $j$ is any power of the characteristic $p$ up to $n$ in characteristic $p>0$. The powers include $p^0=1$.

When the semisimplifications differ, one such $P$ and one such $j$ certify the difference. The size bounds are

$$
d\le 2n^2,\qquad
L\le \min\!\left(d-1,\ \left\lfloor 4n\log_2(2n)+8n-4\right\rfloor\right).
$$

If both original modules are semisimple, the criterion decides simultaneous conjugacy. Under this promise, a deterministic algorithm uses a polynomial number of field operations to produce a separating $P,j$ or certify equality. The algorithm uses linear algebra over the input field and is polynomial-time in the bit model over an explicitly represented finite field.

## Proof of separation

Let $V_A,V_B$ be the two $H$-modules with the actions given by the block projections. The free algebra maps onto $H$, so its invariant subspaces, simple factors, and isomorphisms agree with those for $H$. Put $J=\operatorname{Jac}(H)$. By Artin–Wedderburn,

$$
H/J=\prod_i M_{s_i}(D_i)
$$

for finite-dimensional division algebras $D_i$ over $F$. Denote the identity of factor $i$ by $e_i$ and its unique simple left module by $S_i$. On each semisimplification the central idempotent $e_i$ is the identity on copies of $S_i$ and zero on other factors.

If the semisimplifications differ, choose $i$ with different multiplicities of $S_i$ and lift $e_i$ to $u\in H$. A composition-series basis on either original module makes the action of $u$ block triangular. The diagonal blocks are the actions of $e_i$ on the simple factors. Therefore the determinant polynomials for the two projections of $u$ are $(1+t)^{r_A}$ and $(1+t)^{r_B}$, where

$$
r_X=\dim_F(S_i)\,[V_X^{\mathrm{ss}}:S_i].
$$

In particular, $r_A\ne r_B$ as integers. In characteristic zero the coefficient of $t$ distinguishes them. In characteristic $p$, set $\Delta=|r_A-r_B|$, $a=v_p(\Delta)$ and $\delta=\Delta/p^a$. The identity $(1+t)^\Delta=(1+t^{p^a})^\delta$ shows that the first nonzero coefficient of $(1+t)^\Delta-1$ is at $t^{p^a}$, with value $\delta$ in $F$. Multiplying by $(1+t)^{\min(r_A,r_B)}$ and taking the appropriate sign gives the determinant difference. Its first nonzero coefficient is still at $t^{p^a}$, with $p^a\le\Delta\le n$.

Choose a basis from the joint word evaluations of length at most $L$ and write $u$ in that basis. This gives $u=P(A)\oplus P(B)$ using at most $d$ words. Conversely, if the semisimplifications are isomorphic, every test has equal products of diagonal-block determinants in composition-series bases, hence equal determinants on the original modules.

The block diagonal realization gives $d\le 2n^2$. Starting with the empty word, adjoining products with one more letter increases the word span until it stabilizes. Stability implies closure under right multiplication by every generator and therefore equality with $H$. This gives $L\le d-1$. Applying Shitov's bound to the joint $2n\times 2n$ generators gives the other bound. [Shitov, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.09310)

## Computing the test for semisimple tuples

If both modules are semisimple, $J$ kills their faithful direct sum, so $J=0$. Compute a joint word basis by traversing successive lengths, starting with $I_n\oplus I_n$. At each length retain a word if its evaluation is outside the span of the retained words. Extending only the retained words suffices. Indeed, a discarded word is already a linear combination of retained words of no greater length, and the relation persists under right multiplication. Stop after the first layer with no new word. Store the retained words and their joint values, and solve commutation equations in that basis to compute $Z=Z(H)$.

The center is a product of fields $Z_i$, one for each simple factor of $H$. Write $q_{X,i}=\dim_{Z_i}(e_iV_X)$. This is the multiplicity of $S_i$ times the positive integer $\dim_{Z_i} S_i$. Comparing the profiles $q_{A,i}$ and $q_{B,i}$ for all $i$ therefore compares the multiplicities.

To compute a profile without splitting $Z$, start with $M=V_X$ as a $Z$-module. For nonzero $v\in M$, compute $I_v=\{z\in Z:zv=0\}$ by solving linear equations. Solve $bx=x$ for each basis element $x$ of $I_v$ with $b$ constrained to $I_v$. Every ideal in a product of fields is itself a product of some of the fields, so the solution is its unique identity $b$, including $b=0$ for the zero ideal. Record the support idempotent $e=1-b$, then replace $M$ by $M/Zv$. This strictly drops the $F$-dimension. On each field factor the cyclic module $Zv$ has dimension one when $e_i e=e_i$, and zero otherwise. Thus $q_{X,i}$ is the number of recorded supports containing the factor $i$.

Refine a common partition of the identity using the recorded supports for both modules. Start with the single part $1$, and for each support $e$ replace each part $f$ by the nonzero members of $fe,f(1-e)$. The parts remain pairwise orthogonal central idempotents, so there are at most $\dim_F Z$ of them. Both profiles are constant on each part. If they agree everywhere, the modules are isomorphic. Otherwise choose a part $f$ where they differ. On the field factors in this part the difference has one common nonzero value, and

$$
\operatorname{rank}_F(f|_{V_A})-\operatorname{rank}_F(f|_{V_B})
=\sum_{i:e_if=e_i}[Z_i:F](q_{A,i}-q_{B,i})\ne0 .
$$

The two actions of $f$ are idempotent, so their determinants are powers of $1+t$ with these ranks as exponents. Expand $f$ in the joint word basis to obtain $P$, compute the two ranks by elimination, and choose $j$ by the coefficient argument above.

Every cyclic quotient, center calculation, word-span step, and partition refinement uses linear algebra in dimensions polynomial in $n,h$. There are at most $2n$ cyclic quotients across the two modules and at most $d$ word-span increases. This proves the complexity claim. A negative certificate can be checked by evaluating $P$ on both inputs and comparing the indicated coefficients. For the computed idempotent witness, it is enough to check idempotence and use the two integer ranks to compute the coefficients.

## Scope and example

Over $\mathbb F_p$, take $A_1=0_p$ and $B_1=I_p$. Both modules are semisimple. All ordinary traces $\operatorname{tr}(P(A))$ and $\operatorname{tr}(P(B))$ agree, yet $P=x_1$ gives determinant polynomials $1$ and $1+t^p$. Taking $n=p^a$ gives the same phenomenon at any required $p$-power coefficient.

The algorithm applies to fixed-arrow isomorphism of semisimple quiver representations, including quivers with oriented cycles. Form the direct sum of the vertex spaces, extend every arrow matrix by zero outside its designated block, and add the named vertex projections to the tuple. Intertwining the vertex projections requires the conjugating map to preserve the vertex decomposition, so conjugacy of the marked tuples is equivalent to fixed-arrow isomorphism. Marked conjugacy is also the prescribed-generator case of Grochow–Qiao's matrix-space conjugacy problem. Unmarked matrix spaces introduce the further variable of an unknown change of basis among their generators. [Grochow–Qiao, Part I, §2.3](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full)

The proof uses the determinant version of the Brauer–Nesbitt argument. Its quantitative statement combines a sparse word expansion, a restricted set of coefficient indices and a factorization-free method to extract one separating test under the semisimple promise. Matrix-tuple characteristic coefficients and general tuple-isomorphism algorithms have established literature. Verification here is by mathematical proof. [Derksen–Makam, matrix invariants in arbitrary characteristic](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.06617) · [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, module isomorphism](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf)
