# Sparse Polynomial Calculus and circuit IPS for degree-one mapping equations

**Theorem.** There is a family of closed smooth simply connected stably parallelizable six-manifolds $M$, with torsion-free homology, whose ordinary degree-one cohomology systems for maps $M\to(S^2)^3$ have minimum Polynomial Calculus degree $\Theta(b_2(M))$ and minimum sparse Polynomial Calculus size $2^{\Theta(b_2(M))}$ over every field of characteristic different from two. The systems have polynomial-size Hilbert-like Ideal Proof System certificates of total-product degree $O(b_2(M))$. The integral cup tensors have $O(b_2(M))$ nonzero entries of bounded absolute value. The same integral manifold family works over all these fields. The equations use only cup squares of the three sought classes and the degree-one triple pairing. The theorem measures their literal expanded polynomial presentation in the constructed integral bases.

A second family, from the one-sided rectangular inverse equations of the Rank Principle, has sparse Polynomial Calculus size at least $r^{\Omega(r)}$ and minimum degree at least $r+2$, with polynomial-size Hilbert-like IPS certificates, over every field of characteristic different from two. Here $r$ is the inner rank parameter and $b_2(M)=\Theta(r^2)$. This combines the framed cubic realization from the earlier Manin work with the lower-bound and certificate methods used in the algebraic proof complexity of tensor problems. It concerns a cohomology system defined by an integral cup tensor. [Framed cubic realization and sphere lifts](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/b373edcc6eb25fa29980380503da5096ffe84588/ManinTop20/ManinPersistenceAndDegrees.md#4-mapping-degree-spectra-in-arbitrary-connectivity) · [Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S3) · [Grochow–Pitassi on circuit IPS](https://doi.org/10.1145/3230742)

### The cubic construction and ordinary proof systems

Let $q_j(x)$, $1\le j\le m$, be any list of integral polynomials of degree at most two. Write $Q_j(U,x)=U^2q_j(x/U)$ for their quadratic homogenizations. Use coordinates $U,x,b,c,Y_j,W_j$ on a free integral lattice $V$. Specify a symmetric integral trilinear form $\mu$ by its diagonal cubic
$$
 \mu(v,v,v)=6\left(Ubc+\sum_{j=1}^mY_jQ_j(U,x)+\sum_{j=1}^mW_jY_j^2\right).
$$
Integral polarization is available since each coefficient is divisible by six. The explicit framed construction realizes it as the triple cup pairing on $H^2(M;\mathbb Z)$ of a closed smooth simply connected stably parallelizable six-manifold. In brief, use one $S^2\times S^4$ per basis vector and a product of three 2-spheres for each displayed monomial of coefficient $6a$. The latter contributes $6l_1l_2l_3$ on three new coordinates. Impose $l_1=a\xi_1,l_2=\xi_2,l_3=\xi_3$ by surgery on dual 2-cycles, allowing repeated original coordinates $\xi_i$. The cycles are independent and primitive because their coefficients in the new coordinates form an identity block. The stably framed connected sum allows disjoint framed sphere representatives. The trace preserves the triple pairing on the prescribed sublattice, and the independent primitive surgeries preserve torsion-free homology and give $b_3=0$. This is precisely the earlier framed realization. [Realization by framed surgery](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/b373edcc6eb25fa29980380503da5096ffe84588/ManinTop20/ManinPersistenceAndDegrees.md#framed-realization-of-cubics)

Use three unknown coordinate vectors $v_1,v_2,v_3$ and the equations
$$
  A_{\ell,i}=\mu(v_\ell,v_\ell,e_i)=0
  \quad(1\le\ell\le3,\ e_i\text{ a basis vector}),\qquad
  d-1=0,\quad d=\mu(v_1,v_2,v_3).
$$
Integral solutions are exactly the triples of pullback classes of degree-one maps to $(S^2)^3$, by the square-zero sphere-lifting theorem for stably parallelizable simply connected closed six-manifolds and Poincaré duality. [Exact sphere lifts in dimension six](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/b373edcc6eb25fa29980380503da5096ffe84588/ManinTop20/ManinPersistenceAndDegrees.md#exact-square-zero-lifting-on-stably-parallelizable-six-manifolds)

For one class, polarization gives three groups of useful equations:
$$
  \mu(v,v,e_{W_j})=2Y_j^2,\qquad
  \mu(v,v,e_{Y_j})=2Q_j(U,x)+4W_jY_j,\qquad
  \mu(v,v,e_b)=2Uc,\quad \mu(v,v,e_c)=2Ub.
$$
Over any field of characteristic different from two, the first gives $Y_j=0$ at solutions. The $e_U$ equation then gives $bc=0$. Hence each square-zero class occupies at most one of the $U,b,c$ axes. When all three classes are square-zero, their triple product is the polarization of $6Ubc$. A triple of degree one therefore uses three distinct axes and has some $U\ne0$. Its $x/U$ solves the original quadrics. Conversely, a solution of the original quadrics gives a solution of the degree-one system with
$$
 v_1=(U=1,x,\text{other coordinates }0),\qquad v_2=e_b,\qquad v_3=e_c.
$$
Over the integers the degree-one product of the three distinguished coordinates is $1$, so they are units. The same equivalence with the original equations thus holds integrally, and gives equivalence with the actual existence of a degree-one map. In particular this realizes arbitrary integral degree-at-most-two extension gates as fixed-target manifold degree-one tests.

In the displayed specialization each literal input becomes zero or a fixed nonzero scalar multiple of an original $q_j$. Each new variable maps to a constant or one original variable. Substitution therefore pulls back any ordinary PC derivation without increasing degree or expanded monomial count, apart from adjoining the required original inputs and fixed scalings. Circuits of Hilbert-like IPS certificates also specialize. Further monomial substitutions of degree bounded by a fixed constant simulate each multiplication in boundedly many steps without increasing the number of monomials in a line. These claims concern expanded polynomials. A monomial either becomes zero or a scalar times a single monomial under any such substitution. [Definition of low-degree PC reductions](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S2.SS1)

### Exact decomposition over rings

There is also an exact scheme description over any commutative ring (A) in which two is a unit. In the degree-one coordinate ring the ideal (J) of the (3m) coordinates (Y_{ell,j}) has (J^{3m+1}=0). After quotienting by (J), the equations for each (ell) are precisely (U_ell b_ell=U_ell c_ell=b_ell c_ell=0) and (Q_j(U_ell,x_ell)=0). The triple pairing is the sum of the six monomial products obtained by assigning (U,b,c) to the three classes in all possible orders. Distinct products have product zero, since they choose different axes for some class, and their sum is one. They are therefore orthogonal idempotents and decompose the coordinate ring into six factors.

In each factor the distinguished product is one, so its three coordinates are units with product one, and every other axis coordinate is zero. For the class carrying (U), rescale (x) to (x/U) to obtain precisely (q_j=0). For both other classes the (x) variables satisfy the homogeneous degree-two parts (q_j^{(2)}=0). The (3m) coordinates (W_{ell,j}) are arbitrary. Writing (V_A) for an affine zero scheme over (A), the quotient scheme is consequently the disjoint union of six copies of
[
 V_A(q) 	imes_A (mathbb G_m)^2 	imes_A V_A(q^{(2)})^2 	imes_A mathbb A^{3m}.
]
In particular every quadratic affine zero scheme occurs as a retract of one branch, by setting the spectator (x)'s and the (W)'s to zero and the two torus coordinates to one. The explicit nilpotent thickening accounts for the circuit calculation below.

### A circuit calculation for nilpotent coordinates

Suppose $f$ is a polynomial with a displayed expression $f=\sum_i a_i u_i$, and the ideal of the current equations has Hilbert-like representations for $u_i^{e_i}$, with prescribed positive integers $e_i$. Put $K=1+\sum_i(e_i-1)$. There is a polynomial-size circuit calculation, polynomial in the number of coordinates and in $K$, that gives
$$
       f^K=\sum_i H_i u_i^{e_i}
$$
with coefficient circuits, provided the $a_i$ are part of the input circuits for this calculation. If $a_i$ has degree two and $u_i$ degree one, all products on the right have degree at most $3K$.

Indeed write $s_i=a_i u_i$. Process successive partial sums of the $s_i$, computing powers through $K$. At each step use the binomial expansion of the sum of the previous partial sum and $s_i$. Store separately the contribution from powers $s_i^j$ with $j\ge e_i$, factoring out $s_i^{e_i}$. For other powers carry along the already factored contributions of earlier indices. The unfactored part is exactly the sum of monomials satisfying every individual exponent bound $<e_i$ for that prefix. At exponent $K$ no unfactored part remains. The recurrence uses polynomially many operations and gives homogeneous total degree $K$ in the $s_i$. Substitution of $a_i u_i$ and the supplied certificates gives the assertion. Throughout the proof, combining ideal representations means keeping one coefficient circuit per literal input. Sums, multiplication by a polynomial, and the identity $A_1\cdots A_t-B_1\cdots B_t=\sum_i A_1\cdots A_{i-1}(A_i-B_i)B_{i+1}\cdots B_t$ have polynomial bookkeeping overhead. The latter is used with fixed order or in a shared arithmetic circuit.

In our system $d$ is a sum of quadratic multiples of the three $U_\ell$ and of the $3m$ coordinates $Y_{\ell,j}$. If $U_\ell^{D}$ has a certificate in the square equations of its class, for each $\ell$, the preceding construction yields a certificate for $d^K$ with $K=3(D-1)+3m+1$. From it the identity
$$
 1=d^K-(d-1)(1+d+\cdots+d^{K-1})
$$
gives a Hilbert-like IPS refutation of the literal equations. If the certificates for $U_\ell^D$ are homogeneous of total-product degree $D$, then this refutation has total-product degree at most $3K$.

A general degree transfer follows. Suppose $1=\sum_j h_jq_j$ has coefficient circuits and bounded total-product degree $D_0$, taking $D_0$ large enough that every $h_j$ has degree at most $D_0-2$. Homogenize the coefficient polynomials to degree $D_0-2$, giving $U^{D_0}=\sum_j H_jQ_j$. The circuit homogenization is polynomial in $D_0$ and the original circuit size. One can compute the homogeneous components through that degree at each gate using truncated coefficient multiplication. Replacing $Q_j$ by the literal $e_{Y_j}$ equation divided by two leaves a sum of multiples of the $Y_j$. Its $(m+1)$-st power has a polynomial-size representation in their squares by the nilpotence calculation. Telescoping the powers then gives a homogeneous certificate for $U^{D_0(m+1)}$. This proves polynomial preservation of bounded-degree Hilbert-like circuit IPS certificates for arbitrary quadratic systems. The converse certificate transformation is the displayed coordinate specialization.

For the rectangular inverse system take $q$ to be the entries of $PQ-I_{r+1}$, with inner dimension $r$. Its determinant proof gives a polynomial-size Hilbert-like certificate of degree $O(r)$. Explicitly, $\det(PQ)$ vanishes identically, so $\det(I_{r+1})-\det(PQ)$ belongs to the ideal of the entries $I_{r+1}-PQ$ by telescoping columns. The coefficients are cofactors of mixed matrices and have polynomial-size division-free determinant circuits. The degree transfer above gives a polynomial-size circuit refutation of the degree system. The exact rank-principle degree and the factorial sparse-size lower bound transfer in the opposite direction by the specialization. [Rank Principle degree and sparse-size separation](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/09884ecd143d669cf5a5b9f1734264d8240c4e10/ExactMatrixProofDegrees/RankPrinciple.md)

### The sharp bounded-degree family

Take bounded-degree expanders with $n$ vertices and an odd total Tseitin charge. Use two complementary literal variables $a_{i,0},a_{i,1}$ for each edge $i$. Their axioms are $a_{i,b}^2-a_{i,b}$ and $a_{i,0}+a_{i,1}-1$. For each forbidden assignment at a vertex insert the product of the corresponding literals. Call this the source PCR encoding, with sparse size counted as monomial occurrences. The signed encoding uses $s_i^2=1$ and one equation $\prod_{i\ni v}s_i=\epsilon_v$ per vertex, where $\prod_v\epsilon_v=-1$. The expander PC lower bound gives degree $\Omega(n)$ for the signed encoding over every field of characteristic different from two. In the proof by cuts the parity of a vertex subset is simply replaced by the sum of its charges, which is additive under symmetric difference. [Buss–Grigoriev–Impagliazzo–Pitassi, Theorem 10](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/lineargaps/journalfinal.pdf#page=15)

The PCR degree lower bound follows by substituting $a_{i,b}\mapsto(1+(-1)^bs_i)/2$. The Boolean and complement equations have degree-two consequences. The product for a forbidden assignment can be multiplied by the signed vertex monomial. Reduction using $s_i^2=1$ yields the sign of that assignment times the same product. The vertex equation gives the opposite sign, so subtraction and division by two yield the product as a constant-degree consequence. The degree cost is bounded by twice the fixed maximum degree of the graph. Thus a sublinear PCR degree would contradict the signed lower bound. The Alekhnovich–Ben-Sasson–Razborov–Wigderson size–degree theorem gives a sparse PCR lower bound $2^{\Omega(n)}$ because there are $O(n)$ variables and the input degree is bounded. [PCR size–degree tradeoff](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/MYPAPERS/RAZBOROV/GENERATOR/FINAL/abrw00.pdf#page=16)

Compile every forbidden product into a chain of quadratic gates. Use private variables for successive partial products, with an equality between each variable and the product of a preceding variable and the next literal, and set the final product to zero. A chain of length zero suffices for a forbidden product of degree at most two, using that product itself as an equation. These gates give the $q_j$ with $O(n)$ variables, equations and total support. On setting every private variable to its intended monomial, each gate becomes zero or an original PCR input. Every substituted monomial has degree at most the fixed maximum vertex degree. The two sparse substitutions, first from the degree system and then through the gates, prove the degree and size lower bounds in the theorem. The resulting cubic has $O(n)$ nonzero trilinear entries and $b_2(M)=\Theta(n)$. All coordinates and equations here are fixed by the expander and the charge and require no choice depending on the coefficient field.

### A linear-degree circuit certificate for the sharp family

In the chart $U=1$ of one square-zero class, set $f_j=q_j+2W_jY_j$ and $g_j=Y_j^2$. These are the literal $e_{Y_j}$ and $e_{W_j}$ equations divided by two and dehomogenized. Work first modulo the $f_j,g_j$. Each old equation $q_j$ is a multiple of a specified square-zero coordinate, and each has the explicit representation $q_j=f_j-2W_jY_j$. We show that the parity contradiction can be recovered here by bounded-degree local certificates followed by one linear-degree circuit calculation.

For each edge use the first literal coordinate $a=a_{i,0}$ and put
$$
       A_{i,0}=3a^2-2a^3,\qquad A_{i,1}=1-A_{i,0}.
$$
Since $(a^2-a)^2=0$ modulo the current equations, both $A_{i,b}$ are idempotent, and they are exactly complementary. For the first assertion, $(3a^2-2a^3)^2-(3a^2-2a^3)$ is divisible over $\mathbb Z$ by $(a^2-a)^2$. This can be seen from double vanishing at $0,1$, or by polynomial division by the monic product. Also $A_{i,0}-a_{i,0}=(a^2-a)(1-2a)$. The second corrected literal differs from $a_{i,1}$ by a combination of the error of the first Boolean axiom and the complement error. Thus all the preceding claims have constant-degree local ideal representations where needed.

Fix a forbidden assignment at one vertex. Its original literal product is zero modulo the gate equations, with a bounded-degree representation because the vertex degree is bounded. Its corrected product $T$ differs by multiples of the errors of the relevant Boolean, complement and gate equations. Consequently modulo the $f_j,g_j$, $T$ lies in the ideal of a bounded number $k$ of specified $Y_j$ coordinates. Each has square zero, so $T^{k+1}=0$, with an ideal representation of bounded degree by a finite expansion or the nilpotence calculation. Yet $T$ is idempotent, being a product of idempotents in a commutative ring. Thus $T=0$ modulo the $f_j,g_j$ by a bounded-degree identity. For example $T^{k+1}-T=(T^2-T)(1+T+\cdots+T^{k-1})$. These calculations derive one corrected forbidden product at a time from only a bounded number of literal inputs.

Now set $s_i=A_{i,0}-A_{i,1}$. Its square is one modulo the local inputs. Expand the signed product at a vertex into the finitely many literal products. The forbidden products vanish, and each allowed product has the prescribed sign. Since the sum of all the products is one by the complement equations, this proves the exact vertex equation for the $s_i$, with a bounded-degree Hilbert-like representation. Multiply all those vertex equations telescopically. Each edge now occurs twice, and the square identities yield the total product $1$. The prescribed vertex signs multiply to $-1$. Consequently $1$ has an ideal representation in the $f_j,g_j$ of total-product degree $O(n)$ and polynomial-size coefficient circuits, since all the local operations have bounded degree and the global products contain $O(n)$ factors of bounded degree. Division by two is valid over the working field.

Homogenize this degree-$O(n)$ identity with $U$. Its labeled generators homogenize precisely to the original $e_{Y_j}$ and $e_{W_j}$ equations, up to the same factor of two. It gives $U^D$ in the original square equation ideal, with a homogeneous certificate of total-product degree $D=O(n)$ and polynomial circuit size. Use this for the three classes. Since $m=O(n)$, the $d^K$ construction above gives a polynomial-size Hilbert-like IPS refutation of degree $O(n)$. Expanding the coefficient polynomials of that degree in $O(n)$ variables and implementing their monomial multiples and sums in PC gives degree $O(n)$ and sparse size $2^{O(n)}$. The coefficient expansion and every intermediate line have at most $\binom{O(n)}{O(n)}=2^{O(n)}$ terms. This proves the matching upper bounds.

The negative topological instances here are degree-one mapping problems to one fixed target. The proof does not require varying a coordinate field equation or a Boolean axiom on the sought cohomology vectors. The Boolean relations belong to the lower-bound source and occur as consequences on the calibrated chart, not as extra literal inputs to the cohomological system.

The constructed topological examples have no map of any nonzero degree, since a nonzero integer triple pairing yields a rational solution of the Tseitin constraints. Over each field of the stated characteristic, replacing one by any nonzero field element gives the same complexity bounds by scaling the first class. Polynomial Calculus degree is also unchanged under an invertible change of cohomology basis. Both the variables and the square-equation labels then change linearly, so each variable multiplication can be simulated by the corresponding linear combination without increasing degree. The sharp sparse-size bounds use the specified integral basis.

## Characteristic two has polynomial-size PC

Every geometrically infeasible degree-one cup system of this form has a Polynomial Calculus refutation of degree at most six and polynomial sparse size in characteristic two, for an arbitrary cup tensor.

Write the square equations as $Ly_\ell=0$, where $y_{\ell,j}=v_{\ell,j}^2$ and $L_{ij}=\mu(e_j,e_j,e_i)$. The mixed terms cancel in characteristic two. The polynomial $d^2$ is a trilinear form $D(y_1,y_2,y_3)$ with squared coefficients. Every coordinate of each $y_\ell$ has a square root in the algebraic closure. A nonzero value of $D$ on $(\ker L)^3$ would therefore give three square-zero classes with nonzero $d$. Scaling one would give $d=1$. Geometric infeasibility implies that $D$ vanishes on $(\ker L)^3$.

Choose linear coordinates on each $y_\ell$ with initial coordinates a basis of the row space of $L$. In these coordinates every surviving monomial of $D$ contains one of the initial coordinates, since the restriction of $D$ to the three kernels is zero. Substitute back to express $d^2$ as a sum of literal square equations times polynomials of degree at most four. This uses polynomially many terms in expanded form. Then $1=d^2-(d-1)(d+1)$ gives the claimed refutation.

## Sum-of-squares degree and stability of the mapping equations

For the expander family above, the minimum degree of a Sum-of-Squares refutation over the reals is $\Theta(N)$, where $N=b_2(M)$. The same equations have a uniform real stability exponent. If the three cohomology-coordinate vectors have absolute coordinate bounds one and every square-equation residual has absolute value at most $\varepsilon\le1$, then
$$
  |\mu(v_1,v_2,v_3)|\ \le C_\Delta\bigl(\varepsilon^{1/4}+N\sqrt{\varepsilon}\bigr).
$$
Here $\Delta$ is the fixed degree bound of the expander family and $C_\Delta$ is independent of the number of vertices. This is an approximate nonexistence bound for the same concrete equations used in the proof-complexity separation. A related quantitative obstruction estimate for algebra homomorphisms into an exterior algebra is used in [Berdnikov–Guth–Manin, Lemma 2.19](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10562505#page=19).

### Sum-of-squares degree

Let $G$ be a connected graph on $n$ vertices with $|\delta S|\ge h|S|$ for $|S|\le n/2$, where $h>0$ is an absolute constant and $\delta S$ is its edge cut. Write $\epsilon_v\in\{1,-1\}$ for the signed charges, and put $\chi(S)=\prod_{v\in S}\epsilon_v$. Take an integer $k$ with $2k/h+1<n/2$ and $4k/h<n/2$. For a monomial of degree at most $2k$ in signed edge variables $s_e$, let $D$ be the set of edges with odd exponent. Define a linear functional $E$ on these monomials by
$$
  E(m)=
  \begin{cases}
     \chi(S),&D=\delta S,\quad |S|<n/2,\\
     0,&\text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
$$
The eligible $S$, if present, is unique by connectedness and has $|S|\le2k/h$. Also $E(1)=1$. The functional annihilates all degree-$2k$ multiples of $s_e^2-1$ because the parity support is unchanged. To check the vertex equation $\prod_{e\ni v}s_e-\epsilon_v$, consider one multiplying monomial allowed by the degree cutoff. Multiplication by the vertex monomial toggles the cut $\delta\{v\}$. If either parity support has a small witness $S$, the other has witness $S\triangle\{v\}$, still of size less than $n/2$. Their signs differ by $\epsilon_v$. If neither has a small witness, both expectations vanish. This annihilates all allowed multiples of the vertex equations.

On monomials of degree at most $k$, declare $m\sim m'$ if the parity support of $mm'$ has a small cut witness. This is an equivalence relation. In the only nontrivial transitivity step the witness is the symmetric difference of two witnesses, each of size at most $2k/h$, whose sum of sizes is less than $n/2$. For a class choose a representative $m_0$. If $m,m'$ lie in its class, the unique small cut witness for $mm'$ is the symmetric difference of the witnesses for $mm_0$ and $m'm_0$. Therefore $E(mm')=E(mm_0)E(m'm_0)$. The moment matrix is zero between different classes and a rank-one positive semidefinite block within each class. Thus $E(p^2)\ge0$ for every polynomial $p$ of degree at most $k$. A degree-$2k$ refutation would express $-1$ as sums of such squares and the allowed axiom multiples, contrary to $E(-1)=-1$. Taking $k$ a sufficiently small positive constant times $n$ gives a linear-degree lower bound for the signed system. The cut argument for the equivalence relation is the same one underlying [Buss–Grigoriev–Impagliazzo–Pitassi, Theorem 10](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/lineargaps/journalfinal.pdf#page=15).

Specialize the manifold variables to the degree-one slice from the preceding construction, then substitute every private chain variable by its intended literal monomial and every literal by $(1+(-1)^b s_e)/2$. A sum of squares stays a sum of squares, and this substitution multiplies degree by at most the fixed vertex-degree bound. Each substituted literal input is zero or a scalar multiple of a Boolean, complement, or forbidden-product input under the sign substitution. Complement inputs become zero and Boolean inputs become $(s_e^2-1)/4$. A forbidden product $L=\prod_{e\ni v}(1+\sigma_e s_e)/2$ belongs to the signed axiom ideal in degree at most $2\Delta$. For each factor $\ell_e=(1+\sigma_e s_e)/2$, the identity $s_e\ell_e-\sigma_e\ell_e=\sigma_e(s_e^2-1)/2$ gives the product relation $L(\prod_{e\ni v}s_e-\prod_{e\ni v}\sigma_e)$ by telescoping. Subtract $L$ times the vertex equation and divide by $2\epsilon_v$, since $\prod_e\sigma_e=-\epsilon_v$. Substituting these bounded-degree ideal representations gives a signed Sum-of-Squares refutation from any cohomology Sum-of-Squares refutation, with degree at most a constant times the original degree plus a constant. The lower bound follows. The explicit $O(N)$-degree Hilbert-like certificate in the preceding construction is an ideal-only Sum-of-Squares refutation after a sign change, giving the upper bound.

### Uniform stability of the real obstruction

An assignment to the dehomogenized Tseitin quadratic gates with residuals at most $\eta$ rounds to an exact solution when $\eta$ is below a positive constant depending only on $\Delta$. For each literal $a$, the bound $|a(a-1)|\le\eta$ makes its distance to $\{0,1\}$ at most $2\eta$, since at least one of the two distances is at least one half. Complementary pairs still sum to one after rounding if $5\eta<1$. For each private chain, all original literals have absolute value at most two. If $|z-\bar z|\le B\eta$ for a private partial-product value and its intended rounded value, the gate $|z'-za|\le\eta$ gives $|z'-\bar z\bar a|\le (2B+3)\eta$. A chain has length bounded by $\Delta$. Thus its final rounded forbidden product is zero, since the old product is within $O_\Delta(\eta)$ of zero and the rounded product is either zero or one. All rounded vertex assignments satisfy their charges, contradicting the odd total charge. Hence an approximate gate solution at such residuals is impossible.

In one cohomology vector the square equations give $|Y_j|\le\sqrt{\varepsilon/2}$ and $|Q_j(U,x)|\le\varepsilon/2+2\sqrt{\varepsilon/2}$ because $|W_j|\le1$. If $U\ne0$, dividing the latter by $|U|^2$ bounds all residuals at $x/U$. The rounding contradiction gives $|U|\le C_\Delta\varepsilon^{1/4}$, including the case $U=0$. The $Ubc$ terms of the triple pairing now contribute $O_\Delta(\varepsilon^{1/4})$. All other terms have at least one $Y_j$ factor, uniformly bounded coefficients and $O_\Delta(N)$ total support, contributing $O_\Delta(N\sqrt{\varepsilon})$. This proves the bound.
