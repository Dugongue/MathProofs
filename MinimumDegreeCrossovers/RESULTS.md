# Four consequences of the minimum faithful permutation degree proof

All four proofs are informal. The input model and approximation convention are specified in each theorem.

1. **NP-completeness of epimorphism onto the fixed dihedral group D₈.** This answers Elder–Shen–Weiß Open Question 4.1 as stated for finite presentations. The hardness already uses finite class-two groups of exponent dividing four. There is also a bounded-orbit permutation-generator reduction, plus #P-completeness for counting epimorphisms and counting transitive four-point actions up to equivalence. Together with the published non-nilpotent case and a central quotient lift, this classifies the decision problem for every fixed finite dihedral target. [Proof](Counting.md) · [Published question](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39487v1#S4)
2. **The optimal approximation factor is p at supplied orbit cap p³.** With explicit generators, a deterministic p-approximation constructs a faithful action for every permutation p-group with this orbit cap. Estimating the degree from above within any smaller fixed factor is NP-hard, already for class-two exponent-p groups at odd p and class-two exponent-four groups at p=2. The lower bound uses the exact span of reciprocal tensors in a finite field. In particular the sharp factors are three at orbit cap 27 and two at orbit cap 8. [Proof](Approximation.md)
3. **Exponential inapproximability for central quotients of Heisenberg powers.** At fixed odd p, the ambient group is a direct power of the order-p³ Heisenberg group and its supplied action already has minimum degree, with p²-point orbits. A central quotient turns numerical degree minimization into a minimum-weight basis problem for a linear code, where the cost of a word is exponential in its Hamming weight. The degree decision is NP-complete. A two-sided approximation factor $2^{c_p\sqrt{L/\log(2+L)}}$ is NP-hard for some $c_p>0$ in complete-image-list bit length $L$, and this exponent scale matches an upper estimator. Abstract isomorphism for those same quotients is equivalent to monomial code equivalence. A binary variant gives the corresponding results for central quotients of powers of D₈. [Proof](Quotient.md)
4. **One p³-point orbit makes exact minimization NP-complete.** All other supplied orbits have p² points. The odd-prime hard groups have exponent p, and the binary hard groups have exponent four. Removing the one large orbit leaves a minimum-degree action of a quotient by a central subgroup of order p. With every supplied orbit at most p², an optimal action is computable in deterministic polynomial time for any permutation p-group. [Proof](PrimeHardness.md)

The shared mechanism is that central characters dictate the sizes of orbits needed for faithfulness. The four applications use this relationship for, respectively, a fixed epimorphism target, a sharp approximation ratio, large central quotients, and an exact one-orbit threshold. The finite controls in this run check selected algebraic identities. The proofs themselves provide the general statements. Publication priority and external review are pending.
