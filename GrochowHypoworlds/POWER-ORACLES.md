# High-power oracles and exact extraction

The same cancellation argument refutes two degree-unrestricted exact oracle claims in characteristic zero. In the constant-depth model it refutes the modern version of Grochow's determinantal-ideal conjecture formulated as Conjecture 2 at ITCS 2026. In the general division-free model it refutes Grochow's black-box strengthening of the Factor Conjecture when the oracle polynomial may have unrestricted degree. The usual explicit-input Factor Conjecture remains a separate question. [Grochow, §3 and Conjecture 6.3](https://eatcs.org/beatcs/index.php/beatcs/article/download/620/633) · [Dey and Guo, Conjecture 2](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol362-itcs2026/html/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.49/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.49.html)

## Power elimination

Consider a polynomial \(H(x)\) computed by a division-free circuit with power-oracle calls \(Y_j=G_j(x)^{a_j}\), where \(G_j\) is the result of evaluating the base polynomial of that call on its actual arguments. Arguments may depend on earlier oracle calls. Cut each oracle output edge and replace it by a formal variable. Substitute the actual scalar values for those calls with constant \(G_j\), including zero. The remaining ordinary output polynomial in \(x,Y\) is \(R(x,Y)\). Put \(H_0(x)=R(x,0)\). Suppose the expansion of \(H-H_0\) obtained by putting in the real oracle outputs has at most \(r_0\) nonzero terms \(p_i(x)\prod_j G_j(x)^{a_j b_{ij}}\), where for each term some \(b_{ij}>0\) and each base occurring there is nonconstant. Then \(H=H_0\) whenever
\[
\min_j a_j > r_0(r_0-1)+r_0\max_i\deg p_i+\max(\deg H,\deg H_0).
\]

Indeed suppose \(h=H-H_0\ne0\). Restrict to a generic affine line preserving nonzeroness and degrees of the finitely many relevant polynomials. In one variable, write \(h\) as a linear combination of a minimal independent sublist of \(r\le r_0\) terms. Work with their Wronskian, nonzero in characteristic zero. One proof is to change to a basis of polynomials of distinct degrees. The leading determinant of their derivative matrix is a Vandermonde in those degrees. Put \(D_i=\sum_j a_j b_{ij}\deg G_j\), and \(a=\min_j a_j\). Every entry in column \(i\) is divisible by
\[
\prod_{j\ \mathrm{with}\ b_{ij}>0}G_j^{a_j b_{ij}-(r-1)}.
\]
This follows from the product rule, and remains valid when bases share roots. The Wronskian therefore has degree at least \((1-(r-1)/a)\sum_i D_i\). Replace a column of maximal \(D_i\) by \(h\). Minimality makes its replacement coefficient nonzero, so the new determinant is a nonzero scalar multiple of the old one. Its degree is at most \(\sum_{i\ne\max}D_i+r\max\deg p_i+\deg h\), using just the sum of column degrees as the upper bound. Since \(\sum_i D_i\le rD_{\max}\) and \(D_{\max}\ge a\), comparison gives \(a\le r(r-1)+r\max\deg p_i+\deg h\), contrary to the premise.

The crucial feature is that the cutoff does not depend on the degrees of the actual \(G_j\). Cutting the outputs produces a polynomial expression of bounded size even for nested adaptive oracle calls. Replacing each call by its selected scalar or zero preserves the depth and does not enlarge the ordinary circuit. With explicit wires counted, the number of variables, the log of the degree of \(R\), and the log of its monomial count are polynomial in ordinary circuit size and the number of input variables. This follows by converting ordinary gates to binary gates for degree estimation, and bounding monomials of the resulting degree in the bounded number of variables. The depth-preserving replacement itself does not require changing fan-in.

## Determinantal ideals

Take \(f_n=\det_n^{a_n}\) with \(a_n=2^{2^n}\). Laplace expansion places \(\det_n\) in the half-minors ideal, hence also \(f_n\). A proposed polynomial-size extraction circuit for a determinant of polynomially growing size has a cut output whose degree and monomial count are at most \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(n)}\). The extracted determinant has polynomial degree, so the cutoff applies for all sufficiently large \(n\). Therefore the proposed exact constant-depth oracle circuit yields an ordinary constant-depth circuit of polynomial size for that determinant. This contradicts the characteristic-zero lower bound for determinant. The same proof permits arbitrary nesting of the stated polynomial oracle gates. [Limaye–Srinivasan–Tavenas, determinant consequence in their survey, Theorem 8](https://pure.itu.dk/files/92949831/column.pdf)

In unrestricted depth the ordinary determinant circuit already suffices, so the constant-depth interpretation matters. The ITCS theorem retains a dependence on the oracle degree. The Andrews–Forbes result retains a border limit, which changes the setting because the ordinary coefficients used before taking the limit can have poles. Those two positive results are compatible with the obstruction.

## Factor oracle reductions

Over \(\mathbb Q\), choose multilinear polynomials \(h_j\) on \(j\) variables requiring superpolynomial ordinary arithmetic circuits. For each \(j\) there are finitely many wire-count-bounded topologies, with each of their coefficient maps parametrized by at most that many scalars. For size \(2^{j/2}\) their images lie in a finite union of proper algebraic subsets of the \(2^j\)-dimensional coefficient space. Choose a rational coefficient vector outside this union, using density of rational points. This is a nonuniform choice, as allowed for polynomial families. Each \(h_j\) still has a displayed monomial circuit of size \(O(j2^j)\).

Put \(L_j=2^{2^j}\) and \(f_j=h_j^{2^{L_j}}\). The family \(h_j\) is a polynomially bounded factor family of this unrestricted-degree family. With only a size-matched \(f_n\) oracle, power elimination directly implies that any polynomial-size exact oracle reduction to \(h_n\) yields an ordinary polynomial-size circuit for \(h_n\), a contradiction.

The same conclusion holds if there are polynomially many calls at different explicitly indexed members of the oracle family. For a proposed size-\(n^{O(1)}\) circuit, let \(J\) be the largest index with \(L_J\le n^K\), where \(K\) is a sufficiently large constant depending on that size bound. Inline all oracles with index at most \(J\), using the monomial circuit and repeated squaring, which costs polynomial size since \(L_J\le n^K\). For bounding degrees of the output skeleton, regard each of these low-index gates as an operation with total degree at most \(J2^{L_J}\). Cut the high-index output gates as before. The log of the degree of the resulting polynomial \(R\) is at most \(O(s(L_J+\log(J+1)))\) for wire size \(s\) of the original circuit. There are at most \(n+s\) formal variables. The log of the monomial count is at most \(O((n+s)s(L_J+\log(J+1)))\). The coefficient polynomials have the same degree bound as \(R\). Since \(L_{J+1}=L_J^2\) and \(L_J>n^{K/2}\), the minimum surviving high exponent \(2^{L_{J+1}}\) exceeds the power-elimination cutoff for large \(K\). Substitute the corresponding scalars and zeros and retain the inlined lower-index circuits. This once again gives ordinary polynomial-size circuits for \(h_n\), contradicting the choice of the family.

This counterexample uses unrestricted oracle degrees and exact evaluation. It supplies neither a small ordinary circuit for the high-power inputs nor a counterexample to Bürgisser's bound in terms of the explicit input's own circuit size. These proofs are informal.
