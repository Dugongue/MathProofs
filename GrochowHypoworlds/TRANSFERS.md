# Proofs of cross-question transfers

## Epimorphism promises and exact factors

For G06 choose a bottom-to-top prime order for the target's abelian Sylow tower. Such an order can be found in polynomial table time by testing prime orders. If there are \(t\) distinct primes, \(t!\) is at most their product, hence at most the table cardinality. The following construction actually factors all homomorphisms into a group of that fixed tower type.

For first prime \(p\), let \(N\) be the subgroup generated by all \(p\)-elements of the source. It is characteristic and \(G/N\) is \(p'\), since images of Sylow subgroups are Sylow in a quotient. Every homomorphism to a target of the chosen type takes \(N\) into its normal \(p\)-Sylow group, and so gives a homomorphism on the remaining quotient. Recursively construct the universal quotient of \(G/N\) for the remaining prime order. Let \(M\) be the preimage of its kernel. Every homomorphism of interest takes \(M\) into the abelian \(p\)-Sylow target group. Thus it kills \(K=[M,M]M^{p^a}\), where \(p^a\) bounds the \(p\)-exponent of the admissible targets and can be chosen as the larger of the source and specific target \(p\)-parts. The subgroup \(K\) is normal in \(G\), and \(M/K\) is an abelian \(p\)-group. The quotient \(G/M\) has the required remaining prime tower. For an empty list the quotient is trivial. This proves that \(G/K\) is allowed in G06 and that every relevant homomorphism factors uniquely through it. Conversely composition with the quotient preserves epimorphisms. The construction preserves exact counts and specified map values. It also works with elementary layers by using \(a=1\) throughout, or with a nilpotence class bound on each layer by adding its corresponding lower central subgroup to \(K\).

For G07 and G08 the radical-free quotient determines only polynomially many candidate preimages of the radical. Compute a chief series. For any epimorphism to a radical-free quotient, its kernel is an intersection of the centralizers of some nonabelian chief factors. In fact the surviving chief factors form a chief series of the quotient. The intersection of centralizers of the surviving nonabelian factors in the quotient is solvable, by intersecting its normal subgroup with this series. It is therefore trivial. Each surviving factor is equivariantly isomorphic to its source factor, giving the claimed intersection. At most \(|G|\) intersections occur. Retain candidates giving the correct quotient type and order.

In G07 let \(L\) be such a preimage candidate. Apply the elementary-layer tower construction inside \(L\) using the order of the target radical's primes. Its removed subgroup \(K\) is characteristic in \(L\) and hence normal in \(G\). The radical of \(G/K\) is \(L/K\); it has the required tower. The quotient above it is unchanged. Consequently the tame Sylow condition on primes shared with this quotient is inherited, since those radical primes are a subset of the target radical primes and the quotient has the matching Sylow types. For G08 instead kill \([G,L]\) and then the \(p\)-multiples of the central image of \(L\) for the target elementary exponent \(p\), killing \(L\) entirely if the target radical is trivial. The resulting quotient has the required central elementary radical and matching simple-product quotient. All epimorphisms with the given radical preimage survive. Conversely every epimorphism from one of these source quotients maps its radical onto the target radical, because the radical maps into the target radical and the induced epimorphism of radical-free quotients has equal orders. Thus the branches are disjoint, and both transformations also preserve search and exact counts across the branches. The decision versions give the two-way scope comparisons in the ledger. [Group Epimorphism questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7) · [Tame extensions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.01917)

## Colored abelian WL and coprime extensions

For \(A\rtimes H\) with \(A,H\) abelian and coprime orders, the primes of \(A\) identify it as the set of elements of orders supported on those primes. This is a bounded-variable word predicate. A same-size comparison group that does not give an abelian normal subgroup of the stated size and abelian quotient is distinguished by additional fixed-variable tests. For each element of the quotient, color it by the characteristic polynomials of its conjugation action on the layers \(p^i A_p/p^{i+1}A_p\), for every relevant \(p,i\). The coloring is independent of the coset representative since \(A\) is abelian. Each color is definable with a constant number of local variables using counts of the kernels of \(f(\operatorname{conj}_h)\) on those layers, with \(f\) running over irreducible factors of \(X^{|H|}-1\). The action is semisimple on these layers. A kernel count is a count of elements of the layer preimage satisfying a word predicate: products of powers of \(h^j xh^{-j}\) evaluate the action polynomial on \(x\), and membership in the next layer is an existential powering condition. Word evaluation here recycles a fixed number of registers. The exponents and formula lengths may depend on the group order because the assertion concerns stable WL dimension.

An isomorphism of the colored quotients matches the action characters on every layer, since the eigenvalue multiplicities of every marked group element match. Lift roots of unity of order dividing \(|H|\) to one fixed abstract system of complex roots. Character orthogonality for the abelian complement gives equality of all multiplicities of the scalar characters after splitting, and grouping Galois orbits gives the original finite-field layers. For the finite primary abelian module, the layers determine the action up to isomorphism. To see this, lift each simple module to the projective cyclic-length summand over \((\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z)[H]\) by lifting a primitive idempotent from the semisimple algebra modulo \(p\). A summand of longest length embeds by lifting from the last nonzero layer through multiplication by \(p^{e-1}\). The embedding is injective on its socle and hence injective. It splits additively by character extension over a finite cyclic ring, and averaging a retraction over \(H\) makes it equivariant. Iteration gives the decomposition. Layer multiplicity differences recover the lengths of all summands of each type. This proves that the colored quotient characterizes the extension, using Schur–Zassenhaus for splitting.

Translate counting formulas on the quotient to the group by representatives, replacing a threshold \(t\) by \(t|A|\). The equivalence relation, quotient multiplication, and the color predicates just described have bounded local variable overhead. If the ordinary group WL version starts with marked generated subgroups, those initial colors can also be expressed by testing equalities of evaluated words and their colors, keeping the bounded tuple and a fixed number of scratch variables. Thus an absolute ordinary WL dimension for arbitrary colored abelian groups would imply one for this coprime abelian extension class, giving G16 implies G14 and then the corresponding second-game consequence. The existing colored abelian counterexample leaves the consequent open. [Grochow–Levet, Questions 8.1 and 8.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.11487v7#S8) · [Group games](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.13725v7#S5)

## Two transfers of algorithmic hypotheses

For G12, with a constant number of source generators, find a generating tuple by testing all tuples with the hypothesized deterministic logspace membership algorithm. Given an image tuple in the target quasigroup, use the same membership routine in the product table on the paired generators. The subquasigroup so generated is the graph of a homomorphism exactly when every source element has a unique partner. Its domain already covers the source and closure gives the homomorphism property. Conversely the graph of any homomorphism must contain the generated subquasigroup, which then matches it on each source element. Add a bijectivity test for isomorphism. Count all accepted image tuples and stream a witness by table scans. A virtual product table needs logarithmic space, and the constant tuple length makes the count logarithmic in the input length. [Collins–Grochow–Levet–Weiß, Question 8.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00133#S8)

For G22 to the fixed-rank branch of G23, write an additive code over \((\mathbb F_p^b)^n\) by full row rank generators, with columns \(v_{ij}\) per coordinate \(i\) and slot \(j\). In the augmented column space add independent vectors \(e_i\) tagging coordinates and \(d_j\) tagging slots. Use the multiset of bare \(v_{ij}\) with each copy repeated \(M>b+2\) times, each \(e_i\) once, each \(d_j\) with a distinct multiplicity from 2 through \(b+1\), and \(e_i+d_j+v_{ij}\) with multiplicity \(b+2\). The types are disjoint and equal bare values simply add multiples of \(M\). A linear map on the augmented columns must preserve the bare span, permute the \(e_i\), and fix the \(d_j\). The tagged columns then enforce the same coordinate permutation in every slot. Conversely a block permutation equivalence extends. Comparing the resulting linear codes under column permutation is therefore exactly additive block-code equivalence. The length is \(O_b(n)\), so the time postulated by G22 is strictly subexponential in \(n\) on this branch. This leaves cyclic composite alphabets separate. [Babai–Codenotti–Grochow–Qiao, §7.7](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=7fd239cee76609d5c148686b94b738c495ad6446&repid=rep1&type=pdf)

## Finite-ring tensor isomorphism and proof measures

For fixed finite commutative rings \(R,S\), 3-TI over \(R\) many-one reduces in deterministic polynomial time to 3-TI over \(S\) if and only if 3-TI over each prime field of the characteristic support of \(R\) reduces to the same \(S\) problem. The ring compiler identifies each fixed-ring problem with simultaneous prime-field tests by primary idempotents and the same-prime reduction. Each prime-field problem embeds singly into the ring problem by taking trivial yes-instances on the other components. Conversely, apply the given prime reductions and combine their outputs with the ordinary conjunction construction for named tensor structures, which yields one TI instance over \(S\). This also applies when the target is rational TI, using its conjunction construction. It makes the comparison to a ring target precise without inferring reduction into a particular one of its prime components. [Fixed rings and prime support](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/PrimePowerTensorIsomorphism)

The polynomial-degree claims AP01 and AP04 coincide in the shared geometric system. Three-mode border rank \(b\) is at most ordinary rank \(r\), while all flattening ranks are at most \(b\), implying \(r\le b^2\) by decomposing along bases of two support spaces. Different exponents of rank and border rank accordingly survive. To pass to sparse proof size, at degree cutoff \(D\) in \(v\) variables there are \(M=\binom{v+D}{v}\) monomials. Follow the given proof retaining only lines independent of preceding retained lines. Before a multiplication step that needs a discarded line, reconstruct that exact line from the retained basis by at most \(M\) linear combinations. This preserves its actual degree at that step, and all reconstructed intermediate sums have degree at most \(D\). At most \(M\) independent lines are retained, so \(O(M^3)\) total monomial occurrences suffice. For balanced side length \(n\), \(v=O(n^2)\) and a polynomial rank-degree bound gives \(M=\exp(O(n^2\log n))\), subexponential in dense input length. This statement uses geometric no-instances throughout. Appending finite-field equations changes the proof problem and does not follow from scalar extension. [Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She, §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7)
