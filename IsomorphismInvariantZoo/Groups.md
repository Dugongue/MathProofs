# Five indexed invariants for finite groups and their extensions

The common design choice is to keep comparisons attached to a shared index. The Ward and Trellis experiments used this to retain compatible geometric comparisons. The Rune analogy asks which part of the record must be retained, and the Sigil analogy asks what changes under the joint isomorphism action. Here the relevant indices are factors, subspaces, projective parameters, coset constraints or kernel primes. The parallel subgroup tower algorithms used in Sections 1 and 4 are given in the [companion proof](ParallelDescentDependency.md). The following are informal results. The underlying subgroup series, pencils, cohomology and codes have established antecedents.

## 1. Factor-marked descent diagram

**Definition and covariance.** Given $H\leq Q$ and a marked normal subgroup $A=\prod_{\alpha\in I}S_\alpha$ that is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups, let $T_0=Q$ and $T_{i+1}=\langle H^{T_i}\rangle$. On the common set $I$, keep the $T_iA/A$-orbit partition for each $i$, the marks $[H,S_\alpha]\ne 1$, and the stabilizer labels
$$
 \left|\operatorname{Stab}_{T_iA/A}(\alpha)\big/
 \bigl(\operatorname{Stab}_{T_iA/A}(\alpha)^2
       [\operatorname{Stab}_{T_iA/A}(\alpha),\operatorname{Stab}_{T_iA/A}(\alpha)]\bigr)\right|.
$$
Here $P^2$ denotes the subgroup generated by squares. Use the same factor labels in every row. Every isomorphism preserving $H,A$ permutes the labels and transports the rows jointly. A characteristic choice of $A$, when available, removes the corresponding input mark.

**Exact recovery.** Write $U_i=T_i\cap A$. The chain $U_{i+1}\unlhd U_i$ makes each $U_i$ a product of listed factors. If a factor disappears at step $i$, then $T_i$ centralizes it. Indeed, $[T_i,S_\alpha]\leq U_i$ by normality in the previous term. Moving $S_\alpha$ would give a nonidentity commutator component in that factor, and preserving it instead gives commutators in $S_\alpha\cap U_i=1$. Later terms still centralize it. Conversely a term containing a nonabelian factor cannot centralize it. Thus for $i\geq 1$, the support of $U_i$ is exactly the union of the $(i-1)$-st row orbits containing marks. If $V_i$ is the complementary product, then
$$
 T_i=C_{\pi^{-1}(\pi(T_i))}(V_i),\qquad \pi:Q\longrightarrow Q/A.
$$
The preimage is $T_iA$, the complementary product is normal there, and $C_A(V_i)=U_i$, which proves the identity.

**Separation.** Let $Q=S^4\rtimes (C_4\times C_2)$ for a fixed nonabelian simple $S$. The quotient acts on one pair of simple factors by the nontrivial character with kernel $C_4$, and on the other pair by the nontrivial character with kernel $C_2^2$. Here $A=Q'$ is characteristic. Mark $H=S_\alpha$ in just one pair. Marking either pair gives one marked factor and the same chain of subgroup orders $|Q|,|S|^2,|S|$. The top row stabilizer labels at the marks are $2$ and $4$, respectively. Thus no isomorphism of the marked pairs identifies them.

**Cost and scope.** Given generators of $H,A$ and the factors in a permutation-group quotient, the parallel tower algorithm, factor orbit computations, point stabilizers and the first lower exponent-$2$ step compute the diagram in deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$. The subgroups for the stabilizer labels are obtained by lifting the point stabilizers of the factor action. This computes the record and the indicated layer support. Simultaneous equivalence of arbitrary records is a separate matching problem. The classical comparison is a permutation action on the simple direct factors of characteristic sections. The extra information here is how the same factor labels and stabilizers occur throughout a marked descent. [Kantor–Luks–Mark, good normal series and factor representations](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf#page=20) · [Seress, subgroup core and normal-closure chains](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511546549)

## 2. The obstruction to gluing local central extensions

**Definition and covariance.** Fix an odd prime $p$, finite-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$-spaces $V,A$, and the trivial action of $V$ on $A$. Consider families of classes in $H^2(P,A)$ indexed by the subspaces $P\leq V$ of dimension at most two. Require the classes to agree on every shared subspace and form the vector space $\mathscr L_2(V,A)$ of such compatible families. The obstruction is the class of a family in
$$
 \mathscr L_2(V,A)\big/\operatorname{im}\bigl(H^2(V,A)\longrightarrow\mathscr L_2(V,A)\bigr).
$$
A simultaneous change of basis of $V,A$ transports the quotient and the obstruction class. A nonzero obstruction certifies that the proposed local extensions cannot come from any one central extension with those identifications.

**Exact description.** A central extension of $V$ by $A$ in odd characteristic is determined by a linear power map $\lambda(v)=\tilde v^p$ and an alternating bilinear map $\omega(v,w)=[\tilde v,\tilde w]$, with the values written additively in $A$. The power map is linear because the correction in the $p$-th power of a product is a commutator to the $\binom p2$-th power. Choosing a basis and lifts yields a presentation with precisely these powers and commutators, so the maps determine the extension. Conversely any $\lambda,\omega$ are realized. In basis coordinates use the central cocycle $\frac12\omega(v,w)$ plus the sum of the coordinate addition carries multiplied by the corresponding $\lambda(e_j)$. Each carry obeys the cocycle identity and provides the prescribed basis power. Hence on any subspace $P$ the class is exactly specified by $(\lambda|_P,\omega|_{P\times P})$.

Compatible local power maps glue automatically, since any pair of vectors lies in a space of dimension at most two. A local alternating form on a plane restricts to zero on every line, so the overlap tests put no further conditions on the plane forms. If $V$ has dimension $r\geq 2$ and $b$ planes, the dimension of the obstruction space is
$$
 \bigl(b-\tbinom r2\bigr)\dim A .
$$
Indeed, there is one free $A$-valued alternating coordinate per plane, and a global alternating form is determined by its values on the $\binom r2$ coordinate pairs. The restriction map is injective.

**Repair and separation.** There is a global extension if and only if the local data are realizable on each subspace of dimension at most three. For sufficiency, define $\omega(v,w)$ by the assigned class on $\langle v,w\rangle$. Any additivity or homogeneity test on these values is contained in a space of dimension at most three and so holds by its assumed realization. Necessity follows by restriction. For a counterexample to pairwise overlap alone take $V=\mathbb F_p^3$, $A=\mathbb F_p$, zero power maps, and a nonzero alternating class on exactly one plane. All overlaps agree. A nonzero alternating form on $V$ has a radical line and vanishes on precisely the planes containing that line. It is therefore nonzero on $p^2$ planes. The prescribed one-plane support does not glue.

**Cost and scope.** On the explicit local table, restrictions, consistency, the obstruction space and the rank-three repair are linear-algebra computations. With a Cayley table for $V$ all subspaces of bounded dimension at most three can be enumerated from tuples of bounded length in time polynomial in the table length. In compact vector coordinates the full table can be exponentially large. This result is about odd characteristic and trivial coefficients; it does not assert arbitrary subgroup-detection or arbitrary extension gluing. The use of cohomological restriction and essential classes is classical. The extra test here is the precise failure of pairwise overlaps and the exact arity-three repair in this degree and setting. [Aksu–Green, essential cohomology](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/42749) · [Grochow–Qiao, extension and cohomology compatibility](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.1776)

## 3. Projective image intersections for square-zero actions

**Definition and covariance.** Let $V=\mathbb F_p^2$ act on $A$ by $\rho(v)=I+N_v$, where $N_vN_w=0$ for all $v,w$. Put $W=\sum_v\operatorname{im}N_v$. Then $N_{v+w}=N_v+N_w$. Define the indexed test on sets of projective points by
$$
 J(X)=\dim\bigcap_{[v]\in X}\operatorname{im}N_v\quad
 (X\subseteq\mathbb P(V),\ X\ne\varnothing).
$$
A simultaneous module and parameter isomorphism transports $W$ and the projective point indices. The same definition works after extending the coefficient field in the pencil.

**Exact calculation.** A left singular block of length $l\geq1$ is the map $L_l(s,t)\colon F^l\longrightarrow F^{l+1}$ with columns $s e_i+t e_{i+1}$. At each projective point its image is a hyperplane with normal vector $(t^l,-st^{l-1},\ldots,(-s)^l)$. Distinct points give a homogeneous Vandermonde system. The normal vectors for $k$ points have rank $\min(k,l+1)$, as ordinary Vandermonde after a change of affine chart when necessary, or directly by treating the infinity vector as the final monomial coordinate. Thus on a direct sum of such blocks
$$
 J(X)=\sum_l m_l\max(l+1-|X|,0),
$$
where $m_l$ counts blocks of length $l$. If enough distinct field points are available, differences of this sequence recover the multiplicities. Over a smaller base field, the length-reconstruction statement may use a supplied finite extension with enough points. The definition and each available finite test still apply over the base field.

**Separation.** Use two square-zero actions with parameter pencils $L_1\oplus L_3$ and $L_2\oplus L_2$. Specifically take $A=F^4\oplus F^6$, with the pencil as an operator from the first summand to the second and zero thereafter. Both have rank $4$ at every point and intersection dimension $2$ at every pair of distinct points. The triple intersections have dimension $1$ for the first action and $0$ for the second. This works even over $\mathbb F_2$, where there are exactly three projective points. The split extensions are nonisomorphic even as abstract groups. Any abelian subgroup $H$ of one extension that is not contained in $A$ has a nonzero image $v$ in $V$. The intersection $H\cap A$ then lies in $\ker N_v$, which has dimension $6$. Since the quotient image has order at most $p^2$, we get $|H|\leq p^8$. Thus $A$, of order $p^{10}$, is the unique abelian subgroup of that order and every group isomorphism must carry it to its counterpart.

**Cost and scope.** Each value is found by linear algebra. All entries of fixed arity can be listed in time polynomial in the field cardinality and the action matrix size, and selected entries avoid enumerating the whole field. Two-variable Kronecker canonical form already classifies pencils. The contribution of this test is a low-arity experimental comparison with a sharp pair-versus-triple separation and a direct recovery formula on this subclass. There is no completeness claim for general actions or arbitrary pencils. [Van Dooren, singular pencil canonical form](https://perso.uclouvain.be/paul.vandooren/publications/VDooren79.pdf) · [Berger and Trenn, Wong chains and minimal indices](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2015.01.015)

## 4. Shared affine fibers of relative commutator tests

**Definition and covariance.** Let $A\unlhd Q$ be abelian, and fix a marked coset $xA$. For any indexed policy $(B_0,\ldots,B_{m-1};C_0,\ldots,C_m)$ of supplied normal subgroups of $Q$, let $T_0(u)=\langle u^Q\rangle$ and $T_{i+1}(u)=[T_i(u),B_i]$. Keep the indexed fibers
$$
 F_i=\{u\in xA:T_i(u)\leq C_i\}.
$$
Use the same coset coordinates for all entries, including if several policies are supplied. Isomorphisms carrying the marks transport the whole system jointly. Changing $x$ within its coset translates the coordinate variable in $A$. This is an invariant of marked group data with the supplied policies.

**Exact computation.** Every fiber, and the intersection of any supplied list of fibers, is empty or an affine coset of a subgroup of $A$. It has an explicit deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$ computation for a permutation-group quotient. First test the containment at the image of $x$ in $Q/A$ using the parallel relative commutator chain. If it fails, the fiber is empty. Otherwise select generators $a_1,\ldots,a_k$ of $A$. In $Q^{k+1}$, form $E$ generated by the ambient diagonals and $z=(x,xa_1,\ldots,xa_k)$. Start with the normal closure of $z$ in $E$. At each round use the normal closure in $E$ of the diagonal $B_i$ generators as the corresponding commutator partner. Compute the full relative chain in $E$.

An element reached by a formal word $w$ in these generators, including the normal-closure and commutator operations, has component values $w(x), w(xa_1),\ldots,w(xa_k)$. Modulo $C_i$ they lie in the abelian group $AC_i/C_i$, since the residual containment passed. Moreover the function $a\mapsto w(xa)\bmod C_i$ is affine with domain the additive group $A$ and range $AC_i/C_i$. To see this, move every occurrence of the correction and its inverse to one side of the word. Conjugation on $A$ depends only on the fixed cosets of the other factors, and the corrections commute in $A$. Hence the function is determined by its value at $a=1$ and its differences at the $a_j$. Thus evaluation at any fixed $a$ defines a homomorphism from the generated tuple group in the abelian output coordinates. Checking its generators suffices to check all formal reached words.

The formal words using the ambient diagonals include all constraints for $T_i(xa)$ in $Q$. Allowing the extra symbol $z$ in normal-closure partners imposes no additional constraints on a genuine solution. Under substitution $z\mapsto xa$, the normalizing constants evaluate inside $Q$, and conjugates of a $B_i$ generator still lie in $B_i$ by normality. This proves the exact equivalence. Solve the resulting affine constraints by taking the preimage under a homomorphism from $A$ to a product of output quotients. This has a generating tuple image and uses the parallel quotient lifting algorithm. Several tests use the same variable $a$, so one combined homomorphism finds their common fiber.

**Separation.** Over any odd $\mathbb F_p$, use a class-two exponent-$p$ group with five base coordinate symbols $a,x,y_1,y_2,b$ and two central bracket coordinates $z_1,z_2$. The symbol $b$ is central. Prescribe $[a,y_j]=z_j$, $[x,y_2]=z_2$, $[x,y_1]=1$, and other unspecified brackets to vanish. This is realized by the group law adding half the prescribed alternating bracket to the central coordinates. Let $A=\langle a,z_1,z_2\rangle$ and $B_j=\langle y_j,z_1,z_2\rangle$. These subgroups are normal, and $A$ is abelian. Test separately whether a lift centralizes $B_1$ and $B_2$. In $xA$, the $a$-coordinate of a solution is respectively $0$ and $-1$. In $bA$, it is $0$ for both. The individual fibers all have size $p^2$, but the shared intersection is empty in the first coset and nonempty in the second.

**Cost and scope.** The bound allows polynomially many supplied tests and cosets. Listing all cosets of an abelian normal subgroup of exponential index would exceed this bound. This strengthens one-word correction with a simultaneous relative chain and tests one shared affine variable across all marks. The closest prior tool is the linearization of word equations over an abelian kernel. [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Lemma 3.6 and Proposition 3.7](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf#page=12)

## 5. Cross-prime rank tests for constituents of coprime actions

**Definition and covariance.** Let $Q=A\rtimes V$ with $V=\mathbb F_2^r$ and $A=\prod_{\ell\text{ odd}}\mathbb F_\ell^{d_\ell}$, with finitely many prime factors. Each action on an $\ell$-component is a direct sum of sign characters, since commuting involutions diagonalize over odd fields. Write the character as a vector in $V^*$, using $0$ for the trivial one. Form a column matrix $M$ with those vectors, repeated according to multiplicity and with colors given by their kernel primes. Keep the indexed ranks $\operatorname{rank} M_I$ for chosen subsets of columns $I$, including subsets crossing kernel primes. These are the restrictions of the ordinary matroid rank function of the columns. Changes of coordinates act by a common row-basis change and permutations respecting colors. Local computations inside each prime let different quotient basis changes act independently, whereas the cross-prime record uses only one.

**Exact comparator.** The binary relation code $\ker M$ together with the colors and the quotient dimension $r$ is complete in this split family, up to color-preserving coordinate permutation. Equal kernels after a permutation mean that the map sending each column to its partner extends to an isomorphism of their spans. Extend this linearly to the full dual space. The resulting quotient basis change matches the character multiplicities in each kernel prime. Conversely a group isomorphism preserves $A$, which is exactly the set of odd-order elements, and must transport the modules under one quotient change, so it preserves the colored code. For fixed coordinate labels, such as distinct primes each supplying one column, this is equality of binary subspaces. Allowing unknown permutations inside a color yields a colored Code Equivalence problem.

**Unbounded arity.** For every $k\geq2$, take $r=k+1$, use one-dimensional components at $r+1$ distinct odd primes, and let their first $r$ characters be $e_1,\ldots,e_r$. In the first group let the last character be $e_1+\cdots+e_k$, and in the second let it be $e_1+\cdots+e_{k+1}$. Every chosen set of at most $k$ columns is independent in both groups and both matrices have full row rank. Yet their unique nonzero dependency words have weights $k+1$ and $k+2$. The groups are nonisomorphic. This also proves that arbitrarily high cross-prime arity can be required even when every single-prime test and the rank of the full action agree.

**Cost and scope.** With action matrices supplied, joint eigenspaces are found by intersecting the plus and minus eigenspaces of the generators. At every merge only nonzero simultaneous eigenspaces are retained, and their number is at most the dimension of the module. The rank tests for a fixed arity and the relation code are then computable by linear algebra. No unrestricted polynomial-time Code Equivalence claim is used. The comparator via the constituent code is a known approach for coprime extensions. The added candidate is the explicit cross-prime rank hierarchy and its arity separation, not a claim to a first code-equivalence reduction. [Qiao–Sarma–Tang, coprime extensions](https://jcst.ict.ac.cn/cn/article/pdf/preview/10.1007/s11390-012-1255-7.pdf) · [Levet, parallel algorithms via Code Equivalence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13953)

### A compact permutation-group realization and its epimorphism problem

For a binary linear code $C\leq\mathbb F_2^s$, let $G_C$ be the preimage of $C$ under the coordinate sign map $S_3^s\to\mathbb F_2^s$, with each $S_3$ acting on its own triple. The group is $\mathbb F_3^s\rtimes C$, where $c\in C$ acts on coordinate $i$ by $(-1)^{c_i}$. It is metabelian. From any generating matrix of $C$, list a separate 3-cycle on each triple and one permutation per row which applies a chosen transposition in precisely its $1$-coordinates. These permutations generate $G_C$, since they include the whole sign kernel and generate $C$ in the image. The construction has degree $3s$, order $3^s2^{\dim C}$, and polynomial length in the code input. Conversely, given permutation generators, one can recognize the class with all orbits of size three and a supported 3-cycle on each orbit. Compute the orbits and test the individual 3-cycles by constructive membership. For a group in this class, the orbitwise signs of the supplied generators recover a spanning list of $C$. Thus the recognition and extraction are in uniform $\mathsf{NC}$.

The code comparator above shows that $G_C\cong G_D$ if and only if $C,D$ are permutation-equivalent. Indeed, the row space of the constituent matrix is $C$ in any basis of the quotient, so the relation code is $C^\perp$. This treats abstract isomorphism on the specified permutation inputs, including trivial characters and repeated characters. The analogous published sign-preimage construction over $S_5$ establishes hardness for Fitting-free input. Here the sign kernel is elementary abelian and the proof uses the sign characters of its conjugation action. [Grochow–Johnson–Levet, Theorem 8.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19777v1#S8)

**Epimorphism criterion.** Let $D\leq\mathbb F_2^t$. There is an epimorphism $G_C\to G_D$ exactly when $D$ is a puncture of $C$, after choosing an ordering of its retained coordinates. To see the forward implication, let $f$ be such an epimorphism. In each group the odd-order elements form the normal elementary abelian 3-subgroup. Its source subgroup $A_C$ maps into $A_D$. The quotient $G_D/f(A_C)$ is a 2-group, being an epimorphic image of $G_C/A_C$. Thus every element of $A_D$ has trivial image there, so $f(A_C)=A_D$. The induced quotient map $\varphi:C\to D$ is surjective. On the kernels, $f$ is an $\mathbb F_3$-linear surjective intertwiner for the source sign action and the target sign action pulled back by $\varphi$. This follows by conjugation and does not require $f$ to carry the chosen complements into each other, since the kernel in the target is abelian.

For every sign character of $C$, a source eigenspace must supply at least the number of target coordinates of that character. This follows because the intertwiner respects the simultaneous eigenspaces and is surjective. The argument applies to the trivial character in the same way. Choose a distinct matching source coordinate $i_j$ for each target coordinate $j$. The character equality says
$$
 \varphi(c)_j=c_{i_j}\qquad(c\in C).
$$
Hence $D$ is exactly the image of this ordered coordinate projection. Conversely, such a projection on the codes and the same projection on the ternary kernels give a surjective homomorphism of the semidirect products. This recovers the column-injection criterion of Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar in a concrete family, with size measured in permutation generators. Their polynomial-time result for coprime extensions takes Cayley tables as input. [Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar, Theorem 2 and §5.2](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol374-icalp2026/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.104/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.104.html)

**Compact-input complexity.** Epimorphism testing on this family is $\mathsf{NP}$-complete, even with equal dimensions of the two elementary abelian 2-quotients. Membership follows from the coordinate-injection certificate and binary row-space comparison, after extracting the codes. For hardness, start from CLIQUE in a connected simple graph $X$ on $n$ vertices, with desired size $k$. Connected instances suffice, since adding one universal vertex and increasing the requested size by one preserves the answer. Let $Y$ be the simple graph consisting of $K_k$ and $n-k$ leaves attached to one clique vertex. For a graph, take its binary cut code, the row space of the vertex-edge incidence matrix. Puncturing a cut code to an edge set gives the cut code of the corresponding spanning subgraph. Both $X$ and $Y$ are connected, so their cut codes have rank $n-1$.

If $X$ has a $k$-clique, extend a spanning tree of this clique to a spanning tree of $X$, then keep all clique edges and the $n-k$ extra tree edges. The extra edges are bridges, and their cut values can be chosen independently after choosing the vertex potentials on the clique. Thus the selected cut code is $\operatorname{Cut}(K_k)\times\mathbb F_2^{\,n-k}$, up to coordinate order, exactly the cut code of $Y$. Conversely, an equivalent puncture to $Y$ has a subset corresponding to the clique edges of $Y$. It has $k(k-1)/2$ edges and binary incidence rank $k-1$. For a simple graph with nontrivial connected component sizes $d_j+1$, this rank is $r=\sum_j d_j$. In each component, path sums of incidence columns span the even-parity subspace. Its edge count is at most
$$
 \sum_j \binom{d_j+1}{2}\leq \binom{r+1}{2}.
$$
Equality forces one nontrivial component and all its possible edges, since the difference between the two binomial expressions is $\sum_{i<j}d_i d_j$. At $r=k-1$ this gives a $k$-clique in $X$. Therefore the epimorphism criterion maps CLIQUE to the two groups $G_{\operatorname{Cut}(X)}$ and $G_{\operatorname{Cut}(Y)}$ by a polynomial-time many-one reduction with explicit permutation generators. The input orders can be exponential in their permutation degrees, so the complexity statement is specific to the generator encoding.

The NP-completeness of equivalent punctured code was established by Wieschebrink. The direct graphic reduction above supplies hard instances with equal quotient ranks for this permutation-group realization.
