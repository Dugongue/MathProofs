# Five invariant types for tensor transport

The terms Rune, Ley, Sigil, Ward and Trellis refer to the recovered early notes. The five entries below name concrete tensor constructions. Their mathematical ingredients have published antecedents. Each entry explains the role of the construction beyond that antecedent.

## Tensor 1. Joint adjoint determinant image

**Definition.** Over a fixed finite field $F$, for a 3-tensor $T$ let $A_{ij}$ be its pair-adjoint algebra. For the first two modes in matrices it is the space of pairs $(a,b)$ satisfying $aT_k=T_k b$ on every slice. Under multiplication of pairs this is an associative algebra. An invertible pair gives an ordinary tensor stabilizer by using $a,b^{-T}$ and the identity. For each $A=A_{ij}$ form the semisimple quotient $A/J=\prod_s M_{d_s}(E_s)$, where $J$ is the Jacobson radical. Record, for each common simple-factor label $s$, the multiplicities $m_{is},m_{js}$ of its simple module in the radical filtrations of the two associated $A$-modules. Choose the representation conventions in which the two slice equations are $aT_k=T_kb$, so the second stabilizer determinant is $\det(b)^{-1}$. The new observable is the simultaneous determinant image of all three adjoint unit groups in $(F^\times)^3$.

**Guarantee.** In mode pair $i,j$, the image is generated by the cyclic subgroups with parameter $u\in F^\times$ on the common factor $s$ and outputs $u^{m_{is}},u^{-m_{js}}$ in those two coordinates and one in the third. The radical filtration is stable and $1+J$ acts unipotently. A simple $M_{d_s}(E_s)$-module contributes $N_{E_s/F}(\det_{E_s} a_s)$, and the norm on finite-field units is onto. All semisimple units lift to units of $A$. Determinants of words multiply, so adding generators for the three pair images gives exactly the determinant image of the subgroup these pair-adjoint units generate. Multiplicities follow from the corresponding graded dimensions and simple dimensions. Radical, simple-factor decomposition and module coordinates are polynomial over a fixed finite field.

**Use and control.** Given one ordinary transporter, checking its determinant defect against this image is an exact test for correcting it by these units. For a $2\times4$ slice space containing every matrix unit, both adjoints are the same scalar. Over $\mathbb F_7$, this gives $(u^2,u^{-4})$, an image of size three with both projections size three. The independent projection test would admit nine choices. **Trellis** suggests keeping the common factor label and **Sigil** suggests passing to the determinant quotient only after accounting for the available gauge. The result treats the subgroup generated by the three adjoint unit images. An arbitrary stabilizer can contribute additional classes. Reduced norms and adjoint decomposition are classical. [Wilson, decomposing $p$-groups through adjoints](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.0201) · [Bürgisser–Ikenmeyer, stabilizer characters](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02927)

## Tensor 2. Principal determinant image from the tensor differential

**Definition.** Let $R=\mathcal O/p^a$ for an unramified finite extension $\mathcal O$ of $\mathbb Z_p$. Use the usual explicit power-basis encoding. Choose $k\ge1$ when $p$ is odd and $k\ge2$ when $p=2$. For an arbitrary tensor $T$ over $R$, let
$$
 L_T(X)=\sum_i (1\otimes\cdots\otimes X_i\otimes\cdots\otimes1)T .
$$
Record the additive submodule
$$
 I_k(T)=\{(\operatorname{tr} X_i)_i\mid X_i\in p^k\operatorname{End}(V_i),\ L_T(X)=0\}.
$$
It is basis-independent by conjugation. It retains the joint trace constraint instead of separate trace ranks.

**Guarantee.** The determinant image of the stabilizers of $T$ congruent to the identity modulo $p^k$ is exactly $\exp I_k(T)$, coordinate by coordinate. In the stated range the $p$-adic log and exp are inverse on the principal matrix domains and descend modulo $p^a$. For the latter assertion, changing a lift by a matrix divisible by $p^a$ changes its $j$-th power by a matrix divisible by $p^{a+(j-1)k}$, by telescoping products before dividing by $j!$ or $j$. The linear operators inserting the various $X_i$ into tensor modes commute. Consequently
$$
 \bigotimes_i \exp X_i=\exp\!\big(\sum_i 1\otimes\cdots\otimes X_i\otimes\cdots\otimes1\big).
$$
If $DT$ is zero modulo $p^a$, for $D$ the displayed sum, every nontrivial term $D^j T/j!$ is zero modulo $p^a$ because it has valuation at least $a+(j-1)k-v_p(j!)\ge a$. Conversely if the tensor-product action $G$ fixes $T$, then $\log G$ kills $T$ by the same argument on the terms $(G-I)^jT/j$. The identity $\det(\exp X)=\exp(\operatorname{tr}X)$ gives the claim.

**Use and control.** Given an ordinary transporter $g$, the existence of a transporter of prescribed determinants with the same reduction modulo $p^k$ is decided exactly. Require the determinant defect to be principal at depth $k$, take its log, solve one augmented linear system over $R$, and exponentiate a solution. $O(a)$ terms at precision $O(a)$ suffice by the valuation bound for factorials, and integer normal forms solve the linear problem in polynomial bit time. In particular for odd $p$, this computes the determinant freedom within a fixed residue class over every unramified precision. For a diagonal unit tensor, the first-order equations on the diagonal entries enforce the sum of the three traces to be zero. For the $2\times2\times2$ tensor supported on $100,010,001$, the three trace rows are independent modulo the three diagonal equations over any residue characteristic: if a combination of the trace rows is an edge-incidence row combination, its three edge weights must equal some $c$ and comparing the two coordinates in one mode gives $2c=c$, hence zero. Over the finite chain ring this gives unrestricted principal corrections by the same diagonal linear solve. **Rune** suggests retaining the exact congruence depth and **Ward** keeps the traces coupled. The exponential identity is standard $p$-adic Lie theory. This use computes a finite tensor stabilizer's exact deep determinant image even without smoothness assumptions. For $p=2$ a separate residue calculation is needed between levels two and four. [Lazard correspondence in matrix congruence groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.2060) · [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, tensors over commutative rings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00735)

## Tensor 3. Canonical joint contraction flags

**Definition.** For $U\subseteq V_1^*$ let $S_2(U)$ and $S_3(U)$ be the two support spaces of the contracted subspace $T(U)\subseteq V_2\otimes V_3$. For rational $\lambda\ge0$ record the largest minimizer of
$$
 f_\lambda(U)=\dim S_2(U)+\dim S_3(U)-\lambda\dim U
$$
together with the two corresponding support spaces. Rotate the chosen source mode if desired. The lattices and the support spaces are covariant under every ordinary transporter.

**Guarantee.** Each support map carries sums to sums and carries an intersection into the intersection of images. Thus the dimension sum is submodular on the subspace lattice. The largest minimizer exists and is unique by closure of minimizers under sums. For $\lambda<\mu$, uncrossing minimizers $X$ at $\lambda$ and $Y$ at $\mu$ against $X\cap Y$ and $X+Y$ gives $(\mu-\lambda)(\dim X-\dim(X\cap Y))\le0$. The largest minimizers therefore form a flag. To compute for $\lambda=r/s>0$, combine all tensor slices as a matrix space from $V_1^*$ to $V_2\oplus V_3$. Repeat the domain $r$ times and codomain $s$ times with independent matrix-space copies between each ordered pair. If a subspace $W$ of the repeated domain projects collectively onto $U$, meaning $U$ is the sum of its coordinate projections, its joint output is $S_2(U)^s\oplus S_3(U)^s$. The maximum of the dimension deficit occurs at $W=U^r$, and its objective is $-s f_\lambda(U)$. Use a maximum shrunk-subspace algorithm, adding zero rows or columns to square the matrix space if useful. For dummy domain directions all maximizers include them, and projection removes the fixed contribution. Perturb $\lambda$ by a positive rational less than $1/(s(1+\dim V_1))$ to select the largest minimizer. Breakpoints are ratios of dimension differences with numerator bounded by $\dim V_2+\dim V_3$ and denominator bounded by $\dim V_1$, so the whole flag is computable in polynomial time by testing them and midpoints, also taking $\lambda=0$ and a point after the last breakpoint.

**Use and control.** The output is a shared collection of actual subspaces on raw tensors, available as anchors in a reduction. A small-gap instance may give inexpensive determinant blocks. To see why optimizing the two destinations independently loses information, take the direct sum of the tensor with two independent input slices in the same row and in different columns and its transpose. For projections to the two input summands of dimensions $a,b$, the two support dimensions are $\mathbf 1_{a>0}+b$ and $a+\mathbf 1_{b>0}$. At slope one each unary minimum is $-1$ and they occur on different summands. The joint minimum is zero. This is the **Ward and Trellis** principle of keeping constraints on the same anchor. The optimization and flag ideas have direct quiver and matrix-space antecedents. The specific tensor construction uses both output supports of the same contraction family and its application is to extracting raw TI flags. Some full tensors yield a single large block. [Ivanyos–Qiao–Subrahmanyam, noncommutative rank](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03531) · [Hamada–Hirai, vanishing subspaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02060) · [Quiver semistability and canonical flags](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.99)

## Tensor 4. First nonlinear stabilizer jet in equal characteristic

**Definition.** Over a perfect field $F$ of characteristic $p$, keep the intrinsic subspace of first-order stabilizer directions that lift to $F[t]/t^{p+1}$. It can be computed by the following map from $\ker L_T$ to $\operatorname{coker} L_T$. In each mode use
$$
 E_i(t,X)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}t^jX_i^j/j!.
$$
Take the class of the degree-$p$ coefficient of $\bigotimes_i E_i(t,X)T-T$. For the special-linear version augment $L_T$ by each trace and augment the output coefficient by each $\det E_i-1$ coefficient. All changes of basis intertwine these maps.

**Guarantee.** Lower degrees vanish by the truncated exponential identity and, for SL, the analogous determinant identity. Hence a degree-$p$ adjustment realizes exactly the additions in the image of the (possibly augmented) linearization. Conversely, every lower-order lift with the same first jet can be changed to agree with $E(t,X)$ through order $p-1$ by multiplying, successively, by $E(t^j,Y)$ for $2\le j<p$ with $Y$ a stabilizer first jet. At each step the leading discrepancy belongs to the Lie kernel because both choices stabilize through order $p-1$. Each correcting exponential already stabilizes through degree $p$, since its first possible error has degree $jp>p$. This proves that the vanishing criterion is independent of the lower lift. Multiplying two chosen truncated exponentials adds their degree-$p$ errors and adds their first jets. The same independence thus makes the obstruction additive. Scaling $X$ by $c$ scales the error by $c^p$. It follows that one basis calculation and ordinary semilinear kernel computation give the entire subspace, in polynomial time when $p$ is fixed. Over a finite field, inverse Frobenius on coefficients is explicit.

**Use and control.** In characteristic two, take $T=e_{001}+e_{010}+e_{100}$ and let each $X_i$ send $e_0$ to $e_1$ and $e_1$ to zero. The first derivative cancels in pairs. The quadratic coefficient is $e_{111}$, which lies outside the derivative image since one mode change can increase Hamming weight by at most one. These $X_i$ are traceless and their truncated exponentials already have determinant one. Hence both GL and SL can have nonintegrable tangent jets on a concise tensor of format $2\times2\times2$. **Rune** asks for the first precision that can distinguish the data, while **Ward** keeps the identical first jet across all lifts. Infinitesimal stabilizers and lifting obstructions are standard. The tensor-specific formula and example give a concrete test for the point where the linear proxy first fails. Success at the first nonlinear jet does not imply a lift through arbitrary higher orders. [McNinch, exponential maps in positive characteristic](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-4049(2801%2900038-X)

## Tensor 5. Apolar volume pins

**Definition.** Suppose an even-dimensional mode $V$ of dimension $2m$ has a covariant alternating-form space $S\subseteq\Lambda^2V^*$, supplied or efficiently recovered. Use a finite field of characteristic greater than $m$. Given a basis, let $P=P_S$ be its nonzero homogeneous Pfaffian polynomial. Use the graded apolar algebra
$$
 B_P=\operatorname{Sym}(S)/\{a\mid a(\partial)P=0\}
$$
with the marked generator map $S\to B_P$ and the functional $\ell_P([a])=(a(\partial)P)(0)$. The functional retains the volume scale. Multiplying $P$ by a unit leaves the underlying algebra and generating map unchanged.

**Guarantee.** For a tensor transporter with mode map $g$, write its induced action $s:S\to S'$ as $g^T S'(s x)g=S(x)$. The classical identity gives $P_{S'}(s x)=(\det g)^{-1}P_S(x)$. The change-of-variables rule for directional derivatives gives a well-defined map $\phi:B_P\to B_{P'}$ induced by $s$, with $\ell_{P'}\phi=(\det g)^{-1}\ell_P$. The algebra is generated by the image of $S$, so a compatible algebra map must be this map. Since $P\ne0$ and the characteristic exceeds $m$, a degree-$m$ monomial derivative detects one of its coefficients, so $\ell_P\ne0$. Pin $\delta^{-1}\ell_P$ to $\ell_{P'}$ to enforce $\det g=\delta$ exactly. Compile the algebra, its generator map and functional, the form evaluation relation, and the original tensor as a constant-block partitioned tensor with shared covariant and dual copies. This is the same multilinear-system compiler as in the previous monomial determinant proof. Constant-part removal then gives one ordinary TI query.

**Cost.** Suppose the exact Pfaffian polynomial is available in sparse coefficient form, with $s_0$ terms, and let $h=\dim B_P$. Starting with $P$, differentiate each accepted basis vector in each coordinate direction and accept each independent result. Every accepted vector is a monomial derivative of $P$ with at most $s_0$ terms. At most $h$ vectors are accepted. Their span is derivative-closed, hence it is the full derivative space and its dimension is $h$. Compute the structure constants of $B_P$ by applying the product of two corresponding differential monomials to $P$ and expressing its result in this basis. Reading the value at zero gives the marked functional. All calculations are polynomial in $s_0,h,m,\dim S$ and the field encoding. If obtaining the sparse polynomial is expensive, it must be given as exact auxiliary data or recovered by another proved procedure. The reduction does not assume a general sparse Pfaffian recovery algorithm.

**Use and control.** The previous support-Pfaffian result in our notes pins the full truncated symmetric algebra. Here **Rune** motivates discarding exactly the annihilator of the polynomial. **Ley** supplies the geometric pencil, and **Sigil** motivates preserving its scale-dependent action. The apolar data can be far smaller when the number of alternating parameters grows. For example use a linear alternating pencil
$$
 \begin{pmatrix}0&x_1 I_m\-x_1 I_m&C(x_2,\ldots,x_r)\end{pmatrix},
$$
where the coefficient matrices of the alternating lower-right block are linearly independent. Its Pfaffian is $\pm x_1^m$ because every matching pairs all $m$ first coordinates to the last $m$. Thus $P$ is visibly computable from this input, $\dim S$ can be quadratic in $m$, and $\dim B_P=m+1$. A dense degree-$m$ symmetric-algebra pin can be superpolynomial on the same parameter count. Arbitrary pencils can still have enormous apolar dimension. This combines classical Macaulay apolarity with our existing determinant pin and proves the exact compressed pin under the explicit complexity conditions above. [Lauret–Oscari, Pfaffian forms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.3060) · [Macaulay inverse systems and apolarity](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.05686) · [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, partitioned TI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00735)
