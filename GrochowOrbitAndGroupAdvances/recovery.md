# Single-orbit recovery without four-dimensional quaternionic constituents

Let \(G\) act faithfully and orthogonally on a real finite-dimensional inner-product space. If its representation has no quaternionic-type irreducible of real dimension four, one generic real orbit Gram graph with numerical inner-product labels determines the actual regular permutation group on the observed vertices. Competitors can be arbitrary faithful real orthogonal representations on their own generic loci, including quaternionic ones. This is an informal proof that removes the odd-residual-quotient hypothesis of the earlier result. [Mixon–Vose, Conjecture 2.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17434#S2) · [Earlier theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightEleventh/invariants.md)

## Point-fixing Gram symmetries

Write \(A=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}G\) inside the real endomorphisms of the representation. Require the seed \(x\) to be free and to avoid every nonzero quadratic equality between functions \(y\mapsto\langle gy,hy\rangle\), \(g,h\in G\). These are finitely many dense-open conditions. Let \(f\) be a Gram-label-preserving permutation of \(Gx\) fixing \(x\), viewed as a permutation of labels \(G\) fixing \(1\). Then
\(\langle f(g)y,f(h)y\rangle=\langle gy,hy\rangle\) for all \(y\). Taking squared norms of arbitrary real linear combinations shows that \(\sum_g c_g g=0\) implies \(\sum_g c_g f(g)=0\). Therefore \(f\) extends to a real linear bijection \(L:A\to A\) satisfying
\[
 L(I)=I,\qquad L(T)^*L(T)=T^*T\quad(T\in A).
\]
The operator identity follows by equality of the corresponding quadratic forms for all seeds. It also implies injectivity.

The real double commutant decomposition gives \(A\cong\prod M_a(\mathbb D)\) as a real algebra with adjoint, where \(\mathbb D=\mathbb R,\mathbb C,\mathbb H\) and adjoint is conjugate transpose in orthonormal models. The squared norm identity shows \(L\) preserves each factor: for input from one factor the other squared norm components are zero. In particular it fixes each factor identity. Applying the identity to \(T+I\) shows that \(L\) preserves the self-adjoint part of \(T\). If \(T\) is self-adjoint, its image \(T+K\) with \(K\) skew-adjoint has the same Frobenius norm as \(T\). Orthogonality of the two parts gives \(K=0\).

For self-adjoint \(S\) and skew-adjoint \(K\), subtract the separate squared norm identities for \(S,K\) from that for \(S+K\) to obtain \([S,L(K)-K]=0\). In each factor except \(M_1(\mathbb H)\), the centralizer of the self-adjoint part is the center. For \(a\ge2\), commutation with diagonal projections forces a diagonal matrix, and commutation with off-diagonal self-adjoint matrices with arbitrary conjugate entries shows that its diagonal entries agree and centralize \(\mathbb D\). The size-one real and complex cases are immediate. Thus \(L\) is the identity on real factors and quaternionic factors of size at least two, since their centers are real. On a complex factor of size at least two it preserves the Frobenius inner product and differs from the identity on skew-adjoint matrices only in the skew center. It fixes the trace-zero skew-adjoint subspace and acts by a sign on \(iI\). The sign is \(+1\): for nonzero trace-zero skew-adjoint \(K\), the squared norm operators of \(K+iI\) and \(K-iI\) differ by \(-4iK\). Under the theorem's assumption the only remaining factors are \(M_1(\mathbb C)\), where \(L\) is the identity or complex conjugation.

## Recognizing the factors that can reflect

The actual labelled Gram matrix determines the orbit as Euclidean points in its span, and determines the eigenspaces of its covariance \(C_x=\sum_{z\in Gx}zz^{\mathsf T}\). Each label-preserving symmetry preserves this covariance. For a generic seed, each positive eigenspace is one real irreducible. To see this with multiplicities, write an isotype as \(M_{a,m}(\mathbb D)\), where \(U=\mathbb D^a\) is its irreducible and the seed is \(X\in M_{a,m}(\mathbb D)\). The covariance is right multiplication by \(|G|X^*X/\dim_{\mathbb R}U\) on this isotype. In fact it commutes with the group, so is a right multiplier. Pairing it with right multiplication by a Hermitian matrix \(B\) gives real operator trace \(|G|\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(X^*XB)\) by equivariance. The real operator trace pairing on right multipliers is \(\dim_{\mathbb R}U\) times the real matrix trace pairing. Distinct isotypes have no off-diagonal equivariant maps.

Require each \(X\) to have maximal rank, and the positive eigenvalues of these right multipliers to be distinct over \(\mathbb D\) within each isotype and across different isotypes. These are dense open conditions: perturb singular values within each block and independently rescale the blocks. The inherent real multiplicity \(\dim_{\mathbb R}U\) of each such eigenvalue gives precisely one copy of \(U\). Every isotype has a positive block. Zero eigenvalues, including those from rank-deficient isotypes with more columns than rows, do not enter the observed span.

A positive two-dimensional eigenspace has finite irreducible orthogonal image. A cyclic rotation image gives a regular projected orbit polygon. An image with a reflection is dihedral and generically gives a nonregular polygon with alternating unequal angular gaps. The exceptional projected directions are finitely many lines in this irreducible plane. Avoidance is dense open even with multiplicity: on a simple positive singular eigenspace, the projected seed vector is represented by \(Xw\ne0\) for a unit right eigenvector \(w\). Replacing the real two-row matrix \(X\) by a nearby left rotation leaves \(X^*X\) unchanged while rotating those directions, simultaneously avoiding the finite sets for each positive eigenvalue. Locally the eigenprojections vary continuously, giving openness. Hence the planes with regular projected polygons are precisely the positive complex size-one factors, with orientation-preserving action. This argument also applies to a competing representation regardless of quaternionic constituents in other dimensions.

## Identifying the regular action

Identify two matching generic Gram orbits by their Euclidean Gram isometry. For any competing permutation \(\sigma\), choose \(g\in G\) with \(\sigma(x)=gx\) and compose with true left translation by \(g^{-1}\). This composite fixes \(x\) and induces a map \(L\) as above. Both groups preserve orientation on all positive two-dimensional eigenspaces with regular projected polygon. On each such plane the composite fixes the nonzero projected seed, so it acts as the identity. Complex conjugation on the corresponding factor would act as a reflection on that plane relative to its projected seed, so it is excluded. Thus \(L\) fixes the entire image algebra and the composite fixes all orbit points. Every competitor permutation is therefore a true left translation. Since the two free orbits have the same size, their actual regular permutation subgroups coincide.
