# Classical tensor transporter questions

These five results answer existing source-derived project questions 25–27, 30, and 39 in the tensor-isomorphism lane of the October 8 research catalogue. The statements below specify the operating fields and reduction models. They are written mathematics. The general special-linear problem in Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Open Question 1.9, is a separate question. Publication priority is unverified.

Throughout, coefficients of finite fields are given in explicit polynomial presentations. Isomorphism means an actual transporter in the stated field. A kernel reduction constructs each output from its own input. Side-length bounds refer to dimensions, and all finite-field reductions below write explicit dense coefficient arrays.

## 25. Orthogonal similitude tensor isomorphism

**Theorem.** Given three independently specified nonsingular quadratic forms on the two endpoints of a tensor comparison over a finite field \(F\), the existence of a tensor transporter whose three modes are quadratic similitudes reduces to one ordinary three-tensor comparison over \(F\). The reduction is deterministic polynomial bit time uniformly in \(F\), including characteristic two, and allows arbitrary independent multipliers. The version with symmetric bilinear forms in place of quadratic forms has the same conclusion. Both comparison problems, with forms supplied as part of their query, are ordinary-TI complete by kernel reductions.

**Upper reduction.** In each mode give the form its own one-dimensional target space \(L_i\) with displayed generator \(e_i\). For a bilinear form \(b_i\) the marked map is \((x,y)\mapsto b_i(x,y)e_i\). A tensor-system isomorphism acts on its two copies of the source mode by the same \(g_i\), and on the line by \(e_i\mapsto \lambda_i e'_i\) with \(\lambda_i\in F^\times\). Hence its form equation is genuinely the similitude equation
\[
 b'_i(g_i x,g_i y)=\lambda_i b_i(x,y).
\]
All three \(g_i\) are the maps occurring in the tensor transport equation. Each actual similitude transporter extends by its multipliers. Any restrictions supplied as additional linear or bilinear relations on the three multiplier lines can also be imposed on that same witness. For instance, a named tensor \(e_1\otimes e_2\otimes e_3\) enforces \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3=1\).

For a quadratic form use its formal coefficient vector \(q_i\in\operatorname{Sym}^2(V_i^*)\otimes L_i\). Include the surjective symmetric multiplication \(V_i^*\otimes V_i^*\to\operatorname{Sym}^2(V_i^*)\) as an order-three tensor, and the evaluation pairing tying the action on \(V_i^*\) to the inverse transpose of the actual \(g_i\). The multiplication is onto because the \(x_ax_b\), \(a\le b\), form the coordinate basis. It forces precisely the action \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(g_i^{-T})\); preservation of the coefficient vector now gives \(q'_i(g_i x)=\lambda_i q_i(x)\). This uses formal coefficients, so it retains the square terms in characteristic two. Given any admissible \(g_i,\lambda_i\), its uniquely induced dual and symmetric-square maps preserve these attachments. All roles and components have fixed number, degree at most three, and polynomial size. The fixed-type tensor-system compiler produces the ordinary tensor pair. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4) · [Linear-length mixed tensor-system compiler and witness extraction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IsomorphismCohomologyCrossovers/LinearLengthRings.md)

**Lower reduction.** In mode \(V\) use \(H(V)=V\oplus V^*\) with quadratic form \(q(x,\ell)=\ell(x)\), or with symmetric bilinear form \(b((x,\ell),(y,m))=m(x)+\ell(y)\). The relevant form is nonsingular in either model, including characteristic two. Place the input tensor wholly in the three primal summands. Every ordinary \(g_i\) lifts as \(g_i\oplus g_i^{-T}\), with multiplier one. Every padded transporter, including a similitude, takes each intrinsic mode support to its counterpart. These supports are contained in the respective primal summands. Restrict the mode map to those supports and extend it by complementary bases to a full primal invertible map. All tensor coefficients are supported in the product of the three supports, so the extended maps are an ordinary transporter. This proves hardness even with identical split forms at the two endpoints. [Hyperbolic support argument](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TIEqTISp.lean)

## 26. Symplectic similitudes reduce to symplectic tensor isomorphism with witness recovery

**Theorem.** The three-mode symplectic similitude problem over any explicitly presented finite field has a deterministic bit-polynomial kernel reduction to symplectic three-tensor isomorphism. Its output sides are linear in the sum of the original sides. Every input transporter lifts, and every output transporter yields an input transporter in polynomial bit time. Both directions include characteristic two. In fact both comparisons have the ordinary TI degree over the same field.

In the bilinear construction of Question 25 use nondegenerate alternating forms \(\omega_i:V_i\times V_i\to L_i\). Each multiplier is tied by the actual equation \(\omega'_i(g_i x,g_i y)=\lambda_i\omega_i(x,y)\), with no enumeration of the multiplicative group. The typed-to-ordinary compiler of *Linear-length tensor isomorphism over commutative rings*, Sections 1–3, supplies the multiplication table of a unital triangular algebra of rank \(O(1+\sum_i\dim V_i)\). The source-to-output and output-to-source maps follow its explicit proof. In particular, an isotopy of multiplication is normalized to an algebra isomorphism using the two images of \(1\). Conjugation by the unit \(\sum_j f(e_j)e'_j\) aligns the distinguished vertex idempotents. The prescribed corners are recovered as annihilators of their marked projection corners. Restricting there and dividing by the two scalar anchors returns the original role maps, including \(g_i,\lambda_i\) on the same witness. All these operations are field linear algebra, multiplication, and inversion of units of explicitly represented algebras. Conversely a system isomorphism extends by taking the scalar anchors to be \(1\), by the induced maps on all quotient corners, and by identity on the rank tags. [The explicit compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IsomorphismCohomologyCrossovers/LinearLengthRings.md)

Each mode support of a unital multiplication tensor is its entire mode. In one input contraction, multiplication by \(1\) followed by output coordinate functionals spans the full dual of the other input, and in the output contraction multiplication by \(1\) spans the algebra. Thus hyperbolic padding of the ordinary output into \(H(W_i)=W_i\oplus W_i^*\), with the form \(m(x)-\ell(y)\), makes the whole primal \(W_i\) intrinsic as the mode support. Every symplectic transporter therefore restricts to an invertible map on the original ordinary mode. Conversely the ordinary witness extends by the inverse transpose on the dual half. Both maps preserve the tensor, and the same calculation of original \(g_i,\lambda_i\) now gives exact recovery. The lift doubles the side lengths. Its coefficients use only the original coefficient data and \(0,1,-1\). The same hyperbolic padding from an arbitrary ordinary pair proves the lower bound for the similitude problem, by the support and complementary-basis argument in Question 25. [Hyperbolic padding with exact transporters](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SymplecticTensorFusions/RingTransporters.md)

## 27. Finite-field semilinear classical tensor transport

**Theorem.** Let \(k\) be an explicitly presented finite field and \(E/k\) an explicitly presented finite extension. For an \(E\)-tensor paired with bilinear, quadratic or Hermitian forms, allow a common \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/k)\) on the three modes. Each mode map is \(\sigma\)-semilinear, the tensor transforms by the induced \(\sigma\)-semilinear action, and forms are transported semilinearly. These comparisons reduce to one ordinary \(k\)-TI pair in deterministic polynomial bit time, uniformly in \(k\) and the extension degree. The same upper bound covers independent \(\sigma_i\in\operatorname{Gal}(E_i/k)\) when the tensor belongs to the tensor product of the underlying \(k\)-spaces of three possibly different extension modules. For fixed characteristic, the common-Frobenius problems for symplectic, split orthogonal and hyperbolic unitary forms are ordinary-TI complete over either \(k\) or \(E\) when forms are supplied. For Hermitian forms throughout, the supplied involution is the finite quadratic involution on \(E\), and \(k\) lies inside its fixed field.

**Upper bound.** Use \(E\) as a named \(k\)-space, retaining its multiplication and \(1\). For each mode retain the scalar action \(E\times V_i\to V_i\). The field-role map is exactly a \(k\)-algebra automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(E\), and each mode equation is \(g_i(ax)=\sigma(a)g_i(x)\). Relative bilinear and Hermitian forms are \(k\)-bilinear maps to the field role, so the same attachment forces \(b'_i(g_ix,g_iy)=\sigma(b_i(x,y))\). The involution of a finite field commutes with \(\sigma\). For a quadratic form, view its \(E\)-valued polynomial as a \(k\)-quadratic polynomial in the expanded \(k\)-coordinates. Include its formal coefficient vector and the symmetric multiplication map as in Question 25. The resulting equation on the same \(g_i,\sigma\) is \(q'_i(g_i x)=\sigma(q_i(x))\). The formal coefficients may also be recovered by evaluating the displayed quadratic polynomial on the \(k\)-basis vectors and on their pairwise sums. No division or point enumeration is needed.

To force the original balanced \(E\)-tensor transport explicitly, retain the two successive \(k\)-bilinear tensor-product maps
\[
 V_1\times V_2\longrightarrow V_1\otimes_E V_2,\qquad
 (V_1\otimes_E V_2)\times V_3\longrightarrow V_1\otimes_E V_2\otimes_E V_3.
\]
Their values span, so their attached actions are forced to be the induced actions, and the original tensor is a vector mark in the final module. These induced actions exist because all three maps have the same field automorphism. For a tensor over \(k\) with three independent extension structures, use one field role and corresponding scalar action per mode, separately, and use the tensor over \(k\) itself. Every permitted source action extends to the attached roles, while every system transporter supplies precisely the required field and mode maps. The number of roles is fixed. Standard field arithmetic and the ordinary fixed-type compiler give the claimed size and time. A \(k\)-tensor permits independent twists because it is balanced only over \(k\); a tensor balanced over one common \(E\) uses the common twist.

**Hardness at fixed characteristic.** Start from an ordinary \(E\)-tensor and adjoin an \(E\)-line with the two marked vectors \(e\) and \(\alpha e\), where \(\alpha\) is the supplied polynomial generator of \(E/k\). First use the ordinary mixed system compiler to get one ordinary \(E\)-tensor. The compiler is natural for application of any field automorphism to the coefficients, because its only structural constants are \(0,1,-1\). Therefore a \(\sigma\)-semilinear transporter of the output is a linear transporter from the compiled \(\sigma\)-twist of the source to the target. Reflection to the typed system imposes \(e\mapsto e\) and \(\sigma(\alpha)e=\alpha e\), hence \(\sigma=\mathrm{id}\). The converse holds by exact extension. Now place the compiled tensor into three primal halves of the hyperbolic symplectic, quadratic or Hermitian forms. The standard hyperbolic matrices have coefficients \(0,1,-1\), so the same padding commutes with all allowed twists. Recover a semilinear ordinary transporter by restricting to tensor supports and extending complementary bases. Forward extension into the dual half uses \(g^{-T}\) in the bilinear models and the inverse conjugate transpose for the Hermitian one. This gives ordinary \(E\)-TI hardness. The prior same-characteristic comparison transfers the hardness and upper bound between \(E\) and \(k\) for fixed characteristic. The upper reduction to \(k\) itself uses no fixed-characteristic assumption. [Fixed-characteristic ordinary TI comparison](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FixedCharacteristicTensorIsomorphism/Proof.md) · [Tensor-system compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IsomorphismCohomologyCrossovers/LinearLengthRings.md)

## 30. Compact quaternionic-unitary tensor isomorphism

**Theorem.** For complex tensors with Gaussian-rational coefficients, independent or linked actions of the compact quaternionic-unitary groups \(\operatorname{Sp}(n)\) in their complex realizations reduce to ordinary complex unitary three-tensor isomorphism, and from it, in deterministic polynomial time. The acting groups here have complex unitary matrix witnesses, as in the rational-input complex unitary problem. The reductions use linear side lengths in a fixed action type. For the underlying real quaternionic model, real tensors with rational coefficients and quaternionic-linear isometries of \(\mathbb H^n\) reduce to ordinary real orthogonal TI.

**Complex model.** On the standard complex model \(V=\mathbb C^{2n}\), the compact quaternionic-unitary group is precisely
\[
 \{g:g^*g=1,\ g^T Jg=J\},\qquad
 J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_n\\-I_n&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Indeed, under the usual identification of a quaternion as a pair of complex coordinates, commuting with right multiplication by the second quaternionic unit is \(g J=J\bar g\) (up to common sign of \(J\)). Given unitarity, this is equivalent to \(g Jg^T=J\), which is equivalent to \(g^T Jg=J\) since \(J^{-1}=-J\). Thus one may work with these two matrix equations without forming a tensor product over the noncommutative quaternion division ring.

For the upper reduction use the existing linear-length labelled-unitary construction. Its three anchor lines \(kz_1,kz_2,kz_3\) carry multipliers \(t_1,t_2,t_3\) of unit norm. The tensor \(z_1\otimes z_2\otimes z_3\) forces \(t_1t_2t_3=1\). The unitary maps on the linked plus and minus copies of a role in mode \(j\) are consequently \(t_j g\) and \(t_jg^{-T}\), respectively, for one unitary \(g\) on that role. For each quaternionic role add one fixed tensor \(J\) on two minus copies placed in different physical modes, with the corresponding \(z_j\) in the unused mode. The three factors \(t_i\) cancel. Its equation is \(g^{-T}Jg^{-1}=J\), exactly the defining compact symplectic form equation. If a data component already occupies the same blocks, use fresh plus and minus copies of that role and the same inverse-transpose identity couplings to link them to the reference copies. This increases the number of blocks by a constant for a fixed source action. The unitary rank gadget forgets the labels while lifting every named witness and recovering each actual named role on any returned witness. It uses orthogonally split standard Hermitian forms and copies the coefficients of the prescribed form \(J\). [Explicit unitary copy, anchor, and rank construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FiniteUnitaryLinearLength/Proof.md)

For the reverse reduction, place each ordinary unitary tensor mode \(V\) in the first summand of \(V\oplus\bar V\) with its standard positive definite Hermitian form and the complex alternating \(J\) above. The lift \(g\oplus\bar g\) is in the compact symplectic group. A transporter of the padded tensors restricts to a unitary map on each intrinsic mode support in the first summand, even when the support is deficient. Extend each restricted map to a unitary map of \(V\) by extending orthonormal bases. This gives an ordinary unitary transporter. A linked target action follows by first arranging full support using unitary rank padding, then embedding the independent support roles as labelled, mutually orthogonal direct summands of a common quaternionic space for each shared variable. On an inverse-transpose occurrence use the fixed \(J\) to conjugate that factor, since \(g^{-T}=JgJ^{-1}\) on the compact symplectic group. After making the ordinary unitary input concise, each physical support in the linked target is the full primal half of its designated quaternionic summand, up to the fixed coordinate change for inverse transpose. Covariance therefore preserves that primal half (or the conjugate half if the inverse-transpose map is used). The compact group commutes with the defining antiunitary quaternionic structure, which exchanges the two halves. Thus it preserves the entire designated quaternionic summand. Restricting and undoing the fixed coordinate changes recovers the independent unitary witnesses. Every such witness lifts blockwise.

**Real model.** On the real underlying space of \(\mathbb H^n\), keep the Euclidean inner product and the two rational fixed endomorphisms that right multiply by the quaternionic units \(i,j\). A real-linear map is quaternionic-linear precisely when it commutes with these two endomorphisms. For a quaternionic-linear map, preservation of the real part of the quaternionic Hermitian form implies preservation of the full form. Its other three coordinates are recovered by taking real parts after right multiplying one argument by \(i,j,ij\). Encode the two commuting equations by their order-two mixed tensors with the same normalized linked occurrence maps, and apply the orthogonal version of the labelled-unitary compiler. All supplied forms and endomorphisms have rational coordinates.

## 39. All five symplectic tensor actions at linear side length

**Theorem.** Over every field, the five actions
\[
 (P,Q,R),\quad(P,P,Q),\quad(P,P^{-T},Q),\quad(P,P,P),\quad(P,P,P^{-T})
\]
with all variables symplectic are pairwise equivalent by kernel reductions with output side lengths \(O(1+n_1+n_2+n_3)\). Over explicit finite fields the reductions are deterministic polynomial bit time uniformly in the field, including characteristic two. The comparison uses alternating bilinear forms, with the vanishing-on-the-diagonal convention in all characteristics.

**Reduce each source action to independent symplectic TI.** Regard the input as a mixed tensor on the named variables, with each repeated name and variance as displayed. Attach the fixed alternating form once per variable. The fixed-type ordinary compiler gives one unital multiplication tensor with linear side length. Its support is full in all modes. Apply the hyperbolic construction of Question 26 to pass to the independent symplectic action. Alternatively use the previously established exact ordinary-to-symplectic compiler. [Ordinary fixed-type compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IsomorphismCohomologyCrossovers/LinearLengthRings.md) · [Exact ordinary-to-symplectic compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SymplecticTensorFusions/RingTransporters.md)

**Make independent tensors concise without changing their symplectic comparisons.** Consider modes \(U,V,W\), with the original split symplectic form in each. To make support full in \(U\), take an additional hyperbolic tag plane in \(W\), with two standard partners \(z_1,z_2\). For each tag \(j\) add private symplectic coordinate summands \(P_j\) in \(U\) and \(C_j,D_j\) in \(V\), with \(\dim C_j=s=\dim U\) and \(\dim P_j=\dim D_j=R_j\). Take \(R_1=2(s+1)\), \(R_2=4(s+1)\); they are even, each exceeds \(s\), and \(R_2>R_1+s\). Place two identity matrices as one tag slice \(M_j\), one between old \(U\) and the private \(C_j\), and one between private \(P_j,D_j\). All data elsewhere occupy the original roles. Write \(s\ge 2\) for nonzero \(U\), though the construction also works for \(s=0\).

Contractions in \(W^*\) have rank at most \(s\) exactly when they annihilate the tag plane. On any nonzero tag coefficient the corresponding private matrix contributes at least \(R_1>s\). Thus a symplectic transporter preserves the tag plane and its symplectic perpendicular, the old \(W\). On the tag dual plane, singleton-support contraction ranks lie in the disjoint intervals \([R_j,R_j+s]\), while two nonzero tag coefficients have rank at least \(R_1+R_2>R_2+s\). Consequently each tag line is itself fixed across the two endpoints. The two row or column supports in the first physical mode are \(U\oplus P_1,U\oplus P_2\), which intersect in exactly the old \(U\). In the second physical mode the sum of their supports is \(C_1\oplus D_1\oplus C_2\oplus D_2\), whose symplectic perpendicular is the old \(V\). Thus any new symplectic transporter restricts to an old symplectic transporter. Conversely an old transporter acting by \(g\) on \(U\) extends with \(g^{-T}\) on each \(C_j\) and identity on the private \(P_j,D_j\) and tag plane; the inverse transpose of a standard symplectic map is again symplectic. The added identities remain fixed, so this is a genuine lift.

Every old support remains inside the new support by contracting solely in the old coordinate spaces, since each marker has new coordinates in at least one contracted mode. Both new marker supports together fill the enlarged \(U\); they include every auxiliary coordinate of \(V\). Contraction on paired private coordinates of \(P_j,D_j\) recovers the tag direction \(z_j\) in \(W\). Apply the construction successively with each mode as the role being filled. The resulting tensor is concise, all dimensions are even, and the fixed three rounds have linear size. This adapts the earlier definite-form marker argument to symplectic tag planes. [Unitary rank marker predecessor](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FiniteUnitaryLinearLength/Proof.md)

**Pass to any target sharing pattern.** Given an independent concise symplectic tensor, put its three separate modes into the three occurrences of distinct tagged direct summands, combining them by symplectic direct sum whenever the target shares a variable. Each occurrence uses its own summand even when it shares its ambient group with another occurrence. For an inverse-transpose occurrence, conjugate the embedded tensor on that summand by its fixed symplectic form matrix \(J\); in standard symplectic blocks \(P^{-T}J=JP\). An independent symplectic transporter extends block diagonally on each combined variable. Conversely, the mode support at each occurrence is exactly the designated full summand. Thus a transporter preserves every summand in the indicated occurrence. For an inverse-transpose action this says \(P^{-T}\) preserves it, equivalently \(P\) preserves it because it is a standard symplectic coordinate block and the full block form matrix satisfies the same \(P^{-T}J=JP\). Restricting to the summands, and undoing the fixed \(J\) coordinate maps, gives an independent symplectic transporter of the concise pair. The dimensions add, so the side bound remains linear. This settles every ordered pair among the five symplectic actions.
