# Quantitative bounds for low-degree graph multiplicities

Grochow and Urisman ask for a growth bound on the number \(f(d)\) of possible degree-\(\le d\) multiplicity profiles used in Proposition 6.10. An explicit bound is
\[
\log f(d)\le \left(\sum_{t=0}^{2d}p(t)\right)
     \log\bigl(1+(d+1)(2d)^{4d}\bigr)
     =\exp(O(\sqrt d))\qquad(d\ge1),
\]
where \(p(t)\) is the partition number. The resulting counting argument yields a degree lower bound of order \((\log\log n)^2\). An explicit pair of cluster graphs raises this to \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\) for the first degree at which their filtered multiplicities differ. In their full orbit modules the first differing Specht weight \(n-\lambda_1\) also has order \(\sqrt n\), with an exact first weight. [Grochow–Urisman, Proposition 6.10 and the paragraph following it](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26244v2#S6.SS4)

## A bound uniform in the number of vertices

Work in characteristic zero. For an \(n\)-vertex graph, take the image of the polynomial space of degree at most \(d\) in the coordinate ring of its \(S_n\)-orbit, using the displayed vertex variables \(x_{ij}\). This image is a quotient representation of \(\bigoplus_{a=0}^d (\mathbb C^n)^{\otimes 2a}\): send an ordered list of \(2a\) vertices to the corresponding product of \(a\) edge variables. This also gives the estimate for unordered edge variables, absent loops, and the Boolean quotient, by further equivariant quotients.

Each tensor power is a permutation module on ordered tuples. Partition the positions by equality of their vertices. There are at most \((2d)^{2d}\) such equality patterns for each \(a\le d\), counting the degree-zero tuple once. A pattern with \(t\) distinct vertices has stabilizer \(S_{n-t}\). Repeated application of the symmetric-group branching rule shows that \(S^\lambda\) has multiplicity zero in \(\operatorname{Ind}_{S_{n-t}}^{S_n}1\) if \(\lambda_1<n-t\). Otherwise the multiplicity is the number of standard tableaux of the skew shape \(\lambda/(n-t)\). Such a tableau labels its \(t\) boxes bijectively, so the multiplicity is at most \(t!\le(2d)^{2d}\). Semisimplicity lets us use these bounds for every quotient. There are at most \(\sum_{t=0}^{2d} p(t)\) labels \(\lambda\) permitted by \(\lambda_1\ge n-2d\), as all boxes below the first row form a partition of their number. Thus every possible coordinate multiplicity is an integer between zero and \((d+1)(2d)^{4d}\), proving the displayed estimate. If the complete list of profiles at every filtration stage \(0,\ldots,d\) is retained, multiply its logarithmic bound by \(d+1\).

For the asymptotic estimate, take \(u=1/\sqrt{m}\) for \(m\ge1\). The partition generating function gives
\[
p(m)e^{-um}\le\prod_{j\ge1}(1-e^{-uj})^{-1},\qquad
\log\!\prod_{j\ge1}(1-e^{-uj})^{-1}
 =\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{1}{j(e^{uj}-1)}
 \le \frac{1}{u}\sum_{j\ge1}j^{-2}.
\]
Hence \(p(m)\le \exp(O(\sqrt m))\). The polynomial factors in the bound on \(\log f(d)\) are absorbed in \(\exp(O(\sqrt d))\).

Let \(n\) be the product of the first \(z\) primes. Each nontrivial factorization \(n=k\ell\) with \(k,\ell>1\) gives the graph \(kK_\ell\). Its automorphism group is transitive on edges and transitive on nonedges, so it is symmetry-characterized in the sense of Grochow and Urisman. Distinct values of \(\ell\) give distinct degrees and thus nonisomorphic graphs. Every graph in this family is disconnected and each complement is connected. There are \(2^z-2\) graphs. Taking \(d=\lfloor c(\log z)^2\rfloor\) with a sufficiently small absolute \(c>0\) gives \(f(d)<2^z-2\), even when retaining all filtration stages, so two graphs have the same profile through degree \(d\). Bertrand's postulate gives \(\log n=O(z^2)\), and \(\log n\ge z\log 2\), so this choice is of order \((\log\log n)^2\).

## The first differing Specht weight for a specific pair

Let \(m\ge2\), \(n=6m^2\), \(X=mK_{6m}\), and \(Y=2mK_{3m}\). All multiplicities in the full orbit modules \(\mathbb C[S_n/\operatorname{Aut}(X)]\) and \(\mathbb C[S_n/\operatorname{Aut}(Y)]\) agree for every \(\lambda\) with \(n-\lambda_1\le m\). At weight \(m+1\) the multiplicity of \(S^{(n-m-1,m+1)}\) is greater by one for \(Y\).

To prove equality, consider each Young permutation module \(M^\rho\) with \(\rho=(n-t,\rho_2,\ldots)\) a partition and \(t\le m\). It acts on ordered disjoint distinguished subsets of sizes \(\rho_2,\rho_3,\ldots\), letting the undistinguished subset be their complement. For the automorphism group of a cluster graph, an orbit on this set is determined by the multiset of nonzero occupation vectors of its cliques. Each occupation vector counts the elements of each distinguished subset lying in that clique. The sum of all these vectors is the prescribed vector \((\rho_2,\rho_3,\ldots)\). The converse holds because we can permute cliques and then permute points within each clique. There are at most \(t\) occupied cliques, and each contains at most \(t\) chosen points. Both \(X\) and \(Y\) have at least \(t\) cliques of size at least \(t\), so both realize all such multisets. The invariant dimensions of \(M^\rho\) for the two groups therefore agree.

Young's rule decomposes \(M^\rho\) with Kostka coefficients \(K_{\lambda,\rho}\). These vanish unless \(\lambda\) dominates \(\rho\), and \(K_{\rho,\rho}=1\). Any dominating \(\lambda\) satisfies \(\lambda_1\ge \rho_1\). Thus the invariant-dimension equalities, read in decreasing dominance order, imply equality of the invariant dimensions of every \(S^\lambda\) with \(n-\lambda_1\le m\). Frobenius reciprocity identifies these dimensions with the corresponding full orbit-module multiplicities.

Now count orbits on \(m+1\)-element subsets, using \(M^{(n-m-1,m+1)}\). Clique sizes are greater than \(m+1\) on both sides. The multiset of positive occupation counts is a partition of \(m+1\), with at most \(m\) parts for \(X\) and at most \(2m\) for \(Y\). The only partition realized by \(Y\) and not by \(X\) is the partition into \(m+1\) ones. The two-row Young permutation module decomposes with one copy of \(S^{(n-j,j)}\) for each \(0\le j\le m+1\), by the horizontal-strip Pieri rule. Subtracting the already equal weights \(j\le m\) forces the asserted one-copy difference at \(j=m+1\). Both arguments are written proofs.

## Square-root degree separation in the filtered orbit ring

For the same pair \(X,Y\), the least filtration degree at which their multiplicity profiles differ lies between \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1\) and \(m\). Thus the worst-case lower bound in the authors’ filtered model is \(\Omega(\sqrt n)\) along the explicit orders \(n=6m^2\), even without counting profiles.

Let \(W_d\) denote the image of the degree-at-most-\(d\) polynomials on the orbit of a cluster graph \(kK_\ell\). Fix \(t\) distinct distinguished vertices. The \(S_{n-t}\)-fixed subspace of \(W_d\) consists exactly of the restrictions of degree-at-most-\(d\) polynomials in the adjacencies among those \(t\) vertices. For this claim, average each edge monomial over the permutations of the other \(n-t\) vertices. The average is a fixed scalar times the count of injective placements of the monomial's nondistinguished vertices, with its distinguished vertices held fixed. An original loop variable evaluates to zero and can be omitted with its monomial. On injective placements each remaining edge tests the reflexive relation of belonging to the same clique. Keep this relation when applying inclusion-exclusion to remove injectivity. Append an isolated vertex for every distinguished vertex absent from the monomial. Inclusion-exclusion for coincidences expresses the injective count as a linear combination of unrestricted placement counts of quotients of this augmented pattern. A quotient identifying two different distinguished vertices contributes zero. Loops in the quotient add no condition because the tested relation is reflexive.

For each remaining quotient pattern, examine a connected component with \(a\) unrestricted vertices. If it has at least one distinguished vertex, it contributes \(\ell^a\) times the indicator that all of its distinguished vertices lie in one clique. If it has no distinguished vertex, it contributes \(k\ell^a\). Choices in distinct components are independent because injectivity has been removed. The indicator for a component with \(s\ge1\) distinguished vertices is a product of \(s-1\) adjacency variables of a tree on those vertices. The sum of these degrees is at most the number of edges in the quotient pattern, hence at most \(d\). The averaged monomial belongs to the asserted span. In the other direction every such polynomial uses only vertices fixed by \(S_{n-t}\) and is fixed. This proves the claim.

Now \(k,\ell\ge t\) implies that every set partition of the \(t\) vertices occurs as their pattern of sharing cliques. On that full set of partitions the preceding invariant span depends only on \(d,t\), and the action of permutations of the distinguished vertices is the same on both sides. Consequently its subsequent fixed dimensions for \(S_{\rho_2}\times\cdots\), for \(\rho=(n-t,\rho_2,\ldots)\), are also the same. If \(d\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), both \(X\) and \(Y\) satisfy the capacity condition for all \(t\le2d\). Applying Young's rule with the same triangularity as above, their multiplicities in \(W_d\) agree at every weight up to \(2d\). All higher weights have multiplicity zero by the ordered-tuple argument, so their filtered profiles agree.

At degree \(m\), take \(t=m+1\). For each partition \(\pi\) of the \(t\) vertices, the function of another partition \(\sigma\) given by \(1_{\pi\le\sigma}\), where the order is refinement, is realized by a product of adjacency variables of degree \(t-\#\pi\le m\). These functions span all functions on the set of partitions by inversion of the upper triangular incidence matrix of refinement. Their restrictions therefore span all functions even on a proper subset of partition patterns. Thus, at this degree, the \(S_{n-t}\)-fixed space of \(W_m\) is the full space of functions on realized patterns. Taking invariants under \(S_t\) makes its dimension the number of multisets of clique occupations. The two graphs differ by one in that count at \(t=m+1\) as above. Unequal invariant dimensions imply unequal multiplicity profiles.
