# Abelian quotients of class-two groups and four algorithmic results

All five results below have written proofs. The open questions are identified by their exact recorded scopes. Publication priority is unverified.

## 1. The Easdown–Praeger inequality for all finite groups of class at most two

**Theorem.** If \(G\) is a finite nilpotent group of class at most two and \(G/N\) is abelian, then \(\mu(G/N)\leq\mu(G)\), where \(\mu\) is minimum faithful permutation degree. There is no exponent, center-rank, or prime restriction. This establishes the class-two case of the conjecture on distinguished abelian quotients posed by Easdown and Praeger. Moretó still lists the general conjecture as open in 2026. [Original question](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0C55A2C207214D2BFE4768F1D11B9EA0/S0004972700027489a.pdf/on-minimal-faithful-permutation-representations-of-finite-groups.pdf) · [Franchi's result for a \(p\)-group with an abelian maximal subgroup](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5D49D6A19BA7CF8B139EF6F44F1CAC05/S000497271100267Xa.pdf/on_minimal_degrees_of_permutation_representations_of_abelian_quotients_of_finite_groups.pdf) · [Moretó, introduction](https://doi.org/10.2140/ant.2026.20.219)

It suffices to handle a nontrivial \(p\)-group \(G\) and its abelianization \(A=G/G'\). Abelian quotients have componentwise no larger cyclic invariant factors, and their faithful degrees are the sums of their prime-power cyclic factors, so any further abelian quotient has no larger degree. To reduce a general nilpotent group to the Sylow cases, every subgroup splits across the Sylow factors, by taking powers to isolate each primary component. Replace a coset orbit of index \(\prod_p d_p\) by the corresponding separate Sylow coset orbits, omitting \(d_p=1\). The new actions have the same common kernel. Their degree \(\sum_{p:d_p>1}d_p\) is at most the product, since each retained integer is at least two. This proves Sylow additivity for nontrivial nilpotent groups, the reverse inequality coming from the disjoint union of minimum Sylow actions. If a quotient is trivial the assertion also holds under the positive-degree convention.

### Reducing the faithful action to cyclic-center factors

Put \(S=Z(G)[p]\). Since every nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite \(p\)-group meets \(S\), stabilizers \(H_i\) for a faithful action satisfy \(\bigcap_i(H_i\cap S)=1\). An orbit with \(H_i\cap S=S\) can be omitted. If \(D=H_i\cap S\) has codimension \(d\geq1\), choose \(d\) hyperplanes \(D_k\) whose intersection is \(D\), and replace \(H_i\) by the \(H_iD_k\). Their traces are exactly \(D_k\) and each new index is \([G:H_i]/p^{d-1}\). The inequality \(d\leq p^{d-1}\) ensures that the total degree does not grow. Thus a minimum faithful action may be taken to have hyperplane traces.

Fix one such stabilizer \(H\). The group of complex roots of unity admits extension of a character from any subgroup of a finite abelian group. Choose a character of \(Z(G)\) trivial on \(H\cap Z(G)\) and nontrivial on the one-dimensional quotient \(S/(H\cap S)\). This is possible by defining it first on \(S(H\cap Z(G))\) and extending. Let \(J\) be its kernel, let \(Q=G/J\), and write \(H_0=HJ/J\) and \(D_0=Z(G)/J\). Then \(H_0\cap D_0=1\), and \(D_0\) is cyclic and contains \(Q'\). Extend a faithful character of \(D_0\) to \(Z(Q)\) so that it is trivial on \(H_0\cap Z(Q)\). This prescription on the product of the two subgroups is consistent as their intersection is trivial. Let \(J_0\) be the kernel of this second character. The quotient \(P=Q/J_0\) has cyclic center \(Z(Q)/J_0\). Indeed \(J_0\cap Q'=1\), so an element becoming central already commutes with \(Q\). The stabilizer \(H_0J_0/J_0\) intersects \(Z(P)\) trivially and has trivial core. Its lifted stabilizer in \(G\) has the original trace on \(S\) because \(J_0\cap D_0=1\). Each enlargement weakly decreases the orbit index. Doing this for all orbits yields a faithful action with orbit image groups \(P_i\) having cyclic centers and a subdirect embedding \(G\hookrightarrow P=\prod_iP_i\). The sum of the new orbit sizes is at most \(\mu(G)\).

### Lower bound for an orbit and upper bound from power subgroups

Fix a nonabelian \(P_i\), suppress its index, write its center as \(Z=C_{p^z}\), and write its derived group as \(C_{p^a}\leq Z\). The commutator induces a nondegenerate alternating pairing on \(V=P_i/Z\) with values in the cyclic group \(Z\). The domain decomposes into hyperbolic pairs of cyclic orders \(p^{a_1},\ldots,p^{a_m}\), where \(a=\max a_k\). Indeed choose an element \(x\) of greatest order \(p^a\) in \(V\). Nondegeneracy makes its pairing functional have this same order, so there is \(y\) with value of order \(p^a\). This forces \(x,y\) to be independent and both of order \(p^a\). Their pairings split their span off: for any vector, subtract the unique combination of \(x,y\) that makes its pairings with both vanish. Induction applies on the orthogonal complement. This also proves the description of the derived exponent. Put \(s=\sum_k a_k\). The image in \(V\) of a stabilizer disjoint from \(Z\) is isotropic, since its commutators lie in its trivial intersection with \(Z\). Its order is at most \(\sqrt{|V|}=p^s\) by nondegenerate finite character duality. Thus its orbit index is at least \(p^{z+s}\).

For any finite \(p\)-group \(B\) let \(B^{p^j}\) denote the subgroup generated by the \(p^j\)-th powers. Define
\[
 \Xi(B)=\sum_{j\geq0}w_j\log_p|B^{p^j}|,\qquad
 w_0=p,\quad w_1=p^2-2p,\quad
 w_j=(p-1)^2p^{j-1}\quad(j\geq2).
\]
All weights are nonnegative. For a cyclic \(C_{p^e}\) the sum is \(\sum_j w_j(e-j)_+=p^e\) for \(e\geq1\), from its first and second differences in \(e\). Therefore \(\Xi\) equals \(\mu\) on nontrivial abelian \(p\)-groups, is zero on the trivial group, and is additive on direct products of \(p\)-groups.

We claim the local bound
\[
 \Xi(P_i)\leq p^{z+s}+p^a
 \tag{1}
\]
for every nonabelian factor. For an abelian cyclic orbit factor, \(\Xi(P_i)=|P_i|\) is the orbit index and no \(p^a\) term is needed.

To prove (1), choose lifts of the displayed cyclic generators of \(V\). Introduce an abelian group \(E\) with the same center generator and its order, and generators corresponding to the chosen lifts, with the same prescribed powers into \(Z\). A unique collected normal form for either \(P_i\) or \(E\) is a central coordinate and one exponent reduced modulo its order in \(V\) for each lift. In \(E\), the central generator retains its order: an integral combination of the power relations that cancels all the noncentral generator coordinates has all coefficients zero. Thus there is a bijection by normal forms and \(E/Z=V\).

For \(j\geq1\), put \(D_j=(P_i')^{p^j}\) for \(p\) odd, and \(D_j=(P_i')^{2^{j-1}}\) for \(p=2\), viewed as a subgroup of \(Z\). The label in \(E\) of a \(p^j\)-th power differs from the \(p^j\) multiple of its original label by an element of \(D_j\). Indeed the class-two collection formula uses commutator exponents divisible by \(\binom{p^j}{2}\), whose \(p\)-valuation is at least \(j\) for odd \(p\) and exactly \(j-1\) at \(p=2\). When multiplying two elements with projected coordinates in \(p^jV\), their reduced coordinates are multiples of \(p^j\) or zero, so the additional commutator terms in that product are powers divisible by \(p^{2j}\) and in \(D_j\). Carrying the coordinate exponents into the center uses the same power relations as in \(E\). It follows that
\[
 |P_i^{p^j}|\leq |p^jE+D_j|.
 \tag{2}
\]
The sets on the right contain the identity and are closed under the collected group multiplication in \(P_i\), hence under inverses by finiteness. When \(p\) is odd, \(D_j\subseteq p^j Z\). The same holds at \(p=2\) if \(a<z\). In the remaining case \(p=2\), \(a=z\), the extra log size in (2) is at most one for \(j\leq a\) and zero thereafter. Thus
\[
 \Xi(P_i)\leq \mu(E)
 \quad\text{or, only if }p=2,\ a=z,\quad
 \Xi(P_i)\leq \mu(E)+2^a-2.
 \tag{3}
\]
If \(E\) has exponent \(p^{z+a}\), then \(p^aE=Z\), because it is contained in \(Z\) and has an element of order \(p^z\). In that case every \(D_j\) is already in \(p^jE\), including when \(p=2\).

The cyclic exponents for \(E\) weakly dominate those for \(V\) termwise, with zeros appended. Their total increase is \(z\). Write \(a=\max a_k\). A positive increment \(h\) in a baseline cyclic exponent at most \(a\) costs at most \(p^a(p^h-1)\), including a zero baseline, for which the cost is \(p^h\leq p^a(p^h-1)\). Since \(\sum_i(p^{h_i}-1)\leq p^{\sum_i h_i}-1\), this gives
\[
 \mu(E)\leq \mu(V)+p^{z+a}-p^a
 \leq p^{z+a}+(2m-1)p^a.
 \tag{4}
\]
For \(m\geq2\) the last quantity is at most \(p^{z+s}\) whenever \(p^z\geq3\). In fact \(s-a\geq m-1\) and \(p^z(p^{s-a}-1)\geq2m-1\) in these cases. When \(p^z=2\), \(z=a=1\) so \(s=m\). The bound in (4) is \(4m+2\), which is at most \(2^{m+1}\) for \(m\geq3\) and exceeds it by two for \(m=2\). Here (3) incurs zero extra penalty. This proves (1) for \(m\geq2\). When \(m=1\), (4) gives \(p^{z+a}+p^a\). The only case with additional cost in (3) is \(p=2,\ a=z\geq2\). If the largest exponent of \(E\) is \(2^{2a}\) there is no additional cost by \(2^aE=Z\). Otherwise the largest is at most \(2^{2a-1}\). The second and third cyclic exponents are at most \(2^a\), and there are at most three cyclic factors: \(E\) is generated by two lifts and the center; moreover \(2^a E\subseteq Z\) is cyclic, so only one cyclic factor can have exponent greater than \(2^a\). Consequently \(\mu(E)\leq2^{2a-1}+2^{a+1}\leq2^{2a}\). Equation (3) proves (1) also in this case.

### The commutator deficit pays for all local extra terms

Keep the subdirect embedding \(G\hookrightarrow P\) from the faithful action, and let \(a_i\) be the derived exponent logarithm of each nonabelian \(P_i\). For every \(j\geq0\) we prove
\[
 \log_p|P^{p^j}|-\log_p|A^{p^j}|
 \ \geq\ \sum_{i\,:\,P_i\text{ nonabelian}}(a_i-j)_+.
 \tag{5}
\]
Only use factors with \(a_i>j\). The quotient map from \(P^{p^j}\) onto \(V_j=\prod_i p^j(P_i/Z(P_i))\) is onto because each factor projection is onto. Let \(L\) be the image of \(G^{p^j}\) and \(c=\log_p[V_j:L]\). Then \([P^{p^j}:G^{p^j}]\geq p^c\). The commutator pairing
\[
 \beta_j(p^j x,p^j y)=p^j\beta(x,y)
\]
is well defined with values in \(D_j'=\prod_i (P_i')^{p^j}\). The ambiguity of either lift has \(p^j\)-multiple zero and changes \(p^j\beta\) by zero. The image in \(D_j'\) of \((G')^{p^j}\) is \(U=\langle\beta_j(L,L)\rangle\): every element of \(L\) is the \(p^j\) multiple of the projected coordinate of some element of \(G\), and commutators generate \(G'\).

Let \(C\) be the character subgroup of \((D_j')^*\) vanishing on \(U\). For \(\chi\in C\), denote the order of its \(i\)-th coordinate by \(p^{t_i}\). The scalar pairing \(\chi\beta_j\) has \(L\) isotropic, and its quotient by its radical has order at least \(p^{2\sum_i t_i}\). Indeed in each factor a maximal hyperbolic pair of order \(p^{a_i}\) gives a truncated hyperbolic pair of order \(p^{a_i-j}\), on which the character yields a pairing of rank length \(2t_i\). Thus \(p^{\sum_i t_i}\leq[V_j:L]\), using the isotropy bound on the radical quotient.

For any subgroup \(C\) of a product of cyclic \(p\)-groups, at least one element has the sum of its coordinate order logarithms at least \(\log_p|C|\). To see this, filter \(C\) by the kernels of projection onto the first \(i\) coordinates. Choose, at each step, one lift of a generator of the cyclic coordinate image of the preceding kernel. Add that lift to a running sum if its current coordinate has smaller order than the chosen generator. This raises that coordinate order to the generator's order, and each later lift is zero on all previous coordinates. The sum of the logarithms of the cyclic image orders is \(\log_p|C|\). Applying the observation and the isotropy bound gives \(|C|\leq p^c\), so \(|U|\geq |D_j'|/p^c\). The group \(G'\cap G^{p^j}\) contains \((G')^{p^j}\), and so its order is at least \(|U|\). Now \(A^{p^j}\) is the image of \(G^{p^j}\) with kernel \(G'\cap G^{p^j}\), proving (5).

Multiply (5) by the nonnegative \(w_j\) and sum. Each nonabelian factor contributes \(p^{a_i}\). Thus
\[
 \mu(A)
 \leq \Xi(P)-\sum_i p^{a_i}
 =\sum_i\Xi(P_i)-\sum_i p^{a_i}
 \leq \sum_i [P_i\,:\,\text{orbit stabilizer}]
 \leq \mu(G),
\]
where the subtractions are over nonabelian factors and we used (1) and the orbit-index bound. This proves the theorem.




### Strict inequality for odd primes and for derived exponent at least four at \(p=2\)

If \(G\) is nonabelian, equality can hold in the theorem only if \(p=2\) and \(G'\) has exponent two. Under either of the other hypotheses, the same proof gives \(\mu(G/G')<\mu(G)\).

Indeed let \(a=\max_i a_i\) over the nonabelian orbit factors. It equals the derived exponent logarithm of \(G\) because the embedding is subdirect. Suppose the inequality chain is an equality. Then every local bound in (1) must be an equality. For \(p\) odd, a factor with at least two hyperbolic pairs has strict local inequality. A factor with one pair is also strict if the largest cyclic factor of \(E\) has exponent less than \(p^{z+a_i}\), since \(E\) has at most three cyclic factors and then \(\mu(E)\leq3p^{z+a_i-1}\leq p^{z+a_i}\). For \(p=2\), a factor with \(a_i=a\geq2\) and at least two pairs is strict since its extra penalty is at most \(2^{a_i}-2\), and (4) is at most its orbit lower bound. A one-pair factor at \(p=2\) with \(a_i\geq2\) and without the largest possible cyclic exponent has \(\mu(E)\leq2^{z+a_i-1}+2^{a_i+1}\leq2^{z+a_i}\), again making the local inequality strict even with the penalty from (3).

Thus for every factor with \(a_i=a\), its abelian model has exponent \(p^{z+a}\) and \(p^aE=Z(P_i)\). Its actual power subgroup also satisfies \(P_i^{p^a}=Z(P_i)\). All \(p^a\)-th powers are central. An element whose abelian label has maximal order has \(p^a\) multiple generating the center, and the commutator correction in its actual power is trivial for odd \(p\) or in \(Z(P_i)^2\) for \(p=2,\ a\geq2\). In both cases the actual power generates the center. Now at \(j=a\) the right side of (5) is zero and the weight \(w_a\) is positive in the claimed range. Equality implies both \(G^{p^a}=P^{p^a}\) and \(G'\cap P^{p^a}=1\). Choose an element of \(G'\) projecting to an element of order \(p^a\) in a chosen maximal-exponent factor, using surjectivity onto \(P_i'\), and raise it to \(p^{a-1}\). This nontrivial element lies in \(G'\), vanishes in the lower-exponent factors, and lies in \(Z(P_i)=P_i^{p^a}\) in every remaining factor. This contradicts \(G'\cap P^{p^a}=1\).


## 2. Parallel computation of the solvable radical with bounded simple factors

**Theorem.** Fix \(M\). Let \(K\trianglelefteq G\leq S_n\) be given by permutation generators. If every nonabelian simple composition factor of \(G/K\) has order at most \(M\), the inverse image of \(\operatorname{Rad}(G/K)\), a faithful permutation representation of its Fitting-free quotient, and the minimum faithful degree of that quotient can be computed in uniform \(\mathsf{NC}\). Input generator lists may have arbitrary polynomial length. This supplies a different proof for bounded-order simple factors, based on normal closures and incidence patterns. An earlier project argument treats the unrestricted parallel radical and quotient-degree questions. [Introduction and Remark 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039v3) · [Earlier unrestricted project proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ParallelGroupAlgorithmsAndIsomorphism/QuotientRadicalNC.md)

Use the Babai–Luks–Seress parallel normal series for the permutation group \(G\), with polylogarithmically many levels and each factor either abelian or a direct product of nonabelian simple groups. Map the series into \(G/K\), representing every term by its inverse image in \(G\) generated by the original term and \(K\). Images of the abelian layers are abelian. Quotients of the nonabelian semisimple layers are again products of nonabelian simple groups. Discard trivial layers. All these operations use uniform \(\mathsf{NC}\). The permutation subgroup order, membership, normal closure and pointwise stabilizer algorithms used below are those in Lemma 2.2 of the cited paper. [Parallel normal series](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039v3#S3)

Consider one nonabelian layer \(B=L/N=\prod_{i=1}^k S_i\), temporarily treating the factor indexing as unknown. In a simple direct product, a normal subgroup is the product of a set of factors. To see this, if an element of a normal subgroup has a nontrivial coordinate in \(S_i\), commuting it with some element supported on \(i\) isolates a nontrivial element in \(S_i\). Normal generation of that element yields \(S_i\). Thus the normal closure in \(B\) of an element records exactly its nontrivial coordinates.

List all words of length at most \(M^2\) on the supplied generators of \(L\) and their inverses, and for each take the normal closure in \(L\) of the word together with \(N\). The number of words is polynomial for fixed \(M\). Call the resulting normal subgroups of \(B\) the listed supports. For any ordered pair \(i\ne i'\), at least one support includes \(i\) and excludes \(i'\). Indeed the projected generators generate \(S_i\times S_{i'}\), a group of order at most \(M^2\), and its generating Cayley graph reaches a nontrivial element of \(S_i\times1\) by a word of the given length. A listed support contains each \(i\) for at least one word, by generation.

Meets and joins of two represented supports are computable in uniform \(\mathsf{NC}\). The join uses the union of their generators. For the meet, the commutator subgroup of the two factor products is their intersection, because distinct factors commute and each common factor is perfect. On the inverse images this is obtained from the normal closure in \(L\) of the commutators of pairs of generators, together with \(N\). Taking normal closure is sufficient because killing those generator-pair commutators forces the two generated subgroups to commute. A meet or join of polynomially many terms is computed by a balanced binary tree.

Recover all realized incidence patterns of single factors in the supports, without enumerating all binary strings. Start with leaves of a balanced binary tree over the polynomial list, and merge the realized patterns from the two child blocks. A candidate pattern with positive set of supports and negative set of supports is realized precisely when \(|P|>|P\cap R|\), where \(P\) is the meet of the positive supports and \(R\) is the join of the negative supports, with empty meet \(B\) and empty join \(1\). This test says that some factor survives the positives and is absent from the negatives. Compute orders by the parallel permutation algorithms on the inverse images. Every block has at most \(k\) realized patterns, and \(k\leq\log_2 |G|\leq O(n\log n)\). Testing the polynomial number of pairs and compacting the accepted records at each of the logarithmically many merge levels is within uniform \(\mathsf{NC}\). Directed separation guarantees that the meet of all positive supports for a full realized pattern is exactly one simple factor.

For each recovered factor, list its elements as cosets of \(N\) by enumerating all words of length at most \(M\) in its inverse-image generators and removing duplicates by membership in \(N\). The ambient \(G\) conjugates factors to factors since both \(L\) and \(N\) are normal in \(G\). Its conjugation action on the disjoint list of nonidentity elements from the individual factors is computed by testing equality of conjugate representatives modulo \(N\), and its kernel is exactly the centralizer of the whole layer. The list is of size at most \(Mk\), hence polynomial.

Combine the conjugation actions from all nonabelian layers in disjoint union. Any solvable normal subgroup of \(G/K\) acts trivially on each such layer. In the quotient by the preceding term, its image is solvable and normal, meets the centerless semisimple layer trivially, and commutes with it because their commutator lies in their intersection. Conversely, the kernel of the combined action modulo \(K\) is solvable. Intersect it with the series. Its image in a nonabelian layer must lie in the center of that layer, which is trivial, and its image in an abelian layer is abelian. Thus the kernel is exactly the solvable radical. The inverse-image generators can be obtained by taking the pointwise stabilizer of the added list in the graph of the action on the original and added domains. The same list gives a faithful permutation representation of the Fitting-free quotient. Apply the permutation-input part of the Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar minimum faithful degree algorithm to that representation. [Theorem 5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039v3#S5)

## 3. Compact exact epimorphism counting for quotients of field Heisenberg groups

**Theorem.** Given two groups promised to be quotients of generalized Heisenberg groups over finite fields, epimorphism decision, witness construction and exact counting take deterministic polynomial bit time in compact commutator coordinates. For nonabelian inputs use alternating commutator matrices over the prime field and the squares of the chosen quotient-basis lifts when the prime is two. For abelian inputs give the elementary abelian rank in basis coordinates. No parent field, cover, or quotient kernel is supplied. The prime can vary. This strengthens the earlier Cayley-table result for the Lewis–Wilson family in Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar Open Question 3(3) and answers its exact-counting problem on this family in compact coordinates of the stated form. [Open Questions 2 and 3(3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7) · [Prior Cayley-table decision and search](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GenusTwoGroupEpimorphism/HEISENBERG.md) · [Lewis–Wilson family](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.5466)

A nonabelian quotient \(X\) of the Heisenberg group over \(K\) is a quotient by a subgroup of the center, since a normal subgroup containing a noncentral element contains all of the center, by the commutators of that element. Its center and derived group coincide. Write \(F=\mathbb F_p\), \(V_X=X/Z(X)\), \(W_X=Z(X)\), and let \(P_X\) be the space of scalar alternating forms obtained by composing its commutator with \(W_X^*\). On \(K^{2r}\) each member of \(P_X\) has the form \(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(u h)\) with \(h\) the standard \(K\)-valued symplectic form. Every nonzero scalar form is nonsingular by trace duality. Choose a nonzero \(\beta_X\in P_X\) and represent each other form as \(\beta_X(t\,\cdot,\cdot)\). The ratio operators comprise a space \(U_X\) containing \(1\), and generate a field \(L_X\) since in the parent coordinates they are scalar multiplications by elements of \(K\). Recover \(U_X\) by matrix inversion and \(L_X\) by linear span closure of operator products. All arithmetic uses the given matrix coordinates.

An epimorphism induces onto maps \(A:V_G\to V_H,\ B:W_G\to W_H\) whose effect on commutator forms is \(A^* P_H\subseteq P_G\). This makes \(A\) injective because the pullback of a nonzero form is nonsingular. Thus unequal \(F\)-dimensions, or unequal primes for nontrivial inputs, give no epimorphism. An admissible \(A\) uniquely determines
\[
  A^*\beta_H=\beta_G(a\,\cdot,\cdot),\qquad a\in U_G\setminus0,\qquad
  \iota(t)=A^{-1}tA\quad(t\in L_H).
\]
The conjugated ratios lie in \(L_G\) since \(a\iota(U_H)\subseteq U_G\) and \(a\) is a unit, so \(\iota:L_H\hookrightarrow L_G\) is an \(F\)-embedding. The map \(B\) is then unique, by dualizing \(A^*|_{P_H}\).

For each embedding \(\iota\), compute the linear space
\[
 T_\iota=\{a\in U_G:a\,\iota(U_H)\subseteq U_G\}
\]
by testing a linear basis of \(U_H\). It contains \(p^{\dim_F T_\iota}-1\) nonzero parameters and finding one takes linear algebra. To compute the embeddings without enumerating a field, let \(d=[L_H:F]\). If \(d\nmid [L_G:F]\) there are none. Otherwise the nullspace of \(x\mapsto x^{p^d}-x\) in \(L_G\) is its unique subfield of size \(p^d\), with multiplication constants in the computed basis. Use Lenstra's deterministic finite-field isomorphism algorithm and compose with the \(d\) powers of Frobenius on \(L_H\) to list all embeddings. Repeated squaring computes all displayed powers in polynomial bit time. [Lenstra's algorithm](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

For odd \(p\) and a fixed nonzero parameter \(a\), the two desired scalar forms are \(\beta_H\) and \(\beta_G(a\,\cdot,\cdot)\). They give \(L_H\)-valued nonsingular alternating forms via trace duality, using the \(\iota\)-action on the source. For example reconstruct the form \(h\) by \(\operatorname{Tr}_{L_H/F}(c h(x,y))=\beta(\iota(c)x,y)\) for a basis of \(c\). Commutativity and self-adjointness of the ratio operators give the \(L_H\)-bilinear balance identities. The \(L_H\)-dimensions are equal, so constructive symplectic bases give an isometry. Exactly the \(\iota\)-semilinear isometries between these forms realize the desired \(a,\iota\), since their pullbacks on all \(L_H\)-coefficients are determined and include the required scalar forms.

At \(p=2\), let \(q_0\) be the target- or source-square coordinate with polar form \(\beta_X\). There is a hyperbolic quadratic form \(Q_X\) over \(L_X\) with scalar square coordinates \(q_t(x)=\operatorname{Tr}_{L_X/F}(tQ_X(x))\) for \(t\in U_X\). To see this in parent coordinates, take the trace transfer of the scalar multiple of the parent form \((x,y)\mapsto\sum_j x_jy_j\) giving the reference coordinate. Both coordinate halves remain totally singular of half dimension, and the polar form is nonsingular by trace duality. To compute \(Q_X\) on demand without the parent field, solve
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{L_X/F}(cQ_X(x))=q_0(\sqrt c\,x)
\]
for a basis of \(c\). The finite even field has a unique square root obtained by inverse Frobenius. For the fixed parameter \(a\), transfer \(aQ_G\) to \(\iota(L_H)\) by field trace and compare to \(Q_H\), using the given embedding to view both over \(L_H\). The trace transfer is again hyperbolic by the same trace and coordinate-half argument. Its \(\iota\)-semilinear isometries are exactly the admissible maps for the fixed pair. In fact any square-preserving map realizing the chosen pair preserves the necessary base-coordinate trace, and applying it to every scalar multiple reconstructs the \(L_H\)-quadratic form by the displayed duality. This precludes extra maps with the correct polar forms but incorrect squares.

Hyperbolic bases in characteristic two can be constructed without searching the field. Take a decomposition of the polar form into symplectic planes. Test each plane for a nonzero singular vector by checking its second basis vector and solving \(u+v x+w x^2=0\) for the vectors of first coordinate \(1\). The latter is an affine \(\mathbb F_2\)-linear system since Frobenius is additive. If all planes are anisotropic and there are two or more planes, rescale a nonzero vector in each of two planes to have quadratic value \(1\), using square roots, and add the vectors. Their sum is singular by orthogonality. In the remaining dimension-two hyperbolic case the individual test succeeds. For the found singular vector choose any partner with polar value \(1\), then add the proper multiple of the singular vector to make the partner singular. Split this hyperbolic plane and iterate in its orthogonal complement.

For every successful pair \((a,\iota)\) the choices of \(A\) constitute a torsor under the target isometry group, whose order depends only on \(q=|L_H|\) and \(2m=\dim_{L_H}V_H\). At odd \(p\) this order is \(q^{m^2}\prod_{i=1}^m(q^{2i}-1)\) for the symplectic group. At \(p=2\) it is \(2q^{m(m-1)}(q^m-1)\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}(q^{2i}-1)\) for the split orthogonal group. Once \(A,B\) are fixed, each basis lift of \(V_G\) has \(|W_H|\) independent choices of image in the required coset. These choices respect the commutator relations and the basis-lift powers. Those relations together with the central relations present the source, by collecting words into the central subgroup and a product of the quotient basis lifts with exponents below \(p\). Thus each choice yields a distinct group epimorphism. The total is the appropriate isometry group order times \(|W_H|^{\dim_F V_G}\sum_\iota(p^{\dim_F T_\iota}-1)\). [Classical group orders](https://maths.qmul.ac.uk/~pjc/class_gps/cg.pdf)

For a nontrivial abelian target all maps factor through the elementary abelianization of the source, so count full-rank linear maps over the common prime field by the usual independent-row product. An abelian source has no epimorphism to a nonabelian target. The trivial target always has one epimorphism. The argument yields a witness by finding one successful parameter, solving the displayed isometry problem, dualizing and selecting any lifts. Every matrix dimension, exponent bit length and output count bit length is polynomial in the compact input length, as required.

## 4. Exact epimorphism counts for bounded-width factor-center arrangements

**Theorem.** Suppose both input groups have central elementary abelian radicals and their radical quotients are direct products of nonabelian simple groups. Given the Cayley tables, one can count epimorphisms in deterministic polynomial bit time when either factor-center arrangement has bounded branchwidth or when the target perfect center has bounded dimension. Each positive instance in these cases also admits exact uniform sampling of the epimorphisms in polynomial expected bit time. The bounded-width results extend the earlier decision and witness algorithms to the counting question in Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar Open Question 2. The bounded target-center count is included as a comparison with the existing result. [Open Questions 2 and 3(5)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7) · [Prior decision algorithms and definitions](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FactorCenterEpimorphisms/PROOF.md) · [Prior bounded-center count](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowLiteratureTransfers/CentralEpimorphisms.md)

Use the factor-center notation of the prior lifting criterion. Each group is its perfect derived group times an elementary abelian direct factor, after fixing a complement in the center. For the perfect groups, an epimorphism is specified uniquely by an injective match of target simple factors to source simple factors and a local epimorphism for each match. The local maps on the centers must respect all source-center relations. Omitted factors have the unique trivial map. In the direct products with the abelian factors, a map from the perfect source to the abelian target is trivial. Conversely the source abelian factor must map inside the center of the full target if the perfect source surjects onto the perfect target. Each epimorphism on the abelian factors then has an additional freely chosen map from the source abelian factor into the target perfect center. Accordingly, multiply the perfect count by the ordinary elementary abelian epimorphism count, and by the order of the homomorphism group from the source abelian factor into the target perfect center.

In the source-width dynamic program, give a state the number of partial matches with the specified set of used target factors and the specified restriction to the boundary subspace. Each leaf gets one contribution per eligible local map, or one for the unique omitted-factor choice. At an internal node, a realizable state is determined by compatible child states together with the underlying two partial matches. The child target sets are disjoint and the maps agree on their intersection, so the combined assignment is unique after the two underlying assignments have been chosen. Its own restriction is determined by the recorded child restrictions. Consequently add the product of child counts for every compatible pair that yields the parent state. At the root all target indices are used and the boundary is zero, giving the desired perfect count.

In the target-width program, each state records the used source set and the residual map from the relation space to the cut intersection. Weight a leaf by the number of local epimorphisms giving that residual. At a join take disjoint source sets, add their residual maps, and retain precisely the pairs whose combined residual lands in the parent boundary. Again each partial match determines its own leaf choices and states. Thus the corresponding product and summation rule counts each partial match exactly once. At the root the residual is zero, precisely the requirement that the local maps descend through every source-center relation. Omitted source factors carry their unique trivial maps. The two methods retain the polynomial bound from the prior width analysis, since every integer count is bounded by the number of possible functions on a generating list of the source.

For bounded target perfect-center dimension, enumerate the linear maps from the source perfect center to that target center as in the decision proof. An admissible full matching induces exactly one such map. For each enumerated map, count matches by a subset dynamic program as the source factors are processed. A used target set is the state. Each available target \(j\) has transition weight the number of local epimorphisms to \(j\) with the specified center restriction. The omit transition has weight \(1\) if the proposed center map vanishes on that source center and is disallowed otherwise. This avoids multiplying the count for dummy vertices. The target factor count is at most \(\log_2 |H|\), so all the subsets have polynomial total size. Sum the full-target counts over the enumerated center maps and apply the separate abelian factors just described.

Uniform sampling uses the same exact counts. From the accepting state choose a child pair or a predecessor with probability proportional to its contribution, and recurse with independent choices in disjoint subproblems. Choose any counted local epimorphism uniformly at a leaf. The abelian full-rank map can be sampled by successively taking uniform independent rows, and the independent central homomorphism is sampled in basis coordinates. A uniform integer in any written interval is obtained by binary rejection sampling with constant expected retries. The resulting distribution gives each admissible epimorphism exactly the inverse of the total count.



## 5. Minimum faithful transitive degree when the central quotient is elementary abelian and the derived group is cyclic

**Theorem.** Let \(P\) be a class-two \(p\)-group given by permutation generators. Suppose \(P/Z(P)\) is elementary abelian and \(P'\) is cyclic. Deterministic polynomial time suffices to compute the minimum faithful transitive degree and a generating list for an optimal stabilizer. The center may have arbitrary order, rank and exponent. The algorithm includes \(p=2\). It gives a tractable class for the faithful transitive optimization, which has prior hardness theorems with unbounded derived rank. [Earlier faithful transitive complexity result](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FaithfulActionAdvances/transitive-degree.md) · [Earlier unrestricted-orbit optimization with cyclic derived group](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FaithfulActionAdvances/commutator-parameter.md)

Put \(Z=Z(P)\) and \(V=P/Z\). The subgroup \(P'\) has order at most \(p\), since \([x,y]^p=[x^p,y]=1\). A core-free stabilizer \(H\) meets \(Z\) trivially, and this condition also suffices for core-freeness, since every nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite \(p\)-group meets \(Z\). Thus \(H\) projects isomorphically to a subspace \(L\leq V\). The commutator \(\beta:V^2\to P'\) must vanish on \(L\). Also, if a lift \(x\) of \(v\in V\) is chosen, the element
\[
  Q(v)=x^p\bmod Z^p \ \in\ Z/Z^p
\]
must vanish on \(L\), because every element of \(H\) has order dividing \(p\). Conversely, if these two conditions hold, choose lifts of a basis of \(L\) and adjust each one by a central element so its \(p\)-th power is the identity. The adjustments exist by the condition on \(Q\). The lifts commute by the condition on \(\beta\). They generate an elementary abelian subgroup that projects isomorphically onto \(L\). The optimum transitive degree is \(|P|/p^{\max\dim L}\).

For odd \(p\), \(Q\) is linear by the class-two power formula and the exponent of \(P'\). Work on \(\ker Q\). The full scalar alternating form \(\beta\) has a maximum isotropic subspace of dimension \(\dim\ker Q-\operatorname{rank}(\beta|_{\ker Q})/2\), by splitting off its radical and constructing a symplectic basis. Treat the zero commutator as the zero form.

For \(p=2\), the square map \(Q\) is quadratic with polar form the image of \(\beta\) in \(Z/Z^2\). If that image is zero, \(Q\) is linear and one applies the preceding alternating algorithm to the full commutator restricted to \(\ker Q\). Otherwise the image of \(P'\) is a line, with injective map from \(P'\) to that line. Choose a basis of \(Z/Z^2\) starting with that line. All components of \(Q\) except the first are linear. On their joint kernel \(U\), only the scalar quadratic form \(q\) in the first component remains. A totally singular subspace for \(q\) is isotropic for its polar form, so it also has vanishing full commutator by injectivity on \(P'\). Conversely a feasible \(L\) is totally singular for \(q\) on \(U\).

Compute the polar radical \(R\) of \(q\) on \(U\) and a symplectic complement \(W\). The restriction \(q|_R\) is linear. If it vanishes, the largest singular dimension is \(\dim R\) plus the Witt index of \(q|_W\), and an optimal subspace is their corresponding direct sum. One obtains a Witt decomposition over \(\mathbb F_2\) by splitting off a singular vector and its adjusted partner whenever a vector exists. In dimension two test the three nonzero vectors. In larger dimension take a decomposition into planes for the polar form. If every plane is anisotropic, any two nonzero vectors in different such planes have sum of quadratic value zero, providing a singular vector. When \(q|_R\) is nonzero, choose \(r\in R\) with \(q(r)=1\) and a maximal isotropic \(L_0\leq W\) for the polar form. The function \(q\) on \(L_0\) is linear. Then \(\ker(q|_R)\) together with the vectors \(w+q(w)r\) for \(w\in L_0\) yields a totally singular subspace of dimension \(\dim R-1+\dim W/2\). No totally singular subspace can exceed this bound: its intersection with \(R\) lies in \(\ker(q|_R)\), and its projection to \(W\) is polar isotropic. This proves optimality in every case.

Center and commutator generators, orders, cyclic coordinates for the abelian sections, and membership with lifts are computable in polynomial bit time in the permutation-group model. The power and commutator data use a basis of \(V\) and evaluate its lifts using the input permutations. For the required central \(p\)-th root of a prescribed element known to lie in \(Z^p\), a solution comes from linear congruences in the cyclic coordinates of \(Z\). The resulting stabilizer is output by its generators. The degree formula only requires binary integers, so it has no output-size dependence on printing the entire transitive action.
