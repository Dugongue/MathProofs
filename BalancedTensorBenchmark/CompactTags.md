# Smaller rank tags for the balanced tensor reduction

This note proves a modification of the partition-erasing step in the Lean-checked balanced reduction. The modification and benchmark code are checked informally and by executable controls. The Lean theorem certifies the original rank tags and the equivalence with the named tensor system. [Original Lean proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/BalancedOrdinaryReduction.lean).

## Distinct singleton ranks suffice

Consider one partition-erasure stage, with old frontal and column spaces $A,V$, and row space $U=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N-1}U_i$. Put $u_i=\dim U_i$. The original stage adjoins frontal marker directions, a separate copy of $V$ for each marker, and independent rectangular tag blocks. With tag dimension $e_i$, its slice at $a\in A$ and marker coordinates $q=(q_i)$ has rank
$$
\operatorname{rk} M_X(a,q)=\dim V+\sum_{i:q_i\ne0}(u_i+e_i)\quad(q\ne0).
$$
For $q=0$, the slice factors through $V$ and the rank is at most $\dim V$. The rank formula follows from the direct sum of the tag blocks and the $U_i$ contributions from the nonzero markers. The marker copies make the $V$ input injective into a separate block, regardless of contamination by $X(a)$. This is the same rank computation as the original constructor.

Let $M=\max(N,1+\max_i u_i)$, and choose $e_i=M+i-u_i$. Each tag has positive dimension. Every singleton slice rank lies in the interval $[\dim V+M,\dim V+2M)$, with different values for different marker labels. Every slice with at least two active markers lies above or at the right endpoint. It follows that a transporter preserves the original frontal subspace and recovers each marked quotient line, including when its chosen representative is contaminated by an original slice. The remainder of the original transporter proof only uses these two conclusions and the marked slices' image and kernel formulas. Those formulas hold with arbitrary positive tag dimensions. The forward direction uses the original lift with the identity on the tag dimensions. These facts apply separately to each erasure, for all choices of which mode is treated first, since the surviving blocks follow the cyclic relabeling. The encoder's schedule search only compares dimensions and picks the same schedule for both inputs.

The tag dimensions can be tightened further if desired. On the quotient by the original frontal subspace define $\rho(0)=0$ and $\rho(q)=\operatorname{rk} M_X(a,q)-\dim V$ for nonzero $q$, independent of $a$. For positive weights $w_i=u_i+e_i$, equality $\rho(x+y)=\rho(x)+\rho(y)$ holds exactly for disjoint supports. Indeed at any index active in both summands, the right side counts its positive weight twice and the left counts it at most once. Thus singleton lines are the nonzero vectors that cannot be expressed as a sum of two nonzero vectors with equality of weights. Any pairwise distinct positive weights with $w_i>u_i$ recover the labeled singletons. The simpler interval proof above is the compact variant used for the main benchmark.

## Solving the named tensor system

The first stage constructs three trees of tensor products. The payload is the original coefficient array reshaped along their roots. Each internal product tensor forces the root action of its subtree to be the Kronecker product of its leaf actions. The Lean theorem $\mathsf{leafIso\_iff\_mixed}$ gives the equivalence between leaf isomorphism and isomorphism of the named system. The marked ordinary instance produced by the compiler is equivalent to that named system. A backend given the compiler's trusted decomposition may therefore act on the small leaf variables, using the named product equations to eliminate all internal variables. This representation stores the same payload and the tree constraints. It applies to instances produced by the compiler, with the coding layouts and marker data known.

For binary leaves of dimension at most three, choose two leaf modes $i,j$, nonzero covectors $u,v$, and a bipartition of the remaining leaf modes. Contract against $u,v$ and take the binary matrix rank across that cut. If $B=(\bigotimes_k P_k) A$, the rank at $(u,v)$ in $B$ equals the rank at $(P_i^{\mathsf T}u,P_j^{\mathsf T}v)$ in $A$. The remaining factors act by invertible row and column maps. Thus the equalities define necessary compatibility tables on pairs of candidate factor maps. Arc consistency and search may remove only unsupported candidates. After pruning, the implementation exhausts compatible tuples up to an explicit timeout and checks the coefficient identity on any positive return. Optional rank cuts change only the extent of pruning. The independent Python verifier rechecks the complete original coordinate identity and every factor's invertibility.

The covariant-then-verify architecture is reused from Problem 1.8. Its orthogonal Gram identity assumes orthogonal factors. Over $\mathbf F_2$, an invertible shear sends a pure tensor with one nonzero component to one with two nonzero components. The two encodings have the same fixed gadgets and their coefficient parities differ. Orthogonal maps preserve the squared norm, which over $\mathbf F_2$ is that parity. So the shear example shows why calling the orthogonal solver directly would lose valid ordinary isomorphisms. Binary contraction ranks are equivariant under the full general linear action. [Problem 1.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00591v1#S1.SS5).

The grouped-rank control instead contracts an entire root mode against a decomposable covector. It computes ranks in the other two root modes. For each leaf of the contracted group, fix its covector and histogram these ranks over all choices at the other leaves of that group. Under an isomorphism each other leaf merely permutes the set of nonzero covectors, and the two uncontracted root factors multiply the matrix by invertible maps. So the histogram transforms by pullback on the fixed leaf alone. Filtering by histogram equality preserves every valid tuple. Comparing balanced and half group sizes changes the contraction shapes and histograms while leaving the subsequent completion search identical.
