# Executable balanced tensor compilation

The coordinate interface is compile_tensor in encoder.py. It represents a tensor by its shape, one contiguous or strided payload block, and unit-valued runs for fixed coefficients. Arbitrary-precision shape arithmetic precedes any capped materialization. The geometric policy with cyclic schedule implements the operations from TensorOrderReduction.ordinary: the three product trees, named-space packing, linked partitions, and three applications of one-axis partition erasure. The code fixes an explicit compatible ordering for the direct-sum bases. The mixed layer returns the root payload and the exact product-tree constraints. The half grouping is a control with the same compiler and different trees. [Lean proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/BalancedOrdinaryReduction.lean).

The prior-work comparator compile_published implements Grochow–Qiao's two-arm path-algebra quotient. Its basis consists of vertex idempotents and retained paths, with the longer arm's full paths rewritten by the input tensor in the shorter arm's full-path basis. It produces the bare multiplication tensor of this unital associative algebra, for which isotopy and isomorphism coincide. Given an isotopy $Z(ab)=X(a)Y(b)$, the equations $Z=L_{X(1)}Y=R_{Y(1)}X$ make both unit images invertible. Then $a\mapsto X(1)^{-1}Z(a)Y(1)^{-1}$ is an algebra isomorphism, by associativity. [Grochow–Qiao, Figure 6](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full).

## Compact rank tags

For one-axis erasure, write $X$ as a frontal family of maps $V\to U=\bigoplus_{i<N}U_i$ with row blocks of dimensions $u_i$. The existing proof's gadget gives the mixed slice with nonzero selector vector $q$ the rank
$$
 \dim V+\sum_{i\in\operatorname{supp}q}(u_i+e_i),
$$
where $e_i$ is the tag dimension. The data-only slices have rank at most $\dim V$, since they factor through $V$. Set $M=\max(N,1+\max_i u_i)$ and $e_i=M+i-u_i$. Each singleton weight is in $[M,2M)$, and all multi-element sums are at least $2M$. Thus the two rank tests used by $\texttt{one_axis_reverse_exact}$ still recover the data space and each singleton selector line. Its remaining recovery works with arbitrary tag spaces given those two conclusions. This proves the compact implementation correct over all fields. Its new rank estimates are informal.

One can permute the axes before each erasure. The implementation tries all valid orders that erase each surviving partition once, and minimizes the exact dense output entry count. Both inputs choose the same schedule because it depends only on the dimensions. Within each non-erased axis, augmented coordinates are attached to its first block.

## Binary spectral erasure

The optional $\texttt{binary_spectral}$ policy specializes to $\mathbb F_2$. It replaces one identity-copy row sector per partition block with three, using small matrices with distinct minimal polynomials and their joint centralizer.

Write the input as $X:A\times V\to U=\bigoplus U_i$. Pick distinct irreducible monic $f_i$ of the same degree $r$ over $\mathbb F_2$, each with nonzero constant term. Put $K_i=\mathbb F_2[t]/(f_i)$ and $\widetilde U=\bigoplus(U_i\otimes K_i)$. Identify $U$ with $U_i\otimes 1$ in each summand. Define $D_0,D_1,D_2$ on $\widetilde U$ as identity, multiplication by $t\bmod f_i$ in each summand, and coordinatewise squaring in $K_i$. All three operators are invertible. The actual output spaces are
$$
 A\oplus\mathbb F_2^3,\qquad V\oplus\widetilde U_{\rm aux}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^2 E_j,\qquad
 \widetilde U\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^2 V^j\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^2 E_j .
$$
The original frontal data $X(a)$ are embedded from $V$ into $\widetilde U$. Marker $g_j$ applies $D_j : \widetilde U_{\rm aux}\to\widetilde U$, copies $V$ to its $j$-th row copy, and copies $E_j$ from column to row. All other components of $g_j$ are zero.

For a nonempty marker support $S\subseteq\{0,1,2\}$, the rank of $X(a)+\sum_{j\in S}g_j$ is
$$
 \dim V+\operatorname{rank}\!\left(\sum_{j\in S}D_j\right)+\sum_{j\in S}e_j .
$$
The image of the $V$ summand projects injectively into a nonzero $V^j$ copy. The other column summands contribute only to $\widetilde U$ and the independent tag spaces. Consequently the data term $X(a)$ does not affect this rank. Choose integers $e_j\in[1,19]$ to distinguish each singleton support's rank from every other nonempty support. Each equality to exclude cuts out a proper affine plane, with coefficient vector $\mathbf e_j-\mathbf1_S\ne0$. There are at most $3(7-1)=18$ planes, and each has at most $19^2$ points in the integer box. The code finds a valid triple by exhaustive search. Positive tags also distinguish every nonempty support from data of rank at most $\dim V$.

Suppose two enlarged tensors are isomorphic, written in the convention $\widetilde Y(a,b)=P\widetilde X(Ra,Qb)$. Rank preserves the data space $A$, and on $A\oplus\mathbb F_2^3$ each marker basis element is carried to itself plus a data term. This uses uniqueness of the nonzero coefficient on a binary singleton support. For the resulting contaminated slices $X(a_j)+g_j$, the images are $\widetilde U\oplus V^j\oplus E_j$, since $D_j$ is invertible. The kernels are $\bigoplus_{h\ne j}E_h$, since a vector in the kernel must have zero $V$-component. Intersect all marker images to recover $\widetilde U$, sum their kernels to recover $\bigoplus_jE_j$, and intersect their preimages of $\widetilde U$ to recover $\widetilde U_{\rm aux}$. For the last claim every $V$-coordinate and every tag coordinate is excluded by at least one marker, whereas all $\widetilde U_{\rm aux}$ coordinates are admitted by all three. These same spaces are recovered from the uncontaminated markers of $\widetilde Y$. Thus $P$ restricts to $\widetilde U$, $Q$ restricts to $\widetilde U_{\rm aux}$, and $Q$ induces an invertible action on the quotient $V$.

Restrict the marker equations to $\widetilde U_{\rm aux}$, where the data perturbations vanish. They give $P_{\widetilde U}D_jQ_{\rm aux}=D_j$. The identity marker gives $Q_{\rm aux}=P_{\widetilde U}^{-1}$, so $P_{\widetilde U}$ commutes with both $D_1,D_2$. Commuting with $D_1$ preserves each $\ker f_i(D_1)=U_i\otimes K_i$, and is $K_i$-linear there because the class of $t$ generates $K_i$. Commuting with $D_2$ forces each matrix entry over $K_i$ to be fixed by squaring, hence to lie in $\mathbb F_2$. The induced row action therefore preserves the original $U$ and respects every $U_i$. Restricting the data equations to $A$, and passing to the column quotient $V$, recovers the original isomorphism. The front $A$ restriction respects the survivor partition because the input front lies in its original coordinate blocks and all new marker directions are attached to the first such block. The quotient $V$ respects its survivor partition because the removed carrier $\widetilde U_{\rm aux}\oplus\bigoplus E_j$ belongs wholly to the first column partition block.

In the forward direction extend any blockwise row action to $P_i\otimes I_{K_i}$, use its inverse on $\widetilde U_{\rm aux}$, repeat the inverse original column action on each $V^j$ and act identically on all tags and marker indices. This preserves all marker slices and respects the tracked partitions. Rotate and apply this one-axis equivalence three times to obtain ordinary tensor isomorphism exactly. All steps apply to raw binary inputs. This is an independent informal adaptation of the rank-based erasure, with spectral operators used inside the constructed markers.

## Scope of the size comparison

For six binary modes of side 2 and all-one input, the geometric encoder has around $1.74\cdot10^{46}$ dense entries. Its exact representation uses hundreds of run descriptors. Compact cyclic uses $1.25\cdot10^{17}$ dense entries and $11{,}278{,}795$ nonzeros. Choosing the best erasure order drops that to $5.86\cdot10^{15}$ entries and $616{,}308$ nonzeros. The binary spectral policy with the best schedule uses $9.62\cdot10^{13}$ entries and $366{,}490$ nonzeros. The published quotient needs $50^3=125{,}000$ dense entries and $334$ nonzeros on the same input. The spectral policy improves the compound gadget, while the prior construction's smaller constants dominate this sample. A solver using the product-tree constraints can skip the ordinary-enforcement coordinates.

## Extension to each fixed finite field

There is also an informal version of the spectral proof over any fixed $\mathbb F_q$. The executable spectral policy above takes binary inputs. In the proof choose a prime $r>q$ so large that $(q^{r-1}-1)/r\ge N$. The trace-$1$ affine hyperplane in $\mathbb F_{q^r}$ has $q^{r-1}$ elements. Its only elements that can have degree below $r$ lie in $\mathbb F_q$, where $\operatorname{Tr}(a)=ra$ gives exactly one such element. The degree-$r$ elements split into Frobenius orbits of size $r$, so choose $N$ distinct irreducibles with root trace $1$. Use the $q$-power Frobenius operator for $D_2$. For every non-singleton marker vector $q'\in\mathbb F_q^3$ and each singled-out marker, exclude the affine plane that makes their rank equal. Also enforce pairwise distinct tag sizes. The same finite grid argument finds positive tag sizes bounded by $3q^3+1$, independent of tensor dimensions.

The transported markers are then of the form $a_jg_j+X(b_j)$ with nonzero $a_j\in\mathbb F_q$, and the same contaminant-independent carrier spaces are recovered. The equations on $\widetilde U_{\rm aux}$ give $P_{\widetilde U}D_jP_{\widetilde U}^{-1}=(a_0/a_j)D_j$. All eigenvalues of $D_1$ have trace $1$ in $\mathbb F_{q^r}/\mathbb F_q$, so any scalar taking its spectrum to itself equals $1$. For $D_2$, taking the $r$-th power shows $(a_0/a_2)^r=1$, which forces $a_0/a_2=1$ since $\gcd(r,q-1)=1$. Thus $P_{\widetilde U}$ still commutes with both operators, and their joint centralizer acts by independent $\mathbb F_q$-linear maps on the original row blocks. For fixed $q$ one can choose $r=O(\log(N+2))$ by Bertrand's postulate once $N$ is large, and enumerate irreducibles in polynomial time in $N$. This extension has no benchmark measurements.
