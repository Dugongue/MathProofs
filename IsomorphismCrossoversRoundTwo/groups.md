# Isomorphism of central extensions beyond the socle

AI-assisted informal proof. The comparison is with [Grochow–Qiao, Theorem 7.1 and Open Problem 8.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.1776). Their open question asks for the permutation-kernel case. The theorem below handles its coprime case and many cases of shared primes, and allows some quotient actions that permute the socle factors. Priority has not been established.

**Theorem.** Fix a nonnegative integer $k$. Let $G,H$ be groups of order $n$ given by Cayley tables with solvable radical equal to center. For $G$, put $A=Z(G)$, $Q=G/A$, and $S=\operatorname{Soc}(Q)$. For each prime $p\mid |A|$, suppose there exists a subgroup $J_p\le Q$ generated by at most $k$ elements such that
$$
p\nmid [Q:S J_p],\qquad p\nmid |(S\cap J_p)_{\rm ab}|.
\tag{1}
$$
Let $s_1,\ldots,s_t$ be the sizes of the conjugation orbits of $Q$ on the nonabelian simple direct factors of $S$. Then decision, search, automorphism generators and exact counting take deterministic time
$$
n^{O_k(1)}\left(\prod_i s_i!\right)^{O(1)}.
$$
Neither the $J_p$ nor a quotient isomorphism is supplied. In particular this is polynomial time at bounded orbit size. If $G$ equals its permutation kernel, all $s_i=1$. Condition (1) always holds for $k=0$ if $|A|$ and $[Q:S]$ are coprime. It also holds for fixed $k$ when the Sylow $p$-subgroups of $Q/S$ for the primes in the center have at most $k$ generators and admit a section into $Q$.

## 1. Detecting a scalar extension class with two restrictions

Let $D=C_{p^e}$ with trivial $Q$-action, and let $J=J_p$ satisfy (1). The joint restriction map
$$
H^2(Q,D)\longrightarrow H^2(S,D)\oplus H^2(J,D)
\tag{2}
$$
is injective. Here is a proof that also explains the section hypotheses. A class with zero restriction to $S$ gives an extension whose inverse image of $S$ splits. Since $S$ is perfect, two homomorphic sections differ by a homomorphism $S\to D$ and are identical. The unique copy of $S$ is normal in the extension group. Dividing by it gives a central extension of $Q/S$ whose pullback gives the original class. Thus $\ker\operatorname{res}_S=\operatorname{im}\operatorname{inf}_{Q/S}$. Write the class as the inflation of $u\in H^2(Q/S,D)$.

Restriction of $u$ to $S J/S$ inflates to the restriction of the original class on $J$. Inflation from $J/(S\cap J)$ is injective here. Indeed, if the pullback to $J$ splits, the splitting is a lift of the map $J\to S J/S$ to the extension. Its restriction to $S\cap J$ is a homomorphism into $D$, which is zero by (1). The lift descends and splits the extension over $S J/S$. Thus if the original class is zero on $J$, then $u$ is zero on $S J/S$. The composite of restriction to this subgroup and transfer back is multiplication by $[Q:S J]$. That integer is prime to $p$ and hence invertible on the $p$-primary cohomology group. This gives $u=0$ and proves (2).

The proof uses ordinary restriction and transfer with trivial coefficients. A joint injectivity statement is necessary here because local restrictions must be taken using the same coefficient map. Testing independent coefficient automorphisms on $S$ and $J$ would not suffice.

## 2. Quotient maps as permutations of short blocks

Compute and compare the centers, quotient tables and socle types. The socle is a product of nonabelian simple groups as the quotient has trivial solvable radical. Use the standard polynomial-time Cayley-table socle and direct-factor procedures of [Babai–Codenotti–Qiao](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/papers/icalp12.pdf) and Grochow–Qiao. Group the simple factors into $Q$-orbits and write $S=\prod_i S_i$ for their products. Their type multiset is invariant under quotient isomorphism. Two $S_i$ have the same type exactly when their simple factor types have the same multiset. Each simple type comparison can be carried out by testing images of two generators. Match types, label all equal-type blocks by copies of a common concrete representative, and reject if the multisets differ.

Conjugation injects $Q$ into $\prod_i \operatorname{Aut}(S_i)$. Its kernel is $C_Q(S)$, a normal subgroup disjoint from $S$ because $S$ is centerless. Any nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite group contains a minimal normal subgroup and hence meets the socle, so this kernel is trivial. Every quotient isomorphism restricts to independent isomorphisms on the blocks followed by a permutation of equal-type blocks. Moreover it acts on the embedded $Q$ by conjugation by this socle map. Conversely a socle map of this form conjugating one embedded subgroup onto the other gives a quotient isomorphism. The restriction on each $S_i$ is indeed the prescribed socle map, by the faithful inner action of $S$.

For each possible tuple $\delta$ of within-block automorphisms, twist the source along $\delta$. We use the convention that the remaining map $\sigma$ is a permutation of equal-type blocks after this twist. There are at most $\prod_i s_i! |S_i|^2$ tuples, since the simple factors are two-generated and every block automorphism permutes its simple factors. They are enumerable in this time times a polynomial in $n$. The embedded quotients are explicit subsets of a product of alphabets $\operatorname{Aut}(S_i)$. Encode their elements as edges of a colored hypergraph on
$$
O\left(\sum_i (1+\log |\operatorname{Aut}(S_i)|)\right)
=O\left(\log n+\log\prod_i s_i!\right)
\tag{3}
$$
vertices. Use a marker per block and binary digits for the letters. Colors distinguish the bit positions and block types. Structural edges link each marker to its own bit vertices. Include one extra fixed vertex in every word edge, so the all-zero word is a nonempty edge even in the word family of a prescribed fiber. Thus the possible marker permutations are precisely the equal-type block permutations that carry one quotient onto the other. A marked quotient element is enforced by an additional singleton family of word edges with its own identifier. This is the small-alphabet encoding from the earlier product-quotient proof. [Luks hypergraph isomorphism](https://doi.org/10.1145/301250.301427).

For every $p\mid|A|$ find one tuple generating $J_p$ by testing all tuples of at most $k$ quotient elements for condition (1). For a proposed matching tuple in the target, first check that its generated subgroup is isomorphic to $J_p$ under the given generator images. This is computed by simultaneously following generator words in the two subgroup tables, checking consistency and bijectivity. Then its marks in the quotient hypergraphs enforce this exact $J_p$ restriction on every remaining quotient map. No factor-by-factor choice of a subgroup is made.

## 3. Compare the extension classes in a fixed marked branch

Use the [companion product-quotient proof](ProductQuotientProof.md) for the finite-ring orbit lemma. Write $c\in H^2(Q,A)$ for the extension class. For each cyclic type $D=C_{p^e}$ in the center, the scalar subgroup is
$$
X_D(c)=\{f_*c:f\in\operatorname{Hom}(A,D)\}\subseteq H^2(Q,D).
$$
The collection of all these subgroups encodes the full $\operatorname{Aut}(A)$-orbit. In detail their product with multiplicities equals $\operatorname{End}(A)c$ in the coefficient decomposition of cohomology. In a finite unital ring the generators of a cyclic submodule form one unit orbit. Quotient isomorphisms commute with coefficient maps, so the lift condition is equality for all the corresponding transported scalar subgroups at once.

Fix $p$ and a marked target tuple, so the induced isomorphism of $J_p$ is fixed. For each cyclic type $D$ of this prime, compute the joint image of $X_D(c)$ in (2) by cohomology restriction. Put both $J_p$ cohomology spaces in the same coordinates using the fixed isomorphism. The subgroup of paired values is explicit: $|X_D(c)|\le |\operatorname{Hom}(A,D)|\le |A|$, as is seen one cyclic summand of $A$ at a time. It can be enumerated by applying all these homomorphisms or by additive closure of their images. Cocycle restriction and cohomology coordinates use polynomial-size linear congruences and Smith normal form. This enumeration does not list the whole of $H^2(Q,D)$ or $H^2(J_p,D)$.

The $S$ coordinate splits as $\prod_i H^2(S_i,D)$, naturally under all block permutations, since its factors are perfect. Each $|H^2(S_i,D)|\le |S_i|^{18}$ by the direct product decomposition into simple groups, the generator bound of Grochow–Qiao Lemma 7.9, and annihilation by the order of each simple group. Put word edges for this coordinate on $O(\log n)$ bit vertices. For each occurring $J_p$ cohomology value $z$, make a separate word family for the fiber over $z$. The possible comparison values $z$ occur in the two explicitly enumerated subgroups, so there are polynomially many. Require equality of matching word families, with the same marker permutation in all tests. This leaves the $J_p$ cohomology value untouched, as its isomorphism was already marked. Testing whole fibers retains correlations between $S$ and $J_p$. Injectivity of (2) then makes these fiber conditions equivalent to equality of the transported scalar subgroups.

Each marked comparison for a fixed prime returns a permutation coset by colored hypergraph isomorphism and permutation-coset intersection. The degree of each hypergraph is bounded by (3). The degree of coset intersection is at most the number of blocks. Both procedures have singly exponential bounds in these degrees and polynomial bounds in the explicit lengths. Hypergraph witnesses and automorphism generators can also be recovered by individualization. All source code families are marked in the same block convention, so intersections are exact.

## 4. Remove the separate prime marks and lift

For a fixed $p$, union the branches over all possible target images of its source tuple. The result is the set of all pure block permutations inducing a quotient isomorphism and preserving the scalar subgroups for that prime. In particular, this union is itself a coset. A witness comes from any successful branch. To obtain its source group, run the same search from the source quotient and extension to themselves. Retain a witness from every nonempty branch and generators of the automorphism group in the branch fixing the tuple. Two automorphisms mapping the tuple identically differ by an automorphism fixing it. These witnesses and generators therefore generate the entire source group. There are at most $n^k$ branches. No product over the choices for different primes is enumerated.

Intersect the resulting cosets for the separate primes, retaining the quotient condition even when $A=1$. This gives exactly the liftable pure block permutations for the current diagonal twist. Doing this for all enumerated $\delta$ covers every quotient isomorphism. An empty result for all $\delta$ is precisely nonisomorphism. For one successful quotient map recover an actual group isomorphism using the [fixed-quotient abelian extension algorithm](AbelianExtensionLinearization.md).

To obtain all automorphisms of the source, run the comparison on the source against itself, retain one representative over every nonempty diagonal part, and adjoin generators of the liftable pure permutation subgroup over the identity diagonal. These generate the full quotient image, since the difference of two maps with the same diagonal is a pure permutation after taking the difference in the composition order $\beta_0^{-1}\beta$ when $\beta=\delta\sigma$ (or in the reversed order if the convention is $\sigma\delta$). Lift the generators and add generators for the fixed-quotient kernel by the marked-search procedure in the companion manuscript. Compute the order of the resulting permutation group on $G$ using a stabilizer chain. Any isomorphism coset is a torsor for this group and has exactly that size.

Each coefficient type, fiber and tuple list is polynomial in $n$ at fixed $k$. The hypergraph costs (3) give $n^{O(1)}(\prod_i s_i!)^{O(1)}$. The number of diagonal parts fits the same bound. All other operations have polynomial Cayley-table cost. This proves the stated runtime. A trivial socle means the radical-free quotient is trivial, and the problem is abelian group isomorphism.

## Scope and sources

The detection condition includes quotients that are substantially larger than their socle and allows a different witness $J_p$ for each prime. For example, if $Q=S\rtimes W$ and each relevant Sylow subgroup of $W$ has a fixed generator bound, one may take those Sylow subgroups in $W$. One family with shared primes is $Q=A_5^r\rtimes C_2$, where the involution acts on each simple factor by conjugation by an odd involution in $S_5$. The complement serves for $p=2$ and the trivial subgroup serves at every odd prime. The pullback along $C_4\to C_2$ gives an example with a nontrivial central extension over the complement. The quotient is not perfect and therefore falls outside the old product-of-perfect-groups theorem. More generally, (1) only requires covering the relevant prime part of the outer quotient and imposes an abelianization condition on the overlap with the socle. Unrestricted Open Problem 8.3 would require handling quotients without such a detection argument.

The exact control in `check_group_restriction.py` uses the perfect normal subgroup $\mathrm{PSL}(2,5)$ in $\mathrm{PSL}(2,5)\times C_2$ to check the detection lemma itself. It distinguishes four constructed cohomology classes using both restrictions, with the two coordinates supplied by the matrix double cover and a cyclic central extension. This fixture tests the lemma independently of the radical-free hypothesis for the algorithm.

The underlying central extension comparison is Grochow–Qiao's. Small-domain graph and coset algorithms are Luks's. The quotient encoding follows the established treatment of Fitting-free groups as codes of automorphisms of their socles. The new combination couples that encoding to the two-restriction cohomology test, keeping all coefficient maps consistent while allowing different marked subgroups at different primes.
