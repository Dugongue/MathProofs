# Abelian group bases and canonical labellings in uniform FOLL

**Theorem.** Given any finite abelian group by an arbitrarily labelled binary Cayley table, deterministic logspace-uniform polynomial-size Boolean circuits of depth $O(\log\log n)$ compute a basis of cyclic direct factors, a canonical representative Cayley table, and a bijective isomorphism between the input and that representative, in both directions. The group order is $n$. The circuit uses unbounded-fan-in AND and OR gates and input negations. For an isomorphic pair of input tables it also produces an explicit isomorphism. The earlier depth $O(\log n)$ basis bound is Proposition 4.5 of Johnson, Levet, Vojtěchovský and Widholm. [Earlier decomposition work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06478v1).

## 1. Parallel greedy bases that respect element orders

For $p\mid n$, let $G_p$ be the Sylow $p$-subgroup and write the group additively in this section. Let
$$
V_p=G_p/pG_p,\qquad V_{p,j}=\operatorname{im}(G_p[p^j]\to V_p).
$$
For any conceptual decomposition $G_p=\bigoplus_i C_{p^{a_i}}$, the quotient $V_p$ has one basis vector for each cyclic factor. In a factor of order $p^{a_i}>p^j$, an element killed by $p^j$ is divisible by $p$ and has zero image in $V_p$. In a factor of order at most $p^j$, the entire factor is killed. Consequently $V_{p,j}$ is spanned by exactly the coordinates for which $a_i\le j$. This describes the filtration independently of finding a decomposition of the input.

Order the elements of $G_p$ by increasing order, breaking ties by input label. For each such $x$, select $x$ exactly when
$$
x\notin\langle pG_p\ \cup\
\{y\in G_p:y\text{ precedes }x\}\rangle.
$$
This is the linear greedy basis rule in $V_p$ applied to the ordered list of images of all elements, since the subgroup in this condition contains $pG_p$. At every prefix the selected images span the images of everything in the prefix. In particular the selected elements of order at most $p^j$ give a basis of $V_{p,j}$, for every $j$. Hence the selected elements of order $p^j$ are exactly $\dim V_{p,j}-\dim V_{p,j-1}$ in number, the correct multiplicity of cyclic factors of that order.

This set of actual elements generates $G_p$. If $H$ is the subgroup they generate, then $H+pG_p=G_p$. Iterating gives $H+p^sG_p=G_p$ for every $s$ because $pH\subseteq H$, and the second summand vanishes for large enough $s$. The homomorphism from the direct product of the selected cyclic groups to $G_p$ is therefore surjective, and its source has the same order as $G_p$ by the multiplicity calculation. It is an isomorphism. Combining these bases across all primes gives the full group basis. In particular the number of chosen elements is at most $L=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$.

The selection tests are parallel, despite their greedy description. The test for $x$ uses the **whole prefix of candidates**, which has the same span as the chosen elements in that prefix. It does not need the answers to previous selection tests. Compute all candidate order flags by applying the uniform FOLL table-power circuits of Barrington, Kadau, Lange and McKenzie for exponents at most $n$. The identity need not be marked in the input. Test possible identity labels in constant depth by their action on every element. The predicate $y\in G_p$ is $[y^{p^{v_p(n)}}=1]$ in multiplicative notation and the predicate $y\in pG_p$ asks whether $y=z^p$ for some $z\in G_p$, checked by an OR over all $z$. An exact order flag for $p^j$ tests equality to the identity at exponent $p^j$ and inequality at exponent $p^{j-1}$. The candidate membership bits for each prefix thus have FOLL circuits. Apply the logspace-uniform FOLL abelian Cayley group membership algorithm, also of Barrington, Kadau, Lange and McKenzie, to the original abelian table and those bits as its subset input. This algorithm accepts arbitrary subset inputs, so the subset itself need not be given as generators or previously simplified. Serial composition of the constant number of FOLL stages adds their depths. The polynomially many membership queries for different primes and points run in parallel. [Powering and abelian Cayley group membership](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.2001.1764).

## 2. Deterministically indexing a sparse chosen basis

Place the chosen generators in increasing order of their group orders, breaking ties by input label. To give the actual generator in each output slot without a long prefix scan, we need an exact count of sparse subsets. The following construction handles every subset of a fixed universe of $n$ labels with at most $L$ active elements, and all its prefixes at once. The case $n=1$ has no generators and is immediate.

Use $b=8L^2$ buckets and a prime $q>100(n+L^2)$ with $q=O(n+L^2)$, obtained by trial division. For $u,v\in\mathbb F_q$, take the hash
$$
h_{u,v}(x)=(ux+v\bmod q)\bmod b.
$$
For distinct inputs $x,x'$, their residues before the last reduction are independent uniform elements of $\mathbb F_q$ when $u,v$ are uniform. Each bucket has probability at most $1/b+1/q\le 2/b$, so their collision probability is at most $2/b$. For a fixed active set of at most $L$ labels the expected count of colliding pairs is less than 1. At least one of the $q^2$ displayed hashes is therefore injective on the set. The circuits use the full list of hashes and no randomness.

For every hash test injectivity by requiring, for every pair of labels assigned to the same bucket, that at most one is chosen. These are constant-depth tests. For each candidate $x$, for each hash and every bucket, compute a bit saying whether that bucket receives any selected $y$ that precedes $x$ in the required global order. The global comparison uses increasing numerical element orders, then input labels, which are tested from the available prime-power order flags. Every predecessor-count problem is now a sum of just $b$ occupancy bits. For an injective hash it is the exact count of the active prefix.

Count these $b$ bits in bounded-fan-in, logspace-uniform depth $O(\log b)$ as follows. Treat each bit as a separate binary integer padded to width $\lceil\log_2(b+1)\rceil$. Reduce triples of summands to two summands using carry-save addition. At each position the sum bit and the carry bit shifted one position are fixed Boolean functions of just three bits, so each round has constant depth. The number of summands falls geometrically until at most two remain. A final ripple addition at this width has depth $O(\log b)$. All numbers are nonnegative and the overall sum is at most $b$, so the chosen width is sufficient. Check the count against a slot number and OR over the hashes that passed the injectivity test. Every such hash gives the same count. A chosen $x$ goes in slot one plus that count. This gives a unique generator for each occupied slot and a flag for its order. Fill unoccupied slots with the identity and order one.

The size is polynomial: $b=O(\log^2 n)$, $q=O(n+\log^2 n)$, and the counts run for all hashes and candidate elements. The added depth is $O(\log b)=O(\log\log n)$, in addition to constant-depth Boolean operations and earlier order flags. All wiring uses arithmetic on $O(\log n)$-bit integers at construction time, and the generator runs in logarithmic space. For example, it can enumerate every pair of labels and decide whether their hardwired hash values coincide. No choice of a hash is made adaptively.

## 3. An actual canonical labelling and its inverse

For each group order $n$, enumerate all sorted lists of cyclic prime-power factor orders with product $n$, as in the [canonical representative construction](boolean.md). Each list has an encoding of length $O(\log n)$ because the sum of the binary logarithms of its factors is $\log_2 n$, and there are at most $\log_2 n$ factors. The circuit construction machine can enumerate the polynomially many encodings, verify their product and ordering in $O(\log n)$ space, and compute each digit of a standard mixed-radix representation with the listed bases in the same work space.

For each such list, compare the order flags from the actual chosen generator slots to the list, padding the list with order-one slots. Exactly one list matches. For a fixed standard table label $t$ and any candidate list, all mixed-radix digits $a_i(t)$ are hardwired constants for the circuit. For each generator slot, compute its element raised to $a_i(t)$ by the table-power circuits, using the identity if the digit is zero. When the slot's raw label is dynamic, this can be done by computing the requested powers for every raw label in parallel and multiplexing the one selected by the slot. Multiply the at most $L$ resulting group elements in a balanced binary tree. Each table lookup for two dynamic element labels uses constant circuit depth by enumerating both labels and the possible result. So the tree has depth $O(\log L)=O(\log\log n)$. Select the table and the product results belonging to the matching list by a constant-depth OR over the candidate lists guarded by their match bits.

For the correct list, the output product is exactly the isomorphism from that standard direct product to the input, by Section 1. Its inverse on a given input label is obtained by comparing that label to the outputs on all standard labels and selecting the unique match, a constant-depth operation. Outputs for two groups compose to produce an input-to-input isomorphism if and only if the two sorted lists coincide. Neither the circuit nor its construction must multiply dynamic radix bases.

## 4. Comparison and scope

Johnson–Levet–Vojtěchovský–Widholm Proposition 4.5 gives an $\mathsf{AC}^1$ basis algorithm by iterative splitting. The proof here uses order-filtered basis extraction modulo $pG_p$ so that each greedy choice is an independent Cayley membership query. The final sparse indexing step is what turns parallel selection flags into a usable canonical isomorphism. This is a stronger output than the canonical representative alone in the [companion FOLL decision proof](boolean.md). The latter also gives constant-depth circuits when the exponent is fixed; the basis and canonical-labelling claim here is in FOLL. A targeted literature review located the prior $\mathsf{AC}^1$ basis result and the earlier threshold-dependent decision bounds, but it does not establish priority. [Johnson–Levet–Vojtěchovský–Widholm, Proposition 4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06478v1).

Status: AI-assisted informal proof. All claims are for explicit Cayley tables of finite abelian groups. General Group Isomorphism and Grochow–Kardeş–Levet's unrestricted depth-three question are outside its scope.
