# Graphs as concise ordinary three-tensors with controlled witnesses

Inputs here are finite simple undirected graphs. A tensor has three ordered modes with independent general linear changes of basis. A witness is an actual triple of matrices. Graphical matrix spaces and the decision reduction from GI to 3TI have earlier literature, notably [Li, Qiao, A. Wigderson, Y. Wigderson and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04815) and [Grochow and Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309). The construction below tracks all the witnesses, gives a decoder on its image, and is an informal argument without a priority claim.

**Theorem.** There is a polynomial construction from G to a nonzero concise three-tensor T(G) over any field k with the following property. Every isomorphism T(G)→T(G') is uniquely a graph isomorphism G→G' together with two nonzero field scalars. These data respect composition, the two scalars act centrally, and the third matrix is forced. In particular over F2 the construction is fully faithful on the groupoid of graph isomorphisms. No claim about arbitrary graph homomorphisms is needed. Over Fq each graph witness has exactly (q−1)² lifts, the smallest possible positive fiber for the standard scalar action on nonzero three-tensors.

### Preparing the incidence graph

To an n-vertex input adjoin as a disjoint component a tree with three branches of lengths 1, 2 and n+3 at its unique branching vertex. This tree is rigid and has n+7 vertices, more than any input component. The resulting graph P has 2n+7 vertices. Its size determines n and the extra component is identifiable. Form H on the original vertices of P and one subdivision vertex for every missing pair of distinct vertices: put an edge on each original adjacent pair and a length-two path on each nonadjacent pair. The original vertices of H have degree |P|−1, the new ones degree two, and the graph induced on the former is P. Add one universal apex to H to obtain J. The apex is unique since P contains a nonedge and no vertex of H is universal in H. Thus J is connected, simple, of minimum degree at least three, and its graph isomorphisms recover and extend those of G uniquely. This works for the empty input as well. All global extensions can be made functorial on graph bijections using the literal incidences and the fixed ordered branch positions.

Write A for the vector space on vertices of J, B for that on edges, and I for its ordered vertex-edge incidences, with cell vectors a_v⊗b_e. In their span take the hyperplane S of sums whose coefficients sum to zero. Set C=S* and define T by the inclusion C*→A⊗B using finite-dimensional biduality. Differences of cells against any one base cell give an efficient basis. All three modes have dimension O((n+1)²) and the dense output length is O((n+1)^6). The third flattening is injective. In every row one can make nonzero pure tensors using differences of two incident edge vectors. Projection onto a row by its dual gives the whole span of the incident edge vectors, since a desired coefficient sum can be balanced at a different row. These facts prove full support in the first two modes as well.

### Which linear witnesses survive?

For nonzero x in A examine the dimension of the space of y for which x⊗y is in S. For a coordinate point x=a_v this dimension is deg(v)−1≥2. If x has two or more supported rows, y must be supported on edges incident with every one of those vertices. In a simple graph at most one edge is common to two distinct vertices. Thus the row coordinate lines are intrinsic, characterized by dimension at least two. Once these lines are known, projecting S onto each row recovers its incident edge space in B. Two distinct such spaces intersect in the edge line of their unique joining edge or in zero. All column lines and all incidences are recovered. Therefore transporters on A,B must be monomial with permutations induced by the same J-isomorphism.

After removing this permutation, write the two diagonal scaling lists as u_v and w_e. Preserving S requires u_v w_e to be constant on all incidence cells, since multiplication by these entries must preserve the kernel of the all-ones functional. The incidence graph is connected. Hence all u_v are a common u and all w_e a common w. Conversely every such pair works and the third map is uniquely the contragredient of the induced action on S. This proves the witness and count statements. Over arbitrary k the target scalar subgroup is (u I,w I,(uw)⁻¹ I).

### Polynomial decoding in unknown coordinates

The dimension characterization alone does not supply an efficient search for the lines. There is a linear-algebra decoder. Take the image S of the third flattening of an arbitrary change of basis of T(G), then regroup S⊗S inside A⊗A⊗B⊗B. Intersect it with Fix(swap on A⊗A) tensored with the span of y⊗z−z⊗y in B⊗B. In characteristic two that span still has zero diagonal in every basis. Contract the last two modes of this intersection by every element of a basis of (B⊗B)* and call the span of the answers D.

In coordinates of the construction a nonzero coefficient with two distinct row indices must have two distinct column indices, since the column diagonal vanishes. Row symmetry and column antisymmetry would then force all four cells in the corresponding rectangle to be incidences. A simple graph has no two edges incident with the same two distinct vertices. Therefore D is contained in the span of a_v⊗a_v. Conversely the allowable y at row v form a space of dimension at least two. For independent such y,z the intersection contains a_v⊗a_v⊗(y⊗z−z⊗y). Some contraction gives a nonzero multiple of its row square. Thus D is precisely the span of the squares.

Solve for the X with (X⊗1)D⊆D. They form precisely the full diagonal algebra on A in the hidden coordinates. Over fixed Fq, refine A into simultaneous eigenspaces for a computed basis of this algebra by trying all q eigenvalues. The final summands are its coordinate lines. Row projections and pairwise intersections recover the column lines and the unlabelled graph J as above. The graph preprocessing can now be undone. These are deterministic polynomial-time operations for a fixed finite field. The maps remain equivariant on lines, regardless of how individual generators of those lines are chosen.

For recognition beyond the promise, reject nonconcise input or failing dimension and incidence checks. From the candidate lines form their coordinate cells. Check that S is a hyperplane in their incidence span with all defining coefficients nonzero and that after rescaling rows and columns its defining coefficients are all one. The rescaling condition is the system u_v w_e=c_ve for the nonzero coefficients in chosen bases, solvable on the connected incidence graph by propagation and verification. Check that the reconstructed J is of the padded form above, e.g. undo and rebuild and compare via the incidence identifications. A tensor agreeing on its third-flattening image and concise in that mode differs only by an invertible third map. This verifies membership of the decoded construction.

**Transfers.** A tensor search witness on the explicitly built pair yields the graph witness by reading off the row permutation. Tensor automorphism generators project onto generators of the graph group. A tensor count over a fixed finite field yields the graph count by division by (q−1)². A tensor canonizer returning actual canonical tensor coordinates yields a canonical graph representative: decode deterministically from the common tensor bytes and number the recovered vertices deterministically. Conversely, on the hidden-basis image over fixed Fq a graph canonizer can canonize the tensor by decoding, canonizing the original graph and rebuilding the tensor in a fixed order. None of these transfers by itself proves a better worst-case runtime for GI; the conversion cost is part of the runtime.
