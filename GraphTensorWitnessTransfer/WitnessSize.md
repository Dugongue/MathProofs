# How large must a graph be to represent every tensor symmetry?

**Positive and negative statements in the same model.** Fix a finite field Fq. For a three-tensor of maximum mode dimension D an ordinary graph of q^O(D) vertices can realize its entire group of linear isomorphisms faithfully. Conversely, for infinitely many balanced concise three-tensors of dimensions (D,D,D), any ordinary graph whose automorphism group has a subgroup surjecting homomorphically onto the full tensor automorphism group needs at least q^(D−2) vertices. Thus the worst-case growth in D is exponential for that witness requirement. It is superpolynomial in the dense tensor input length at fixed q.

For the upper bound view a covariant three-tensor as a bilinear map U×V→W by dualizing the first two mode spaces. Put every element of U,V,W into separate sorts. Encode vector addition and multiplication by each fixed field scalar, and list the value of the bilinear map on each ordered pair. Every relational isomorphism is exactly a tuple of compatible linear maps; dualization gives the required tensor maps in the original convention. There are O(q^(2D)) entries. Position-tagged incidence records and rigid color labels yield an ordinary graph with polynomial overhead and unique witness extensions. This is an explicit all-input realization. The smaller polynomial graph classes in [ToGraphs.md](ToGraphs.md) benefit by avoiding the full lists.

For the lower bound let d≥3 and use the unital square-zero extension algebra Fq⊕Fq^d with multiplication
$$(s,x)(t,y)=(st,sy+tx).$$
Its multiplication tensor has all three mode dimensions D=d+1 and is concise: multiplying by the unit sees every input, and products span the output. Acting by an arbitrary element of GL_d on the vector ideal and fixing the unit is an algebra automorphism. Acting on both inputs and the output in the corresponding tensor convention embeds this GL_d faithfully in the ordinary three-mode stabilizer. Thus a subgroup of a graph automorphism group surjecting onto that stabilizer has a subgroup surjecting onto PSL_d(q), by taking the preimage of the embedded SL_d and then its central quotient.

Two elementary degree estimates finish the argument.

1. If a subgroup H of the permutations on N points maps onto a nonabelian simple group P, then P has a faithful permutation representation on at most N points. For a point stabilizer H_x whose image is proper, its image has index at most [H:H_x]≤N. Simplicity makes the coset action faithful. If the image of H_x is all of P, restrict to H_x on the other N−1 points and induct. This also works when the original action has several orbits.
2. In PSL_d(q), the subgroup of block matrices with upper-left SL_(d−1)(q), last column a vector above a final 1, and remaining bottom entries zero, contains the additive normal subgroup E=Fq^(d−1). None of the nonidentity translations becomes scalar in the projective quotient. SL_(d−1) is transitive on the nonzero characters of E: fix a nontrivial additive character of Fq and pair it with every linear functional; as the functional varies these are all additive characters, and the special linear group is transitive on nonzero functionals since d−1≥2. In a faithful permutation action of PSL_d(q), the complex permutation module restricted to E has at least one nontrivial character, since E acts nontrivially. Conjugation then forces all q^(d−1)−1 nontrivial characters to occur. The constant vector supplies the trivial character too. The permutation degree is at least q^(d−1).

Use the classical simplicity of PSL_d(q) for d≥3. [Keith Conrad, proof of simplicity of PSL](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/grouptheory/PSLnsimple.pdf). Since a graph automorphism acts faithfully on its vertices, the two estimates give the asserted lower bound. Small numerical examples are not needed to establish this asymptotic statement.

**Consequences.** Even allowing a kernel of extra graph symmetries cannot give a polynomial-size universal graph construction with composition-preserving decoding onto all tensor symmetries. An ordinary many-one decision reduction can discard witnesses. A search procedure can use multiple queries or decode witnesses nonhomomorphically. An algorithm can compute a group order without representing the group as graph symmetries. None of these is ruled out. The lower bound concerns the representation requirement and does not separate GI and TI as decision problems. It identifies why an induced graph with polynomially many interchangeable coordinates can work on some tensor inputs yet fail to preserve all witnesses on others.
