# A tensor-derived parity test on bare graphs

Let G be a finite simple graph on a displayed vertex ordering. One can ask for a graph isomorphism of prescribed parity on the full ordered vertex set or on a specified invariant/marked subset X, ordered in each input. The order is the reference for parity and is not an additional order the witness must preserve. The following construction uses only data computed from the bare graph and its specified mark.

Let A be the adjacency matrix over F3, and let D be the diagonal indicator of its degree-one vertices (other graph-invariant diagonal choices work). The principal submatrix on X of DA−AD is alternating. Call it B_G. For an isomorphism carrying X to the target mark, its permutation matrix P on these ordered coordinates satisfies $P^\top B_H P=B_G$. If B_G is nonsingular, both alternating matrices have even size and their Pfaffians obey
$$\det P=\mathrm{Pf}(B_G)/\mathrm{Pf}(B_H).$$
This follows from the Pfaffian congruence identity; over F3 the two nonzero field elements distinguish even from odd. Hence before finding a single graph isomorphism we can determine the parity of *every possible one*. When the requested sign agrees, one ordinary marked-GI test suffices; otherwise the answer is no. Zero-dimensional matrices have Pfaffian one by convention.

### Small radical still permits one ordinary GI instance

Write K=ker B_G with dimension k, and precompare dimensions. For each ordered basis U of K, regarded as a column matrix, choose any columns C completing it to a full basis. The restriction of B_G to the complement is nonsingular. Define
$$Q_G(U)=\det[U\ C]/\mathrm{Pf}(C^\top B_G C).$$
Changing C to C S+U T multiplies numerator and denominator by det S. Changing U by a basis transformation T multiplies the answer by det T. Comparing C with PC in the target gives $Q_H(PU)=\det(P)Q_G(U)$. These identities are exact over F3.

Construct a colored incidence graph over each input. Keep the original graph and mark X. Add a record for every radical vector, attached through signed coordinate ports to its nonzero entries on X. These records have no freely permutable duplicates. Add one record for each ordered radical basis, joined by distinct slot labels to its vectors. Make two vertices recording the two possible Q signs and join each basis record to its corresponding sign. To test a desired sign, distinguish the sign vertices in both outputs by two colors, assigning the target colors in the same or opposite order according to the requested sign. A graph witness projects to a marked original witness and has the required determinant by the identity for Q. Conversely each qualifying original witness has a unique lift through the added records. For k=0 the empty basis record attaches to its actual Q sign and the two colors still force the correct check. Distinct argument positions and rigid colors can be encoded as ordinary graphs. The cost per mark is polynomial in the old graph size times 3^(O(k²)). Thus k²=O(log L) for explicit input length L gives a polynomial many-one reduction. Multiple marked constraints can be included together, with their separate sign requirements, in the same instance.

For additional graph-canonical covectors such as walk counts or color indicators, the condition can sometimes be weakened. Choose in fixed list order a maximal collection R of columns whose restrictions as functionals on K are independent. On the marked space and one labeled formal coordinate per column form the bordered matrix with blocks B,R,−Rᵀ,0 (in row order). Pair its first block equation with K; the independence forces the formal coordinate to vanish. Its radical is exactly the vectors in K killed by Rᵀ, extended by zero. The induced isomorphism on the augmented space is P plus the identity, so its determinant is still the requested sign. Use the reduced radical dimension in the same procedure, representing added fixed coordinates as distinguished vertices. No spanning condition is assumed for such optional covectors.

### What can be forced by trivial enrichment?

Take the graph J(G) from [FromGraphs.md](FromGraphs.md), which has minimum degree at least three and preserves every graph isomorphism uniquely. Attach one private leaf to each vertex. The old vertices and leaves are now intrinsically distinguished by degree and every old isomorphism extends uniquely. The entire graph is connected. In core-then-leaf coordinates the commutator made with the degree-one indicator has zero diagonal blocks and off-diagonal blocks ±I. It is nonsingular irrespective of the original G. In particular every automorphism of this larger graph is even: it permutes the core and its leaves in the same way. This proves GI-hardness even on this polynomially recognizable nonsingular-commutator promise (the promise is the rank test, not exact recognition of the padded image). It also explains the transfer limit. For an arbitrary input this padding determines parity on the *larger graph*. It does not determine the sign on the original vertices. To constrain the original sign one must track the original mark and pass its own test.

[check_orientation.py](check_orientation.py) checks the alternating volume formula and examples; the general argument is informal. This construction applies a standard alternating-form identity to the graph isomorphism problem with prescribed sign. It makes no unrestricted GI runtime or priority claim.
