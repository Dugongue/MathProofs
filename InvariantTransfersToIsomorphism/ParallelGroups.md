# Parallel computation of the lower central series

**Theorem.** Let $K\unlhd G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(n)$ be specified by generating permutations. The full lower central series of $Q=G/K$ can be computed in deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$, giving generators for each full preimage in $G$. The algorithm outputs the chain through its first stable term. In particular, it answers Babai–Luks–Seress §10, Problem 3, on the descending central series of a nilpotent permutation group. For nilpotent input the stable term is trivial in the quotient. [Babai–Luks–Seress, §10](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf#page=11)

Write $\gamma_1(H)=H$ and $\gamma_{j+1}(H)=[\gamma_j(H),H]$ for the lower central series of any group $H$. Commutators may use the convention $[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$, which yields the same generated subgroups as the inverse-first convention. Every strict descent at least halves the order. It suffices to compute all indices up to $L=2+n^2$, compare adjacent orders, and retain the initial segment through the first repeated term, discarding its repeated copy. The extra indices also allow the trivial group case.

## Parallel quotient operations

We use the parallel algorithms for permutation-group order, normal intersections, normal closures, derived series and constructive membership. A witness for constructive membership can be obtained as a polynomial-size, polylogarithmic-depth group program in the supplied generator symbols. For membership and witnesses modulo a normal denominator, append its generators to the lifted generators, compute a program in the containing permutation subgroup, and replace the extra symbols by the identity in the quotient. [Babai–Luks–Seress, Theorems 1.1 and 7.1](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf) · [Kantor–Luks–Mark, §§2.2 and 3.2, Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf)

We first give the computation for solvable $Q$. Obtain a $G$-normal tower $G=N_0\geq N_1\geq\cdots\geq N_s=K$ of polylogarithmic length with each nontrivial factor a product of elementary abelian groups, potentially over different primes. First compute the derived series of $G$ and join each term with $K$. This gives the derived series of $G/K$ with full preimages, because images commute with taking commutator subgroups. Its length is $O(\log^2 n)$ by the parallel derived-series theorem. Since the quotient is solvable, this reaches $K$. For each abelian factor $A$ in this series the characteristic properties of power subgroups ensure normality in $G$. Use a simultaneous power refinement to keep the full sequence polylogarithmic even when many primes occur. Put $m=\prod_{p\leq n, p\text{ prime}}p$ and use the descending power filtration $A\geq A^m\geq A^{m^2}\geq \cdots$. Every factor is a product of elementary abelian groups over different primes. The exponent of one primary factor is at most $n$, because it divides the maximal primary order of an ambient permutation. Thus $O(\log n)$ successive squarefree layers suffice. We use this simultaneous version below. Within a squarefree layer, all calculations are independent in each primary component, so that their number contributes polynomial width only. Power subgroups of an abelian quotient are generated by powers of supplied generators. The exponents have polynomial bit length, and permutation powers can be evaluated in parallel from cycle lengths by modular reduction. [Kantor–Luks–Mark, §2.2](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf)

For an elementary abelian subgroup quotient $B/M$ over $\mathbb F_p$, retain a generator lift if it is outside the subgroup generated by all earlier lifts and $M$. These independent prefix-membership tests yield a basis. The coefficient of any element in a given basis direction is recovered by trying all $p\leq n$ powers of that basis element and testing membership in the subgroup generated by $M$ and the other basis lifts. This yields exact coordinates and coordinate lifting, via products of powers, in $\mathsf{NC}$. For a squarefree abelian layer use these computations independently on each primary component. The projections to primary components are powers by Chinese remainder exponents computable from the primes at most $n$. [Mulmuley, parallel rank](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579205)

## Evaluating a shared group program across an abelian layer

An input program here is a topologically ordered list of multiplication and inverse gates whose inputs are the original generators or the identity. Its outputs can be shared by many later gates. The program can have polynomial length and arbitrary depth. Suppose all its values have already been evaluated in $G/N_i$, and we wish to evaluate it in $G/N_{i+1}$, whose kernel over $G/N_i$ is the abelian squarefree layer $A=N_i/N_{i+1}$.

Choose an arbitrary known representative $t_j\in G$ of the old value of each gate $j$. The new gate value has the form $a_jt_j$ modulo $N_{i+1}$, with $a_j\in A$. For multiplication gate $j=(u,v)$, the required equation in the additive group of $A$ is
$$
 a_j=a_u+\rho(t_u)a_v+(t_ut_vt_j^{-1}\bmod N_{i+1}),
$$
where $\rho(t_u)$ denotes conjugation on $A$. For inverse gate $j=u^{-1}$ the equation is
$$
 a_j=-\rho(t_u^{-1})a_u+(t_u^{-1}t_j^{-1}\bmod N_{i+1}).
$$
The corrections for the input gates follow from their prescribed values. The displayed constant terms belong to $A$ because the old representative values obey the gate equations modulo $N_i$. Evaluate all constants and action matrices in exact layer coordinates. The use of abelian-kernel word corrections is closely related to Kantor–Luks–Mark Lemma 3.6 and Proposition 3.7. [Kantor–Luks–Mark, §3.2](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf#page=12) For each prime component this is one triangular linear system with identity diagonal, whose unique solution is computable by parallel linear algebra. Thus the entire program is raised through one layer in $\mathsf{NC}$, without a dependence of depth on its gate depth. Starting from the trivial quotient also shows that arbitrary polynomial-size group programs over solvable quotients in this model can be evaluated in $\mathsf{NC}$.

## The lower central series as a simultaneous lift

Let $F$ be the free group on symbols for the original generating permutations of $G$. Maintain a single shared group program and, for each $1\leq j\leq L$, an output list of words in $\gamma_j(F)$ whose evaluations generate the $j$-th lower central term of the quotient computed so far. For the trivial quotient choose the empty list for every $j$. Keeping the words in the specified free-group term guarantees that every evaluation in a later quotient retains the lower-central membership.

Consider a single layer from an old quotient to a larger quotient $H$ with kernel $A$ elementary in each primary component. Evaluate the entire previously constructed group program in $H$ by the linear lift above, and call its old $j$-th output list, now evaluated in $H$, $S_j$. Each $S_j$ lies in $\gamma_j(H)$ and generates its image modulo $A$. Put $U_j=\gamma_j(H)\cap A$. For every $j<L$ independently compute
$$
 L_j=\langle [s,g]^H:s\in S_j,\ g\text{ an original generator}\rangle,
 \qquad D_j=L_j\cap A.
$$
These are normal closures and normal intersections in the quotient model, computed by joining the normal denominator to the raw lists. We have $\gamma_j(H)=\langle S_j,U_j\rangle$, by surjectivity modulo $A$, and thus
$$
 \gamma_{j+1}(H)=L_j[U_j,H], \qquad U_{j+1}=D_j[U_j,H].
$$
For the first identity, the commutators between two generating lists normally generate the commutator of their normal subgroups in this case. More directly, killing the normally generated indicated commutators makes every generator of $\gamma_j(H)$ commute with every original generator of $H$, so its image is central. The reverse containment is immediate from the definitions. The second identity follows by intersecting with $A$, since $[U_j,H]\leq A$.

Within one primary component of $A$ over $\mathbb F_p$, let $V$ be the linear span of the matrices $\rho(g)-I$ for original generators $g$ of $H$. For any invariant subspace $U\subseteq A$, the subgroup $[U,H]$ in this component is $VU$. Indeed, the identity $\rho(gh)-I=(\rho(g)-I)\rho(h)+(\rho(h)-I)$ and the corresponding identity for each generator inverse reduce every difference to the original generators acting on vectors of $U$. The opposite inclusion follows from the commutator formula on $A$. Write $V^t$ for the span of products of exactly $t$ such matrices, with $V^0$ the span of the identity. The recurrence in this component has the explicit solution
$$
 U_j=V^{j-1}A+\sum_{1\leq i<j}V^{j-1-i}D_i,
$$
where every subgroup on the right is interpreted in the chosen primary component. Here $U_1=A$. Thus there are no sequential updates along the lower central index $j$. Compute all required exact powers $V^t$ by binary squaring and spans of pairwise products of bases, and all bases of the displayed spaces by parallel rank tests. The computations for distinct primary components run independently.

## Word witnesses for the next layer

The lists for $D_j$ can be represented by words of the necessary free-group weight. In $H\times H$, let
$$
 J_j=\langle (g,g),\ ([s,g],1):g\text{ an original generator},\ s\in S_j\rangle.
$$
The diagonal generators and the generators in the first coordinate give exactly the pairs $(xy,y)$ with $x\in L_j$ and $y\in H$. For each chosen primary basis element $d$ of $D_j$, express $(d,1)$ by constructive membership in $J_j$, using full permutation preimages on the disjoint union of two copies of the ambient domain. Treat both denominator generator lists as formal identities in the resulting quotient program. Evaluate its two coordinates as formal words $u,v$ in the original free generators, substituting the already stored expressions for the elements of $S_j$. Then $uv^{-1}$ is a word evaluating to $d$ in $H$ and lying in $\gamma_{j+1}(F)$. To see the latter, use the same diagonal construction in $F\times F$, where every $[s,g]$ is formally in $\gamma_{j+1}(F)$ and this term is normal in $F$. The difference of the coordinates of any pair in the constructed subgroup lies in that normal term.

A basis of $A$ itself can be annotated by arbitrary words in the original generators, using constructive membership in $G$. For each exact power of $V$, choose a basis from the candidate products at its multiplication stages, so each basis matrix has an explicit list of its $t$ original factors $\rho(g)-I$. Keep the factor lists by concatenation at each multiplication stage. Whenever such a basis matrix acts on a chosen basis vector of $A$ or $D_i$, the output vector is represented in $H$ by repeatedly taking commutators of its stored input word with the indicated original generators, in the order specified by the matrix product. For example, our convention gives $(\rho(g)-I)a=[g,a]$. Each step raises the formal weight by one. Hence every generated candidate for the basis of $U_j$ is represented by a word in $\gamma_j(F)$, even when its weight was derived on a different quotient layer. Choose each vector basis as a subset of those candidates. The next generator list for $\gamma_j(H)$ contains the earlier words for $S_j$ and the selected words giving a basis of all primary components of $U_j$. This proves the inductive contract.

## Size and depth

A shared program stores a gate once and later occurrences refer to its index. Each quotient layer adds only a polynomial number of constructive membership programs, each of polynomial size over the explicit generator lists. Each selected commutator witness is extended by at most $L$ new commutators, with a constant number of group gates per commutator. Thus each layer adds only polynomially many gates even if it uses the same preceding outputs many times. Every selected basis contributes at most the dimension of its ambient module, and each intermediate subgroup can be represented by a polynomial-length generating list. The number of quotient layers is polylogarithmic. Within a layer all work indexed by $j$, by a primary prime, or by a candidate basis element is parallel. The algebra powers, rank tests and subgroup calls have polynomial size and polylogarithmic depth. The lifted program evaluation uses a parallel linear system per layer, so the possibly large depth of the stored word program never becomes circuit depth. In the final quotient, take the evaluated generators of each lower central term and append the original $K$ generators. Compare consecutive orders to obtain the required finite chain.

The construction gives logspace-uniform $\mathsf{NC}$ circuits with predictable padded array sizes. It also gives DLOGTIME-uniform circuits by evaluating the logspace wiring predicate for every potential wire in uniform $\mathsf{NC}^2$ and then evaluating all gates simultaneously for a polylogarithmic number of rounds.

## Arbitrary quotients

For an arbitrary $Q=G/K$, compute its last derived term $P=Q^{(\infty)}$ by the same parallel derived-series procedure on the full preimages. The term $P$ is perfect by stability, and $Q/P$ is solvable. The subgroup $P$ lies in every $\gamma_j(Q)$, since $P=[P,P]\subseteq[\gamma_j(Q),Q]$ whenever $P\subseteq\gamma_j(Q)$. The image of $\gamma_j(Q)$ under $Q\to Q/P$ is $\gamma_j(Q/P)$ by surjectivity and the definition of commutators. Thus the full preimages computed for the solvable quotient are exactly the desired subgroup preimages for $Q$. This handles the perfect and trivial cases as well.

## Scope and provenance

The argument is informal. The source asks for the descending central series of a nilpotent permutation group. The theorem covers that setting and additionally treats every quotient of a permutation group. The published normal-series and membership machinery supplies the quotient operations. The lower central computation uses the simultaneous solution of the commutator recurrence in each abelian layer and the lifting of its shared word witnesses. Parallel evaluation of circuits over a fixed finite solvable group is a known antecedent. The evaluation argument here works for varying groups supplied as quotients of permutation groups, and returns representatives needed by the lifting procedure. [Beaudry–McKenzie–Péladeau–Thérien, *Finite Monoids: From Word to Circuit Evaluation*](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539793249530) No result for arbitrary p-group intersections is used in this proof. [Babai–Luks–Seress, §10, Problem 3](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf#page=11)

## Relative commutators and upper-central membership

Let $Q=G/K$ be solvable, and let $N\unlhd Q$ be supplied by generators. Define $\Gamma_0=N$ and $\Gamma_{j+1}=[\Gamma_j,Q]$. The full sequence of preimages of $\Gamma_j$ is computable in deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$. Add the supplied generators of $N$ as additional formal symbols in the free group on the input generators. Use their normal closure as the zeroth free-group term, and define later free-group terms by taking relative commutators with the free group. At each abelian layer the image of $N$ in that layer's larger quotient is available from its input list. Thus $U_0=N\cap A$, interpreted in that larger quotient, is obtained by normal intersection. Its basis elements have witnesses in the supplied $N$ generator symbols by constructive membership in that image. Compute $D_j$ and its annotated basis with the same normal-closure and diagonal pair construction. The weight proof applies because every relative free-group term is normal. With these index conventions the exact recurrence is
$$
 U_j=V^jU_0+\sum_{0\leq i<j}V^{j-1-i}D_i.
$$
The same circuit bounds apply. In particular, for a proposed $x\in Q$, compute $N=\langle x^Q\rangle$ by the parallel normal-closure algorithm. The inductive definition of the upper central series gives $x\in Z_j(Q)$ exactly when $\Gamma_j=1$. Indeed, $x\in Z_j(Q)$ implies $N\leq Z_j(Q)$ by normality, and commutation with $Q$ lowers upper central index. Conversely, $\Gamma_j=1$ implies $N\leq Z_j(Q)$ by induction on $j$. Thus all upper-central membership answers for supplied elements, in particular in nilpotent permutation groups, are available in parallel. This computes membership, whereas Babai–Luks–Seress §10, Problem 4 asks for subgroup generators. [Babai–Luks–Seress, §10](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf#page=11)

## Upper central series of metabelian quotients

Suppose $Q=G/K$ is metabelian, and write $A=Q'$ additively. Compute its full preimage by the parallel derived subgroup algorithm. For each supplied generator $g$ of $Q$, let $T_g\in\operatorname{End}(A)$ be conjugation by $g$ minus the identity. For $j\geq 0$, let $W_j$ be the additive subgroup of $\operatorname{End}(A)$ generated by all ordered products of exactly $j$ of these operators. The product of length zero is the identity. Put
$$
 K_j=\{a\in A : T(a)=0\text{ for every }T\in W_j\}.
$$
Then $K_j=Z_j(Q)\cap A$. The assertion holds at $j=0$. Inductively the condition at $j+1$ is that $T_g(a)\in K_j$ for each supplied $g$, and by normality of $K_j=Z_j(Q)\cap A$ that is equivalent to the coset of $a$ being central in $Q/K_j$. This is equivalent to $a\in Z_{j+1}(Q)$, since every commutator of $a$ lies in $A$. In fact every commutator of $Q$ belongs to $A$, so we have the whole-group identity
$$
 Z_{j+1}(Q)/K_j=Z(Q/K_j).
$$
Thus computing all $K_j$ and applying parallel center computations in the quotient model gives generators for all upper central terms. For this last step use the quotient normal centralizer theorem in [the companion proof of parallel normal centralizers](QuotientCenterDependency.md), with both subgroup inputs equal to the whole group.

Here is a construction of the $W_j$ and the $K_j$ directly in the permutation-quotient model, valid for all finite abelian $A$. Take a supplied list $a_1,\ldots,a_k$ generating $A$. The image tuple $(T(a_1),\ldots,T(a_k))$ represents an operator $T$ uniquely as an element of $A^k$. This representation preserves addition. Each $W_j$ is therefore a subgroup of an explicitly accessible product quotient, using disjoint copies of the permutation domain. From any list of candidate tuples, discard a tuple when it belongs to the subgroup generated by earlier tuples. All prefix tests run in parallel, and the retained list generates the same subgroup and has length at most $\log_2|A^k|$. To compose two operators, express each output of the right operator as a group program in the $a_i$, via parallel constructive membership modulo the normal denominator. Evaluate that program on the left operator's output tuple. Because the left operator is an endomorphism and the denominator symbols are interpreted as identity, the result represents the operator composition. Thus a generating list for $W_{r+s}$ is obtained from pairwise compositions of the lists for $W_r$ and $W_s$, followed by the prefix tests. This equality uses the additivity of composition in both inputs. Doubling and binary products compute every needed exact length in parallel.

For a list $T_1,\ldots,T_\ell$ generating $W_j$, the graph of $a\mapsto (T_1(a),\ldots,T_\ell(a))$ is the subgroup of $A^{1+\ell}$ generated by the tuples on the $a_i$. Its intersection with the first factor gives $K_j$, and generators are obtained by normal intersection followed by coordinate projection. Empty lists are handled by the trivial homomorphism. A bound of $2+n^2$ indices again suffices because each strict ascent at least doubles the order. All products have polynomial degree and all lists are compressed to polynomial length. The doubling and each quotient operation have polylogarithmic depth. This answers Problem 4 for metabelian nilpotent permutation groups, and computes the same series for arbitrary metabelian quotient input. [Babai–Luks–Seress, §10](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf#page=11) · [Kantor–Luks–Mark, quotient normal subgroup operations](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf)
