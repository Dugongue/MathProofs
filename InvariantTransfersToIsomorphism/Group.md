# Parallel normal cores and subnormality in permutation-group quotients

Let $K\unlhd G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(n)$ be given by generating permutations and write $Q=G/K$. A subgroup of $Q$ is represented by the generators of its full preimage. The following algorithms are deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$, with size polynomial in the input length and depth polylogarithmic in that length.

1. For **every** input $H\leq Q$, compute the full preimage of $\operatorname{Core}_Q(H)$, the largest $Q$-normal subgroup contained in $H$.
2. For $Q$ solvable and **every** input $H\leq Q$, compute the iterated normal-closure chain $H_0=Q$, $H_{j+1}=\langle H^{H_j}\rangle$, through its first stable term. In particular, decide subnormality and compute the minimum subnormal defect if $H$ is subnormal.
3. For **every** $Q$, compute the full preimage of $Z_\infty(Q)$.

The first theorem gives a parallel algorithm for the core case of Kantor–Luks P5(i), including its subgroup-in-quotient version. Kantor and Luks give a sequential polynomial-time algorithm. Their more general P5(ii) allows a second subgroup outside the ambient group and takes the core of the unknown intersection. The input here supplies a subgroup of the ambient quotient. Seress presents sequential algorithms for subnormal intersection and cores, and for all upper central terms of nilpotent permutation groups. The new hypercenter bound concerns the stable term. [Kantor–Luks, P5 and §6](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/quotient.pdf) · [Seress, §§6.1.5 and 7.4.2](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511546549)

## Parallel primitives

We use parallel subgroup order, membership, constructive membership, normal closure, derived subgroups, and normal intersections in the permutation representation. The same computations apply to full preimages in the quotient, joining the denominator where necessary. Constructive membership modulo the denominator gives a polylogarithmic-depth group program by obtaining a word in the generators of the full preimage and treating the denominator symbols as identity. It also enables evaluation of a homomorphism on any computed element once its values on generators are given. An explicit homomorphism into a quotient of a polynomial-degree permutation group admits kernel and preimage computations via LIFT. In particular, when $J\leq\pi(H)$ for a quotient projection $\pi$, the full preimage of $J$ in $H$ is generated by a lift in $H$ of each generator of $J$, together with $H\cap\ker\pi$. The inclusion $J\leq\pi(H)$ suffices. [Babai–Luks–Seress, Theorems 1.1 and 7.1](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf) · [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf)

We use our parallel quotient normal-centralizer theorem, from [Parallel normal centralizers and socles in quotient permutation groups](QuotientCenterDependency.md), to compute $C_Q(A)$ for $A\unlhd Q$. The core algorithm only needs this for an abelian or a centerless semisimple $A$. For a centerless semisimple factor one can alternatively use the marked action from the parallel quotient radical construction. A good normal series of polylogarithmic length in $G$ is available by the BLS/KLM construction. Each factor is abelian or a direct product of nonabelian simple groups, with generators for the normal terms and for the simple factors. Apply the natural map to $G/K$ to obtain such a series in $Q$. This preserves the factor types after deleting trivial terms, because a normal quotient of a direct product of nonabelian simple groups is obtained by removing factors. [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Lemma 3.14 and its proof](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf#page=20)

### Conjugation constraints on an abelian section

Suppose $A$ is an abelian section normal in $Q$, and $D\leq A$ is supplied. We will compute the largest $Q$-invariant subgroup of $A$ contained in $D$. If $A=1$, return the identity. Otherwise let $a_1,\ldots,a_k$ be a generating list. Represent an endomorphism $T$ of the additive group $A$ by the tuple $(T(a_1),\ldots,T(a_k))$ in $A^k$. The representation is injective and additive. To compose two such maps, express each output of the right map as a group program on the $a_i$ and evaluate that program on the generator images under the left map. Extra denominator symbols evaluate to identity. This requires only parallel constructive membership and program evaluation.

Let $B_m\leq \operatorname{End}(A)$ be the additive span of all compositions of at most $m$ of the conjugations by the generating list of $Q$ and their inverses. Include the identity from length zero. Then $B_{2m}$ is generated additively by all pairwise compositions from additive generating lists for $B_m$. This follows from bilinearity of composition and splitting words of length at most $2m$. At each round, use prefix membership in the tuples to keep a generating sublist of size at most $\log_2|A^k|$. A strict extension at least doubles the subgroup order, and equality of consecutive spans in word length implies stabilization by multiplication by generators. Consequently word length $1+\lceil\log_2|A^k|\rceil$ suffices. Compute this stabilized span by a padded logarithmic number of doublings.

Given its generators $T_1,\ldots,T_\ell$, take the joint kernel of the maps $\pi_D T_i\colon A\to A/D$, where $\pi_D$ is the quotient homomorphism. The graph on $A\times (A/D)^\ell$ is generated by the tuples of images of the $a_i$. Intersect with the first factor, a normal subgroup of the ambient product, and project. The resulting generators describe precisely $\bigcap_{g\in Q}D^g$, since the conjugation operators themselves span the computed additive subgroup. Products of sections here are represented on disjoint copies of the original permutation domain, so the construction has polynomial degree and polylogarithmic depth.

## Proof of the core algorithm

The construction recurses through the supplied normal series. In the two layer cases below, $A\unlhd Q$ is its bottom nontrivial term and the single recursive call is in $Q/A$. The base case is the trivial group. Let $C=\operatorname{Core}_Q(H)$.

**Abelian layer.** Compute $\overline U=\operatorname{Core}_{Q/A}(HA/A)$ by recursion, and replace $H$ by its restriction to the preimage of $\overline U$ using the lifting procedure above. This preserves $C$, because its image is a normal subgroup of $Q/A$ contained in $HA/A$. The restricted $H$ maps onto the normal subgroup $\overline U$. Apply the abelian-section construction to $D=H\cap A$. It gives $B=\operatorname{Core}_Q(H\cap A)$, which equals $C\cap A$ and is contained in $H$.

Pass locally to $Q/B$, denoting the images by the same symbols. The core of the image of $H$ is the image of $C$, because $B$ is normal and was contained in $H$. We now have $C\cap A=1$. Since $C,A\unlhd Q$, this implies $[C,A]=1$. Hence replacing $H$ by $H\cap C_Q(A)$ preserves the core. The ordinary quotient normal intersection computes the replacement. Since $A$ is abelian, $A\leq C_Q(A)$. The new image of $H$ in $Q/A$ is therefore $\overline U\cap (C_Q(A)A/A)$, still normal in $Q/A$.

Let $L=\langle H^Q\rangle$, computed by parallel normal closure. The preceding restriction gives $L\leq C_Q(A)$, and normality of the image of $H$ gives $L=H(L\cap A)$. In particular, $L\cap A$ is central in $L$, $H\unlhd L$, and $L/H$ is abelian. Thus $L'\unlhd Q$ and $L'\leq H$. Apply the abelian-section construction to $H/L'$ inside $L/L'$, with conjugation by $Q$. Its preimage in $L$ is $\operatorname{Core}_Q(H)$. Restore the local denominator $B$ to obtain the core in the original group at this stage.

**Centerless semisimple layer.** Suppose $A=\prod_{\alpha}S_\alpha$ with all $S_\alpha$ nonabelian simple. In parallel, determine which factors $S_\alpha$ are contained in $H$ by testing their generators for membership. Conjugation by each generator of $Q$ explicitly permutes the factors, and their orbits are computable by graph connectivity. For instance, to find the target factor of one simple factor it suffices to take any one of its nonidentity generator images and test its membership in the listed factors. Let $B$ be the product of all factors belonging to orbits entirely contained in $H$. Every $Q$-normal subgroup of $A$ is such a product of full orbits. Hence $B=C\cap A$. Pass locally to $Q/B$ and again use the same symbols for images. The remaining $A$ is centerless. As above, $C\cap A=1$ forces $C\leq C_Q(A)$, so replace $H$ by $H\cap C_Q(A)$.

Write $J=C_Q(A)$. The map $J\to Q/A$ is injective since $J\cap A=Z(A)=1$. Its image is normal, and the injection respects conjugation by $Q$. Therefore $\operatorname{Core}_Q(H)$ is the lift through $J\to Q/A$ of $\operatorname{Core}_{Q/A}(HA/A)$. Compute this once recursively in $Q/A$, lift its generators inside $J$, and restore $B$. The quotient $Q/A$ is the same whether or not this stage has passed through $Q/B$.

Each layer uses only a polynomial-size batch of parallel group and abelian-section computations, with one recursive call on the shorter original series. A quotient introduced locally can be described by its normal preimage in the ambient permutation group. The number of layers is polylogarithmic. Generators of the original denominator are included in the final full preimage, also in the trivial case.

## Proof of the solvable normal-closure tower

For a solvable quotient use a polylogarithmic-length normal series with squarefree abelian factors. One construction refines each Abelian derived-series factor by its repeated powers with exponent the product of the primes at most $n$. Each Sylow exponent of a permutation subgroup quotient divides the largest power of its prime at most $n$, so logarithmically many such layers suffice per derived term. The derived series is available by the parallel permutation-group algorithms and has polylogarithmic length. Work independently in the elementary abelian primary components of a squarefree factor $A$.

We use the shared word program lift from [Parallel computation of the lower central series](ParallelGroups.md). Across a squarefree abelian layer, each gate of the old program has an arbitrary representative $t_j$ of its old output. Writing a new output as $a_jt_j$ with $a_j\in A$, the multiplication and inverse gates give linear equations in the $a_j$ via conjugation by known representatives. The system is triangular with identity diagonal, and is solved simultaneously by parallel linear algebra over each primary field. This evaluates a polynomial-size program of possibly polynomial depth within the parallel bound. [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Lemma 3.6 and Proposition 3.7](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf#page=12) · [Mulmuley, parallel rank](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579205)

Use a free group $F$ with symbols for the input generators of $Q$ and dedicated extra symbols for the input generators of $H$. Let $H_F$ be the subgroup generated by the $H$ symbols, and define $F_0=F$, $F_{i+1}=\langle H_F^{F_i}\rangle$. Every $F_i$ includes $H_F$. Keep shared formal programs for generators at every current tower term, with the $i$-th list made of words that lie in $F_i$. This membership ensures that evaluating the words in a larger quotient gives elements of its true $i$-th tower term. In the trivial quotient, the supplied $H$ symbols can already appear in each list. At each layer, keep the original input generator symbols in the zeroth list.

For a single lift through $A\unlhd X$, let $S_i$ be the old $i$-th generator list evaluated in $X$, including the $H$ generators. The true $i$-th term, denoted $T_i$, is generated by $S_i$ and $U_i=T_i\cap A$, since the old lists generate modulo $A$. Put $P_i=\langle S_i\rangle$, $L_i=\langle H^{P_i}\rangle$ and $D_i=L_i\cap A$. Compute the $D_i$ independently by normal intersections. Conjugation on a primary component of $A$ is $\rho$. Define
$$
 W_i=\operatorname{span}\{\rho(s):s\in P_i\},
 \qquad
 V_i=\operatorname{span}\{M(\rho(h)-I):M\in W_i,\ h\text{ an input generator of }H\}.
$$
The $W_i$ are computed by parallel doubling of spans of matrix products, including inverses and identity among initial generators. Stop after a length bound larger than the dimension of the matrix space, since word-length spans that agree with their next span are stable. Retain each basis as a subset of candidate products. Every retained basis matrix is therefore annotated with an explicit word from $S_i^{\pm1}$. The same convention for $V_i$ annotates its basis operations as commutation with an $H$ generator followed by conjugation by a recorded word from $S_i^{\pm1}$.

We claim $T_{i+1}=L_i(V_iU_i)$, taking the direct product of the primary spaces for the second factor. Each generating vector of $V_iU_i$ is a conjugate by $P_i$ of a commutator of an $H$ generator with an element of $U_i$, and thus lies in $T_{i+1}$. Also $L_i\leq T_{i+1}$. Conversely $V_iU_i$ is normalized by $P_i$ by definition of $W_i$ and centralized by $U_i$ because $A$ is abelian. Modulo $V_iU_i$, the image of $U_i$ centralizes $H$. It is normal in the group generated by $P_i,U_i$. It follows that this image centralizes every conjugate of $H$ by $P_i$, so $L_i(V_iU_i)$ is normal in $T_i$ and contains $H$. This proves the claim.

Intersecting with $A$ now gives the affine recurrence
$$
 U_0=A,\qquad U_{i+1}=D_i+V_i U_i.
$$
For each $i$, expand it as the sum of $V_{i-1}\cdots V_0 A$ and the contributions $V_{i-1}\cdots V_{j+1}D_j$ for $0\leq j<i$. Every interval product is the span of pairwise matrix products under balanced subdivision. All ranks, basis selections and applications run in parallel. This is why the depth is polylogarithmic even when the tower has many successive strict terms.

To keep formal witnesses, for each $D_i$ use the diagonal subgroup construction in $P_i\times P_i$. The subgroup generated by $(s,s)$ for $s\in S_i$ and $(h,1)$ for $h$ an input generator of $H$ consists exactly of $(xy,y)$ with $x\in L_i$, $y\in P_i$. For each desired $d\in D_i$, find a membership program for $(d,1)$ by using the quotient representation, then subtract the second coordinate multiplicatively from the first. The formal difference lies in $F_{i+1}$, since all formal words for $S_i$ lie in $F_i$ and the first-coordinate-only generators lie in $H_F$. Start witnesses for a basis of $A=U_0$ by ambient constructive membership. The annotated operators taking $U_i$ to $V_iU_i$ are commutations with $H_F$ and conjugations by $F_i$. These take $F_i$ into $F_{i+1}$ by its definition as a normal closure. Hence the basis candidates from the expanded recurrence carry words of the proper formal tower term. Choose bases as subsets of the candidates. To generate $T_i$ at the new level, use $S_i$ and these basis witnesses for $U_i$.

There are at most $1+\lceil\log_2|Q|\rceil\leq 2+n^2$ relevant indices, because a strict descent at least halves the order and a repeated term is stable. Keep all indices in parallel and compare adjacent orders. Each layer adds only a polynomial number of polynomial-length witness extensions and group programs. Reuse earlier program gates by reference. The shared program lifts with the parallel linear evaluation described above. This proves the uniform circuit bound. The closure chain ends in $H$ exactly if $H$ is subnormal. Indeed, a subnormal chain of any length $t$ ending in $H$ must contain $T_i$ at each corresponding position, by induction from the definition of normal closure, and hence has $T_t=H$. Conversely, the closure chain itself is subnormal and exhibits the minimum length when it reaches $H$.

## Proof of the hypercenter theorem

For each prime $p$ dividing $|Q|$, write $O^p(Q)$ for the normal subgroup generated by all elements of order prime to $p$. It is the normal closure of one Sylow $q$-subgroup for each $q\neq p$. Indeed the normal closure contains all Sylow $q$-subgroups by conjugacy, and a $p'$-element is a product of its commuting primary powers. The quotient $Q/O^p(Q)$ is a $p$-group. Use the parallel Sylow algorithm and normal closure to compute $O^p(Q)$. The first theorem of this note also gives $O_p(Q)=\operatorname{Core}_Q(S_p)$ for one Sylow $p$-subgroup $S_p$. Compute the normal subgroup
$$
 C_p=O_p(Q)\cap C_Q(O^p(Q)).
$$

The $p$-part of the hypercenter is exactly $C_p$. For the forward inclusion, the hypercenter is nilpotent and normal. Its Sylow $p$-subgroup has a conjugation-invariant filtration by intersection with the upper central series. Conjugation acts trivially on every factor. The automorphisms of a finite $p$-group acting trivially on each factor of such a central filtration form a $p$-group. This follows by induction through a first central subgroup $D$ of the filtration. The kernel of the map to the group of automorphisms on the quotient consists of automorphisms fixing $D$ and inducing the identity modulo $D$. It embeds into the additive group of homomorphisms into $D$, by taking the central displacement. Thus $p'$-elements act trivially. For the converse, the normal $p$-group $C_p$ has all external conjugation induced by the action of the $p$-group $Q/O^p(Q)$. The resulting semidirect product is a finite $p$-group, so enough iterated commutators of $C_p$ with that acting group vanish. Since the quotient has the same action on $C_p$ as $Q$, this gives $C_p\leq Z_\infty(Q)$. Equivalently, one may use the published characterization of hypercentral $p$-elements directly. [Critical characterization, Lemma 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01358) · [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Proposition 5.2](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf)

The complete hypercenter is the product of the $C_p$, since every Sylow factor of a finite nilpotent normal subgroup is characteristic within it and hence normal in the ambient group. All prime-indexed tasks run in parallel, with primes at most $n$, and the generators of the denominator are kept. This proves the asserted bound. The group-theoretic characterization already appears in the literature, and sequential polynomial algorithms for hypercenters are known. The contribution here is the parallel bound for arbitrary permutation-group quotients. [Murashka, polynomial-time formation hypercenter algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13606)

## Localizing every upper central term to a Sylow subgroup

Let $S_p$ be any Sylow $p$-subgroup of $Q$. It contains the normal $p$-subgroup $C_p$. Its image in $Q/O^p(Q)$ is the whole quotient. Since $O^p(Q)$ centralizes $C_p$, conjugation on $C_p$ by any element of $Q$ equals conjugation by some element of $S_p$. Thus for every subgroup $X\leq C_p$ the commutators $[X,Q]$ and $[X,S_p]$ coincide and remain in $C_p$. Induction gives equality at every iterated relative commutator step. Applying this to a cyclic subgroup generated by an element of $C_p$ proves $Z_j(Q)\cap C_p=Z_j(S_p)\cap C_p$ for every $j\geq 0$. As $Z_j(Q)$ is contained in the hypercenter, we have the exact formula
$$
 Z_j(Q)=\prod_{p\mid |Q|}\bigl(C_p\cap Z_j(S_p)\bigr).
$$
In particular, deciding membership in all upper central terms for supplied elements is in deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$ for arbitrary quotients. First test membership in the computed hypercenter. An element outside it belongs to no term. For an element inside, compute its primary powers by the Chinese remainder exponents from $|Q|$. For each such power test membership in $Z_j(S_p)$ using relative commutators in this solvable group, as proved in [Parallel computation of the lower central series](ParallelGroups.md). This works for every $j$ in the polynomial index range simultaneously. All arithmetic uses integers with polynomially many bits, and permutation powers are computed by reducing the exponents modulo the cycle lengths of their lifted representatives.

The same formula gives the full intermediate generators when every Sylow subgroup of $Q$ is metabelian. Use the metabelian upper series computation from the lower central series note on each $S_p$, take the normal intersection with $C_p$, then take the product across primes. More generally, if full upper series are computable for arbitrary $p$-group quotients in uniform $\mathsf{NC}$, the displayed formula transfers that bound to every quotient in the same model. For ordinary permutations, the reduction uses only ordinary Sylow permutation subgroups.

The arguments are informal. Babai–Luks–Seress §10, Problem 4 asks for generators for all intermediate terms on unrestricted nilpotent permutation-group inputs. The algorithms for membership and for metabelian Sylows supply distinct parts of that task. [Babai–Luks–Seress, §10](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf#page=11)
