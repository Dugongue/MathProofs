# Determinant-one monomial tensor isomorphism over varying finite fields

**Theorem.** Fix $k\geq 1$. Given explicit tensors over a presented finite field $\mathbb F_q$, consider independent monomial actions of determinant one in every ordered mode. There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm with a graph-isomorphism decision oracle, uniform in $\log q$, on the following efficiently recognizable class. On the nonzero support, the modular relation module of the incidence matrix is generated by relations using at most $k$ support entries. The algorithm accepts every yes instance and rejects every no instance on this class. A second checkable class allows arbitrary supports whenever the rational pseudoinverse of the support incidence matrix has denominators coprime to $q-1$. The class includes all full-support tensors of any fixed order $d$ with $k=d+2$, also the same formats with any support containing a complete coordinate star, and matrices of arbitrary graph support whose induced cycles have length at most $k$.

Here a monomial matrix sends each input basis vector to a nonzero multiple of one output basis vector. These are the actual mode bases in the input. The field is supplied by a polynomial presentation supporting arithmetic in time polynomial in $\log q$. There is no discrete-log oracle, primitive-element computation, factoring of $q-1$, or extraction of field roots. The proof is informal. Tensors with a zero mode dimension and matching format are zero and have the identity transporter.

## 1. Explicit local gauge comparisons

Put $C=\mathbb F_q^\times$, $M=q-1$, and let $I$ be the labelled nonzero support. The support incidence matrix $B$ has one row per $e\in I$ and one column per coordinate $v$ of each mode, with a $1$ when that coordinate occurs in $e$. Let $B$ also denote the map $C^V\to C^I$ acting multiplicatively by these exponents. For a candidate permutation of mode coordinates sending the first support to the second, align the second coefficients with the first and let $r_e$ be target divided by source at $e$. A diagonal lift of this permutation exists exactly when $r\in\operatorname{im}B$.

For any $w\in(\mathbb Z/M)^I$, write $r^w=\prod_{e\in I}r_e^{w_e}$. The bicharacter between $C^I$ and $(\mathbb Z/M)^I$ is perfect. For example, for the proof only choose an abstract generator of $C$, after which this is the ordinary perfect pairing under exponents. Thus $r\in\operatorname{im}B$ exactly when $r^w=1$ for all $w\in R=\ker B^\mathsf T\bmod M$. In characteristic two with $q=2$ the cyclic group and relation module are trivial, and the same assertions hold. This use of perfect pairings is also an immediate consequence of integer Smith normal form. [Kannan–Bachem, algorithms for Smith and Hermite normal forms](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

For each ordered tuple $U$ of at most $k$ distinct support entries, form its small incidence matrix by retaining only the coordinates it uses. Order the resulting columns by mode, then by first occurrence in the tuple. The resulting integer matrix depends only on the equality pattern of the indexed tuple. A deterministic Smith algorithm applied to it computes a list of $(\mathbb Z/M)$-module generators of $\ker B_U^\mathsf T\bmod M$ in that exact order. Attach to $U$ a colour consisting of the products $T_U^w$ for these generators. Every exponent is interpreted modulo $M$, so modular arithmetic and binary powering evaluate the colour in polynomial bit time. Take the span of the same relation generators extended by zero outside their tuples, and check by Smith computation modulo $M$ whether the span is all of $R$. This checks the theorem's promise. The field labels themselves do not affect the promise.

A coloured graph encodes the original mode coordinate vertices, one vertex per support entry with edges to its coordinates, and a relation vertex for each ordered tuple. The tuple vertex is coloured by its list above and joined to the entries by separate role-marked incidence vertices. Use disjoint palettes and distinguish modes. Thus the graph records indexed comparisons and keeps one common permutation of each mode in all of them. For a supported input, the graph's projected isomorphisms are exactly the mode permutations lifting to monomial isomorphisms. Preservation of all tuple colours forces the ratios to annihilate $R$ by the promise, and a diagonal lift preserves every recorded product. There are polynomially many vertices and labels for constant $k$.

A GI decision oracle gives a graph isomorphism by individualization and gives automorphism generators by computing transversals of the pointwise stabilizer chain, with polynomially many queries. Apply the projection to get a permutation $p_0$ lifting to a transporter and generators $p_1,\ldots,p_b$ of the group of source permutations whose lifts preserve the tensor. A negative graph-isomorphism answer also rejects the determinant-one problem.

## 2. Evaluate determinant classes without choosing a diagonal lift

Let $D$ have one row per mode, with $1$ in exactly that mode's coordinate columns, so $D$ takes a tuple of diagonal multipliers to its determinant tuple. The diagonal stabilizer is $\ker B$, and $K=D(\ker B)\subseteq C^d$ is its determinant image. The set of determinants of any monomial lift of the same permutation is one coset of $K$.

Compute a generating list $(h_j,w_j)$ for the solution module
$$
 D^\mathsf T h_j=B^\mathsf T w_j\pmod M,
 \qquad h_j\in(\mathbb Z/M)^d,\quad w_j\in(\mathbb Z/M)^I .
$$
The projected $h_j$ generate the annihilator of $K$. Indeed, $h$ annihilates $D(\ker B)$ exactly when $D^\mathsf T h$ annihilates $\ker B$, which is equivalent to membership in $\operatorname{im} B^\mathsf T$ by perfect duality. Thus
$$
 \chi(y)=\bigl(y^{h_j}\bigr)_j
$$
is a faithful representation of $C^d/K$ in a product of copies of $C$. For a permutation $p$ which has a monomial lift, let $\sigma(p)$ be its modewise permutation signs as elements of $C$. The determinant class of every lift is represented by
$$
       \chi(\sigma(p))\cdot \bigl(r^{w_j}\bigr)_j .
$$
In fact if the diagonal part has entries $z$ then $B(z)=r$, and $(D(z))^{h_j}=(B(z))^{w_j}$. All signs are computed directly in the given bases, including in characteristic two. These formulas require no lift and use only the numerical input entries and the permutation.

For automorphisms, the determinant-class map from the projected permutation group to the represented $C^d/K$ is a homomorphism, since composing any two lifts multiplies their determinant tuples. All transporters are one transporter followed by an automorphism of the source. The determinant-one test is therefore membership of the inverse class of $p_0$ in the subgroup of $C^{\#j}$ generated by the classes of the $p_i$. If the membership test succeeds, some transporter has determinant tuple in $K$, which can be cancelled by a diagonal automorphism by the definition of $K$. The argument gives a decision algorithm. It does not require printing that diagonal automorphism.

## 3. Exact membership with only small-prime arithmetic

Let $N$ be the maximum mode size, taking $N=1$ when all the positive dimensions are $1$. The projected permutation group is a subgroup of the product of symmetric groups of degrees at most $N$. Its exponent divides $\Lambda=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,N)$. Hence every coordinate of every determinant-class generator has order dividing $\Lambda$. A candidate with some $y^\Lambda\ne1$ cannot belong. Compute $\Lambda$ and its prime-power factors by enumeration up to $N$. The bit length of $\Lambda$ is polynomial in $N$. Put $L=\gcd(M,\Lambda)$. Every coordinate in the remaining test has order dividing $L$.

For each prime $\ell\mid L$, with $L_\ell$ its full primary factor, project all coordinates to their $\ell$-primary parts by powering by the CRT exponent that is $1\bmod L_\ell$ and $0$ modulo the other prime powers of $L$. Fix one output coordinate. Find the order of each projected generator by repeated $\ell$-powering and choose one of maximum order $\ell^a$. The projected generator entries in that coordinate generate exactly its cyclic group. A target entry lies in that cyclic group exactly when its $\ell^a$-th power is $1$, because $C$ is cyclic. If it lies in the group, recover its discrete logarithm and the logarithms of the projected generator entries relative to the chosen element by digit extraction. In detail, after determining the lower $t$ digits, cancel their contribution and raise to the power $\ell^{a-t-1}$. This isolates one power of an element of order $\ell$. Comparing the $\ell$ possibilities reads the next digit. The case $a=0$ is trivial and each nontrivial prime satisfies $\ell\leq N$.

Now solve the linear congruences in generator coefficients simultaneously across the output coordinates for that prime, using Smith computation. Repeat for every prime. The primary membership tests are jointly sufficient because in a finite abelian subgroup the primary projections are again elements of the subgroup, by powering, and any independent solutions in all primary components assemble by multiplication. All arithmetic is polynomial in the dense input length and in $\log q$. No step enumerates the large-prime part of $q-1$.

## 4. Examples and scope

A complete coordinate star consists of one supported tuple $a$ together with its support neighbours $a[i\gets x_i]$ for every mode $i$ and every coordinate $x_i$. For any tuple $x$ in the support, the relation consisting of $x$, $d-1$ copies of $a$, and the negatives of the $d$ one-coordinate replacements belongs to $\ker B^\mathsf T$ over the integers. It involves at most $d+2$ distinct entries. A ratio vector annihilating these relations satisfies
$$
 r(x)=r(a)^{1-d}\prod_i r(a[i\gets x_i]),
$$
which is a product of mode factors and therefore lies in $\operatorname{im}B$. Perfect duality means these relations generate the whole modular kernel, for every modulus. This includes full support and genuinely sparse supports. Zero entries elsewhere place no constraints on the diagonal part. For matrices the support incidence can be changed to an oriented graph incidence by negating one side's columns. Every modular circulation is generated by oriented cycles, by eliminating the edges outside a spanning forest and then peeling its leaves. If all induced cycles have length at most $k$, chord decomposition gives generators of support at most $k$.

The same argument is valid for a supplied intrinsic frame of unordered coordinate lines when an independent tensor theorem guarantees that every allowed transporter maps those frames. In arbitrary bases the corresponding determinant values incorporate the determinant of each frame matrix. This contributes a fixed factor on a transporter coset and cancels on automorphisms. The result does not posit a general way to recover such frames from raw tensors.

The earlier cyclic-phase work used local colour data for the unconstrained monomial problem. The present mechanism adds arbitrary support with a checkable relation-generation condition and enforces all mode determinants using exact evaluation in the quotient by the diagonal stabilizer. The earlier small-block graph work handled fixed small fields by enumerating block vectors. Here the algorithm is polynomial in $\log q$ on its stated class, even if most primes dividing $q-1$ are large. This is a Turing reduction to GI. The one-mode monomial variant studied in the code-equivalence literature has a different group action when its remaining modes admit general linear maps. Priority for the stated combination of local relations and determinant quotient evaluation remains unverified. [D'Alconzo, monomial tensor isomorphism](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-024-01375-0)

## 5. A second construction without bounded support of relations

Let $B^+$ be the rational Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse of the support incidence matrix. Equivalently, $B^+$ is the inverse of $B$ from the orthogonal complement of $\ker B$ onto $\operatorname{im}B$, extended by zero on the orthogonal complement of $\operatorname{im}B$. The rational dot product is positive definite, so these decompositions exist. This description gives equivariance under independent row and column permutations. Compute it by choosing a full-rank factorization $B=UV$ over $\mathbb Q$ and using
$$
 B^+=V^\mathsf T(VV^\mathsf T)^{-1}(U^\mathsf T U)^{-1}U^\mathsf T.
$$
The Gram inverses exist by the rank hypotheses, the formula agrees with the description, and rational Gaussian elimination computes it with polynomial bit complexity. The zero matrix has pseudoinverse zero.

Suppose each denominator of $B^+$ is a unit modulo $M$. Reduce the identity $BB^+B=B$ modulo $M$, and put $P=I-BB^+$. Then $PB=0$ and $Py=0$ implies $y=BB^+y$ in the modular module. Hence $\ker P=\operatorname{im} B$ over that ring. In the multiplicative module $C^I$, this means the vector
$$
    \left(\prod_{e'\in I}T_{e'}^{\,P_{e,e'}}\right)_{e\in I}
$$
separates exactly the diagonal gauge classes. The matrix $P$ conjugates by the edge permutation on relabelling. Therefore just colour the support vertices by these normalized coefficients and connect them to the mode coordinates. The resulting graph again has exactly the projected monomial isomorphisms. Apply Sections 2 and 3 to impose the determinants. This second branch works without the bounded local relation-generation condition.

The denominators are computable from the unweighted support. For a fixed support, every prime power of $q-1$ avoiding their prime divisors is permitted. A mixed version removes the prime-power part of $M$ supported on these divisors by repeated gcd, uses the pseudoinverse on the coprime part, and tests local relation generation only on the remaining part. The two evaluations use CRT power projections in $C$ and record both colours with the same coordinate and support vertices. Two aligned vectors are equivalent under the full diagonal group exactly when they are equivalent in both factors, by the product decomposition of that group. The determinant-class calculation still uses the full $M$.
