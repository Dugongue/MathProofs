# Determinant profiles for long nilpotent anchors

Informal mathematics. Fix $F=\mathbb F_q$, with $q$ fixed. Consider a marked square-pencil transporter problem. The input prescribes a map on a marked label subspace $W$, including a paired invertible slice, and a map on the full label quotient by $W$. Unknown maps of complementary lifts are allowed arbitrary shears into $W$. Normalize the paired invertible slices to identity. The proposed transporter is now simultaneous conjugation of the residual slice tuple, subject to the ordered anchor equations and additions from the normalized marked span.

Suppose the normalized marked slices generate a commutative local algebra $A$ with radical $J$ and $A/J=F$. Suppose the normalized matrix spaces are free $A$-modules and the residual slice operators commute with the action of $A$. The prescribed anchors identify the two represented algebras. Let $\bar X$ be the ordered source residual tuple on the reduction modulo $J$, and let $C$ be its simultaneous centralizer algebra over $F$. The last hypothesis is
$$
 C/\operatorname{rad} C\cong F^a
$$
for some $a$; there is no bound on $a$. Then the exact determinant profile of the marked problem is deterministically computable in polynomial time without TI decision queries. There is no requirement on the nilpotence length or dimension of $J$, the number of anchors, or the multiplicity of the free $A$-module. The condition on $C$ holds, for example, if the residual tuple is a direct sum of pairwise nonisomorphic absolutely simple modules. It also permits nonsemisimple residual tuples whose endomorphism algebra has the displayed quotient.

## 1. A transporter in an explicit triangular action

The following elementary algorithm is used below. Let $G$ be a subgroup of the invertible upper triangular $d\times d$ matrices over $F$, given by explicit generators. Suppose $G$ acts linearly on an explicitly represented vector space $V$, the action of any group element is computable in polynomial time, and we have a complete $G$-invariant flag in $V$. One can decide and construct a transporter of two vectors in polynomial time at fixed $F$.

Here are details to keep the generator bounds independent of the group order. The ambient upper triangular group has a normal chain of length $O(d^2)$ with quotients of size at most $q$ and with concrete quotient coordinates. First require the diagonal coordinates to be one successively, using their multiplicative characters. Once all diagonals are one, require the superdiagonals to vanish in order of increasing distance from the diagonal, one entry at a time. When every smaller-distance entry vanishes, the leading entries add under multiplication. Conjugation by a diagonal matrix scales each leading entry, and conjugation by a unitriangular matrix preserves those entries. Thus each refined kernel is normal in the ambient group, and each new condition has index at most $q$.

For completeness here is a bounded-index sifting procedure. At each level $i$ store at most one element of $G$ in the $i$-th ambient subgroup for each nonidentity value of its quotient coordinate. Include identity as the representative of the identity coordinate. To sift an element in the ambient subgroup at level $i$, multiply on the left by the inverse of the stored representative of its quotient value and proceed down the chain. If a representative is missing, insert the current remainder and stop. Sift all initial generators. To close the tables, at each level use as generators all currently stored elements at that and deeper levels. Compute the subgroup of that level's quotient coordinate generated by their images, and use its at most $q$ elements to fill any missing representatives by words in the current generators. With these representatives, construct the Schreier generators for the kernel of the quotient coordinate. Sift them starting one level deeper. Repeat the level scans until no new entry is inserted. The total number of insertions is $O(qd^2)$. A scan uses polynomially many matrix and quotient operations, since each level has polynomially many generators and at most $q$ quotient points. Sifting initial generators never loses their span, and all new representatives belong to the generated group. At stability the standard Schreier identity says that the kernel at each level is generated by entries at deeper levels. This gives a polynomial-size generating table and a polynomial-time sifting procedure for any input subgroup. The Schreier identity follows by reading a word in generators along the coset action and inserting the representative of its successive coset between consecutive letters.

Use coordinates adapted to the invariant flag, with the first processed coordinates chosen so their transformed values depend only on those coordinates. Suppose the current source vector already has the target values on the processed prefix. Its remaining permitted moves are the subgroup preserving that prefix of the target vector. On the next coordinate this subgroup acts by an affine permutation of $F$, because the prefix is fixed and one more coordinate is a one-dimensional extension. Compute its orbit and a transversal by breadth-first search on at most $q$ values. If the next target value is unreachable, reject. Otherwise move the source to a vector with the next matching value and pass to the stabilizer of the extended target prefix. Compute its generators by the same bounded-index Schreier identity for the original next-coordinate value, then conjugate them by the selected representative to stabilize the new value. Compress the resulting generators using the triangular sifting procedure. Every possible future match of the longer prefix differs by an element of that subgroup, so greedily choosing the representative never loses a solution. At the end the product of the selected matrices is a transporter. The group stays in the same ambient triangular group, the orbit steps number $\dim_F V$, and the total time is polynomial.

This is the bounded-index stabilizer method for triangular groups. For context on orbit and membership algorithms for solvable matrix groups, see [Hulpke, matrix-group orbit constructions](https://www.math.colostate.edu/~hulpke/paper/matclass.pdf). The argument above supplies the particular constructive bound used here.

## 2. Residual scalar shifts

Construct an exact anchor match by module isomorphism, and use it to put both sides in the same normalized matrix coordinates. Let $S\subseteq A$ be the span of their common anchors. It contains $1$. The choice of paired label quotient lifts gives tuples $X_i,Y_i\in\operatorname{Mat}_r(A)$, and the equations are $gX_i g^{-1}=Y_i+s_i I_r$ with $s_i\in S$. Here $g$ commutes with the anchors and therefore is $A$-linear. Compute $A$ by span closure and its radical $J$ by finite algebra algorithms. Lift a basis of $V/JV$ to $V$. On the promised free module it gives an $A$-basis by Nakayama's lemma and the free dimension, and the map from $A^r$ is explicitly checked. Any operator commuting with $A$ is represented in this basis over $A$. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

If the matrix modes are zero-dimensional, the label determinant is the prescribed one and the two matrix determinants are one, so handle that case directly. Otherwise, modulo $J$, there are at most $r$ feasible scalar shifts. Take independent $c_i$ over $F$ in an explicit finite extension $E$, and an eigenvalue $\alpha$ of $\sum_i c_i\bar X_i$ in an explicit extension $K/E$ of degree at most $r$. A feasible shift has $\alpha-\sum_i c_i\bar s_i$ among the roots of the characteristic polynomial of $\sum_i c_i\bar Y_i$ in $K$. List these roots, recover coefficients by linear algebra, and verify each shift by constructive ordered module isomorphism. The finite extensions have polynomial degrees, and the fixed-characteristic factoring algorithm runs in polynomial time. It splits squarefree algebras by their $p$-Frobenius fixed subspaces and handles multiplicities using derivatives and inverse Frobenius. Empty tuples are handled directly. [Shoup, deterministic field construction](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

For each feasible residual shift $\bar s$, add its constant lifts to the target, choose one residual conjugator, and pull the target back by its lift with entries in $F$. The remaining group is $G=\{g\in\operatorname{GL}_r(A):\bar g\in C^\times\}$, and further shifts are in $S\cap J$. This is exact since $1\in S$ and $A$-linear maps fix the scalar matrices by conjugation.

## 3. Solve all higher radical layers in one orbit computation

The kernel of $G\to C^\times$ is $1+\operatorname{Mat}_r(J)$. For any nilpotent algebra $I$, the groups $1+I^j$ have successive additive quotients $I^j/I^{j+1}$. Products of $1+x$ for lifts of bases over $\mathbb F_p$ of the successive quotients generate $1+I$ by cancellation of the leading class. Apply this to $\operatorname{Mat}_r(J)$ and $\operatorname{rad} C$. For the rest of $C^\times$, lift the independent generators of $(F^\times)^a$ through its semisimple quotient. The lifts are units because the radical is nilpotent. Lift the generators of $C^\times$ through the constant section. Together these are polynomially many explicit generators for $G$.

The radical filtration for $C$ on $F^r$ has semisimple grades over $F^a$. Refine each grade by one-dimensional simple modules to get a computable complete invariant flag. On $J^jA^r/J^{j+1}A^r$, an element of $G$ acts by copies of its reduction in $C^\times$, since $J$ annihilates the grade. Choose bases for the multiplicity spaces $J^j/J^{j+1}$ and refine by the flag on $F^r$ in every copy. This gives a complete invariant flag for $G$ acting faithfully on the $F$-space $A^r$.

View $\operatorname{Mat}_r(A)$ as a subspace of $\operatorname{End}_F(A^r)$. The tensor product of the flags of $A^r$ and its dual, ordered lexicographically in adapted bases, triangularizes conjugation on the endomorphism space. Intersect its complete flag with $\operatorname{Mat}_r(A)$, and take direct sums over the tuple positions. Quotient by the central shifts $(s_iI_r)_i$ with $s_i\in S\cap J$. The projected flag is invariant and complete after removing zero increments. Every operation on spaces and coordinates is linear algebra.

The required higher-order lift is exactly a transporter between the source and aligned target vectors in this quotient. Apply Section 1. Read the remaining shifts from the differences of the verified tuple matrices, add the earlier constant offsets, and undo the references. A preimage of each allowed shift matrix in the marked label space gives the label shear. These preimages exist by the definition of $S$; injectivity of the slice map is unnecessary. The chosen subspace and quotient maps make the resulting label map invertible.

## 4. Exact determinant profiles

If no residual branch lifts, the marked transporter set is empty. Otherwise take one found transporter. Source automorphisms fixing the marked subspace and the quotient map act on the labels by an additive $p$-group of shears. The kernel is the unit group of the endomorphism algebra of the full ordered slice tuple. The exact determinant-triple subgroup equals that of the kernel because $(F^\times)^3$ has order coprime to $p$. Compute it from the radical layers and semisimple factors of the kernel algebra. Each factor contributes the norm of one arbitrary determinant in its field to the powers given by its multiplicities in the two matrix modes. Norms are surjective and quotient units lift. Multiply this subgroup by the known raw determinant triple of the chosen transporter, undoing all basis changes. The resulting coset is the exact answer.

For example, take $A=F[t]/(t^k)$ with arbitrary $k,r$, mark all scalar multiplication operators on $A^r$, and choose residual tuples containing lifts of the diagonal coordinate idempotents $E_{ii}$ and only diagonal residues for their other entries. Their residue centralizer is $F^r$. Above degree zero the coefficients of the matrices need not commute with one another. The same algorithm works with any commutative split local algebra satisfying the given hypotheses, including when its radical needs multiple generators.

The theorem removes a bound on nilpotence length and solves all higher-order deformations in this residual class. The prescribed subspace and quotient maps, freeness, and the condition on the residual centralizer remain hypotheses. These informal arguments do not establish the unrestricted Step 17.
