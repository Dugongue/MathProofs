# Tensor isomorphism with identical pointwise slice similarity

**Theorem.** Let $k$ be any field. There is a deterministic polynomial-time reduction from ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $k$ to ordinary isomorphism of multiplication tensors of commutative local algebras over $k$. At every fixed parameter, the regular multiplication slices of all outputs of a given format have exactly the same similarity class. This holds even for singular slices and after arbitrary field extension. Given a positive integer $h$, the reduction can arrange that for every $h$-dimensional subspace of one common coordinate block, one simultaneous similarity works on the whole span of that subspace and the fixed unit slice. The carrier dimension is $O((h+1)^2(a+b)^2)$ for positive input format $(a,b,c)$.

There is also a final-digit version. Let $R$ be a finite commutative local ring with residue field $k$ and nonzero maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. Supply $0\ne s\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(\mathfrak m)$. The reduction to free $R$-multiplication tensors retains exact pointwise slice similarity and the small-subspace simultaneous-similarity promise. All outputs of a format agree modulo $sR$. For $R=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$ one may take $s=p^{e-1}$, so even the Smith forms of all individual slices, centered slices and their powers agree at every parameter. A second construction converts these outputs to fully symmetric Frobenius tensors with a fixed perfect metric and preserves simultaneous metric congruence on every marked small subpencil in all three modes.

The ordinary and symmetric reductions lift field input isomorphisms to determinant-one output transporters and reflect arbitrary ordinary output transporters. The bound is polynomial in the explicit dense input and $h$, with polynomial bit cost over finite fields and explicit prime-power rings. For general $R$ the bound is in ring arithmetic, residue conversions and unit inverses, with the identification $k\simeq sR$ supplied.

## 1. A high-rank quadratic space that remembers a tensor

Write the original tensor as a matrix space $M\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(E,F)$ with a possibly redundant slice list, where $\dim E=a$, $\dim F=b$, and the list has length $c$. For matched formats, equivalence of the matrix spaces under independent source and target changes is equivalent to tensor isomorphism. An isomorphism on the image of the slice map lifts to an invertible change on the two $c$-dimensional slice domains by choosing complements of the equally dimensional kernels.

Put $Z=E\oplus F$, $d=\dim Z$, and for $A\in M$ let $\widetilde A\in\operatorname{End}(Z)$ take $E$ into $F$ by $A$ and kill $F$. These endomorphisms multiply pairwise to zero. Form the matrix space
$$
\mathcal B_M=\{[xI_Z+\widetilde A,\ yI_Z]: x,y\in k,\ A\in M\}
   \ \subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(Z\oplus Z,Z).
$$
The low-rank subset of this space, meaning rank strictly less than $d$, is exactly the data space $x=y=0$. If $y\ne0$ the second block is invertible. If $y=0$ and $x\ne0$ the first block is invertible. The data matrices have rank at most $\min(a,b)<d$. Furthermore, any two arrow labels independent modulo the data space give a joint isomorphism $Z\oplus Z\to Z\oplus Z$. The stacked matrix is an invertible scalar $2\times2$ matrix tensored with $I_Z$, plus blocks in the square-zero space of the $\widetilde A$. After factoring out its scalar part the correction squares to zero.

Conversely equivalence of the $\mathcal B_M$ gives equivalence of the $M$. The low-rank data is intrinsic. Take the quotient of the data source by the common kernel of its matrices and restrict the target to the span of their images. This yields the supported matrix space of $M$. Independently extend the quotient and image maps by complements in $E$ and $F$ and recover the slice-domain map as above. A source and target change on $E,F$ extends directly to $\mathcal B_M$ using the same block map of $Z$ in both copies of the source and in the target.

Set $m=h+1$. On a space $V=X\oplus Y$ of formal linear variables with $\dim X=md$ and $\dim Y=2md$, form the quadratic space $H_M\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)$ of all polynomials
$$
q_B(x,y)=x^{\mathsf T} B^{\oplus m}y,\qquad B\in\mathcal B_M.
$$
Its polarization is the matrix with off-diagonal blocks $B^{\oplus m}$ and its transpose, of rank $2m\operatorname{rank}B$ in every characteristic. Thus every nonzero element of $H_M$ has polarization rank at least $2m$, including after any field extension. If $U\subseteq V$ has dimension at most $h$, every element of $UV\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)$ is a sum of at most $h$ products of linear forms, so its polarization rank is at most $2h$. Consequently
$$
H_M\cap UV=0\qquad(\dim U\le h).
\tag{1}
$$
In particular $uv\notin H_M$ for nonzero $u,v$, since the symmetric algebra is a polynomial ring.

Equivalence of matrix spaces $\mathcal B_M$ induces linear equivalence of the quadratic spaces by block changes of variables. For the converse, a quadratic-space equivalence induces a shared arrow relabeling and left-right equivalence of the polarized ordered tuples. After a column permutation, each tuple is the direct sum of $m$ copies of the original rectangular tuple and $m$ copies of its transpose. The polar rank picks out the low-rank data exactly as above. Choose two arrow labels $e,f$ independent modulo the data. In the original rectangular part each map is surjective and the joint map is an isomorphism, so $\ker e+\ker f$ is the entire source of that part. In the transposed part both maps are injective, so their kernels contribute nothing. Therefore $\ker e+\ker f$ cuts out the source of the original rectangular copies, and its image under $e$ cuts out their target. The shared arrow relabeling from the equivalence respects these two spaces and yields an isomorphism of the $m$-fold ordered rectangular tuples.

Finite-dimensional representations of the ordered parallel-arrow quiver have direct-sum cancellation by Krull–Schmidt. To see the ingredient, on an indecomposable representation the Fitting decomposition makes an endomorphism either invertible or nilpotent. Its endomorphism ring is local, since if a sum of two nonunits were a unit, multiplying by that unit's inverse would express $1$ as the sum of a nilpotent and a nonunit. Projection between two decompositions of the same representation then matches every indecomposable by the usual identity-as-sum argument. Thus equality of the $m$-fold multiplicities gives equality of the original multiplicities. Cancel the copies to obtain equivalence of the original tuples with the recovered arrow relabeling. Over finite fields a single-copy isomorphism can be recovered constructively in polynomial time by the module-isomorphism algorithm, using the ordered arrow matrices and two idempotents as generators. This makes the whole reverse map constructive from a supplied output witness over finite fields. [Brooksbank–Luks, *Testing isomorphism of modules*](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf).

## 2. The carrier and all pointwise normal forms

Put $D=\dim V=3md$, $W_0=\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)/H_M$, and write $\beta(u,v)=[uv]$. Give $W=W_0\oplus S$ a fixed dimension $\dim\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)$ by inert padding $S$ chosen in quotient coordinates. Its dimension is $\dim H_M\ge 2$. In the nonfield ring case add one more inert dimension if needed so $N=1+D+\dim W$ is a unit. All dimensions and the common coordinate decomposition depend only on the input format, $h$, and the coefficient ring.

For the field construction take $R=k$, $s=1$, $\mathfrak m=0$. In either case let $V_R,W_R$ be the free modules in the chosen bases, and $\jmath:k\to sR$ send $\bar a$ to $sa$. Over a nonfield this is well defined and injective because $\operatorname{Ann}(s)=\mathfrak m$, and $s\in\mathfrak m$. Set
$$
A_M=R1\oplus V_R\oplus W_R,\qquad
uv=\jmath\big(\beta(\bar u,\bar v)\big)
$$
for $u,v\in V_R$, interpreting $\jmath$ coefficientwise in the $W_R$-basis. All augmentation products involving $W_R$ are zero. This is commutative and associative, with augmentation ideal $I^3=0$. The first-mode slice fixing $1$ is the identity. The whole multiplication array modulo $sR$ is independent of $M$ in the nonfield case.

In a field the augmentation ideal is the unique maximal ideal. In a nonfield the trace of $L_{r1+u+w}$, where $L_x$ denotes regular multiplication by $x$, is $Nr$ by triangularity, so $I$ is its intrinsic trace kernel. The annihilator of $I$ inside $I$ is $\mathfrak m V_R\oplus W_R$, because each nonzero residue $u$ gives an injective map $\beta(u,-)$ by (1). Thus $\bar W$ and $\bar V=(I/\mathfrak m I)/\bar W$ are intrinsically recovered. Dividing the product by $s$ using $\jmath^{-1}$ recovers $\beta$ and hence the original quadratic space as the kernel of $\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)\to W$. An ordinary multiplication-tensor transporter normalizes to an algebra isomorphism by evaluating both input maps at $1$ and dividing by the resulting two units. Conversely a quadratic-space equivalence acts on $V,W_0$ and freely on $S$. Lift its matrices to $R$; the product only uses residue maps. Scale one inert coordinate by the inverse of the resulting determinant to obtain a determinant-one algebra isomorphism. [Albert, *Non-associative algebras I*](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968960).

For $x=r1+u+w$ write $L_x=rI_N+L_{u+w}$. If $\bar u=0$, the map $L_{u+w}$ takes $1$ to $u+w$ and kills $I$, so it is the same matrix for every output at that parameter. If $\bar u\ne0$, choose a basis $u,v_2,\ldots,v_D$ of $V_R$. The vectors $\beta(\bar u,\bar u),\beta(\bar u,\bar v_2),\ldots,\beta(\bar u,\bar v_D)$ are independent. Choose $w_1,\ldots,w_D$ lifting them, and extend to a basis of $W_R$. In the carrier basis replace $u$ by $u+w$. The matrix of $L_{u+w}$ has the fixed weighted chains
$$
1\longmapsto u+w\longmapsto s w_1\longmapsto 0,
\qquad
v_j\longmapsto s w_j\longmapsto0 \quad(2\le j\le D),
$$
and kills the remaining vectors. Thus the entire pointwise similarity class is common, including when $r=0$ or a nonunit. Over fields the three Jordan profiles centered at $r$ have ranks of the first two powers $(D+1,1)$ if $u\ne0$, $(1,0)$ if $u=0,w\ne0$, and $(0,0)$ at $u=w=0$. In the nonfield case similarities hold over the ring itself. Over a chain ring they imply identical Smith forms for every one-variable polynomial in each corresponding slice.

For simultaneous similarity let $U_R\subseteq V_R$ be any submodule whose image $U\subseteq \bar V$ has dimension at most $h$. By (1) the two multiplication maps $\beta$ identify $UV$ with their images in the two $W$-spaces. The map $\beta_M(u,v)\mapsto\beta_{M'}(u,v)$ on these images extends to a linear isomorphism of the whole $W$-spaces. Lift it to $W_R$, fixing $1,V_R$. The resulting map intertwines $L_x$ for every $x\in R1+U_R$, including ones with nonunit scalar coefficient. There is a free complement to the lifted image of $UV$ in $W_R$, since $\dim UV\le hD<\dim W$. Scaling one basis vector of that complement on the target side makes the simultaneous similarity determinant one. These are simultaneous similarities of each marked subpencil, so in particular every two-generator pencil in this chart has the same strict-equivalence class when $h\ge2$. Construction of these local similarities uses only linear extension and lifting of bases.

## 3. Fully symmetric tensors with simultaneous metric congruence

Form the commutative trivial extension $A_M\oplus A_M^*$, with multiplication $(a,f)(b,g)=(ab,ag+bf)$, where $(ag)(c)=g(ac)$. Let $z$ be the functional taking the unit coefficient. If the coefficient ring is a nonfield, add a free summand $Q$ with products $q q'=b_Q(q,q')z$ for a fixed perfect symmetric metric on $Q$, killing all its mixed products with the old augmentation ideal. Choose $Q$ of rank $0$ or $2$ with split metric in odd residue characteristic, or rank $1$ with norm one in residue characteristic two, to make the total rank a unit. Over fields take $Q=0$. Keep the same unit. Denote the resulting algebra by $E_M$ and let $\lambda$ be evaluation at the old unit on the dual, extended by zero. Write
$$
F_M(x,y,v)=\lambda(xyv).
$$
The perfect pairing $G=F_M(1,-,-)$ pairs $A_M$ with $A_M^*$ by evaluation and restricts to $b_Q$ on $Q$. It is fixed, hyperbolic over fields, and makes $F_M$ fully symmetric. The algebra is associative since all new products involving $Q$ in the augmentation ideal either vanish or land in its annihilator $Rz$. The final-digit agreement follows from the same agreement of $A_M$ and its dual action.

For reflection, the perfect pairing converts any independent-mode transporter to a multiplication isotopy of $E_M$ and hence an algebra isomorphism. Its augmentation ideal is intrinsic by nilpotence over fields and by the trace over nonfields, since augmentation multiplication is strictly triangular in the order $1,V_R\oplus W_R^*,W_R\oplus V_R^*,Q,z$. Its annihilator inside itself is $Rz$ by the middle perfect pairing. Its triple product, divided by $s$, recovers a symmetric form on the residue augmentation space. After quotienting its contraction radical the form is
$$
\tau((u,\eta),(v,\theta),(w,\psi))
=\eta(\beta(v,w))+\theta(\beta(u,w))+\psi(\beta(u,v))
\quad\text{on } V\oplus W_0^*.
$$
Indeed all other summands are inert for triple products, and the discarded part of $W^*$ is the annihilator of $W_0$. The form on the displayed space has zero radical because $\beta(u,-)$ is injective for $u\ne0$ and the values of $\beta$ span $W_0$. At $(u,\eta)$ with $u\ne0$, the two blocks of the contraction given by $\beta(u,-)$ and its transpose contribute $2D$ to rank. The $V$-by-$V$ block from $\eta$ cannot increase that rank, since the map from $W_0^*$ already surjects onto $V^*$. For $u=0$ the rank is at most $D$. Thus $\tau$ recovers $W_0^*$ intrinsically as the rank-at-most-$D$ locus. Pairing two vectors of its quotient against $W_0^*$ recovers $\beta$ up to the common socle scalar. This recovers $H_M$ exactly.

An algebra isomorphism of $A_M$ extends contragrediently to the dual and fixes the optional $Q$. It gives a diagonal determinant-one isometry preserving $G,1,z$. Likewise every simultaneous similarity from Section 2, extended contragrediently, intertwines the normalized operators $G^{-1}F_M(x,-,-)$ for $x\in R1+U_R$. On the dual summand these operators are the transposes of regular multiplication, and on $Q$ they act by the scalar unit coordinate. The extended similarity preserves $G$, so it also gives simultaneous congruence of the symmetric slices themselves. Over a field, on the whole coordinate subspace $x=r1+u+b+cz$, with $u\in V$ and $b\in W\oplus V^*$, the centered ranks of successive powers are $(2D+2,2,0)$ when $u\ne0$. The first rank follows from the $2D$ middle contraction plus the unit and socle contributions. The square has nonzero $W$-component $\beta(u,u)$, so its left multiplication has rank $2$; the cube vanishes since this $x$ has no $W^*$-component. When $u=0$, the centered map has rank $2$ for $b\ne0$, rank $1$ for $b=0,c\ne0$, and rank zero otherwise, and its square vanishes.

## 4. Scope and precedents

The common profile specifies the operator similarity class at every parameter for the ordinary carrier, including over the coefficient ring. It also simultaneously conjugates arbitrary marked subpencils of bounded direction dimension. The symmetric carrier supplies simultaneous congruence with the common metric, and the displayed symmetric pointwise Jordan calculation concerns the specified coordinate subspace. Taking $h$ to be any polynomially bounded number keeps the dense output polynomial in size. In the fixed-ring setting the usual fixed-characteristic equivalence gives the upper reduction as well. [Earlier common-Hessian construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SymmetricFinalFour/Tensor.md) · [Fixed finite ring equivalence](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/PrimePowerTensorIsomorphism/PROOF.md).

Jordan type of multiplication is an established invariant of Artinian algebras. Constant Jordan type is also studied for restrictions of group-scheme modules. Grochow–Qiao supply the tensor-isomorphism complexity framework and linear-length reductions to algebraic isomorphism. The preceding rank-cutoff Gorenstein construction used padding to separate two summands. Here the high polarization rank instead supplies injectivity on every $UV$ with $\dim U\le h$, and the ordered-arrow argument preserves the original matrix space under this strengthening. The proof is informal and priority for the exact common-profile reduction remains unverified. [Iarrobino–Macias Marques–McDaniel, *Free extensions and Jordan type*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02767) · [Carlson–Friedlander–Pevtsova, *Modules of constant Jordan type*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.3845) · [Grochow–Qiao IV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16317).

The exact finite control uses one rank-one and one rank-two matrix datum over $\mathbb F_2$ and $\mathbb F_3$, and the lifts to $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z$. It passed 912 joint-anchor tests, 32 two-direction quotient-rank tests, and 32 weighted-chain tests.
