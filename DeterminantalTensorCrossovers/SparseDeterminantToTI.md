# Exact sparse determinant equivalence by one tensor isomorphism

**Theorem.** Let $k$ be any field. There is a deterministic construction from a sparse polynomial $f\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_N]$ and an integer $n\ge1$ to one pair of ordinary three-tensors of maximal side $O(n^2)$. For every nonzero commutative $k$-algebra $B$, these tensors are isomorphic over $B$ if and only if
$$
f(x)=\det_n(Lx+C)
$$
over $B$ with a surjective $B$-linear map $L:B^N\to M_n(B)$ and a constant matrix $C$. The same holds when the target equivalence is required to be linear, by setting $C=0$. The construction uses a polynomial number of field operations in the sparse input length, with polynomial bit complexity over explicitly presented finite fields and over $\mathbb Q$. A labelled action transporter gives an explicit determinant presentation. The ordinary three-tensor wrapper is the linear-size labelled-diagram compiler. [Compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/927d262c8992bd305b625ad56ca25564555700f0/HigherOrderUniversality/RingUniversality.md)

This concerns the field-exact and scalar-extension-sensitive version of the sparse determinant equivalence question. The earlier portfolio already gives geometric decision over characteristic zero and uniform deterministic decision over finite fields. The new assertions are the single-instance TI reduction over arbitrary fields, the same output for all coefficient algebras, and the $O(n^2)$ sides, including in characteristic two. ## 1. Supply enough sparse input to pay for Hasse expansions

Reject if the total degree is different from $n$. Put $P=f_n$, the top homogeneous part, and let $s$ be its number of monomials after collecting terms. For $1\le j\le n$, let $H_j(P)$ be the space of coefficients in $x$ of the part of $P(x+v)$ of $v$-degree $j$. This space commutes with any invertible linear coordinate change, since a substitution on the $x$ variables replaces the coefficient vectors by invertible linear combinations and the same substitution on $v$ acts on the output space. For the generic determinant, Laplace expansion gives
$$
\det(X+V)_{(\deg_V=j)}
=\sum_{\substack{I,J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\\|I|=|J|=j}}
  \epsilon_{I,J}\det(V_{I,J})\det(X_{I^c,J^c}).
$$
Minors associated with distinct row-column index pairs have disjoint nonempty sets of monomials on either side. Hence $\dim H_j(\det)=\binom{n}{j}^2$ over every field, with the same conclusion after including redundant variables. Each monomial of total degree $n$ contributes at most $\binom n j$ terms in the split of degrees $(n-j,j)$: label its $n$ occurrences and choose the $j$ going to $v$. Thus any positive input satisfies
$$
 s\ \ge\ \binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\ \ge\ \frac{2^n}{n+1}.
$$
The same necessary condition holds when it becomes positive over a nonzero $k$-algebra, by reduction at a maximal ideal. Test $n\le s$ first for $n\ge2$, followed by the binomial bound, before allocating any space polynomial in the numerical value of $n$. Now $2^n\le (n+1)s$. All literal Hasse expansions, affine shifts, and fixed-direction substitutions below produce at most $2^n$ terms per input monomial. Unused input variables can be dropped and reinserted as zero columns into a recovered $L$.

The case $n=1$ is decided directly: a nonconstant affine-linear polynomial has a surjective linear part, and the linear comparison also requires its constant term to vanish. In all rejecting branches output a fixed pair of nonisomorphic tensors, for instance scalar tensors $0$ and $1$.

## 2. Intrinsic active coordinates and translation

Compute the kernel
$$
K=\{u\in k^N : D_uP=0\}
$$
by a sparse linear solve. For a positive instance the determinant cofactors are independent by the same monomial argument, so $K=\ker L$ and $\dim(k^N/K)=n^2$. Require this dimension. In small characteristic, vanishing of ordinary directional derivatives does not by itself show independence along $K$. Instead verify the formal identity $f(x+tu)=f(x)$ for every vector in a basis of $K$. This test follows from any determinant presentation since $\ker L=K$ there. Over a coefficient algebra, surjectivity of $L$ gives a splitting of the source with target coordinates and hence the same equality of derivative kernels. The test is polynomial time by Section 1. The verified translation identities imply that $f$ factors through the quotient: apply one direction identity at a time with a separate formal translation indeterminate. Choose a complement to $K$ spanned by coordinate vectors, restrict to it, and keep the quotient projection for witness recovery. Thus we can assume that $N=n^2$ and $D_uP=0$ only for $u=0$.

For affine equivalence solve for the unique $b\in k^{n^2}$ such that $D_bP=f_{n-1}$ and verify $f(x-b)=P(x)$ by sparse expansion. Any affine determinant presentation after the quotient has invertible linear part and must satisfy $b=L^{-1}C$. The linear system is injective and its solution, if it exists over any nonzero $k$-algebra, exists already over $k$. For linear equivalence just require that $f=P$. Thereafter it is sufficient to treat $P$, homogeneous in $n^2$ active variables.

## 3. Formal affinity along the recovered rank-one cone

Put $Q=H_2(P)\subset \operatorname{Sym}^2 W^*$, where $W=k^{n^2}$ is the active space. Let $Q_0$ denote the space of $2\times2$ minors in standard matrix coordinates. Require $\dim Q=\binom n2^2$. For a positive instance $Q$ is taken to $Q_0$ by the linear coordinate change, by Section 1.

Before making the tensor comparison, verify for every $j\ge2$ that each coefficient in $x$ of $[t^j]P(x+tv)$ vanishes in degree $j$ of $R=k[v]/(Q)$. For a determinant this holds: after substituting $v=a b^{\mathsf T}$, each term of order at least two uses two proportional columns and vanishes formally. In standard coordinates the algebra $k[v]/(Q_0)$ embeds into $k[a,b]$ by $v_{ij}\mapsto a_i b_j$. Indeed modulo the minors all monomials with the same row-index multiset and column-index multiset are equal, by swaps of their pairings. Images of distinct pairs of multisets are distinct monomials. This works over every base algebra, and the degree $j$ dimension over $k$ is $\binom{n+j-1}{j}^2$.

Here is a bounded algorithm to compute the quotients without finding determinant coordinates. Set $E=W^*$, and let $D\subset E\otimes E$ be the preimage of $Q$ under the natural quotient $E\otimes E\to\operatorname{Sym}^2 E$. It includes the commutativity relations. The graded algebra $R$ is the tensor-algebra quotient by the two-sided ideal of $D$. For $j\ge2$, use
$$
R_j=(E\otimes R_{j-1})/\operatorname{im}(D\otimes R_{j-2}),
$$
where the second $E$-factor of $D$ multiplies $R_{j-2}$ using the already computed quotient map. In fact, modulo the degree-$j$ relations whose first position is after the first tensor slot the quotient is $E\otimes R_{j-1}$, and the remaining relations are exactly the image displayed. Store each quotient map by linear algebra. Reject as soon as the computed dimension exceeds $\binom{n+j-1}{j}^2$. Thus for every $j\le n$ the previous dimensions are bounded by $16^n$, which is polynomial in $s$ by Section 1. Expand the sparse monomials and evaluate their $v$-parts in these quotients by repeated multiplication by variables. All membership tests are linear algebra. Both nonvanishing tests and dimension tests are valid beforehand: scalar extension from $k$ to a nonzero $k$-algebra is faithfully flat. A genuine determinant presentation over that algebra necessarily gives the standard quotient and all the indicated vanishings there.

If $k$ has characteristic zero or characteristic greater than $n$, these extra tests are automatic once $Q$ is taken to $Q_0$. On a rank-one line, its second Hasse derivative at an arbitrary translate is zero by the definition of $Q=H_2(P)$. The coefficient for a term $c_jt^j$ in that derivative is $\binom{j}{2}c_j t^{j-2}$, and $\binom{j}{2}$ is invertible for $2\le j\le n$. Omit the quotient routine in these characteristics. This yields polynomial bit bounds over $\mathbb Q$: the sparse shift and direction expansions use products of degree at most $n$ and polynomially many additions, all other operations are a fixed number of layers of linear systems over rationals of polynomial bit length, and no growing tower of compressed quotients is needed. In finite characteristic over an explicitly presented finite field even the full quotient routine has polynomial bit complexity, since all entries stay in that field.

Suppose $Q$ is taken to $Q_0$ by a coordinate isomorphism over any nonzero commutative $k$-algebra. In those coordinates the verified identities show that $P$ is formally affine along every rank-one direction. Taking a direction supported in row $i$, setting the original row to zero, and giving the new row independent coordinates shows every monomial has degree at most one in row $i$. Hence homogeneity of degree $n$ makes $P$ multilinear in its $n$ rows. Taking the same direction vector in rows $i,j$, zero in the others, says that the coefficient of $t^2$ vanishes, so the multilinear form vanishes when two rows are equal. Expansion at a sum gives the transposition sign relation, including in characteristic two. The space of alternating $n$-linear functions on a free module of rank $n$ is free of rank one with generator the determinant. Thus $P=c\det$. The coefficient ideal of $P$ is the unit ideal because an original nonzero coefficient belongs to $k^\times$, and invertible linear changes preserve this ideal in both directions. Consequently $c$ is a unit and can be absorbed by multiplying one row by $c$.

## 4. The infinitesimal action detects the minor module, including in characteristic two

For $Q$ define by linear equations
$$
\mathfrak a_Q=\{A\in\operatorname{End}(W):
[t]\,q(v+tAv)\in Q\text{ for every }q\in Q\}.
$$
Write $W_0=k^n\otimes k^n$ for standard matrices. The algebra $\mathfrak a_{Q_0}$ is precisely the space $\mathfrak a_0$ of sums of separate left and right matrix actions. One inclusion follows by differentiating the separate linear actions that preserve minors. Conversely $A\in\mathfrak a_{Q_0}$ sends a nonzero decomposable vector $u\otimes w$ into its tangent space $u\otimes k^n+k^n\otimes w$. This tangent formula follows by a rank-one pivot and the linear terms of the $2$-minors. Testing basis products shows that $A$ only changes one coordinate factor at a time. Testing $(e_i+e_j)\otimes e_a$ shows that each off-diagonal coefficient acting in the second factor is independent of the first index. Testing $e_i\otimes(e_a+e_b)$ shows the analogous statement for the first factor. Subtract the resulting separate off-diagonal operators. The remaining diagonal coefficients $d_{ia}$ satisfy $d_{ia}-d_{ib}-d_{ja}+d_{jb}=0$ by testing $(e_i+e_j)\otimes(e_a+e_b)$. They therefore split as a sum of a row scalar and a column scalar. This proves the description and $\dim\mathfrak a_0=2n^2-1$ over every field. The same tangent argument appears in the earlier determinant-recognition proof cited above.

We claim that an action isomorphism from $(\mathfrak a_Q,W)$ to $(\mathfrak a_0,W_0)$ must transport $Q$ to $Q_0$ under the two dimension tests $\dim Q=\binom n2^2,\ \dim\mathfrak a_Q=2n^2-1$. Such an isomorphism identifies the operator spaces under conjugation. After changing coordinates we must therefore recognize $Q_0$ among subspaces of that dimension stable by both matrix actions on quadratic forms.

If the characteristic differs from two, write $W_0^*=U\otimes V$. The plus eigenspace for the simultaneous swap on $(U\otimes U)\otimes(V\otimes V)$ gives
$$
\operatorname{Sym}^2 W_0^*
=(\operatorname{Sym}^2U\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2V)
 \oplus(\Lambda^2U\otimes\Lambda^2V).
$$
These are simple modules for the two Lie actions and are nonisomorphic. To see simplicity, the commuting diagonal matrix units split each summand into one-dimensional weight spaces, including in odd characteristic: a coefficient $2$ in one position has different weight from coefficients $1,1$ in two positions. In the alternating square all distinct unordered pairs have different weights. Off-diagonal units connect every basis element to every other by successive replacements of one factor. The possible coefficient $2$ in the symmetric square is invertible. The product version uses independent matrix units on each of the two factors. The summands have distinct dimensions, and the second is $Q_0$ by the explicit minor vectors. Thus the only submodule of dimension $\binom n2^2$ is this second summand.

In characteristic two, decompose the quadratic monomials by their simultaneous diagonal left and right weights. A repeated row or column index has weight zero in that factor, and a pair of distinct indices has a nonzero weight distinguishing that pair. Weight $(0,0)$ consists entirely of squares. A nonzero component of weight $(\{i,j\},0)$ is $\sum_a c_a x_{ia}x_{ja}$. Choose $a$ with $c_a\ne0$ and use the column replacement from $a$ to $b\ne a$: the result is $c_a(x_{ib}x_{ja}+x_{ia}x_{jb})$, a nonzero multiple of a minor. The weight $(0,\{a,b\})$ is handled by row replacement. In weight $(\{i,j\},\{a,b\})$ the two monomials are the two products of the minor. Equal coefficients give a minor, and unequal coefficients give a nonzero component of weight $(0,\{a,b\})$ after one row replacement. Matrix-unit replacements generate all minors from any single minor. Every invariant subspace not contained in the squares thus contains $Q_0$. A squares-only subspace is infinitesimally stabilized by all of $\operatorname{End}(W)$, since all derivations kill squares. This is excluded by $\dim\mathfrak a_Q=2n^2-1<n^4$. The dimension test now forces $Q=Q_0$.

The claim holds over every nonzero commutative $k$-algebra $B$ for the transported $Q\otimes B$, which is a free direct summand. Its reductions at all maximal ideals of $B$ equal $Q_0$ by the field proof. In odd characteristic the projection to the alternating-alternating summand is an isomorphism, since this square map of free modules has unit determinant in all residue fields. The transported submodule is therefore the graph of a module map to the other summand. The equivariance equations are linear with coefficients in $k$, so their kernel commutes with base change to $B$. Over $k$ the kernel is zero by nonisomorphism of the simples, so the graph is the standard summand. In characteristic two the weight projections are polynomials in the diagonal matrix units with coefficients in $k$; they preserve the transported $Q\otimes B$ and split it into finitely generated projective summands. A weight space outside the minor weights vanishes by localization and Nakayama at every maximal ideal. At each minor weight, projection onto one cross-product monomial is an isomorphism to $B$, by the same fiber check for a map of projective modules. The weight space is generated by $u+c v$ for the two cross-product monomials. Applying a row replacement with result $(1+c)x_{ia}x_{ib}$ lands in a vanishing weight space, so $c=1$. This proves exact recovery also over rings with nilpotents.

## 5. The single three-tensor instance

Output the comparison of the two labelled bilinear maps
$$
(\mathfrak a_Q,W,\ \mathfrak a_Q\times W\to W),\qquad
(\mathfrak a_0,W_0,\ \mathfrak a_0\times W_0\to W_0),
$$
with the input and output occurrences of the second module carrying the same label. An isomorphism consists of $g:W\to W_0$ and $h: \mathfrak a_Q\to\mathfrak a_0$ satisfying $g(Aw)=h(A)g(w)$, so it is exactly a conjugacy of the operator spaces, with $h$ forced. Compute $\mathfrak a_Q$ by imposing the displayed linear equations and require its dimension $2n^2-1$. The dimension is necessary even for a positive presentation over $B$: the linear equations define a kernel commuting with the flat scalar extension $k\to B$, and in determinant coordinates it has the standard action.

Apply the existing ring compiler to these constant-label bilinear diagrams. It produces one ordinary three-tensor pair, with each side bounded by a constant times $\dim\mathfrak a_Q+\dim W+1=O(n^2)$, over $k$. Over any $B$, a determinant presentation gives the labelled isomorphism and every earlier check. Conversely a labelled isomorphism recovers $Q_0$ by Section 4 and the actual determinant identity by Section 3. Evaluate the transported $P$ at the identity matrix to compute $c$, and absorb it by scaling one row. The quotient coordinates and the shift restore $L,C$. The construction uses the pivot idea from determinantal rank-drop analysis at two points: the tangent space at a rank-one pivot identifies the two matrix actions, and formal rank-one directions turn these actions back into an exact polynomial identity. No factorization, field splitting or algebraic-closure arithmetic is used.

## Explicit witness extraction already requires square roots

The field-operation decision model in the sparse question differs from asking for a change of coordinates over $\mathbb C$. For every nonzero $a\in\mathbb C$, the polynomial
$$
f_a(x)=x_1^2-a x_2^2+x_3x_4
$$
is a nondegenerate quadratic form and hence complex-equivalent to the generic $2\times2$ determinant. If an invertible matrix $A$ satisfies $f_a(x)=\det_2(Ax)$ in fixed four-coordinate matrix order, compare Gram determinants. The Gram determinant of $f_a$ is $a/4$; that of $\det_2(x)$ is $1/16$. Thus $\det(A)^2=4a$ and $\det(A)/2$ is a square root of $a$. Witness extraction uniformly solves square-root extraction even for $n=2$.

An arithmetic algorithm allowed only field operations, equality tests and decision-oracle bits cannot output missing roots in this model, regardless of the number of such oracle calls. All scalar registers remain in the field generated by the input scalars and the finitely many constants in the algorithm, because the oracle returns only discrete decisions. The constants generate a finitely generated field over $\mathbb Q$, whose algebraic subfield over $\mathbb Q$ is finite. Choose a rational prime with square root outside it. For this input no change-of-coordinates witness can be output by those operations. This shows a concrete root requirement for the search problem accompanying Question VII.2. The earlier decision result with a PIT oracle remains valid. [Grochow, Question VII.2](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~billey/classes/lie/bulletins/grochow.2012.pdf#page=10)

## One black-box query tests both PIT and central-simple-algebra splitting

Over $\mathbb Q$, black-box formal determinant equivalence is deterministically polynomial-time Turing equivalent to polynomial identity testing together with splitting of a structure-constant central simple algebra. The lower direction is stronger: one determinant-equivalence query tests the conjunction that the input polynomial is identically zero and the algebra is split. The previous geometric result tests PIT alone. The FMAI connection and earlier randomized reductions are due to Garg–Gupta–Kayal–Saha and Murthy–Nair–Saha. The point here is the combined query and a deterministic upper reduction using PIT as a decision oracle. [Garg–Gupta–Kayal–Saha](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2019.62) · [Murthy–Nair–Saha](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2020.72)

**Reduced norms and the base field.** Let $A$ be a central simple algebra of degree $n$ over a characteristic-zero field $k$, and $P_A=\operatorname{Nrd}_A$ its reduced norm. It is evaluable from the structure constants by polynomially many field operations. For $a\in A$, compute
$$
p_i=\operatorname{Tr}(L_{a^i})/n,\qquad
e_0=1,\qquad j e_j=\sum_{i=1}^j(-1)^{i-1}e_{j-i}p_i .
$$
Here $L$ denotes left multiplication on the $n^2$-dimensional algebra. Over a splitting field $p_i$ is the ordinary matrix trace of $a^i$, since left multiplication acts on $n$ copies of the standard module. Newton's equations give $P_A(a)=e_n$. The recurrence also gives a polynomial-size arithmetic circuit, since $a^i$ is computed in the displayed algebra basis.

The norm $P_A$ is linearly equivalent over $k$ to $\det_n$ exactly when $A$ is split. One direction is immediate from the definition. For the other, assume $n\ge2$. Under scalar extension to a splitting field the infinitesimal action space of $H_2(P_A)$ is the sum of the left and right actions by Section 4, so it equals $L(A)+R(A)$ already over $k$. In characteristic zero its derived Lie algebra is the direct sum of $L(A^0)$ and $R(A^0)$, where $A^0$ is the reduced-trace-zero subspace. Indeed the commutator spans $A^0$ by scalar extension, the left and right factors commute, and their intersection consists of scalar identity operators, excluded by trace zero. The two summands are absolutely simple because after splitting they identify with $\mathfrak{sl}_n$. An equivalence of the norm with the generic determinant identifies this derived Lie algebra with the standard one under conjugation. It carries each of the two simple ideals to one of the two standard ideals. Adding the span of the identity to the ideal for $L(A^0)$ gives $L(A)$, which is therefore isomorphic as an associative algebra to a standard copy of $M_n(k)$ or its opposite. Either is split. The case $n=1$ is automatic.

**Conjunction reduction.** Let $h$ be an input polynomial on $s$ variables with degree at most $D$, where $D\ge1$ is supplied. Take $H(Y,u)=u^D h(Y/u)$ as a formal homogenization. Its black box at all points, including $u=0$, follows by interpolation of $h(tY)$. In $A'=M_m(A)$ put $M=mn$, with $m$ chosen so $M-n>D+1$ and $M^2\ge s+3$. Choose a basis of $A'$ starting with $e=\operatorname{diag}(1_A,0,\ldots,0)$, and let $w$ be its coordinate. Use $s+2$ other distinct coordinates for $Y,u,v$. Produce the degree-$M$ homogeneous black box
$$
P(X)=\operatorname{Nrd}_{A'}(X)+w^{M-D-1}vH(Y,u).
$$
If $h=0$, this is equivalent to $\det_M$ precisely when $A'$, hence $A$, is split. If $h\ne0$, inspect the Taylor expansion at $e$ over a splitting field. Its matrix rank is $n$. A rank-$r$ point of the generic determinant has lowest nonzero homogeneous Taylor piece equal, up to invertible linear change and nonzero multiplier, to the generic $(M-r)$-square determinant. To see this, move the point to $\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0)$ and expand by rows; the degree-$(M-r)$ terms all take the constants on the first $r$ diagonal entries. This Taylor piece is irreducible, since the singular-matrix locus is the irreducible image of rank factorizations and a corank-one point is smooth. At an invertible point the lowest piece is a nonzero constant. Thus the norm term has order $M-n$ at $e$. The perturbation has lower order $D+1$ and initial piece $vH$, reducible because $H$ is nonzero homogeneous of positive degree and has no $v$-variable. The lowest piece of $P$ is therefore reducible, which is impossible under any invertible affine change of the generic determinant. This is the rank-pivot initial-form test from the earlier PIT reduction, now applied at a prescribed rational non-minimal rank in a twisted determinantal form. [Earlier PIT reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/927d262c8992bd305b625ad56ca25564555700f0/SparseDeterminantEquivalence/PROOF.md)

**Deterministic upper reduction over $\mathbb Q$.** Use the earlier geometric equivalence algorithm with the PIT decision oracle to reject exactly the polynomials that are not geometrically equivalent to $\det_n$. The earlier algorithm finds the infinitesimal determinant stabilizer by polynomially many oracle-guided rank increases using the features $x_j\partial_i P$; each feature evaluation is obtained by univariate interpolation. For a surviving homogeneous input the infinitesimal stabilizer is a $\mathbb Q$-form of $\mathfrak{sl}_n\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_n$. For any rational determinant equivalence the centroid splits. Its dimension is two, and its splitting is determined by the discriminant of the minimal polynomial of any nonscalar centroid element. The square test and roots of a rational square use integer square roots. If the centroid fails to split, reject. Otherwise extract either ideal $\mathfrak h$ by linear algebra and put $\mathcal A=\mathbb Q I\oplus\mathfrak h\subset\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Q}(\mathbb Q^{n^2})$. This is an associative central simple algebra because after scalar extension to an algebraic closure it is a standard left or right full matrix algebra. Query whether $\mathcal A$ is split.

For soundness on a split answer, identify $\mathcal A$ with $M_n(\mathbb Q)$ and its $n^2$-dimensional module with $n$ copies of the standard module. Existence of these identifications suffices for decision. On a chosen representation of the standard module the linear action of $\mathfrak h$ is exactly the trace-zero matrix action on the left of the matrix of $n$ columns. The infinitesimal invariance equations imply invariance under all elementary row transvections by differentiation in their parameter in characteristic zero. They generate $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb Q)$, whose action by left multiplication has determinant fibers as orbits on invertible matrices. Consequently $P(X)=c\det X$ on invertible matrices, and hence as a polynomial, by density and homogeneity. The nonzero scalar $c\in\mathbb Q$ is absorbed by one row scaling. Conversely a rational determinant equivalence forces both ideals to give split algebras. The same upper reduction handles affine inputs using the earlier top-degree comparison and PIT verification of the affine shift. The $n=1$ case is direct. Together with the conjunction reduction, this gives the claimed decision equivalence. It leaves the complexity of the central-simple-algebra splitting oracle itself unspecified.

## Verification and scope

These are written proofs. The geometric sparse decision and the black-box PIT comparison were obtained earlier. The new theorem connects the arithmetic-field decision problem to ordinary TI, and gives a single raw instance whose behavior survives arbitrary scalar extension. The black-box extension separates the rational splitting requirement from geometric determinant equivalence and combines it with PIT in one query. The unrestricted TI-completeness questions remain outside these theorems. Literature priority for the new action-tensor reduction remains provisional.
