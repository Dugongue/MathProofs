# Polynomial Calculus and circuit IPS for tensors with semisimple adjoints

**Theorem.** Let $k$ be any field and let $T,S$ be same-format three-tensors that are not isomorphic over an algebraic closure. Suppose at least one is geometrically a direct sum of rectangular matrix multiplication tensors, with zero-padding allowed. Put $n=\max(\dim X,\dim Y,\dim Z,2)$. On the literal cubic action and two-sided inverse equations, without field equations, there is a Polynomial Calculus refutation of degree $O(n)$ and sparse size $2^{O(n\log n)}$. If $\operatorname{char}k=0$, there is a constant-degree refutation of polynomial sparse size. There is also a polynomial-size Hilbert-like circuit IPS refutation of degree $O(n)$ over $k$. For a concise source the proof obtains the same bounds against an arbitrary target, including one outside the geometric matrix multiplication class. [Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She, Questions 7.1–7.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19320)

The result uses the full-Hom reconstruction and separability criterion in the companion proof. It gives a sufficient positive case of the polynomial-degree question, and an explicit sparse size bound for the same class. It does not assert those bounds for arbitrary tensors.

## 1. Polynomial certificates for the adjoint and its action

Use bilinear notation $T:X\times Y\to Z$ and $S:X'\times Y'\to Z'$. Let $P,Q,R$ be the forward matrices, with named inverses $P',Q',R'$. From the raw cubic action and inverse equations, one obtains
$$
S_u=R T_{P'u}Q',\qquad T_x=R'S_{Px}Q.
\tag{1}
$$
These are the raw action in the bilinear convention and its reverse. One may express them with either orientation of transposes by renaming matrices. To derive the reverse, apply the three inverse actions to the tensor equation and telescope the three inverse products. All resulting certificates have degree at most six. Equations below use polynomial ideal certificates, so their stated degree bounds imply ordinary Polynomial Calculus bounds by expansion and addition.

First trim both tensors to their flattening supports. In constant degree the forward and inverse matrices carry the appropriate supports into one another as follows. Adapt constant bases to the two sets of supports, use (1), cancel actions in the other modes by their inverses, and multiply on the constant flattening by a fixed right inverse of its independent supported rows. This zeros the forbidden blocks. The transformed raw action contains the supported action and the two inverse equations descend by using these forbidden-block equations in the matrix products. When the support dimensions differ, this gives a rectangular rank contradiction. The determinant certificate in Section 3 handles it in degree $O(n)$. In characteristic zero one can instead take traces of the two rectangular products and subtract, proving the mismatch in constant degree. Henceforth assume the active shapes agree. These substitutions and derivations have constant certificate overhead. They preserve proof degrees $O(n)$ and preserve the stated sparse bounds after expansion, since linear substitutions increase the monomial count of a degree $O(n)$ polynomial by at most $n^{O(n)}$.

For a concise tensor write $D_T=\{(A,B):A T_x=T_x B\ \forall x\}$, an algebra of operator pairs on output and second input. Define the polynomial coordinate map
$$
\Phi(A,B)=(R A R',Q B Q').
$$
Its reverse $\Psi$ uses $R',Q'$ on the left and $R,Q$ on the right. The map carries the fixed source adjoint to the target adjoint, in the following certificate sense. Apply (1) to each basis slice $S_u$, insert $\Phi(A,B)$ in the adjoint equation, and cancel $R'R,Q'Q$. The result is $R( A T_{P'u}-T_{P'u}B )Q'$, which is identically zero. If $\mathcal L_S$ is the constant linear map defining the target adjoint, choose a constant projection $\pi_S$ onto its kernel. There is a constant linear map $H$ with $w-\pi_S(w)=H\mathcal L_S(w)$ for every $w$, by choosing a complement to the kernel. Thus the polynomial coordinates of $\Phi(A,B)$ can be replaced by coordinates in the fixed basis of $D_S$, with constant-degree certificates. Use the same argument in reverse for $\Psi$.

Within these fixed bases, both maps satisfy the unit, product and inverse-map identities modulo constant-degree certificates. For instance, for products of transported operators insert the adjacent inverse equations in $R A R' R B R'$ and $Q A' Q'Q B'Q'$, and use fixed linear projections as above to pass to basis coordinates. The identities remain available on given constant inputs and also after the bounded-degree substitutions used below. To prove that these maps preserve centers, let $e$ be central in the source. For each target basis element $b$, the commutator $[e,\Psi(b)]$ vanishes because $\Psi(b)$ has already been expressed in the fixed source adjoint basis. Transport this equality and cancel $\Phi\Psi(b)=b$. This shows that $\Phi(e)$ commutes with every target basis element. Projecting to the kernel of this second fixed linear system puts $\Phi(e)$ in the fixed center, again with constant-degree certificates.

## 2. Certificates when the target fails recognition

A full-Hom, separable source forces both properties on the target with constant-degree certificates from (1). If $D_S$ is inseparable, the linear equations for a separability element are inconsistent over $k$. Take a fixed source separability element $e=\sum_i a_i\otimes b_i$, and transport it as $\sum_i\Phi(a_i)\otimes\Phi(b_i)$. Its multiplication is the unit by the product identity. For every target basis element $d$, use $\Psi(d)$ in the source commuting equation
$$
\sum_i \Psi(d)a_i\otimes b_i=\sum_i a_i\otimes b_i\Psi(d).
$$
This equation is linear in $\Psi(d)$, so it follows by expanding in the fixed source basis. Transport and use $\Phi\Psi(d)=d$. The resulting polynomial coordinates of the transported element satisfy the entire linear target system, with constant-degree certificates. A fixed inconsistent linear combination gives a refutation.

If the target slices do not span the full Hom space for the target adjoint, choose a constant intertwiner $L'$ outside the span of $S_u$. Let $L=R'L'Q$. It intertwines the source adjoint: for each basis pair carry it forward by $\Phi$, use that $L'$ intertwines every constant target basis pair, and cancel inverses. By the full-Hom hypothesis on the source, solve the fixed intertwiner equations to express $L=T_x$ with $x$ obtained from $L$ by a constant linear map. Use a fixed projection as in Section 1 to justify this polynomial expression with constant degree. Pushing forward gives $L'=S_{Px}$ by (1). A constant linear functional vanishing on every $S_u$ and nonzero on $L'$ gives a refutation. Thus every failure of geometric recognition outside the promised class is handled. The case of a zero source is immediate from a nonzero target entry and the raw action.

## 3. Certificates within the geometric class

Work over a field where the two adjoint centers split. This may initially be the algebraic closure. Let $e_i,e_j'$ be their primitive central idempotents. They project onto the summands of the geometric rectangular matrix multiplication descriptions. Write
$$
\Phi(e_i)=\sum_j f_{ji}e_j',\qquad
\Psi(e_j')=\sum_i g_{ij}e_i.
$$
The displayed coefficients have degree at most two. Transport multiplication of idempotents and the unit to get $f_{ji}^2=f_{ji}$, $f_{ji}f_{ji'}=0$ for $i\ne i'$, and $\sum_i f_{ji}=1$, with the corresponding equations for $g$. To see $f_{ji}=g_{ij}$, the equality $e_j'\Phi(e_i)=f_{ji}e_j'$, pulled back, gives $g_{ij}=f_{ji}g_{ij}$ on the $i$-th coordinate. Conversely push forward $e_i\Psi(e_j')=g_{ij}e_i$ to get $f_{ji}=g_{ij}f_{ji}$ on the $j$-th coordinate. Thus $f$ has both row and column sums one and both row and column orthogonality.

Each summand has a label consisting of its three support dimensions, which determine its rectangular format $(a,b,c)$ from $(ab,bc,ac)$. For the second input and output, the projectors onto these support spaces are the two components of $e_i$. For the first input let $E_i$ be the projection characterized by $T_{E_i x}=(e_i)_Z T_x$, where the notation on the right denotes the output component. The projection exists and is unique by the direct sum decomposition and conciseness. Equation (1) gives
$$
S_{P E_i P'u}=R T_{E_iP'u}Q'=(\Phi(e_i))_{Z'} S_u.
$$
Since the target slice map is injective, a constant left inverse proves $P E_i P'=\sum_j f_{ji} E_j'$. In reverse use $\Psi$ to obtain the same equations with the transpose coefficient matrix $g=f^{\mathsf T}$. Thus in all three modes the appropriate conjugations of the summand projectors are controlled by the same $f$.

For a source summand $i$ and target summand $j$, in any one mode let the dimensions be $u,v$. Restrict its forward action to source $i$ and project to target $j$ to get $A$, of size $v\times u$. Restrict the reverse action to target $j$ and project to source $i$ to get $B$, of size $u\times v$. The two transported-projector equations give
$$
AB=f_{ji}I_v,\qquad BA=f_{ji}I_u.
\tag{2}
$$
These have constant-degree certificates because the preceding projector identities do.

If $u\ne v$, in characteristic zero take traces in (2) and subtract to get $(u-v) f_{ji}=0$. In arbitrary characteristic take the determinant on the larger side of (2), say of order $v$ with $v>u$. The determinant of $AB$ is identically zero by Cauchy–Binet, so this gives $f_{ji}^v=0$. The idempotent equation yields $f_{ji}=0$ by the factorization of $f_{ji}^v-f_{ji}$. The same calculation works when $u>v$. Hence every entry between unequal labels vanishes.

For a label with different multiplicities in source and target, use the submatrix $M$ of $f$ with this label on both sides. Because incompatible entries vanish, it retains the row and column sums one and orthogonal idempotent properties. In characteristic zero subtract the sum of all its row sums from the sum of all its column sums to refute the system. In all characteristics argue $MM^{\mathsf T}=I$ and $M^{\mathsf T}M=I$ of the corresponding orders, and take the determinant of the larger identity. Its rectangular expression has zero determinant. This gives a refutation, including when one multiplicity is zero. If all labels have the same multiplicities, the split rectangular-block classification gives a geometric isomorphism, contrary to the hypothesis. Therefore the required differing label always exists.

For all the determinant steps, there is an explicit Hilbert-like certificate. Given equally sized square matrices $U,V$, telescope $\det(U)-\det(V)$ by replacing one column at a time and expand each difference along the replaced column. This expresses the difference as a linear combination of entry differences with coefficients given by ordinary cofactors of mixed-column matrices. Division-free polynomial-size determinant circuits give polynomial-size circuits for all those coefficients. In (2) the polynomial matrix entries have degree at most two. For the final rectangular matrix $M$ the entries have degree at most two, so its product entries have degree at most four. Hence every determinant identity and every coefficient substitution has degree $O(n)$. The projectors and basis changes before the determinant steps use a bounded number of operations of constant degree and polynomially many entries. Composing these certificates preserves polynomial circuit size and the $O(n)$ degree bound. In characteristic zero the trace and counting calculation avoids this last determinant step, producing a constant-degree identity with polynomially many summands.

Finally one may descend the degree and sparse size bounds to the original field. The selected constants, projectors and witnesses lie in some finite extension. Expand the certificate and apply a $k$-linear functional on that extension taking $1$ to $1$, coefficient by coefficient, to preserve the identity on the original axioms. This does not increase degree or monomial count. There are polynomially many input equations and $O(n^2)$ variables. In degree $O(n)$ the number of monomials is $2^{O(n\log n)}$, and at constant degree it is polynomial. Expand the resulting Hilbert-like identity into monomial products of axioms to obtain the stated sparse PC bounds. For circuit size over nonsplit centers use the following finite étale descent, which avoids a common splitting field.

## 4. Polynomial circuit size over nonsplit centers

The circuit IPS conclusion in fact holds over every coefficient field. Only the geometric rank-label calculation needs attention. We can perform its determinant steps over finite étale algebras of polynomial dimension, without finding a common splitting field.

Write $C=Z(D_T)$ and $C'=Z(D_S)$. The semisimple recognition conditions ensure these centers are finite étale. For every ordered support-dimension label $\ell$, let $c_\ell\in C$ be the sum over the geometric central idempotents with that label, and define $c'_\ell$ for the target. These aggregate idempotents are defined over $k$. Indeed the support dimensions are unchanged by the action on geometric components of a finite Galois splitting field, so the sums descend by elementary Galois descent. Use a zero idempotent when the label is absent. Write $C_\ell=c_\ell C$ and $C'_\ell=c'_\ell C'$.

For an étale algebra $C$, let $\Delta_C\in C\otimes_k C$ be its diagonal idempotent. Over a splitting field it is $\sum_i e_i\otimes e_i$. This expression descends and satisfies $(a\otimes 1)\Delta_C=(1\otimes a)\Delta_C$ and $\mu(\Delta_C)=1$, where $\mu$ denotes multiplication. Let
$$
\mathbf f=(\operatorname{id}_C\otimes\Phi)\Delta_C\in(C\otimes_k C')[\text{witness variables}].
$$
The projection to fixed center coordinates from Section 1 makes this expression a polynomial. After splitting, its $(i,j)$-entry is exactly $f_{ji}$. The identity $\mathbf f^2=\mathbf f$ modulo the axioms has constant-degree certificates over $k$, by descending the corresponding split certificate coordinate by coordinate. The transposed construction from $\Psi$ equals $\mathbf f$ with the factors swapped, with the same constant-degree bounds, by the equality of the coefficients $f_{ji}=g_{ij}$.

Fix two distinct labels $\ell,\ell'$ occurring on the source and target respectively, and work over $H=C_\ell\otimes_k C'_{\ell'}$. It is finite étale of dimension $O(n^2)$. Choose a mode where the two dimensions differ, say $u\ne v$. Apply the respective mode action of the center to the second tensor factor of $\Delta_C$ and of $\Delta_{C'}$. With the first tensor factors embedded in $H$ this gives source and target $H$-linear projectors. Over a splitting field for $H$ they select the appropriate source and target support summand in each component. Consequently their images are free $H$-modules of ranks $u,v$: each simple factor of $H$ is a field, and the dimensions of the images are constant across factors. Choose an $H$-basis on each image. Restrict and project the witness matrices to these images in those bases, obtaining rectangular matrices $A,B$ over $H$ with polynomial entries. They satisfy $AB=\mathbf f_{\ell,\ell'} I_v,\ BA=\mathbf f_{\ell,\ell'} I_u$ modulo constant-degree certificates over $H$. To see the degree assertion, split $H$ by a finite Galois extension and apply the exact projector derivation of (2) in each component, then descend the resulting constant-degree identities to $H$ by applying a $k$-linear functional taking $1$ to $1$ in the extra field of scalars. Since the degree is constant, their coefficient polynomials always have polynomial sparse size. A determinant of the larger product and idempotence now certify $\mathbf f_{\ell,\ell'}=0$ over $H$, by the polynomial circuit construction of Section 3.

These circuit certificates over $H$ yield polynomial-size certificates over $k$ for all their coordinates. Choose a $k$-basis of $H$. Replace an $H$-valued gate by its vector of coordinates and implement multiplication via the fixed multiplication table in that basis. This has polynomial overhead in $\dim_k H$, preserves the degree, and yields coefficients over $k$ for each coordinate of the Hilbert-like identity. The same works after the natural embedding $C_\ell\otimes C'_{\ell'}\subseteq C\otimes C'$. Perform this for the polynomially many incompatible pairs of labels.

Let $\operatorname{Tr}_{C/k}$ denote the trace of multiplication on the regular module $C$ as a $k$-linear operator, with $\operatorname{Tr}_{C/k}(e_i)=1$ in split coordinates. The formula
$$
\Phi(c_\ell)=(\operatorname{Tr}_{C/k}\otimes\operatorname{id})\bigl((c_\ell\otimes 1)\mathbf f\bigr)
$$
holds by splitting the diagonal idempotent, and is a fixed linear operation on coordinates. The incompatible-pair certificates thus prove that $\Phi(c_\ell)$ is supported on $c'_\ell$. Since $\sum_\ell\Phi(c_\ell)=1$, multiplying by $c'_\ell$ gives $\Phi(c_\ell)=c'_\ell$ for every label. If that label occurs with different multiplicities, the dimensions of the two aggregate support projectors in any of the three modes are unequal. The corresponding transported-projector relation leads to a rectangular inverse equation on their images over $k$ itself. The same determinant argument refutes it in degree $O(n)$, with polynomial circuit size. This includes missing labels by allowing a zero-dimensional image. Thus no large-extension descent is needed for the final circuit certificate.

## 5. Sharp degree on the matrix multiplication class

Suppose both tensors have geometric border rank at most $b$. Their flattening support dimensions are at most $b$, since the $(b+1)$-minors of each flattening vanish on ordinary rank at most $b$ and therefore on its closure. The support reduction in Section 1 uses only constant-degree certificates. Run the rest of the proof on the supported format and then substitute the constant-degree support certificates. This improves the bound to $O(b+1)$, independently of the ambient format, and likewise gives $O(r+1)$ when ordinary rank is at most $r$.

The positive-characteristic degree bound is sharp up to constants even on this restricted class. The earlier diagonal examples $T_h=\sum_{i=1}^h e_i\otimes e_i\otimes e_i$, with $h=3q,4q$ and $q$ a sufficiently large power of the fixed characteristic, are zero-padded sums of scalar matrix multiplications. Their ordinary and geometric border ranks both equal $h$, by a rank-one decomposition and the flattening lower bound. Their literal TI system requires degree $\Omega(q)$ and sparse size $2^{\Omega(q)}$. Take the largest admissible $q$ below $b/4$ or $r/4$ to obtain lower bounds on each parameter scale. Together with the upper bounds this gives the worst-case degree $\Theta_p(b+1)$ and $\Theta_p(r+1)$ on the promised class in every fixed positive characteristic. In characteristic zero its worst-case degree is bounded by an absolute constant. [Earlier diagonal lower bounds](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/927d262c8992bd305b625ad56ca25564555700f0/TensorSparsePCBounds/Proof.md)

This gives the sharp quantitative degree in the portion of Questions 7.1 and 7.4 consisting of geometric direct sums of matrix multiplications. The diagonal lower bounds also show that the exponential scale of the sparse upper bound cannot be replaced by $2^{o(n)}$ in common mode size in positive characteristic, even on this class.

The fixed-format $M(a,b,c)$ case, and more generally the recognizable geometric direct sum class, need no bound on tensor rank or on the number of matrix multiplication blocks. The result applies in particular to the ordinary equations in Questions 7.1 and 7.2 on this class. These are written proofs, with provisional literature priority.
