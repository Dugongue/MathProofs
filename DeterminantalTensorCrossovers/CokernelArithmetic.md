# Determinantal cokernels in characteristic two and integral tensor genera

**Result 1.** Over an explicitly presented finite field $k$ of characteristic two, symmetric matrix tuple isometry and witness recovery are deterministic polynomial time whenever the source's left–right endomorphism algebra is commutative. This condition is recognized by linear algebra. It holds for every regular pencil with generic corank one on each irreducible component of its determinant, including repeated determinant factors. This answers the determinantal case of the characteristic-two Matrix Tuple Isometry question in Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Part V, §1.4. [Part V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00735)

**Result 2.** For a smooth plane model over a Dedekind domain, a fixed determinant and fixed generic left–right class give unmarked tensor isomorphism classes indexed by a quotient of Picard $d$-torsion. For each $t\ge2$, there exist $2^{t-1}$ pairwise nonisomorphic integer tensors of format $3\times8\times8$ that are rationally isomorphic, are isomorphic over every $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ and modulo every positive integer, and share the same ternary determinant, irreducible of degree eight over $\mathbb Q$. The fixed geometric building block is one integral symmetric $4\times4$ pencil with smooth plane quartic determinant. All three ordinary integral changes of basis may act.

## 1. Cokernel endomorphisms

Let $M(x)=\sum_i x_iM_i$ be a square matrix of homogeneous linear forms over $S=k[x_1,\ldots,x_m]$, and let $f=\det M\ne0$. Its left–right endomorphism algebra is
$$
E_M=\{(P,Q): P M_i=M_iQ\ \text{for all }i\},
$$
with componentwise multiplication. The graded presentation $0\to S(-1)^n\xrightarrow{M}S^n\to L\to0$ gives a map from $E_M$ to the degree-zero endomorphisms of $L$. It is injective because the image of $M$ has zero degree-zero part, and because $M$ is injective.

The adjugate annihilates $L$ by $f$. If $\mathfrak p$ is an associated prime of $L$, then $S_{\mathfrak p}$ is regular local and $L_{\mathfrak p}$ has depth zero and projective dimension at most one. Auslander–Buchsbaum gives $\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p\le1$, and $f\ne0$ lies in the prime. Thus localization at the nonzerodivisors of $S/(f)$ is injective on $L$. This localization is the product of its stalks at the height-one primes $(p)$ dividing $f$. If $M$ has rank $n-1$ modulo $(p)$ at each such prime, Smith normal form over $S_{(p)}$ gives $L_{(p)}\cong S_{(p)}/(f)$. A squarefree $f$ automatically has this property, since its valuation at $(p)$ is one. The local endomorphisms are multiplication by $S_{(p)}/(f)$. Consequently $E_M$ injects into the product of these commutative algebras. It is commutative, and reduced if $f$ is squarefree.

If $\det M$ defines a smooth geometrically integral plane curve, then $E_M=k$. A constant pair induces an endomorphism of the cokernel line bundle on the curve, hence a scalar by proper geometric integrality. In the presentation $0\to\mathcal O(-2)^n\to\mathcal O(-1)^n\to i_*L\to0$, twisting by one and taking global sections identifies the codomain $k^n$ with $H^0(L(1))$. Its endomorphism is therefore the same scalar matrix. Injectivity of the presentation forces the same scalar on the domain.

## 2. Deterministic tuple isometry in characteristic two

Compute $E_M$ by solving linear equations and check commutativity by multiplying its basis elements. Left–right equivalence of arbitrary matrix tuples over a finite field can be solved constructively in deterministic polynomial time by module isomorphism. Realize the tuples as arrows of a two-vertex quiver, using the two projections as named endomorphisms and each matrix as an off-diagonal block endomorphism on the direct sum of vertex spaces. Module isomorphism respects the projections and gives the two invertible intertwining matrices. If this test fails there is no congruence. [Brooksbank–Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2008.07.014)

For symmetric $M,N$, let $UM=NV$ be the resulting witness. The involution of $E_M$ is $(P,Q)^*=(Q^{\mathsf T},P^{\mathsf T})$. For $D=V^{\mathsf T}U$, symmetry gives $D M=M D^{\mathsf T}$, so $d=(D,D^{\mathsf T})$ is a self-adjoint unit of $E_M$. An isometry exists exactly when $d=h^*h$ for some $h=(P,Q)\in E_M^\times$. Indeed this gives
$$
V^{\mathsf T}NV=D M=Q^{\mathsf T}P M=Q^{\mathsf T}M Q,
$$
so $G=V^{-\mathsf T}Q^{\mathsf T}$ satisfies $N=G M G^{\mathsf T}$. Conversely such a $G$ gives $Q=G^{\mathsf T}V$ and $P=G^{-1}U$, hence $PM=MQ$ and $Q^{\mathsf T}P=D$.

Let $E$ be a finite commutative $k$-algebra with involution. Write $q=|k|$, $D_E=\dim_k E$, and choose $b\ge0$ with $2^b\ge D_E$. Its nilradical $J$ is the kernel of the $\mathbb F_2$-linear map $z\mapsto z^{2^b}$, since nilpotents have nilpotence index at most $D_E$. Compute $J^i$ and their quotient bases by linear algebra. The reduced algebra $\bar E=E/J$ is a product of finite fields. The unique square root $\bar s$ of $\bar d$ is self-adjoint and satisfies $\bar s^*\bar s=\bar d$. Compute it by solving the square-map equation over $\mathbb F_2$, and choose any lift $s$, a unit since the kernel is nilpotent.

Write $P_i=1+J^i$ and $N_*(u)=u^*u$. Commutativity makes $N_*$ a homomorphism. Every unitary element of $\bar E^\times$ has a unitary lift: if $v$ is any lift, then $N_*(v)\in P_1$, whose exponent divides $2^b$. Put $m_0=\prod_{i=1}^{D_E}(q^i-1)$, an odd multiple of the exponent of $\bar E^\times$. Raise $v$ to an exponent divisible by $2^b$ and congruent to $1$ modulo $m_0$, obtained by the Euclidean algorithm. Since all solutions of $N_*(\bar u)=\bar d$ differ from $\bar s$ by a unitary element, it suffices to solve $d\,N_*(s)^{-1}\in N_*(P_1)$.

Each $P_i/P_{i+1}$ is the elementary abelian two-group $J^i/J^{i+1}$ under $1+j\mapsto j$. Lifts $1+j$ of all quotient bases over $\mathbb F_2$ generate $P_1$. Start with their norms, tracking the preimages. At stage $i$ the list generates $N_*(P_1)\cap P_i$. Choose independent pivots spanning its image in the quotient by Gaussian elimination. Adjust each nonpivot generator by the pivots to lie in $P_{i+1}$. The adjusted nonpivots and the squares of the pivots generate the subgroup kernel, because the exponents of the independent pivots in a kernel element are all even. If the target quotient image is outside the span, the norm equation is insoluble. Otherwise clear its image by multiplying by the inverse product of the appropriate pivots, and continue in $P_{i+1}$. Track preimages for the adjusted generators and pivot squares. Once the target is $1$, the accumulated product of preimages supplies the norm witness. There are at most $D_E$ stages and the list never grows. Ring arithmetic, exponentiation and the linear systems have polynomial bit complexity.

For reduced $E_M$, the unique self-adjoint square root of $d$ suffices. This simplification also works over any perfect field of characteristic two. In the general alternating case a regular pencil can have a square determinant and generic corank two. The stated algorithm applies when the computed algebra is commutative. [Ivanyos–Qiao, §3.5.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03495) · [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Part V, §1.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00735)

The radical test can be decisive even when the entire determinant pencil has generic corank one. Over $\mathbb F_2$ take $B=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}$ and $J_0=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{psmallmatrix}$, and compare $(B,BJ_0)$ with $(B+BJ_0,BJ_0)$. The left–right witness is $U=B(1+J_0)B^{-1}$, $V=1$. The two pencils have the same determinant $x^2$, and each has rank one at its generic zero. Their involutive pair algebra identifies with $\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^2)$ with identity involution, using $t\mapsto (BJ_0B^{-1},J_0)$. The witness gives the self-adjoint unit $1+t$. Every unit of this algebra has square one, so the norm test rejects. Directly, the first matrix in the source is alternating and the first in the target is not, so the tuples are indeed inequivalent.

## 3. Unmarked slice mixing over Dedekind domains

Let $A$ be a Dedekind domain with fraction field $K$, and let $C=V(f)\subset\mathbb P^2_A$ be a smooth plane curve of degree $d$ with geometrically integral fibers. Consider matrices of homogeneous linear forms with determinant a unit times $f$. Fix one such matrix $M_0$ and write $L_0$ for its cokernel line bundle in the convention
$$
0\to\mathcal O(-2)^d\xrightarrow{M_0}\mathcal O(-1)^d\to i_*L_0\to0.
$$
The corank is one at every geometric point of $C$, since vanishing of all cofactors forces the derivative of the determinant to vanish there. Local elimination makes the cokernel invertible on $C$, and the resolution gives fiberwise acyclicity.

The presentation is intrinsic to its fiberwise acyclic line bundle $L$. On each fiber $L(1)$ is globally generated with $d$ sections and no higher cohomology by regularity and Riemann–Roch. The evaluation kernel on the plane is locally free of rank $d$: locally the map onto a free rank-one module over the Cartier divisor is surjective and row elimination applies. On each fiber the kernel's twist by one is $0$-regular by the evaluation sequence and has $d$ sections, so is trivial. Cohomology and base change then give the relative presentation, with two rank-$d$ base modules $E_L,F_L$ in place of the free modules. The codomain $E_L$ is the direct image of $L(1)$; the domain follows from the evaluation kernel. Thus isomorphisms of $L$ lift uniquely. Since the determinant cuts out the given $f$, $\det E_L$ and $\det F_L$ have isomorphic pullbacks to the plane and hence are isomorphic. Steinitz classification makes the presentation free precisely when this common determinant line is trivial. [Beauville, Determinantal hypersurfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9910030)

Two line bundles on $C$ isomorphic over $K$ differ by a unique pullback from $\operatorname{Pic}(A)$. A generic trivialization gives a vertical divisor on regular $C$, and the prime vertical divisors are the individual reduced integral fibers. The pullback is injective because projection has direct image $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}A}$ by the plane hypersurface sequence. Twisting $L_0$ by a base line $P$ twists both presenting modules by $P$, with determinant class $d[P]$. Thus the left–right classes in a fixed generic left–right class are indexed by $\operatorname{Pic}(A)[d]$ after choosing $L_0$.

Now allow the third change of basis in the three variables. Let $\Gamma$ consist of projective transformations induced by matrices $g\in\mathrm{GL}_3(A)$ preserving $f$ up to a unit and preserving the generic isomorphism class of $L_0$. Write $g^*L_0\cong L_0\otimes P_{\delta(g)}$, with base pullback implicit. The intrinsic presentation gives a unique $\delta(g)\in\operatorname{Pic}(A)[d]$. Pullback acts identically on base lines, so $\delta$ is additive under composition. An unmarked tensor isomorphism gives a pencil equality with a variable substitution $g$. Taking determinants forces preservation of $f$ up to a unit, and cokernels then identify the Picard classes up to $\delta(g)$. Conversely any such equality of line bundles lifts to the two matrix factors. The full unmarked classes in this family are therefore indexed by $\operatorname{Pic}(A)[d]/\operatorname{im}\delta$.

## 4. An explicit quartic with unbounded integral genera

Use the same integral symmetric pencil as in the preceding determinantal proof:
$$
M_0(x,y,z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
x+y&z&0&0\\
z&x+2y&z&0\\
0&z&x-2y&z\\
0&0&z&x-y
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its determinant is $f=x^4-5x^2y^2+4y^4-3x^2z^2-3y^2z^2+z^4$, by the recurrence for leading tridiagonal determinants. The curve is smooth over $B=\mathbb Z[1/2310]$. Indeed writing $f=Q(x^2,y^2,z^2)$, the Hessian of $Q$ is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
2&-5&-3\\
-5&8&-3\\
-3&-3&2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its size-one principal minors are $2,8,2$, its size-two minors are $-9,-5,7$, and its determinant is $-198$. In characteristics outside $S=\{2,3,5,7,11\}$, any gradient zero would give a nonzero vector of squares of the nonzero coordinates in the kernel of one of these invertible principal submatrices. The smooth geometric plane fibers are integral since distinct components would intersect.

The geometric projective stabilizer of $f$ is finite, since the smooth quartic has genus three, and embeds in the geometric curve automorphism group. Fix a finite Galois extension $F/\mathbb Q$ containing the entries of a projective representative of each symmetry. For each $t\ge2$, choose distinct primes $p_1,\ldots,p_{t-1}$, all $1$ modulo $8\prod_{\ell\in S,\ell\ne2}\ell$, and a further prime $r\equiv3\pmod8$ with $-r$ a quadratic nonresidue modulo every odd $\ell\in S$. Require these primes to be unramified in $F$. The Chinese remainder theorem and Dirichlet's theorem give these choices. Then $D=-r\prod_jp_j$ is a negative odd fundamental discriminant congruent to $5$ modulo $8$. Write $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)$, $A=\mathcal O_K$. The primes in $S$ are inert in $K$. The primes ramifying in $K$ lie outside the ramification of $F$, so $K$ is not contained in $F$ and $K\cap F=\mathbb Q$. Inverting the inert primes in $S$ preserves $\operatorname{Pic}(A)$, as each prime above them is principal.

The $t$ ramified prime ideals generate a subgroup of order $2^{t-1}$ of $\operatorname{Pic}(A)[2]$. Each squares to a principal ideal and their full product is $(\sqrt D)$. If a proper nonempty subproduct were principal then so would its complement. Take the smaller norm $1<N\le\sqrt{|D|}$ of the two products. For $|D|>16$, an element of $A$ with nonzero imaginary part has norm at least $|D|/4>\sqrt{|D|}$, using its form $(a+b\sqrt D)/2$. A rational algebraic integer has square norm, while $N$ is squarefree. This proves the asserted number of classes.

For each of these classes choose an invertible $A$-module $P$ and a generator $\tau$ of $P^{\otimes2}$. Regard $M_0$ as a symmetric tensor with linear-form coefficients on $A^4$, and tensor it with $\tau$ to obtain the symmetric map from the dual of $A^4\otimes_A P$. This module is free by Steinitz since $P^{\otimes4}\cong A$. Choose its basis with determinant volume from the standard one on $A^4$ tensored with $\tau^{\otimes2}$, by multiplying a basis vector by a unit if needed. The determinant of a symmetric tensor takes values in the square of its determinant line, so the resulting symmetric pencil $M_P$ has determinant exactly $f$. This works over all of $A$. Over $A[1/2310]$ its line bundle is $L_0\otimes P$, since $\tau$ identifies $P^\vee$ with $P$ in the source.

For this family the slice-mixing map $\delta$ over $A[1/2310]$ is zero. Indeed the projective matrix ratios of a slice change preserving $f$ lie in $K\cap F=\mathbb Q$. Choose a primitive representative $H$ over $B$, meaning that its entries generate the unit ideal. If $aH$ is an invertible representative over $A[1/2310]$, primitivity implies $a\in A[1/2310]$, and its determinant being a unit implies $\det H\in A[1/2310]^\times$. Valuations at primes outside $S$ show that $\det H\in B^\times$. Thus $H\in\mathrm{GL}_3(B)$. If its action preserves the generic line bundle after extension to $K$, it preserves it over $\mathbb Q$, since $H^0$ commutes with field extension and a nonzero section of the ratio remains nowhere vanishing. The vertical divisor argument over $B$, where $\operatorname{Pic}(B)=0$, gives $H^*L_0\cong L_0$ already there. Base extension proves $\delta=0$.

## 5. Restricting the two matrix factors while keeping three slices

Choose $\omega\in A\setminus\mathbb Q$ and the shear $J_0=I_3+\omega E_{12}$. Form $M_P(J_0 x)$ and restrict the two free $A$-modules of rank four to free $\mathbb Z$-modules of rank eight, keeping the three slice coordinates over $\mathbb Z$. Express the three resulting operators in integral bases to obtain $T_P$ of size $3\times8\times8$. Its determinant is $N_{K/\mathbb Q}(f(J_0 x))$: after the scalar extension splitting the two embeddings of $K$, the matrix is block diagonal with its two conjugate pencils. The two determinant factors are distinct even over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, because proportionality would put the nontrivial unipotent shear $J_0\overline{J_0}^{-1}$, which has infinite order, in the finite projective stabilizer of $f$. Thus the product is squarefree. The two geometrically irreducible factors are exchanged by the nontrivial automorphism of $K$, so the product is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.

The tensor recovers $A$ intrinsically. Its pair endomorphism algebra over $\mathbb Q$ is commutative by Section 1 and contains $K$ acting as scalars on the two matrix factors. Every pair therefore commutes with $K$, so it is $K$-linear. The invertible shear over $K$ does not affect this pair algebra. The $K$-linear pair algebra of the original smooth pencil is $K$ by Section 1. Among these scalar pairs, the ones preserving the two integral lattices are exactly $A$, as both lattices are free over $A$.

Any full unmarked integer tensor isomorphism $T_P\to T_Q$ conjugates the pair algebras even when it mixes all three slices, because the defining equations are linear in the slices. The resulting ring automorphism of $A$ is induced by identity or conjugation of $K$. Both matrix-factor maps are semilinear for this same automorphism $\sigma$. Applying $\sigma$ to the source coefficients gives an $A$-linear comparison of $M_{\sigma(P)}(\sigma(J_0)x)$ with $M_Q(J_0 Cx)$ for some $C\in\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb Z)$, using the $\sigma$-conjugate choice of presentation for $\sigma(P)$. The relative variable change $J_0 C\sigma(J_0)^{-1}$ is integral over $A$ and preserves $f$ up to a unit by taking determinants. Section 4 forces $[\sigma(P)]=[Q]$. Conjugation of a quadratic field inverts ideal classes, since the product of an ideal with its conjugate is the principal norm ideal. Thus conjugation fixes the chosen $2$-torsion, and $[P]=[Q]$.

Over $A\otimes\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ the invertible module $P$ is free, since the ring is semilocal. Choosing a generator trivializes $M_P$ up to change of basis and multiplication by a unit, which an independent matrix-factor change absorbs. The same holds over $K$. Keeping the identical shear and restricting the two factors gives isomorphisms over $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ and $\mathbb Q$, respectively. Reduction of a prime-local equivalence modulo $p^a$ gives equivalence modulo every prime power. The Chinese remainder theorem gives equivalence modulo every positive integer.

## Scope and verification

These are written proofs. The characteristic-two result treats a checkable sufficient condition for polynomial-time tuple isometry, with actual witnesses. The general characteristic-two question remains open here. The arithmetic classification is for a fixed determinant and a fixed generic left–right class. The integer genus uses the full ordinary action and requires no auxiliary tensor coordinates to pin the coefficient ring. Prior integral pencil principality reductions and balanced ideal triples already provide arithmetic interpretations of two-slice tensors. The new arithmetic construction uses a geometric ternary determinant and recovers the coefficient ring intrinsically. Priority is provisional.
