# A reverse Polynomial Calculus reduction for the published code tensor

Let $M,N$ be full-row-rank $k\times s$ matrices over $\mathbb F_3$. The ordinary tensor $r(M)$ of Grochow–Qiao, in the layout used by Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She, is the bilinear map
$$
 T_M(x,t)=\big(t_0 Mx,\ (x_i t_{ib})_{1\le i\le s,\ b=1,2}\big).
$$
It has dimensions $(k+2s)\times s\times(1+2s)$. Its data slice is $M$ above zero rows. Its other slices are single entries in the private rows. **The ordinary TI equations for $r(M),r(N)$ and the monomial code equivalence equations for $M,N$ simulate each other by constant-degree ordinary PC substitutions.** All witnesses have both inverse equations. The result also holds for matrices over the prime subfield with PC coefficients in any characteristic-three field. The published forward reduction is Proposition 5.5. The reverse below uses two minors of orders two and four. [Published tensor and forward simulation](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S5)

## Extracting the code witness

Use the transport convention $P T_M(x,t)=T_N(Yx,Zt)$, coefficient by coefficient, with witnessed inverses $P',Y',Z'$. It is equivalent to the all-primal three-mode convention by inverse transposes on argument legs. One obtains the reverse action on $N$ by substituting the given inverses and multiplying by $P'$. No inferred one-sided inverse is used.

First fix $x=e_j$. The matrix of $t\mapsto T_M(e_j,t)$ has rank at most three, since it has only three possibly nonzero columns. Thus all four-minors of $T_N(Ye_j,-)=P T_M(e_j,-)Z'$ vanish by Cauchy–Binet, an ordinary polynomial identity. In particular the four private rows and columns belonging to two distinct indices $i,h$ give
$$
 y_{ij}^2 y_{hj}^2=0 .
$$
The inverse action gives the same equations for $Y'$. For $s=1$ there are no column collisions to prove.

Use a bar for entrywise third powers. Over characteristic three, Frobenius preserves every matrix product entrywise when applied to all factors. The barred matrices still satisfy the given inverse and action equations because the tensor constants lie in $\mathbb F_3$. This step is valid in the polynomial ring, including in the presence of nilpotents in a quotient. Concretely the cube of a derived equation $f=0$ is derived by multiplying by $f^2$, then collecting terms. No field equations $y^3=y$ are used.

The displayed four-minors imply column collisions of $B=\bar Y$ by multiplying by $y_{ij}y_{hj}$, and likewise for $B'=\bar Y'$. Put $p_{ij}=B_{ij}B'_{ji}$. From $BB'=B'B=I$ its row and column sums are one. Distinct entries of a column of $p$ collide by $B$, and distinct entries of a row by $B'$. An entry times its column sum gives its idempotence. Multiplying a column sum by $B_{ij}$ gives $p_{ij}B_{ij}=B_{ij}$, and multiplying a row sum by $B'_{ji}$ yields $p_{ij}B'_{ji}=B'_{ji}$. These supports give the remaining row collisions for both matrices. Consequently $B,B'$ are witnessed monomial matrices and $B_{ij}$ has inverse $B'_{ji}$ on the idempotent $p_{ij}$.

Suppose $k\ge2$. Fix a private argument $t=e_{jb}$. The map $x\mapsto T_M(x,e_{jb})$ has rank one. In the transformed map $x\mapsto T_N(x,Ze_{jb})=P T_M(Y'x,e_{jb})$ the first $k$ rows are $z_{0,jb}N$. Choose any nonzero two-minor of $N$. Its determinant, a nonzero field constant times $z_{0,jb}^2$, must vanish. Thus $\bar Z_{0,jb}=0$. The inverse gives $\bar Z'_{0,jb}=0$. Set $z=\bar Z_{00}$ and $z'=\bar Z'_{00}$. The inverse equations now give $zz'=1$.

In the barred tensor equation the image of a private input $t$ on the right has zero data-row component. On the left the vectors $T_M(e_j,e_{jb})$ are exactly the private row basis vectors. Thus $\bar P$ maps the private row space into the target private row space, and $\bar P'$ does the reverse. The induced data-row blocks $A,A'$ satisfy $AA'=A'A=I$ by block multiplication. Modulo the private rows the equation at $t=e_0$ reads $AM=z NB$. Hence $A_c=z'A$, $A'_c=z A'$ and $B,B'$ give
$$
A_cM=NB,\qquad A_c A'_c=A'_c A_c=I .
$$
In the convention $XMY^{\mathsf T}=N$ take $X=A_c$ and $Y=(B')^{\mathsf T}$.

For $k=1$ let $m_j,n_i$ denote the row entries. If $m_j=0$ the source contraction at $e_j$ has rank at most two. In the barred target contraction the three-minor on its data row, its two private rows at $i$, and argument columns $0,i1,i2$ equals $(NB)_{1j} B_{ij}^2$. Multiplying by $p_{ij}$ gives $n_i B_{ij}^3$ on that support. If $n_i\ne0$, multiplying by $(B'_{ji})^3$ and the field inverse of $n_i$ gives $p_{ij}=0$. The reverse calculation forbids a nonzero $m_j$ from matching a zero $n_i$. Take $A_c=1$. On two nonzero positions set the new code matrix entry to $(m_j/n_i)p_{ij}$, on two zero positions to $p_{ij}$, and otherwise to zero. Transpose the reciprocal scalings for its inverse. The Boolean permutation equations give both inverse identities and $A_cM=NB_{\rm new}$. For $k=0$ the code is zero on both sides and one can choose identity witnesses. This covers all ranks of full-row-rank inputs, including zero columns.

All minors in this argument have fixed order. Cauchy–Binet is used after deriving each entry of the corresponding matrix equality, replacing factors in a minor one at a time. The inner sums have polynomially many index tuples. Every other step uses a bounded number of matrix factors, cubings, or previously exhibited polynomial multipliers. Expanding these as monomial multiples gives polynomial-size ordinary PC derivations of absolute constant degree. Taking the barred equalities only once is sufficient. The Lean lemma verifies the cubed-support identity in all commutative rings of characteristic three. [Lean lemma](Cube.lean)

## Specializing to graphs, including unequal isolate counts

For a simple graph take $M_G=[I_m\ I_m\ I_m\ D_G]$, where $D_G$ is the unsigned edge-by-vertex incidence matrix. The published forward graph-to-code substitution is Proposition 5.4. For the reverse, write $c_i,d_j$ for actual columns, $A M_G=M_H B$ for the code equations and $p_{ji}=B_{ji}B'_{ij}$ for the Boolean supports. The same support and inverse identities now follow directly from the monomial equations. We have
$$
 p_{ji} A c_i=B_{ji}d_j,\qquad
 p_{ji} A' d_j=B'_{ij}c_i .
$$
A zero column can match only another zero column, by these identities and the indicated local inverses. The submatrix of $p$ on the actual zero columns supplies the **vertex variables for isolated vertices** with their row and column sums. This uses even a rectangular isolate submatrix directly. There is no step requiring a low-degree refutation of unequal isolate counts.

For nonzero columns the distinct vectors are distinct projective lines because their entries are zero or one and their supports differ. If two source columns represent the same line, their matches cannot represent different target lines. To derive this on a joint support $p_{ji}p_{j'i'}$, choose a constant linear functional killing one target column and taking value one on the other. Apply it to the two forward vector equations on that support, then multiply by the displayed local inverse. This derives zero for the joint support. Applying the reverse equations gives the converse.

For source type $c$ choose one representative $i$ and put $q_{dc}=\sum_{j:d_j=d}p_{ji}$. It is independent of the representative in PC. Multiply one such sum by the column sum for another representative. Only terms choosing the same target type survive by the joint-support rule. Doing this in both orders proves equality. Using a fixed target representative analogously defines the inverse type selector. Its product with $q_{dc}$ equals each of the two selectors separately, by expanding the other representative's row or column sum and applying the forward and reverse joint-support rules. The selectors therefore agree, so $q$ has all Boolean permutation equations. These arguments require no choice of a witness-dependent representative.

Each nonzero type has multiplicity $1,3,4$ or $5$. The private type for edge $e$ occurs three times plus once per degree-one endpoint of $e$; every vertex of degree at least two contributes its own singleton type. If the source multiplicity exceeds a target multiplicity $b$, pick $b+1$ source positions of that type. The product of their $b+1$ equal expressions for $q_{dc}$ equals $q_{dc}$. Expanding, every term sends two positions to the same target position and vanishes by row collision. The transposed argument treats the other inequality. Thus only equal multiplicities match, in bounded degree even when the difference is three.

Write $s_{fe}=q_{e_f,e_e}$ for the private-edge type match. On $q_{dc}$, the equation $q_{dc}A c=t_{dc}d$ holds with an explicit local unit $t_{dc}$. For a coordinate $h$ with $d_h=1$ take $t_{dc}=q_{dc}(Ac)_h$. Sum the representative's matched-coordinate equations over type $d$ to obtain the identity. Using an entry where $c$ equals one and $A'$ in the reverse calculation gives $t'_{cd}$ with $t_{dc}t'_{cd}=q_{dc}$. In particular $A_{fe}$ is supported on $s_{fe}$ and is a local unit there. This follows by summing $s_{f'e}Ae_e=t_{e_{f'},e_e} e_{f'}$ over $f'$.

For nonleaf vertex types $c,d$, on $q_{dc}s_{fe}$ the $f$-th entry of $Ac=t_{dc}d$ reduces to $A_{fe}c_e=t_{dc}d_f$, since edge collisions remove every other entry from row $f$. Therefore this support vanishes if $c_e\ne d_f$, by its local units. Use $q$ for nonleaf vertex matches. On leaves match the first leaf on one edge with the first on the other, and likewise the second when present, using $s_{fe}$. Multiplicity equality supplies the required slot counts. For isolates use the zero-column block already constructed. Cross-type vertex entries are zero. The three blocks give Boolean permutation equations and enforce all edge-incidence mismatches. If a source edge $ij$ is sent to a target nonedge $uv$, every target edge misses $u$ or $v$. Multiply the relevant incidence mismatch by the other vertex selector and sum over target edges using the edge column sum. The product of the two vertex selectors is zero. Reverse the argument for a target edge and a source nonedge. This proves every graph axiom with polynomial-size, bounded-degree derivations. The case of no edges has a fixed vertex bijection for graphs of equal order. [Published graph equations and forward substitution](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S5)

Thus the **published Section 5 ordinary tensor on the actual incidence matrix** and the **bare multiplication tensor of Grigoriev's graph algebra** are mutually low-degree PC equivalent, over $\mathbb F_3$, for every simple graph pair of equal order and edge count. The proof allows unequal isolate counts without changing the graphs. Compose the reverse on one route with the forward on the other through the same graph equations. A degree-$d$ refutation transfers with degree at most $O(d+1)$ in each direction.
