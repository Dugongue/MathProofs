# Two graph-to-tensor reductions are equivalent in Polynomial Calculus

**Theorem.** Over every field, including $\mathbb F_3$, the incidence-code route through diagonal matrix Lie conjugacy and the Grigoriev graph-algebra route give ordinary three-tensor equations that simulate each other in constant-degree ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC). Use the FGS layouts and cyclic orders specified below, the usual two-sided inverse witnesses, and simple graphs of equal order and edge count. The comparison on unchanged inputs covers pairs with equal numbers of isolated vertices. A uniform version valid on every such graph pair adds two pendant vertices per original vertex on the incidence side alone. Both directions have polynomial-size derivations for each substituted axiom. [FGS cancellation and the two constructions](Modular.md) · [Incidence and pendant proofs](Incidence.md) · [Graph-algebra proof](Algebra.md)

This answers the two-route comparison in **Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She, Question 7.5**, for the specified realizations. The other routes in their question, including the semisimple Lie and alternating-matrix routes over $\mathbb F_3$, remain outside this result. Our previous four-route proof used characteristic zero. [Original question](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS5) · [FGS construction](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.09219) · [Prior characteristic-zero comparison](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FourRoutePolynomialCalculus/FOUR-ROUTES.md)

## What changes in characteristic three?

In the incidence code one private weight per edge occurs three times plus once per leaf endpoint. A vertex of degree at least two has its own weight of multiplicity one. A modular trace can assign the same value to multiplicities one and four. The new proof compares the blocks through the two inverse matrices. If a putative bijection matches a one-dimensional block to a four-dimensional block, a $2\times2$ minor of the latter's identity must factor through dimension one. On that match its determinant is simultaneously zero and the matching idempotent, so PC rules out the match. All nonzero graph weights have multiplicity at most five. [Local multiplicity proof](Incidence.md)

The compiler itself adds marker blocks whose dimensions grow with the graph. Applying large determinants there would lose the constant-degree bound. Instead, use the arrows that share a private column. They force a row endomorphism to act by a single scalar on an entire old coordinate block. At the successive stages the original identity links tie those scalars together. The only possible marker matches left can be distinguished by bounded-order minors. This proves cancellation in ordinary PC, including when ambient dimensions are multiples of three. [Compiler proof](Modular.md) · [Shared-column lemma in Lean](Marker.lean)

## Which tensors are compared?

- **Incidence.** Start with $M_G=[I_m\ I_m\ I_m\ D_G]$ and the diagonal pencil $\operatorname{diag}(M_G^{\mathsf T}z)$. Encode the single contragredient link by FGS Theorem 4.1, Case 1. In the layout with ambient spaces $X,Y$ and coefficient space $Z$, remove the $Z,Y,X$ partitions using row/column/slice orientations $(X,Y,Z),(Z,X,Y),(Y,Z,X)$. [Stage-by-stage proof](Modular.md)
- **Algebra.** Take the radical-square-zero graph algebra with one idempotent per vertex and one arrow per oriented edge. Use the actual FGS Corollary 3.3 tensor $K=T(x,y,z)+x_0\langle y,z\rangle+y_0\langle x,z\rangle$. Remove the two nontrivial partitions using $(Z,X,Y),(Y,Z,X)$. The decoder works for an arbitrary linked $(1,2)$ tensor in this layout. [FGS Corollary 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.09219#S3) · [Partition recovery](Modular.md)
- **Assembly.** Recover graph variables from either ordinary TI system and substitute them into the forward construction of the other. Every proof of nonisomorphism of degree $d$ on one side yields a proof of degree $O(d+1)$ on the other, for the same comparison pair. The constructions use no equations constraining the witnesses to a finite field and no PC+Inv rule. If isolate counts differ, the pendant version compares the incidence tensor of $G^+$ to the algebra tensor of $G$, and likewise for $H$. The degree transfer concerns these precise outputs. [Vertex recovery](Incidence.md) · [Algebra recovery](Algebra.md)

## Further consequences

1. The FGS one-link encoding also gives a two-way bounded-degree PC comparison for matrix-space conjugacy with an injective parametrization. This includes noncommuting matrix spaces. Placing the coefficient axis first makes the single-marked-coordinate argument independent of simultaneous diagonalization. [Matrix-space proof](Signatures.md)
2. The Grochow–Qiao tensor $r(M)$ used in Section 5 of Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She admits an all-field reverse substitution of degree at most two to monomial code equivalence for arbitrary same-shape matrices. Two- through four-minors handle zero columns and redundant rows. For the original incidence matrices its comparison with the bare graph-algebra multiplication tensor covers even unequal isolate counts without a graph transformation. The zero-column permutation provides the missing isolate variables. [General reverse theorem](Minors.md) · [Graph specialization and alternative Frobenius proof](PublishedTensor.md) · [Published forward reduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S5)
3. For any unital associative algebra, the output leg of a multiplication-tensor isomorphism and its witnessed inverse suffice to recover an algebra isomorphism by a quadratic substitution. In characteristic three, degree two is optimal for recovering the induced vertex permutation on graphs with a nontrivial automorphism. [Proof and exact scope of optimality](Algebra.md)

## Verification

The full PC simulations have written proofs. Four Lean theorems verify the shared-column argument, two algebraic steps in the characteristic-free rank-three minor argument, and the cubed support identities in characteristic three. They were independently kernel-checked with transitive axiom audits. [Shared columns](Marker.lean) · [Minor calculations](MinorCover.lean) · [Frobenius selectors](Cube.lean)

Run `python check_markers.py` and `python code_tensor.py` with Python 3.9 or later. The first constructs sparse FGS arrays, checks 13 successive transported tensors, and computes 33 row-endomorphism dimensions over $\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5$. The second checks 224 basis equations over four prime fields and validates the recovered code transformations, including a rank-one case with zero data-slice scalar. These finite controls supplement the written arguments. A search of the cited literature located the forward PC reductions and the general algebraic encodings. Publication priority for the reverse PC simulations has not been established.
