# Prescribing the easy characteristics of sparse tensor isomorphism

**Theorem.** For every fixed nonempty finite set $S$ of primes, there is an infinite family of pairs of integral $0/1$ tensors of format $N\times N\times N$, with $O(N)$ nonzero entries. Each is the multiplication of a split basic unital associative algebra with radical square zero after base change to any field. The tensors in each pair are nonisomorphic over every field. The minimum ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) refutation degree of their tensor-isomorphism equations, with explicit two-sided inverse witnesses and unrestricted matrix entries, is

$$
\begin{cases}
O_S(1) & \operatorname{char} K\in S,\\
\Theta_S(N) & \operatorname{char} K\notin S .
\end{cases}
$$

The tensors and the constants do not depend on $K$. In particular, for $S=\{3\}$ the same two integral multiplication tables give constant-degree proofs in characteristic three and require linear degree in characteristic two and characteristic zero. The two FGS graph encodings in the all-field comparison inherit the same profile in the original graph order. Their expanded side lengths can be larger.

## One prime

Fix $p$ and an even constant degree $r\ge4$ admitting simple $r$-regular expanders of uniform positive edge expansion on all sufficiently large orders. Use orders $n\equiv1\bmod\prod_{p\in S}p$. On every directed half-edge put a variable valued in $\mathbb Z/p$. Impose opposite values on the two halves of each edge and impose sum $1$ at each vertex. Adding the equations forces $0=n=1$ in $\mathbb Z/p$. Write $B_{n,p}$ for the one-hot Boolean CSP system recording exactly one value of each variable and forbidding each locally invalid tuple. This is the Boolean Tseitin principle of Buss, Grigoriev, Impagliazzo and Pitassi. Their Corollary 21 gives PC degree $\Omega_p(n)$ over every field of positive characteristic different from $p$, with a constant depending on $p,r$ and expansion. For fixed $p,r$, the one-hot and bounded-width clause presentations translate in bounded degree by expanding all Boolean assignments on the bounded local variable set. [Buss, Grigoriev, Impagliazzo and Pitassi, Sections 2 and 7](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/lineargaps/journalfinal.pdf)

The same lower bound holds in characteristic zero. For rational input equations, any fixed-degree proof over an extension of $\mathbb Q$ descends to $\mathbb Q$ in the same degree. Take a finite coefficient space containing $1$, all line coefficients, and the individual scalar multiples used in the linear-combination steps. In a basis containing $1$, derive every rational coordinate line by induction. Scalar multiplication on the span of coefficients of each input line is represented by a rational matrix into the chosen space. Monomial multiplication commutes with taking coordinates. The coordinate of the final $1$ is a rational refutation. Clearing or avoiding finitely many denominators then gives the same degree over sufficiently large prime fields, contradicting the uniform positive-characteristic bound.

Use the colored group-CSP incidence graphs of Berkholz and Grohe. In the source graph use the homogeneous equations with zero charges. In the target use charges one. Each variable $e$ has a color class of $p$ vertices indexed by its possible values. Each constraint $c$ has one color class whose vertices are its satisfying tuples. Connect each tuple to the variable vertices for its entries. The scopes consist of distinct variables, each with its own color, so the variable colors identify the coordinate positions without edge colors. Both graphs have the same size and edge count, bounded degree, bounded color-class sizes and no isolated vertices. Their sizes are $\Theta_p(n)$. [Berkholz and Grohe, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.04287)

For the lower bound, substitute the Tseitin indicator for a shift of $b-a$ into the graph variable mapping $a$ to $b$ in a variable class. For a tuple in the homogeneous graph mapping to a charged tuple, substitute the product of the corresponding shift indicators. Set cross-color entries to zero. Every row or column axiom of the graph follows from at most one bounded constraint scope. After fixing those indicators, translation is a bijection between the corresponding fibers. A forbidden-pair axiom follows by fixing the union of the scopes of its two source vertices. Incompatible shifts give zero, and a common shift preserves every incidence and nonincidence because it is bijective on the relevant value classes. Formally, multiply the one-hot sums on this bounded union, discard the forbidden assignments using the Tseitin axioms, and evaluate the substituted polynomial on each remaining assignment. This gives ordinary PC derivations of bounded degree, including the Boolean axioms. A graph refutation of degree $d$ consequently yields a Tseitin refutation of degree $O_p(d+1)$.

For the upper bound, the all-zero source tuple exists in every constraint class. Write $P_{u,v}$ for the graph variables and $t_e=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z/p} aP_{(e,0),(e,a)}$ over a field of characteristic $p$. Multiply each variable row sum by the potential image indicator $q_b$ of the all-zero tuple at $c$. Adjacency eliminates every entry except the corresponding coordinate $b_e$, so $q_b t_e=b_e q_b$. Summing the required local linear relation against the tuple row sum $\sum_bq_b=1$ gives the relation on the $t_e$ in degree at most two. Summing the vertex relations and subtracting the opposite-half relations now gives $0=1$. Over any field, an actual isomorphism would yield a genuine $\mathbb Z/p$ solution from the images of those same zero vertices, which is impossible.

## Combining primes without losing the linear lower bound

For polynomial systems $E_i$ in disjoint variables over a field, the least PC refutation degree of their union is the minimum of their individual degrees, allowing $\infty$. The upper bound uses one subsystem. For the reverse, fix a degree $d$ below every individual degree. Write $V_i$ for the vector space of all polynomials obtainable by degree-$d$ derivations in factor $i$. It excludes $1$ and is closed under variable multiplication when the product stays within degree $d$. Choose a basis of polynomials of degree at most $d$ adapted to both the degree filtration and $V_i$, containing $1$ in its complementary part. Such a basis is obtained degree by degree, first extending the basis of the filtered subspace and then its complement. Tensor products of these basis vectors of total degree at most $d$ form a filtration-adapted basis for polynomials in all variables up to degree $d$.

Take the span of all these product basis elements that have at least one $V_i$ factor. It contains all eligible input axioms and excludes $1$. For a span member of degree at most $d-1$, every term in its basis expansion has total degree at most $d-1$. Multiplication by a variable in factor $j$ preserves a marked factor if it lies elsewhere. If the marked factor is in $j$, its product is again in $V_j$ and expands only in marked basis elements. Thus the span is closed under all allowed PC steps. This proves the degree identity. The same construction handles Boolean axioms when present by putting them in their respective factors.

Give every color in every $p$-block its own name and take the disjoint union of the colored graphs on each side. A refutation for one block uses the union's equations after eliminating cross-color variables. Conversely, restricting those variables to zero gives a degree-one substitution back to the union of the separate block equations. Cross-block forbidden-pair axioms with both entries of the correct colors impose no additional constraints, since both pairs are distinct nonneighbors. The degree identity now makes the union hard in all characteristics outside $S$ and the one-prime upper bound makes it easy throughout $S$.

## Removing colors in linear size and bounded PC degree

Enumerate the colors consistently in the two graphs. Add a path with one anchor $a_i$ per color, connect $a_i$ to every original vertex of its color, and hang one leaf from each anchor, with a second leaf on $a_1$. This gives two ordinary simple connected graphs, of bounded maximum degree $\Delta_S$ and equal order and edge count. Indeed old degrees were at least two. The colored system gives a witness for the ordinary system by fixing anchors and leaves and extending the original permutation; its substituted axioms follow in bounded degree.

Conversely, all color restrictions follow in ordinary PC degree at most $\Delta_S+2$. To verify this, suppose that cross-class variables of a current partition have already been ruled out in both directions. If vertices $u,v$ have distinct neighbor counts in a given class, use the side with the larger count, say $k$. Multiply $P_{u,v}$ by the row sums of the $k$ neighbors in that class, expanding one row at a time. Prior cross-class exclusions, adjacency incompatibility and column conflicts eliminate every term, since the target has fewer than $k$ eligible vertices. The transposed argument uses column sums. The argument yields $P_{u,v}=0$ in bounded degree, independently of how many previous rounds of refinement have taken place; previously derived polynomials can be reused as individual lines. Starting with one class, degrees identify the leaves. Leaf-neighbor counts distinguish the first anchor, the remaining anchors and the data vertices (taking common refinements as needed). The set of anchors induces a path with a distinguished endpoint. Distances along it refine every anchor to its own class, by successively counting neighbors in the preceding singleton. Adjacency to those singletons recovers every original color, and leaves have already been identified. Discard all cross-color terms from the row and column sums on the old vertices. This recovers the colored axioms. The size of the graphs and of every fixed-degree substituted-axiom proof is polynomial in $n$. The number of vertices is $\Theta_S(n)$.

## Ordinary tensors

Replace each undirected edge by both arcs. The graph algebra has one vertex idempotent per vertex and one radical basis vector per arc. Its nonzero basis products are the idempotent squares and the left and right actions of the arc endpoints. Its dimension is $N=|V|+2|E|=\Theta_S(n)$ and its multiplication tensor has exactly $|V|+4|E|$ nonzero entries, all one. Use the two-way graph/multiplication-tensor PC simulation. Forward, a vertex permutation acts on an arc via the product of its two endpoint indicators. Backward, the output leg $Z$ of an isotopy gives the algebra isomorphism $a\mapsto (ZL_aZ^{-1})1$, and the vertex quotient and arc actions recover the graph variables in constant degree. The argument is uniform over fields. This transfers both the bounded upper bound and the linear lower bound. For the matching $O(N)$ upper bound over the remaining fields, branch in the graph system on one image for each of its $|V|$ vertices by multiplying row sums. Every complete term violates either injectivity or adjacency, so the sum expansion refutes in degree at most $\max(2,|V|)$. The reverse simulation gives degree $O(N)$ in the tensor system. The FGS transfer uses the corresponding two-way simulations on these isolate-free graph pairs. [Graph-algebra construction](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110) · [All-field graph and tensor simulations](Background/README.md)

Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She proved linear PC degree lower bounds for tensor isomorphism using graph and code encodings and other constructions. The assertion above controls precisely which characteristics have bounded degree on the very same integral pair and achieves the gap inside sparse associative multiplication tables. The constructions of expanders, Tseitin lower bounds and the colored group-CSP graphs are prior results. This is a written proof, and no priority claim is made.
