# Recovering a simultaneous congruence from the FGS solid-link tensor in every characteristic

**Theorem.** Let $A,B:F^m\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mat}_n(F)$ be injective pencils, with $m,n\ge1$. Over every field $F$, simultaneous congruence of their spans with a free pencil-basis change and ordinary tensor isomorphism of the FGS tensors specified below admit constant-degree ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) substitutions in both directions. The degree bound is absolute, and each substituted axiom has a polynomial-size derivation. The matrices need satisfy no symmetry condition. For alternating pencils this proves the compiler step of the alternating-matrix route of Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She, including in characteristic two. [FGS, Theorem 4.1, Case 2 and Corollary 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.09219) · [Original open question](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS5)

### The tensor

Use a solid link between the first two axes of $T_A(x,y,z)=x^{\mathsf T}A(z)y$. In FGS Case 2 the intermediate auxiliary vertex on the third axis has dimension $n$. The two dotted links put identity matrices in the two off-diagonal corners. The singleton unit block remains. Thus the linked-to-partitioned array, with exactly the needed auxiliary parts, is
$$
K_A=T_A(x,y,z)+y_0x^{\mathsf T}w+x_0y^{\mathsf T}w+x_0y_0t .
$$
The parts of $X,Y,Z$ respectively have sizes $(n,1),(n,1),(m,n,1)$, with $Z$ coordinates $(z,w,t)$. Apply FGS Lemma 2.2 with row, column and slice axes successively $(X,Y,Z),(Z,X,Y),(Y,Z,X)$. In each application a slice part of size $p_i$ has its own $2^{i-1}r$ copies of a marker with one row and $p_i$ columns, where $r=1+\min(\text{row dimension},\text{column dimension})$. These rows and columns extend the first old row and first old column parts. The old slices and all new identity entries are exactly those of the printed construction.

Diagrams below use the pencil convention $P M(u)=M'(Su)Q$ with two-sided inverse witnesses. Transposes and the given inverse matrices convert between this and the trilinear tensor convention in bounded degree. A claim that a variable matrix lies in a fixed linear space means deriving each of its defining linear equations. A linear implication on fixed coefficient matrices follows by a constant linear combination of their equations, valid over arbitrary coefficient rings. Products involve only boundedly many variable matrices at any step.

### Removing the first three markers together

Keep the $X,Y$ partitions and first ignore the $Z$ partition on the old data. The fixed endomorphism algebra of this diagram is scalar. Indeed, write the row endomorphism as $\operatorname{diag}(L,a)$ and the column endomorphism as $\operatorname{diag}(R,b)$. The $w_i$ slice gives $Le_i=b e_i$ and $e_i^{\mathsf T}R=a e_i^{\mathsf T}$; the $t$ slice gives $a=b$. This uses only coefficients zero and one, independently of $A$.

Project a transporter of the augmented diagram and its inverse onto their old-to-old blocks at every vertex. Call these pairs of maps $f,g$. Since the augmentation is a direct sum of pencils, projection of the arrow equations gives old-data intertwining equations with the original slice map $S$ and its inverse. Their composites $gf,fg$ are endomorphisms of the two respective old diagrams, by substitution of the slice inverse identities. Both are therefore scalars at all their vertices. **Neither marker rows nor marker columns were added at the singleton row vertex $x_0$.** At this vertex the compressed products are exactly the full inverse products, hence both equal one. This proves that the compressed maps themselves are witnessed isomorphisms.

The four corners of the old pencil separately read $A(z),w,w^{\mathsf T},t$. The recovered row and column maps are block diagonal and invertible at every vertex. If any one of these corner maps vanishes on a source slice direction, its target counterpart vanishes after applying $S$. For the original large corner this follows in the reverse implication as well because $A,B$ have fixed linear left inverses on matrix coordinates. Each of the three distinct kernels is the direct sum of the other two parts. Applying these linear statements in both directions shows that $S,S^{-1}$ are block diagonal on $(z,w,t)$. This removes the first augmentation even when $m=n$ or in characteristics dividing all three marker multiplicities.

### The two remaining augmentations

The following elementary two-part cancellation fact is useful. Suppose in one FGS step the old pencil $D$ has only scalar endomorphisms over every extension, has row dimension greater than one, and the two marker arities are one and a number greater than one in the same order on both inputs. The other vertices retain their partitions. Then the unpartitioned slice map and its inverse preserve both slice parts in bounded-degree PC, and one can cancel the markers.

For details, in the direct-sum presentation $D\oplus N_1^{r}\oplus N_2^{2r}$ the endomorphism algebra has diagonal factors $F,\operatorname{Mat}_{r},\operatorname{Mat}_{2r}$. A marker has scalar endomorphisms; the distinct markers have zero Hom in either direction since their supporting slice variables are disjoint. Every composite through a different type and back to a single copy is zero: a nonzero scalar composite would split off that copy, which is impossible for nonisomorphic summands having scalar endomorphisms. Thus the off-type subspace is an ideal and has zero diagonal in products with arbitrary endomorphisms. At the fixed row and column vertices use the trace forms $(a,b)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(ab)$. Their joint kernel removes the off-type ideal and any diagonal factor whose dimensions per copy at **every** vertex vanish in $F$. The ordinary trace pairing on a full matrix algebra is nondegenerate in every characteristic, and both markers survive because their rank per copy at the first row is one. Vertexwise conjugation and its inverse preserve these forms by the inverse identities. Hence the isomorphism induces maps on the visible quotient and on its center, both defined on fixed linear coordinates. The images of the center's coordinate idempotents give a matrix of Boolean selectors with the permutation equations. Row sums and row orthogonality come from the unital algebra map; the inverse gives column equations, since on multiplying the inverse identities by one entry, the row collisions identify the inverse with the transpose.

On a selector a marker matrix algebra cannot go to a scalar factor. In a commutative image $E_{12}E_{21}=E_{21}E_{12}$, so the images of $E_{11}$ and $E_{22}$ agree. Orthogonality kills the image of $E_{11}$ and then multiplication by matrix units kills the whole identity. The inverse excludes the reverse match. Here $r\ge2$. To compare markers, take a source one-copy projector and compress its transported endomorphism to the target candidate marker block. This compression acts by a matrix $C$ on the copy indices and the identity on each copy's coordinates. On the selector $q$, the row trace gives $q\operatorname{tr} C=q$: the initial row trace is one, other visible factors have zero component on $q$, invisible factors have zero vertex trace, and off-type blocks have trace zero. For any $u$ with zero source marker coordinates, the source pencil times the projector vanishes. Transport and compress this arrow equation on the candidate block, then take the copy-index trace. The result says $q S$ sends the kernel of the source marker coordinate projection into the kernel of the target projection. The inverse yields the reverse containment. On $q$ the induced maps of quotient spaces are thus witnessed inverses. For different arities a principal two-minor of the larger identity would factor through dimension one, forcing $q=0$. Correct marker matches are the only ones left. Summing the selector equations proves preservation of both parts.

Finally, compress the forward and reverse maps to the old data. Once the parts are preserved, identify every twisted marker with its fixed counterpart using the corresponding slice-block map or its inverse. Each composite $D\to N_i\to D$ in the defect of the compressed inverse is zero. Indeed the two maps now satisfy the defining linear equations for the fixed Hom spaces, after accounting for the common twist on both endpoints. Equivalently, in the composite on the target data, identify the intermediate source marker with the target marker using its slice-block map, so both Hom spaces and the endpoint are those of the target diagram. The zero composition is the bilinear identity on these fixed spaces proved above. This argument applies to each copy, and to the opposite composite. Thus compression already gives inverse maps. Forward extension uses the prescribed marker twists. The trace and minor calculations never infer that a variable with zero square vanishes.

Here is why the fact applies twice. At each reorientation, for any marker row index $a$ created in the preceding step, its private column coordinates become slices. They give all arrows $e_j e_a^{\mathsf T}$ for $j$ in its old slice part. The endomorphism equation for row map $L$ and column map $Q$ reads $(Le_j)e_a^{\mathsf T}=e_j(e_a^{\mathsf T}Q)$. It fixes $L$ to one scalar on each such part and fixes the entire $a$th row of $Q$ to that scalar times its coordinate row.

* In orientation $(Z,X,Y)$ the row action on $(z,w,t)$ is $\operatorname{diag}(\lambda I_m,\mu I_n,\nu)$. The arrow $y_0$ maps original $x$ to $w$ by the identity and $x_0$ to $t$. Consequently the projection of the column endomorphism onto original $x$ is $\mu$ times that coordinate projection, including on new inputs, and its $x_0$ scalar is $\nu$. Each $y_i$ sends $x_0$ to $w_i$, forcing $\mu=\nu$. Its old $x$-to-$z$ corner forces $\lambda=\mu$ since at least one fixed coefficient of $A$ is nonzero. The preceding private-arrow equations fix all the other column rows. Thus all endomorphisms are scalar. The next slice arities are the enlarged first $Y$ part and its singleton.
* In orientation $(Y,Z,X)$ the new private arrows force the row action to scale the enlarged first $Y$ part by $d$ and $y_0$ by $e$, and fix all the new $Z$ row projections of the column map accordingly. An $X$ index that was a first-stage private row now gives an identity from its corresponding old $Z$ part into its private coordinates in the enlarged first $Y$ part, zero on other $Z$ coordinates. Taking one such index for each of the three original $Z$ parts fixes all old $Z$ row projections of the column map to $d$ times the coordinate projections. The $x_0$ slice has the $y_0t$ coefficient and forces $e=d$. The data therefore again has only scalar endomorphisms. The final arities are the enlarged first $X$ part and its singleton.

Both data row dimensions exceed one. The reverse decoding sequence starts with this last step, then the second, then the simultaneous compression at the first step.

### Normalization and graph consequence

In the recovered three-part tensor equivalence let $a,b,c$ be the scalars on $x_0,y_0,t$. Its unit entry gives $abc=1$. Divide the three full axis maps by their respective scalars using the supplied singleton inverses. The tensor equation is unchanged and all three scalars become one. For normalized original maps $P,Q$ and auxiliary $w$ map $W$, the two identity corners give $P^{\mathsf T}W=Q^{\mathsf T}W=I$, hence $P=Q$. The remaining large corner is exactly simultaneous congruence with a free $z$-basis change. Conversely a congruence witness sets $Q=P$, $W=P^{-\mathsf T}$ and all singletons to one, and extends through all FGS copies by their explicit marker maps. Each stage uses finitely many factors in any polynomial identity, independently of the sizes, and there are just three stages. Expansion gives polynomially many monomials per substituted axiom and a degree-$O(d+1)$ simulation of a degree-$d$ refutation.

For Grochow–Qiao's graphical gadget, a graph on $k\ge2$ vertices receives $k$ private leaves at each original vertex. Each edge gives a separate elementary alternating matrix, so the resulting pencil is nonzero and injective. The established all-field PC reduction to graph isomorphism applies directly to this pencil: the derivative action on the exterior square identifies the commutator-generated ideal of its stabilizing pattern algebra, the kernel on original vertices. Compressing its witnessed conjugator to the original block yields the Boolean vertex variables $p_{ui}=X_{ui}X'_{iu}$. Edge/nonedge mismatch follows from the two-minor on vertices and the inverse. In characteristic two the alternating matrices have zero diagonal, and the exterior-square and the commutator matrix-unit equations use no division by two. The cases $k\le1$ are fixed isomorphic equal-shape pairs. Composing with the compiler proof gives the published padded alternating route on all equal-order, equal-edge-count simple graph pairs over every field. The argument about arbitrary pencils also applies to the compiler on unpadded He–Qiao inputs with at least one edge; that statement concerns the compiler alone. [Grochow–Qiao, Proposition 9 and Lemma 8](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.16) · [All-field graph-to-AMSI proof](Background/Padded.md) · [He–Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07200)

As a finite control, direct row reduction gives row-endomorphism dimension one at all three stages over each of the prime fields of orders two, three and five for three test pencils: a scalar pencil, the span of the two off-diagonal units in dimension two, and an upper triangular one-parameter pencil in dimension two. These 27 checks include a case where the data and auxiliary marker arities coincide. The proof above is informal. FGS supplies the underlying algebraic isomorphism reduction. The additional conclusion concerns the inverse simulation in ordinary PC on this solid-link layout.
