# Tensor isomorphism inside the conjugacy transporter of a matrix algebra

Grochow and Qiao embed rectangular tensor slices in a metabelian matrix Lie algebra by adjoining a diagonal idempotent. Their construction gives a polynomial-time decision reduction. For the same matrices, the entire conjugacy transporter is the product of the tensor transporter with affine space. In ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) the section and retraction both use linear substitutions. [Grochow–Qiao, Corollary 8.2 and its proof](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S8)

## The transporter and its fibers

Let $K$ be a field, $a,b,m\ge1$, and let $A,B$ be two lists of $m$ fixed $a\times b$ matrices. Write $A(x)=\sum_i x_i A_i$ and similarly for $B$. The tensor transporter $T(A,B)$ consists of invertible $(P,Q,R)$ of sizes $a,b,m$ with
$$
P A(x)=B(Rx)Q.
$$
All invertible matrices throughout have explicit two-sided inverse witnesses. Put $E=\operatorname{diag}(I_a,0_b)$ and let $N(C)$ have $C$ in its upper-right block and zeros elsewhere. Define $L_A(s,x)=sE+N(A(x))$. Its parametrized conjugacy transporter $C(A,B)$ consists of invertible $(S,H)$ of sizes $a+b,m+1$ with $S L_A(s,x)=L_B(H(s,x))S$ for every basis input $(s,x)$. These are polynomial equations in witness entries.

**Theorem.** There is an isomorphism of affine $K$-schemes
$$
C(A,B)\ \cong\ T(A,B)\times\mathbb A^m_K .
$$
It is natural under scalar extension to every commutative $K$-algebra, including rings with nilpotents. In fact the same identity holds over any commutative base ring for these parametrized lists. The projection to $T$ and the section at zero both use coordinate polynomials of degree at most one. Arbitrarily dependent lists and zero tensors are permitted. For linearly independent lists the conjugacy witness $H$ is uniquely determined by the ambient conjugator, so this describes conjugacy of the embedded algebras themselves. Their products satisfy $uv=s(u)v$ where $s$ takes the coefficient of $E$. Their radical has square zero and codimension one. For nonzero slice space the commutator Lie algebra is metabelian with derived ideal exactly the arrow space.

**Proof.** Write $H(1,0)=(h,t)$, $H(0,x)=(\beta(x),Rx)$ and $S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}P&U\\V&Q\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. In the equation for $E$, the lower-left block immediately gives $V=0$. The inverse intertwining equations follow by multiplying the original equations by $S^{-1}$ and applying the linear change of parameter $H^{-1}$. Their equation for the target $E$ gives the same zero lower-left block for $S^{-1}$. Thus $P,Q$ have two-sided inverses given by its diagonal blocks. In the upper-left block of the original equations one obtains $P=hP$ and $0=\beta(x)P$, hence $h=1$ and $\beta=0$. Apply the same argument to $H^{-1}$, or multiply the two triangular parameter maps. The lower-right blocks $R$ and its counterpart in $H^{-1}$ are mutually inverse. The remaining nontrivial blocks read
$$
U=-B(t)Q,\qquad PA(x)=B(Rx)Q .
$$
Consequently $(S,H)$ is uniquely prescribed by $(P,Q,R,t)$. The inverse parametrization is
$$
S=\begin{pmatrix}P&-B(t)Q\\0&Q\end{pmatrix},\qquad
H=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\t&R\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}P^{-1}&P^{-1}B(t)\\0&Q^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
H^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-R^{-1}t&R^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Substitution verifies both products with each inverse and every basis intertwining equation. These formulas use only additions and multiplications and have degree at most two. They are inverse on all commutative scalar extensions; equivalently, they induce inverse maps on coordinate rings. This proves the theorem.

Over $\mathbb F_q$ each tensor witness has exactly $q^m$ conjugacy witnesses above it. When the input lists are bases these count distinct ambient conjugating matrices. On stabilizers the product law sends $(t_2,R_2),(t_1,R_1)$ to translation coordinate $t_2+R_2t_1$, so the stabilizer is the semidirect product $\mathbb G_a^m\rtimes\operatorname{Stab}_{\rm TI}(A)$ with the parameter-leg action. For dependent lists the count concerns the full pair $(S,H)$.

## Proof degree

In the bilinear presentations displayed above, the smaller system's variables and inverse variables are the indicated blocks of $S,H,S^{-1},H^{-1}$. Conversely at $t=0$ the big matrices are block diagonal in those same variables. Every substituted axiom has a bounded-degree PC derivation of polynomial size. For the only step involving a derived inverse equation, first multiply $S L_A(v)-L_B(Hv)S$ by coefficients of $H^{-1}w$ and sum over coordinate vectors $v$. Replace $HH^{-1}w$ by $w$ with the inverse axioms. Multiply the resulting identity on the left and right by $S^{-1}$ and use the two inverse axioms to obtain the reverse intertwiner. The rest of the argument uses block extraction, fixed linear combinations and a bounded number of additional matrix products with witness entries. Each such product expands into polynomially many ordinary PC multiplications by variables. The number of consecutive products is independent of all dimensions. In particular no one-sided inverse principle, determinant computation or division by a variable enters the proof.

The linear substitutions transport a degree-$d$ PC refutation in either direction to one of degree at most $\max(d,c)$ for a universal constant $c$. With a linear polynomial in place of a variable, a multiplication inference is simulated by multiplying the previous line by each variable separately and taking their linear combination. Thus wherever either minimum refutation degree exceeds $c$ the two minima are identical. The usual three-leg tensor axiom is obtained from the bilinear one by multiplying by the appropriate inverse witness, in either direction at bounded degree and without changing variable degrees. [Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She, inverse-witness encodings and PC reductions](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320)

## A unital algebra fixed in each arrow dimension

For independent, nonempty slice lists one can require both embedded algebras to contain the identity. Add $F=I-E$ to the basis. The resulting unital algebra is abstractly the path algebra of the quiver with two vertices and $m$ arrows from the second to the first. Its unit is $E+F$, and its radical is the $m$-dimensional arrow space. The abstract multiplication depends only on $m$. For these particular faithful representations of that algebra, the same product theorem, finite-field fiber counts and linear PC section and retraction all hold under ambient conjugacy with the algebra basis change allowed.

Indeed, denote the images under ambient conjugation by primes. Since $[E,N(A_i)]=N(A_i)$ and all commutators of the target algebra have only an upper-right block, each $N_i'$ is a pure arrow. Write $E'=hE+kF+N(B(t))$. From $E'N_i'=N_i'$ and $N_i'E'=0$ obtain $(h-1)N_i'=0$ and $kN_i'=0$. Conjugate back and take a fixed nonzero entry of some $A_i$ to get $h=1$ and $k=0$, even after extension to rings with nilpotents. Independence gives a fixed linear left inverse of the target coefficient map, preserved by base change. Therefore the parameter image of $F$ is exactly $F-N(B(t))$ because its matrix is $I-E'$. In the coefficient ordering $(E,F,\text{arrows})$, the last row of blocks of the parameter matrix is $(t,-t,R)$ and the first two rows are the standard coordinate rows for $E,F$. The preceding block proof for $S$ applies verbatim, including the inverse argument. The coefficient inverse has last row of blocks $(-R^{-1}t,R^{-1}t,R^{-1})$. This supplies the inverse formula and the same bounded-length PC derivations. Faithfulness and the nonzero-entry condition here are imposed on the fixed input matrices over $K$.

## Removing the injectivity assumption from the FGS compiler

For an arbitrary linear map $D$ from a positive-dimensional parameter space $W$ to $s\times s$ matrices, $s\ge2$, take the coefficient axis first and form
$$
H_D=z^{\mathsf T}D(x)y+x_0 z^{\mathsf T}y+x_0y_0z_0 .
$$
Apply FGS Lemma 2.2 three times in row/column/slice orders $(X,Y,Z),(Z,X,Y),(Y,Z,X)$, amalgamating each private extension with the first partition as in that lemma. **For every pair of such maps of matching shape, parametrized matrix-space conjugacy and ordinary TI of these FGS outputs simulate each other in constant-degree PC over every field.** Neither the ranks of the parametrizations nor their equality must be supplied. We give the additional argument relative to the prior one-link proof and its marker-cancellation lemma. [Prior one-link proof](Background/Signatures.md) · [All-field bounded-minimum cancellation criterion](Background/Modular.md) · [FGS construction](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.09219)

The criterion tests each source marker against all eligible target indecomposable types, in both directions. Eligible means one-dimensional at the first row vertex and supported only at the first row and first column vertices. Its arity is its first-column dimension. Unequal arities whose minimum is bounded are excluded by factoring the larger identity through the smaller module on the matching selector and taking a principal minor of order one greater than that minimum. If the remaining match is the corresponding marker with its prescribed integer multiplicity, the criterion gives bounded-degree PC cancellation. This is the all-characteristic bounded-minimum case of the cited lemma.

1. In the first data diagram the big $X$ row contains the span of the coefficient vectors of $D$, namely the entry functionals on $W$ in the chosen coordinates. Its complementary row space splits off as copies of the row-only simple, since every slice has zero projection there. On the span and the other vertices the argument for injective lists applies directly. The separate $x_0$ row is scalar, its $z_i$ arrows read every $y_i$, and $z_0$ reads $y_0$. The remaining equations fix each entry vector to that same eigenvalue, hence fix their entire span. This residual diagram has scalar endomorphisms and occupies the extra $x_0$ vertex, so it is absolutely indecomposable and ineligible, even when the span is zero. The only eligible data type has arity zero. The two markers have arities $s,1$ and occur only in their prescribed numbers. A one-minor excludes zero against either arity, and a two-minor separates the two markers.
2. At step two the first private generators, as $Y$ slices, are the matrix units linking shared columns to every basis direction within each old $Z$ block. Commuting with these units forces the row endomorphism on $Z$ to be $\operatorname{diag}(u I_s,v)$. The current column singleton $x_0$ has scalar endomorphism $w$. Each old $y_i$ slice sends $x_0$ to $z_i$ and the $y_0$ slice sends it to $z_0$, so $u=w=v$. Regardless of what happens on the unused old coefficient coordinates, every endomorphism on the entire row acts by one scalar. An eligible summand would supply an idempotent of rank one on that row, whose dimension is $s+1$. No such summand exists. The two marker arities are the enlarged big $Y$ dimension and one.
3. At step three, the second private generators force the same scalar action $d$ on the enlarged big $Y$ row and a scalar $e$ on $y_0$. A first-step private $X$ row for the singleton $z_0$ reads that old $Z$ coordinate into a private coordinate in big $Y$ and vanishes on every other column. Its equation forces the entire coordinate row at $z_0$ of the column endomorphism to be $d$ times the $z_0$ coordinate row. In the $x_0$ slice the $y_0$ output reads precisely $z_0$, giving $e=d$. Once more every endomorphism acts by a scalar on the whole row of dimension greater than one, excluding eligible data. The marker arities are the enlarged big $X$ dimension and one.

The bounded-minimum criterion applies three times, with constant two as the largest minor order needed to compare arities. The recovered partitions in $H_D$ have three singleton scalars whose product is one. Normalize each leg by the corresponding inverse singleton scalar. The term $x_0 z^{\mathsf T}y$ then imposes inverse transposes on the ambient legs, and the data term imposes parametrized conjugacy. The forward map extends a conjugacy witness by the identities on the units and the prescribed private marker transformations. There are only three fixed stages, so the composed degree is bounded independently of $s,\dim W$.

Taking $D=L_A$ now gives an explicit three-tensor encoding whose ordinary PC degree is within a constant factor, up to an additive constant, of the original TI instance for **every** input of positive shape, without first removing redundant slices. The sharper fixed floor established above belongs to the conjugacy intermediate. Grochow and Qiao supply the ambient algebra construction; the product description, linear PC retraction and the extension to arbitrary FGS parametrizations are the claims proved here.
