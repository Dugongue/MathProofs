# Recovering uniform hyperedges from Grochow's adjoint representation

**Theorem.** Fix an arity $k\ge 2$. Let $G,H$ be simple $k$-uniform hypergraphs with common positive vertex and edge counts and with every vertex on an edge. Over any field $F$, their vertex-permutation equations and the ordinary tensor-isomorphism equations below simulate each other in ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) in degree bounded in terms of $k$ alone. Each substituted axiom has a polynomial-size derivation for fixed $k$. The substitutions use the ordinary two-sided inverse variables. The graph case $k=2$ in every characteristic other than two uses the adjoint matrices of Grochow's Lemma 3.5 verbatim. In characteristic two we use $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ in the same construction. [Grochow, Lemma 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/1112.2012) · [GGPS, Question 7.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS5)

## Construction and faithfulness

Set $S=\mathfrak{sl}_2(F)$ in characteristic different from two, and $S=\mathfrak{sl}_3(F)$ in characteristic two. Write $L=S^n$. For each hyperedge choose a vertex order and form the tensor product of $k$ copies of the vector space $S$. Their direct sum is $V_G$. On the block for an edge $e$, the representation $\rho_G$ sends $a=(a_i)$ to the sum of $\operatorname{ad}(a_i)$ acting at each position $i\in e$, with identities on the other factors. This is a Lie representation because actions at different positions commute. Use the coefficient-first one-link FGS compiler on the matrix pencil $\rho_G:L\to\operatorname{End}(V_G)$, likewise for $H$. [Compiler reversal, including the order of partition removals](Background/Signatures.md) · [Original FGS construction](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.09219)

Both choices of $S$ are centerless, perfect, with only scalar endomorphisms commuting with all adjoint operators. For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, $\operatorname{ad}(h)$ has distinct weights $2,0,-2$ in the basis $e,h,f$. The actions of $e$ and $f$ connect all three weight lines by nonzero coefficients. Hence the adjoint module is simple, with scalar commutant, even in characteristic three. For $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ in characteristic two there is a short exact finite certificate: in the basis of six off-diagonal matrix units and $E_{11}+E_{22},E_{22}+E_{33}$, the associative span of the adjoint words of lengths zero through three has rank 64 over $\mathbb F_2$. The included verifier builds the commutators by matrix multiplication, retains independent word matrices by binary elimination, and multiplies every retained word on the left by each generator. Discarding a dependent word preserves the span of all its continuations. A nonzero full minor over the prime field stays nonzero in every characteristic-two extension. The adjoint actions therefore generate the full matrix algebra. This proves simplicity of the adjoint module and its scalar commutant. In both cases the Lie algebra is nonabelian, so simplicity of the adjoint module also gives perfectness and zero center. [Reproducible certificate](adjoint_certificate.py)

Each individual $S_i$ acts faithfully on an incident edge. If $\rho_G(a)=0$, then $\rho_G([a_i,b_i])=[\rho_G(a),\rho_G(b_i)]=0$ for all $b_i\in S_i$. Faithfulness on this summand gives $[a_i,S_i]=0$ and zero center gives $a_i=0$. Thus the pencil is injectively parametrized, as the compiler theorem requires.

## From a vertex witness to conjugacy

Write $p_{ui}$ for the Boolean permutation entries; the hypergraph equations include a zero product for each ordered $k$-tuple sent from an edge to a nonedge or conversely. Use $Z=P\otimes I_S$ on $L$. Between two edge blocks, use the sum over their $k!$ endpoint bijections $\sigma$ of the tensor-factor permutation weighted by $\prod_{i\in e}p_{\sigma(i),i}$. Use transposes for the inverses in the chosen tensor-product bases. Distinct assignments from the same edge are orthogonal by vertex collisions. Summing over all target tuples gives one by the column sums: repeated target vertices have zero coefficient and nonedges vanish by the mismatch equations. The reverse sum follows from row sums. These identities give both inverse identities for the edge-block map $X$. After multiplying by a given assignment weight, only its proposed endpoint image survives in $Z$. The tensor-factor permutation sends the corresponding action on $e$ to that on its image. Summing gives $X\rho_G(a)X^{-1}=\rho_H(Za)$ entrywise. Every weight has degree $k$ and products use a bounded number of such weights.

## The reverse substitution

Write $Z'$ and $X'$ for the inverse variables. Applying conjugacy to the brackets of fixed basis elements and multiplying the two conjugacy identities gives $\rho_H(Z[a,b])=\rho_H([Za,Zb])$. A fixed linear left inverse of $\rho_H$ removes it. The reverse equations follow by exchanging the witnesses. Thus $Z$ and $Z'$ preserve brackets, with bounded-degree PC derivations.

Let $E_i$ be the vertex projector on $L$. The commutant of all $\operatorname{ad}_L(a)$ is exactly the split coordinate algebra $\sum_i F E_i$. Its off-diagonal blocks vanish because they kill all brackets in their source summand, which is perfect. Its diagonal blocks are scalar by the preceding calculation. The bracket equations give $Z\operatorname{ad}_L(a)Z'=\operatorname{ad}_L(Za)$. Expanding an arbitrary fixed target argument using $Z'$ shows that $ZE_iZ'$ commutes with the whole adjoint action. Membership in the fixed commutant is a system of linear equations, obtainable by constant linear elimination, including after extension of the coefficient ring. Extract
$$
ZE_iZ'=\sum_u p_{ui}E_u.
$$
Thus $p_{ui}$ is quadratic in the linked witnesses. The images add to the identity and are orthogonal idempotents, giving sums, collisions and Boolean identities in every row. Conjugation by $Z'$ gives entries $q_{iu}$ with the same properties and inverse matrices $PQ=QP=I$. Multiply an entry of $PQ$ by $p_{ui}$ to get $p_{ui}q_{iv}=\delta_{uv}p_{ui}$; the reverse equation gives $q_{iu}p_{uj}=\delta_{ij}q_{iu}$. In particular $q_{iu}=p_{ui}$, proving the column equations.

For distinct source vertices $i_1,\ldots,i_k$ the fixed product
$$
\rho_G(b_1)\cdots\rho_G(b_k),\qquad b_j\in S_{i_j},
$$
vanishes identically when their set is a nonedge: every block misses some factor. On an edge, choose the same fixed $h$ in every factor with $\operatorname{ad}(h)\ne0$. On that edge block the product is the tensor power of this nonzero matrix and has an explicitly nonzero entry. For instance take the standard $h$ for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, or $h=E_{11}+E_{22}$ acting on $E_{23}$ for $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ in characteristic two.

Suppose the source tuple is a nonedge and the distinct target vertices $u_1,\ldots,u_k$ form an edge. Set $t=\prod_j p_{u_j i_j}$, an idempotent. For each target $c_j\in S_{u_j}$, the inverse projector identity gives $E_{i_j}Z' c_j=p_{u_j i_j} Z' c_j$: multiply $ZE_{i_j}Z'$ on the left by $Z'$ and use its action on $c_j$. Substitute $b_j=E_{i_j}Z'c_j$ in the identically zero source product, expand multilinearly in fixed bases, and conjugate by $X$. Its value on the target side is $t\prod_j\rho_H(c_j)$. Read the nonzero entry above to obtain $t=0$. Swapping source and target gives the opposite mismatch. This calculation multiplies by polynomial selectors and uses no division by a variable. It is valid for the full equations, even with nilpotents in their coordinate ring. Bounded word length and fixed matrix products yield a bound independent of vertex and edge counts. The one-link theorem now transfers both directions from conjugacy to ordinary three-tensor equations. For graphs in odd characteristic, composing with the preceding incidence and Grigoriev decoders gives the semisimple comparison on the same isolate-free inputs. Equal isolate counts permit common deletion and a fixed bijection on the removed vertices; mixed isolate/nonisolate entries vanish by an adjacent vertex and its permutation sum. The empty common core can be handled as a constant yes pair. Grochow's original $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ adjoint has a center in characteristic two. The all-field statement there concerns the explicit replacement above.
