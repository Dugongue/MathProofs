# Matrix-conjugacy certificates from trace, split images and cyclic words

We use the ordinary Polynomial Calculus encoding for fixed, labelled tuples
$(A_i)$ and $(B_i)$ of $n$-by-$n$ matrices. Its variables are entries of $X,Y$,
its equations are the entries of $XA_i-B_iX$, $YX-I$, and $XY-I$, and its
degree is measured in entries of $X,Y$. Matrix products involving only input
matrices therefore introduce no proof-variable degree. [Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She, §7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

## 1. Exact degree-two test for a labelled tuple

For $F$ a field, compute the subspace
$H=\{T:TA_i=B_iT\text{ for all }i\}$ by row reduction. The encoding has a
degree-two refutation **if and only if** there are constant matrices $P,Q$
with
$$
TP=QT\quad(T\in H),\qquad \operatorname{tr}(P)\ne\operatorname{tr}(Q).
$$
This test works over every characteristic. It takes one linear system for
$H$, then another for the pair $(P,Q)$ using only the chosen basis of $H$.
When a pair exists, it constructs a polynomial-size degree-two proof.

For sufficiency, each entry of $XP-QX$ is a linear form vanishing on $H$,
so it belongs to the linear span of the input intertwining equations.
Trace cyclicity gives the polynomial identity
$$
 \operatorname{tr}(P)-\operatorname{tr}(Q)
 =\operatorname{tr}(Y(XP-QX))
  +\operatorname{tr}((I-YX)P)
  +\operatorname{tr}((XY-I)Q).
$$
Expand the first term by multiplying linear equations by entries of $Y$
and take linear combinations. Divide by the nonzero trace difference.
For instance, $P=f(A_i)$ and $Q=f(B_i)$ always obey the compatibility condition.
The linear test can also select pairs outside the algebra generated by
the labelled tuples. [Universal certificate identity](B.lean)

For necessity, take a basis $T_1,\ldots,T_h$ of $H$ and let $L$ be the image
of the linear map
$$
 (U_1,\ldots,U_h)\longmapsto
 \left(\sum_j U_jT_j,\sum_j T_jU_j\right).
$$
If $(I,I)$ lies in $L$, fix matrices $U_j$ representing it. On polynomials
of degree at most two use a linear functional with value $1$ on $1$,
zero on linear monomials and pure $X$ or pure $Y$ quadratic monomials,
and value $\sum_j (T_j)_{ab}(U_j)_{cd}$ on $X_{ab}Y_{cd}$. It kills the
inverse equations and all allowed products of an input intertwining
equation with a single variable. To see that it kills an entire degree-two
derivation, take the span of these polynomials and the input intertwining
equations. Its degree-at-most-one members are exactly scalar linear
combinations of the input intertwining equations, since the other
polynomials are even and the constant must be zero under the functional.
This span is therefore closed under every allowed degree-two rule and
cannot contain $1$.

If $(I,I)$ lies outside $L$, take a linear functional vanishing on $L$
and nonzero on $(I,I)$. Every such functional on a pair of matrices can be
written $(C,D)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(CP)-\operatorname{tr}(DQ)$ because
matrix trace pairing is nondegenerate, as witnessed by elementary
matrices. Evaluating on one coordinate $U_j$ at a time gives
$\operatorname{tr}(U_j(T_jP-QT_j))=0$ for all $U_j$. Nondegeneracy gives
$T_jP=QT_j$ and hence the claimed pair. The Lean source verifies the universal
identity giving the sufficiency certificate. The full exact criterion has
the written proof above.

## 2. A cubic certificate from split polynomial images

Let fixed matrices $P,Q$ satisfy $XP=QX$ and $YQ=PY$ in the available
proof lines, and suppose they admit split image factorizations
$P=I_1 S_1$ and $Q=I_2 S_2$, where each $I_i$ has a fixed left inverse
$L_i$ and each $S_i$ has a fixed right inverse $T_i$. These factorizations
exist over any field, by choosing bases of the images. They can also be
supplied over commutative rings with split free image modules.

Set $U=S_2 X T_1$ and $V=S_1 Y T_2$. Right multiplication of the
intertwining equations by $T_1,T_2$ gives $XI_1=I_2U$ and $YI_2=I_1V$.
Multiplying the first equation by $Y$ and the second by $U$ and subtracting
from the relevant inverse axiom gives $I_1(VU-I)=0$, so $VU=I$. The
same argument gives $UV=I$. Their rectangular traces give the equality
of the two image dimensions as scalars. When those scalars differ, this
is a quadratic contradiction once the two intertwining equations are
available as linear equations. The Lean theorem proves the entire
dimension equality over a commutative ring under the fixed splitting
hypotheses.

For the original forward-only presentation $E=XA-BX$, recover
the reverse intertwining equation by the explicit identity
$$
 AY-YB=YEY-(YX-I)AY+YB(XY-I).
$$
This uses degree at most three, after which the reverse equation is a
linear proof line. Polynomial telescoping now gives both directions for
$P=f(A), Q=f(B)$, with no further cost in degree. In characteristic zero
or characteristic greater than $n$, a test with distinct image dimensions
exists for every nonsimilar $n$-by-$n$ pair. Indeed the successive
nullities of powers of irreducible polynomials recover the elementary
divisors by consecutive differences divided by the degrees of those
irreducibles. The proof gives a short image-side variant of the
previously obtained degree-three bound. [Earlier kernel-side and degree-three proofs](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/MatrixIsomorphismPC)

There is a small example that also tests the exact criterion in §1.
Use coordinates $0,1,2,3$. Let $A$ send $e_0$ to $e_1$ and $e_2$ to $e_3$
and kill the other basis vectors. Let $B$ send $e_0$ to $e_1$ and kill
the other basis vectors. These have distinct image dimensions in every
field. The tables below give six terms $(\lambda,T,U)$ in a formal
inverse mixture, with $E_{ij}$ the elementary matrix:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\lambda&T&U\\\hline
 1&E_{00}+E_{11}&E_{11}\\
 1&E_{02}+E_{13}&E_{31}\\
 -1&E_{10}&E_{01}\\
 1&E_{00}+E_{11}&E_{00}\\
 1&E_{20}&E_{02}\\
 1&E_{32}&E_{23}
\end{array}
$$
Every $T$ intertwines, and both sums $\sum \lambda UT$ and
$\sum \lambda TU$ equal $I$. This is a degree-two functional even over
characteristic two, and the image argument has degree three there as
well. The resulting exact degree-three example uses integer entries.

## 3. A signature giving unique pointed simultaneous conjugacy

For each labelled family, pick a vector and a common indexed list of
generator words whose evaluations on those vectors form bases. Include the
empty word. Then a **unique** simultaneous conjugator carrying the first
chosen vector to the second exists if and only if the one-letter
transition coordinate vectors agree for every basis word and every labelled
generator. A candidate sends each first word vector to the corresponding
second one. Correspondence for the empty word maps the chosen vectors,
and the transition equalities tell us that the candidate intertwines on a
basis. Conversely any pointed intertwiner must map all evaluated words to
their counterparts by induction, which proves both the coordinate
condition and uniqueness. For finite tuples and finite-dimensional spaces
this is a finite verification and an explicit recovery procedure.

The chosen words and cyclic vectors are part of the hypotheses. This
has the same reachability mechanism as the word span of a weighted automaton
or the reachable basis of a linear system. The stated result is proved in
Lean for arbitrary generator index sets over a field. [Lean theorem](B.lean) · [Reachability in weighted automata](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~prakash/Pubs/wa_metrics_journal.pdf)
