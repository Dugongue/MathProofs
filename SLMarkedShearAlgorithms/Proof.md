# Exact marked shear algorithms from spectral shifts and quotient flags

Informal mathematics over fields of fixed characteristic. Consider two raw matrix pencils $M_A,M_B$ with prescribed maps on a marked label subspace $W$ and the label quotient $U/W$, as in the earlier MathProofs marked slice-module theorem. The surviving freedom in the label map is an arbitrary linear shear from $U_A/W_A$ into $W_B$. Matrix modes need not be square unless a result explicitly uses an invertible anchor. The determinant profile is the set of possible triples $(\det f,\det g,\det h)$ for the transporter $M_B(fv)=hM_A(v)g^{-1}$.

There is a deterministic polynomial-time Turing reduction of the exact determinant-profile task for **every prescribed marked shear branch** to ordinary TI. No matrix-factor dimension bound or condition on the shear span is needed. Some genuinely multidimensional nonscalar shear classes also admit deterministic polynomial-time algorithms without TI queries, including a simple normalized anchor algebra or certain square-zero central anchors.

## 1. Exact marked shears by adaptive quotient flags

Choose a basis $u_i+W_A$ of $U_A/W_A$ and paired target lifts $u_i'$. Write
$$
 f(u_i)=u_i'+s_i,\qquad s_i\in W_B.
$$
The original branch test is ordinary TI with prescribed subspace and quotient maps, realized by typed inclusion and quotient arrows and pinned maps on the marked subspace and quotient.

One can also prescribe exactly one scalar coordinate of one $s_i$, without constraining its other coordinates. Let $\ell$ be a nonzero covector on $W_B$, choose $w\in W_B$ with $\ell(w)=1$, and put $K_B=\ker\ell$, $K_A=f_W^{-1}K_B$, where $f_W$ is the prescribed map on marks. The subspace $V_A=W_A+F u_i$ is forced to map to $V_B=W_B+F u_i'$ by the prescribed quotient map. The two-dimensional quotient $V_A/K_A$ can be given the prescribed map to $V_B/K_B$ that sends its marked line by $f_W$ and sends $u_i+K_A$ to $u_i'+c w+K_B$. This extra equation holds exactly when $\ell(s_i)=c$. Include $V_A,V_B$ with their inclusions into the label spaces and their quotient maps onto these two-dimensional spaces. It fits the same typed tensor-system encoding as the original markings. Polynomially many such constraints can be represented in labeled direct sums and compiled with fixed signature. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

After one feasibility query, select the coordinates of all the shears one at a time. Use a fixed basis of covectors on $W_B$, try all $q$ scalar values at the current coordinate and keep a value with a YES answer. The selected earlier flags remain in every query. The branch has a transporter with the final exact label map because the last query said YES. With this label map fixed, ordinary ordered module isomorphism on the vertex projections and labeled slices constructs the actual $(g,h)$ in deterministic polynomial time. [Brooksbank and Luks, Testing Isomorphism of Modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

To compute the exact determinant profile, let $G$ be the source stabilizer of the prescribed branch and $K$ its kernel on the label space. The quotient $G/K$ is an additive subgroup of $\operatorname{Hom}_F(U_A/W_A,W_A)$, so is a $p$-group. The image of $G$ in $(F^\times)^3$ equals the image of $K$, because the determinant group has order prime to $p$. The group $K$ is the unit group of the ordered-pencil endomorphism algebra and has label determinant $1$. Its two matrix-mode determinant subgroup is computed from the radical filtration of the ordered-pencil endomorphism algebra. For every matrix factor of its semisimple quotient, count the multiplicity of its natural simple module on the two matrix-mode filtrations. Its determinant contribution is the same field norm raised to those two multiplicities. Norms are surjective and quotient units lift through the nilpotent radical, so multiply these subgroups in the fixed-size determinant group. Multiply by the determinant triple of the one transporter just constructed. This gives the full profile for the original branch, including shear choices that the later queries did not retain. The number of queries is at most $1+q\dim W_A\dim(U_A/W_A)$, polynomial for fixed $q$.

## 2. A polynomial spectral list for simultaneous scalar shifts

Let $K$ be an explicitly represented finite field of fixed characteristic. Given two ordered lists of $r\times r$ matrices over $K$, find all vectors $(s_i)$ such that conjugating the first list gives the second up to adding $s_iI$ to each designated position, and all other positions are fixed. When $r>0$, there are at most $r$ feasible vectors. The algorithm below does not enumerate the field $K$, so its cost is polynomial in $\log |K|$ as well as the matrix and list sizes.

Suppose $m\ge1$ positions can shift. Choose an extension $E/K$ of degree $m$ with a $K$-basis $c_1,\ldots,c_m$. Its construction and univariate factoring are deterministic at fixed characteristic. To construct the extension one can use Shoup's reduction to prime-field factoring. For a squarefree polynomial over any explicit coefficient extension, splitting by the fixed space of $z\mapsto z^p-z$ in its quotient algebra gives deterministic factoring over that extension, by $F_p$-linear algebra and splitting gcds at each of the $p$ scalar values. Repeated-factor reduction uses the derivative and inverse coefficient Frobenius. [Shoup, New Algorithms for Finding Irreducible Polynomials over Finite Fields](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

Form $A=\sum_i c_i A_i$ and $B=\sum_i c_i B_i$ from the designated matrices. In the convention $gA_i g^{-1}=B_i+s_iI$, any simultaneous transporter gives $gA g^{-1}=B+\delta I$ for $\delta=\sum_i c_i s_i$. Find one root $\alpha$ of the characteristic polynomial of $B$ in a represented field $L/E$ of degree at most $r$, using any irreducible factor. Factor the characteristic polynomial of $A$ over $L$, and enumerate its roots $\beta$ in $L$. Every true $\delta$ occurs as $\beta-\alpha$, because $\alpha+\delta$ belongs to $L$. There are at most $r$ differences. Retain those in $E$, recover their $K$-coordinates, and test the entire original shifted list by constructive module isomorphism over $K$. This also checks every fixed matrix, and can enforce any explicit linear constraints on the shifts over a represented subfield. With no shifted positions use ordinary module isomorphism. With zero matrix dimension the equations impose no restrictions on scalar shifts and the determinant is one.

On a nonempty positive-dimensional input, the possible shifts form an affine subspace over the prime field. Indeed the source stabilizer up to shifts maps homomorphically to the additive shift group, and its image is the difference set of the shift values of two transporters. An explicit affine basis comes from one shift and the differences with the other shifts in the spectral list. This is a second proof of the earlier MathProofs scalar-shift theorem using a translation-invariant free algebra, with linear rather than exponential-size abstract shift space enumeration.

## 3. Nonscalar shears with a simple normalized anchor algebra

Suppose the marked subspace contains a specified invertible slice. Align the two ordered marked tuples by constructive module isomorphism, and normalize the matrix maps by the common marked invertible slice. Then any candidate matrix transporter is a conjugator $g$ commuting with all normalized marked slices, and each unknown quotient-lift change is addition of an element of the common normalized marked slice span $S$. The original matrix transporter is recovered from the normalized one by the two invertible anchor maps and any initial alignment.

Let $C\subseteq\operatorname{End}_F(V)$ be the unital algebra generated by the normalized marked matrices. Suppose $C$ is simple, with explicit field and matrix units $C\simeq\operatorname{Mat}_d(K)$, obtained by the fixed-characteristic finite-algebra algorithm. The domain $V$ is the direct sum of $d$ copies of $K^r$, such that a member $a$ of $C$ acts in block $i,j$ by $a_{ij}I_r$. Every element of the centralizer of $C$ is the same $K$-linear matrix $G$ on all $d$ copies. These bases are obtained by taking the image of one diagonal matrix unit, finding a $K$-basis there and carrying it to the other blocks. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#S3.SS5.SSS1)

A residual normalized slice need only be $F$-linear. For each of its $d^2$ blocks $K^r\to K^r$, write it uniquely as
$$
 \sum_{j=0}^{e-1} M_j \operatorname{Frob}_{q}^{\,j},\qquad e=[K:F],
$$
with coordinatewise Frobenius and $M_j\in\operatorname{Mat}_r(K)$. For the scalar space $K\to K$ this follows by dimension and independence: a nonzero polynomial $\sum_{j<e}a_j X^{q^j}$ has degree less than $|K|$ and cannot vanish at every element of $K$. Apply this coordinatewise to get the matrix decomposition, computable by a linear solve over $F$. Conjugation by $G$ maps the $j=0$ coefficient to $GM_0G^{-1}$. A shift by $a\in S$ changes exactly those $j=0$ coefficients by $a_{ij} I_r$. Therefore every genuine shift is in the spectral list for the ordered tuple of all these $K$-linear coefficient matrices on $K^r$. Its cardinality is at most $r$. Each shift vector specifies a unique matrix shift in $S$ if it lies in the coordinate image of $S$. Solve these linear image conditions, and get a label shear by taking a preimage of each element of $S$ in the marked label subspace. Compute an exact transporter by testing the *full* shifted original ordered slices, including all semilinear parts, via constructive module isomorphism. The matrix kernel argument in Section 1 gives the exact profile from one success. This covers arbitrary residuals, including those that do not commute with the normalized anchors, and arbitrarily large nonscalar shear spans.

The same proof applies when $C$ is semisimple and projection onto a fixed number of its simple factors is jointly injective on $S$. Use the vertex idempotents to split $V$, recover spectral lists from the diagonal blocks at the selected factors, and take the polynomial Cartesian product of those lists. This recovers the exact shifts, after which the full module check includes all off-diagonal blocks. Neither individual simple multiplicity nor the dimension of $S$ is bounded in this result.

There is an alternate unrestricted-factor-count case. If $C$ is commutative semisimple and each residual commutes with $C$, the full tuple decomposes across the field factors $K_j$ and each block is $K_j$-linear. Compute the exact affine shift set over the prime field separately on each block using Section 2. Pull it back to the original $F$-vector shear space via the known projection $S\to K_j$, and intersect all these affine constraints. This is exact since separate block conjugators combine directly. It allows nonsplit fields of polynomial extension degree, arbitrarily many independent central field factors, and arbitrary linear coupling across allowed shears.

## 4. A square-zero central shear theorem via first-order perturbations

Assume the two marked normalized slices are $I$ and $N$, with $N^2=0$ and $\operatorname{rank}N=\dim_F V/2$, and that the residual normalized matrices all commute with $N$. In that case $V$ is a free module over $D=F[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ with $\varepsilon=N$. Find a $D$-basis by lifting a basis of $V/NV$, and use the same representation for both tuples after initial alignment. Ask for a conjugator that changes each residual by arbitrary elements of $F I+F N$.

Suppose the reductions modulo $\varepsilon$ of the residual tuples are isotypic direct sums of absolutely simple modules on each side. This can be checked by constructive module decomposition and the endomorphism ring of one simple constituent. A simple constituent of either reduced tuple can be found as an indecomposable direct summand. Its endomorphism ring being $F$ is equivalent to absolute simplicity for a simple finite-field matrix representation. To check that a constituent is simple in the isotypic-semismple test, use its explicit generated algebra, whose quotient is computed by the same finite-field decomposition: the action is absolutely simple exactly when the generated algebra on the constituent is $\operatorname{Mat}_d(F)$.

First enumerate every scalar shift of the two simple constituents by Section 2, with at most $d$ candidates when their common dimension is $d>0$. Unequal simple dimensions, unequal multiplicities, or a failing absolute simplicity test violate this instance condition. For each retained scalar shift, incorporate it into the target, and align the reductions by a $D$-linear basis change lifting a computed ordinary module isomorphism. In split multiplicity coordinates the residual tuples take the form
$$
 X_i=A_i\otimes I_r+\varepsilon B_i,\qquad
 Y_i=A_i\otimes I_r+\varepsilon C_i,
$$
where $A=(A_i)$ is the common absolutely simple tuple and $r$ is its multiplicity. Every candidate conjugator is $(I_d\otimes G)(1+\varepsilon H)$ with $G\in\operatorname{GL}_r(F)$ and any $H\in\operatorname{Mat}_{dr}(F)$, since its reduction must commute with all $A_i\otimes I_r$.

The effect of $1+\varepsilon H$ is to add the tuple $([H,A_i\otimes I_r])_i$ to $B=(B_i)$. Its image as $H$ varies is $\operatorname{im}\partial\otimes_F\operatorname{Mat}_r(F)$, where
$$
 \partial:\operatorname{Mat}_d(F)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mat}_d(F)^m,
 \qquad T\longmapsto([T,A_i])_i .
$$
Pass to the quotient of $\operatorname{Mat}_d(F)^m$ by $\operatorname{im}\partial$ and choose a basis. The perturbations $B,C$ now become ordered lists of $r\times r$ matrices. Each allowed nilpotent shear $\varepsilon s_i I$ is a scalar shift of these quotient matrices, by a known $F$-linear map from $(s_i)$. The $G$-action is simultaneous conjugation on the quotient lists. Apply the spectral list and impose this linear image condition. A resulting $(G,(s_i))$ reconstructs $H$ by a linear solve, because quotient equality says precisely that the remaining difference is in $\operatorname{im}\partial\otimes\operatorname{Mat}_r(F)$. Thus the algorithm recovers an actual original matrix transporter whenever one exists. The determinant-profile assembly is again Section 1. This result allows arbitrary multiplicity, including dimensions divisible by the characteristic. It uses no TI queries.

The split simplicity condition in this section is applied to the reduced tuple modulo $\varepsilon$, not to the original tuple. The original anchor $N$ is genuinely nilpotent, and the first-order perturbation can distinguish large families of nonisomorphic lifts of the same reduced tuple.

## Scope

The adaptive quotient-flag construction removes the entire width problem after a compatible marked subspace map and quotient map have been prescribed. It provides a polynomial Turing reduction. A universal procedure that recovers those two maps from arbitrary raw tensors, or a single ordinary TI test for the determinant profile of every marked branch, is still open here.
