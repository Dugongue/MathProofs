# Deterministic bounded-mode tensor decisions and transporters

Assume deterministic complete univariate factorization in degree \(d\) over each explicitly presented finite field \(\mathbb F_q\), in time \(d^{O(\log(d+1))}(\log q)^{O(1)}\). Write \(N\ge2\) for the size of the tensor arrays and their dimensions, apart from the coefficient field bit length.

**Theorem 1.** For arbitrary ordinary \(a\times b\times k\) tensor pairs over \(\mathbb F_q\), one can deterministically compute a finite list of positive integers \(e_i\) such that the tensors are isomorphic over \(\mathbb F_{q^e}\) exactly when some \(e_i\mid e\). The cost under the factoring premise is
\[
N^{\,2^{O((k+1)^2)}\log N}(\log q)^{O(1)}.
\]
For fixed \(k\) this gives deterministic quasipolynomial decision, including for non-toral positive-dimensional projected transporters, and exact extension-degree decisions with \(e\) in binary. All three basis changes in the original problem are unrestricted.

**Theorem 2.** On the promise that the reduced connected component of the projected source stabilizer in the \(k\)-mode is the unit group of a matrix subalgebra, an actual rational transporter can be constructed or excluded in \(N^{O((k+1)^4\log N)}(\log q)^{O(1)}\). The subalgebra need not be given and the result allows noncommutative subalgebras, including block upper triangular matrix algebras. This search theorem and the earlier diagonal-torus search theorem give different sufficient hypotheses.

The exact extension criterion in Theorem 1 uses Lang's theorem to turn a Frobenius-stable component into existence of a rational point of the full projected transporter. It does not find such a point in an arbitrary positive-dimensional component. The following proofs distinguish deciding existence from returning a witness.

## 1. Equations for the true projected transporter

Let \(z\) be the small-mode matrix. For fixed invertible \(z\), the question is whether the mixed first slice tuple \(A(z)\) is isomorphic to the second tuple \(B\) as a representation of the quiver with \(k\) arrows between two vertices. Isomorphisms of these representations give the other two maps in the original ordinary tensor equations, with transpose or inverse according to the adopted matrix-action convention. All comparison matrices below are of polynomial size in \(N\), with entries affine in \(z\).

We use the three-rank criterion from the earlier bounded-mode certificate proof. Here are the construction and its algorithmic details. Over an algebraically closed field choose one representative \(S\) of each indecomposable type in \(B\). Let \(D_S(T),D^S(T)\) be the two constraint matrices for \(\operatorname{Hom}(S,T)\) and \(\operatorname{Hom}(T,S)\), respectively. Let \(P\) be the matrix of the bilinear pairing \((f,g)\mapsto\ell(gf)\) on unrestricted node-map coordinates, where \(\ell\) is the map to the scalar residue of the local algebra \(\operatorname{End}(S)\), linearly extended to unrestricted endomorphism coordinates. The rank of \(P\) restricted to the two Hom kernels is the rank of the bordered matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}P&D_S(T)^{\mathsf T}\\ D^S(T)&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
minus the ranks of the two constraint matrices. This follows by row and column reduction of the two borders to identity pivots, which eliminate precisely the blocks outside their kernels. The restricted pairing rank is the multiplicity of \(S\) in \(T\). A composite through a distinct indecomposable cannot be a unit, since it would split off an isomorphic summand. Each copy of \(S\) contributes the rank-one product of its two residues. Matching the three ranks for every \(S\) therefore tests isomorphism, since the matched summands account for the whole dimension of \(B\). The antecedent gives the same criterion for IPS certificates. [Earlier bordered-rank criterion](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ExactMatrixProofDegrees/BoundedModeIPS.md).

To obtain these tests over small coefficient fields, compute \(D=\operatorname{End}_k(B)\) by linear algebra, its radical by the deterministic finite-field radical algorithm, and the center of \(D/\operatorname{rad}D\) by commutation equations. Factor the commutative center into fields \(E_i\) by minimal-polynomial factorization and CRT, or use the explicit center procedure for nonsplit square-zero algebras. A simple factor of \(D/\operatorname{rad}D\) is a full matrix algebra over its center \(E_i\), and the algorithm of Ivanyos–Karpinski–Rónyai–Saxena, Theorem 1.8, gives an explicit splitting in deterministic quasipolynomial time. Work separately over each \(E_i\), whose degree is at most \(\dim_k D\). In the scalar extension to \(E_i\) select the center factor associated to the identity embedding and a rank-one idempotent there. Lift that idempotent over the nilpotent radical, and apply it to the node spaces of \(B_{E_i}\) to get \(S_i\). This lifting is effective, for example by the iteration \(x\mapsto x-(2x-1)^{-1}(x^2-x)\), which squares the current nilpotent error since all expressions commute in the subalgebra generated by \(x\). Its residue endomorphism field is \(E_i\), so \(S_i\) is absolutely indecomposable. All absolute indecomposable types of \(B\) are obtained from these \(S_i\) by embeddings of their individual coefficient fields. This follows by base change of the radical and the separable center factors. The residue functional for \(S_i\) is computed from its finite endomorphism algebra. No common compositum is constructed. [Rónyai radical computation](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(08)80017-X), [Explicit finite matrix algebra isomorphism, Theorem 1.8](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-2011-02505-6), [Coherent finite centers](../QuasipolynomialFactoringFallout/SquareZero.md).

For each test matrix and its value \(r\) at \(B\), use formal folded-Vandermonde row and column matrices with entries \((u\omega^i)^j\) and \((v\omega^i)^j\), respectively in the row matrix and transposed in the column matrix. The three seeds \(\omega,u,v\) are independent indeterminates. When \(r\) is the smaller dimension of the matrix the upper bound is automatic. Otherwise with \(r+1\) rows and columns the compressed determinant is identically zero in these seeds exactly when the rank is at most \(r\). Indeed a basis of \(r+1\) independent column vectors has distinct leading degrees after elimination, and its folded evaluation matrix has a nonzero leading coefficient with a Vandermonde factor in powers of the transcendental \(\omega\). The transpose argument completes column compression. Take all coefficient polynomials in the three seeds, then write their coefficient coordinates in a \(k\)-basis of \(E_i\). This imposes the upper ranks for all conjugate tests, since the embedding matrix of the finite separable field is invertible.

For the lower ranks, take the compressed size-\(r\) minor \(\Delta_{i,j}(z;\omega,u,v)\) for each test, with empty value 1 for rank zero. Use the polynomial
\[
P(z;\omega,u,v)=\prod_{i,j}N_{E_i/k}\bigl(\Delta_{i,j}(z;\omega,u,v)\bigr).
\]
The norm is the determinant of multiplication in \(E_i\). At any true geometric parameter all conjugate factors are nonzero polynomials in the same formal seeds, so their product is nonzero. Conversely vanishing of any necessary size-\(r\) rank makes the product identically zero. Thus, after the upper tests, at least one coefficient of \(P\) in the seeds is nonzero exactly when all ranks are right. For every such coefficient polynomial \(P_j(z)\), form a chart with one added variable and equation \(w\det(z)P_j(z)=1\). The number of coefficient polynomials and their degrees are polynomial in \(N\) because the number of seeds is three and all degree bounds are polynomial. Dense determinant and norm arithmetic therefore has polynomial size for fixed \(k\). No field-size threshold is needed. [Deterministic rank compression](../QuasipolynomialFactoringFallout/Transporters.md), [Rank condensers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.7455).

Over the algebraic closure the full projected transporter \(\mathcal T\), when nonempty, is a translate of the projection of the source stabilizer. That projection is closed because it is a constructible subgroup of an algebraic group. In reduced structure its connected component is a smooth connected linear algebraic group. Its irreducible geometric components are disjoint translates of that group, all of the same dimension. A chart above is a base-field-defined open subset of \(\mathcal T\), with an extra coordinate giving the displayed inverse. Thus each nonempty chart meets some of these components in open subsets of the same dimension. Frobenius preserves each chart as a family and its component permutation is the restriction of the full permutation to the components meeting the chart. [Algebraic group images and connected components](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/iAG2022.pdf).


## 2. Computing Frobenius cycles from an affine chart

Consider any of the charts, an affine set in \(s=k^2+1\) coordinates, and ignore its nilpotents for geometric statements. A bounded-variable Gröbner basis detects emptiness and its geometric dimension \(d\). All nonempty components have the same dimension \(d\) by §1. Enumerate all \(d\)-element subsets \(y\) of the coordinate list. Every geometric component dominates the affine space of at least one of these projections: among the coordinate generators of its function field, some \(d\) are algebraically independent. For any one projection the geometric components that dominate it have zero-dimensional generic fibers, because they have dimension \(d\). Other components disappear after passing to \(k(y)\).

Introduce an independent indeterminate \(\tau\), and take the linear form \(\ell_\tau(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\tau^i x_{i+1}\). On the geometric generic fiber this separates all distinct points. In fact values on two points with coordinates algebraic over \(k(y)\) can coincide only if their difference polynomial in the transcendental \(\tau\) has every coefficient zero. Extending by \(\tau\) neither modifies the component orbits nor introduces extra finite algebraic constants. We keep \(\tau\) formal throughout.

Adjoin \(U-\ell_\tau(x)=0\). Over \(k\) use an elimination order to eliminate coordinates other than \(y,\tau,U\). The elimination ideal is generated by the retained Gröbner polynomials. Over \(k(y,\tau)[U]\) take their gcd and clear rational-function denominators and polynomial content to obtain its primitive generator \(G\in k[y,\tau,U]\). This computes the generic eliminant by elimination and localization. If there is no dominating component the generator is 1. Otherwise the extension of the eliminated ideal to the rational function field is nonzero because the fiber is finite. Computing the gcd can be done by fraction-free subresultants. Thus nothing here requires a factoring oracle over a rational function field. Factor \(G\) as a dense multivariate polynomial over \(k\), dropping repeated factors and constant factors. Use the deterministic dense reduction to finite-field univariate factoring. [Dubé's Gröbner degree bound](https://doi.org/10.1137/0219053), [Dense multivariate factoring, Theorem 26 and its cited reductions](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol383-ccc2026/html/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.20/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.20.html).

After passage to \(\bar k(y,\tau)\), the coordinate ring of each dominating geometric component is a finite field extension of \(\bar k(y,\tau)\). Its \(\ell_\tau\) value has one irreducible minimal polynomial. Different components give different factors: their sets of geometric generic-fiber points are disjoint and \(\ell_\tau\) separates those points. Pure inseparability may repeat a root within a minimal polynomial, but cannot create new polynomial factors or merge the factors for distinct components. Nilpotents in the input coordinate ring can only add multiplicities to the generic eliminant, so do not affect the factors used. Conversely every irreducible factor comes from some point of the geometric generic fiber. Gauss's lemma identifies these factors with the primitive positive-\(U\)-degree irreducible factors in \(\bar k[y,\tau,U]\). It follows that each irreducible factor \(g\) over \(k\) encodes exactly one Frobenius orbit of geometric components dominating the chosen projection.

To read its orbit length \(h\), extend the constants to \(\mathbb F_{q^{\deg g}}\) and factor \(g\) again, with \(\deg g\) its total degree. This degree is a multiple of \(h\), because the absolute factors of an irreducible polynomial over a finite field form one Frobenius orbit with equal total degrees. Those factors are distinct since a finite field is perfect. All are therefore defined over this extension, and their number is exactly \(h\). The extension is computed deterministically using Shoup's reduction of field construction to univariate factoring. This works individually for each \(g\) and never needs the compositum of all factor fields. [Shoup, deterministic construction with a factoring oracle](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf).

The chart and coordinate enumeration covers every geometric component of the full transporter. Repetitions of an orbit length from different charts or projections do not affect the divisibility test. Over \(\mathbb F_{q^e}\), a rational transporter parameter lies on a component fixed by the \(e\)-th power of Frobenius, because the geometric components are disjoint. Conversely a fixed component is a torsor over \(\mathbb F_{q^e}\) under the reduced connected projected stabilizer, which is smooth over this perfect field. Lang's theorem gives a rational point of that full component. It is not necessary that the chart used to discover the component contain that rational point. At the resulting rational parameter, Noether–Deuring descends the two-mode module isomorphism to \(\mathbb F_{q^e}\), so it is an actual tensor-isomorphism parameter. This proves Theorem 1. [Lang's theorem for linear algebraic groups](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372673), [Noether–Deuring and module-isomorphism detection](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf).

All Gröbner computations here involve \(k^2+O(1)\) variables with polynomially many equations of polynomial degree. Standard bounded-variable degree and elimination bounds give \(N^{2^{O((k+1)^2)}}\) field operations and degree bounds of the same shape. The dense representations, integer exponent bookkeeping, constructed finite field degrees, and the number and degrees of all univariate factor calls stay within that bound. Applying the stipulated quasipolynomial factor algorithm gives the displayed time. For a binary extension-degree query it suffices to compute residues modulo the listed \(h\), without forming that extension.


### Relative finite field construction and bit dependence

For clarity, construct the relative extension of degree \(m\) using only oracle degrees at most \(m\). Factor the integer \(m\) by trial division. For a prime power \(\ell^a\mid m\) with \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k\), adjoin a primitive \(\ell\)-th root of unity using a factor of \(X^\ell-1\) other than \(X-1\). The degree \(r\) of that field divides \(\ell-1\). In the current field containing this root, lift it repeatedly to an \(\ell\)-th root in the same field using univariate factoring until the lift has no root. The orders increase by a factor \(\ell\) each time, so this takes at most the bit length of the cardinality of the field. The last element is an \(\ell\)-th nonresidue. Adjoining its \(\ell\)-th root gives an extension of degree \(\ell\) by Kummer theory. Repeat \(a\) times, then take the subfield of degree \(\ell^a\) over \(k\) by a Frobenius fixed-space computation in the degree-\(r\ell^a\) field. For \(\ell=\operatorname{char}k=p\), use successive degree-\(p\) Artin–Schreier steps \(X^p-X-c\), choosing \(c\) with nonzero absolute trace by testing field basis elements. Combine the resulting coprime-degree fields by tensor product. These computations use field sizes of bit length \(O(m^2\log q)\). An explicit absolute polynomial basis, if required, can be obtained by finding a generator from the prime-power subfields of the final field over its prime field. Each is a Frobenius fixed space, and the sum of generators of the prime-power fields is outside every maximal proper subfield. This step uses only field and integer linear algebra on dimensions polynomial in \(m\) and \(\log q\). Thus any field-extension requests from the factorization and eliminant steps still have polynomial dependence on \(\log q\).

## 3. Actual construction on a non-toral class

For Theorem 2, use the upper Hom-rank locus with the fixed rank of \(\operatorname{End}(B)\). The true transporter is a closed translate of a subgroup and open in that locus by the adjugate-pivot argument. At a true point choose a nonzero pivot minor of the correct rank. Inside the upper-rank locus its nonvanishing puts the rank at exactly that value. The adjugate projector onto the Hom kernel on this chart preserves the chosen transporter up to scaling at the point, so gives invertible maps throughout a relative open neighborhood. These are the only facts about the upper locus required. [Upper-rank and isolated-point construction](../QuasipolynomialFactoringFallout/Transporters.md).

For a rational parameter \(a_0\) of a positive instance, its component is \(a_0\mathcal A^\times\) over the algebraic closure, with the connected-stabilizer promise \(\mathcal A^\times\). The closure of \(\mathcal A^\times\) in the ambient matrix space is the linear space \(\mathcal A\), and \(\mathcal A^\times=\mathcal A\cap\mathrm{GL}_k\) by Cayley–Hamilton. The stabilizer is over the base, so this linear space descends to the base. Consequently the component is exactly \(W\cap\mathrm{GL}_k\) for the base-field linear subspace \(W=a_0\mathcal A\).

Enumerate the dimension \(d\) of \(W\) and every \(d\)-element subset of matrix coordinates. At least one projection on those coordinates is a linear isomorphism on \(W\). For this projection the determinant is a nonzero polynomial of degree \(k\) in its \(d\) coordinates. Thus one tuple in any \((k+1)\)-element grid to the \(d\)-th power cuts this component to a single invertible rational matrix. The point is isolated in the sliced upper-rank system because the transporter is open there and distinct components are disjoint. Include one inverse variable for the determinant and apply the preceding mixed-dimensional isolated-point solver, with factorization degrees \(N^{O((k+1)^2)}\). Enumerate all such slices and test the returned parameters by constructive module isomorphism. Below the solver's field-size threshold the short parameter space can be directly enumerated. The threshold is \(N^{O((k+1)^2)}\), so enumeration costs \(N^{O((k+1)^4)}\) field operations. All verification uses the original tensor equations. This proves Theorem 2 without knowing \(W\) or \(\mathcal A\) in advance.

Examples of \(\mathcal A\) include arbitrary parabolic block-upper-triangular matrix algebras and endomorphism algebras. For instance the \(\mathrm{GL}_k\) projected stabilizer of a singular Kronecker pencil with no regular part is covered. More generally a factor-algebra promise is a sufficient search condition on the algebraic stabilizer, even with arbitrary disconnected components. Unlike a general transporter coset, its rational components here are linear spaces with the singular matrices removed.

## 4. Recognizing a dense ordinary tensor search class

For square slices \(n\times n\times k\) with \(3\le k<n\), an efficiently checked linear-algebra condition on one input covers a fraction at least
\[
1-\frac{2n^2+k^2-2}{q}
\]
of uniformly random tensors over \(\mathbb F_q\). Whenever one input passes, deterministic transporter search against every target takes \(N^{O((k+1)^4\log N)}(\log q)^{O(1)}\) under the factoring premise.

The condition is that the infinitesimal stabilizer have dimension two, tested by linearizing the three ordinary changes of basis. All stabilizers contain the two-dimensional scalar torus on the three modes whose product is one. If the Lie dimension is two, the reduced connected component is exactly that scalar torus, since its dimension is at most the tangent dimension and it already contains this closed two-dimensional connected subgroup. Thus the projected stabilizer is finite modulo scalars. This implication does not assume separability of the projection of stabilizers.

To prove the probability estimate in every characteristic, exhibit a tensor with that Lie dimension over an algebraic closure. Choose distinct \(t_1,\ldots,t_n\) and put \(\ell_i(x)=x_1+t_i x_2+\cdots+t_i^{k-1}x_k\). Choose upper-triangular slices with these diagonal linear forms, with \(A_1=I\), \(A_2\) diagonal, and \(A_3\) having ones on its first superdiagonal. Then \(f(x)=\det A(x)=\prod_i\ell_i(x)\). In the bilinear matrix-pencil convention, an infinitesimal stabilizer has matrices \(Z,X,Y\) obeying
\[
A(Zx)+A(x)X=YA(x).
\]
Taking determinants over the dual numbers shows \(D_Z f=(\operatorname{tr}Y-\operatorname{tr}X)f\). Reducing modulo each distinct linear factor shows that \(\ell_i(Zx)\) is a scalar multiple of \(\ell_i(x)\). Any \(k\) of the first \(k+1\) forms are independent by the Vandermonde determinant. The \(k+1\)-st therefore has nonzero coefficients in the basis given by the first \(k\). Hence \(Z\) is scalar. Then \(A_1=I\) determines \(Y\) from \(X,Z\), and the remaining equations say \(X\) commutes with all \(A_i\). Commuting with \(A_2\), whose diagonal entries are distinct, forces \(X\) diagonal. The indicated entries of \(A_3\) force all its diagonal values equal. This gives exactly two independent scalar parameters for the Lie stabilizer.

The linearized equation matrix has entries linear in the input tensor. The example shows that some minor of size \(2n^2+k^2-2\) is a nonzero polynomial over the ground field. Schwartz–Zippel bounds the probability that it vanishes. Testing the full rank directly is deterministic. This is a large-field probability statement for a verifiable input class, and gives no expected-time claim for deterministic search on the remaining inputs. It differs from fixed-field average-input results for large balanced dimensions. [Li–Li–Qiao–Tao–Wang, average-case landscape](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00591).

## 5. Comparison and verification status

The earlier OneHomSearch argument gives a one-sided randomized bounded-mode search by random compression, an invertibility chart, and random affine cuts. A geometric component invariant under Frobenius has a rational point by Lang's theorem, and for large \(q\) effective Lang–Weil and the pairwise-independent cut argument give a constant chance to find one. That argument does not supply a deterministic rational slice for general non-toral components. Here formal seed coefficients remove the chart randomness, and irreducible factors of a generic eliminant replace random rational slicing for decision. On an unrestricted positive input one may still use OneHomSearch for witness construction. Combining it with the deterministic negative test gives a Las Vegas algorithm of the same expected quasipolynomial time for fixed \(k\): only a proven positive reaches the repeated witness trials, and a successful output is always checked. The deterministic construction is established separately on the conditions in §§3–4 and on the earlier diagonal-torus class. [OneHomSearch](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ExactMatrixProofDegrees/OneHomSearch.md), [Earlier torus construction](../QuasipolynomialFactoringFallout/Transporters.md).

These are written proofs relying on the specified factoring premise and the stated standard algebraic algorithms. The extension-spectrum and decision claims do not require a fully deterministic constructive Lang algorithm on arbitrary groups.
