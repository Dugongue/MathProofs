# Graph Isomorphism for noncommutative semisimple holonomy tensors

Tensor Isomorphism reduces to Graph Isomorphism on a recognizable class of recovered invertible matrix-line supports with noncommutative semisimple holonomy. Three fixed bounds govern the reduction. They constrain a root's eccentricity, the word length needed to span each simple factor, and the number of factors needed for a projective normalization. Matrix degrees, center extension degrees, the number of factors, and their module multiplicities can all grow with the input. The reduction constructs a tensor isomorphism from a graph match. A genuinely noncommutative subclass already admits a deterministic reduction from arbitrary Graph Isomorphism.

## Recovering the support

Work over an explicitly represented finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$. For a 3-tensor, let $S\subseteq L\otimes R$ be the image of contraction against the dual of the third leg. Suppose $L=\bigoplus_u U_u$, $R=\bigoplus_v W_v$, and $S$ is the direct sum of lines $F M_{uv}\subseteq U_u\otimes W_v$ over edges of a connected bipartite graph. There is at least one edge. The vertex spaces have a common positive dimension, and every $M_{uv}:W_v^*\to U_u$ is invertible. A product map on the first two legs that sends $S$ to the target image lifts to a tensor isomorphism for tensors of the same format. To see this, identify the two image spaces and choose splittings of the two surjective contraction maps. Identify their kernels to build the third-leg map.

The decompositions need not be supplied. Compute each leg's endomorphism algebra preserving $S$ by linear equations on a basis of $S$. The unknown support block projections are in these algebras and form complete orthogonal primitive systems. For instance, in the corner at $U_u$ an endomorphism $X$ preserving $S$ must satisfy $XM_{uv}\in F M_{uv}$ on an incident line, so the corner consists of scalars. The same argument applies on the right. Two complete orthogonal primitive systems in a finite-dimensional algebra are conjugate by a unit, up to ordering. Decompose the right regular module using the idempotents, match the indecomposable summands by Krull–Schmidt, and realize the resulting right-module automorphism as left multiplication by a unit. Units in either leg algebra preserve $S$. Thus the computed primitive systems recover the blocks up to product maps preserving $S$. One can test the common dimensions, the invertible-line condition, and the connected support on the computed blocks. The same conjugacy argument adjusts any tensor isomorphism to carry computed blocks to computed blocks. [Grochow and Qiao](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110), [Chistov, Ivanyos, and Karpinski](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf).

Use $U_u$ and $W_v^*$ as the vertex spaces. An arc from $v$ on the right to $u$ on the left carries $M_{uv}$, and its reverse carries the inverse. A block-respecting equivalence is a part-preserving support alignment and projective vertex switches
$$
[G'_e]=[X_{\operatorname{head}(e)}G_eX_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}^{-1}].
$$
The brackets denote the class up to a common nonzero scalar in $F$. The actual map on each right tensor block is the inverse dual of its vertex switch.

## Recognizing the class

Fix nonnegative integer constants $\Delta,s,a$. A root $o$ must have eccentricity at most $\Delta$. Take as an alphabet all directed closed walks at $o$ of length at most $2\Delta+1$, with their full vertex sequences. Repeated vertices are allowed. This alphabet contains a fundamental generating set of the based graph fundamental group. Choose a breadth-first tree, and for each non-tree arc go from the root to its tail, traverse the arc, and return on the tree from its head. All these walks obey the length bound. Products of their gains and inverses give every rooted gain. Inverses of invertible matrices belong to the unital algebra generated by the matrices, by Cayley–Hamilton.

Compute the algebra $A$ generated by the gains by closing their linear span under multiplication. Require that its radical is zero, and present its simple factors as
$$
 A=\prod_i A_i,\qquad A_i=M_{d_i}(K_i),\qquad [K_i:F]=f_i.
$$
The root space is a direct sum of $m_i$ copies of the natural $K_i^{d_i}$ module over each factor. The integer $m_i$ is the $F$-dimension of the image of the corresponding central idempotent divided by $d_i f_i$. Require for each factor that the projected gain words of at most $s$ alphabet letters, including the identity, span $A_i$ over $K_i$. This is an explicit rank test in the simple matrix algebra. Scaling any alphabet gain by an element of $F^\times$ preserves all these spans. The condition concerns the algebra, so its individual generators may have nonsquarefree minimal polynomials. Finite-field algorithms compute the radical, simple factors, and explicit matrix realizations in Las Vegas polynomial time. [Rónyai](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(08)80017-X), [Shitov on matrix-algebra length](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.09310).

Finally, require an ordered tuple of at most $a$ distinct factors with $\gcd(q-1,d_{i_1}f_{i_1},\ldots,d_{i_t}f_{i_t})=1$. With $q=2$ the empty tuple qualifies. Enumerating tuples of fixed maximum length and checking the integer gcd recognizes this condition. Call a root and such an ordered tuple admissible when all these tests pass. The class consists of tensors with at least one admissible choice. Every condition is invariant under block equivalence and support alignment, as follows from conjugating based gains and preserving their classes up to scalars. Finding all admissible choices takes expected polynomial bit time for fixed bounds.

## Determinant and norm remove the common scalar

For an admissible tuple, use a fixed extended gcd procedure on its ordered dimensions to choose integers $c_j$ with $\sum_j c_j d_{i_j}f_{i_j}\equiv1\pmod{q-1}$. For a unit $h\in A$ put
$$
 \chi(h)=\prod_j \operatorname{Norm}_{K_{i_j}/F}\bigl(\det h_{i_j}\bigr)^{c_j}.
$$
With the empty tuple put $\chi=1$. This is a multiplicative map to $F^\times$. Since the determinant scales in degree $d_{i_j}$ and the norm scales in degree $f_{i_j}$, it satisfies $\chi(\lambda h)=\lambda\chi(h)$ for every $\lambda\in F^\times$. Therefore $h/\chi(h)$ is a well-defined normalized representative of the projective gain. The formula is preserved by an $F$-algebra isomorphism that matches the ordered tuple. Indeed a simple-factor isomorphism transports its center, and after accounting for that field map it is an inner matrix automorphism. The field norm is preserved by the center map. This normalization measures simple-factor degrees directly, independent of the repeated-module multiplicities.

## Why the factor traces suffice

Let two indexed tuples in $M_d(K)$ each span the matrix algebra by words of length at most $s$. Suppose the usual $K$-valued traces agree on all corresponding words of length at most $2s+1$. Choose source basis words of length at most $s$, including the identity. The trace pairing $(U,V)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(UV)$ is nondegenerate since pairing with $E_{ji}$ reads entry $(i,j)$. Its Gram matrix on the chosen words is invertible in every characteristic, even when $\operatorname{tr}(I)=0$. Matching traces up to length $2s$ give the same Gram matrix in the target, so the corresponding words are a target basis. For a generator $h$ and a basis word $w_i$, the traces of $h w_i w_j$ against every basis word $w_j$ determine the coefficients of $h w_i$ in the source basis. Length $2s+1$ suffices for these equations, so the basis map intertwines all generator left multiplications. It sends the identity to the identity and, by expanding all elements in words, it respects multiplication. It is the required algebra isomorphism carrying the indexed tuples.

This is the short-word trace argument of Freedman, Gupta, and Guralnick, applied inside each individual matrix factor. Their treatment also explains why the trace on the entire representation can lose composition factors in positive characteristic. Here the trace is taken on a single natural simple module over its center. The multiplicity is an independent integer invariant. [Freedman, Gupta, and Guralnick, Section 2](https://msp.org/pjm/1997/181-3/pjm-v181-n3-p07-s.pdf).

## Graph reduction and witness

For each admissible root and ordered factor tuple build a connected colored relational encoding with the following data.

- Include the two parts of the support, its edges, and the marked root. Represent each short based walk by an ordered incidence relation on its sequence of vertices.
- Give each factor color $(d_i,f_i,m_i)$. Connect it to its $f_i$ embeddings $K_i\to K(f_i)$ in one fixed common model of degree $f_i$, with the Frobenius successor as a directed relation. Mark the position of each factor in the ordered normalization tuple.
- For every ordered word of at most $2s+1$ short walks and every center embedding, record the embedded reduced trace of the corresponding product of normalized gains in that factor. Record the field degree with the trace.

For both tensors choose the same field model for each extension degree, so equal field elements have equal colors. Relative finite-field isomorphism finds one embedding and successive Frobenius powers list the others. This avoids a common splitting field for all factors. A colored hub connects all objects of an encoding, including its factor data, and the output is the disjoint union over all admissible choices. The number of roots, factor tuples, short walks and words is polynomial for fixed $\Delta,s,a$. The field degrees and all labels have polynomial bit size. The resulting exact many-one reduction uses Las Vegas polynomial expected bit time. Ordered relations reduce to colored graphs with position-colored ports. Convert colors to ordinary graph structure, for example by attaching two private cycles of distinct lengths $3$ and $4+c$ to each original vertex of color ID $c$. Original vertices then have degree at least four and auxiliary cycle vertices have degree two. [Lenstra on finite-field isomorphism](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf), [Babai](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03547).

A tensor isomorphism induces a bijection of all admissible choices and their encodings. It permutes the factors and their center embeddings, and under that identification projective normalization and reduced trace agree. Conversely, any matched pair of connected encodings matches the root, support, and ordered factors. Within each matched factor, take a matched center embedding and place the two normalized tuples over the common field model. The trace argument gives a field-linear factor isomorphism, which transports back to an $F$-algebra isomorphism of the two displayed factors. Combine these to an isomorphism $\alpha:A\to A'$ taking every normalized short gain to its counterpart. The equal multiplicities let one realize $\alpha$ by a linear map $K$ on the root spaces. Explicitly choose matrix units in each factor and bases of the images of $E_{11}$ and $\alpha(E_{11})$ over the respective centers. Transport the bases using $E_{j1}$ and $\alpha(E_{j1})$, applying the center map to coefficients.

Choose a common breadth-first tree in the aligned supports. If $P_v,P'_v$ denote its tree transports from the root, take $X_v=P'_v K P_v^{-1}$. These maps give the required switches on tree arcs by construction. The fundamental gains on non-tree arcs are among the short gains, and conjugacy of their normalized representatives gives the projective switch equation on those arcs. Recover the third-leg map using the image argument. A graph match therefore gives an explicitly verifiable tensor isomorphism. The unrestricted group switching step is the based-walk conjugacy criterion of Cavaleri and Donno. The new ingredient is a polynomial-size encoding that finds an unknown alignment from these factor traces. [Cavaleri and Donno, Theorem 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12428).

## GI hardness with growing matrix factors

Over $F=\mathbb F_3$, encode an arbitrary simple graph $X$ on the support's left side. Add two left vertices $a,b$. For each original vertex $v$ add a right helper adjacent to $a,v$. For each original edge $uv$ add a right vertex adjacent to $a,u,v$. Add two private degree-one right neighbors of $a$ and one of $b$. Their counts identify both special vertices, and all remaining left vertices reconstruct $X$ using the degree-three right vertices.

Give $a,b$ a set of right length-two channels. One channel has identity transport and the others have gains $I+E_{ij}$ for all $i\ne j$ in $M_d(\mathbb F_3)$, for odd $d\ge3$. Use one identity on each channel's first arc, and identity transports on every edge outside these channels. Let these matrices act on as many repeated copies of $\mathbb F_3^d$ as desired. The root $a$ has eccentricity at most three. The short words contain the transvections relative to the identity channel. Subtracting identities yields off-diagonal matrix units, and multiplying two yields diagonal units, so words of length at most two span the full matrix factor. The dimension $d$ is coprime to $q-1=2$, so one factor suffices for normalization. The simple degree and repeated-module multiplicity can both be chosen to grow polynomially with $|V(X)|$. An isomorphism of original graphs extends fixing the core and all assigned matrix transports. A tensor isomorphism induces the support isomorphism and hence an isomorphism of the originals. This proves a deterministic GI lower reduction in a noncommutative subclass with $(\Delta,s,a)=(3,2,1)$.

## Finite control and priority

Over $\mathbb F_3$, give four $a$–$b$ channels gains $I,U_{12},U_{23},U_{31}$ with $U_{ij}=I+E_{ij}$. Add one private leaf at $a$ to distinguish the two left vertices. Compare with the tuple obtained by conjugating each gain by the upper triangular unipotent matrix with entries $K_{12}=K_{23}=1$, $K_{13}=0$, then multiplying the second and fourth channel matrices by $-1$. For the negative comparison replace only $U_{31}$ by $I+2E_{31}$ in the original tuple. The two original and perturbed generating lists have span dimensions $4,7,9$ at lengths $1,2,3$. Their individual reduced traces agree, and the reduced trace of the product of the three displayed generators changes from $1$ to $2$. Taking three copies of the module makes every ambient trace vanish in characteristic three.

The finite control enumerated the channel permutations and both possible root directions before imposing the leaf. For a proposed channel alignment, choose the first matched reference channel and multiply its inverse by each other channel gain. For the reversed direction invert all channel transports first. Normalize the resulting based gains by their determinants. On each of the $48$ alignments per comparison, compare traces of words through length seven and independently enumerate all $11{,}232$ root matrices in $\mathrm{GL}_3(3)$. The trace and direct tests agreed throughout. The positive comparison had three successful root-preserving alignments, with two root maps each. The perturbed comparison had three successes only when the root was reversed. The leaf forbids that reversal and gives zero successful alignments in the negative comparison.

The cited trace criterion, finite algebra algorithms, and group switching theorem predate this construction. The contribution is their combination with determinant–norm normalization, simultaneous graph alignment, and a GI-hard noncommutative class of unbounded matrix degree and multiplicity. It extends the earlier commutative holonomy route under a rooted short-word span condition. The argument and finite control do not establish priority for the combined reduction. There is no Lean formalization.
