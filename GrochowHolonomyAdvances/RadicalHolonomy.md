# Tensor isomorphism with commuting holonomy and nilpotent radicals

The earlier finite-field character reduction handled commuting semisimple holonomy. It extends to commuting holonomy with arbitrary nilpotent depth and arbitrary faithful module structure. The parameter that must stay bounded is the largest dimension of $J_i/J_i^2$ over the residue field of a local factor. A second result gives a polynomial-time isomorphism test for **all** commuting holonomy when the underlying support alignment is supplied. Both constructions recover tensor transporters.

## Setting and statement

Work over an explicitly represented finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$. Contract the third leg of a tensor to obtain the matrix space $S\le U\otimes W$. Assume $S$ splits into invertible matrix lines along the edges of a connected bipartite support $G$ of positive size, with equal block dimension $r$. The support and decomposition can be hidden. This is the matrix-line input model of the semisimple result.

The two algebras of one-leg endomorphisms preserving $S$ can be computed by linear equations. The block projections lie in these algebras. At a block, a corner endomorphism preserving an incident line of an invertible matrix acts as a scalar, so the corresponding projection is primitive. Complete primitive systems of idempotents are conjugate up to permutation by units, by Krull–Schmidt for the right regular module. These units preserve $S$. Thus one can recover the hidden support by computing any complete primitive system in each algebra and testing the block conditions. The same argument lets us adjust every tensor equivalence to respect the recovered blocks. [Grochow–Qiao](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110) · [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, Proposition 11](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf)

Treat each matrix line as a projective map from the right dual block to the left block, with its inverse on the reverse arc. A block-respecting isomorphism is equivalent to a part-preserving support alignment and projective switches at the vertices. With a root and spanning tree, transport all arcs back to the root. Tree arcs become the identity, and each non-tree arc gives a based fundamental gain $H_e$. For a proposed alignment, simultaneous conjugacy of the projective fundamental gains suffices. If $P_v,P'_v$ are the tree path transports and $K$ is a root conjugator, the vertex switches are represented by $P'_v K P_v^{-1}$. On the right take the inverse dual to obtain actual block maps. The map on the contracted leg is recovered by splitting the two surjections to the common image and matching their equal-dimensional kernels. This basis transport works for nonconcise tensors as well. [Cavaleri–Donno on switching and closed-walk gains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12428)

Assume the $H_e$ commute as linear operators. Let $A=F[H_e]$ and write $A=\prod_i A_i$ as a product of finite local algebras, with radicals $J_i$ and residue fields $K_i$. The local **embedding dimension** is
$$
s_i=\dim_{K_i}(J_i/J_i^2).
$$

**Theorem.** Fix $\ell$ and $s$. Suppose the integral cycle lattice of $G$ is generated by cycles of length at most $\ell$, and $s_i\le s$ for all factors. Then the stated class of tensor isomorphism has a polynomial-size many-one reduction to Graph Isomorphism, with Las Vegas polynomial bit preprocessing. Block dimensions, field size, nilpotency indices, and local module multiplicities can all grow. From an isomorphism of the encoded graphs one can recover an explicit tensor isomorphism. With a supplied support alignment the commuting case admits a Las Vegas polynomial bit algorithm without bounds on $\ell$, $s$, or nilpotency index.

The graph reduction requires only a polynomial-size, support-isomorphism-equivariant list of ordered cycles that generates the integral flow lattice. Bounded length is one sufficient condition. Another is a fixed bound on the cycle rank of each biconnected support block. In that case enumerate binary cycle-space elements in each block, keep the connected simple cycles, and include both orientations and every starting vertex. A bounded-length list gives size and preprocessing bounded by the input size to an exponent depending on $\ell$ and $s$. The per-block cycle-rank variant permits unbounded circumference.

## Separate the residue field and the radical

Compute $A$ by multiplying a growing basis by the fundamental gains and retaining independent matrices. Let $D=\dim_F A\le r^2$. For a sufficiently large $q$-power $Q\ge D$, the map $a\mapsto a^Q$ is an $F$-linear algebra endomorphism of $A$. Its kernel is the radical $J$: it kills the nilpotent ideal, and the induced map on the reduced quotient $A/J$ is a Frobenius automorphism. Its image $B$ maps isomorphically onto that quotient. Indeed the induced map is surjective, and any image element in $J$ is the power of a radical element and hence zero. Thus $A=B\oplus J$ as vector spaces, with an intrinsic coefficient algebra $B$. Binary powering and linear algebra compute both $B$ and $J$. We may choose $Q<qD$ when $D>1$.

Decompose $B=\prod_i B_i$ into fields by primitive idempotents, with $B_i\cong K_i$. Use the same idempotents to split $A$ and the root representation $V=\bigoplus_i V_i$. The residue projection $A_i\to B_i$ is now explicit. Compute $J_i^2$ as the linear span of products and test the embedding dimension condition by its dimension. This gives a recognition procedure together with the commutativity test and the integral lattice test by Smith normal form. Chordal bipartite graphs satisfy the cycle condition at $\ell=4$ by splitting a longer cycle at a chord. [Kannan–Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040) · [Golumbic–Goss](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.3190020209)

For an ordered cycle $C$ in the chosen list let $H_C$ be its gain transported to the root. Commutativity makes the transported gain independent of the path to the cycle's start. Changing the root or the block bases conjugates the algebra. Any change of the arc representatives multiplies a cycle gain by a scalar in $F^\times$. Write
$$
H_{C,i}=b_{C,i}(1+N_{C,i}),
\qquad b_{C,i}\in B_i^\times,\quad N_{C,i}\in J_i.
$$
The vector of residues is projective and every $N_{C,i}$ is unaffected by the scalar choice. Since the integral cycle lattice is generated by the selected cycles, every fundamental gain is a scalar times a product of powers of selected gains. Inverses of invertible matrices belong to the algebras they generate. Consequently the selected $H_C$ generate $A$, and the selected $N_{C,i}$ generate $A_i$ over $B_i$.

A **chart** at factor $i$ is an ordered tuple of $s_i$ selected cycles whose $N_{C,i}$ form a basis modulo $J_i^2$. At $s_i=0$ use the empty tuple. Such charts exist: modulo $J_i^2$, a polynomial in the $N_{C,i}$ has only its constant and linear terms. Any chart generates $A_i$ over $B_i$. To see this, let $E$ be its generated $B_i$-subalgebra. Since $J_i\subseteq E+J_i^2$, multiplication gives $J_i^j\subseteq E+J_i^{j+1}$ for every $j\ge1$. Iteration and nilpotence give $J_i\subseteq E$. This argument places no bound on the number of nonzero radical powers.

## Encode the data and reconstruct a witness

Use a colored relational graph. Include the bipartite support, the selected ordered cycles, and a vertex for each local factor with colors recording $[B_i:F]$, $\dim_F V_i$, and $s_i$. Represent ordered cycles by their ordered incidence with support vertices. Include the $F$-embeddings $\phi:B_i\to K_d$ for $d=[B_i:F]$ as vertices linked to their factor. Here $K_d$ is a common field model for the compared inputs. Join these embedding vertices in their directed $q$-Frobenius cycle.

1. For each selected cycle and each pair of embedding vertices, encode the ratio of the two residue values. For degrees $d,e$ put the ratio in a common model of $\mathbb F_{q^{\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)}}$ using fixed embeddings of the two models chosen jointly for the inputs. These pair fields have degree at most $r^2$. A match respecting factor and Frobenius edges induces field isomorphisms $\alpha_i:B_i\to B'_{i'}$. Equal ratio labels say that the discrepancies between the target residues and the $\alpha_i$-images coincide across embedding pairs. Comparing a value with its Frobenius successor puts it in $F^\times$. Comparing different factors makes it the **same** scalar $c_C$ for the entire cycle. [Lenstra](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf) · [Brieulle et al. on field embeddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.01221)
2. Include the relation saying which ordered cycles form a chart. Fix a field generator $\eta_d$ of each model $K_d$ over $F$. For each embedding $\phi$ of factor $i$, chart $(C_1,\ldots,C_{s_i})$, and selected cycle $C$, add a relation with those ordered arguments. Its color is the simultaneous similarity class over $F$ of the operator tuple on $V_i$
   $$
   \bigl(\phi^{-1}(\eta_d),N_{C_1,i},\ldots,N_{C_{s_i},i},N_{C,i}\bigr).
   $$
   The field entry acts by its matrix in the root representation. Choose the color IDs for both inputs jointly by testing all pairs of these tuples. Constructive polynomial-time module isomorphism computes simultaneous similarity of equally named operators. This avoids any classification or enumeration of the possible local modules. [Brooksbank–Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

A block-respecting tensor isomorphism induces a graph match. Its root conjugator maps the coefficient algebra and radical, preserves the Frobenius structure and chart test, and gives all the required simultaneous similarities. Projective scalars cancel from the ratios and from the normalized radical entries.

Conversely take a graph match. For each factor, choose a matched embedding and a matched chart. If the cycle list is nonempty, use any one extended-tuple similarity to obtain an invertible local map $K_i$ matching the field generator and the chart operators. For each further cycle $C$, its tuple color guarantees that **some** map matches both those named operators and $N_{C,i}$. All maps matching the named operators agree by conjugation on the algebra they generate. That algebra is $A_i$ by the chart argument. Thus the original $K_i$ maps every $N_{C,i}$ to the corresponding target value simultaneously. If the list is empty, the connected support is a tree, so arbitrary root identification works. The residue ratios give a common scalar $c_C$ across factors, and $K=\bigoplus_i K_i$ therefore conjugates each projective $H_C$ to the target. Since the transport group is abelian projectively, the short-cycle equality extends to the entire integral cycle lattice. The tree switches give the requested tensor transporter.

There are at most $r$ embedding vertices and at most the number of selected cycles to the power $s$ choices of a chart at each factor. The pairwise tests used to define colors remain polynomial for fixed $s$ and a polynomial-size list. Encode each relation as a colored vertex with separately colored ordered ports. One can encode the colors in an ordinary graph by attaching two private cycles of lengths $3$ and $4+c$ for color ID $c$. The original vertices have degree at least four and new vertices have degree two, so colors are recoverable. Finite-field construction, embeddings, and idempotent decomposition have Las Vegas polynomial algorithms. Babai's algorithm then gives an expected quasipolynomial isomorphism algorithm on the stated class. [Babai](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03547)

## Aligned supports and the scope of the improvement

Given an alignment, take the same tree on both sides and use the full ordered tuples of fundamental gains. Extract the coefficient fields as above. Choose any source residue-field embedding. A conjugacy must send it to one of at most $r$ target embeddings of the same degree. For each candidate use this single matching embedding to compute the target-to-source residue ratios on **all** fundamental gains. Reject a ratio outside $F^\times$. Otherwise divide the target operators by those scalars and run one full simultaneous similarity test. A positive test gives a root conjugator, and any projective conjugacy supplies one of the tried embedding matches. This proves the aligned result without charts or bounds on local embedding dimension.

To see that the graph theorem handles unbounded nilpotency depth and nonsplit fields together, use the regular representation of
$$
B[x,y]/(x,y)^m,\qquad B=\mathbb F_{q^d}.
$$
On $K_{2,4}$ choose one of the length-two path transports as the identity and the other three as multiplication by $\eta_d$, $1+x$, and $1+y$. These units generate the displayed algebra. It has local embedding dimension two and a radical with nilpotency index $m$. The field degree and depth can both grow. The test also permits arbitrary faithful modules over this algebra. It uses the module similarity colors explicitly.

The finite controls distinguish square-zero pairs with identical ranks for every individual nonzero radical combination and different joint images. There were 48 matches for a conjugate and zero for the transpose fixture across $12$ alignments and $168$ root maps per alignment. A second control uses the five-dimensional ring $F_2[t]/(t^5)$. Sending $t$ to $t+t^2$ gives a positive simultaneous substitution. The negative target keeps $1+t$ and replaces $1+t^2$ by $1+t^2+t^3$. The second radical operator has the same ranks of all its powers, yet a transporter fixing the first operator must fix its square. All 32 eighth powers in this local ring also agree with the asserted residue projection. The [control program](check_radical_holonomy.py) implements these examples. The proof combines established gain switching, coefficient-field decomposition, and module isomorphism with a finite chart encoding for **unknown** support alignment. Priority for the combination remains unverified.

## Combining higher-dimensional edges, radicals, and long cycles

The chart construction also applies to the field-multiple edge spaces in [FieldTorsorBlocks.md](FieldTorsorBlocks.md). Assume the field action around each closed walk is trivial, so the root gains act linearly over its vertex field $K$. Use $A=K[H_e]$ and take a sufficiently high $|K|$-power for the coefficient-algebra construction. This coefficient algebra contains $K$, since the power fixes $K$ pointwise. The image and kernel arguments still apply, and each local normalized radical operator is independent of the larger scalar ambiguity in $K^\times$.

Keep the restriction links from residue embeddings to root-field embeddings in the field-multiple encoding. Compare residues only at pairs of embeddings with a common restriction to $K$. The $|K|$-Frobenius comparisons and the cross-factor ratios give one discrepancy scalar from $K^\times$ for each cycle. For the chart colors keep the same simultaneous-similarity test over the original ground field $F$, naming a generator of the full residue field over $F$. A matched chart transporter therefore also sends the marked subfield $K$ by the common base-field map. The chart-agreement proof preserves all radical operators. The field-multiple tree switches then give a tensor transporter.

Consequently the higher-dimensional edges, bounded local radical embedding dimension, and bounded per-block cycle rank can occur together in a polynomial-size GI reduction, with unbounded block dimension, field size, nilpotency depth and cycle length. The short-cycle lattice hypothesis can also be used in place of the per-block cycle-rank bound.
