# Tensor holonomy at bounded local or total cycle rank

**Results.** Bounded cycle length can be replaced by bounded maximum cycle rank of a biconnected support block. The resulting ordinary Graph Isomorphism reduction has size $2^{O(b)}\operatorname{poly}(N)$ for local cycle rank $b$ and allows unbounded circumference, field size, and tensor block dimension. Already on bouquets of arbitrarily long even cycles, the restricted problem over $\mathbb F_3$ with split semisimple holonomy is GI-complete. Bounding the **total** cycle rank $k$ gives a Las Vegas $2^{O(k\log(k+2))}\operatorname{poly}(N)$ algorithm without a GI oracle. For the last result the holonomy algebra can be noncommutative, provided it is semisimple.

The proofs give linear witnesses that can be checked directly on the input tensors. They are informal mathematical proofs. The finite controls at the end were also enumerated.

## 1. Passing from tensors to projective transports

Contract the last leg of a 3-tensor over $F=\mathbb F_q$ and take the image $S\subseteq L\otimes R$. Consider the class with a decomposition into invertible matrix lines
$$
S=\bigoplus_{uv\in E(G)}F M_{uv},\qquad
L=\bigoplus_u U_u,\quad R=\bigoplus_v W_v,\quad
M_{uv}:W_v^*\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}U_u
$$
on a connected simple bipartite support $G$ with an edge and common block dimension $r$. Here $N$ means the bit size of the dense input over an explicitly represented finite field. The hidden block projections can be found by linear equations and primitive decomposition. In each leg, solve for the associative algebra of endomorphisms whose action preserves $S$. Each displayed block projection is in its respective algebra. Its corner is scalar, since an operator on that block preserving an incident invertible matrix line must be scalar. Thus the projections in either leg form a complete primitive orthogonal system.

Any two such systems in a finite-dimensional algebra are conjugate up to permutation by an algebra unit. The idempotents decompose the right regular module into indecomposable right ideals. Krull–Schmidt matches these ideals by a module automorphism, which is left multiplication by an algebra unit and conjugates the projections. The leg algebra units preserve $S$ and so one may compute any complete primitive systems, check the displayed conditions on their block projections, and restrict the isomorphism search to mappings of the recovered blocks. A product map between the contraction images lifts to a tensor isomorphism for equal tensor formats by identifying the two quotient spaces and their equal-dimensional contraction kernels. [Grochow–Qiao](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110), [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, Proposition 11](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf).

On an edge from the right block to the left block use $M_{uv}$ as its transport, and use its inverse on the reverse edge. The residual isomorphism is a part-preserving alignment of supports and a projective linear switch at each vertex. For an aligned support, choose a common spanning tree and root. Let $P_v$ and $P'_v$ be the respective tree transports from the root to $v$. For each nontree arc $e:u\to v$, the rooted transports are $H_e=P_v^{-1}G_eP_u$ and $H'_e=(P'_v)^{-1}G'_eP'_u$. A single root matrix $K$ satisfying $[KH_eK^{-1}]=[H'_e]$ for all nontree arcs suffices. The switches are $P'_v K P_v^{-1}$. This proves equivalence by direct multiplication, including on tree arcs. The switch at a right block acts on its dual space and gives the inverse dual map on the right tensor leg. [Cavaleri–Donno, Theorem 3.3 and Remark 3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12428).

## 2. Unbounded circumference and bounded local cycle rank

Assume for this section that the holonomy algebra $A=F[H_e]$ is commutative semisimple. It is a product of finite fields $A_i$ of degrees $d_i$, with module multiplicities $m_i$ in the natural $r$-dimensional representation. Invertible scalar changes to the arc representatives do not change $A$. Changing the tree gives another set of generators, and moving the root conjugates the algebra by a connecting transport. For a closed cycle, transporting its matrix back to the root along a different path conjugates it by a rooted holonomy, which commutes with it. Rotating the starting vertex has the same effect after returning to the root. Reversing the direction inverts the cycle value.

Let $b$ be the maximum of $|E(B)|-|V(B)|+1$ over the biconnected blocks $B$ of $G$, counting bridges as rank zero. Every simple cycle belongs to a biconnected block. For a block of rank $b_B$, enumerate all $2^{b_B}$ subsets of the fundamental binary cycle basis. XOR their edge vectors and retain exactly the nonempty connected edge sets of degree two at every occupied vertex. This enumerates every simple cycle once. This use of the binary cycle space enumerates candidates. The completeness proof uses the integral cycle lattice, in which the fundamental oriented simple cycles are an integral basis.

Encode the part-colored support, one vertex for each simple cycle, and two indistinguishably colored orientation vertices attached to each cycle vertex. On each orientation vertex record its directed successor arcs by ternary relations on (orientation, tail, head). A graph isomorphism must preserve these cyclic successor relations, so it carries orientations according to its action on the support. Include a vertex colored $(d_i,m_i)$ for each factor and $d_i$ associated embedding vertices $\phi:A_i\to K_{d_i}$ in a directed Frobenius cycle. The field models $K_s=\mathbb F_{q^s}$ can be chosen once per input pair. For each required $s\mid a$, fix a common $F$-embedding $K_s\to K_a$, taking the identity for $s=a$.

Finally, for each oriented cycle and each ordered pair of embedding vertices, record the ratio of their eigenvalues on that cycle, using the two embeddings into $K_{\operatorname{lcm}(d_i,d_j)}$. Each pair field has degree at most $r^2$. The ratio is independent of the scalar ambiguity in the cycle matrix. Distinct ordered ports implement all the bounded-arity relations by colored vertices and edges. Ordinary graphs can encode the colors by attaching two private cycles of lengths $3$ and $4+c$ at each vertex of color ID $c\ge 0$. Original vertices have degree at least four and private vertices have degree two, so this preserves isomorphism. Field models, embeddings and factor decomposition have Las Vegas polynomial bit algorithms. [Lenstra, Theorem 9.3](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf), [Shoup](https://doi.org/10.1006/jsco.1994.1025).

For completeness, a graph match induces an $F$-algebra isomorphism $\alpha:A\to A'$ by matching factor embeddings and their Frobenius cycles, and the factor colors match their module multiplicities. For a matched oriented cycle, all embedding values of the discrepancy $H'_C\alpha(H_C)^{-1}$ have equal pairwise images. Pairing any embedding with its Frobenius successor forces its value into $F^\times$. Pairing across factors then forces a single common scalar. The cycles recorded include a fundamental integral basis. Because the based projective holonomy is abelian, this equality holds on every closed walk. The multiplicities realize $\alpha$ by a linear root isomorphism, yielding the switches of Section 1. Conversely, any tensor isomorphism acts on the support, on factor embeddings, and on orientations, and keeps the ratio labels unchanged.

There are at most $\sum_B 2^{b_B}$ cycles, with arity bounded independently of circumference. Accounting for successor relations, field labels, and the ordinary graph gadgets gives size and expected construction time $2^{O(b)}\operatorname{poly}(N)$. For fixed $b$ this is a polynomial-size GI reduction. Cacti have $b\leq1$ and may have arbitrarily large total cycle rank and circumference. Over a fixed field with split holonomy, the construction is deterministic polynomial for fixed $b$. The field models there are all the base field, and the primitive-idempotent algorithm is deterministic polynomial for fixed characteristic. [Babai](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03547).

## 3. GI-completeness on long-cycle bouquets

For a simple input graph $X$ with $n\geq 2$ vertices and $m\geq1$ edges, make a bipartite bouquet with one even cycle of length $2L$ per vertex and a common left vertex. Choose the same $L$ for all petals, for example $L=n+2$. The center is its unique vertex of degree greater than two. A part-preserving isomorphism can arbitrarily permute petals and independently reverse them. Work over $\mathbb F_3$. Use block dimension $r=m+2$, identity transports except for one edge on each petal, and give the petal of vertex $v$ the holonomy
$$
D_v= I_2\ \oplus\ \operatorname{diag}\bigl((-1)^{\mathbf1[v\in e]}:e\in E(X)\bigr).
$$
Encode this connection as invertible matrix lines and then as a tensor by taking a basis of the matrix-line space as slices. The construction is polynomial in the input graph size.

The holonomy algebra is split. Its joint eigenspaces are the two-dimensional common all-ones space and one line for each edge incidence column. Distinct edges of a simple graph give distinct nonzero incidence columns. The projector onto any joint sign pattern $\chi$ is the product of $(I+\chi(v)D_v)/2$ over the petals. A projective common conjugation must match the unique two-dimensional factor. Since its eigenvalue is one on every petal in both instances, all projective scalars are one. Reversing a petal has no effect because $D_v^2=I$. Matching the other factors and the petal permutation is now exactly matching rows and columns of the vertex-edge incidence relation, which is equivalent to an isomorphism of $X$. In reverse, a graph isomorphism gives those permutations, a root conjugation, and switches along the support tree. Graphs of different sizes and elementary zero-edge cases can be decided directly in the reduction.

This proves deterministic GI-completeness on the stated split subclass of cactus tensors over $\mathbb F_3$ by Section 2. It does not follow from hardness for plain supports. The support bouquet itself depends only on $n$, while the common matching of the algebra factors carries the incidence information. Its least cycle length grows with $n$ in the displayed construction.

## 4. Polynomial aligned testing for noncommutative semisimple holonomy

Here $A$ and $B$ may be arbitrary semisimple matrix algebras, generated by invertible tuples $(A_i)$ and $(B_i)$ of common size $r$ with corresponding indices. The goal is to decide and find $X$ such that $X A_i X^{-1}=\lambda_i B_i$ with $\lambda_i\in F^\times$. The supplied ordering is essential for the following algorithm. For a support alignment these are the corresponding fundamental holonomies.

### Projective scalar equations

Build a word basis of $A$ by starting with the identity, repeatedly multiplying on the left by generators, and retaining independent words. There are at most $r^2$ basis words, each of length at most $r^2-1$. The same words must be a basis of $B$ under any proposed equivalence. Test this by linear algebra including the dimension of $B$. Let $u_j$ count occurrences of each generator in basis word $j$, and let $e_i$ be the $i$th unit vector of the integer exponent lattice. Let $c_{ijk}$ and $d_{ijk}$ be the coefficients of left multiplication of basis word $j$ by generator $i$, in the respective word bases. The zero patterns must coincide. On nonzero coefficients the exact equations are
$$
\lambda^{e_i+u_j-u_k}=c_{ijk}/d_{ijk}.
$$
Their sufficiency for an algebra isomorphism follows because the word-basis map sending the source word $j$ to $\lambda^{u_j}$ times the target word intertwines every left multiplication and sends identity to identity. Applying this successively to generators proves multiplicativity on every pair of algebra elements.

Add the necessary determinant equations $\lambda_i^r=\det(A_i)/\det(B_i)$. The equation exponent matrix has row lattice containing $r\mathbb Z^k$, where here $k$ is the tuple length. Apply integer Smith normal form. Unimodular row transformations multiply right-hand constants, and unimodular column transformations give invertible monomial changes of unknowns. A zero row requires right-hand constant one. All nonzero Smith diagonals divide $r$, because the row-lattice quotient is killed by $r$. Thus the transformed system has independent univariate equations of degree at most $r$, without a discrete logarithm step. Finite-field factorization finds a particular solution $\lambda_0$ or proves there is none. The homogeneous solution group $H$ has explicit generators: enumerate the field roots of $z^{s}=1$ in each Smith coordinate of degree $s$, pick a generator of the resulting cyclic group by testing powers, and invert the monomial change of coordinates. The full scalar solution set is $\lambda_0H$. [Kannan–Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040), [Cantor–Zassenhaus](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1981-0606517-5).

### Matching the ambient modules

Write $\alpha_0:A\to B$ for the resulting algebra isomorphism. A homogeneous tuple $\mu\in H$ acts on $A$ by the automorphism $\gamma_\mu$ scaling word $j$ by $\mu^{u_j}$. The map associated to $\lambda_0\mu$ is $\alpha_0\gamma_\mu$. Compute the primitive central idempotents of $A$ using its center. Semisimplicity is recognizable by computing the radical. Each simple factor has a unique simple module up to isomorphism, so the representations match exactly when the ranks of all corresponding central idempotents agree. In detail, color an idempotent $f$ by $a(f)=\operatorname{rank}(f)$ in the source and by $b(f)=\operatorname{rank}(\alpha_0(f))$ in the target. The requirement is $a(f)=b(\gamma_\mu(f))$ for all $f$. The generators of $H$ give explicit commuting permutations of the central idempotents by their action on the word basis. [Friedl–Rónyai](https://doi.org/10.1145/22145.22162).

The simultaneous color matching under this abelian permutation group is polynomial by the following orbit calculation. Let $h_1,\ldots,h_s$ denote the known generators and use integer vectors $z$ to represent their powers. In each orbit fix an anchor $x_0$ and compute a path vector $v_x\in\mathbb Z^s$ carrying the anchor to each orbit point $x$. The lattice of vectors fixing the anchor is generated by $v_x+e_j-v_{h_jx}$. Each difference fixes the anchor, and telescoping along any closed generator path gives the converse, allowing signs for reverse steps. Since the group is abelian, two elements with the same image of the anchor have the same permutation on its entire orbit. Test each possible image for the desired color match. The successful elements, if any, form one coset of the stabilizer of the source coloring on this orbit. In integer coordinates it is a coset of the lattice generated by the anchor-fixing lattice and the differences of all successful path vectors from one of them. Require a single $z$ in these cosets for all orbits. Introduce coefficient variables for each lattice basis and solve the resulting linear equations over the integers by Smith normal form. The number of central idempotents is at most $r$, so this calculation has polynomial bit complexity. It is a direct instance of the use of small transitive constituents for permutation group transporters. [Luks–McKenzie](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(88)90044-X).

Finally, solve linear equations for an intertwiner on each pair of matched central-idempotent images. The spaces are isomorphic modules by the rank check. On one simple component the solution space identifies with a full matrix algebra over a finite field, and a uniform element is invertible with probability at least $\prod_{i\geq1}(1-2^{-i})\geq1/4$. Sampling separately on each component avoids a success probability that decays with the number of components. Sum the component intertwiners and check the resulting matrix and every claimed equality directly. This proves a Las Vegas polynomial bit algorithm for aligned semisimple holonomy, with neither commutativity nor a bound on the number or length of its generators. It does not choose the support alignment.

## 5. A fixed-parameter algorithm for total cycle rank

Let $k=|E(G)|-|V(G)|+1$. Remove the pendant trees by taking the 2-core, keeping their rooted isomorphism types as colors on the core vertices. These types can be computed by bottom-up sorting of child types, retaining the bipartition colors. Any two extensions of the same core alignment act identically on the rooted closed-walk transports, since every simple cycle is in the core. Consequently it is enough to find one extension of each valid core alignment.

For $k=0$ the support is a tree and there is no holonomy constraint. For $k=1$ the 2-core is a single cycle, with at most twice its length many alignments. For $k\geq2$, suppress every maximal path through core vertices of degree two to an edge of a multigraph, allowing loops and parallel edges. Keep the sequence of rooted-tree and part colors along the path, with orientation. The core identity $\sum_v(\deg v-2)=2k-2$ bounds the suppressed multigraph by $2k-2$ vertices and $3k-3$ edges. Enumerate its vertex and edge bijections and the path orientations. Test the incidence and color sequences, expand each surviving alignment, and extend into the attached trees. This covers all possible core alignments in $2^{O(k\log(k+2))}\operatorname{poly}(N)$ time. Use the aligned test of Section 4 on each one to get the claimed Las Vegas bound. Ordinary support GI is itself known to be FPT in feedback vertex number. The reason for enumerating the suppressed alignments here is to also test their interaction with the tensor holonomy. [Kratsch–Schweitzer](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~pascal/docs/feedback_griso_KratschSchw.pdf).

## 6. Exact controls and scope

For the bouquet construction, a four-vertex path and a three-leaf star both use four equal petals and three edge factors. Enumerating all $24$ petal permutations gave two self-matches of the path's incidence data and zero matches to the star. Independent reversal choices cannot alter this outcome.

For the aligned test, take $F=\mathbb F_3$ and the semisimple algebra $M_2(F)\times M_2(F)$ with holonomy generators
$$
(I,-I),\qquad (U,U),\qquad (V,V),
\quad U=I+E_{12},\quad V=I+E_{21}.
$$
They generate the algebra since the first separates the central components and the other two generate each matrix factor. Use module multiplicities $(1,3)$ in the source, and $(3,1)$ or $(2,2)$ in the targets. All natural dimensions are eight. The abstract algebra and determinant tests in both cases permit the two signs of the first generator and fix the signs of the unipotent generators. The latter signs must be positive for an ambient match by their eigenvalues. For the finite check, left multiplication was computed in the eight-word basis $1,U,V,UV$ and their products on the left by $(I,-I)$. Enumerating the eight scalar tuples against those coefficients and then testing the first generator's eigenspace dimensions gives the unique successful tuple $(-1,1,1)$ for $(3,1)$ and none for $(2,2)$. In the positive case a permutation of the natural two-dimensional summands gives the explicit conjugation, which was also checked against the input matrices. These tuples can be realized on a fixed spanning tree with three additional arcs by assigning the matrices to the nontree transports.

The local cycle-rank encoding handles unknown alignment with commutative semisimple holonomy. The noncommutative algorithm requires a supplied alignment, or an enumeration such as the total-cycle-rank algorithm. It is complementary to an unknown-alignment short-word encoding with a separate generation hypothesis. All results concern the recovered invertible-line class of Grochow and Qiao's Tensor Isomorphism framework. The proof isolates new sufficient conditions and an exact lower bound on the restricted class. Priority for the combined constructions remains unverified.
