# Tensor isomorphism with finite-field matrix blocks

**Theorem.** Fix a cycle length bound $\ell\ge4$. There is a recognizable class of finite-field 3-tensors whose nonzero recovered matrix blocks have arbitrary dimension $d$ and consist of the maps $K_u G=G K_v$. Here $K_u,K_v$ are degree-$d$ extension fields of the ground field acting on the endpoint spaces, and $G$ is invertible. Assume the support is connected, every closed walk acts trivially on its vertex field by conjugation, the linear cycle holonomies commute and are semisimple over that field, and the integral cycle lattice is generated by simple cycles of length at most $\ell$. Isomorphism for this class is GI-complete under polynomial-time reductions with Las Vegas preprocessing for the upper bound. The reduction constructs a tensor isomorphism from a graph matching. It also gives exact tensor isomorphism counts in polynomial expected time with a GI oracle.

The edge dimension and the field size are unrestricted. The case $d=1$ recovers the invertible-line theorem. Even for $d=2$ over $\mathbb F_2$ with trivial holonomy, the recognized class is GI-hard. The argument extends the matrix-space approach to tensor isomorphism by recovering the fields from the stabilizer algebras and tracking the common field action in the cycle labels. [Grochow–Qiao](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110) · [Couvreur–Debris-Alazard–Gaborit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04611)

## Intrinsic recovery of the fields and support

Let $F=\mathbb F_q$. Contract the third leg of a tensor to get a linear map from an $F$-space of dimension $c$ into the space of maps from $R^*$ to $L$, and write $S$ for its image. A product equivalence of image spaces acts by $M\mapsto XMY^{-1}$ for $X\in\mathrm{GL}(L)$ and $Y\in\mathrm{GL}(R^*)$. For tensors of the same format, every such product equivalence lifts to a third-leg map by splitting the contraction maps and identifying their kernels.

Assume there are decompositions of $L$ and $R^*$ into vertex spaces for a connected bipartite graph with at least one edge. In every nonzero block from $v$ to $u$, the image is $E_{uv}=K_uG_{uv}=G_{uv}K_v$, where $G_{uv}$ is invertible and $K_u,K_v$ are unital fields of endomorphisms at the vertices. The full space is the direct sum of these blocks. Connectivity forces all vertex dimensions to be equal and all fields to have the same degree $d$.

Compute the two full associative one-leg stabilizer algebras $B_L=\{a:aS\subseteq S\}$ and $B_R=\{b:Sb\subseteq S\}$ by linear equations on a basis of $S$. The displayed block projections lie in them. In the corner of a vertex $u$ on the left, any stabilizer of an incident block satisfies $aK_uG_{uv}\subseteq K_uG_{uv}$. Since $G_{uv}$ is invertible and $1\in K_u$, this implies $a\in K_u$. Every element of $K_u$ preserves all incident blocks. Thus the corner is exactly $K_u$. The right corner is obtained by the same argument with $G_{uv}K_v$, and is $K_v$. Every block projection is primitive because its corner is a field.

Compute complete orthogonal primitive idempotent systems of $B_L,B_R$. Any two such systems in a finite-dimensional algebra are conjugate by a unit, after a permutation. This follows by decomposing the right regular module into the indecomposable right ideals given by the idempotents, applying Krull–Schmidt, and realizing the resulting module automorphism by left multiplication. The units of $B_L,B_R$ stabilize $S$. Therefore the computed systems give the hidden block structure up to an image-space automorphism. Every product equivalence can be adjusted by such units to send the computed systems to one another. [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski](https://doi.org/10.1145/258726.258751) · [Eberly–Giesbrecht](https://doi.org/10.1006/jsco.1999.0308)

This gives a recognition algorithm. Project $S$ to each recovered block pair. On each nonzero image $E_{uv}$ choose a nonzero $G$ and test invertibility. In the promised class every nonzero element is invertible. Calculate $E_{uv}G^{-1}$ and $G^{-1}E_{uv}$ and check that both are unital fields and that the recovered field agrees across incident edges at each vertex. Test the support, dimension, and holonomy hypotheses on these data. A field test can use closure, commutativity, injectivity of the $q$-power map and dimension one of its fixed space. Indeed a finite reduced commutative $F$-algebra is a product of finite fields, each with a one-dimensional fixed space. An input passing the tests has the required displayed form. Every input of that form passes because the recovered projections differ from the original ones by stabilizer units.

## Relative projective holonomy

On an edge take $G$ as the transport and $G^{-1}$ for the reverse direction. Two representatives differ by left multiplication by an element of the head field's unit group. Transport induces a well-defined isomorphism of fields by conjugation. A block-respecting equivalence consists of a part-preserving support alignment and vertex maps $X_v$ which conjugate the vertex fields and carry every edge transport class to the target class. The right tensor-leg map is the dual of the inverse on its right vertex spaces.

Choose a root $o$, a spanning tree, and tree transports $P_v$ from the root to each vertex. Put $K=K_o$ and define $H_e=P_v^{-1}G_eP_u$ for non-tree arcs $e:u\to v$. The tree-normalized transports generate the based walks and test whether every closed walk induces the identity on $K$. In this case the $H_e$ are $K$-linear. Changing edge representatives scales each based holonomy by a unit of $K$. Changing the spanning tree changes the based generators, and changing the root conjugates the data by a transport which identifies the root fields. These assertions follow by inserting the tree paths between consecutive arcs of a walk and canceling. [Cavaleri–Donno, Theorem 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12428)

When the $H_e$ commute and are semisimple, the $K$-algebra $A=K[H_e]$ is finite étale over $K$. Indeed, commuting semisimple matrices over finite fields diagonalize simultaneously over an algebraic closure. Write $A=\prod_i A_i$, $[A_i:F]=a_i$, and let $\mu_i$ be the rank of the corresponding factor's vector space over $A_i$. The inclusion $K\hookrightarrow A$ gives an inclusion into each $A_i$, so $d\mid a_i$. The sum of the $a_i$ is at most the vertex dimension $r$ over $F$. The generated algebra and the multiplicities are intrinsic under changes of representatives, tree, and root. They can be computed by multiplication, linear algebra, and finite-algebra decomposition. To test semisimplicity one can instead use squarefree minimal polynomials over $K$.

For an oriented simple cycle $C$, transport it to the root using a tree path from the root to its starting point. Denote its matrix by $H_C$. The rooted matrix is independent of that path because two choices differ by a based closed holonomy, which commutes with $A$. Different edge representatives leave its class in $A^\times/K^\times$ unchanged. This group is abelian. Therefore short-cycle classes determine all based cycle classes if the short cycles generate the integral cycle lattice. One can check this by reading the signed non-tree coordinates of the short cycles and taking Smith normal form. [Kannan–Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

## A graph encoding with shared base embeddings

For each required degree $a$ use a common model $F_a$ of $\mathbb F_{q^a}$ in both inputs. Degrees used below are multiples of $d$. Equip each $F_a$ with a marked inclusion $j_a:F_d\hookrightarrow F_a$. For each comparison in $F_{\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)}$, choose embeddings of $F_a$ and $F_b$ into that field agreeing with the marked inclusions of $F_d$. Such embeddings exist because finite subfields of a fixed degree are unique. One can adjust any chosen embedding by a power of Frobenius to align its restriction. Only pairwise coherence over $F_d$ is needed. These choices use fields of degree at most $r^2$ over $F$, not a splitting field of all factors. [Lenstra](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf) · [Shoup](https://doi.org/10.1006/jsco.1994.1025)

Encode the following bounded-arity colored relational structure.

1. Include the support graph with its two parts distinguished.
2. Include the $d$ embeddings $\kappa:K\to F_d$ as a directed Frobenius cycle. Include one vertex for each field factor of color $(a_i,\mu_i)$ and its $a_i$ embeddings $\phi:A_i\to F_{a_i}$, also in a directed Frobenius cycle using $q$-power Frobenius. Link each $\phi$ to the base embedding $j_{a_i}^{-1}\phi|_K$.
3. For every ordered simple cycle $C$ of length at most $\ell$ and ordered pair of factor embeddings $\phi,\psi$ with the same base restriction, add one relation consisting of the cycle vertices and the two embeddings in order. Its label is the ratio $\phi(H_{C,i})/\psi(H_{C,j})$ in the common pair field, using the marked inclusion choices.

The ratio is unchanged by scaling $H_C$ by $K^\times$, because the two embeddings have the same restriction to $K$. A tensor equivalence, adjusted to match the computed blocks, conjugates $A$ and maps its subfield $K$ to the target base field. It therefore identifies the structures, including the restriction links and ratio labels.

Conversely, a structure isomorphism gives a support alignment. Commuting with the Frobenius cycles implies that matched embeddings of each factor induce one factor-field map. The restriction links make their restrictions agree on $K$. Thus the maps assemble to $\alpha:A\to A'$ mapping $K$ onto $K'$. For any short cycle let $U_C=H'_C\alpha(H_C)^{-1}$. At all embeddings of a given factor over a fixed base embedding, its values coincide by the ratio conditions. In particular a factor embedding and its $q^d$-Frobenius successor give the same value, which therefore belongs to the marked copy of $F_d$. Pairing across all factors with that same base embedding forces every value to be the image of the same element of $K'^\times$. Thus $U_C\in K'^\times$. This establishes coincidence on the short generators and hence on all based cycle classes.

The multiplicities $\mu_i$ allow a linear map $J$ at the root realizing $\alpha$ by conjugation. Choose module bases in the matched factors and apply their field maps to coefficients. This map is $F$-linear and is semilinear relative to $\alpha|_K$. For a common tree after alignment set $X_v=P'_v J P_v^{-1}$. These maps implement the exact equality on the selected tree representatives and the required equality of classes on the other edges. They reconstruct the image-space equivalence. Splitting the original contractions then gives a deliberately checkable tensor isomorphism.

For fixed $\ell$ there are at most $O(|V|^\ell)$ ordered short cycles and at most $r^2$ embedding pairs. A bounded-arity relation can be represented in a colored graph by a unique relation vertex and position-colored ports. The color labels can be sorted and given consecutive identifiers jointly for the pair. Standard color gadgets reduce colored graph isomorphism to ordinary GI with polynomial growth. All preprocessing is polynomial expected bit time. [Babai](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03547) · [Grochow–Qiao on linear-length reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16317)

## GI-hardness in dimension two

Given a simple graph on $n\ge3$ vertices, form its bipartite incidence graph with original vertices on the left and edges on the right. Add one further right vertex joined to every left vertex. This vertex is uniquely identified by its degree $n$, whereas ordinary right vertices have degree $2$. Hence part-preserving isomorphism recovers exactly the original graph isomorphism. The support is connected. Use a spanning tree consisting of all edges to the universal vertex and one edge from every ordinary right vertex to any incident left vertex. Every non-tree edge gives a fundamental four-cycle. Small graphs can be handled directly.

Take $F=\mathbb F_2$, let each vertex space be $\mathbb F_4$, and set each edge block to $\mathbb F_4 I$ in the standard field bases. These blocks have dimension two over $F$, and their holonomy is trivial. Write a contraction with the two field-basis maps in each edge block as its images. This is polynomial in the graph size. Graph isomorphisms give tensor isomorphisms, and the primitive recovery argument reduces every tensor equivalence to a part-preserving support alignment. The numerical formats can first be compared, including the original graph's vertex and edge counts. This establishes GI-hardness within the stated class.

## Exact stabilizer and isomorphism counts

Consider an accepted tensor and let $g$ be the number of automorphisms of its colored relational encoding. The encoding automorphisms correspond bijectively to pairs consisting of a support permutation and an algebra map $\alpha$ compatible with the projective holonomies. A pair determines each embedding permutation because it prescribes precomposition with $\alpha^{-1}$, and the restriction links prescribe the base embeddings. Conversely the graph-to-tensor proof constructs a unique $\alpha$ from the permutations. Relation vertices and position-colored ports have no additional permutations fixing the arguments, since each relation tuple and label occurs only once. Compute $g$ with polynomially many GI queries by fixing vertices successively and counting each vertex orbit under the preceding point stabilizer. This counts the colored structure before adding any color gadgets.

For each pair, the maps at the root form a coset of the centralizer of $A$, of size $C_A=\prod_i|\mathrm{GL}_{\mu_i}(q^{a_i})|$. Fixing the root, the tree determines all switch classes. Each nonroot vertex switch has $q^d-1$ representatives, and choosing them independently leaves the fundamental conditions unchanged. Therefore the number of block-respecting equivalences of the image space to itself is

$$
g C_A (q^d-1)^{|V|-1}.
$$

To obtain all product automorphisms, let the two unit groups $B_L^\times$ and $B_R^\times$ act on their unordered complete primitive systems by conjugation. Each action is transitive. The units on each side independently give product automorphisms. Hence every pair of target systems lies in the image of a product automorphism, and all fibers have the same size as the block-respecting stabilizer above.

For either $B$, write its radical as $\mathfrak r$ and its semisimple quotient as $\prod_j M_{t_j}(\mathbb F_{q^{h_j}})$. Each recovered primitive corner is a field, and its image in the quotient is still a field corner since the kernel is nilpotent. In particular each primitive idempotent projects to one minimal matrix idempotent in one factor. Units can only permute them within those components. Conversely all such permutations are realized: lift a unit giving the permutation in the semisimple quotient. Its conjugated idempotents $e_i'$ have the same residues as the desired permuted idempotents $e_{\pi(i)}$. The element $\sum_i e_{\pi(i)} e_i'$ is $1$ modulo the radical, hence a unit, and intertwines the two systems. The subgroup fixing each idempotent is the product of the corner field unit groups. If $v_B$ denotes the number of recovered blocks on this leg, the orbit size of the unordered system is consequently

$$
O_B=\frac{q^{\dim_F\mathfrak r}\prod_j|\mathrm{GL}_{t_j}(q^{h_j})|}
          {(q^d-1)^{v_B}\prod_j t_j!}.
$$

The numerator follows from surjectivity on quotient units, with kernel $1+\mathfrak r$. Both radical and matrix factors are obtainable by finite-algebra algorithms. Multiplication by $O_{B_L}O_{B_R}$ accounts for product maps moving the recovered systems. [Eberly–Giesbrecht](https://doi.org/10.1006/jsco.1999.0308)

Finally put $s=\dim_F S$. For each product equivalence, choose splittings of the source and target contractions. The third-leg map has a fixed induced map on their $s$-dimensional quotients, an arbitrary invertible map on the $(c-s)$-dimensional kernels, and an arbitrary linear map from a quotient summand into a kernel. Thus it contributes $|\mathrm{GL}_{c-s}(q)|q^{s(c-s)}$ possibilities. The full tensor automorphism count is

$$
g C_A (q^d-1)^{|V|-1}\ O_{B_L}O_{B_R}\ |\mathrm{GL}_{c-s}(q)|q^{s(c-s)}.
$$

For a pair, reject when there is no isomorphism. Every nonempty set of isomorphisms is a coset of the source automorphism group. Integer sizes are polynomial in the input bit length because the count is at most the order of the product of the three ambient linear groups.

## With a supplied alignment

When the field action of every closed walk is trivial, an aligned-support test can sometimes omit both commutation and semisimplicity. Suppose the algebra generated by the fundamental transports over $K$ has a simple factor $M_j(L)$ modulo its radical with $\gcd(j,q^d-1)=1$. This includes a field factor, in particular any commutative holonomy algebra. Choose such a factor and enumerate the $d$ possible isomorphisms on the two base fields. For each choice enumerate possible matches of the chosen simple factor with one of equal matrix size and degree, together with all relative center embeddings. A projective semilinear conjugacy sends the generated algebra to the target algebra, since multiplying any generator by a base-field unit does not alter the generated algebra.

For each candidate, take the determinant of each fundamental transport in the matched simple factors. Their ratio must be $c_e^j$ for a scalar $c_e\in K'^\times$. An algebra isomorphism of full matrix algebras preserves the determinant after its coefficient field map, since a linear algebra automorphism of a full matrix algebra is inner. If a ratio lies in $K'^\times$, raise it to the inverse exponent of $j$ modulo $q^d-1$ to obtain the unique candidate scalar. For $q^d=2$ there is only the scalar $1$. Rescale the fundamental generators and append one named operator: an $F$-algebra generator of $K$ in the source and its image under the proposed base-field map in the target. Test simultaneous similarity of these augmented tuples over $F$ by module isomorphism, constructing an intertwiner on acceptance. The extra operator forces the root map to induce exactly the proposed map on the vertex field. The full tuple then certifies all projective fundamental equations, irrespective of which candidate factor supplied the scalars. Existence of a conjugacy ensures its factor choice and scalars appear in the enumeration, proving completeness. This gives polynomial expected bit time with a supplied support alignment, without the short-cycle assumption. [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski](https://doi.org/10.1145/258726.258751)

## Finite controls and scope

For a cycle test take $F=\mathbb F_2$, $K=\mathbb F_8=\mathbb F_2[\omega]/(\omega^3+\omega+1)$ and $K^2$ as the vertex spaces on $K_{2,3}$. Every edge subspace is three-dimensional over $F$. View the three two-edge paths between the left vertices as diagonal transports $\operatorname{diag}(1,\omega^{a_i})$, taking the first edge representative to be the identity. Compare path exponents $(0,1,3)$ with $(0,2,6)$ and with $(0,1,2)$. The first target applies the common base Frobenius on the given alignment, whereas the second has no match. Direct enumeration tested all 12 support alignments, all three base twists, and all 3,528 invertible $2\times2$ matrices over $K$, trying the seven scalar multipliers for each fundamental transport. The first target passed six alignments with 49 root maps each. On the given alignment only the nontrivial twist $x\mapsto x^2$ passed. The second target passed none.

For an exact counting control use $K_{2,2}$ over $\mathbb F_2$, with vertex field $\mathbb F_4$ and all edge blocks $\mathbb F_4 I$. With a full contraction onto the eight-dimensional image space this gives $S=M_2(\mathbb F_4)$. The formula gives 648 block-respecting maps, orbit size 10 on each leg, and 64,800 full automorphisms. Independently, if $XSY^{-1}=S$, then $XY^{-1}\in S^\times$ by evaluating at $I$. Therefore $Y$ normalizes $S$ and any choice of $XY^{-1}\in S^\times$ works. The normalizer of $S$ is the normalizer of its center field, since $S$ is the endomorphism algebra over that center. Enumeration of $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_2)$ by its four columns counted 360 maps normalizing the center and 180 commuting with it. Their product again gives 64,800. This control includes permutations among isomorphic primitive components and product maps moving the computed decomposition.

The results are informal mathematical proofs with exact finite controls. The declared graph-reduction scope still uses trivial closed-walk action on the base field and semisimple commuting holonomy. Gain-graph switching, finite algebra algorithms, and general GI-to-tensor reductions are established ingredients. A bounded literature check did not establish priority for the relative-field encoding, its witness, or the full count.
