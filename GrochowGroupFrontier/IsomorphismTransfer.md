# Code equivalence to lattice isomorphism over extension fields, and small decision queries for class-two group isomorphism

These are informal proofs. The first theorem gives a deterministic Karp reduction from monomial linear-code equivalence over every fixed prime-power field to Euclidean lattice isomorphism. Its uniform running time is polynomial in the input length and field cardinality. The second theorem gives linear-length decision queries for finding and counting monomial transporters at fixed field size. Applied to the projective-column model of central quotients of Heisenberg powers and dihedral powers, it gives search and counting in the same quotient family.

## 1. An extension-field reduction to lattice isomorphism

**Theorem 1.** For each fixed prime power $q$, the linear equivalence problem over $\mathbb F_q$ reduces in deterministic polynomial time to Euclidean lattice isomorphism by one many-one instance. Given $\mathbb F_q$ in a standard polynomial-basis representation, the construction is uniform in time polynomial in the input length and $q$. Its first stage, permutation code equivalence over the prime field, has length $O(nq)$, where $n$ is the input code length.

Horlemann, Mazumder, Schaller, and Weger ask in §6 for reductions from linear equivalence to lattice isomorphism over non-prime fields. Their question specifies neither a convention for variable field size nor a bound in $\log q$. This theorem establishes the reduction for every fixed non-prime field and when the field cardinality is polynomially bounded. One lattice isometry also suffices to recover a code isometry, as in the survey's search formulation. The first stage enforces field multiplication after restriction of scalars. [Survey, §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12870#S6)

### Restriction of scalars with coordinate cycles

Take generator matrices $A,B$ with full row rank $k$ for two codes of length $n$. Different dimensions or numbers of zero columns decide the input immediately. Delete the zero columns, which can be restored in any monomial equivalence. For $k=0$ the answer is immediate. For $q=2$, monomial equivalence is already permutation equivalence. For the construction below take $q\ge 3$, which includes every extension field. Write the remaining columns as $a_i,b_i$, retaining every coordinate index. The codes are monomially equivalent exactly when there are a linear $T\in\operatorname{GL}_k(\mathbb F_q)$, a permutation $\pi$, and nonzero scalars $s_i$ with $T(a_i)=s_i b_{\pi(i)}$. This is the row-change criterion for full-row-rank generators.

Let $q=p^h$, put $V=\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_p}\mathbb F_q^k$, and choose a generator $\theta$ of $\mathbb F_q^\times$. Finding one by enumerating elements and testing their powers takes time polynomial in $q$. Add two independent $\mathbb F_p$-spaces with bases $e_{i,\lambda}$ and $f_{i,\lambda}$, respectively, for every $i$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb F_q^\times$. In $V\oplus E\oplus F$, include the following literal column vectors with the indicated multiplicities.

| Multiplicity per occurrence | Column |
|---:|---|
| 1 | $e_{i,\lambda}$ |
| 2 | $f_{i,\lambda}$ |
| 3 | $e_{i,\lambda}+f_{i,\lambda}$ |
| 4 | $e_{i,\lambda}+f_{i,\theta\lambda}$ |
| 5 | $e_{i,\lambda}+\lambda a_i$ |
| 6 | $\lambda a_i$ |

The first five types have distinct vectors within each type and are mutually disjoint by their supports, since $a_i\ne 0$ and $\theta\ne 1$. The pure vectors in the last type may coincide and always have total multiplicity divisible by 6. Hence multiplicities distinguish all six types. The resulting column set spans its ambient space. In particular, the pure vectors span $V$ over $\mathbb F_p$ because the $a_i$ span $\mathbb F_q^k$ and all their nonzero scalar multiples occur. Build the matrix on the other side using $b_i$. This is an ordinary, uncolored permutation code equivalence instance in the prime field, with at most $21n(q-1)$ columns.

An equivalence gives one ambient linear map sending the literal column multiset of $A$ to that of $B$. The pure-column span and the first two types give three invariant summands. The first two types permute their respective bases. The third type identifies the two basis permutations. The fourth forces their common permutation to commute with the permutation $(i,\lambda)\mapsto (i,\theta\lambda)$. Its cycles are indexed by $i$, so the common permutation sends $(i,\lambda)$ to $(\pi(i),s_i\lambda)$ for some nonzero $s_i$.

Write $T$ for the restriction to $V$. The fifth type now gives $T(\lambda a_i)=\lambda s_i b_{\pi(i)}$. For each $\mu\in\mathbb F_q^\times$, the maps $T\circ m_\mu$ and $m_\mu\circ T$, where $m_\mu(x)=\mu x$, agree on all $\lambda a_i$. Those vectors span $V$ over the prime field, so the maps are equal. This proves that $T$ is $\mathbb F_q$-linear and induces the required original monomial equivalence. Conversely, the original $T,\pi,(s_i)$ give the corresponding basis permutations, preserve the multiplicities, and map every type of column to its counterpart. The pure and mixed columns are literal vectors throughout this argument.

### From the prime-field code to a lattice

Compose the previous reduction with the deterministic Karp reduction from prime-field permutation code equivalence to signed permutation code equivalence of Cheraghchi, Shagrithaya, and Veliche. Use Construction A for the last step as in Bennett–Win. Here is the last-step argument, including the field-size dependence. [Reductions Between Code Equivalence Problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07916) · [Relating Code Equivalence to Other Isomorphism Problems](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/782)

For two codes over $\mathbb F_p$, repeat every coordinate $p^2+1$ times. This preserves signed permutation equivalence both ways. Indeed, signed equivalence for full-row-rank generator matrices is equivalence of multisets of column sign classes $\{v,-v\}$ by a row change, and a common multiplier for each multiplicity cancels. Then form $\Lambda(C)=\{z\in\mathbb Z^D\mid z\bmod p\in C\}$ using the repeated code. Compute a basis in polynomial time from systematic generators of $C$, adjoining $p$ times the coordinate vectors outside the information set. A lattice vector with nonzero residue has at least $p^2+1$ nonzero coordinates and squared norm exceeding $p^2$. The shortest nonzero vectors of $\Lambda(C)$ are therefore exactly $\{\pm p e_j\}$. An isometry preserves this set, hence is a signed coordinate permutation and induces signed equivalence of the residue codes. The converse holds by construction.

The reduction also decodes a witness from one lattice isometry. Read its signed permutation from its action on the vectors $p e_j$. Compute the row change induced on the repeated generator matrices by solving a linear system. The corresponding original signed column classes can be matched using their identical multiplicities after cancelling the repetition factor. Corollary 3.11 of Cheraghchi, Shagrithaya, and Veliche gives the original permutation code equivalence constructively, by restricting the recovered block row map and removing the common scalar on the first block. Finally, the coordinate cycles recover the original $T,\pi,(s_i)$ via the restriction and tags in the first stage. Cases decided during preprocessing can use explicit constant yes and no lattice pairs for the decision reduction. Witnesses for those yes cases are immediate.

The running time is polynomial in the input length and $q$, including enumeration of $\theta$ and all nonzero scalars. The stronger separate survey question asking for polynomial dependence in $\log q$ remains open for this method.

## 2. Linear-length queries for projective configurations and codes

**Theorem 2.** Fix a finite field $\mathbb F_q$. Given two linear codes of length $n$ and rank $k$ by generator matrices, finding a monomial equivalence and counting all monomial equivalences each reduce to ordinary decision by $O(n^2+1)$ calls. Every oracle code has length $O_q(n+1)$ and rank at most $k+O(\log(n+1))$. The work outside the oracle is deterministic polynomial time.

Biasse, Micheli, Prada, and Waitkevich already give a deterministic polynomial-time search-to-decision reduction with $O(n^2)$ calls on same-rank codes of length at most $n^2(n+1)/2$. This proof uses the additional $O(\log n)$ rank to bring query length down to linear for fixed $q$, and includes the exact transporter count. [A Search-to-Decision Reduction for the Linear Code Equivalence Problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31517)

A full-row-rank code has a spanning weighted configuration of lines in $V=\mathbb F_q^k$, obtained by identifying the proportional nonzero columns. The weights record multiplicity. A row map carries one weighted configuration to the other exactly when it can be completed to a monomial equivalence on the nonzero coordinates. Zero-column counts are compared separately.

To implement a decision query with colors on the distinct data lines, assume the color labels range over $1,\ldots,t$, where $t\ge 1$. Let $Z=\mathbb F_q^s$, with $s$ minimal so that $Z$ has at least $t$ lines. Choose distinct marker lines $w_c$ in $Z$, using the same choices on both sides. The uncolored weighted configuration in $V\oplus Z$ is as follows.

- A data line $u\subset V$ of weight $m_u$ has weight $4m_u$.
- Each of the $s$ coordinate lines $\langle e_j\rangle$ in $Z$ and each of the $s-1$ lines $\langle e_j+e_{j+1}\rangle$ has its own distinct weight $4i+2$, using consecutive integers $i\ge 0$.
- For every data line $u$ with color $c$, insert each of the $q-1$ lines of $\langle u,w_c\rangle$ other than $u,w_c$, with weight 1.

The mixed lines are distinct and outside both $V$ and $Z$. Projection gives their unique pair $(u,w_c)$. Weights modulo 4 distinguish data, marker, and mixed lines. Consequently the ambient row map preserves $V$ and $Z$. The distinct marker weights fix the coordinate lines separately, so the map on $Z$ is diagonal. The tied marker lines $e_j+e_{j+1}$ force agreement of all diagonal entries, hence every $w_c$ is fixed projectively. The mixed lines enforce the colors. Conversely, any weighted color-preserving row map extends by the identity on $Z$. The code instance is obtained by taking a nonzero representative of each line and repeating it according to its weight. The rank is $k+s$ and the length is at most $4n+(q-1)n+O(s^2)$. For the applications below $t\le n+1$, so this is $O_q(n+1)$.

Start by testing equivalence. Then choose the image of each distinct data line successively by giving the pinned pairs distinct private colors and querying candidate target images of the next line. Use a common color on unpinned lines. Since every affirmative answer guarantees an extension, this finds a weighted projective correspondence after at most $n^2$ pinning queries. To convert the correspondence to a row map, choose $k$ independent source lines. Their target lines are independent. Express all lines in the respective bases. In these coordinates the desired map is diagonal with nonzero entries $\alpha_j$. The condition for one further matched pair of lines with coordinate vectors $x,y$ is equal support and the existence of nonzero $\beta$ such that $\alpha_j x_j=\beta y_j$ on the support. Eliminate $\beta$ using any one index from the support. Propagation of the resulting pairwise ratios assigns the $\alpha_j$ in polynomial time. Existence follows from the decision queries. Pair coordinates within the matched lines and choose their unique nonzero coordinate scalars. The zero coordinates can be matched arbitrarily. Empty codes and zero-dimensional cases can instead be handled directly.

For counting, work with the automorphism group of the source weighted configuration as a subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}(V)$. Once equivalence has been established, the transporter is a coset of the automorphism group. Fixing each line in turn gives a stabilizer chain. Its successive indices are the orbit sizes, computed by the same pinned decision queries. The final subgroup fixes every line. On an independent basis of listed lines it acts diagonally. Its diagonal entries must agree within the support of every other listed line. The resulting equality graph with $c$ components gives a kernel of size $(q-1)^c$. Multiply this by the stabilizer indices to count row maps. Each row map permits $\prod_u m_u!$ coordinate bijections between the matched nonzero lines and each selected bijection determines the nonzero coordinate scalars uniquely. If there are $n_0$ zero columns, multiply further by $n_0!(q-1)^{n_0}$. These integers have polynomial bit length.

## 3. Quotients of Heisenberg and dihedral powers

**Corollary.** Fix an odd prime $p$, and let $H_p$ be the group of upper unitriangular $3\times 3$ matrices over $\mathbb F_p$. Allow as instances the quotients $H_p^M/K$ with $K$ any central subgroup. The same result holds for central quotients $D_8^M/K$ when $p=2$. For a pair of groups in the relevant family, finding and counting isomorphisms reduces in deterministic polynomial time to decision in the same central-quotient presentation model. The reduction uses $O(M^2+1)$ queries, where $M$ is the larger input power. The queries have $O_p(M+1)$ power factors, and their group orders are bounded by a fixed power of the larger input group order for nontrivial instances. The class can also be recognized and its code configuration extracted from ordinary permutation generators in polynomial time, with decision queries still in the central-quotient presentation model.

To prove this, represent the commutator images of the $M$ constituent factors by columns $w_i$ spanning $W=[G,G]$. A nonzero column contributes one symplectic plane to $V=G/Z(G)$, with bracket in the projective line it spans. Across different lines the planes are mutually orthogonal. Thus
$$
 V=\bigoplus_{\ell}V_\ell,\qquad
 \dim V_\ell=2m_\ell,\qquad
 [V_\ell,V_j]=0\ \text{for }\ell\ne j,
$$
where $m_\ell$ is the number of columns in $\ell$. Each restriction is a nonsingular alternating form valued in $\ell$. A zero column contributes two independent central dimensions to a complement of $W$ in $Z(G)$. At $p=2$, every nonzero column also gives the hyperbolic quadratic plane in the squaring map on its summand. A quotient by any $K$ has exactly this presentation because $K$ imposes relations only on the original central generators. Conversely, choose a chart on $W$ and include $m_\ell$ columns in each line $\ell$, along with $\dim(Z(G)/W)/2$ zero columns. Its kernel in the original center gives the required presentation.

To recover the blocks intrinsically, call the bracket $\beta$, and solve the linear equations for the adjoint algebra
$$
 \mathcal A=\{(f,g)\mid \beta(fx,y)=\beta(x,gy)\},
 \qquad (f,g)(f',g')=(ff',g'g).
$$
The radical of $\beta$ is zero on $V$, so the second component of an adjoint pair is uniquely determined by the first and vice versa. For $x\in V_\ell,\ y\in V_j$ with $\ell\ne j$, the adjoint equation equates a value in $j$ with one in $\ell$, forcing both to vanish. Nonsingularity forces the corresponding off-diagonal blocks in $f,g$ to vanish. On a single block, every endomorphism has a unique adjoint. It follows that $\mathcal A$ is a product of the full endomorphism algebras, and the simultaneous eigenspaces of its center are precisely the $V_\ell$. This proves necessity of the intrinsic weighted-line correspondence for group isomorphism.

Conversely, each invertible map $b$ of the derived spaces carrying weighted lines to weighted lines lifts to a block isomorphism. For odd $p$, rescale one vector in every selected symplectic pair by the prescribed nonzero factor on the line. At $p=2$ use matched hyperbolic bases, since each line has one nonzero element. Extend arbitrarily by an isomorphism of central complements of equal dimension. Brackets and the required powers agree. The usual ordered generator normal form now gives a group isomorphism. Explicitly, central generators are sent by the selected central map, and tangent generators by chosen lifts of the block isomorphism. Relations sufficient for the normal form are the prescribed commutators, powers, and centrality, since they give a representative with one residue in $\mathbb F_p$ for each tangent and central generator. The map is bijective since it induces isomorphisms on both the center and the quotient.

Theorem 2 therefore finds an appropriate $b$ and computes the exact number $B$ of such maps using queries again in the group family. To turn a query code into a quotient, use the kernel of its generator matrix as the central subgroup. Treat zero-column counts directly. An instance with power $M$ has order $p^{2M+\dim W}$, so the query-size claim follows from the linear code-length bound.

For the exact group transporter count on an affirmative pair, put $d=\dim W$, $t=\dim(Z(G)/W)$, and $v=\dim V$. For each $b$ the set of block lifts on $V_\ell$ is a torsor for $\operatorname{Sp}_{2m_\ell}(p)$ at odd $p$, and for the isometry group $O^+_{2m_\ell}(2)$ of a hyperbolic form in characteristic two. The central map contributes $|\operatorname{GL}_t(p)|p^{dt}$ by its arbitrary invertible complement map and arbitrary complement-to-$W$ map. Finally, the image of each of the $v$ tangent basis generators can be modified independently by an element of $Z(G)$, since central elements have exponent $p$ and adding one preserves the specified powers. These choices are recovered uniquely from the homomorphism. Hence the number is
$$
 B\,|\operatorname{GL}_t(p)|\,p^{dt+v(d+t)}
 \prod_\ell
 \begin{cases}
 |\operatorname{Sp}_{2m_\ell}(p)|,&p\ \text{odd},\\
 |O^+_{2m_\ell}(2)|,&p=2.
 \end{cases}
$$
All factors are polynomial-time computable. The symplectic formula comes from counting ordered symplectic bases, with $p^{2m}-1$ choices of a first nonzero vector, $p^{2m-1}$ choices of a partner pairing to 1, and recursion in the perpendicular complement. The hyperbolic analogue has $2^{2m-1}+2^{m-1}-1$ choices of a first nonzero isotropic vector, and $2^{2m-2}$ choices of an isotropic partner pairing to 1. The first count follows from the hyperbolic model $Q(x,y)=x\cdot y$. For the second, adding the first vector pairs up the candidates with pairing 1 and exchanges their quadratic values. In dimension zero both group orders are 1.

### Recognition on raw permutation generators

Given permutation generators, compute $W$ from their pairwise commutators after verifying those commutators commute with every generator. Check that the commutators have order dividing $p$. For odd $p$, verify the generators have order dividing $p$ and put $N=W$. For $p=2$, verify the generators have order dividing 4 and put $N=\langle W,g_i^2\rangle$. In the binary case the squares are central because the group has class at most two and $W$ has exponent at most 2. In both cases $N$ is central elementary abelian and $G/N$ is elementary abelian. Compute bases on these sections by greedily adding a generator precisely when subgroup membership fails. For any elementary abelian section with lifted basis $a_1,\ldots,a_j$ and lower subgroup $D$, find coordinate $i$ of a lift $g$ by testing, for all $u\in\mathbb F_p$, whether $g a_i^{-u}$ belongs to the subgroup generated by $D$ and the basis lifts other than $a_i$. This uses polynomially many ordinary permutation-group membership tests. The radical of the commutator map on $G/N$ is $Z(G)/N$, so linear algebra computes center generators by lifting the radical basis and adjoining generators of $N$. Verify the center is elementary abelian and use the same membership method for $Z(G)/W$ and $G/Z(G)$. This supplies all coordinates for the subsequent bracket and squaring tests. [Permutation Group Algorithms, Seress](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511546549)

Given the bracket, compute $\mathcal A$ and its center by linear algebra, and refine simultaneous eigenspaces over $\mathbb F_p$ for a basis of central endomorphisms. Reject if this does not split completely. Verify that the resulting blocks have zero crossed brackets, distinct line images, and nonsingular form within each block. Also check that $\dim Z(G)/W$ is even. In the binary case verify that the quadratic map restricts on each block to a nonsingular hyperbolic form with values in its line. The block check suffices for the whole square map by polarization and the zero crossed brackets. Hyperbolicity can be checked by splitting off hyperbolic pairs recursively. In dimension at least 4, find a nonzero isotropic vector by testing basis vectors and their pairwise sums. If all basis vectors are anisotropic and every pair pairs to 1, the sum of any three basis vectors is isotropic. A partner pairing to 1 can always be made isotropic by adding the first vector when necessary. Dimension 2 is tested directly.

These conditions hold on the stated family. Conversely the checked forms and the normal form argument construct the stated quotient. This gives raw recognition and extraction as claimed. The compact query presentation is the ambient power together with a basis for the central kernel. The corollary concerns decision in this model. Its polynomial size bound for the input to the oracle uses that compact presentation.

### Relation to published tensor and group questions

Grochow and Qiao ask for search-to-decision bounds in an alternating matrix-space setting in §10 of their group and tensor isomorphism paper. The general alternating setting and the query-bound goals there differ from this central-quotient setting. Part IV gives further results for the general alternating search problem. The scope here is the recognizable class with split adjoint blocks and one-dimensional bracket image per block. The established consequences are fixed-field linear-length decision queries and exact transporter counts for that class, together with the extension-field lattice reduction from Section 1. [Grochow–Qiao, §10](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309) · [Tensor Isomorphism and Group Isomorphism IV, §7.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16317)
