# Constructing minimum faithful permutation actions for class-two groups

**Theorem.** For an arbitrary finite group of nilpotency class at most two given by a Cayley table, a minimum-degree faithful permutation action is constructible in deterministic polynomial time. The prime, exponent, and ranks may all vary. For a class-two permutation group given by explicit generators of written length $L$, the minimum degree and generators for stabilizers of a minimum action can be computed in $|Z(G)|\operatorname{poly}(L)$ time. The action itself can be printed in time polynomial in $L+|Z(G)|+\mu(G)$. These are informal proofs.

This treats the class-two Cayley-table case of the minimum-degree problem discussed by Kantor and Luks. In the succinct permutation-generator model, the parameter bound complements the hardness of small-orbit instances, whose centers can be exponentially large. Related character-theoretic work gives degree formulas in cyclic-center families. The algorithm below directly constructs point stabilizers and determines which central characters should be used. [Kantor and Luks, §13 Problem 1](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/quotient.pdf#page=9) · [Das and Thakkar on the Cayley-table model](https://doi.org/10.1137/24M1685353) · [Prajapati and Udeep, cyclic-center formulas and antecedents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06257)

## 1. Reduce to one central direction at a time

First let $G$ be a finite $p$-group, write $C=Z(G)$ and $S=C[p]$. Every nontrivial normal subgroup meets $S$, by the class equation and the presence of an element of order $p$ in any nontrivial central $p$-group. Thus a family of coset actions is faithful exactly when the intersections of their point stabilizers with $S$ have trivial intersection. A minimum action can be chosen in which these intersections are hyperplanes defined by a basis of $S^*$. Indeed select a minimum action with as many orbits as possible. An orbit with stabilizer $H$ meeting all of $S$ can be removed. If $D=H\cap S$ has codimension at least two, choose strictly larger subgroups $S_1,S_2$ of $S$ with $S_1\cap S_2=D$, using complementary nonzero subspaces of $S/D$. Replacing $H$ by $HS_1,HS_2$ preserves faithfulness and gives two indices each at most $[G:H]/p$. This lowers the degree or preserves it and increases the number of orbits. Finally a dependent mark can be dropped. [Elias, Silberman and Takloo-Bighash, Minimal Permutation Representations of Nilpotent Groups](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2010.10129066)

For a fixed nonzero mark $\lambda:S\to C_p$, the cheapest orbit is the minimum index of $H$ with $H\cap S=\ker\lambda$. View characters as taking values in $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$. Choose a character $\chi:C\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ extending $\lambda$ and killing $H\cap C$. Such an extension exists because $\lambda$ and the zero map agree on $S\cap H$, and any character of a subgroup of a finite abelian group extends to the whole group. For the extension fact, adjoin one element at a time and divide the already specified value of its least positive multiple in the subgroup by that multiple in $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$. Put $K=\ker\chi$. Replacing $H$ by $HK$ leaves the mark unchanged and can only lower the index, because $HK\cap C=K$. Conversely any subgroup of $G/K$ disjoint from the central cyclic group $C/K$ pulls back to a stabilizer with the required mark. It suffices to enumerate all $|C|$ characters and optimize for each, then take the least cost on each projective line of marks.

## 2. Pass to a quotient with cyclic center

Fix such a $\chi$, put $Q=G/K$, $C_0=C/K$ and $Z=Z(Q)$. Identify $C_0$ with its character image under the faithful character $\chi_0$ induced by $\chi$. The commutator subgroup of $Q$ lies in $C_0$. To minimize the index of a subgroup of $Q$ disjoint from $C_0$, it is enough to consider $\eta:Z\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ extending $\chi_0$, and then minimize the index of a subgroup of $Q/\ker\eta$ disjoint from its center.

In fact, given a subgroup $B$ disjoint from $C_0$, choose an $\eta$ that also kills $B\cap Z$, by the same character-extension argument. Replacing $B$ by $B\ker\eta$ can only lower the index and gives $B\ker\eta\cap Z=\ker\eta$. The center of $Q/\ker\eta$ is exactly $Z/\ker\eta$. If an element becomes central, each of its commutators lies in both $C_0$ and $\ker\eta$, so each is trivial. Conversely a subgroup disjoint from the new center lifts to a subgroup disjoint from $C_0$. The quotient has cyclic center of order $|\eta(Z)|$. Its quotient by that center is always the same abelian group $A=Q/Z$, independent of $\eta$.

## 3. Compute an optimal stabilizer when the center is cyclic

Let $P$ be a class-two $p$-group with center $D=C_{p^c}$, identify $D$ with $\mathbb Z/p^c$, and write $A=P/D$ additively. The commutator gives a perfect alternating bicharacter $\beta:A\times A\to D$. Its radical is zero by definition of the center, and $p^c A=0$ because the $p^c$-th power of every element commutes with the group. Consequently the injective map $A\to\operatorname{Hom}(A,D)$ defined by $\beta$ is bijective by cyclic decomposition.

Write $q(x)=\widetilde x^{p^c}\in D$, with powers read in additive center coordinates. It is well-defined on $A$. For integer $k$ it satisfies $q(kx)=kq(x)$. By the class-two power identity,
$$
q(x+y)=
\begin{cases}
q(x)+q(y),&p\ \text{odd},\\
q(x)+q(y)+2^{c-1}\beta(x,y),&p=2 .
\end{cases}
$$
The commutator sign in the second formula is immaterial since its contribution has order at most two.

A subgroup of $P$ disjoint from $D$ projects injectively to an isotropic subgroup $U\subseteq A$ on which $q=0$. Conversely for any such $U$ the preimage is abelian of exponent at most $p^c$. In that finite abelian group the cyclic subgroup $D$ splits as a direct summand. For example extend the identity character of $D$ to the whole preimage, and its image still lies in $C_{p^c}$ by the exponent bound. Its kernel is the desired complement. Thus minimizing the index is equivalent to maximizing the order of $U$.

In any finite abelian group with a perfect alternating form, every isotropic subgroup has order at most $\sqrt{|A|}$. One of this order can be constructed by decomposing into symplectic pairs. Choose an element $x$ of maximum order $p^e$ and one $y$ for which $\beta(x,y)$ has order $p^e$. Such $y$ exists by perfection, and a test on a generating set suffices. Their span is a nondegenerate pair of cyclic factors of equal order, and can be split off by solving the two pairing equations. Continue on its perpendicular complement. All operations on the listed cyclic factors use abelian-group linear equations.

For odd $p$, let $T=\ker q$. Its perpendicular is cyclic because $A/T$ is cyclic. A cyclic subgroup of an alternating group is isotropic, so $T^\perp\subseteq T$. The alternating group $T/T^\perp$ is perfect. Lift a maximum isotropic subgroup of the quotient to obtain an isotropic subgroup of $A$ of order $\sqrt{|A|}$ lying in $T$. The same construction applies when $q=0$, in which case $T^\perp=0$.

For $p=2,c=1$, $A$ is an elementary abelian group and $q$ is an ordinary nonsingular quadratic form with polarization $\beta$. If its Arf invariant is zero, the largest totally singular subspace has order $\sqrt{|A|}$; if its Arf invariant is one, the largest has half that order. The dimension-zero form is of the first type. The ordinary symplectic splitting algorithm and the two-dimensional hyperbolic/anisotropic decomposition compute both the invariant and a largest totally singular subspace. [Conrad, Bilinear forms and the characteristic-two quadratic classification](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/linmultialg/bilinearform.pdf)

For $p=2,c\ge2$, set $T=\ker(2q)$. Then $2q$ is a character into $D$; by perfection there is a unique $a_0\in A$ with $2q(x)=\beta(a_0,x)$ for all $x$. Thus $T^\perp=\langle a_0\rangle$ is cyclic and lies in $T$. The character $2q$ kills $A[2]$, since $q(2x)=2q(x)$. It follows that $a_0\in(A[2])^\perp=2A$, where equality follows from the size of the annihilator and the inclusion $2A\subseteq(A[2])^\perp$. Write $a_0=2v$. Then
$$
 q(a_0)=2q(v)=\beta(a_0,v)=0.
$$
So $q$ on $T$ descends to $T/T^\perp$, with values of order at most two. Decompose the perfect alternating quotient into symplectic pairs $x,y$ of orders $2^e$. For $e\ge2$ the subgroup generated by $2^{\lfloor e/2\rfloor}x,2^{\lceil e/2\rceil}y$ is isotropic of order $2^e$, and $q$ vanishes on it by even divisibility of both generators and the polarization formula. For $e=1$, the term $2^{c-1}\beta$ vanishes on the pair, so $q$ is linear and a line in its kernel suffices. Their direct sum and its lift give $U$ of order $\sqrt{|A|}$.

The exact minimum index is therefore
$$
       p^c\sqrt{|A|},
$$
with a factor of two precisely in the cyclic-center-order-two case of Arf invariant one. All constructions give generators of an optimal stabilizer, including abelian groups with cyclic center, for which $A=0$. The cyclic-center formula has character-theoretic antecedents. The proof above constructs the corresponding subgroup by isolating the power-map obstruction, including cyclic centers of arbitrary size. [Prajapati and Udeep, odd characteristic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06257) · [Behravesh, characteristic two](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/groups-st-andrews-2001-in-oxford/quasipermutation-representations-of-2groups-of-class-2-with-cyclic-centre/E8B0B9E21C7BBA056549BA90CFC03A3D)

## 4. Optimize over extensions without enumerating the larger center

In Section 2, the least possible order of $\eta(Z)$ is $p^k$, where $k$ is the least integer such that $\chi_0$ kills $C_0\cap p^k Z$. Necessity follows because an $\eta$ of order dividing $p^k$ kills $p^k Z$. For sufficiency descend $\chi_0$ to the image of $C_0$ in $Z/p^k Z$, then extend the character on that quotient. The extended image is killed by $p^k$. Find a witness to the extension by Smith normal form on the equations for the cyclic factors and the fixed restriction.

The square-root factor $\sqrt{|A|}$ is independent of the chosen $\eta$. If $p$ is odd or $p^k\ge4$, choose any extension of that least order. The remaining case has $p=2,k=1$, and $C_0$ of order two because $\chi_0$ is faithful and nontrivial. The order-two extensions are an affine space over $\mathbb F_2$, with differences the homomorphisms $\delta:Z\to C_2$ killing $C_0$. On $A=Q/Z$, which is now elementary abelian, their quadratic forms differ by the linear functionals $x\mapsto\delta(\widetilde x^2)$. This is well-defined because squares of central elements are killed by $\delta$, and linear because all commutators lie in $C_0$. Compute the subspace of possible differences by linear algebra. Identify it with a subspace $D_1\subseteq A$ via the perfect alternating pairing, so each difference has the form $\beta(v,-)$ for $v\in D_1$.

For one chosen extension with quadratic form $q_0$, changing by $\beta(v,-)$ changes the Arf invariant by $q_0(v)$. This follows from the change of variable $x\mapsto x+v$ in the quadratic sign sum; its sign is the Arf invariant, as is seen on symplectic planes. If $q_0$ has Arf invariant one, test whether some vector in $D_1$ has value one. A basis vector or a sum of two basis vectors suffices because $q_0$ is quadratic. If such a vector exists, realize that difference by an explicit $\delta$ and use the split type. If it does not exist, every order-two choice has the factor-of-two loss, so every such choice costs $4\sqrt{|A|}$. An extension of larger order cannot improve it. This computes the optimum over all $\eta$ from polynomially many linear computations, even when the auxiliary center $Z(Q)$ is exponentially larger than $C$.

## 5. Assemble and bound the algorithm

For each character $\chi$ of the original center with nonzero restriction to $S$, compute the exact cheapest index above and a corresponding stabilizer by pulling back the successive subgroups. Store the cheapest stabilizer for each projective mark line. Sort by index and greedily take independent lines until they span $S^*$. A minimum-weight basis of vectors in a matroid is obtained by this greedy rule; equivalently apply the elementary basis-exchange argument to the first selected line differing from an optimal basis. The independent-mark theorem in Section 1 gives precisely the optimum faithful action. Prints of the coset actions use the generators of $G$ and the computed stabilizers.

Here are the group operations behind the bit-time claim. Constructive permutation-group membership and subgroup orders are polynomial. The derived subgroup of a class-two group is abelian and generated by the pairwise generator commutators. For an abelian section given by generators, use successive adjoining and relative-order relations to obtain cyclic coordinates by Smith normal form. Relative orders are powers of $p$, found by testing powers for membership. Relations are read in the older generators by constructive membership. Coordinates of quotients can use preimages: membership is membership of the enlarged subgroup containing the known denominator, and relations from constructive membership are projected to the section. If the denominator generators are included, their projected coordinates are zero.

In particular the center of $G/K$ is computed as the kernel of the map $g\mapsto([g,s_i]\bmod K)_i$ on generators $s_i$, with codomain a power of the abelian derived quotient. The kernel follows from abelian linear equations on the generator powers and contains the derived group. Taking ordered products of the computed relation generators and including the derived group generators gives the full kernel. This follows by collecting any word to an ordered product modulo the derived group. The same computation with $K=1$ gives the original center. The commutator and power maps above are computed on cyclic-section generators by evaluating group operations and converting to the computed central coordinates. Abelian preimages, complements, pullbacks and bicharacter perpendiculars use these coordinates and linear equations. Group orders and index arithmetic have bit length polynomial in the original permutation encoding, since $|G|$ is at most the factorial of the supplied permutation degree. Enumeration of $|C|$ characters is just enumeration of their tuples in cyclic coordinates. This proves the claimed parameter bound. Coset-action output takes polynomial time in the input and the total degree printed, by enumerating cosets using subgroup membership. [Seress, Permutation Group Algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511546549)

A class-two group is nilpotent. For mixed primes, work on its direct-product Sylow factors. The Sylow generators can be computed by CRT powering of the supplied generators, whose orders come from cycle decompositions. A subgroup in a direct product of groups of pairwise coprime orders splits into the intersections with the factors by the same powering argument. A coset orbit thus has index equal to the product of the factor indices. Replacing it by the separate nontrivial factor coset actions preserves faithfulness across the full family and does not increase degree, since a product of integers at least two is at least their sum when at least two factors occur. Accordingly the minimum degrees add. This proves the parameter bound for mixed primes as well.

Finally a table of size $|G|$-by-$|G|$ supplies the regular permutation action in polynomial time. Then $|Z(G)|\le |G|$ and $\mu(G)\le |G|$. The full optimal action is therefore a polynomial-time output in the table model. The algorithm works without an exponent bound or a bound on central rank in that model.

The [finite check](check_cyclic_actions.py) enumerates subgroups disjoint from the center in 190 explicit two-generator central-extension laws over the primes 2 and 3. It reproduces the cyclic-center optimum in each case, including the quaternion exception.
