# Group epimorphisms, code equivalence, and minimum faithful actions

Four informal proofs connect finite-group quotients, matrix spaces, and orbit algorithms.

1. **Polynomial-time epimorphism onto Fitting-free groups.** Given Cayley tables of an arbitrary source and a target with no nontrivial abelian normal subgroup, the algorithm decides surjectivity, constructs one map, and counts all surjections. This answers Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar Open Question 3(1) as stated. It also enumerates every normal subgroup with Fitting-free quotient. The crucial bound is that retaining or killing a minimal normal subgroup both decrease the ratio of the current group order to the order of the retained product. The note also answers the constant-size clause of Question 3(2), extends it to bounded-generator nilpotent targets of fixed class, and proves the single-field Heisenberg-quotient case of Question 3(3). [Proof](CayleyEpimorphism.md) · [Published questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)

2. **Fixed nilpotent targets from short descriptions.** For a fixed finite nilpotent target, surjection testing is polynomial-time for abelian targets and NP-complete for every nonabelian target. The classification applies to finite presentations, to ordinary permutation generators, and to permutation quotients. In the ordinary permutation model hardness holds even for solvable sources of bounded derived length and exponent whose orbits have bounded size. The proof amplifies a Boolean rank-one condition, encodes it using commutators and powers, and lifts through central subgroups of the target. It includes and extends the answer to Elder–Shen–Weiß Open Question 4.1 for the order-eight dihedral group. For counting, one padded query transfers Samperton and Weiß's counting theorem for all homomorphisms to surjections for any fixed finite target in the presentation model. The nilpotent case transfers to both permutation models. A bounded wreath-product construction forces all maps to the fixed target to factor through the required quotient while keeping the permutation orbits bounded. [Proof](FixedTargets.md) · [Dihedral question](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39487v1#S4) · [Fixed-target counting questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02885v2#S1.SS6)

3. **Extension-field code equivalence reduces to lattice isomorphism.** A single Euclidean lattice isomorphism instance tests monomial equivalence over every fixed finite field. A lattice isometry also gives a code isometry. Coordinate cycles preserve the action of all field scalars under restriction to the prime field, where existing code-to-lattice reductions apply. This answers the extension-field reduction question in Horlemann–Mazumder–Schaller–Weger §6 for every fixed field. Uniform runtime is polynomial in the field cardinality. The separate bound polynomial in its logarithm remains open here. A companion construction gives linear-length decision queries for finding and counting code transporters at fixed field size and carries over to central quotients of Heisenberg or dihedral powers. [Proof](IsomorphismTransfer.md) · [Survey questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12870v2#S6) · [Earlier code-to-lattice scope](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.07916#S1)

4. **Constructing a smallest faithful action for class-two groups.** The whole class-two Cayley-table case has a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm, with arbitrary primes, exponent, and ranks. For a class-two permutation group, the degree and stabilizers can be computed in time proportional to the center order times a polynomial in input length. The proof optimizes one central character at a time, handles the binary quadratic obstruction, and assembles the optimal orbits. This strengthens the algorithmic side of the minimum faithful degree problem and complements short-encoding hardness. An exhaustive computation checks the local cyclic-center formula on 190 explicit group laws. [Proof](MinimumAction.md) · [Kantor–Luks, §13](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/quotient.pdf#page=9) · [Finite check](check_cyclic_actions.py)
