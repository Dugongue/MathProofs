# Epimorphism dichotomy for fixed finite nilpotent targets

Fix a finite nilpotent group $H$. In the **presentation model**, the input is a finite presentation of a source $\Gamma$. In the **permutation group model**, the input is permutation generators for the source. We also use the **permutation quotient model**, in which the input gives permutation generators for $U$ and $N\trianglelefteq U$, on a common point set, and the source is $U/N$. The target is fixed before any input arrives.

**Theorem.** For abelian $H$, deciding and counting epimorphisms onto $H$ are in P and FP, respectively, in all three models. For nonabelian $H$, decision is NP-complete under deterministic many-one reductions and counting is #P-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions in all three models. In the presentation and permutation quotient models, the hardness reductions output finite nilpotent sources of bounded class and exponent. In the permutation group model, they output solvable sources of bounded derived length and bounded exponent. In both permutation models every orbit has size bounded solely in terms of $H$. If $H$ is a finite nonabelian $p$-group whose proper quotients are abelian, the counting hardness holds under a single-call reduction with an explicit multiplicative correction.

This is an informal proof. Elder, Shen and Weiß explicitly ask about decision for $D_8$ in Open Question 4.1; the theorem gives NP-completeness there and for all fixed nonabelian finite nilpotent groups. Samperton and Weiß identify analogous surjectivity dichotomies as open in the introduction to their homomorphism-counting theorem. The reduction below for counting is a consequence of their fixed-target homomorphism-counting theorem. The proof uses short source encodings. The input models here are distinct from the multiplication-table model studied by Grochow, Srivastava and Thakkar. [Elder–Shen–Weiß, Open Question 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39487v1#S4) · [Samperton–Weiß, Theorem 1 and §1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02885v2#S1) · [Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar, §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429#S7)

## 1. Boolean circuits as projective rank-one matrices

Let $p$ be a fixed prime. From a Boolean circuit with AND and NOT gates take one index for each input bit and gate output, and another index $0$. Let $n$ be the number of indices including $0$. Form a linear matrix space $\mathcal L\subseteq \operatorname{Sym}_n(\mathbb F_p)$ by requiring
$$
 M_{ii}=M_{i0}\ (i>0),\qquad
 M_{z0}=M_{xy}\ (z=x\wedge y),\qquad
 M_{z0}=M_{00}-M_{x0}\ (z=\neg x),\qquad
 M_{\mathrm{out},0}=M_{00}.
$$
Additional variables forced to be zero are allowed by requiring $M_{i0}=0$ at their indices. A symmetric rank-one matrix has the form $suu^{\mathsf T}$ with $s\ne0$, in every characteristic. The diagonal conditions first give $u_0\ne0$. Normalize to $u_0=1$. They then imply $u_i^2=u_i$ for all other indices, so every coordinate is zero or one. The same argument works over the algebraic closure, since the roots of the Boolean equation are still zero and one. The remaining relations encode the gate equations and the output value one. Conversely every satisfying assignment, with the uniquely determined gate outputs and the fixed zero coordinates, gives exactly one vector $u$ with $u_0=1$ and one projective rank-one direction. In particular every nonzero rank-one matrix is uniquely $suu^{\mathsf T}$ with $s\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ and $u$ as just described. Denote the number of satisfying assignments by $R$. All linear maps and bases below can be computed by elimination on this matrix space.

## 2. The minimally nonabelian $p$-group targets

Let $B$ be a finite nonabelian $p$-group all of whose proper quotients are abelian. Every nontrivial normal subgroup contains $B'=[B,B]$. A finite $p$-group has a central subgroup of order $p$, so $B'$ is central and has order $p$. Also $Z(B)$ has a unique subgroup of order $p$, since every such subgroup contains $B'$. Thus its abelian group $Z(B)$ is cyclic, say of order $p^a$ and generated by $z$. The commutator is $z^{p^{a-1}}$ to the power of a nondegenerate alternating form on $V=B/Z(B)$. The group $V$ is elementary abelian since each $p$-th power belongs to $Z(B)$, by the class-two commutator law. Nondegeneracy follows from the definition of the center. Write $\dim V=2r$ and choose fixed symplectic coordinates $e_1,f_1,\ldots,e_r,f_r$. Let $b_i,d_i$ be lifts to $B$. Write
$$
 b_i^p=z^{\rho_i},\qquad d_i^p=z^{\sigma_i}
$$
for fixed exponents in $\mathbb Z/p^a$. For $v\in V$, let $P(v)\in\mathbb F_p$ be the exponent of the $p$-th power of any lift, reduced modulo $p$. Multiplication of the lift by a central $z^j$ changes its power by $z^{pj}$, so this definition is independent of the lift. We will need only $P(0)=0$ and the stated basis values. This accommodates anisotropic square forms in characteristic two as well as the case $a>1$.

Make a finite class-two $p$-group $\Gamma$ with elementary abelian tangent coordinate space $A=(X\oplus Y)^{\oplus r}$, where $X=Y=\mathbb F_p^n$. Let $E=\mathcal L^*$ and $d=\dim E$. Choose a basis of $E$ and a central group $C=(\mathbb Z/p^a)^d$. Identify $E$ with $p^{a-1}C$ by a linear map $\iota$. For $T\in E$, write $\widehat T\in C$ for the coordinate lift with coefficients from $0$ to $p-1$. Define $T_j\in E$ to evaluate $M\mapsto M_{j0}$. For each paired basis vector $x_{ij},y_{ij}$ of $A$, prescribe the powers
$$
 x_{ij}^p=\rho_i\widehat T_j,\qquad
 y_{ij}^p=\sigma_i\widehat T_j
$$
in additive notation for $C$. Prescribe all cross commutators within the $i$-th pair of copies by $ [x_{ij},y_{ik}]=\iota(M\mapsto M_{jk})$. All other basis commutators outside these cross pairs vanish. The $C$ generators are central of order $p^a$.

Here is a finite group with these relations. Take the set $A\times C$. In its product add the componentwise base-$p$ addition carries in $A$, each multiplied by the prescribed central power of the corresponding basis generator. Also add the bilinear central cocycle
$$
 ((x_i,y_i)_i,(x'_i,y'_i)_i)
 \ \longmapsto\
 \iota\bigl(M\mapsto \sum_i x_i^{\mathsf T}M y'_i\bigr).
$$
Both additions are cocycles. The $p$-th powers of the individual basis lifts are exactly the prescribed powers, since the bilinear term vanishes on a pure basis direction. The difference between this bilinear term and its transpose gives the stated commutators. Thus the law has exactly $|A||C|$ elements with the displayed basis normal forms. The relations generate these normal forms by collection of central commutators and powers, so they present this group. In particular the presentation length is polynomial in the circuit length at fixed $B$. The group has class at most two and exponent dividing $p^{a+1}$.

Suppose $\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow B$. The image of $C$ is central because the map is onto, and its restriction $\lambda:C\to Z(B)$ has a mod-$p$ row $\bar\lambda\in E^*=\mathcal L$, giving a matrix $M\in\mathcal L$. Indeed on $\iota(E)$ the image is the corresponding pairing times $z^{p^{a-1}}$. The induced tangent map $A\twoheadrightarrow V$ pulls the target form back to a bilinear alternating form of rank $2r$. By the cross-commutator relations, the pulled-back form has rank $2r\operatorname{rank}(M)$. Thus $\operatorname{rank}(M)=1$.

Conversely, for any rank-one $M=suu^{\mathsf T}$ from Section 1, send $C$ to $Z(B)$ by a row lifting $M$ modulo $p$. The induced tangent map must factor as the projection on each copy given by the functional $u^{\mathsf T}$, followed by a bijection $F:V\to V$ scaling the alternating form by $s$. To see necessity, the kernel of the induced surjective tangent map is the radical of the pulled-back form, which is precisely the kernel of the stated projection to the $2r$ coordinates. The factor $F$ is consequently unique. To lift this tangent map and $\lambda$, assign lifts independently to each source basis element. The commuting and commutator relations already hold by the form identity. Power relations can be satisfied exactly if and only if
$$
 P(F e_i)=sP(e_i),\qquad P(F f_i)=sP(f_i)
 \qquad(1\le i\le r).
$$
Indeed the power relation for the index $j$ in an $X$-copy reduces modulo $pZ(B)$ to $P(u_j F e_i)=s u_j P(e_i)$; this is automatic for $u_j=0$, agrees with the displayed equation for $u_j=1$, and $u_0=1$ ensures the equation is imposed. The $Y$-copy is identical with $f_i$. Once a relation holds modulo $pZ(B)$, adjusting a chosen lift by a central element changes its $p$-th power through every element of $pZ(B)$. There are exactly $p$ adjustments giving the exact prescribed power. These assignments extend by the proved presentation, and the resulting map is onto since its tangent map is onto and its central image contains a generator of the cyclic center. The latter follows from $\bar\lambda=M\ne0$.

For $s=1$ the identity $F$ satisfies the displayed conditions. Thus any satisfying assignment yields an epimorphism. Moreover the number of eligible $F$'s for each $s$ depends only on $B$ and has no dependence on the circuit assignment $u$. Let $c_B>0$ be their sum over $s\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. Each $M$ allows exactly $p^{(a-1)d}$ central rows lifting it modulo $p$ and each eligible $F$ admits exactly $p^{2rn}$ assignments of tangent lifts. Hence
$$
                  \#\operatorname{Epi}(\Gamma,B)=c_B\,p^{(a-1)d+2rn}\,R .
$$
The count multiplier is computable in polynomial time, since $B$ is fixed and all large integers involved have polynomial bit length. If $\mathcal L=0$, the same construction gives zero epimorphisms by the rank necessity. This proves NP-hardness and one-call #P-hardness for $B$.

## 3. Amplifying a symmetric rank gap

For two nonzero symmetric matrix spaces $\mathcal A,\mathcal B$ over an algebraically closed field, let $\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B$ act as matrices on the tensor product of the coordinate spaces. Put $r=\min\{\operatorname{rank} A:0\ne A\in\mathcal A\}$. Every nonzero $X\in\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B$ has rank at least $r$, by taking a nonzero block in $\mathcal A$. If some such $X$ has rank $r$, then $\mathcal B$ contains a rank-one matrix.

To prove the last assertion, write $X=(A_{ij})$ in the block coordinates of $\mathcal B$. The blocks are symmetric and $A_{ij}=A_{ji}$. If every diagonal block is zero, any nonzero $A_{ij}$ gives a principal two-by-two block matrix of rank $2\operatorname{rank} A_{ij}\ge2r$. Thus some diagonal block, indexed by $0$, has rank $r$. Factor the symmetric rank-$r$ matrix as $X=Y K Y^{\mathsf T}$ with $Y$ of full column rank $r$ and $K$ invertible. Such a factorization follows by using a basis of the column space for $Y$ and a left inverse $L$ for $Y$. Indeed $X=Y L X$ and, by symmetry, $X=X L^{\mathsf T}Y^{\mathsf T}$, so $K=L X L^{\mathsf T}$ works and has rank $r$. This applies also in characteristic two.

Write $Y_i$ for the row blocks of $Y$. The rank of $A_{00}$ gives full column rank of $Y_0$. Therefore $\operatorname{im}A_{i0}=\operatorname{im}Y_i$, while $\operatorname{im}A_{i0}^{\mathsf T}\subseteq\operatorname{im}Y_0$. Symmetry gives $Y_i=Y_0 T_i$ for some square $r$-by-$r$ matrix $T_i$, with $T_0=I$. Any nonzero linear combination $T$ of the $T_i$ is invertible, because the corresponding matrix $Y_0TKY_0^{\mathsf T}$ is in $\mathcal A$ and has rank $\operatorname{rank}T$. Over the algebraic closure, $T_i$ has an eigenvalue $s_i$. The matrix $T_i-s_i I$ is singular, so it is zero. Thus $X$ has blocks $s_i s_j A_{00}$. Apply a linear functional to the $\mathcal A$ factor which takes the value one on $A_{00}$. Its result is $ss^{\mathsf T}\in\mathcal B$, of rank one since $s_0=1$.

For the circuit space $\mathcal L$ of Section 1, take $\mathcal W=\mathcal L^{\otimes t}$, with $t$ a fixed positive integer. If the circuit is unsatisfiable and $\mathcal L\ne0$, the minimum nonzero rank is initially at least two even over the algebraic closure, and rises by at least one at every tensor step. Thus every nonzero matrix in $\mathcal W$ has rank at least $t+1$. The zero space satisfies the same statement vacuously. If the circuit is satisfiable, $\mathcal W$ contains $uu^{\mathsf T}$ where $u$ is the tensor power of the Boolean vector from Section 1. Its coordinates are Boolean and its all-zero index, denoted $0$, has value one. The dimensions of the matrices and a basis for $\mathcal W$ are polynomial in the input at fixed $t$.

## 4. Decision for class-two $p$-groups with elementary commutator subgroup

Let $B$ be a fixed finite nonabelian $p$-group of class two whose commutator subgroup has exponent $p$. Write $Z=Z(B)$ additively and $Z[p]=\{z\in Z:pz=0\}$. Since $[g^p,h]=[g,h]^p=1$, the quotient $V=B/Z$ is elementary abelian. Choose a basis $v_1,\ldots,v_b$ and lifts $s_i\in B$ with $P_i=s_i^p\in Z$. The commutator map $\beta:V\times V\to Z[p]$ is alternating and bilinear over $\mathbb F_p$. We use $t=b$ in Section 3 and index the rows of $\mathcal W$ by $0,\ldots,m-1$.

Put $E=\mathcal W^*$, with basis $e_1,\ldots,e_d$, and $C=Z^d$. For $z\in Z$, $e\in E$, write $z[e]$ for the element of $C$ whose $\nu$-th coordinate is the integer representative in $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$ of the $\nu$-th coordinate of $e$, times $z$. For $z\in Z[p]$, this assignment is linear in $e$. Write $E_{jk}\in E$ for the evaluation $M\mapsto M_{jk}$. Define a source $\Gamma$ with central $C$, with one generator $x_{ij}$ for each $1\le i\le b$, $0\le j<m$, and relations
$$
 [x_{ij},x_{ak}]=\beta(v_i,v_a)[E_{jk}],\qquad
 x_{ij}^p=P_i[E_{j0}].
$$
The central group has its usual direct product presentation. Here $\Gamma/C$ is the elementary abelian group with the $bm$ generators $x_{ij}$ as a basis. To verify consistency, take normal forms $(a,c)\in\mathbb F_p^{bm}\times C$, as in Section 2. The multiplication adds carries with the prescribed powers and adds the bilinear cocycle that on an ordered pair of distinct tangent basis elements equals the displayed commutator and is zero on reversed pairs and identical basis elements. The difference with its transpose gives all commutators because $\beta$ is alternating and $E_{jk}=E_{kj}$. The cocycle and collection arguments of Section 2 then give the stated presentation, finiteness, and an exponent dividing $p\exp Z$. The class is at most two.

Suppose $f : \Gamma\twoheadrightarrow B$. Then $f(C)\subseteq Z$, and $f$ induces a linear surjection $\bar f : \mathbb F_p^{bm}\to V$. Choose a linear functional $\xi:Z[p]\to\mathbb F_p$ nonzero on a target commutator. The matrix for the pullback $\xi\beta(\bar f(-),\bar f(-))$ is nonzero and has rank at most $b$. Its $(i,a)$-block has $(j,k)$-entry $\xi f(\beta(v_i,v_a)[E_{jk}])$. As a function of $E_{jk}$ this is linear, so each block is a matrix in $E^*=\mathcal W$. At least one is nonzero. Its rank at most $b$ contradicts the amplified gap for an unsatisfiable circuit.

If the circuit is satisfiable, choose $M=uu^{\mathsf T}\in\mathcal W$ as above. Send $C=Z^d$ to $Z$ by
$$
 \lambda((c_\nu)_\nu)=\sum_\nu \widehat{M(e_\nu)}\,c_\nu,
$$
with the integer representatives in $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$. Because $M\ne0$, at least one coefficient is prime to $p$ and acts as an automorphism of $Z$. Thus $\lambda$ is onto. Send $x_{ij}$ initially to $s_i^{u_j}$. Every commutator relation holds: on $Z[p]$, $\lambda(z[e])=M(e)z$, and $M(E_{jk})=u_j u_k$. For the power relation,
$\lambda(P_i[E_{j0}])=u_jP_i$ modulo $pZ$, since $M(E_{j0})=u_j$. Adjust the lift $s_i^{u_j}$ by a central element whose $p$-fold multiple is the required difference in $pZ$. This preserves every commutator and realizes the exact power. The images generate $Z$ and map onto $V$, since the $j=0$ images project to the basis $v_i$. Therefore they give an epimorphism.

If desired, pad the circuit with a fixed number of extra variables constrained to zero. Independently of satisfiability, the source then has an elementary abelian quotient of dimension $bm$ exceeding the generator ranks of any of finitely many fixed intermediate targets. This proves deterministic NP-hardness for all $B$ in this section without requiring $\beta$ to have one-dimensional image or $Z$ to be cyclic.

## 5. Decision for every fixed nonabelian $p$-group

Induct on the order of the fixed target $T$. Let $A_T=Z(T)[p]$, and let $\Phi(T)$ be the Frattini subgroup. If $A_T\not\subseteq \Phi(T)$, choose a central order-$p$ subgroup $D$ omitted by some maximal subgroup $T_0$. Since $D$ is central, the maximal subgroup and $D$ form a direct product $T=T_0\times D$. The smaller factor $T_0$ is nonabelian. Keep the same source presentation. An epimorphism $f : \Gamma\twoheadrightarrow T_0$ extends to an epimorphism onto $T_0\times C_p$ whenever one chooses a homomorphism $g : \Gamma\to C_p$ that does not factor through $f$. Indeed the image of $(f,g)$ projects onto $T_0$ and either contains the second factor or is the graph of a homomorphism $T_0\to C_p$. The padding in Section 4 ensures strictly more homomorphisms $\Gamma\to C_p$ than $T_0\to C_p$. The converse is projection onto $T_0$.

Now suppose $A_T\subseteq\Phi(T)$. If $T/A_T$ is abelian, then $T'\subseteq A_T$ and Section 4 applies to $T$. Otherwise use the inductively produced presentation $\Gamma=\langle S\mid R\rangle$ for target $T/A_T$. Replace it by
$$
 \Gamma^\sharp=\langle S\mid [w,s]=1,\ w^p=1\quad(w\in R,\ s\in S)\rangle .
$$
The map $\Gamma^\sharp\to\Gamma$ kills the central elementary abelian subgroup generated by the old relators. So the new source is finite, its nilpotency class grows by at most one, and its exponent grows by at most a factor $p$. It retains the generator-rank lower bound because it maps onto $\Gamma$. An epimorphism onto $T/A_T$ gives $|A_T|^{|S|}$ choices of generator lifts to $T$. All satisfy the new relations. They generate $T$, since any maximal subgroup of $T$ contains $A_T$ and maps to a proper subgroup of $T/A_T$. Conversely, an epimorphism $\Gamma^\sharp\to T$ sends the original relators into $A_T$: their images are central in $T$ by surjectivity and have exponent dividing $p$. Reduction modulo $A_T$ then factors through $\Gamma$ and is onto. Thus the decision answers agree. There are only constantly many induction steps. All bounds and relator lengths stay polynomial at fixed $T$.

For a finite nilpotent target $H$ with a nonabelian Sylow $p$-subgroup $T$, take the source from the $p$-group theorem and its direct product with the product of all the other Sylow factors of $H$. A homomorphism preserves prime-power orders, so the direct product maps onto $H$ exactly when the $p$-source maps onto $T$. Add the fixed presentation of the other factor and the required pairwise cross commutators. This proves NP-hardness in the presentation model, with the claimed bounds on the source.

## 6. From presentations to permutation quotients

Fix a class bound $c$ and exponent $e$ that hold for the target $H$. Let $F(n)$ be the relatively free group on $n$ generators in the variety of nilpotent groups of class at most $c$ and exponent dividing $e$. Every grade of its lower central series is generated as an abelian group by the simple iterated commutators of its weight in the distinguished generators. Thus each grade is finite, bounded in size by $e^{n^k}$ for grade $k$. For each $j\le c$, the finite group $F(j)$, with its distinguished generators, is a fixed table.

Take every subset $S$ of the generators with $|S|\le c$, and delete the other generators to get the projection into $F(|S|)$. The product of these projections is injective. To verify this, regard each deletion of an individual variable as an endomorphism $D_i$ of $F(n)$, and then of each abelian grade. On a grade-$k$ generating commutator, $D_i$ acts as zero if the variable occurs and identity if it does not. The commuting idempotents $D_i$ and $1-D_i$ give the identity decomposition into all exact supports, via the product expansion of $\prod_i(D_i+(1-D_i))$. A support with more than $k$ generators acts as zero on every grade-$k$ generating commutator, so it acts as zero on the entire grade. If an element is killed by all projections to $|S|\le c$, its component with exact support $S$ in any grade it occupies vanishes: one first projects to $S$, then applies $\prod_{i\in S}(1-D_i)$ in that grade. Induction starting at grade one pushes the element down all $c$ grades, proving triviality.

Use regular actions of the constant-size factors on disjoint point blocks. The product of the projections of each generator is an explicit permutation, defining the ambient group $U$ of degree $O(n^c)$ with bounded orbits. Given a presentation $\Gamma=\langle S_0\mid R\rangle$ with $n=|S_0|$, evaluate the relators in $U$ and compute their normal closure $N$ by adjoining conjugates by generators and their inverses until the current generated subgroup is normal. Permutation membership and order computation are polynomial-time. Each successful adjunction at least doubles the subgroup order, so there are at most $O(\log |U|)$ such adjunctions. Thus this produces the input $U,N$ in polynomial time. The natural map $\Gamma\to U/N$ induces a bijection on homomorphisms to $H$, because every map to $H$ obeys the class and exponent identities and kills the relators. This preserves epimorphisms exactly. The bounded-support construction is in the spirit of the finite permutation group argument in [Samperton–Weiß, §2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02885v2#S2.SS4). Here arbitrary presentation relators are retained as a normal subgroup of the explicit permutation group.

For the finite-presentation version of the same factorization, there is an even simpler compiler with bounded class and exponent. Add the simple commutator relations of weight $c+1$ and the $e$-th powers of all simple commutators of weights $1,\ldots,c$. The simple commutators of weight $k$ normally generate the $k$-th lower central subgroup. This follows by induction: if their commutators with all original generators vanish in a quotient, then their normal closure is central. The new presentation therefore has class at most $c$ and each grade has exponent dividing $e$. It is finite, and its exponent divides $e^c$ by successive powering down the grades. Every homomorphism to $H$ factors through this new presentation, again preserving surjectivity. There are polynomially many constant-length new relators.

## 7. Counting hardness and the upper bounds

Let $H$ be any fixed nonabelian finite group and $\Gamma$ an $n$-generator finitely presented group. Write $h=|H|$, $b_H$ for the number of proper subgroups of $H$, and $\theta=\max\{|K|/h:K<H\}<1$. Choose $k=O_H(n+1)$ with $h^n b_H\theta^k<1/2$. For every homomorphism $f : \Gamma\to H$, extend it to the free product with the free group $F_k$ by independently assigning its $k$ free generators. A failure of surjectivity requires all these assignments to lie in a proper subgroup. At most $b_H\theta^k h^k$ assignments can fail for each $f$. Consequently
$$
 0\ \le\ \#\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,H)
       -h^{-k}\#\operatorname{Epi}(\Gamma*F_k,H)\ <\tfrac12 .
$$
Rounding recovers the homomorphism count with one oracle call. By Samperton and Weiß's homomorphism-counting dichotomy, this gives #P-hardness under Turing reductions for every fixed nonabelian finite target in the unrestricted finite-presentation model. For finite nilpotent targets, compile each resulting presentation as in Section 6 for the presentation and permutation quotient models. Section 8 handles ordinary permutation groups. Section 2 gives the stronger direct reduction for targets with all proper quotients abelian. [Samperton–Weiß, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02885v2#S1)

In the presentation model the witness for an epimorphism is its list of target images of generators, with fixed unique encodings. Evaluation checks all relators and generation. In the permutation quotient model, if the input generators of $U$ are $g_i$ and the guesses are $h_i\in H$, compute the graph candidate subgroup $J=\langle(g_i,h_i)\rangle\le U\times H$ using a fixed regular action of $H$ on a disjoint point block. Its projection onto $U$ is surjective, and it is a graph exactly if $|J|=|U|$. Then each displayed generator $a$ of $N$ must satisfy $(a,1)\in J$, and the $h_i$ must generate $H$. Each epimorphism is represented by exactly one witness. This proves NP and #P membership.

For abelian $H$, in the presentation model compute the abelianization by Smith normal form of the exponent-sum matrix. It gives the homomorphism counts to all subgroups of the fixed target, and subtraction over the subgroup lattice gives the epimorphism count. In the permutation quotient model, normal-close the commutators of generating pairs to obtain $U'$. For each fixed cyclic prime-power order $p^j$ needed for a cyclic decomposition of a subgroup of $H$, calculate
$$
 |U:(U'\,N\,U^{p^j})|.
$$
For the final factor $U^{p^j}$, it suffices to add the $p^j$-th powers of the original generators to $U'$ and $N$, since the quotient modulo $U'$ is abelian. This index equals the number of homomorphisms to $C_{p^j}$, by the finite abelian decomposition of $U/(NU')$. Multiplying for direct products gives counts to each fixed subgroup of $H$; the same lattice subtraction gives the exact number of epimorphisms. All counts have polynomial bit length. This closes the upper bounds in these two models. They apply immediately to an ordinary permutation group by setting $N=1$.

## 8. Removing the quotient from a permutation input

The following construction converts the preceding permutation quotient reductions to ordinary permutation group inputs. Let $U$ be given by permutations on a point set of size $d$, let $N\trianglelefteq U$ have given subgroup generators, and assume the exponent of $U$ is bounded by a fixed constant. Choose a fixed $k\ge5$ divisible by that exponent and with $k!/2>|H|$. For the reductions here, the exponent bound is the target-dependent constant $e$ from Section 6, so any sufficiently large multiple of $e$ works uniformly for every input. The $A_k$ generators and their actions on the copy indices are likewise fixed. The construction works for any fixed finite $H$ given suitable $U,N$; the hardness application to ordinary permutation inputs uses the bounded-exponent ambient groups supplied for nilpotent targets. On $k$ disjoint copies of the point set, use the wreath product $U^k\rtimes A_k$, where $A_k$ permutes the copies. In its base group write $\operatorname{diag}(u)=(u,\ldots,u)$ and $d_{ij}(a)$ for the tuple with entries $a,a^{-1}$ in positions $i,j$ and identity elsewhere. Generate a permutation group $K$ by the diagonal copies of the generators of $U$, by $A_k$, and by $d_{12}(a)$ for every given generator $a$ of $N$. This uses polynomial-size permutation generators. Every $A_k$ orbit in the copy index has size at most $k$, so every resulting orbit has size at most $k$ times the maximum orbit size of $U$.

Every homomorphism $f:K\to H$ kills the chosen copy of $A_k$, by simplicity and the order inequality. Since $A_k$ is two-transitive, the images of $d_{ij}(a)$ equal an element $b_a$ independent of the ordered pair of distinct indices. On three distinct indices, $d_{ij}(a)d_{j\ell}(a)=d_{i\ell}(a)$, hence $b_a^2=b_a$ and $b_a=1$. Also
$$
 \operatorname{diag}(a)=\prod_{j=2}^{k}d_{j1}(a),
$$
because the entry in coordinate one is $a^{-(k-1)}=a$. The restriction to diagonal $U$ therefore kills $N$. Conversely, all base coordinates of each element of $K$ have the same coset in $U/N$, because this holds for the generators and is preserved under coordinate permutations and multiplication. This defines a homomorphism $K\twoheadrightarrow U/N$ taking that coset as its value and killing $A_k$. Every $f$ factors through it, by comparing values on the three sets of generators. The factorization is unique and preserves the image. Thus $U/N$ and the explicit permutation group $K$ have exactly the same epimorphism count to $H$.

For the **solvable-source and bounded-orbit strengthening**, first handle a fixed $p$-group target $T$. The construction in Section 6 makes $U$ a $p$-group, by taking $e=\exp T$ and using the fact that the successive lower central factors of $U$ have exponent dividing $e$. Take $k=p^a>2$ divisible by $e$, chosen once from the fixed target, and replace $A_k$ throughout by the affine group $A=\operatorname{AGL}_1(\mathbb F_k)$ acting on the $k$ copies. It is two-transitive. Every homomorphism $A\to T$ kills the scalar subgroup of order $k-1$. For a nonidentity scalar $s$, its commutators with translations run through all translations, since multiplication by $s-1$ is invertible. Thus every homomorphism to $T$ kills the translations too. The preceding proof applies verbatim with this reason for killing $A$. The resulting group $K$ is an extension of a subgroup of the fixed-class, fixed-exponent $p$-group $U^k$ by the metabelian, constant-size group $A$. This gives fixed bounds on derived length and exponent, and on every orbit.

Finally let $H=T\times R$, with $T$ a fixed nonabelian Sylow $p$-subgroup and $R$ the product of the other Sylow subgroups. For reductions from instances for $T$, construct the preceding ordinary permutation group $K$, then represent $K\times R$ on disjoint point sets by adding a fixed regular action of $R$. Every homomorphism to $T$ kills the source factor $R$, and every homomorphism to the target $R$ kills $K\cap U^k$ and hence factors through the fixed quotient $A\times R$. A map to $T\times R$ is onto if its projections to both factors are onto. For the converse, the order of the image is divisible by both of the coprime orders $|T|$ and $|R|$, which forces it to be the full product. Accordingly,
$$
 \#\operatorname{Epi}(K\times R,T\times R)
 =
 \#\operatorname{Epi}(K,T)\,\#\operatorname{Epi}(A\times R,R).
$$
The second factor is a positive constant because the projection $A\times R\to R$ is onto. This transfers both hardness bounds with solvable ordinary permutation sources for all fixed nonabelian finite nilpotent targets. For a $p$-group with all proper quotients abelian, the affine construction preserves the stronger single-call count exactly.
