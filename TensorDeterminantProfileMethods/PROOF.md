# Special-linear tensor isomorphism after the November literature review

The four results in this folder are informal proofs over fixed finite fields. The unrestricted containment in Open Question 1.9 remains open in this work. The question asks whether special-linear tensor isomorphism reduces to ordinary tensor isomorphism, and whether the classes are equal. Here the strongest target attempted is one deterministic, polynomial-size, same-field ordinary 3-TI pair on input of any two tensors. This is a many-one reduction on the input pair. A kernel reduction would additionally construct each output independently from its corresponding input. The published paper uses kernel reductions as its usual model. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Part III](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

Ordinary TI reduces to SL-TI by adding one zero coordinate in each mode. Extend a transporter in each mode by its inverse determinant on the new coordinate. For the converse, every padded transporter restricts to an isomorphism between the intrinsic tensor support spaces. These restrictions extend to the original ambient spaces. This proof covers nonconcise inputs over any field. MathProofs already contains a Lean proof of this direction with zero-padding to twice each dimension. [Existing Lean proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/JouxNarayanan54.lean) It also gives the worst-case reduction asked in [Joux and Narayanan, Question 9](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.62). Their hyperdeterminant theorem has a different purpose, distinguishing random and correlated orbit pairs under its hypotheses. Padding introduces singular covectors and cannot transfer that guarantee unchanged. Over the two-element field the two GL and SL actions coincide.

## Results from the four approaches

- [Unordered determinant factors](UnorderedPencilDeterminantProfiles.md) computes the complete determinant profile of raw pencils from symmetric operator aggregates. The sums discard individual geometric factor matches and normalizing scalars. A small simultaneous commutant pays for all label-map possibilities. The paper includes a growing family over the field of three elements with scalar stabilizer and scalar pair adjoints, where any constant number of the individual determinant factors leaves a large commutant.
- [Split determinant divisors](SplitPencilDeterminantTests.md) uses local Frobenius frame families to synchronize determinant lines. This gives one ordinary TI pair when factor orbits can be paired by intrinsic tags and their common kernel is at most logarithmic. Arbitrary orbit matchings admit an adaptive polynomial-time TI reduction under the same kernel bound. The one-instance construction avoids enumerating all simultaneous Frobenius shifts even when each individual field has growing degree.
- [Semisimple derivation envelopes](SemisimpleDerivationDeterminantProfiles.md) computes actual transporter witnesses for each determinant triple by exploring a normalizer orbit on a smaller space of multilinear maps. It is polynomial in the actual orbit size and in the input length. This extends the computational use of one-dimensional densors to a checkable logarithmic-dimensional class and to small orbits in larger spaces under the envelope conditions.
- [Ordered bilinear form tuples](FormTupleDeterminantTests.md) compares an upper bound from independently preserving the forms with a subgroup of actual tensor automorphisms. Both determinant images are computed by radical reduction and multiplicities in simple modules. If the images agree, one ordinary TI pair detects the determinant-constrained transporter, even with large relative isometry groups. A polynomial-size covering list of successful branches can be packed by the positive disjunction construction.

[The factor recovery appendix](GeometricPencilFactorRecovery.md) gives the deterministic bounded-degree construction used by the first two proofs. It avoids a common splitting field and gives the explicit raw factors in separate polynomial-degree fields.

## Exact interface with the triangular construction

The [truncated-corner construction](TruncatedCornerCharacters.md) makes the determinant of an algebra isomorphism a prescribed weighted product of the determinants of the three residue maps. The residue bimap is arbitrary. This explains why computing the radical, normalizing the primitive idempotents and setting all corner lengths in advance leave the central difficulty intact. Once a residue pair belongs to one of the exact-profile classes, its table determinant test follows by evaluating that weighted product. A class that reads only the label determinant and the matrix determinant ratio has the character scope stated in its own theorem.

One polynomial-size ordinary TI pair that recognizes the determinant-one isomorphisms for every such exact triangular table would solve the fixed-finite-field direction using the existing weighted-character normalization. The four methods give no coverage theorem for general residue tables, including tensors whose derivation envelope is scalar, whose pencil has no suitable low-degree factors or frames, and for which no suitable form tuples have been recovered. The search did not identify a theorem that supplies the missing coverage. All scope and priority claims here are limited to the checked sources and informal arguments above.
