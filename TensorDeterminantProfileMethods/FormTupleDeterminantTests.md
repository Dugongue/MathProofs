# One-instance determinant tests from tuples of bilinear forms

Informal proof. Fix a finite field \(F=\mathbb F_q\) of odd order. Let \(T,S\) be explicit three-mode tensors over \(F\). For each tensor mode suppose we are given an ordered tuple of bilinear forms, with no rank assumption on any form. The algorithm below reduces a determinant-constrained transporter problem to one ordinary TI instance under a polynomially checkable subgroup-equality condition. It works for prescribed triples in \((F^\times)^3\) and for any prescribed character value. It also applies to a polynomial list of such tuples that covers the requested transporters. In particular, the dimensions of the isometry groups of the forms may grow arbitrarily.

## 1. Determinants of algebra unitary groups

Let \(A\) be an explicit finite-dimensional unital \(F\)-algebra with an \(F\)-linear involutive anti-automorphism \(*\), and let \(V_1,\ldots,V_s\) be explicit left \(A\)-modules, with \(s\) fixed. We compute all vectors
\[
 \bigl(\det_F(u|_{V_1}),\ldots,\det_F(u|_{V_s})\bigr),
 \qquad u^*u=1.
\]
Use the deterministic finite-characteristic version of the constructive radical and Wedderburn decomposition algorithm of [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495). Write \(J=\operatorname{rad}(A)\) and \(A/J=\prod_i M_{d_i}(K_i)\), where each \(K_i/F\) is a finite extension. The radical is stable under \(*\). Let \(m_{j,i}\) be the total multiplicity of the natural simple module of block \(i\) in the grades \(J^a V_j/J^{a+1}V_j\). Compute it by taking the image dimensions of the corresponding central idempotent in each grade and dividing their sum by \(d_i[K_i:F]\). These grades and actions use only products and linear algebra on the given bases. For a unit \(u\) with residue components \(\bar u_i\),
\[
 \det_F(u|_{V_j})
   =\prod_i N_{K_i/F}(\det_{K_i}\bar u_i)^{m_{j,i}}.
\]
Indeed, the block acts on each copy of its simple module by its defining representation, and restriction of scalars takes its determinant to the field norm. Multiplication of determinants along the radical filtration gives the expression.

Every unitary residue lifts to a unitary element. Lift it first to a unit \(v\). Set \(z=v^*v=1+r\), with \(r\in J\). If \(p=\operatorname{char}F\), then \(z^{p^b}=1\) for a power \(p^b\) at least the nilpotence index of \(J\). Choose an integer \(t\) with \(2t\equiv-1\bmod p^b\). The lift \(vz^t\) is unitary, since \(z^*=z\) and \(z^{2t+1}=1\). Its residue is still the prescribed residue.

Consequently the determinant image is generated independently by the following contributions from the orbits of \(*\) on the blocks.

- For a pair of exchanged blocks \(i,i'\), \(\bar u_i\) varies over its full general linear group and \(\bar u_{i'}\) is its inverse under the anti-isomorphism. The norms of their determinants are inverse. The contribution consists of the vectors with coordinate \(a^{m_{j,i}-m_{j,i'}}\) as \(a\) ranges over \(F^\times\); the field norm is surjective. The anti-isomorphism preserves the norm since it acts by an \(F\)-isomorphism on the centers and by transpose and conjugation after identifying the full matrix blocks.
- On a fixed block whose center involution is nontrivial, the determinant over \(K_i\) has norm one to the fixed field. Its norm to \(F\) is therefore one.
- On a fixed symplectic block the determinant over \(K_i\) is one, for instance by the Pfaffian identity for a preserved nondegenerate alternating form.
- On a fixed orthogonal block the determinant over \(K_i\) can be either sign. A reflection in a vector of nonzero quadratic value attains minus one. Its contribution is generated by the vector with coordinate \((-1)^{[K_i:F]m_{j,i}}\).

The types are computable from the involution on the quotient. First test its permutation of the central idempotents, then its action on the center of each fixed block. For a first-kind involution on \(M_d(K)\), the dimension over \(K\) of the self-adjoint space is \(d(d+1)/2\) in the orthogonal case and \(d(d-1)/2\) in the symplectic case. Indeed the involution is adjoint to an invertible symmetric or alternating matrix, and multiplication by the Gram matrix transports the self-adjoint subspace to symmetric or alternating matrices, respectively. This uses the ordinary semisimple \(*\)-algebra classification. The determinant target group has constant size when \(s,q\) are fixed, so all subgroup products are polynomial time. This computes determinant images without finding generators of the potentially large unitary group.

## 2. Relaxation and certified corrections

The forms on each mode may be split into their symmetric and alternating parts, since the characteristic is odd. This preserves their common isometries and transporters. Take all forms below to have one of those two symmetry types. If a pair of corresponding forms changes type under this split, keep the symmetric parts together and the alternating parts together, including any zero components.

Use constructive simultaneous isometry of ordered form tuples to find a reference map \(r_i\) from the source decorations to the target decorations on mode \(i\), or reject that decorated branch. This is deterministic polynomial time at fixed odd \(q\) by [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 7 and Corollary 9](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem7). It returns an isometry even for tuples with a common radical. No tensor transporter is used to find \(r_i\). Write \(\delta_0=(\det r_1,\det r_2,\det r_3)\).

For each mode let
\[
 E_i=\{(a,b)\in\operatorname{End}(V_i)\times\operatorname{End}(V_i)^{\rm op}:
               B(av,w)=B(v,bw)\text{ for all marked }B,v,w\}.
\]
It is a unital algebra computed by linear equations, with involution \((a,b)^*=(b,a)\). Each symmetry type of \(B\) permits this interchange. A unitary pair has \(b=a^{-1}\) and acts on \(V_i\) by \(a\). Conversely every isometry gives such a unitary pair. Part 1 computes the determinant images \(U_i\) of these three groups. All decorated transporters of \(T\) to \(S\) have determinant in \(\delta_0 U\), where \(U=U_1\times U_2\times U_3\), by comparing with \(r_i\) in each mode separately.

Now compute determinants of genuine decorated autotopisms fixing one mode. Fix mode \(k\), and view the source as the tuple \(M(v)\) of linear maps \(V_i^*\to V_j\) obtained by contracting \(v\in V_k^*\). In \(E_i\times E_j\) take the subalgebra \(D\) cut out by
\[
 a_j M(v)=M(v)b_i^{\mathsf T}\qquad(v\in V_k^*).
\]
This is linear in the pair coordinates. It is closed under products since the first projection and the transpose of the second projection are algebra homomorphisms. Take \(D\cap D^*\), a unital \(*\)-algebra. Its unitary elements are exactly the adjoint-pair representations of decorated tensor autotopisms that act by identity on \(V_k\). Indeed the displayed equation with \(b_i=a_i^{-1}\) is exactly the tensor equation; an invertible pair satisfying it also satisfies it upon inversion, so its adjoint lies in \(D\). The units from \(D\cap D^*\) satisfy the same equation by construction. Apply Part 1 using the two representations on the original \(V_i,V_j\). Insert determinant one in the fixed mode.

Let \(H\) be generated by the resulting triple subgroups for all three fixed modes. One may additionally include any explicitly exhibited decorated autotopisms after checking their tensor and form equations and determinants. By construction every vector in \(H\) is realized by a genuine decorated autotopism. The inclusion \(H\le U\) follows from the decoration constraints.

## 3. Reduction and scope

Suppose \(H=U\). If any decorated transporter exists, its determinant triple is some \(\delta\in\delta_0U\). Composing it with genuine source autotopisms realizes all of \(\delta H=\delta_0U\). Thus one can decide membership of the desired determinant triple in \(\delta_0U\) before making any TI query. If it fails, emit a fixed negative pair. If it passes, emit the ordinary TI encoding of the tensor and all the ordered form tuples. A character condition is handled by applying the character to the two subgroups and to \(\delta_0\), so equality of their images suffices. An ordinary no on the emitted encoding rejects the input regardless of the preliminary coset computation.

A form is a bilinear map to a shared copy of \(F\) with its scalar multiplication. A linear algebra automorphism of this copy fixes \(1\), hence acts identically. The original tensor is a third-order data component. The [tensor-system reduction of Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2) applies to a fixed signature. Pack a growing list of ordered form occurrences by direct sums of copies, projection by an indexed idempotent algebra and equality maps to the original sorts. To force the indices fixed, include their cyclic shift and a first-index mark. The independent actions on occurrence copies are then exactly the original mode actions. This keeps the signature fixed and adds polynomially many coordinates.

Each form branch is thus a single ordinary TI instance. For a polynomial family of branches, skip any that fails the subgroup equality or target-value test. Assume every desired witness, if there is one, appears in some retained subgroup-equality branch before the target-value test. Take a balanced OR of the resulting ordinary TI tests. To compile an OR of two equality-of-isomorphism-type tests \(a=b\) and \(c=d\), compare the multisets of ordered pairs \(\{(a,c),(b,d)\}\) and \(\{(a,d),(b,c)\}\). Their possible straight and crossed matchings give exactly the OR. Expand the balanced tree of such terms, making at most a polynomial number of copies. Encode the rooted tree with an algebra of node idempotents and incidence maps, using fixed colors to distinguish ordered children and free permutations for unordered children. Attach the leaf tensor spaces using each leaf idempotent as projection onto its component. Thus the completed structure matches precisely when a term matches, and one application of the tensor-system reduction outputs one ordinary TI pair.

For example, on \(V=W\oplus W\) preserve the dot-product Gram matrix and its products with the first-block projection and block swap. Commuting with the projection makes an isometry block diagonal, and commuting with the swap makes the two blocks equal. The resulting group \(g\oplus g\), for orthogonal \(g\) on \(W\), has determinant one even as \(\dim W\) grows. Three such decorations give \(U=H=1\) for any tensor satisfying the branch conditions. Covariantly found ordered forms, including tuples built from spectral moments of permutable low-degree pencil marks, can play the role of these decorations. Splitting such moments into symmetric and alternating parts when needed preserves their ordered equations.

The reduction also applies to an exact triangular table insofar as its residue transporter is forced into one of the qualifying branches and the residue character is evaluated on that branch. Arbitrary triangular tables allow arbitrary residue tensors. This argument supplies no universal form-extraction or subgroup-equality theorem for those tensors. No Lean verification or priority claim is made for this note.
