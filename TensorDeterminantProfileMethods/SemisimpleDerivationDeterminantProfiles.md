# Exact determinant profiles from semisimple derivation envelopes

Informal proof. Fix a finite field \(F=\mathbb F_q\). Given a bilinear map \(T:X_1\times X_2\to X_3\), use the action
\[
(g\cdot T)(x,y)=g_3T(g_1^{-1}x,g_2^{-1}y).
\]
This is the corner-map convention for the truncated triangular algebras. For a standard covariant tensor, replace the first two linear maps by their inverse transposes and invert their determinant coordinates.

## 1. Statement

From a dense tensor, compute its derivation algebra
\[
D_T=\{x=(x_1,x_2,x_3): x_3T(u,v)=T(x_1u,v)+T(u,x_2v)\}
\]
inside \(P=\prod_i\mathrm{End}_F(X_i)\), with componentwise Lie commutator. Let \(R_T\subseteq P\) be the unital associative subalgebra generated by the vector space \([D_T,D_T]\). Here and below brackets between algebras denote the linear span of brackets. Consider the polynomially checkable conditions
\[
R_T\text{ is semisimple},\qquad [R_T,R_T]\subseteq D_T.
\]
For such \(T\), let \(W_T\) be the space of all bilinear maps annihilated by \([R_T,R_T]\) under the displayed derivation action. Put \(r=\dim_F W_T\). A tensor is concise here when its two input kernels are zero and its image spans the output.

**Theorem.** For concise inputs satisfying these conditions, one can compute the complete set of determinant triples of tensor transporters, and return one explicit transporter for each realized triple, deterministically in time \(L^{O(1)}q^{O(r)}\), where \(L\) is total dense input length. In fact the algorithm is polynomial in \(L\) and the size of the ordinary normalizer orbit used in the proof. In particular \(r=O(\log L)\) gives polynomial-time SL-TI and every weighted determinant-character test on this class. This is a same-field many-one reduction to ordinary TI by returning a fixed YES or NO instance according to the algorithm. No exterior chain is needed.

This refines the general derivation-densor method of Brooksbank, Maglione and Wilson. Their Theorems 1.4 and 6.1 give an ordinary transporter and stabilizer generators when the hypotheses there hold and the densor is one-dimensional. Those two results already imply an exact determinant profile in that one-dimensional setting over fixed finite fields: multiply the transporter's determinant triple by the subgroup generated by the determinant triples of the stabilizer generators. The proof below instead computes a joint orbit in a possibly larger densor for a checkable associative-envelope class. [Brooksbank–Maglione–Wilson, *Tensor isomorphism by conjugacy of Lie algebras*](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.04046)

## 2. Constructive normalization of the envelope

Derivations, commutator spans, and simultaneous annihilator equations are linear algebra. Form \(R_T\) by starting with a basis of \([D_T,D_T]+F1\) and adjoining independent matrix products until closure. There are at most \(\sum_i(\dim X_i)^2\) additions. A tensor transporter \(g:T\to S\) sends \(D_T\) to \(D_S\) by componentwise conjugation. It consequently sends \(R_T\) to \(R_S\) and \(W_T\) to \(W_S\). A failed structural test on one side and a successful one on the other, or a mismatch of dimensions, rejects a genuine transporter automatically. For arbitrary pairs outside the stated class the algorithm may report that the class test failed.

Use constructive finite-algebra decomposition to find the radical and identify each semisimple \(R\) with
\[
R=\prod_j M_{d_j}(E_j),\qquad e_j=[E_j:F].
\]
The required maps in both directions are supplied by the finite-field result cited in Theorem 18 of Ivanyos–Qiao. Its deterministic time is polynomial in ambient matrix dimension, \(\log q\), and the characteristic. The product \(P\) of endomorphism rings embeds faithfully as block-diagonal matrices, so all its hypotheses hold. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03495#page=18)

For each of the three modes, recover its standard \(R\)-module multiplicities \(m_{ij}\). If \(e^{(j)}_{ab}\) denotes a matrix unit of simple factor \(j\), find an \(E_j\)-basis of \(e^{(j)}_{11}X_i\). A greedy basis of this \(E_j\)-space is computed by testing \(F\)-linear spans of the known scalar basis times the chosen vectors. Its images under \(e^{(j)}_{a1}\) give the module coordinates, by the matrix unit relations. The type of \(j\) is \((d_j,e_j,m_{1j},m_{2j},m_{3j})\).

Fix an \(F\)-field model for each occurring degree and transport these coordinates into it. Deterministic algorithms for finite field isomorphism suffice. Alternatively, enumerating a center of degree \(e\) and finding a root of the other model's generating polynomial costs polynomial in \(q^e\) and in \(L\). The bound on \(q^e\) needed for this alternative is proved below. [Lenstra, *Finding isomorphisms between finite fields*](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

Compare the multisets of types for \(T\) and \(S\). A discrepancy rules out a transporter. Otherwise pair the factors by type and define \(h_i:X_i\to Y_i\) by those coordinate identifications, so \(hR_Th^{-1}=R_S\). Set \(T_0=h\cdot T\). Then \(T_0\in W_S\). Every transporter \(g:T\to S\) factors as \(nh\) with \(n\) in the mode-preserving normalizer \(N\) of \(R_S\). Conversely if \(n\in N\) carries \(T_0\) to \(S\), then \(nh\) is an original transporter. Thus it remains to compute a joint orbit of \(T_0\) under an explicitly describable group.

## 3. Generate the full modewise normalizer

The kernel of \(N\to\operatorname{Aut}_F(R_S)\) is its pointwise centralizer. On the \(j\)-isotypic component of mode \(i\), it is no larger or smaller than \(\mathrm{GL}_{m_{ij}}(E_j)\) on the multiplicity coordinates. Indeed commuting with matrix units makes all natural-coordinate blocks agree and commuting with the center makes the common block \(E_j\)-linear. All these centralizer factors act independently.

The image on \(R_S\) consists exactly of arbitrary permutations within each identical type, together with independent inner automorphisms of its simple factors and field automorphisms of their centers. Every algebra automorphism must permute the primitive central idempotents. Types record both the intrinsic simple-algebra parameters and their multiplicities in each representation, so any normalized map preserves these types. Conversely the coordinated permutation in all modes realizes every type-preserving transposition. On one factor, compose an algebra automorphism with the inverse of its scalar automorphism acting entrywise. The resulting \(E_j\)-algebra automorphism of \(M_{d_j}(E_j)\) is inner. This follows directly from the matrix-unit description: their images give a second natural basis, whose change of basis conjugates them, and \(E_j\)-linearity determines the action on the whole ring. In module coordinates, the same natural-coordinate conjugator on every incident mode realizes that inner automorphism. Entrywise Frobenius realizes the chosen generator of the field automorphism group, and does so simultaneously on the incident modes.

Thus one constructs all required generators by taking centralizer factors, natural-coordinate inner factors, one \(q\)-Frobenius per center, and adjacent factor exchanges. For a \(\mathrm{GL}_k(E)\) factor, transvections and diagonal scalings by all nonzero \(E\)-elements suffice by Gaussian elimination. A row swap itself is a product of transvections and a diagonal sign. All resulting maps are explicit and invertible over \(F\). Any normalizer map differs from a product of the chosen lifts of algebra automorphisms by a centralizer element, proving that the list is complete.

## 4. Search exactly the densor orbit and all its determinant labels

The normalizer preserves \([R_S,R_S]\) and therefore \(W_S\). Let
\(\Delta(g)=(\det_F g_1,\det_F g_2,\det_F g_3)\).
Use the list of generators and inverses to perform breadth-first search on reachable states
\[
(U,c)\in W_S\times(F^\times)^3,\qquad
(U,c)\longmapsto (k\cdot U,\Delta(k)c).
\]
Start at \((T_0,\Delta(h))\). Exactly the states \((S,c)\) in this search have \(c\) in the determinant profile of transporters for the input pair. This follows by the factorization \(g=nh\) and the fact that the edges give precisely all words in the generators. Store a parent edge at each visited state to recover an actual matrix tuple for every accepted label. A determinant label is represented by three field elements, so the sign condition and any specified character condition can be evaluated directly.

It remains to bound the generator list and the state count. Write \(f_j\) for the central idempotent on simple factor \(j\), and choose a mode \(i\) where \(f_j X_i\ne0\). Such a mode exists by faithfulness of the realization in \(P\). Acting by \(f_j\) in that single mode defines a linear projection on tensor space, by precomposition in inputs and postcomposition at output. This projection preserves \(W\), since \(f_j\) commutes with the mode components of every element of \([R,R]\). Conciseness guarantees that the projection of the given tensor is nonzero: an input has zero kernel, and output images span the entire output. The projection image in \(W\) is a nonzero \(E_j\)-vector space under just that mode's central action. It follows that \(e_j\le r\). There are at most \(q^r(q-1)^3\) augmented states and \(L^{O(1)}q^r\) individual generator operations to precompute. All individual field or matrix operations have polynomial-size labellings, giving the stated time.

For the stronger orbit-sensitive estimate, let \(\Omega=N\cdot T_0\). For each \(j\) act by \(\lambda\in E_j^\times\) on its isotypic component in only a mode where this component is nonzero. This is a member of the centralizer. Distinct \(\lambda\) give distinct tensors: after projecting onto that component, their difference acts by an invertible field scalar on the nonzero projected tensor. For input precomposition the action uses \(\lambda^{-1}\), still distinct. Hence \(|E_j|-1\le|\Omega|\). This pays for all field element and generator enumeration using the actual ordinary orbit, and the augmented BFS visits at most \((q-1)^3|\Omega|\) states. Concentration of a tensor orbit can therefore suffice even if the full densor dimension is large.

## 5. An intrinsic multiplicity bound in every characteristic

The ordinary orbit size can be bounded independently of the matrix dimensions \(d_j\) of the simple factors. For a natural-coordinate transvection set \(X=\lambda e^{(j)}_{ab}\), \(a\ne b\), and \(H=e^{(j)}_{aa}-e^{(j)}_{bb}\). Both belong to \([R,R]\), since \([e^{(j)}_{aa},X]=X\) and \([e^{(j)}_{ab},e^{(j)}_{ba}]=H\). Componentwise \(X^2=0\) and \(HX=X\).

For \(U\in W\) put \(Y=U(X_1x,X_2y)\). The derivation identity for \(X\) at \((x,X_2y)\) gives \(Y=X_3U(x,X_2y)\), hence \(H_3Y=Y\) by \(H_3X_3=X_3\). The derivation identity for \(H\) at \((X_1x,X_2y)\) gives \(H_3Y=2Y\), using \(H_iX_i=X_i\) for the two inputs. Thus \(Y=0\) over every characteristic. The derivation identities for \(X\) also give \(X_3 U(X_1x,y)=X_3U(x,X_2y)=0\). Expanding now gives
\[
(1+X_3)U((1-X_1)x,(1-X_2)y)=U(x,y).
\]
All determinants of such a transvection equal one over \(F\), since the mode perturbations square to zero. The natural-coordinate image of \(\mathrm{SL}_{d_j}(E_j)\), generated by transvections, therefore acts trivially on the full augmented space. Natural-coordinate inner lifts contribute at most \(|E_j^\times|\) possible actions. Let \(b_\tau\) be the number of factors of each type \(\tau\). Bounding centralizers by \(\lvert\mathrm{GL}_m(E)\rvert\le |E|^{m^2}\) shows that the size of every augmented orbit is at most
\[
\left(\prod_j e_j q^{\,e_j(1+\sum_i m_{ij}^2)}\right)
\left(\prod_\tau b_\tau!\right).
\]
Every normalizer element is a product of centralizer, natural and field lifts, and a type-preserving permutation. Reduce the natural lifts modulo their trivial special-linear action to obtain the count. This gives a deterministic polynomial-time sufficient condition over every fixed finite field when the displayed product is polynomial in \(L\), with unrestricted natural dimensions.

## 6. Full-width examples, removed radicals, and the Step 17 interface

The envelope and densor conditions are attainable with all mode dimensions unbounded. For \(n\ge2\), set
\[
X_1=F^n\oplus F,\quad X_2=F\oplus F^n,\quad X_3=F^n\oplus F^n,
\qquad T((u,a),(b,v))=(bu,av).
\]
Test the derivation equation first on \((u,0),(b,0)\), and then on \((0,a),(0,v)\). These tests force the output derivation to preserve its two blocks with internal maps \(A+\beta I\), \(B+\alpha I\), where \(A,B\) are the two large input blocks and \(\alpha,\beta\) the two input scalar actions. Testing on \((u,0),(0,v)\) forces both large-to-small input off-diagonal blocks to vanish, and testing on \((0,a),(b,0)\) forces both small-to-large ones to vanish. Conversely these formulas always give a derivation. Thus the derived Lie algebra applies independent \(\mathfrak{sl}_n(F)\) actions to the two pairs of large blocks and vanishes on the scalar inputs. Its unital associative envelope is \(M_n(F)\times M_n(F)\times F\): the elementary matrices in each \(\mathfrak{sl}_n\) generate each full matrix factor and the identity supplies the common scalar factor on the scalar inputs. The commutator span is the same simultaneous derived action. Invariants in the bimap space are just the two displayed channel maps with independent coefficients. To check this, each large output summand can receive a matching large input only paired with the scalar input on the opposite side. Involving the other independent factor would impose an invariant vector or covector in its natural module, which has none because all elementary off-diagonal matrices act. The allowed channel endomorphisms commute with the full matrix algebra generated by the elementary matrices, and hence are scalars. Thus \(r=2\). As a finite control, direct rank computation gives \(\dim D_T=2n^2+2\) and \(r=2\) in all 12 cases with \(n\in\{2,3,4\}\) and \(q\in\{2,3,5,7\}\).

A raw degenerate tensor can first be replaced by its concise core: quotient the two input kernels and shrink output to the span of values. In Gaussian-elimination adapted bases, the raw determinant triple of any lift of a core transporter is the core triple times the determinants of maps on the discarded spaces, with explicit source-target basis calibration. Every prescribed nonzero determinant on a nonzero discarded space is attained by a diagonal invertible map. Block-diagonal extension in adapted bases satisfies the tensor equation. Conversely restriction and quotient of any raw transporter give exactly these data. Therefore the same algorithm applies if the two concise cores satisfy the class and the same size bound. For the zero bimap the profile is immediately the unrestricted determinant profile on each equal-dimensional mode.

For the explicitly constructed Step 17 triangular tables in their construction bases, apply the preceding algorithm to the three corner quotients, then apply the [truncated-corner determinant formula](TruncatedCornerCharacters.md). It gives the exact algebra transporter determinant profile. A determinant-one algebra map lifts to an SL isotopy of multiplication tensors by taking the two unit multipliers equal to one. Conversely the product of the three covariant determinants of any isotopy is the inverse determinant of its underlying algebra map, since the two unit-multiplication contributions cancel.

The same decision applies to arbitrary coordinate presentations **promised to be isomorphic to unpadded normalized tables of this construction**. It needs no supplied normalized bases. Compute \(J\) and the three ordered primitive idempotents of \(A/J=F^3\), then choose orthogonal idempotent lifts. Explicitly a lift \(a\) of a quotient idempotent satisfies \(a^2-a\in J\). With \(J^N=0\), polynomial Chinese remaindering modulo \(X^N\) and \((X-1)^N\) gives \(p(X)\) with residues zero and one, respectively. Hence \(p(a)\) is an idempotent lifting the same element. Apply this on successively complementary corners to lift the full family. Order the three vertices by the directed support of the nonzero off-diagonal corners, which is the total order of the construction.

Choose polynomial-time compatible parameters \(t_p\) in the diagonal radicals. The equations asking that left multiplication by \(t_p\) and right multiplication by \(t_{p'}\) agree on all basis vectors of the \(pp'\) corner are linear in the three parameters. Each \(t_p\) must be nonzero in its diagonal \(J_p/J_p^2\), a one-dimensional space. Enumerate its three nonzero scalar residues over fixed \(F\) and solve the affine system. The promise gives a solution. Powers of each \(t_p\) give the diagonal basis, and lifting an arbitrary basis of each corner modulo \(t_p\) and multiplying by its successive powers gives an adapted basis. This follows from freeness of that corner over \(F[t]/t^{k_i}\) and the nonzero residue of the chosen parameter. Reduction of the corner product modulo \(t\) gives the explicit core tensor. Any actual algebra isomorphism, after a determinant-one radical-inner alignment, is filtered in these bases. Its calibrated determinant is the product of the mode determinant powers and the common diagonal-parameter factor, and normalization cancels the latter.

Every core transporter extends existentially in this arbitrary-coordinate case. Indeed choose any hidden isomorphisms to the promised standardized source and target tables, transport the core maps through their induced quotient maps, extend coefficientwise there, then conjugate back. The algorithm only needs the exact determinant, which the preceding adapted-basis formula computes for every extension. Multiplying by the ratio of the two known change-of-basis determinants translates it into the raw table bases. Thus the decision test does not require solving for the hidden standardized coordinates. This is a sufficient raw Step 17 subclass.

## 7. Literature cross-check and the universal gap

The source exact query for \(\texttt{densor}\) through the federated MathProof DAG found the historical rank-one benchmark and its source copies. The displayed historical check concerns the numerical benchmark \(\texttt{densorDimensionForPolyTime = 1}\). The finite construction above uses the actual published normalizer orbit method, semisimple-algebra decomposition, and the truncated-corner determinant formula. Mathlib's \(\texttt{IsSemisimpleRing.exists_algEquiv_pi_matrix_divisionRing_finite}\) and the Atlas tensor-category import provide abstract decomposition infrastructure. The algorithmic decomposition and transport are supplied by the finite-field theorem cited in Section 2. [Mathlib Wedderburn–Artin source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/RingTheory/SimpleModule/WedderburnArtin.lean)

The standard trivial extension \(A\ltimes A^*\) gives a symmetric Frobenius algebra and a natural lift of any isomorphism \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha\oplus\alpha^{-*}\). The two diagonal determinants are inverse, so the determinant of this lift is always one. More generally an isomorphism preserving the dual ideal and the canonical trace pairing has determinant one by the same calculation on the ideal and quotient. Recovering the orientation via a trivial extension would need additional input-dependent data that couple to the original quotient action. The classification of trivial extensions by quivers and relations gives constructive access to quiver/relations and explains why the plain extension may also identify distinct initial algebras. [Saorín, *Automorphism groups of trivial extensions*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-4049(01)00031-7), [Fernández–Schroll–Treffinger–Trepode–Valdivieso, *Characterisations of trivial extensions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04581)

The recent paper on TI over commutative rings makes the distinctions among free and nonfree modules precise and extends tensor gadgets across these models. Its principal ideal result concerns a specific integral tensor format. Hexagon's Cartan determinant paper concerns determinants of composition-multiplicity matrices. In the truncated triangular construction these integer composition data describe dimensions and multiplicities, whereas the target is the determinant of a particular \(F\)-linear algebra transporter. The two correspondences give useful normal forms but supply no character readout here. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1), [Tang, *Cartan determinants for almost laura, quasi-laura, semilinear clannish and piecewise Bäckström algebras*](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00062v1)

The semisimple-envelope class still has structural hypotheses and an orbit-size condition. If \([D_T,D_T]=0\), for instance, \(R_T=F1\) and the densor is the full tensor space. Small orbit or multiplicity is then unavailable in general. The universal Step 17 polynomial-size ordinary-TI construction remains open. [Published Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)
