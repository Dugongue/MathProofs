# A GI reduction from polynomially many local frame choices

Use the explicit fixed-field, fixed-signature Transportensor conventions of [the algebra note](Unordered.md). Sum the edge maps of each threshold into a block matrix on the direct sum of all vertices' spaces in the same mode. A block from v to w is the sum over edges of that threshold from v to w. Include any named unary endomorphisms as block-diagonal generators. For a pair of inputs use these generators simultaneously, by direct sum across the two inputs, putting zero in any absent threshold generator. The identity is also a generator. Starting from the identity, keep a basis of independent matrix words, extending each retained word once by each generator. Dependent words are in the span and their extensions are accounted for by linearity. The dimension is bounded by the square of the total dimension. Thus the resulting list of words and their evaluations is polynomial even on networks with many closed walks. The diagonal block of each word at v is a sum of closed-walk operators and local unary operations. A global isomorphism intertwines *every* block at v with the corresponding block at its image. This does not require the individual closed walks to have been matched first.

**Theorem.** Suppose the linear commutant of this list of blocks at every vertex-mode has at most N^c elements for one fixed c. Then isomorphism with unknown graph correspondence reduces to one GI instance in polynomial time. There is no constant bound on a mode dimension or on the number of allowed permutations of vertices. All ordinary edge maps may be singular.

**Proof.** Classify the ordered tuples of local block matrices up to simultaneous similarity across both inputs. Compute their linear intertwiner spaces by elimination. If two tuples are isomorphic, the dimension of their intertwiner space equals each of their two commutant dimensions, by multiplication with a fixed isomorphism. Reject a proposed equivalence of these tuples when that necessary equality fails. Otherwise enumerate the polynomial-size intertwiner space and test invertibility. This proves both recognition and construction of all equivalences on the promised inputs.

For each resulting type choose one representative R across the pair. At each occurrence x make a set T_x containing every linear isomorphism from R onto x intertwining the tuples. It is a torsor under right composition by the units of the commutant of R. Encode as a separate named binary relation the right action of each such unit, using common names for the two inputs. Any allowed bijection of two torsors of the same type must be postcomposition by a unique linear isomorphism: its action on one frame determines it on all frames by the right action. Retain occurrence identities and types.

For every edge and pair of frames at its endpoints record its matrix in these frames. For every vertex operation record its coefficient array in each tuple of frames on the involved modes (a frame on a repeated mode can be reused). Colors include argument order and operation names. Zero maps, thresholds and parallel edges retain their separate incidence data. Each record is polynomial in length, and fixed arity takes only a fixed product of polynomial frame counts. Every original isomorphism induces a relational isomorphism on frames and records. Conversely the reconstructed linear maps preserve every edge and vertex tensor by the recorded coordinates. Encoding these bounded-arity records by colored incidence and then ordinary GI completes the reduction. The class contains GI using identical one-dimensional local spaces and unit edge maps for all sufficiently large inputs. A small commutant is a sufficient test; large commutants can still have other useful descriptions.

**Example with locally unmarked coordinates.** Use a directed n-cycle over F5, n≥3, of mode dimension m≥2. At vertex i place the tensor e1⊗e1. Let P=E11, D=I−P and let S cyclically shift the m standard basis vectors. From i to i+1 put four edges distinguished by threshold with matrices ε_i D, ε_i P, ε_i S D and ε_i S P, where each ε_i is ±1. Every edge is singular; the vertex tensor alone leaves many changes of coordinates. Sums of the first two generators and of the last two propagate by signed I and signed S. Taking n−1 steps with the first sum and then one suitable step gives diagonal blocks C I, C S and C P in the generated span, with C the product of all ε_i. The common commutant of P and S is scalar: a commuting matrix preserves the e1 line and S carries its generator around a basis.

The graph match must be a rotation. Summing the first two edge equations forces a fixed ratio of adjacent basis maps, and going around the cycle forces equal source and target sign products. When they agree, the closed operators force a scalar root map and the tensor restricts it to ±1. Both choices extend uniquely for each rotation, so there are exactly 2n isomorphisms for equal sign products and zero otherwise. This example uses no named unary operator or vector constant. The walk computation is a way to *discover* the small set of allowable local changes of basis from the given input.
