# Exact counting when only shear transformations remain

Work over a fixed finite field k of characteristic p and fixed tensor signature. At every vertex-mode obtain an ordered list of marked vectors and an ordered list of marked covectors. They may be given as named constants and functionals, or generated by common expressions from the input. For example generate vector sections by pointwise single-output operations and thresholded sums of incoming transported vectors. Generate covector sections by dualizing appropriate operations and summing pullbacks over outgoing edges. Keep the first independent sections on the direct sum of all vertex spaces (separately for vectors and covectors). Multilinearity computes each closure in polynomial time for fixed arity; use shared expression names on the two sides or generate on their disjoint union. Isomorphisms preserve these lists. Using just the given markings gives a weaker but equally valid criterion.

At an occurrence let U span the marked vectors, D span its marked covectors, and K be the common kernel of D. Test K⊆U by elimination. Either marked span can be proper. First suppose a vertex bijection is given. Verify dimensions and equality of the relations of matched vector lists, and solve the linear equations A u=u' and d' A=d for all marks. Any solution is then injective on U. Its kernel lies in K⊆U, so it is invertible. Do this at every occurrence. The choices of alignments, if any, differ by the direct product H of the local groups I+Hom(V/U,K), computed in aligned coordinates. Indeed the difference from the identity must vanish on U and have image in K. Products of any two such difference maps are zero, so group composition is addition. Represent H by an explicit prime-field basis.

**Theorem.** Under this kernel containment, deciding, constructing and exactly counting all lifts of the vertex bijection is polynomial-time, even for arbitrary parallel edges and arbitrarily high shear dimension.

To see this first handle a single tuple of tensors or edge maps as a vector in a direct sum of tensor products of primal and dual spaces. Stabilizing a tensor operation is equivalent to fixing this mixed tensor under the usual invertible action. Choose bases adapted to K⊆U⊆V and reverse the dual basis order. Every element of H acts upper unitriangularly, also on duals, tensor products in lexicographic order and their direct sums. Integers in counts below refer to prime-field dimensions. A transporter from x to y can be computed as follows. Start with the whole group as a coset. Work from the last coordinate to the first. If a representative already attains the required suffix, its allowable adjustments form a subgroup S. On the next coordinate the displacement caused by S is additive because the suffix is fixed and each element acts trivially on that one-dimensional flag quotient. More explicitly, differences lie in the span of the current and earlier coordinates and the action fixes the current coordinate on this subspace. Evaluate the displacement on a basis of S, solve a linear equation over the prime field, and either reject or update the representative and replace S by its kernel. This gives the exact affine transporter in H, including stabilizers, with polynomial-size arrays for fixed tensor arity. Although the tensor action can contain nonlinear products of shear parameters, this computation does not linearize it incorrectly: it linearizes *one flag quotient after fixing the suffix*. Apply it first to all named vertex tensors together.

For edges partition by endpoints and threshold, comparing source and target multisets of tuples of matrices after the initial alignments. In each bucket partition the union of the two lists into H-orbits using the single-tuple transporter algorithm. Unequal total multiplicity in an orbit rejects. In each nonempty orbit choose a record x, compute its stabilizer S_x and offsets modulo S_x for all distinct records. Since H is abelian this orbit is a translation space under H/S_x. A valid translation must send one chosen source offset to some target offset. Try all such targets and compare multiplicities of the translated lists. If any succeed their offsets are one coset of the subgroup of periods of the multiplicity table. Compute its period space by taking the prime-field span of differences of all successes. Lift this affine space back to H and intersect the resulting constraints over all orbits and buckets and with the vertex transporter. This takes polynomial time in the lists, with no enumeration of H. For each solution the number of actual edge bijections is the product of the factorials of target multiplicities of identical records within buckets. Multiply this product by the cardinality of the final affine subspace, interpreting an empty intersection as zero. Choices of actual permutations among equal records give explicit witnesses.

**Unknown vertices.** For a threshold, retain a move from v to w when w is the unique distinct head at that threshold at v and v is the unique distinct tail at that threshold at w. If such moves, traversed both ways, connect each weak component, an image of one root forces the whole component vertex map. Try the finitely many target root images, check the maps and sum the lift counts. Across components the positive pair weights form complete bipartite blocks by composition of isomorphisms. Detect unmatched components. A balanced block of m components on each side with common positive weight w contributes m! w^m. Use component-local marking generation when comparing individual components. This gives a polynomial algorithm with no supplied vertex match on this recognizable class.

For a worked count put m identical threshold-zero edges on every step of a directed n-cycle (n≥3). At every vertex take two r-dimensional summands, a named operator J=diag(J_r,J_r) for the nilpotent forward shift, the first vector in the first summand and the last coordinate functional on the second. Every edge has matrix J. Iterating J on the vector and its dual on the functional generates exactly the first summand and the dual of the second. Thus K=U is the first summand. The local shear block X from the second summand to the first must commute with J_r, so X is a polynomial in J_r of degree less than r (it is determined by its value on the first cyclic vector). The edge equation is J_r(X_next−X_current)=0. The first r−1 coefficients are common to every vertex and the last is free at each. The exact full automorphism count is n (m!)^n q^(r−1+n). All ordinary transports are singular. No part of the computation requires the local shears to be few in number.
