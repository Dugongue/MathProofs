# Sharp probe arities for reconstructing a costed action

A finite groupoid has identities, composition and inverses. A Reverscale gives its arrows nonnegative costs, invariant under inversion, zero at identities and subadditive under composition. A Symmetrinet assigns each object a graph with thresholds and sends every arrow to a threshold-preserving graph isomorphism. Here the graphs, their objects, their full vertex and edge sets, and the incidence and thresholds are known. The arrows, action and costs are to be recovered. Faithful means faithful on each hom-set. A base at object s is an ordered tuple T of graph atoms (vertices or edge identities) whose pointwise isotropy stabilizer acts trivially on every atom there.

An experiment picks two objects and ordered atom tuples u,v of the same length in their respective graphs. A Boolean experiment reports whether there exists ONE arrow sending u to v coordinatewise. A numerical experiment reports the minimum cost among such arrows, with infinity for no arrow. Include empty tuples, also for empty graphs. For shortest-word costs this is the shortest-path distance in the tuple graph obtained by moving all coordinates with the same generator; composing the recorded moves and reading an arrow word prove equality in both directions. For general costs the result assumes these actual arrow-minimum measurements.

**Reconstruction theorem.** If every object has a base of length at most b, numerical measurements through length b and Boolean measurements through length b+1 reconstruct the entire effective action with its minimum lift costs. They reconstruct the original costed action when faithful. For fixed b reconstruction from the exact data takes polynomial time in the explicit graphs and measurements.

**Proof.** Find a base by testing all candidate tuples T of length ≤b. Check for each pair x,y of atoms at that object that reachability from (T,x) to (T,y) implies x=y. This says exactly that the stabilizer acts trivially and uses length at most b+1. For each target object and reachable tuple v from T, all arrows sending T to v have the same action on atoms: the quotient of any two is in the stabilizer. Conversely, every effective arrow occurs this way. Recover its image of x as the unique y for which (T,x) reaches (v,y). Label this arrow with the measured minimum cost from T to v. Compose the recovered bijections and retain their endpoints, thereby recovering the groupoid structure. Minima over lifts obey the cost axioms by inversion and composition of minimizing lifts. No observations of this form can distinguish two arrows of identical action individually. All loops and parallel edges of the given graphs are accounted for by using their edge identities. There are polynomially many tuple lookups for fixed b. One-object unweighted recovery is closely related to Wielandt's classical result that a permutation group with base size b is (b+1)-closed. [Freedman et al., statement of Wielandt's theorem](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00605-023-01822-5).

**Boolean sharpness for every b≥1.** On b+1 directed triangles, assign a different edge threshold to each triangle. The entire automorphism group of this graph is the abelian rotation group A=(F3)^(b+1). Within it consider
H={x: x1+...+x_b+x_(b+1)=0} and K={x: x1+...+x_b+2x_(b+1)=0}.
Use zero cost at the identity and cost one at every nonidentity. Both groups project surjectively onto any ≤b triangle coordinates: any missing coordinate with nonzero coefficient completes the defining equation. Prescribing one atom in each of b distinct triangles fixes the remaining coordinate, so the base size is precisely b, and fewer cannot suffice by dimension. A tuple of ≤b atoms sees only the corresponding coordinate projection. The orbit tests and minimum transport costs of that arity are identical for both groups, including failures caused by inconsistent observations on the same triangle. For unchanged tuples the identity realizes zero cost. But the groups are distinct, and no graph automorphism conjugates one to the other since A is abelian. The rotation (1,0,...,0,−1) can be detected on b+1 atoms and occurs only for H. Thus even numerical information through b cannot replace the extra Boolean coordinate.

**Numerical sharpness for every b≥1.** Take instead b distinguished triangles and their full rotation group A=(F3)^b. Its base size is b. In one action assign cost one to every nonidentity; in the other raise the cost of the two rotations ±(1,...,1) to two. Both costs satisfy the axioms: a sum of two nonzero costs is at least two and inverse costs agree. Every proper coordinate prescription asking for a nonzero motion has at least three completions, of which at least one retains cost one. If no motion is required the identity gives zero. Thus all numerical queries of length at most b−1 agree, and all Boolean queries of *every* length agree because the action itself is identical. One atom per triangle forces the all-ones motion and distinguishes its two costs. The cost multisets also differ, so the costed actions are nonisomorphic. These constructions establish the optimal arities in the worst case for the stated observation model. They do not presume the recovery of graph incidence from the measurements.
