# Four results on unlabelled coordinates, residual symmetries and synchronized observations

These are informal mathematical proofs. The algorithmic input models and scope are stated in each note. The research concerns the proposed Tensystem, Transportensor, Reverscale and Symmetrinet definitions and their combinations. No published open-question closure or publication priority is claimed.

1. [Unordered algebra coordinates](Unordered.md). On a network with a named commutative unital algebra at each mode over a fixed finite field, construct unordered, polynomial-size sets of test vectors and covectors from the raw multiplication tables. Residue degrees and the number of local factors are unbounded; each individual local radical has polynomial cardinality. Reducing the resulting evaluation tables to GI recovers the original isomorphism problem, even with extra tensors, unknown vertex maps and singular transports.
2. [Frames from closed walks](Walks.md). If the algebra of operators recovered from thresholded walks has a commutant with polynomially many elements on each mode, all possible local frames form polynomial-size lists. The mixed network again reduces to GI in one many-one instance. This can apply when every ordinary edge is singular, the local dimensions grow, and there are no unary observations or vector constants.
3. [Exact counting with residual shear groups](Shears.md). If the kernel of the marked covectors is contained in the span of the marked vectors, an arbitrarily large group of remaining basis choices is handled by exact linear algebra rather than enumeration. The algorithm counts lifts of a given vertex match even with arbitrary parallel, singular edges. It recovers unknown vertex matches as well when the stated threshold moves connect each component.
4. [Sharp probe arities for reconstructing symmetries and their costs](ProbeArity.md). Given the acted-on graphs and synchronized observations, faithful actions of base size at most b can be reconstructed from costs on at most b atoms and reachability on at most b+1. Both bounds are individually sharp for every b≥1. Invisible transformations give the effective quotient with minimum lift costs. The one-object unweighted upper bound is classical.

## A mixed corollary of the first two proofs

One GI instance still suffices when different modes pass *different* coordinate tests. At each vertex-mode perform the same invariant selection of a successful method (for example in a fixed signature order). For an algebra mode use its two unordered spanning sets. For a walk mode take every frame from its finite torsor and apply it to a common basis of the type representative. Use the union of the resulting vectors as P. Use the union of the inverse-pullbacks of the dual basis as D. Both sets span and are functorial on isomorphisms, since postcomposition permutes the torsor. Their sizes gain at most a factor of the dimension. Now apply the single pairing and evaluation construction from the first theorem to *every* tensor. No change of basis can be chosen independently for different occurrences of the same mode. All couplings across the two methods are checked by the same argument.

For example, take one algebra mode k^m with multiplication and one different mode k^d with a cyclic coordinate shift and the projector onto its first coordinate. The former may admit all m! coordinate permutations, while the latter admits only nonzero scalars. Permit an arbitrary additional tensor coupling these modes. The algebra mode need not have a small walk commutant, and the second mode does not need a named multiplication. Both types of ambiguity are represented simultaneously in the GI instance. This is a sufficient theorem about inputs on which the listed recovery tests succeed.

## Finite checks and evidence

[check_examples.py](check_examples.py) examines all 20,160 invertible binary maps in dimension four on the algebra F4[ε]/(ε²). Exactly six preserve multiplication and all six preserve the two recovered sets. It compares both sharpness constructions at b=1 through 5 and checks the shear count on four cycle networks. The checked shear counts are 192, 384, 128 and 96. These tests exercise examples, not the unrestricted theorems. The examples and proofs are not Lean-certified. Existing comparison points are cited in the individual notes, including Wielandt's classical base-size theorem and earlier reductions using pairing data.
