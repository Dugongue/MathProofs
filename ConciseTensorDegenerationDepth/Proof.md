# Quadratic degeneration chains with fixed slice determinant

**Informal proofs.** The first result gives quadratic degeneration depth for concise cubic tensors, even with an identity slice and a fixed slice determinant. The second separates individual-orbit depth from the difference rank of the orbit relation. Publication priority has not been established.

## 1. Quadratic depth in a fixed-determinant family

Work over an infinite field \(k\), with Zariski closure. For \(k=\mathbb R,\mathbb C\), the constructed steps also hold in the Euclidean topology. We use algebraic orbit dimensions after extending scalars to an algebraic closure.

**Theorem.** For \(N\ge 8\), take the smallest \(r\ge 1+\lceil\sqrt{N-2}\rceil\) with \(N-r\) even and put \(m=(N-r)/2\). There is a chain of at least \(m(m+1)/2\) strict degenerations under \(\mathrm{SL}_N^3\) among tensors in \(k^{N\times N\times N}\). Each tensor has all three flattening ranks equal to \(N\), has an identity slice in the same coordinate, and has the same determinant polynomial of the entire slice space in these coordinates. Each step is also strict under \(\mathrm{GL}_N^3\). The lower bound is \(N^2/8-O(N^{3/2})\).

### The pencil chain

Use the standard Kronecker notation \(P_i,I_j,J_\ell\) for the preprojective of dimension \((i,i+1)\), preinjective of dimension \((j+1,j)\), and regular block of length \(\ell\) in one fixed tube. Set \(J_0=0\). For \(h\ge1\), put \(a=\lceil h/2\rceil\) and \(b=\lfloor h/2\rfloor\). There are \(h\) strict steps from \(J_h\) to \(P_0\oplus I_0\oplus J_{h-1}\).

1. Move one box at a time from the first Jordan block to the second until reaching \(J_a\oplus J_b\). Jordan dominance gives \(b\) steps.
2. Replace \(J_a\) by \(P_0\oplus I_{a-1}\), keeping \(J_b\).
3. Replace \(P_0\oplus I_j\oplus J_c\) by \(P_0\oplus I_{j-1}\oplus J_{c+1}\) for \(j=a-1,\ldots,1\).

We give the exact sequences so the steps work over the ground field. A subrepresentation \(U'\subseteq V'\) gives a degeneration \(V'\leadsto U'\oplus V'/U'\): choose a vector-space complement at each vertex and multiply the subspace at each vertex by \(t\), which multiplies the off-diagonal arrow blocks by \(t\). The matrices have an entrywise limit at \(t=0\). For step 1, in \(k[x]/(x^u)\oplus k[x]/(x^v)\) with \(u\ge v+2\), the cyclic submodule generated by \((x,1)\) has length \(u-1\) and the cyclic quotient has length \(v+1\). When \(v=0\), the second coordinate is zero. This gives each box move.

Take \(J_c=(N_c,I_c)\), where \(N_ce_i=e_{i-1}\) with \(N_ce_1=0\). Realize \(I_j\) with arrows \([I_j\;0]\) and \([0\;I_j]\), where the identity matrices in the brackets are of size \(j\). In step 2 take the one-dimensional subspace spanned by \(e_a\) at the target of \(J_a\) and zero at its source. The quotient is \(I_{a-1}\) after reversing the bases, so splitting gives the claimed step.

For step 3 let \(f_j=e_1^*\) on \(k^c\), and define \(f_i=f_{i+1}N_c\) for \(1\le i<j\). If \(c=0\), all these functionals are zero. Map \(I_j\oplus J_c\) onto \(I_{j-1}\) by taking the first \(j\) source coordinates of \(I_j\) plus the values of \(f_1,\ldots,f_j\) on the source of \(J_c\). At the target, take the first \(j-1\) coordinates of \(I_j\) plus the values of \(f_2,\ldots,f_j\). The recursion makes the maps commute with the first arrow, and the second arrow commutes by the shift in coordinates. On the kernel only the last \(I_j\) coordinate \(s\) and the \(J_c\) coordinate \(z\) are free. The two arrows there are \((s,z)\mapsto(-f_jz,N_cz)\) and the identity. This is \(J_{c+1}\). Splitting gives the claimed step with \(P_0\) unchanged.

The distinct Kronecker decompositions ensure strictness, also over the algebraic closure. Padding the \((h-1)\)-chain by \(P_0\oplus I_0\) preserves closure by the block embedding and strictness by Krull–Schmidt cancellation. Induction gives \(1+\cdots+m\) steps for \(m\times m\) pencils. For the classical Hom-order description underlying the original pencil construction, see [Bongartz, Degenerations for representations of tame quivers](https://www.numdam.org/item/?id=ASENS_1995_4_28_5_647_0/).

### Lift and determinant

Write \(k^N=U\oplus W\oplus Z\) with dimensions \(m,m,r\). Split \(Z=Z_1\oplus k\) with \(\dim Z_1=r-1\). Choose \(N-2\) linearly independent matrices \(F_1,\ldots,F_{N-2}\) supported on \(Z_1\), for example distinct matrix units. Fix \(\lambda\in k\setminus\{0,1\}\) and \(E=\operatorname{diag}(0_U,I_W,\lambda I_Z)\). Encode a pencil \(A,B:W\to U\) as the tensor with slices

\[
T(A,B)=(I_N,\ E,\ X(A)+F_1,\ X(B)+F_2,\ F_3,\ldots,F_{N-2}),
\]

where \(X(A)\) has only the off-diagonal \(W\to U\) block and the \(F_i\) are extended by zero. The identity slice ensures full flattening rank in both matrix modes. Restricting a linear relation among slices to the two diagonal \(U,W\) blocks kills the first two coefficients. Independence of the \(F_i\) kills the rest. Thus the slice mode also has full rank.

The determinant of \(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}x_i T_i(A,B)\) is always

\[
x_0^m(x_0+x_1)^m
\det\!\left((x_0+\lambda x_1) I_Z+\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}x_{i+1}F_i\right).
\]

The block upper-triangular form also fixes the characteristic polynomial of every linear combination of slices.

### Strictness under slice mixing

The pencil action \((P,Q):(A,B)\mapsto(PAQ^{-1},PBQ^{-1})\) is realized by slice-wise conjugation with
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(P,Q,I_{Z_1},(\det P\det Q)^{-1}).
\]
The scalar on the reserved line has no effect on the slices and makes \(\det D=1\) over the ground field. The reciprocal actions on the matrix modes lie in \(\mathrm{SL}_N^2\), and the slice mode is fixed. The affine-linear map \(T\) takes every pencil degeneration to a special-linear degeneration, including the Euclidean limits above.

Let \(H=\mathrm{GL}_N\times\mathrm{GL}_N\) act on the matrix modes only, by \(M\mapsto LMR^{-1}\). In the stabilizer of \(T(A,B)\), the identity slice forces \(L=R\). Fixing \(E\) forces this common matrix to be block diagonal \(\operatorname{diag}(P,Q,S)\). Fixing the remaining slices imposes exactly the pencil stabilizer equations on \((P,Q)\) and the common commutant equations for the \(F_i\) on \(S\). Thus

\[
\dim\operatorname{Stab}_{H}(T(A,B))
=\dim\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathrm{GL}_m^2}(A,B)
+\dim\bigcap_i C_{\mathrm{GL}(Z)}(F_i).
\]

The last term is fixed. A strict pencil degeneration strictly drops the source orbit dimension and thus the \(H\)-orbit dimension of its image. Invertible slice mixing commutes with \(H\), so it preserves \(H\)-orbit dimension. Full general-linear tensor isomorphism also preserves it. Hence the two images are inequivalent under the full group at each step, and therefore under its special-linear subgroup. ∎

Group dimensions give the matching quadratic upper bound over an algebraically closed field. The chain persists in the relative topology of the concise locus. A continuous kernel reduction to an orbit relation carries every step to a strict step in its target. The same obstruction holds on the displayed family when using \(O_i\) equal to the images of the individual pencil orbits. In either setting alternate the sets \(O_i\times O_i\) and \(O_{i+1}\times O_i\). They have alternating answers, and each lies in the closure of the preceding set. Thus the relation cannot be closed and its closed-set difference level is at least \(m(m+1)+1\). A continuous kernel reduction to any closed equivalence relation is impossible, including to real orthogonal or complex unitary equivalence. These conclusions concern continuous kernel reductions.

## 2. Arbitrarily high difference rank with all orbits closed

**Theorem.** For every \(s\ge1\) there is a linear action of \(\mathbb G_a^s\) on \(\mathbb C^{3s}\) with all orbits closed whose orbit relation has closed-set difference rank exactly \(2s+1\), for both the Euclidean and Zariski topology.

For one factor, use \(t\cdot(x,y,z)=(x,y+tx,z+2ty+t^2x)\). If \(x\ne0\) the orbit is the parabola in the plane of fixed \(x\) given by \(xz-y^2=f\), where \(f\) is fixed. If \(x=0,y\ne0\) it is the line of fixed \(x,y\). If \(x=y=0\) it is a point. All are closed, as are products of them.

In the space of pairs, let \(C\) be given by \(x=x'\) and \(xz-y^2=x'z'-y'^2\). Inside \(C\) put \(B=\{x=x'=0,\ y'=-y\}\) and \(L=\{x=x'=y=y'=0,\ z=z'\}\). The relation is \((C\setminus B)\cup L\). The set \(C\setminus B\) is dense in \(C\), because \(x\ne0\) is open dense in the irreducible hypersurface \(x(z-z')=(y-y')(y+y')\). It is also Euclidean dense over \(\mathbb C\). The middle stratum \(B\setminus L\) is dense in \(B\). In the \(s\)-fold product take one factor at a time through the three strata \(C\setminus B,B\setminus L,L\), holding the other factors in affirmative strata. This gives \(2s+1\) nonempty sets alternating in membership, each contained in the closure of the preceding set. The difference level is at least \(2s+1\).

For the upper bound work inside the closed set \(C^s\). Use the finite product poset of three levels per factor. Every principal up-set pulls back to a closed product of copies of \(C,B,L\). Thus every up-set has closed inverse image. A level vector is affirmative precisely when no coordinate is the middle level. Give it the maximum length of an alternating chain ending there that starts affirmative. The bottom vector is affirmative, so the length is at least one at every vector. Its parity records membership. The maximum length is monotone, since for greater vectors of opposite membership a chain can be extended and for those of the same membership its last point can be replaced. A strict chain can increase the sum of levels by at most \(2s\), so the length is at most \(2s+1\). The inverse images of its superlevel sets give a nested closed presentation of the relation by alternating differences. ∎

The example separates degeneration of individual orbits from simultaneous specialization of pairs in the relation, even for linear actions on affine space. Its orbits are infinite. The finite-orbit equality between depth and difference level is compatible with the example.
