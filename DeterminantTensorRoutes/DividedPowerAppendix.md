# Divided-power apolar determinant pins in fixed characteristic

Fix a finite field $F$ of characteristic $p$. Suppose a tensor mode of dimension $2m$ has an alternating-form space $S$ supplied as part of a marked system or recovered covariantly from the tensor. Let $P$ be its nonzero formal Pfaffian polynomial of degree $m$ on $S$. Let $h$ be the dimension of the span of all Hasse derivatives of $P$, including $P$. This note gives a randomized construction of one ordinary TI instance enforcing a prescribed determinant on this mode, in time polynomial in the raw input and a promised bound on $h$. It uses only evaluation of the raw Pfaffian. There is no sparsity, individual-degree or Frobenius-power promise on $P$.

## 1. Recover a finite algebra by translations

Use a fixed, explicitly presented finite extension $E/F$ large enough for the polynomial bounds below. The same model of $E$ is used on both sides of the comparison. In the truncated divided-power algebra $D=\Gamma^{\leq m}(S\otimes_F E)$, write $D^{[\alpha]}$ for the Hasse differential operator in a coordinate basis. Multiplication has the binomial constants from composition of Hasse derivatives. Discard degrees above $m$. For $a\in S\otimes_F E$, define
$$
   U(a)=\sum_{|\alpha|\le m}a^\alpha D^{[\alpha]} .
$$
It acts on $P$ by translation, and $U(a)U(b)=U(a+b)$. Let $B$ be the quotient by the annihilator of $P$. The quotient has dimension $h$ and its elements can be represented by their polynomials of action on $P$.

Choose $m+1$ distinct scalars in $E$. The translations by these scalars times a fixed coordinate vector span all the Hasse operators on that axis by interpolation. Products over the axes span $D$. Thus, beginning with $U(0)$, apply this polynomial list of axis translations to each accepted basis item and keep its result if linearly independent. The accepted elements are translations $U(a_j)$, stored by their shift vectors. When the span is closed it is the whole quotient. A proposed next item is tested by Gaussian elimination on evaluations of $P(x+a_j)$. To evaluate $P$ at any point use the Pfaffian of the corresponding raw alternating matrix. A fresh set of random probe points for each independence test gives failure probability at most $(H+1)m/|E|$ when testing at most $H+1$ polynomials and the promised dimension bound is $H$. Indeed for a fixed independent list, at each new row there is a nonzero polynomial of total degree at most $m$ in the kernel of the rows so far. The chance of failing to gain rank is at most $m/|E|$. Conditional on all prior choices the polynomials for the next test are fixed. There are at most $(H+1)\dim(S)(m+1)$ such adaptive tests if the process stops when it exceeds $H$. Choose $|E|$ at least a hundred times $(H+1)^2 m\dim(S)(m+1)$ per input, and increase this constant for additional pencils or a smaller failure target. Over fixed $F$ the extension is constructible in polynomial time, for example by enumerating candidate defining polynomials of the needed logarithmic degree and testing irreducibility.

On successful tests this gives an exact basis, and the probability of success can be set at least $0.99$ for both inputs together. Once a correct basis is found, take a probe matrix of full column rank on it. Such full rank is an observable certificate of injectivity on the basis span. One can use the full-rank probe set from the last successful addition. Now recover products $U(a_i+a_j)$ and any other requested translation by solving for that probe vector in the basis. These products lie in the span by its closure. Define the linear functional $\ell$ by $\ell([b])=(bP)(0)$, computed on translations by $\ell(U(a))=P(a)$. The resulting algebra, multiplication, and functional are over $E$, and can be used as $F$-linear data with the scalar action of a pointwise fixed $E$.

## 2. Force only the action needed on the socle

The strictly positive-degree ideal of $D$ is nilpotent, and its quotient is $E$. Its image $J$ is the maximal ideal of the nonzero algebra $B$. It is spanned by the differences $U(a_j)-1$. The product pairing $(u,v)\mapsto\ell(uv)$ on $B$ is nondegenerate. Indeed if $uP$ is nonzero, some Hasse coefficient at zero is nonzero, and the corresponding operator $v$ detects it. The socle $Z=\operatorname{Ann}(J)$ has dimension one: if $z\in Z$ satisfies $\ell(z)=0$, then $\ell(zv)=0$ for all $v$ since $v$ differs from a scalar by an element of $J$. Thus $\ell$ embeds $Z$ into $E$. The last nonzero $J^k$, allowing $k=0$, lies in $Z$, so they are equal.

An automorphism of $B$ inducing the identity on $J/J^2$ induces the identity on every $J^k/J^{k+1}$: lift a spanning list of products of $k$ elements of $J/J^2$. Hence it fixes $Z$ pointwise. It suffices to tie the action on the indecomposables $J/J^2$ to the actual mode map. The rest of the action on $B$ can be left free.

For each $p^j\le m$ there is a natural linear map from the $j$-fold Frobenius twist of $S\otimes E$ into $J/J^2$. A direction $v$ is mapped before linearization of the twist to the class of the coefficient of $t^{p^j}$ in $U(tv)$. In coordinates this coefficient modulo $J^2$ is
$$
   \sum_i v_i^{p^j}[D_i^{[p^j]}] .
$$
All mixed terms are products of positive-degree operators and vanish in this quotient. The coefficients $[D_i^{[p^j]}]$ are recovered by univariate interpolation of the already computable $U(t e_i)$. These maps span $J/J^2$: by the binomial constants and Lucas' theorem, a pure $D_i^{[d]}$ with $d$ other than a power of $p$ decomposes as a product with an invertible coefficient by splitting the base-$p$ digits of $d$. A monomial using multiple axes also decomposes. Covariance follows from the natural translation law under the induced linear map on the alternating-form coordinates.

To impose the twists, use that the span of pure $p$-th powers inside $\operatorname{Sym}^p(W)$ is the Frobenius twist of $W$. Include the truncated symmetric algebra through degree $p$, its degree-one generators, and the inclusion of the pure powers. Repeat from the preceding twist up to $\lfloor\log_p m\rfloor$. This uses only $E$-linear structures with dimension polynomial for fixed $p$. It avoids requiring the coordinate Frobenius itself to be $F$-linear. Include the maps to $J/J^2$ and the quotient and inclusion maps for $J,J^2,Z$.

## 3. Read the determinant

For a physical mode map $g$, let $s:S\to S'$ be the induced map. In the convention $g^T S'(sx)g=S(x)$, Pfaffian covariance says $P'(s x)=(\det g)^{-1}P(x)$. The induced canonical algebra isomorphism $B_P\to B_{P'}$ sends the translation at $a$ to the translation at $s(a)$. Its composition with $\ell'$ therefore equals $(\det g)^{-1}\ell$. Any admissible isomorphism of the linked auxiliary data induces the same map on $J/J^2$ by the spanning twist maps. Relative to the canonical map it fixes the one-dimensional socle, by the preceding argument. Restrict the marked functionals to the socles and pin them with the required ratio. Since each restricted functional is nonzero, this is equivalent to the desired determinant of the actual mode map. Every actual transporter with that determinant extends by the canonical maps on all auxiliary structures.

On successful randomized extraction, the data are exact. Form the same linked linear and bilinear tensor system as in the flag route. Repeated twist steps and module blocks can be bundled in direct sums tagged by primitive idempotents of a fixed split label algebra, with a separate aggregate map for each relation type. The labels can be fixed by the path operator on that algebra. Each relation is supported in its corresponding indexed source and target blocks. The number of map types is constant, independent of $m$. The resulting constant-part partitioned tensor reduces to one ordinary 3-TI instance over $F$. To implement the extension data over the original field, expand every auxiliary module in an explicit $F$-basis and attach its $E$-action as an $F$-bilinear map. Pin the $E$ sort pointwise, so the resulting $F$-linear isomorphisms are exactly the required $E$-linear ones. The field extension is auxiliary storage, and the oracle remains over $F$. This applies separately to any mode with a supplied or recovered pencil and a small Hasse-derivative span. More generally the same proof applies to any nonzero homogeneous covariant polynomial with a known relative character in place of the Pfaffian determinant. The construction uses only evaluation, the induced linear coordinate action, and the bound on the derivative span, so it pins that character under the same hypotheses.

For example, $P=x_1^{p^j}$ has Hasse-derivative span two, even with arbitrarily many alternating-form parameters. The mixed-degree example $P=x^p y$ has derivative span $\langle x^p y,x^p,y,1\rangle$ and apolar algebra $E[u,v]/(u^2,v^2)$ with $u=D_x^{[p]}$ and $v=D_y^{[1]}$. Its socle is generated by $uv$. Thus even when ordinary top differentiation vanishes and no Frobenius deflation applies, the twisted generator data and the socle still carry the scale. More generally this construction retains every Hasse layer that survives in the small quotient, so exact $p$-power shape plays no role in the theorem. It replaces the large full polynomial representation by the determinant action on a one-dimensional socle. The unresolved general question is whether arbitrary tensors admit sufficiently informative covariant data of this kind with polynomial extraction.
