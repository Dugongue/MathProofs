# Determinant constraints from overlapping tensor flags

Fix a finite field $F$ and write $M=|F|-1$. All tensors have three modes with explicit dense input encoding of length $L$. The problem is to decide whether there is a transporter with any prescribed determinant triple, especially $(1,1,1)$. This note gives a sufficient criterion on raw tensors and a single-query ordinary-TI construction for that criterion. The extraction uses the joint contraction flags from the invariant zoo. The criterion can apply when individual flags have large layers.

## 1. Extract a linear diagram, then use its common endomorphism algebra

For a mode $i$ as contraction source, apply the joint contraction optimization to $U\subseteq V_i^*$. Record the maximal minimizing subspace and both image supports at each slope break and intervening interval. For the diagram in the physical modes, take the annihilators of subspaces in $V_i^*$ and the actual support subspaces in the two destination modes. Label all subspaces by the weights and source/destination roles. These are polynomially many marked objects and every original tensor transporter sends each label to its counterpart.

One can also use unequal nonnegative integer support weights $a,b\leq \dim V_1+\dim V_2+\dim V_3$, not both zero. For each pair of weights minimize
$$
 a\dim S_j(U)+b\dim S_k(U)-\lambda\dim U .
$$
The same tensor matrix-space construction works by repeating the two codomain banks $a,b$ times independently, as well as clearing the denominator of $\lambda$. The dimension argument is unchanged: the image of a subspace of the repeated domain depends only on the sum of its coordinate projections. Its deficit is maximized by using the full repeated copy of that sum. The largest minimizer is obtained by a rational slope perturbation that favors larger dimension. Candidate breakpoints have bounded numerator and denominator because they are differences of integer support costs divided by differences of input dimensions. Thus a uniform finite list of weights, slopes, and perturbations suffices on any fixed format. The determinant criterion below already works with just the weight pair $(1,1)$. [Ivanyos–Qiao–Subrahmanyam, constructive noncommutative rank](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03531)

Make a linear diagram with the three modes as vertices and an inclusion arrow from every labeled subspace. More generally the theorem works with any polynomially computable covariant labeled linear diagram containing the mode spaces. For two diagrams, either reject if they are nonisomorphic or find a reference diagram isomorphism $g_0$ in deterministic polynomial time by the constructive module-isomorphism algorithm. To apply the algorithm, take the direct sum of the vertex spaces and give one endomorphism for each vertex projection and each arrow extended by zero. Simultaneous module isomorphisms are exactly diagram isomorphisms, since the vertex projections force the block decomposition. Pull the target tensor back through the three mode parts of $g_0$. Both diagrams now agree. Write $A$ for the endomorphism algebra of this diagram. Its elements are tuples of vertex maps satisfying simultaneous linear equations. Any original transporter is now the restriction of a unit of $A$. An arbitrary unit can fail the original tensor equations, so the output query retains the full tensors. An invertible element on the sum of vertex spaces has its inverse in $A$ by Cayley–Hamilton. [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, constructive module isomorphism](https://doi.org/10.1145/258726.258751)

## 2. Keep precisely the determinant factors that matter

Compute the Jacobson radical $J$ and decompose
$$
 A/J=\prod_s \operatorname{Mat}_{d_s}(E_s).
$$
For each original mode $V_i$, compute the total multiplicity $m_{is}$ of the natural simple module of factor $s$ in the radical filtration of $V_i$. On each grade it is the dimension of the image of a corresponding primitive matrix idempotent divided by $[E_s:F]$. These are standard finite-algebra operations over fixed $F$. For every $u\in A^\times$ the determinant triple of its original mode maps is
$$
 \prod_s \left(t_s^{m_{1s}},t_s^{m_{2s}},t_s^{m_{3s}}\right),
 \qquad t_s=N_{E_s/F}(\det_{E_s} u_s).
$$
The formula follows by block triangularity along the radical filtrations, then the finite-field reduced norm on each simple grade. The same factor uses the same $t_s$ in every mode. This is the joint multiplicity calculation from the adjoint work applied to the common diagram.

Let $K\leq(F^\times)^3$ be any explicitly known subgroup realized by actual stabilizers of the pulled-back target tensor. For instance, use scalar stabilizers and the three pair-adjoint unit groups, with radical filtration multiplicities at their endpoints and inversion on the opposite endpoint. $K=1$ always works. The ambient group of triples has constant size. Call factor $s$ relevant if some $t\in F^\times$ has $(t^{m_{is}})_i\notin K$. Every other factor can be omitted from the exterior construction, regardless of $d_s$. This is legitimate because its entire contribution already belongs to the available correction subgroup.

**Sufficient condition.** For fixed $C$, suppose each relevant factor satisfies $d_s\le C\log(2+L)$. A deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction tests any prescribed determinant triple by one ordinary TI query. There is no bound on the radical length, individual flag widths, residue-field degrees, or sizes of the omitted matrix factors.

Indeed, let $c_i$ be the prescribed determinant divided by the determinant of the reference mode map $g_{0,i}$. In normalized coordinates, the condition is existence of an original tensor transporter $u$ preserving the diagrams such that
$$
 \left(\prod_{s\,\mathrm{relevant}} t_s^{m_{is}}\right)_i\in (c_i)_i K .
$$
Every required transporter satisfies this. Conversely, the omitted factors contribute an element of $K$, so any accepting transporter can be adjusted on the target by an actual stabilizer correcting its full determinant. Adjustment may change the chosen diagram isomorphism. That does not affect the ordinary transporter property, and on a covariant raw diagram every actual target stabilizer already respects the labels. The reference alignment need only be a diagram isomorphism.

## 3. Enforce the coset in one linked tensor system

Here is a constant-signature construction. Represent $A$ as a subspace of the endomorphisms of the direct sum $V$ of the diagram vertices. Include the evaluation $A\times V_{\rm fix}\to V$, with $V_{\rm fix}$ the same coordinate vector space but fixed pointwise. Force the action on $V$ to be block diagonal by the action of the fixed split algebra of vertex labels. Include the original tensor inside the appropriate summands or link those summands to three explicit mode copies by inclusions. Evaluation forces the action on $A$ to be actual left multiplication by the action $u$ on $V$. Taking the identity element in $A$ proves $u\in A$, and invertibility proves $u\in A^\times$. Conversely any desired $u$ acts this way. Include the quotient map $A\to A/J$. The induced action on the quotient is left multiplication by its image.

For every relevant factor choose one matrix column left ideal $W_s\subseteq \operatorname{Mat}_{d_s}(E_s)$. It is stable under that induced action. In a single aggregate sort include the algebra $\prod_s \Lambda_{E_s}(W_s)$ with its fieldwise exterior multiplication, degree-one subspaces, and top exterior lines. Include the direct sum of the $W_s$ via the embeddings into $A/J$ and into the exterior algebra. Also include the fixed base-field algebra $\prod_s E_s$ as the degree-zero subalgebra. Thus the exterior maps are forced by the linked block actions. The top lines are scaled by $\det_{E_s}(u_s)$. The exterior algebra dimension over $F$ is
$$
 \sum_{s\,\mathrm{relevant}} [E_s:F]2^{d_s},
$$
which is polynomial. If there are no relevant factors omit these sorts and use only the coset variables described next.

All norm and product relations can be enforced by a constant number of linear and bilinear relations even when the number of factors grows. Make a scalar register space $B=\bigoplus_j E_{s(j)}$, including registers over $F$. Mark the coordinatewise action of the fixed ring $\prod_j E_{s(j)}$ on $B$. Its intertwiners are exactly independent field scalings $z_j\in E_{s(j)}^\times$. Link each selected top exterior line to its input register by its chosen $E_s$-basis isomorphism. Pack every field multiplication gate into its own ordered input and output coordinates of one bilinear map $B\times B\to B$, with each gate contributing only its designated component. Preserve that map precisely when $z_{\rm out}=z_{\rm left}z_{\rm right}$ for each gate. One linear map similarly carries increasing-index Frobenius gates, enforcing $z_{\rm out}=z_{\rm in}^{q^j}$ when the map copies the $q^j$-power to a fresh output register, where $q=|F|$. Norms are products of these copies. For each computed norm register over $E_s$, link it to one register over $F$ using the linear inclusion $F\hookrightarrow E_s$. Preserve that inclusion exactly when the two scalings agree. Each gate uses its separate output coordinate. Reuse a register as input to many gates freely. To keep Frobenius and inclusions unambiguous use separate linear relations for those two types, and a separate projection or inclusion relation for the bank of top lines.

Represent $K$ by generators of triples with exponents modulo $M$ in a fixed generator of $F^\times$. An unconstrained $F$-register for each such generator supplies its exponent via its freely chosen scalar. Powers and products of these scalars produce exactly all of $K$, including when $M$ is composite. Use further product registers to impose equality of the three retained factors divided by the chosen $K$ factor to the respective $c_i$. Concretely, compute the retained factors and the $K$ products separately. Include three maps of fixed one-dimensional scalar spaces marking the ratio with the requisite source/target coefficient, or introduce inverse registers via multiplication equal to a pinned unit. In the latter construction force the final scalings to equal $c_i$ by marking basis vectors on the source and $c_i$ times those vectors on the target. All registers are nonzero scalings by construction. The number of registers is polynomial using one Frobenius copy per extension degree and binary powering for multiplicities. Choice of an element of $K$ is existential inside this one system.

For completeness, any fixed vector space in this construction can be pinned pointwise by identifying it with a split algebra $F^D$ marked by its multiplication and by the directed path endomorphism $e_i\mapsto e_{i+1}$, $e_D\mapsto0$. An algebra automorphism permutes the primitive idempotents, and commuting with this path fixes each of them. Pin each fixed sort this way. There are constantly many sorts and map types in the whole construction. Encode the linear and bilinear maps and marked vectors by a partitioned 3-tensor. Use an individual occurrence copy in each slot and identity tensor links to the common covariant or dual action. Scalar anchors in the three modes normalize the occurrence copies so that all identity links and actual relations are enforced. With three covariant and three dual core copies a six-cycle of identity links imposes one common action and its inverse transpose. Finally remove the constantly many partitions by the standard partitioned TI construction. This gives one ordinary 3-TI query and proves the criterion. [Grochow–Qiao, constant-part tensor compiler](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317) · [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Part V](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S3.SS3.SSS2)

## 4. A raw test case with wide overlapping flags

For $r\ge1$ let $V_1,V_2$ have ordered bases $e_i,f_j$ with $1\le i,j\le r$. Let $V_3$ have basis $h_{ij}$ for $i+j\le r+1$. Consider the raw tensors in the ordinary orbit of
$$
 T=\sum_{i+j\le r+1} e_i\otimes f_j\otimes h_{ij}.
$$
The $(1,1)$ joint contraction objective for $U\subset V_1^*$ of dimension $d$ has minimum support cost $d+d(d+1)/2$. Its unique minimizer at that dimension is the suffix subspace of dimension $d$. In fact if $i_0$ is the smallest active coordinate of $U$, the mode-two support has dimension $r-i_0+1\ge d$, and equality forces $U$ to be that suffix. The mode-three support is the sum, over $j$, of the dimensions of the projections of $U$ on its first $r-j+1$ coordinates. Each is bounded below by $\max(0,d-j+1)$, and the suffix attains every lower bound. The minimum support cost has increasing first differences as $d$ grows, so suitable slopes recover every suffix from the raw tensor. Exchanging the first two modes recovers the other suffix chain.

In mode three the corresponding support flags consist of full row suffixes and full column suffixes of the triangular basis. Each of these two flags has a largest layer of dimension $r$. Their joint endomorphism algebra in mode three, however, only permits entries $h_{ij}\mapsto h_{i'j'}$ with $i'\ge i$ and $j'\ge j$, by intersecting the two suffix conditions. In an ordering extending this partial order it is triangular, and the strictly off-diagonal entries are its nilpotent radical. Its semisimple quotient is a product of copies of $F$, with $d_s=1$. The other modes have complete annihilator flags and the same matrix-size-one conclusion. Further constraints from the weighted flags only restrict this triangular algebra. A unital subalgebra of a triangular algebra has nilpotent strictly off-diagonal kernel and split commutative diagonal image, so its simple factors still have size one. Thus the theorem gives a uniform raw reduction for this family for any determinant triple, despite the two recovered mode-three flags having unbounded width.

## Scope

Ward and Trellis carry over through the common indexing of all subspace constraints. The contribution is the combination of raw contraction flags, the *common* endomorphism algebra, and omission of factors whose determinants are already correctable. The older residual-quiver route in our notes uses the same radical multiplicities and norm pins after matching slices. Here neither a slice match nor an implementation of a general transporter search is needed on the stated criterion. The raw extraction can yield just a full mode, in which case the algebra can have a large relevant matrix factor. The theorem is informal and publication priority is unverified.
