# Separating degree can be strictly smaller than field-generation degree

**Question 5.10 of Blum-Smith, Garcia, Hidalgo and Rodriguez has an affirmative answer.** For every $r\ge4$, there is a faithful $4r+1$ dimensional representation of the elementary abelian group $(C_2)^{3r}$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic two such that
$$
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}=4,\qquad
\beta_{\mathrm{field}}=r+1.
$$
The ratio of field-generation degree to separating degree is unbounded. The proof below is informal. [Original Question 5.10, Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra 228 (2024), 107693](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.05626v6#S5.SS2).

## The action

Let $K$ be algebraically closed of characteristic two. Work with coordinate variables $x_i,x_i',y_i,y_i'$ for $1\le i\le r$, together with one variable $z$. Use three commuting involutions in each block, acting on the coordinates by the following changes and fixing the coordinates omitted from the table.

| Generator | Changes |
|---|---|
| $a_i$ | $y_i\mapsto y_i+x_i$, $z\mapsto z+x_i$ |
| $c_i$ | $y_i'\mapsto y_i'+x_i'$, $z\mapsto z+x_i'$ |
| $b_i$ | $y_i\mapsto y_i+x_i'$, $y_i'\mapsto y_i'+x_i$ |

They are independent, since each of their coefficients can be read from their action on the $y$ variables. The action is faithful. The variables other than $z$ describe the quotient representation $W$ obtained by forgetting the last coordinate. Put
$$
Q=z^2+\sum_i(x_i y_i+x_i' y_i').
$$
Each of the displayed generators fixes $Q$.

## Degree four separates all orbits

It is enough to separate the orbits on each block of $W$ and to adjoin $Q$. Indeed, after using the group to align their quotient coordinates, any two points having the same $Q$ have equal last coordinates, since squaring is injective.

For one quotient block, suppress subscripts. The $a,c$ invariants
$$
\alpha=y^2+xy,\qquad \gamma=(y')^2+x'y'
$$
together with $x,x'$ separate the $a,c$ orbits. At a fixed nonzero $x$, two solutions with the same $\alpha$ differ by $0$ or $x$, and for $x=0$ equality of squares implies equality. The same applies to $x',\gamma$.

Write $S=x+x'$, $U=x'S$ and $T=xS$. The remaining involution $b$ shifts $(\alpha,\gamma)$ by $(U,T)$. Use the following $b$-invariant polynomials in addition to $x,x'$:
$$
\alpha^2+U\alpha,\qquad \gamma^2+T\gamma,\qquad x\alpha+x'\gamma.
$$
They have degrees four, four and three. Equality of the first two says that the difference in the quotient coordinates has the form $(\epsilon U,\epsilon' T)$ with $\epsilon,\epsilon'\in\mathbb F_2$. If both $U,T$ are nonzero, then $xU=x'T\ne0$. Equality of the third invariant forces $\epsilon=\epsilon'$. If one of $U,T$ vanishes, at most one coordinate can change, so any allowed difference is already $0$ or $(U,T)$. This proves separation in all cases. Taking these invariants for every block and adjoining $Q$ gives $\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}\le4$.

## The first odd dependence has degree $r+1$

Put $u=z+\sum_i(y_i+y_i')$. The simultaneous invariants of the subgroup generated by all $a_i,c_i$ are exactly
$$
K[x_i,x_i',\alpha_i,\gamma_i,u \mid 1\le i\le r].
$$
In these coordinates the generators $a_i,c_i$ fix $u$ and act as independent transvections on the individual $y$ variables. For one transvection the assertion follows by writing a polynomial uniquely as $f_0(\alpha)+y f_1(\alpha)$ over the fixed coordinate ring, where $\alpha=y^2+xy$ is monic in $y$. Invariance forces $xf_1(\alpha)=0$ and hence $f_1=0$. Apply this independently to each of the $y$ variables.

In this ring we have $Q=u^2+\sum_i(\alpha_i+\gamma_i)$. Write
$$
R=K[x_i,x_i',\alpha_i,\gamma_i,Q\mid 1\le i\le r].
$$
It is a polynomial ring, and the preceding invariant ring is free over $R$ with basis $1,u$, by separating the even and odd powers of $u$. In the original polynomial grading, the $x$ variables and $u$ have degree one and $\alpha_i,\gamma_i,Q$ have degree two.

Every full-group invariant has a unique expression $A+uB$ with $A,B\in R$. For $S_i=x_i+x_i'$, the action of $b_i$ on these coordinates is
$$
u\mapsto u+S_i,\qquad
\alpha_i\mapsto\alpha_i+x_i'S_i,\qquad
\gamma_i\mapsto\gamma_i+x_iS_i.
$$
It fixes $Q$ and the other blocks. Comparing coefficients of $1,u$ gives $b_i(B)=B$ and
$$
b_i(A)-A=S_iB.
$$
Let $I_i=(x_i,x_i')$ in $R$. The change in $A$ belongs to $I_i^2$, since the nontrivial changes in $R$ are the two changes in $\alpha_i,\gamma_i$, both in $I_i^2$. Since $S_i$ is a nonzero linear form in these two independent variables, $S_iB\in I_i^2$ implies $B\in I_i$. Thus $B$ belongs to every $I_i$. Their variables are disjoint, so every monomial of $B$ uses at least one $x_i$ or $x_i'$ from each block. If $B$ is nonzero its weighted degree is at least $r$. It follows that every full-group invariant of degree at most $r$ belongs to $R$ and is even in $z$.

The subfield generated by all those invariants is contained in $K(W)(z^2)$. It cannot equal $K(V)^G$, because $K(V)/K(V)^G$ is finite Galois while $K(V)/K(W)(z^2)$ is a nontrivial purely inseparable extension. This proves $\beta_{\mathrm{field}}\ge r+1$.

## The exact field degree

On a quotient block the rational fixed field is generated by $x,x'$, $w=x\alpha+x'\gamma$, and $N=\alpha^2+U\alpha$. From those invariants, $\alpha$ has an equation of degree two, $\gamma$ is rational in $\alpha$ and the invariants, and $y,y'$ satisfy quadratic equations. For all blocks together this bounds the degree of the rational function field over the subfield of the listed invariants by $2^{3r}$. Faithfulness and the fixed-field theorem give exactly that degree for the full invariant field. Thus $K(W)^G$ is generated in degrees at most four.

There is a full-group invariant of degree $r+1$,
$$
f=u\prod_i x_i+\sum_i\gamma_i\prod_{j\ne i}x_j.
$$
The generators $a_i,c_i$ fix its terms. The change in the first term under $b_i$ agrees with the change in the $\gamma_i$ term, so they cancel in characteristic two. The coefficient of $u$ is nonzero. Hence $K(V)=K(W)(f)$, with $f$ transcendental over $K(W)$ and fixed by $G$. This gives $K(V)^G=K(W)^G(f)$, either by comparing the finite extension degrees or by applying the fixed-field theorem after adjoining a transcendental. The upper bound $\beta_{\mathrm{field}}\le r+1$ follows.

## The exact separating degree

Since $r\ge4$, every invariant of degree at most three belongs to $R$. In these degrees its dependence on the weight-two variables $\alpha_i,\gamma_i$ is linear, with coefficients $A_i,B_i$ that are polynomials of degree at most one in the $x$ variables. Invariance under $b_i$ gives
$$
S_i(x_i'A_i+x_i B_i)=0 .
$$
Since $R$ is a domain and $x_i,x_i'$ are relatively prime, $A_i=x_i C_i$ and $B_i=x_i'C_i$ for a constant $C_i$. Thus every degree-at-most-three invariant belongs to the algebra generated by the $x$ variables, by $Q$, and by the degree-three invariants $x_i\alpha_i+x_i'\gamma_i$.

On the subspace with every $x_i=x_i'=0$ and $z=0$, these generators vanish. The group fixes the entire subspace pointwise. Zero and any nonzero point there are distinct orbits that agree on every invariant of degree at most three. Therefore $\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}\ge4$, completing the proof.
