# Independent-alternating determinant lower bounds in every odd characteristic

Over any field of odd characteristic, polynomial families computing the determinant by arithmetic circuits invariant under independent even row and column permutations require exponential orbit size on an unbounded sequence of dimensions. The witnesses use only zero, one, and minus one. This extends Theorem 5.1 to all odd characteristics and resolves the scope explicitly left open after its proof. It concerns independent even row and column permutations. The division-free polynomial-size conjecture for simultaneous row and column symmetry remains open. [Dawar–Wilsenach, Lower Bounds for Symmetric Circuits for the Determinant, Section 5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.10986#S5.SS4) [ITCS 2022 version, after Theorem 19](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol215-itcs2022/LIPIcs.ITCS.2022.52/LIPIcs.ITCS.2022.52.pdf)

Take the balanced cubic bipartite base graphs of Section 5.2 with order `2m` and the `Omega(m)` well-connectedness used in Lemma 5.11. Give the two cross-gadget bit edges for each base edge `e` the same weight `t_e` in `{+1,−1}`. All local gadget edges have weight one. Write `B(t)` for the resulting biadjacency matrix of size `10m` for the weighted CFI graph of their Section 5.1. This uses their ten-vertex gadget with four inner vertices and six outer ports at each base vertex. Each port consists of two bit vertices and corresponds to one incident base edge.

## Weighted nonsingularity

List inner vertices before outer ones in the full weighted symmetric adjacency matrix. It has block form `[0 M; M^T Q]`, with `Q` the weighted permutation matrix of the disjoint cross-gadget bit edges. It is invertible exactly when `M Q^(-1) M^T` is invertible by the Schur complement because `Q` is invertible. On the four inner coordinates at each base vertex, use the Walsh basis consisting of the constant vector `u_0` and the three vectors `u_j` of values `(-1)^(s_j)` on parity labels `s`, one for each incident port `j`. The basis is orthogonal with squared norms four, hence a basis in every odd characteristic.

The local matrix `M` sends the sum of the two standard basis vectors of port `j` to `u_0` and their difference to `u_j`. The transpose sends `u_0` to twice the sum of all three port sums. It sends `u_j` to twice the difference at port `j`, with zero entries at all other ports, because fixing another bit leaves that Walsh sign equally often positive and negative. The matrix `Q^(-1)` carries each port sum or difference to its matching port across the base edge, multiplied by `t_e^(-1)`.

Consequently the Schur complement splits as the constant channels and the bit channels. On the constant channels it is twice the base adjacency matrix with edge weights `t_e^(-1)`. On the bit channels it pairs the two port differences for each edge by entries `2/t_e`, so this block is invertible. Thus `B(t)` is nonsingular precisely when the base biadjacency matrix with weights `t_e^(-1)` is nonsingular, since both the CFI graph and the base graph are bipartite and balanced.

The symbolic determinant of the base biadjacency matrix is a nonzero multiaffine polynomial in its edge weights. A perfect matching exists by regularity and Hall's theorem, and its monomial has coefficient `+1` or `−1` with no other matching inducing the same monomial. A nonzero multiaffine polynomial cannot vanish on the entire `{+1,−1}` cube over an odd field by two-point interpolation. Choose such a nonvanishing sign assignment, with inverse weights equal to the same signs. We now have `det B(t)≠0` over the given field.

## Restricted game and circuit lower bound

Use `B(t)` and `B'(t)` obtained by swapping the two column bit vertices of an outer port, as in Lemma 5.11. Their determinants are negatives, hence distinct. All of the source's coherent permutations preserve each base gadget and each outer port as a set. Their cycle automorphisms swap corresponding pairs of bit vertices across a set of base edges and adjust the inner labels. An internal gadget edge is always of weight one, and both cross links for a base edge always have the same nonzero weight. Therefore each cycle automorphism remains an automorphism of the weighted graph.

For precision, the invariant in their game is a coherent permutation that becomes an automorphism after the port-pair transposition at its distinguished defective base edge. The defective edge has both endpoints in the unpebbled large component. The same statement holds with weighted automorphisms. The source's two moves replace the distinguished port transposition by another, or insert a cycle automorphism between those two transpositions when the moved pebble hits the old port. These moves preserve the invariant with exact weights, as they are the same port-pair operations confined to the same base gadgets. The parity of the bijections is unchanged. Consequently the strategy from Lemma 5.11 is winning with `Omega(m)` pebbles in the same independent-alternating group game on these field-valued matrices. The symmetry lower-bound transfer in Theorem 4.13 accepts field-valued indexed functions, so the orbit lower bound follows. The proof is informal.

## Rational square-symmetric determinant in every characteristic

For comparison, an exact polynomial-size square-symmetric rational circuit with one division exists over every field. Form all powers of the input `X` through degree `2n−1` using equivariant matrix multiplication, and set `T_ij=tr(X^(i+j))` for `0≤i,j<n`. Its determinant is nonzero as a polynomial. On a diagonal matrix of algebraically independent diagonal entries it factors as the square of the Vandermonde determinant in every characteristic. Cayley–Hamilton gives `T c=−(tr(X^(n+i)))_i`, where `c` is the vector of the lower coefficients of the characteristic polynomial in increasing order. Use Cramer's rule for `c_0`, and multiply by the fixed sign `(-1)^n` to recover `det X`.

Each entry of the numerator and denominator matrices is an invariant trace scalar. Their row and column tags are power indices, fixed by the input-label group. Any ordinary division-free determinant circuit on these trace scalars therefore extends the actual input-label symmetries, since all its new gates may be fixed. The final one division is likewise symmetric. This is an exact rational-function identity. Evaluation at a numerical input by this circuit requires a nonzero discriminant. The proof is in prose. [Dawar–Wilsenach, Symmetric Arithmetic Circuits, Section 4](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v021a014/v021a014.pdf)
