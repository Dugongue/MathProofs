# Exact epimorphism counts through the odd-prime class reduction

For an odd prime \(p\), there is a deterministic polynomial-time single-query reduction from counting epimorphisms between Cayley-table groups of exponent dividing \(p\) and class at most \(p\) to counting epimorphisms between groups of exponent dividing \(p\) and class at most two. The query has polynomial table size, uses the same prime, and its answer is multiplied and divided by explicitly computable positive integers. This is a restricted counting reduction for Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar Open Question 2. [Open Question 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)

## 1. The two fibers over a diagram map

Use the compact diagrams and ordinary class-two compiler from the earlier epimorphism decision proof. Write \(A_G=\log(G/\gamma_p(G))\) and \(D_G=\log([G,G])\), with their two alternating bimaps as in that proof. An admissible diagram map is a pair of surjective linear maps \((\alpha,\delta)\) on \(A,D\) preserving the bimaps. The earlier witness argument lifts every such map to an epimorphism \(G\to H\). If the dimension checks for a surjective diagram fail, the answer is zero. The trivial cases can be counted directly. [Prior decision and witness reduction, Sections 1–3](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightThird/groups.md)

Fix one epimorphism inducing a given diagram. Every other epimorphism inducing that diagram differs from it by pointwise multiplication by an element of \(C_H=\gamma_p(H)\). This difference function is a homomorphism because \(C_H\) is central. Conversely, multiplication by any homomorphism \(G\to C_H\) preserves both induced diagram maps, since the homomorphism vanishes on \([G,G]\). The new map still surjects onto \(H/C_H\) and on \([H,H]\), which contains \(C_H\). Thus each diagram map has exactly
\[
 M=p^{\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(G/[G,G])\,\dim_{\mathbb F_p} C_H}
\]
original epimorphisms above it.

Let \(X_G,X_H\) denote the two encoded class-two groups. In the half-bracket model, an epimorphism induces surjections on the abelianization and derived spaces. The abelianization spaces were padded to the same dimension, so this map \(h\) is invertible. Each compatible \(h\) determines exactly one map of derived spaces, since the values of the bracket span the source derived space. Its surjectivity follows from the invertibility of \(h\) and the fact that the target bracket spans its own derived space. In addition there are \(p^{u v_H}\) arbitrary central shifts, where \(u\) is the common abelianization dimension and \(v_H=\dim [X_H,X_H]\). In coordinates an epimorphism with given maps \(h,k\) is exactly \((x,z)\mapsto(hx,kz+\lambda x)\) for a linear central shift \(\lambda\). This follows directly from the half-bracket law, or its class-two logarithm.

## 2. Padded core lifts

In the encoded abelianization, let \(C=A^+\oplus D^+\oplus\mathbb F_p u_0\) denote the common padded core. The three spaces in that decomposition are called core blocks. Each block has two independent markers. Contraction-image recovery in the earlier proof implies that \(h\) induces invertible maps on the two padded core roles and a nonzero scalar \(t\) on the anchor. The role maps \(T_A,T_D\) that induce a fixed diagram map are characterized by
\[
 \pi'_A T_A=t\alpha\pi_A,\qquad \pi'_D T_D=t\delta\pi_D.
\]
Indeed the brackets with the anchor imply these equalities, with the unpadded maps scaled by \(t^{-1}\) in the decoder. Conversely these equalities give the compatible maps of base center forms, by the bimaps and the two brackets with the anchor. For a role with padded dimension \(m\), target quotient dimension \(a\), and \(k=m-a\), the two maps onto the target quotient have \(k\)-dimensional kernels. Their invertible lifts are counted by \(|\mathrm{GL}(k,p)|p^{ka}\), from choosing an isomorphism on kernels and then a lift on a fixed complementary space. The number of choices of the full core map for each diagram map is therefore
\[
 L=(p-1)\prod_{R\in\{A,D\}} |\mathrm{GL}(m_R-a_R,p)|p^{(m_R-a_R)a_R},
\]
using the respective role dimensions. This count is independent of the chosen diagram map.

## 3. The private and heavy blocks

Let \(F_i\), for \(1\leq i\leq6\), denote the six source marker forms. For each marker write \(V_i\) for its own core block, \(W_i=V_i^*\) for its private dual block, and \(J_i\) for its heavy symplectic block with form \(\omega_i\). These blocks are independent among markers, even for two markers of the same core block. Write \(d_i=\dim V_i\), \(e_i=\dim J_i\). Write \(\mathcal B_G\) for the source space of base forms, pulled back from \(C\), and let
\[
 U_i=\{\eta\in\wedge^2 C^*: \eta|_{C_{-i}\times C_{-i}}=0\},
\]
where \(C_{-i}\) is the sum of the other core blocks.

For each core lift in Section 2 there is a chosen compatible map \(h_0\) sending the private dual blocks by inverse duality and fixing the heavy blocks. In particular it pulls each target marker back exactly to its source marker. Write any other map with this core lift uniquely as \(h=h_0 f\), with \(f\) acting as the identity on the core quotient. Such an \(f\) fixes every form pulled back from \(C\), so it imposes no extra conditions on target base forms. The rank bands from the earlier proof imply that compatibility of \(h\) is equivalent to asking independently for all six markers that \(f^*F_i\in\lambda_i F_i+\mathcal B_G\) for some \(\lambda_i\ne0\). The rank of a pure target marker lies in its own singleton band, all singleton bands are disjoint, and any combination using two or more heavy markers has rank exceeding those bands. Thus no further marker terms can appear. [Prior rank-band recovery](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightThird/groups.md)

On the private-plus-heavy subspace, the common radical of the six forms is the sum of the \(W_i\). The six heavy form radicals then force \(f\) to act on the heavy quotient by separate symplectic similitudes \(R_i\) on \(J_i\) with respective multipliers \(\lambda_i\). The core-to-private pairing forces its restriction to the private dual blocks to be multiplication by \(\lambda_i\) on each \(W_i\), with no off-diagonal private components. The remaining equations on a core input and a heavy input force each heavy-to-private component into \(W_i\) to vanish on \(J_j\) for \(j\ne i\). On \(J_i\) this component is an arbitrary map \(L_i:J_i\to W_i\). The same equations determine the core-to-\(J_i\) component \(Y_i\) uniquely by
\[
 \omega_i(Y_i(c),R_i z)=-\langle c_i,L_i z\rangle,
 \qquad z\in J_i,
\]
where \(c_i\) is the \(V_i\)-coordinate of \(c\). Nondegeneracy gives existence and uniqueness, and \(Y_i\) depends only on \(c_i\).

For the equation on two core inputs, let \(Z_i:C\to W_i\) be the core-to-private component of \(f\). Its contribution is the alternating form
\[
 (c,c')\longmapsto \langle c_i,Z_i(c')\rangle-\langle c'_i,Z_i(c)\rangle.
\]
The displayed linear map in \(Z_i\) is onto \(U_i\), since cross terms can be set independently and the alternating part of the restriction to \(V_i\) can be assigned using \(1/2\). Its kernel is the space of symmetric forms on \(V_i\), of dimension \(d_i(d_i+1)/2\). The remaining term \(\omega_i(Y_i(c),Y_i(c'))\) lies in \(U_i\) since it depends only on \(c_i,c'_i\). Requiring their sum to lie in \(\mathcal B_G\) therefore gives exactly \(p^{d_i(d_i+1)/2+\dim(U_i\cap\mathcal B_G)}\) choices of \(Z_i\). Choices for distinct markers are independent. The resulting block matrix is invertible because the core quotient, private restriction and heavy quotient are all invertible. Each unrestricted \(L_i\) has \(p^{d_i e_i}\) possibilities. Hence for each fixed core lift the number of compatible abelianization maps is
\[
 J=\prod_{i=1}^6 |\mathrm{GSp}(e_i,p)|\,
 p^{d_i e_i+d_i(d_i+1)/2+\dim(U_i\cap\mathcal B_G)}.
\]

Combining the counts gives the exact identity
\[
 \#\mathrm{Epi}(G,H)=\frac{M\,\#\mathrm{Epi}(X_G,X_H)}{LJp^{u v_H}}.
\]
Each intersection dimension is ordinary finite-field linear algebra on the source's explicitly known form space. The orders of the two matrix groups in the factors follow from counting ordinary and symplectic bases. For example \(|\mathrm{GL}(k,p)|=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(p^k-p^i)\) and \(|\mathrm{GSp}(2s,p)|=(p-1)p^{s^2}\prod_{i=1}^s(p^{2i}-1)\) for positive \(s\). All dimensions are \(O(\log_p(|G|+|H|))\), so all arithmetic and the query construction take polynomial time in table length. This is a paper proof.

## 4. Count-free Version I for factors with short power expressions

Fix constants \(d,c\). Suppose that in a fully refined direct product decomposition of \(G\) of order \(n\), every indecomposable factor \(S\) has a generating tuple of length at most \(d\) such that every element is a product of at most \((1+\log_2|S|)^c\) powers of those generators. If the decomposition is unique as a collection of subgroups, or if each factor is perfect or centerless, count-free group Weisfeiler–Leman Version I identifies \(G\) against arbitrary groups in constant dimension and \(O(\log\log n)\) rounds. The perfect-or-centerless case addresses Remark 1.7 of Johnson–Levet–Vojtěchovský–Widholm under the stated power-expression hypothesis. For any fixed \(d,b\), factors that are \(d\)-generated and have an abelian subgroup of index at most \(b\) meet the hypothesis after increasing the constant tuple length. [Remark 1.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06478#S1)

In the Version I language of equality and ternary multiplication, a power relation \(x^a=y\) for a fixed \(0\leq a\leq n\) has a constant-variable formula of rank \(O(1+\log\log(n+2))\). For large \(a\), set \(b=\lceil\sqrt a\rceil\) and \(a=bq+r\) with \(r<b\). Choose \(u,v,w\) and require \(u=x^b\), \(v=u^q\), \(w=x^r\) and \(y=vw\). Each recursive test has exponent at most \(b\) and two free variables, so the remaining variable names can be reused. Small exponents have constant-rank formulas. The earlier power-checking pebble lemma achieves the same bound by splitting binary exponents. A balanced multiplication tree tests a product of \(l\) powers with another \(O(1+\log l)\) rank and a constant number of reusable intermediate variable names. [Grochow–Levet, Section 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.11487#S7)

For each isomorphism type \(L\) of a target factor, choose one marking \(l_i\) and a power expression \(w_a\) for each \(a\in L\), reducing exponents modulo generator orders. A candidate tuple \(x_i\) generates a marked copy of \(L\) exactly when \(w_a(x)w_b(x)=w_{ab}(x)\) for all \(a,b\), the \(w_a(x)\) are all distinct, and \(w_{l_i}(x)=x_i\) for every \(i\). These are a conjunction of the preceding formulas. They define an injective homomorphism whose image contains all marked generators and lies in their subgroup. Membership in that subgroup is tested by a disjunction of the equations \(z=w_a(x)\). Formula rank is \(O(\log\log n)\) because each word has polylogarithmically many powered factors. Only the factor types of the fixed target group are used in these formulas.

Use the earlier direct-factor certificate on this marked copy \(S\) in an arbitrary group \(X\). Set \(C=C_X(S)\). Check \(X=SC\), closure of the set \(W\) of products of \(w(d)\) commutators from \(C\), and injectivity and purity of \(S\cap C\) in \(C/W\). Closure ensures \(W=[C,C]\). Purity asserts for each \(1\leq k\leq n\) that if \(z\in S\cap C\), \(y\in C\), and \(z^{-1}y^k\in W\), then \(z=a^k\) for some \(a\in S\cap C\). Membership, multiplication and power relations have the rank bound just proved. The number of extra simultaneously quantified elements is constant, since Nikolov–Segal's commutator width \(w(d)\) is constant. A subgroup passing the certificate splits off, because purity makes its center a direct summand of the abelianization of its centralizer. Every target factor passes, since its centralizer is a product of an abelian group and \(d\)-generated groups. [Direct-factor certificate](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightFourth/groups.md) [Nikolov–Segal, Theorem 1.7](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2007/165-1/p05)

In the unique-decomposition case, the certified subgroups in \(G\) are exactly its indecomposable direct factors. Distinct factors commute and cross homomorphisms into the other factor's center are zero, as a nonzero map would give a graph subgroup replacing one factor. This pair condition is expressible using two markings, the known types for the homomorphism test, and the marked membership and commutation tests. If it holds in the comparison group, projections onto its certified factors kill every other certified factor, since they commute and have no cross homomorphisms to centers. Hence they form an internal direct product. For each factor type \(L\), express that an element is a product of at most \(j\) elements from certified factors of that type by splitting the product into balanced halves. This adds \(O(\log j)\) rank with variable reuse. Existence of an element whose minimum such expression length is exactly \(j\) detects at least \(j\) factors of type \(L\). The bound \(j\leq\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor\) covers all factors. Equal multiplicities force the comparison product to have the same order as \(G\), hence to exhaust the comparison group and be isomorphic to \(G\).

For the perfect-or-centerless case, first certify and count only the perfect target types. A perfect indecomposable direct factor occurs as the same subgroup in every fully refined decomposition, since its abelianization is trivial. Impose pairwise commutation on the comparison group's distinct certified perfect factors and repeat the multiplicity test. Cross homomorphisms to centers vanish automatically from perfect factors. The product \(P_X\) of the certified perfect factors is itself a direct factor. The product of projections onto its factors is a retraction, and its kernel is the intersection of direct complements of the individual factors, so it centralizes \(P_X\). Membership in \(P_X\) is expressible by the balanced product of at most \(\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor\) certified elements of any perfect type, allowing the identity. The remaining factors in \(G/P_G\) are centerless, so their decomposition is unique. Apply the preceding unique-case formulas to the quotients of equal order. Replace their atomic equality and multiplication tests by the corresponding relations modulo \(P_X\), expressed using membership in \(P_X\). The replacement adds \(O(\log\log n)\) rank and constant variable count. Isomorphism of the quotients and equal perfect multiplicities imply isomorphism of the full groups. The fixed-variable formulas give the claimed count-free Version I bounds by the standard pebble-game characterization. [Version I game and Theorem 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06478)

If \(A\leq S\) is abelian of index at most \(b\), Schreier's lemma gives a bounded number of generators for \(A\). Mark these generators and the bounded-size transversal of \(A\) in \(S\). Every element is a product of a power of each abelian generator followed by one transversal element. The condition therefore applies even to the perfect centerless groups \(V_p\rtimes A_5\) of Sabatini, with \(V_p\) the four-dimensional deleted permutation module over \(\mathbb F_p\) for \(p>5\). Their fixed-vector space is zero. The groups are directly indecomposable, since in a direct decomposition the factor projecting trivially to \(A_5\) would lie in \(V_p\) and centralize a factor projecting onto \(A_5\). Given any \(k\) generators, vector coordinates of words of length at most \(L\) are combinations of at most \(60k\) vectors with coefficients between \(-L\) and \(L\), by expanding the semidirect product. Thus reaching every vector requires \((2L+1)^{60k}\geq p^4\). The family and the large-diameter mechanism have prior sources. This application uses the power-expression theorem despite the polynomial lower bound on ordinary diameter for bounded-size markings. [Sabatini, Example 9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10231-022-01276-w) [Sabatini, polynomial diameter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13577)
