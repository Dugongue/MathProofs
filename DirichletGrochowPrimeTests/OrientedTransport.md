# Constructing determinant-constrained tensor transport over prime fields

Fix a prime $p$ written in binary and put $F=\mathbb F_p$. Write a pair of explicit three-tensors as matrix families $M_S(w),M_T(w)$ from $U$ to $V$, linear in $w\in W$, with the three dimensions positive. The input also specifies an invertible projective map $[C_0]$ on $W$ and three nonzero determinant targets. A transporter is a triple $(A,B,C)$ with $B M_S(w)=M_T(Cw)A$ for every $w$ and $C$ in $[C_0]$.

**Theorem.** Under Dirichlet GRH, there is a sufficient criterion, computed in deterministic polynomial bit time from the raw input, under which a transporter can be constructed for every feasible prescribed determinant triple. Infeasibility for any triple is decidable unconditionally in the same time bound. The criterion needs no supplied field-algebra decomposition. The construction uses no polynomial factorization or factorization of $p-1$.

Let $E$ be the endomorphism algebra of the source matrix tuple, $J$ its radical, and $D$ a maximal torus of $E/J$ computed by de Graaf–Ivanyos. Here a torus is a unital finite étale $F$-subalgebra. Let $v$ be the pair of composition multiplicities of $U,V$ on a simple factor of $E/J$. For each such $v$ and exact field degree $d$, let $D_{v,d}$ collect the torus field factors with those labels and put $r_{v,d}=\dim_F(D_{v,d})/d$. Set $h=p-1$ and $s=(0,\dim V,\dim W)$. The criterion is $L_D=L$, where
$$
 L=\left\langle h\mathbb Z^3,\ s,\ (v,0)\text{ for all occurring }v\right\rangle_{\mathbb Z},
 \qquad
 L_D=\left\langle h\mathbb Z^3,\ s,\ r_{v,d}(v,0)\text{ for all nonzero }D_{v,d}\right\rangle_{\mathbb Z}.
$$
The proof also constructs a transporter for any individual target whose relative determinants lie in the monomial image of $L_D$, even when $L_D\subsetneq L$. A sufficient case is that for each $v$ the gcd of its nonzero $r_{v,d}$ is coprime to $p-1$.

## Uniform norms in an étale algebra

Let $D_0$ be an explicitly presented finite étale $F$-algebra, all of whose field components have known degree $d$. Put $q=p^d$. Given $a\in F^\times$, we construct a unit $b$ with
$$
 b^{(q-1)/(p-1)}=a\,1_{D_0}.
$$
Thus every component has norm $a$, even without knowing the primitive idempotents. The algebra norm of $b$ is $a^r$ when there are $r$ components.

For a prime $\ell\mid h$, write $h=\ell^t m$, $\gcd(\ell,m)=1$. Dirichlet GRH gives an $\ell$th nonresidue in $F$ below a quadratic bound in $\log p$ by Bach's subgroup theorem. Enumerate the bound, testing $x^{h/\ell}\ne1$ on units. Raising a successful $x$ to $m$ gives a scalar $w$ of order $\ell^t$.

The algebra $B_0=\ker(x\mapsto x^p-x)$ is computed by linear equations and is an unknown product of copies of $F$. For $x,y\in B_0$, the quantity $1-(x-y)^h$ is the idempotent of the components on which they are equal. Suppose $g\in B_0$ has order $\ell^t$ in every component and $z$ is componentwise in its cyclic group. Starting from $z_0=z$, use
$$
 z_i^{\ell^{t-1-i}}=(g^{\ell^{t-1}})^j,\quad j=0,\ldots,\ell-1
$$
to mark the $i$th digit $j$ by its equality idempotent. Multiply $z_i$ by the idempotent blend of $g^{-j\ell^i}$ to strip this digit and continue. This is the usual cyclic Sylow logarithm algorithm performed simultaneously on separate components. Each round takes polynomial bit time in $\ell,\log p$ and the algebra dimension.

To extract an $\ell$th root of a unit in $B_0$ known to be an $\ell$th power on each component, project it onto the Sylow part by the exponent $m(m^{-1}\bmod\ell^t)$. Apply the digit algorithm with $g=w$. The low digit is zero, and each higher digit $j$ contributes the root factor $w^{j\ell^{i-1}}$ on its marked components. On the prime-to-$\ell$ part, raise to $\ell^{-1}\bmod m$. A trivial part contributes $1$.

For $\ell$th root extraction in $D_0$, let $a'$ be a unit which is an $\ell$th power in every factor. Solve the $F$-linear equation $c^p=(a')^{h/\ell}c$. Any abstract $\ell$th root of $a'$ in one factor is a nonzero solution there. All solutions in that factor are scalar multiples of the root by $F$. For a basis element $c_i$ of the global solution space its support is the idempotent $c_i^{q-1}$. Every factor is in the support of some basis vector. Multiply each vector by its support with the earlier supports removed, then add. This gives a unit solution $c$. Now $y=c^\ell/a'$ satisfies $y^{h/\ell}=1$, so $y\in B_0$ and is an $\ell$th power in each copy of $F$. Divide $c$ by the root of $y$ computed in $B_0$. The result has $\ell$th power $a'$.

Set $s_d=(q-1)/h$. Since $s_d\equiv d\bmod h$, the primes of $h$ whose Sylow order increases from $F$ to a component are exactly those dividing $\gcd(d,h)$. Find them by trial division of $d$. Decompose $a$ into the corresponding primary components $a_\ell$ and a component of order dividing the remaining factor $h_0$ of $h$, by exponent projections from the integer Chinese remainder theorem. On the latter component the inverse of $d\bmod h_0$ gives the required scalar norm lift, since the norm of a scalar is its $d$th power.

For each $\ell$ involved let $t'=v_\ell(q-1)$. Starting at the scalar $w$ of order $\ell^t$, take $t'-t$ successive $\ell$th roots in $D_0$. All these roots exist because the order is below $\ell^{t'}$ before each extraction. Each root raises the order by $\ell$ on all components. The resulting element $u$ therefore has order $\ell^{t'}$ on every component and $u^{s_d}\in B_0$ has order $\ell^t$ on every component. Run the masked digit procedure with base $g=u^{s_d}$ and target $z=a_\ell$. Multiply the factors obtained by blending the corresponding powers of $u$ on the digit idempotents. The resulting element has componentwise norm $a_\ell$. Multiplying the contributions gives the asserted norm lift. For $p=2$ the sole norm target is $1$. The integer $q$ and all exponents have bit length polynomial in $d,\log p$.

## The full determinant image

Construct an ordinary transporter at $C_0$ by the Brooksbank–Luks constructive module-isomorphism algorithm for the two-vertex quiver with one arrow per basis vector of $W$. A failure excludes the entire projective class. Otherwise fix $(A_0,B_0,C_0)$. In this section $B_0$ denotes the mode transporter. The full set of transporters in the specified projective class is
$$
 (A_0P,\lambda B_0Q,\lambda C_0),\qquad
 (P,Q)\in E^\times,\quad \lambda\in F^\times,
$$
where $E$ is the algebra obtained by the linear equations $Q M_S(w)=M_S(w)P$. Indeed substituting gives a transporter, and division by the fixed transporter and the projective scalar reverses the construction.

For each simple factor $M_{m_i}(K_i)$ of $E/J$, let $v_i=(\mu_{U,i},\mu_{V,i})$ count the copies of its simple module on the associated graded spaces of the radical filtrations of $U,V$. A unit of $E/J$ lifts to a unit of $E$ by nilpotence of $J$. Its determinant on either space is the product over the factors of the determinants on the composition factors. There the determinant equals the norm from $K_i$ to $F$ of the matrix determinant on $K_i^{m_i}$. Both the field norms and matrix determinants are surjective and the factors vary independently. Thus the relative determinant image is the monomial image of the columns $(v_i,0)$ and $s$. Division of the target by $(\det A_0,\det B_0,\det C_0)$ gives its relative target.

For the unconditional decision, compute the source radical and multiplicity idempotents as in the fixed-projective-mode determinant-coset result. For completeness, the radical algorithm starts with an ideal $I$ containing $J$. For $x\in I$, inspect the coefficients of $z^{p^a}$ in $\det(1-zL_x)$, with $L_x$ left multiplication on the original regular module. If $I/J$ is nonzero then the least $a$ for which one such coefficient is nonzero on a basis is the least $p$-valuation among the regular-module multiplicities of active simple factors. Applying inverse Frobenius and a minus sign to that coefficient gives the sum of the reduced traces, then field traces, on exactly those factors, with the corresponding reduced nonzero multiplicities. This follows by expanding the reciprocal characteristic polynomial over the composition factors. The traces on distinct factors have independent support, and on each included factor the trace pairing is nondegenerate. The kernel of the resulting pairing $(x,y)\mapsto\tau(xy)$ on $I$ therefore drops those factors while retaining $J$. If no inspected coefficient remains, $I=J$. The algorithm terminates in at most $\dim E$ steps.

The center of $E/J$ is a product of fields. As modules over this center, the component ranks of $E/J$ and the associated graded spaces for $U,V$ are $m_i^2,m_i\mu_{U,i},m_i\mu_{V,i}$. Compute these ranks with center-matrix elimination on finite presentations. On a pivot, find the identity of the ideal it generates by linear equations. It indicates where the pivot is invertible. Work separately on this piece and its zero complement. These splits partition the center factors and the number of pieces is bounded by the dimension. The rank labels give the multiplicity vectors $v_i$ and the central idempotents for their strata by polynomially many field operations. Thus the calculation does not need actual field components.

With $F^\times$ identified abstractly with $\mathbb Z/h\mathbb Z$ by any generator, the full image is $L/h\mathbb Z^3$. A Smith form of the columns with $h\mathbb Z^3$ adjoined gives unimodular row operations and positive diagonal entries $b_i\mid h$. Transform the relative target by the row operations acting as monomial maps. Membership is the test that the $i$th resulting coordinate has $(h/b_i)$th power $1$ for each $i$. Neither a generator nor a root extraction is needed to decide this.

## The torus construction and lift

Apply de Graaf–Ivanyos to the left regular representation of $E/J$ to obtain a maximal torus $D$. A maximal torus contains the center, and after extending to an algebraic closure becomes a product of full diagonal algebras in the matrix factors. Consequently the determinant on each standard $K_i^{m_i}$ of a torus element is its norm from the torus block to $K_i$. The composite norm to $F$ is therefore the product of its component field norms, each carrying the same weight $v_i$. This requires no matrix units or explicit field decomposition.

The idempotents from the multiplicity stratification cut $D$ into pieces indexed by $v$. Within each such piece form, for each $d=1,\ldots,\dim_F D$, the ideal generated by the image of the $F$-linear map $x\mapsto x^{p^d}-x$. A field component has zero image precisely when its degree divides $d$. Subtract the identity of the ideal from the identity of the piece to mark those factors. Subtract the marks of exact degrees properly dividing $d$ to recover the exact-degree idempotent. These pieces are $D_{v,d}$, and their component count is $\dim_F(D_{v,d})/d$. All powers and ideal computations have polynomial bit cost.

To realize parameter $a\in F^\times$ for the column $r_{v,d}(v,0)$, solve the uniform component norm problem in $D_{v,d}$, put identity on all other torus pieces, and lift arbitrarily to $E$. The determinant calculation gives exactly the indicated column raised to parameter $a$. The scalar column lifts directly. These corrections can be composed in any fixed order.

For a relative target in this smaller monomial image, use Smith form with both row and column multipliers on the matrix of the columns $s$ and $r_{v,d}(v,0)$, without the lattice columns $h\mathbb Z^3$. The multipliers give invertible monomial changes of coordinates. Zero rows require target $1$. The positive diagonal equations are $z^b=a$ in $F^\times$. The nonzero Smith entries are polynomially bounded as integers, since there are three rows and each entry of the original matrix is polynomially bounded. In detail, the product of the nonzero Smith entries is the gcd of the nonzero minors of maximal rank, whose absolute values are at most $6\max(1,\max_{ij}|a_{ij}|)^3$ for original matrix entries $a_{ij}$.

Set $b'=\gcd(b,h)$. Solvability is equivalent to $a^{h/b'}=1$. Factor $b'$ by trial division and extract the successive prime roots by the prime-field case of the root algorithm. Any choice of intermediate prime root remains in the required subsequent power image. In the cyclic group of order $h$, the kernel of the first prime power map is contained in the remaining power image since the full exponent $b'$ divides $h$. Choose $u$ with $bu\equiv b'\bmod h$ and raise the resulting $b'$th root to $u$. Writing the Smith identity as $P A Q=D_{\mathrm{Smith}}$, apply the monomial map $Q$ to the solved Smith-domain parameters to obtain the original parameters. The case $h=1$ is trivial.

The two lattice indices after adjoining $h\mathbb Z^3$ test their equality, because $L_D\subseteq L$. Compute the matrix corrections and verify the original tensor and determinant equations. The norm algorithm is polynomial in the written dimensions and $\log p$ because the primes it uses divide one of the computed degrees or a bounded Smith entry. The only use of Dirichlet GRH is the prime-field nonresidue search.

## Examples and remaining scope

For $U=V=W=L$, where $L/F$ is a field extension and $M_S(w)$ is left multiplication by $w$, the identity slice forces $P=Q$. Commuting with every multiplication operator then forces multiplication by $P(1)$. Thus $E=L$, with one degree stratum of count one and multiplicity vector $(1,1)$. The criterion applies with all mode dimensions growing. A determinant target can require a genuine norm lift. For example, when $p$ is odd and $[L:F]$ is even, a common nonsquare determinant on $U,V$ can be realized by the norm of a field element even though no base scalar has that determinant.

For a repeated-factor example use the same regular action and the full slice space of the étale algebra $F^2\times\mathbb F_{p^2}^3$. Again $E$ is the algebra itself. The degree strata share multiplicity $(1,1)$ and have counts two and three. They jointly recover the full column $(1,1,0)$ even when $p-1$ is a multiple of six and neither stratum alone suffices. With just $F^2$ and odd $p$, its sole lifted column is $2(1,1,0)$. Holding the third determinant fixed, the full determinant image can have a nonsquare common correction outside this constructed subgroup.

A complete polynomial-size list of projective candidates also gives a deterministic search algorithm when the stated criterion holds. This follows by ordinary comparison and the unconditional determinant decision on each candidate, followed by the norm lift on a feasible candidate. The theorem begins with a specified projective class or such a list. Unrestricted same-field reduction of special-linear tensor isomorphism to ordinary tensor isomorphism remains open.

The earlier fixed-projective-mode theorem gives unconditional decision by computing the full determinant coset. Its decision procedure does not supply a uniform witness algorithm in variable prime size. The extension proved here is the explicit correction from raw data, via simultaneous norms in unidentified étale factors and the equality of the two computed Smith images. The proof is informal. The companion code verifies all 75 nonzero norm targets across 12 quotient presentations with two hidden field components of each common degree. Each computed norm is independently evaluated modulo both irreducible factors.
