# Small-prime certificates for determinant-constrained module isomorphism

Let $A,A'$ be explicit rational matrix tuples on $n$-dimensional spaces $V,V'$. Let $W_j\subseteq V$, $W'_j\subseteq V'$ be corresponding named subspaces with ordered bases, for $1\le j\le m$, where $m\ge1$. We prescribe nonzero rational determinants $d_j$ for the restrictions of an invertible intertwiner $X\colon V\to V'$. Include the ambient determinant by marking the whole space. The input can supply one ordinary marked intertwiner $X_0$, or one can compute it by the polynomial-time module-isomorphism algorithm over number fields. To encode marks for that algorithm, adjoin their spaces, a block projection for each space, and one inclusion arrow from each mark to the ambient space. Extend the original tuple by zero on the new blocks. An intertwiner is block diagonal by the projections, and commuting with the inclusion arrows says precisely that each new block is the corresponding restriction. If this preliminary test finds no ordinary marked intertwiner, the rational answer is NO. The prime schedule below applies to the remaining case with an ordinary marked intertwiner, so the exponent matrix and normalized targets are defined. [Chistov, Ivanyos and Karpinski](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf)

Put $E=\{Z:ZA_i=A_iZ,\ ZW_j\subseteq W_j\}$ and $J=\operatorname{rad}E$. Assume $E/J=\prod_{i=1}^u S_i$ with each $S_i$ central simple over $\mathbb Q$. This is equivalent to the center of $E/J$ being $\mathbb Q^u$. Write $r_i^2=\dim_{\mathbb Q}S_i$, choose lifts $L_i\in E$ of its primitive central idempotents, and set
$$
 M_{ji}=\operatorname{tr}(L_i|W_j)/r_i,\qquad
 a_j=d_j/\det(X_0|W_j).
$$
Assume $M$ has full row rank and positive Smith invariants $2^{s_j}$, with $s=\max_j s_j$. Let $UMT$ be a Smith form with unimodular multipliers $U,T$. For each $s_j\ge3$ impose $2^{s_j}\mid\sum_\ell U_{j\ell}v_2(a_\ell)$. This is a directly computable necessary test for rational solvability.

**Theorem.** There is a deterministically computable integer $B>0$ of polynomial bit length such that, assuming Dirichlet GRH, the prescribed rational transporter exists exactly when it exists over $\mathbb R$ and over each $\mathbb F_p$ with $p\nmid B$ and
$$
 p\le C4^s\log^2(2^{s+1}B)
$$
for an effective absolute $C$. The real test is linear over $\mathbb F_2$. Each failing good prime has a Hilbert-like Ideal Proof System refutation whose circuit size is polynomial in the input bit length and $\log p$, using the written marked intertwining and determinant equations and the field equations. The prime bound has polynomial bit length. If $2^s$ is polynomially bounded in the input length, the entire test and production of a circuit refutation at a failing prime take deterministic polynomial bit time. In particular, Smith invariants 1 and 2 require no $2$-adic valuation conditions.

## The exponent matrix from the given module

Every ordinary marked transporter has the form $X_0Z$ with $Z\in E^\times$. Over an algebraic closure, each $W_j$ has a composition series as an $E$-module. The simple module of factor $S_i$ has dimension $r_i$. The lift $L_i$ acts as the identity on factors of type $i$ and as zero on all others. Thus $M_{ji}$ counts these factors, and is independent of the lift. The matrix of $Z$ is block triangular along the composition series, which gives the polynomial identity on $E$
$$
 \det(Z|W_j)=\prod_i\operatorname{Nrd}_{S_i}(\bar Z_i)^{M_{ji}} .
$$
This identity holds for all $Z$, including singular elements. Units lift across the nilpotent ideal $J$.

By Hasse–Schilling–Maass, $\operatorname{Nrd}(S_i^\times)=\mathbb Q^\times$ when $S_i\otimes\mathbb R$ is split and $\mathbb Q_{>0}$ otherwise. Over $\mathbb R$ there is no constraint on the magnitude of a norm. Since the rows of $M$ are independent, the logarithms of positive target magnitudes are always attainable. The real test is therefore $M\epsilon=\operatorname{signbits}(a)$ over $\mathbb F_2$, with $\epsilon_i=0$ on nonsplit factors. If this sign test passes and the monomial equations $x^M=a$ have a solution in unrestricted $\mathbb Q^\times$, their absolute values give an admissible magnitude vector, and the real test gives an admissible sign vector. Combining the two and applying the reduced-norm theorem gives a rational solution in $E^\times$. [Reduced-norm theorem](https://msp.org/pjm/2018/292-2/pjm-v292-n2-p06-p.pdf)

The real splitting test uses rational linear algebra. The regular trace pairing $\operatorname{tr}(L_{uv})$ on one factor is a positive multiple of the reduced-trace pairing. On $M_r(\mathbb R)$ its positive index minus negative index is $r$. On $M_k(\mathbb H)$ it is $-2k=-r$. Pairs of opposite off-diagonal entries contribute equally in both signs. One diagonal real coordinate contributes one positive direction, while one diagonal quaternion has quadratic part proportional to $x_0^2-x_1^2-x_2^2-x_3^2$. Rational symmetric elimination computes this signature.

## Effective good reduction

Compute $E$ by exact linear elimination. The radical $J$ is the kernel of its regular trace pairing $\operatorname{tr}(L_x L_y)$. Indeed, over an algebraic closure the pairing is the sum of positive integer multiples of the matrix trace pairings of the simple factors, by a composition series of the regular module. All factors occur, because the regular module surjects onto the semisimple quotient. All those weights are nonzero in characteristic zero. This computes $J$ and the structure constants of $S=E/J$. Compute the center of $S$ by commutator equations. A basis $z_1,\ldots,z_u$ of the split center admits an element $\sum_{\ell=1}^u h^{\ell-1}z_\ell$ with all $u$ distinct coordinates for some $h\in\{0,\ldots,\binom{u}{2}(u-1)\}$, since each pair of coordinate maps excludes at most $u-1$ values. Test the condition by the degree of its minimal polynomial. Deterministic rational polynomial factorization and Lagrange interpolation give its primitive central idempotents. The multipliers $U,T$ are obtained by Kannan–Bachem's polynomial-bit Smith algorithm with unimodular multipliers, not by uncontrolled integer elimination. [Rational polynomial factorization](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01457454) · [Smith form with multipliers](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

Choose exact rational bases and products as follows. Use a basis of $E$ starting with a basis of $J$, a basis of $Z(S)$, and a basis of each $e_iS$ with its rank witness. Store the restrictions to each mark in its specified basis. Store nonzero pivot minors for the input frame, the linear equations computing $E$, every intermediate basis restriction, the commutator equations computing $Z(S)$, the ranks of the operators of multiplication by $e_i$ on $S$, and the Gram determinant of the regular trace form on $S$. A nonzero pivot and the rational rank identity ensure the exact same rank after reduction for each such matrix. Include also the input entry denominators, the denominators of all stated structure constants, idempotents, restrictions and changes of bases, and the nonzero numerator and denominator of $\det X_0$ and each $a_j$. Form $B$ as the product of these denominators and absolute values of the nonzero pivot numerators and other indicated numerators, times $\max(2,n,\dim E)!$. This can be done in polynomial bit time. All ranks and rational matrices involved have polynomial dimensions. Fraction-free linear algebra, determinant bounds, and powers with exponents bounded by the algebra dimension bound their heights polynomially.

At $p\nmid B$, the reduced $E_p$ is the actual marked endomorphism algebra of the reduced tuple, by ranked linear elimination, and the reduction of $X_0$ is an ordinary transporter. The reduced $J_p$ is an ideal and is nilpotent: all product identities for strings of $\dim E$ basis elements of $J$ hold over the integral localization and reduce. The quotient $S_p$ has nondegenerate regular trace pairing. An element in its radical has nilpotent product with every element and hence lies in the kernel of that pairing. Therefore $S_p$ is semisimple and $J_p$ is exactly $\operatorname{rad} E_p$. The reductions of the $e_i$ are orthogonal central idempotents that sum to one, and their multiplication-operator ranks remain $r_i^2>0$. The center still has dimension $u$ by its commutator rank witness. These $u$ orthogonal nonzero central idempotents already span it, so each $e_i S_p$ is a central simple factor of dimension $r_i^2$, with no two such factors merged and no one further decomposed. Wedderburn's theorem for finite division rings identifies it with $M_{r_i}(\mathbb F_p)$. The trace of $L_i|W_j$ in either characteristic is the integer $r_i$ times the corresponding number of simple factors, reduced in the finite field. These two nonnegative integers are at most $n<p$, hence equal. The marked determinant equations over the good residue field are consequently exactly $x^M=\bar a$ over $\mathbb F_p^\times$.

## The small-prime test

Smith multipliers act as monomial group automorphisms, yielding the scalar equations $y_j^{2^{s_j}}=b_j$ with $b_j=\prod_\ell a_\ell^{U_{j\ell}}$. This uses binary exponents and never needs expansion of $b_j$ as a rational numerator and denominator. If $b_j$ is a rational power, its rational root is a unit at each good prime, since $b_j$ is such a unit. This shows that the rational monomial condition implies every good modular condition regardless of the denominators of a particular rational transporter.

For a failed row, write $b_j=c^{2^k}$ with maximal $k<s_j$, choosing $c>0$ when $k\ge1$. The square class of this nonsquare $c$ is supported on primes dividing $2B$. Its quadratic character is nontrivial on the subgroup $1\bmod 2^{k+1}$. For $k=0$ this follows from nonsquareness. For $k=1$, a positive nonsquare has no quadratic conductor dividing 4. For $k\ge2$, the valuation hypothesis makes $v_2(c)$ even, so positivity and nonsquareness require an odd prime in the square-free part. Apply the smoothed Dirichlet-GRH estimate to $p\equiv1\bmod 2^{k+1}$, $\chi_c(p)=-1$, and $p\nmid B$. Its indicator on units has mean $1/(2\varphi(2^{k+1}))$, an expansion in Dirichlet characters with sum of absolute coefficients at most one, and common modulus dividing $2^{k+3}B$. The estimate yields a qualifying prime below $C4^k\log^2(2^{k+1}B)$. In $\mathbb F_p^\times$ the discrete logarithm of $c$ in a primitive generator is odd. The discrete logarithm of $b_j$ is therefore divisible by $2^k$ and indivisible by $2^{k+1}$, a statement well defined modulo $p-1$. The modular row fails. This is the scalar argument for supplied linked monomial systems. The actual exponent matrix, stable primes, real correction and proof transformer here are additional. [Prior prime estimate and scalar theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/RiemannGrochowTransfers/PrimeSpecialization.md)

## A circuit refutation from any good prime where the test fails

Choose a failed Smith row and set $e=\gcd(2^{s_j},p-1)$. The $2^{s_j}$-th powers and $e$-th powers in $\mathbb F_p^\times$ coincide. Reduce that row of $U$ modulo $e$, obtaining $\beta_\ell\in[0,e-1]$. Then $c=\prod_\ell a_\ell^{\beta_\ell}$ is still not an $e$-th power in the residue field, and every $t_i=\sum_\ell\beta_\ell M_{\ell i}$ is divisible by $e$.

Project the unknown matrix $X$ linearly onto the space of marked intertwiners, obtaining $\widehat X$, where every entry of $X-\widehat X$ is a scalar linear combination of the written equations. Take $Z=X_0^{-1}\widehat X$. The restriction determinant of an arbitrary $X$ is written using the source frame and a fixed left inverse of the target frame. On projected intertwiners this is the actual restriction. Since the reduced semisimple factor maps $\rho_i$ are linear maps to $M_{r_i}(\mathbb F_p)$, the identity proved above gives a polynomial identity
$$
 P(Z)=\prod_\ell\det(Z|W_\ell)^{\beta_\ell}
     =\left(\prod_i\det(\rho_i(Z))^{t_i/e}\right)^e .
$$
Its normalized root $R$, given by the product inside parentheses, has a polynomial-size arithmetic circuit by binary exponents and division-free determinant circuits. This size bound does not require computing the factor maps. For an explicit deterministic construction when $e$ is polynomially bounded, track all coefficients of $P(I+\tau(Z-I))$ up to its degree $D\le e mn$ using truncated polynomial arithmetic. The true $e$-th root has constant term 1. At each degree its unknown coefficient appears in the $e$-th power with factor $e\ne0\bmod p$. Determine it using an $e$-th power of the already known truncation, and continue to degree $D$. Uniqueness of the normalized truncated power series and the degree bound prove exactness. Evaluate at $\tau=1$. [Division-free determinant circuits](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(84)90018-8)

At any division-free circuit's output, its difference between two input lists is a circuit-linear combination of the coordinate differences. This follows by keeping both values at each gate and propagating their differences under addition and multiplication. Apply it first to substitute $\widehat X$ for $X$ in the written determinant equations, then to substitute $a_\ell$ for the determinants of $Z$ in $P(Z)$. Thus $R^e-c$ is a circuit-linear combination of the original equations. Frobenius gives a circuit-linear combination for $R^p-R$ in terms of the coordinate field equations $x^p-x$. It is additive, and a multiplication gate uses $(FG)^p-FG=G^p(F^p-F)+F(G^p-G)$. Constants contribute zero. All powers take polynomial circuit size by binary exponentiation.

Write $v=(p-1)/e$ and $S_v(u,w)=\sum_{i=0}^{v-1}u^{v-1-i}w^i$. Then $c^v\ne1$, and in the polynomial ring
$$
 R(R^e-c)S_v(R^e,c)-(R^p-R)=(1-c^v)R.
$$
The sum has a binary recursion, $S_{2v}=(u^v+w^v)S_v$ and $S_{2v+1}=uS_{2v}+w^{2v}$. Multiply the identity by $R^{e-1}/(1-c^v)$, subtract $R^e-c$, and divide by $c$ to obtain 1. Replacing written generators by placeholders yields the required Hilbert-like IPS circuit. The construction checks a genuine polynomial identity, including at inputs outside the solution set. It uses no new proof variables beyond placeholders. To find a refuting prime in the polynomial schedule, enumerate the prime bound and check the scalar rows by modular exponentiation, then construct the circuit just described. [Ideal Proof System](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.3820)

## Scope

For an actual coupled example take rational Jordan chains of lengths 1 and 3 at distinct eigenvalues. Mark the sum of the first chain with the first level of the second chain, and mark the ambient space. The exponent matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, of Smith type $(1,2)$. Solvability is exactly the square condition on the ratio of normalized determinants. Repeating chains of the same type produces nonscalar matrix factors and a nonzero radical with the same exponent matrix. The known single-operator determinant formula anticipates the norm idea. The theorem applies it to raw simultaneous marked modules, derives uniform good primes including for central simple factors nonsplit over the rationals, and supplies polynomial-size residue-field IPS refutations. It makes no claim on sparse ordinary Polynomial Calculus size or unrestricted tensor isomorphism.
