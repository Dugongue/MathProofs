# Cyclotomic Galois transport of finite relational structures

Assume GRH for Dirichlet L-functions. Let the conductor m be given in binary. An element of the cyclotomic field K = ℚ(ζₘ) is given as a sparse sum of powers of ζₘ with binary exponents and rational coefficients. The vertex set of each structure is a finite subset of K given by these sparse representations, with the common signature and every relation of arbitrary arity explicitly listed. Given a unary bound B, there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm that reports if the joint Galois closure of the labels has more than B elements. Otherwise it decides whether one automorphism of K carries the first structure to the second. In the positive case it outputs a unit residue inducing a witness, generators for the full arithmetic transporter coset, and the number of distinct induced permutations of the label closure that transport the structure.

## Recovering the action

For m = 1 the action is trivial. For m ≥ 2 write U for the units modulo m. The Galois action sends ζₘ to ζₘ raised to the corresponding unit. Under Dirichlet GRH, Bach's bound gives a prime coprime to m and at most 3(log m)² outside each proper subgroup of U. Thus the primes coprime to m and at most that bound generate U. Find all coprime primes up to 3(1 + ceil(log₂ m))² by trial division and gcd. This interval contains the analytical bound. [Bach](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1990-1023756-8) · [Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan, Section 1.1](https://www.math.ksu.edu/about/personal/xiannan/papers/QuaResL1ver2Sound.pdf)

Apply these generators by multiplying exponents modulo m. Test equality of sparse labels by clearing denominators in their difference and applying unconditional sparse cyclotomic identity testing. Clearing denominators and conjugating do not increase representation size beyond a polynomial. Keep distinct label values in a breadth-first search started at both input label sets. If B + 1 distinct values appear, these certify oversize. Otherwise the final finite set W is closed under the chosen generators and their inverses, hence under U. Their explicit permutations of W generate the image G of U. Each generator keeps its residue lift. [Balaji, Perifel, Shirmohammadi, and Worrell, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13179)

## Transporting the listed tuples

Include the vertex set as a unary relation. Within any subgroup of G, one can find a transporter from an ordered tuple to another by successively prescribing the images of its entries. For each entry traverse its orbit under the subgroup fixing all previous entries, and update a current coset representative and that subgroup by one point stabilization. Each point orbit has at most |W| elements. This also computes a tuple stabilizer. Use standard deterministic stabilizer-chain algorithms for explicit permutations to prune redundant generators, express constructive membership, and compute orders. Keep a residue lift for every stored group word by multiplying and inverting the original residues modulo m. [Seress, Permutation Group Algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511546549)

For each relation use pairwise tuple transporter queries to partition only its listed source and target tuples into their G-orbits. A cell contains source and target lists P and Q. Unequal cardinalities give rejection. For a nonempty cell fix x in P, and let aH be the current candidate coset, initially all of G. For each listed y in Q try to find h in H with ahx = y. Verify directly whether ahP = Q. All elements sending x to y agree on the whole G-orbit of x, since G is abelian. Consequently testing one h suffices for that entire fiber, even for arbitrarily long tuples.

If no h succeeds, reject. Otherwise let h₀ be one successful candidate. The new coset has representative ah₀ and subgroup generated by the stabilizer Hₓ and all differences h₀⁻¹hᵧ for successful representatives hᵧ. Indeed the successful elements in H are exactly the union of hᵧHₓ, and translation by h₀⁻¹ identifies this union with the subgroup of H preserving P. Iterate the cells and compute the final subgroup order. Store at most ceil(log₂(|W|!)) generators of each subgroup by retaining a new generator only when it is outside the group already generated. Each addition at least doubles the subgroup order. The number of queries and checks is polynomial in the explicit tuple input and |W|. No full tuple orbit is enumerated.

## Lifting the coset to arithmetic residues

To recover the kernel of U → G, successively stabilize all points of W in the arithmetic group. Maintain a list L of lifted generators for the current image and a list C of generators already known to act trivially on all of W, with L and C together generating the current arithmetic subgroup. At each point, traverse its orbit with the image of L and keep a lifted transversal. Schreier's lemma gives the next arithmetic stabilizer from the lifted Schreier products and C. The products involving C add only C itself because every residue in C fixes the points and commutes with the transversal. When a lifted generator u is redundant in the image, express its image as a word in retained generators and lift the word to v. Append uv⁻¹ to C and drop u from L. This preserves generation. Each iteration adds polynomially many residues to C and C is never used for orbit traversal. After the last point the image is trivial and C generates the full kernel N.

Adjoin C to lifts of the final transporter subgroup generators. The lift of the representative and this generating list give the full coset in U. If the final image subgroup has size h, the induced-transporter count is h and the arithmetic-transporter count divided by Euler's totient φ(m) is h/|G|.

## Field operations

For input label lists A and D let H_A and H_D be their pointwise stabilizers in G. Their generated fields lie in the fixed field Kᴺ with Galois group G. Subgroup inclusion gives field inclusion and equality. The degrees of ℚ(A), ℚ(A)ℚ(D), and ℚ(A) ∩ ℚ(D) are the indices of H_A, the pointwise stabilizer of the combined list, and the subgroup generated by H_A and H_D. The computation permits degrees exponential in the number of labels since it outputs them in binary.

For any of these corresponding subgroups H the Galois group is the abelian quotient G/H. Its elementary divisors follow from the orders of its successive subgroups of pʲ-th powers. These groups are the images of the subgroups generated by H and the pʲ-th powers of the generators of G. The base-p logarithm of the ratio of two successive orders gives the number of p-primary cyclic factors of exponent at least j. Primes p at most |W| suffice since |G| divides |W|!. This computes the degrees and Galois group structures without expanding field bases.

## Scope and checks

A supplied generating set of U enables all later steps unconditionally. Direct enumeration is polynomial in m, which may be exponential in its bit length. Factorization of m and generators of its prime-power unit groups also suffice via the Chinese remainder theorem. An unconditional randomized alternative samples uniform units. There are at most |U| ceil(log₂ m) maximal subgroups of finite abelian U, each of index at least two. A union bound gives high-probability generation in O(log m + log(1/ε)) draws. Gcd rejection sampling has expected polynomial cost since with r distinct prime factors the ith smallest prime is at least i+1, which gives φ(m)/m ≥ 1/(r+1). The conditional result supplies deterministic coverage without factoring m.

A positive witness and an oversize certificate can be verified without GRH. The small generating set gives conditional exactness for the whole transporter and for rejection. This addresses arithmetic transport of labeled structures. Grochow–Urisman's Question 1.9 asks for ordinary graph isomorphism around symmetry-characterized graphs, for which no such arithmetic labels are supplied. Fieker's sparse representation and sparse-CIT supply prior ingredients. The compact high-arity transporter and arithmetic witness algorithm above are the contribution. Priority remains unestablished. [Fieker](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2007.10129012) · [Grochow–Urisman, Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26244v2#S1.SS3)

The proof is informal. Finite controls on 2,160 monomial-label pairs over twelve small conductors gave the same transporter as exhaustive unit enumeration, with 754 positive and 1,406 negative cases. The controls exercised coset refinement, using exhaustive small-group operations, and did not exercise sparse-CIT. For a size contrast take the conductor to be the product of the fourteen odd primes from 3 through 47. A tuple containing one primitive root of each of those orders has joint label closure of size 312 and tuple orbit of size 85,287,729,364,992,000 by the Chinese remainder theorem.
