# Determinant cancellation for wide three-tensors over F₃

These are informal proofs. The input field is F₃ and the tensors are explicit dense arrays. A sign is written as an additive bit. The two-sign comparison imposes two determinant-one conditions on a single transporter. The special-linear comparison imposes all three. Write L for the input bit length.

The results below allow all three mode sizes and all three pair-adjoint commutant dimensions to grow. They give sufficient structural conditions for a single two-sign comparison or a direct polynomial-time decision.

## 1. Two tools for dropping one sign

If an explicit nonzero vector b is known to occur as the three signs of a source or target automorphism, a transporter sign set is invariant under adding b. The set contains zero iff its projection along b contains zero. If all transporters satisfy a known nonzero character equation λ(s)=c, a negative value c=1 rules out the all-positive triple. For c=0 keep two complementary rows along with that identity. An ordinary tensor-system mark enforcing λ(s)=0 on actual transporters likewise allows two complementary signs to be retained in the same instance. These facts use the previous projection and signed compilation theorems. A product of designated determinants can be read through the direct sum of the associated roles with the canonical inclusions. [Sign projections](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/SignProjections.md) · [Retaining two signs](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignChain/RetainedSigns.md)

For a finite-dimensional explicitly represented F₃-algebra A, compute its radical J and its quotient ∏_j Mat_(d_j)(K_j) with explicit maps. On any module, the determinant of a unit is the product of Norm_(K_j/F₃)(det u_j) raised to the total multiplicity of the natural simple for j. This follows on the radical layers, and the kernel 1+J acts unipotently. The norms are onto and quotient units lift, so the entire sign subgroup on any fixed list of such roles is generated by the multiplicity parity vectors. This works for left or right modules. [Ivanyos–Qiao, finite-field algebra algorithms](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

We use constructive module decomposition and isomorphism over F₃. For isomorphism, in the endomorphism algebra of the direct sum of two modules, test whether the two summand idempotents have equal ranks in every matrix factor of the radical quotient. If so, construct inverse corner maps there, lift them and correct their compositions by corner inverses. The lifts then give an isomorphism. To decompose a module, lift primitive diagonal matrix-unit idempotents from its endomorphism quotient, successively in complementary corners. Each lift can be taken as a high characteristic power of an element lifting the idempotent, since its idempotence error is nilpotent and commutes with the element. Image spaces are explicit indecomposables, and the matrix factors identify the repeated types.

## 2. Cancellation of transpose-paired odd multiplicities

Write T as a slice map from Z to Hom(X,Y), allowing rectangular matrices. Fix the labels z∈Z and decompose this matrix tuple as a direct sum of indecomposable representations, using its strict endomorphism algebra. For each isomorphism type, keep one copy if its multiplicity is odd. Call this reduced tuple O_T. Dualizing a tuple transposes all its slices, exchanging the two matrix modes and dualizing their spaces. In O_T retain as D_T all self-dual types and both members of every dual pair present, and put the remaining types into U_T.

**Theorem.** If U_T has total matrix-mode dimension O(log L), with a fixed constant, the raw special-linear comparison reduces in deterministic polynomial time to one two-sign instance. No bound is placed on the dimensions, number, or multiplicities of the canceled summands. Zero unpaired dimension gives a sign relation with no exterior-algebra construction.

First compute the sign subgroup of units in the strict endomorphism algebra using Section 1. These units act on the two matrix modes while fixing Z. If that subgroup is nonzero, a known parity vector gives the projection at once. Otherwise every indecomposable summand is even-dimensional in each matrix mode, since negating both of its spaces is a strict automorphism. Also every strict automorphism of any chosen direct sum of summands has positive signs, by extension by the identity.

Choose ordered bases on the indecomposables, using identical frames on copies of one type via strict intertwiners. Record the input and output frame factors to the raw arrays. Any full transporter with label map g gives a strict isomorphism to the target tuple pulled back along g. It can be replaced by a blockwise isomorphism whose maps on all copies of one type are that same local isomorphism. The replacement has the same two signs, since the two witnesses differ by a strict automorphism. Block reordering is sign-neutral in each matrix role because each summand has even dimension. Consequently even multiplicities disappear from each sign, and the product of the two raw signs is the calibrated product from the induced isomorphisms on D_T and U_T.

The duality relation of the strict types is intrinsic under pullback of labels. Choose a strict isomorphism a_T:D_T→D_T^∨ at each endpoint, by the module algorithm or by pairing the listed blocks. If h:D_T→g*D_S is one blockwise transporter on the paired part, then
\[
 a_T^{-1}h^\vee a_S h
\]
is a strict automorphism of D_T, with the target duality map interpreted on pulled-back labels. Its determinant on the first matrix mode is positive. Since the dual of h goes in the reverse direction and uses the transpose of the other matrix map in that mode, the product of the two signs of h equals the ratio of the first determinants of a_T and a_S. This is an endpoint calibration. The same reasoning includes the case D_T=0 and empty bases.

To mark the unpaired part, choose an idempotent of the strict endomorphism algebra projecting onto the selected single copies of the U_T types. Include the strict algebra, its two faithful jointly represented actions, and that idempotent in the ordinary tensor system. Require the induced algebra conjugation to carry this source idempotent to its target counterpart. Every allowed full transporter can be replaced while preserving all three signs by one satisfying this mark, by matching the designated copies in the blockwise replacement. Conversely on a marked transporter the induced maps on U_T give the correct remaining determinant product. Indeed a blockwise replacement of its complement keeps the same signs there, because automorphisms of the complementary tuple extend by identity and are positive. The marked tensor system therefore retains exactly the sign profiles needed for this test. Extract the two image spaces of the marked idempotent and mark the calibrated volume line in the exterior algebra of their direct sum. This enforces positivity of the raw matrix-mode sign product. Its size is polynomial since the sum of the two dimensions is O(log L). Retain the other two independent signs using the signed compiler. Comparisons with incompatible invariant formats or hypotheses on only one endpoint reject in the promise problem.

The theorem uses strict duality at the common labels, with those labels allowed to move arbitrarily in the final comparison. It applies, for example, to arbitrarily many full rectangular tuples R_i and their transposes, with unrelated private label coordinates and one shared label coordinate tying the pieces together. Even unpaired copies may be included without changing the reduced tuple.

## 3. An intrinsic evaluation map with a small kernel

Let S be the slice subspace of Hom(X,Y), identified with the label space by conciseness, and let
\[
 A=\{a\in\operatorname{End}(X)\mid S a\subseteq S\}.
\]
It is a unital algebra obtained by linear equations. The balanced evaluation is the surjective linear map
\[
 \mathrm{ev}:W=S\otimes_A X\longrightarrow Y,\qquad s\otimes x\longmapsto s(x).
\]
The balanced tensor product is explicitly a quotient of the ordinary tensor product by the relations sa⊗x−s⊗ax. Both W and N=ker ev are intrinsic and polynomial to compute. Every raw transporter induces maps on A, W and N.

Let d_j be the matrix sizes in the semisimple quotient of A. Choose a lift of its semisimple complement and a first diagonal idempotent e_j in each simple matrix factor. We use the usual Wedderburn–Malcev theorem over the finite field: a semisimple quotient has a complement, and two complements with the same quotient identification are conjugate by 1+rad A. Computations of complements can also be made directly. Lift matrix units via successive corner idempotents and correct inverse corner maps. Lift a generator of each center into its first diagonal corner and solve its separable minimal polynomial by Newton iteration, whose error squares and terminates in the nilpotent radical. Use the matrix units to transfer the field to all diagonal corners. Each step has polynomial cost. [Finite-field quotient algorithms](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

**Theorem.** Suppose S as a right A-module is projective, with multiplicities t_j of the projectives e_j A. Suppose for some common c∈{0,1} one has t_j≡c d_j mod 2 for every j. If dim N=O(log L), there is one deterministic polynomial-time two-sign reduction. The free case S≅A^t satisfies the parity condition with c=t mod 2 for arbitrary t. The analogous assertion holds if X instead is projective as a left A-module and its projective multiplicities satisfy this condition.

Projectivity and the t_j are effective from raw data. Compute the simple multiplicities on S/SJ, write the indicated projective candidate ⊕_j(e_j A)^{t_j}, and check module isomorphism constructively. This test verifies projectivity and obtains the frames. The left-module case uses the analogous top. All dimensions involved are polynomial.

The unit a∈A^× gives an actual automorphism (s↦sa⁻¹, x↦ax, y↦y), including when N is nonzero. If its computed sign subgroup is nonzero, use the projection of Section 1. Otherwise all unit signs on X are positive. In particular ν_j=dim_F₃(e_jX)/[K_j:F₃] is even for every j. This is exactly the simple multiplicity of type j in the natural module, since applying e_j to a composition series is exact and turns a simple of type j into a single K_j-line.

It follows that every A-linear automorphism F of S induces positive determinant on W. In the decomposition W≅⊕_j(e_jX)^{t_j}, the semisimple factors of End_A(S) are Mat_(t_j)(K_j) for the active j. Indeed the projectives e_jA are indecomposable with residue endomorphism field K_j, and their tops distinguish the types. Quotient units can act by the lifted scalar matrices on the repeated projective copies, giving on W the norm determinants raised to ν_j. The radical kernel acts unipotently on W. This positivity is valid even when F does not preserve N.

Fix an actual transporter and its induced algebra isomorphism φ. By composing with an idealizer unit at the target, which by the remaining case has positive signs on all three roles, normalize its action on the chosen complements and matrix units up to the actual factor permutation and field isomorphisms. Conjugacy of complements handles the first normalization. Inside each matched matrix factor a change of matrix units is inner, which handles the second. The X map then acts as the same h_j:e_jX→e'_(π(j))X' on all d_j matrix-unit copies of type j. A reference semilinear map on S applies φ coefficientwise to the chosen projective summands in any matching order. The actual S map differs by an A-linear automorphism, whose action on W we just proved positive. In the induced W frames the reference W map repeats h_j exactly t_j times. When c=0 all these repeated W blocks are even-dimensional and the internal exponents are even. When c=1 the parity lists for X and W agree, so the same permutation has the same sign on both. In the chosen frames, the W determinant bit therefore equals c times the X determinant bit. Restoring endpoint frames adds a computable constant.

The exact sequence 0→N→W→Y→0 now gives
\[
 s_Y+c\,s_X=s_N+\text{endpoint calibration}
\]
in the induced kernel and quotient frames. Attach the exterior algebra of the small intrinsic kernel N and mark its calibrated top line to enforce s_Y+c s_X=0 on the actual transporter. In the zero-kernel case this is a known sign relation without a mark. Keep the two complementary signs. The proof with X projective swaps the module roles and obtains s_Y+c s_S. The only independent unit corrections used are actual automorphisms of the original tensor, while the reference comparison on W is only a determinant calculation.

Thus all concise invertible translates S=M A of unital square matrix algebras are covered. Here S≅A and balanced evaluation is an isomorphism. This removes any bound on the odd multiplicities in that earlier class. More generally if the right Cartan matrix C of A satisfies ker(C^T mod 2)⊆span(d_j mod 2), the parity hypothesis is automatic in the zero-unit-sign branch. The multiplicities of S as a sum of simples are t^T C, so trivial signs for right multiplication by all units imply C^T t=0 modulo 2. This includes the case of invertible mod-two Cartan matrix, and in particular semisimple A.

If both projective parity alignments hold, the zero-unit-sign branch gives the two relations s_Y+c_S s_X=s_N+κ_S and s_Y+c_X s_S=s_N+κ_X. When at least one of c_S,c_X is one, they eliminate two independent sign conditions. If N is nonzero and small, the two required values for its sign must first agree, after which one exterior mark and one live sign suffice. If N=0, both comparisons are endpoint-calibrated constants and any inconsistent constant rejects.

For evaluation with zero kernel and both modules projective, a further criterion uses the individual corners of A. Write S=⊕_i(e_i A)^{t_i}, X=⊕_j(A e_j)^{u_j}. On a representative corner D_ij=e_i A e_j define the three weights (t_i d_j,d_i u_j,t_i u_j). If a nonzero binary character λ annihilates these weight vectors for all nonzero corners, the full comparison reduces to two signs. To see this, an A-linear automorphism on either of S and X extends independently to an actual tensor automorphism by its induced map on W=Y. Compute their sign subgroups from their endomorphism algebras acting on these roles. If any is nonzero, use its safe projection. Otherwise replace the actual S and X maps by their reference semilinear maps on the projective copies after the same idealizer-unit normalization. Every reference uses the identical induced map on D_ij, repeated respectively t_i d_j, d_i u_j and t_i u_j times in the three roles. In the first and second roles this follows by splitting the regular factors with matrix units. In the third it follows from (e_i A)⊗_A(A e_j)≅e_i A e_j. The internal determinant contribution cancels in λ. To account for permutations, order the corner blocks in all three frames first by the triple of their three size parities and then by a common corner order within a bucket. The transporter preserves the parity buckets. The sign vector of a permutation within a bucket is its parity times the common parity triple, since swapping two blocks contributes the product of their dimensions modulo two. The character vanishes on each such parity triple by hypothesis. Hence λ(s) is a computable endpoint calibration. This test permits growing corner dimensions, arbitrary repeated factors and arbitrary projective multiplicities.

**Growing example with a nonzero kernel.** Take even d≥4 and even t≥4, A=Mat_d(F₃), X=A and S=A^t. Choose C₀ with I,C₀,C₀² independent, and let N⊂A^t be the two-dimensional space span(I,C₀) in the first coordinate. Use the tensor (s,x)↦(s_i x)_i mod N. Its three modes have even sizes td², d² and td²−2. Any nonzero right ideal of A has dimension at least d, so N contains no nonzero right submodule. This proves injectivity of the slice assignment. The other input has zero common annihilator because a coordinate other than the first can be used, and taking x=I proves output spanning. An unaltered row forces every right idealizer element to be left multiplication by an element of A, by considering the slice with that row equal to I and the remaining rows zero. The idealizer is therefore A and the balanced kernel is exactly N.

The pair adjoint fixing labels is scalar. On an unaltered row the adjoint must commute with left multiplication by A, hence is right multiplication by b∈A. Preserving N then requires b and C₀b to lie in span(I,C₀), so b is scalar. The pair fixing X consists of the A-linear maps F on S that carry N into itself, with their induced maps on Y. For any such adjoint, commutation modulo N gives F(sx)−F(s)x∈N. Replacing x by xy shows that this error times every y∈A lies in N, so it is zero. The quotient algebra of these F has factors F₃ and Mat_((t−1)d)(F₃). On the former factor the multiplicities in S and Y are d² and d²−2, both even. On the latter they are both d. The pair fixing Y is the idealizer action on S and X and its simple multiplicities are td and d. Thus every fixed-mode pair adjoint has positive unit signs and each of the pair commutant dimensions grows. All three mode dimensions also grow. The small-kernel theorem supplies a two-sign instance for this family from the raw tensor.

## 4. Arbitrarily many coupled blocks

Suppose an isomorphism-equivariant deterministic polynomial-time procedure recovers direct-sum blocks in all three modes. Equivariance means every tensor transporter permutes the recovered blocks in its own mode. The support is the tripartite hypergraph of nonzero block tensors. The input to the algorithms in this section includes the raw extraction rule. An example where it comes from the Lie algebra is given below.

When block dimensions are bounded by a constant, the full sign profile is computable in deterministic polynomial time if either the support incidence degree or its treewidth is bounded. Construct a colored graph coding the block decomposition and all local tensor tables. A block node is colored by mode and is joined to nodes for its dual vectors. Encode the addition triples within a block by relation nodes with position-colored connectors. A nonzero block triple gets its own support node. Encode every evaluation of its tensor on three dual vectors by a relation node colored by its value and joined using ordered role connectors. Since block sizes are constant, all local tables have constant size. Graph isomorphisms give exactly the tensor transporters after forgetting any auxiliary permutations. Indeed addition forces F₃-linear bijections, and the local evaluation tables enforce the tensor equations. Conversely a valid transporter extends on the graph.

Bounded incidence degree yields bounded degree of this graph. Bounded incidence treewidth yields bounded graph treewidth by expanding each block or support node in a decomposition bag to its constant-size gadget. Cross-gadget edges only follow base incidences. Thus one can decide graph isomorphism with ordinary polynomial algorithms in either case. Colors and individualization can be preserved by attaching three leaves and a tag path of length at least two to each original graph vertex, with different lengths for different colors. The original vertices are exactly those of degree at least four. This preserves a degree bound and a treewidth bound, even if the lengths grow polynomially. Individualization gives one isomorphism and generators of the source automorphism group using polynomially many queries. For generators, at each vertex position fix the preceding vertices and choose one representative for each realizable image of that vertex, using individualization to find the whole map. These representatives generate the stabilizer chain. Calculate all determinant signs from the induced tensor maps. The common transporter set has sign profile equal to the sign of the chosen transporter plus the subgroup spanned by the generator signs. [Luks, bounded-valence isomorphism](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/iso.pdf) · [Grohe–Neuen–Schweitzer–Wiebking, bounded-treewidth isomorphism](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06858)

There is a different polynomial-time algorithm with growing block size b=O(√log L) whenever the support isomorphisms of each connected component pair can be listed in polynomial time. Fix one such map. The variable for a block is its unknown local linear isomorphism in computed bases. The relation at a support triple is the full list of triples of local maps carrying its tensor to the target block tensor. It can be made in time 3^{O(b²)}. When nonempty, it is a coset of the local stabilizer subgroup in the product of the three general linear groups. It is closed under the componentwise Mal'tsev operation (u,v,w)↦uv⁻¹w. Overlapping supports share the same variables. Use identity padding to embed different-size general linear groups into GL_b(F₃). For each of the three modes add an XOR prefix chain for the determinant bits of its local maps. Use a fixed order-two subgroup to represent the bit variables. The chain relations and pinned bits are preserved by the same Mal'tsev operation, since determinant is a group homomorphism. The required final values include raw frame and block-permutation signs. All domains, the operation table and every local relation are polynomially sized. The extensional Mal'tsev constraint algorithm decides the resulting system in polynomial time. Test all eight final sign profiles, union over listed support maps, and assemble disconnected components by matching and the signs of transpositions of repeated ordinary component types. [Bulatov–Dalmau, Mal'tsev constraints](https://doi.org/10.1137/050628957)

Here the domain itself may grow polynomially. The explicit Mal'tsev algorithm works with compact representations containing at most a polynomial number of tuples in the number of variables and the domain size. Its relation-filtering step enumerates assignments to the current scope plus a bounded number of auxiliary positions. Those scopes have size at most three here. Thus its table operations retain a polynomial bound with this growing domain. [Dalmau, explicit algorithm in Sections 4.1–4.3](https://www.math.u-szeged.hu/~mmaroti/okt/2011o/dalmau.pdf)

An independent proof uses permutation-group algorithms and works also for logarithmic block size when each local tensor orbit has polynomial size. Once a support isomorphism is fixed, identify corresponding block frames and use the direct product G of the local general linear groups, represented by its faithful permutation action on the disjoint union of their vector spaces. Its permutation degree is polynomial for block size O(log L). Maintain the feasible assignments as a coset gH with generators for H. On the next block tensor, enumerate its orbit under H and record a lift to H for each orbit point. The target condition, after applying g⁻¹ to the target block, is either impossible or selects one coset of the source-block stabilizer in H. Standard Schreier generators for that stabilizer use the recorded lifts, so their number is polynomial when the orbit is polynomial. Keep a polynomial generator list by removing generators already in the subgroup generated by their retained predecessors using polynomial-time membership. Each retained generator at least doubles the subgroup order, so this list has size at most log₂|G|. Once the last local condition is processed, apply the three mode determinant homomorphisms to g and to the generators of H and add the known offsets. This gives the complete sign coset. In the O(√log L) regime all three-block orbits are polynomial automatically. For logarithmic block size, bounded multilinear rank of each local tensor also suffices, since each possible image is determined by the three restrictions to its bounded-dimensional mode supports. [Furst–Hopcroft–Luks, permutation-group computation](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/f360d936-ad6c-47ab-8ceb-105c662e23d0)

One example of the polynomial-list hypothesis is that every support block is present in exactly two nonzero block tensors. The graph on nonzero triples has one incident edge of each color, each colored edge standing for the corresponding shared block. In a connected component its colored isomorphisms are determined by the image of one root, since every color gives one neighbor. Therefore their entire list is found by propagation. There is no treewidth bound in this case.

The earlier Lie extraction comes from a connected properly three-edge-colored cubic bipartite graph of girth at least six and edge connectivity at least three. Put one line in a mode for each edge of that color, and put a nonzero tensor coefficient at the triple of incident edges of each graph vertex. Compute the infinitesimal stabilizer by linear equations. An off-diagonal entry replaces one color edge e by a distinct edge e' of the same color. In the tensor equation the replacement term at either endpoint of e can cancel only if that endpoint is adjacent to an endpoint of e' by one of the other colors. Cancellation at both endpoints would give a triangle or four-cycle. Hence its coefficient is zero. Diagonal entries satisfy graph vertex-sum equations. For distinct edges e,f there is a cycle using e and avoiding f, since removing f leaves e nonbridging. The alternating values on that cycle distinguish the two diagonal coordinates. The simultaneous eigenspaces are therefore exactly the original lines. This works for arbitrary nonzero vertex coefficients. [Earlier Lie recovery for line blocks](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SLTensorProjectionPacking/LieGraphSelectors.md)

The new local-map algorithm also applies to tensor blocks of dimension eight in all three modes after arbitrary rebasing. On each colored edge take an eight-dimensional block. At each graph vertex, use the local tensor
\[
 \sum_{i,j\in\mathbb Z/8}a_{ij}\,e_i\otimes f_j\otimes g_{i+j},
 \qquad a_{00}=-1,\quad a_{ij}=1\ \text{otherwise}.
\]
Each mode has eight linearly independent slices because their supports are disjoint. The local infinitesimal stabilizer consists of scalar triples with sum zero. For diagonal entries, the equations x_i+y_j+z_(i+j)=0 force all cyclic differences of z to be equal, hence zero since eight is invertible in F₃, and then x and y are constant. For an off-diagonal displacement d≠0, write X_i for the entry from e_(i+d) to e_i, Y_j for the entry from f_(j+d) to f_j, and Z_(i+j) for the entry from g_(i+j) to g_(i+j+d). The equations are
\[
 a_{i+d,j}X_i+a_{i,j+d}Y_j+a_{i,j}Z_{i+j}=0 .
\]
Only the three positions (−d,0), (0,−d), and (0,0) have a coefficient different from one. Two consecutive rows outside {0,−d} exist. Subtracting them gives equal cyclic differences of Z, so Z is constant. One clean row makes Y constant, and a clean position in each row makes X constant. Comparing the three exceptional equations to an ordinary equation now forces X=Y=Z=0.

The graph off-diagonal argument applies to the eight-dimensional blocks using local conciseness, so the global infinitesimal stabilizer preserves each block. The local calculation forces its entries to be scalar on each block. Graph cycle flows distinguish their scalar profiles, so the computed joint eigenspaces recover exactly the eight-dimensional blocks. For a concrete growing graph family, take vertices A_i,B_i for i modulo an even N≥8 and join A_i to B_(i+s) in color s for s=0,1,3. The six ordered differences of the three shifts are distinct modulo N, so there are no four-cycles. Colors 0 and 1 form a Hamiltonian cycle. Any cut of size at most two would have a shore that is a proper interval of this cycle, with its shorter shore having length at most N. Number the Hamiltonian cycle positions so the color-3 matching sends even position j to odd position j+5. The first odd vertex of such an interval has its mate before the interval, and a singleton also has a color-3 edge leaving it. Thus there is no cut of size two. The pair adjoints are scalar on the blocks in each bichromatic component, by setting the third mode infinitesimal action to zero. There are at most three such components for any color pair, because the counts are the greatest common divisors of N and the shift differences. Each pair-unit determinant on a mode is positive since each block has even dimension. The pair commutant dimensions grow with N, and all mode dimensions equal 8N. This gives a raw wide family whose exact sign profiles the block-group algorithm computes.

The unrestricted reduction remains open. These sufficient results replace a bound on the full transporter parameter space by a bound on the unpaired odd residual, by a module evaluation identity with a small kernel, or by exact constraints on local block maps.
