# Fixed-field surface localization and weighted difference constraints

## GP-0994

Fix a prime $p$, including any one fixed odd prime. Let weighted difference minimization over $\mathbb F_p$ be the problem of minimizing the total weight of unsatisfied constraints $y_v-y_u=b_e$ on an arbitrary graph, with nonnegative rational edge weights. This problem and weighted support localization of a given $1$-homology class over $\mathbb F_p$ on closed orientable triangulated surfaces are polynomial-time approximation equivalent. The reductions preserve every multiplicative factor and supply solution maps. One direction has equality of optimum, and the other has a factor-six normalization of every optimum. In particular the best achievable polynomial-time approximation ratio, including the existence of a PTAS or an arbitrarily strong gap at the same fixed alphabet, is exactly the one for unrestricted weighted difference minimization over this field. This is a comparison of thresholds. [Surface-localization and unique-games context](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.06800#S6)

### Localization to differences

Orient the surface and orient each edge. A representative of the given homology class is $z+\partial y$, with $y$ a field-valued face chain. The boundary coefficient at an edge orients its two face incidences oppositely, so its value is $z_e+y_v-y_u$ after ordering the incident faces appropriately. An edge is absent from the support precisely when the difference equals $-z_e$. Thus the dual graph gives one weighted difference constraint per edge, with exactly the same objective. A vertex labeling gives a face chain and hence a representative. Conversely for a given representative solve the linear boundary system to recover a face chain.

### Differences to a flat surface instance

Use the sphere-and-cylinder construction of GP-0996 with the regular translation action of $\mathbb F_p$. A variable has an anchor in a punctured sphere and one triangular boundary per incidence. For each boundary vertex use two edge-disjoint paths to the anchor within its sphere, with all these paths edge-disjoint over the different ports. The construction needs no degree splitter here. For a variable of arbitrary input degree the same finite fan triangulation is used once per port, so the total is polynomial. Insert a triangular prism cylinder for each constraint. Its six cross edges carry the translation $b_e$ from the first anchor region to the second, while the sphere edges carry zero. Every triangle has zero sum of oriented edge labels. Components can be joined with zero-weight constraints, without imposing any positive-cost condition. Distinct ports also allow a loop constraint.

Give each cross edge the weight $w_e$ of its input constraint and every sphere edge a weight at least the largest $w_e$. A labeling constant in each sphere has violation cost exactly six times the original constraint cost. In any labeling, read the field labels at the anchors. If input constraint $e$ is violated by these anchor labels, all six disjoint anchor-to-anchor paths through its cylinder must contain at least one violated edge. Every edge on a path has weight at least $w_e$, and the paths for all constraints are edge-disjoint. Hence the decoded input assignment costs at most one-sixth the surface violation cost. The assigned translations are an $\mathbb F_p$ cocycle on the resulting primal triangulation. ### The dual cycle and simplicial output

Take the dual cellular surface. Its oriented dual edges correspond to primal edges, with signs chosen by the surface orientation. The cocycle of translations gives a dual cycle $z$. Primal coboundaries become dual face boundaries, so the minimum weight of $z+\partial y$ is precisely the violation cost of the flat instance. This gives exact correspondence before any output refinement.

The cellulation can be made a genuine simplicial triangulation without changing the optimum or weakening decoding. Each dual polygon has a simple boundary in the dual of a simplicial surface. Subdivide it into triangles by adding a private center joined to its boundary vertices. Retain the old dual weights, set $z$ equal to zero on the new edges, and give each new edge a weight $M$ larger than the sum of all old weights. Old face chains with a constant coefficient on each polygon extend with no cost on new edges. For any candidate representative recover a face chain by solving the boundary system in the refined triangulation. If its coefficients are constant on the triangles in each polygon, it already gives an old face chain and the cost agrees. Otherwise some pair of triangles inside one polygon has distinct coefficients across a shared new edge, because their adjacency inside a fan is connected. This representative incurs weight at least $M$. Any constant-on-polygons replacement has cost at most the sum of all old edge weights and therefore does not increase its cost. Project to that replacement and decode. The triangulation and the integer or rational choice of $M$ have polynomial encoding length.

This extends the earlier regular binary and unweighted finite-action transfers to exact rational-weight approximation comparisons at each fixed prime. The arguments use the support objective on field coefficients and make no assertion about minimizing the sum of integer magnitudes of lifted coefficients.
