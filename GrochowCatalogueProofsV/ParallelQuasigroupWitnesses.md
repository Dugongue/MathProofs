# Parallel construction of quasigroup membership witnesses

## GP-0529

There is a logspace-uniform $\mathsf{NC}^3$ algorithm which takes a finite quasigroup table, a generating subset $X$, and an element $t$, and either certifies nonmembership or outputs a polynomial-size term circuit over $X$ evaluating to $t$. Using multiplication and the two standard quasigroup divisions, the output circuit has depth $O(\log^2 n)$, where $n$ is the quasigroup order. A multiplication-only term circuit with polynomial size and polynomial depth can also be output in uniform NC.

Let $R_0=X$ and let $R_{j+1}$ contain $R_j$ and every result of one of the three operations on two elements of $R_j$. Apply the Reachability Lemma of Collins–Grochow–Levet–Weiß to the subgroupoid generated by $X$ under these operations. It is a finite quasigroup with the restricted operations, so every one of its elements has a straight-line program of length at most $C(1+\log n)^2$ over $X$ for an absolute constant $C$. The witness gates in any such program have depth at most its length. Therefore, for $J=\lceil C(1+\log n)^2\rceil$ one has $R_J$ equal to the generated subquasigroup. The empty set is handled directly. [Reachability Lemma, Lemma 7.13](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00133#S7)

For each element $a$ at each round, simultaneously test its presence at the previous round and the existence of a predecessor pair $(b,c)$ already present with the indicated operation giving $a$. There are at most $3n^2$ pair-operation choices. Multiplication is a table lookup, and the two division tables are obtained by transposing the corresponding triples of the given multiplication relation. With bounded-fan-in balanced disjunctions and comparison of binary indices each round has $O(\log n)$ Boolean depth and polynomial size. Computing all $J$ rounds gives $O(\log^3 n)$ depth. All wiring, including the table transpose requests, is generated in logarithmic space.

Give each possible element-round pair one record. An already-present element uses the identity wire from the preceding round. A newly present element records the lexicographically first pair and operation passing the test. In parallel compute this first choice by prefix disjunctions over the finite choice order. Unused records can be replaced by dummy gates copying any chosen generator when $X$ is nonempty. Refer only to earlier rounds and declare the record $(J,t)$ to be the output. By induction on the round every active record evaluates to its named element. The generator records are input references to named elements of $X$. Thus the output is an explicit acyclic term circuit, and the membership answer is read from the saturation bit at $(J,t)$.

For a multiplication-only presentation, fix $a$ and let $L_a$ be the permutation $u\mapsto a*u$. Given $b$, find the smallest positive $j\le n$ with $L_a^j(b)=b$ using parallel permutation squaring and comparing all $j$. Then $a\backslash b=L_a^{j-1}(b)$, which is a chain of at most $n-1$ products with $a$. Use $R_a:u\mapsto u*a$ in the same way for right division. The powers for all possible operands and exponents can be tabulated with polynomial-size bounded-fan-in circuits of polylogarithmic depth, by successively doubling permutation powers and composing the binary-selected powers. Replace a division gate by a fixed block of $n$ locations, with the desired chain chosen by the computed length. All lengths and connections depend on the named operand values already recorded. This preserves polynomial output size and uniform NC construction. This version can have polynomial term depth because the individual chain uses one repeated multiplication per step.

The construction separates the parallel work of finding a circuit from evaluation of its output when only multiplication gates are permitted. The short-witness lemma is the point that bounds the number of saturation rounds, so there is no need to enumerate quasigroup programs.
