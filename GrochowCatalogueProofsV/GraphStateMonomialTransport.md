# Compact local-unitary symmetries from graph-state Pauli axes

## GP-0974 and GP-0975

There is a polynomial bit-time compact description of all labeled local-unitary projective self-symmetries of any graph state satisfying the following checkable condition. Every qubit either has a nontrivial one-qubit stabilizer or belongs to a two-qubit reduced state whose Pauli expansion has exactly one nonidentity term supported on both qubits. Two-qubit connected components may be handled separately as Bell factors. The output explicitly gives the identity component and a finite group presentation by a normal form, with exact root-of-unity representatives. This is a sufficient condition for the GP-0974 output problem.

For GP-0975, ordered lists of graph states on the same labeled qubits admit polynomial bit-time simultaneous transport whenever a specified anchor pair is local-Clifford equivalent and the source anchor meets the preceding condition without Bell factors. This includes identical anchors and product-state anchors. There is also a polynomial algorithm when the transporters are required to normalize prescribed Pauli axes, expressed in arbitrary local Clifford coordinates, whether or not an anchor forces that restriction.

### How small reduced states force the axes

An LU stabilizing a pure state up to scalar preserves each reduced density matrix. If a one-qubit marginal has nontrivial Pauli term $P$, its local factor must preserve $P$. A two-qubit marginal with sole nonidentity term $P\otimes Q$ must preserve that product. By unique factorization of nonzero simple tensors and because conjugation preserves the Pauli spectra, its local factors each preserve the corresponding Pauli axis as an unordered pair $\{P,-P\}$ or $\{Q,-Q\}$. The axes are found by binary linear algebra on the stabilizer generators. In a qualifying two-site marginal there are no other terms, so this argument uses a literal equality of product operators. Choose local Clifford coordinates sending each selected axis to $Z$. Every permitted factor is then monomial in the computational basis. Projectively it has the unique form $X^{b_j}\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{2\pi i\theta_j})$, with $b_j\in\mathbb F_2$ and $\theta_j\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$. The one-qubit condition is included because it certainly forces this same containment, and the eventual equations enforce its exact stronger constraint. If several pairs choose axes at one site, using any one axis is sufficient. [Graph-state small-support symmetries](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.07106)

A graph state on disconnected components is a tensor product. A product of one-qubit operators stabilizes it projectively exactly when the subproduct on every component stabilizes that factor. A graph with one edge gives a Bell state after a local Clifford change. Its projective symmetry has the form $U\otimes\overline U$ in Bell coordinates, by vectorization of the identity matrix, so this connected compact factor is handled explicitly.

### Polynomial integer lattices from affine stabilizer support

A stabilizer state in any computational Clifford basis has support $a+V\subseteq\mathbb F_2^n$, equal amplitude magnitudes there, and relative phases $i^{r(z)}$ at $x(z)=a+Bz$, where the columns of $B$ form a binary basis for $V$. Here
$$
r(z)=\sum_i c_i z_i+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}z_i z_j\pmod4
$$
after suppressing the constant. These data are computable in polynomial time by stabilizer elimination. In detail, the diagonal stabilizers impose the affine support constraints and the binary $X$ masks of the full stabilizer generate their homogeneous solution space. The equality of these spaces follows from orthogonality of the two mask types and dimension, since a pure-state stabilizer has binary dimension $n$. Choose stabilizer lifts of a mask basis. Applying their ordered products to one point of the affine support gives all amplitudes up to the normalization factor. Each lift contributes one linear phase and Pauli commutations contribute even quadratic terms. A change of binary affine coordinates preserves this phase form modulo four because XOR of bits agrees modulo four with their integer sum minus twice the sum of their pairwise products. [Binary quadratic stabilizer representation](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0304125)

We use a lattice fact. Given such $x(z)$ and any phase function of the displayed form, write $q(z)=x(z)-x(0)$ over the integers. The subgroup generated by all
$$
w(z)=(q(z),r(z),z)\quad\hbox{in}\quad
\mathbb Z^n\oplus\mathbb Z/4\oplus\mathbb Z^{\dim V}
$$
has a polynomial-time basis computation in the integer cover including the vector that has four in the middle position and zero elsewhere. To see this, let $e_i$ be a unit selector. The changes $z\mapsto z+e_i$ in binary arithmetic satisfy
$$
w(z+e_i)=w(e_i)+M_i w(z)
$$
in the displayed group, for known additive linear maps $M_i$. The first coordinates transform by signs because flipping one selector flips a fixed set of support coordinates. In the last coordinates only the $i$th sign changes. The middle coordinate is changed by a linear function of $z$ modulo four in addition to the constant $r(e_i)$, by the quadratic phase formula. Therefore the desired subgroup is the smallest subgroup containing the $w(e_i)$ and stable under all $M_i$.

Compute that subgroup by adding a translated generator whenever integer lattice membership fails. Use lifts with residues $0,\ldots,3$ in the middle coordinate. The first and last coordinates of iterated translated generators remain in $\{-1,0,1\}$, since the linear maps act there by coordinate signs. Including the vertical vector with entry four bounds all entries of the actual retained generators by four. Maintain Hermite normal form for membership. At a fixed rank the index of the evolving lattice in its rational saturation starts at most $(4\sqrt D)^D$ by the minor bound, where $D$ is the ambient integer dimension. Each strict addition at that rank decreases the index by a factor of at least two. The rank can increase at most $D$ times. Thus only polynomially many generators and membership tests are needed. Stability gives every $w(z)$ by induction over selector flips, and conversely the full generated group is stable by the same displayed identity.

### All projective monomial self-symmetries

For a monomial operator write its action on a computational basis vector as
$$
|x\rangle\longmapsto e^{2\pi i\theta\cdot x}|x+b\rangle.
$$
Preserving the affine support requires $b\in V$. Every such $b$ is the mask of a Pauli stabilizer, so multiply by a Pauli stabilizer lift to remove this mask. The remaining diagonal operator is a projective stabilizer exactly when $\theta\cdot q(z)=0$ in $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ for all selectors $z$. The first-coordinate lattice of the preceding computation, using zero phase function, gives equivalent polynomially many integer equations. Smith normal form computes their kernel as a product of circles and finite cyclic groups. The circles give the identity component, since mask bits are discrete. Torsion generators have rational angles whose denominator lengths are bounded by polynomial-bit minors, so they have exact root-of-unity representations.

Taking one product of Pauli lifts for each binary mask together with this diagonal kernel gives a unique normal form once the lift convention is fixed. The entire group is generated by the diagonal kernel and the basis Pauli lifts. For the finite component group, take the finite Smith factors of the diagonal group together with those mask lifts modulo the diagonal identity component. Multiplication uses multiplication of the chosen Pauli words and the coordinate-sign conjugation action of a mask on the diagonal phases. Both operations are polynomial on the compact coordinates. Any scalar phases suppressed in the projective output can be restored by adding the independent local scalar phases. This proves the claimed exact description whenever the small reduced states force the prescribed axes.

### Simultaneous monomial transport

For each ordered source-target pair compute their two affine supports in prescribed Clifford coordinates. Reject if their homogeneous support spaces differ. The allowed shift $b$ must carry each source support to the corresponding target support. Intersect those affine linear conditions over $\mathbb F_2$. If the intersection is nonempty write it as $b_0+Wh$, with independent basis columns of $W$ and unknown binary selectors $h$.

For a fixed pair, parametrize the source support by $x(z)=a+Bz$ and the target support by $x(z)+b_0$. Since every column of $W$ belongs to its homogeneous support, write $Wh=B Hh$ for a known binary matrix $H$. Compare the two quadratic phase polynomials at these coordinates. After suppressing a global phase, the required relative phase for the actual shift $b_0+Wh$ is
$$
r_0(z)+2\sum_\ell h_\ell\phi_\ell(z)\pmod 4.
$$
Here $r_0$ is the target phase minus the source phase at shift $b_0$, and each $\phi_\ell$ is a known binary linear form in $z$. Indeed the difference $r(z+s)-r(s)-(r(z)-r(0))$ of a stabilizer phase polynomial is twice a bilinear form in $z,s$ modulo four. This follows by expanding XOR for its linear part and using the ordinary polarization identity over $\mathbb F_2$ for its even quadratic part.

Write $\lambda_\ell=h_\ell/2$ in $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$. Required phase equalities are precisely
$$
\theta\cdot q(z)-\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell\phi_\ell(z)=r_0(z)/4
\quad\hbox{in }\mathbb R/\mathbb Z,\qquad
2\lambda_\ell=0.
$$
With the last equations present, the binary linear forms use their integer linear expressions in $z$ without changing the constraints. Hence the lattice computed above for $w(z)=(q(z),r_0(z),z)$ reduces all these equalities to polynomially many integer-linear congruences in torus variables. Take their union over all pairs and solve by integer Smith normal form, with denominator-four rational right sides. Nonzero diagonal Smith entries can always be divided in the torus, and zero rows permit only zero right sides. Solutions output exact rational turns, and $2\lambda_\ell=0$ recovers the binary shift bits.

Finally suppose a labeled anchor pair admits a local Clifford map $C$. One such map is found by the polynomial stabilizer local-Clifford algorithm. Pull back every target by $C^{-1}$. They are still stabilizer states. A common transporter then belongs to the anchor source's stabilizer, which the preceding small-support condition forces inside the selected monomial axes. Thus solving the simultaneous monomial problem gives exactly the unrestricted simultaneous LU answer under this checkable anchor promise. Bell factors in that anchor are excluded from this last construction unless additional constraints separately force their local axes. [Single-pair graph-state equivalence context](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00527)
