# Exact rational purification and bipartite local unitary equivalence

## GP-0176

Bipartite mixed-state local unitary equivalence is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to complex unitary three-tensor isomorphism. Both reductions preserve Gaussian-rational input coordinates and work without any eigenvalue or rank promise. They also preserve real input coordinates if the respective acting groups are orthogonal. The acting matrices themselves may be complex unitary or real orthogonal over the ambient field, respectively.

### A small exact purification

Write the bipartite system as $V\otimes W$, where the field is $\mathbb Q(i)$ for complex input. Let $\rho$ be a Hermitian positive semidefinite matrix of size $h=\dim(V)\dim(W)$ with trace one. Symmetric elimination gives independent columns $L$ and a positive diagonal rational matrix $D$ with $\rho=LDL^*$. For existence and the algorithm, choose a positive diagonal entry as a pivot, take its Schur complement, and repeat on the nonzero remaining part. Positive semidefiniteness is preserved. If every diagonal entry of a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix is zero then all its entries are zero, by its two-by-two principal minors. Thus elimination stops precisely when the remaining matrix is zero. Reordering rows and columns returns the original order. Exact elimination takes polynomial bit time using the determinant bounds for rational Gaussian elimination, applied after clearing a common denominator of polynomial bit length.

Each positive rational diagonal entry $a/b$ is a sum of a polynomial number of rational squares, constructively and unconditionally. Binary-expand the positive integer $ab$. A nonzero bit at an even position $2j$ contributes $(2^j)^2$. A bit at an odd position $2j+1$ contributes two copies of $(2^j)^2$. Divide every square root in the resulting sum by $b$. There are at most $2(1+\lfloor\log_2(ab)\rfloor)$ terms and their bit lengths are polynomial. Apply this to the entries of $D$ using disjoint sets of columns, obtaining an exact factorization $\rho=XX^*$ with $X$ Gaussian rational and of polynomial size. In the real case it is rational. Add zero columns so the two supplied states have the same ancillary dimension.

### Preserving equivalence in both directions

Regard $X$ as a tensor in $V\otimes W\otimes E$, where $E$ indexes its columns. An action on $V,W$ changes $X$ by left multiplication by $U\otimes V_0$, and a unitary action on $E$ changes $X$ by a right unitary multiplication, after a harmless transpose convention. Thus product unitary equivalence implies local unitary equivalence of the two partial traces.

Conversely, if $\rho'=(U\otimes V_0)\rho(U\otimes V_0)^*$, the matrices $X'{}^*$ and $X^*(U\otimes V_0)^*$ have identical Gram matrices. The map sending their corresponding columns to each other is well-defined on their spans, because every linear relation has squared norm determined by the common Gram matrix. It is an isometry, as the common Gram also determines pairwise inner products. Extend it to a unitary isometry of the common auxiliary space by choosing orthonormal complements. This gives exactly the missing third-mode transporter. The same argument applies to orthogonal actions.

For the other reduction, flatten each three-tensor along the third mode into $X_i$. If its squared norm is zero, the tensor is zero. Unequal squared norms give a fixed negative answer, since each unitary action preserves the norm. When the norms are nonzero and equal, form the normalized densities $X_iX_i^*/\operatorname{tr}(X_iX_i^*)$. The preceding equivalence applies and all entries remain Gaussian rational. The dimensions and bit lengths grow polynomially. Fixed unequal-spectrum diagonal density operators can serve as the negative instance, and identical ones as the positive instance. These reductions compare exact orbits rather than orbit closures.

The Gram argument is the usual unitary freedom of purification. The additional arithmetic step constructs the purification over the input field without a square-root or number-theoretic oracle. It gives an exact relationship with the ordinary three-factor unitary problem, whose general polynomial-time decision remains open.
