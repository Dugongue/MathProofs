# Marked kernels and joint images for nonabelian semisimple targets

## A common kernel-enumeration theorem

Given a finite group $G$ by its Cayley table, all distinct normal subgroups $K$ with $G/K$ nonabelian simple can be enumerated in deterministic polynomial time. Their quotients are computed as tables. There are at most $\log_2|G|$ of them, and the product of all their quotient maps is onto the full product of their simple quotients.

Construct a chief series $1=N_0<N_1<\cdots<N_t=G$ by working in successive quotient tables. In each quotient compute the normal closure of each nonidentity element and select one of minimum nontrivial order. This is a minimal normal subgroup, since any proper nontrivial normal subgroup inside it contains the normal closure of each of its nonidentity elements. All the closures are found by multiplication-table saturation. The series has at most $\log_2|G|$ steps.

If $\phi:G\twoheadrightarrow S$ has nonabelian simple target, take the least $i$ with $\phi(N_i)\ne1$. Put $\overline G=G/N_{i-1}$ and $A=N_i/N_{i-1}$. The induced map restricts to an isomorphism $A\to S$: its image is nontrivial normal in $S$ and the kernel on $A$ is normal in $\overline G$, so chief minimality applies. In particular $A$ is nonabelian simple. Every $g\in\overline G$ acts on $A$ by the unique inner automorphism determined by $\phi(g)$, because the restriction on $A$ is an isomorphism. Conversely if all conjugations on this simple $A$ are inner, the unique implementing element of $A$ is a homomorphic function of $g$. It is a retraction with kernel $C_{\overline G}(A)$. This gives the unique possible $K$ at this chief layer, namely the inverse image of that centralizer. All tests and the retraction are computable by enumeration in the quotient table. Simplicity of $A$ can itself be checked by testing that all its nonidentity elements normally generate $A$. Distinct selected layers give distinct kernels, since the first chief layer with nontrivial quotient image is determined by the kernel.

For the product assertion, it suffices to prove that a family of onto maps to nonabelian simple groups with different kernels induces an onto product map. Induct on the number of factors. The image projects onto the product $P$ of the previous factors and onto the new simple factor $S$. If it does not fill $P\times S$, Goursat's lemma makes it the graph of a surjection $P\to S$, since the only nontrivial quotient of $S$ is $S$ itself. In such a surjection the images of the factors of $P$ are commuting normal subgroups in $S$. Exactly one image is nontrivial, since $S$ is nonabelian simple with trivial center. The surjection restricts to an isomorphism from that one simple factor. This repeats a kernel, a contradiction. If two kernels agree, their quotient maps differ by an isomorphism of their simple targets and contribute only one distinct coordinate to the product image. This also shows that intersections obtained from distinct subsets of the enumerated kernels are distinct, by testing a nontrivial element of a differing coordinate in the full product.

Matching the simple quotient types to target factors takes polynomial table time. One can find a generating pair in a finite nonabelian simple group by enumerating pairs, using the two-generation theorem. [Guralnick–Kantor, generation of finite simple groups](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/gkfinal.pdf) Fix such a pair and try all ordered pairs of possible images in the other table, checking whether the resulting forced map is a bijective homomorphism. To split a supplied semisimple target table into its simple factors, enumerate its minimal normal subgroups by normal closures. For a direct product of nonabelian simple groups these are precisely the direct factors.

## GP-0353 · Marked distinct images

For arbitrary Cayley-table $G$ and $H$ a varying product of nonabelian simple groups, the marked kernel count is computable in deterministic polynomial time. Marked subgroups may be supplied by generating lists in the table.

For each simple isomorphism type in $H$, select the required number of distinct kernels of that type from the above enumeration. The selected product map is onto $H$ and has kernel the intersection $K$ of the selected kernels. Conversely every map onto $H$ gives one such selection, and differing selections yield different $K$. Thus all candidate kernels can be enumerated in at most $2^t\le |G|$ selections. For each one compute $L_iK$, enumerating the points of $L_i$ from its generators and using normality of $K$. The quotient images are pairwise distinct exactly when these products are pairwise distinct. Count the accepted selections. This is an exact polynomial algorithm on the stated target class, without listing all epimorphisms or their target automorphisms.

## GP-0356 · Maximal joint images

For arbitrary Cayley-table $G$, suppose both targets $H_1,H_2$ are products of nonabelian simple groups and each admits an epimorphism from $G$. For each simple isomorphism type $S$, write $m_S$ for the number of available simple kernels and $r_{j,S}$ for its multiplicity in $H_j$. The maximum joint image order is exactly
$$
\prod_S |S|^{\min(m_S,r_{1,S}+r_{2,S})}.
$$
Indeed the joint image, up to coordinate identifications, is the full product of the distinct simple kernels used across the two target maps. Subject to the individual nonrepetition constraints, the displayed union size is attainable by selecting the two lists with the smallest possible overlap. The lists and both epimorphism witnesses follow directly from the enumeration and type matching.

The same algorithm handles targets with additional finite abelian factors of square-free exponent. For each prime $p$ use the dimension $d_p=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}G/(G'G^p)$ and, for the two added elementary abelian $p$-parts of ranks $a_{1,p},a_{2,p}$, multiply the answer by $p^{\min(d_p,a_{1,p}+a_{2,p})}$. This follows by choosing the two dual subspaces of linear functionals with maximal span, as in the distinct-coordinate selection above. Different prime parts combine independently, and their joint image with the nonabelian product is the direct product by Goursat, since that nonabelian product is perfect and has no nontrivial abelian quotient. The original general-target questions require additional work when normal layers do not separate in this way.

The chief-layer method identifies every matching simple kernel rather than merely deciding existence of one epimorphism.
