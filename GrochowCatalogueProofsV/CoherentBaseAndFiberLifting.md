# Intersection-number bases and lifting on bounded fiber size

## GP-0782

Let $\mathcal X$ be a homogeneous coherent configuration of order $n$, and write $R_0$ for its diagonal. Let $v_i$ be the valency of $R_i$ and $i^*$ its transpose. For $i\ne0$ define $a_i=\sum_j p^i_{j,j^*}$ from the intersection numbers. For any integer $1\le k\le n$, the explicit condition
$$
\frac{n}{2\binom nk}\sum_{i\ne0}v_i\binom{a_i}{k}<1
$$
guarantees that the combinatorial base number is at most $k$. A witnessing base is computable deterministically in polynomial time from the relation table when the inequality holds.

Indeed, for a pair $(x,y)\in R_i$, there are exactly $a_i$ anchor vertices $z$ for which the colors of $(x,z)$ and $(y,z)$ agree. This is the definition of $p^i_{j,j^*}$ summed over $j$. A uniform $k$-subset fails to distinguish this pair by its incoming color signatures with probability $\binom{a_i}{k}/\binom nk$. Summing counts over all ordered pairs and dividing by two yields exactly the displayed expectation of indistinguished unordered pairs. If that expectation is below one, there is a choice distinguishing every pair. Individualizing those anchors produces the discrete partition in the coherent point extension, since each singleton anchor preserves its original incident colors and hence all distinctions of signatures.

The method of conditional expectations finds the set. After choosing some anchors, retain exactly the undistinguished pairs. For each such pair count remaining vertices with equal incident colors at that pair. Its contribution for a uniform completion with $r$ further anchors is the corresponding ratio of binomial coefficients for that count and the whole remaining universe. For each possible next anchor evaluate the total conditional expectation under a uniform completion. Choose an anchor with value no greater than the average, which equals the current expectation. At the end the nonnegative integer collision count is strictly below one. These are polynomially many operations on integers of polynomial bit length.

For example, the cyclotomic scheme on the additive group of $\mathbb F_p$ using a multiplicative subgroup of order $a$ has collision number $a-1$ on each nontrivial relation. For distinct $x,y$, equality of their nonzero difference colors to $z$ forces $(z-x)/(z-y)$ to be a subgroup element other than one, and each such element gives exactly one $z$. The scheme is primitive because an additive group of prime order has no nontrivial subgroup. Thus the criterion guarantees a base of size $k$ whenever $\binom p2\binom{a-1}{k}<\binom pk$. For every fixed $k\ge3$ and fixed subgroup order $a>k$, this holds along the primes $p\equiv 1\pmod a$ for sufficiently large $p$, even though single anchors leave nontrivial collisions. [Chen–Ponomarenko, coherent point extensions and base questions](https://www.pdmi.ras.ru/~inp/cp01.pdf)

## GP-0812

For every fixed $b$, coherent configurations with every fiber of size at most $b$ admit deterministic polynomial-time lifting tests for supplied algebraic isomorphisms, with an actual vertex bijection on success. The class is directly recognizable, includes nonseparable configurations, and has unbounded combinatorial base number already for $b=16$.

An algebraic isomorphism pairs the diagonal basis relations and therefore forces the fiber matching. Use the same ordered vertex color on each pair of corresponding fibers. Turn the configuration into a directed, edge-colored complete graph with one color per basis relation. Rename the target's relation colors via the specified algebraic isomorphism. For a simple vertex-colored graph encoding, replace every ordered pair $(x,y)$ by a private length-three path from $x$ to $y$, with internal vertices distinguishing the two directions. Color the two internal vertices by their positions, the source and target fiber colors, and the basis-relation color. This works for loops as a triangle with two differently colored internal vertices, and for opposite directions as two distinct private paths. Every vertex color class has cardinality at most $\max(b,b^2)$. A color-preserving graph isomorphism is precisely a lift on the original vertices, extended uniquely by the path data. Apply the deterministic polynomial-time bounded-color-class graph isomorphism algorithm. A witness follows either from constructive canonization or by the standard self-reduction fixing the image of one vertex in each successive pair of instances, which only decreases color classes. [Babai–Luks, canonical labeling of graphs](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/CanLabel.pdf)

For nonseparability, let one fiber be the rank-three scheme of the $4$-by-$4$ rook graph, and compare it with the rank-three Shrikhande scheme. Both have parameters $(16,6,2,2)$, hence the same rank-three intersection numbers. They are nonisomorphic, since the rook graph has cliques of size four whereas every local neighborhood of the Shrikhande graph is a 6-cycle. Form a configuration with $m$ distinguished copies of the rook scheme as fibers and one complete rectangle as the only relation for each ordered pair of distinct fibers. A second configuration replaces one rook fiber by Shrikhande. Within each fiber the intersection numbers agree. Across fibers they depend only on sizes and internal valencies because cross relations are complete rectangles, so the indicated relation map is an algebraic isomorphism. It has no lift because of the modified fiber. In the all-rook configuration any base must touch every fiber. Otherwise a nontrivial automorphism supported on the untouched fiber survives the point extension. The resulting family has base number at least $m$ and maximum fiber size 16. The same algorithm works without any separability promise.

This lifting result uses the same principle of preserving the full family under a common relabeling as the prior joint permutation-family result. The small fibers alone provide the group constraint here. [Prior compact permutation-family canonization](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/f1a463ce1a77aa494b7d33b81a4cbaac5dcb708b/GrochowCatalogueProofsIV/PermutationFamilyCanonization.md)

The bounded-class method also gives a class of homogeneous schemes with the same two properties. Fix a positive integer $b$. In a homogeneous scheme, take the union $E$ of the basis relations of valency strictly less than $b$. Require $E$ to be an equivalence relation, all its blocks to have size at most $b$, and the quotient by $E$ to be thin, meaning every quotient basis relation has valency one. Also require every basis relation outside $E$ to be a union of complete ordered rectangles between blocks. All checks can be made from the relation table. The union $E$ is algebraically characteristic because valencies are intersection numbers. The algebraic isomorphism induces a correspondence of quotient relations, each of which is realized by one or more original colors with the same block-pair support. For every possible image of one designated quotient point there is at most one quotient permutation with the prescribed correspondence: the unique quotient relation from the designated point to each other point determines that point's image. Enumerate these choices and check the correspondence. For each one, give the matched $E$-blocks matching vertex colors of size at most $b$ and run the same bounded-color-class lift algorithm. This covers the entire stated homogeneous class in polynomial time.

For an explicit family take $b=16$ and $m\ge2$ blocks indexed by $\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$. Merge the three internal colors of the rook schemes across all blocks, and give each nonzero quotient difference its own basis relation, consisting of complete rectangles between the corresponding ordered blocks. The same recipe works if one rook block is replaced by the Shrikhande scheme. To verify coherence, multiply the adjacency matrices of the relations. Internal multiplications have the same structure constants in every block. Multiplying an internal color of valency $v$ by a cross color, on either side, yields $v$ times that cross color. Multiplying two cross colors yields $16$ times the cross color of their sum, or, if the sum is zero, $16$ times the sum of the three internal matrices. Thus both homogeneous schemes have identical structure constants. The specified relation correspondence has no lift because the changed block is not isomorphic to any rook block. In the all-rook version any untouched block has a nontrivial automorphism supported inside it, so a combinatorial base still needs at least $m$ anchors. The internal colors have valencies $1,6,9$ and all cross colors have valency 16, precisely as required by the characteristic cutoff.
