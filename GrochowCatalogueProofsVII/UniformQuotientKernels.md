# Uniform quotient-kernel sampling for simple and abelian factors

## GP-0355

Given Cayley tables of $G$ and $H$, with $H$ a product of nonabelian simple groups and an arbitrary finite abelian group, one can sample a uniform kernel of an epimorphism $G\to H$ in expected polynomial bit time, whenever such a kernel exists. The algorithm also detects the empty case. No epimorphism decision oracle is needed on this class. The abelian sampling step is polynomial even in compact abelian invariant-factor coordinates.

For the nonabelian part, enumerate the list of distinct simple quotient kernels of $G$ and match types. For each target type, choose a uniformly random ordered selection of distinct kernels of the required size, and compose their quotient maps with uniformly random isomorphisms to the displayed target factors. To sample those isomorphisms in polynomial table time, enumerate all image pairs of a fixed generating pair of the simple source, testing the induced bijections. If a type lacks sufficient kernels, there is no epimorphism. This gives the uniform onto map to the nonabelian direct product. [Simple-quotient enumeration and classification](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/53ffa5dcacaca5757e11adca8df99ba0d38f8bc5/GrochowCatalogueProofsV/SemisimpleMarkedQuotients.md)

For the abelian part, factor through $A=G/G'$ and work separately on its $p$-parts. Use cyclic coordinates of lengths $i$ in $A$ and lengths $j$ in the abelian target. The homomorphism matrix entry from a source of length $i$ to a target of length $j$ is a multiple of $p^{\max(j-i,0)}$ modulo $p^j$. The induced matrix modulo $p$ therefore has target rows of length $j$ supported on source coordinates of lengths at least $j$. The map is onto exactly when these rows are independent. Process target rows in decreasing order of their lengths. The permitted coordinate spaces are nested increasingly, so when choosing a row, all earlier chosen rows lie in its permitted space. If its dimension is $s$ and $h$ previous rows have already been chosen, it has exactly $p^s-p^h$ allowed new rows. Reject if this is zero. Otherwise sample uniformly from outside the previous span, by choosing a basis beginning with the previous independent rows, sampling a nonzero vector in the $s-h$ complementary coordinates, and independently sampling the first $h$ coordinates. Uniform vectors use just uniform integers below specified powers of $p$, sampled by ordinary exact rejection with constant expected retries.

Since every available choice count depends only on the dimensions, this generates the uniform full-rank permitted matrix modulo $p$. Conditional on its entries, sample every higher digit of every allowed map entry independently and uniformly. The number of these digits is independent of the chosen residues, so the resulting onto homomorphisms are uniform. The prime factors sample independently. For Cayley tables one obtains the abelian coordinates by integer Smith reduction of the presentation with one symbol per group element and the multiplication relations. [Top-layer epimorphism criterion](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/53ffa5dcacaca5757e11adca8df99ba0d38f8bc5/GrochowCatalogueProofsVI/MarkedQuotientSeparation.md)

Finally an onto map to the nonabelian product and an onto map to the abelian product are jointly onto, since the former product is perfect and a proper subdirect product would give a common nontrivial quotient. Every epimorphism to $H$ decomposes into exactly such a pair. The independent sampling therefore gives the uniform epimorphism. Each kernel has exactly $|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|$ epimorphisms above it, so returning the kernel produces the exact uniform kernel distribution. Truncating the elementary random-integer draws with polynomially many retries yields any prescribed inverse-polynomial total variation tolerance.
