# Sharp orbit-sum separation for graphs of maximum degree two

## GP-0886

For every $n\ge 6$, the least edge degree of subgraph orbit sums separating all Boolean adjacency orbits within the class of $n$-vertex graphs of maximum degree at most two is exactly $\lceil n/2\rceil$. In particular the degree for arbitrary $n$-vertex graphs is at least $\lceil n/2\rceil$. The upper bound on this class needs only path and cycle orbit sums, over the rationals or integers.

An orbit sum of a squarefree edge monomial counts edge-subgraphs of the corresponding type. For a graph $F$ with no isolated vertices, $\operatorname{inj}(F,G)=|\operatorname{Aut}(F)|$ times this count. Powers of variables reduce to the squarefree support on Boolean inputs. If an invariant includes isolated vertices in its description, those impose no additional edge conditions.

Write $P_L$ and $C_L$ for the path and cycle on $L$ vertices. A graph of maximum degree two is their disjoint union, with isolated vertices counted as $P_1$. Put $b=\lceil n/2\rceil$. For every $3\le L\le b$, the count of $C_L$ is its number of components, since a cycle in such a graph exhausts its component. Subtract the contributions of these known components to the path counts. Write $p_j$ for the resulting number of unoriented simple paths of $j$ edges for $1\le j\le b$, and write $p_0$ for the remaining vertex count. A path component $P_L$ contributes $\max(L-j,0)$ to $p_j$, and a remaining cycle of length greater than $b$ contributes $L$ to every $p_j$, including $j=0$. Therefore for $0\le j<b$ the first difference $p_j-p_{j+1}$ is precisely the number of path components with at least $j+1$ vertices. Second differences identify all path lengths below $b$, and $p_{b-1}-p_b$ counts the remaining paths. The residual vertices comprise paths of length at least $b$ and cycles of length at least $b+1$. The total order is at most $2b$. Two residual components are possible only if both are $P_b$, and if there is only one its order and whether it is a path are known. This reconstructs the component multiset.

For sharpness at even $n=2k$, compare $C_{2k}$ and $C_k\sqcup C_k$. Both have the same counts of all connected path and cycle types with at most $k-1$ edges. At odd $n=2k+1$, compare $P_{2k+1}$ and $P_k\sqcup C_{k+1}$. For every $0\le j\le k$, both have $2k+1-j$ unoriented paths of $j$ edges, using the convention that zero edges counts vertices, and neither has a cycle of at most $k$ edges.

These matching connected counts imply equality of all edge-subgraph orbit sums up to the stated cutoffs, including disconnected patterns. For connected $F$ with at most that many edges, group its homomorphisms into each target by exact image vertex and edge sets. Every image is a connected subgraph with maximum degree at most two and with at most $|E(F)|$ edges. For each possible image type, the number of maps onto its entire vertex and edge sets depends just on the type. The displayed equalities therefore give equal connected homomorphism counts. Multiplicativity gives equality for any possibly disconnected $F$ within the edge bound. Finally Möbius inversion on vertex partitions expresses $\operatorname{inj}(F,\cdot)$ as a fixed linear combination of $\operatorname{hom}(F/\pi,\cdot)$. Quotients cannot increase the edge bound, and those creating loops contribute zero. Thus the pairs agree on all orbit sums of degrees below $b$, while one additional cycle count separates them.
