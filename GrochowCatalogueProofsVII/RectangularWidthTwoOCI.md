# Orbit-closure intersection for rectangular two-slice tensors

## GP-0267

Let the two unequal matrix sides be $a,b>0$, and treat the tensor as a homogeneous pencil $P(x,y)=xA+yB$ with $a$ rows and $b$ columns over $\mathbb C$. Product-special-linear orbit-closure intersection on rational inputs of this format is decidable in deterministic polynomial bit time. After swapping the matrix sides if necessary, put $b>a$ and $d=b-a$. If $d$ does not divide $a$, every pair has intersecting closures. If $a=kd$, let
$$
\Phi_P\colon \mathbb C[x,y]_{k-1}^{\,b}\longrightarrow
          \mathbb C[x,y]_{k}^{\,a},\qquad u\longmapsto P(x,y)u .
$$
Then the two closures intersect exactly when the two values of $\det\Phi_P$ agree. The determinant is of order $k(k+1)d$ and is taken in fixed monomial-coordinate bases. This handles all rectangular formats with width two. The question for larger fixed widths remains. [Fixed-width OCI context](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol383-ccc2026/html/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.32/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.32.html)

### A dense left-right pencil orbit

Write $a=kd+s$ with $0\le s<d$. Put $L_j(x,y)$ for the $j$ by $j+1$ block with $x$ on the diagonal and $y$ immediately above it, allowing the $0$ by $1$ block. A pencil $P_0$ with $d-s$ blocks $L_k$ and $s$ blocks $L_{k+1}$ has a dense orbit under $\mathrm{GL}_a\times\mathrm{GL}_b$. To see this, use the action $P\mapsto X P Y^{-1}$. From a source block $L_v$ to a target block $L_u$, the differential at the identity maps pairs of matrices to
$$
 (H,K)\longmapsto H L_v-L_u K.
$$
Here $H$ has shape $u$ by $v$ and $K$ has shape $u+1$ by $v+1$. The kernel condition says that the top $u$ rows of $K$ are $[H\ 0]$ and the bottom $u$ rows are $[0\ H]$. Overlapping those rows says each row of $H$ is the previous row shifted one position to the right, with zeros at the vacated first positions and the necessary trailing zeros of the first row. Thus the kernel dimension is $\max(v-u+1,0)$. When $u=0$ this is the unrestricted dimension $v+1$ of $K$. Since $u,v\in\{k,k+1\}$, the kernel dimension equals
$$
uv+(u+1)(v+1)-2u(v+1)=v-u+1,
$$
the difference of the domain and target dimensions. Thus every block of the differential is onto. The orbit is dense because its tangent reaches the whole pencil space.

### The invariant ring

Let $f$ be a nonzero homogeneous invariant of positive degree $e$ for the product-special-linear group. Invariance under the central scalar roots in the first two factors forces $a\mid e$ and $b\mid e$. By factoring any $X,Y$ into a determinant-one factor and a scalar, one gets
$$
 f(XPY^{-1})=(\det X)^{e/a}(\det Y)^{-e/b} f(P).
$$
The dense orbit forces $f(P_0)\ne0$. On a single block $L_j$ of $P_0$, multiply both its domain and range by the same scalar $\lambda$. This stabilizes $P_0$ and contributes the multiplier $\lambda^{e(j/a-(j+1)/b)}$. If $s\ne0$, one has $j/a\ne (j+1)/b$ for any integer $j$, so no such invariant can exist. The invariant ring consists of constants.

If $s=0$, then $a=kd$ and $b=(k+1)d$, and $\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)=k(k+1)d$. The determinant of $\Phi_P$ is invariant under row and column determinant-one changes and under determinant-one substitutions in $(x,y)$. For the last claim, both symmetric powers of $\mathbb C^2$ have determinant one under $\mathrm{SL}_2$, as seen first on diagonal and unipotent generators. At a block $L_k$, the polynomial solutions to $L_k u=0$ are the multiples of
$$
 \bigl((-y)^k,x(-y)^{k-1},\ldots,x^k\bigr).
$$
The recurrence $x u_i+y u_{i+1}=0$ and coprimality of $x,y$ give this description directly. No nonzero vector of degree $k-1$ occurs, so $\Phi_{P_0}$ is invertible on the direct sum of these blocks. Its determinant is a nonzero homogeneous invariant of degree $k(k+1)d$. Any two homogeneous invariants of the same degree are proportional by the semi-invariance rule on the dense orbit. Therefore the invariant ring is generated by $\det\Phi_P$. Over $\mathbb C$, reductive invariant polynomials distinguish the closed orbits and hence distinguish orbit-closure intersection. The displayed determinant has order polynomial in $a+b$, and its rational entries come from the input slices. Exact rational determinant computation gives the stated bit complexity.
