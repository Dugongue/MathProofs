# Exact doubling lifts of a prescribed dyadic tensor transporter

## GP-0102

Let $R=\mathbb Z/2^e\mathbb Z$. Given explicit quadratic forms in the three modes, two tensors over $R$, and a specified tuple of residue matrices over $\mathbb F_2$, there is a deterministic bit-polynomial algorithm to decide and construct an exact form-preserving tensor transporter reducing to that tuple. The same result holds for the literal bilinear equations, including the naive orthogonal model $g^{\mathsf T}g=I$. The forms may be degenerate. Thus given a polynomial-size complete list of residue transporters, the full decision and search problem is polynomial for that input. No smoothness or successful greedy lift at any individual level is needed. [Tensor and local-ring context](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1)

### The congruence group and the simultaneous data

For each mode use the full linear action on its tensor factor and the inverse change of variables on its quadratic or bilinear form. These are linear actions on the displayed coefficient modules, so their direct sum gives one $R$-linear group action on a vector containing all the data. This encodes transport between possibly different forms, and preserves all their literal coefficients. Choose any lift of each specified invertible residue matrix. This is invertible over $R$ by the determinant criterion. Apply the lift to the source, obtaining $v$, and let $w$ be the combined target. Reject if $v\not\equiv w\pmod2$. It remains to solve $kv=w$ for the product $K$ of the principal congruence groups, with every factor equal to identity mod two.

For $1\le j<e$ the layer of $K$ equal to identity mod $2^j$ modulo the next layer is the direct sum of the full matrix spaces in the modes over $\mathbb F_2$. The layer map is the set of entries of $(g-I)/2^j$ mod two, and elementary corrections $I+2^j E_{ab}$ attain all standard basis vectors. These corrections at all layers generate $K$, by matching successive lowest nonzero layers. Commuting with a principal congruence element fixes each layer pointwise, because multiplying matrices shows the commutator of elements congruent to identity mod $2$ and $2^j$ is identity mod $2^{j+1}$. Thus refining each layer by entry-coordinate flags gives a central series in successive quotients with factors of order two. Its length is $(e-1)$ times the sum of the squared mode dimensions, and the first nonzero coordinate of any element is computable directly.

### Compact subgroups and linear characters

Any subgroup specified by generators can be compressed in polynomial time to at most one pivot generator at every level of this series. First sift each input generator. If its first nonzero coordinate has a pivot, multiply by the inverse of that pivot on the left, which removes the coordinate. Otherwise insert it there. Next sift the square of each current pivot and the conjugate of each later pivot by each earlier one, starting over after every new insertion. At most the series length many insertions are possible, and each pass and each sift take polynomial time. When all tests succeed, descending induction proves that the normal products in the pivots with exponents zero or one form a subgroup. At each step the earlier pivot normalizes the already constructed suffix subgroup by the conjugation tests, and its square lies in that suffix. The conjugates of later pivots have no coordinates before their own pivot, so these tests use only that suffix. Every insertion is in the original generated group and every original generator sifts into the normal products. Thus this is exactly the specified subgroup. The argument also supplies a constructive membership test.

For a bit-valued homomorphism on such a subgroup, evaluate it on the current generators. If it is identically zero, its kernel is the whole subgroup. Otherwise find a pivot or generator of character one and use it with the identity as the two coset representatives. Schreier's rule gives at most twice the number of generators for the kernel. Compress them by the previous procedure. A homomorphism to a vector space over $\mathbb F_2$ can be handled one coordinate at a time in this way. To find a prescribed preimage, solve a linear system on generator images and multiply generators with the resulting zero-one exponents.

### Retaining every lift

Maintain a transporter $g$ taking $v$ to $w$ mod $2^j$ and a compressed generating set for the subgroup $K_j$ fixing $v$ mod $2^j$. Initially $g=1$ and $K_1=K$. The map
$$
 K_j\longrightarrow\mathbb F_2^M,\qquad
 k\longmapsto \frac{kv-v}{2^j}\bmod 2
$$
is a homomorphism, where $M$ is the total coefficient count. Indeed every $k\in K_j$ acts trivially on the combined coefficient module mod two, so in the product expansion the two displacements simply add at this layer. The new target displacement is $(g^{-1}w-v)/2^j$ mod two. If it is outside the image of the homomorphism, no member of the entire current coset $gK_j$ works at the next level. Otherwise multiply $g$ on the right by any computed preimage and replace $K_j$ by the computed kernel $K_{j+1}$. The complete set of transporters at the next level is exactly the updated coset. Iterating until $j=e$ gives existence, a witness, and a compact description of all lifts of the specified residue tuple. All group operations and coefficient actions are polynomial-bit over $R$ and the number of filtration steps and pivots is polynomial. This is a fixed-residue result, so it does not assume an algorithm for finding residue tuples in unrestricted input.
