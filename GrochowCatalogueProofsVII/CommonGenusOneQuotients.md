# Largest common genus-one quotient of two class-two groups

## GP-0357

For two Cayley-table groups of class at most two and odd prime exponent $p$, compute in polynomial input time the largest common quotient whose commutator subgroup has dimension at most one over $\mathbb F_p$. The algorithm returns the quotient table and both maps. This solves the unrestricted largest-common-quotient problem on the class where either input already has commutator dimension at most one. The prime may vary with the tables.

For one input $G$, let $W=G'$ and $V=G/W$, and put $d=\dim V$. These are elementary abelian. The commutator defines an alternating bilinear map $b:V\times V\to W$. Both spaces, their coordinates and the structure constants are found by ordinary table subgroup generation and linear algebra. The maximum-dimensional abelian quotient common to the two inputs is elementary abelian of dimension $\min(d_1,d_2)$.

Enumerate every nonzero linear functional $\lambda:W\to\mathbb F_p$. There are at most $|W|$ of them. The quotient by $\ker\lambda$ has commutator of order $p$. Put $2r=\operatorname{rank}(\lambda b)$. The centre of this quotient consists of the preimage of the radical in $V$. Modulo its one-dimensional commutator, its centre has dimension $d-2r$. Choosing a symplectic basis of the nondegenerate quotient and taking lifts shows that the group is a direct product of the exponent-$p$ extraspecial group of order $p^{2r+1}$ and an elementary abelian group of dimension $d-2r$. More explicitly, the symplectic lifts and a generator of the commutator satisfy the usual normalized symplectic commutator relations and have order $p$. They give distinct collected words of length coordinates $0,\ldots,p-1$, so their subgroup has the stated order and intersects the centre in precisely the commutator. A complementary central space supplies the remaining direct factor.

Any further quotient preserving this commutator has kernel central and disjoint from it: for an element of the kernel every commutator with a group element lies in the intersection of the kernel and the commutator. Consequently the extraspecial rank $r$ remains unchanged and precisely the elementary abelian dimension can decrease. Every value from zero to $d-2r$ is achievable by quotienting a subspace of the complementary central factor. Conversely, a quotient with commutator of order $p$ must first choose such a $\lambda$, as the image of the original commutator is the commutator of the quotient.

For each rank $2r$ appearing on both lists, the largest common quotient of that rank has order $p^{2r+1+\min(d_1-2r,d_2-2r)}$. Compare these values with the maximum abelian quotient and select the largest. A symplectic basis and a basis of the retained central complement give a common table model and a coordinate map from each input, by coordinate lifting and collection. Alternatively one may enumerate the cosets of the displayed kernels in each input and identify the two quotient tables by their symplectic and central bases. All output orders are bounded by both input group orders. If the input primes differ, the largest common quotient is trivial.
