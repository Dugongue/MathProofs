# Exact homology localization by width over a binary prime field

## GP-0998

For an explicit finite simplicial complex, let $w$ be the width of a supplied tree decomposition of the bipartite incidence graph between its edges and triangles. A minimum-weight support representative of any given $1$-homology class over $\mathbb F_p$ can be constructed in deterministic time $p^{O(w+1)}\operatorname{poly}(L)$, where $L$ is the bit length of the instance and the decomposition. For $p$ given in binary, even the decision threshold problem is NP-hard under zero-error expected-polynomial-time reductions at an absolute constant incidence width. The hard instances are closed connected orientable simplicial surfaces and may include a constant-width decomposition. Consequently an exact running time $f(w)\operatorname{poly}(L)$ in this binary input model would give a zero-error expected-polynomial-time algorithm for NP-complete languages.

### Width algorithm

Orient edges and triangles and let $z$ be the given cycle. Every homologous representative is $z+\partial y$ for some triangle chain $y$. The objective is $\sum_e w_e[\,z_e+\sum_\tau\partial_{e,\tau}y_\tau\ne0\,]$. Use a nice decomposition with each incidence edge introduced exactly once in a bag containing both endpoints. At a bag store the value of every present triangle variable and the accumulated boundary sum from introduced incidences for every present edge constraint. Keep the least cost incurred for edge constraints already forgotten. Introducing a triangle variable permits any field value. Introducing an edge constraint initializes its accumulated sum to zero. Introducing an incidence adds its signed contribution. Forgetting an edge constraint charges its weight precisely if its final sum plus $z_e$ is nonzero. Forgetting a triangle minimizes over its stored values. At a join the triangle values agree and the two partial sums add entrywise, since the introduced incidences in the two subtrees are disjoint. Thus the table invariant gives the exact optimum by induction, and backpointers give a minimizing chain. There are at most $p^{w+1}$ states per bag and at most their square many pairs at a join. The rational weights can be cleared by a common denominator of polynomial bit length.

### Constant-width source instances

Use SUBSET SUM on positive integers $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ and target $B$ with $0\le B\le\sum_i a_i$. Choose a prime $p>\sum_i a_i$ of polynomial bit length by sampling the appropriate dyadic interval above the sum and using exact primality testing. The interval has prime density of inverse-polynomial order in its bit length by the prime number theorem. The sampling is Las Vegas.

On vertices $0,\ldots,m$ put two parallel translation constraints $y_i-y_{i-1}=0$ and $y_i-y_{i-1}=a_i$ of weight one for each $i$. Close this doubled path with the constraint $y_m-y_0=B$ of weight $m+1$. Every labeling violates at least one constraint from each parallel pair. It has cost at most $m$ precisely when it satisfies the closing condition and takes one allowed difference per pair. Since all sums are smaller than $p$, this holds exactly for yes instances. The simple underlying graph is a cycle or a path for small $m$, so has treewidth at most two, and maximum incidence degree with parallel edges is at most four. [Polynomial-time exact primality testing](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781)

### Surface and preservation of width

Use the bounded-degree sphere-and-cylinder construction on this constraint multigraph, with regular translations of $\mathbb F_p$. For each variable place a fixed-size triangulated punctured sphere with one triangular port per incidence and an anchor. The preceding construction provides two edge-disjoint paths from each port vertex to the anchor, all edge-disjoint across the ports. The incident regions are joined by a triangulated prism with six cross edges carrying the constraint translation and identity on other edges. Give the six cross edges weight equal to the corresponding input weight, and give every region edge weight at least the largest input weight. Each triangle has trivial translation holonomy. A region-constant labeling violates weight exactly six times its original cost. Conversely, if the two anchor labels violate an input constraint, each of its six edge-disjoint routed paths has a violated edge of at least that constraint weight. The routed paths are edge-disjoint even between different constraints. The optimum is therefore exactly six times the original optimum. These fixed-size pieces work independently of $p$ and the binary encoding of the translations. [Bounded-degree surface construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/a32630798eebd129ef621986ca4e0b8ce5edfcf3/GrochowCatalogueProofsIII/FlatSurfaceConstraints.md)

Read the translations as a cocycle on the primal surface. Its dual is a cycle $z$, and adding dual face boundaries corresponds to taking primal coboundaries, so the support weight in the dual is exactly the violation objective. Triangulate each dual polygon by a private fan center. Extend $z$ by zero on the added edges and give every such edge a weight greater than the sum of all old weights. A face chain constant on the triangles within each old polygon has no cost on added edges. If a chain is nonconstant there, one of the added edges has different face coefficients on its two sides, costs the large weight, and cannot be optimal. Hence the triangulation preserves the optimum. [Cycle transfer and fan refinement](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/a32630798eebd129ef621986ca4e0b8ce5edfcf3/GrochowCatalogueProofsV/FixedFieldSurfaceThreshold.md)

For the width bound, assign every primal vertex to its variable region. The prism adds triangles and edges using only vertices on its two ports. Every final fan triangle belongs to the polygon of a single primal vertex. Group fan triangles by that primal vertex's variable region. Each group has absolute constant size because all tiles and their incidences have constant size. Two final triangles sharing an edge lie in the same group or in groups for adjacent source variables: edges within a dual fan stay within one polygon, and a dual edge connecting two polygons corresponds to a primal edge whose endpoints lie in one region or at the two ends of one cylinder. Replace each bag of a width-two decomposition of the simple source by the union of its face groups. This gives a decomposition of the graph on final triangles joined across their shared edges, with constant bag size. For the boundary-incidence decomposition, attach a leaf bag containing the two incident triangles and their edge constraint to any bag containing both triangles. This has absolute constant width, is explicitly computable, and completes the reduction with threshold $6m$.
