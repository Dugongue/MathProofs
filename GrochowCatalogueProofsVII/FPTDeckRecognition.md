# Recover all graph preimages from a deck with one low-diversity card

## GP-0875

Given $n$ graphs on $n-1$ vertices, put $k$ equal to the smallest number of twin classes of any card. In deterministic $2^{O(k\log(k+2))}\operatorname{poly}(L)$ time, where $L$ is the deck input length, the algorithm lists one canonical graph from every isomorphism class with exactly this deck. Thus deck legitimacy and the exact number of preimages are fixed-parameter tractable in a measure computed from the presented deck. The other cards have no promised structure.

Two vertices are twins when their adjacencies to every vertex outside their pair agree. Twinhood with equality is an equivalence relation. Each class is a clique or an independent set, and edges between any pair of classes are all present or all absent. The **twin quotient** gives each class its size and whether its internal edges are present, leaving the flag fixed to zero on singletons. It is a complete isomorphism invariant up to permutations of the quotient vertices. Indeed an isomorphism transports classes and preserves all flags, and a quotient isomorphism extends using arbitrary bijections of the corresponding classes. For at most $q$ classes, a canonical encoding follows by trying all $q!$ quotient orders. The partition can be checked directly from the adjacency matrix.

Fix a card with $k$ twin classes. An extension by one vertex splits each of its twin classes into at most two classes, according to adjacency to the new vertex. Each part remains a twin class or a subset of one. There is at most one additional class for the new vertex, so every true preimage has at most $2k+1$ classes. Deleting a vertex cannot increase the number of twin classes. Reject immediately when some supplied card has more than $2k+1$ classes.

If $n\le 2k+1$, generate all extensions of the fixed card by choosing the new neighborhood in $2^{n-1}$ ways. This covers every preimage. If $n>2k+1$, every preimage contains distinct twins $u,v$. Its card deleting $v$ is among the inputs. Rebuild it by cloning $u$, using the same incidences to vertices outside the twin pair and independently trying both possibilities for the edge to $u$. Run this extension for every vertex of every card. The number of candidates is at most $2n(n-1)$. Discard candidates with more than $2k+1$ twin classes. For each other candidate, compute canonical encodings of its entire vertex deck and compare the resulting multiset with the supplied encoded multiset. Keep precisely the candidates that pass, deduplicating by the candidates' own canonical encodings. Both completeness branches include every true preimage, and the check supplies soundness. All quotient enumeration costs are bounded by $(2k+1)!$ times a polynomial factor, proving the stated bit bound.

The parameter is often small even when the original problem is presented without a structural promise. For example a card of vertex-cover number $d$ has at most $d+2^d$ twin classes. This proof gives a parameterized deck-testing extension of the structural preimage algorithms in [Complexity results in graph reconstruction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2006.04.038).
