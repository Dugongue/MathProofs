# When additive block transport recovers field semilinearity

## GP-0335

Let $K/\mathbb F_p$ be an explicitly presented finite extension. Given any additive block transporter between two $K$-linear codes of length $n$, one can decide in deterministic polynomial bit time whether its coordinate permutation is realized by a $K$-semilinear monomial transporter, and construct that transporter when it exists. A single same-length additive comparison with witness suffices whenever the fundamental support is connected. More generally it suffices when the degrees of the cycle-generated subfields of the different components are pairwise coprime. No enumeration of the field elements is used. [Code equivalence and extension-field context](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12870)

Apply the supplied coordinate permutation first. The additive transporter identifies the ranks of all puncturings: their additive dimensions are the extension degree times their $K$-dimensions. In particular the two codes have common information sets. Choose the same one and write both generator arrays as $[I\mid A]$. For a nonbasis coordinate $j$, the code equation is $x_j=\sum_i a_{ij}x_i$. If the local additive maps are $T_i$, the transporter condition, with coordinate permutation now removed, is
$$
T_j\,M_{a_{ij}}=M_{a'_{ij}}\,T_i
$$
on each term of these equations, where $M_b$ means multiplication by $b$ on $K$. Setting one information coordinate nonzero at a time proves the condition for all pairs. In particular the two support graphs agree.

Independently rescale each displayed code by diagonal nonzero field scalings to make its entries one on a common spanning forest in the bipartite support. This is possible by assigning scalars recursively from a root in each component. Conjugate the $T_i$ by the scalings. Along the forest the equations now say that all maps in the same component equal one map $T$. For each off-forest edge the remaining condition is $T M_b=M_{b'}T$ on the corresponding two chord labels.

In one component the chord labels generate a subfield $L\le K$ over $\mathbb F_p$. Conjugation by the invertible $T$ identifies the multiplication algebra generated by the source labels with the one generated by the target labels. Both are the multiplication representation of the same subfield, since a finite field has one subfield of each available size. The induced isomorphism is a power of Frobenius on $L$. If $[L\colon \mathbb F_p]=h$, its exponent is unique modulo $h$, since the labels generate $L$. A component without chords uses $L=\mathbb F_p$. If there are isolated coordinates, their constraints are vacuous. The original additive automorphisms can be larger: in forest coordinates the componentwise endomorphism algebra of a code whose labels generate $L$ is precisely $\operatorname{End}_L(K)$, by the same commuting equations.

A global $K$-semilinear monomial transporter with this permutation exists exactly when a single Frobenius exponent on $K$ has the required residues in all components. For necessity, after the forest normalizations the local maps of such a transporter are $v\mapsto c_i v^{p^s}$ with one common $s$, and forest equations identify the $c_i$ within each component. Thus $s$ must send every normalized chord label to its mate. For sufficiency, apply the same Frobenius map without local scalars in all forest-normalized coordinates and undo the normalizations. Test all $[K\colon \mathbb F_p]$ possible exponents by repeated Frobenius and chord comparisons. This gives the asserted bit complexity and constructs a witness. A single component always has an allowed exponent, and pairwise coprime residue moduli give one by the Chinese remainder theorem.
