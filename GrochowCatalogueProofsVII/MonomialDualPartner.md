# Equivariant dual partners for linear codes

## GP-0313

The monomial-equivariant partner and nonsingular pairing requested in Section 6 of the code-equivalence survey cannot hold on all codes, over any finite field. More generally, there is an exact orbit-by-orbit criterion. A partner on one monomial orbit exists if and only if the code has a complement invariant under its monomial stabilizer in the coordinate representation. Given stabilizer generators, the criterion is constructive in polynomial bit time. [The partner question in Section 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12870#S6)

Use column-vector notation. Let $V=k^n$ and let the monomial group act by $M$ on $V$ and by $M^{-\mathsf T}$ on $V^*$. A partner for a $d$-dimensional code $C$ is a $d$-dimensional subspace $F\subset V^*$ whose evaluation pairing with $C$ is nonsingular. The rule must send $MC$ to $M^{-\mathsf T}F$. Therefore $F$ must be invariant under the contragredient action of $H=\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{mon}}(C)$. The annihilator of $F$ is then an $H$-invariant complement to $C$. Conversely the annihilator of any $H$-invariant complement supplies such an $F$. Defining the value on the whole orbit by contragredient transport is well-defined because $H$ preserves it.

Equivalently, solve for a linear map $P:V\to C$ such that its restriction to $C$ is the identity and it intertwines each supplied generator of $H$. In chosen bases these are linear equations. Their solution gives the complement $\ker P$ and its annihilator. The condition holds, for example, if the quotient of $H$ by its scalar subgroup has order prime to the field characteristic. Indeed conjugation on the affine space of retractions factors through that quotient, so averaging any retraction gives the required one.

For the universal obstruction, let $C=k\mathbf 1$ be the repetition code with length $n$ a positive multiple of the characteristic. Every coordinate permutation lies in $H$ and acts trivially on $C$. An $H$-equivariant retraction would be of the form $P(v)=\lambda(v)\mathbf 1$ for a functional $\lambda$ invariant under all permutations. The values of $\lambda$ on all coordinate unit vectors must be equal, say to $a$. Thus $\lambda(\mathbf 1)=na=0$, whereas the retraction condition would give one. This rules out an equivariant partner even on this one orbit. The criterion applies to the exact partner axioms, independently of whether another monomial-code reduction to weighted graph isomorphism exists.
