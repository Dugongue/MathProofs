# Group epimorphisms with power maps and nonreduced centroids

## Elementary abelianization and derived subgroup with a possibly nonzero power map

**Theorem.** Fix `r ≥ 0`. Let `G` be a finite class-at-most-two `p`-group for odd `p`, with elementary abelian derived subgroup, elementary abelian abelianization, and `dim_F G' ≤ r`. Given the Cayley tables of `G` and any finite group `H`, one deterministic polynomial-time algorithm decides whether `G` surjects onto `H` and constructs an epimorphism in the affirmative case. There is no restriction on the size of the central padding. The hypothesis allows exponent `p²`.

The algorithm first handles the trivial group and rejects any nontrivial target that is not a `p`-group of class at most two with both abelianization and derived subgroup elementary. These properties pass to quotients. The direct tests are scans, subgroup closure, and finite arithmetic. In the remaining cases put `W_X = X'` and `T_X = X/W_X`. Write `D_X = Z(X)/W_X ⊂ T_X`; choose a complement identified with `V_X = X/Z(X)`. For odd `p`, halving a commutator is defined since the derived subgroup has odd exponent. The same linearization now makes `X` an additive abelian `p`-group. Its `p`-multiplication lies in `W_X`, which is killed by `p`. This defines an `F`-linear map `P_X:T_X→W_X`, written `P_X^V + P_X^D` in the chosen decomposition.

An additive homomorphism whose prescribed restriction on `W_G` is `B` and whose induced map on `T_G` is `L_0` exists exactly when `B P_G = P_H L_0`. To see sufficiency, choose lifts of a basis of `T_G` and a basis of `W_G`. The additive group has the presentation consisting of commutativity, `p` times each `W_G` basis vector equal to zero, and `p` times each lift given by its assigned value under `P_G`. These relations present the group because they permit at most `|W_G||T_G|` elements, and the presented group surjects onto a group of exactly this order. Pick arbitrary target lifts of the specified images of the top-basis elements. The power-map condition gives a homomorphism by the presentation. Its being surjective is equivalent to both indicated maps being surjective, and its preserving brackets is exactly the bimap condition on `V_X` and `W_X`.

Enumerate surjective `F`-linear `B:W_G→W_H`. There are at most `|W_H|^r`. For a candidate `B` put `R = rad(B b_G)` and calculate the induced bimap on `V_G/R`; its values span `W_H`. A putative surjective map on the central quotients has kernel precisely `R`, so it is a quotient map followed by an isometry `Ã:V_G/R→V_H` of the ordered alternating tuples whose common output is `W_H`. Write `A` for the composite with the quotient. The only remaining component of a map `T_G→T_H` is a linear map `L:V_G⊕D_G→D_H`, since the `V_H` component is `A` on `V_G` and zero on `D_G`. Surjectivity holds exactly when `L` maps `R⊕D_G` onto `D_H`. Compatibility with powers is

```
P_H^D L = B P_G − P_H^V A.
```

Let `E = im P_H^D` and `π:W_H→W_H/E`. Restriction to `R⊕D_G` requires that `Q := B P_G|_(R⊕D_G)` land in `E` and surject onto `E`. A surjective compatible `L` on `R⊕D_G` exists precisely when additionally `dim ker Q ≥ dim ker P_H^D`. Indeed choose a surjection of these kernels, and map a complement of `ker Q` into a section of `P_H^D`. These conditions depend only on `B`. In particular they allow `π B P_G^V` to descend through `R`. On the remaining input vectors the equation for `L` is solvable exactly when

```
(π P_H^V) Ã = the map induced by π B P_G^V on V_G/R.
```

There is a polynomial joint test for this equality and the tuple isometry condition. Given both spaces `U = V_G/R` and `U' = V_H`, adjoin a three-dimensional auxiliary space with dual basis `e_1*, e_2*, e_3*`. Extend the old forms by zero on the auxiliary space. Add the three ordered forms `e_i* ∧ e_j*`, for `i < j`, and, for every coordinate covector `p_k` of the specified projected power map, append the ordered form `e_1* ∧ p_k`. Any isometry of the new ordered tuples must preserve the original space, which is the common radical of the three auxiliary forms, and also the auxiliary space, which is the common radical of the original forms. The same holds when the original space is zero and the original tuple is empty, by defining the common radical of an empty tuple to be the whole space. On the auxiliary space the three auxiliary forms force the map to be `±I`: their individual radicals are the three coordinate lines, so the map is diagonal and every product of two diagonal entries is 1. Consequently the map on the original space matches the marked covectors up to a single common sign. Multiplying it by that sign preserves its action on the old alternating forms and gives the exact covector equality. Conversely an isometry with the exact covector equality extends by the identity on the auxiliary space.

Apply the deterministic form of the Ivanyos–Qiao alternating-tuple isometry algorithm to the augmented tuples. Its time is polynomial in tuple length, dimensions, and `p`, which suffices for Cayley tables. The returned isometry allows the compatible `L` to be constructed by the previous linear-algebra argument. The presentation then constructs the output group epimorphism. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Algorithms based on *-algebras, Theorem 7(1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03495#page=6).

The same proof applies if the source commutator bimap has one nonzero directly indecomposable factor of bounded dimension over its centroid coefficient field, even if `dim_F G'` is unbounded. In that case the enumeration in §1.5 of the accompanying proof generates all relevant `B` candidates using target-adjoint field actions and images of a bounded number of bracket inputs. Conjugation by a fixed-output isometry keeps `B` unchanged. After generating `B`, the augmented tuple test above enforces the actual power-map conditions. A direct decomposition of the target bimap need not preserve its power map, so the argument here does not in itself extend the coefficient-field theorem to multiple source factors at exponent `p²`.

## A nonreduced-centroid extension at exponent p

**Theorem.** Fix nonnegative constants `r,s,c`. Let `G,H` have class at most two and exponent dividing the same odd prime `p`. Suppose, in every directly indecomposable commutator factor of `G`, the output `W` is generated by at most `r` elements over its local centroid `C`, and `C` is generated as an `F`-algebra by at most `s` elements. Suppose the dimension of the self-adjoint subspace of the target adjoint algebra is at most `c dim_F V_H`. Then an epimorphism from `G` onto `H` can be decided and found deterministically in polynomial Cayley-table bit time. This bound on source module generators allows arbitrary nilpotence lengths and unbounded coefficient-field output dimensions.

Use the target-frame and subset assembly of [the companion proof](PROOF.md). In a source factor `C` acts faithfully on `W`, because a centroid action vanishing on `W` vanishes on the domain by the common radical property. Evaluation on a generating tuple injects `C` into `W^r`, so it is possible to enumerate `C` itself. Its algebra generators can be found by enumerating at most `|C|^s` tuples and computing their algebra closures. Select at most `r` values `w_j = b_i(x_j,y_j)` whose residues generate `W/J(C)W` over the residue field. This can be done by taking pairs of basis vectors for the source domain, since their bracket values span `W` over `F`. Nakayama's lemma shows that the chosen values generate `W` over `C`.

For each nonzero orthogonal restriction of the target, a bimap epimorphism from this source factor induces a self-adjoint unital action of `C` on the restricted domain. Indeed the kernel of the map on domains is the common radical of the output-compressed source bimap, and is stable under `C` by balancing. The action need not be faithful. Every self-adjoint map of the restricted domain extends by zero on its orthogonal complement and is self-adjoint for the whole target. The hypothesis thus bounds the number of candidates for each of the `s` generator images by `|V_H|^c`. For each tuple of candidate images, evaluate the algebra expressions for a precomputed basis of `C`, then check that the resulting linear map preserves the unit and basis multiplications and has self-adjoint image. This produces precisely all allowed actions on the restricted domain in polynomial time for fixed `s,c`.

For each action `ρ`, enumerate the target images `u_j,v_j` of the chosen `x_j,y_j`. The output map must send `a w_j` to `b(ρ(a)u_j,v_j)` for every source scalar `a`. For a basis of `C` these bracket values specify the output map on an `F`-spanning family. Check linear consistency, surjectivity, and ordinary ordered-tuple isometry after quotienting by the common radical. These checks suffice exactly as for coefficient fields, and there are at most `|V_H|^(2r)` choices of images. The subset assembly constructs a global map. The nonzero radical of `C` causes no exception to any step.

## Arbitrary sources with a bounded number of target ratio generators

**Theorem.** Fix an integer $s$. Let $H$ be a class-at-most-two group of odd prime exponent dividing $p$. For the nonabelian case let $P_H$ be the space of scalar alternating forms of its commutator bimap on $V_H=H/Z(H)$. Suppose there is a nondegenerate form $\beta\in P_H$ and $s$ forms $\gamma_i\in P_H$ whose ratios with $\beta$ generate the algebra of all ratios, as a unital $F=\mathbb F_p$-algebra. Then from the Cayley tables of $H$ and any $G$, one can decide and find an epimorphism $G\twoheadrightarrow H$ in deterministic polynomial time.

Here the ratio of $\gamma$ with $\beta$ is the unique endomorphism $T_\gamma$ satisfying $\gamma(x,y)=\beta(T_\gamma x,y)$. The algebra generated by a list of ratios is computed by linear span closure under multiplication. Thus the target promise and a witnessing list can be found by enumerating references and at most $s$ generators from $P_H$. Since $|P_H|=|H'|$, this is polynomial in the target table length. For an abelian target use the ordinary abelianization rank test over $F$.

Replace $G$ by its quotient by the subgroup generated by all $p$-th powers and the third lower-central subgroup. Every homomorphism to $H$ factors through this quotient, which is computable in polynomial table time. Apply the abelianization rank test of the companion proof and work with the commutator spaces of the quotient. A surjective bimap morphism pulls $\beta$ to a form $\delta\in P_G$. Its input kernel is exactly $N=\operatorname{rad}(\delta)$ since $\beta$ is nondegenerate and the morphism is surjective. All pullbacks of $P_H$ must annihilate $N$ and therefore descend to $V_G/N$.

Enumerate $\delta$ and the $s$ possible pullback forms of the $\gamma_i$ from the forms in $P_G$ annihilating $N$. Compare the resulting ordered $s+1$ forms on $V_G/N$ to $(\beta,\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_s)$ on $V_H$ by simultaneous isometry. Every successful isometry induces the same pullback on the *whole* space $P_H$: the chosen forms determine the pullback of $\beta$ and the conjugation of each generating ratio, which determines the conjugation of every algebra element by multiplication and linearity. Check that these pullbacks belong to the descended source space. Then dualizing their inclusion gives the surjective output map and the simultaneous isometry gives the input map. Conversely a true group epimorphism supplies the reference and generator pullbacks among the guesses, so the test accepts. Bounds for ordered alternating-tuple isometry are those used in the companion proof. The enumeration costs at most $|H'|^{s+1}|G'|^{s+1}$ trials times a polynomial in the tables, with the source size interpreted before or after the quotient.

For example, this theorem applies when the target is a central quotient of a field Heisenberg group whose ratio field over $F$ has prime-power degree. In this case there is a unique maximal proper subfield if the degree exceeds one. Some ratio lies outside it, since the whole ratio space generates the field. That single element generates the field. Degree one needs no ratio generators. The [Heisenberg quotient proof](HEISENBERG.md) explains how to recover the ratio field.

These are informal proofs. The field-based enumeration and subset assembly are detailed in the [companion genus-two proof](PROOF.md).
