# Epimorphism testing with bounded-rank solvable radical

**Theorem.** Let $G,H$ be arbitrary finite groups given by multiplication tables, and put $N=\max(2,|G|,|H|)$. Write $R=\operatorname{Rad}(H)$. Suppose $R$ has a specified series of length $d$, whose terms are normal in $R$, with abelian factors generated by at most $r$ elements. Using the Cayley-table isomorphism coset algorithm stated in Theorem 9.1 of the Qiao–Zhang manuscript, there is a deterministic $N^{O(1+dr+r^2)}$-time algorithm to decide whether $G$ surjects onto $H$, produce an epimorphism on a yes instance, and count all epimorphisms. The same bound applies with no supplied series when the derived series of $R$ has these bounds.

The main case gives polynomial time when $d,r$ are fixed, even if $R$ has arbitrarily large order. The source group is unrestricted. Examples include targets with a radical that is abelian of bounded rank, and targets that are nilpotent with bounded class and bounded generator count. A further case allows arbitrary elementary abelian central radical provided that its intersection with the perfect derived group has bounded dimension. [Qiao–Zhang manuscript](https://github.com/sigact-stoc/stoc2027/releases/download/papers-2/1723.pdf)

For the same bounded-layer hypothesis, the algorithm also supports prescribed pairs $g_i\mapsto h_i$, including extension of a supplied homomorphism on a subgroup of $G$. Time remains $N^{O(1+dr+r^2)}$ after reading the marks.

## 1. List the possible preimages of the target radical

Take a chief series $1=G_0\lhd G_1\lhd\cdots\lhd G_\ell=G$. For each nonabelian chief factor $G_i/G_{i-1}$, let $C_i$ be its centralizer in $G$ under the section conjugation action. Every normal $L$ with $G/L$ Fitting-free is an intersection of some $C_i$'s. The empty intersection is $G$.

To see completeness, project the chief series to $G/L$. A factor maps either injectively or trivially because the kernel of the map on that factor is $G$-invariant. The surviving sections form a chief series in $G/L$, with compatible actions. Select the indices of its nonabelian factors. The intersection of their centralizers in $G/L$ is solvable. Indeed, intersect it with that chief series. A nontrivial intersection section in a nonabelian chief factor would be the whole factor by minimal normality. Its representatives centralize the factor, which would force the factor to be abelian. Each section of the intersection is therefore abelian or trivial. The radical-free hypothesis forces the intersection to be trivial, proving the asserted recovery of $L$.

For the converse, a nonabelian chief factor is a direct product $S$ of nonabelian simples. Its action group $G/C_i$ contains $\operatorname{Inn}(S)$, which is self-centralizing in $\operatorname{Aut}(S)$. An automorphism commuting with every conjugation by $x$ must send $x$ to another element giving the same conjugation, and $S$ is centerless. A solvable normal subgroup of $G/C_i$ meets $\operatorname{Inn}(S)$ trivially and thus commutes with it, so is trivial. The quotient by any intersection of the $C_i$'s is subdirect in the product of the corresponding $G/C_i$'s. A solvable normal subgroup maps to a solvable normal subgroup in each factor, so is trivial as well.

The series has length at most $\lfloor\log_2|G|\rfloor$, giving at most $|G|$ candidates. To construct the series, repeatedly choose a smallest nontrivial principal normal closure in the current quotient. Every proper nontrivial normal subgroup of such a choice would contain an element with a smaller closure. Quotients, closures, centralizers and intersections all use polynomial table scans. One can compute $R$ by checking which principal normal closures in $H$ are solvable. An element belongs to $R$ exactly when its principal normal closure is solvable. This step reuses the MathProofs chief-factor description from the Fitting-free epimorphism theorem. [Fitting-free quotient algorithm](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GroupIsomorphismAndEpimorphisms/EpimorphismsToFittingFreeGroups.md)

## 2. Bound the number of lifts across an abelian layer

For a finite group $L$ acting on a finite abelian group $A$ with at most $r$ generators, let $Z^1(L,A)$ be the additive group of functions satisfying $f(gh)=f(g)+g\cdot f(h)$. We claim
$$
 |Z^1(L,A)|\le |L|^r\,|A|^{3r^2+1}.
$$
This estimate allows nontrivial action and arbitrary exponents.

Work first with a nontrivial $p$-primary component $A_p$ of rank $r_p\le r$. Let $P$ be a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $L$. Restriction $H^1(L,A_p)\to H^1(P,A_p)$ is injective. An elementary proof uses the affine action $g*x=g\cdot x+f(g)$. If the restriction is a coboundary, this action has a $P$-fixed point $x$. Average the points $g*x$ over the left cosets $gP$ by multiplying the sum by the inverse of $[L:P]$ in the coefficient ring of $A_p$. The action of $L$ permutes the cosets and commutes with this affine average because the coefficients sum to one. The average is $L$-fixed, so $f$ is a coboundary. Hence
$$
 |Z^1(L,A_p)|\le |A_p|\,|Z^1(P,A_p)|.
$$
Let $K$ be the kernel of the $P$-action. Restriction to $K$ maps cocycles to $\operatorname{Hom}(K,A_p)$. Its kernel is the group of cocycles inflated from $P/K$. The homomorphism count is at most $|K|^{r_p}$: abelianize $K$ and factor $A_p$ into $r_p$ cyclic groups, each accepting at most $|K|$ homomorphisms. A cocycle on $P/K$ is determined by its values on generators.

Every $p$-subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(A_p)$ is generated by at most $3r_p^2$ elements. Here is a uniform proof. Write $A_p$ as $V/D$, where $V=(\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z)^{r_p}$ and $D\subseteq pV$ is the submodule of the cyclic-factor relations. Any automorphism lifts by choosing representatives of the images of the standard generators. The matrix preserves the relations and is invertible modulo $p$, since $V/pV\cong A_p/pA_p$, and hence is invertible over $\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$. The subgroup of invertible matrices preserving $D$ therefore surjects onto $\operatorname{Aut}(A_p)$. In the preimage of a $p$-subgroup, a Sylow $p$-subgroup still surjects onto it by order counting.

Let $S$ be such a $p$-subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}_{r_p}(\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z)$. For $1\le j<e$, the $j$-th congruence subgroup of $S$ has quotient by its next subgroup identified with a subspace $W_j$ of $M_{r_p}(\mathbb F_p)$ via $I+p^jX\mapsto X\bmod p$. For $2\le j<e-1$, taking $p$-th powers preserves this leading term in the next layer. This follows directly from the binomial formula modulo $p^{j+2}$, including $p=2$. Thus $W_j\subseteq W_{j+1}$ starting at $j=2$. Lift a basis vector only when it first appears in these nested subspaces. Its powers give the same leading term in every subsequent layer. Successive cancellation of leading terms shows that the congruence subgroup at $p^2$ is generated by these at most $r_p^2$ lifts. The layer at $p$ adds at most $r_p^2$ generators, and the image in $\operatorname{GL}_{r_p}(\mathbb F_p)$ adds at most $r_p(r_p-1)/2$ by the order of a Sylow subgroup there. Missing layers when $e\le2$ contribute zero. This proves the generator bound.

Consequently
$$
 |Z^1(L,A_p)|\le |A_p|^{3r_p^2+1}|P|^{r_p}.
$$
The actions preserve the primary components and a cocycle is the product of its component cocycles. The product of the orders of the relevant Sylow subgroups divides $|L|$, so multiplication gives the claimed bound. The proof only uses primary decomposition, restriction to Sylow subgroups and the congruence filtration.

## 3. Enumerate the lifts and count the epimorphisms

Let $1\to A\to E\to B\to1$ be one layer of the target-radical series, with $A$ abelian, and suppose a homomorphism $\psi:L\to B$ has been constructed. Choose lifts $s(b)\in E$ of the elements of $B$. Every lift of $\psi$ has the form $g\mapsto a(g)s(\psi(g))$, with $a(g)\in A$. Multiplication requires
$$
 a(gh)=a(g)+\psi(g)\cdot a(h)+c(\psi(g),\psi(h)),\qquad
 c(b,b')=s(b)s(b')s(bb')^{-1}\in A.
$$
Here the action is by conjugation through $s(b)$. It depends only on $b$ because $A$ is abelian. The difference of two solutions is a cocycle for the pulled-back action. Thus the lift set is empty or a coset of $Z^1(L,A)$.

These constraints are linear congruences using cyclic-factor coordinates of $A$. They can be decided in polynomial bit time by integer Smith normal form, with one extra integer variable per congruence to implement its modulus. Compute cyclic coordinates, for example, from the abelian presentation with a generator for each table element and a relation for each product, using the same normal form. To enumerate solutions, fix the values $a(g)$ in table-label order, retain only extendible prefixes, and test each of the $|A|$ possible choices at each node by the congruence solver. The number of extendible prefixes is at most $(|L|+1)$ times the number of solutions. This gives polynomial overhead per solution, including an immediate feasibility check for an empty branch. [Polynomial-time integer normal forms](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

Start at the trivial quotient of $R$ and apply this procedure down its normal series. At every level the number of surviving homomorphisms is bounded by the product of the bounds in §2. Multiplying the orders of the abelian layers gives
$$
 |L|^{dr}|R|^{3r^2+1}.
$$
The number of layers and every table construction cost at most another polynomial in $N$.

If $f:G\to H$ is surjective, then $L=f^{-1}(R)$ has Fitting-free quotient $G/L\cong H/R$. Its restriction $f|_L$ is onto $R$. Therefore for every $L$ from §1 enumerate the homomorphisms $L\to R$, keep the surjections, and collect their kernels $J$. Discard $J$ if it fails normality in $G$, and deduplicate the remaining subset masks. For each $J$, test $G/J\cong H$ with the Cayley-table coset algorithm. A successful test gives an epimorphism by composing the quotient map with the returned isomorphism. Conversely every epimorphism has appeared by its kernel. This comparison uses the full groups and so requires no compatibility test on any chosen radical quotient isomorphism.

For each passed kernel the number of epimorphisms is $|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|$, because the isomorphisms $G/J\to H$ form a torsor for that group. The Cayley-table coset algorithm applied to $(H,H)$ gives permutation generators of $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$, whose order is computable in deterministic polynomial time by a stabilizer chain. Count the distinct passed kernels and multiply. The time bound, including the bit cost of the output count, follows because a function $G\to H$ needs only $|G|\log_2|H|$ bits to write. The follow-up paper's exact marked transport also uses permutation-group operations on these cosets. [Zhang–Wang–Zhuang, §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18084v1#S2.SS2)

To impose marks, first remove repetitions and reject conflicting requirements on the same label. At kernel $J$, each requirement becomes the point-image condition $g_iJ\mapsto h_i$ on $\operatorname{Iso}(G/J,H)$. Restrict the coset by the individual-mark procedure of the follow-up paper. Concretely, if the current coset is $\varphi A$, find an element $a_0\in A$ sending the next source coset label to $\varphi^{-1}(h_i)$ by orbit traversal. Reject this kernel if no such element exists. Replace the coset by $\varphi a_0 A_{g_iJ}$, where $A_{g_iJ}$ is the point stabilizer computed by standard permutation-group operations. The final nonempty coset returns a marked epimorphism and its cardinality is the order of its final source stabilizer. Sum these orders over the distinct kernels. Full subgroup extension can be imposed by marking the supplied group's generators. [Zhang–Wang–Zhuang, Lemma 2.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18084v1#S2.SS2)

## 4. Special cases and the relation to the published questions

When $H$ is solvable, $R=H$, so the homomorphism enumeration itself already provides decision, search, counting and marks without the external isomorphism theorem. For example, a target nilpotent of class $c$ with $k$ generators admits the lower central series with abelian layers generated by at most $k^i$ elements in weight $i$. The basic commutators of weight $i$ in those generators span that layer, by bilinearity of the successive graded commutator maps. Its lower central series is directly computable from the table. Thus fixed $c,k$ give an unconditional unrestricted-source polynomial epimorphism algorithm. This covers bounded nilpotency class within the constant-generator target clause of Open Question 3(2).

There is a larger central-radical case. Suppose $H=H_0\times A$, where $H_0$ is perfect with a radical as in the theorem and $A$ is arbitrary abelian. For every epimorphism $f:G\to H_0$, its kernel maps onto $G/[G,G]$ since $f([G,G])=H_0$. Thus every epimorphism $G/[G,G]\to A$ combines with $f$ to give an epimorphism onto $H_0\times A$. Every epimorphism to that product is uniquely the pair of its projections. Consequently the count is the product of the two epimorphism counts.

Computing epimorphisms between explicit finite abelian groups and counting them uses cyclic coordinates. For one prime write the domain and target as direct sums of cyclic groups of orders $p^{a_i}$ and $p^{b_j}$, respectively. Sort the $b_j$ in descending order. Modulo $p$, the target row $j$ can have a nonzero entry at column $i$ exactly when $a_i\ge b_j$. These allowed row subspaces are nested. If $t_j=|\{i:a_i\ge b_j\}|$ is below $j$, there is no surjection. Otherwise when choosing row $j$ there are $p^{t_j}-p^{j-1}$ vectors outside the span of earlier rows. Each allowed entry has a constant number $p^{b_j-1}$ of lifts once its residue is chosen, and a forbidden entry has $p^{a_i}$ possible lifts. Full row rank modulo $p$ is equivalent to surjectivity by successive lifting through powers of $p$. Multiplication over rows, entries and primes gives the count with polynomial bit complexity and provides a search algorithm.

In particular, assume that $R=\operatorname{Rad}(H)$ is central elementary abelian and that $H/R$ is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups. Since $H/R$ is perfect, $H=[H,H]R$. Centrality then makes $[H,H]$ perfect as well. A complement $A$ to $R\cap[H,H]$ in $R$ gives a direct product $H=[H,H]\times A$. The radical of the derived group is exactly $R\cap[H,H]$, since its quotient by this intersection is Fitting-free. If that intersection has bounded dimension, the theorem and product rule apply with no bound on the full dimension of $R$. This handles a restricted part of Open Question 3(5) and extends the Fitting-free target result beyond the case of trivial radical. The remaining unrestricted constant-generator and central-radical clauses are separate. [Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar, §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)

The Qiao–Zhang manuscript treats isomorphism and cohomology transport between fixed quotient groups. Its Theorem 9.1 supplies the last coset test here. The new steps in this argument are the bounded cocycle enumeration and its composition with the chief-factor quotient list, which allows unknown kernels and unequal group orders. The proof is informal. The algorithmic consequence that invokes Theorem 9.1 inherits that manuscript's status.
