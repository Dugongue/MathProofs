# Determinant constraints on tensors over an intrinsic chain ring

Work over the fixed field F of three elements. All proofs below are informal. The input is a pair of explicit raw bilinear tensors with separate frames in all three modes. The algorithms produce one ordinary TI instance under deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions.

**Theorem.** Consider nonzero concise tensors whose centroid is a local chain ring and whose three mode modules have only even cyclic summand lengths over that ring. On this polynomial-time recognizable family, ordinary TI, one-sign TI, two-sign TI and SL-TI each have exactly the degree of unrestricted ordinary TI. The lengths, their multiplicities and the residue field degree may all grow. The construction applies to arbitrary bilinear multiplication data on the admitted modules. No unitality, associativity or input frames over the ring are required.

## 1. Recover the intrinsic modules

For a bilinear tensor b from U times V to W compute triples (a,b₁,c) with b(ax,y) = b(x,b₁y) = c b(x,y) by linear equations. They form its centroid C. Commutativity follows by moving two centroid operators between different inputs and using surjectivity onto the span of products and conciseness. The three modes are C-modules. Every tensor transporter intertwines their C-actions with one common unital algebra isomorphism φ between the centroids. A pair of endpoints with unmatched dimensions or intrinsic module lengths is rejected.

Compute the nilradical J by taking the kernel of x ↦ x^(3^h) for 3^h at least dim C. This is a linear map over F. The reduced algebra C/J is a finite product of fields. It is a field precisely when its cubing-fixed subspace has F-dimension one. This follows factor by factor. Write d for its F-dimension and recover the coefficient field K of C as the fixed subspace of x ↦ x^(3^d). Indeed reduction is injective on this subspace, and raising a residue lift to a sufficiently high iterated 3^d-power gives a fixed lift since any initial difference is nilpotent. All powers used here are computed by composing known linear maps.

Require dim_K J/J² = 1, and choose t in J lifting a basis. Nakayama gives J = (t). If J^m = 0 first occurs at m, expansion in successive radical powers or the induced surjection K[X] → C shows C = K[t]/(t^m). The excluded field case has only length-one blocks for nonzero modes, so does not belong to the theorem. Compute K-bases of the modes by linear algebra, then Jordan chains for multiplication by t as a nilpotent K-linear map. Let m_(i,s) be its number of blocks of length s in mode i. The family test requires m_(i,s) = 0 whenever s is odd. Compute the frame matrix B_i for the change from the chain coordinates, expanded in a single chosen F-basis of K, to each raw mode basis. All these operations are polynomial in the explicit tensor size.

## 2. All raw determinant signs share one scalar

For an ordinary transporter let σ be the isomorphism on coefficient fields, and write φ(t) = α t′ modulo J′² with α in K′×. For a mode map f_i, define

    E_i = Σ_s m_(i,s) s(s−1)/2,     η_i = det(B′_i)/det(B_i),

where B_i in the formula denotes the frame matrix. Then

    det_F f_i = η_i Norm_(K′/F)(α)^{E_i}.

To verify it, strip off the reference semilinear map sending each chain generator to the corresponding generator and each coefficient times t^j to its φ-image. It is well defined because φ(t) generates J′. On chain grades its diagonal blocks are α^j σ. Each length s therefore contributes det_F(σ)^s and Norm(α)^{s(s−1)/2}. Here det_F(σ) is in the independently chosen two field bases. All s are even, so even this basis-dependent sign vanishes. The residual map is C′-linear and invertible. On each t^j M/t^(j+1) M, order chains of length s>j by length. Maps from a shorter generator to a longer chain are divisible by the difference of the chain lengths in powers of t, giving a block triangular matrix on every such grade. The diagonal block A_s is the same invertible residue matrix for every j<s, namely the constant term on the equal-length generators. Thus its F-determinant over the entire module is the product of Norm(det_K A_s)^s. Again it is one. Restoring the raw frames gives the formula. Inverse-transpose tensor conventions leave the F-determinant signs unchanged.

Consequently each requested determinant sign prescribes either a constant check on η_i or one value of Norm(α). If any constants or prescriptions conflict return a fixed no instance. If no norm is required return the original ordinary comparison. Otherwise keep its single required value ε in {1,−1}.

## 3. Force the tangent norm on the same transporter

Include the input tensor, the centroid algebra and its three actions, the coefficient field embedded in the centroid, the ideal J with its inclusion, and J → J/J² with the induced K-module structure. These maps are all computed in the two raw inputs. The three centroid actions force the algebra map to be the one induced by the actual transporter. The field, ideal and quotient maps force its genuine semilinear map on the tangent line.

Introduce one more copy H of the regular one-dimensional K-module and its K-action. On the source include H × H → J/J² given by (x,y) ↦ xy times the class of t. On the target include (x,y) ↦ cxy times the class of t′, where c in K′× has norm ε. An extension on H necessarily acts by x ↦ h σ(x). Preservation of the product is precisely α = c h². In any finite field of odd order extending F, the square subgroup is the kernel of its norm to F×. Hence exactly the allowed transporters extend.

For ε = 1 take c = 1. For ε = −1 deterministically find a nonsquare in K′ as follows. Begin at a = −1. If a has a square root, replace a by that root. The multiplicative order, initially two, doubles at each successful replacement. This terminates in at most log₂(|K′|−1) successful steps. To test and extract roots, form the two-dimensional K′-algebra K′[x]/(x²−a). If it is a field its F-cubing-fixed subspace has dimension one. Otherwise it is K′ × K′ and the fixed space has F-dimension two. Take a fixed element outside F, represented by a linear polynomial in x. Its evaluations in the two factors are distinct F-elements. Computing gcds of that representative minus each of the three F-scalars with x²−a isolates a linear factor, hence a root. This uses polynomially many ground-field operations even for unbounded d.

The tensor system in this paragraph has constant signature and all maps have order at most three. Thus its isomorphism verdict compiles into one ordinary tensor pair in polynomial time, with both directions checked above. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

## 4. Ordinary TI hardness already holds with dual-number centroid

Start with an arbitrary ordinary comparison and compact to the three intrinsic supports. Compare their dimensions, handle zero tensors directly, and thereafter write the concise nonzero tensor as b from U times V to W. Form a split triangular algebra B with three diagonal scalar idempotents e₀,e₁,e₂, off-diagonal corners U in position (0,1), V in position (1,2) and W in position (0,2). Multiplication between off-diagonal terms is the single supplied map U times V to W. The table is associative since every triple off-diagonal product vanishes. The radical consists of the three off-diagonal spaces and its square is W by conciseness. The arrows of J/J² thus give the directed path 0 → 1 → 2, which fixes the vertices under any algebra isomorphism. Its images f_i of the idempotents agree with the target idempotents e′_i modulo the radical. The element z = Σ f_i e′_i is congruent to one modulo the radical and f_i z = z e′_i, so conjugation by its inverse aligns all three idempotents. The recovered corner maps are then exactly an ordinary transporter of b. Conversely every such transporter extends. A central element commutes with the three idempotents, so is diagonal. Commutation with the nonzero U and V corners equates all three diagonal scalars. Consequently Z(B) = F.

Take X to be the multiplication tensor of B. For any finite-dimensional unital associative algebra a multiplication isotopy p(x)q(y) = r(xy) yields units u=p(1), v=q(1) from the bijectivity of left multiplication by u and right multiplication by v. Then u⁻¹p(x) = q(x)v⁻¹ defines an algebra isomorphism, and conversely algebra isomorphisms give isotopies. Substituting the unit in the centroid equations also proves Cent(X) = Z(B). Thus the construction supplies a polynomial-size ordinary-equivalent concise pair X,X′ of scalar centroid. Extend each X bilinearly to R = F[e]/(e²) on three free modules, then expand the resulting tensor back to F-coordinate arrays. Multiplication by R gives centroid operators. Every centroid operator commutes with R because the expanded tensor is concise and its centroid is commutative. It is therefore R-linear in each mode. The R-linear centroid equations in the original rational frames are the F-linear centroid equations of X extended to R. Linear equations over a field commute with such scalar extension, so the full centroid is exactly R. Each mode has only length-two chains.

Every ordinary transporter of the expanded pair preserves the intrinsic maximal ideal and induces a transporter modulo e. On the quotient one obtains exactly X and X′, because the residue field automorphism fixes F. Conversely an ordinary transporter of X extends coefficientwise to R. Each of its three expanded maps has determinant the square of the original determinant and consequently has positive sign. The same construction thus reduces unrestricted ordinary TI to each of the four restricted comparisons.

The preliminary scalar-centroid construction is an elementary triangular-algebra specialization of the tensor-to-algebra method. The extension discards the signs of the unextended maps. It establishes no reduction from unrestricted SL-TI to ordinary TI.

## 5. Related reduction for a more general centroid

Suppose each mode is free of rank r_i over its full commutative centroid C, and every local factor C_j has even composition length ℓ_j over its residue field K_j. Choose free bases by constructive module isomorphism with the appropriate regular-module powers. Let φ be the induced centroid isomorphism and δ its F-determinant in the chosen centroid bases. Any invertible C-linear matrix on a free module has F-determinant one. On the radical grades of factor j its reduction over K_j repeats with total multiplicity ℓ_j, so its character is Norm(det over K_j)^{ℓ_j}. Consequently the raw determinant in mode i is η_i δ^{r_i}, for the corresponding free-frame ratio η_i.

All prescribed signs reduce to consistency of constants and at most one choice for δ. When δ is required, choose any constrained mode of odd rank and enforce just its original prescribed determinant by relabeling it as the tested mode, reflecting one target coordinate if needed. This is one one-sign TI query on the actual pair. When the test is ordinary, an ordinary comparison also maps to the one-sign problem by adding an unused coordinate to the tested mode. Thus the three-sign problem reduces to one-sign on this broader domain, and reduces to ordinary whenever all three ranks are even. Freeness can be tested without selecting a random unit or guessing a vector. [Brooksbank and Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

All of the statements here are informal. The chain-ring theorem compares restricted problems with the unrestricted ordinary TI degree. No priority claim is made.
