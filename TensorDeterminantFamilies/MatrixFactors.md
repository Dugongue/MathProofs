# Extracting matrix multiplication factors with exact transporter determinants

Work over the fixed field F₃ with three ordered modes and explicit dense inputs. A sign is the bit of a determinant in F₃×. Ordinary TI tests for an invertible transporter, and the three other problems require positive determinants in one, two or three named modes of the **same** transporter. Reductions below are deterministic, polynomial-time and many-one. The arguments are informal.

## The classification

For a nonzero concise tensor, fix one mode W as the label and regard a basis of slices as maps U → V. Decompose the resulting Kronecker representation as a sum of distinct indecomposables with positive multiplicities m_i. Define k_W as the gcd of these multiplicities. Define k_U and k_V cyclically. Changing slice bases applies an invertible change of arrow variables and preserves the multiplicities. On nonconcise inputs compute these three integers on the supports. A mismatch in formats, support dimensions or k-values rules out ordinary isomorphism.

For **every prescribed triple of positive integers** (k_U,k_V,k_W) and tested subset of modes I, the degree on that stratum is as follows. The comparison on the right is unrestricted over F₃.

| Even integers in the triple | Degree on the stratum |
| --- | --- |
| None | The ordinary, one-sign, two-sign or SL degree specified by I itself |
| Exactly one, k_W | One-sign TI if W is tested, ordinary TI otherwise |
| At least two | Ordinary TI, for any choice of I |

The single-even row applies after any permutation of mode names. No assertion that these displayed unrestricted degrees are distinct is needed. Each stratum is recognizable in polynomial time. Comparisons outside a prescribed stratum can be assigned verdict no. The upper reductions are uniform even when the integers are computed from the input. For the hardness directions the three prescribed integers are fixed constants. The degree statements also hold if one prescribes just their parities.

## 1. Construct the factor

Write D_W(k) for tensoring with the two-mode identity of size k in the U,V modes, leaving W alone. Its slices are k copies of the old pencil along the diagonal. A tensor is in its ordinary isomorphism image if and only if k divides every m_i. In that case take m_i/k copies of each indecomposable and keep the original arrow coordinates. For **each individual label map** s, comparing the enlarged tensors with s fixed is equivalent to comparing their roots with that same s fixed. This follows by applying Krull–Schmidt before and after dividing all multiplicities. It proves uniqueness of a root up to ordinary TI.

These operations are effective. Form the fixed-label endomorphism algebra by the linear equations bM_j=M_j a. Compute its radical and its explicit semisimple quotient. Lift primitive diagonal idempotents in successive complementary corners and take their images on both vertices. For a lift x of an idempotent, x²−x lies in the nilpotent radical J. If 3^h is at least the nilpotence index, x^(3^h) is idempotent because x² and x commute. A primitive residue idempotent stays primitive after lifting. The simple factor Mat_m(K) of the quotient records one indecomposable type of multiplicity m. Constructive fixed-arrow module isomorphism gives bases identifying the repeated copies with their representative. This supplies actual coordinate changes, including their determinants. The deterministic algebra structure algorithm is polynomial in the characteristic and input length, as required for F₃. [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495) · [Brooksbank and Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

Enlarging across one edge leaves the other two multiplicity lists unchanged as lists of multiplicities, although the constituent dimensions can change. To see this in the other slicing, the new arrows are M_j pr_l from X^k to Y. An endomorphism (P,Q) satisfies Q M_j pr_l = M_j pr_l P for every j,l. The common kernel of the original M_j is zero by conciseness. Hence P has zero off-diagonal blocks, and all its diagonal blocks coincide. The endomorphism algebra is exactly the old one. The dual calculation uses that the images span Y for arrows inc_l M_j. Thus the simple matrix factors of the semisimple quotient and their matrix sizes are unchanged. Removing an edge factor preserves conciseness, since a rank deficiency in its root would persist after inflation. On the enlarged edge itself all multiplicities are multiplied by k.

Consequently the three maximal factors can be extracted in any order. The tensor left over has all three gcds equal to one. The three identity factors on the three edges together form a rectangular matrix multiplication tensor up to the ordering and dual identification of coordinates: their entries are the product of three Kronecker deltas identifying the two occurrences of each edge index. The remaining tensor is unique up to ordinary TI by the single-edge cancellation and commutation of the three operations. With frames, any fixed extraction order suffices. There are three extractions and each acts on arrays no larger than the input.

## 2. Recover the entire sign set

Let D be the set of three-bit sign vectors of actual transporters of a root pair. Empty sets are allowed. In repeated-copy coordinates the enlarged pair has sign set

    P_k D + H,       P_k = diag(k mod 2, k mod 2, 1)

for an enlargement in the first two modes; sums of sets are Minkowski sums over F₂. Here H is the sign image of the fixed-label unit group of the enlarged source with zero third bit. To prove the formula, at any feasible label map duplicate any root witness k times. Every other enlarged witness with this very label differs from it by a fixed-label source automorphism. Conversely every such correction is available with that same label. The existence of a root witness at each feasible label was proved in Section 1. In the raw input frames add the two computed frame-change sign vectors for the endpoints. Signs of inverse and dual maps agree over F₃.

To compute H let E be the enlarged fixed-label algebra and E/J its quotient, a product of Mat_b(K). The subgroup 1+J has determinant one on each vertex, by nilpotence. On the radical filtration of a vertex space X, the natural simple module for each factor occurs h_X times across the semisimple grades. A unit in the factor contributes Norm(det_K u) to the determinant raised to h_X. This follows from block triangularity on the grades and the determinant formula for restriction of scalars. The quotient map on units and each finite-field norm are onto. Thus H is the binary span of (h_U,h_V,0) mod 2 for all factors. One can compute these integers by the dimensions of the factor components of the grades divided by b[K:F₃]. Equivalently, on a primitive summand of that type they are its vertex dimensions divided by [K:F₃]: its lifted primitive idempotent picks one copy of K on each natural simple composition factor. This shows H is unchanged by enlarging multiplicities. For odd k it is already absorbed in D, including at each fixed label. Choices of block or basis order need no invariance assumption because the actual frame determinants are retained.

Iterating gives the exact raw sign set as η + P D_core + H_total, with P the product of the three diagonal maps, η an explicit vector, and H_total an explicit binary subspace. Update shifts and correction spaces by these same affine maps at each step. Empty D_core still gives the empty set. For unequal support dimensions reject. For equal proper supports first compact, use standard frames in full modes, and include the coordinate directions for unused complements in H_total at the raw end. Indeed any map on a support extends with either determinant on a positive-dimensional complement, independently in each deficient mode. The zero tensor can be decided directly.

With no even factors, odd repetitions preserve D up to frames and freely adjustable unused coordinates. For the degree upper bound the original instance itself already suffices. With exactly one even factor, P retains only its label bit. Test which of 0 and 1 on that label make the specified signs attainable using η and H_total. Return a fixed no if there is no allowed bit, ordinary TI on the core if both work, or one sign on the core label if only one works. Asking for negative sign becomes a positive-sign query by a fixed reflection of the target label coordinates. When W is untested the label bit cannot affect the verdict. If an ordinary query is needed as a one-sign query, double a pair of modes including the tested one: an ordinary witness duplicates with positive tested determinant, and cancellation gives the converse. With two even factors on different edges, P is zero. Any sign constraint then amounts to a three-bit linear feasibility test and one ordinary comparison of the cores.

## 3. Exact hardness on the stated strata

There is a simple way to start with all three gcds equal to one **without changing the signed problem**. To a concise tensor attach a direct-sum block given by multiplication in R=F₃[t]/(t^n), with n larger than every input side and divisible by four. Each endpoint uses the same coordinate frames for this block. It has three properties.

* It is a single indecomposable in each fixed-label slicing. Identify the tensor with the symmetric trilinear form taking the coefficient of t^(n−1) in xyz, using the nondegenerate coefficient pairing. In any view its slices include the identity and the single Jordan shift after the pairing identification. Its fixed-label endomorphism algebra is R, a local ring.
* Every ordered-mode autotopism has determinant one in each mode. For an autotopism of a unital associative algebra, write f(x)g(y)=h(xy). The elements a=f(1), b=g(1) are units because the corresponding multiplication maps are invertible. Thus f(x)=aφ(x), g(y)=φ(y)b and h(z)=aφ(z)b for one algebra automorphism φ. Multiplication by a unit of R has determinant its constant term to the nth power. An automorphism sends t to c t plus higher powers and has determinant c^(n(n−1)/2). Both exponents are even. Dualizing a mode does not change these conclusions.
* In the direct sum it cannot mix with old centroid blocks. For concise tensors the centroid of a direct sum is the product of the centroids: testing the centroid equations on inputs in the two different summands kills off-diagonal maps, by nondegeneracy in each input and spanning of the output. The centroid of this multiplicative block is R, because the centroid equations evaluated at 1 force all maps to be multiplication by one common element. It has no nontrivial idempotent. Its larger dimension distinguishes it from every primitive centroid summand of the original. Hence the total sign profile is unchanged. In each slice view the block gives an indecomposable of multiplicity one: all original summands vanish on the entire marker label subspace, whereas the new summand does not. All gcds are therefore one, even for a previously highly divisible tensor.

For a comparison whose input has deficient support, save the independently free coordinate bits before adjoining the block. Restore them by appending one unused dimension per such mode to **both** outputs after the constructions below. With matching raw formats, support sizes and chosen support bases on both sides, Section 2 proves this restores precisely the intended freedom. Full modes can keep their standard bases at compaction. Trivial yes/no inputs can be represented by fixed pairs on each prescribed stratum, for example by adjoining the marker to two identical small tensors or to inequivalent concise tensors and inflating. Inequivalent small tensors of one format exist, for example the multiplication tensors of F₉ and F₃×F₃; their centroids are nonisomorphic.

Now apply the three prescribed enlargements to the marked pair. In the all-odd case all original sign bits survive, including the freely restored bits. In the one-even case start with an arbitrary one-sign instance on its label if this label is tested, or an ordinary instance otherwise. In enlarged coordinates a repeated witness has positive signs on both matrix modes of the even edge. Subsequent odd inflations preserve these signs and allow every such witness. Conversely a fully qualifying enlarged witness has a root witness with the same protected label sign if that bit matters, by the odd-step equality and the fixed-label cancellation at the even edge. In the two-even case any ordinary yes witness becomes positive on all three modes by duplication, and every enlarged ordinary witness descends. Families obtained this way have exactly the prescribed gcds on their supports.

In the ordinary-input hardness branch with exactly one even k_W and W untested, only matrix modes can be constrained. No protected label sign is required. When a protected source-label sign is already free because that input mode was deficient, appending its unused dimension after enlargement restores the same freedom. Canonical copy coordinates can always be used in constructing the endpoints, so the hardness construction introduces no endpoint sign shift.

## Consequence for the unrestricted question

Every comparison supported on two even directions has ordinary TI degree, and one even direction leaves at most the one-sign degree. The stratum k_U=k_V=k_W=1 still has **exactly the original degree at every tier**, by the neutral marker. In particular proving a reverse comparison there would give the same unrestricted reverse comparison. The theorem gives a polynomial-time classification by the removable factors, without proving equality or strict inequality of any two unrestricted degrees. The recovery uses the Kronecker decomposition of fixed-label modules and relates to the adjoint-ring approach to bimaps. No priority claim is made.
