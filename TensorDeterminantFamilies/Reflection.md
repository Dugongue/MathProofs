# Two-sign tensor isomorphism is complete for special-linear comparison of alternating matrix spaces

Over the field of three elements, the special-linear comparison of arbitrary lists of alternating matrices, under **independent** left and right changes of basis and an arbitrary label change, has exactly the deterministic polynomial many-one degree of unrestricted two-sign tensor isomorphism B. This is an informal reduction. The upper bound also holds for all fixed-label self-transpose lists by the companion proof. The lower-bound construction here also works for symmetric matrices on replacing the minus sign in the final block matrix by a plus sign.

## Normalize the signs

In a matrix pencil write input, output, and label signs as (u,v,c). Inverse transpose leaves these signs unchanged. The prior exact binary sign projection takes an unrestricted B comparison with tested bits x,y to a comparison whose full attainable sign set consists of exactly (x,0,y) for attainable input triples. It preserves emptiness. We use the prior exact even, balanced, concise normalization, preserving this entire sign set, and take the common side n to be even and at least four. These two dependencies are the earlier informal [sign projection](SignProjection.md) and [balanced normalization and sign-coordinate construction, Sections 1 and 2](StructuralComparison.md). The nonzero projection uses the three-arm output of equal sides a+b+M with a,b odd and M even, so its three sides are even. Equivalently one can apply the exact balanced normalization after projecting. Constant small exceptional formats can always be decided directly.

Compute the fixed-label matrix sign subgroup H on the source, as in the upper-bound proof. If it is nonzero and differs from the line generated by (1,0), the comparison has no ordinary transporter: on every ordinary-positive input its sign set is invariant under H and has second bit zero throughout. Return fixed alternating no. If H is that line, the tested bit x can be corrected independently of y, so the required verdict is simply existence of a transporter with c=0. Project again to (0,0,c), with exact even balanced concise normalization. For this second pair if H is nonzero return fixed alternating no, again by the exact profile. Thus on every remaining branch H is zero, dimensions and labels are n, and the desired pair of equations on that tensor is

    u+v=0 and c=0.

This holds both for the initial (x,0,y) and for the second (0,0,c) projection. All control decisions use algebra computations, never a tensor-isomorphism oracle.

## Reflect the slice representation

Let A: U tensor W → V be the combined slice map, surjective by concision. Write K for its kernel. The inclusion K → U tensor W gives a pencil R: K → U with labels W*, by contracting against the label covector. An invertible label map on W acts by inverse transpose on the reflected labels. A pair of endpoint maps a on U and c on W carrying kernels onto kernels is equivalent to existence of a unique invertible induced map b on the quotient V. Conversely a reflected transporter gives precisely that kernel equality by the contractions against all covectors. These constructions also identify fixed-label transporter groups.

Choose the basis of each K so that its concatenation with any lifts of the ordered quotient basis of V has determinant +1 in the ordered tensor-product basis of U tensor W. This is achieved by flipping one kernel vector if necessary, as n²−n is positive. Different choices of lifts have the same determinant. For each transporter the determinant bit on K is consequently n u+n c+v=v. The reflected vertex signs are v,u and its label sign is c. In particular, if H is zero, both reflected vertex signs are individually fixed for each feasible label map.

Every indecomposable summand of the original slice representation has both vertex dimensions positive by concision. Let its input and output dimensions be i,j, and let d be the field degree of its endomorphism division algebra modulo radical. Then i/d and j/d are integers, and both are even since H is zero. Indeed an automorphism of this one copy extends by identity on all the other copies. On its vertex space the determinant of an invertible endomorphism is its residue field norm raised to i/d or j/d, by the radical filtration of this local endomorphism algebra, and all residue units lift. In particular i≥2 and j≤n.

Reflection acts on each direct summand and gives dimensions (ni−j,i). The reflected constituents stay indecomposable: its endomorphisms correspond to the original endomorphisms by the same kernel and quotient argument, including noninvertible maps. The matrix on K is uniquely determined by the maps on U and W (for an endomorphism W is fixed), and the old matrix on V is uniquely determined on the quotient. For n≥4, i≥2 and j≤n imply ni−j>i. Thus every reflected indecomposable has strictly larger input than output dimension. Taking its transpose gives exclusively the opposite strict inequality. The conclusion holds after every invertible relabeling.

## One alternating pencil with both original tests

If R(z) is the reflected matrix pencil, output the ordered alternating pencil

    Z(z) = [   0       R(z)  ]
           [ −R(z)ᵗ     0    ].

As a fixed-label quiver representation this is a direct sum of R and −Rᵗ, by reordering the input side. The latter is isomorphic to Rᵗ using a scalar. Because of the two opposite strict dimension inequalities, Krull–Schmidt separates all constituents from the two sides. In particular an arbitrary label change of Z is feasible exactly when it is feasible for R, even if actual matrix intertwiners have off-diagonal components. Necessity uses uniqueness of the multiset of indecomposable types. Sufficiency extends a reflected transporter on both halves by its dual inverse maps.

More explicitly, write R′(Cz)=P R(z) Qᵗ. The block diagonal map diag(P,Q) on both physical matrix modes gives a transporter Z to Z′. Its matrix determinant bits are both u+v and its label bit is c. Every other fixed-label transporter for the same C differs from it by a fixed-label automorphism of Z. All such automorphisms have both matrix signs zero: in the unit algebra modulo radical, the simple factors for R and −Rᵗ are disjoint, since they share no indecomposable type. Every quotient unit is realizable by a block-preserving automorphism from the two separate endomorphism algebras. The remaining multiplicative kernel consists of units of the form 1+j with j nilpotent, which act with determinant one on each vertex. Both component automorphisms have zero vertex bits by H=0 and the reflection correspondence, and transpose interchanges the two bits. This proves that the entire physical sign set of Z is exactly

    { (u+v,u+v,c) : (u,v,c) attainable before reflection }.

Therefore the special-linear verdict for this one literal alternating raw tensor pair is precisely the desired unrestricted B verdict. Source and target use the identical order of the two skew-block spaces, so no unmatched permutation factor appears. Known negative output can use unequal ranks in equal-size alternating matrices. All pencils, kernel bases and algebra operations have polynomial size and construction time. Replacing the lower left block by +R(z)ᵗ proves the same lower bound for literal symmetric pencils, proving B-completeness for their S restriction as well.

The hardness statement concerns left-right tensor isomorphism with three independent physical maps. It gives no claim about previously published pseudo-isometry, where the two matrix maps are required to be the same. The Krull–Schmidt, module isomorphism and algebra structure ingredients are standard; the exact sign projection used in the first step is previous informal MathProof work. Publication priority has not been established. [Brooksbank and Luks](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf) · [Ivanyos and Qiao](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)
## The entire ladder for these pencils

For the lower bound on either first-matrix-sign or both-matrix-sign decision, project an unrestricted one-sign comparison to (x,0,0) and follow exactly the same computation. If H vanishes, the final profile consists of (x,x,0) for the original feasible values of x. If the positive-input-compatible H is generated by (1,0), the x constraint is free on ordinary yes pairs. The second projection (u,v,c) ↦ (0,0,c) is still a nonzero linear map, and now c is identically zero on feasible triples. Its final output has profile {(0,0,0)} when ordinarily feasible, or the empty set otherwise. The first-matrix-sign and the both-matrix-sign verdicts therefore both recover the original one-sign question. No-transport branches give no through the same rules. For ordinary hardness use the same construction on an ordinary query with an unused coordinate in its first mode. It preserves ordinary feasibility throughout, since every early negative output is ordinarily negative. The fixed-label transposition upper bound gives the reverse reductions. Hence in order of zero, one, both matrix, and all three signs, the degrees on each of the two literal pencil families are ordinary TI, one-sign TI, one-sign TI, and two-sign TI. The proof uses the earlier sign-coordinate reductions and the explicit kernel version of quiver reflection. [Bernstein, Gelfand and Ponomarev](https://doi.org/10.1070/RM1973v028n02ABEH001526)
