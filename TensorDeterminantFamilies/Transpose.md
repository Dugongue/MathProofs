# Removing determinant equations by transposing a slice system

All tensors in this note are explicit, over the fixed field of three elements, with three ordered modes. Write O, A, B, and S for ordinary tensor isomorphism and the restrictions to determinant one in the first mode, first two modes, or all three modes. Determinant signs are written additively in the field of two elements. All results are informal.

## A recognizable class containing every symmetric or alternating matrix space

Think of a tensor X of format n by n by r as a list of square matrix slices indexed by a label vector. Write Xᵗ for transposing each matrix. Call X eligible if there is a pair of invertible matrix maps taking X to Xᵗ while fixing all labels. This definition includes every list of symmetric matrices and every list of alternating matrices, even if the generic slice is singular, every slice is singular after field extension, or the slices are linearly dependent.

**Theorem.** On eligible tensors, B reduces to one A instance and S reduces to one B instance in deterministic polynomial time. The reductions work with independent, hidden changes of basis in the two matrix modes. Eligibility is recognized, and any required change of basis is constructed, in deterministic polynomial time. Any eligible B comparison can therefore also be combined with an independent ordinary comparison in one A instance using the determinant-neutral conjunction construction.

### Transpose with calibrated determinant signs

Write a transporter X to Y as g = (P,Q,C), with Y(Cz) = P X(z) Qᵗ. Swapping the first two maps takes Xᵗ to Yᵗ with the same label map C. Construct fixed-label maps α_X = (A_X,B_X,I) from X to Xᵗ and α_Y from Y to Yᵗ. This is constructive isomorphism of two modules for the r-arrow two-vertex quiver. It can be implemented by the two vertex projections and one off-diagonal operator for each arrow on the direct sum of the vertex spaces. The usual basis map on the domain vertex is Q⁻ᵗ. The algorithms return the actual matrices from which the signs are read. Eligibility is invariant under arbitrary ordinary TI, since the transport g and its transpose transport any such α_X to an α_Y with fixed label. A zero or exceptional format entails no extra hypothesis. [Brooksbank and Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

Put a = sign(det A_X) + sign(det A_Y) and b = sign(det B_X) + sign(det B_Y), where sign(det M) denotes the binary determinant bit. If g has sign bits (p,q,c), the transporter α_Y⁻¹ swap(g) α_X has sign bits (q+a,p+b,c). Their quotient is a fixed-label automorphism of X.

### Computing the fixed-label signs

Compute the endomorphism algebra E of the quiver representation from the linear equations b X_i = X_i a on source and target vertex endomorphisms (a,b). Its units are exactly the fixed-label automorphisms, with physical maps (b,a⁻ᵗ). Their binary determinant image on the two physical modes is a subspace H of the two-dimensional binary vector space. The two modes are ordered output, then input.

For completeness, compute E/J as a product of full matrix rings of degrees m_i over extension fields K_i, where J is its radical. On each vertex take the grades J^k V/J^(k+1)V. In each grade, project to factor i with its central idempotent. Divide the sum of these projected dimensions by m_i times the degree of K_i over the base field to get the integer t_i(V). The determinant of an E-unit on V is the product over i of the field norms of its quotient determinants raised to t_i(V). This follows by semisimplicity on the grades and block triangularity on the filtration. All quotient units lift, and each norm is onto. Thus H is spanned by the pairs (t_i(output),t_i(input)) modulo two. Inverse transpose leaves the sign unchanged. All steps are deterministic polynomial over this fixed field. [Ivanyos and Qiao, algebra structure algorithm, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

If H is nonzero, choose a nonzero vector h in it. The set D of signs of transporters is closed under adding (h,0), since arbitrarily chosen transporters may be composed with that fixed-label automorphism. The B verdict therefore requires just one linear equation in the two matrix signs, namely vanishing of a nonzero linear functional with kernel generated by h. For S also require a zero label sign on the same transporter. An invertible binary change of determinant coordinates puts these one or two equations on the first one or two physical modes in a single ordinary tensor pair. This step uses the earlier exact sign projection construction for ordered three-tensors over the field of three elements. It is a dependency for making the readouts standard physical-mode queries. For h=(1,0) or (0,1), simply select the surviving matrix mode. For h=(1,1), the surviving matrix equation is p+q=0.

If H is zero, signs on both matrix modes are constant within each fixed-label transporter fiber. The two transporters above consequently satisfy p+q=a=b. If a or b is nonzero, return a fixed no instance for the signed verdict. Otherwise p=q on every transporter. B now asks for p=0, and S asks for p=c=0. A mode permutation suffices. For an empty transporter set the original signed predicates and the resulting tests are all false, including in any branch returning a fixed no instance. This completes the proof.

Only the invertible case of the prior sign projection is needed here. The earlier [construction in Section 2](StructuralComparison.md) is included as a supporting informal lemma. The transposition argument uses no assertion that a left-right transporter is a congruence. It also does not need a nonsingular pencil element or a realizable Pfaffian evaluation. The proof concerns independent matrix maps on ordered tensors and does not assert an equivalence for the separate problem of pseudo-isometry.

## All three modes under a cyclic permutation

**Theorem.** Suppose X and Y have equal-sized modes with their displayed coordinates and each tensor is unchanged by the same cyclic permutation of the three slots. On this entire class A and B give identical answers on the same pair. S asks exactly one total-sign equation p+q+c=0 and hence reduces to one ordinary A-format instance by the existing sign projection. The hypothesis holds for symmetric and alternating trilinear forms and is checked directly in the displayed coordinates.

The relative sign set D is empty or affine: choose one transporter and then compose with source stabilizers. It is closed under cyclic rotation ρ. For any d in D, the sum d+ρd+ρ²d belongs to D since it is an odd affine combination. That sum repeats the parity of d in all three positions. Thus existence of an attainable even parity is equivalent to attainment of zero, proving the S assertion.

If A succeeds there is an attainable d with a zero coordinate. When at least two coordinates vanish, rotate them into the first two positions. When exactly one vanishes, d has even weight and the preceding argument puts zero in D. This proves B. The converse is immediate. This proof uses exactly the common cyclic symmetry in the displayed coordinates. Independent changes of bases must be calibrated before applying this second theorem.

## Scope

These are deterministic many-one reductions on the specified raw families. General ordered tensors have neither guaranteed fixed-label transpose equivalence nor a given common cyclic symmetry. No unrestricted many-one degree merger or claim of publication priority follows. The output queries preserve ordinary nonisomorphism and enforce the surviving determinant equations on one common transporter.
