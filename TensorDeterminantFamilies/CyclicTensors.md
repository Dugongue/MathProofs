# The exact signed degree of cyclic three-tensors over F₃

The inputs in this theorem are ordered arrays on F₃^n ⊗ F₃^n ⊗ F₃^n whose **entries in their given frames** satisfy T_ijk=T_jki=T_kij, at both endpoints. A transporter still has three independent invertible mode maps. The theorem does not concern equivalence under a single simultaneous change of basis, and it does not require invariance under a transposition. Outside the recognizable input family one may assign verdict no.

**Theorem.** Ordinary TI on these inputs has the degree of unrestricted ordinary TI. Each of first-mode sign, first-two-mode signs, and three-mode SL-TI on these inputs has the degree of unrestricted first-mode sign TI. These are deterministic polynomial-time many-one statements. Within the cyclic family, first-mode and first-two-mode decision give identical yes sets. SL has its own verdict.

## Upper bounds

Write D for the sign triples of actual transporters, using bits in F₂. If nonempty it is an affine subspace, since it is a translate of the stabilizer sign image. Cyclic invariance at both endpoints implies that a cyclic rotation of any witness is also a witness. For d in D the sum of its three rotations therefore belongs to D and equals (s,s,s), where s is the sum of the coordinates of d. In particular SL is equivalent to existence of one even-total-sign witness. For the two-sign test, a witness with first bit zero has weight zero, one or two. A vector of weight one rotates to one with the first two bits zero. The three rotations of a weight-two vector sum to zero. The converse is immediate. The empty profile gives no in every case. For the one-entry cyclic tensors [1] and [−1], every attainable triple has odd total sign. First-mode and first-two-mode TI both accept, whereas SL rejects.

Here is explicitly how to turn the even-total-sign test into one first-mode test. If any mode has unused directions, an ordinary witness extends across its complement with either sign, so use the ordinary comparison with an additional unused coordinate in the tested mode. Handle a zero tensor or dimension one directly. Otherwise the input is concise. Make all sides odd if necessary by adjoining the evaluation block of format (ab,a,b) for odd a,b larger than any original side. This block is concise with scalar centroid (the matrix units in the full map of the two small spaces onto their tensor product force this), and its larger format prevents exchange with any original indecomposable centroid block. Its total sign is always positive, as the large map is determined by the Kronecker product of the two small maps, with both exponents odd. With no block needed the original sides are equal and odd, greater than one.

Use calibrated castling on the first mode of the resulting format (u,v,w). If L is its u-dimensional flattening image in V⊗W, output the tensor of its annihilator in the dual ambient space. Complete the ordered basis of L to one with ambient determinant one at each endpoint, using a rescaling of one complement vector. The complement is positive dimensional: for an originally odd cube n>1 this is n²−n>0; after the evaluation padding the cross terms make vw>u. A pair of changes in V,W on the old data preserves the flattening image exactly when its dual maps preserve the new annihilator. The old and new first-mode maps are forced by these restrictions. The first-mode sign on the new pair is the old first sign plus w times the old second and v times the old third, by the determinant on the ambient quotient. Passing to inverse or dual does not alter signs in F₃. As v,w are odd this is exactly the total sign. Thus there is one ordinary one-sign query. The other two upper bounds use the original arrays with one tested mode.

## Reverse bound for every signed tier

We give the construction since a cyclic sum on overlapping coordinates can introduce extra witnesses. The one-axis encoding in this argument uses calibrated castling and the regular action of a local algebra.

Start with two nonzero tensors with matching formats in a first-mode sign comparison. If the tested dimension is odd, the stabilizing scalar −I in that mode, compensated by −I in another mode, permits either tested sign. If the tested mode has unused directions it likewise has both signs. In either case reduce the original ordinary predicate to a new instance with full even tested dimension by compacting all supports and then doubling the tested mode and one other mode with the two-mode identity. Ordinary witnesses duplicate with positive tested sign. Conversely, fixed-label Kronecker cancellation shows that ordinary isomorphism of the doubled pair implies ordinary isomorphism of the compact inputs. The double stays concise. Mismatched support sizes and zero cases can be handled directly.

We now have an even positive first dimension u with full first support. Pad the unconstrained second and third modes with unused coordinates to odd sizes v,w greater than u and no smaller than the previous sizes. Padding preserves the tested first sign because restriction to the supports in the untested modes loses no first-mode maps. Calibrated castling as above gives a tensor of odd format (vw−u,v,w) whose total sign is exactly the old first sign: the two unaltered sign terms cancel those in the new first sign. The new tensor is concise. Its new matrix space has codimension u in the full v by w matrix space. A common kernel line or image in a proper subspace costs at least min(v,w)>u in codimension. The label flattening is injective by construction.

Write this tensor as a surjective concise bilinear map m:U×V→W. Make the unital local algebra

    B = F₃1 ⊕ U ⊕ V ⊕ W

with uv=m(u,v) its only nonzero radical products. Its radical J has J²=W, and the two input subspaces in J/J² are recovered as the opposite one-sided annihilators of the induced product. Therefore algebra isomorphisms give precisely the old transporters on the three subquotients, and every old transporter extends by direct sum. Their determinants on B are the old total signs by the radical filtration. The dimension b of B is even.

Take the action tensor X of B on B⊕B by left multiplication, of format (b,2b,2b). Every ordinary transporter (s,g,h) of these action tensors satisfies L_{s(x)}=hL_xg⁻¹, whence h=L_{s(1)}g and φ(x)=s(1)⁻¹s(x) is an algebra isomorphism. The element s(1) is a unit since left multiplication by it is invertible. The equation says g is semilinear over φ, and the map φ⊕φ gives an extension for every φ. Unit multiplication on B has positive determinant because it induces its residue scalar on every radical grade and b is even. A B-linear automorphism of B⊕B has on each radical grade two copies mixed by the same invertible two-by-two residue matrix. Its determinant on the full module is the determinant of that residue matrix to the power b, hence positive. The φ⊕φ contribution is also positive. Thus this tensor has exactly the physical sign triples (d,0,0) for the attainable d of the starting one-sign instance. These statements also show that a pair with no ordinary transporter in the intermediate comparison has empty final profile.

The centroid of X is Z(B). Evaluating the centroid conditions at the identity slice identifies its matrix-mode operators with left multiplication by the label image of 1, and the remaining slices force this element to be central. As B is local with scalar residue, Z(B) has no nontrivial idempotent. Thus X is concise and indecomposable under ordinary direct sums.

Use separate spaces L,M,N of dimensions b,2b,2b and set E=L⊕M⊕N at both endpoints, in the same summand order. Place X in L⊗M⊗N inside E⊗E⊗E and add its two cyclic rotations. Call the resulting tensor Y. It has literal cyclic invariance, including in characteristic three, because the three blocks have disjoint support. Its three centroid-primitive summands have ordered formats (b,2b,2b), (2b,b,2b) and (2b,2b,b). They are pairwise distinct. The product-of-centroids argument for concise direct sums forces every ordinary transporter to respect all three summands. The restrictions on the three blocks are independent because in every physical mode the three summands occupy disjoint spaces. A single block that has sign bit d in its label contributes that bit only to the corresponding physical mode. In computing the determinant on E, the identifications of its coordinate blocks use the same order at both endpoints.

If D₁ is the set of attainable bits of the original one-sign query, the new profile is exactly D₁ × D₁ × D₁. Thus testing any nonempty subset of the new signs recovers the old one-sign verdict. Fixed cyclic yes and no examples can represent constant branches, e.g. the same nonzero scalar tensor versus itself, or a zero tensor versus a nonzero tensor. For ordinary hardness, start with a concise compacted ordinary comparison, use its local algebra and the same three rotated action blocks. No parity assumptions on b or sign calculations are necessary since ordinary transporters are the only requirement. This proves the ordinary degree as well.

The cyclic restriction is substantive: a pair given after unknown independent rebasings can lose literal cyclic invariance, and detecting such a presentation while transporting signs would require a further argument. The result supplies no unrestricted reverse comparison in the four-class chain.
