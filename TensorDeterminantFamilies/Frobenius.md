# Determinant constraints on raw local Frobenius multiplication tensors

This is an informal deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction over the fixed field F of three elements. Let the tensors be arbitrary independent rebasings of the multiplication tables of finite-dimensional associative unital local Frobenius F-algebras with residue field F. The algebras may be noncommutative and ungraded. The theorem gives a recognizable sufficient condition for imposing any subset of the three physical determinant signs in a single ordinary tensor comparison.

## The computable condition

Write J for the radical, S for the one-dimensional socle, and s for the last nonzero power of J. For 0 ≤ i,j ≤ s form

    X_ij = J^i ∩ Ann(J^(j+1)),
    Q_ij = X_ij / (X_(i+1,j) + X_(i,j-1)).

Use J^0 = R and Ann(R) = 0. For each i the product on Q_ii is a perfect bilinear pairing into S. Choose a nonzero socle vector ω, write the pairing as B_i times ω, and form K_i = rad(B_i − B_i^T). Scaling ω does not affect K_i. Put k = max_i dim K_i, counting an empty or zero space with dimension zero. For a dense written input of length L, the reduction runs in time polynomial in L + 2^k. Thus any fixed bound k ≤ C log(2+L) gives a polynomial algorithm to produce the single ordinary TI pair. There is no joint width bound on all K_i.

The commutative subquotient pairing underlying this construction is classical. Iarrobino's symmetric decomposition uses Q_ij on diagonals of constant i+j. The application here tracks determinants, compiles the bounded pieces separately, and also handles noncommutative local Frobenius algebras. [Iarrobino and Macias Marques](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03586)

## Algebra and pairing

A nonzero left or right ideal of R meets its respective socle, by taking a last nonzero radical multiple. The left and right socles coincide in S. For clarity, for a local Frobenius algebra both socles have dimension one because the regular module is dual to the opposite regular module and the residue dimension is one. Each socle is a two-sided ideal and J acts trivially on its one-dimensional opposite side since J is nilpotent. Choose a functional l with l(ω)=1. The form l(xy) is nondegenerate in both variables by the ideal property above. Nondegeneracy determines the Nakayama automorphism ν through l(xy)=l(yν(x)). Associativity gives multiplicativity of ν by applying this identity twice, and nondegeneracy makes ν invertible. It preserves J and every power of J.

The left orthogonal of J^i consists of the elements annihilating J^i on the left, since xJ^i is a right ideal. The right orthogonal consists of the right annihilators. These two annihilators coincide. Indeed ν sends the left orthogonal to the right orthogonal and it preserves the left annihilator as an ideal intrinsically defined from J^i. This proves that the annihilators in the displayed definition are unambiguous. Both orthogonal operations interchange Ann(J^i) and J^i by dimension and inclusion.

For x in X_ij and y in X_ji, the product xy belongs to S, since yJ ⊆ J^(j+1) and x annihilates that power. The denominators pair to zero. For example J^(i+1) annihilates the paired y on the left, and Ann(J^j) annihilates y on the right of x. For perfection, the left orthogonal of X_ji for the form l(xy) equals Ann(J^j) + J^(i+1). Intersect it with X_ij. If x = u+v lies in that intersection, with u in Ann(J^j) and v in J^(i+1), then u also belongs to J^i, and v also belongs to Ann(J^(j+1)). This is exactly the denominator of Q_ij. The same intersection argument in the other variable proves zero right kernel. This gives the claimed perfect pairing Q_ij × Q_ji → S.

Radical grade J^i/J^(i+1) has the increasing filtration by images of X_ij as j increases. Its successive quotients are exactly Q_ij by the intersection formula. Thus any independently chosen frames for the Q_ij can be lifted in lexicographic filtration order to a single frame of R. All algebra isomorphisms respect these filtrations.

## The determinant of every isomorphism

For an isomorphism φ sending ω to λω′, choose arbitrary frames in Q_ij for i<j and product-dual frames in Q_ji, independently at the two endpoints. The two induced matrices then have determinant product λ^(dim Q_ij). For each i choose a basis of K_i and a symplectic basis of the quotient Q_ii/K_i for its nondegenerate alternating form obtained from B_i − B_i^T. Lift these bases to a frame of Q_ii. If this quotient has dimension 2r_i, its induced map has determinant λ^r_i. This follows by applying the Pfaffian congruence identity to the two standard symplectic matrices. It holds in characteristic three for arbitrary r_i, since that identity is integral. The K_i contribution is the determinant of the induced map on K_i.

Build the full frames by the preceding filtration rule. Every algebra isomorphism has determinant in these frames

    det φ = λ^b · product_i det(φ on K_i),
    b = sum_(i<j) dim Q_ij + sum_i r_i.

All frame changes, B_i, radicals, exponents and lifts are computed independently by linear algebra at each endpoint. Unequal dimensions of corresponding intrinsic data rule out any ordinary transporter.

## Physical determinants and ordinary compilation

The raw input can be recognized with its coordinate frames. View it as a bilinear table μ: U × V → W of cubic format n and require the first flattening injective. Compute E = {(P,Q): Q M(u) = M(u) P for every u}, where M(u):V→W is the slice. Require dim E = n and identify V,W with the left regular E-module by constructive module isomorphism. In these frames M(u) is right multiplication by its value at 1, and these values give an isomorphism U→E. The recovered table is multiplication on E with opposite product. Conversely a unital associative table and any raw rebasings pass this test, using the right regular operations as its fixed-label adjoints. Compute J using the explicit finite-field algebra algorithm. Test residue dimension one and the left/right socle dimensions one. They imply the common-socle Frobenius test above. A one-dimensional table is immediate. [Brooksbank and Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf) · [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03495)

Compose the three algebra frames with the raw mode frames and absorb their separately computable target/source determinant ratios into the requested signs. An isotopy p(x)q(y)=r(xy) of associative unital algebras has p=L_u φ, q=R_v φ and r=L_u R_v φ, where u=p(1), v=q(1) are units and φ is an algebra isomorphism. Invertibility of the multiplication maps by u,v follows from the invertibility of p,q,r by setting one variable to 1. Substitution gives φ(x)=u^(-1)p(x)=q(x)v^(-1) and proves multiplicativity. Over F, taking mode duals or inverses preserves signs. Unit multiplications have determinant equal to their residue to the nth power, by the radical filtration. Thus if d=det φ, the three signs are (a d, c d, a c d) with a,c ranging independently over {1} for even n and {1,−1} for odd n. Scalar units attain every listed choice. After calibration, enumerate these constant-size possibilities to determine the allowed set of d. The empty set gives a fixed no pair. Both signs give ordinary TI. For a singleton impose the determinant formula above.

Here are constant-signature details for the last case. Include the unital algebra and the socle with its inclusion. Store the numerators X_ii and quotients Q_ii as direct sums over i, tagged by a split algebra F^(s+1) acting by projections. Give the latter its successor shift on coordinate idempotents to fix every label in order. Link the sums of numerators to R by the sum of inclusions, and to the sums of Q_ii by the direct sum of quotient maps. Link the direct sum of the K_i by inclusion into the latter. The projections together with the map to R force the actual induced maps in every component. Take the componentwise direct product of the algebras ΛK_i, tagged by the same ordered labels, with the degree-one inclusions. Each individual algebra has dimension at most 2^k, and its generators force exactly the exterior extension of the given map. Link the top lines by their inclusions. The resulting scalar on line i is exactly det(φ on K_i) in the chosen coordinate top wedges. Omit zero-dimensional cells or use the unit as their volume.

To test a product of line scalars, include one extra factor given by the socle line when b is odd. A sequence of one-dimensional auxiliary spaces has multiplication maps from the preceding auxiliary line times the next factor line to the following auxiliary line, sending chosen basis vectors to the following basis vector. Mark the initial basis vector and the prescribed multiple d of the final basis vector. Then the marks extend exactly when the product is d. All such maps are a single bilinear map on labeled direct sums; ordered idempotent labels and their fixed shift identify predecessors, successors, and factor lines. Set unused cross products to zero. The number of sorts and maps stays constant. Repeated or zero-line cases amount to a unit factor, and an empty product is tested directly. The ordinary tensor-system compiler gives one ordinary three-tensor comparison over F, of size polynomial in L + 2^k. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

## Two size checks

Take one truncated chain of length 2j for every 1≤j≤t, and identify the units and the final nonzero vectors of all chains. Write x_j^(2j)=ω. Mixed positive-degree products from different chains and all products ωJ vanish. An explicit basis is 1, ω, and x_j^a for 1≤a<2j. The dimension is t²+2. The internal monomial x_j^a occupies the unique cell Q_(a,2j−a) because its radical depth is a and the last power of J with which it has nonzero product is 2j−a. The unit and socle occupy (0,2t) and (2t,0). The displayed monomial bases simultaneously split both filtrations. Every Q_ii for 1≤i≤t is a single line, whereas their total dimension is t. Even t makes the table even-dimensional. A single exterior algebra on the combined lines has dimension 2^t, whereas the separate algebras here have total dimension 2t. This compares those two specific constructions.

A second example is R = F1 ⊕ V ⊕ Fω with uv = B(u,v)ω on V and ωJ=Jω=0. Choose any nonsingular alternating B on an even-dimensional V. It gives an associative noncommutative local Frobenius algebra. The entire unbounded Q_11=V has skew part 2B nondegenerate, hence K_1=0. Every algebra isomorphism has determinant fixed by its socle multiplier and the calculated frame changes. Both examples allow arbitrary independent rebasings of the three input modes.

These results apply to an explicitly recognizable family. Arbitrary tensors need not represent a local algebra; even for such algebras a diagonal skew radical can have unrestricted dimension. No claim of unrestricted many-one equivalence or publication priority is made.
