# Trace-indistinguishable traceless matrix triples

**TheoremDB P2750.** For ordered triples in $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb F_5)^3$ under simultaneous $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_5)$ conjugation, the largest fiber of the all-word trace profile contains 32 conjugacy classes. The unique maximizing fiber is the profile of the zero triple. For $N=E_{12}$, its canonical representatives are
$$
(0,0,0),\qquad (N,bN,cN),\qquad (0,N,cN),\qquad (0,0,N),
\quad b,c\in\mathbb F_5.
$$
The cutoff is seven traces of words of length at most three. The companion [exact enumeration](check_p2750.py) computes every conjugacy orbit independently of archived files. This is an informal proof with a reproducible finite certificate. [TheoremDB P2750](https://www.theoremdb.org/statements/P2750/)

## Seven traces determine the entire profile

Work over $k=\mathbb F_q$ with $q$ odd. For traceless $2\times2$ matrices $X,Y$, Cayley–Hamilton and its polarization give
$$
X^2=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(X^2)I,\qquad XY+YX=\operatorname{tr}(XY)I.
$$
Order the letters $A<B<C$. Rewrite adjacent inversions using the second identity and remove adjacent repeated letters using the first. Each rewrite decreases either the length or, at the same length, the inversion count. Every word reduces to a combination of $I,A,B,C,AB,AC,BC,ABC$, with coefficients polynomial in the six self- and pairwise traces. The traces of the first four monomials are fixed, so the six Gram entries and $\operatorname{tr}(ABC)$ determine the trace of every word. Conversely, each of these seven invariants is itself a word trace. The empty word has trace 2 for every triple. For background on trace invariants of $2\times2$ tuples, see [Elmer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05717).

## Conjugacy fibers over all odd finite fields

On $V=\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)$, the symmetric form $B(X,Y)=\operatorname{tr}(XY)$ is nondegenerate. The basis $E_{12},E_{21},\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ gives a hyperbolic plane and an orthogonal nonisotropic line. The form $\omega(X,Y,Z)=\operatorname{tr}(XYZ)$ is alternating, since the trace is cyclic and the repeated-argument case reduces to the trace of a traceless matrix. It is nonzero on the displayed basis.

Conjugation preserves $B$ and $\omega$ and acts faithfully modulo scalar matrices. The resulting injection $\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{SO}(V,B)$ is onto since both groups have order $q(q^2-1)$. Thus the trace fiber is determined by the Gram matrix $G$ of the three vectors and their oriented volume.

- **Gram rank three.** The triple is a basis. Equal Gram matrices give an isometry carrying the three basis vectors to their counterparts. Equality of oriented volumes forces determinant one. The fiber has one orbit.
- **Gram rank two.** The span is a nondegenerate plane. The vector relations are exactly $\ker G$, so assignment of the three vectors to their counterparts gives a well-defined isometry of the planes. Witt extension extends that isometry to $V$. If its determinant is $-1$, reflect the orthogonal complement of the target plane. The fiber has one orbit.
- **Gram rank one.** A nonzero diagonal entry exists, since otherwise every principal minor of size two forces its off-diagonal entry to vanish. After relabeling the positions for the proof, fix the first vector to be $h$ with this prescribed nonzero norm. Witt extension and a reflection fixing the resulting first vector justify this normalization in $\mathrm{SO}(V,B)$. The Gram matrix determines the components of the other two vectors along $h$. Their remaining components in $h^\perp$ are isotropic and orthogonal to each other. If the plane is anisotropic they are both zero. If it is hyperbolic, a nonzero pair lies on one of its two isotropic lines. The determinant-one stabilizer of $h$ preserves each line and acts on it by all nonzero scalings, since on the plane it has matrices $\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1})$ in an isotropic basis. There are $q+1$ projective pairs on each line, and one zero pair. The fiber has either one class or $2q+3$ classes.
- **Gram rank zero.** The span is totally isotropic and has dimension at most one, because $V$ has Witt index one. The triple is zero or has the form $(aN,bN,cN)$ for a nonzero nilpotent $N$. All nonzero nilpotent $2\times2$ matrices are conjugate, and $\operatorname{diag}(t,1)$ scales $E_{12}$ by $t$. Thus the nonzero orbits are indexed by the projective points $[a:b:c]$ of $\mathbb P^2(k)$, with first nonzero coordinate normalized to 1. Their volume is zero. This is one profile with $q^2+q+2$ classes.

For $q\ge3$ the last count strictly exceeds $2q+3$. Thus the maximum and its unique profile are determined over every odd finite field. The proof uses classical trace and orthogonal-group facts. The conclusion specific to P2750 is the comparison of every trace fiber and the classification of the maximum.

## Complete enumeration over the field of order five

The program enumerates all 480 invertible two-by-two matrices, computes their 120 distinct conjugation actions on the 125 traceless matrices, and enumerates the action diagonally on all $125^3=1{,}953{,}125$ triples. For each first unseen triple it marks its complete orbit and checks that every member has the same seven invariants. It verifies the 32 representatives in the statement by comparing their canonical orbit representatives with the enumerated maximizing fiber.

| Conjugacy classes in the fiber | Number of fibers |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 15,562 |
| 13 | 62 |
| 32 | 1 |

This gives 16,400 orbits in 15,625 fibers. An optional output argument writes a tab-separated certificate with one row per orbit, containing the lexicographically least triple, orbit size, and trace profile.
