# Audit of the Qiao–Zhang dependency of local-ring tensor lifting

The audited manuscript is the 3 November 2026 [Qiao–Zhang submission](https://github.com/sigact-stoc/stoc2027/releases/download/papers-2/1723.pdf), 282 PDF pages, SHA-256 EB9A3355EBF7E1BF3411126E84255BB2C8C90B452DD7F9EFCFCDFEC173D52AE2. This is a mathematical dependency and proof-interface audit. It does not certify the entire upstream manuscript.

## Exact calls in the transfer

- **Theorem 8.1, printed page 71.** The input permits a subgroup specified by matrix generators in the product of the three general linear groups. The output is emptiness or an actual transporter and generators of the source stabilizer. The runtime is $q^{O(n_1+n_2+n_3)}$ times a polynomial in input length. Thus supplying the three elementary-generated special linear groups is a legitimate call, including in low characteristic.
- **Definition 25 and Theorem 8.10, printed pages 91–92.** The input is an affine action on an explicit invariant subquotient $N/P$ of a direct sum of prime-field tensor products of degree at most three. The summand labels are fixed. The cocycle need only hold in the quotient. The output gives the complete transporter with elements and words in the original generator encoding. The exponent charges the dimensions of the original moving representations, including the faithful group encoding, once. The fixed copies enter the explicit input length.
- **Local-ring instantiation.** The quotient of the congruence ideal is a residue vector space. A basis makes its tensor layer a fixed number of copies of the residue three-tensor. Each copy is an equivariant quotient of the prime-field tensor product by scalar balancing. Both the tangent image and the further congruence translation space are explicit invariant denominators. The latter is prime-field linear, as required, even if it is not residue-field linear. The original group remains faithfully represented on the direct sum of the three residue modules. Hence the exponent is $p^{O((\log_p q)\sum_i n_i)}$, independent of the number of congruence steps. The determinant-restricted version uses a trace-zero tangent and the same width.
- **Word issue.** The outer lift needs words for residue output generators to evaluate chosen lifts at the previous level. Theorem 8.10 explicitly provides them. Alternatively, constructive membership on the disjoint union of the three residue vector sets gives straight-line expressions with polynomial cost in $\sum_i q^{n_i}$. After each step, trim the residue generators and retain actual matrices, so algorithm-output length is not repeatedly fed back as input length.
- The free and cyclic-module lifting proofs do not call the Cayley-table theorem 9.1. The prior full-table module alignment via the marked group encoding can instead use the degree-two conjugation instance on the residue endomorphism algebra and the explicit nilpotent idempotent correction. See the final part of [the local-ring proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/NovemberGroupIsoTransfers/LocalLifting.md).

## Checks on the proof routes

The proof of 8.1 uses Theorems 8.8 and 8.9 for the full group and the coset intersection theorem 7.3 for the supplied subgroup. The subgroup intersection is necessary for the special-linear instantiation. The 8.9 tree calculation is arithmetically consistent once Lemma 8.7 is given. The bound on the number of retained children between resets is multiplied across logarithmically many resets, giving $p^{O(d)}$. The exponent lost along any path telescopes across resets, so restoring the discarded candidate children costs another $p^{O(d)}$. This check does not establish the structural and local-cost estimates of Lemma 8.7.

Appendix E.3 proves 8.10 by supplying a local solver to Proposition 7.11. The affine exactness steps check directly. First quotient by translations of the kernel of the joint linear action. For a group element $g$ and kernel element $h$, the cocycle identity gives $c(ghg^{-1})=L_g c(h)$, so the translation image is invariant and the quotient action descends. If the action has been solved on a quotient and its answer is $aS$, the remaining problem is an affine action of $S$ on the fiber through the source, translated back by $a^{-1}$ on the target. This returns a single recursive fiber after all nonempty cosets on the quotient have been united. This step preserves the full stabilizer and needs no equivariant section.

Two local constructions in E.3 can be checked independently.

1. **Lemma E.33, pairing insertions.** Add $B_i$ to argument $X_i$ and add only the terms with one new-coordinate pairing and two original coordinates. A shear $r_i:X_i\to B_i$ acts on forms by subtracting exactly the corresponding inserted pairing. No terms containing two shears occur. The preimage of the allowed denominator under the insertion map enforces the exact quotient relation. Semidirect product lifts, followed by the full inverse image in the original group, preserve the complete coset. Its use of 8.1 charges the dimension of the pairing spaces, with the dimensions of the allowed shear coefficients appearing in the generator list.
2. **Lemma E.36, presentations of semisimple submodules.** If the types of a semisimple module $M$ all occur as composition factors of $U$, the annihilator of $U$ in the group algebra annihilates $M$ as well. Thus $M$ is a module over the represented envelope $A\subseteq\operatorname{End}(U)$. The regular module $A$ injects into fixed copies of $U$ by evaluation on a basis, and fixed copies of $A$ surject onto $M$. Explicit subspaces and quotient maps follow by elimination. The construction does preserve the width and transfers the quotient-valued cocycle exactly.

The proof then uses Lemmas E.34 and E.35 to detect the cubic constituents through pairing quotients, and Theorems 6.2 and 6.3 for their representation and cohomology assertions. In C.2 the finite general-linear and special-linear stability range agrees with [Putman, Theorem 12.1](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~andyp/papers/TwistedStability.pdf). The diagonal lift of a general-linear element to the next special-linear rank gives the claimed trivial action on cohomology by inner conjugation and injective stabilization. The functor comparison used in C.3 is indeed [Franjou–Friedlander–Scorichenko–Suslin, Theorem A.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9909194). The symplectic comparison range invoked in C.28 matches [Djament–Vespa, Corollaries 3.32–3.33](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0808.4035). Matching these interfaces does not verify all intervening functor-Ext calculations or the low-characteristic unitary and orthogonal arguments.
