# Exact optimal catalyst costs for thin Peirce multiplication tensors

A type is a finite directed corner graph \(\Gamma\) on a chain of at least two vertices and an integer degree for each supported corner. The diagonal corners are supported and have degree zero. All strict corners go forward, have positive degree, and give the full chain by reachability. A profile \(S\) specifies which triples \((x,y,z)\) with \(x<y<z\) and all three corners supported have a nonzero product. Its algebra \(A(S)\) has one basis vector \(p_{xy}\) for each supported corner, with vertex idempotents \(p_{xx}\) and strict products \(p_{xy}p_{yz}=p_{xz}\) for the triples in \(S\). All other strict products vanish. We admit a profile if this table is associative, respects the indicated degrees, and its \(h\)-th radical power is spanned by the corners of degree at least \(h\). These conditions are independent of the coefficient field. The thin tables used to realize general contact lattices have this form. [Realizing contact lattices](ContactSeed.md)

For admissible profiles \(S,T\) of one type with \(T\subseteq S\), consider integer weights \(w(x,y)\) on the supported corners, with \(w(x,x)=0\). For products in \(T\) require defect \(w(x,z)-w(x,y)-w(y,z)=0\), and for products in \(S\setminus T\) require defect at least one. Form a three-layer directed graph on \(A_x,B_x,C_x\), with the reward edges for each supported corner \(x\to y\)
\[
 B_x\to A_y :\ w(x,y),\qquad A_x\to C_y :\ w(x,y),\qquad C_y\to B_x :\ -w(x,y).
\]
Let \(\rho(w)\) be the maximum over nonempty directed closed walks of total reward divided by one third of the length. The diagonal corners give \(\rho(w)\ge0\). Define \(c(S,T)\) as the minimum \(\rho(w)\) over the required weights, with value \(+\infty\) if none exist or if \(T\not\subseteq S\). Define \(d(S,T)\) as the shortest path cost in the finite directed graph of admissible profiles, with edges of weight \(c(S,T)\) for finite comparisons. A path of length zero is allowed. Both costs are effectively computable and field-independent.

Consider a heterogeneous product \(\mathcal A=\bigotimes_{i=1}^r A(S_i)\) and \(\mathcal D=\bigotimes_{i=1}^r A(T_i)\), with \(r\ge1\) and the same multiset of types on both sides. Enumerate the profiles so that the \(i\)-th source and target types agree. Write
\[
 C=\min_{\pi}\sum_{i=1}^r c(S_i,T_{\pi(i)}),\qquad
 D=\min_{\pi}\sum_{i=1}^r d(S_i,T_{\pi(i)}),
\]
where \(\pi\) ranges over type-preserving permutations. A pole cap triple bounds the forward entry poles of the three arbitrary regrouped Laurent mode maps of the multiplication tensors.

**Theorem.** Over every field \(k\), the unamplified comparison \(\mathcal A\rightsquigarrow\mathcal D\) has exactly the cap triples with sum at least \(\lceil C\rceil\). With arbitrary nonzero finite-dimensional associative unital \(k\)-algebra catalysts, the union of attainable cap triples is exactly those with sum at least \(\lceil D\rceil\). One catalyst attaining all these triples is the tensor product of the intermediate-profile algebras on optimal paths for an optimal matching. Use the scalar algebra for an empty product. The same thresholds apply to the minimum polynomial presentation order. An infinite threshold means that there is no witness.

Consequently every catalyst cancels exactly if and only if \(\lceil C\rceil=\lceil D\rceil\). For the \(r\)-fold power of a single-profile comparison the thresholds are \(\lceil r\,c(S,T)\rceil\) and \(\lceil r\,d(S,T)\rceil\). Exact cancellation for every power holds if and only if \(c(S,T)=d(S,T)\).

## 1. Computing a cap sum from a cochain

Scalar rescaling of the three modes reduces any cap triple to \((0,0,p)\) with \(p=p_1+p_2+p_3\), and conversely gives arbitrary cap distributions with that sum. The integral thin weight criterion then uses vertex values \(\alpha_x,\beta_x\) and the inequalities
\[
 w(x,y)\le\alpha_x+\beta_x-\beta_y,\qquad
 w(x,y)\le\beta_x,\qquad
 w(x,y)\ge\alpha_x+\beta_x-p.
\]
On the diagonal these give \(\alpha_x,\beta_x\ge0\), \(\alpha_x+\beta_x\le p\). For integer coboundary adjustments \(w=w_0+u_y-u_x\), take \(C_x=u_x,\ A_x=u_x+\beta_x,\ B_x=u_x+\alpha_x+\beta_x\). The inequalities become
\[
 A_y-B_x\le-w_0(x,y),\quad C_y-A_x\le-w_0(x,y),\quad B_x-C_y\le p+w_0(x,y).
\]
Their cycle cost is \(p\) times a third of the length minus the reward. Summing around each cycle is necessary. If all cycles have nonnegative cost, shortest paths from an added root with zero edges to all vertices give potentials, with integers for integer \(p\). Thus the cap bound for the fixed weight class is \(\lceil\rho(w_0)\rceil\). The coboundary also preserves the cycle rewards by telescoping. With the resulting integral coordinates the diagonal algebra transporter and its two diagonal unit multipliers realize the \(0/1\) target over \(k((t))\). [Thin cap extraction and the layered cycle mean](PowerOrderSeed.md)

A maximum mean is attained on a simple cycle. To test finiteness of \(c(S,T)\), solve the rational linear defect constraints. Any rational solution can be multiplied by a positive common denominator, preserving the zero defects and the positive lower bounds. This supplies a first integer cochain and an upper integer bound \(P\) on cap sum. Every cochain with \(\rho\le P\) admits an integer gauge at caps \((0,0,P)\), which puts all its weights between \(-P\) and \(P\) by the three inequalities and their diagonal instances. Thus finite enumeration computes \(c\) and attains it when finite. Associativity, grading, and radical powers are finite product-table checks. [Maximum cycle means](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(78)90011-0)

For a Cartesian product of thin corner graphs with retained different-coordinate products, a weight cochain splits up to a vertex coboundary into its reference fiber cochains \(w_i\). Every simultaneous edge is the sum along its subdivision into singleton steps, and every mixed square has zero circulation. Subdivide signed closed walks and commute distinct-coordinate steps using these square identities. After grouping steps by coordinate, the walk is a sum of loops in the reference fibers. Hence the difference of the original cochain and the sum of the fiber cochains has zero circulation and is an integer coboundary. The reward graph of their sum is the synchronous product of the factor reward graphs. Projecting closed walks gives an upper bound by the sum of the means. Conversely start a maximizing cycle in each base at a \(B\)-layer vertex and repeat them to a common length. Their synchronous product attains the sum. Therefore
\[
 \rho(w)=\sum_i\rho(w_i). \tag{1}
\]
This includes heterogeneous types and gauges depending on the entire grid. Conversely sum any cochains feasible for a list of coordinate comparisons. A product defect is the sum of its factor defects and has the required sign or zero. The difference constraints give the product witness at total cap \(\lceil\sum_i\rho(w_i)\rceil\).

## 2. Extracting local profiles from an arbitrary catalyst witness

Take any comparison with catalyst \(B\). Extend its coefficients to \(F=\overline{k}\), keeping \(t\) as uniformizer, and use the unital normalization over \(R=F[[t]]\). The normalized law \(\Lambda\) has the desired special fiber, a generic algebra transporter \(H\), and three maps of the original caps. The maps are \(L_{ab}R_{b^{-1}}H^{-1}\), \(L_bH^{-1}\), and \(HL_{(ab)^{-1}}\) for source units \(a,b\). The two factors \(\mathcal A,\mathcal D\) have the same based radical filtration because the factor types agree at their original labels. In particular the generic and special radical dimensions agree, by the tensor-product radical formula. The lattice \(\Lambda\cap\operatorname{Jac}(\Lambda\otimes_R F((t)))\) is saturated, and its special image is a nilpotent ideal of that full dimension, so it specializes exactly. The two endpoint dimensions of each radical power agree as well, because the \(h\)-th power of the tensor-product radical is the sum of \(J_{\rm out}^i\otimes J_{\rm cat}^j\) for \(i+j=h\). Unit-minor elimination gives exact specialization for these powers. Lifting complete orthogonal idempotents and using their corner projections gives exact ranks of all corner spaces and all graded corners. The normalized generic isomorphism therefore supplies a common permutation of endpoint simple blocks preserving all these ranks. [Unital degeneration normalization](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.19) [Radical specialization and matrix frames](UniversalCatalystCancellation.md)

We may replace the catalyst by one with acyclic support and full matrix diagonal corners, without increasing any cap. Indeed, after lifting the simple quotient block idempotents in \(B_F\), sum them by strongly connected components of the corner-support digraph. Let \(I\) be the ideal generated by the radical corners of these sums. In the full product, support components are a fixed outer vertex times a catalyst component, since the outer support is acyclic with nonzero diagonal. Thus taking the ideal generated by these component-diagonal integral radical corners gives \(\mathcal A_F\otimes I\) in the generic fiber and \(\mathcal D_F\otimes I\) in the special fiber. On the generic side this follows by simultaneous conjugacy of idempotent systems with the same quotient images, and invariance of two-sided ideals under conjugation. The dimensions agree. A full-rank special minor of the product map generating the ideal makes its image a free direct summand. The multipliers and \(H\) preserve and match the respective ideals, so all three scaled lattice inclusions giving the caps descend. The quotient catalyst \(B_F/I\) has all its old component-internal strict corners killed and has full matrix diagonal corners. Its remaining strict arrows between old components form an acyclic digraph. Denote this quotient catalyst by \(E\).

Apply the same normalization facts to the resulting witness and write \(P\) for its simple-block permutation, using common source and target vertex labels. The reachability poset has the \(r\) outer chain factors and the reachability poset of \(E\). In each connected Hasse component, factor its underlying undirected graph into Cartesian primes. This displays each outer chain as a prime path factor. The permutation \(P\) matches prime edge classes, including between different components, and is coordinatewise up to the permutation of factors. Indeed two vertices differing in one factor stay within a layer of its matched factor, so the coordinate in a given target factor depends only on its matched source coordinate. [Canonical Cartesian prime factorization](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/jf/FHS-DAM85.pdf)

Call a position active if its orbit under \(P\) among factor positions of connected components meets an outer factor. Each such position is a path factor. Label its ordered chain by transporting the labeling of an outer factor along \(P\). Between outer positions, \(P\) preserves increasing order, so these labels are consistent. The nonzero single-coordinate corner pattern of an active position is its inherited outer \(\Gamma\), uniformly in the other positions, because applying an iterate of \(P\) sends it to that outer factor. The corresponding corner degrees are inherited in the same way by exact graded ranks. If two outer positions are in the same orbit, they have the same type. Holding the inactive coordinates fixed gives an active grid. Let \(q\) denote the tuple of these fixed coordinates together with the component label, and \(p(q)\) its image under the induced finite permutation of these tuples.

We give the matrix and product details that permit the cost estimate on each active grid. All simple block sizes on a fixed active grid are equal to a size \(b\). To see this, send the direction of any single Hasse step to an outer factor under an iterate of \(P\). There the catalyst vertex stays fixed, and the two block sizes agree. Such steps connect the active grid. Every corner on the grid whose coordinate pairs are supported has dimension \(b^2\), and other corners are zero. This follows by induction on the number of changed coordinates. The single-coordinate case aligns to an outer factor with a full matrix diagonal in the remaining algebra. For a multi-coordinate corner, align one changed coordinate to an outer factor. At that iterate the corner factors as its outer thin corner and the corner with only the other changes. Use the inductive rank for the latter corner and transport the rank back. Its full matrix endpoint actions make each \(b^2\)-dimensional corner a simple regular bimodule, so it occupies one radical grade. This uses the equality of endpoint diagonal blocks with their semisimple blocks, after the strongly connected component quotient.

For such a grid let \(\chi_\pm(x,y,z)\) be the indicator of a nonzero corner product in the respective associated graded endpoint. Generic vanishing forces special vanishing in the matched free integral graded corner, giving
\[
 \chi_-(Px,Py,Pz)\le \chi_+(x,y,z). \tag{2}
\]
If one step moves a single active coordinate and the other step changes only disjoint coordinates, then \(\chi_+=\chi_-\) at all iterates of the triple under \(P\). This holds even with several unrelated exterior profile changes, because an outer coordinate uses at most one strict step and never takes a strict triangle. Equation (2) makes this indicator constant around its orbit. Align the singleton direction to an outer factor. Its product is then nonzero by the tensor factorization, since each factor multiplies by an identity in one step. Thus both endpoints have all these mixed products. In particular mixed squares allow the two orders of two distinct singleton moves.

On the grid choose regular matrix-bimodule frames. Products of supported graded corners are scalar multiples of matrix multiplication, by equivariance over the outer blocks and balancing over the middle block. Taking the \(11\)-unit in each frame reduces associative identities for product statuses to scalar identities. Every supported simultaneous edge can be expanded into singleton edges up to a nonzero scalar, using the mixed products. Any two consecutive different-coordinate moves can be commuted up to a nonzero scalar, by their mixed square. A single-coordinate triangle has constant nonzero status across other active spectators. To see this, move one other active spectator by a Hasse step and compare the product of the two triangle steps followed by that step to the product with the spectator step first. The two mixed squares and the nonzero mixed multiplication with the triangle chord identify the two triangle coefficients up to nonzero factors. Connectivity across the spectator coordinates proves the assertion. These arguments hold in both endpoint laws.

We may consequently define a local source and a local target profile at each state \((q,i)\), where \(i\) is an active position of that grid. Read its strict product statuses along the coordinate fiber. Positions belonging to the original exterior list have the prescribed profiles \(S_i,T_i\). At any other position the same-label source and special profiles agree, since all exterior factors are fixed at diagonal idempotents. Each local table is associative after replacing its nonzero coefficients by \(1\). Indeed the local scalar corner law is associative, and equality of two parenthesizations implies that their coefficients are both zero or both nonzero. Replacing nonzero coefficients by \(1\) preserves that identity, including when one of the intermediate corners is unsupported.

The regular matrix frames give integer weights on the supported corners of the whole active grid from the arbitrary witness. Recall why this retains all caps. On the generic graded corner, \(H\) is \(X\mapsto\eta_{xy}S_xXS_y^{-1}\) in integral frames, after relabeling by its block permutation. The quotient block maps are conjugation by \(S_x\), and the remaining bimodule map is scalar. Let \(v\) denote least entry valuation. The weights and vertices can be taken to be \(w_{xy}=v(\eta_{xy})+v(S_x)-v(S_y)\), \(\beta_x=v(b_xS_x^{-1})+v(S_x)\), and \(\alpha_x=v(a_xb_xS_x^{-1})-v(b_xS_x^{-1})\). For a source nonzero product the coefficient valuation is the defect of these weights. It is zero for a matched nonzero target and positive for a matched zero. The original cap inequalities follow by inserting the three normalized maps into these frames, using \(v(X)+v(X^{-1})\le0\) and that the least entry valuation of \(Y\mapsto MYN\) is \(v(M)+v(N)\). This is precisely the regular matrix calculation of the earlier proof, used only on the active grid, so arbitrary off-grid multiplicities play no part. [Integer extraction with the three separate mode bounds](UniversalCatalystCancellation.md)

The weights on the whole grid satisfy the retained mixed relations already established. For each \(q\), write \(w_{q,i}\) for their reference fiber cochains on its active positions. By (1), if the total cap of the given witness is \(P_0\), then
\[
 \sum_i \rho(w_{q,i})\le P_0. \tag{3}
\]
The defect constraints for a fiber compare its source profile at \((q,i)\) to the target profile at \((p(q),P(i))\).

## 3. Paths, matching, and optimal catalysts

Choose an orbit of the spectator permutation \(p\), of length \(h\). On the finite set of its states \((q,i)\), the successor map is \((q,i)\mapsto(p(q),P(i))\). Each state cycle hits at least one exterior position because \(i\) is active. Cut each cycle into segments running from one exterior position to the next. Start with the source profile at the first exterior position and end with the target profile at the next. At an internal position the target profile arriving from the preceding step equals the source profile for the next step. By (2) the segment gives a monotone chain of product supports. All profiles in it have the same type.

Each intermediate profile in a segment is admissible. We have proved associativity of its \(0/1\) table, and every listed product respects the degree since it is recovered in the associated graded. The table lies by inclusion of nonzero products between the two admissible exterior endpoints. For each \(a\), the associative \(a\)-fold product of strict basis corners is nonzero exactly when its particular composable product path gives a nonzero product in the table. Inclusion of tables therefore gives inclusion of the corresponding based radical powers. The endpoint radical powers are equal by the common type, so the intermediate radical powers are the same. Thus the segment is a path in the graph used to define \(d\). The cochain at every step is feasible for the corresponding edge, by (2) and the integer defects. Its \(\rho\) is at least that edge's \(c\), so the segment's sum is at least the shortest cost from its starting to its ending exterior profile.

The \(h\) grids contain \(h\) copies of each labeled exterior position. Each is used exactly once as a source segment endpoint and once as a target segment endpoint. The segments therefore define a type-preserving correspondence of these copies, with total shortest-path cost at least \(hD\). One way to see the bound is to forget the spectator labels, obtaining a type-preserving \(h\)-regular bipartite multigraph on the two exterior lists. Hall's condition follows by counting the \(h\) incident edges per vertex, and removing a perfect matching leaves an \((h-1)\)-regular multigraph. Repeating decomposes the correspondence into \(h\) type-preserving matchings, each with cost at least \(D\). Summing (3) on the \(h\) grids gives \(hP_0\ge hD\). This proves the lower bound \(\lceil D\rceil\) for every algebra catalyst, with \(+\infty\) excluding all witnesses. When the catalyst is the scalar algebra, there is a single grid and every active position is exterior. The recovered permutation directly matches the exterior positions, and (3) then gives \(P_0\ge C\).

For the upper bound when \(C<+\infty\), choose a minimizing type-preserving permutation and a minimizing integer cochain for every matching comparison. Reorder target factors by that permutation and apply the product construction of Section 1 at total cap \(\lceil C\rceil\). For the catalytic upper bound, suppose \(D<+\infty\). Choose a minimizing matching and a shortest profile path for each matched pair. Form one common catalyst as the tensor product of its intermediate-profile algebras, counting occurrences. A path \(S=Q_0\to Q_1\to\cdots\to Q_a=T\) supplies the axes \(Q_0,\ldots,Q_{a-1}\) on the source side and \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_a\) on the target side. Match each consecutive pair \(Q_j\to Q_{j+1}\). These are just permutations of the original exterior factors and the common intermediate factors on each side. A length-zero path uses an identity comparison on the corresponding exterior factor. Choose minimizing cochains for each edge and use (1) and the difference constraints on the regrouped product. The cap threshold is \(\lceil D\rceil\), with rounding only after summing all edge costs. Factor permutations are constant basis permutations, so the construction remains over \(k\) and keeps the prescribed caps.

The multiplication tensors are concise by unitality. Clearing the poles of any Laurent witness and truncating each resulting power-series matrix at the summed cap keeps the leading tensor and gives a polynomial presentation of that order. Conversely, the three polynomial matrices in a presentation with concise leading tensor are generically invertible. Dividing one by the leading power of \(t\) gives a Laurent witness with a cap sum at most that order. This proves the assertion about polynomial presentation order.

## 4. A many-face cancellation criterion

For two nested admissible profiles \(S,T\) put \(\Delta=S\setminus T\). Consider the lattice of defect vectors on \(\Delta\) arising from integer cochains with all defects on \(T\) zero. Suppose every nonzero vector of this lattice in the nonnegative orthant is strictly positive on every coordinate of \(\Delta\). In a finite-cost profile path from \(S\) to \(T\), discard stationary steps and look at the first change. Its cochain gives a nonnegative lattice vector, positive exactly on the faces removed at that step. By hypothesis it must delete all of \(\Delta\). Hence every finite path already pays the direct cost, and \(c(S,T)=d(S,T)\). This proves exact cancellation by every algebra catalyst for every power. One instance is a rank-one contact lattice generated by a strictly positive vector, with any number of marked faces and with any integral divisibility of that generator. The formula in the theorem also applies when admissible paths split the deletions or the lists contain distinct profiles and types.

This is a written proof. The finite graph and matching characterize the minimal cap sum over arbitrary algebra catalysts for the stated thin chain types. The proof permits \(0/1\) tables with many changing products and overlapping face constraints.

## 5. An explicit exponential advantage from one intermediate profile

Fix an integer \(a\ge2\). For each \(m\ge2\) there are profiles \(S_0\supset S_1\supset S_2\) of a common thin type such that, with \(N=2^{m-1}\),
\[
 c(S_0,S_2)\ge \frac{aN}{3},
 \qquad
 c(S_0,S_1)+c(S_1,S_2)\le \frac{N}{3}+O_a(1).
 \tag{4}
\]
Thus the catalyst \(A(S_1)\) saves an exponentially growing amount of pole sum and polynomial presentation order for fixed \(a\). The ratio between unamplified and optimal catalytic thresholds has limit inferior at least \(a\) as \(m\) grows. In particular a catalyst can genuinely improve these fixed-source comparisons.

Construct a fixed finite triangulated orientable genus-zero surface with \(a\) input boundary circles and one output boundary circle, each a three-edge circle. Such a triangulation is obtained from a triangulated sphere by removing \(a+1\) disjoint open triangles with pairwise disjoint vertex sets. For example, after choosing \(a+1\) distinct faces, triangulate each with a smaller internal triangular face surrounded by a triangulated annulus, then remove those internal faces. Cone-cap each input circle, leaving the output circle free. Attach \(m-1\) consecutive Möbius-and-annulus doubling blocks to the output circle, using the earlier sparse telescope, and leave the last circle uncapped. At the \(i\)-th cone cap, after barycentric subdivision mark one small triangular flag \(\sigma_i\) of the form (cone vertex, spoke, original triangle). Its closure is interior to that cap. Choose the mark so that the rank orientation of each \(\sigma_i\) contributes the same sign to the surface boundary relation. There are opposite choices: the two spoke flags in the same original cone triangle share the cone-vertex to face-barycenter edge, so their increasing-rank orientations induce the same orientation of that edge but opposite signs in the oriented surface. [The doubling blocks and barycentric flag encoding](SparseSeed.md)

Make the corner graph from all directed face inclusions of the subdivided complex, ordering vertices by rank and then by labels. Add missing successor corners with all strict products involving them zero. This gives reachability a full chain. Let \(S_0\) contain all original flag products, \(S_1\) delete only \(\sigma_1\), and \(S_2\) delete every \(\sigma_i\). Every output corner of a deleted flag has a second flag in its own original triangle with the same output corner, and that flag is retained. Thus the based square of the radical agrees for all three tables. All triple products of strict corners are zero, since outputs of flag products go from rank zero to rank two and added corners have zero strict products. This proves associativity and the common radical grading, so all three profiles are admissible.

For an integer cochain retain the defect convention \(w(x,z)-w(x,y)-w(y,z)\), and write its defects at the marked flags as \(c_i\). When all defects on the unmarked flags vanish, it is a cocycle on the subdivided complex with the marked open flags removed. The bottom surface is then genus zero with \(a+1\) boundary circles. Its first homology is free on the \(a\) puncture boundaries, and the output boundary is their oriented sum, with a single overall choice of sign. The sign choices for the marks and the orientation of the output give output circulation \(-\sum_i c_i\). The terminal subdivided circle of the telescope has circulation \(-N\sum_i c_i\) by the doubling identity. In the layered reward graph, traversing that alternating six-edge circle in the direction of positive reward gives a walk with that absolute reward and three triples of steps: insert an identity step between each forward strict edge and its backward neighbor. Direct deletion requires \(c_i\ge1\) for every mark. This proves \(c(S_0,S_2)\ge aN/3\).

For the first step take prescribed defects \((1,0,\ldots,0)\). An integral cocycle exists on the fixed punctured bottom surface because the puncture boundaries freely generate its first homology. It can be represented by a simplicial integer cochain, for example by evaluating the corresponding homology map on edges closed through a spanning tree. Its restriction to the subdivided output circle, of circulation \(-1\), can be adjusted by an integer vertex gauge to the same fixed integer pattern as the input boundary of the telescope certificate. Two integral cochains on a circle with the same circulation differ by an integral vertex coboundary, and the boundary gauge extends arbitrarily to the remaining bottom vertices.

On the telescope blocks themselves use the earlier compatible lifted-angle certificate of unit input circulation. It provides an integer cochain and a rational gauge for which all actual incidence weights lie in \([-N/3,0]\). Its raw integer input boundary pattern and rational gauge values there are fixed independently of the number of blocks: the initial three signed boundary increments are \(-1/3\), and the gauge residues are in \(\{0,1/3,2/3\}\). The subsequent block with input circulation of magnitude \(d\) has triangle lift diameters at most \(2d/3\), giving the asserted final bound. Extend this rational gauge to the fixed bottom surface, where its weights remain bounded independently of \(m\). At each added successor corner choose an integer weight whose gauged value is in \((-1,0]\), possible since that corner enters no strict product. The gauged weights are in \([-N/3-O_a(1),O_a(1)]\). Every three-layer cycle uses equally many edges of each of its three types. Its normalized reward is bounded by the sum of two upper weight bounds and the negative of a lower weight bound. Consequently the first-step cost is at most \(N/3+O_a(1)\). [Lifted-angle certificate on the doubling blocks](ExactSparseOrders.md)

For the second step prescribe defects \((-(a-1),1,\ldots,1)\). The negative defect at \(\sigma_1\) is allowed because that product is absent from \(S_1\). Again an integral bottom cocycle with those puncture evaluations exists. Its circulation on the output is zero, so an integer boundary gauge makes it identically zero on the output circle. Extend the cochain by zero across all telescope blocks and take zero weights on added successor corners. Every weight is now bounded independently of \(m\), so the second-step cost is \(O_a(1)\). The upper bound in (4) follows. The one intermediate catalyst realizes the two steps in different tensor factors simultaneously, so it pays the ceiling of their sum rather than separate integer roundings.
