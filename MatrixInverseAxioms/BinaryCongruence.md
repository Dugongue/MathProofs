# Exact Polynomial Calculus degree for all bilinear forms in characteristic two

Let $A,B$ be square matrices of the same positive side over a field $F$ of characteristic two. Use ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) on the entries of $XAX^T-B$, $XY-I$, and $YX-I$. The degree is the maximum total degree of an actual line. No field equations are present. The following formula includes nonsymmetric, singular and mixed inputs in their original coordinates. It addresses the degree clause of Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She's matrix-congruence question. [Published equations](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

**First test the displayed coordinates.** Degree zero occurs if $A=0$ and $B\ne0$, or if $A$ is alternating and some $B_{ii}\ne0$. Here alternating means $v^TAv$ vanishes identically. A nonzero off-diagonal entry of $XAX^T$ as an indexed polynomial cannot vanish identically for $A\ne0$, since its two rows of variables are independent. Thus this list detects all literal nonzero constant axioms. There are no nonconstant linear axioms.

Define $L(0)=0$. For nonzero $A$, take $L(A)$ to be the alternating matrices if $A$ is alternating, the symmetric matrices if $A$ is symmetric and nonalternating, and all matrices otherwise. After the degree-zero test, $B\notin L(A)$ gives exactly degree two. All other refutable pairs have degree at least three.

**Canonical counts.** Over an algebraic closure, split off the singular Gabriel blocks $J_s(0)$, the nilpotent Jordan blocks of sides $s\ge1$. Use the following list for the nonsingular summands. Each entry in the list is a *type*. Its count is the number of copies of the indicated whole matrix block. A reciprocal pair is identified under interchanging $\lambda$ and $\lambda^{-1}$.

* Each singular size $s$ is one type.
* For $\lambda\notin\{0,1\}$ and length $m$, the block with off-diagonal entries $J_m(\lambda),I_m$ and zero diagonal blocks is one type indexed by $\{\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\},m$.
* At eigenvalue one and length $m$, record a single type with a flag on each side where it occurs. The alternating flag uses the whole doubled block $H_m=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&J_m(1)\\I_m&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. For odd $m$ the nonalternating flag uses the single block $\Gamma'_m$ of Szechtman. Even $m$ has only the alternating flag. Choose the normal form in which the two flags do not coexist at one length in one matrix. In particular a doubled block contributes one unit, even though its asymmetry has two Jordan chains.

These are Szechtman's normalized forms, including Theorems 3.1 and 8.1–8.4. At odd $m$ the flag is the alternating status of the symmetric multiplicity form induced by $f((\sigma-1)^{m-1}u,v)$ on the length-$m$ primary part modulo $\sigma-1$, for $f$ the original form and $\sigma$ its asymmetry. Thus the flag can also be read without choosing the representative $\Gamma'_m$. [Classification and representatives](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.0565)

**The formula.** If all counts and all flags on positive counts match, the degree is infinite. Otherwise discard the matching types. Give a remaining type weight zero if just one side has positive count, or if both have positive counts and opposite flags. In all other cases the counts $u<v$, sorted by size, are positive and distinct. Give that type weight
$$
 d(u,v)=\min\{u+2,\ 2\cdot 2^{v_2(v-u)}\}.
$$
When neither of the first two tests applies, the exact degree is the maximum of three and the minimum of the remaining weights.

For symmetric forms this specializes to a rank formula. For two nonalternating forms use the two ranks, and independently the two nullities. For two alternating forms use half the ranks, and independently the full nullities. Equal types and ranks give a geometric isometry. An alternating source and a symmetric nonalternating target give degree zero. The reverse ordering gives degree three. For example, the two nonsymmetric invertible matrices $\Gamma'_3\oplus\Gamma'_3$ and $H_3$ have identical ordinary asymmetries up to similarity and opposite residual flags. Their ordinary PC refutation degree is exactly three in either direction.

## Degrees below three

Work over an algebraic closure $K$. Passing from $F$ to $K$ preserves and reflects existence of a PC proof through any given degree. Indeed the closure is obtained by iterated spans, intersections with a subspace of smaller degree, and multiplication of that intersection by variables. These operations commute with field extension. For bounds at least two, changing constant bases in $A,B$ also preserves derivability within the bound. The induced substitutions on $X,Y$ are homogeneous linear and the transformed axioms are scalar combinations of the old axioms of degree at most two. The separate original-coordinate degree-zero test matters because cancellation in such a combination can expose a constant.

Until a constant is derived, the closure at degree two is the span of the axioms. Their only lower-degree terms are constants. The two inverse families have quadratic part of bidegree $(1,1)$ in $(X,Y)$, and the Gram family has bidegree $(2,0)$. A combination from the inverse families whose quadratic part vanishes has zero constant by evaluating at $X=Y=I$. Hence degree at most two is equivalent to a linear functional vanishing identically on $XAX^T$ and nonzero on $B$.

The values of $XAX^T$ span $L(A)$. For alternating $A\ne0$, choose an input pair with a nonzero pairing and place it in two arbitrary output rows; these values span the alternating matrices. For nonalternating $A$, rank-one matrices $X$ give all multiples of $ww^T$, up to a fixed nonzero scalar. These span the symmetric matrices in every characteristic via $w=e_i,e_j,e_i+e_j$. If $A$ is nonsymmetric use two rows detecting its asymmetry. Modulo the already spanned symmetric matrices this yields a single nonzero off-diagonal class for each unordered index pair, which spans the rest. This proves the test. If $A\notin L(B)$ the reverse Gram equations give an upper bound of three. Indeed $G=XAX^T-B$ gives
$$
 XA-BY^T=GY^T-XA(YX-I)^T .
$$
This linear identity is derived through degree three. Multiplying it on the left by $Y$ and using $YX-I$ then gives $A-YBY^T$ in the same bound. These low-degree arguments work over every field, with alternating matrices understood as skew-symmetric with zero diagonal.

## The reduction to multiplicity spaces

Represent $A$ by a two-arrow Kronecker representation $R_A$ with both vector spaces $K^n$ and arrows $A,A^T$. Duality swaps the two vertices, takes transposes and interchanges the arrow labels. It fixes $R_A$ with its evident self-duality. A map from $R_A$ to $R_B$ is a pair $(U,V)$ with $VA=BU$ and $VA^T=B^TU$. The candidate $u=(Y^T,X)$ satisfies these two identities through degree three by the preceding identity and its version for $A^T$. The adjoint is $u^*=(X^T,Y)$.

Project $u$ linearly to the actual constant space $\operatorname{Hom}(R_A,R_B)$ and take the adjoint of that projection for the reverse map. The discrepancies are linear combinations of the obtained linear equations. Using a variable times a discrepancy in each inverse product yields both compositions equal to identity, with proof degree at most three. More explicitly, changing one factor at a time uses linear times linear, and the original compositions have entries from the two inverse families or their transposes.

Take the endomorphism algebra of $R_A\oplus R_B$ and pass to its quotient by the Jacobson radical. In a Krull–Schmidt decomposition this quotient is the product of the matrix algebras of the multiplicities of the indecomposable Kronecker representations. Indeed the endomorphism algebra of an indecomposable has local endomorphism ring, with residue field $K$ since $K$ is algebraically closed. Off-type blocks and entries in the local radicals form the categorical radical and give exactly the Jacobson radical on this finite direct sum. Transpose duality preserves the radical as an anti-automorphism. Quotient entries are fixed linear functionals of actual projected morphisms. Applying them to both composition identities gives the corresponding rectangular inverse identities, without any increase in proof degree. One can extend each functional arbitrarily from the endomorphism space to the full vector space of matrix pairs to perform this step entrywise.

For a Gabriel block, sorting the basis positions by parity splits its two-arrow representation into two nonisomorphic dual summands. For size $2t+1$ their dimension vectors are $(t,t+1)$ and $(t+1,t)$ and their arrows are the two consecutive coordinate inclusions and their projections; this includes the simples at $t=0$. For size $2t$ the summands are the regular pencils $(I,J_t(0))$ and $(J_t(0)^T,I)$. Within each pair different $t$ give distinct indecomposables, as is also seen from Kronecker normal form. For the reciprocal nonexceptional blocks the two summands are the regular chains with distinct eigenvalues $\lambda,\lambda^{-1}$ and common length $m$. In each of these cases a single simple factor gives ordinary rectangular maps on the copy counts, with both inverse identities.

At eigenvalue one and length $m$ there is one self-dual Kronecker type, the length-$m$ regular chain. In the semisimple quotient the adjoint on its multiplicity space has the form $P\mapsto S_A^{-1}P^TS_B$ on rectangular maps, with the evident restrictions to nonzero multiplicities. To see the form of this adjoint, compose it on the full multiplicity matrix algebra with the usual transpose. The resulting algebra automorphism is inner. Involutivity gives a Gram matrix with $S^T=cS$, $c^2=1$, hence $c=1$ in characteristic two. The self-adjoint projectors onto $A,B$ split their multiplicity forms orthogonally, as do the projectors onto orthogonal block copies.

One $\Gamma'_m$ contributes a single regular chain by the elementary divisor of its asymmetry, so its residual form is one nonzero scalar. One $H_m$ contributes two copies of that chain. Its two crosswise vertex summands are exchanged by duality, because both diagonal blocks in $H_m$ are zero. In particular the two coordinate idempotents in the quotient are adjoints of one another. This forces the residual Gram matrix to have zero diagonal and invertible off-diagonal entries. Thus the former copies give a nonalternating diagonal form, normalized to identity over $K$, and the latter copies give an alternating form, normalized to hyperbolic planes. This calculation establishes the flag directly on the quotient used for the PC derivation. It does not require an assertion that every quotient isometry lifts.

If a type is missing on one side, one composition gives the identity equal to zero. For opposite positive flags, the two inverse equations on the quotient give both Gram identities between the multiplicity forms after multiplying by their constant inverses. In one direction an alternating Gram matrix would have to produce a nonalternating matrix. Its diagonal gives a scalar contradiction. For like nonalternating flags, or a pair of distinct dual types, the usual rectangular inverse proof gives $d(u,v)$. The like alternating case uses the next lemma on the numbers of whole hyperbolic planes.

## Symplectic rectangle over every field

Let $0<u<v$, let $J_a$ denote the direct sum of $a$ standard hyperbolic alternating planes, and let $P$ have shape $2v$ by $2u$. Write $P^*=J_u^{-1}P^T J_v$. For $PP^*=I_{2v}$ and $P^*P=I_{2u}$ the exact degree is $\min(u+2,2p^{v_p(v-u)})$ in characteristic $p>0$ and two in characteristic zero.

For the upper bound use $P J_u P^T=J_v$ and $P^T J_v P=J_u$, which are constant linear rewritings since $J_a^{-1}=-J_a$. For $j\ge0$ sum the determinants of all $2j$ by $2j$ minors of $P$ on unions of $j$ full standard pairs in both row and column indices. Applying the Pfaffian minor-summation identity to the first Gram matrix on each such row set, or to the second on each such column set, gives this same polynomial. The identity follows by expanding the $j$th divided exterior power of the alternating form, which selects $j$ distinct full pairs once, with no division; equivalently it is an identity over the integers. Replacing Gram entries by the constant targets in these Pfaffians by telescoping costs degree $2j$. The constants obtained are $\binom vj$ and $\binom uj$. In positive characteristic the first difference occurs at $j=p^{v_p(v-u)}$ by factoring $(1+t)^v-(1+t)^u$; in characteristic zero take $j=1$.

For the second upper bound take one row from each of the $v$ target hyperbolic pairs, always its first row. The $v$ selected rows in $K^{2u}$ have all pairings zero, and their second partner rows yield a right inverse by their pairings. Here all assertions mean degree-two Gram equations. Put $k=u+1$. We first derive every $k$-minor of this $v$ by $2u$ matrix zero in degree $k$. Choose any $k$ of its rows $w_i$ and form their wedge $W$. For $1\le t\le\lfloor k/2\rfloor$, the divided symplectic contraction $C_t(W)$ removes $t$ distinct full coordinate pairs and sums the resulting wedges. Its coefficients are degree-$k$ consequences of the isotropy equations. Expanding on the row vectors gives signed sums of a $2t$-row Gram Pfaffian times a $(k-2t)$-row wedge. Each Pfaffian is a sum of products of $t$ pairings, so each term is a monomial multiple or a linear combination of multiples of a pairing within the degree bound. This expansion follows by expanding the divided alternating form along the row choices, over the integers.

Fix a coordinate $k$-set $S$, containing $d$ full pairs, some single columns, and omitting $d-1$ pairs. In particular $d\ge1$. Keep its single columns fixed. For any subset $T$ of the omitted pairs, take the coefficient on the single columns together with the full pairs in $T$ in $C_{d-|T|}(W)$. Up to a fixed conventional sign this sums $W_{\mathrm{singles}+2J}$ over all $d$-subsets $J$ of the nonsingleton pair positions containing $T$. The removed pairs always consist of two adjacent entries in the sorted wedge, so their removal has the same sign at every position. Sum these zero coefficients with weights $(-1)^{|T|}$. Exactly the $J$ disjoint from all omitted pairs survive, namely the full pairs of $S$. Thus $W_S=0$. All contractions used have positive order. Using just the first contraction would omit equations needed in small characteristic.

Now descend the minors from $k$ to zero. If all $(l+1)$-minors of the selected-row matrix $M$ vanish, choose $l+1$ rows and $l$ columns. Their cofactor row $c$ has $cM=0$ by those minors and repeated-column determinants. Its support is on the chosen rows. Multiplication by an entry of the right inverse $N$, and comparison with $c(MN-I)=0$, gives $c=0$ in degree $l+2$. Every $l$-minor occurs since $v>u\ge l$. Descending from $l=u$ thus gives a refutation in degree $u+2$.

For the lower bound substitute $P=Z\otimes I_2$. The symplectic adjoint becomes $Z^T\otimes I_2$, leaving the signed rectangular inverse system on a $v$ by $u$ matrix $Z$. Its degree is the stated value. The preceding signed-rectangle proof for odd characteristic applies unchanged in characteristic two. The group algebra of elementary sign exponents has its distinct group elements as a basis even when its characteristic is two; multiplying by a generator toggles the edge exponent modulo two. Its proof uses this basis and restriction and extension of matching designs, requiring neither distinct scalar signs nor diagonalization of the group algebra. ## Lower bound after assembly

For a normalized original congruence block $C$ with unequal counts $u,v>0$ of the same flag, specialize the copy-index part of $X$ to an independent $v$ by $u$ matrix $Z_C$ and the copy-index part of $Y$ to $Z_C^T$, tensored with identity on the entire original block. Set all cross-block entries to zero and matched blocks to identity. Each specialized Gram entry on that type is a scalar multiple of an entry of $Z_C Z_C^T-I_v$, because the same coefficient of $C$ occurs on both sides. Both specialized inverse equations are the signed rectangle or zero, with repetitions. This is why the whole doubled block contributes one column or row to the rectangle. Scalar multiples and zero axioms can be replayed without increasing any degree bound at least two. The proof uses normalized bases only after the degree-zero and degree-two tests.

Different mismatches give independent rectangle variables. The minimum ordinary PC degree on a disjoint union equals the minimum of the individual degrees. One proof chooses in each block's space of polynomials of degree through $D$ a basis simultaneously adapted to its degree filtration and its bounded-degree consequence subspace. Tensor products of total degree at most $D$ with a consequence factor span a space containing all axioms and closed under all legal degree-$D$ proof steps. When each local system fails to refute in degree $D$, choose $1$ as the degree-zero basis element outside that consequence subspace in every block. Then the global span excludes $1$. This proves the desired lower bound. When a missing type or opposite flag already supplies degree three, the low-degree tests supply its sharp lower bound without specialization. The equal-data case follows directly from congruence of the normalized representatives.

The argument gives the literal minimum degree in original coordinates. The inputs and reductions are algebraic and do not require Dirichlet GRH. The general theorem has a written proof.
