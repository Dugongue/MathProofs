# One use of the Inversion Rule for matrix and mixed-graph nonisomorphism

**Theorem.** Every geometrically negative instance of the raw two-sided Polynomial Calculus encoding has a degree-at-most-three, polynomial sparse-size PC+Inv refutation for each of the following classes:

- Matrix conjugacy and arbitrary directed quiver representations, over every field.
- Matrix congruence, over every field, including characteristic two.
- Arbitrary mixed graphs of linear maps and bilinear forms in characteristic different from two, and pure bipartite form graphs in every characteristic.

At most one Inversion Rule inference is needed. All its substitution entries are homogeneous linear forms or zero over the input field. If the dimensions agree at each vertex, the same size and degree claims hold with just one side of the inverses written at each vertex. This uses at most two distinct substitutions for the rule, with multiple consequent lines for the first substitution. In particular, the one-sided matrix encoding with factorial-scale ordinary PC proofs admits polynomial size in PC+Inv. [Original PC+Inv system and rank reduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S1.SS2) · [Mixed-graph degree](MixedGraphDegree.md)

**Preparing the maps.** Use the linear preparation identities for directed arrows and bilinear edges, followed by linear projection onto the forward and reverse intertwiner spaces. For a mixed graph take the doubled quiver and the projected forward map and its adjoint, and for bipartite forms use the undoubled quiver. In the ordinary quiver case project independently on both Hom spaces. The projected product-minus-identity equations have PC derivations of degree three. They come from telescoping both products through the linear errors. Each of these is a scalar linear combination of the prepared arrow errors. All these operations take polynomial sparse size. There are polynomially many needed coefficient lines, each expanded polynomial of degree at most three has polynomially many monomials in the original variables, and every needed contraction is a sum over at most polynomially many original matrix coordinates. [Preparation identities](MixedGraphIdentities.lean)

**Work in the original coefficient field.** If the underlying quiver representations used in that preparation are nonisomorphic over the input field $F$, their images in
$$
 E/\operatorname{rad}E=\prod_i M_{u_i}(D_i),
 \qquad E=\operatorname{End}(R_A\oplus R_B),
$$
have distinct ranks at a factor. Here each $D_i$ is a finite-dimensional division algebra over $F$. To justify this, write $e,f$ for the two complementary projection idempotents. Equal ranks at every factor give opposite-corner inverse maps in the quotient. Lift both to the corresponding corners of $E$. Their compositions within the two corners differ from $e,f$ by radical elements and are therefore units. This makes the lifted maps isomorphisms, contradicting the premise. The regular column action on $D_i^{u_i}$ now gives an $F$-linear matrix representation of the mismatched factor. On the two corner images the dimensions are the distinct division-ring ranks multiplied by $\dim_F D_i$. Taking its entries is a linear operation. Applying it to the two projected product equations yields an ordinary rectangular inverse pair with one product equal to the larger identity. No coefficient extension or scalar descent of an Inv application is used. [Corner argument for directed diagrams](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/420ef7b8444398afe94821a4589df0246c97d23f/DirichletGrochowPrimeTests/FiniteFieldMarkedIPS.md)

In the directed and bipartite cases a negative instance implies the required quiver nonisomorphism. For mixed graphs in characteristic different from two, after algebraic closure the symmetric and alternating multiplicity forms of any given dimensions are unique in their respective normalizations. Thus the underlying doubled quiver determines the geometric isomorphism class, using the selfadjoint decomposition. A geometric nonisomorphism therefore also forces nonisomorphism of the doubled quiver over $F$. In characteristic two for a single matrix, different ordinary quiver multiplicities again force nonisomorphism of that doubled quiver. If its ordinary multiplicities agree and the two forms are geometrically noncongruent, the normalized characteristic-two flags differ. The degree-three flag refutation in ordinary PC from the preceding matrix theorem handles this case, with polynomial size. Its coefficient descent is just the fixed-degree linear closure and involves no Inv step. A fixed-degree PC proof of unspecified length can be replaced by a polynomial-size proof by retaining a basis of consequences through that degree, inserting each new basis direction via one variable multiplication and combinations of the current basis. [Characteristic-two flags](BinaryCongruence.md)

**Apply the rule.** Let $P$ have shape $s$ by $r$, $r<s$, and $Q$ the reverse shape, with the prepared equations $PQ=I_s$. Pad $P$ with zero columns and $Q$ with zero rows in size $s$. Their forward product remains $I_s$. One Inv inference gives a diagonal entry of the reverse product minus identity. That entry is identically $-1$ in the padded part, so it yields the refutation. For this construction all degree-three lines are in preparation, and the dimensions of all substitution matrices are bounded polynomially by the sum of the two input dimension vectors. If only one inverse direction is written at each equal-dimension vertex, form one block-diagonal pair with the order of the two factors chosen independently at the vertices to have the written product first. Use its single rule substitution to obtain the omitted entries, each as its own inference, then use the construction above.

**Uniform construction over finite fields.** The proof can be constructed in deterministic polynomial bit time over any explicitly presented finite field, without factoring polynomials. Use the joint center ranks of $S,eS$ in the degree algorithm. Take any central sector on which the two corner ranks differ. In that sector their difference has a fixed sign as an integer, since the two ranks are constant over its center factors. On its entire left regular representation the corner dimensions therefore differ by a nonzero integer. Use that representation for the same rectangle argument. This takes linear algebra on explicit vector spaces, with no primitive idempotent extraction. If the sole defect is a characteristic-two flag, degree-three ordinary PC saturation constructs the needed proof in polynomial time by linear algebra in the polynomial space of fixed degree. With only one side of the inverse axioms present, do the initial Inv application first. The corner construction also produces a deterministic polynomial-size Hilbert-like IPS refutation, by replacing the final Inv with the standard division-free determinant certificate for the prepared rectangle and substituting its polynomial identities from the preparation. The use of Inv to refute the Rank Principle goes back to Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She. The contribution is the semisimple-corner certificate for every input in the stated classes, including cases where row or column ranks of the original forms and maps agree and the distinction only appears after passing to indecomposable types. This is a written proof, and it leaves the PC+Inv degree of unrestricted three-tensor isomorphism open.

For the one-sided IPS corollary the omitted inverse equations have polynomial-size certificates from the written ones. If $XY=I$ is written, use the polynomial identity
$$
 YX-I=(\det X)\,Y(XY-I)\operatorname{adj}(Y)
      -(YX-I)(\det(XY)-1).
$$
The last determinant difference is itself a sum of multiples of $XY-I$ by row replacement. Swapping $X,Y$ handles a vertex with the opposite product written. All determinants and adjugates have division-free circuits of polynomial size. Composing each of these identities with the Hilbert-like certificate for the two-sided system preserves polynomial size.
