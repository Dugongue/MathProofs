# Sharp sparse Polynomial Calculus bounds for displayed thin quiver representations

Use the literal forward arrow and two-sided inverse equations from the [exact degree theorem](QuiverProofDegree.md), with one variable per matrix entry and ordinary binary scalar linear combination and single-variable multiplication. Count every expanded monomial occurrence on every used line. The following theorem includes ordinary left-right matrix equivalence of the different-rank rectangular matrices \(I_r\oplus0\) and \(I_{r+g}\oplus0\) as a single-arrow example. The bounds allow proofs of arbitrary degree.

## Statement

Fix a finite quiver and a finite list of pairwise nonisomorphic thin indecomposable representations \(T\) over the coefficient field \(F\). Thin means that each vertex space has dimension zero or one. Display each input \(A,B\) as a direct sum of these same based representatives, with identical coefficients on all copies of a type. Assume \(A,B\) have the same dimension vector. In a family indexed by the maximum node dimension \(n\), assume every listed type occurs at least \(cn\) times on **both sides**, for some fixed \(c>0\), and assume that the signed differences \(g_T=b_T-a_T\) of type multiplicities are fixed integers, at least one nonzero. Put \(q=1\) in characteristic zero and
\[
 q=\min_{T:g_T\ne0}p^{v_p(|g_T|)}
\]
in characteristic \(p>0\).

At a quiver vertex \(v\), form the bipartite graph on source types and target types with nonzero \(T_v\). Join source type \(T\) to target type \(U\) when some morphism \(T\to U\) evaluates nonzero at \(v\). There is always an edge between the source and target copies of the same type. Thus every connected component corresponds to a set \(\mathcal C\) of types.

* If \(\sum_{T\in\mathcal C}g_T\ne0\) in \(F\) for a component at some vertex, the exact degree is two and the minimum sparse size is \(\Theta(n^2\log(2n))\).
* Otherwise the minimum sparse size is between \(\Omega(n^{\max(4,2q)})\) and \(O(n^{\max(4,2q)}\log(2n))\). Its exponent is exactly \(\max(4,2q)\). In this case the minimum degree is \(\max(3,2q)\) for sufficiently large \(n\).

In particular the second alternative applies to a fixed interior rank gap \(g\) for one arrow, provided the ranks and nullities on both sides at both vertices are proportional to \(n\). For \(g=1\) the sparse exponent is four for this ordinary matrix-equivalence encoding, while the exact degree is three. This contrasts with the quadratic-logarithmic size of conjugacy for square diagonal projections of the same two ranks. Equioriented or arbitrarily oriented path quivers have thin interval indecomposables and are among the applications when the inputs have the stated display and multiplicity conditions. [Conjugacy comparison](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/55f3f9c1c78a81d4d617f902845a8c94a8455c33/SparseMatrixProofBounds/CriticalMatchings.md)

## Forward evaluation and the degree-two case

For each vertex, the image of the Hom space under evaluation is spanned by matrix units on the edges of the graph obtained by replacing types by their individual copies. This follows because the morphism conditions split over pairs of summand copies, and a single output coordinate at that vertex is a scalar. Each type graph component lifts to a connected component on the copies. Indeed all classes are nonempty and each type has its own cross-edge; an allowed pair of types gives every edge between the appropriate copies. The degree-two trace criterion requires a different total number of source and target coordinates in some connected component, measured in \(F\). The criterion is seen directly by assigning equal diagonal labels to the endpoints of every allowed matrix unit, and arbitrary labels between components. Diagonal choices realize all necessary label assignments. Each thin summand is absolutely indecomposable: its nonzero vertex spaces are joined by the underlying graph of its nonzero arrows, since otherwise it splits. Any endomorphism therefore has a single scalar on all its nonzero vertex spaces. The exact degree formula then gives the stated large-\(n\) degree in both cases.

For the size upper bound in the degree-two case, fix a component with a visible count gap. The rectangular maps obtained by restricting the vertex variables to that component have trace discrepancy. Every forward variable outside the component with one endpoint inside is a linear consequence of the original arrow entries involving only that copy pair. There are \(O(1)\) coordinates and equations per pair. Use those lines and their single-variable multiples to erase all crossing terms from the required diagonal inverse entries. Each entry costs \(O(n\log(2n))\) sparse occurrences in a balanced binary sum, including the input inverse line. There are \(O(n)\) diagonals. Summing the two traces and subtracting cancels all quadratic terms and gives a nonzero scalar at a total cost \(O(n^2\log(2n))\).

For the lower bound, specialize each mismatched type to its independent rectangular board of matching variables, exactly as in the proof of the degree theorem. Use the transpose board for the reverse variables and fixed identities on equal-multiplicity types. At each board choose a random near-bijection and move groups of unmatched columns to disjoint sets, with a common set of \(\Theta(n)\) probe indices. This is possible because the gaps are fixed. On each probe every arrow equation vanishes. Each inverse equation, at any quiver vertex, is identically zero or a diagonal unmatched-column indicator on its type board. Thus every input equation has support on at most one probe. A binary proof deriving the constant one must accumulate a sum of \(\Omega(n\log n)\) nonzero evaluations up through the first line whose support covers at least one quarter of the probes. This follows by tracing each covered index backwards to an input through at most two parents per step. The expected number of changes between the base probe and the other probes is \(O(1/n)\) per fixed monomial, even when a monomial has large degree, by the uniform injection estimate on each board and the fixed number of boards. Before that first line the variation dominates support. Therefore there must be \(\Omega(n^2\log n)\) monomial occurrences. All these probe bounds are precisely the independent-board calculation in [CriticalMatchings](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/55f3f9c1c78a81d4d617f902845a8c94a8455c33/SparseMatrixProofBounds/CriticalMatchings.md). Repeating the same matching variables at different quiver vertices changes only exponents in a specialized monomial, which leaves that calculation valid.

## Preparing short proofs in the other case

Each displayed arrow has at most one nonzero entry per row or column. In the [reverse derivation](QuiverProofDegree.md), every entry of \(Y_v(X_vA_a-B_aX_u)Y_u\) is a sum of \(O(n^2)\) variable multiples of forward equations of sparsity at most two. Each entry of either remaining term uses \(O(n)\) variable multiples of inverse entries, each of size \(O(n)\). A balanced binary sum costs \(O(n^2\log(2n))\) per reverse entry. There are \(O(n^2)\) such entries, as the quiver is fixed.

Project onto the true forward and reverse Hom spaces separately for every ordered pair of copies. Each projection uses a constant-size linear system. Its errors are linear combinations of \(O(1)\) already prepared equations. Each telescoped projected inverse entry is obtained by \(O(n)\) products of bounded-size linear forms, plus its single raw inverse equation of size \(O(n)\). Its cost is \(O(n\log(2n))\), since every product is a bounded-size sparse combination of single-variable multiples. Every residue pair \(P,Q\) has entries of constant support. The residue for an equal-type pair of copies is its scalar value at one fixed nonzero vertex of the type, since \(\operatorname{End}(T)=F\). Compositions through different indecomposable types contribute zero to this scalar, since a nonzero scalar would make one split off the other. Hence the rectangle inverse equations for a type are among these projected inverse entries, in the residue variables.

Choose a type with minimal \(q\). The size-\(q\) principal-minor coefficient for the two rectangular products is identical by Cauchy–Binet. Its values at the two identities differ in \(F\), by the coefficient of degree \(q\) in their binomial expansions. Each determinant expansion uses \(O(n^q)\) terms in total at fixed \(q\). Replace the \(q\) product entries in each term by their identity entries one at a time, using the inverse axioms and the recurrence for a difference of products. A product entry has at most \(O(n)\) terms after expansion of the constant-support residue entries. Thus the balanced sums and intermediate expansions cost \(O(n^{2q}\log(2n))\) in total. All additions and products can be expressed with the two ordinary PC rules, with no new variables. Including preparation gives the upper bound.

## A quartic lower bound when every component balances

Temporarily keep boundedly many copies of every type on each side while preserving each signed gap. Each type then has positive counts on both sides and can be partitioned into classes of identical copies of size at least two and nonzero in \(F\). This can be arranged by making the bounded counts sufficiently large and using the two coprime sizes \(p+1,2p+1\) in characteristic \(p\); in characteristic zero take one large class on each side. Write \(c_i,r_j\) for the source and target class sizes.

Work over an algebraic closure. For every source class \(i\) and target class \(j\), choose a single morphism between their types that is nonzero at each vertex at which evaluation of their Hom space is nonzero. A finite collection of nonvanishing linear-coordinate conditions can be met simultaneously over the infinite closure, since none of the relevant linear functionals is identically zero. Assign its scalar vertex coordinates \(M_{ji,v}\) to the respective forward entries, constant on pairs of classes. Every forward arrow equation vanishes. For each \(v\), choose reverse class constants \(N_{ij,v}\) satisfying the diagonal inverse requirements
\[
 \sum_i c_i M_{ji,v}N_{ij,v}=1
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \sum_j r_j N_{ij,v}M_{ji,v}=1
\]
whenever the corresponding class has vertex dimension one. These equations reduce to a bipartite row and column sum problem on the support of \(M\): the edge variable \(Z_{ji}=r_j c_i M_{ji,v}N_{ij,v}\) needs target sums \(r_j\) and source sums \(c_i\). A solution exists exactly when the prescribed sums are balanced in each component. For sufficiency, take a spanning tree of each component and eliminate leaf sums one by one, with the final vertex consistent by balance. The class support graph is a blow-up of the type graph and has the same components. The required balance is precisely the assumed condition on \(\sum_{T\in\mathcal C}g_T\). This constructs all \(N\) without asking for a true inverse assignment.

To see why these constants obstruct narrow monomials, view each source and target **copy** as a vertex of a second graph, regardless of the quiver node at which a variable lives. A matrix entry touches the two copies indexing it. A monomial has formal parity at each copy, obtained by adding the parities of its variable factors. A polynomial whose monomials touch at most three copies satisfies the following linear conditions in any degree. In a parity component with no odd copy, or with one odd source and one odd target, require that its value under the class-constant assignment is zero. In a component with two odd targets, record the evaluation separately according to its unique source copy, and require the vector of these evaluations to be constant within each source class. Use the transposed condition for two odd sources. These exhaust all possible parity components of monomials touching at most three copies. For each variable contributes even total endpoint parity and the source and target parity sums agree.

Every forward axiom has just one source and one target copy as its endpoints. Its value is zero by the morphism construction. The diagonal inverse axioms have no odd copies and evaluate to zero by the chosen sums. The off-diagonal inverse axioms have two odd endpoints on one side, and their evaluations are constant by class on the other side. Scalar combinations preserve this invariant.

Consider multiplication by a single matrix variable when the resulting line still touches at most three copies per monomial. Multiplication is injective on monomials, so terms cannot cancel merely to regain this width bound. If old and new grades both require zero total evaluation, preservation is immediate. If the old grade requires zero evaluation and the new grade has two odd targets, the old grade has one odd source and one odd target. The multiplying variable adds a second target and uses the same source. All resulting monomials therefore use this one source, and the evaluated coordinate there vanishes by multiplicativity. Interchange source and target for the symmetric case. If the old grade has two odd targets, every term must already use the multiplying variable's source and the multiplier's target must be one of those two targets; otherwise there would be four copies in the product. The old evaluation vector is supported on this single source. As it was constant on classes of size at least two, it is identically zero, and the new zero-evaluation condition follows. The case of two odd sources is identical. Thus the invariant survives any PC proof all of whose lines respect the width bound. It excludes the conclusion one. This is the [four-vertex invariant for congruence](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/55f3f9c1c78a81d4d617f902845a8c94a8455c33/SparseMatrixProofBounds/FourVertexCongruence.md) with the linear arrow axioms in its one-edge grade.

Now independently choose the indicated bounded live subsets in each original type on both sides, and pin the equally sized complements of that type by a uniform bijection of copies. On pinned pairs assign the identities at all nonzero vertices, keep every variable between live copies, and make other entries zero. Every axiom restricts to a residual axiom or zero. This holds for a forward arrow entry because the pin is the same morphism on every quiver vertex, and for inverse entries by the block-diagonal substitution. On every restriction some original monomial occurrence must survive and touch four distinct live copies by the invariant. The probability for a fixed occurrence is \(O(n^{-4})\), uniformly in the original monomial's degree. Collapse all factors on each pair of endpoints to one edge. A component with more than one edge must be wholly live if the monomial survives, since pinned edges are isolated among surviving edges. If any component has at least four vertices, four prescribed vertices must be live, which costs \(O(n^{-4})\). If there are at most three components of at most three vertices each, take the union over their at most nine vertices for the four live vertices that supply the witness. Otherwise prescribe one edge in each of four distinct components. They are a matching, and each one must either have its source endpoint live, costing \(O(n^{-1})\) per such endpoint, or be matched to its fixed partner by the independent complementary pinning, costing \(O(n^{-1})\) per such edge conditional on the chosen live sets. Sum over the at most sixteen choices of cases. The probability estimate follows in all cases. A union bound proves the \(\Omega(n^4)\) size lower bound.

For the independent \(\Omega(n^{2q})\) lower bound, specialize the original forward and reverse coordinates to independent matching boards on the discrepant types with identities on equal types. This kills the forward axioms and gives only the rectangular two-sided inverse axioms on those boards. Apply the normalized product-design lower bound, retaining bounded sets in each board and conditioning on matching the complements. Exactly as in that result, each restriction needs some original occurrence yielding at least \(p^{v_p(|g_T|)}\) distinct live edges on one board in positive characteristic. The probability for any fixed occurrence is \(O(n^{-2q})\), with zero or single-variable images even when the same board entry represents several raw coordinates. In characteristic zero take order zero and the same exponent-two estimate. The matching result is used in its fixed-gap range only.
