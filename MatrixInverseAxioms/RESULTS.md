# Inverse axioms, exact degree algorithms, and sparse proofs for matrix isomorphism

On the same two diagonal projections of ranks $r$ and $r+1$ in side $n=2r+1$, deleting one inverse direction changes the minimum ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) degree from two to $r+2$ for conjugacy, and from three to $r+2$ for congruence. It also raises sparse proof size from polynomial to $n^{\Theta(n)}$ in both systems. The inverse direction is already a consequence over any commutative ring. The separation holds over every coefficient field in the original variables, with no field equations. [Proof and exact scope](OneSidedMatrix.md) · [Question 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

| Equations on these projections | Both inverse directions | Only $XY=I$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conjugacy | Exact degree 2 and size $\Theta(n^2\log n)$ | Exact degree $r+2$ and size $n^{\Theta(n)}$ |
| Congruence | Exact degree 3 and size $\Omega(n^4)$ to $O(n^4\log n)$ | Exact degree $r+2$ and size $n^{\Theta(n)}$ |

For congruence the choice of single inverse direction matters. Keeping only $YX=I$ gives exact degree three and the same quartic exponent as keeping both directions. Deriving even **one** omitted entry of $YX-I$ from $XY-I$ alone already has factorial-scale sparse cost and linear degree. The latter result concerns a consequence of a satisfiable system. For a diagonal entry the lower bound zeroes a column and a row so the consequence becomes a refutation of the one-sided Rank Principle. [Orientation comparison](OneSidedMatrix.md) · [Single-entry inverse derivation](RedundantInverse.md)

The partial-inverse theorem quantifies how much the extra direction helps. For a size-$s$ forward identity with inner dimension $r$, supply just a principal reverse block on $r-k$ indices. If $s-r=O(k+1)$, the minimum sparse size is **$s^{\Theta(k+1)}$**, uniformly in $k$. The same bound transfers to complementary diagonal conjugacy and holds for the rectangular transpose version. In particular, omitting linearly many indices from that reverse block forces factorial-scale size even for proportional rank gaps divisible by the characteristic. [Partial reverse theorem](PartialReverse.md)

For the square Inversion Principle, Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She give a lower bound $n/2+1$ and an upper bound $2n+2$ for degree. The new individual-entry bounds place the diagonal case between $n+1$ and $n+3$, and the off-diagonal case between $n$ and $n+3$, with sharp factorial-scale sparse size in both. [Published Theorem 1.6 and Observation 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S3) · [Individual entry theorem](RedundantInverse.md)

## Exact degree beyond one matrix

* **Arbitrary diagrams of linear maps.** The degree formula applies to every finite quiver with vertex-wise invertible maps, any dimension vector, and any coefficient field. A linear test decides degree two in the original coordinates. Above that threshold, the exact degree comes from the discrepancy of indecomposable multiplicities. At fixed positive characteristic even a one-arrow rank mismatch can require linear degree. The proof gives the exact least maximum vertex dimension needed for any threshold $d\ge4$ among equal-dimension-vector pairs. [Quiver theorem](QuiverProofDegree.md)
* **Diagrams mixing maps and bilinear forms.** The formula extends to arbitrary mixed graphs in characteristic different from two. Pure bipartite form graphs work in every characteristic, including the original two independent matrix-equivalence actions and based matrix tuples. The proof uses the doubled quiver and the adjoint on its semisimple quotient. Symmetric and alternating multiplicity spaces require separate rectangular arguments. [Mixed-graph theorem](MixedGraphDegree.md)
* **An algorithm for the exact degree.** For the two matrix systems in Question 7.3 over any explicitly presented finite field, deterministic polynomial bit time computes minimum degree, including infinity for geometric satisfiability. It needs no polynomial factoring. A center-rank algorithm reads the multiplicities without splitting the simple factors, and a linear image test reads both alternating flags in characteristic two. The algorithm extends to the directed, bipartite, and odd-characteristic mixed settings above. [Degree algorithm](FiniteFieldDegree.md)
* **One Inversion Rule inference suffices with both inverse directions.** All geometrically negative instances in the stated matrix and diagram settings have degree-at-most-three, polynomial sparse-size PC+Inv refutations. With one inverse direction per equal-dimension vertex, an additional rule substitution supplies all omitted entries as separate inferences. Over finite fields the construction is deterministic polynomial bit time without factoring. This extends the original Rank Principle use of Inv to semisimple discrepancies in diagrams. [PC+Inv construction](InversionRule.md) · [The original Inversion Rule](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S1.SS2)

## Sparse size in the original variables

* For fixed-gap, displayed thin quiver representations with macroscopic multiplicities, the presence of a trace-visible imbalance in one Hom support component gives exact $\Theta(n^2\log n)$ sparse size. In the other cases the sharp exponent is $\max(4,2q)$ up to one logarithm, where $q=1$ in characteristic zero and $q$ is the smallest $p$-part of the nonzero gaps in characteristic $p$. This includes the quartic exponent for fixed interior rank gaps in the literal linear one-arrow encoding. [Sparse thin-quiver theorem](ThinQuiverSparseSize.md)
* For arbitrary displayed conjugacy inputs over every field, whenever a degree-two refutation exists there is one of sparse size $O(n^4\log^2(2n))$. For different minimal polynomials the bound is $O(n^4\log(2n))$. These bounds exploit short linear derivations for polynomial intertwiners while charging the actual expanded lines. The method also gives a degree-two bound for commuting tuples with a visible joint multiplicity difference. [Dense-coordinate proofs](PowerCalculus.md)
* If a polynomial of degree at most two has rank at most one on the target and strictly larger rank on the source, there is a degree-at-most-three refutation of size $O(n^3\log(2n))$ in arbitrary coordinates. A family with both input matrices dense, identical characteristic and minimal polynomials, and exact degree three has exact sparse size $\Theta(n^3)$ in every characteristic. [Sharp dense family](DenseRankOne.md)

The strongest item for the Grochow briefing is the inverse-axiom separation. It adds a direct exponential separation in the literal matrix variables and explains why listing both inverse directions can matter. The exact finite-field algorithm and all-diagram degree formula strengthen the same briefing entry. The unrestricted sparse-size bounds for two-sided proportional prime-power gaps and for all dense degree-three inputs remain open.
