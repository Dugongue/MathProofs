# A redundant inverse axiom changes sparse matrix proof size from polynomial to factorial scale

Let $X,Y$ be independent square variable matrices over a field, and use ordinary Polynomial Calculus with binary linear combinations and multiplication by one variable. Sparse size counts all monomial occurrences on used lines. Put $A=\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0_{r+1})$ and $B=\operatorname{diag}(I_{r+1},0_r)$, of side $n=2r+1$. Over every field the following bounds hold:

| Matrix problem | Using $XY=I$ | Using $XY=YX=I$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conjugacy, $XA=BX$ | Exact degree $r+2$, sparse size $n^{\Theta(n)}$ | Exact degree two, sparse size $\Theta(n^2\log n)$ |
| Congruence, $XAX^T=B$ | Exact degree $r+2$, sparse size $n^{\Theta(n)}$ | Exact degree three, sparse size between $\Omega(n^4)$ and $O(n^4\log n)$ |

The single inverse equation already forces both inverse equations for matrices over any commutative ring, by the determinant and adjugate identities. The displayed systems use no coordinate field equations. The separation is about proofs in the original variables on identical input matrices. The two-sided bounds are the published project results on these systems. [Question 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3) · [Two-sided bounds and exact degree](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/55f3f9c1c78a81d4d617f902845a8c94a8455c33/SparseMatrixProofBounds/RESULTS.md)

In the same congruence example, keeping only $YX=I$ has degree three and has sparse size between $\Omega(n^4)$ and $O(n^4\log n)$. Thus the two choices of a single inverse constraint already give the degree and size separation.

## Rectangular rank certificates

For $0<r<s$ the independent one-sided system $PQ=I_s$, with $P$ of shape $s$ by $r$ and $Q$ of shape $r$ by $s$, has exact PC degree $r+2$. The same is true of its symmetric version $PP^T=I_s$, in every characteristic.

For the common upper bound use the first $r+1$ target rows, and the corresponding right-inverse columns. Start with the identically zero minors of size $r+1$. Given every $(j+1)$-minor zero, take any $j+1$ rows and any $j$ columns. The cofactor row $c$ on these positions has $cP=0$ by the larger minors and repeated-column identities. Multiplying by each entry of $Q$ and subtracting $c(PQ-I)=0$ gives the $j$-minors themselves in degree $j+2$. Eventually the empty minor gives one. The same steps allow $Q=P^T$. There are at most $2^{2r+1}$ minors per size, each cofactor has at most $j!$ monomials, and every inverse entry has at most $r+1$ monomials. Expanding the cofactor products term by term and summing in binary trees costs $2^{O(r)} r!\operatorname{poly}(r)$ occurrences in total.

For the lower bound in characteristic $p>0$, enlarge $s$ to $S=r+p^a\ge s$ with $2p^a\ge r+2$. Every original forward product equation is a literal entry of the enlarged forward product, on its first $s$ rows and columns. A proof therefore replays in the enlarged two-sided system. Its lower bound is $r+2$ in the independent case by the weighted matching design argument. In the signed case use the analogous lower bound for $PP^T=I_S,\ P^TP=I_r$: restrict to partial matchings and send each live edge variable to $t_e x_e$, where $t_e$ is an independent sign-group generator. The lower-bound argument uses the basis of the group algebra with $t_e^2=1$ and the matching designs in its sign components. This argument works in characteristic two as well, since the underlying group elements still form a basis, and does not require an actual character taking distinct signs in the field. In characteristic zero a degree-bounded proof of these integer-coefficient systems descends to rational coefficients by degree-filtered linear closure. Reducing its finitely many coefficients at any prime avoiding all denominators preserves its derivations and degree bound. This contradicts the lower bound in positive characteristic if the original degree was below $r+2$. [Independent rectangle and one-sided Rank Principle](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/55f3f9c1c78a81d4d617f902845a8c94a8455c33/ExactMatrixProofDegrees/RankPrinciple.md) · [Signed matching proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/55f3f9c1c78a81d4d617f902845a8c94a8455c33/SparseMatrixProofBounds/SymmetricCongruenceDegree.md)

Both one-sided systems have sparse size $r^{\Theta(r)}$ uniformly when $r<s\le C r$ for fixed $C$. Here is the transfer in a form that covers the symmetric case directly. Choose a bipartite $(\gamma r,1)$ boundary expander with $s$ row vertices and $r$ column vertices and absolute bounded row degree $\Delta$. Constants $\gamma$ and $\Delta$ can depend on $C$. Such graphs exist by sampling eight distinct uniform neighbors per row. If $t$ rows have fewer than $t$ unique neighbors, their union uses at most $k=\lceil 9t/2\rceil$ columns. A union bound is
$$
 \binom{s}{t}\binom{r}{k}(k/r)^{8t}
 \le (C'(t/r)^{3/2})^t .
$$
Indeed $k/t\le 11/2$ and use the elementary binomial estimates. For $t\le\gamma r$, choose $\gamma>0$ so the sum is below one. For each permutation of the column labels substitute the Boolean edge variable for an allowed position in $P$ and its transposed position in $Q$, and zero otherwise. In the symmetric case use that substitution only on $P$. In the quotient by Boolean and collision equations, off-diagonal product entries vanish and diagonal product entries give the row totality equations of the graph. A PC refutation all of whose normalized lines have matching degree below $k_0$ gives a source PC refutation in degree at most $\max(\Delta,k_0,2)$. To see this, after each multiplication normalize by deleting collisions and reducing repeated exponents, via individual monomial multiples of the Boolean or collision equations. Row totalities follow by expanding the constant-width pigeon clauses and dropping the collided terms. This contradicts the Mikša–Nordström functional pigeonhole lower bound for $k_0=\lfloor c r\rfloor$ and sufficiently small fixed $c>0$. Therefore some surviving original monomial has at least $k_0$ distinct matching edges. For each fixed monomial, the probability of this event under random permutation is at most $\Delta^{k_0}/(r)_{k_0}$. The bound follows by choosing any prescribed $k_0$ distinct edges from its fixed support, which must all be allowed after permutation. Conflicting original supports vanish throughout. The union bound gives $r^{\Omega(r)}$ across all occurrences, with no restriction on their original degrees. [Mikša–Nordström, Theorem 5.8](https://jakobnordstrom.se/docs/publications/GeneralizedMethodPCdegreeJournal.pdf)

The alternating form version is equally sharp. Write $J_b$ for the sum of $b$ hyperbolic planes. On an independent $2s$-by-$2r$ variable matrix $P$, the single equation $P J_r P^T=J_s$ has degree $r+2$ and sparse size $r^{\Theta(r)}$ for $r<s\le Cr$. In bases grouping first and second hyperbolic partners, substitute $P=\operatorname{diag}(U,V^T)$ for independent rectangular $U,V$ of shapes $s$ by $r$ and $r$ by $s$. The Gram equations become $UV=I_s$ and its transposed duplicates. This proves both lower bounds. For the upper bound the first rows from $r+1$ target planes are mutually isotropic, and the second rows provide a right inverse by their pairings. The integral divided symplectic contractions give the $(r+1)$-minors zero, after which cofactor descent gives degree $r+2$. Both stages use only the forward Gram equations. Direct expansion involves at most $2^{O(r)}(2r)!$ terms per determinant or Pfaffian stage and at most exponentially many indexed minors, giving size $r^{O(r)}$. [Divided symplectic contraction and cofactor descent](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/55f3f9c1c78a81d4d617f902845a8c94a8455c33/SparseMatrixProofBounds/BinaryCongruence.md)

## Conjugacy in the original variables

In displayed semisimple coordinates let $a_\lambda,b_\lambda$ be the multiplicities of each eigenvalue in $A,B$ and put $r=\min_{\lambda:b_\lambda>a_\lambda}a_\lambda$. The exact degree using only $XA=BX,\ XY=I$ is $r+2$. This applies to arbitrary semisimple matrices in their given coordinates by scalar extension and invertible linear substitution. When $r$ is a positive constant fraction of $n$, the split diagonal presentations have minimum sparse size $n^{\Theta(n)}$.

For the upper bound, the forward intertwining equations give each entry of $X$ crossing unequal labels as a scalar multiple of an input. In each deficient diagonal block of $XY=I$ eliminate cross terms by multiplying those linear entries by their companion variables. This gives a forward one-sided rectangle of inner size $a_\lambda$ and outer size $b_\lambda$ in degree two, including when $a_\lambda=0$. Choose one achieving $r$ and apply the corresponding rank proof. The preparation has polynomial sparse cost in the displayed variables. At $r=0$ the proof uses degree two, since an absent target label gives a row of vanishing linear entries of $X$. Degree one cannot refute any instance because the linear intertwining inputs are homogeneous and the inverse inputs have degree two.

For the lower bound use a single independent board $P,Q$ with inner dimension $r$ and outer dimension $r+\max_\lambda(b_\lambda-a_\lambda)$. Within each deficient label block use constants to match $a_\lambda-r$ coordinates identically. On the remaining coordinates substitute the first $r+b_\lambda-a_\lambda$ rows of $P$ and the corresponding columns of $Q$. All other inverse blocks of $XY$ vanish off the diagonal label blocks. On a nondeficient label block, use coordinate inclusion and projection constants to realize its forward right inverse. Set all matrix entries crossing labels to zero. Thus the forward intertwining equations vanish and the inverse equations become zero or input equations of the single one-sided board. This monomial substitution does not increase sparse size or degree. The rectangular exact-degree result proves the lower bound, and the sparse result applies when $r\ge cn$, since the outer board dimension is at most $n$. Swapping the kept inverse equation swaps the roles of the two multiplicity lists.

## Congruence in the original variables

More generally, take $A=\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0_{n-r})$, $B=\operatorname{diag}(I_s,0_{n-s})$ with $r<s\le n$. The first $s$ rows and columns of $XAX^T=B$ already give the one-sided symmetric Rank Principle. For the converse specialize $X$ to $\operatorname{diag}(P,U)$ and $Y$ to $\operatorname{diag}(P^T,U^T)$, where $P$ is $s$ by $r$ and $U=[I_{n-s}\ 0]$ is $(n-s)$ by $(n-r)$. Then every equation of $XAX^T=B,\ XY=I$ maps to zero or an entry of $PP^T-I_s$. In particular the same bounds apply with or without $XY=I$, and the exact degree argument also works with arbitrary geometric coordinates by invertible linear substitution and scalar descent. For alternating forms of ranks $2r<2s$ the first Gram block is $P J_r P^T=J_s$. Set the corresponding block of $Y$ to $J_rP^TJ_s^{-1}$ and use the same constant coordinate restriction on the radical. The standard partner matrices are signed permutations, so substitution again preserves sparse size and degree. The signed-rectangle conclusion for ordinary projections therefore has a hyperbolic counterpart.

## The other inverse direction in congruence

For the unit-gap projections in the first table, set $G=XAX^T-B$ and keep only $YX-I$. The identity
$$
 H=XA-BY^T=GY^T-XA(YX-I)^T
$$
derives all its entries in degree at most three. In the rank-block ordering this gives $X_{21}=0$ and $X_{11}=Y_{11}^T$ in degree three. Put $P=X_{11}$. For each diagonal entry of the rank-active source block of $YX-I$, discard every product involving $X_{21}$ by multiplying the corresponding zero entry by its companion variable. Use $X_{11}=Y_{11}^T$ to replace the other terms and obtain the diagonal entry of $P^TP-I_r$. The corresponding target Gram block is literally $PP^T-I_{r+1}$. Subtracting their traces gives the unit $(r+1)-r=1$ in every field. Each entry of $H$ costs $O(n^2\log n)$ monomial occurrences because $A$ is diagonal and both Gram and inverse entries have $O(n)$ monomials. There are $O(n^2)$ entries, and the final preparations and trace sums cost at most $O(n^3\log n)$. Thus total sparse size is $O(n^4\log n)$. The quartic lower bound and the degree-three lower bound for the system with both inverse directions apply to this subsystem.

The auxiliary symmetric and alternating rank principles, and the conjugacy and congruence separations, are written proofs. They use no prime-distribution hypothesis.
