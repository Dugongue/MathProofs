# The ordered-rank-four case of symmetry-characterized graphs

Let $X$ be a finite simple graph for which every graph fixed by $\Gamma=\operatorname{Aut}(X)$ is isomorphic to $X$, its complement, the empty graph, or the complete graph. Suppose that $\Gamma$ has two orbits on unordered pairs of distinct vertices and that $X$ and its complement are connected. Then $\Gamma$ is primitive. If its ordered permutation rank is greater than three, it is exactly four and $\Gamma$ is almost simple. In particular, the remaining non-rank-three part of the two-orbit case of Grochow–Urisman Question 1.9 can be confined to almost simple permutation actions. The graph order, the simple socle, and the point stabilizer can all vary. [Grochow–Urisman, §1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26244v2#S1.SS3)

## Primitivity and ordered rank

If $\Gamma$ has at least three vertex orbits, there are at least three orbits on pairs crossing distinct vertex orbits. If it has two vertex orbits of size at least two, the two within-orbit sets and the cross set give at least three pair orbits. The other intransitive possibility has a singleton and one larger orbit. Then the two pair orbits must be the pairs through the singleton and the remaining pairs. The graph is a star or its complement, contrary to the two connectivity hypotheses.

If the transitive $\Gamma$ is imprimitive, the within-block pairs and the between-block pairs are two nonempty invariant sets. Each must be one pair orbit. Since the graph is neither empty nor complete, it is a disjoint union of equal cliques or its complement. This again contradicts the connectivity hypotheses.

Each unordered-pair orbit is either one ordered orbital or splits into two paired ordered orbitals. If both split, no group element can interchange a pair of distinct vertices. In particular, there are no nontrivial involutions. Cauchy's theorem and the odd-order theorem make $\Gamma$ solvable. A finite solvable primitive group has a regular elementary abelian normal subgroup. Indeed a minimal nonidentity normal subgroup of a solvable group is elementary abelian, primitivity makes it transitive, and a faithful transitive abelian action is regular. More generally, any graph with a regular abelian automorphism subgroup is an undirected Cayley graph on that subgroup. If $x,y$ are two vertices in additive coordinates, the map $z\mapsto x+y-z$ is an automorphism interchanging them, since the connection set is inverse-closed. Thus neither orbit splits in the regular abelian case. The non-rank-three case has exactly one split.

## Eliminating the other primitive types

If $\Gamma$ preserves a homogeneous Cartesian decomposition of the vertex set into $k\ge 2$ factors of size at least two, the Hamming distances $1,\ldots,k$ on unordered pairs are distinct nonempty invariant sets. Hence $k=2$, and the two unordered pair orbits are precisely the two Hamming distances. The graph, being a nonempty proper union of pair orbits, is $H(2,q)$ or its complement, where $q$ is the factor size. Both graphs admit the full product-action wreath group $S_q\wr S_2$, which can interchange the two endpoints of any pair by swapping their values in each coordinate. Neither orbit splits.

Suppose a regular normal subgroup $M$ has order divisible by at least three distinct primes. Identify vertices with $M$ by regularity. Every point stabilizer element at the identity acts as an automorphism of $M$ by conjugation. Thus the order of $a^{-1}b$ is invariant on ordered pairs $(a,b)$, and switching the pair inverts this element and preserves its order. Cauchy's theorem therefore gives at least three unordered-pair orbits. Together with abelian inversion, this handles the regular normal subgroups in the affine, holomorph-simple, holomorph-compound, and twisted-wreath types. In the nonabelian cases the regular normal subgroup is a product of copies of a nonabelian simple group, whose order has at least three prime divisors by Burnside's two-prime theorem.

In simple diagonal type, the socle is $M=T^k$ for a nonabelian simple $T$, with $k\ge 2$, and the vertex set is the coset space $M/D$ for the full diagonal $D$. Use the left action on cosets $gD$ and normalize an ordered pair to $(D,gD)$ by an element of $M$. The multiset

$$
\left\{\operatorname{ord}(g_i^{-1}g_j):1\le i<j\le k\right\}
$$

is unchanged by left or right multiplication of $g$ by $D$. A point stabilizer element induces an automorphism of $M$ preserving $D$, hence permutes the simple factors and acts on them by a common automorphism of $T$. The multiset is invariant under these changes. Normalizing the reversed pair replaces $g$ by $g^{-1}$. Since $g_i g_j^{-1}$ is conjugate to the inverse of $g_i^{-1}g_j$, reversal preserves the multiset as well. For each prime $p\mid |T|$, take $g=(t,1,\ldots,1)$ with $t$ of order $p$. The nonidentity orders in the multiset are all $p$, so at least three unordered-pair orbits occur. This excludes simple diagonal type.

The O'Nan–Scott classification of finite primitive permutation groups leaves almost simple type. Product action and compound diagonal preserve homogeneous Cartesian decompositions. The other types were excluded above. The argument uses the standard O'Nan–Scott theorem of Liebeck, Praeger, and Saxl, *On the O'Nan–Scott theorem for finite primitive permutation groups*, Journal of the Australian Mathematical Society, Series A 44 (1988), 389–396.

## Literature and exact scope

- The preprint of Grochow and Urisman formulates the one- and two-promised graph isomorphism cases and unpromised recognition. Its suggestion that the full automorphism group always has ordered rank three excludes real possibilities. The seven-cycle already has three unordered-pair orbits and ordered rank four. For the two-unordered-orbit branch the theorem above identifies the remaining type when ordered rank is four. It neither constructs a general isomorphism algorithm for that type nor proves that graphs of the type exist. [Grochow–Urisman, §1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26244v2#S1.SS3)
- Ponomarenko's Theorems 1.1–1.2 control automorphism groups and fixed-index Weisfeiler–Leman identification for cyclotomic graphs at sufficiently large field order. The identification is against arbitrary graphs, which is useful for the one-promised version. The classification of Li, Lim, and Praeger supplies the relevant semilinear models for edge-transitive homogeneous factorizations. These sources support the separately proved three-unordered-orbit branch and do not cover all symmetry-characterized graphs. [Ponomarenko](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18304v1) · [Li–Lim–Praeger](https://emis.de/ft/43761)
- Nguyen's Hexagon preprint supplies a sublinear exponent for exact counting in bounded-primal-degree binary constraint networks. It supports the separate graph-obstruction algorithm obtained by the incidence-network reduction. That algorithm has a separate factor $2^{O(d\log(d+2))}$, leaving a jointly uniform $n^{o(d)}$ bound across degree regimes open. Claude's clique-contraction proof is upstream of the counting result. [Nguyen, Theorem 1.2](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00004v1) · [Claude, Appendices A and B](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00577v1#page=28)
- Pyle's tree algorithm identifies a nearby graph-theoretic route to general-position problems, outside Grochow–Urisman Question 1.9. The weighted-counting and counting-limits preprints address bounded-locality representations and do not supply a general graph or tensor transporter construction. [Pyle, §7](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00448v1) · [Weighted counting](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00594v1) · [Counting limits](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00149v1)
