# November isomorphism literature and consequences

## Which published questions are answered?

**Grochow–Qiao Part IV, Question 2.** Assume Qiao and Zhang's Theorem 9.1 on arbitrary Cayley tables. Decide the original pair in polynomial time. Output a fixed yes pair or a fixed no pair of class-two exponent-three groups according to the answer. For example take the order-27 Heisenberg group over the field of three elements for the yes pair, and compare it with its direct product with the cyclic group of order three for the no pair. Both groups have exponent three and class exactly two. This meets the polynomial many-one decision-reduction formulation. The upstream group algorithm supplies all the computational content. A structural encoding of the source into a special group is a stronger objective. This implication was already recorded in the previous catalog. [Original question](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S1.SS1) · [Qiao–Zhang manuscript, Theorem 9.1](https://github.com/sigact-stoc/stoc2027/releases/download/papers-2/1723.pdf)

**Arvind–Torán search and counting, recorded as Grochow–Qiao Part IV, Question 3.** The same upstream theorem returns the empty set or an isomorphism and generators of the source automorphism group on the explicit Cayley-table elements. The isomorphism solves search. Count the group using a permutation stabilizer chain, multiplying the orbit lengths. Translation by the returned isomorphism bijects this group with the isomorphism set. All counts have polynomial bit length since they are at most the factorial of the group order. These are polynomial-time search and counting algorithms and hence zero-query Turing reductions to decision. Search and counting are two clauses of one numbered question. This implication also appeared in the previous catalog. [Original question](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S1.SS1) · [Upstream theorem](https://github.com/sigact-stoc/stoc2027/releases/download/papers-2/1723.pdf)

**Grochow–Levet, conference Question 5.1.** In fixed dimension k the second-arity refinement records, for a tuple and each choice of positions, an isomorphism type of a directed edge-colored graph on the group elements. Its edge colors are the old colors of tuples formed by the corresponding substitutions. For a joint version put all the relevant colors into one ordered edge label. The replacements and the initial marked-subgroup checks cost polynomial time for fixed k. Compare all such graphs across both groups, assigning common integer names to exactly equal types. This requires polynomially many graph-isomorphism calls per round. Edge directions, loops and colors have polynomial-size ordinary graph encodings, for example by role-labeled incidence vertices, replacing labels with distinguishable polynomial-size gadgets. Retaining old colors ensures stabilization after at most twice the number of k-tuples. The claimed graph-isomorphism time of Hajiaghayi and JafariRaviz on polynomial-size graphs gives exp(O(log² n)) total time. There is a material source discrepancy. One indexed rendering of the conference paper prints a big O. The NSF-hosted proceedings copy and the revised journal text print a little o. The calculation establishes the big-O bound conditional on the graph-isomorphism preprint and leaves the little-o question open. We do not count a conference closure. [Proceedings copy, Question 5.1](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10494292) [Conference question](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~eptcs/paper.cgi?GandALF2023.12.pdf=) · [Revised question](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.13725v7#S5) · [November graph-isomorphism manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01399v2)

## New informal arguments

- [Algebraic hitting sets](Arithmetic.md). A single clause yields an explicit small annihilator for the full map including its Boolean-axiom coordinates. This addresses the negative alternative of GKSS Question 3 for that encoding. The clause-only map and the positive proof-complexity lower-bound direction have different hypotheses.

- [Colored elementary abelian groups](ColoredGroups.md). A fixed-width game-equivalence class contains a growing family of inequivalent exterior forms. This strengthens the earlier answer to the colored Question 8.3.
- [Symmetry-characterized graphs](Graphs.md). The exceptional two-unordered-orbit primitive case with ordered rank four can only have almost-simple type. The full recognition and isomorphism targets remain open.
- [Code equivalence over different fields](Codes.md). A common coordinate permutation can be sought across a list of monomial code pairs with independent diagonal scalings in each pair. Binary permutation-coset intersection handles the coupling. The exponent depends on the shared coordinate length. The subexponential permutation-code target in §7.7 of the earlier paper remains open.

## Other November sources checked

The finite-ring follow-up uses the Cayley-table group theorem for ring isomorphism. Its ring input and time parameter differ from the characteristic-two matrix-tuple problem. In particular Qiao–Zhang Theorem 1.3 states an exponential dependence on matrix dimension. [Zhang–Wang–Zhuang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18084) · [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00735)

The Hexagon counting paper applies to a bounded-degree constraint representation. The graph note explains where the cost of constructing or using that representation enters the proposed Grochow–Urisman application. The degree dependence still matters for the requested uniform bound. [Nguyen](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00004v1)

**Quiver mutations give a boundary for tensor-isomorphism reductions.** Qiyue Tang's November 6 preprint claims undecidability of mutation equivalence up to relabeling for one fixed source quiver. Assuming the theorem, even with that source fixed, there is no computable function of the target's bit length bounding the shortest mutation sequence for every reachable target. A proposed bound would decide reachability by enumerating all sequences through that length, then testing the finitely many vertex relabelings. A fixed source has fixed vertex count, and mutation and relabeling commute after renaming the mutated vertex. The same enumeration argument shows that decidability of labeled reachability from that source would decide the unlabeled question. Thus enlarging ordinary isomorphism to include quiver mutation crosses a decidability boundary: an effective compilation of this relation into any decidable finite-field tensor-isomorphism problem would give a contradiction. This consequence uses the stated theorem. We inspected the deposit abstracts, without an audit of Tang's undecidability reduction. [November 6 theorem](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00158v1) · [November 7 refinement distinguishing mutation-acyclic and mutation-cyclic inputs](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00180v1)

Two November 12 deposits by Tejasvi Singh Tomar address the binary matrix multiplication tensor. One reports a rank lower bound of 35 for the four-by-four multiplication tensor. The other reports depth-18 local optimality for five rank-47 decompositions. The latter excludes short replacements within those decompositions and leaves global rank 46 undecided. Those conclusions were checked against the deposit abstracts. [Rank lower bound](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00372v1) · [Local optimality](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00375v1)
