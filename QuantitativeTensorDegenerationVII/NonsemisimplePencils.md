# Exact Loewy order for regular multiplication pencils

Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic different from two. Consider a standard graded commutative Artinian $k$-algebra $A$ of Hilbert function $(1,e,2)$ whose multiplication pencil is regular. At each singular projective direction $j$, let $c_j$ be its corank and $\mu_j$ its determinant multiplicity. Thus $c_j$ is the number of Jordan blocks at that direction and $\mu_j$ is their total length. Put
\[
 R=\#\{j:c_j\ge2\},\qquad B=e-\sum_{j:c_j\ge2}c_j.
\]
The exact diagonal-source order is two if and only if
\[
 R\le B+3\qquad\text{and each direction has at most one Jordan block of length greater than one}. \tag{1}
\]
For all regular pencils an explicit upper bound is $\max(3,1+\ell)$, where $\ell$ is the largest Jordan block length. In particular, when $\ell\le2$, the exact order is three if (1) fails. The orders of all such length-at-most-two factors add under tensor products. One consequence, valid over every field, is a family of dimension $7^r$ with exact order $3r$ and Loewy degree $2r$.

Qualitative smoothability for Hilbert function $(1,e,2)$ was established by Cartwright, Erman, Velasco, and Viray. The statements here classify whether the least order attains the Loewy degree in the regular class and give explicit split smoothings. [Cartwright–Erman–Velasco–Viray, *Hilbert schemes of 8 points*, Proposition 4.10](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.0341)

## 1. Split conductor and order two

For a based $n$-dimensional unital algebra $A$, the diagonal-source order $o_k(A)$ is the least $d$ such that three polynomial mode maps send the multiplication tensor of $k^n$ to $t^d T_A+O(t^{d+1})$. Set $V=k[[t]]$ and $K=k((t))$. A split smoothing lattice is a unital full-rank $V$-subalgebra $L\subseteq V^n$ with $L/tL\simeq A$. Its conductor exponent $c(L)$ is least with $t^{c(L)}V^n\subseteq L$. The least such exponent equals $o_k(A)$.

Indeed, divide one input map of a polynomial arc by $t^d$, giving an integral law $m(x,y)=C(Ux\odot Wy)$ reducing to multiplication in $A$. The three entry-pole caps $p_1,p_2,p_3$ sum to at most $d$. Write $e$ for the target unit, $a=Ue$, $b=We$, $S_1=m(-,e)$, and $S_2=m(e,-)$. The slices $S_1=C\operatorname{diag}(b)U$ and $S_2=C\operatorname{diag}(a)W$ are integrally invertible, since their reductions are identity. Choose $h\equiv I\bmod t$ with $he=m(e,e)$, for instance $I+(m(e,e)-e)\ell$ with $\ell(e)=1$. Direct substitution shows that the normalized integral law $h^{-1}m(S_1^{-1}hx,S_2^{-1}hy)$ is coordinatewise multiplication transported by $H=h^{-1}C\operatorname{diag}(a\odot b)$. Its generic characters $H^{-1}$ are integral on the normalized finite algebra, since $V$ is integrally closed, and their rows are primitive since they take value one at the unit. Thus $L=H^{-1}V^n$ is a split smoothing lattice. Let $c_i$ be least with $t^{c_i}e_i\in L$. The minimum valuation of column $i$ of $H$ is $-c_i$. The identities
\[
 US_1^{-1}h=\operatorname{diag}(a)H^{-1},\quad
 WS_2^{-1}h=\operatorname{diag}(b)H^{-1},\quad
 h^{-1}C=H\operatorname{diag}((a\odot b)^{-1})
\]
give $v_t(a_i)\ge-p_1$, $v_t(b_i)\ge-p_2$, and $-c_i-v_t(a_i)-v_t(b_i)\ge-p_3$. Hence $c(L)=\max_i c_i\le d$. Conversely, with $M$ the column matrix of a lattice basis lifting the target basis, use $M$ on both inputs and $t^{c(L)}M^{-1}$ on the output. These maps are integral and truncating them through degree $c(L)$ preserves the leading tensor. This tracks order in the unital tensor-to-algebra normalization. [Bläser–Lysikov, *On degeneration of tensors and algebras*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04253)

For a local split special fiber $A$, set $F^jA=\operatorname{im}(L\cap t^jV^n\to A)$. The filtration is multiplicative. Every branch reduces to the residue character, so $F^1A=\mathfrak m$ and $\mathfrak m^j\subseteq F^jA$. Also $F^{c(L)+1}A=0$. Thus $c(L)$ is at least the Loewy degree.

For a finite set of distinct affine $k$-points $P$, filter $k^P$ by the evaluations of polynomials of degree at most $j$. Say that $A$ has a point presentation if it is the associated graded of this filtration. An adapted basis of $k^P$, scaled by powers of $t$ equal to the first filtration degrees of its vectors, gives a split smoothing of conductor equal to the Loewy degree of $A$.

For the algebras considered here a split smoothing of conductor two conversely yields a point presentation. Regularity means that some linear functional on $A_2$ induces a nonsingular pairing on $A_1$. Hence every nonzero degree-one class has nonzero product with a degree-one partner, and the socle is precisely $A_2$. If $c(L)=2$, a class of $F^2A$ with nonzero degree-one component would have such a product in $F^3A=0$. Thus $F^2A=\mathfrak m^2$. Let $E_j$ be the reductions of $t^{-j}(L\cap t^jV^n)$ in $k^n$. Taking leading coefficients identifies $F^j/F^{j+1}$ with $E_j/E_{j-1}$ compatibly with products, since $tL\cap t^jV^n=t(L\cap t^{j-1}V^n)$ for $j\ge1$. Here $E_{-1}=0$, $E_0=k\mathbf1$, and $E_2=k^n$. Because the associated graded is generated in degree one, $E_j$ is the evaluation space of polynomials of degree at most $j$ in the vectors lifting a basis of $E_1/E_0$. Their rows are distinct because $E_2=k^n$. This produces the required point presentation.

## 2. Restrictions on point pencils

With a point presentation the $e+3$ points affinely span $k^e$. Normalize $e+1$ of them to $0,e_1,\ldots,e_e$, and let the remaining two be $p,q$. Subtracting affine-linear interpolation on the normalized basis gives
\[
 M(u,v)=u pp^{\mathsf T}+v qq^{\mathsf T}-D,qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(up+vq).
 \tag{2}
\]
Write $P=\sum_i p_i$, $Q=\sum_i q_i$, $a=1-P$, $b=1-Q$, and $h=a u+b v$. No coordinate pair $(p_i,q_i)$ can be zero, and $a,b$ cannot both be zero. Otherwise a coordinate vector or the all-ones vector is in the common kernel. For a fixed direction let $m$ be the number of zero diagonal entries of $D$. Across distinct directions the sum of $m$ is at most $e$. The determinant identity is
\[
 \det M=(-1)^e\det D\left[ab+h\sum_i\frac{p_iq_i}{up_i+vq_i}\right]. \tag{3}
\]
This follows from the rank-two determinant lemma. If $X=p^{\mathsf T}D^{-1}p$, $Y=q^{\mathsf T}D^{-1}q$, and $C=p^{\mathsf T}D^{-1}q$, its bracket is $(1-uX)(1-vY)-uv C^2$. The identities $uX+vC=P$ and $uC+vY=Q$ give (3).

At a non-axis direction $uv\ne0$ with $m=0$, a kernel vector $x$ is determined by $\alpha=p^{\mathsf T}x$ and $\beta=q^{\mathsf T}x$, since $x=D^{-1}(up\alpha+vq\beta)$. If the corank were two, both standard pairs $(\alpha,\beta)=(1,0),(0,1)$ would occur, forcing $uX=vY=1$ and $C=0$. Then $P=Q=1$, which is impossible.

At a non-axis direction with $m>0$, denote the zero diagonal group by $I$. The kernel equations give $\alpha=\beta=\theta$, and $x_i=\theta$ outside $I$. Taking the scalar product with $up+vq$ gives $h\theta=0$. If $h\ne0$, the kernel has dimension $m-1$, since the vectors are zero outside $I$ and have just the one nonzero linear constraint $p^{\mathsf T}x=0$. At $h=0$ the dimension is $m$. For each $\theta$, set the outside coordinates to $\theta$ and solve $p^{\mathsf T}x=\theta$ on $I$. The scalar-product identity and $v\ne0$ give $q^{\mathsf T}x=\theta$ as well. At the axis $v=0$ the corank is $m$ if $a\ne0$ and $m+1$ if $a=0$. This follows since all support coordinates of $p$ must equal $p^{\mathsf T}x$, and the other coordinates are free. At $u=0$ the result uses $b$ instead.

There are at least $R-2$ non-axis directions with corank at least two. They all have $m>0$. If $a,b\ne0$, only the direction $h=0$ can avoid the loss of one in the bound $m-1$, and the axes give no gains. If $ab=0$, $h=0$ is an axis, and at most one axis gains one. Thus the sum of coranks at the directions with $c_j\ge2$ is at most $e-R+3$. This proves the first condition of (1).

We next show that at most one Jordan block at any direction can have length greater than one. For a local coordinate $z$ at the direction, the number of length-greater-than-one blocks equals the nullity of $M'(0)$ restricted as a bilinear form to $\ker M(0)$. Indeed eliminate the invertible complement of the kernel over $k[[z]]$. The remaining matrix is $z M'(0)|_{\ker M(0)}+O(z^2)$. The count follows by reducing this matrix after division by $z$. Its local elementary divisors are the sizes of the Jordan blocks of a relative matrix formed with a nonsingular pencil member.

At a non-axis direction with $m>0$, use the chart $u=1$ and derivative with respect to $v$. The kernel contains the subspace $H$ of vectors supported on $I$ with $q_I^{\mathsf T}x=0$. Its dimension is $m-1$. On $H$ the derivative form is $-\operatorname{diag}(q_I)$ restricted to this hyperplane. It is nonsingular if $\sum_{i\in I}q_i\ne0$, and has radical of dimension one otherwise, since its orthogonal complement in the group space is the line of all ones. If $h\ne0$, the kernel is exactly $H$, proving the assertion. If $h=0$ and $\sum_{i\in I}q_i\ne0$, $H$ is nonsingular of codimension one in the kernel. In the remaining case $h=0$ and $\sum_{i\in I}q_i=0$, the entire group's contribution to the rational sum in (3) is zero. Its denominator is common up to scalars because $p_I$ and $q_I$ are proportional. The bracket in (3) then takes the nonzero value $ab$ at the direction, and $\det M$ has multiplicity exactly $m$, equal to the corank. So every block there has length one.

At $v=0$ with $a\ne0$, the kernel is supported on $I$ and the derivative there is an invertible diagonal matrix plus a rank-one matrix. If $a=0$, the kernel additionally includes the all-ones vector on the support of $p$. In the resulting basis the derivative is $-\operatorname{diag}(q_I,Q_{\rm out})+(q_I,Q_{\rm out})(q_I,Q_{\rm out})^{\mathsf T}$, where $Q_{\rm out}=\sum_{i\notin I}q_i$. For $Q_{\rm out}\ne0$ its corank is at most one. For $Q_{\rm out}=0$ the last row vanishes, and the group part is nonsingular because $Q\ne1$. The other axis is symmetric, and a direction with corank at most one requires no further bound. This proves necessity of (1).

## 3. Constructing the desired Jordan structure

Over the stated field the elementary divisors determine a regular symmetric pencil up to simultaneous congruence, after choosing the same parameter coordinates. For a short proof, take a nonsingular member and reduce the relative matrix to its Jordan form. If $D_0,D_1$ are the two resulting nonsingular symmetric forms for the same relative matrix, then $T=D_0^{-1}D_1$ commutes with that matrix and is self-adjoint for $D_0$. An invertible matrix $T$ has a polynomial square root over this field. For each eigenvalue choose a nonzero square root and solve the coefficients recursively modulo powers of its linear factor, using invertibility of twice the chosen square root. Combine the local polynomials by the Chinese remainder theorem. The resulting $T^{1/2}$ is self-adjoint and commutes with the relative matrix, and congruence by it identifies both pairs of forms.

We construct (2) for any data satisfying (1). Matching the prescribed $c_j,\mu_j$, together with the just-proved bound of one long block per direction, will match all elementary divisors. A non-axis direction is written as $L_j=b_j u+v=0$, where $b_j\ne0$. To allocate a group of size $m_j$ to it, take $p_i=b_j q_i$ with each $q_i\ne0$. Its contribution to the rational sum of (3) is $r_j/L_j$, with $r_j=b_j\sum q_i$. Over an infinite field, a group of size at least two can realize any sum of nonzero entries. Groups of size one need nonzero sum. An axis group at $v=0$ uses $p_i=0$, $q_i\ne0$, and one at $u=0$ uses $q_i=0$, $p_i\ne0$. They contribute no rational term.

**Case $R\le B+2$.** Choose a direction with corank at least two as the anchor $v=0$ if one exists, otherwise any eigen-direction. Choose the other axis outside the spectrum. Write $c_*$ for the anchor corank. Give its group size $c_*-1$. For each non-anchor direction with $c_j\ge2$, allocate a group of size $c_j+1$, nonzero residue $r_j$ when $\mu_j=c_j$, and zero residue when $\mu_j>c_j$. Fill the remaining dimension with singleton dummy groups at distinct non-axis directions away from the spectrum. The number of singleton groups is $B+2-R$ if $R>0$, and $e$ if $R=0$.

Call a non-axis group active if its residue is to be nonzero. There is at least one active group. If there are no dummy groups and no active groups, $R>0$ and $B=R-2$, but all $R-1$ non-anchor repeated directions would have $\mu_j-c_j\ge1$, contradicting the definition of $B$. Let $g$ be the number of active groups. Allocate a binary form $G$ to the determinant multiplicities still missing, namely $\mu_*-c_*$ at the anchor, $\mu_j-c_j-1$ at each zero-residue group, and $\mu_j$ at each non-anchor corank-one direction. These multiplicities are nonnegative and sum to $g-1$. One sees this also by subtracting the fixed factors $v^{c_*}$ and the group contributions of degrees $m_j-1$ for active groups and $m_j$ for zero-residue groups from the total degree $e$. Normalize $G(0,1)=1$.

The expansion $G=\sum_j r_j\prod_{i\ne j}L_i$ on active groups is unique by evaluation at their distinct directions. All these $r_j$ are nonzero, since $G$ has no root at an active group. Choose the corresponding group entries as above. Then $P=\sum_j r_j=1$. Choose $Q\ne1$, using freedom in the anchor entries if that group exists. If there is no anchor group, there were no repeated directions, all groups are dummy groups, and $Q=G(1,0)/\prod_j b_j$. Choose the dummy directions so $Q\ne1$. Thus $a=0$, $b\ne0$, and $h=bv$. Formula (3) now gives exactly the prescribed determinant including $G$. The axis corank is $c_*$ and each other repeated group has corank $c_j$ by the preceding kernel calculation. The remaining roots avoid the groups and have corank one.

**Case $R=B+3$.** Choose three directions of corank at least two as the two axes and the exceptional direction $L_*=b_*u+v=0$. Give each of these three a group of size $c_j$. For each remaining direction with $c_j\ge2$, assign size $c_j+1$, with nonzero residue if $\mu_j=c_j$ and zero residue otherwise. This uses exactly $e$ coordinates. Fix nonzero $a,b$ with $h=b L_*$ and set the desired sums $P=1-a$ and $Q=1-b$. Let the active groups now mean the nonexceptional non-axis groups with nonzero residue. Choose a binary form $G$ with the missing multiplicities. These are $\mu_j-c_j$ at the three designated directions, $\mu_j-c_j-1$ at zero-residue groups, and $\mu_j$ at corank-one directions. Its degree equals the number of active groups. The expansion
\[
 G=C\prod_j L_j+h\sum_j r_j\prod_{i\ne j}L_i
\]
is unique on those active groups, using separately the roots of $h$ and $L_j$. The coefficients $r_j$ are nonzero. Choose the scalar of $G$ so that $C\ne ab$, and set $r_*=(C-ab)/b$, which is nonzero. This works also if $C=0$ because of a root of $G$ at the exceptional direction. The exceptional group contributes $h r_*/L_*=b r_*$ in (3). Give every non-axis group its assigned residue, and use freedom in the axis groups to prescribe $P,Q$, since those groups both have size at least two. Formula (3) gives the desired determinant. The three designated coranks equal their group sizes, the other repeated coranks are one less than their group sizes, and the remaining roots have corank one.

In both cases the determinant is nonzero as a polynomial. The constructed pencil has the given elementary divisors and therefore is congruent to the target. Its two forms are independent, since the given two multiplication forms are independent. Thus the extra points $p,q$ are distinct from each other and from the affine basis, because a duplicate would make their forms equal or one zero. Their degree-two evaluations span the two additional dimensions by independence of their forms, so these points give a point presentation. This proves sufficiency of (1) and the exact order-two criterion.

## 4. A constructive upper bound for every regular pencil

Choose target basis $z,w$ of $A_2$ so that the coefficient form of $z$ is nonsingular. By the congruence argument of Section 3, a block of length $m$ with relative eigenvalue $\lambda$ may be written on $k[X]/(X^m)$ with pair products into the degree-two space given by
\[
 (f,g)\longmapsto \,z\, [X^{m-1}](fg)+w\,[X^{m-1}]((\lambda+X)fg).
\]
The first form is antidiagonal and nonsingular, and the relative matrix is multiplication by $\lambda+X$, a single Jordan block. This fixes the target blocks.

For each block choose $m$ distinct scalars $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in k$ and use the free algebra $C_m=V[X]/\prod_j(X-ta_j)$. It evaluates into $m$ private branches of the split coordinate algebra. Let $J_m(f)$ be the vector in $Vz+Vw$ defined by the same two coefficient extractions from the representative of $f$ in $C_m$. The two remaining top basis vectors are realized by indicators of two of three common base branches. Under this identification use the lift $X_m(f)$ with private values $t\operatorname{ev}(f)$ on this block and common base values $-t^2J_m(f)$, and zero on other private blocks.

Take the $V$-span of the unit, the lifts $X_m(f)$ of the power basis for each block, and the two base indicators scaled by $t^3$. These independent vectors form a full-rank lattice, by their private evaluation matrices and the third base branch supporting only the constant vector. Products within a block satisfy
\[
 X_m(f)X_m(g)=t X_m(fg)+t^3 J_m(fg)+t^4 J_m(f)J_m(g),
]
where the last two terms are supported on the two base branches. Thus this specializes to the desired multiplication on the degree-one block. Different blocks have products supported on the base branches with entries divisible by $t^4$, and all products involving a top basis vector and another positive-degree basis vector likewise vanish in the special fiber. The lattice is therefore closed and has the desired special fiber.

For a private block of size $m$, its evaluation matrix in the power basis is a constant Vandermonde matrix times $\operatorname{diag}(1,t,\ldots,t^{m-1})$. To represent arbitrary coordinate data divisible by $t^d$, first subtract $t^d$ times the appropriate scalar multiple of the unit, clearing the third base branch. On a private block, solve for the coefficients of the lift using the inverse of $t$ times this matrix. The coefficients have valuation at least $d-m$, so their base corrections have valuation at least $d-m+2$. For $d\ge\max(3,1+\ell)$ the corrections are absorbed by the top basis vectors, proving the conductor upper bound.

## 5. Exact additivity and a seven-dimensional family

Suppose all Jordan blocks have length at most two. The order is then exactly two or three as determined by (1). An order-three factor is quadratic and its degree-two relation space is spanned by products of annihilating pairs. To verify this, use the block basis of Section 4. Each singleton $y_i$ contributes $y_i^2$ with a nonzero output. A block of size two has basis $x_i,y_i$ with $x_i^2=0$, $x_i y_i=z+\lambda_i w$, and $y_i^2=w$. Thus after discarding the zero cross-block terms and the squares $x_i^2$, any relation is represented by a sum of $L_i y_i$ with $L_i$ linear in its block. It differs from the single vanishing product $(\sum_i L_i)(\sum_i y_i)$ only by cross-block terms.

For an order-three factor with at least two size-two blocks, the outputs of any block can be expressed using the other blocks, since every size-two block contributes the entire degree-two space. If the order-three factor has at most one size-two block, then only the numerical condition in (1) can fail, which gives at least four repeated directions. The other directions then supply outputs spanning the degree-two space after removing any one block. In both cases all cubic monomials already vanish in the quadratic presentation. For a cubic supported in one block, express one of its degree-two factors by outputs of other blocks and use the cross-block zero relations. Thus the quadratic presentation holds. Also $e\ge4$ for any order-three factor, so the annihilator of any degree-one element within that factor has dimension at least $e-2\ge2$.

Let $L$ be any split smoothing of a tensor product of such factors and give its special fiber $A$ the filtration $F$ of Section 1. For an order-three factor $A_i$, write $W_i=(A_i)_1$. We claim that $F^3A\cap (A_i)_2\ne0$. If the intersection were zero, levelness of the factor would give $F^2A\cap W_i=0$. Choose linear lifts of $W_i$ to $L\cap tV^n$ and form their first coordinate jets as a projected point set. For $xy=0$ in $W_i$, the residue of $XY/t$ has degree-one part in $W_i$. Indeed choose $z\in W_i$ with $yz=0$ independent of $x$, and use $(XY/t)Z=X(YZ/t)$. The cross component in $W_i\otimes W_j$ for $j\ne i$ equates $z\otimes u_j$ with $x\otimes v_j$, forcing $u_j=0$. Thus for every quadratic relation $f$, the residue of $f(X)/t$ lies in $W_i+\mathfrak m_A^2$. Subtract a lift of the degree-one part and a lift in $t^2V^n$ of the rest. The difference belongs to $tL$, so the first jets show that $f$ evaluates on the projected points as an affine-linear function. This gives a graded surjection from $A_i$ onto the degree-associated graded of those points, since $A_i$ is quadratic.

If a homogeneous form $f$ of degree $j=1$ or $2$ evaluates to a lower-degree polynomial $\sum_{h<j}g_h$ there, then $f(X)-\sum_{h<j}t^{j-h}g_h(X)$ is a lift of the same class with coordinate valuation at least $j+1$. The two assumed zero intersections force its class to vanish. Thus the surjection is an isomorphism, contradicting the order-three factor's lack of a point presentation. This proves the claim.

Choose a nonzero class in $F^3A\cap(A_i)_2$ for every order-three factor, and any nonzero degree-two class for the other factors. Their product is a nonzero simple tensor with filtration index at least the sum of the individual orders. The upper bound is attained by tensoring the individual lattices. Therefore the product order is exactly the sum.

For a concrete example, let $A$ have basis $1,x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2,z,w$ with $x_i,y_i$ in degree one and $z,w$ in degree two. Its nonzero radical products are $x_i y_i=y_i x_i=z$ and $y_i^2=w$. Its pencil has two size-two Jordan blocks at the same rational direction over every field. The derivative obstruction in Section 2 works over any field at a rational direction. The kernel and determinant computations use no division by two, and the derivative matrix on the kernel for this example is identically zero on a two-dimensional space. Thus its order is greater than two over any field, including $\mathbb F_2$. Its indicated blocks already have the canonical form used in Section 4, so the upper construction applies directly with the two distinct constants $0,1$. The tensor-product lower bound above uses only the quadratic relations, the annihilator dimensions, and the nonexistence of order two, all of which hold over the same field. Hence over every field, its $r$-th tensor power has dimension $7^r$, Loewy degree $2r$, and diagonal-source order $3r$. The explicit split arc and the full-rank unit flattening give border rank exactly $7^r$.

The proofs are informal.
