# Uniform coefficient access and exact pole optimization for thin tensor degenerations

The input consists of explicitly listed \(0/1\) multiplication tables of two admissible thin Peirce profiles of a common type. The type has a finite ordered set of vertices, one supported line per indicated forward corner, and the same corner degrees at both endpoints. The strict support generates the total reachability order. The basis of each radical power is specified by the corner degrees. Let \(S\) and \(T\) be the respective sets of retained strict corner products. This includes the sparse constructions that realize integer contact lattices by marked flag deletions. [Thin profiles and cap extraction](OptimalCatalystSeed.md) [Contact lattice realization](ContactSeed.md)

**Theorem.** For these explicitly represented pairs, over every coefficient field:

1. Feasibility without a pole bound is decidable in deterministic polynomial bit time. Whenever feasible, a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm produces a monomial diagonal Laurent witness whose exponents have \(O(N)\) bits, where \(N\) is the number of corner and vertex variables in the cap formulation below. Thus the relative trilinear-form degeneration is p-definable in the sense of Grochow, Mulmuley and Qiao, even when its minimum order is exponential. For polynomial-time generated explicit input families, this gives polynomial-time uniform generation of a coefficient circuit.
2. Feasibility at a fixed absolute pole cap sum is NP-complete, even for pairs whose radicals cube to zero and which admit a uniform constant-order witness. An exact optimum and an optimal exponent table can be computed in \(\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}\). The optimization function is complete for that class under polynomial-time Turing reductions. An optimal witness still has a polynomial-size coefficient circuit.
3. With a fixed number of integral cochain coordinates after the tree gauge and retained equations, exact optimization and coefficient-circuit generation are deterministic polynomial-time operations. The same holds when some exact contact values are part of the input, with the fixed bound applied after imposing those equations.

## 1. The integral cap system and a polynomial-bit witness

The earlier thin cap extraction tests arbitrary independent forward Laurent maps by the following integral system. If \(T\not\subseteq S\), the comparison is impossible. Otherwise let \(w(x,y)\) be the corner weight, including \(w(x,x)=0\), with vertex variables \(\alpha_x,\beta_x\). For each source nonzero strict product the defect is
\[
 \delta(x,y,z)=w(x,z)-w(x,y)-w(y,z).
\]
Require \(\delta=0\) on \(T\) and \(\delta\ge1\) on \(S\setminus T\). Redistribute the modes by scalar powers of \(t\) whose exponents sum to zero to put the requested caps in the form \((0,0,p)\). The cap inequalities on every corner are
\[
 w(x,y)\le\alpha_x+\beta_x-\beta_y,\qquad
 w(x,y)\le\beta_x,\qquad
 w(x,y)\ge\alpha_x+\beta_x-p .
\tag{1}
\]
On the diagonal they give \(\alpha_x,\beta_x\ge0\) and \(\alpha_x+\beta_x\le p\). Thus every weight is in \([-p,p]\). Conversely these variables construct the three diagonal maps for each corner, with exponents in bilinear-map notation
\[
 a_{xy}=\alpha_x+\beta_x-\beta_y-w(x,y),\quad
 b_{xy}=\beta_x-w(x,y),\quad
 c_{xy}=w(x,y)-\alpha_x-\beta_x .
\tag{2}
\]
Their substitution multiplies the retained coefficients by \(1\) and the other nonzero source coefficients by \(t^\delta\). This is the exact integer witness criterion, including lower bounds for completely changing frames. A polynomial presentation of order \(p\) gives a Laurent witness with cap \((0,0,p)\) by dividing the output by \(t^p\). For the reverse implication multiply the diagonal output in (2) by \(t^p\).

When no cap is prescribed, minimize \(p\) in the rational relaxation of (1) and the defect equations. Feasibility of this rational LP implies integer feasibility since scaling by a common denominator preserves all constraints and increases the positive defects. The minimum, when finite, is attained and has a vertex solution. Indeed the variables other than \(p\) are bounded by \(p\), so any nonempty bounded-cap truncation is compact, and the minimum face is bounded. A deterministic polynomial-bit-time LP algorithm can return an optimal vertex, for example by successive coordinate minimization on the optimal face.

At such a vertex use an independent square system of active rows. All rows have at most four nonzero entries from \(\{-1,1\}\) after merging diagonal constraints, and their right sides are \(0,\pm1\). Hadamard's inequality bounds the absolute determinant by \(2^N\) and every determinant with a column replaced by the right side by \(5^{N/2}\). Here \(N\) counts \(p\), the off-diagonal corner variables, and the two vertex variables per vertex. Multiplying by the absolute active determinant gives an integral witness with \(p\le5^{N/2}\). At the *integer* optimum one can therefore also choose all corner and vertex weights with \(O(N)\) bits, since they are bounded in absolute value by its cap. In (2), \(a_{xy},b_{xy}\) lie in \([0,2p]\) and \(c_{xy}\) lies in \([-p,0]\). This follows directly from (1) and the diagonal bounds. It proves the same bit bound for the three Laurent exponent tables.

For a given binary \(p\), (1) and the defects give an NP certificate with polynomially many bits. To optimize \(p\), first decide unbounded feasibility, then binary search below the exponential bound with this NP predicate. At the optimum fix a binary encoding of the bounded variables and determine its lexicographically first feasible bit string by a prefix extension query for each bit. Every prefix query is in NP. This actually outputs the optimal integer cap and the corresponding exponent table in \(\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}\).

## 2. Coefficient circuits and their uniformity

Write the multiplication tensor as its trilinear form in three labelled variable blocks. The two input maps and the transpose of the output map give one block-diagonal linear substitution. Each row of this substitution has its single nonzero entry \(t^e\). The coefficient of \(t^k\) in a requested entry is therefore the predicate that the row and column are the same diagonal position and that \(k=e\) in that row, with zero on any affine-constant or off-block coordinate. The signed exponent, row, and column indices all have polynomial bit length. A polynomial-size circuit computes this predicate by selecting a row of the exponent table and comparing its bits. Boolean gates on \(0,1\) implement these operations over any field, for example AND by multiplication and NOT by subtraction from \(1\). This is the coefficient-access circuit in the definition of p-definable one-parameter degeneration. Both the polynomial-time LP output and the optimal output from the oracle algorithm give such circuits. The latter need no oracle once their exponent table is supplied. [Grochow–Mulmuley–Qiao, Section 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.02815)

Repeated squaring also computes a specified positive monomial exponent by \(O(\log(2+|e|))\) multiplications, or a negative exponent by working with \(t^{-1}\) as a second input. This is an arithmetic size assertion with exponential parameter degree allowed. The p-definability assertion is about the relative degeneration between the two given trilinear forms, with polynomially many variables in the family index. In particular the result supplies uniform coefficients even when optimizing the exponent table from an input pair is expensive.


## 3. Plain cap feasibility is NP-complete

The earlier contact construction reduces NAE-3SAT to a sparse marked flag complex with output generator loops \(y_i\), each a three-edge circle. Its integral cocycles realize positive marked contacts \(c_i\), and a satisfying assignment exists exactly when all \(c_i\) can be at most \(2\). Both yes and no instances admit some positive contact vector. To recall the arithmetic, use variables \(z,x_j\) and rows \(z,2z,x_j\). Testing that every row lies in \(\{1,2\}\) forces \(z=1\) and gives a Boolean assignment by \(x_j-1\). The literal rows in a clause are \(x_j\) for a positive occurrence and \(3z-x_j\) for a negative occurrence. Add the row given by their sum minus \(3z\). Its membership in \(\{1,2\}\) is exactly the NAE clause. Unrestricted positivity is witnessed by \(z=2\) and all \(x_j=3\). The realization uses a punctured cap on each \(y_i\), with orientation chosen so its marked contact is its signed circulation. [Contact lattices, Section 3](ContactSeed.md) [Schaefer](https://doi.org/10.1145/800133.804350)

We convert the simultaneous bound on contacts into one cap sum. In these NAE instances the core cochain has a uniform constant bound, as follows. Use scalar multiples by \(2,3\), signed sums, and optional sign changes in the explicit generator definitions of the contact realization. Expand a clause row as the sum of its three literal expressions minus \(3z\). Its total absolute coefficient sum before collecting equal variables is at most \(15\). Each arithmetic subexpression can be formed by accumulating terms and doublings with the same bound. This gives a presentation with at most three generator letters per relation and all intermediate generator evaluations of magnitude at most \(45\), both on a yes assignment and on the unrestricted assignment \(z=2,x_j=3\).

Assign each generator's circulation to a single oriented edge of its bouquet triangle and zero to the others. A relation walk consists of at most nine edges and has total zero evaluation. At its \(i\)-th visit to a bottom vertex, let \(h_i\) be the accumulated evaluation from the start, so \(|h_i|\le405\) and the final value is again zero. In the attaching strip give its fresh top vertex \(d_i\) value \(h_i\) and use the lift \(h_i\) for the \(i\)-th bottom visit. The two triangles of each strip sector inherit weights by taking differences. This is consistent when a bottom edge is encountered several times, because the difference of its two visit values is its prescribed signed edge weight. Give the cone apex of the top polygon value zero. All new edges now have magnitude at most \(405\), and the retained triangles have defect zero. In the barycentric subdivision choose one original vertex in each simplex. The corner weight of a nesting is the difference of these chosen vertex values in a local simplex potential. On overlaps the potentials differ by an additive constant. Each new weight still has magnitude at most \(405\), since the two chosen vertices lie in one original simplex.

On the punctured output caps use the explicit unit-contact cochain supported on three corners of the cap, scaled by the chosen \(c_i\). Orient its boundary to match the signed output circulation. Its weights have magnitude at most \(4\) for both assignments above. Its boundary differs from the already defined six-edge cochain by a coboundary with vertex potential of magnitude at most \(6(405+4)=2454\). Extend that potential by zero to the cap interior. After the correction the resulting cochain is bounded by \(4+2\cdot2454<5000\). Thus take \(M=5000\) for the whole core, before ordering corners, both on yes assignments and on the known unrestricted assignment. The construction is effective once the assignment is given, and its uniform bound is independent of the formula. [Explicit cap cochain](ExactSparseOrders.md)

Attach an \(L_0\)-block Möbius-band and annulus telescope at each \(y_i\), with its base core glued to \(y_i\) and all other pieces disjoint. The marked caps remain in place. Put \(K=2^{L_0}\). This extends the core without new marked faces and multiplies each output circulation by \(K\) on its new terminal circle. Take the barycentric flag table, with all strict products given by flags in the source and the prescribed marks deleted in the target, and add any missing successor corners using the usual rank ordering. Each strict product of three edges is zero. Each rank-two output of a deleted flag is retained through the other rank-one intermediate simplex, as in the core construction. The endpoints thus have the same graded radical powers and lie in the promised admissible class. Adding a telescope retracting to its input circle leaves the feasible marked contact vectors unchanged. [Möbius telescope](https://doi.org/10.1007/s41468-018-0021-5)

For any Laurent witness, the base marked contact \(c_i\) forces terminal circulation \(\lvert Kc_i\rvert\). Traversing the six incidences of the subdivided terminal circle in the appropriate direction gives a positive-reward layered cycle with three triples of steps. At each pair of forward and backward incidences insert an identity to complete a triple. The difference constraints for (1) then give
\[
 p\ \ge\ Kc_i/3 .
\tag{3}
\]
In a no instance some \(c_i\ge3\), hence \(p\ge K\).

In a yes instance take a core cochain bounded by \(M\) with each \(c_i\in\{1,2\}\). On each new telescope use the compatible-angle construction with boundary circumference \(c_i\). Its normalized corner weights are in \([-Kc_i/3,0]\). For completeness, take lifts proportional to the horizontal coordinate on each rectangular grid, and match the new annular vertices \(a_j\) to the old boundary vertices \(b_{2j}\). The angle changes double across each block and a triangle lift spans at most the terminal circumference divided by three. Taking the minimum lift value on each simplex gives the normalized weights on barycentric inclusions. The minima are in \(\tfrac13\mathbb Z\) and give bounded residues \(u_x\in[0,1)\) such that the normalized weight is an integer plus \(u_y-u_x\). On the starting circle the normalized weights have magnitude at most \(c_i/3\). The residues differ by at most \(2/3\), so the integer weights have magnitude at most \(2\) for \(c_i\le4\). [Compatible angle construction](ExactSparseOrders.md)

The difference between the core and telescope integer cochains on the shared six-edge circle has zero circulation. Integrate it there from a chosen vertex to obtain an integer coboundary with potential bounded by \(6(M+2)\) in absolute value. Extend this potential by zero off the boundary and add its coboundary to the telescope integer cochain. The cochains now agree exactly on the overlap, with all defects unchanged. To choose a rational normalization on the whole graph, use zero gauge on the core and the angle residues on the nonboundary telescope vertices. The change from each original normalized angle weight is bounded by \(12(M+2)+2\). Therefore, with \(\Delta=12(M+2)+3\), all normalized weights lie in \([-2K/3-\Delta,\Delta]\). On any added successor corner choose an integer cochain value so that its normalized weight lies in \([-1,0]\), using that coboundary's fractional part; these corners participate in no strict products.

For the normalized weights use \(\alpha_x=\beta_x=\Delta\) at every vertex. Then (1) holds with rational cap \(2K/3+3\Delta\). To recover integer weights and vertex variables at the ceiling of this cap, write the normalized weights as an integer cochain \(w_0\) plus the rational vertex coboundary \(u_y-u_x\). With \(C_x=u_x,\ A_x=u_x+\beta_x,\ B_x=u_x+\alpha_x+\beta_x\), the inequalities become
\[
 A_y-B_x\le-w_0(x,y),\quad
 C_y-A_x\le-w_0(x,y),\quad
 B_x-C_y\le p+w_0(x,y).
\tag{4}
\]
At the integer ceiling, these are difference constraints with integer costs and no negative cycle. Shortest path distances from a new vertex having zero-cost edges to all others give integer potentials. They reconstruct a valid integer gauge and isotope weights at that cap.

Here \(\Delta=60027\), so the fixed choice \(K=2^{20}\) satisfies \(K>9\Delta+3\) and the yes cap is at most \(K-1\). The construction is polynomial time with twenty new blocks per output. The same upper-bound construction applied to the known unrestricted assignment has \(c_i\le4\); replacing \(2K/3\) by \(4K/3\) in the weight interval gives an integer cap at most \(\lceil4K/3+3\Delta\rceil=1578183\) for every constructed pair. This explicit fallback has normalized image degree at most \(4\). Thus the reduction proves NP-completeness at the fixed threshold \(K-1=1048575\), even with the constant-order promise. It also proves NP-completeness of the binary-cap problem. Comparing an exact optimum to the specified bound decides this NP-complete problem. Hence computing the exact optimal cap, alone or with the optimal exponent table, is \(\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}\)-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions. For any fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) one may increase \(L_0\) so the yes cap is at most \((2/3+\varepsilon)K\). In particular a polynomial-time approximation with any fixed ratio strictly below \(3/2\) would give a polynomial-time algorithm for NAE-3SAT.

## 4. A fixed-dimensional integral parameter

Normalize each integer cochain by an integer vertex coboundary to make its values zero on a fixed spanning tree in the undirected support graph. The remaining corner coordinates determine the gauge class uniquely. Impose the retained-product equations, and also any prescribed equalities for exact marked contacts. Compute an integral particular solution and kernel basis for these equalities by Smith form. If there is no integral solution, the instance is infeasible. Otherwise the tree-normalized cochains satisfying the equalities are precisely \(w_0=v+Lz\) with \(z\in\mathbb Z^d\), where \(d\) is the kernel rank. All remaining contact lower or upper bounds are linear inequalities in \(z\).

For an integer trial cap \(p\), substitute \(v+Lz\) into (4) and treat its layer potentials as continuous variables. This is a mixed integer linear system with only \(d\) integer variables. For fixed \(z\), feasibility of the potentials over the rationals is equivalent to integral feasibility at this integer \(p\), by the shortest-path argument. Thus an exact decision algorithm is polynomial in the explicit input bit length for fixed \(d\), by fixed-integer-dimension mixed integer programming. Recover an integer witness by computing those distances. Search over caps using the bound in Section 1 in the homogeneous case. With prescribed binary contact equalities or inequalities, the standard small-solution bound for integer linear systems provides a computable polynomial-bit cap bound whenever feasible. It applies by splitting signed variables into positive and negative parts and adding slack variables. [Lenstra, Section 5](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1983i/art.pdf) [Papadimitriou](https://publications.csail.mit.edu/lcs/pubs/pdf/MIT-LCS-TM-152.pdf)

The same algorithm computes the exact fractional base slope. For a fixed representative \(w_0\), it is the maximum of the layered cycle rewards divided by one third of the cycle length. A simple cycle uses at most the number of base vertices in each layer, so an attained optimum over integer cochains has reduced denominator at most that number. Attainment follows by restricting to cochains whose slope is bounded by a feasible integer cap, since such cochains can be integer-gauged into the bounded box from Section 1. Use (4) with rational trial \(p\) for a slope test, and binary-search numerators for each allowed denominator. Integer presentation is then recovered at the ceiling, and the resulting exponent table gives the uniform coefficient circuit.

The algorithmic distinction is between generating a compact witness and selecting its smallest cap. The former is polynomial for all feasible explicit thin profiles. Optimal selection is NP-hard even in the marked sparse subclass, while the selected witness always has a small coefficient-access circuit. No circuit lower bound follows from its parameter degree. These are written proofs without Lean kernel verification.
