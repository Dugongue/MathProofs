# Sparse cubic forms with exponential Laurent complexity

Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic different from two. A Laurent presentation of a cubic orbit arc means one invertible linear substitution over \(K=k((t))\), with the resulting cubic having coefficients in \(R=k[[t]]\). The image degree of a polynomial arc is the greatest \(t\)-degree of a coefficient of the cubic. Symmetric pole depth bounds the poles of the substitution matrix and its inverse.

**Theorem.** There are explicit cubic forms \(F_m^+,F_m^-\) on \(M=O(m)\) variables with \(O(M)\) nonzero monomials and a direction \(E_m\) supported on a bounded number of monomials, such that:

1. For any extension field and nonzero \(c\), the form \(F_m^-+cE_m\) is linearly equivalent to \(F_m^+\) exactly when \(c\) is a \(2^{m-1}\)-st power in that field. The geometric degree-one arc with \(c=t\) lifts over \(k((t^{1/e}))\) exactly when \(2^{m-1}\mid e\).
2. Every polynomial image arc with a Laurent presentation over \(k((t))\) has degree at least \(2^{m-1}/O(M^8)\). There is such an arc of degree \(2^{m-1}\).
3. The least symmetric pole depth is \(\Theta(2^m)\).

The cubic construction allows arbitrary linear substitutions in all its variables. Its recovery uses the kernels of three Hessian slices and the adjoint algebra of a symmetric bilinear map. It works in characteristic three using polarization by finite differences.

## 1. A labeled symmetric algebra that retains the marked products

Use the terminal-cap algebra from [the contact proof](ContactSeed.md) and [the sparse seed](SparseSeed.md). Denote the table by \(A_a\) when the coefficient on its single marked flag is \(a\). The mark lies at a flag consisting of the cap apex, a spoke and an incident cap face. Its oriented boundary in the punctured complex is \(2^{m-1}\) times a primitive initial circle, up to sign. The old vertex idempotents have a fixed total reachability order, with one-dimensional strict corners. Apart from endpoint actions the only nonzero products of radical basis vectors are the flag products \(p_{ij}p_{j\ell}=a_{ij\ell}p_{i\ell}\), all coefficients \(1\) except for \(a\) on the mark.

Add a private root \(o\) and a private path \(c_1,\ldots,c_v\) for the \(v\) old vertices. Place the root and path before the old vertices. Give \(o\to c_1\) corner dimension \(4\), \(o\to c_i\) for \(i>1\) dimension \(2\), and \(o\to i\) for old vertices \(i\) dimension \(3\). Add one-dimensional arrows \(c_i\to c_{i+1}\) and \(c_i\to i\). Set every product involving a new arrow and a radical vector to zero. We may also add a square-zero loop at a vertex, with its products with every radical vector zero, as described below. The added edges preserve associativity and add only linearly many entries.

Let \(C_a=A_a\ltimes A_a^*\) be the trivial extension with product
\[
 (x,f)(y,g)=(xy,xg+fy),\qquad (xgy)(z)=g(yzx).
\]
Its functional \(\tau(x,f)=f(1)\) gives the perfect symmetric pairing \(\tau((x,f)(y,g))=f(y)+g(x)\). Its radical is \(J_C=J_A\oplus A_a^*\), since this ideal is nilpotent and the quotient is the split algebra of vertex idempotents. Products with two dual elements are zero. The tensor of the multiplication still has only linearly many entries: each nonzero basis product in \(A_a\) contributes one original entry and two transposed module-action entries.

For each vertex \(i\), the dimension of \(e_i C_a\) is the dimension of \(A_a e_i\) plus the dimension of \(e_i A_a\). If this integer is zero in \(k\), add one of the square-zero loops at \(i\). This raises the dimension by two. Hence every resulting left dimension is a unit of \(k\). These choices do not depend on \(a\). The trace pairing \(\operatorname{Tr}(L_xL_y)\) of the regular representation of \(C_a\) has kernel \(J_C\): it vanishes if one input is radical, since their product is nilpotent, and it induces a diagonal pairing on the split quotient with these nonzero left dimensions. The same holds for \(C_a^{\rm op}\), since the left and right dimensions agree for the trivial extension.

The dimensions of the based radical powers of \(C_a\) are constant between \(a=1\) and \(a=0\). To verify this, the original summand contributes \(J_A^h\) to \(J_C^h\), and the dual summand contributes \(\sum_{i+j=h-1}J_A^i A_a^* J_A^j\). In degree \(h=2\) this dual sum includes the duals of vertices incident to a radical arrow, and the duals of strict edges that occur as an input in some nonzero radical product. Added loops have zero radical products, and their duals do not occur in this sum. For \(h=3\) the dual sum consists exactly of the duals of vertices appearing in a nonzero flag product. Higher powers vanish. These descriptions follow by evaluating a product of a dual basis vector and original radical basis vectors on an original basis vector. For a marked cap flag, the apex-to-spoke edge has another incident cap triangle, and the spoke-to-face edge can use the other endpoint of the spoke. The output edge survives through the other rank-one intermediate vertex. Every vertex of the marked flag also appears in a retained one. Thus deleting the mark changes none of the lists.

The off-diagonal corners of \(C_a\) occur in both directions and have equal dimensions in the two directions. They identify the root as the unique vertex incident to corners of dimensions \(2,3,4\). The dimensions distinguish the private path and its endpoint from the old vertices, and the path and its matching edges identify every old vertex. This labeling also works in \(C_a^{\rm op}\). The two orientations of the labeled algebra are distinguishable by graded dimensions: a retained flag chord is an original degree-two edge in its forward direction, while its reverse is the dual of an original degree-two edge and has degree one. There is at least one retained flag at both endpoints. Therefore, in a product \(C_a^{\rm op}\times C_a\), the original-order component can be recognized by dimensions of labeled positive-degree corners. The label arguments are independent of the loop choices, which affect only diagonal corners.

## 2. The cubic construction and a rational recovery of its subspace

Set \(B=M_2(C_a)\). Its left and right argument variables are a pair \((X,Y)\in B\oplus B=\mathcal X\). Write \(\mathcal Y=(B^4)^*\), using coordinates \(Z=(Z_{ij})_{1\le i,j\le2}\). Use the cubic
\[
 F_a(X,Y,Z)=\sum_{i,j} Z_{ij}(X E_{ij}Y).
\]
Here \(E_{ij}\) are constant matrix units. The variable count is \(24\dim C_a\), and the multiplication sparsity proved above gives linearly many cubic monomials. The coefficients vary affinely in \(a\), with only a bounded number depending on \(a\). Put \(F_m^+=F_1\), \(F_m^-=F_0\). Constant matrix amplification makes the number of monomials depending on \(a\) independent of \(m\).

For a cubic \(F\) use its full three-variable polarization, the sum over the subsets of three arguments of \(F\) on their sum with inclusion-exclusion signs. This is symmetric trilinear and equivariant under linear substitutions, including in characteristic three. At a point \(v\) its contraction with \(v\) is called a Hessian slice. For the written \(F_a\), its Hessian slice at \((X,Y,Z)\) on \(\mathcal X\oplus\mathcal Y\) has the block form
\[
 \begin{pmatrix} A_Z&C_{X,Y}^{\mathsf T}\\ C_{X,Y}&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 C_{X,Y}(u,v)=(uE_{ij}Y+XE_{ij}v)_{ij}.
\]
When \(X,Y\) are units, the kernel of \(C_{X,Y}\) consists exactly of \((Xa,-aY)\) where \(a\) commutes with all constant matrix units, so \(a\in I_2\otimes C_a\). This follows by adding the identities for the two diagonal matrix units and then applying the off-diagonal identities. The rank is \(\dim\mathcal X-\dim C_a\), and this is the global maximum over all \(X,Y\): the unit locus is dense over an algebraic closure, so higher minors vanish identically.

For a symmetric block matrix of the displayed form with \(\operatorname{rank} C=q\), its rank is \(2q+\operatorname{rank}(A_Z|_{\ker C})\), by choosing bases for \(\ker C\) and a complement and eliminating the invertible mixed blocks. Hence the Hessian rank is at most \(\dim\mathcal X+q_{\max}\). At equality its kernel lies in \(\mathcal Y\) and equals the annihilator of \(\operatorname{im} C\).

Choose any three linearly independent invertible matrices \(T_1,T_2,T_3\) in \(M_2(k)\), for example \(I_2,I_2+E_{12},I_2+E_{21}\). Set \(X=Y=T_i\otimes1\), and choose \(Z\) only in the \(11\) output slot, taking that functional to be
\[
 b\longmapsto \tau\big((X^{-1} b Y^{-1})_{11}\big).
\]
Then \(A_Z|_{\ker C}\), in the coordinates \(a\in C_a\) above, is \((a,b)\mapsto-2\tau(ab)\), as is seen by expanding \((Xa)E_{11}(-bY)+(Xb)E_{11}(-aY)\). It is perfect by the hypothesis on characteristic. Thus these three fixed points give maximal-rank slices. For such \(X,Y\), view tuples indexed by \(ij\) as linear maps on \(M_2(k)\) with values in \(B\), and identify left-right multiplication with \(M_2(k)\otimes M_2(k)\) on the matrix part. The image of \(C_{X,Y}\) is
\[
 (M_2(k)\otimes kT_i+kT_i\otimes M_2(k))\otimes C_a .
\]
Indeed its two summands are all maps \(E\mapsto u E T_i\) and \(E\mapsto T_i E v\), respectively. The intersection of the three displayed subspaces is zero: extend the three independent \(T_i\) to a basis, and an entry in the tensor-product basis cannot have at least one coordinate equal to each of three different indices. Therefore the three Hessian kernels span \(\mathcal Y\). This description is invariant under linear substitutions: every maximal-rank Hessian has kernel in \(\mathcal Y\), and some three span it.

Now start with an arbitrary Laurent witness with special form \(F_0\). Evaluate the Hessian slices at the same three fixed special points just constructed. Their ranks remain maximal over \(K\) since an appropriate minor is a unit at \(0\) and the generic form is equivalent to \(F_1\). Each kernel has a free integral frame from unit-pivot elimination. The sum of the kernels has the full written special rank, and has at most this rank generically since it lies in the transported generic \(\mathcal Y\). Thus it is a free direct summand, realizing that transported \(\mathcal Y\), with the indicated special fiber. Polarization vanishes whenever two arguments belong to it, as this holds generically. We obtain a symmetric bilinear map on the free quotient by this subspace, taking values in its dual. At the written endpoints it is the map
\[
 b((x,y),(u,v))=(xE_{ij}v+uE_{ij}y)_{ij}.
\]
This recovery also applies to exact field equivalences of the line forms.

## 3. Intrinsic recovery of the contact and its pole cost

Use the adjoint algebra of the recovered symmetric bimap \(b\): it consists of pairs \((P,Q)\) with \(b(Px,y)=b(x,Qy)\), with multiplication \((PP',Q'Q)\). This construction is defined by linear equations on the operator entries. Summing the two diagonal output components gives the simpler bimap \(xv+uy\). Its adjoint diagonal blocks have the form \(P=\operatorname{diag}(R_a,L_b)\) and \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(R_b,L_a)\), with \(R,L\) denoting right and left multiplication, apart from possible off-diagonal blocks. To check this, compare each pair of argument slots and evaluate at the unit. The off-diagonal blocks would be left or right multiplication by elements annihilating every commutator on the respective side, so vanish: \(E_{12}E_{21}-E_{21}E_{12}\) is an invertible commutator of \(B\). On returning to every output component, the diagonal blocks must in addition satisfy \(aE_{ij}=E_{ij}a\) and \(bE_{ij}=E_{ij}b\). Hence \(a,b\in I_2\otimes C_a\), and the adjoint algebra is \(C_a^{\rm op}\times C_a\). This is the symmetric version of the earlier [adjoint-block calculation](AdjointSeed.md). [Wilson on adjoint algebras](https://www.math.colostate.edu/~jwilson/papers/Central.pdf)

Along the witness the adjoint kernel has constant rank, since its generic and special dimensions agree by this formula. Unit-pivot elimination gives a free integral algebra \(D\) with the prescribed fibers. The radical is recovered as the kernel of its regular trace pairing, using the nonzero trace weights arranged above. Its powers have constant endpoint ranks by Section 1, so are split with the prescribed special fibers. The quotient by the radical \(J\) is a split integral product of copies of \(R\), by lifting the special idempotents. One can improve an approximation \(h\) by \(h-(2h-1)^{-1}(h^2-h)\), then lift the remaining idempotents in the complementary factors. The lifted idempotents act on each associated graded piece and give free graded corners with the written ranks.

The labeling and orientation rules from Section 1 now identify every old original-order edge in the integral law. On a thin old graded corner the generic isomorphism acts by a scalar \(\eta_{ij}\). For an original flag its coefficient in integral frames is \(\eta_{i\ell}/(\eta_{ij}\eta_{j\ell})\). On retained faces its valuation is zero. On the omitted face it has positive valuation. The negative valuations of the edge scalars thus define the same cocycle as in the contact proof. The marked valuation is a positive multiple of \(2^{m-1}\). In a static field comparison with all coefficients nonzero, the retained equations read \(1\), and the multiplicative cochain gives exactly a power \(c\in(k^\times)^{2^{m-1}}\). Conversely any such power is attained by a cochain, whose diagonal algebra isomorphism extends to the trivial extension, the matrix amplification and the cubic. This gives both the ramification statement and the upper degree bound.

A depth-\(b\) cubic substitution acts on the recovered quotient \(\mathcal X\) with its matrix and inverse bounded by \(b\) in integral frames. Its conjugation action on the adjoint operators has symmetric depth at most \(2b\), and the same bound carries to the split associated graded corners. Thus \(|v_t(\eta_{ij})|\le2b\) on the old edges. The terminal subdivided cycle has six traversals and circulation of magnitude at least \(2^{m-1}\), so \(12b\ge2^{m-1}\). A primitive initial integral cocycle extended through the doubling blocks and then the fixed punctured-cap pattern has all edge values \(O(2^m)\). Its diagonal map on \(A_a\) has the same bound on all the induced spaces, since taking the dual negates each weight, and constant matrix amplification just repeats weights. This proves the matching upper bound on symmetric depth.

## 4. Polynomial degree without individual eigenvectors or idempotents

In the recovered adjoint algebra let \(Q=D/J\), \(W=J/J^2\), \(U=J^2/J^3\). The trace pairing of the regular representation of \(Q\) is unimodular, since in its split idempotent basis its matrix is the identity. Write \(\theta\) for the inverse tensor of this pairing. The map
\[
 W\otimes W\longrightarrow U\otimes Q,\qquad
 x\otimes y\longmapsto\sum_{i,j}\theta_{ij}((xq_i)y)\otimes q_j
\]
is defined in any basis \(q_i\) of \(Q\). In an integral idempotent frame it tags every product by its middle vertex. Together with the two endpoint idempotents this splits the map by triples of vertices. The original marked flag gives a one-by-one block since its two input and output corners are thin. It is nonzero generically and divisible by \(t^{2^{m-1}}\). Every maximal-rank minor is divisible by the same power, by the block decomposition. Other pieces may have larger corner dimension and do not affect this conclusion.

To bound the degree of a nonzero minor, say that an array has height at most \(h\) if it has a common polynomial denominator, a unit at zero, and that denominator and its numerators have degree at most \(h\). Product heights add, and the adjugate bounds the inverse of an invertible-at-zero size-\(s\) array by \(sh\). For a constant-rank system with common denominator, unit-pivot kernel frames have height bounded by the size of the pivot times the coefficient height: clear the common denominator and solve the numerator equations. The free-coordinate entries also give a constant projection to kernel coordinates.

For a cubic image arc of degree \(d\), the three Hessian kernels have frames of height \(O(Md)\), and selecting an independent subset gives the same height for \(\mathcal Y\) and its complement, since only three denominators are involved. The recovered bimap, a contraction of polarization with these frames, has height \(O(Md)\). The adjoint kernel equations have at most \(2M^2\) unknowns. Solving them by unit minors gives frames of height \(O(M^3d)\), and multiplication of pairs read in their free coordinates has this same height bound. The trace equations for \(J\) have at most \(M\) unknowns and give a basis of height \(O(M^4d)\). Products of at most three such vectors have the same order of height and use a fixed power of a common denominator. Choose a basis adapted to the radical filtration by taking independent columns for the successive powers, supplementing with standard vectors. This basis has height \(O(M^4d)\), and its inverse costs a factor at most \(M\). Hence the multiplication in the adapted basis has height \(O(M^5d)\). The inverse trace pairing of \(Q\) and the displayed tagged map have height \(O(M^6d)\). The map has rank at most \(M^2\), so a nonzero maximal-rank minor has numerator degree \(O(M^8d)\) and denominator a unit at zero. Comparing with its divisibility proves the required image-degree lower bound.

These are written proofs. The new reduction preserves witness costs by rational recovery from a single cubic and uses no chosen generic change of basis in the height argument. It complements the exact tensor-isomorphism reductions to cubic forms with quantitative statements for a specified sparse degeneration. [Grochow–Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)
