# Realizing contact lattices in sparse tensor degenerations

Fix a field \(k\), put \(R=k[[t]]\), and put \(K=k((t))\). A relative Laurent witness from \(U\) to \(V\) is a tensor-valued arc in \(R\) with special value \(V\), whose generic point is obtained from \(U\) by three invertible mode matrices over \(K\). A polynomial image arc has entries in \(k[t]\). Its degree is the maximum degree of those entries, measured after normalizing the special value to occur in degree zero. All formats below are square on three modes.

## 1. Every integer contact lattice occurs

**Theorem.** Given an integer matrix \(B\in\mathbb Z^{q\times d}\), one can construct explicit pairs of concise \(0/1\) tensors \(U_B,V_B\), and \(q\) marked structure coefficients. Their side length and number of nonzero entries are linear in
\[
d+q+1+\sum_{B_{ij}\ne0}\log(2+|B_{ij}|).
\]
For every field, an intrinsic vector of the \(q\) contact orders can be read from each relative Laurent witness. Its possible values are exactly
\[
B\mathbb Z^d\ \cap\ \mathbb Z_{>0}^{\,q}.
\]
For each such vector \(b\), a diagonal Laurent presentation realizes the actual coordinate arc \(V_B+\sum_\ell t^{b_\ell}E_\ell\), where \(E_\ell\) has one nonzero entry at the corresponding mark. More generally, the tensor \(V_B+\sum_\ell c_\ell E_\ell\), with all \(c_\ell\in k^\times\), is in the ordinary three-mode orbit of \(U_B\) over \(k\) if and only if \(c_\ell=\prod_j u_j^{B_{\ell j}}\) for some \(u_j\in k^\times\). The latter equivalence is valid over any extension field.

### A finite simplicial model

Start with a graph that is a bouquet of three-edge circles, one per formal generator. Reserve \(d\) generators \(x_j\) and \(q\) distinct output generators \(y_\ell\). Introduce auxiliary generators, in definition order, for signed sums and doublings. For example \(z=x+x\) uses the word \(zx^{-1}x^{-1}\). A zero definition uses the one-letter word \(z\). Binary expansion and running sums express each row of \(B\) with at most a constant times its bit length plus one short definitions. Arrange relations so that after eliminating the newly defined generators, \(y_\ell\) has the desired linear expression in the free \(x_j\). A sign convention for these definitions is specified below.

Realize a relation word by attaching a disk along the corresponding edge walk in the bouquet. An explicit simplicial implementation uses a new polygon with separate vertices \(d_0,\ldots,d_{L-1}\), where the edge walk reads \(v_0,\ldots,v_{L-1},v_0\). The strip faces are \([d_i,v_i,v_{i+1}]\) and \([d_i,d_{i+1},v_{i+1}]\), followed by coning the new polygon to a fresh vertex. Consecutive \(v_i\) are distinct, and \(L\ge3\), since each letter traverses a three-edge circle. The strip is the mapping cylinder of the edge walk: it is the quotient of the triangulated polygonal cylinder by its bottom edge map. Its displayed triangles are distinct, and multiple coincident bottom edges are precisely the identifications of that quotient. Thus the strip and cone implement the attaching map with \(O(L)\) simplices. All new vertices for different relations are distinct.

Cap each output circle separately by a cone on a fresh vertex. Barycentrically subdivide the entire complex \(X\). In each output cap designate a flag triangle \(\sigma_\ell\) consisting of the apex, a spoke, and an incident cap face. Its closure is interior to the cap. Let \(X^-\) remove just the interiors of these flag triangles. A punctured cap retracts to its output circle, so \(H_1(X^-;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^d\), with the stated \(x_j\) as a basis, by elimination in the abelianized presentation. Orient all flag faces by increasing barycentric rank. The oriented boundary of each marked face represents its output generator up to a fixed sign from its cap. Use the appropriate sign in defining \(y_\ell\) so that
\[
[\partial\sigma_\ell]=\sum_j B_{\ell j}[x_j].
\]
This sign depends only on the orientation of its own cap and may be set when drawing the relation.

### The tensor and the exact orbit computation

Use the sparse multiplication-table encoding from the [earlier relative-pole construction](SparseSeed.md). Its details also fix the labeling. Order vertices of \(X\) by barycentric rank and then by a fixed order within a rank. Add any absent successor edges in this total order, with zero positive-length products involving an added edge. Use a basis \(e_i\) of orthogonal idempotents on vertices and one vector \(p_{ij}\) per oriented edge, acted on by its left and right endpoint idempotents. If \((i,j,k)\) is a flag triangle, put
\[
p_{ij}p_{jk}=a_{ijk}p_{ik}.
\]
Other edge products vanish. Take every \(a_{ijk}=1\) in the source, and only the marked coefficients zero in the target. All triple products of edges vanish, so the laws are associative. The sum of the \(e_i\)'s is the unit, which certifies conciseness of multiplication tensors. The vertex and edge count is linear in the number of simplices before barycentric subdivision. Each vertex, edge, and face contributes a bounded number of nonzero entries.

The span \(J\) of edges equals the span of commutators, and \(J^3=0\). The span \(J^2\) is the same for both tables. For every flag with given rank-zero and rank-two endpoints there are two possible rank-one intermediates in that triangle, and only one flag is removed in any cap face. A basis edge occurs in exactly one positive radical degree. The action of a generic algebra isomorphism on the ordered vertices of the quotient must be the identity, since the directed support has total reachability order. On each positive-degree associated graded corner the isomorphism is scalar, because that corner is one-dimensional.

An isotopy of unital associative products implies an algebra isomorphism. In particular, if \(H(xy)=P(x)Q(y)\), set \(a=P(1)\) and \(b=Q(1)\). The one-sided multiplication identities show that both \(a,b\) are units. The map \(x\mapsto a^{-1}H(x)b^{-1}\) is an isomorphism. This is also the relevant step of the standard passage from unital multiplication tensors to algebra degenerations. [Bläser–Lysikov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04253)

For a Laurent witness, contract with the written special unit in both input modes. These two contractions are identity modulo \(t\) and therefore invertible over \(R\). Precomposing by their inverses yields a unital associative \(R\)-law \(\nu\) with the same special law and with generic fiber algebra-isomorphic to the source. Indeed if the original product is \(C\mu^+(Ax,By)\), put \(a=Ae,b=Be\). The corresponding contractions are \(CR_bA\) and \(CL_aB\), so \(a,b\) are units. In \(C\)-coordinates the corrected law is \(x(ab)^{-1}y\), which is associative unital and isomorphic to the source by \(x\mapsto abx\). The unital and associative identities hold over \(R\) by torsion freeness. The families \(J,J^2\) in \(\nu\) are free direct summands with the stated special fibers: compute them as the span of commutators and then products of two such vectors, and use equal generic and special ranks with a unit minor. The quotient is the split algebra \(R^{v}\), obtained by lifting the special primitive idempotents in the commutative quotient. Hence the two graded positive pieces have integral rank-one corner frames and an intrinsic labeling by reachability. The generic algebra isomorphism gives a scalar \(\eta_{ij}\in K^\times\) at each corner.

The generic structure coefficient at a face is
\[
a_{ijk}(t)=\frac{\eta_{ik}}{\eta_{ij}\eta_{jk}} .
\]
The marked orders are well-defined relative to the normalized law since changing integral corner frames multiplies coefficients by units. A retained coefficient is a unit and a marked coefficient has positive valuation. Put \(w_{ij}=-\operatorname{val}(\eta_{ij})\). On the original edges these integers define a simplicial cocycle on \(X^-\) with \(\operatorname{val}(a_{ijk})=w(\partial[i,j,k])\). In particular the marked order vector is \(B(w(x_1),\ldots,w(x_d))\).

Conversely every homomorphism \(H_1(X^-;\mathbb Z)\to\mathbb Z\) is represented by an integral cocycle. For example set its values to zero on a spanning tree and on each remaining edge evaluate it on the edge closed through the tree. On added successor edges take any weight, say zero. A diagonal algebra change of weight \(-w_{ij}\) gives exactly the claimed arc. This also proves the orbit result over a field: replace additive integer cochains by multiplicative \(k^\times\)-valued cochains, and use precisely the same spanning-tree construction. Conversely the graded action of any arbitrary transporter of the two displayed unital laws gives such a multiplicative cocycle, by isotopy normalization.

## 2. Exponential ramification and exponential polynomial image degree

**Theorem.** There are explicit concise \(0/1\) tensors \(U_m,V_m\) of side \(N_m=O(m)\), with \(O(N_m)\) nonzero entries, and a binary basis tensor \(E_m\) such that the geometric orbit contains \(V_m+cE_m\) for each nonzero scalar \(c\). The particular degree-one arc \(V_m+tE_m\) admits a lift over \(k((t^{1/e}))\) exactly when \(2^{m-1}\) divides \(e\). Every polynomial image arc over \(k[t]\) that admits a Laurent witness over the original \(k((t))\), from \(U_m\) to \(V_m\), has degree at least \(2^{m-1}/O(N_m^4)\). An image arc of degree \(2^{m-1}\) has such a lift. The claims hold over every coefficient field.

Use the triangulated Möbius-band-and-annulus telescope of the earlier construction, with three-edge circles \(C_1,\ldots,C_m\) and \([C_{i+1}]=2[C_i]\). This telescope retracts to \(C_1\). Put the cap on \(C_m\), and puncture one interior flag triangle there. The punctured cap retracts to \(C_m\). Thus \(H_1(X^-;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb Z[C_1]\), and the marked boundary represents \(\pm2^{m-1}[C_1]\). The circle construction and cap change do not alter the linear size estimates. The exact cochain result gives the orbit membership criterion \(c\in(K^\times)^{2^{m-1}}\). This proves geometric orbit membership and the precise ramification statement, since \(t\) has valuation \(e\) in \(k((t^{1/e}))\) with normalized uniformizer \(t^{1/e}\). Sufficiency follows from the monomial \(2^{m-1}\)-th root of \(t\) in that field. The same cochains give the degree \(2^{m-1}\) polynomial image lift. The telescope is based on the construction of Filakovský, Franek, Wagner, and Zhechev. [Their Lemma 7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6777513/)

### An intrinsic map that detects contact from an arbitrary polynomial arc

The contact just computed uses integral frames and may look unrelated to the degrees in the original coordinates. To connect them without solving for primitive idempotents, use \(Q=\nu/J\), \(W=J/J^2\), \(Z=J^2\). Let \(\theta\) be the tensor inverse of the regular trace pairing
\[
Q\otimes Q\to R,\qquad (x,y)\mapsto \operatorname{Tr}(L_x L_y).
\]
The pairing is unimodular, since in the split idempotent basis it is the identity in every characteristic. There \(\theta=\sum_j e_j\otimes e_j\). Define the intrinsic tagged multiplication map
\[
\mathcal M:W\otimes W\to Z\otimes Q,\qquad
x\otimes y\longmapsto \sum_{u,v}\theta_{uv}\, ((xq_u)y)\otimes q_v
\]
for any basis \(q_u\) of \(Q\). Here the right action on \(W\) and the multiplication \(W\otimes W\to Z\) are well-defined by \(J^3=0\). In corner frames the map sends the two edges of a flag to its structure coefficient times \(p_{ik}\otimes e_j\). Each flag uses a distinct input basis vector and a distinct output basis vector, since all corners are thin. All remaining input basis vectors map to zero. Thus the map has one nonzero diagonal entry per flag up to permutations of bases, and the sum of valuations of its nonzero invariant factors is exactly the marked contact. In particular every minor of maximal generic rank is divisible by \(t^{2^{m-1}}\). Changing frames by integral invertible matrices preserves the ideal of the maximal-rank minors.

Suppose the image arc has polynomial entries of degree at most \(D\). Say that a rational array has height at most \(h\) if it has a common denominator, a unit at zero, of degree at most \(h\), and every numerator has degree at most \(h\). Products of arrays, including multilinear contractions with fixed coefficients, add heights. For an \(r\times r\) array of height \(h\) invertible at zero, its inverse has height at most \(rh\), by the adjugate identity. Unit normalization has height \(h_0=(2N_m+1)D\) by applying this rule to the two contractions. Commutator columns have that same denominator, and products of two commutator columns have its cube as denominator and height at most \(3h_0\). Select suitable columns for \(J^2\), augment by commutator columns for \(J\), then by standard basis vectors to a basis of the whole algebra. All selections can be made independent at \(t=0\) and give one adapted matrix of height at most \(3h_0\), since the displayed denominators divide the same cube. In that basis the multiplication has height at most \(h_1=(3N_m+7)h_0\), by accounting for both inputs and the inverse basis change on the output. The quotient multiplication, right action and product used in \(\mathcal M\) are subarrays of this multiplication. The trace pairing has height at most \(2h_1\), and its inverse has height at most \(2\dim(Q)h_1\). Therefore \(\mathcal M\) has height at most \((2N_m+2)h_1\). Its rank is at most \(2N_m\), since each rank-zero to rank-two corner belongs to exactly two input flags. Any nonzero maximal-rank minor has a polynomial numerator of degree at most
\[
2N_m(2N_m+2)(3N_m+7)(2N_m+1)D .
\]
Its denominator is a unit at zero. For a nonzero polynomial the valuation is at most its degree. Comparing with the required divisibility gives the stated lower bound. Over the original Laurent field the extremal image degrees are therefore exponential in the side length. The degree-one arc concerns geometric orbit membership. In particular it may require a purely inseparable ramified extension in characteristic two.

### Determinant-one and symplectic versions

The contact lattice and degree bound also hold with all three transformations of determinant one, after adding a new last vertex and one successor edge leading to it. Every edge product involving this new edge vanishes. For a constructed diagonal algebra isomorphism, scale the new edge by the inverse of the determinant of its action on the old algebra. The extended map has determinant one and gives exactly the same face coefficients. Lower bounds for unrestricted transformations apply to the enlarged algebra by the same associated-graded and trace arguments. This also preserves the criterion for constant values of the marked coefficients.

For the symplectic version replace each mode by the corresponding hyperbolic space \(V\oplus V^*\), with the tensor supported on the three \(V\) summands. Every ordinary Laurent witness lifts with identical padded image degree, by replacing the three matrices \(P_i\) with \(\operatorname{diag}(P_i,P_i^{-\mathsf T})\). Conversely project any symplectic image arc onto the three original spaces. This projects the action on the source to its three upper-left blocks. The generic blocks are invertible because the projected limit is concise: the corresponding generic flattening has rank at least its special rank and at most the rank of that block. Thus projection recovers an ordinary Laurent witness of no larger image degree. Over each Laurent field the two existence statements are equivalent, also for the padded degree-one line and its required ramification. The earlier hyperbolic retraction in the Lean tree establishes the qualitative bridge; conciseness makes this degree comparison exact here. [Hyperbolic padding and orbit closures](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SymplecticTensorFusions/OrbitClosures.md)

## 3. A bounded-contact decision problem is NP-complete

Consider the marked presentations above, including the specified sparse multiplication tables and their corner/mark descriptions. The decision problem asks whether all marked orders can be at most \(2\) in one Laurent witness. It belongs to NP: guess a vector of ones and twos and check whether the integer simplicial coboundary equations have a solution, by computing a Smith or Hermite normal form. The equations impose zero on retained faces and the guessed orders on marked faces. The membership test uses a standard polynomial-bit-time integer normal form algorithm. [Kannan–Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

Reduce NAE-3SAT to this case. Let \(z,x_i\) be free integer coordinates, and include \(z,2z\) and \(x_i\) as rows of the contact vector. Membership in \(\{1,2\}\) forces \(z=1\), with Boolean variable encoded as \(x_i-1\). For a positive literal use its value \(x_i\), and for a negative literal use \(3z-x_i\). Include one more row per three-literal clause: sum its three literal values and subtract \(3z\). This row is in \(\{1,2\}\) exactly when its truth values are not all equal. The theorem implements these rows in a sparse pair with linear size in the formula. Even the no-instances admit unrestricted Laurent witnesses: take \(z=2\) and all \(x_i=3\), giving contacts at most \(4\). The reduction and criterion hold over every field. Thus the decision problem is NP-complete even on pairs promised to degenerate. This result bounds the intrinsic marked contacts. The image-degree bound in Section 2 is a different quantitative assertion. [Schaefer](https://doi.org/10.1145/800133.804350)

## 4. The same construction realizes finite diagonalizable stabilizer factors

Write \(G=\mathbb Z^d/B^{\mathsf T}\mathbb Z^q\). For the source multiplication tensor the \(G\)-character group scheme \(D(G)=\operatorname{Hom}(G,\mathbb G_m)\) is a direct factor of its stabilizer as a scheme. All the other factors may be taken to be affine spaces and split tori. In particular any finite abelian group can be realized as the character group of such a factor, with the construction linear in the bit lengths of its cyclic orders. The terminal-cap construction gives a \(\mu_{2^{m-1}}\) factor, including over characteristic two where it is nonreduced for \(m>1\).

To see this over any commutative test ring, the commutator span of the written source algebra is still its strictly off-diagonal ideal \(J\). Every algebra automorphism fixes the labeled quotient idempotents: reduce at each maximal ideal, apply the reachability argument, and then use that an idempotent with zero image in every residue field is zero. If its vertex images are \(f_i\), the unique \(z\in1+J\) such that \(zf_i=e_i z\) is \(z=\sum_i e_i f_i\). This formula gives an element of \(1+J\); uniqueness follows because an element in \(1+J\) centralizing every vertex is the identity. Once vertices have been aligned, each edge corner is a free line, so the automorphism is a tuple of units satisfying the multiplicative cocycle equations on *all* faces. Tree normalization separates the vertex ratios as a split torus. The remaining coordinates represent \(\operatorname{Hom}(H_1(X;\mathbb Z),\mathbb G_m)\) and independently the free cycles from added successor edges. Filling the punctures of \(X^-\) kills precisely the rows of \(B\), giving the character group \(G\). The group of units of the algebra is a split torus times the affine space of radical coefficients as a scheme. The unital isotopy formula adds two copies of this unit scheme to the algebra automorphism scheme. All formulas use regular operations on these functors, establishing the asserted scheme factors, and make no assertion that every displayed factor splits the full stabilizer group law.

## Verification status

These are written proofs. The proof uses the earlier associated-graded recovery argument and the same labelled thin multiplication-table encoding. The new ingredients are realization of general contact lattices, moving the cap to turn doubling into forced divisibility, and computing the tagged multiplication without choosing rational primitive idempotents. The earlier sparse relative-pole construction concerned large presentation poles even for a degree-one Laurent-liftable image arc. The degree lower bound here concerns the different pair with its cap on the terminal cycle.
