# Proof-degree separations across tensors, associative algebras and small-exponent groups

This is an informal proof. All ranks and isomorphisms use the stated field. The arguments below reuse existing decision reductions and prove polynomial-calculus simulations for their displayed equations.

## 1. Statements and accounting

A tensor witness consists of three square factor maps and their two-sided inverse witnesses. The action axioms are cubic. For a bilinear map $\beta:U\times V\to W$, the equivalent witness presentation consists of maps $P,Q,R$ and their inverses with
$$
R\beta(u,v)=\beta'(Pu,Qv)
$$
on basis pairs. The two presentations translate into each other by exchanging an input action matrix with its inverse transpose, then contracting the action equation with the two input factors or their inverses. Expanding the products and reducing the two inverse pairs gives bounded-degree ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) derivations. The witness-variable exchanges are coordinate permutations. When the field equations over a fixed finite field are included, those exchanges preserve them.

The following hold.

1. Over every field, for ordinary three-tensor isomorphism of any common positive format, there is a linear-dimension encoding into isomorphism of ordinary unmarked unital associative algebras with split semisimple quotient $F^3$ and radical cube zero. The usual inverse-witness equations for each of the two problems reduce to those for the other by constant-degree substitutions and constant-degree ordinary-PC derivations of each image axiom. The same holds for ordinary TI on the bare multiplication tensors of the encoded algebras. From the larger equation systems to the original TI equations, the refutation translation uses coordinate substitutions and has polynomial overhead in sparse proof size even at unbounded degree.
2. The existing linear-size tensor-to-matrix-space encodings preserve ordinary-PC degree and sparse-size lower bounds. The targets are alternating matrix-space isometry and concrete matrix-space conjugacy where every product of two matrices in either input space is zero. The alternating result extends to equivalence of a single alternating trilinear form using Grochow–Qiao's linear-size reduction.
3. For each fixed prime $p$ there are explicit infinite families of pairs of equal-order special $p$-groups with elementary abelian center, given by structure constants on quotient and center. Write $d=\log_p |G|$. Their standard inverse-witness isomorphism equations in these succinct coordinates require ordinary-PC degree $\Omega_p(d)$ and sparse size $2^{\Omega_p(d)}$. For odd $p$ the groups have exponent $p$ and the equations preserve the commutator map. For $p=2$ they have exponent four and the equations preserve the square map. The lower bounds hold even with the field equations on the matrix variables over $\mathbb F_p$. These are succinct-coordinate equations. Cayley-table permutation equations have a different variable set.

Over each fixed field, combining these transfers with the prior tensor lower bounds gives $\Omega(n)$ ordinary-PC degree and $2^{\Omega(n)}$ ordinary-PC sparse size in the relevant side dimension on the resulting associative, alternating and concrete matrix-space families. The paper's original inversion rule compresses the same systems to their maximum axiom degree with a single inversion inference. For algebra-isomorphism and group-isomorphism equations this is degree two in the geometric formulation. With the field equations over $\mathbb F_p$, the group bound is $\max(2,p)$. These bounds refer to proof-line degree and monomials. The polynomials supplied as inversion parameters can have large degree and size. [Existing tensor lower bounds](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/TensorSparsePCBounds) · [Inversion argument](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IsomorphismProofCrossovers/circuits.md)

The reduction convention is that an input equation $f=0$ becomes its image under a polynomial substitution. An image axiom proved by boundedly many products and replacements of bounded-degree factors has an ordinary-PC proof of absolute constant degree and polynomial sparse size. Expand each product and perform variable multiplications term by term. Sums over indices are polynomial-size in this assertion because each summand has only a bounded number of factors. A coordinate substitution sends each variable to a variable or a scalar. It never increases the monomial count, and replacing projected axioms by their derived lines costs polynomial sparse size. In a polynomial substitution of degree $k$, a proof of degree $D$ pulls back to one of degree at most $\max(kD,D_0)$, where $D_0$ covers the image axiom derivations. The pullback may expand sparse size when the substitution is dense.

## 2. Associative algebras and bare multiplication tensors

For $\beta:U\times V\to W$, put
$$
A_\beta=F e_1\oplus F e_2\oplus F e_3\oplus U\oplus V\oplus W.
$$
The $e_i$ are complete orthogonal idempotents. Give $U,V,W$ the Peirce positions $12,23,13$, respectively, with their endpoints acting identically and all other idempotent actions zero. The only nonzero radical multiplication allowed is $uv=\beta(u,v)$. The Peirce rules give associativity with idempotents, and every triple radical product is zero. Thus $J=U\oplus V\oplus W$ is the radical and $A_\beta/J=F^3$. This is the three-vertex Peirce encoding from our earlier determinant normalization.

A bilinear-map witness gives the algebra map $\operatorname{diag}(I_3,P,Q,R)$ and the corresponding diagonal inverse. Each destination axiom either is identically zero or is an inverse or bilinear-map axiom. This is a coordinate substitution.

For the reverse direction write $M,N$ for the algebra map and inverse. We use only ordinary PC. Unit preservation follows in degree at most three. Write $u=M(1)$. Multiplicativity gives $L_uM=M$, and composition with $N$ yields $L_u=I$, hence $u=1$. Inverse multiplicativity follows by substituting $N(x),N(y)$ in the identities for $M$, reducing $MN=I$, then applying $N$. This uses degree at most five.

Every element of a strict upper Peirce block is a linear combination of commutators, since $[e_i,x]=x$ on block $ij$ for $i<j$. Every target commutator vanishes in its commutative quotient. The multiplication equations therefore give $M(J)\subseteq J'$ by linear cancellation. Apply the same argument to $N$. Let $p_{ui}$ and $q_{iv}$ be the entries of the induced quotient maps in the idempotent bases. The inverse equations reduce to $pq=qp=I$. Unit and multiplication preservation give row sums one, Boolean idempotence, and collisions of different entries of each row. The same identities hold for $q$. Multiplying $(pq-I)_{uv}$ by $p_{ui}$, and using collisions and idempotence in row $u$, gives
$$
p_{ui}q_{iv}=\delta_{uv}p_{ui}.
$$
Swapping $p,q$ gives $q_{iu}p_{uk}=\delta_{ik}q_{iu}$. Taking $v=u$ and $k=i$ and subtracting gives $q_{iu}=p_{ui}$. This derives the column sums and column collisions for $p$ in bounded degree.

Set $f_i=M(e_i)$ and $e'_i=\sum_u p_{ui}e_u$ in the target algebra. Both triples are complete orthogonal idempotents according to the derived identities, and $f_i-e'_i$ is radical. Define
$$
a=\sum_i e'_i f_i,\qquad b=1-(a-1)+(a-1)^2.
$$
The diagonal part of $a$ is one because $(e'_i)^2=e'_i$ and $\sum_i e'_i=1$. Therefore $a-1\in J'$. Since $(J')^3=0$, expanding and killing the diagonal part gives $ab=ba=1$. The orthogonality identities give $a f_i=e'_i a$. Normalize the map to $M_0(x)=aM(x)b$ and the inverse to $N_0(y)=N(by a)$. Every displayed identity for these maps follows by replacing factors in the expansions and using $ab=ba=1$, multiplicativity of $M$, and the two-sided inverse equations. The maps have degree at most seven. In particular $M_0$ and $N_0$ are two-sided inverses, $M_0$ is multiplicative, and $M_0(e_i)=e'_i$.

We next derive that the quotient map is the identity, without testing a list of permutations. Fix $i<j$ and a nonzero source basis element $x$ of block $ij$. This is available for every $i<j$ by positivity of the three mode dimensions. The identity $M_0(x)=e'_i M_0(x)e'_j$ follows from multiplicativity. Multiplying by $\theta=p_{ui}p_{vj}$ and using the column collisions yields
$$
\theta M_0(x)=\theta e_u M_0(x)e_v.
$$
The right side is zero for $u\ge v$ because $M_0(x)$ is radical and all diagonal and lower Peirce blocks of the radical are zero. Applying $N_0$ and taking the coefficient of $x$ gives $p_{ui}p_{vj}=0$ for $i<j$ and $u\ge v$. This is a bounded-degree derivation. For example, the product on the left before expanding has degree at most eleven, applying $N_0$ adds at most seven, and using the inverse identity costs degree at most sixteen. The row and column equations then force $p=I$. Indeed for $j>1$, multiply the sum of column $1$ by $p_{1j}$ and kill all terms with the forbidden products to get $p_{1j}=0$. Row $1$ gives $p_{11}=1$ and column collisions eliminate the other entries of column $1$. Repeat with row and column $2$ and then row and column $3$. This takes only bounded-degree substitutions for zero and one.

It follows that $M_0$ fixes each displayed idempotent. Multiplicativity shows $M_0$ maps each strict upper Peirce block to itself. Multiplying the idempotent identities by the inverse map gives the analogous statement for $N_0$. Project $M_0,N_0$ onto the three blocks. Their restricted identities give the two-sided inverse axioms on each block. Multiplicativity on $U\times V$ gives the source bilinear-map axioms. All expressions used have a bounded number of products, including above choices of basis elements and every multiplication and inverse derivation after projection. The dimensions affect the number of summands. The substitution and derivation degrees stay bounded. Direct real sum-of-squares substitutions work for the lower-bound direction: the forward algebra axioms expand as bounded-degree linear combinations of monomial multiples of source axioms, so a sum-of-squares certificate pulls back with constant degree overhead.

For the bare multiplication tensor of a unital associative algebra, the usual TI equations are equivalent to the isotopy presentation with $Z(ab)=X(a)Y(b)$. The action-to-isotopy translation exchanges input inverse-transposes and then multiplies by the input witness matrices, with bounded-degree factor replacements. In the forward direction set $X=Y=Z=M$ and use $N$ for their inverses. In the reverse direction put
$$
u=X(1),quad v=Y(1),quad u'=Y Z^{-1}(1),quad v'=X Z^{-1}(1).
$$
Here and below inverses denote the existing variables. From $Z=L_uY=R_vX$ we get two-sided linear inverses $YZ^{-1}$ for $L_u$ and $XZ^{-1}$ for $R_v$. Evaluation at $1$ gives $uu'=v'v=1$. The identity $u(u'u-1)=(uu'-1)u$ and the inverse for $L_u$ yield $u'u=1$. Similarly $vv'=1$. Then the maps $a\mapsto u'Z(a)v'$ and $b\mapsto Z^{-1}(ubv)$ are inverse, and $X(a)=Z(a)v'$ and $Y(b)=u'Z(b)$ show multiplicativity. The factor replacements have absolute constant degree. This is the unital isotopy normalization already verified in our graph-algebra note. [Previous ordinary-PC proof for graph algebras and isotopy](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IsomorphismCrossoversRoundThree/proof-complexity.md)

If field equations are part of the input over a fixed finite field, the forward coordinate substitutions preserve them. The bounded-degree reverse substitutions do too in ordinary PC at a larger fixed degree. For every polynomial expression $g$, its Frobenius power $g^q$ distributes over sums and products. Coefficients lie in $\mathbb F_q$. For each monomial the difference between its $q$-th power and itself telescopes by $x^q-x$ one factor at a time. The resulting degree bound depends on the fixed field size $q$ and the absolute degree of $g$.

## 3. Alternating spaces, concrete matrix algebras and alternating trilinear forms

Use the constructions from the earlier projection reduction. In the alternating version write $A$ on $U\wedge V$ with slice axis $L$. Add the fixed coevaluation from $L^*\wedge F_0$ with slice axis $L$, where $F_0$ is a line. Finally add eight tag slices with disjoint private labels. A tag for a block $B$ pairs $B\oplus R_j$ with $D_j$ by a full coordinate identity and its negative transpose. The two copies of each block tag and their rank-separated private parts force recovery of the labelled data blocks from every isomorphism. In the conjugacy version the same construction has one primal and one dual factor instead of an alternating pair. Its slices lie in $X\otimes Y^*$ inside $\operatorname{End}(X\oplus Y)$, so the product of any two matrices in the space vanishes. The slice lists are independent even when the source tensor is zero. [Exact orbit reflection and dimension bounds](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ArbitraryTensorMatrixSpaceProjections/Proof.md)

The polynomial-calculus reduction uses the explicit forward transporter. On the original blocks place the source maps or the relevant transposed inverse maps from the two-sided witnesses. On $F_0$ and each $R_j$ use the identity. On $D_j$ use the inverse transpose of the action on $B\oplus R_j$. On the slice axis use the source slice map on $L$ and the identity on the tag subspace. For inverses of all maps reverse these assignments. Each target witness entry is a source variable or $0,1$. Every target two-sided inverse axiom becomes an inverse identity on one such block or zero. On the data block the image of the target action is the original tensor action, with its transpose or sign on the redundant reversed block in the alternating case. The coevaluation term uses just the product of the original slice matrix with its inverse. The tag equation uses the product of the action matrix on $B\oplus R_j$ with the inverse of that action matrix. All mixed equations vanish identically since the substituted maps are block diagonal. The sign on reversed alternating blocks is a field coefficient of the input structure constants, so it requires no substitution that expands monomials. All identity reductions have bounded degree and polynomial sparse size. The isomorphism reflection argument establishes geometric nonisomorphism whenever the source is geometrically nonisomorphic. This completes the lower-bound transfer with linear side dimensions. It is a forward proof substitution. The recovery proof by matrix ranks is used only for decision equivalence.

One more linear-size reduction transfers the alternating-space result to a single alternating trilinear form. Write the source as $\tau\in \bigwedge^2 X\otimes Z$ and place its image in $\bigwedge^3(X\oplus Z)$ by alternating multiplication of the disjoint blocks. Add $X^*$, a new variable $g$, and a private even-dimensional space with a standard full-rank alternating form $\omega$. Add the following tensor.
$$
(\operatorname{coevaluation}_{X\wedge X^*}+\omega)\wedge g.
$$
Taking the private rank as in Grochow–Qiao forces orbit reflection with linear increase. If the source alternating-space witness is $(P,S)$, the trilinear witness has blocks $(P,S,P^{-T},I,1)$ and the inverse has the corresponding inverse blocks. Projected action axioms are copies or linear combinations of the alternating-space action axioms on the data and inverse equations on the tags. The copies come from the multilinearity of alternating multiplication on disjoint blocks. Thus this is again a coordinate substitution with bounded-degree image axiom derivations. It applies in characteristic two as well, where the alternating trilinear form still vanishes on repeated arguments. [Grochow–Qiao IV, Theorem 6.1 and Equation 11](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S6.SS1)

## 4. Transfer to special groups

Work over $F=\mathbb F_p$. Take the alternating-space construction of Section 3 and view its output as a bracket $\beta:E\times E\to C$, with $E$ dual to the matrix coordinate space and $C$ the slice axis. It is surjective as a linear map $\bigwedge^2 E\to C$ because its slices are independent. Its common radical vanishes. Indeed each tag forces a common-radical vector to vanish on $(B\oplus R_j)\oplus D_j$, since the tag is nondegenerate there. These coordinate positions cover the entire domain.

For odd $p$ use the explicit group law on $E\oplus C$
$$
(x,z)(y,w)=(x+y,z+w+\tfrac12\beta(x,y)).
$$
Bilinearity proves associativity. The bracket is its commutator, and the $p$-th power is zero. For $p=2$ use the same group law with $\gamma(x,y)$ in place of $\tfrac12\beta(x,y)$. Here $\gamma$ is the ordered tensor obtained by keeping only the term $a\otimes b$ for each designated wedge term $a\wedge b$ in the entire construction. Choose the indicated order separately for the data, coevaluation and each tag pair. Thus $\gamma(x,y)+\gamma(y,x)=\beta(x,y)$. The group has commutator $\beta$ and square map $q(x)=\gamma(x,x)$. Since its polar form is nonzero, $q$ is nonzero and the group has exponent four. In both cases the center equals $C$, the derived subgroup equals $C$, and $C$ is elementary abelian. The Frattini subgroup also equals $C$ because the quotient is elementary abelian and the derived subgroup already equals the center.

Any group isomorphism induces invertible linear maps $P:E\to E'$ and $S:C\to C'$ preserving the bracket, since the center and its elementary abelian quotient are characteristic. For odd $p$, any such maps lift directly in the displayed coordinates. For $p=2$ the necessary condition is that they preserve the square maps. It is sufficient. The difference
$$
D(x,y)=S\gamma(x,y)-\gamma'(Px,Py)
$$
is a bilinear map with zero diagonal if the square maps are preserved. In characteristic two this implies $D(x,y)=D(y,x)$. Fix an ordered basis $(e_i)$ and set
$$
t\!\left(\sum_i x_i e_i\right)=\sum_{i<j}x_i x_j D(e_i,e_j).
$$
Then $t(x+y)-t(x)-t(y)=D(x,y)$. Consequently $(x,z)\mapsto(Px,Sz-t(x))$ is a group isomorphism. The sign on $t$ is immaterial over $\mathbb F_2$.

The natural succinct isomorphism equations therefore impose two-sided inverse identities and field equations on $P,S$. For odd $p$ also impose $S\beta(x,y)=\beta'(Px,Py)$ on basis pairs. For $p=2$ impose $S q(x)=q'(Px)$ as coefficient equations of homogeneous degree-two polynomials in the dummy coordinates of $x$. These coincide with equality as functions on $\mathbb F_2$ because evaluation on basis elements determines the diagonal coefficients and evaluation on sums of two basis elements determines every other coefficient. Their polar equations give the bracket equations by summing coefficients. The isomorphism and inverse equations have degree at most two in the witness variables. With the field equations the maximum is $\max(2,p)$.

In odd characteristic Section 3 gives the projected target axioms, exchanging the action convention with inverse transpose as needed. In characteristic two, every fact used in Section 3 for the forward lift already proves preservation of the ordered tensor $\gamma$. The data, coevaluation and private-pair assertions there used the ordered term separately, before adjoining its transpose. Substituting $x=y$ gives the square-map coefficient equations by linear combination of ordered coefficients. In both characteristics the witnessed map and inverse entries are coordinates or constants. Therefore the size- and degree-preserving witness projections apply. A nonisomorphic input tensor pair yields nonisomorphic output groups because any group isomorphism gives a bracket transporter, which reflects ordinary TI.

For each fixed prime, use the explicit unequal-rank hard tensor pairs from the existing positive-characteristic sparse-PC theorem, including field equations. The total dimension $\dim E+\dim C$ is linear in the source mode dimension, and the source pairs have equal format. This proves the two lower bounds in the statement for equal-order groups with the same center and derived subgroup orders. A solution to the group equations over the algebraic closure, even after omitting the field equations, would still preserve the alternating bracket by coefficient comparison. The block recovery from Section 3 would then give an isomorphism of the original unequal-rank tensors over the algebraic closure, which is impossible. The one-inversion degree bound thus applies both with and without field axioms. [Baer correspondence and the standard odd-characteristic commutator presentation](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S2.SS1) · [Grochow and Qiao on matrix-generator presentations](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S7)

## 5. Scope and comparison

The new contribution in these arguments is the bounded-degree proof interpretation, the resulting strong ordinary-PC size bounds on the restricted targets, and the succinct group separation in both exponent settings. The underlying three-vertex decision encoding, matrix-space gadgets and odd-characteristic correspondence are prior ingredients. The alternating and conjugacy transfers are one-way proof interpretations. They supply hardness for these presentations without claiming a proof-system equivalence for every route in Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She Question 7.5. Targeted literature searches found the published decision reductions and earlier tensor and graph proof bounds. Publication priority for the stated extensions remains unestablished. [Published proof-complexity question](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS5)
