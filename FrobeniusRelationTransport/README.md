# Determinant-constrained tensor transport through nonsplit quadratic relations

**Theorem.** Let the input consist of two dense based bilinear maps over an odd prime field, with dimensions \(n,n,m>0\), and three prescribed nonzero determinants. The following class is recognizable in deterministic polynomial bit time. On this class there is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time algorithm to decide the prescribed-determinant transporter problem, and to exhibit all three matrices of one witness when feasible. The bit bound is polynomial in the written tensor size and \(\log p\). The decision proof is elementary apart from standard finite-field isomorphism and integer linear algebra. For uniform witness construction at variable \(p\), we use the Dirichlet \(L\)-function theorem and the previously derived effective norm and root algorithms. At any fixed prime, elementary enumeration of base-field nonresidues suffices.

* Each output is spanned. For each input \(T\colon U\times V\to Z\), the linear space of \(J\colon U\to V\) with \(T(x,Jy)=T(y,Jx)\) is one-dimensional, generated by an isomorphism.
* Put \(b(xy)=T(x,Jy)\) and \(R=\ker b\) in \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 U\). The linear algebra \(D\) below is a commutative reduced associative algebra of dimension \(n\). Its simple factors have pairwise distinct degrees over the ground field.

Section 6 also allows repeated degrees at a binary prime when trace counts distinguish the factors. At a fixed odd prime it replaces distinct degrees by a computable color-multiplicity bound and allows arbitrarily many factors of one degree. These hypotheses are intrinsic and require no marked slice, tangent basis, mode map, or supplied isomorphism. The independent maps \(P,Q,S\) obey \(S T(x,y)=T'(Px,Qy)\). Specializing their determinants to \(1,1,1\) gives three-mode special-linear tensor isomorphism. For symmetric **congruence** with prescribed determinant of the tangent and quotient maps, begin directly at a surjective \(b\) with the stated property of \(D\), omitting the first condition involving \(J\). The algorithms decide either problem on pairs in the recognized class. They give same-field many-one reductions there by returning the scalar tensor pair \((1,1)\) or \((1,0)\) according to the decision.

## 1. Recovering the hidden fields

Compute \(J\), \(b\), and \(R\) by linear equations. Replacing \(J\) by a nonzero scalar multiple preserves \(R\). For \(A\in\operatorname{End}(U)\), write \(\partial_A(xy)=(Ax)y+x(Ay)\). Compute
\[
       D=\{A\mid \partial_A R\subseteq R\}.
\]
This is a linear system. Check dimension, commutativity, and closure under ordinary matrix multiplication on its basis. It contains the identity. In a commutative finite algebra reducedness is equivalent to injectivity of \(x\mapsto x^p\). Indeed a nilpotent yields a nonzero element killed by Frobenius, while in a reduced algebra \(x^p=0\) implies \(x=0\). A finite reduced commutative algebra over a finite field is a product of fields.

For each \(e\le n\), form the ideal generated by the image of \(x\mapsto x^{p^e}-x\) and compute its annihilator. The latter is supported exactly on the factors of degree dividing \(e\), since the map vanishes identically precisely on those factors. Compute the identity of this ideal by linear equations and subtract the idempotents already found for its proper divisors. An exact-degree piece of dimension \(e\) is one field. Dimension greater than \(e\) fails the distinct-degree test. All exponent lengths are polynomial. Degrees are intrinsic labels. Distinct endpoint degree multisets exclude every isomorphism.

Writing the surviving idempotents as \(e_i\), one has \(U=\bigoplus U_i\) with \(U_i=e_iU\). Each \(U_i\) is a faithful nonzero module over its field \(K_i=e_iD\). Since \(\sum [K_i:F]=n=\dim U\), each has field rank one. Choose any nonzero module basis in each piece. Align fields of matching degrees at the two endpoints by Lenstra's deterministic isomorphism algorithm on explicit multiplication tables. Conjugation by any congruence \(g\) carries \(D\) onto \(D'\) because the derivative construction commutes with change of basis. Relative to the chosen alignments, it acts separately on each piece as \(x_i\mapsto a_i x_i^{p^{k_i}}\), with \(a_i\ne0\) and \(k_i\) modulo \(d_i=[K_i:F]\). This exhausts the possible normalizer maps. [Lenstra, *Finding isomorphisms between finite fields*](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

Over the algebraic closure, \(D\) is the full diagonal algebra in the basis of primitive idempotents acting on the chosen module generators. On each quadratic monomial, a diagonal idempotent acts by its multiplicity in that monomial. The joint weights distinguish all monomials since \(0,1,2\) are distinct in odd characteristic. Stability under the simultaneous diagonal operators implies that \(R\) is spanned by a subset of those monomials. In particular every unit scaling in the fields preserves \(R\). Setting \(H_{ii}=\operatorname{Sym}^2 U_i\) and \(H_{ij}=U_i\otimes U_j\) for \(i<j\), we have \(R=\bigoplus R_{ij}\) with \(R_{ij}=R\cap H_{ij}\). This equality descends by scalar extension and dimension. Compute these intersections over the ground field.

## 2. Finding one global choice of Frobenius powers

Fix the field identifications from Section 1. For each pair \(i<j\) enumerate the \(d_id_j\) pairs of powers, testing whether their action takes \(R_{ij}\) onto \(R'_{ij}\) by ordinary linear algebra. For \(i=i\) enumerate the \(d_i\) diagonal powers acting through the symmetric square. Scalars impose no further restriction by Section 1. Each nonempty local answer list is a coset of its source stabilizer in the indicated abelian group. An empty list excludes an isomorphism.

In each local list subtract one accepted tuple from every tuple. These differences, together with the coordinate periods, generate the integral preimage of the local stabilizer. Impose one integer variable \(k_i\) per field. For each pair require its projected tuple to equal the chosen offset plus an arbitrary integral combination of those local generators. The resulting single integer linear system detects compatibility of all blocks. Its Smith form supplies a particular solution and generators of the integral kernel. Project the latter onto the \(k_i\) and reduce modulo their periods. This gives generators of **all** homogeneous admissible phases, because each locally admissible tuple has integer coefficients for every local lattice. Together with one particular solution they give precisely the globally admissible phases. There are at most \(\sum_{i<j}d_id_j+\sum_i d_i\) local trials, polynomially many variables and equations, and polynomial coefficient bit lengths. Computing transforming matrices for Smith form has polynomial bit complexity. This avoids enumerating the product of the \(d_i\) or constructing their common splitting field. [Kannan–Bachem, polynomial Smith and Hermite algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

A particular solution gives a based map \(g_0\) by taking all \(a_i=1\). The induced \(h_0\) on \(Z\) is the unique solution of \(h_0 b=b'\operatorname{Sym}^2g_0\). It is invertible because \(g_0\) maps kernels onto kernels. Each homogeneous phase generator similarly gives an actual source automorphism pair \((g,h)\). In the geometric basis from Section 1 pure phases permute the chosen idempotent basis without coefficients, and permute surviving monomials on the quotient. Thus \((\det g,\det h)\in\{\pm1\}^2\). Compute their determinants in the supplied bases and generate this at-most-four-element image, retaining one word per image element. Only this small image must be combined with the scalar calculation.

## 3. From norms to three independent mode determinants

For each field orbit set
\[
 w_i=\frac{2\dim R_{ii}+\sum_{j\ne i}\dim R_{\min(i,j),\max(i,j)}}{d_i},
 \qquad c_i=n+1-w_i .
\]
Frobenius acts transitively on the \(d_i\) geometric basis vectors in this orbit. Every vector occurs with the same multiplicity \(w_i\) in the relation monomials, counting a square twice. This proves integrality. All monomials of the symmetric square together have multiplicity \(n+1\) at each vector. Hence on the tangent and quotient the field scalings have respective determinants \(\prod_i t_i\) and \(\prod_i t_i^{c_i}\), where \(t_i=N_{K_i/F}(a_i)\). Each \(t_i\) ranges independently over all ground-field units by surjectivity of the finite-field norm.

For any independent-mode transporter \(P,Q,S\), the map \(QJP^{-1}\) is a target symmetrizer, so equals \(\lambda J'\) with \(\lambda\ne0\). Conversely each symmetric transporter \(g,h\) gives exactly the independent-mode triples
\[
             (P,Q,S)=(g,\lambda J'gJ^{-1},\lambda h).
\]
All determinant statements here are made in the original bases. Divide the target triple componentwise by
\((\det g_0,(\det J'/\det J)\det g_0,\det h_0)\). For each homogeneous phase image \((u,v)\), divide further by \((u,u,v)\). What remains must be in the monomial image with one column \((1,1,c_i)\) for each norm parameter and one column \((0,n,m)\) for \(\lambda\). This test is equivalent in both directions by the transporter decomposition.

Compute Smith form of this three-row matrix with its row and column transformations. Unimodular matrices act invertibly on tuples of units by monomials. Zero rows require target 1. A nonzero row gives \(x^s=y\), soluble precisely when \(y^{(p-1)/\gcd(s,p-1)}=1\). For witnesses, each nonzero invariant factor is at most the product of the nonzero factors, itself bounded by the largest absolute maximal-rank minor, at most \(6\max(1,n+1,m)^3\). The prior bounded-exponent root construction therefore applies. Briefly, factor \(b_0=\gcd(s,p-1)\) by trial division, then take successive prime roots using the small-prime Sylow digit algorithm. Since \(b_0\mid p-1\), every intermediate choice stays in the required remaining power image. Raise the resulting \(b_0\)-th root to an exponent \(v\) with \(sv\equiv b_0\bmod(p-1)\). Invert the Smith coordinate changes and lift each \(t_i\) to \(a_i\) with the explicit finite-field norm algorithm. It uses primes dividing \(\gcd(d_i,p-1)\), so trial division of \(d_i\) suffices. For both algorithms the variable-prime nonresidues are supplied by Bach's bound for Dirichlet characters. Compose the lifts and phase word with \(g_0,h_0\), recover \(Q,S\) as above, and verify on all basis products. [Explicit norm lifts and bounded-exponent roots, pinned prior proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/5d2a1a370c051bbdcb0406ecce525b2125b730e0/DirichletGrochowPrimeTests/OrientedTransport.md) · [Bach's subgroup bound](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1990-1023756-8) · [Sutherland's independent verification, including the Dirichlet statement](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

In the ternary square-zero conjugator model with even \(n\) and odd \(m\), use symmetric congruences and combine \(\det g\det h\). There is no independent \(\lambda\) in this model. The same algorithm decides the combined target by multiplying the two norm characters and phase determinants. For a raw matrix-space presentation include the endpoint ambient frame ratio \(\delta'/\delta\) from the preceding reconstruction theorem. This gives one ordinary TI instance on this recognizable subfamily. [Based sign and raw square-zero reconstruction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/5d2a1a370c051bbdcb0406ecce525b2125b730e0/EvenConjugatorF3/FilteredRelations.md)

## 4. Infinite ternary family beyond a ground-field monomial frame

Take distinct even integers \(d_i\ge10\), \(U=\bigoplus_i\mathbb F_{3^{d_i}}\). Over an algebraic closure label each orbit of idempotent lines by residues modulo \(d_i\) in Frobenius order. Form \(R\) from the unordered pairs of circular difference \(1\) or \(3\) up to sign in each orbit and no others. Each of these two sets contains \(d_i\) edges. The monomial subspace descends to the ground field by Frobenius stability. Let \(b\) be the symmetric-square quotient. Then \(n=\sum d_i\), \(\dim R=2n\), \(m=n(n-3)/2\).

The derivative algebra is exactly diagonal geometrically. For an off-diagonal coefficient \(A_{ji}\) to survive, every neighbor of \(i\) must be a neighbor of \(j\), by applying the derivative to every edge from \(i\) and inspecting its replacement monomial. All degrees are four, so inclusion requires equality. Distinct components have disjoint neighborhoods. Within an orbit, neighborhoods of vertices in opposite parity classes are disjoint. Those in one parity class give distinct cyclic translates of a consecutive interval of length four in a cycle of length \(d_i/2\ge5\). Thus all off-diagonal coefficients vanish. Descent gives precisely the original product of fields. A ground-field monomial basis would place the full split diagonal algebra in \(D\) and hence equal it by dimension, impossible here. For a single field even an intrinsic proper nonzero tangent subspace is impossible, since the field units preserve \(R\) and act transitively on nonzero tangent vectors.

All diagonal scalar forms survive in the quotient dual over the closure. Self-adjointness relative to each forces a symmetrizer to be diagonal. Every surviving off-diagonal pair forces equality of its two diagonal entries. The complement of the relation graph is connected: each parity class within an orbit is a clique, difference five joins the classes, and all cross-orbit pairs survive. Thus the symmetrizer space is exactly the scalars. This also implies scalar centroid, since a centroid's tangent component satisfies these equations and surjectivity determines its output component. A rational nonsingular slice is \(\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(a_i x_i y_i)\) for nonzero \(a_i\). It is geometrically diagonal and nonsingular by separability. Its discriminant can take either square class by the surjectivity of norms, so a split slice exists by the classification of even-dimensional forms over a finite field.

Here \(w_i=4\). Every unit scaling has determinant 1 on \(R\) over the ternary field. A cyclic shift on each orbit permutes its two edge sets in identical cycles, so also has determinant 1 on \(R\). Degrees distinguish the orbits. Consequently every congruence automorphism has \(\det h=\det g\), since \(n\) is even and \(\det(\operatorname{Sym}^2g)=(\det g)^{n+1}\). Independently the phases in this family have \(\prod_i d_i\) choices; taking consecutive distinct even degrees gives superpolynomially many in the dense input length.

* \(n=10,m=35\) (more generally one orbit \(n\equiv2\bmod4\)). Reflecting one based output coordinate leaves an ordinary transporter and makes every congruence have combined determinant \(-1\). This is an explicit odd-only case of the even-conjugator problem. For **three independent modes**, \(\lambda=-1\) restores the third determinant and preserves the second one.
* \(n=12,m=54\) (more generally one orbit \(n\equiv0\bmod4\)). The same reflection gives ordinary TI YES and special-linear TI NO. Indeed \(P\) with determinant 1 forces the congruence output sign to be negative on the reflected pair, and \(\lambda^{54}=1\). This is a distinction between orbits on the same input; it does not separate the computational complexity of the two decision problems.

## 5. A compatibility obstruction that pairwise tests miss

There are actual tensors for which testing every Frobenius pair separately gives the wrong ordinary-isomorphism decision. Use three of the preceding orbits of respective lengths \(10,14,18\), keeping all internal edges. On each cross-block place every edge whose endpoint parities have prescribed sum modulo two. Both choices on a block descend. Take all three sums zero in the source and change exactly one sum to one in the target.

At either endpoint the derivative remains the diagonal algebra. Inside each orbit the old distinct equal-size internal neighborhoods still prevent containment. For vertices \(i,j\) in different orbits, \(i\) has at least five neighbors in the orbit of \(j\), whereas \(j\) has only four there. Thus no inclusion is possible. The symmetrizer is again scalar: inside each orbit the surviving graph stays connected and there are surviving cross edges. The fields have distinct degrees, and a transporter therefore has one shift \(k_i\) per orbit. Each cross condition separately permits a shift pair, but the required equations \(k_i+k_j=\epsilon_{ij}\bmod2\) sum to \(0=1\) around the triangle. No congruence or independent-mode ordinary transporter exists. Changing two of the three sums gives an ordinary transporter by shifting their common endpoint. This demonstrates the need for a global compatibility calculation on raw tensors in the class.

## 6. Repeated field degrees over the ternary field

**Stronger ternary criterion.** Drop the pairwise-distinct degree hypothesis. Compute the individual simple factors and the following colors. Initialize each field by its degree and \(\dim R_{ii}\). Repeatedly refine its color by the multiset of pairs consisting of the color of another field \(j\) and the integer \(\dim R_{ij}\). Use common color codes for both endpoints. If all final colors are unique within each endpoint, the entire theorem, including witness production, holds. More generally it holds when the product of the factorials of the resulting source color multiplicities is at most \(L^C\) for a fixed \(C\), where \(L\) is the dense input length. A color-multiplicity mismatch is an immediate NO.

To recover the factors at \(p=3\), compute \(B=\ker(x\mapsto x^3-x)\) in \(D\), a product of copies of the ground field. For every vector \(v\) of a ground-field basis of \(B\) and each \(t=0,1,2\), multiply the current idempotent pieces by \(1-(v-t1)^2\), discarding zero pieces. For each \(v\) these three indicators partition each old piece. Two distinct coordinates differ on some element of \(B\) and hence on some basis element. The resulting nonzero pieces are therefore exactly the primitive idempotents, irrespective of how often a degree occurs. The number of intermediate pieces is at most \(n\). The same proof works at any fixed odd prime, using all \(t\) and exponent \(p-1\).

Every congruence permutes these primitive pieces and preserves each displayed color and intersection dimension. Enumerate the color-preserving bijections, at most the stated factorial product, aligning fields of equal degree afresh for each. For each bijection, Sections 2 and 3 apply unchanged: holding that permutation fixed, the relative homogeneous group of phases fixes every factor. The union of the answer sets over the enumerated bijections is exhaustive. The color refinement stabilizes in at most the combined number of vertices. This also allows arbitrarily many copies of one field degree. For instance take \(r\) copies of degree \(d=4r+6\). In orbit \(s\), for \(s=1,\ldots,r\), take the undirected edges of differences \(1,3,\ldots,2s-1\), and no cross edges. Each neighborhood is an interval of length \(2s<d/2\) in the other parity class. The same neighborhood and complement arguments as in Section 4 give the full diagonal derivative and scalar symmetrizer. The values \(\dim R_{ss}=sd\) give distinct intrinsic colors for all \(r\) copies. Neither a field label nor an ordering of these repeated factors is supplied.

**Uniform refinement for binary primes.** Even with repeated degrees one can sometimes separate every field deterministically without splitting a ground-field polynomial or iterating over all \(p\) elements. Begin with the exact-degree idempotents from Section 1, keeping the entire piece in case of repetition. Suppose the current pieces are \(C,E\); write \(U_C=1_CU\). Use \(R_{CE}=R\cap(U_C U_E)\), using the symmetric square for \(C=E\). For \(A\in D_C\), form the linear functional
\[
       A\longmapsto {\rm Tr}_F(\partial_A|R_{CE}).
\]
The restriction exists by the geometric monomial decomposition already proved; that proof uses maximal étaleness, regardless of degree repetitions. The trace pairing of the separable algebra \(D_C\) is nondegenerate (over the closure it is the usual dot product). Solve linear equations to obtain the unique \(z_{CE}\in D_C\) representing this functional via \({\rm Tr}_{D_C/F}(A z_{CE})\).

In geometric coordinates the coefficient at a vertex of \(C\) is its incidence count in the monomials of \(R_{CE}\), reduced modulo \(p\), with squares counted twice. This number lies between zero and \(n+1\) before reduction. It is constant on each Frobenius orbit. For each distinct residue of \(0,\ldots,n+1\), take the annihilator of \(z_{CE}-t1_C\) inside \(D_C\). These ideals isolate exactly the pieces with that count. Refine by all these partitions simultaneously, using the existing degree and successive count tuples as intrinsic labels; repeat to stability. All operations are ground-field linear algebra, with at most \(n\) strict refinements and polynomially many possible residues. If every resulting piece of degree label \(d\) has dimension \(d\), each is a single field and the labels align them uniquely. Sections 2 and 3 now give the full deterministic witness theorem at a binary prime under this **strictly more general** criterion. Endpoint labels that disagree preclude transport. Coincident residues may leave a larger piece; the dimension test certifies when refinement succeeds.

For the repeated-degree example just above, with a single initial color the trace coefficients are \(2s\), one per orbit. Whenever \(p>2r\), they distinguish every orbit already in the first round, and all the geometric arguments apply in odd characteristic. This gives a uniform binary-prime family with arbitrarily many copies of the **same** extension field and no supplied matching.

## Verification and scope

The proof above is written mathematics. The accompanying Lean file checks the additive-coset assembly identity once a base point is known. It does not formalize the algorithm, the example family, or the full TI question. The executable control builds dense ternary tensors from primitive polynomial presentations of degrees 10 and 12. For each circular difference \(j\ne1,3\) up to \(n/2\) it uses \(xy\) when \(j=0\) and \(xy^{3^j}+x^{3^j}y\) otherwise. In geometric coordinates these read the surviving monomials of that difference; row reduction removes duplicate coordinates for the antipodal difference. It computes both linear spaces from the table, checks the quotient actions on every input pair for a primitive scalar and Frobenius, and verifies both reflected outcomes. The primitive-polynomial check certifies that the residue of the variable has order \(3^n-1\), hence generates every nonzero ring element. A separate combinatorial control enumerates all 2,520 phase tuples for each of the eight triangle parity targets and computes the geometric trace coefficients for four degree-22 blocks over the field of order 11. The control uses the existing repository's ternary matrix routines. Run it with Python 3 as `python FrobeniusRelationTransport/controls.py` in the repository root. [Existing matrix routines](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/5d2a1a370c051bbdcb0406ecce525b2125b730e0/LargeRelationEvenTI/CycleCompiler.py)

The earlier projective-mode theorem begins with a specified projective map or a complete list of candidates. The earlier monomial relation proof works over the ground field. The new sufficient class reconstructs nonsplit fields and compatible mode maps from the dense input. Unrestricted TI versus special-linear TI remains open in this work.
