# Determinant signs from covariantly recovered contraction hyperplanes

Fix a finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$. Let $T,S$ be explicit 3-tensors. In one mode let $M_T(x)$ and $M_S(x)$ be their matrix contraction pencils, with parameter $x$ in the dual of that mode. Take the greatest common divisor of the nonzero maximal generic-rank minors of each pencil. Suppose the normals to the $F$-rational linear factors of this divisor span the physical mode. Then the full transporter determinant coset, or its emptiness, is computable in randomized polynomial time using ordinary TI decisions. If the chosen pencils are square and their nonzero determinants split into $F$-rational linear factors, the reduction is deterministic under that promise, including repeated linear factors. No factorization, frame, or transporter is supplied.

In particular this gives a raw balanced scalar-stabilizer family over $\mathbb F_3$, of arbitrarily large even mode size, on which all determinant signs are decided in polynomial time using ordinary TI. The result works by reducing the set of possible destinations for an exact pin, so it does not assume a general hyperplane-membership query.

## A polynomial covariant set of candidate destinations

Suppose in one physical mode we can compute on each input a polynomial-size set of projective lines which spans that mode and is carried to its counterpart by every ordinary transporter. Take a basis from the source lines. To find one transporter, start with an ordinary yes answer, then guess the images of that ordered basis one at a time. For each image try the polynomial list of nonzero vectors on the target lines, keeping all previous exact pins. The next correct image exists, and the entire first mode is thereby specified. A constant-signature list-pin gadget fixes a vector and a nilpotent shift on an auxiliary sort, forcing its whole ordered basis, and includes the map from that basis to the chosen vectors. The fixed-signature tensor-system compiler makes each query ordinary TI. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

With the first-mode matrix $F_1$ specified, the other two modes are recovered jointly by constructive ordered-module isomorphism. In the tensor convention $B_i=X(\sum_j(F_1)_{ij}A_j)Y^{\mathsf T}$, the equations are $XC_i=B_iZ$, with $C_i=\sum_j(F_1)_{ij}A_j$, $Z=Y^{-\mathsf T}$, and $X,Z$ invertible. These are the intertwiners for ordered representations of the parallel-arrow quiver. Use both vertex projections and the arrows as the module generators. [Brooksbank–Luks](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

If the whole determinant coset is needed, the projected stabilizer in this mode acts faithfully on the computed nonzero-vector bank because the bank spans. Precompute its pointwise-basis-chain orbits in the bank by the same exact pins. Maintain the subgroup generated by selected projected automorphisms and their actual lifts. If one of its chain orbits misses a known destination, lift using greedy completion and the ordered-module solver, then adjoin that automorphism. This at least doubles the current subgroup order. When every chain orbit agrees, orbit–stabilizer and the trivial pointwise stabilizer of a basis prove equality with the full projected group. All explicitly computed group operations are on the polynomial-size bank, and at most $r^2\log_2 q$ new lifts are needed in a mode of dimension $r$.

The kernel of the chosen-mode projection is the unit group of $\{(X,Z):XA_i=A_iZ\ \forall i\}$. This algebra is obtained by linear equations. Computing the semisimple quotient and the simple multiplicities on the radical layers of the two natural modules gives its full determinant image by finite-field norm powers, inverting the third-mode values for $Z^{-\mathsf T}$. The determinants of the selected lifts and this kernel image generate the stabilizer determinant image. Multiplying by the determinant of any computed transporter gives exactly the desired coset. If the stabilizer is already scalar and every mode size is divisible by $q-1$, its determinant image is trivial and a single witness settles the sign. [Ivanyos–Qiao, finite-algebra algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03495)

## Extracting the bank from determinantal divisors

For a matrix pencil $M(x)$ over $F$ of generic rank $\rho>0$, let $\Delta_M$ be the gcd of its nonzero $\rho$-minors in $F[x]$. The ideal generated by all $\rho$-minors is unchanged by invertible constant row and column operations, by Cauchy–Binet in both directions. Hence both the generic rank and the gcd up to a scalar are covariant under the tensor action on the pencil. For a homogeneous linear factor written $\ell(x)=x(v)$, the associated line is $\langle v\rangle$ in the physical, undualized mode. Indeed, the substitution $x\mapsto g_1^{\mathsf T}x$ sends the coefficient vector $v$ to $g_1v$. These are the correct covariance signs for the exact vector-pin procedure.

One can extract all rational linear factors without constructing the exponentially long list of minors. Evaluate $M$ at a uniform point of a polynomial-size field extension $K/F$. A fixed nonzero $\rho$-minor has degree $\rho$, so with probability at least $1-\rho/|K|$ the observed rank equals $\rho$. Choose a nonzero minor at the sample point of size equal to the observed rank. Its indices alone define a nonzero polynomial minor $P$ over $F$ with a polynomial-size determinant circuit. Compute all its rational linear factors using sparse-factor extraction for circuits. This step can detect linear factors even with positive-characteristic multiplicities. [Kaltofen, §§5–6](https://kaltofen.math.ncsu.edu/bibliography/89/Ka89_slpfac.pdf)

For each candidate $\ell$, evaluate $M$ at a uniform point over $K$ in the hyperplane $\ell=0$. Keep $\ell$ exactly if the observed rank is below $\rho$. A true divisor factor passes at every point. For a false candidate, some $\rho$-minor does not vanish identically on that hyperplane, since a polynomial vanishing identically there is divisible by $\ell$. That minor has degree at most $\rho$, so the false candidate passes with probability at most $\rho/|K|$. With at most $\rho$ distinct candidates, one union bound controls all such tests, including both input tensors. Choose the extension and factoring accuracy for any desired inverse-polynomial error. On the success event the bank is exactly the covariant list and the subsequent decision computations are exact. If the list does not span return that the sufficient criterion is unavailable. The case $\rho=0$ corresponds to the zero pencil and can be handled directly.

This approach also applies when the ordinary determinant is identically zero or the pencil is rectangular, as long as the computed factor lines span the chosen mode. The number of lines is at most $\rho$. In particular a single singular balanced pencil cannot meet this spanning criterion because $\rho$ is smaller than its parameter dimension. Conversely rectangular tensor formats are allowed whenever their generic rank is at least the dimension of an eligible parameter mode.

## Deterministic extraction for a split square determinant

Suppose $P(x)=\det M(x)\ne 0$ is a product $c\prod_i \ell_i(x)^{m_i}$ of pairwise nonproportional $F$-rational linear factors, with $d=\deg P=\sum_i m_i$. The deterministic method below uses determinant evaluation only. It does not require the exponents or the number of distinct factors.

In the parameter coordinates $x_1,\ldots,x_r$, the moment curve $x(t)=(1,t,\ldots,t^{r-1})$ meets each hyperplane of a nonzero linear form at most $r-1$ times. Work over an explicit extension field $K/F$ of polynomial cardinality larger than $d+1+(r-1)(d+d(d-1)/2)$. A fixed finite field permits deterministic construction of such an extension by testing monic polynomials of its chosen small degree. Find $a=x(t)$ with $P(a)\ne0$ by evaluating the curve. Then some curve point $b$ has every ratio $\ell_i(b)/\ell_i(a)$ nonzero and all these ratios distinct. Each failure is the vanishing of one nonzero linear form in $b$, and the field-size bound avoids all these finitely many hyperplanes.

For each candidate $b$, interpolate $P(a+u b)$ as a univariate polynomial in $u$, using $d+1$ evaluations at distinct $u$-values on that affine line. Retain a degree-$d$ candidate with the largest number of distinct roots in $K$, counted by enumerating $K$. This obtains one with every ratio distinct, as that is the only way to attain the number of distinct factors. Each root point $x_i=a+u_i b$ lies on precisely one hyperplane, with multiplicity $m_i$ computed in the univariate polynomial. For any direction $v$, the order-$m_i$ coefficient of $P(x_i+t v)$ is
$$
 c\ell_i(v)^{m_i}\prod_{j\ne i}\ell_j(x_i)^{m_j}.
$$
It vanishes exactly when $\ell_i(v)=0$, regardless of characteristic and multiplicity. Find a coordinate $e_b$ where it does not vanish, then for each $e_j$ test $e_j-\lambda e_b$ for all $\lambda\in F$. The unique zero reveals each normalized coefficient $\ell_i(e_j)/\ell_i(e_b)$. The number of directions, interpolations and field points is polynomial. This uses the split promise, and does not claim deterministic recognition of that promise for arbitrary determinant circuits.

## An explicit balanced scalar-stabilizer family

Let the mode dimensions all equal $n\geq 4$, and write $J$ for the regular strictly upper shift with entries $J_{i,i+1}=1$. Use the pencil
$$
 M(x)=\operatorname{diag}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)+(x_1+\cdots+x_n)J.
$$
It is concise because the slice labels have independent diagonals and some slice combination is invertible. Its determinant is $\prod_i x_i$, so its rational factor normals span. We verify that every tensor stabilizer is a scalar triple.

First the parameter map of a stabilizer is monomial, by the determinant hyperplanes. Put $L(x)=\sum_i x_i$. When $L(x)\ne0$, the submatrix in rows $1,\ldots,n-1$ and columns $2,\ldots,n$ is lower triangular with diagonal entries $L(x)$. Thus the rank is at least $n-1$. At $x=e_i-e_j$ the rank is two since the matrix is diagonal. As $n-1>2$, its image under the monomial parameter map must have $L=0$ too. Varying $i,j$ shows the monomial multipliers all coincide. These arguments apply in $\mathbb F_3$ and also after base extension.

To distinguish the hyperplanes themselves, over an algebraic closure take the open points of $x_i=0$ with all other coordinates nonzero and $L(x)=1$. On these points the right kernel of $M(x)$ is spanned by the vector with $j$-th coordinate
$$
 (-1)^{j-1}\prod_{h<j}x_h\quad (j\le i),
$$
and zero entries for $j>i$. The kernel vectors span the entire first $i$ coordinate directions. If $i<n$, use one of the remaining coordinates to satisfy $L=1$. The displayed monomials in the first $i-1$ freely variable coordinates are independent. If $i=n$, eliminate $x_{n-1}=1-\sum_{h<n-1}x_h$. The resulting products of degree $0,\ldots,n-1$ have distinct top degrees and nonzero highest terms. In both cases independence persists on the indicated nonempty open set. Allowing all points with $L\ne0$ gives the same prefix span by the kernel recurrence. A stabilizer must therefore preserve the distinct kernel-span dimension $i$ of each hyperplane, so its parameter permutation is trivial. Its first-mode map is scalar.

Absorb that scalar into another mode. The two remaining ordered maps then have an invertible pair $X,Z$ satisfying $X(E_{ii}+J)=(E_{ii}+J)Z$ for all $i$. Subtract the $j$-th equation. For each off-diagonal row-column pair choose $j$ distinct from both indices. The equations for $E_{ii}-E_{jj}$ force every off-diagonal entry of both $X,Z$ to vanish and match their diagonal entries. Finally $XJ=JZ$ equates consecutive diagonal entries. Thus the ordered maps are inverse scalars, as required. For $F=\mathbb F_3$ and even $n$, each scalar triple has determinant one in all modes. The algorithm consequently determines all three signs of any transporter from raw representatives in this ordinary orbit. This gives unbounded widths and an exact scalar stabilizer.

### Deterministic recognition of this orbit

For the displayed model one can recognize the raw ordinary orbit and recover a transporter without any TI oracle. On a candidate input of the same format run the split-detector above to produce candidate factor normals, rejecting if it fails to find $n$ independent lines. For tensors in the model orbit these are exactly the determinant hyperplanes. Work in their dual coordinate basis. The pullback of $L$ has nonzero coefficients in all these coordinates. Its coefficient ratios can be read from the ranks at $e_1+t e_j$ for $t\in F^\times$. There is exactly one $t$ of rank two for each $j>1$, namely the value for which $L(e_1+t e_j)=0$. Normalize the coefficient at $e_1$ to one.

Find the kernel-span dimensions used above by sampling a single affine line in each hyperplane $\ell_i=0$. Choose $a,b$ in that hyperplane over the same type of polynomial-size extension, with $L(a)=1$, $L(b)=0$, and all coordinates of $b$ except $i$ nonzero. To find $b$, use the moment curve in any basis of $\ker L\cap\ker\ell_i$, avoiding the coordinate hyperplanes. None of their restrictions to this subspace is zero, because the $n-1$ coefficients of $L$ there are nonzero and $n-1\geq 3$. The affine line $a+ub$ has at most $n-1$ bad parameters where another coordinate vanishes. In model coordinates each of the first $i-1$ coordinates on the line has nonzero slope. The displayed kernel vector has coordinates that are polynomials of distinct degrees $0,\ldots,i-1$ in $u$ with nonzero leading terms. At $i$ distinct nonbad parameters their evaluation matrix is invertible by the Vandermonde determinant. Thus $n$ distinct nonbad values suffice without knowing the label $i$ in advance. The dimension of the span of the computed kernel lines forces exactly the correct label.

All this data is transported from any model representative. The parameter map from the candidate to the model must identify the now-labeled hyperplanes and preserve the kernel of $L$. Those two requirements determine its monomial matrix in the recovered coordinates up to common scalar. Choose that scalar arbitrarily, since an opposite scalar in another mode corrects the tensor. Use the ordered-module solver for the other two modes. If the candidate belongs to the orbit, this procedure constructs a transporter. For arbitrary candidates, reject if any required check fails and otherwise verify the complete transporter by direct substitution before accepting. Thus a false factor list or failed model formula cannot cause false acceptance. Applying this recognition to both inputs gives the whole determinant coset on the orbit, and when $q-1\mid n$ the scalar stabilizer makes it a singleton.

## Scope relative to the coarse-query problem

The earlier exact-marking lower bound has an opaque target and supplies no covariant finite destination list. The present algorithm inspects the raw contraction pencil and proves a polynomial destination list on the stated support condition. It does not implement a universal query asking whether the image of an arbitrary vector lies in an arbitrary supplied hyperplane. The use of determinant factors and polynomial factorization has classical and project antecedents. The additions here are the generic-rank maximal-minor extraction, the exact determinant-coset reduction from a spanning candidate bank in a single mode, the elementary fixed-field split recovery including repeated factors, and the explicit scalar-stabilizer control. The unrestricted balanced case remains unresolved by this method. [Grochow–Qiao IV, §§7.3–7.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317v2#S7.SS3) · [Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)
