# A common four-layer transporter through a finite algebra

This note gives one ordinary three-tensor isomorphism instance for a coupled four-unitriangular word in a supplied matrix algebra. Its radical can have arbitrary size and nilpotence length. The cost of the determinant controller depends on the matrix degrees of the semisimple factors that contribute to the requested determinant. Extension-field degrees can grow with the input.

## 1. Collapse four layers before encoding the tensor equations

Let $F$ be a finite field and $A\subseteq\operatorname{End}_F(V)$ a given unital subalgebra. Let $J$ be its Jacobson radical and present the quotient as
$$
                 \pi:A\longrightarrow A/J=\prod_\alpha M_{d_\alpha}(E_\alpha),
$$
where each $E_\alpha/F$ is explicitly presented. Take four supplied nilpotent associative subalgebras $N_1,N_2,N_3,N_4$ of $A$. Their images are the full products of strictly upper, strictly lower, strictly upper, and strictly lower matrix subalgebras, respectively, in the displayed quotient bases. Suppose each element of $\prod_\alpha SL_{d_\alpha}(E_\alpha)$ has a residue factorization for which the conjugated cover $(*)$ below holds at every grade. This hypothesis holds automatically for the full-preimage layers. Then the full set of products is
$$
 (1+N_1)(1+N_2)(1+N_3)(1+N_4)
     = K_A :=\{g\in A^\times:\det_{E_\alpha}\pi_\alpha(g)=1
                                     \text{ for every }\alpha\}.
$$

Each factor $1+x$, $x\in N_i$, is invertible by a finite geometric series. Its residue is unitriangular. Thus the left side lies in $K_A$. Conversely the four-factor unitriangular theorem applies to each split simple factor over its field. For $d_\alpha=1$ all four layers there are the identity. Choose compatible quotient factors and lift each one to $1+N_i$. Their product agrees with $g$ modulo $J$.

Inductively let the current factors $f_i\in 1+N_i$ have product $P\equiv g\bmod J^a$. The factors act by conjugation on $J^a/J^{a+1}$, so the changes must be transported to the same endpoint. For $P_i=f_{i+1}\cdots f_4$, the sufficient linear condition is
$$
 J^a/J^{a+1}
     =\sum_{i=1}^4 P_i^{-1}\big((N_i\cap J^a)+J^{a+1}\big)P_i/J^{a+1}.
 \tag{*}
$$
If this condition holds at the chosen residue factorization, choose $\delta_i$ solving
$$
                 \sum_i P_i^{-1}\delta_i P_i=P^{-1}g-I
                           \pmod{J^{a+1}}.
$$
Replacing $f_i$ by $f_i(1+\delta_i)$ keeps each in its layer. Expanding the new product modulo $J^{a+1}$, terms with at least two $\delta_i$ lie in $J^{2a}\subseteq J^{a+1}$. The desired congruence follows. Conjugation on the graded piece only depends on the unchanged residue of $P_i$, since $1+J$ acts trivially there. Induction proves exact word coverage under $(*)$ at every grade.

In particular, the word-coverage theorem holds unconditionally for the **full preimages** $N_i=\pi^{-1}(\bar N_i)$ of the four prescribed strictly triangular subalgebras. In that case every $N_i$ contains $J$, so $(*)$ is automatic for every residue factorization. Each full preimage is nilpotent: a sufficiently long product first lands in $J$ by nilpotence of its image, and a product of sufficiently many such blocks vanishes. In fact after lifting any quotient factorization one can place the whole correction $P^{-1}g\in 1+J$ in the last layer. This gives a direct proof in the full-preimage case. When all layer residues are the identity and the desired group is $1+J$, the cover $J^a=\sum_i(N_i\cap J^a)+J^{a+1}$ suffices because all $P_i\in 1+J$. For smaller layers with nonidentity residues, the sufficient hypothesis is $(*)$, for at least one quotient factorization of each desired quotient product. [Vavilov–Smolensky–Sury, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.5414)

A nilpotent associative subalgebra of endomorphisms is strictly triangular in an adapted basis. For example, use its power images on $V$ as a filtration and refine to a full flag. Thus the factors really act unitriangularly, with their own flags. These flags can share the radical filtration while being opposite within the supplied residue matrix blocks.

## 2. Encode the complete transporter in one ordinary TI instance

Take two raw tensors and on each designated mode restrict the shared transporter to $K_A$, using the same supplied algebra presentation on both sides. Suppose all $d_\alpha=O(\log L)$, where $L$ is the explicit input bit length. The existence of the common transporter reduces deterministically to one ordinary TI instance over $F$. The other mode actions may have their own such constraints or remain ordinary actions.

Use a pointwise-fixed reference copy $V_0$ of $V$. A shift on its supplied basis and its first vector fix the reference pointwise. Attach evaluation $A\times V_0\to V$ of an element of $A$ as a linear operator. If the actual mode map is $g$, the induced map on $A$ must be $x\mapsto gx$ by equality of evaluations on every basis vector of $V_0$. This exists invertibly exactly for $g\in A^\times$. Necessity follows from $g=gI\in A$, and an element of a unital matrix algebra invertible as a matrix has its inverse in the algebra by Cayley–Hamilton. Sufficiency uses the invertible left multiplication map itself.

For each residue factor use its natural column module $W_\alpha=E_\alpha^{d_\alpha}$ and the surjective linear map $A\to W_\alpha$, $x\mapsto\pi_\alpha(x)e_1$. This forces the action on $W_\alpha$ to be $\pi_\alpha(g)$. Include the spaces $\Lambda^j_{E_\alpha} W_\alpha$ and the maps $\Lambda^{j}_{E_\alpha} W_\alpha\times W_\alpha\to\Lambda^{j+1}_{E_\alpha} W_\alpha$ for all $1\le j<d_\alpha$, viewed as bilinear maps over $F$. Induction forces their actions because elementary wedges span the next space over $F$ when the input vectors range over their $F$-bases. The action on the top space is multiplication by $\det_{E_\alpha}\pi_\alpha(g)$. Fix its standard nonzero top vector, so the residue determinant equals one. The sum of the $F$-dimensions in this construction is at most $\sum_\alpha [E_\alpha:F]\,2^{d_\alpha}$. Thus it is polynomial under the stated matrix-degree bound, without a bound on the individual field degrees.

The data compile with a constant number of block types. Pack all residue columns, exterior grades, and marks into direct sums by role. Use the coordinate action of a split label algebra $F^b$ to mark each summand. Give this label algebra a directed-path endomorphism on its primitive idempotents, so its action fixes every label. Include one packed linear quotient map, one packed wedge multiplication relation, and the sum of the top markers on their distinct labels. For the wedge multiplication the same column module may be included both in the direct sum of active degrees and in the repeated slot of the packed map, joined by an inclusion. This preserves the common action. The actual tensor is another relation on the three raw modes.

For precision, any such constant list of linear maps, bilinear maps, and marked vectors admits the following shared-action encoding. Pad each lower-arity relation to three slots by scalar anchor lines. The tensor on the three anchor lines has coefficient one, so the three line actions have product one. For each module type put covariant and dual copies in each of the three modes. Padded identities between cross-mode opposite-variance copies form a six-cycle. Dividing each block action by its mode's anchor scalar, an identity relation requires the two normalized maps to be inverse transposes. This synchronizes all copies of the same variance. Use fresh non-anchor copies for every relation slot and connect them to the corresponding cores. The blocks realize exactly the shared action, and every genuine system isomorphism extends with anchor scalars one. Remove the partitions by Theorem 3.12 of Part V, one mode at a time. Its forward construction extends the action block diagonally in the other two modes, and its reverse extracts the data diagonal blocks. Hence the restrictions on the unprocessed modes persist. A constant number of part types and explicit polynomial dimensions give one polynomial-size query. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Theorem 3.12](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S3.SS3.SSS2)

For the full-preimage layers of §1, every ordinary isomorphism of the constructed tensors decodes to exact complete word actions on the raw tensors. The factorization argument produces a word, whose intermediate tensors are its actual successive images. Conversely a word carrying the source to the target supplies one controller action. No factor is an independent isomorphism variable in the query.

As an explicit unbounded-width example, take $A=M_2(E[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^s))$ acting on its natural module, with $E/F$ a supplied finite extension. The residue matrix degree is two, while both the module dimension and the nilpotence index can grow. The coupled word allows all off-diagonal and diagonal radical entries. The result applies to arbitrary tensor coefficients on those ambient spaces when the word action, or equivalently $K_A$, is the prescribed mode action.

## 3. Prescribe the raw determinant and omit correctable factors

Consider the more general action $A^\times$ on a raw mode. Let $m_\alpha$ be the total multiplicity of the simple $M_{d_\alpha}(E_\alpha)$-module in the filtration of $V$ by $J^aV$. A unit preserves the filtration, and its induced action on each simple is the residue column action. Consequently
$$
          \det_F(g)=\prod_\alpha
            \operatorname{Norm}_{E_\alpha/F}
                    \big(\det_{E_\alpha}\pi_\alpha(g)\big)^{m_\alpha}.
$$
The multiplicities are computable from the dimensions of the central-idempotent images on the graded modules, divided by the $F$-dimensions of their simple modules. Use the algebra controller of §2 and leave the wedge top lines free, with their forced residue determinants. A residue factor with $m_\alpha\equiv0\bmod(q-1)$, where $q=|F|$, contributes one without any wedge computation.

For a needed residue factor, the $E_\alpha$-line carries multiplication by a scalar $\lambda$. Link it to a fresh $E_\alpha$-line by relative Frobenius $x\mapsto x^q$. This is $F$-linear and forces multiplication by $\lambda^q$ on the fresh line. Iterate through $[E_\alpha:F]$ copies and multiply the conjugate scalars using field multiplication as a bilinear map between labeled lines. This forces the scalar $\operatorname{Norm}_{E_\alpha/F}(\lambda)$ on the output. Link the subspace $F\cdot1$ of that line by an inclusion, giving the corresponding $F$-scalar action. Further scalar products and repeated squaring apply the multiplicity exponents and multiply all contributions. Pin only this final value, with source marker 1 and target marker the requested determinant. All field computations, including relative Frobenius, are polynomial in the explicit field representation. The maps and marks pack by labels as before. Thus only the residue matrix degrees of the nontrivial contributions need to be logarithmic. Multiplicity and norm accounting here is the usual semisimple determinant calculation. [Hofmann, finite-ring unit groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00266)

There is a further exact reduction of the required matrix degrees when actual determinant corrections are available. For three mode algebras $A_i$, let $C\le(F^\times)^3$ be the determinant image of supplied generators in $\prod_i A_i^\times$ that stabilize the source tensor. Check the generators directly on the source. At fixed $F$ compute $C$ by enumeration in the constant-size determinant group. To ask for a determinant triple $\delta$, enforce just the characters trivial on $C$. These characters separate the quotient $(F^\times)^3/C$, by duality of finite abelian groups. Each character has an exponent in each mode, so the same norm-and-product controllers implement it. Skip a residue factor whenever every resulting exponent $c_i m_\alpha$ vanishes modulo $q-1$. A transporter passing all the pins has determinant in $\delta C$; multiplying it on the right by an actual source stabilizer corrects the determinant and preserves the tensor equation. The reverse is immediate. The subgroup generated by the given corrections need not be the whole stabilizer. The guarantee is conditional on the supplied mode-algebra restriction, or on a separate proof that all desired raw transporters admit that restriction.

## 4. The endpoint tests these constructions avoid

A finite algebra cannot keep two independent scalar evaluations synchronized merely by requiring a common unit. If a finite-dimensional associative $F$-algebra $B$ has two distinct unital $F$-algebra maps to $F$, they are projections to different $F$ factors of its semisimple quotient. Every pair of endpoint invertible matrices lifts to an invertible matrix over $B$. To see this, prescribe the pair on those two simple factors, use identity matrices on the others, and lift modulo the nilpotent radical. Thus replacing polynomial paths over $F[t]$ by an algebra with two scalar endpoints loses the determinant agreement used in the existing polynomial-ring reduction.

There is an analogous exact boundary for purely linear-arrow restrictions. Endomorphisms of a finite linear diagram form a finite-dimensional unital algebra $B$. Let $\rho:B\to M_n(F)$ project the raw mode and $b:B\to D$ jointly project auxiliary modules required to be fixed pointwise. Suppose the induced actions of $u\in B^\times$ with $b(u)=1$ contain $SL_n(F)$, for $n\ge2$. Its elementary transvections linearly generate the off-diagonal units after subtracting the identity. Their products give the diagonal units. Hence $\rho(B)=M_n(F)$. The ideal $\rho(\ker b)$ contains a nonzero difference of a realized transvection from the identity, so it is the whole $M_n(F)$. Therefore $(\rho,b)$ surjects onto $M_n(F)\times b(B)$. Units lift along a quotient of finite-dimensional algebras: on the semisimple quotient fill missing simple factors by the identity, then correct the target discrepancy by a lifted radical unit. This gives an extension with $b(u)=1$ for every $g\in GL_n(F)$. When $|F^\times|>1$, a universal linear-arrow controller of this kind cannot isolate $SL_n(F)$. The bilinear norm, exterior, and collapsed-layer relations above yield exact positive controllers on their stated classes.
