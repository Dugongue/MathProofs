# Point counting for determinant signs of nonsingular tensor pencils

Fix a positive odd integer $n$. For $T\in(\mathbb F_3^n)^{\otimes3}$, let $f_T(x)$ be the homogeneous degree-$n$ determinant of its slice matrix in mode one. Suppose $f_T(x)\ne0$ for every nonzero $x\in\mathbb F_3^n$. For each projective line take its first-pivot representative in the raw basis and put
$$
  P(T)=\prod_{[x]\in\mathbb P^{n-1}(\mathbb F_3)}f_T(x).
$$
The product lies in $\{1,-1\}$. The purpose of this note is to evaluate it in polynomial time on the following promise class using only the raw tensor.

Fix constants $D,K,C$. An absolute factor $h$ of $f_T$ has degree $d\le D$ and uses $k\le K$ essential linear variables. Normalize $h$ by one nonzero coefficient in the raw coordinates. Its coefficient field is $E=\mathbb F_{3^e}$ with $ed=n$. Write $h(x)=H(\ell_1(x),\ldots,\ell_k(x))$ using a basis of the essential linear forms over $E$. Let $W$ be the image of the raw $\mathbb F_3^n$ under this list of forms, inside $E^k$. Require that $W$ is the common zero set in $E^k$ of additive polynomials of the form
$$
        \sum_{j=1}^k \sum_{s=0}^{w} a_{j,s} y_j^{3^s},
        \qquad a_{j,s}\in E,\qquad
        w\le C\log_3(2+n).
$$
The condition is independent of the basis of essential forms. Composing with an invertible $E$-linear change of the $y_j$ preserves the degree and additive form of these equations. It is also preserved under coefficient Frobenius and raw mode changes.

**Theorem.** Under these promises, $P(T)$ is computable in deterministic polynomial time without a supplied factor, factor field, basis of forms, or additive equations. Consequently SL-TI on such pairs reduces to one ordinary TI instance over $\mathbb F_3$. The case $k=d$ meets the additive condition with $w=0$. The method also allows $k>d$, for example an $E$-linear subspace read in coordinates after bounded Frobenius substitutions. The theorem uses the nonsingularity and bounded absolute degree promises. It does not claim a polynomial-time recognizer for the whole promise from arbitrary inputs.

## 1. Why this sign suffices

A transporter $g=(g_1,g_2,g_3)$ in the displayed tensor convention gives $f_{gT}(x)=\det(g_2)\det(g_3)f_T(g_1^{\mathsf T}x)$. The number of projective lines is $(3^n-1)/2$, which is odd. For $a\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_3)$, write $a r_L=\eta_L r_{aL}$ on the raw projective representatives. The product of the $\eta_L$ is the sign of the permutation of all nonzero vectors under $a$, since exchanging two two-element line fibers is even. This sign is $\det a$. Indeed elementary transvections have odd order and contribute even parity, and a one-coordinate sign flip acts by $3^{n-1}$ transpositions. These operations generate the group. Homogeneity of odd degree now gives
$$
        P(gT)=\det(g_1)\det(g_2)\det(g_3)P(T).
$$
An ordinary transporter of product determinant one can be corrected to determinant one in each mode by central scalar matrices, since $n$ is odd and their scalar product can be one. Compare $P$ on the two tensors and submit the original ordinary TI pair if the values agree. Unequal values map to a fixed no-instance.

## 2. Recover the absolute factors from the determinant circuit

First $f=f_T$ is irreducible over $\mathbb F_3$. If it had a nonconstant factor of degree less than $n$, that factor could be taken homogeneous and would have a nonzero $\mathbb F_3$-zero by Chevalley–Warning. Since $\mathbb F_3$ is perfect, its irreducible polynomial has distinct absolute factors. To check the last assertion directly, a repeated absolute factor divides every first derivative, so the field conjugates would make their product divide every first derivative. They would all be zero by degree. Then $f$ is a cube, since its coefficients lie in a perfect field. Frobenius acts transitively on the absolute factors, otherwise the product of a proper orbit, after normalizing, is a proper ground-field factor. Thus, after coefficient normalization, $f=c\prod_{i=0}^{e-1}h^{(3^i)}$ with $c\in\mathbb F_3^\times$, and the coefficient field of $h$ has degree $e=n/d$. For rational $x$ this is $f(x)=c\,\mathrm N_{E/\mathbb F_3}(h(x))$.

Here is deterministic recovery on the degree promise. For any list of at most polynomially many distinct irreducible homogeneous factors of degree at most $D$, there is a polynomial-size universal list of lines $x=v+tu$, over a common extension $K_0/\mathbb F_3$ of degree $O(\log n)$, that contains a line on which every factor retains its degree, has simple roots, and shares none with any other distinct factor. The bad conditions are the vanishing of the leading coefficients, discriminants and pairwise resultants of the restrictions in $t$. Each condition is a nonzero polynomial of total degree bounded in terms of $D$ in $(u,v)$. To see that these polynomials are nonzero, choose $u$ over the algebraic closure such that each leading coefficient and directional derivative is nonzero. In the coordinates with first direction $u$, every factor is primitive as a polynomial in the first variable since its leading coefficient is a nonzero constant. It remains irreducible over the function field of the remaining coordinates, has nonzero derivative in the first variable, and distinct factors remain coprime. The generic $v$ then avoids every stated bad condition.

For completeness, bounded-degree monomial separation produces the line list without finding the factors. In $M$ parameters, to separate all monomials of degree at most the fixed bound $B$, assign coordinate $i$ the exponent $\sum_{j=0}^B i^j R^j$ for $R>BM^B$. No carries occur on any monomial of that degree. Its exponent records the number of indices and their first $B$ power sums. Newton identities over the rational integers show that these determine the multiset of indices. Substituting the resulting powers of one parameter in any nonzero bad polynomial leaves a nonzero univariate polynomial over the algebraic closure. The product of polynomially many such polynomials has polynomial degree. Enumerate more parameter values than that degree in $K_0$, of polynomial cardinality. Use a bound large enough for all candidates below. Restriction of the determinant to any line is obtained by ordinary univariate interpolation or polynomial matrix arithmetic.

Find a line with full degree and squarefree restriction by the preceding list. Over a single explicit extension $L$ of degree $[K_0:\mathbb F_3]n\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,D)$ over $\mathbb F_3$, the restriction splits. Indeed all absolute factors have coefficient degree dividing $n$, and over the compositum with $K_0$ each root has degree at most $D$ and of degree dividing $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,D)$ after taking the common splitting extension. Construct $L$ in deterministic polynomial time at fixed characteristic and embed $K_0$. Finding roots of a squarefree polynomial already known to split over $L$ also takes deterministic polynomial time. For a basis $(b_i)$ of $L/\mathbb F_3$, repeatedly split by the gcd with $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/\mathbb F_3}(b_i t)-a$ mod the polynomial for $a\in\mathbb F_3$. The trace pairing separates distinct roots. This also finds a root of the defining polynomial for $K_0$ to embed it in $L$. [Shoup on deterministic finite-field construction](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

Extend $u$ to an explicit basis and write $x=v+tu+C_0 z$, where $C_0$ lists the complementary basis vectors. For each root at $z=0$, compute its simple-root formal series $t_i(s)$ under each substitution $z=s\beta$, through degree $D$. Determine each next coefficient using the nonzero derivative at the initial root. The determinant in the truncated series ring can be computed by a division-free determinant circuit. Take every subset of at most $D$ roots, multiply its corresponding monic linear factors through the same precision, and recover the homogeneous coefficient of each order in $z$ by bounded-degree interpolation. One may use the power substitutions above, with $M=n-1$, followed by univariate interpolation. This produces polynomially many polynomial candidates. A true factor appears, since it is monic in $t$ after dividing by its leading coefficient, and its total degree is at most $D$, so the indicated jets recover all its coefficients.

Change back to raw coordinates, keep homogeneous degree $d$ candidates with $d\mid n$, and normalize their first nonzero coefficient. Keep candidates fixed by coefficient $3^{n/d}$-Frobenius. For each, form the proposed norm product of $n/d$ coefficient conjugates. Evaluate it at the original $u$ and rescale by the unique constant making that value $f(u)$, discarding a zero evaluation. These proposed products can be compared to $f$ without general polynomial identity testing on the promise. Test equality of the restrictions along every line in the universal list. The true product passes. Among distinct proposed products, there is a line retaining the degrees of all of their distinct absolute factors and separating their restricted roots. Indeed the union of these factors has polynomial cardinality, each factor has degree at most $D$, and therefore the same bounded-degree argument applies. On that line two distinct products remain distinct, since their factor multiplicities or scalar coefficients differ. Hence every candidate passing all line tests is the true product. Its individual factor is proportional to an actual absolute factor, since all actual factors have the same degree, and the candidate degree is at most their degree if we take a passing candidate of smallest degree. More explicitly, at least one passing candidate has the true absolute degree. Every passing candidate of smaller positive degree would divide the true product yet have degree less than any of its absolute factors, which is impossible.

Recover $E$ as the fixed subfield of the $3^e$-Frobenius on $L$ by linear algebra. Present it explicitly in polynomial time. For example, find an element avoiding its distinct maximal proper subfields by choosing its basis coordinates consecutively to keep the sum of their conditional membership densities below one. This sum starts below one, since there are at most $\log_2 e$ such subfields each of dimension at most $e/2$, and their proportions can be computed by Gaussian elimination. At the end the sum is an integer, hence zero. The powers of the resulting degree-$e$ element give a polynomial basis.

## 3. Find the essential forms and their additive image

The least linear-variable space of $h$ can be recovered by linear algebra and recursion, including when characteristic-three derivatives miss Frobenius powers. Solve for the subspace $D_0$ of directions with zero ordinary directional derivative. In coordinates $(x,y)$ along a complement and $D_0$, respectively, the polynomial is a sum of $x$-monomials whose coefficients are cubes of polynomials in $y$. A direction of full formal translation invariance lies in $D_0$. Within $D_0$, it is a direction of full invariance exactly when it is so for each cube root coefficient. Recursively find these spaces at degree at most one third and intersect them. The recursion has bounded depth. The annihilator of the result is precisely the essential form space: in adapted coordinates, full formal translation invariance is equivalent to independence of those variables. Choose any basis $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_k$, compute $H$ by an $E$-linear right inverse to the forms, and form the $\mathbb F_3$-basis of $W$ by their evaluations on the standard label basis. This image has $\mathbb F_3$-dimension $n$, since a nonzero kernel vector would have $h(x)=f(x)=0$.

Search all coefficient vectors of the allowed linearized equations by solving the linear system over $E$ for vanishing on the $\mathbb F_3$-basis of $W$. An $E$-basis of the resulting equation space has at most $k(w+1)$ elements. Every equation is additive over $\mathbb F_3$, so its joint kernel is computed by $\mathbb F_3$-linear algebra, and check that it equals $W$. When $k=d$, the inclusion $W\subseteq E^d$ is equality by dimensions, so no equations are needed.

## 4. Count the chosen signs without enumerating field elements

Both $e$ and $d$ are odd, so $-1$ is a nonsquare in $E$. In each nonzero $\mathbb F_3$-line of $W$, choose the unique representative whose first nonzero $E$-coordinate is square. This leaves $(3^n-1)/2$ representatives. In chart $j$, count solutions in $E$ of the additive equations for $W$, the equations $y_1=\cdots=y_{j-1}=0$, and
$$
        y_j=s^2,\quad s\ne0,\qquad t^2=H(y).
$$
This number is four times the number of representatives in chart $j$ on which $H$ is square. Each $s,t$ then has two choices. The value of $H$ is nonzero by the nonsingularity promise. Enforce $s\ne0$ by subtracting the count with $s=0$. The number of variables is at most $K+2$, the degree bound is polynomial in $n$, and the number of equations is $O(\log n)$. Count common zeros by inclusion–exclusion from the counts of zeros of the products of all nonempty sublists of equations. Their zero sets are unions. This requires polynomially many hypersurface point counts, each in polynomial bit time by Lauder–Wan Theorem 1, with fixed characteristic and number of variables. It does not require the hypersurfaces to be smooth. The number of nonsquare values is the total number of representatives minus the square count, and its parity gives the product of the $E$-quadratic signs of $H$. [Lauder–Wan Theorem 1](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/lauder/papers/LWZetaOct26MSRI.pdf)

To convert this product to the original raw projective representatives, compute the flag $U_j=\{x : \ell_i(x)=0\text{ for }i<j\}$ in the $\mathbb F_3$ label space. Choose an $\mathbb F_3$-basis adapted to the flag with earlier groups preceding later groups. A first-pivot representative in $U_j\setminus U_{j+1}$ projects to a first-pivot representative in the induced frame for $\ell_j(U_j)$, with $3^{\dim U_{j+1}}$ lifts. This multiplicity is odd. The sign used to convert such a representative to the square convention is the quadratic character of its $\ell_j$-value, because $-1$ is a nonsquare in $E$. For an $\mathbb F_3$-subspace of $E$ in an ordered basis $b_1,\ldots,b_m$, the product of its first-pivot representatives is the product of $P_{W_i}(b_i)$, with $W_i$ the span of the subsequent basis and $P_{W_i}(X)=\prod_{w\in W_i}(X-w)$. Compute these evaluations with
$$
   P_{W+\mathbb F_3 b}(X)=P_W(X)^3-P_W(b)^2P_W(X),
$$
which follows by grouping the three translates of $W$ and using additivity of $P_W$. This computes the correction for every chart in polynomial time. The sign change from the adapted frame to the raw frame is the determinant of the known basis change, by Section 1. Finally $\chi_{\mathbb F_3}(\mathrm N_{E/\mathbb F_3}a)=\chi_E(a)$, and the constant $c$ contributes its own sign because the number of labels is odd. Multiplying all these contributions gives exactly $P(T)$.

This proof reuses the projective-sign covariance, the additive subspace product recurrence, and the good-line Hensel strategy from earlier MathProofs work. The extra computation is a multivariate point count over the coefficient field of an absolute factor, with raw recovery of the bounded-degree additive constraints. It can cover images with overlapping Frobenius coordinate groups. Exact publication priority is unestablished. The unrestricted nonsingular branch, and hence the general TI versus SL-TI question, remains open. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

## 5. An unbounded nonlinear example and the equal-width limit

The case $k=d$ should not be interpreted as an infinite nonlinear family for each fixed $d$. If $k=d\ge3$, then $W=E^d$, so $H$ would be an absolutely irreducible degree-$d$ polynomial with no nonzero $E$-zero. For $d$ fixed, Lang–Weil gives an $E$-point on the projective hypersurface $H=0$ for all sufficiently large $|E|$. The nonlinear growing examples below have $k>d$. [Lang–Weil](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372655)

Let $e\ge3$ be odd, $E=\mathbb F_{3^e}$, and $L=\mathbb F_{3^{3e}}$, with $n=3e$. Take the pencil $M_x(y)=xy+x^3y^{3^e}$ on $L$. If it were singular for $x\ne0$, there would be a nonzero $y$ with $y^{3^e-1}=-x^{-2}$. The norm to $E$ of the left side is $1$, whereas that of the right side is $-\mathrm N_{L/E}(x)^{-2}$, a nonsquare since $e$ is odd. This proves nonsingularity.

Over a splitting field, index the coordinates $x_i$ by the $3$-Frobenius embeddings of $L$. On the coordinates of $y$, the map $y\mapsto y^{3^e}$ cycles each residue class of $i\bmod e$ with length three. The determinant of the corresponding diagonal-plus-cycle block is
$$
   \prod_{i\equiv j\bmod e}x_i+\prod_{i\equiv j+1\bmod e}x_i.
$$
Each of these is absolutely irreducible since it is linear in one of its six variables, with relatively prime coefficient and constant term in the other variables. The blocks give distinct factors for $e\ge3$. One factor, defined over $E$, is the sum of the norms of $x$ and $x^3$ from $L$ to $E$ on raw rational labels. Written in a fixed $E$-basis of $L$, the $E$-coordinates $z_1,z_2,z_3$ of $x$ and the forms $w_i=z_i^{(3)}$ obtained by cubing the raw coefficients are six independent $E$-linear forms on the extended raw space. This follows from their two disjoint sets of split coordinates and the invertible basis changes within each set. They span the essential form space, since the six derivatives of the displayed two-monomial factor are linearly independent. On raw rational inputs $w_i=z_i^3$. Therefore $W=\{(z,w):w_i=z_i^3\}$ has the required additive equations with maximum Frobenius offset $1$, in unbounded size and with $k=6>d=3$. This example is a member of the previously analyzed binomial twisted-pencil family and illustrates the reach of the new computation. It does not provide an example with only scalar stabilizers.

## 6. The exact determinant product over a fixed field

The algorithm and reduction hold over every fixed finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$ when $\gcd(n,q-1)=1$. Use coefficient $q$-Frobenius and equations $\sum_{j,s}a_{j,s}y_j^{q^s}=0$ with $w\le C\log_q(2+n)$. The conclusions use $P(T)\in F^\times$ defined by the same raw first-pivot product. The factor-recovery argument uses Chevalley–Warning over $F$ unchanged. In recovering the essential form space, use powers of the characteristic $p$ for the recursive derivative step. The finite extensions can be constructed over the fixed prime field and the specified subfields embedded by splitting, as before. Lauder–Wan is polynomial here because $p$ and the number of variables are fixed.

For $a\in\operatorname{GL}_n(F)$, the product $\prod_L\eta_L$ of projective representative rescalings is a homomorphism. It is trivial on transvections, whose order is $p$ and whose images would have order dividing $q-1$. A one-coordinate diagonal scaling by $b$ acts with rescaling $b$ on the $q^{n-1}$ representatives whose first coordinate is their pivot. Its contribution is $b^{q^{n-1}}=b$. Since these elements generate the general linear group, $\prod_L\eta_L=\det a$. The number $N=(q^n-1)/(q-1)$ of projective points is congruent to $n$ modulo $q-1$. It follows that
$$
   P(gT)=\bigl(\det(g_1)\det(g_2)\det(g_3)\bigr)^n P(T).
$$
The $n$-th power map on $F^\times$ is an automorphism. Thus equality of $P$ on an ordinarily equivalent pair exactly means that the product determinant is one, and the individual determinants can be corrected by central scalars with product one.

Since $e\mid n$, the $e$-th power map on $F^\times$ is also bijective. The $(q-1)$-powers of $E^\times$ equal the kernel of $\mathrm N_{E/F}$, by cyclicity. Each $F$-line in $W$ therefore has one representative whose first nonzero $E$-coordinate is a $(q-1)$-power. For each $b\in F^\times$, take $\gamma_b\in F^\times$ with $\gamma_b^e=b$. In chart $j$ replace the two power equations of Section 4 by $y_j=s^{q-1}$, $s\ne0$, and $H(y)=\gamma_b t^{q-1}$. Include the same flag and additive equations. The count is $(q-1)^2$ times the number of representatives whose $H$-value has norm $b$. Taking the product of the resulting powers of the $b$'s gives the product of norms. This also handles $q=2$, when the determinant target group is trivial. To recover the original representatives, the multiplier on a first-pivot label with nonzero $\ell_j$-value $a$ is $\mathrm N_{E/F}(a)^{-1/e}$, where $1/e$ denotes the inverse power map on $F^\times$. The multiplicity of each projected value in an adapted flag chart is $q^{\dim_F U_{j+1}}\equiv1\bmod(q-1)$. Use the same subspace-polynomial recurrence with $q$ in place of $3$ to multiply the values $a$, then take the indicated norm powers. If $A$ has the adapted frame as columns in raw coordinates, and $a_L$ is the multiplier from an adapted first-pivot representative to the norm-one representative, the precise formula is $P_{\rm raw}=c^N P_{\rm norm1}(\prod_L a_L)^{-n}(\det A)^{-n}$, where $P_{\rm norm1}$ here is the product of the norms of $H$ on the normalized representatives. This follows from degree $ed=n$ and the first-pivot representative rescaling identity.
