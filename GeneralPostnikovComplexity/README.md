# Complexity of relative lifting for finite Postnikov towers

These are written proofs about fixed, simply connected Postnikov pieces with only finitely many nonzero homotopy groups, all finitely generated. Inputs are explicit finite simplicial pairs with boundary maps in a fixed effective model of the target. We also consider mixed instances with the equality–congruence arithmetic from the [two-stage theorem](../MixedPostnikovTrichotomy/), where selected integral cohomology classes of the target can be evaluated on supplied integral cycles and their periods constrained by arithmetic variables. All targets, available classes and arithmetic signatures are fixed. The proofs use different hypotheses for the rational hardness and finite homotopy dichotomies.

## Finite homotopy groups give an exact dichotomy

**Theorem.** Let $Y$ be a fixed simply connected Postnikov piece with finitely many nonzero homotopy groups, all finite. The problem of extending a specified map from a subcomplex to an explicitly given finite simplicial complex is in P if $Y$ is an $H$-space and is NP-complete otherwise. The same result holds for mixed instances with the independent arithmetic template when there are no observed integral periods, using $Q(M_0)$ as the additional condition for tractability.

Choose a Kan cochain Postnikov model $S$ for $Y$ with finite simplex sets in each degree, by iterating the principal cocycle models for finite abelian groups. Fix $L$ strictly larger than the greatest degree of a nonzero homotopy group. Maps and relative homotopies extend over cells in dimensions above $L$ by obstruction theory. For existence it suffices to use the pair truncated at $L$, retaining the given boundary on the whole pair. The following finite template describes the truncated problem. It has one sort $S_q$ per dimension $0\le q\le L$, the graphs of all face maps between those sorts, and every singleton pin. One variable per simplex, with face equalities and boundary pins, gives the reduction to this template. The simplicial domain can be an ordered complex with free degeneracies; a map on its nondegenerate simplices is specified by these face constraints. In particular membership in NP is automatic.

For the reverse reduction, start with an arbitrary template instance. Create one simplex for each variable in its sort, together with all its formal faces. Interpret each face-graph atom as an identification of the designated face with the other variable's simplex. Quotient by all these identifications, propagating them to the faces of identified simplices. This is a finite semisimplicial set, with size linear in the instance up to a constant depending on $L$, before taking the quotient. Its maps to the underlying semisimplicial set of $S$ are precisely the satisfying assignments prior to pins. Close the pins under faces to obtain the marked subcomplex and the specified boundary map, rejecting conflicting prescribed values. Freely adjoining degeneracies identifies semisimplicial maps with simplicial maps to $S$. The geometric comparison of mapping spaces into a Kan complex identifies existence of an extension with existence on realization, including the specified boundary. Two barycentric subdivisions convert the finite semisimplicial realization to an ordinary complex in polynomial size. In the first subdivision each simplex has distinct vertices, since its vertices have strictly increasing original dimensions. It may still share its vertex set with another simplex. In the second subdivision distinct simplices are chains in the face poset and are identified by their vertex sets. A simplicial boundary encoding is obtained from the fixed last-vertex subdivision maps, whose realizations are homotopic to the subdivision comparison. Homotopy extension transfers the existence question along this boundary homotopy. Thus the finite template and the original extension problem are polynomially equivalent. [Hatcher, simplicial CW structures](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATsimplicial.pdf)

If the finite template has a Taylor polymorphism, the finite cyclic-term theorem gives an idempotent cyclic operation of a common prime arity $p$ on all its sorts. One obtains the common term by applying the theorem to the product of the sorted carrier algebras. Preservation of the face graphs makes the operations compatible with faces. Consequently they yield a cyclic map on the fat $L$-skeleton of $S^p$, with diagonal equal to the identity on that skeleton. Extend the map, and the homotopies implementing cyclicity and the diagonal identity, over the remaining cells, using the vanishing homotopy groups above the height of $Y$. The fat realization of a simplicial set maps naturally to its ordinary realization by a homotopy equivalence. This transports the operation to a homotopy cyclic, homotopy idempotent operation $Y^p\to Y$. Its axis maps are homotopic to the same map $f:Y\to Y$. The diagonal and the product calculation on homotopy groups give $p f_* = 1$, so $f_*$ is an isomorphism. It follows that $f$ is a homotopy equivalence. Insert a homotopy inverse of $f$ on two axes of the operation and the basepoint on all other axes. This yields an $H$-space multiplication. Thus a non-$H$ target has a finite face template without Taylor, and the finite CSP dichotomy gives NP-hardness.

Conversely, a connected simply connected Kan model of an $H$-space admits an actual simplicial Mal'cev operation. Here is a direct construction. Represent the multiplication by a simplicial map $\mu:S^2\to S$ and make its unit strict by homotopy extension on the wedge, using the homotopy unit. Consider the shear $s(u,v)=(\mu(u,v),v)$. Its induced maps on homotopy groups are triangular isomorphisms, so it is a weak equivalence. It takes the union of the two axes isomorphically to the union of the horizontal axis and the diagonal. Viewed under that latter union, the shear is a weak equivalence between cofibrant-fibrant objects, since both inclusions are monomorphisms and the ambient Kan complexes are fibrant. It has a homotopy inverse $r$ relative to the union, including a homotopy $sr\simeq1$ relative to the diagonal. Put $m_0(x,y,z)=\mu(\operatorname{pr}_1 r(x,y),z)$. On $x=y$ it is $z$ strictly. On $y=z$ the first coordinate of $sr\simeq1$ gives a homotopy from $m_0$ to $x$, constant on the triple diagonal. These homotopies agree on the intersection and extend across $S^3$ because the union of the two partial diagonals is a simplicial subcomplex and $S$ is Kan. The final map $m$ satisfies $m(x,x,z)=z$ and $m(x,y,y)=x$ strictly. Its maps on the finitely many carriers preserve all face graphs and singleton pins. The finite Mal'cev CSP theorem gives polynomial time. For independent arithmetic, the two problems can be solved separately, and either one embeds using trivial instances of the other. [Cyclic terms](https://lmcs.episciences.org/673) · [Finite CSP dichotomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.01914) · [Mal'cev algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1137/050628957)

As a corollary, NP-hardness transfers to any fixed target with a non-$H$ finite Postnikov homotopy retract by composing the boundary with the section and using the retraction for the converse. This applies to $K(\mathbb Z^r,d)\times Y$ and gives the same classification for this product with bottom period probes, since the remaining integral cocycle equations belong to the arithmetic CSP. There is also a nonsplit application when the higher fibre over the bottom free stage is a non-$H$ finite Postnikov piece. In the chosen cocycle model, give the entire $d$-skeleton constant boundary values, together with the prescribed fibre boundary. Those data agree because the fibre is $d$-reduced. Then the bottom cocycle vanishes identically and every lift belongs to the fibre. This transfers its NP-hardness without requiring the tower to split.

The finite theorem also admits genuinely mixed probes. Let $A_0=\pi_d(Y)$ be the first nonzero homotopy group and allow the bottom periods in $A_0$ to be equated to finite arithmetic variables which can participate in arbitrary atoms of the fixed integer and finite arithmetic language. Use a $d$-reduced model, so $S_d=A_0$ and all lower sorts are singletons. Adjoin the finite face template to the arithmetic signature, together with an $A_0$ sort with its addition and pins, and a bridge identifying an $S_d$ value with its $A_0$ value. Evaluation on an arbitrary integral $d$-cycle is a linear combination over $A_0$ of the bridged simplex variables, expressible by addition and binary doubling. Conversely, realize a template instance as above. For each bridge on a simplex $\sigma$, attach a new $d$-simplex $\tau$ along the same boundary and pin $\tau$ to the constant value. This imposes no new constraints on the lower faces. The period on the integral cycle $\sigma-\tau$ is exactly the bridged value. Subdivision carries the cycles by the subdivision chain map and preserves the prescribed periods by naturality. Thus the expanded equality–congruence template and the mixed geometric problem are polynomially equivalent. Its arithmetic-core Taylor criterion gives a P versus NP-complete classification, with a non-$H$ target already NP-hard in its purely topological subclass.

When the original arithmetic contains the shared $A_0$ sort with addition and pins, the criterion simplifies to $Y$ an $H$-space and $Q(M_0)$. In the $H$ case the strict simplicial Mal'cev acts on $S_d$ as $x-y+z$, since $d$-simplices with constant boundary represent precisely their group elements and the identities determine the map on the three homotopy axes. The finite face template augmented by $A_0$ and its bridge is therefore Taylor. At each arithmetic modulus choose a common sufficiently large prime arity for cyclic terms of this finite template and of the exact arithmetic image. Both operations on the shared finite group must be the same average, by addition, idempotence and cyclicity. To see preservation of arbitrary exact modular pp images, retain all existential variables, apply the arithmetic operation to the exact image of all arithmetic-only atoms, and apply the finite operation to the state-only atoms. They agree on $A_0$, so the resulting finite witness is compatible with an integral arithmetic witness for the entire system. Projection then gives preservation of the original pp image. If either criterion fails, its pure subclass gives NP-hardness. This includes arbitrary fixed tower depth and permits arithmetic interactions mediated by the observed finite bottom periods.

## Rationally split towers have an NP upper bound

**Theorem.** If the fixed target $Y$ is rationally an $H$-space, mixed extension with integral period probes in its rational coordinates is in NP in a fixed finite-residue presentation. Bottom-stage free-coordinate probes are included. The arithmetic has the same fixed template and binary coefficient encoding as the earlier mixed theorems.

The rationalization of $Y$ is a product of rational Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces. Choose integral cohomology classes spanning its rational homotopy coordinates, by clearing denominators, to obtain $Y\to B=\prod_i K(\mathbb Z^{r_i},d_i)$ inducing a rational isomorphism on homotopy. The bottom free-coordinate classes can be chosen integrally and exactly by Hurewicz and the universal coefficient theorem. In degree two the map on homotopy is therefore onto, since the free quotient has an integral basis. Its homotopy fibre is simply connected and has finitely many nonzero homotopy groups, all finite. A relative principal Postnikov tower for the map has finite coefficient groups and simply connected bases. Additional fixed integral classes are allowed among the probes if their evaluations can be expressed and checked in the finite-residue coordinate presentation; this theorem asserts the bound for the displayed rational coordinates.

Here is finite-residue descent for that relative tower. For moduli $m_e=m_0P^e$, with $P$ divisible by the coefficient primes, reduce $B$ to the product $B_e$ of the corresponding finite cyclic Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces. The fibre of $B\to B_e$ is the product of $K(m_e\mathbb Z^{r_i},d_i)$. Under change from $e+1$ to $e$, the fibre maps act by multiplication by $P$ in lattice bases. For a prime $p\mid P$, pullback by multiplication by $p$ sends the positive-degree ideal in mod-$p$ cohomology of this product into its square. Indeed, the coordinate-axis terms in the pullback by addition give $p$ times the class, while all other terms have positive degree in at least two factors by the field Künneth theorem. Iteration kills that map in any fixed positive degree range. Factoring multiplication by $P$ through multiplication by $p$ gives the same eventual vanishing. Induction using short exact coefficient sequences yields eventual vanishing with finite $p$-primary coefficients, and finite direct sums deal with every coefficient group in the tower.

Apply the natural cohomological Serre spectral sequences to the maps from the original total space to its finite-base versions. For the first stage these are the maps $B\to B_e$. Taking filtered colimits kills all positive fibre-degree rows on $E_2$, by the preceding vanishing and simple connectivity of the bases. Finite filtrations in each total degree then identify the colimit of the base cohomology with the cohomology of the fixed total space. Thus the first finite-coefficient $k$-invariant descends at some $e$. Pull back its finite stage to larger $e$, and repeat the argument. The fibre of the comparison is still the same product of lattice Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces by homotopy pullback. As there are finitely many layers, for one fixed $m$ the target has a model $B\times_{B_m}F_m$ with $F_m$ degreewise finite and $B_m$ the product of the reduced base spaces. Only the finite degrees used in the input algorithm need be tabled. This is a theorem for fixed presentations, with the finite data built into the verifier.

Guess the finite state for each relevant simplex and check face equations and boundary states. The remaining integral base coordinates are ordinary cocycles with fixed boundary, checked residues, and linear period constraints. Guess finite arithmetic values and the branches of each Boolean combination of affine equations and congruences. Integer normal forms solve the equations and congruences. Any remaining disequality removes a proper affine hyperplane in a lattice parameterization, or is constant. A polynomial-sized choice of the one-parameter test $(T,T^2,\ldots,T^r)$ avoids finitely many proper hyperplanes by the polynomial root bound. This gives polynomial-bit certificates. In particular a sufficient P test is to adjoin the fixed finite face tables and their residue links to the arithmetic language and apply its exact modular Taylor criterion. Hardness of that expanded constraint language does not automatically transfer through the integral lift requirement.

For a concrete failure of this transfer, the finite target $F=\operatorname{hofib}(\beta x\smile y)$ over $K(\mathbb F_p,d)^2$ is non-$H$, where $\beta$ is the mod-$p$ Bockstein and $d\ge2$. The separate maps from a Moore space $M(\mathbb Z/p,d)$ on the $x$-axis and from $S^d$ on the $y$-axis lift. Combining them with an $H$-space multiplication would give the specified bottom classes on their product. There the class $\beta x\smile y$ is nonzero by Künneth, a contradiction. However the pullback along $K(\mathbb Z^2,d)\to K(\mathbb F_p,d)^2$ is split, because the reduction of an integral class lifts modulo $p^2$, so its Bockstein is zero. The pullback is a product of Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces and has a polynomial-time cochain algorithm with arithmetic satisfying $Q$. Thus a non-$H$ finite completion can have a tractable integral pullback.

**Free-bottom tractability theorem.** Suppose the lowest nonzero homotopy group of $Y$ is $\mathbb Z^r$ in degree $d$, and all higher homotopy groups are finite. If $Y$ is an integral $H$-space and $Q(M_0)$ holds, the mixed problem with bottom integral period probes is in P.

Write $Y=K(\mathbb Z^r,d)\times_{B_m}F_m$ and, at any larger available modulus $m'$, write $F_{m'}=B_{m'}\times_{B_m}F_m$. We show that some $F_{m'}$ is itself an $H$-space. Choose a unit-preserving multiplication on $Y$. Its projection to the bottom is addition up to homotopy relative to the wedge: the restriction in degree $d$ from the product to the wedge is an isomorphism since $Y$ is $(d-1)$-connected, and restriction in degree $d-1$ is split-surjective. Straighten the multiplication over the bottom addition by homotopy lifting rel wedge. Compose it with $Y\to F_m$ to give a lift of bottom addition mod $m$ on $Y^2$, with the canonical axis values.

The cohomology colimit argument above applies to the comparisons $Y^2\to F_{m'}^2$, since their fibres are products of the same lattice Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces. It also applies to the wedge subspaces by taking their cohomology direct sums in positive degree, and then to cohomology relative to the wedge by the exact sequence. On $F_{m'}^2$ start with base addition modulo $m$, with the prescribed axis data, and lift through the fixed finite principal stages of $F_m$. At every stage the relative obstruction pulls back to zero on $Y^2$, so it vanishes at some deeper modulus by the colimit assertion. A lift there can be adjusted to pull back to the specified lift on $Y^2$ up to relative homotopy: the difference class has finite coefficients and descends by the same cohomology colimit. Induction gives a map $F_{m'}^2\to F_m$ over bottom addition modulo $m$ with strict unit on the axes. Combine it with bottom addition modulo $m'$ inside the pullback to obtain a multiplication on $F_{m'}$ with unit.

The finite state template at $m'$ therefore has a strict Mal'cev polymorphism, acting as $x-y+z$ on the degree-$d$ residue sort. This action follows from the lowest homotopy group just as in the finite-valued period proof. With residue addition included, cyclic operations for this template act on the bridge sort as the modular average. For each arithmetic modulus, first pass to a common multiple of that modulus and $m'$, choose a common prime cyclic arity for the exact arithmetic image and the finite state template, and apply the two operations to the full witnesses before projection. They agree on residues at $m'$, so they preserve the residue bridges as well as the original constraints. This is exactly the Taylor test for the expanded arithmetic and state template. Its constructive algorithm proves the claim. This establishes tractability for free-bottom integral $H$-space towers at arbitrary fixed depth. General hardness in the complementary free-bottom case still requires a realization argument.

## Rational nonsplitting gives undecidability with full lower-degree probes

**Theorem.** If the fixed $Y$ is rationally non-$H$, its mixed extension problem is computably enumerable complete when the probes span the integral representatives of the rational coordinates of the prefix preceding its first nonzero rational $k$-invariant. The hardness instances can use empty boundary and just the given integer additions and pins $1$.

Let $N$ be the degree of that $k$-invariant and $G$ its finitely generated coefficient group. The preceding stage is rationally a product of Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces. Choose integral period coordinates as in the preceding theorem. Its rational $k$-invariant is a graded polynomial in those coordinates with values in $G\otimes\mathbb Q$. Choose one nonzero monomial and make a product of spheres with one axis of the indicated degree for each occurrence of a variable in this monomial. The dimensions sum to $N$ and each dimension is at least two. Pin every rational coordinate's periods on a full homology basis in its degree. On singleton sphere axes assign the coordinate its intended scalar periods, and set all other periods to zero. Pullback of the rational invariant now evaluates as a nonzero vector times the product of the assigned scalars. Indeed every monomial except the selected one misses a top axis or repeats one, and repeated equal even generators contribute a nonzero factorial multiplicity. Odd generators occur at most once.

Use two such products with their proper skeleta wedged and a single top cell attached by the sum of their top attaching maps, the usual oriented connected-sum construction. In the two products use scalar assignments $(x,y,1,\ldots,1)$ and $(-z,1,1,\ldots,1)$ in the first two slots, giving rational top obstruction proportional to $xy-z$. All integral coordinate periods are multiplied by the common modulus $m$ obtained for the preceding stage in the finite-residue descent. Thus they can be realized by integral cocycles with zero residues, and the constant finite-state lift realizes the entire earlier stage. Conversely the probes determine all relevant coordinate classes uniquely on these carriers. If the coordinate sorts for the first two slots differ, the same carrier with assignments $(x,1,\ldots)$ and $(-1,y,\ldots)$ imposes $x=y$ between those sorts.

It remains to handle torsion in $G$. If the original attaching map of the top cell is $\alpha$, attach instead by $E\alpha$, where $E>0$ kills the torsion subgroup of $G$. There is a map back to the original carrier, identity on the proper skeleton and degree $E$ on the top cell. One way to see this is to represent $E$ times the relative homotopy class of the original top disk, whose boundary is $E\alpha$. The proper skeleton has no $(N-1)$-cells. Both carriers have top homology $\mathbb Z$ and zero homology in degree $N-1$, so top $G$-cohomology is detected exactly by evaluation. Pulling back the earlier lift multiplies its obstruction by $E$. Consequently vanishing of the rational evaluation is sufficient as well as necessary for extension. No later stage obstructs on this $N$-dimensional carrier. The fixed CW pairs can be replaced by fixed triangulated pairs with the corresponding integral cycles. This yields multiplication exactly, and Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich gives hardness. Enumerating witnesses in the effective tower models gives membership. [Matiyasevich](https://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/~yumat/papers/21_paper/21_eng.pdf) · [Manin, rational homotopy and extension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10632)

The scope of the probe hypothesis is visible already for $\operatorname{hofib}(u_2^4-v_4^2)$ over $K(\mathbb Z,2)\times K(\mathbb Z,4)$. This target is rationally non-$H$. With only bottom probes and empty or point boundary, every bottom cocycle extends by choosing the degree-four cocycle to be its square. The two top cup products then agree on cochains by associativity. Thus the bottom-only problem in this restricted boundary model reduces to the arithmetic CSP and is in P whenever $Q$ holds. This example does not decide what arbitrary higher-stage boundary data can enforce. The remaining general case also includes non-split rationally $H$ targets with free homotopy: a locally legal finite-residue witness can have a nonzero Bockstein and fail to lift to integral cocycles.
