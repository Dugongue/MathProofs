# Root extraction and homomorphism extension in permutation groups

**Theorem.** Fix $m\ge2$ and $b\ge1$. Given a permutation group $H$ by generators, with every point orbit of size at most $b$, and a marked element $h\in H$, deciding whether $x^m=h$ for some $x\in H$ is classified as follows.

- If $p^{v_p(m)+1}>b$ for every prime $p\mid m$, the problem is in deterministic polynomial time.
- If $p^{v_p(m)+1}\le b$ for any prime $p\mid m$, the problem is NP-complete. Hardness holds with $H$ a $p$-group of exponent at most $p^{v_p(m)+1}$ and $h$ central of order $p$.

In particular, the threshold for square-root hardness is an orbit of size four. The hardness already holds for class-two $2$-groups of exponent four. It proves that Kantor and Luks's **Extendibility of Homomorphism** problem and Wuu's **HomExtPerm** are NP-complete even with source $C_4$ and prescribed subgroup $C_2$. This answers Wuu's Open Problem 1.6 affirmatively and gives a negative answer to Open Problem 1.8 unless $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$. The proof below also gives NP-completeness with the fixed target $D_8$ when the source can vary. [Kantor–Luks, *Computing in quotient groups*, §13, Problem 2](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/quotient.pdf#page=9) · [Wuu, *Homomorphism Extension*, §1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.08656#S1.SS2)

## Four-point proof of the square-root case

Use $D_8$, the dihedral group of order eight, in coordinates $(a,b,c)\in\mathbb F_2^3$ with multiplication
$$
(a,b,c)(a',b',c')=(a+a',b+b',c+c'+ab').
$$
Here $(a,b,c)^2=(0,0,ab)$ and $z=(0,0,1)$ is the half-turn in the faithful square action. For example, the two coordinate basis elements $(1,0,0)$ and $(0,1,0)$ are noncommuting involutions with a product of order four. First-two-coordinate projection is an epimorphism to $\mathbb F_2^2$ and its kernel is $Z(D_8)$.

Given a graph $G$, introduce bits $s,a_v,b_v$ for each vertex $v$, with the bit $s$ shared throughout. Give each vertex one dihedral factor whose first two coordinates are $(s+a_v,s+b_v)$, each edge $uv$ a factor with $(s+a_u+a_v,s+b_u+b_v)$, and one final factor with $(s,s)$. These prescribed tuples specify a single linear map from the assignment space to the product of the first-two-coordinate spaces.

In the disjoint union of the four-point actions, let $H$ be generated by lifts of the images of the assignment basis vectors and the element $h$ that equals $z$ in the final factor and the identity elsewhere. Give third coordinates zero in the basis lifts. Since the projection is a homomorphism and $h$ has zero image, the image of $H$ is exactly the linear image of the assignment space. The permutation generators have degree $4(|V(G)|+|E(G)|+1)$. The group has class at most two and exponent dividing four.

The last coordinate of $x^2=h$ requires $s=1$. The identity required at a vertex gives $(1+a_v)(1+b_v)=0$, precisely $(a_v,b_v)\ne(0,0)$. At an edge the same test on the difference of the endpoint pairs excludes equal colors. The three nonzero pairs thus give a proper three-coloring. Conversely every proper coloring lifts to a root by multiplying the chosen basis lifts with the corresponding exponents. This proves the reduction.

The assignment map is injective because the last factor reads $s$ and the vertex factors then read all remaining bits. Each proper labeled coloring gives exactly $|H\cap Z(D_8^{|V(G)|+|E(G)|+1})|$ roots, by the constant fiber size of a group homomorphism. The factor is computable from the order of $H$ by dividing by $2^{1+2|V(G)|}$.

## General orbit threshold

Let $L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,b)$. All element orders divide $L$. In the polynomial case, every prime divisor of $m$ occurs in $L$ to exponent at most $v_p(m)$, so $x^m$ always has order coprime to $m$. Conversely if $\gcd(|h|,m)=1$, choose $u$ with $um\equiv1\bmod |h|$ and take $h^u$. Group element order is computable from a permutation's cycle lengths. The argument also works when $m$ is supplied in binary whenever the stated condition holds.

For hardness, take $p\mid m$ with $r=e+1$ and $e=v_p(m)\ge1$ satisfying $p^r\le b$. Let $W_1=C_p$ and $W_j=W_{j-1}^{\,p}\rtimes C_p$, where the top $C_p$ cyclically permutes the $p$ copies. This group acts faithfully on $p^j$ points in $p$ blocks. Let $z_1$ generate $W_1$, and set $z_j=(z_{j-1},\ldots,z_{j-1};0)$. Each $z_j$ is central of order $p$.

Define a surjective homomorphism $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_j):W_j\to\mathbb F_p^j$ recursively. At the first level take the exponent. At level $j$ record the top shift as $\lambda_j$, and for each lower coordinate sum over the $p$ blocks. This respects multiplication because a shift permutes the summands. All lower values can be prescribed in one block, proving surjectivity. Also $\lambda(z_j)=0$ for $j\ge2$. The following formula holds for all $g\in W_j$:
$$
g^{p^{j-1}}=z_j^{\lambda_1(g)\prod_{i=2}^j\lambda_i(g)^{p-1}}.
$$
The first level is immediate. If the top shift is zero, $g$ is a base element, killed by $p^{j-1}$ by induction. If the top shift is nonzero, the $p$-th power has in each block a cyclically reordered product of all the base elements. Those products have the same lower coordinates. Raising further to $p^{j-2}$ gives the same power of $z_{j-1}$ in every block, by induction. This establishes the formula because the top coordinate to the power $p-1$ is the indicator of a nonzero shift. It proves that the exponent divides $p^j$.

Use a disjoint union of copies of $W_r$ with each copy's $\lambda$-tuple a prescribed linear function of a common assignment over $\mathbb F_p$. As before, let $H$ be generated by lifts of assignment basis vectors and the target central element $h$, giving exactly the prescribed joint $\lambda$-image. Write $u=m/p^e$. In a factor where the exponent in the formula is required to be $c$, use $z_r^{uc}$ for the target coordinate. Then $x^m=h$ enforces precisely $c$ in every factor, since $u$ is a unit modulo $p$.

For odd $p$, reduce proper $p$-coloring. Use assignment variables $s,a_v$ over $\mathbb F_p$. A final factor sets all coordinates to $s$ and requires exponent $1$. Its output is $s$, so $s=1$. For each edge $vw$, require exponent $1$ with $\lambda_1=s$, $\lambda_2=a_v-a_w$, and $\lambda_i=s$ for $i\ge3$. With $s=1$, the condition says $a_v\ne a_w$ exactly. For each vertex $v$, add a readout factor with $\lambda_1=a_v$ and all other coordinates zero, requiring exponent zero. This ensures the assignment map is injective and imposes no additional restrictions on the coloring. For fixed $p>3$, $p$-coloring is NP-hard by adjoining a clique on $p-3$ vertices and connecting its vertices to every original vertex of a three-coloring instance.

For $p=2$, use the vertex, edge and final tuples in the four-point construction as the first two coordinates. Set all later coordinates to $s$. The final factor forces $s=1$, so in every factor the formula becomes the product of the first two coordinates. This yields the same three-coloring equivalence.

All factors have constant size for fixed $p,r$. The recursion computes lifts directly, or via a fixed local table. The permutation degree is $O(|V(G)|+|E(G)|+1)$ and the number of generators is $O(|V(G)|+1)$. Each entry of each generator is a constant-size local lookup after testing which vertex labels occur in its factor. This takes linear time in the number of written permutation entries in the usual word model, and the reduction is logspace computable by scanning the input edge list and retaining only current counters and vertex names. The mark $h$ has order $p$ because its final coordinate is $z_r^u$ with $u$ invertible modulo $p$, and all its coordinates have order dividing $p$. NP membership uses a guessed permutation, polynomial-time permutation-group membership, and powering by repeated squaring.

## Extension problem and scope

The general hard root instance gives a fixed cyclic source $G=\langle g\rangle\cong C_{mp}$ and prescribed subgroup $M=\langle g^m\rangle\cong C_p$. Send $g^m$ to $h$, a legitimate homomorphism because $h$ has order $p$. It extends exactly when $x^m=h$ for an $x\in H$, since that equality implies $x^{mp}=h^p=1$. The square instance gives $G=C_4$ and $M=C_2$.

For general permutation source and target, NP membership uses a polynomial-size presentation of the source and polynomial-size straight-line words for the supplied subgroup generators. Guess images of the presentation generators, check membership in the target and the relators, and then evaluate the subgroup words to check the restriction. Kantor and Luks give this upper bound in the original problem. The verification also works with Wuu's partial map consisting of arbitrary supplied elements of the subgroup.

## Hardness with a fixed target

Let $F$ be the free group among groups of class at most two and exponent dividing four on the symbols $A,B,x_v$ for $v\in V(G)$. On $k=|V(G)|+2$ generators, its elements can be coordinatized by $u\in\mathbb F_2^k$ recording generator exponents modulo two, and central coordinates $s_i,c_{ij}\in\mathbb F_2$ for generator squares and pair commutators. More explicitly, multiplication adds the vectors and adds $u_i u_i'$ to $s_i+s_i'$, and $u_i u_j'$ to $c_{ij}+c_{ij}'$ for $i<j$. These bilinear formulas are associative, and the coordinate generators and their squares and commutators generate the group. They are the free group in the indicated class. In every group of class at most two and exponent dividing four, commutators are central and have order dividing two. Indeed the class-two power formula for $(xy)^4$ gives $[x,y]^6=1$, and the exponent law gives $[x,y]^4=1$. Collection of words therefore gives normal forms with at most the number of possibilities in the coordinate model. That model attains this number, so its presentation has exactly the universal relations.

Realize $F$ as a permutation group by combining maps to $C_4$ on single indices and $D_8$ on pairs. The single map at $i$ outputs $u_i+2s_i\bmod 4$. The pair map at $i<j$ outputs $(u_i,u_j,c_{ij})$ in the dihedral coordinates used above. Each is a homomorphism by the multiplication formulas. Together they detect every coordinate. Use a separate four-point action for each map, yielding explicit permutation generators of degree $4(k+\binom{k}{2})$ with orbit bound four.

Let $M$ be generated by $A,B$ and by the central elements
$$
r_v=(x_vAB)^2,\qquad r_{vw}=(x_vx_wAB)^2 \quad (vw\in E(G)).
$$
Define $M\to D_8$ by taking $A,B$ to $(1,0,0),(0,1,0)$, respectively, and all $r_v,r_{vw}$ to the identity. This prescription defines a homomorphism for every simple graph. To see this, the only listed central generator with a nonzero coordinate at $c_{vw}$ is $r_{vw}$. A linear combination of the listed central generators in $\langle A,B\rangle$ thus uses no edge generators. Each remaining $r_v$ has its own $s_v$ coordinate, so the combination is zero. The central span intersects $\langle A,B\rangle$ trivially and commutes with it. The displayed prescription on $\langle A,B\rangle$ comes from the pair projection and extends across this direct product.

By freeness, the extension amounts to assigning images of all $x_v$ in $D_8$ and respecting the central constraints. The image of $AB$ has first two coordinates $(1,1)$, so $r_v=1$ in the image precisely when the image of $x_v$ has a nonzero pair of first coordinates. Similarly $r_{vw}=1$ precisely when the two vertex pairs differ. Therefore extensions are equivalent to proper three-colorings. This proves NP-completeness for fixed target $D_8$.

## Tractable input models

For an abelian target $H$, extension from an arbitrary permutation source and subgroup is in P. Abelianize a short presentation for $H$, obtaining $H=\mathbb Z^t/L_H$ with $L_H$ generated by exponent vectors of defining relators. Use the exponent vectors of unknown target images as integer variables. The source relators and the prescribed values on the subgroup give linear congruences modulo $L_H$. Introduce an independent integer combination of its generators in each congruence to make an integer linear system. All dimensions and coefficient bit lengths are polynomial. Straight-line word evaluation in a free abelian group preserves polynomial bit bounds. Integer normal-form algorithms solve this system, and a solution specifies the desired generator images.

The target in the hardness construction grows with the graph and is supplied by permutation generators. A complete Cayley table instead allows an element-by-element scan. Goldmann and Russell give a polynomial-time algorithm for one equation over each fixed finite nilpotent group in the fixed-group input model. [Goldmann–Russell, *The Complexity of Solving Equations over Finite Groups*](https://doi.org/10.1006/inco.2002.3173)
