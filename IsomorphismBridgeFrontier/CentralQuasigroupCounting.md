# Counting central quasigroup isomorphisms in deterministic logspace

**Theorem.** Given the multiplication tables of two quasigroups, at least one of which is central, a deterministic logarithmic-space transducer outputs the number of isomorphisms as a binary integer. In particular, it outputs the exact automorphism-group order of any central quasigroup. The proof is informal. This strengthens the existing MathProofs decision answer to Johnson–Levet–Vojtěchovský–Widholm Question 7.5. [Original question](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06478v3#S7) · [Existing decision proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/d323f03a2d337824255b28baff79790cf894d3be/CayleyTableAndGroupAlgebra/CentralQuasigroupIsomorphism.md)

## 1. The diagram and its units

Apply the existing logspace decision algorithm. A nonisomorphic pair has count zero. For an isomorphic pair, composing with any one isomorphism gives a bijection with the first quasigroup's automorphisms. It suffices to count those.

Recover the affine data $Q(A,\phi,\psi,c)$, with $A$ abelian and $x*y=\phi(x)+\psi(y)+c$. These data are available from the original table by the affine reconstruction in the decision proof. Put $D=1-\phi-\psi$, $m=\exp A$, and $R=\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$. The singleton case has count one. Otherwise, use the same diagram as in the decision proof. Its vertices are $A,A\oplus R,R$, with loops $\phi,\psi$ on $A$, the natural inclusion $A\to A\oplus R$ and projection $A\oplus R\to R$, and $d:A\oplus R\to A$ given by $d(a,k)=Da+kc$. Let $E$ be its endomorphism ring. [Johnson et al., §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06478v3#S6)

A unit of $E$ acts by $\gamma$ on $A$, by a scalar unit $u$ on $R$, and by $(a,k)\mapsto(\gamma a+kt,uk)$ on $A\oplus R$. The inclusion and projection force this block shape. The other arrows give $\gamma\phi=\phi\gamma$, $\gamma\psi=\psi\gamma$, and $\gamma c=Dt+uc$. The homomorphism $E^\times\to R^\times$ taking $u$ is onto, since simultaneous scalar multiplication on all vertices is a diagram automorphism for each scalar unit. Its fiber at 1 is in bijection with quasigroup automorphisms via $(\gamma,t)\mapsto(x\mapsto\gamma x-t)$. To see exhaustiveness, a quasigroup isomorphism transports the regular translation group generated by $L_xL_y^{-1}$, so it is affine. Substitution into the product formula gives exactly the three displayed constraints at $u=1$. Hence
$$
|\operatorname{Aut}(Q)|=|E^\times|/|R^\times|.
$$
All vertices and arrows split across primes, so $E=\prod_p E_p$, where $E_p$ is the endomorphism ring of the $p$-primary diagram. For a prime power $p^e$ exactly dividing $m$, the factor in $|R^\times|$ is $p^{e-1}(p-1)$.

## 2. Size of the ring and nilpotent kernel

Fix $p$. Let $X$ be the direct sum of the three primary vertex groups. Use their cyclic prime-power coordinates from the decision proof. Encode each additive map entry from a cyclic group of order $p^s$ to one of order $p^t$ as $p^{\max(0,t-s)} y$ in $\mathbb Z/p^t\mathbb Z$, where $y$ ranges over $\mathbb Z/p^{\min(s,t)}\mathbb Z$. The commutation equations for the diagram arrows are one additive homomorphism $L$ from the product of cyclic variable groups of orders $p^{a_i}$ to the product of equation groups of orders $p^{b_j}$. The additive group of $E_p$ is its kernel.

The cokernel has order $p^\nu$, where $\nu$ is the least $p$-adic valuation of the maximal minors of $M=[L\mid \operatorname{diag}(p^{b_j})]$. The diagonal columns impose the equation moduli and the resulting integer quotient is a $p$-group. Therefore
$$
\log_p |E_p|=\sum_i a_i-\sum_j b_j+\nu.
$$
The valuation algorithm in the decision proof computes $\nu$ on compact data. In brief, Cauchy–Binet expands $\det(M\operatorname{diag}(T,T^2,\ldots)M^{\mathsf T})$ into squared minors weighted by the sum of their column indices. Expressing the columns in a basis of their $p$-adic lattice and reducing modulo $p$ shows that the minimum-valuation bases form a vector matroid. The distinct column weights select a unique minimum-weight basis in this matroid. Its term is the unique term of valuation $2\nu$ at its exponent. Division-free determinant evaluation reads the coefficients modulo $p^{2\sum_j b_j+1}$, which suffices by the diagonal minor bound. [Berkowitz determinant](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(84)90018-8)

Let $B$ be the image of $E_p$ on $V=X/pX$. The kernel consists of maps taking $X$ into $pX$, so its products of length $e$ vanish. Write $S=B/J(B)$. The preimage of $J(B)$ in $E_p$ is nilpotent and its quotient is semisimple, so it equals $J(E_p)$. Any unit of $S$ lifts to a unit of $E_p$ by geometric-series correction. The kernel on units is $1+J(E_p)$. This gives
$$
|E_p^\times|=p^{\log_p|E_p|-\dim_{\mathbb F_p}S}|S^\times|.
$$
The decision proof gives compact uniform circuits for $B$, $J(B)$, and ranks. Its congruence routine produces a basis of the image modulo $p$, and its characteristic-polynomial trace filtration gives the radical. These routines take any such diagram endomorphism ring, including that of a single diagram. [Congruence and radical routines](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/d323f03a2d337824255b28baff79790cf894d3be/CayleyTableAndGroupAlgebra/CentralQuasigroupIsomorphism.md)

## 3. Counting units by enumerating the center

Write $S=\prod_i \operatorname{Mat}_{r_i}(\mathbb F_{p^{f_i}})$. Every simple factor occurs in the composition factors of the faithful $B$-module $V$. If one were absent, a lift of its central identity idempotent would act as zero on all composition factors and therefore nilpotently on $V$, contradicting its nonzero idempotent image in $S$. Thus $\sum_i f_i\le\sum_i r_i f_i\le\dim V$. For $n=|A|$ we have $|X|\le n^4$, so $|Z(S)|=p^{\sum_i f_i}\le |V|\le n^4$.

Compute the linear preimage of $Z(S)$ in $B$ by imposing $[z,b]\in J(B)$ for every basis vector $b$ of $B$. Choose a basis of this preimage modulo $J(B)$ using rank increases on its ordered list of spanning vectors. A base-$p$ counter now enumerates the center in $O(\log n)$ bits. A central element $z\ne 0$ is primitive if $z^2=z$ modulo the radical and there is no other nonzero central idempotent $z'\ne z$ with $zz'=z'$. Testing this uses a second center scan. For a primitive $z$, compute $f_i=\dim_{\mathbb F_p} zZ(S)$ and $r_i^2 f_i=\dim_{\mathbb F_p} zS$ by taking the ranks of the products of $z$ with the corresponding bases, modulo the radical. The matrix ring contributes
$$
\big|\operatorname{GL}_{r_i}(\mathbb F_{p^{f_i}})\big|
=\prod_{j=0}^{r_i-1}\left(p^{f_i r_i}-p^{f_i j}\right)
$$
to the unit count, by choosing independent columns in order.

## 4. Output space

If $N$ is the total table length, all matrices in the ring computations have $(\log N)^{O(1)}$ bits. Compact uniform bounded-fan-in circuits of polylogarithmic depth in that size evaluate a bit by depth-first traversal in $(\log\log N)^{O(1)}$ workspace, in addition to the $O(\log N)$-space callback producing a raw input bit. The center and prime scans use a fixed number of counters of $O(\log N)$ bits. Each factor of the final product is available bitwise by recomputing the corresponding prime and primitive central idempotent.

The ring has $O((\log N)^2)$ bits per element, since its entries encode a bounded number of group maps on $O(\log N)$ generators. The same bound holds for the logarithms of all the resulting counts. Uniform parallel circuits for iterated multiplication and exact integer division compute the output bits of the product and the quotient by $|R^\times|$. Pad the product by neutral factors and omit leading zeros on output. Evaluation uses the same workspace bound with a fixed number of callback levels. This proves the theorem. [Hesse–Allender–Barrington](https://www.cs.rutgers.edu/~allender/papers/division.pdf)

The new step is exact counting through the small semisimple center and the congruence multiplicities. The published question asks for decision, already addressed by the cited MathProofs proof. Prior work on enumerating isomorphism classes of central quasigroups counts a different set. Publication priority for the logspace count is unestablished. [Stanovský–Vojtěchovský](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03534)
