# Exact determinant profiles from a narrow mode in each centroid block

Work over $F=\mathbb F_3$ on explicitly written three-tensors. A sign bit is zero for determinant $+1$. The profile of a tensor pair is the set of the three sign bits attained by genuine transporters, with empty profile when there is no transporter. The following result is informal.

**Theorem.** Compact the three supports of a raw tensor $T$, and decompose the result into its primitive centroid blocks. For each such block $B$, let $K_B$ be the intrinsic coefficient field of its local centroid, let $d_B=[K_B:F]$, and let $r_B$ be the minimum of the three block-mode dimensions over $K_B$. For any fixed $C$, if
$$
 d_B(r_B^2-1)\le C\log_3(2+L)
$$
in every source block, the exact determinant sign profile of the pair $(T,T')$ is computable deterministically in time polynomial in the raw pair length $L$. The source test is computable without a supplied decomposition or transporter. Large blocks may have different narrow modes. There is no bound on the number or multiplicities of ordinary block types. The bound also holds if each source block has minimum raw mode dimension at most $C'\sqrt{\log_3(2+L)}$ for a fixed $C'$.

**Packing consequence.** When either one of two independent one-sign TI inputs satisfies this condition, there is an exact deterministic polynomial-time reduction from their conjunction to one one-sign TI instance. Evaluate the qualified input with the profile method. In the positive case output the other input. In the negative case output a fixed negative instance. The unrestricted case in which neither input meets a sufficient condition remains open.

## Recover the centroid blocks and their coefficient fields

The support in each mode is the image of the corresponding flattening. Reject unequal raw formats or unequal support dimensions because transporters preserve these dimensions. Work on the supports initially. A transporter on the supports extends to the original dimensions by arbitrary invertible maps on the complements, so each nonzero complement adds an independently adjustable sign bit. Use adapted source and target bases for this step, retaining their determinant ratios. If both tensors are zero and the formats match, the profile consists of all sign choices on the positive-dimensional modes. A zero-dimensional mode contributes bit zero.

For a concise tensor with physical spaces $V_1,V_2,V_3$, its centroid consists of the triples $(e_1,e_2,e_3)$ whose actions on the three modes separately yield the same tensor. The equations are linear. Projection to each mode is faithful by conciseness. Actions in distinct modes commute, and a product of two centroid actions can be moved to another mode by first moving one factor and then moving the other. Moving through both other modes also reverses the factors in the original mode. Faithfulness proves that the centroid is a commutative unital algebra. A transporter conjugates it in all three modes.

Compute its radical and the field factors of its semisimple quotient with the deterministic finite-algebra procedure of Ivanyos and Qiao. The deterministic bound is polynomial in the matrix size, $\log |F|$ and the characteristic, so it is polynomial here. A lift $x$ of a quotient primitive idempotent satisfies $x^2-x$ in the nilpotent radical. For $3^k$ at least the nilpotence index, $e=x^{3^k}$ is idempotent and still lifts that quotient idempotent. Orthogonality and summing to the unit follow because a nilpotent idempotent is zero. These are the primitive centroid idempotents. The image of each summand idempotent in the three modes gives one centroid block. The tensor has support only in matching triples of idempotent images because the three actions of any given idempotent on it are equal. Consequently any transporter permutes the blocks and restricts to local transporters, and any such matching with local transporters extends to a full transporter. [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

A block centroid $R$ is local with residue field of size $3^d$, for $d=[R/\operatorname{rad}R:F]$. Its coefficient field $K$ is the fixed space of the $3^d$-power map on $R$. Raising a lift of any residue element to $3^{ds}$ for sufficiently large $s$ gives a fixed lift, because the initial failure to be fixed is nilpotent. A nilpotent fixed element is zero. Thus the fixed ring maps isomorphically to the residue field. Compute it with linear equations because cubing in a commutative $F$-algebra is $F$-linear. Its three mode actions make each mode a $K$-vector space, and every genuine block transporter is semilinear with respect to the same induced field isomorphism. The displayed width tests are therefore invariant under ordinary block isomorphism. An ordinarily isomorphic target satisfies them whenever the source does. Reject a target that fails the width test before computing any local profiles.

## Compute the local profiles by fixing one mode map

Consider a source and target block pair, rejecting immediately if sizes or centroid residue degrees differ. Choose a source mode $j$ achieving $r=r_B$, and use computed $K$-bases in this mode at both endpoints. List all $d$ isomorphisms $\sigma:K\to K'$. For each $\sigma$, enumerate invertible $r\times r$ matrices over $K'$ normalized so their first nonzero entry in the fixed matrix order is $1$. Each member gives one fixed $F$-linear map $A\sigma$ in mode $j$; its sign can be computed directly in block coordinate bases. There are at most
$$
  d\,\frac{(3^{d})^{r^2}-1}{3^d-1}
  \le \tfrac32 d\,3^{d(r^2-1)}
$$
candidates, and enumerating the normalized matrices by first nonzero positions has the stated polynomial cost bound under the hypothesis. If the raw minimum dimension of a block is $m$, then $r=m/d$, and $d(r^2-1)\le m^2$.

The field alignment itself is deterministic polynomial time even when $d$ grows. A degree-$d$ generator of $K$ lies outside the maximal proper subfields, each found as the fixed space of the corresponding Frobenius power. The sum of their sizes is less than $|K|$ for $d>1$, since there are at most $\log_2 d$ such fields with dimensions at most $d/2$. Assign the $F$-coordinates of a generator one by one, always choosing a digit whose conditional expected number of these subfields containing the completed element does not increase. The probabilities are computed by linear equations on affine slices. At completion the integer count is less than one. Compute its minimal polynomial from a first linear dependence of its powers. This polynomial splits simply over $K'$. To find one root without enumerating $K'$, use the $d$ trace coordinate maps $t\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{K'/F}(\beta t)$ for an $F$-basis of $K'$. These maps separate elements by the nondegenerate trace pairing. Compute each trace polynomial modulo the minimal polynomial by repeated cubing, then keep a nonconstant gcd of the surviving factor with the trace polynomial minus one of the three values in $F$. After all coordinate maps the surviving factor is linear. This constructs one isomorphism. Compose it with the $d$ Frobenius powers to list all isomorphisms. For $d=1$ use the identity.

For a fixed mode map, the other two physical factors must realize simultaneous left-right equivalence of two explicit tuples of matrices $M_i,M'_i:X\to Y$. To obtain these tuples, use slices in matching bases of the fixed labeling mode. Use the inverse transpose on the remaining domain-mode map when translating into the pencil convention. Its sign is unaffected. The condition is that invertible $P,Q$ satisfy $QM_i=M'_iP$ for all $i$. Reduce to constructive module isomorphism by putting the arrows $M_i$ on $X\oplus Y$ as block matrices with one nonzero block and adding the two vertex projections. An intertwiner must preserve both vertex spaces, so the algorithm gives exactly a reference pair $(P_0,Q_0)$, or no pair. This procedure works for rectangular slices and is deterministic polynomial time. [Ivanyos and Qiao, Section 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#S3.SS3)

If a reference pair exists, all solutions are $(P_0P,Q_0Q)$ where $(P,Q)$ is in the unit group of
$$
 E=\{(P,Q)\in\operatorname{End}(X)\times\operatorname{End}(Y):
               QM_i=M_iP\text{ for all }i\}.
$$
Compute its radical $J$ and quotient split into simple matrix factors $\prod_s\operatorname{Mat}_{a_s}(F_s)$, with finite extensions $F_s/F$, using the same finite-algebra algorithm. The two modules $X,Y$ carry the filtrations $J^iX,J^iY$. In each quotient compute the total multiplicity of the natural simple module of factor $s$ by applying a lifted primitive matrix idempotent for $s$ and dividing its rank by $[F_s:F]$. The lift acts as the intended idempotent on the grade regardless of the choice of lift. Sum over the grades to get $m_{Xs}$ and $m_{Ys}$.

A unit with quotient component $u_s$ contributes
$$
 (\det P,\det Q)
   =\prod_s \left(t_s^{m_{Xs}},t_s^{m_{Ys}}\right),
 \qquad t_s=\operatorname{N}_{F_s/F}(\det_{F_s}u_s).
$$
This is block triangularity on the radical filtrations and the determinant of a linear map on a simple module after restriction of scalars. The norm equality can also be checked on elementary and diagonal generators of the general linear group. The unit group surjects onto the unit group of the quotient because the radical is nilpotent. Norms of nonzero finite field elements are surjective. Thus the two attainable sign bits are the affine profile obtained by translating the binary span of the multiplicity pairs $(m_{Xs},m_{Ys})\bmod 2$ by the reference signs. Include the fixed mode sign to get the joint triple. The computed group of two bits preserves their correlation.

It remains to restore the omitted scalar multiples of $A\sigma$. Given any other mode $h$, a target block has an automorphism acting by multiplication by $\lambda\in K'^\times$ in mode $j$, by multiplication by $\lambda^{-1}$ in mode $h$, and identically elsewhere. This follows from equality of the two centroid actions and holds for any $\lambda$. Any actual mode map normalizes by postcomposing with such an automorphism. Conversely all scalar multiples are restored by that postcomposition. If $r_i$ denotes the dimension in mode $i$ over $K'$, the sign vectors of this adjustment are exactly the span of $r_j e_j+r_h e_h$ modulo two, by the norm computation. Add this span to each computed profile and take the union over the normalized candidates. This recovers every local transporter sign triple exactly.

## Assemble an arbitrary number of matched blocks

Compute local profiles for cross-side block pairs and for enough same-side pairs to classify ordinary block types, using nonemptiness as the equality test. The local computations give the equalities directly and require no ordinary TI oracle. Reject unequal source and target multiplicities. Choose one matching $\pi$ of the two block lists within each common type. Convolve the constant-size local profiles along that matching by adding sign vectors. Add the sign of the block permutation in each mode, with the frame calibration from the original supports. Explicitly, in mode $i$ a crossing between two source blocks of dimensions $d_i,d'_i$ contributes the bit $d_i d'_i\bmod 2$. This is the sign of permuting their coordinate intervals.

Only the block permutation correction remains when the matching changes. Indeed, for each ordinary type, choose conceptual isomorphisms from every source and target block to one fixed representative of their type. Along any matching the product of the chosen local determinants is the product of the source determinants divided by the product of the target determinants, independent of the matching. Every other local isomorphism changes its determinant by the stabilizer profile of its source block. These profiles are conjugacy invariant and independent of the matching. Thus the local profile sum before permutation has no matching dependence.

All alternate matchings act by permutations within repeated ordinary types. For any repeated type, a transposition of its two equal-size blocks changes the permutation signs by the triple of their dimensions modulo two. Every such correction is feasible and every change of matching is generated by those transpositions. Add the binary span of the parity triples of repeated types, then restore the free signs from unsupported ambient complements. This produces the full profile, with no enumeration of matchings or guessing isotype counts.

All matrices and field presentations have polynomial size in the raw lengths. There are only polynomially many centroid blocks and block pairs, and each signed profile contains at most eight vectors. For example a direct sum of arbitrarily many pencils with slices $I_m,J_m$, for $J_m$ a single nilpotent Jordan block and $m\ge3$, has primitive blocks of format $(2,m,m)$. Indeed equality of centroid actions for the identity slice first identifies the two matrix-mode actions, and the shared action on the slice span must be multiplication by $aI+bJ_m$. Preservation of the span after multiplying $J_m$ forces $b=0$ since $I,J_m,J_m^2$ are linearly independent. The centroid is therefore scalar on each block. The criterion handles arbitrary numbers of repetitions, and physical dimensions grow in all three modes. Field multiplication tensors over growing extensions give another example, with each mode of dimension one over the intrinsic centroid field and unbounded dimension over $F$.

This argument combines the earlier centroid and semisimple determinant methods with fixed-mode enumeration modulo intrinsic field scalars. It leaves unrestricted binary one-sign packing open.
