# Packing a signed comparison using adjoint multiplicities

Over $\mathbb F_3$, let $A$ ask whether a pair of explicit three-tensors has a transporter of determinant one in the first mode, and let $O$ be ordinary tensor isomorphism. We give a deterministic single-instance reduction from $A$ to $O$ on an efficiently recognized class defined by a dimension computed from a represented algebra in that mode. One comparison from this class and one arbitrary $A$ comparison therefore pack into one $A$ instance by the existing determinant-neutral ordinary conjunction. The tested mode, its partner modes, and the radical of the represented algebra can all be large.

## 1. The width test

For a tensor $T$ and its tested mode $V$, choose any deterministic polynomial-time rule that computes a unital algebra $E(T)\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb F_3}(V)$ with the following covariance. Every ordinary transporter with tested-mode map $a$ sends $E(T)$ to $E(T')$ by $e\mapsto aea^{-1}$. A projection of either incident pair-adjoint algebra has this property, directly from its defining linear equations. One can also use the algebra generated by both projections, by closing the spans under products inside $\operatorname{End}(V)$. For concise tensors the two incident adjoint projections commute by transporting their maps to different partner modes. The covariance of the generated algebra in fact holds without using that commutativity. A fixed finite list of these choices can be tested and the first choice of least width used.

Compute the radical $J$ of $E=E(T)$ and the semisimple quotient
$$
B=E/J\cong\prod_j B_j,\qquad B_j=M_{d_j}(K_j),\quad K_j/\mathbb F_3 \text{ finite},\quad k_j=[K_j\colon\mathbb F_3].
$$
The constructive finite-field algebra algorithm takes a matrix basis of $E$, gives $J$ and the explicit quotient map onto these simple matrix factors, and runs deterministically in time polynomial in the matrix dimension, $\log q$ and the characteristic. Here $q=3$. Compute the radical flag $J^iV$ by taking spans of images, and let
$$
D=\bigoplus_i J^iV/J^{i+1}V.
$$
It is a $B$-module. For each factor $j$, let $M_j$ be the total multiplicity of its natural simple module in $D$ across all grades. Obtain it by the ranks of the images of the central idempotents, dividing the corresponding $\mathbb F_3$-dimension by $k_jd_j$. See [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18).

Define $\epsilon_j=M_j\bmod2$ and $\delta_j=d_j\bmod2$. The width used here is
$$
w(T)=\sum_j k_j\left(\epsilon_jd_j+\delta_j M_j+\epsilon_j\delta_j\right).
$$
The theorem applies whenever $w(T)\le C\log(2+L)$ for a fixed constant $C$ and the dense input length $L$. Compute the corresponding data on both inputs. A mismatch in dimensions of transported spaces or the two widths rules out any ordinary transporter. Comparing the widths requires neither a transporter nor a choice of how the factors match. The zero-width case holds when every active factor has both $d_j$ and $M_j$ even.

There is also a separate easy class for a single incident pair-adjoint projection on a concise tensor. If some $M_j$ is odd, the source has an automorphism with negative tested determinant. Indeed the incident projections are faithful there, with one opposite-product convention. Choose a unit of $B_j$ with determinant of norm $-1$, possible by surjectivity of the finite-field norm, and identities in the other factors. It lifts to a unit of $E$ since the radical is nilpotent. The corresponding adjoint on the partner mode is an invertible unit, and the adjoint equation produces a tensor automorphism using its inverse on the partner. The first determinant is the product of the norms raised to their multiplicities across the graded pieces, and is negative. Testing the odd multiplicity is enough for the reduction, without finding this unit or any ordinary transporter. An ordinary comparison then has both first signs whenever it has any transporter. If all $M_j$ are even, the width reduces to $\sum_{d_j\text{ odd}}k_jM_j$. For a general generated algebra, its units need not come from individually liftable adjoint units, so use the full width test there.

## 2. Determinant cancellation across all radical grades

Take one explicit simple module $U_j=K_j^{d_j}$ per factor of $B$, form $U=\bigoplus_j U_j$, and let $W=\operatorname{Hom}_B(U,D)$. The center $Z=Z(B)$ acts on the summands in both spaces, and $W_j=\operatorname{Hom}_{B_j}(U_j,D_j)$ has $K_j$-dimension $M_j$. Evaluation implements the linear decomposition
$$
D=\bigoplus_j D_j,\qquad D_j\simeq U_j\otimes_{K_j}W_j.
$$

A true raw transporter with tested map $a$ induces conjugation on $E$, the quotient algebra map on $B$, and the associated graded linear map on $D$. It can be extended by a semilinear map $u:U\to U'$ over this algebra map. On factor $j$ the induced center isomorphism is $\sigma_j:K_j\to K'_{\pi j}$, and an invertible $u_j$ exists because the transported $U_j$ and $U'_{\pi j}$ are the unique simple modules for the corresponding matrix algebras. After scalar-coordinate choices write $u_j=G_j\sigma_j$. The map on $W_j$ is forced by evaluation, $f\mapsto\operatorname{gr}(a)f u_j^{-1}$. Write it as $H_j\sigma_j$. Crucially the same $G_j$ is used on every radical grade. The conjugation $f\mapsto\operatorname{gr}(a)f u_j^{-1}$ covers every choice of $u_j$, so a free choice of basis on a simple module introduces no extra determinant beyond the calculation below.

Fix ordered $\mathbb F_3$ bases of the centers and ordered $K_j$ bases for $U_j$ and $W_j$, with the latter homogeneous by grade. Define ordered $D$ bases by evaluation in factor-first order, using the same center bases. Set $s_j=\det_{\mathbb F_3}(\sigma_j)$ in these center bases, and let $\nu$ be the target-field norm to $\mathbb F_3$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det u_j&=\nu(\det G_j)s_j^{d_j},\\
\det w_j&=\nu(\det H_j)s_j^{M_j},\\
\det(\operatorname{gr}(a)|_{D_j})&=
 \nu(\det G_j)^{M_j}\nu(\det H_j)^{d_j}s_j^{d_jM_j}
 =(\det u_j)^{\epsilon_j}(\det w_j)^{\delta_j}s_j^{\epsilon_j\delta_j}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the third identity, on $D_j$ the two linear factors are $G_j\otimes I$ and $I\otimes H_j$, and the field-coordinate map applies once on each of the $d_jM_j$ coordinates. The final power of $s_j$ corrects for counting the field-coordinate map in both auxiliary factors. This also proves the formula for independent choices of the endpoint center bases. Every displayed ground-field determinant is a sign, allowing exponents to be reduced modulo two.

Form a small space $R=\bigoplus_j R_j$ in the same factor order, where $R_j$ includes $U_j$ when $\epsilon_j=1$, $W_j$ when $\delta_j=1$, and one copy of $K_j$ when $\epsilon_j\delta_j=1$. Every auxiliary extension induces a map $r:R\to R'$. Its within-factor determinant equals the third displayed determinant. The sign of a permutation swapping two factors of dimensions $n_1,n_2$ is $(-1)^{n_1n_2}$. Here
$$
\dim_{\mathbb F_3} R_j\equiv k_jd_j M_j=\dim_{\mathbb F_3}D_j\bmod2.
$$
The determinant of the full block permutation therefore agrees on $R$ and $D$, including permutations of repeated identical simple factors. It follows that $\det(r)=\det(\operatorname{gr}(a))$ in the displayed bases.

Choose arbitrary lifts of the $D$ basis from its graded quotients to the actual corresponding $J^iV$ spaces, in the same factor-first order. They form a basis of $V$ because the original basis is homogeneous by grade. Write $C_T$ for its column matrix in the raw $V$ basis. The filtration determinant equals the product of the induced grade determinants when both frame orders are by grade, and applying the endpoint permutations to factor-first order gives
$$
\det_{\rm raw}(a)=\det(C_{T'})\,\det(C_T)^{-1}\det(r).
$$
Consequently take the marked volume of $R$ to be $(\det C_T)^{-1}$ times the top wedge of its displayed basis. For every extension, preserving that marked volume is equivalent to $\det_{\rm raw}(a)=1$.

## 3. A fixed tensor system and exact packing

The two endpoint systems contain the raw three-tensor, the represented algebra $E$ with multiplication and its action map $E\otimes V\to V$, the quotient $E\to B$, multiplication on $B$, and the included center. The faithful action forces the algebra map on $E$ to be the conjugation by the original tested mode. The surjective quotient and center inclusion force the intended induced maps.

To link the graded pieces to $V$ with a fixed number of maps, put $P=\bigoplus_i J^i V$ and include the linear map $P\to V$ equal to the sum of the inclusions. Also put $D=\bigoplus_i J^iV/J^{i+1}V$ and include $P\to D$ equal to the sum of the quotient maps. On each of $P$ and $D$ include the coordinate projections through an action of the split grade algebra $\mathbb F_3^g$. Mark the directed shift sending consecutive idempotents to their successors and the last idempotent to zero. An automorphism of the grade algebra permutes its primitive idempotents, and commuting with this shift fixes them all. The map $P\to V$ thus identifies the intended restriction on every grade summand, since it is injective separately there. The map $P\to D$ forces the induced quotient maps. Include the $B$ action on $D$.

Use the $B$ action on $U$ and the evaluation map $W\otimes U\to D$. The former sends the $U_j$ to their matching simple modules, with an arbitrary compatible semilinear lift. The latter forces exactly the stated map on $W$. Include the center action or multiplication for each of these spaces as needed to express the factor decomposition. In particular, no choice of a label for a simple factor is rigidified. Keep three spaces, one selected summand of $U$, one of $W$, and one of $Z$, using the linear inclusions of the sums specified in the width formula. Their images are invariant under every compatible extension because the pair $(d_j,M_j)$ is transported with the simple factor. Include their injections into $R$. This forces exactly the direct sum map on $R$. Each map in this list is linear or bilinear, there are a fixed number of them, and their structure constants are obtained by linear algebra, the quotient computation, and evaluation.

Finally include the inclusion of $R$ into the exterior algebra $\Lambda R$, its algebra multiplication, and the marked top volume. Its dimension is $2^w$. The map of exterior algebras is uniquely determined by its restriction to the generating copy of $R$, so the mark tests exactly the factor-first determinant computed above. If $w=0$ one can simply compare the raw frame signs and return the original ordinary comparison or a fixed negative ordinary instance according to that comparison.

Each genuine determinant-one transporter extends to an isomorphism of the systems, by taking any compatible lift on $U$, the forced one on $W$, and the induced exterior map. Every system isomorphism projects to a genuine transporter of the raw tensors, and the top-volume equality gives determinant one in the first mode. Thus this is an exact single ordinary problem. The fixed collection of linear and bilinear maps and one vector mark is an ordinary tensor system of bounded order and a fixed signature. The ordinary compiler gives one dense three-tensor pair. Its size and construction time are polynomial in $L$ and $2^w$, hence polynomial at the stated width. The zero-dimensional cases can omit the corresponding role, with finitely many possible signatures. See [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4).

For two independent signed comparisons, if one meets the width test then take its ordinary output and attach it to the other with the earlier determinant-neutral ordinary conjunction. The resulting one-sign instance accepts exactly when both original signed comparisons do. This checks raw nonisomorphism as well as the sign, and it needs no TI or stabilizer oracle.

## 4. A nonsemisimple growing example

Let $C=\mathbb F_3[\eta]/(\eta^2)$ and take the $V\to V$ slice space of right multiplications on $V=C^{2\times m}$ by every element of $M_m(C)$. The slice dimensions are $(4m,4m,2m^2)$. The identity slice forces the two components of the fixed-label pair adjoint to agree in the map convention $QM(z)=M(z)P$. Commuting with the diagonal $\eta$ action forces $C$-linearity. Then commuting with the right matrix units forces a single two-by-two matrix over $C$ acting on the left, so the projected algebra is $E=M_2(C)$. Its radical is $M_2(\eta C)$. The two radical grades each have natural-module multiplicity $m$ for $B=M_2(\mathbb F_3)$, and the total multiplicity is $2m$. Thus $w=0$, even when the multiplicity on each individual grade is odd and all three input mode dimensions grow. Arbitrary raw basis changes preserve this conclusion. Reflecting one tested coordinate at the target gives ordinarily isomorphic inputs that fail the original determinant test and are rejected by the calibrated frame signs.

## 5. Relation to earlier work

The earlier GI reduction using adjoint multiplicity spaces required all three pair adjoints to be split semisimple and small joint multiplicity dimensions, then used GI to recover residual transporters. The present construction uses one represented algebra, permits a radical and extension-field simple factors, works with total multiplicities across radical grades, and gives one deterministic ordinary TI instance for the signed comparison under a different width condition. Its determinant calculation handles arbitrary permutations of simple factors and arbitrary lifts on the auxiliary modules. The earlier exterior pin still does the final sign test, now on the smaller space computed by the adjoint calculation.

This is an informal proof. The scalar-adjoint case on a large tested mode can still have full width, so this does not prove unrestricted binary conjunction closure.
