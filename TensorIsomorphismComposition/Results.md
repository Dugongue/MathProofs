# Composition, supplied transporters, and tractable form actions in tensor isomorphism

Four informal theorems. TI denotes ordinary isomorphism of explicit three-tensors under one independent invertible map in each mode. SL-TI requires all three determinants to be one. We use the bilinear-map convention for three-tensors, with dual actions in the two input modes. This convention does not affect a determinant-one condition. The results strengthen the earlier comparison-capacity, field-extension, and determinant observation theorems. [Earlier proof packet](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IsomorphismDescentAndObservations/Results.md)

A fact used throughout is that unital associative algebras are isomorphic exactly when their multiplication tensors are isotopic. If P(x)Q(y)=R(xy) is an isotopy, write a=P(1), b=Q(1). Evaluating at the unit gives R(x)=P(x)b=aQ(x). The maps of right multiplication by b and left multiplication by a are bijective, so a,b are units. Then φ(x)=a⁻¹P(x)=Q(x)b⁻¹ is a unital algebra isomorphism. Conversely an isomorphism φ and units a,b give (L_aφ,R_bφ,L_aR_bφ).

## 1. One tensor comparison can evaluate a monotone span program of tensor comparisons

Let R be any nonzero connected commutative ring, including a field or a local ring. Suppose the inputs are a list of ordinary free-module TI comparisons (A_i,B_i), a matrix H over an auxiliary finite field K of polynomial cardinality, a target vector b, and a label i on each column of H. Let I be the columns whose labels are positive input comparisons. In deterministic polynomial time on dense explicit inputs one can construct two ordinary three-tensors over R that are isomorphic exactly when b belongs to the K-span of H_I. The auxiliary field can be unrelated to R. The construction uses ring inputs only as coefficients in free corners, along with zero and one. As usual, arithmetic on arbitrary R coefficients is interpreted in their input representation, and only copying is required here.

### Translating the linear equations into permitted permutations

Make a fiber of vertices (j,a) for each scalar variable x_j, with a∈K. The variable j is a vertex color. For each j and d∈K include a binary relation with ordered pairs (a,a+d). Any color- and relation-preserving map of the fiber has the form a↦a+x_j, since its values at a and a+d have difference d. The relation names are fixed separately.

For each row of H introduce free variables for successive partial sums, and encode the row by relations on triples specifying s_j−s_(j−1)−H_(row,j)x_j=0. Pin s_0 to zero on both sides. Pin the last sum to zero on the source and the corresponding b entry on the target. A translation maps the relation sets on the source to those on the target exactly when its shifts satisfy Hx=b, with the free shifts supplying the partial sums. The unary pins are relations of arity one. For each original column j with label i, attach tensor A_i at position (j,0) and B_i at every other position, on both sides. If A_i and B_i are inequivalent, the shift must fix zero. If they are equivalent, every shift is permitted. Thus the accepted shift vectors are exactly the solutions with x_j=0 for j outside I.

To represent these relations as an ordinary colored graph, make a vertex for every relation tuple and a separate subdivision vertex for each of its positions. Give every relation symbol a tuple color and give its different positions distinct new colors. Attach the tuple vertex to its subdivision vertices and each subdivision vertex to the named fiber vertex. The colors and incidences recover the ordered relations. This costs polynomial space because the largest arity is three and |K| is polynomial. This is the same basic use of translation and linear relations as in the CFI tradition, applied here to ports with tensor equivalence tests. [Torán, On the Hardness of Graph Isomorphism](https://doi.org/10.1137/S009753970241096X)

### Compiling the colored graph with tensors at its ports

Construct a free associative algebra with one diagonal copy of R for every graph vertex. Orient graph edges by a fixed ordering of their distinct endpoint colors. Give each directed edge a rank-one corner. At each tensor port w add three private vertices u,v,z, colored separately by port type and role. Give each arrow from w to those vertices a rank-one corner. For a leaf tensor regarded as a bilinear map U×V→W, set the corners u→v, v→z, u→z to U,V,W. Their product along u→v→z is the supplied bilinear map. Every other off-diagonal product is zero. No product of three off-diagonal elements can be nonzero, and both bracketings therefore agree. The diagonal vertex idempotents act by their specified incidences, which gives associativity when a factor is diagonal.

To make colors intrinsic, give every previously placed vertex one new private incoming source with just one nonzero arrow corner, of rank M_c for its color c. Use distinct ranks M_c all greater than every other corner rank. Then all the marker sources, their unique destinations, and the destination colors are recovered from the large corners. Every vertex is of one of these two forms. All marker ranks are polynomial, including when the number of colors grows.

The ideal J of off-diagonal corners is the commutator ideal: a corner element x from e_i to e_j has [e_i,x]=x, while every commutator is off-diagonal. The quotient is R^v. Connectedness of R makes its standard coordinate idempotents precisely the primitive idempotents of this quotient. An algebra isomorphism Φ permutes them by some σ. In the target algebra the element u=Σ_i Φ(e_i)e_(σ(i)) is congruent to one mod J and hence is a unit, since J³=0. It satisfies Φ(e_i)u=u e_(σ(i)). Conjugation by u⁻¹ therefore aligns the actual idempotents. After alignment, corner ranks recover the graph and colors, and the corner products recover the leaf TI comparisons. Conversely any allowed graph permutation and leaf isotopies extend to an algebra isomorphism, by taking the induced maps on the leaf corners and arbitrary standard identifications on the rank-one and marker corners. This extends the weighted path-algebra construction to controlled permutations with live tensor data in the corners. [Grochow and Qiao, §9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.00309)

The algebra has polynomial dimension, since all corners and marker ranks have polynomial size. Its multiplication tensor therefore has polynomial dense encoding size and can be emitted within that bound. The unit-isotopy calculation at the start proves the promised single TI comparison.

### Consequences

* An OR of arbitrarily many input TI comparisons uses H=(1,…,1), b=1 over F₂. AND uses the identity matrix and the all-ones target.
* To test whether at least t of k inputs are positive, choose k+1 distinct elements a₀,…,a_k of an auxiliary prime field of polynomial cardinality. Column i is (1,a_i,…,a_i^(t−1)), and the target is the same expression at a₀. Vandermonde independence makes the span test exactly the threshold. The cases t≤0 or t>k are constant. A prime of size polynomial in k is found by trial division in a suitable interval using Bertrand's postulate.
* Every explicit monotone formula can be compiled. The direct sum of span programs with the direct sum target implements AND. For OR, identify the target lines of the branches while keeping their chosen complementary spaces separate. A combination giving the common target has zero separate complements, so at least one branch supplies a nonzero multiple of its own target. These operations give span programs of size polynomial in the formula size. Repeated leaves retain their test labels.
* For the earlier three-subspace determinant lock, suppose a polynomial list of target triples covers the image under at least one sign-valid transporter whenever one exists. Each triple passing the determinant check gives an ordinary TI comparison by a four-component tensor system. The OR construction replaces that list of ordinary calls by **one deterministic polynomial-size ordinary TI instance**. The covering list is still a hypothesis.

Any-arity AND and OR for graph isomorphism have prior art. The result here uses ordinary tensor tests as the input gates, and gives span-program aggregation directly over the coefficient ring of those tests. Via Grochow–Qiao's polynomial equivalence for the matrix-generator model at fixed odd prime p, it also gives such aggregation for class-two exponent-p group isomorphism in that model. It makes no polynomial output claim for Cayley-table groups. [Chang and Kadin, AND and OR of sets](https://userpages.cs.umbc.edu/chang/papers/andor/) · [Grochow and Qiao, Lemma 7.5 and Corollary 7.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.00309)

## 2. Special-linear TI remains TI-hard with an explicit ordinary transporter

Over F₃, unrestricted ordinary TI reduces to exact SL-TI even when the two output tensors are multiplication tensors of unital associative algebras with cube-zero radical, differ by a diagonal determinant-−1 involution in one mode, and are accompanied by that ordinary transporter. The sum of the output mode lengths is linear in the sum of the input mode lengths. In the output family, the image of the ordinary automorphism group under the three determinant signs is either the even-parity plane or all of F₂³. Distinguishing the two is TI-hard.

First compare the input format and the support dimensions in every mode. On equality, trim the input and target to their supports, using the intrinsic images of the flattenings. A linear isomorphism of supports extends to the full same-dimensional spaces, so this preserves the verdict. A zero tensor and a dimension mismatch can be handled by fixed instances, obtained below from fixed equivalent or inequivalent nonzero tensors. For a concise bilinear map T:X×Y→Z form the local algebra A_T=F₃1⊕X⊕Y⊕Z, with the sole radical products x·y=T(x,y), and scalar unit action. If J is the radical, J²=Z, J³=0, and the left and right kernels of the induced pairing on J/J² are respectively Y and X. These statements follow from surjectivity and the two other conciseness conditions. Hence an algebra isomorphism induces exactly an isotopy on X,Y,Z. Conversely every such isotopy extends, so A_T determines the TI orbit.

Put C_T=M₂(A_T), and form the triangular algebra

```text
B_T = {(a,x,c) | a∈F₃, x∈M₂(F₃), c∈C_T},
(a,x,c)(a',x',c') = (aa', ax' + x c̄', cc'),
```

where the bar is the scalar residue applied entrywise. This algebra has odd dimension 5+4 dim A_T. Its radical is the off-diagonal M₂(F₃) plus M₂(J) in the bottom corner. Its square is M₂(J²) because the off-diagonal module annihilates the bottom radical. Its cube is zero. The scalar and matrix blocks of its semisimple quotient are distinguished by dimension. A central idempotent must commute with the two vertex idempotents, so is diagonal. Its bottom central coordinate is an idempotent in the center of M₂(A_T), which is the local-ring center of A_T and has only idempotents zero and one. The two diagonal coordinates have equal residues because the unit of the off-diagonal module intertwines them. Thus B_T has no central idempotents besides zero and one.

Any isomorphism of these B algebras can be conjugated by a unit in 1+rad to align the two vertex idempotents, using Σ_i Φ(e_i)e_i as in Section 1. Its bottom-corner map is then an isomorphism of matrix algebras. For local A,A', an isomorphism M₂(A)→M₂(A') can be written as inner conjugation after applying an A→A' isomorphism entrywise. Indeed first align the image of the scalar matrix units modulo the radical by a scalar invertible matrix, using the matrix-unit basis of the two-dimensional column module. For lifted units E'_(ij) congruent to the standard E_(ij), the unit s=Σ_i E'_(i1)E_(1i) is congruent to one and intertwines the two systems. After conjugating again, the map fixes the matrix-unit system and is entrywise from its action on the (1,1) corner. Thus B_T≅B_U exactly when T≅U.

Every automorphism of B_T has square determinant over F₃. The aligning conjugation by 1+rad is unipotent, as seen on the radical filtration. On C_T left and right multiplication by a unit have square determinants because the linear actions repeat on the two columns or rows. Thus inner conjugation has square determinant, and an entrywise map has fourth-power determinant. On the off-diagonal M₂(F₃), an aligned map is x↦s φ̄(x), where φ̄ is the bottom residue-algebra automorphism and s is invertible. This follows by setting x to the identity in the right-module intertwining equation. Left multiplication by s repeats on two columns, and φ̄ is inner on M₂(F₃). The scalar role is fixed. This proves the determinant assertion.

For T,U with equal support shapes take D=B_T×B_U. The two factors are its intrinsically defined connected blocks. An automorphism fixing the blocks has determinant one by the preceding calculation. A swapping automorphism exists exactly when T≅U and has a representative of determinant −1, using an isomorphism and its inverse on the two blocks. Swapping equal odd-dimensional blocks gives that sign. The left and right unit determinant images of D both contain −1, by multiplication by the scalar −1 on just one odd block. Thus the self-isotopy sign triples are exactly (a,b,a+b+c), with a,b arbitrary and c running over signs of algebra automorphisms. Reflecting the output mode gives an explicit ordinary transporter, and there is an SL transporter to the reflected tensor exactly when c=1 is possible. The reflected tensor is itself the multiplication tensor of a unital associative algebra in the bilinear-map convention. Its product is S(xy) for the output reflection S, so for this statement choose S to act as −1 on the first connected factor and as the identity on the second. Then S is multiplication by a central involutory unit. The source and target are algebra-isomorphic by S used on all three slots, and the target radical is likewise cube-zero. The ordinary transporter supplied to the signed problem is (I,I,S), which has precisely one negative sign.

This is a hardness theorem with a witness in the input. It does not give the reverse reduction from unrestricted SL-TI asked by the classical-groups paper. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2)

## 3. All extension spectra occur with a supplied base-field ordinary transporter

For pairs over F₃ with a supplied ordinary base-field transporter, the sets of degrees f admitting literal determinant-one SL-TI over F_(3^f) are exactly the finite unions of sets dℕ_{>0}, including the empty union. The upper bound is the finite Frobenius-component argument in the previous packet. The extra content is the converse under the promised ordinary witness.

### Construct an odd local algebra whose automorphisms all have determinant one

Let K be any field of characteristic three. Start from K[x,y]/(x,y)^5 and impose x³=y³=x²y² and x⁴=x³y=xy³=y⁴=0. Set s=x²y². This has basis

```text
1, x, y, x², xy, y², x²y, xy², s.
```

Indeed the imposed linear relations are independent in the degree-at-most-four space and their nontrivial multiples by x,y are among the four relations killing degree-four monomials. Adjoin three radical generators z,w₁,w₂ and three independent product vectors xz,yw₁,yw₂. On the three generators, prescribe their pairwise products as the coordinate dot product times s. Their only nonzero products with x,y are xz,yw₁,yw₂. They annihilate the main radical square. The three product vectors annihilate the whole radical. Every radical triple involving a new generator or product vector now vanishes in either bracketing, so this is a 15-dimensional commutative local associative algebra B₀. Write m₀ for its radical.

For a finite étale K-algebra E use B(E)=K⊕(m₀⊗_K E) with scalar unit action. Over an algebraic closure its radical splits into one branch of dimension 14 per geometric idempotent of E, with cross-branch products zero. Intrinsic subquotients recover the branches and their determinant contributions as follows. Write m for its radical, S=m⁴, W=m²/m³, and let Y be the radical of the S-valued pairing W×W→S. On each branch Y is spanned by xz,yw₁,yw₂. On W/Y the block with basis x²,xy,y² has a nondegenerate 3-by-3 form valued in the line Ks. The matrix pairs x² with y² and xy with itself. A functional on S gives an ordinary form whose rank is three times the number of nonzero branch coordinates. Therefore its rank-three lines are precisely the branch coordinate lines in S*. Their common-kernel intersections recover the individual blocks W_i in W/Y, and duality recovers the individual S_i.

Let Z be the image in m/m² of m∩Ann(m²). In each branch this is the span of z,w₁,w₂. No x or y component can lie in that image, since multiplying by xy gives independent classes x²y,xy² modulo m⁴. Let X=(m/m²)/Z. Its individual two-dimensional branches X_i are exactly the elements whose square in W/Y lies in W_i. The square is well-defined because the contributions involving Z vanish in W/Y, and a square of a nonzero combination of x,y is nonzero in every occupied branch. The branch of Z is recovered by requiring its pairing with Z to lie in S_i. The pairing Z×Z→S is well-defined using lifts in Ann(m²), and is the nondegenerate dot product on each branch. Multiplication X×Z→Y is well-defined modulo m³ using the same lifts. On X_i it distinguishes the x line by rank one and the y line by rank two. A combination with both coordinates nonzero has rank three.

An automorphism therefore permutes the intrinsic branches and acts on each X_i by separate scalars a,b. It acts on the corresponding Z_i by a matrix O. The higher-degree corrections to x³,y³ vanish under cubing in characteristic three. Sidearm corrections have zero cube as well. In x²y² the higher corrections also vanish, using m⁵=0 and the zero radical triples involving sidearms. The relations x³=y³=x²y²=s therefore force the multiplier on S_i to be a³=b³=a²b². Hence a=b=1. The Gram relation yields det(O)²=1. On m/m² this contributes det(O) once. It contributes the same determinant on the sidearm products in m²/m³, because x,y have scale one and the rank-one and rank-two product kernels distinguish the corresponding direct summands of Z_i. All other main monomial classes in the radical filtration map to their counterparts with scale one. The determinant of the entire branch permutation is (sgn π)^14=1, since each branch has total radical dimension 14. The scalar quotient is fixed. Thus every automorphism of B(E) has determinant **exactly** one, over every extension.

The branch identification via rank is Frobenius-equivariant when K is finite. It follows that B(E) and B(E') are isomorphic precisely when E and E' are isomorphic: an isomorphism must match the geometric branches with their Frobenius action, and every map of the étale algebras gives an isomorphism by tensoring. This conclusion holds over each finite extension as well.

### Realize the desired spectrum

Use the earlier divisor Möbius construction to obtain equal-dimensional, nonzero étale E₊,E₋ with the desired nonempty spectrum U for their isomorphism. Explicitly, let M be the lcm of the generators. Take F(e)=0 on U and M otherwise, and use the positive and negative parts of c_m=(1/m)Σ_(e|m) μ(m/e)F(e) as field-factor multiplicities of degree m for m|M. All c_m are integers. The fixed-point difference of the two Frobenius permutations at degree f is F(f). Their powers at degree f have the same cycle type exactly when F(fk)=0 for all k, equivalent to f∈U. A common field factor can be added to both sides without changing the spectrum. [Earlier inverse spectrum theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IsomorphismDescentAndObservations/Results.md)

Put D=B(E₊)×B(E₋). Both factors are odd-dimensional and local. Over any extension their product has a determinant-−1 automorphism exactly when its local factors are isomorphic. Block-preserving maps have determinant one by the construction. An available swap has determinant −1 by implementing it with an isomorphism and its inverse across blocks, and all swaps have the same determinant. Reflect one output mode of the multiplication tensor. The provided reflection is an ordinary base-field transporter. The unit-isotopy calculation implies that the reflected pair has an exact SL transporter if and only if D has an algebra automorphism of determinant −1. For the forward direction, det(P)=det(Q)=1 forces det(R)=(det φ)⁻¹ before reflection. For the reverse direction take an algebra automorphism of determinant −1 and use a,b each equal to the unit that acts by −1 on one odd block. This makes the first two determinants one and the third −1, so the reflected one is one. For the empty spectrum reflect multiplication of the single algebra B₀, whose algebra automorphisms all have determinant one.

If the extension constraint is quadratic square class instead of literal determinant one, the spectra obtainable under the same promise are exactly those finite divisibility unions containing all even degrees. The upper direction again uses Frobenius on components, now of the transporter lifted to the finite étale cover adjoining a square root of each determinant. Every base ordinary witness becomes square-class positive in even extensions. In odd extensions −1 is nonsquare, so for the reflected family the same parity calculation gives positivity exactly on the prescribed odd degrees. This realizes all possibilities.

## 4. Deterministic polynomial time for form-preserving TI with cyclic asymmetries

Fix a finite field F=F_q. In each mode give a nondegenerate bilinear form, with Gram matrix B, and suppose the asymmetry S=B⁻¹B^T is cyclic, meaning that its characteristic and minimal polynomials coincide. Then form-preserving three-tensor isomorphism is decidable in deterministic polynomial time. The asymmetry may have repeated elementary divisors of arbitrary multiplicity. The algorithm supports prescribed determinant values in the modes, and also different endpoint forms when both endpoint asymmetries are cyclic. This gives an explicit tractable case of the published question on tensor actions preserving forms that are neither symmetric nor skew-symmetric. It is a case of the question with a structural hypothesis. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Open Question 1.11](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2)

### From a tensor orbit to multiplicative equations

Every B-isometry commutes with S, by transposing the isometry equation and comparing. The centralizer of cyclic S is the commutative algebra E=F[S], because a map commuting with S is determined by its action on one cyclic vector. The involution x*=B⁻¹ x^T B preserves E since S*=S⁻¹, and the isometry condition is x*x=1. More generally the joint centralizer of S and a supplied tuple of marks may be used in place of F[S] whenever that centralizer is commutative and stable under the reference adjoint. For example, for additional forms B_j use the operators B⁻¹B_j and their adjoints. A B-isometry preserves B_j exactly when it commutes with B⁻¹B_j, and commutation with its adjoint then follows from x*=x⁻¹. This adjoint-stable commutant is computed by linear equations. [Classical asymmetry decomposition](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-4049%2801%2900123-2)

In different endpoint forms, first test similarity of the asymmetries and align them by rational canonical form. In the aligned coordinates the second form is B D. Both have the same S, so transposing (B D)S implies D*=D. The identity *²(D)=S D S⁻¹ then puts D in E. An isometry from B to B D now has x*x=D⁻¹. Endpoint determinants acquire only the known factors from alignment.

Let A=E₁⊗_F E₂⊗_F E₃ act on the tensor space. Write v,w for the two tensors. Check Av=Aw by computing linear images of the two action maps, and reject if they differ. Otherwise solve r v=w linearly. In the algebra Q=A/Ann_A(v) this solution is unique and is a unit. In fact one can also solve t w=v, giving tr=1 in Q. Therefore the exact tensor orbit constraint is that the product of the three respective unit images in Q equal r. The map from each E_i to Q is an algebra homomorphism. The involution of an E_i is also an algebra homomorphism by commutativity. The isometry and tensor conditions are thus a system of multiplicative equations between units of explicitly given finite commutative algebras. Dimensions of all algebra tensor products are polynomial since the number of slots is fixed. Shared variables, tensor factors carrying duals, and additional fixed-arity tensor components can be handled with the same construction. In a dual action use the transposed algebra representation and the inverse unit, which is legitimate because E_i is commutative.

Prescribed determinants supply further multiplicative equations to F×. On a finite module the principal units act unipotently. On the radical layers the action of a residue unit a in a field factor K of E/J has determinant Norm_(K/F)(a), raised to the total multiplicity of that simple in the layers. These multiplicities and the norm exponents are computed from the layer dimensions and simple idempotents. In the cyclic case the module is E itself, so the multiplicities are just the lengths of the local factors. This reduces the determinant conditions to more residue-field equations.

### Solve the unit equations without discrete logarithms

In an explicit finite commutative F-algebra R, the map x↦x^{q^h} for q^h≥dim_F(R)+1 has kernel J=rad R and image a coefficient subalgebra mapping isomorphically onto R/J. This follows because it kills the nilpotent radical and is an automorphism modulo J, a product of fields. The map is F-linear and is computed on a basis. The image is therefore the intrinsic product of coefficient fields and behaves functorially under the supplied homomorphisms. The reduced algebra splits by computing the subalgebra fixed by the q-Frobenius. Primitive factor idempotents can be separated by refining with 1−(x−c)^{q−1} for basis elements x of the fixed subalgebra and c∈F. This is polynomial for fixed q. Thus units split into independent residue and principal parts, and equations split compatibly.

For principal units, choose F_p-bases of J^i/J^(i+1) and lifts b_ij, where p is the characteristic. Use 1+b_ij as generators of 1+J. Every unit has unique coordinates with each digit in {0,…,p−1}, by removing successive layers of the filtration. For each generator record its p-th power in higher coordinates. The resulting triangular integer relations present the group: they reduce any expression to this unique normal form. Compute images of generators and right-hand sides by the same filtration procedure. Integer Smith or Hermite normal form tests if the right-hand sides lie in the image of the direct product of the input groups in the direct product of the output groups. Concretely, add all target relation columns to the image-generator columns and test integer lattice membership. The presentations have polynomial bit size. [Kannan and Bachem, polynomial algorithms for Smith and Hermite normal forms](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

For residues let K_j be the explicitly listed fields of degrees d_j and let m_j=q^(d_j)−1 be their multiplicative orders. The bit length of L=lcm_j m_j is polynomial. We first synchronize finite field embeddings without constructing their full compositum. For each prime ℓ dividing some d_j, choose a listed field of maximal ℓ-adic degree and take its nested subfields of degrees ℓ^a, computed by Frobenius fixed-point kernels. For each K_j choose an embedding of each needed master subfield into K_j. One computes an embedding for any divisibility of degrees by taking the algebra tensor product of the two fields, splitting it as a product by the fixed-point method above, and taking the projection to one factor. The larger field maps isomorphically onto that factor, so linear inversion yields the embedding of the smaller field. The multiplication map from the tensor product of the chosen prime-power subfields onto K_j is an isomorphism because these degrees are pairwise coprime and their product is d_j. Using these tensor coordinates identifies all K_j with compatible fixed copies in an abstract closure. In particular a coherent map at degree divisibility acts by the master inclusions in these coordinates. Every actual embedding differs by a Frobenius power, found by trying fewer than d_j powers for a domain of degree d_j. Common subfields, at gcd degrees, are identified by the same prime-power coordinates.

We can now read the equations as integer exponent equations in the abstract cyclic group μ_L, because Frobenius embeddings contribute known powers q^a after synchronization. Restrict each variable from K_j by adding x_j^(m_j)=1. To hold and manipulate the right-hand sides, partition L into relatively prime factors D_j, with D_j|m_j and product L. This needs no integer factorization. Process the m_j in order, maintaining remaining factor L'. Set c=L'/gcd(L',m_j) and B₀=bitlength(L), and take D_j=L'/gcd(L',c^B₀). It consists of exactly the remaining maximal prime powers fully supported by m_j. Remove it and continue. Every prime power in L is eventually removed, and all arithmetic has polynomial bit size. Project each right-hand side onto μ_(D_j) by the usual CRT exponent, congruent to one modulo D_j and zero modulo L/D_j. The component of a value from K_i lies in the intersection field with K_j because its order divides both m_i and m_j. Transfer it coherently into K_j.

In each μ_(D_j) apply integer Smith diagonalization to the exponent matrix. Unimodular row operations act on the right-hand side by raising to known integer powers and multiplying in K_j. Unimodular column operations change the variables bijectively. An equation x^s=b in this cyclic group is solvable exactly if b^{D_j/gcd(D_j,s)}=1, with a zero row demanding b=1. The projections are independent, so this decides the full system. Matrix operations, Frobenius, and exponent arithmetic have polynomial bit cost. The decision requires neither computing a discrete logarithm nor representing a field of degree lcm_j d_j.

For a repeated-asymmetry example, let C be companion to f(t)^a for a non-self-reciprocal irreducible polynomial f with nonzero constant term. The form with matrix [[0,I],[C,0]] has asymmetry diag(C⁻¹,C^T). Its two blocks have coprime minimal polynomials f*(t)^a and f(t)^a, so the asymmetry is cyclic and a is unbounded. The procedure therefore handles arbitrarily long nonsemisimple layers as well as semisimple forms.

### A TI-complete family with the same two irreducible asymmetry factors

For every fixed F_q, choose its degree-three extension K and a multiplicative generator α of K×. Its inverse is not Frobenius-conjugate to it, since q^i+1<q³−1 for i=0,1,2. Let C be multiplication by α on K^m viewed over F_q, and again use B=[[0,I],[C,0]]. The two blocks of the asymmetry have disjoint primary factors, so any B-isometry is block diagonal. The isometry equation now says it is diag(P,P^(-T)), with P commuting with C. This is exactly GL_m(K) on the first summand, with its dual on the second.

Embed any three-tensor over K in the product of the three first summands. In the tensor product over F_q, use the field factor of K⊗K⊗K corresponding to the multiplication map onto K. The tensor product over K is naturally identified with this direct summand by the idempotent of that field factor. The group actions respect it and induce exactly the original K-linear actions. Thus TI over K reduces to formed TI on this family, with linear scale in the mode lengths. The reverse reduction to ordinary TI over F_q is the published fixed-component tensor system compiler, adjoining the three forms as marks. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

For completeness, ordinary TI over F_q reduces to ordinary TI over K without admitting extra transporters. For each mode V of the scalar-extended tensor, add named spaces Sym^j(V) for 2≤j≤q and the multiplication maps V⊗Sym^(j−1)(V)→Sym^j(V). Their intertwining equations force the maps on those spaces to be Sym^j(g) for the original mode map g, by induction using surjectivity. Add the K-linear mark L:V→Sym^q(V) given by L(e_i)=e_i^q on the original coordinate basis. The relation Sym^q(g)L=Lg forces every entry c of g to satisfy c^q=c by the Frobenius identity on q-th powers. Conversely all base-field transporters extend. The number of components is constant and their sizes polynomial since q is fixed, so compile to ordinary TI over K by the same theorem. This proves TI-completeness of the formed family over F_q. It has the same two distinct irreducible asymmetry factors as the cyclic tractable family obtained from powers of the minimal polynomial of α. The difference in the promise is cyclicity, not the set of irreducible factors.

This algorithm does not cover noncommutative marked commutants in general. The four theorems here are informal, with the closest methods credited above. No claim of priority or of a resolution of unrestricted SL-TI containment is made.
