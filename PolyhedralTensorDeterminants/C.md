# The dodecahedral diamond quotient changes determinant-one orbit counts

Let $R$ be a nonzero finite commutative local ring and $U=R^\times$. The boundary face poset of a Platonic solid has its vertices, edges and polygonal faces as elements, ordered by inclusion. The incidence algebra of this poset has free basis $E_{xy}$ for $x\le y$, with $E_{xy}E_{zw}=0$ unless $y=z$, in which case the product is $E_{xw}$.

**Theorem.** For the incidence algebra of the dodecahedron, the automorphism determinant image is $U^2\langle-1\rangle$. Its multiplication tensor has exactly $[U:U^2\langle-1\rangle]$ determinant-one orbit classes inside its ordinary three-leg orbit. If instead the multiplication uses the path algebra of the vertex-to-edge-to-face quiver, the automorphism determinant image is $U^3$ and the count is $[U:U^3]$. Passing from the path algebra to the incidence algebra identifies the two paths from any vertex to a face containing it.

In particular the diamond quotient takes the orbit count from one to two over $\mathbb F_5$, and from three to one over $\mathbb F_7$. Over the dual numbers $\mathbb F_2[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ it takes the count from one to two. The incidence algebra over $\mathbb Z/4$ has just one class, even though this ring has the same residue field and an isomorphic unit group as the dual numbers. The distinguished unit $-1$ accounts for the difference.

The incidence-algebra calculation also covers every Platonic solid.

| Solid | Faces at each vertex | Vertices per face | Free rank | Automorphism determinant image |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Tetrahedron | 3 | 3 | 50 | $U^6\langle-1\rangle$ |
| Cube | 3 | 4 | 98 | $U^2\langle-1\rangle$ |
| Octahedron | 4 | 3 | 98 | $U^2\langle-1\rangle$ |
| Dodecahedron | 3 | 5 | 242 | $U^2\langle-1\rangle$ |
| Icosahedron | 5 | 3 | 242 | $U^2\langle-1\rangle$ |

In each row the number of determinant-one classes in the ordinary orbit is the index of the last column in $U$. Over $\mathbb F_q$, the count for the last four rows is two if $q\equiv1\pmod4$ and one otherwise. The tetrahedron multiplies this by three when $q\equiv1\pmod3$.

## From algebra automorphisms to interval weights

In either algebra, order the vertex idempotents $e_x$ by a linear extension of the face poset. The ideal $J$ of all strictly upper blocks together with maximal-ideal diagonal entries is nilpotent. Its reduction modulo the maximal ideal of $R$ is strictly upper triangular, and the maximal ideal of $R$ itself is nilpotent. The quotient is a product of copies of the residue field, so $J$ is the Jacobson radical.

Any algebra automorphism permutes the residual coordinate idempotents. If $f_x$ are the transported idempotents and $\pi$ is their permutation modulo $J$, the element $u=\sum_x f_x e_{\pi(x)}$ is congruent to 1 modulo $J$ and satisfies $ue_{\pi(x)}=f_xu$. It is a unit. Its diagonal coefficients are exactly 1, since each diagonal coefficient of $f_x$ is an idempotent of the local ring and its reduction specifies whether it is 0 or 1. Consequently conjugation by $u$ has determinant one over $R$. Left and right multiplication by $u$ or its inverse are triangular with diagonal ones, using the starting and ending indices of the interval or path basis.

After this conjugation all idempotents are aligned. An automorphism of the incidence algebra permutes the faces by an order automorphism, since $e_xAe_y$ is nonzero exactly for $x\le y$. Every order automorphism lifts by relabeling the interval basis, hence contributes precisely the sign of its interval permutation. The remaining automorphism scales each comparable-pair basis vector $E_{xy}$ by a unit $w_{xy}$. The multiplication law requires
$$
w_{xz}=w_{xy}w_{yz}\qquad(x<y<z),
$$
and every such choice gives an automorphism.

## Why the polygon relations change the image

The order complex of the boundary face poset is the barycentric subdivision of the boundary sphere. Its edges are strictly comparable pairs, and its triangles are vertex-edge-face flags. Orient the edges from lower to higher rank. The relation for $w$ says that the product along two sides of a triangle equals the product along the third side, traversing an edge backwards with reciprocal weight. Since this complex triangulates a sphere, every closed edge path is generated by the triangle relations and backtracks. Equivalently its first integral homology is zero. Multiplying weights from a fixed basepoint to each poset element therefore defines units $g_x$ with
$$
w_{xy}=g_y/g_x .
$$
Conversely every choice of potentials $g_x$ gives an admissible weight system.

The determinant of this interval scaling is $\prod_{x<y}g_y/g_x$. If there are $r$ faces at a vertex and each face has $s$ vertices, the exponent of any vertex potential is $-2r$. The vertex is contained in $r$ edges and $r$ faces. The exponent of any edge potential is zero, since it contains two vertices and lies in two faces. The exponent of any face potential is $2s$, since the face contains $s$ edges and $s$ vertices. Thus the determinant subgroup of the scalings is $U^{\gcd(2r,2s)}$ by Bézout's identity. In the dodecahedron the exponents $-6,0,10$ give $U^2$, and the value 10 uses pentagonal faces.

For the path algebra, the same corner argument identifies the vertex-to-edge and edge-to-face arrow spaces. Each such corner is one-dimensional. After a face relabeling an aligned automorphism therefore scales these arrows independently. Its action on every longer path is forced by the arrow scaling because paths multiply without identifications. Each arrow belongs to its own path and exactly two paths of length two, so its determinant contribution is a cube of its scale. The relabeling signs lie in $U^3$ as $-1=(-1)^3$. This gives $U^3$ as the exact image. The dodecahedron has 62 diagonal paths, 120 arrows and 120 paths of length two, for a free rank of 302. Identifying the pairs of paths with the same vertex and face gives the rank-242 incidence algebra, and creates exactly the triangle relations used above.

## Odd signs and exact counts

The antipodal map fixes no face, edge or vertex of a cube, octahedron, dodecahedron or icosahedron. On every bank of strictly comparable pairs its permutation is again fixed-point-free. There are $2m$ pairs of each of the three kinds when the solid has $m$ edges. Thus each bank contributes sign $(-1)^m$, which is positive since $m=12$ or 30. The sign on the singleton bank is $(-1)^{(|V|+|E|+|F|)/2}=-1$, since this exponent is 13 or 31. In the dodecahedron the sign comes from the 15 swaps of its edges.

For the tetrahedron, a transposition of two of its four vertex labels has negative sign on the vertex and face banks, which cancel, and positive sign on its six edges. Each comparable-pair bank identifies equivariantly with the ordered pairs of distinct labels. For a vertex-edge pair use the prescribed vertex and the other endpoint. For a vertex-face pair use the vertex and the label absent from the face. For an edge-face pair use the label absent from the face and the other label absent from the edge. Each bank has 12 entries, of which the chosen transposition fixes precisely two, so its sign on each is negative. The product sign on the whole interval basis is negative. This completes the determinant-image calculations. [Coxeter geometry and the Platonic incidence data](https://www.csun.edu/~ctoth/Handbook/chap18.pdf)

For either algebra, left multiplication by a diagonal unit at a maximal poset element scales only its trivial interval or path. Right multiplication at a minimal element does the same. Both unit determinant images are therefore all of $U$. The multiplication-tensor stabilizer consists of $(L_a\sigma,R_b\sigma,L_aR_b\sigma)$ for independent units $a,b$ and an algebra automorphism $\sigma$, by evaluating an isotopy at 1. The determinant ratio $\det X\det Y/\det Z$ recovers $\det\sigma$, and the other two multiplicative coordinates recover the two unit determinants. The determinant-one orbits inside the ordinary orbit are consequently indexed by $U/\det\operatorname{Aut}_R(A)$. This also supplies a complete correction test for any given ordinary transporter. Cyclicity of $\mathbb F_q^\times$ yields the displayed field counts.

## Prior work and checks

Stanley, Baclawski, and Drozd–Kolesnik establish the automorphism and multiplicative-cocycle framework for incidence algebras. Quinn studies incidence algebras as quotients of path algebras by parallel-path relations. The calculations here give the determinant images and orbit counts for the Platonic interval bases over finite local rings, and compares the path and incidence tensors related by the dodecahedral diamonds. The comparison uses the simply connected boundary and the explicit symmetry signs. Bibliographic priority for this determinant-and-orbit application remains open.
