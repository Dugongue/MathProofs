# Ceva's theorem as an exact determinant test

Let $R$ be a fixed finite commutative local ring, and choose a nontrivial subgroup $\Gamma\le R^\times$. A connected simple graph $G=(V,E)$ of at least two vertices specifies a collection of ordinary tensor-isomorphism questions. For each edge $e$, give two tensors $T_e,T'_e$ of the same positive format over $R$. Give each vertex $v$ an integer weight $b_v$, with $\sum_v b_v=0$. Let $G_-$ have the same vertices and only the edges for which $T_e$ and $T'_e$ are nonisomorphic. For each component $C$ of $G_-$ put $b_C=\sum_{v\in C}b_v$.

**Theorem.** One can construct in polynomial time a finite free unital associative algebra $A$ over $R$, with center exactly $R$, such that
$$
\det\operatorname{Aut}_R(A)=\Gamma^g,\qquad
g=\gcd(\exp\Gamma,\{b_C:C\in\pi_0(G_-)\}),
\tag{1}
$$
where $\Gamma^g=\{x^g:x\in\Gamma\}$. Its ordinary multiplication-tensor orbit contains exactly $[R^\times:\Gamma^g]$ determinant-one orbits. For any prescribed $\delta\in\Gamma$ a known change of basis gives two genuine multiplication tables with the same unit and a supplied ordinary transporter. They admit a determinant-one transporter exactly when $\delta\in\Gamma^g$. A successful correction provides ordinary witnesses for every edge on which its labelled projective action is nontrivial.

The proof applies the projective side-coordinate form of Ceva's theorem to determinant characters of tensor stabilizers. The graph is fixed by the construction. Its edge states are determined by the actual tensor isomorphisms. ## The triangle gives an exact majority test

For a coordinate triangle, write the possible axis scales as $x_0,x_1,x_2$. Their three cyclic side ratios are $x_0/x_1,x_1/x_2,x_2/x_0$. Such a triple of units is realizable exactly when its product is 1. Indeed if the ratios are $a,b,c$ with $abc=1$, use $(x_0,x_1,x_2)=(ab,b,1)$. In the projective plane this is the concurrency of the three cevians through the marked side points. The use of unit coordinates makes the argument valid over $R$ even if some side labels coincide modulo the maximal ideal.

Take the weights $(1,1,-2)$ and give each side an independent tensor-isomorphism question. If $\Gamma$ has odd order, any nontrivial $\delta\in\Gamma$ is attainable exactly when at least two of the three questions answer yes. If two sides answer no, their two equalities force all axis scales equal, giving determinant 1. If only one side answers no, the components have weights $1,-1$ or $2,-2$. The determinant image is all of $\Gamma$ since squaring is a bijection on the finite odd-order group. If no side answers no, the singleton weights also give all of $\Gamma$.

For instance over $\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z$, take the principal unit subgroup $\Gamma=\langle4\rangle=\{1,4,7\}$. A single determinant-one correction with a supplied transporter tests the majority of three ordinary tensor questions over this same ring. The corresponding ordinary orbit splits into six determinant-one orbits below the threshold and two above it.

## Encoding edges and calibrating vertex determinants

Set $s=|R^\times|$. For a tensor $T:U_1\times U_2\to U_3$, let $P(T)$ have three scalar diagonal corners and the three free modules in Peirce positions $(1,2),(2,3),(1,3)$. The only product of two strictly upper blocks is $u_1u_2=T(u_1,u_2)$. This is associative by bilinearity and vanishing of threefold strictly upper products. Its unit is the diagonal identity, and its augmentation is projection to the first diagonal entry.

Make an outer scalar source and sink with a space $W=R^V$ between them. Denote its coordinate vectors by $e_v$. For each edge choose an orientation $(v,w)$. At each $a\in\Gamma$ place a middle block $M_s(P(T_e))$ if $a=1$, and $M_s(P(T'_e))$ otherwise. Its incoming row and outgoing column are the standard free modules of rank $s$ over the augmented quotient $M_s(R)$. Pair them by ordinary row-column multiplication times $a e_v+e_w$ in $W$.

For each vertex $v$ add one pure matrix block of width $m_v>s$, with standard incoming row and outgoing column, whose paired product takes values in the line $R e_v$. Choose the $m_v$ pairwise distinct, with $m_v+1\equiv b_v\pmod s$. For example disjoint intervals of length $s$ beyond $s$ suffice. All other products of two outer strictly upper blocks vanish. Diagonal actions use the indicated module structures. Each row-column pairing is balanced over its middle quotient, so the resulting multiplication is associative and has a diagonal unit. The algebra is finite free. Its rank is polynomial for fixed $R$, since there are $|E|\cdot|\Gamma|$ data blocks and each marker width is $O(s|V|)$. The center is precisely $R$. Commuting with all block idempotents forces a central element to be diagonal. In $P(T)$ the nonzero free upper corners force its three central scalars equal, and matrix amplification has the same scalar center. The outer row, column and source-to-sink corners force all these central scalars to agree.

## Recovering all permitted axis actions

The refined diagonal vertices are the source, sink, marker factors, and the three internal matrix vertices of every data block. The radical consists of all strictly upper corners and the maximal-ideal matrices on the diagonal. This ideal is nilpotent by the upper filtration and nilpotence of the maximal ideal. The quotient is a product of simple matrix algebras over the residue field. An automorphism first permutes their central primitive idempotents. Align its transported idempotents $f_i$ with designated targets $e_i$ by conjugating with the inverse of $u=\sum_i f_i e_i$. Indeed $u\equiv1$ modulo the radical and $ue_i=f_i u$. Its diagonal projections equal 1 exactly, since each diagonal entry of a transported idempotent is an idempotent congruent to 0 or 1. Left multiplication by $u$ and right multiplication by its inverse are then triangular with identity diagonals in the orders by starting and ending vertices. Alignment has determinant 1 over $R$.

After alignment, the unique width-one source and sink are fixed by their directed corner. Marker widths fix all markers. The width-$s$ vertices recover entire data blocks as the connected components of the induced undirected Peirce support, since each internal $U_1,U_2$ connects its two adjacent vertices. The input algebras are reflected by matrix amplification. In an isomorphism of full matrix algebras, transported matrix units split the free right target module $B^s$ as $P^s$. Krull–Schmidt gives $P\simeq B$, so an inner change of basis aligns the matrix units and the first corner gives an isomorphism of the input algebras. The directed order of $P(T)$ then identifies its three upper spaces and recovers the tensor isomorphism. The same matrix-unit argument for automorphisms factors them as an inner map and an entrywise automorphism. Their determinants are $s$-th powers because left and right multiplications repeat on $s$ columns and rows, and entrywise automorphisms act on $s^2$ copies. All these determinants equal 1. A pure matrix marker has only inner automorphisms, of determinant 1 by cancellation of the opposite multiplication determinants.

The product lines at the pure markers pin the action on $W$ to $\operatorname{diag}(x_v)$ with $x_v\in R^\times$. A data line has exactly two nonzero coordinates, both units. A diagonal action preserves its support and hence sends it only to another label of the same edge, because the graph is simple. In normalized coordinates the label is multiplied by $x_v/x_w$. Comparing the coordinate with coefficient 1 shows this over $R$ without cancellation of zero divisors. Thus $x_v/x_w\in\Gamma$ along every edge. If the two tensors on an edge are nonisomorphic, its unique exceptional label must be fixed and $x_v=x_w$.

Conversely, every such diagonal action is attainable. On a nontrivially translated edge, use the assumed tensor isomorphism to identify its amplified payloads with one fixed model via augmentation-preserving isomorphisms. Move each payload from $a$ to $(x_v/x_w)a$ through these identifications. The arrow spaces may be sent by their standard bases and the incoming row scaled by $x_w$, which realizes the required product multiplier. For a fixed label use the identity on its payload. At marker $v$, scale the incoming row by $x_v$. Together with the diagonal action on $W$ and fixed endpoints, these are algebra automorphisms.

For any aligned automorphism the determinant on payloads cancels around each permutation cycle, up to the determinant of one automorphism of the amplified payload, which is 1. Block permutation signs contribute 1. If $s$ is even all data and arrow ranks are even. If $s$ is odd then $-1=1$ in $R$. For a row-column pairing of rank $d$ carried to another with multiplier $\lambda$, the two arrow determinants multiply to $\lambda^d$ by the determinant of the scaled perfect pairing. Thus all data-arrow determinants contribute 1, and the marker and $W$ determinants give
$$
\det\phi=\prod_v x_v^{m_v+1}=\prod_v x_v^{b_v}.
$$
Since the original graph is connected, an allowed assignment can be written $x_v=t g_v$ with $g_v\in\Gamma$. The $g_v$ are constant on components of $G_-$, and any independent choice of a value from $\Gamma$ per component is allowed. The common scalar $t$ cancels because the weights sum to zero. Consequently the determinant image is the product of $\Gamma^{b_C}$ over components $C$. Bézout's identity for the exponent of $\Gamma$ and the component weights proves (1).

## Supplying a transporter and recovering witnesses

For a multiplication isotopy $(X,Y,Z)$ the map $\sigma(x)=X(1)^{-1}Z(x)Y(1)^{-1}$ is an algebra isomorphism. Evaluating the isotopy at the unit shows that the displayed inverses exist, and multiplicativity follows by substitution. Its determinant is $\det X\det Y/\det Z$. Thus a determinant-one tensor transporter is equivalent to an algebra isomorphism of determinant 1. Scale one coordinate of $W$ by $\delta^{-1}$ to obtain a known ordinary change of basis $P$, and transport the product by $P$ on both inputs and the output in the bilinear convention. This fixes the unit. Corrections correspond to automorphisms of $A$ of determinant $\delta$, by composing a normalized isomorphism with $P^{-1}$. For the orbit count, scaling just the sink diagonal realizes every left multiplication determinant, and scaling just the source realizes every right multiplication determinant. The stabilizer determinant image in the three factors is therefore the full preimage of $\det\operatorname{Aut}_R(A)$ under $(x,y,z)\mapsto xy/z$. Since the three determinant maps are surjective, quotienting by their common kernel gives the asserted index.

A successful correction can be normalized and aligned by the explicit formulas above. Read the diagonal scales on $W$. On any edge with nonidentity ratio, the exceptional amplified block has moved to a block from the other tensor. Align its local matrix units constructively. In each diagonal matrix corner take a column $u$ of the transported $F_{11}$ which is nonzero modulo the maximal ideal and use $F_{i1}u$ as the ordered basis. The vectors are a basis modulo the maximal ideal, hence over $R$. After alignment the $(1,1)$ upper matrix slots recover the three maps on $U_1,U_2,U_3$, which preserve the tensor products in those slots. Conversely supplied ordinary witnesses on the translated edges implement the automorphism and hence the correction.

With weights $+1,-1$ at two terminals and zero elsewhere, (1) gives all of $\Gamma$ exactly when the terminal vertices are disconnected in $G_-$. A correction for $\delta\ne1$ gives a cut of ordinary yes edges by taking the vertices whose scale equals the first terminal's scale. Every crossing edge has a nonidentity ratio, so the procedure recovers its witnesses. In the other direction a cut of ordinary yes edges and their witnesses give a correction by assigning $\delta$ on the first terminal's side and 1 on the other.

Consequently any monotone AND/OR formula in tensor-isomorphism questions reduces to one supplied correction. A leaf is one edge. Series composition of two-terminal graphs gives OR of disconnection, and parallel composition gives AND. If parallel edges occur, subdivide each by adding a fixed no-edge, represented by the scalar tensors $0$ and $uv$, without changing the connectivity of the no-edge graph. The graph and the output multiplication tables have polynomial size in the explicit formula and tensor data.

The previous supplied-correction construction places all varying payloads on a single projective line. This result uses simultaneous side information, the compatibility of the normalized vertex coordinates, and the calibrated determinant character to combine several inputs.
