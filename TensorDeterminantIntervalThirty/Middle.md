# Ten presentations between one-sign and two-sign TI

Work over $\mathbb F_3$. Write $O$ for ordinary three-tensor isomorphism, $A$ for isomorphism with determinant one in mode 1, and $B$ for isomorphism with determinant one in both modes 1 and 2 on a single transporter. A language below denotes its polynomial-time many-one downward closure. All inputs are explicit dense arrays with positive-dimensional modes. Check formats and any supplied certificates as part of the language and reject malformed inputs. The comparisons concern inclusive intervals, so different definitions can have equal degrees.

For a tensor $X$, let $F_i(X)$ ask if $X$ has an ordinary automorphism with determinant $-1$ in mode $i$. The standard reflection $R_i$ acts with determinant $-1$ in just that mode. Thus $F_i(X)$ is the one-sign test on $(X,R_iX)$ in mode $i$. Write $J=A\wedge A$ for two independent one-sign comparisons, the benchmark with $A\le_m^p J\le_m^p B$. This benchmark was already identified in prior work, and is not an extra entry in the table.

| ID | Complete decision problem | Proved location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M1 | Two independent oriented comparisons together with a third independent ordinary comparison | Equivalent to $J$ |
| M2 | Two orientations on the same tensor pair with separate witnesses. Some transporter has first determinant one, and possibly another has second determinant one | Equivalent to $J$ |
| M3 | Shared-reference oriented triple. Given $X,Y,Z$, ask for determinant-one maps from $X$ to $Y$ and from $X$ to $Z$ in mode 1 | Between $A$ and $J$ |
| M4 | Oriented comparison with independent odd automorphism. Given $X,Y,U$, ask for $A(X,Y)\wedge F_1(U)$ | Between $A$ and $J$ |
| M5 | Oriented comparison with odd stabilizer on its other source mode. Given $X,Y$, ask for $A(X,Y)\wedge F_2(X)$ | Equivalent to M4 |
| M6 | Bridged oriented triple. M3 with a supplied ordinary transporter from $Y$ to $Z$ | Equivalent to $A$ |
| M7 | Two-sign comparison with a supplied source automorphism having a nonzero projected sign vector in the first two modes | Equivalent to $A$ |
| M8 | Orientation with ordinary-controlled correction. On independent pairs $(X,Y)$ and $(U,V)$, ask for $O(X,Y)\land(A(X,Y)\lor O(U,V))$ | Equivalent to $A$ |
| M9 | Two-sign comparison of concise even cubes with scalar centroid over the algebraic closure | Equivalent to $B$ |
| M10 | Threefold homogeneous sum comparison. Input two concise scalar-centroid tensors $X,Y$ of equal ordered format, all sides even. Test $B(X^{\oplus3},Y^{\oplus3})$ | Equivalent to $B$ |

## Comparison tools reused

The prior MathProofs exact sign projection sends any ordinary transporter sign profile $D$ to $L D$ for any fixed binary $3\times3$ matrix $L$, including rank-one projections. It preserves emptiness, uses balanced concise even tensors, and uses no isomorphism oracle. In particular choose $L$ sending the first input sign to the chosen single output axis and making the other axes zero. If the output lacks a scalar centroid, append the existing determinant-preserving Jordan-pencil glue. It adds even dimensions and preserves the exact sign triple. Briefly, the added labels have a fixed high-rank pencil, so low-rank labels recover the old label space. Their image and common kernel, and the marker evaluation block, force every transporter to act on the three original factors as an old transporter. The added factors have determinant one. The centroid acts by a common scalar on the evaluation block and labels, then conciseness and the distinct shear supports force the same scalar throughout, also over the algebraic closure. The glue can enlarge the mode dimensions beyond any given polynomial bound by varying its Jordan lengths. Hence the resulting pair is absolutely indecomposable, all its dimensions are even, and its first-role sign is exact. On a self-comparison use the same choices on both endpoints. These are two existing axis and centroid constructions used here as dependencies.

The second tool is neutral conjunction. A one-sign instance and any polynomial list of ordinary TI comparisons can be packed into one one-sign instance. Attach each ordinary comparison in a distinct neutral block, leaving the sign of the original instance unchanged. The neutral block is the multiplication tensor of a two-by-two matrix algebra over the local algebra that encodes the ordinary tensor. Left and right units have determinant one by the two equal column or row copies, and algebra isomorphisms are neutral by matrix-unit conjugation and the four entry copies. Distinct intrinsic indecomposable formats protect all blocks. This construction is useful even when the original one-sign comparison and the ordinary condition share objects.

## M1 and M2

For M1 absorb the ordinary condition into the first one-sign query by neutral conjunction, leaving two independent one-sign queries. Conversely append a fixed ordinary yes comparison to an input of $J$.

M2 reduces to $J$ by assigning different target queries to the two physical modes. For the reverse, apply sign isolation to the two component comparisons, putting their respective signs on axes 1 and 2 and using different formats. Direct-sum the two encoded pairs. Either original ordinary failure makes the new ordinary comparison fail. Otherwise the first and second sign components vary independently. Testing positivity separately returns exactly the original two orientation answers. This also exhibits the difference between M2 and $B$ on raw inputs. The affine profile with two tested sign vectors $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ passes M2 and fails $B$, where 1 denotes determinant $-1$. It occurs on actual tensors: apply the previous exact sign projection to a full-sign profile with image the diagonal line in the first two coordinates, and reflect one target mode. This difference in raw answers establishes no degree separation. A reduction $B\le_m^p J$ is still unresolved.

## M3 through M5

For M3 replace a single input $(X,Y)$ by $(X,Y,Y)$ to get the lower reduction. The two comparisons themselves give the upper reduction to $J$.

For M4 take a fixed yes tensor for $F_1$, for example a zero tensor on three one-dimensional spaces, to get the lower reduction. The reflection formulation of $F_1$ gives the upper reduction to $J$.

For M5 a query to M4 uses the original $A(X,Y)$ pair and the mode-permuted $X$ for its odd automorphism query. For the reverse from M4, put the relative comparison for $X,Y$ in one isolated-axis pair $P,P'$ on mode 1. Encode the self-comparison $U,U$ into one object $Q$ with its first original sign stored on mode 2. In the always-flexible exceptional branch of the odd automorphism query, $Q$ can simply be a fixed sign-slack object on mode 2. The two types can be tagged with distinct formats. Set the new pair to $(P\oplus Q,P'\oplus Q)$. Every actual transporter is a pair of independent component maps. The first sign comes from the relative comparison and the second stabilizer sign of the new source comes from $Q$. Both implications follow, including when no original ordinary transporter exists. This proves M5 equivalent to M4.

## M6 through M8

For M6 verify the supplied bridge. If its first determinant is $+1$, composition with the bridge gives a bijection of the two relative fibers with equal first sign, so one $A(X,Y)$ query suffices. If its first determinant is $-1$, they differ by a sign shift. Both comparisons can be positive exactly when $O(X,Y)$ holds and the nonempty relative sign coset contains both signs. This last condition is equivalent to $F_1(X)$ by composition. Neutral conjunction packs the ordinary test with this one signed self-query. Conversely the choice $Z=Y$ with the identity bridge gives the lower reduction.

For M7 verify the supplied automorphism. Its projected sign vector $h\ne0$ spans a line acting by translation on the relative projected sign set $D$. Let $\ell$ be the unique nonzero functional killing $h$. Then $0\in D$ exactly when $D$ meets $\ker\ell$: a point of $D$ in that kernel lies either at zero or at $h$, and translation by $h$ corrects the latter. Empty ordinary profiles fail both. This is a single sign-character test, compiled by the prior exact sign projection. To reduce $A$ to M7, append one unused coordinate in mode 2 and supply its reflection. An enlarged transporter restricts on the intrinsic mode-2 support and extends in the original ambient dimension while leaving the maps in modes 1 and 3 unchanged. Thus the first sign is preserved, and the new second sign is always freely correctable.

For M8 the earlier optional-swap construction applies after translating mode 1 into total determinant by calibrated castling. Convert the independent ordinary comparison to two equal-format absolutely indecomposable odd-sided blocks. Their individual automorphisms have total sign one and their exchange is possible exactly when the ordinary comparison holds. Use inverse isomorphisms on the two exchanged blocks, so the local maps cancel and the odd block dimensions contribute total sign $-1$. Put both helper blocks in both endpoints, with their common format distinct from the oriented data format so exchange never changes the ordinary data requirement. The helper gives a sign correction precisely when the ordinary condition holds. Revert total sign to a physical single mode using calibrated castling. A fixed ordinary no comparison gives $A$ as a special case. This uses the established optional-swap theorem, and does not give disjunction of two arbitrary one-sign queries.

## M9 and M10

For M9, recognition is a polynomial linear-algebra test: flattenings test conciseness, side dimensions test even cube format, and the common centroid equations are linear. Centroid dimension one over $\mathbb F_3$ is equivalent to dimension one after any field extension. Thus M9 is at most $B$. The previously established mixed alternating/symmetric arm encoding gives a concise even scalar-centroid cube with sign readout $(x+z,y+z,z)$; odd and deficient formats are handled by the earlier exact normalization. Since this binary change of characters is invertible, precompose the inverse exact sign projection to preserve all input signs on the two selected output axes. Thus $B$ reduces to M9. This differs from the earlier full SL problem on recognizably scalar-centroid tensors by testing precisely two physical determinants.

For M10, construct three explicit copies per side. Scalar centroid makes each original tensor absolutely indecomposable. Thus ordinary transporters of the sums permute the three intrinsic summands and use independent local maps in the same original comparison. Because every side of each summand is even, block transpositions contribute determinant one in each mode. If there is any ordinary transporter with full sign profile $D=a+H$, the signs of the sum are exactly $D+D+D=a+H$ in the binary group. The equality follows since $H+H+H=H$ and $3a=a$. If $D$ is empty, matching summands is impossible. This proves M10 reduces to $B$ and preserves the original two-sign decision on these inputs. Applying M9's lower reduction before replication proves the converse. The same argument applies to any fixed odd number of equal even summands. Even replication instead erases the affine offset, explaining why a bare two-copy version cannot be substituted.

The equivalences with already defined degrees and the other stated reductions do not give strict gaps. In particular M3 and M4 do not currently have a verified relationship to each other beyond the common interval bounds. These are informal deductions. For the ambient tensor-system compilation see [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4).
