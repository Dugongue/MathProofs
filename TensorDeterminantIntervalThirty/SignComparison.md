# Equivalence of the three two-equation determinant classes over F₃

In this note signs are additive in $\mathbb F_2$, so a sign zero means determinant one. A transporter is a linear equivalence between two ordinary 3-tensors. The three two-equation languages from the interval table ask whether the transporter sign triple belongs to a specified one-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb F_2^3$, with generator of support size one, two, or three. These are candidates 4, 5 and 6, up to permutation of the modes.

**Theorem.** Candidates 4, 5 and 6 are all polynomial-time many-one equivalent. There is an explicit construction for the hard parity cases which carries the two source equations to two independently readable output signs and pins the other output sign to zero. In particular, the three signs need not be tested using independent candidate transporters.

## 1. Compression to the hard parity cases

If support dimensions disagree, reject. Otherwise compress the three supports, using adapted bases completed to determinant one in each original mode, and keep track of the slack from each nonzero ambient complement. A determinant-one completion is obtained by rescaling one basis vector if needed. The old sign set is the compressed sign set plus the coordinate lines for the nonzero complements. Indeed, the intrinsic supports are preserved by every ambient transporter, arbitrary maps on the complementary quotients extend any support transporter, and possible shears contribute determinant one. Thus any slack line outside the requested sign line increases the dimension of the allowed subgroup to at least two.

For a concise tensor, multiplication in the three modes by signs $a,b,c$ with $abc=1$ fixes the tensor. Its determinant signs restrict to the parity of the physical dimensions. In particular each coordinate line for an odd mode is available if at least one mode is even, because the compensating scalar can be put there. If all modes are odd, the scalar sign subgroup is the whole plane $d_1+d_2+d_3=0$. Again if the scalar slack is outside the desired line, add it to that line. Any resulting plane query is one of the three one-character problems. The preceding comparison of those problems gives reductions between their three types. A plane query of an appropriate type reduces to any desired line query by the subgroup-padding theorem, first permuting modes so the target line lies in the intermediate plane. A whole-group query is ordinary TI, which reduces to every line problem. A zero tensor pair is an immediate yes when both are zero.

It remains to handle concise inputs with either all dimensions even, or only one odd dimension whose coordinate axis is the permitted line. In the latter case it is the unconstrained mode of candidate 4. Thus for candidate 4 denote the two constrained spaces by $V,W$ and the unconstrained one by $U$, so $\dim V,\dim W$ are even and $\dim U$ may be odd. For candidate 5 denote the individually constrained mode by $U$. For candidate 6 use any ordering.

## 2. A local algebra retaining the tensor and selected signs

Use a bilinear-map convention for the data $T:U\times V\to W$. The sign of an inverse dual is unchanged over $\mathbb F_3$, so this convention does not alter the question. Let $m$ be either four or six. Use the associative unital local algebra
$$
 A=\mathbb F_3\,1\ \oplus U\oplus V\oplus W\ \oplus
       \langle t,t^2,\dots,t^{m-1}\rangle \ \oplus U'.
$$
There are two variants. The summand $U'$ is omitted in the unduplicated variant, and is a copy of $U$ in the duplicated variant. On the radical the nonzero products are the old product $uv=T(u,v)$, truncated multiplication of powers of $t$ with $t^m=0$, and $tu=u'$ in the duplicated variant. All other radical products are zero. This is associative: all two-step products other than those on the $t$-string are annihilated on either side by the radical, and no product of two positive powers of $t$ acts nontrivially on an old tangent space. Conciseness gives $J^2=W\oplus U'\oplus\langle t^2,\dots,t^{m-1}\rangle$ for the radical $J$, while $J^i=\langle t^i,\dots,t^{m-1}\rangle$ for $i\ge3$.

On $J/J^2$ the equation $x^{m-1}=0$ singles out the old tangent space $U\oplus V$. The power is independent of the lift because $J^m=0$, and only the top $t$-coefficient can contribute. The product of two old tangents into $J^2/J^3$ has image $W$. The left and right radicals of that product are respectively $V$ and $U$, because the original tensor is concise in both input modes. Thus every algebra isomorphism $\phi$ induces actual tensor equivalences $g_U,g_V,g_W$ on these spaces. Conversely these equivalences lift with $t$ fixed and with $g_U$ on $U'$ when present.

Write $\alpha\in\mathbb F_3^\times$ for the scalar on the tangent quotient line represented by $t$. On the $t^j$ grading it induces $\alpha^j$. In degree two $W$ is already preserved. In the duplicated variant the image of $tU$ modulo $W$ is also intrinsic, because changing the tangent lift of $t$ by an old tangent adds only a product in $W$. On this quotient the action is $\alpha g_U$. The remaining one-dimensional quotient in degree two is acted on by $\alpha^2$. These facts compute the determinant via the radical filtration. Write $d_U,d_V,d_W$ for the data signs. The resulting signs of $\phi$ are:
$$
 \det\nolimits_{\mathrm{sgn}}\phi =
 \begin{cases}
 d_U+d_V+d_W & \text{unduplicated, }m=4,\\
 d_V+d_W & \text{duplicated, }m=4,\ \dim U\text{ even},\\
 d_V+d_W & \text{duplicated, }m=6,\ \dim U\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
$$
For the first two cases $\sum_{j=1}^3j=6$ is even. In the last case $\sum_{j=1}^5j=15$ is odd and cancels the odd exponent from $\alpha^{\dim U}$ on $U'$. The choices made below ensure $\dim A$ is even.

Choose a quotient $C=A/I$ with one or both of the old input directions retained, leaving only the $t,t^2,t^3$ portion of the marker. In other words $I$ consists of the omitted old tangent spaces, $W$, $U'$ when present, and $\langle t^4,\dots,t^{m-1}\rangle$. It is an ideal by the product rules. Any block-diagonal lift of an original transporter preserves the designated ideal, though general algebra isomorphisms can have additional off-diagonal corrections. The tensor below forces the ideal to be preserved. All the chosen retained tangent spaces have even total dimension, making $\dim C$ even. Whenever $\phi(I)=I_{\mathrm{target}}$, its induced quotient sign is the sum of the signs of the retained input spaces. Indeed the marker contributes $\alpha^{1+2+3}=1$, and the old tangent filtrations are carried to their counterparts.

## 3. A rectangular pencil that reads the quotient sign

Use the algebra $A$ as the common label space for a block pencil. Write $L_a$ for left multiplication by the label $a$ on the regular left module $A$, and use two identical copies of this square block. In addition use one rectangular block with two columns and $\dim C$ rows,
$$
                   K(a)=[\,\bar a,\ \epsilon(a)e\,],
              \qquad e=\overline{t^2}\in C,
$$
where $\epsilon$ is the residue map $A\to\mathbb F_3$. The basis of each mode is given by the ordered construction. All dimensions and coefficient counts are polynomial in the original dense format.

For fixed labels the regular block is indecomposable. Its endomorphism algebra is $A^{\mathrm{op}}$, because the unit label identifies its input and output maps and the remaining equations are regular module equations. This local algebra has scalar residue. The rectangular block is indecomposable as well. To see this, write the column action as $G=\begin{pmatrix}p&q\\r&v\end{pmatrix}$, and the row action as $H$, and impose $HK(a)=K(a)G$. On residue-zero labels the second column equation forces $q=0$, and the first forces $H=pI$ on the quotient radical. At $a=1$ the two columns give $H(1)=p+r e$ and $v=p$ since $H(e)=p e$. Thus the endomorphism ring consists of a common scalar plus a linked square-zero shear and is local with scalar residue.

An ordinary transporter with label map $s$ is an isomorphism of these fixed-label representations after substituting $s$ into the target arrows. Krull–Schmidt applies, since both are finite-dimensional representations of a fixed two-vertex quiver with one arrow for each label basis vector. The rectangular and regular summands have different dimension vectors. Therefore for any given feasible $s$, some transporter is block diagonal on these summands, and can use the same maps on both regular copies. Any other matrix transporter with the same $s$ differs by a fixed-label automorphism. Such automorphisms have determinant one in both matrix modes: the semisimple quotient of their endomorphism ring is $M_2(\mathbb F_3)\times\mathbb F_3$ by Krull–Schmidt, the block-scalar contributions on each mode are determinants raised to the corresponding even block dimensions, and units in one plus the radical are unipotent. This standard endomorphism-ring statement follows by reducing diagonal morphisms modulo the local radicals and noting that any composite through an inequivalent indecomposable has zero residue. The kernel is the radical by the Fitting decomposition, since a nontrivial Fitting summand would give a nonzero idempotent with diagonal residue zero. Thus no kernel determinant ambiguity remains, even though the original transporter can mix the summands.

On the regular block the transporter equation is $H_L L_a=L'_{s(a)}G_L$. At $a=1$ this gives $H_L=L'_u G_L$ with the unit $u=s(1)$. Consequently $s(a)=u\phi(a)$, where $\phi$ is an algebra isomorphism, by conjugation on left multiplication. The label sign is $\det_{\mathrm{sgn}}\phi$, since left multiplication by $u$ has determinant $\epsilon(u)^{\dim A}=1$. The two identical regular copies together contribute sign zero in both matrix modes. The kernel of the label map into the rectangular block is $I$. Its preservation implies $s(I)=I_{\mathrm{target}}$, hence $\phi(I)=I_{\mathrm{target}}$. Write $s_C=L_{\bar u}\bar\phi$ for the induced map on $C$.

On the rectangular block the equation becomes $H K(a)=K'(s(a))G$. Its second column at a residue-zero label again forces $q=0$. Its first column gives $H=p s_C+r\,\epsilon(u)e_{\mathrm{target}}\epsilon$. At $a=1$ the second column gives $H(e)=v\,\epsilon(u)e_{\mathrm{target}}$. Thus $s_C(e)=(v\epsilon(u)/p)e_{\mathrm{target}}$. The leading coefficient of $s_C(t^2)$ on the $t^2$ direction in degree two equals $\epsilon(u)\alpha^2=\epsilon(u)$, so $v=p$. The column determinant is $p^2=1$. The row map has determinant $\det\bar\phi$: its scalar multiple $p s_C$ has this determinant since $\dim C$ is even, and its additional rank-one term has zero trace after factoring out $p s_C$ because $\epsilon(s_C^{-1}(e_{\mathrm{target}}))=0$. The rank-one determinant formula then leaves the determinant unchanged.

Every original transporter lifts with $t$ fixed, with $I$ preserved, and with $u=1$. It then lifts to the two regular blocks and the rectangular block, using identity on the two columns of the latter. Together with the reflection proved above this gives exactly the sign-set transformation
$$
            (d_U,d_V,d_W)\quad\longmapsto\quad
       \bigl(\det_{\mathrm{sgn}}\phi,\ 0,\ \det_{\mathrm{sgn}}\bar\phi\bigr)
$$
in a fixed ordering of physical modes. This is a statement about actual common transporters, including no instances. It is independent of choices of matrix-mode block maps and of all scalar unit changes in the label.

## 4. Apply the same wrapper to all three source line types

Choose the spaces and quotient as follows.

| Source | Algebra variant | Retained old part of $C$ | Read signs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Candidate 4, with free $U$ | Duplicate $U$, using $m=4$ or $m=6$ according to its parity | $V$ | $d_V+d_W,\ d_V$ |
| Candidate 5, with anchored $U$ | Unduplicated, $m=4$ | $U$ | $d_U+d_V+d_W,\ d_U$ |
| Candidate 6 | Duplicate $U$, $m=4$ | $U\oplus V$ | $d_V+d_W,\ d_U+d_V$ |

In each row the input is yes precisely when an actual common transporter has both read signs zero. The source cases from Section 1 ensure that the algebra and the quotient have the even dimensions used by the proof. The full output sign set lies in a fixed coordinate plane. Choose the output ordering so that the target sign line is disjoint from that plane except at zero. Such an ordering exists for every nonzero line type: use a nonzero coordinate of its generator as the pinned-zero matrix coordinate of the wrapper. Asking any desired line question on the output thus tests both read signs at once. This proves the many-one equivalence of candidates 4, 5 and 6.

The proof uses the sign-subgroup padding, the one-character comparison, and the classical Krull–Schmidt decomposition. No distinction between those two-equation problems and an endpoint follows from this equivalence. In particular, allowing independent witnesses on a basis of two sign equations is in general insufficient. For a projected affine line $a+G\subseteq\mathbb F_2^2$ disjoint from zero, each coordinate-zero test can pass when the omitted third linear character fails. The construction above avoids this issue by reading both signs of the same transporter.

## 5. Four structural classes also equal unrestricted SL-TI

The pencil construction can preserve all three signs and simultaneously force scalar centroid. It proves that candidates 7 (balanced concise), 9 (scalar centroid), 13 (two unequal-format summands), and 14 (two equal-format summands) are many-one equivalent to unrestricted SL-TI. Candidate 8 (absolute indecomposability) is between the scalar-centroid and unrestricted classes, so is equivalent as well.

First suppose the original source is concise and all its mode dimensions are even. Take the unduplicated local algebra in Section 2, initially with $t^4=0$. Use quotient $C_U$ retaining $U$, and quotient $C_V$ retaining $V$, each with the marker retained and all products of the old tangent killed. Use the two regular blocks, one $K_{C_U}$ block, one transposed $K_{C_V}$ block, and an extra $K_A$ block. These have distinct ordered matrix dimension vectors except for the intentional pair of regular copies. In particular the two proper quotients have dimensions smaller than $A$, and both have dimension greater than two. The transposed block interchanges the row and column determinant readings. With $r=d_U+d_V+d_W$ the exact three output signs, in an appropriate mode ordering, are
$$
                        (r,\ d_V,\ d_U+r).
$$
They vanish simultaneously exactly when all original signs vanish. The proof in Section 3 applies to each rectangular block, with the label $s=u\phi$ shared. Every original transporter lifts preserving the two ideals and the marker, so the output sign formula is attained for each original witness. The new tensor is concise: each regular block contains the unit slice, each $K_C(1)$ has independent columns $1,t^2$, and the first columns of $K_C(a)$ span $C$. Transposition preserves these two conciseness properties, and the faithful regular block witnesses conciseness in the labels.

The geometric centroid is scalar. In a centroid triple write $f$ for its label action in the pencil convention. Comparing with the output-matrix action on a regular diagonal block gives $L_{f(a)}=H_{LL}L_a$. Hence $f(a)=ca$ for $c=f(1)$. Now compare with the input-matrix action on the $K_A$ diagonal block. Its first column gives
$$
                        ca=p a+\lambda\epsilon(a)t^2 .
$$
At $a=1$ this gives $c=p+\lambda t^2$. Setting $a=t$ then gives $\lambda t^3=0$, so $\lambda=0$. Conciseness forces the matrix mode actions to be the same scalar. These arguments are linear identities valid over the algebraic closure. They do not use the determinant group of that larger field.

The all-even and concise source premise is without loss for this reduction. Compress supports and add the ambient determinant slack and the concise scalar slack as in Section 1, now starting from the zero subgroup. If the resulting allowed subgroup is a line, use the corresponding construction from Section 4 with its pinned zero and output plane disjoint from the line. Asking full SL on this output is equivalent to the original line question, and the output is concise and even-sided by construction. If the resulting subgroup is a plane, use the one-character equivalence to the total-sign question. After support compression, if a remaining scalar slack can change the total sign, this is ordinary TI. Otherwise all three dimensions are even or all three are odd. For all-even total-sign inputs use just the unduplicated $t^4$-algebra and two regular blocks, whose output signs are $(r,0,0)$. For all-odd total-sign inputs add two cross-zero marker strings with lengths four and eight to the unduplicated data algebra, keeping the products within each truncated string. The extra radical dimension is $3+7$, making the algebra even-dimensional. The old tangent is the kernel of the cube map to $J^3/J^4$, and its product in $J^2/J^3$ still recovers the data. Modulo the old tangent the shorter marker line is the degree-four power kernel. The filtration on these marker tangent lines is consequently triangular, and on its power layers the diagonal entries are the corresponding powers of the marker scalars. Their total determinant contributions are one because $1+2+3=6$ and $1+\cdots+7=28$ are even. Thus the label determinant is still the total data sign. Use two regular blocks to put zeros in the other modes. This too is concise and even-sided.

If the remaining subgroup is the whole group, first compress the ordinary TI pair and add indecomposable slack tensors whose determinant images together generate $\mathbb F_2^3$. One can use two distinct two-coordinate sign lines and the common sign line from the determinant-subgroup theorem. They are all even-sided. To correct any odd side lengths add large rectangular evaluation tensors of shape $(ab,a,b)$, with $a$ odd and $b$ even, permuted so the odd side is the desired one. These evaluation tensors have scalar centroid, because equality of the two independent actions on their full tensor product forces both to be scalar. Each attached tensor has format distinct from the others and larger than the data in at least one side. Their full combined determinant slack remains $\mathbb F_2^3$, so asking for exact SL of this even-sided concise output is equivalent to the old ordinary question. Trivial cases can map to fixed yes and no instances. This proves the required normalization of arbitrary SL inputs to concise even-sided ones.

For candidate 9, apply the full three-sign construction to these even-sided inputs. For candidate 7, balance an even-sided concise SL instance by appending determinant-trivial Jordan pencils with short dimension two, one for each choice of short mode. Here is a construction for these balancing tensors. Use the three linear forms $x,y,x+y$ on the labels and three pairwise different positive even sizes $m_j$, of sum $N$. In the corresponding diagonal pencil blocks put
$$
                    \ell_j(x,y) I_{m_j}+(x-y)J_{m_j},
$$
where $J_{m_j}$ is a single nilpotent Jordan block. Label automorphisms fix all three projective determinant hyperplanes, hence are scalar and have determinant one. At the invertible anchor $(1,1)$ their remaining common normalized centralizer has block polynomials in $J_{m_j}$ separately for each block, with no mixing due to distinct normalized eigencharacters. Each unit there has determinant a scalar to an even power, hence one. Scalar label compensation also has determinant one on the even matrix modes. The centroid idempotent argument using the scalar label action makes the tensor indecomposable. Its shape $(2,N,N)$ is available for all sufficiently large even $N$. If the current side lengths are $n_i$, choose a large even constant $C$ greater than every side length and long sizes $N_i=C+n_i$. The resulting side in mode $i$ is $n_i+2+\sum_{j\ne i}N_j=2+2C+\sum_j n_j$, independent of $i$. All balancing blocks are intrinsic by their distinct large formats and have trivial determinant image. For candidate 13, start with the scalar-centroid output and add one determinant-trivial Jordan pencil of different format. Its two summands are distinguished geometrically by format, so its SL comparison is exactly that of the original scalar-centroid summand.

For candidate 14 we need a determinant-trivial dummy of the same format as the full-sign wrapper. The following refinement makes the dimensions of $A,C_U,C_V$ all divisible by four. In the full-sign construction keep the $t$-string of length four, and include two more cross-zero truncated strings of lengths eight and twelve. Include another pair of lengths sixteen and twenty precisely when needed to make the algebra dimension divisible by four. The additions have radical dimensions $7+11=18$ and $15+19=34$, both congruent to two modulo four. The two quotients retain the length-four string and, precisely when needed to make the dimension a multiple of four, both length-eight and length-twelve strings. They omit the other chains. Before this change each relevant dimension was even. The determinant arguments remain valid. Modulo the old tangent, each chain is one layer of the intrinsic marker tangent flag determined by the kernels of the successive power maps to $J^j/J^{j+1}$. On every power layer the induced map is triangular in the order of the lengths, with entries $\alpha_i^j$ on the diagonal for the contributing chains. The old tangent comes from the cube kernel as before. Each retained entire chain of length $L\equiv0\bmod4$ contributes $\alpha_i^{L(L-1)/2}=1$ to the determinant. The same is true on the quotients whenever their ideals are preserved. The distinguished $t^2$ has leading square coefficient one from the shortest marker and $t^3\ne0$, preserving the rectangular readout and scalar centroid.

Now let $b=\dim A$, $c_U=\dim C_U$, $c_V=\dim C_V$ for this refined hard wrapper $M(T)$. Make a dummy wrapper $D$ with the plain truncated polynomial algebra $\mathbb F_3[x]/(x^b)$, and its two quotients by $x^{c_U}$ and $x^{c_V}$. Use $x^2$ as each rectangular mark. Use the same two regular blocks and the same three rectangular placements. Its format is exactly that of $M(T)$. The determinant of any algebra automorphism on the whole truncated algebra and each preserved quotient equals $\alpha^{d(d-1)/2}$ on a dimension-$d$ chain, hence one for all three chosen dimensions. The calculations in Section 3 give determinant one for every tensor automorphism in all three modes. This dummy also has scalar centroid, using $x^3\ne0$.

Take $M(T)\oplus D$ and $M(S)\oplus D$. There are two absolutely indecomposable summands of the same format. A nonexchanging transporter contributes the signs of $M(T)\to M(S)$, since the dummy automorphism contributes zeros. If a transporter exchanges blocks, its sign triple is the sum of those of $M(T)\to D$ and $D\to M(S)$, with no block-swap contribution because all mode dimensions are even. Composing the two cross maps gives a genuine $M(T)\to M(S)$ transporter with precisely that sign triple. This proves both directions of the candidate 14 reduction without identifying the summands by type. The five structural equivalences now follow by their already proved upper reductions to SL-TI.
