# Thirty additional candidate degrees in the determinant TI intervals

All problems use explicit dense three-tensors over F3. A class here is the deterministic polynomial-time many-one downward closure of its indicated decision problem. Let O, A, B and S denote ordinary, one-sign, two-sign and special-linear tensor isomorphism, respectively. This catalogue gives thirty additional decision definitions and proofs of both many-one inclusions for each, ten per closed interval. It makes no claim that the thirty degrees are distinct or that any lie strictly between their endpoints.

| Interval | Ten definitions and proofs | Main mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| O through A | [Lower interval](Low.md) | Activate one determinant condition when the raw tensor is recognized as multiplication of an algebra in a specified family. |
| A through B | [Middle interval](Middle.md) | Combine separate oriented comparisons, signed stabilizer checks and recognizable families with two physical signs. |
| B through S | [Upper interval](Upper.md) | Enforce determinant balance across independent plane or line profiles, or use a source stabilizer to correct a relative sign. |

The upper interval includes eight configurations of two-dimensional sign planes. Their normals are nonzero vectors of the dual of the three-dimensional binary sign space. The number of configurations up to change of coordinates, listed for two through seven planes, is 1, 2, 2, 1, 1, 1. This geometric distinction gives a meaningful family of definitions and some explicit reductions by adding planes. It does not establish computational inequivalence.

For independent conjunctions there is a sharper model theorem. If a probe can enforce at most k affine sign equations, its coverage across independent inputs is governed by a total dimension budget k in their separate dual spaces. The result survives adaptive probes, finite disjunctions and independent copies of ordinary transporters and stabilizers. In particular two independent one-sign comparisons use exactly two one-sign probes in that model, while a two-sign comparison requiring the same transporter uses three. The proof gives an exact covering characterization and a computed table. [Conjunctions and exact probe theorem](Conjunctions.md)

The strongest cross-candidate implication is that if the top gap is strict, nine upper candidates are strictly above B. Each is at least as hard as an independent B-and-A comparison, and that conjunction reduces to B exactly when S reduces to B. They could still all coincide with S. No known strictness of unrestricted Karp degrees is asserted.

These proofs build on the prior [exact sign projection](SignProjections.md), [axis encoding](CastlingAndConjunction.md), [scalar-centroid embedding](ScalarCentroidGlue.md) and [neutral conjunction](NeutralConjunction.md). The ordinary tensor-system compilation and the motivating endpoint question are from [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2). The earlier MathProofs proofs are included for reference. All results here are informal. Formal verification and publication priority are separate questions.
