# Lower bounds for removing determinant constraints in tensor isomorphism

Over F3, consider the chain of polynomial-time many-one degrees for ordinary TI, one-sign TI, two-sign TI and SL-TI. This pass proves quantitative lower bounds for precisely defined reduction models at all three gaps. The three unrestricted reverse many-one reductions remain unresolved. All proofs are informal.

| Reduction model | What the lower bound proves |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed tensor markers of order at most d, with coherent lifts of the original special linear group | Dropping even one of k protected determinants requires a space of dimension at least the minimum of binom(n_i, ceil(n_i/d))/(q−1). The theorem holds over each fixed F_q with q>2, and the single-mode exterior construction matches it within q−1. [Proof](GapOneSeparation.md) |
| Fixed markers with a surjective witness projection and no specified coherent lift | The lower bound still applies to odd lifting kernels over F3, including unipotent kernels. Central and small kernels inherit the bound. The exponential bound also covers solvable kernels of order coprime to the field characteristic, and arbitrary nilpotent kernels over F3. [Odd kernels](GapTwoSeparation.md) · [Central and small kernels](Kernels.md) |
| Objectwise equivariant encoders with polynomial coordinate functions | For equal source side length n along an explicit family and output tensor order at most d, a degree-τ encoder that drops a determinant needs a space of dimension at least binom(n, ceil((n−τ)/d))/(q−1) when τ<n. Thus polynomial-size encoders in this model require degree at least n−O(1). The proof uses functions on finite arrays, without assuming formal polynomial identities for their coordinates. [Proof](DegreeDimension.md) |
| Algorithms using only affine equations on hidden relative and stabilizer determinant signs | Ordinary probes cannot resolve one sign. Two signs require exactly three one-sign probes, and three signs require exactly three two-sign probes or seven one-sign probes. Adaptivity, affine slack, independent witness copies, and finite disjunctions within a query do not improve those bounds. [Deterministic proof](GapThreeSeparation.md) · [Randomized bounds](GapTwoSeparation.md) |

## Why the hard determinant profiles are realizable

For a nonzero binary functional on the three signs, permute modes so its first coefficient is one. Use the evaluation tensor embedding the full product of spaces of dimensions a,b into a space of dimension ab. Its last two mode transformations are arbitrary, and its first mode transformation is their inverse-dual tensor product. Thus its ordinary stabilizers have exactly the sign vectors (b x + a y, x, y), where the formula is binary. Choose the parities of b,a to be the other two coefficients of the functional. This realizes the corresponding kernel plane. Reflect a target coordinate in a mode with nonzero coefficient to translate it to the opposite affine plane. A same-format all-zero comparison has the full determinant sign space. The hard profiles in the query lower bounds therefore occur on actual tensors, including when the relevant functional only uses two or one of the signs. The query model hides the tensor entries and only reveals answers to its stated probes.

## Consequences for unrestricted strictness

Write O, A, B and S for the four tiers in order. The previous positive constructions give the upward chain. Also A reducing to O would collapse all four tiers, because S can be decided by seven fixed one-sign comparisons and ordinary TI admits a single-instance conjunction. If B reduces to A, then two independent A comparisons reduce to A: first use the independent-axis packing into B. Iterating this fixed-arity conjunction packs the seven queries for S into A. Therefore a strict top gap would imply strictness at all three gaps, and a strict middle gap would imply strictness at the first.

Even a single unrestricted strict gap would imply P ≠ NP. Membership in NP follows by checking three invertible mode maps and their determinants. If P = NP, all four languages would be in P. Every source language in P reduces to any nontrivial target language by deciding its input and outputting a fixed yes or no instance. This shows why an orbit-count mismatch or a lower bound for a particular gadget does not complete an unrestricted separation.

More precisely, all four fixed-field problems are in NP ∩ coAM. The ordinary orbit nonisomorphism protocol applies to each acting group. To sample a uniform SL matrix, sample a uniform GL matrix and normalize its determinant in one coordinate, using the equal-sized determinant fibers. Uniform GL sampling by rejection has constant expected trials over a fixed finite field, and truncation gives bounded error. The verifier picks an endpoint at random, sends its randomly transported image, and asks for the endpoint bit. The two orbit distributions are identical on yes comparisons and disjoint on no comparisons. Standard constant-round public-coin conversion gives coAM membership for isomorphism. Thus unrestricted strictness would show a language in NP ∩ coAM outside P. See [Grochow and Qiao on tensor orbit nonisomorphism](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.00309) and [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5).
