# Degree and dimension lower bound for equivariant tensor encodings

Work over a fixed finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$, where $q>2$. Let $1\le k\le3$ be the number of determinant-one constraints in an input comparison and let $s<k$ be the number in the output. The case $q=3$, $s=k-1$ gives the three adjacent gaps between ordinary TI and SL-TI.

Fix $d\ge3$. Take $n=m^2>d$, $n\ge5$, where $m$ is divisible by $q-1$. Write $\mathcal X=(F^n)^{\otimes3}$. The protected source group $K=\mathrm{SL}_n(F)^k$ acts in the first $k$ modes, with the remaining modes fixed. Consider an objectwise encoder on $\mathcal X$ that outputs tensors on a fixed list of spaces $W_j$. Each output tensor has order at most $d$ with possible dual factors. The target compares the encoded objects using simultaneous invertible changes of basis of the $W_j$, with determinant one required on $s$ specified spaces. This includes a single output three-tensor as a special case.

Assume the following two conditions.

1. Each space $W_j$ has a representation $\rho_j$ of $K$. For every $T\in\mathcal X$ and $g\in K$, the encoding of $gT$ is the encoding of $T$ acted on by these maps, using the inverse transpose on each dual factor. The same lifts preserve any fixed marker tensors in the output.
2. Every coordinate of every output tensor, as a function of the input entries, is represented by a polynomial over $F$ of total degree at most $\tau$. There is no requirement of a preferred polynomial representative or formal polynomial equivariance. Equivariance on $F$-points suffices.

If this encoder decides the source comparison exactly for every pair in the format, and $\tau<n$, some output space has dimension at least
$$
\frac{1}{q-1}\binom{n}{\lceil(n-\tau)/d\rceil}.
$$
In particular, for polynomial dimension and fixed $d,q$ the degree must be $n-O(1)$ or larger along this sequence of formats. If $\tau\le (1-\varepsilon)n$ for fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the dimension lower bound is exponential. There is no constraint on the number of output tensors.

## 1. Extend the equivariance from the special linear group

Let $a=\lceil(n-\tau)/d\rceil$. Then $1\le a\le \lceil n/d\rceil$ and $d(a-1)+\tau<n$. Suppose each $\dim W_j<\binom{n}{a}/(q-1)$. Apply the diagonal weight argument from Section 1 of [the marker theorem](GapOneSeparation.md) with cutoff $a$. The argument uses only $1\le a\le\lceil n/d\rceil$ and $n\ge5$. For each input factor, every occurring determinant-one diagonal weight on $W_j$ has a unique representative with support less than $a$. This extends the action on $W_j$ to the corresponding general linear factor, with those representatives as the weights of its full diagonal group. Do this for all $k$ factors. The resulting actions commute because each new diagonal operator is a linear combination of weight projectors for its own factor. Write $G=\mathrm{GL}_n(F)^k$ for this extended acting group.

Let $\mathcal P_\tau$ be the image of polynomials of degree at most $\tau$ in the vector space of functions $\mathcal X\to F$. This is a $G$-module by pullback along the inverse of the natural input action. Substitution is linear in the input entries, so the degree filtration is preserved. A diagonal weight appearing in $\mathcal P_\tau$ has support at most $\tau$ in each source mode. This holds for the formal monomial span since each input coordinate uses one basis index per source mode. Evaluation is equivariant for the full diagonal group. Passing to its image cannot introduce weights absent from the monomial span, since this diagonal group is split over $F$ and has order prime to the characteristic.

An output coordinate map into a tensor product $Y$ is an element of $\mathcal P_\tau\otimes Y$. Equivariance under $K$ says that this element is fixed under its simultaneous actions. In any one source factor a full diagonal weight of $\mathcal P_\tau\otimes Y$ has support at most $\tau+d(a-1)$, which is strictly less than $n$. This uses the triangle bound on the support of a sum of weights and applies also to dual factors. A weight trivial on the determinant-one diagonal group is represented by a vector of constant exponents. Such a vector with proper support has all exponents zero. It follows that the entire determinant-one diagonal fixed space is fixed by the full diagonal. In particular, each output coordinate map is equivariant under a diagonal reflection or other generator of the determinant quotient. Together with the original $K$ equivariance this proves equivariance under $G$ on all input arrays. A fixed marker is the degree-zero special case of the same argument.

## 2. A rigid source orbit forces failure

Let $A=\operatorname{Mat}_m(F)$ and use its multiplication tensor $T$ in the two input spaces and the output space, identified with $\mathcal X$ by dual bases. Every automorphism of this tensor has determinant one in all three modes.

To see this, an invertible isotopy satisfies $p(x)q(y)=w(xy)$. Setting $u=p(1)$ and $v=q(1)$ gives $w(x)=p(x)v=u q(x)$. Since $p,q,w$ are invertible, left multiplication by $u$ and right multiplication by $v$ are invertible, so $u,v$ are units. The common map $\phi(x)=u^{-1}p(x)=q(x)v^{-1}$ is a unital algebra automorphism, because the isotopy equation gives $u\phi(x)\phi(y)v=u\phi(xy)v$. Thus $p,q,w$ are obtained from $\phi$ by left and right unit multiplications. Every algebra automorphism of $\operatorname{Mat}_m(F)$ is inner. Indeed, for image matrix units $f_{ij}$, choose a nonzero $z$ in the image of $f_{11}$. The vectors $f_{i1}z$ are a basis, since applying $f_{1j}$ detects the $j$-th coefficient, and in this basis $f_{ij}$ are standard matrix units. Left multiplication by a unit acts identically on $m$ column copies and right multiplication acts identically on $m$ row copies. Their determinants are $m$-th powers in $F^\times$, hence one because $m$ is divisible by $q-1$. Inner automorphisms are also determinant one. Inverse duals preserve this conclusion.

Taking the $s$ tested determinants of the extended output action defines a homomorphism $G\to (F^\times)^s$. It is trivial on $K$ since each special linear factor is generated by transvections of order the characteristic, coprime to $q-1$. Hence it factors through the $k$ input determinants. Since $s<k$, its kernel contains a nontrivial source determinant vector. Choose $g\in G$ with that vector. The source tensors $T$ and $gT$ admit no permitted source transporter, even with arbitrary invertible maps in the untested source modes. Indeed, every ordinary transporter to $gT$ differs from $g$ by an ordinary automorphism of $T$, and the latter always has determinant one. But the extended output maps take the encoding of $T$ to that of $gT$ while obeying every output determinant test. This is a false positive and proves the inequality.

For the tradeoff, if every $\dim W_j\le n^C$ for fixed $C$, then $a$ is bounded. Otherwise choose a fixed integer $b>C$ and use $\binom{n}{a}\ge\binom{n}{b}$ whenever $b\le a\le\lceil n/d\rceil$ and $n$ is large. Since $\binom{n}{b}/(q-1)$ eventually exceeds $n^C$, this is impossible. Bounded $a$ implies $n-\tau=O(1)$. When $\tau\le(1-\varepsilon)n$ the displayed binomial coefficient instead grows exponentially at fixed $d,\varepsilon$.

This lower bound concerns encoders with specified linear lifts and bounded-degree coordinate functions. The hypotheses permit a modular representation, arbitrary tensor-system size at bounded tensor order, and finite-field functions. General polynomial-time many-one reductions can use both objects jointly, can change the target format depending on input entries, and need not preserve any group action. The result therefore leaves the three general complexity separations unresolved.

Earlier MathProofs work gave a characteristic-zero obstruction for fixed affine equivariant encodings. This argument uses the finite-field diagonal character calculation and actual low-degree functions on finite tensor arrays. It does not assert publication priority. For the terminology of objectwise versus pairwise reductions, see [Fortnow and Grochow](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4775).
