# Feasibility of additive Frobenius systems in polynomial bit time

**GP-0758, additive polynomial specialization.** Let $K=\mathbb F_q$ be given explicitly, and consider any rectangular system
$$
\sum_{j=1}^n\sum_{k=0}^{D} a_{ijk}x_j^{q^k}=b_i,\qquad a_{ijk},b_i\in K.
$$
Over $\overline K$, feasibility and the number of distinct geometric zeros, with infinity reported separately, are computable deterministically in bit time polynomial in the input length and $D$. The input can be sparse in the displayed Frobenius powers, provided $D$ is polynomially bounded, for instance by supplying a unary bound. The actual degrees $q^D$ may be exponential. If the characteristic $p$ is fixed, a feasible input has an explicitly constructible solution over an extension of $K$ of degree at most $p(\min(m,n)D+1)$, where $m$ is the number of equations.

## A finite linear feasibility test

Let $T$ act on $\overline K$ as $x\mapsto x^q$, and replace the input by $A(T)x=b$, with $A_{ij}(T)=\sum_k a_{ijk}T^k\in K[T]$. Any nonzero $d(T)\in K[T]$ acts surjectively on $\overline K$, since its action is a nonconstant univariate polynomial. Over the principal ideal domain $K[T]$, let $UAV$ be a Smith form with $U,V$ unimodular. Its nonzero diagonal entries $d_i$ give solvable individual equations, and its zero rows require $U(1)b$ to vanish there. Here $U(T)b=U(1)b$ because $x^q=x$ on $K$. No Smith multipliers need to be computed.

Set $M=\min(m,n)D+1$, substitute $T=1+u$, and solve over the truncated $K$-algebra $R=K[u]/(u^M)$ the linear problem
$$
A(1+u)z=b\,u^{M-1}.
$$
This is an ordinary $K$-linear system in the coefficients of $z$. To prove equivalence, the same Smith transformations act invertibly on $R$, and the transformed right side is $U(1)b\,u^{M-1}$. If the rank is $r>0$, the product of the nonzero Smith entries divides each nonzero $r$-minor and therefore has degree at most $rD$. Thus each $d_i(1+u)$ is a unit times $u^e$ for some $e<M$, and its image contains every multiple of $u^{M-1}$. For zero rows, the constraints are exactly $U(1)b=0$ on those rows. This also covers rank zero. Hence feasibility is determined by the finite linear test without factoring polynomials or constructing large splitting fields.

For an actual solution over fixed characteristic, choose the least power $N$ of $p$ with $N\ge M$. On $\mathbb F_{q^N}$ the $q$-Frobenius is a cyclic $K[T]$-module by the normal basis theorem, with module $K[T]/(T^N-1)=K[u]/(u^N)$. The elements of $K$ form the kernel of $u$, corresponding to the scalar multiples of $u^{N-1}$ with one common nonzero normalization. The same proof therefore gives feasibility over $\mathbb F_{q^N}$. Construct that field by successive degree-$p$ Artin–Schreier extensions. In each current field choose a basis element with nonzero trace to $\mathbb F_p$, and adjoin a root of $X^p-X-a$ for that element $a$. The trace functional is nonzero because its polynomial has degree less than the number of field elements, and $X^p-X-a$ is irreducible when the trace of $a$ is nonzero. For the latter fact, if $\beta^p-\beta=a$, then the Frobenius of the current field sends $\beta$ to $\beta+\operatorname{Tr}(a)$, giving an orbit of length $p$. All tower arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity for fixed $p$. Solve the original system by $K$-linear algebra in the resulting field basis.

## Distinct geometric points

Compute the rank $r$ of $A$ over $K(T)$ by polynomial-matrix elimination. If the system is feasible and $r<n$, there are free coordinates in the Smith system and infinitely many solutions. For $r=n$, let $d_i=T^{e_i}\widetilde d_i$ with $\widetilde d_i(0)\ne0$. The additive polynomial for $\widetilde d_i$ is separable, its derivative being the nonzero constant coefficient $\widetilde d_i(0)$. Its degree is $q^{\deg \widetilde d_i}$, and the extra Frobenius power $T^{e_i}$ is bijective. Thus the number of points is $q^{\sum_i\deg d_i-\sum_i e_i}$.

Both sums can be computed by elementary finite linear algebra. Choose $n$ independent rows of $A$ and put $f\ne0$ equal to their determinant. Let $L\subset K[T]^n$ be the submodule generated by all rows of $A$. The adjugate of the chosen rows gives $fK[T]^n\subseteq L$. For any nonzero modulus $g$, the dimension of
$$
(K[T]/(g))^n/\bigl(L\bmod g\bigr)
$$
is $\sum_i\deg\gcd(d_i,g)$, by Smith form. Compute the dimension directly by reducing each row and each of its multiples $T^j$ for $0\le j<\deg g$ modulo $g$, then taking their $K$-linear rank. Use first $g=f$, giving $\sum_i\deg d_i$, since each $d_i$ divides $f$. Use second $g=T^{v_T(f)}$, giving $\sum_i e_i$. A constant modulus gives a zero-dimensional vector space as usual. These moduli have degree at most $nD$, so the exponent and the resulting integer count have polynomial bit size.

This result concerns additive systems with $q$-Frobenius powers and explicitly bounded indices. It complements the general counting-hierarchy bound without derandomizing arbitrary finite-field Hilbert Nullstellensatz. [General finite-field context, Andrews](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17904)
