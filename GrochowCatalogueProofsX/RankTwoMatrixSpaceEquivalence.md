# Exact equivalence of all upper-rank-two matrix spaces

## GP-0234

Given two matrix spaces of upper rank at most two over an explicitly presented finite field, left-right equivalence is decidable in deterministic polynomial bit time, uniformly in the field size. The theorem covers arbitrary row and column dimensions, arbitrary space dimension, and all characteristics. The upper-rank promise means that each matrix in either input space has rank at most two. This settles the fixed upper rank $r=2$ case of the general bounded-rank problem.

The promise itself is recognizable in deterministic polynomial bit time. For each input basis, test every combination using at most three basis matrices, with coefficients from a fixed four-element set containing zero if $q>3$ and from the whole field otherwise. To justify the test, each $3\times3$ minor of the generic linear combination is a polynomial of degree at most three. Over small fields, reduce each variable using $x^q=x$, without increasing degree or widening the support of any monomial. If a minor is a nonzero function, the reduced polynomial is nonzero. One of its nonzero monomials involves at most three variables. Setting all other variables to zero leaves that monomial and a nonzero polynomial. It has degree in each variable at most three and strictly less than the size of the chosen scalar grid, so it is nonzero somewhere on the grid. This witnesses rank at least three among the tested combinations.

## Reducing to essential dimensions

For a space $S\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(U,W)$, compute the common kernel $K=\bigcap_{A\in S}\ker A$ and the joint image $I=\sum_{A\in S}\operatorname{im}A$ by linear algebra on a basis. The reduced space is in $\operatorname{Hom}(U/K,I)$. Left-right equivalence preserves the dimensions of these two spaces. Conversely any equivalence of the reduced spaces extends to the original equal ambient dimensions by choosing complements and arbitrary isomorphisms on the omitted summands. We henceforth treat reduced spaces and compare only equal dimensions.

If either side has dimension at most one, the result follows immediately: a nonzero reduced space of row or column vectors is the full corresponding row or column space. If a side has dimension two, write a tensor for a basis of $S$ in the three modes row, column and basis label. Permute its modes so the side of dimension two is the basis-label mode. The resulting two matrix slices are independent, since the original space was reduced. The original left-right equivalence is precisely ordinary isomorphism of its basis tensor, because the basis can change by an arbitrary invertible map. After permuting modes this is exactly equivalence of a two-dimensional matrix pencil. An all-field algorithm for such pencils is given below.

## The wide compression case

Suppose both reduced dimensions are at least three. A mixed compression has a hyperplane $D\subset U$ and a line $L\subset W$ such that $A(D)\subseteq L$ for every $A\in S$. Such a pair is unique. If two pairs share their line, distinct domain hyperplanes span $U$ and force the whole joint image into that line. If they share their hyperplane, distinct lines force that hyperplane into the common kernel. And if both entries differ, the intersection of the two domain hyperplanes is a nonzero common kernel. All three conclusions contradict reducedness.

Find one rank-two $A$ by testing the supplied basis and all pair sums. In fact, if every nonzero basis matrix has rank one and no pair sum has rank two, then each pair shares an image line or a row line. If two share only their image line, every third must share it as well, since it cannot share two different row lines. If there is no such pair, all basis matrices share a row line. This proves correctness of the search. If the space has upper rank one, the same argument shows that it cannot be reduced and wide.

In a mixed compression, every rank-two image contains $L$. For $q\leq3$, try each of the at most four lines of $\operatorname{im}A$. For $q\geq4$, take four distinct field scalars $t$ and form $A+tB$ for every basis matrix $B$. Keep the rank-two matrices among them. In compression coordinates a matrix is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\ c&0\end{pmatrix},
$$
and it has rank two precisely when both arms $b,c$ are nonzero. Along $A+tB$ each arm vanishes at at most one $t$. Therefore at least two of the four matrices are retained for each $B$ and their span contains that $B$. The retained matrices span $S$. Their images have one-dimensional intersection in the compression case, since a larger intersection would make all images the same plane and contradict reducedness. Test this candidate line, whenever the intersection is a line. For any candidate $L$, compute the common kernel of the induced maps into $W/L$. It has codimension exactly one precisely when the candidate yields a compression. These tests are polynomial in the bit input and verify the promise they use.

In coordinates for $D,L$, put $U=k\oplus E$ and $W=k\oplus F$, with $E=D$ and the first summand of $W$ equal to $L$. Write $J$ for the one-dimensional matrix space supported only at the pivot entry $a$, and let $M\subseteq E^*\oplus F$ be the image of $S$ on its two arms. Both projections of $M$ are onto, by reducedness of $S$. If $S\cap J=J$, then $S=J\oplus M$. Otherwise $S$ is the graph of a linear form on $M$. Extend the form to $E^*\oplus F$, and use row and column shears to subtract it from the upper-left entry. These shears act by adding arbitrary independent linear forms in $b,c$ and leave the arms unchanged. Finally a subdirect subspace $M$ is the fiber product of its two projections over isomorphic quotients: if $B_0=\{b:(b,0)\in M\}$ and $C_0=\{c:(0,c)\in M\}$, the relation $M$ induces an isomorphism $E^*/B_0\to F/C_0$. Under independent changes of coordinates this is classified exactly by the quotient dimension. Thus two wide compressed reduced spaces are equivalent exactly when the dimensions of their arms and of $M$ and the indicator $S\cap J=J$ agree.

## Primitive alternatives

For wide reduced spaces of upper rank two, the mixed compression case is exactly the nonprimitive case. Over fields of size greater than two the only primitive alternative is the full $3\times3$ alternating space. This is the upper-rank-two case of the Atkinson–Lloyd classification, summarized in the introduction and Proposition 1.1 of the following paper. For $\mathbb F_2$, its Proposition 3.3 proves that primitive spaces have dimensions $3\times3$. Thus over $\mathbb F_2$ compare any two primitive alternatives by enumerating the constant number of pairs in $\mathrm{GL}_3(2)^2$. These structural inputs require upper rank two and reducedness, both satisfied in this branch. [de Seguins Pazzis, primitive spaces of upper rank two](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.1575)

## A deterministic pencil comparison

Use the deterministic partial Kronecker construction in Sections 1–3 of the linked pencil proof: extract singular summands by constructive module isomorphism, split infinity from a finite regular module by iterated preimages, and compute the latter invariant factors by Frobenius form. These operations give both singular minimal-index lists and polynomial data for all regular elementary divisors, without factorization. The next paragraphs state the ordinary comparison explicitly and record the counting information needed for GP-0229. [Brooksbank and Luks, deterministic module isomorphism](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf) · [Factorization-free pencil reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/3454ca222e13e2f12bf232b920771763bf8a7d1a/TISLPencilsAndOrbits/Pencils.md)

Each geometric regular point is labeled by the multiset of its elementary-divisor lengths. A variable change in $\mathrm{GL}_2(k)$ induces a projectivity on these labeled points and preserves both singular lists. To see this, homogeneous substitution preserves degrees in the kernel equations and replaces a local parameter at a regular point by a unit times a local parameter at its image. In both cases the substitution is invertible. Scalar substitutions are irrelevant to strict equivalence. The strict Kronecker classification gives the converse once the singular lists and projective labels match. [Kronecker invariants](https://gauss.uc3m.es/fdopico/papers/laa2016-4.pdf)

The labeled list can be manipulated without extracting its points. At every regular point sort its block lengths and left-pad with zeros to the dimension $r$ of the regular summand. For $1\leq i,a\leq r$, let $D_{i,a}(x,y)$ be the squarefree homogeneous divisor whose roots are the points with entry $a$ at position $i$. At finite points use the $i$-th invariant factor of the finite regular matrix, left-padded with factors equal to one. Invariant factors divide one another, so their ordered valuations at each root give exactly the sorted list. Extract roots of multiplicity exactly $a$ by dividing the squarefree part of the gcd through Hasse derivative order $a-1$ by that through order $a$. This works because the Hasse derivative of order equal to the root multiplicity is nonzero there, and all lower derivatives vanish. Squarefree parts over $k$ require only gcds, derivatives and coefficientwise $p$-th roots: $f/\gcd(f,f')$ detects factors of multiplicity not divisible by $p$, their full powers can be removed by a gcd with a sufficiently high power of that quotient, and the remainder is a $p$-th power. Add infinity to $D_{i,a}$ exactly when its own sorted list has entry $a$ there. All $D_{i,a}$ have polynomial degree. The support is the union of their distinct roots, including infinity when necessary.

Let the support size $s$ be this geometric root count. If $s=0$, only the two singular lists matter. If $s=1$, the unique point is rational and the action is transitive, so only its label matters. If $s=2$, the two points are rational or form a conjugate quadratic pair. This distinction is detected by a gcd with $z^q-z$ on the finite part. Every matching of rational pairs can be realized by a base-field projectivity. Every matching of conjugate pairs can also be realized: for chosen quadratic roots $\alpha,\beta$, express $\beta=a\alpha+b$ with $a\ne0$ and $a,b\in k$. Their partner roots are matched by Frobenius. Hence compare the two unordered labels and the rationality type. The labels as unordered lists can be read by refining the squarefree finite support using gcds with all finite parts of the $D_{i,a}$, and entering the separate label of infinity. For a conjugate pair both labels agree.

For $s\geq3$, build the reduced algebra of affine roots $k[z]/(f)$ for the squarefree finite support polynomial $f$. Adjoin a direct-product factor $k$ for infinity if needed. Its points have homogeneous coordinates $[z:1]$ or $[1:0]$. Tensor together three copies of each of the source and target root algebras. The dimension is polynomial, at most $s^6$ after rejecting unequal sizes. Invert the brackets that enforce distinctness in both triples. A triple of distinct points gives an explicit projective frame, so the two triples give the unique candidate projectivity by multiplying the inverse of one frame matrix by the other. All these computations are polynomial arithmetic operations in the algebra. For example the map $v\mapsto ([v,s_1][s_3,s_2],[v,s_2][s_3,s_1])$ normalizes a triple $s_1,s_2,s_3$.

Separate candidates into charts by the first nonzero entry of their $2\times2$ matrix, and normalize that entry to one. Impose $u^q=u$ on the four entries of each matrix to retain exactly the projectivities over $k$. Finally require that each source divisor $D_{i,a}$, after substitution in the appropriate direction, is proportional to the corresponding target divisor. Polynomial degrees must agree and coefficient proportionality is tested by all two-by-two minors of the two coefficient vectors. At a given surviving point the substitution is invertible, so neither vector is zero. These equations impose exactly label preservation. In a product of fields, inverting $u$ amounts to quotienting by the multiplication kernel $\ker m_u$, and imposing $u=0$ to quotienting by the image $uA$. Thus all conditions are implemented by linear algebra without decomposing the reduced algebra. A chart is nonzero precisely when it contains a valid geometric choice of two triples, hence a matching base-field projectivity. The use of disjoint charts also permits counting without duplication. This is the regular-root algebra method from the prior pencil proof, with a direct label test for ordinary equivalence.

This gives deterministic polynomial bit time for the pencil case and completes the algorithm for all upper-rank-two spaces. The fixed ranks above two require further structural and algorithmic input.
