# Cycle-index characterization with a transposition

**GP-0935.** Let $G$ be a symmetry-characterized finite simple graph in the sense of Grochow and Urisman. Suppose $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ contains a transposition. Every finite simple graph $H$ on the same order with the same full automorphism cycle index is isomorphic to $G$ or its complement. In this subclass, the symmetry-characterized graphs are exactly the disjoint unions of equal cliques and their complements, the stars and their complements, and the complete and empty cases. A compact polynomial-size circuit for the cycle index is explicit on these families. [Grochow and Urisman, Open Question 1.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26244v2#S1.SS3)

## Classifying the transposition case

For a permutation group define a graph whose edges are the pairs for which the group contains the transposition of the pair. The subgroup generated by all these transpositions is the direct product of the full symmetric groups of its connected components. Indeed transpositions along any connected graph generate the full symmetric group on that component. Consequently the transposition graph is a union of cliques on the components, and the component partition is invariant under the whole group by conjugation.

Apply this to $P=\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. If the partition has one component, $P$ is the full symmetric group and $G$ is empty or complete. Otherwise its clique graph $F$ is nontrivial because there is a transposition and at least two components. The automorphism group of $F$ contains $P$. Symmetry characterization forces $F$ to be isomorphic to $G$ or its complement. This property is invariant under complements and relabeling, so $F$ itself is symmetry-characterized.

If $F$ has cliques of different sizes, a proper vertex orbit $O$ of size at least two is obtained from all cliques of any nonsingleton size. The graph consisting of a clique on $O$ and otherwise isolated vertices is invariant under $\operatorname{Aut}(F)$. It is disconnected, whereas the complement of $F$ is connected. Symmetry characterization now implies that $F$ has a single nonsingleton clique. If there are two or more isolates, completing the set of isolates to a separate clique gives another invariant disconnected graph, with two nonsingleton cliques. This violates symmetry characterization. Thus $F$ is a clique with one isolate, the complement of a star. If all cliques have the same size, the vertex action is transitive and has exactly two orbits on unordered pairs in the nontrivial case, according to their component membership. These two orbitals suffice for symmetry characterization. A star and its complement also have exactly two orbits on unordered pairs, given by the distinguished center and the symmetric leaves. Trivial graphs satisfy the definition directly.

## Equal cycle indices

Use the normalized cycle index. The identity coefficient recovers the group order, and the coefficients also give the number of transpositions. Burnside's lemma recovers the number of vertex orbits from the average number $c_1$ of fixed points, and the number of unordered-pair orbits from the average of $\binom{c_1}{2}+c_2$, where $c_2$ is the number of two-cycles. Complementing a graph leaves its automorphism group and the cycle index unchanged.

Suppose first $F$ is a nontrivial union of $t$ cliques of size $b\ge2$, so the order is $n=bt$ and $t\ge2$. An arbitrary competitor $H$ with equal cycle index has a transitive automorphism group and hence all transposition components have one common size $b'$. The number of transpositions is $n(b'-1)/2=n(b-1)/2$, so $b'=b$. The pairs inside components and the pairs in different components are both nonempty invariant sets. Since the total number of unordered-pair orbits is two, each is one orbit. The edge set of $H$ must therefore be the set of inside pairs, its complement, all pairs, or no pairs. The last two have full symmetric automorphism group, of different order from that of $F$. The first two give exactly the claimed possibilities.

Suppose next $F$ is a clique with one isolate and $n\ge3$. The competitor's transposition components have total pair count $\binom{n-1}{2}$. A component of size $n$ would give a strictly higher count. Among partitions into at least two blocks, $\sum_i\binom{b_i}{2}$ has its maximum only on the partition $(n-1,1)$. Merging blocks strictly increases it if at least three blocks remain before merging, and for two blocks the maximum is immediate from $b_1+b_2=n$. Thus the competitor contains the full symmetric group on one $(n-1)$-block. The two group orders equal $(n-1)!$, so this is its entire automorphism group. Its two pair orbits again force a star, its complement, or a trivial graph, with the trivial options excluded by group order. For complete and empty graphs the order is $n!$, which forces the full symmetric group in the competitor. Orders at most two are included directly.

The equal-clique case also has a compact cycle index calculation. Its automorphism group is $S_b$ acting independently inside $t$ blocks, together with $S_t$ permuting them. On a $j$-cycle of blocks, the return permutation on one block is the product of the independent within-block permutations and is uniform in $S_b$. A return cycle of length $i$ contributes a cycle of length $ij$. Hence substitute $Z_{S_b}(x_j,x_{2j},\ldots)$ for each $x_j$ in $Z_{S_t}$. The recurrence $Z_{S_0}=1$ and $mZ_{S_m}=\sum_{j=1}^m x_j Z_{S_{m-j}}$ follows by specifying the cycle containing a distinguished symbol. It gives a polynomial-size circuit for this substitution. The star case has cycle index $x_1Z_{S_{n-1}}$, and the trivial case has $Z_{S_n}$. These calculations do not assume a structural promise on the competitor.
