# Intersection-table recovery from generators of small total valency

**GP-0800.** Let a coherent configuration of rank $r$ have designated basis relations $D$ generating its rational adjacency algebra together with the diagonal fiber projections. With the relation names, source and target fibers, transpose involution, and the list $D$ specified, all intersection numbers are determined by at most
$$
r+\sum_{g\in D} r_{\operatorname{src}(g)} k_g
$$
selected positive intersection numbers. Here $r_F$ is the number of relation types with source fiber $F$, and $k_g$ is the row valency of $g$ on its source fiber. The chosen coefficients and a full table can be computed in polynomial bit time from a table of integer coefficients. The determination holds against arbitrary coherent configurations with the same structural labels, regardless of their initially unknown generating relations. [Ponomarenko, Problem 8.1](https://www.pdmi.ras.ru/~inp/cp01.pdf)

Let $A_i$ be the adjacency matrix of relation $i$, let $i^*$ denote its transpose and let $d_F$ denote the diagonal relation of fiber $F$. The coefficient of $A_{d_{\operatorname{src}(g)}}$ in $A_gA_{g^*}$ is the positive integer $k_g$. Record these numbers for the relations whose valencies are needed, using at most $r$ entries. For each $g\in D$ also record every positive coefficient $p_{g j}^t$ of $A_t$ in $A_gA_j$. The identity
$$
A_g \Bigl(\sum_j A_j\Bigr)=k_g\sum_{t\mid\operatorname{src}(t)=\operatorname{src}(g)}A_t
$$
holds because the sum on the left inside parentheses is the all-ones matrix. For any output $t$ with the appropriate source, $\sum_jp_{g j}^t=k_g$. This means at most $k_g$ of these coefficients can be positive. It also implies that for any competing table extending the recorded positive coefficients, all unrecorded coefficients for these $g,t$ must be zero. Those with the wrong source must also be zero. The data thus recover the complete matrices $L_g$ of left multiplication by the generators in the relation basis.

The left multiplication matrix of a diagonal fiber projection is determined directly by source fibers. In the abstract $r$-dimensional coordinate space let $v_1$ denote the identity element, namely the sum of the diagonal basis vectors. Start with $v_1$ and iteratively apply a diagonal projection or an $L_g$ to the current basis vectors, retaining a vector only when it increases the span. Keep the corresponding multiplication word for each retained vector. At stabilization this span contains all products of generators applied to $v_1$, so it is the full coordinate space by hypothesis. All the resulting words, and their coordinate vectors, are the same for any purported completion. Store also the left multiplication matrix of each retained word by multiplying the matrices already known. Every relation vector is a rational linear combination of the retained words. The same linear combination of word multiplication matrices is its own left multiplication matrix. Their columns give the entire table. This recovers each completion identically, including its structure coefficients for generators absent from $D$.

There are at most $r$ successful insertions, and word representatives may be chosen of length at most $r$. The matrices have integral entries of input bit length, so the bit lengths of the vector and matrix products of this word length are polynomial. Rational elimination and interpolation then take polynomial bit time.

For a homogeneous thin configuration the basis relations are the regular group permutations. One may find a generating set of at most $\lfloor\log_2 r\rfloor$ relations by adjoining elements outside the subgroup generated so far. Each has valency one. Thus the determination uses at most $r(1+\lfloor\log_2 r\rfloor)$ positive entries. At the other extreme, for the complete configuration on $n$ singleton fibers, $r=n^2$ and the $2(n-1)$ elementary matrices on both directions of a path, together with the named diagonals, generate the algebra. This gives at most $r+2n(n-1)$ positive entries even though its full table contains $n^3$ positive entries. The theorem bounds the certificate size by a chosen generating family. It does not specify a universal optimum as a function of $r$ alone.
