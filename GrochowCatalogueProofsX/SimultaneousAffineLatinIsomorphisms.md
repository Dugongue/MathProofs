# Simultaneous isomorphism of affine Latin operations in hidden coordinates

## GP-0523

Given two nonempty ordered lists of Latin operations on two equally large domains, suppose all operations in either list are affine over one common elementary abelian group structure on its domain. The affine coordinates, origin, prime, and linear maps can all be hidden. This promise is deterministically recognizable in time polynomial in the tables. In the same time one can decide whether a single bijection simultaneously isomorphically carries every operation to its partner, and construct it when it exists. The number of operations is unrestricted. The result does not require a single compatible group isotope to be supplied in the input.

First recover a common translation group from the first operation. If $L_x$ denotes its left translation, fix any $x_0$ and generate the group of $L_xL_{x_0}^{-1}$ over all $x$. In affine coordinates where $x*y=A x+B y+c$, these are precisely the regular translations by $A(x-x_0)$. Check directly that the displayed permutations form a regular elementary abelian group. One can check group closure by composing the displayed permutations, match by their distinct values at an origin, and verify commutativity and a common prime exponent. A one-point domain is immediate. Use the regular translation action and a group basis to recover coordinates over $\mathbb F_p$. On each remaining operation recover the affine coefficients from its values along coordinate basis vectors with one argument zero, and check the proposed formula at every input pair. These tests also certify the promise when they pass.

Any isomorphism of the first operations conjugates the intrinsic translation groups, since it sends the entire set of left translation differences to the corresponding set. Thus it is affine in the recovered coordinates: specifically, its value at zero and the induced linear isomorphism on translations determine it. For lists of common dimension $d$, write the candidate as $x\mapsto F x+t$. For each paired operation with coefficients $A_i,B_i,c_i$ and $A_i',B_i',c_i'$, the exact equations are
$$
 FA_i=A_i'F,\qquad FB_i=B_i'F,\qquad
 Fc_i=(A_i'+B_i'-I)t+c_i'.
$$

Try every target point $t$, of which there are only as many as in one table row. For fixed $t$, put $d_i=(A_i'+B_i'-I)t+c_i'$. The full marked matrix problem is one module isomorphism test. On each side adjoin one extra one-dimensional summand. Supply the projection onto that line, the block matrices extending each $A_i,B_i$ by zero on the line, and the map whose only nonzero column sends the basis vector of the line to $c_i$ on the source, or to $d_i$ on the target. Treat these named maps as the actions of free-algebra generators. An invertible intertwiner must have block diagonal shape $\operatorname{diag}(F_0,\lambda)$ by the projection. Its equations give $F_0c_i=\lambda d_i$ and all the rectangular intertwining equations with $F_0$. Divide by the nonzero $\lambda$ to recover $F$. Conversely a valid $F$ extends with $\lambda=1$. Constructive deterministic module isomorphism over the finite field therefore decides each trial and produces the affine map. The cost is polynomial in the full tables.
