# Epimorphisms onto integral class-two groups with cyclic commutator

## GP-0375

Fix a finitely generated torsion-free class-two group $H$ with $H'\cong\mathbb Z$ and free abelianization. On input a finite presentation of $G$, assume the derived group of $G/\gamma_3(G)$ has torsion-free rank at most one. There is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time test for whether $G$ surjects onto $H$, with ordinary word encoding of relators. It includes the obstruction from torsion in the abelianization of the source. The target may be any fixed integral Heisenberg group with any number of additional free central factors.

### Computing the source data

Set $N=G/\gamma_3(G)$, since every homomorphism to $H$ factors through $N$. In the free class-two group on the source generators use collected form $(a,c)$ with abelian coordinate $a\in\mathbb Z^d$ and commutator coordinate $c\in\mathbb Z^{\binom d2}$. Interchanging generators gives a bilinear integer correction in the second coordinate of the product. Powers use scalar multiplication and that correction multiplied by a binomial coefficient. Collect each relator $r_j$, and let $A$ have columns its abelian coordinates.

The relators' normal closure is generated by the $r_j$ together with the central words $[r_j,x_i]$ for each generator $x_i$. Let $T$ be the lattice spanned by the central words. Modulo $T$ the relators commute with all generators. The derived group of $N$ consequently has presentation
$$
\mathbb Z^{\binom d2}/\bigl(T+\langle c(r_1^{u_1}\cdots r_\ell^{u_\ell})\mid u\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}A\rangle\bigr).
$$
The central coordinates for a lattice basis of $\ker_{\mathbb Z} A$ suffice, since the map on that kernel to the commutator lattice modulo $T$ is additive. Smith normal form gives both the basis and a presentation of the derived group. Torsion in that group maps trivially to $H$, so quotient it out. Rank zero rules out a surjection. In rank one write $z$ for the resulting generator of the derived group and work after this quotient. [Polynomial integer normal forms](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

### Removing the remaining central torsion obstruction

Write the abelianization in Smith coordinates as $\mathbb Z^u\oplus\bigoplus_i C_{d_i}$, with $d_i>1$, and choose lifts $t_i$ of the finite cyclic generators. They are central. Indeed $t_i^{d_i}$ is in the derived group, so each commutator with $t_i$ has $d_i$-th power one and lies in the torsion-free derived group. Compute $t_i^{d_i}=z^{b_i}$ by solving for a relator product with the same abelian coordinate and collecting the difference in the basic commutators. Smith transformations give the lattice solutions and final central coordinate.

A surjection takes $z$ to a generator of $H'$ and takes $t_i$ into $H'$, since $H/H'$ is free abelian. Thus $d_i\mid b_i$ is required. If the divisibilities hold, the $t_i z^{-b_i/d_i}$ generate a finite central subgroup that injects into the abelianization: their orders are exactly $d_i$ and their images give the displayed direct sum. Quotienting it out leaves the derived infinite cyclic group unchanged and makes the abelianization free. Every sought map kills that subgroup because $H$ is torsion-free.

### Comparing the two integer alternating forms

For a class-two group with free abelianization and infinite cyclic derived group, lifts of any abelian basis and the derived generator give a presentation with exactly the commutator relations and centrality of the derived generator. Those relations produce collected forms. Uniqueness follows first in the abelianization and then in the infinite cyclic derived group. Thus the commutators give an integral alternating form on the free abelianization.

Let the remaining source form be $\beta_N$ of dimension $u$ and let the target form be $\beta_H$ of dimension $v$. A surjection induces an onto map $P\colon \mathbb Z^u\to\mathbb Z^v$ with $\beta_H(Px,Py)=\pm\beta_N(x,y)$. Since $P$ splits as a map of free abelian groups, this is equivalent to integral congruence of $\beta_N$ with $\beta_H\oplus 0_{u-v}$, up to sign. Conversely the congruence gives a surjection by sending each abelian generator to a lift of its image and the derived generator to the indicated generator. The images surject onto the abelianization and the derived group.

Integral alternating normal form has blocks $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&e\\-e&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, with positive $e$'s dividing one another, and a zero block. To obtain the form, use simultaneous integral basis operations to extract a pair of minimum positive pairing among attainable basis entries. Division with remainder in one of the two rows forces this entry to divide all entries in either row, allowing the two rows and columns to be cleared. If an entry in the remaining submatrix were not divisible by it, add its first basis vector to the paired vector. This retains the first pairing while placing the offending entry in that row, where another division produces a smaller positive entry. Thus the extracted entry divides the remaining matrix, and induction gives the normal form. The ordinary Smith form lists two copies of each $e$. A sign change gives the same congruence type. Compare the nonzero Smith factors and require $u\ge v$. Only polynomial-size lattice transformations, bilinear products, and binary powering in collected coordinates are needed. The algorithm decides existence without computing a congruence basis.
