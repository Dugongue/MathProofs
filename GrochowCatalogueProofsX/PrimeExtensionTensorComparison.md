# Exact tensor-isomorphism reductions between prime and extension fields

## GP-0275

Let $k=\mathbb F_p$ and $E=\mathbb F_{p^e}$ in an explicit polynomial-basis presentation $k[\theta]$. Then $\mathrm{3TI}_E$ reduces by a deterministic many-one map to $\mathrm{3TI}_k$, with bit time polynomial in the array size, $e$ and $\log p$, and with exact reflection of $E$-linear isomorphism. In the reverse direction, $\mathrm{3TI}_k$ reduces to $\mathrm{3TI}_E$ in polynomial bit time in the array size and $e$ for each fixed $p$. Consequently the two problems are equivalent uniformly in extension degree at fixed characteristic. Both maps admit a common intermediate description as systems of at most trilinear tensors of fixed signature. We use the explicit tensor-system-to-ordinary-three-tensor compiler of [Futorny, Grochow and Sergeichuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219).

## Restriction to the prime field

Regard the input array as a bilinear map $\beta:U\times V\to W$, using the two duals as its input modes. Restrict the three spaces to $k$, expand its structure constants in the polynomial basis, and attach three separately marked endomorphisms $m_\theta$, one on each of $U,V,W$, given by multiplication by $\theta$. An isomorphism of the typed system is a triple of invertible $k$-linear maps intertwining the endomorphisms and $\beta$. Intertwining $m_\theta$ implies intertwining multiplication by every polynomial in $\theta$ over $k$, hence by every element of $E$. Conversely, every $E$-linear isomorphism of the original bimap intertwines the displayed multiplication maps. Thus the typed isomorphisms are precisely the desired ones. Expanding $\beta$ on pairs of polynomial-basis coordinates multiplies each side dimension by $e$, and all multiplication tables use the input presentation and polynomially many base-field operations. The compiler uses fixed signature and field-independent dimension bounds. The result rules out extraneous Frobenius witnesses from bare restriction of scalars.

## Extension with an explicit Frobenius constraint

For the other direction, first extend the input to $E$. For each of its three spaces $U$, attach separately typed spaces $S^j U$ for $2\le j\le p$, with $S^1U=U$, and with bilinear maps $S^{j-1}U\times U\rightarrow S^jU$ given by multiplication in the symmetric algebra. In these auxiliary spaces, bases are the degree-$j$ monomials in the original $k$-basis $u_1,\ldots,u_n$, now interpreted over $E$. Also attach the $E$-linear map $F_U:U\to S^pU$ that sends $u_i$ to $u_i^p$. Use independent copies of this construction on the other modes.

An isomorphism with matrix $g$ on $U$ has uniquely determined induced maps $S^j g$ on the attached spaces. This follows inductively by evaluating the multiplication equation on products spanning $S^jU$. In characteristic $p$, $(\sum_i a_i u_i)^p=\sum_i a_i^p u_i^p$. Thus $S^p g\circ F_U=F_U\circ g$ says exactly that $g_{ij}^p=g_{ij}$ for all entries, since the pure basis powers are independent. This cuts out exactly the matrices over $k$. Invertibility over $E$ of a matrix with entries in $k$ is equivalent to invertibility over $k$. Every $k$-linear original isomorphism extends and satisfies all multiplication and Frobenius constraints. Conversely a typed isomorphism over $E$ restricts on the three original spaces to a $k$-linear bimap isomorphism, since its matrices lie in $k$ and both input bimaps have $k$-structure constants.

The largest auxiliary dimension is $\binom{n+p-1}{p}$. The signature contains $O(p)$ fixed types and entries are formed by monomial multiplication or equality. For fixed $p$, the whole typed system and the compiled ordinary array are polynomial in the input and field encoding, with no degree-dependent growth from $e$. This argument gives only the forward bound uniform in $\log p$; the uniform-in-$\log p$ reverse requirement is broader.

For comparison, scalar marks also occur in a different typed group reduction. The reverse reduction here uses a symmetric-algebra constraint on the coefficients of a transporter, rather than a marker for a chosen scalar generator.
