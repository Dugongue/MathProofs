# Quantum witness sizes at coherent multiplicity four

**GP-0965.** There is a polynomial-time, dimension-preserving reduction from binary linear operator equations to quantum isomorphism of ordinary uncolored graphs whose stable color-refinement classes have size at most four. Consequently every simple module of either graph's coherent adjacency algebra has multiplicity at most four, but there is no computable upper bound in the graph orders on the smallest finite matrix dimension of a witness, even restricted to pairs that have finite-dimensional witnesses. The reduction preserves the existence of a witness in each dimension separately.

## Binary systems with tests on at most three variables

A matrix solution specifies self-adjoint involutions $X_i\in M_d(\mathbb C)$, with commuting involutions within each scope $e$, and $\prod_{i\in e}X_i=(-1)^{b_e}I_d$. Slofstra proved that finite-dimensional solvability is undecidable via the perfect-strategy formulation. [Slofstra, The set of quantum correlations is not closed](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.08618)

For each scope with more than three variables, enforce commutation of every original pair $X_i,X_j$ in it by introducing a fresh involution $Y_{ij}$ and the triple equation $X_iX_jY_{ij}=I_d$ with within-scope commutation. Add the successive prefix-product equations of scope three. For $m$ variables in one scope use $T_j=X_1\cdots X_j$ for $2\le j\le m-2$, encoded by $X_1X_2T_2=I_d$, then $T_{j-1}X_jT_j=I_d$, and finish with $T_{m-2}X_{m-1}X_m=(-1)^{b_e}I_d$. All auxiliaries are fresh per scope or pair. On an original matrix solution these auxiliaries can be interpreted as commuting products inside the old scope, in the same dimension. Conversely the first batch restores all original within-scope commutations and the prefix equations give the original parity equation. Thus this step is polynomial and preserves each admissible dimension. Equations of arity one or two may be kept. Empty equations, if present, can be evaluated first.

## Colored assignment-incidence graphs

Create a color for each variable $i$, containing its two labeled bits $0,1$, and a distinct color for each equation $e$, containing the satisfying assignments to its scope. Join an assignment vertex $a$ to the variable vertex $a_i$ for each variable in its scope. Construct the source graph with all right sides zero and the target graph with the original right sides. Every variable class has size two and every equation class has size at most four. The assignment-incidence construction preserves dimensions as follows. [Atserias et al., Quantum and non-signalling graph isomorphisms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09837) · [Roberson and Schmidt, Solution group representations as quantum symmetries of graphs](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12664)

Write a color-preserving magic unitary in $M_d$ as blocks $U_i$ and $U_e$. The two-by-two block of variable $i$ has diagonal $P_i$ and off-diagonal $I_d-P_i$ for a projection $P_i$. The adjacency intertwining equation on an equation-variable block says
$$
\sum_{z\mid z_i=y}(U_e)_{a z}=(U_i)_{a_i y}.
$$
For any fixed source assignment $a$, the right sides for different $i$ are consequently sums from one row projection-valued measure. They commute. On the summand projection indexed by $z$, the involution $X_i=2P_i-I_d$ has sign $(-1)^{a_i+z_i}$. All $z$ have parity $b_e$, and $a$ has parity zero. Multiplying these commuting involutions on this row decomposition gives $\prod_{i\in e} X_i=(-1)^{b_e}I_d$.

Conversely, from a matrix solution let $P_i^0,P_i^1$ be the positive and negative spectral projections of $X_i$. On each equation block define $(U_e)_{a z}=\prod_{i\in e}P_i^{a_i+z_i\bmod 2}$. This is a projection by within-scope commutativity. Every nonzero joint eigenspace has signs of parity $b_e$. Varying $z$ for fixed $a$, or $a$ for fixed $z$, ranges over all these signs, so rows and columns sum to the identity and are orthogonal. The matrix $U_i$ has $(a_i,y)$-entry $P_i^{a_i+y\bmod 2}$. The required equality follows by summing joint spectral projections. Its reverse block equality follows by the corresponding column marginal. All other blocks are zero. This construction stays in the same matrix algebra.

## Removing the colors while bounding coherent multiplicities

Choose three positive lengths for each color, with all lengths distinct across all colors and slots. At each old vertex attach three disjoint pendant paths of its color's lengths. The choices can be the integers $1,\ldots,3c$ for $c$ colors. The resulting uncolored graph has polynomial order. The old vertices are exactly those of degree at least three. A colored magic unitary extends to the enlarged graph by using its old block on every corresponding path position. Each new edge then gives an identity matching between the corresponding two blocks, so the enlarged magic unitary intertwines adjacency in the same dimension.

For the converse and the cell bound, stable ordinary color refinement respects the core indicator and all closed walk-count refinements described next, in the sense that the quantities are constant on stable classes. Put $B$ equal to the adjacency matrix induced on noncore vertices. The degree-one leaf indicator lies in the stable partition, and the stable noncore cells are equitable for $B$. For an old vertex $v$, count walks of length $k$ that enter a noncore neighbor, stay among noncore vertices, and end at a leaf. This is obtained by applying $B^{k-1}$ to the leaf indicator, then summing over the noncore neighbors of $v$. A path of length $L$ contributes zero for $k<L$, contributes one at $k=L$, and has a contribution depending only on $L,k$ thereafter. Induction on $k$ therefore recovers the multiset of pendant lengths at every core vertex from stable colors. This refines the old color classes. For a noncore vertex, the least walk length to a leaf staying among noncore vertices, and the least walk length to each refined core color avoiding the core until the last step, are also constant on stable cells. In its pendant path these recover the core color, length and position. Each such class therefore has at most the number of vertices of its original core color, which is at most four.

Any magic unitary intertwining adjacency preserves every refinement class shared across the two inputs. Indeed it intertwines the all-ones vectors, hence the degree vectors, and the same holds for the neighbor-count vectors of every preserved class. Multiplying a fixed-vector equation by an appropriate projection from its row shows that a nonzero entry can only match equal values. This induction applies to the refinement of the disjoint union with common initial colors. Thus, in the enlarged graphs, restriction to the old vertices gives a color-preserving magic unitary for the assignment-incidence inputs. Equality of dimensions holds in both directions.

The coherent adjacency algebra is the unital matrix algebra generated from adjacency and the all-ones matrix using ordinary multiplication, entrywise multiplication and adjoints. It contains the diagonal projection of every stable color-refinement class. For example, applying adjacency to a class indicator gives neighbor counts, and entrywise polynomials extract indicators of each distinct count, after multiplying entrywise by the identity to keep the diagonal. The same operation starts from the degree vector and iterates. In a unitary decomposition of its representation as a sum of simple modules with multiplicities $m_j$, every projection in the algebra has rank $\sum_j m_j r_j$ for nonnegative integers $r_j$. For each simple summand some stable class projection is nonzero on that summand, since the class projections sum to the identity. Its rank is at most four and at least $m_j$, proving $m_j\le 4$. If the coherent algebra is instead computed on the disjoint union of the pair, each corresponding tag cell contains at most eight vertices and the same argument gives multiplicity at most eight.

If a computable upper bound on finite-witness dimension existed in this class, one could evaluate it on the two graph orders and check each dimension up to that bound. A fixed dimension admits an exact decision by real quantifier elimination on the real and imaginary parts of the matrix entries, using the projection, orthogonality, row sum, column sum and adjacency intertwining equations. This would decide finite-dimensional solvability of every input binary system. Finite-dimensional tracial-algebra witnesses give the same existence criterion, since projection to a simple matrix summand preserves the equations. The asserted bound therefore cannot exist.
