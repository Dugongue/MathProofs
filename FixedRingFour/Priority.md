# Literature and priority check

## The existing fixed-ring theorem

The project proof gives deterministic polynomial-time many-one equivalence for ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over any two fixed nonzero finite commutative rings with the same characteristic prime support. It treats explicitly presented modules, including nonfree ones, and a reduction to free tensors. In particular a fixed $\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb F_p$ have the same decision problem under these reductions. The statement is a positive equivalence for equal prime support. Its complexity constants may depend on the fixed rings. [Pinned project proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/53ffa5dcacaca5757e11adca8df99ba0d38f8bc5/PrimePowerTensorIsomorphism/PROOF.md)

The original proof first makes a bounded-degree augmentation quotient of the modular group algebra of each additive module. The equation $\Delta(g)=g\otimes g$ in a total-degree quotient, together with augmentation one, recovers exactly the additive group elements. Biadditive maps extend to maps on the quotients and retain their original values on these elements. This converts a tensor transporter to an isomorphism of a bounded-signature linear structure over the prime field. The existing structure compiler produces ordinary three-tensor isomorphism. Both the group algebra and the tensor product before quotienting can be avoided computationally by using truncated monomials throughout.

## Closest sources inspected

- **Grochow, Qiao, Stange and Sun, Part V, Theorem 1.6.** The theorem interreduces five actions with the coefficient ring $\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$ held the same within each reduction. Among the consequences are equivalences for free and general modules, and reductions to associative or Lie multiplication tensors under the relevant action. Section 5.3 has unrestricted odd-residue local principal-ideal algorithms for a matrix-tuple isometry subproblem. These results do not state the prime-power-to-prime-field reduction for ordinary three-tensor isomorphism. The relevant line of work was already published at STOC 2025. [Part V](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S1.Thmtheorem6), [STOC proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1145/3717823.3718286)
- **Passi and the dimension-subgroup literature.** The use of augmentation powers and embeddings into their quotients is classical. The fixed-ring proof uses the truncated diagonal coproduct to characterize the original group elements, and marks the linearized values of the input's biadditive maps. The latter steps supply the orbit-condition equivalence needed here. [Passi's monograph](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/BFb0067186)
- **Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk and Grochow–Qiao.** The compiler for tensors in linked vector spaces and its basis-explicit bounded-arity formulation are prior work. Applied to the linearized data they supply the last step of the fixed-ring proof. Their source structures are linear over the target field, so they require a separate construction that can recover the input mixed-characteristic arithmetic. [Wildness for tensors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219), [Grochow–Qiao Theorem 10.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S10.SS1)
- **Prior project results.** Same-characteristic finite-field cases and presentational reductions over the same ring overlap with parts of the theorem. In the other direction an elementary socle embedding already provides field-to-ring hardness. The upper reduction to the residue characteristic is the point for which priority matters. Recent project work also proves prescribed dyadic free lifts using the displacement-and-subgroup algorithm. The extensions here should be credited accordingly. [Same-characteristic fields](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/FixedCharacteristicTensorIsomorphism), [Presented modules at variable order](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowCatalogueProofsVI/PresentedTensorOrder.md), [Prescribed dyadic transport](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/cb95f7fe15deb558cb5014ca2227736554246543/GrochowCatalogueProofsVII/DyadicPrescribedResidueTransport.md)

The sources inspected establish the ingredients and neighboring results. This search did not locate a statement or documented earlier proof of the full fixed-ring coefficient-change theorem, including $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\to\mathbb F_2$ for ordinary tensor isomorphism. This is a bounded literature finding and priority remains open. The four extensions link their more specific predecessors inside their own proofs.

## Audit of the coefficient-change argument

The group-like recovery works over the prime field. If a purported group-like element starts in total degree $d$, its first nonzero piece is primitive for the additive coproduct. A mixed-variable monomial makes a unique mixed tensor monomial, and a single-variable exponent with at least two nonzero base-$p$ summands has a nonzero mixed binomial coefficient. Thus the leading part is a linear combination of powers $x_i^{p^j}$, with coefficients in $\mathbb F_p$, and can be canceled by genuine group elements in that augmentation degree. Distinct base-$p$ digits separate group elements up to the chosen cutoff $p^{e-1}$. For biadditive maps the separate augmentation preservation in each input proves descent by spanning with group basis symbols, so the construction works also for nonfree modules. Marking the actions of the fixed scalars enforces full module linearity. Primary decomposition and zero padding then combine the prime components. The upper reduction's monomial count depends on $p^{e-1}$ in the degree, so it gives no polynomial bound uniform in the input modulus. The logarithmic and congruence algorithms in this package instead have specific shallower transport data as part of their algorithms.
