# Prescribed-residue tensor transporters over finite local rings

**Theorem.** Let $R$ be any nonzero finite commutative local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ and residue field $k$. Give $R$ in additive cyclic coordinates with the identity and products of additive generators. Present finitely generated $R$-modules $M_i,N_i$ by matrices and give
$$
t\in M_1\otimes_R\cdots\otimes_R M_d,\qquad
s\in N_1\otimes_R\cdots\otimes_R N_d
$$
by dense coefficient arrays on products of presentation generators. Supply $k$-linear maps $\bar h_i:M_i/\mathfrak m M_i\to N_i/\mathfrak m N_i$. There is a deterministic polynomial bit-time algorithm that decides whether there are $R$-isomorphisms $g_i$ inducing the prescribed $\bar h_i$ and satisfying $(\bigotimes_i g_i)t=s$. On success it returns a witness, generators for the congruence stabilizer whose coset consists of all witnesses, and the exact number of witnesses. On any subset of modes supplied with free bases, prescribed unit determinants can be imposed with the same guarantees. The bound is polynomial in the complete input encoding, with variable $R$, residue characteristic, nilpotence index and tensor order.

A second consequence is a polynomial-time canonical representative of a tensor's orbit under the product of the mode congruence kernels, relative to fixed presentations of its ambient modules. Thus all the work within a specified residue fiber admits a deterministic algorithm at arbitrary precision.

This resolves a different part of ring tensor isomorphism from the decision reductions for fixed finite rings in the [PrimePowerTensorIsomorphism proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/PrimePowerTensorIsomorphism/PROOF.md) and [PresentedTensorOrder](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowCatalogueProofsVI/PresentedTensorOrder.md). A field-level transporter specifies the residue maps for an invocation of this theorem. The complexity of identifying which residue maps have lifts is outside its scope.

## Linear arithmetic on the input

Write $e$ for the first positive integer with $\mathfrak m^e=0$ and $p=\operatorname{char}k$. The descending powers of $\mathfrak m$ strictly decrease until zero by Nakayama's lemma, so $e\le \log_2|R|$. Since $p1_R\in\mathfrak m$, the additive order of $1_R$ is a power of $p$. Find $p$ by testing exact integer roots of that order and using the largest exponent with an integer root, where exponent one is allowed. Set $A=R/pR$. This is an $\mathbb F_p$-algebra and its nilradical is $\mathfrak m/pR$. The kernel of the $\mathbb F_p$-linear map $x\mapsto x^{p^v}$ on $A$ is exactly that nilradical as soon as $p^v\ge \lceil\log_2|R|\rceil$. Compute the map on an $\mathbb F_p$-basis by binary powering and lift its kernel to recover $\mathfrak m$. Products of additive generating sets give the successive ideal powers. This uses polynomial time even when $p$ is large in comparison with the dimensions.

An $R$-module presentation converts to an integer presentation of its additive group by using the additive generators of $R$ on every presentation generator and imposing the additive relations of $R$ and additive-generator multiples of each module relation. An $R$-linear map is specified by the images of the original module generators. Finding such maps with image constraints in additive subgroups, or with prescribed residue values, reduces to linear equations in finite abelian groups. For example, a linear system in a quotient $\mathbb Z^a/L$ is an integer system with auxiliary variables for the columns of a basis of $L$. Smith or Hermite normal forms, with transformation matrices, give particular solutions, generator images for homogeneous solutions, coordinates of quotients and exact orders. These operations are polynomial in bit size. Composition, equality and application of maps use their values on presentation generators. [Kannan and Bachem, polynomial-time Smith and Hermite normal forms](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040).

Check the residual maps for invertibility and the paired modules for equality of additive order. Solve the linear systems for $R$-linear lifts $h_i$ of $\bar h_i$. If a system has no solution, reject. Each chosen lift is surjective since its cokernel $C$ satisfies $C/\mathfrak m C=0$ and thus $C=\mathfrak m^e C=0$. Equality of orders makes it an isomorphism. Every other lift is uniquely $u_i h_i$, where $u_i$ acts trivially on $N_i/\mathfrak m N_i$. Define $P=\bigotimes_i N_i$. Present $P$ using all products of mode generators and the relations obtained by tensoring each mode relation with generator choices in the other modes. The dense input bounds the size of this presentation and the work of applying mode maps. If a mode has no presentation generators, $P=0$ and the tensor test is vacuous.

## Computing in the congruence kernels

For $r\ge 1$ put
$$
J_{i,r}=\{a\in\operatorname{End}_R(N_i):a(N_i)\subseteq\mathfrak m^r N_i\},
\qquad U_r=\prod_i (1+J_{i,r}).
$$
The congruence group is $U=U_1$. Since $J_{i,r}J_{i,s}\subseteq J_{i,r+s}$, the inverse of $1+a$ is given by the finite geometric sum. Multiplication yields an isomorphism from $U_r/U_{r+1}$ to the additive group $\prod_i J_{i,r}/J_{i,r+1}$. The latter is an $\mathbb F_p$-space because $p\in\mathfrak m$. The same multiplication identity gives $[U,U_r]\subseteq U_{r+1}$. Compute these additive spaces and their coordinates by the linear method above. Flags from ordered bases of the spaces refine the filtration to a series $G_0=U>G_1>\cdots>G_L=1$ with central quotients of order $p$. Group elements retain their mode-map representations. For an element in $G_j$, its coordinate in $G_j/G_{j+1}$ is computable. Lift each one-coordinate basis vector to obtain generators of $U$. The number $L$ is bounded by the sum of the logarithms to base $p$ of the orders of the generator-image spaces $N_i^{n_i}$, with $n_i$ the number of generators of $N_i$.

Here are two operations on a subgroup $B\le U$ given by generators. The details ensure that polynomial time depends on $\log p$.

1. **Membership and order.** Keep at most one pivot $b_j\in G_j\setminus G_{j+1}$ of coordinate $1$ per position. To sift an element of $G_j$ whose coordinate is $\lambda$, left-multiply by $b_j^{-\lambda}$ when the pivot exists. If it is missing and $\lambda\ne 0$, insert the residual element to the power $\lambda^{-1}\bmod p$. This has coordinate $1$. Repeatedly sift the input generators, the $p$-th powers of the pivots, and their pairwise commutators, restarting after an insertion. Every inserted pivot is in $B$. There are at most $L$ insertions. When no change occurs, the pivots give unique expressions as products in increasing position order with exponents in $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$. Indeed, by downward induction the pivots strictly below a given head in the series generate a tail subgroup. Each commutator of the head with a tail pivot lies deeper than both and sifts using only tail pivots, so the head normalizes the tail. Its $p$-th power belongs to the tail. This proves that adding the head yields $p$ cosets of the tail, disjoint by their head coordinate. Consequently the ordered products form a subgroup containing the input generators, and a successful sift detects membership. The order is $p^{\#\mathrm{pivots}}$.

2. **Kernels of linear characters.** Let $\theta:B\to \mathbb F_p$ be an evaluable homomorphism. If $\theta$ is zero on the generators, return $B$. Otherwise use a suitable power of a generator to choose $a\in B$ with $\theta(a)=1$. Initialize a subgroup $D$ with generators $a^p$ and $a^{-\theta(b)} b$ for each generator $b$ of $B$. All these are in $\ker\theta$. Collect a pivot basis for $D$, and adjoin conjugates of its pivots by $a$ until $aDa^{-1}=D$. There are at most $L$ strict growth rounds, and each round can use operation 1. At termination $D$ is normal in $B=\langle a,D\rangle$. The quotient is generated by the image of $a$ of order $p$, since $a^p\in D$ and $\theta(a)=1$. Thus $D=\ker\theta$. For a homomorphism to $\mathbb F_p^q$, process one coordinate at a time on the current kernel.

All powers in these operations are computed by binary powering. In particular the second operation uses a normal-closure calculation inside the explicitly coordinated finite $p$-group. This gives a bound polynomial in $\log p$ even when an index-$p$ transversal would be exponentially long in the input. The group operations build on standard polycyclic collection and subgroup-chain methods. [Luks, Computing in Quotient Groups](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/quotient.pdf).

## Tensor lifting and exact counts

Set $x=(\bigotimes_i h_i)t$. Compare $x$ and $s$ modulo $\mathfrak m P$ and reject if they differ, since $U$ acts trivially on that quotient. After matching $x$ to $s$ modulo $\mathfrak m^jP$, maintain generators for
$$
B_j=\{a\in U: ax-x\in\mathfrak m^jP\}.
$$
Initially $B_1=U$. For $j<e$, the next displacement
$$
\theta_j:B_j\longrightarrow\mathfrak m^jP/\mathfrak m^{j+1}P,\qquad
a\longmapsto ax-x\bmod\mathfrak m^{j+1}P
$$
is a homomorphism to an elementary abelian $p$-group. Indeed $(a-1)P\subseteq\mathfrak m P$ for $a\in U$, by expanding the tensor product of the mode maps. By $R$-linearity this makes the action trivial on every graded layer. In $abx-x=a(bx-x)+(ax-x)$, the first summand therefore reduces to $bx-x$ in the indicated layer.

The image is the $\mathbb F_p$-span of the evaluations on current generators. Solve the linear system for $s-x$ in that image. If it has no solution, reject. Otherwise the coefficients specify a product of generator powers $a_j\in B_j$ with the desired displacement. Replace $x$ by $a_jx$ and record the multiplication in the witness. The stabilizer of the new $x$ modulo $\mathfrak m^{j+1}P$ is $a_j\ker(\theta_j)a_j^{-1}=\ker(\theta_j)$, by normality in $B_j$. Compute its generators by operation 2. Any full solution from the current $x$ matching $s$ modulo $\mathfrak m^jP$ must lie in $B_j$. This proves the rejection rule and the induction after an arbitrary successful correction. It also shows why lifting a correction and forgetting the other corrections at that digit could lose valid witnesses.

At $j=e$, the tracked mode maps $g_i$ are a witness and $B_e=\operatorname{Stab}_U(s)$. All witnesses with the prescribed residues form the left coset $B_e g$, since any pair differ by a unique congruence stabilizer element on the target side. The count is the order obtained from operation 1, with zero returned upon rejection. The bounds on $L,e$, the module presentation sizes, and the number of displacement coordinates bound every solve and collection step by a polynomial. The count has polynomial bit length.

## Determinants and congruence canonicalization

On the chosen free modes, put $\delta(u)=(\det u_i)_i$ for $u\in U$. Determinants can be computed over $R$ by a division-free characteristic polynomial algorithm. [Berkowitz, On computing the determinant in small parallel time](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(84)90018-8). A prescribed determinant tuple $d$ first requires $d\delta(g)^{-1}$ to reduce to $1$ in every listed mode. In the $\delta(g)$ notation the free bases identify source and target determinants for the known isomorphism $g$.

Given the tensor witness and $B_e$, seek $u\in B_e$ with $\delta(u)=d\delta(g)^{-1}$. The group $(1+\mathfrak m)^f$, with $f$ the number of selected modes, has successive layers $(1+\mathfrak m^j)^f/(1+\mathfrak m^{j+1})^f\cong(\mathfrak m^j/\mathfrak m^{j+1})^f$. Match the remaining discrepancy at each layer by solving the linear image equation on generators of the current subgroup. Restrict to its kernel using operation 2 and update the witness as above. At the end the remaining subgroup is the determinant-one congruence stabilizer. This gives the exact set of lifts and count with literal determinant constraints, including all-mode determinant one when all modes are free.

For canonicalization choose once, from the fixed presentations, ordered $\mathbb F_p$-bases of the graded layers of $P$ and a representative lift of each element of $P/\mathfrak m^jP$ to $P/\mathfrak m^{j+1}P$. Additive Smith coordinates give such lifts without listing the quotient. Start from the residue tensor, which is fixed by $U$. Once a digit prefix is canonical, the set of possible next coordinates over that prefix is a single affine coset of $\operatorname{im}\theta_j$, because two tensors in the same orbit with that prefix are related by its stabilizer. Choose the lexicographically least vector of the coset using Gaussian elimination with the fixed coordinate order and $0<1<\cdots<p-1$. At a position the remaining possibilities are either all of $\mathbb F_p$, when one can choose zero, or a single forced value. Determine and apply a correction by the same image solve, then update the stabilizer. Induction on digits gives the same final tensor for every input in a congruence orbit. A recorded sequence of corrections puts the input in that form, so equality of canonical outputs also certifies congruence equivalence.

## Scope and comparisons

Grochow, Qiao, Stange and Sun study equivalence among isomorphism problems for free and general modules, as well as unrestricted tuple isometry over odd-residue finite local principal ideal rings. Their tuple isometry algorithm exploits adjoint algebras, radical decomposition and a semisimple isometry algorithm. The prescribed-residue problem here permits independent tensor mode maps, arbitrary presented modules and unrestricted local-ring structure. The related proof for [orthogonal tensor isomorphism over dyadic Galois rings](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/cb95f7fe15deb558cb5014ca2227736554246543/Problem18FurtherExtensions/DyadicPrecisionOrthogonalTensorIsomorphism.md) gets residue information from specifically recognizable classical-group tensors and uses the structure of the remaining middle isometry equations. The present congruence method imposes no density or rank condition and requires the residue tuple as input. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Part V, §§3.4 and 5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1).

The direct predecessor is the free dyadic prescribed-residue theorem, which already gives the displacement and pivot argument for tensors with accompanying quadratic or bilinear forms. The extensions here are arbitrary presented modules, nonprincipal local rings, uniform polynomial complexity in $\log p$ through the normal-closure step, explicit determinant constraints and canonicalization of congruence orbits. The parallel principal-congruence result supplies a related full-group normal form over a fixed prime field with a supplied adapted basis. The present subgroup compression handles subgroups arising from arbitrary successive displacement kernels in both equal and mixed characteristic. Priority for these extensions remains unverified. [Dyadic prescribed-residue lifting](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/cb95f7fe15deb558cb5014ca2227736554246543/GrochowCatalogueProofsVII/DyadicPrescribedResidueTransport.md) · [Parallel principal-congruence normal forms](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/cb95f7fe15deb558cb5014ca2227736554246543/GrochowCatalogueProofsVIII/PrincipalCongruenceParallelWords.md).

As a nonfree example, take $R=\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z$, $N_1=R\oplus R/4R$ and $N_2=N_3=R$. Prescribe identity residue maps. The first congruence mode consists of maps
$$
(x,y)\mapsto (ax+by\bmod 8,\;cx+dy\bmod 4),
\quad a,d\text{ odd},\quad b,c\text{ even}.
$$
There are $64$ first-mode maps and four scalar choices in each remaining mode. The tensor $t=(2,2)$ has two images, $(2,2)$ and $(6,2)$, each with $512$ witnesses. Restricting the two scalar determinants to $1$ leaves $32$ witnesses to $(6,2)$. The target $(2,0)$ has equal residue and no witness. If one prescribes the residue map that swaps the two generators in mode one, no module lift exists because the image of a generator killed by $4$ cannot have odd first coordinate.

The accompanying finite control applies the collection and lifting algorithm and checks 2,048 exact lift/count comparisons against enumeration, covering all source–target pairs in the example with and without the two scalar determinant constraints.
