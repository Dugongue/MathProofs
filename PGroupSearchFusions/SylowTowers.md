# Search and exact counting from decision for finite groups with a normal Sylow tower

**Theorem.** Given the Cayley tables of two finite groups with normal Sylow towers, deterministic polynomial time and group-isomorphism decision suffice to construct an isomorphism or certify its absence, compute the exact number of isomorphisms, and generate the full automorphism group. All decision queries stay inside the class of groups with a normal Sylow tower, preserve the set of primes in the input order, and have polynomial order with an absolute exponent. The tower height and the primes can vary with the input. An input can be recognized and its tower constructed from the table.

This extends the [same-prime search and counting theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GroupIsomorphismAndEpimorphisms/AllPGroupsSearchCounting.md) to nonnilpotent inputs. It answers the normal-Sylow-tower cases of the search and counting questions of Arvind and Torán. The proof is informal. [Arvind–Torán, Problem 16](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.190/Mitarbeiter/toran/beatcs/column86.pdf) · [Grochow–Qiao, Part IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317v2)

## 1. The chosen tower and its automorphisms

A normal Sylow tower means a normal series whose successive factors are full prime-power parts of the group order, with distinct primes. Equivalently, one repeatedly removes a normal Sylow subgroup in the current quotient. A normal Sylow subgroup for a given prime is characteristic. In a table one checks whether the elements of prime-power order for that prime form a subgroup of the required order. Trial division of the group order has polynomial cost in the table length. At each stage choose the least prime with a normal Sylow subgroup. If a tower exists, quotienting by any normal Sylow subgroup \(P\) preserves this property: take the images of the terms of a tower in the quotient. Terms predating the prime of \(P\) have intersection one with \(P\), and all later terms contain \(P\). The quotient series drops just that factor. Thus the procedure is deterministic and isomorphism invariant.

Write the first step as \(G=P\rtimes C\), where \(P\) is the selected normal Sylow \(p\)-subgroup and \(C\) is a chosen complement. A complement exists and all complements are conjugate by \(P\), by Schur–Zassenhaus. It can be found in polynomial time directly from the table. If \(P\ne1\), take the characteristic nonzero elementary abelian subgroup of elements of order dividing \(p\) in \(Z(P)\). Recurse in the quotient by that subgroup. The preimage of the resulting complement is an extension of a \(p'\)-group by this elementary abelian subgroup. A section's multiplication gives a cocycle, and its adjustment to a homomorphism is a linear system over \(\mathbb F_p\). The system is consistent by Schur–Zassenhaus applied to that extension, or by averaging the cocycle. Explicit construction of all quotients, sections, and equations is polynomial in the table size. After at most \(\log_2|P|\) divisions the kernel is trivial. Apply the same procedure recursively to \(C\), using the chosen prime order from the quotient tower. This chooses nested complements.

An isomorphism is called tower-preserving here if it takes every chosen complement to its target counterpart, successively inside the preceding one. It then restricts on the prime factors to compatible maps of the semidirect products. Given any isomorphism, inner conjugations can align the complements in order by Schur–Zassenhaus, choosing conjugating elements in each successive target subgroup so earlier alignments persist. Hence a tower-preserving isomorphism exists exactly when an unrestricted one does. In particular, adjoining inner automorphisms to all tower-preserving automorphisms generates the full automorphism group.

For a split step \(P\rtimes C\) let \(E=P/\Phi(P)\). The complement acts on \(E\) and its dual. As its order is prime to \(p\), the dual is a direct sum of simple \(\mathbb F_p[C]\)-modules. A marker list consists of prescribed independent simple submodules of this dual, in order. Every simple module is generated by each nonzero vector it contains. One can therefore list all the simple submodules by taking the orbit span of each vector and checking simplicity. In detail, a nonzero span is simple exactly when each of its nonzero vectors generates it. Each enumeration has at most \(|E|\le |P|\) elements, all orbits can be obtained by table scans, and rank and duplicate tests use elimination. Thus even this direct method costs polynomial time in the Cayley-table model. One can also obtain a complete direct-sum list by successively adding a simple module disjoint from the sum so far. Such a module exists whenever necessary by semisimplicity. We use the inverse dual map for the direction of transport of the marked submodules.

## 2. Simultaneous markers on a whole tower

First give a marker on the \(P\)-factor of one split step. Let its ordered simple submodules be \(U_1,\ldots,U_t\subseteq E^*\), in direct sum. Use the [rigid bipartite graph from the same-prime marker](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GroupIsomorphismAndEpimorphisms/SamePrimePGroupMarkers.md). Its left vertices are the \(t\) mark positions, its right vertices are \(J\), and its neighborhoods \(N(i)\subseteq J\) are nonempty and pairwise incomparable. Every automorphism preserving the parts fixes every left vertex. Also \(|J|\le4t\) and each left degree is at most six. The constructions in the cited marker give these bounds: the degree-two right vertices encode a three-branched tree with distinct branch lengths for \(t\ge7\), and for \(t\le6\) disjoint stars of different degrees suffice. Private right neighbors give incomparability in the tree construction.

Take \(A=\mathbb F_p^J\) with trivial complement action and \(D=\bigoplus_{(i,j)\text{ edge}} U_i^*\), with the induced complement action. Let \(\ell_i:E\to U_i^*\) be restriction of evaluation and put
\[
M(v,a)_{ij}=a_j\ell_i(v).
\]
Independence of the \(U_i\) makes the combined restriction map onto, so the values of \(M\) span all of \(D\). The pairing is \(C\)-equivariant. Let \(A'\) be a separate copy of \(A\), with chosen identification \(\iota:A\to A'\), and let \(B=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z\), where \(e=2+\log_p|(A\oplus D\oplus A')\rtimes P|\). Form the old enlarged \(p\)-group \(\widehat P\) with coordinates \((a,d,a',x,b)\), with product
\[
(a,d,a',x,b)(\tilde a,\tilde d,\tilde a',\tilde x,\tilde b)
=(a+\tilde a,d+\tilde d+M(\bar x,\tilde a),a'+\tilde a'+(b\bmod p)\iota(\tilde a),x\tilde x,b+\tilde b).
\]
There is a split projection to \(P\), and the complement acts naturally on \(P,D\) and trivially on \(A,A',B\), giving automorphisms by equivariance. An empty marker can simply use \(\widehat P=P\). The enlarged order is polynomial with a fixed exponent: the two right-coordinate spaces have dimension at most \(4\dim E\) each, and the edge spaces together have dimension at most \(6\dim E\). Before adjoining \(B\) the order is at most \(|P|^{15}\), and after adjoining it is at most \(|P|^{32}\).

We recall why the original group and pairing can be recovered from an isomorphism between these enlargements. Inside \(\widehat P\), the elements satisfying \(y^{p^{e-1}}=1\) form the characteristic subgroup \(T\) with \(b\in pB\). The maximum element order is \(p^e\). The subgroup \(U=\{y\in T:[y,\widehat P]\subseteq[T,T]\}\) consists precisely of \(a=0\) in \(T\), because the added cyclic generator records \(a\) in the independent \(A'\)-coordinate. Consequently \(U=P\times D\times A'\times pB\), and \(\Phi(\widehat P)=\Phi(P)\times D\times A'\times pB\) by the powers-and-commutators formula. The intrinsic commutator on \(U/\Phi(\widehat P)\times T/U\), with values in \([U,T]/[U,U]\), is exactly \(M\), since the \(D\)-outputs span and the \([P,P]\)-part is divided out. These are the original marker's calculations with the same product formula. Restrict any enlargement isomorphism to the original \(P\) and project to the original target group from its counterpart of \(U\). This is a homomorphism inducing the recovered invertible Frattini map. It is onto by the Frattini property and hence an isomorphism since the original group orders agree. All projections used here commute with the actions of the chosen complements.

The bilinear pairing forces the labeled **spaces** \(U_i\), as follows. Its dual image in \(E^*\otimes A^*\) is \(S=\bigoplus_i U_i\otimes W_{N(i)}\), where \(W_{N(i)}\) is the corresponding right coordinate subspace. Its first support is \(E_0^*=\bigoplus_i U_i\). The algebra of all \(X\in\operatorname{End}(E_0^*)\) with \((X\otimes I)S\subseteq S\) is exactly \(\prod_i\operatorname{End}(U_i)\). Indeed a nonzero block from \(U_i\) to \(U_h\) requires \(W_{N(i)}\subseteq W_{N(h)}\), impossible for distinct indexes by incomparability. The primitive central idempotents of this algebra intrinsically identify the summands as a set. A pairing isomorphism thus permutes their labels and carries the corresponding right neighborhood subspaces to each other. A permutation selected by a nonzero determinant term in the invertible right matrix carries each neighborhood into its matching one. The neighborhood dimensions agree, so it carries each neighborhood onto its match and gives a part-preserving graph automorphism together with the left permutation. The left permutation is therefore the identity. Conversely a map taking each marked submodule to its counterpart extends to a pairing isomorphism with the identity on \(A\): on each edge use the unique induced map on \(U_i^*\). If the original maps on \(P\) intertwine the two complement actions via a quotient isomorphism, these maps on \(D\) intertwine those actions as well. This gives an extension isomorphism of the enlarged groups commuting with their split projections.

Recursively mark the complement, obtaining \(\widehat C\) with split projection onto \(C\), and form \(\widehat G=\widehat P\rtimes\widehat C\), where \(\widehat C\) acts through that projection. Repeat at all depths. The query group still has a normal Sylow tower, with the same ordered primes in that exhibited tower. Its order is at most the product of the original Sylow orders to the power \(32\), hence at most \(|G|^{32}\), independently of the tower height. Construct its table by the explicit product formulas.

The resulting unmarked query has an exact meaning: its answer is YES exactly when there is a tower-preserving isomorphism satisfying all input marker lists. For reflection, an isomorphism of the enlarged groups carries their normal Sylow subgroups to each other. Conjugate in the target to match the displayed complements and then the remaining nested complements. At the top step, any further inner conjugations within the aligned \(\widehat C\) act on every marked submodule through \(C\), so they preserve each one. The analogous statement holds recursively. Restrict to the original prime factors and project as above. The complement restriction, followed by projection from the marker group, gives the recursively extracted complement isomorphism. Since its action on the enlarged \(P\)-group factors through the projection, the recovered map on \(P\) intertwines the original complement actions. The split projections composed with the extracted maps therefore give a tower-preserving isomorphism of the original groups with all prescribed marks. For preservation, lift a marked original isomorphism at each level by the explicit pairing extension, leaving the artificial right, copy and cyclic coordinates unchanged. These extensions commute with the projections, so they assemble into an enlargement isomorphism. If the tower primes, corresponding dimensions or list lengths fail to match, the relevant comparison is immediately negative.

## 3. Completing all the marks by a polynomial-time lift

Suppose each mark list is now a complete ordered decomposition of the dual Frattini space into simple complement modules. We compute one tower-preserving isomorphism that preserves these listed spaces and generators of the full marked automorphism group of the target, whenever the set is nonempty. This marked automorphism group is solvable. The algorithm is recursive on the chosen tower. We use the deterministic vector and subspace transporter for solvable permutation groups with an explicit linear representation, a special case of Luks–Miyazaki Proposition 3.17, Problems 20 and 22. Its output is a coset given by a representative and subgroup generators, and its cost is polynomial in the permutation degree, linear dimension and field cardinality. Here the permutation domains, dimensions, and prime field sizes are polynomial in the group order. [Luks–Miyazaki, Polynomial-time normalizers](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/531)

Apply the recursive algorithm first to the two complements, retaining a representative and generators of the target's marked automorphism group. The trivial complement is immediate. At the current step put on each element of either complement the vector of traces of its action on the ordered simple summands. A permissible complement isomorphism must match these trace strings. Conversely matching the strings implies that the ordered pairs of simple modules are isomorphic after pulling back along the chosen complement isomorphism. Here is the reason over \(\mathbb F_p\). The endomorphisms of a simple module form a finite division ring, hence a finite field, and that endomorphism algebra commutes with base change by its description as solutions of linear equations. Over an algebraic closure the module is semisimple and its commuting algebra is commutative, so each absolutely simple summand has multiplicity zero or one. Equal traces on all group elements give equal linear trace functionals on the split semisimple group algebra. In each full matrix factor evaluate on a single diagonal matrix unit. Its trace is exactly the multiplicity zero or one, which works even when the dimension of a module is divisible by \(p\). Thus the absolute modules agree. The Hom equations over the base field then have a nonzero solution since base change preserves their dimension, and a nonzero homomorphism of simple modules is invertible.

Transport the trace string within the marked complement coset by applying the vector transporter to the permutation action on the complement's elements, tensored with the fixed coordinate space of trace vectors. The input and target colors lie in \(\mathbb F_p\). This gives the allowed complement coset and generators of the corresponding trace stabilizer. For each generator and the representative, solve the simple-module Hom equations to obtain a block-preserving compatible Frattini map, using inverse duality to express it on \(E\). The pairs consisting of a complement automorphism and a compatible map on \(E\) form a group above the trace stabilizer. Its kernel consists of the units of the endomorphism field in each prescribed simple block, so is abelian. Generate it by finding the endomorphisms by linear equations and enumerating them in each block: the number of endomorphisms of a simple block is bounded by the cardinality of that block because evaluation at any nonzero vector is injective. The resulting solvable group of pairs, and the representative isomorphism pair, are thus explicit. They describe exactly the maps at the Frattini quotient level. Each pair acts faithfully by disjoint union of its permutations of the complement and of the elementary abelian quotient, with degree at most \(2|G|\).

Lift the first component through the characteristic lower exponent-\(p\) central series of \(P\). Use the [old central extension lifting method](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GroupIsomorphismAndEpimorphisms/AllPGroupsSearchCounting.md#3-lifting-a-specified-frattini-quotient-isomorphism), carrying the complement isomorphism as an additional component and imposing only its already checked compatibility at the Frattini level during these steps. At each step the extension is central with an elementary abelian kernel \(A\) in the Frattini subgroup, and contributes an embedded subspace of \(H^2(Q,\mathbb F_p)\), dual to \(A\), where \(Q\) is the preceding quotient. This embedding is the map taking a functional on \(A\) to the scalar component of the extension cocycle. If a nonzero functional mapped to zero, quotienting by its kernel would split a central extension by a group of order \(p\), contradicting Frattini containment. The cohomology space and the subspace are computed from the table cocycle equations by elimination. A quotient isomorphism lifts exactly when its pullback matches the two subspaces. Equality determines the kernel map, and the remaining lift is a linear coboundary solve.

At each stage the candidate target group of pairs acts linearly on the cohomology space through pullback of the first component. It is solvable and given as explicit permutations on the two table factors. The subspace transporter therefore finds the required matching coset, giving a representative and subgroup generators, or certifies failure. Lift each such generator and the representative by the coboundary solve, keeping the complement map. Adjoin the generators for the kernel \(\operatorname{Hom}(Q,A)\) of lift choice, which again are obtained by linear equations. This extends a solvable group by a \(p\)-group, so solvability persists. Each stage keeps a faithful permutation representation on at most \(2|G|\) elements. Generator lists can be reduced to polynomial length by permutation membership tests, since each successively retained generator at least doubles the order of the generated subgroup, bounded by the factorial of its degree. The central series has at most \(\log_2|P|\) stages. Consequently this calculation returns all provisional pairs in coset form, with genuine isomorphisms on \(P\) and the complement and with the required intertwining on \(E\).

We finish by enforcing the complement action on the whole \(P\). Let \((f,u)\) be a provisional pair. On the target \(P'\), the two \(p'\)-actions of the target complement are its given action \(\alpha'\) and the conjugate of the source action by \(f,u\). They induce the same action on \(E'\). Put \(K=\ker(\operatorname{Aut}(P')\to\operatorname{GL}(E'))\). This is the \(p\)-group computed by the original lift theorem. The two action images lie over the same subgroup of \(\operatorname{GL}(E')\), and each maps injectively onto that subgroup because its intersection with \(K\) is trivial. They are therefore complements to \(K\) in its full preimage of that subgroup. By Schur–Zassenhaus, one conjugating element of \(K\) aligns them, and it aligns the action of each individual complement element since projection on both complements is injective. The correction can be found polynomially. Encode the two action homomorphisms as lists of permutation matrices on the written target \(P'\), one for every complement element. The group \(K\) acts on the list by simultaneous matrix conjugation, linearly over \(\mathbb F_p\); use the vector transporter. Its dimension is at most \(|C'|\,|P'|^2\). Composing \(f\) with the resulting element of \(K\) gives a true split-product isomorphism.

For the automorphism group, apply the same correction to each provisional generator, and adjoin the centralizer of the given complement image inside \(K\), computed by the vector stabilizer of its permutation-matrix list. The corrected gens generate all exact pairs modulo this centralizer: exact pairs surject onto the same image on \((E',C')\) as provisional pairs, and the correction changes neither component of that image. The kernel on exact pairs is precisely the centralizer just computed. In particular the actual group is a subgroup of the solvable provisional group. The actual marked automorphisms act faithfully on the whole group by their split-product formulas. This completes the recursion with one smaller complement call per level, polynomially many linear systems and permutation computations per level, and absolute polynomial exponents. It also proves the exact completion routine for self-comparison.

## 4. Search, automorphism generators and counts

For search, fix a complete ordered simple decomposition at every level of the source tower. Match its summands in order, trying all compatible simple submodules in the corresponding target layer that are independent of the previously chosen ones. The simultaneous-marker decision reduction in Section 2 tests each prefix. A positive prefix guarantees one valid next choice. Each layer has at most \(|G|\) simple submodules, and the number of marks is at most \(\log_2|G|\). Once all choices are made, Section 3 returns the actual isomorphism. An initial negative decision gives count zero, and trivial groups are immediate.

For automorphism generators, let the tower-preserving subgroup act on the ordered list of complete source marks. For each mark in turn, enumerate its orbit under the stabilizer of the previous marks, testing every candidate image with two copies of the source and the simultaneous-marker queries. For each orbit member use the same prefix search and the full-list lift to find one representative automorphism taking the mark to that member. The resulting permutations and the generators for the stabilizer of the full list generate the tower-preserving group by the stabilizer chain. That full-list stabilizer is available from Section 3. Adjoining the inner automorphisms gives the unrestricted group by the tower alignment argument. Standard permutation group order computation gives its exact size, with polynomially many output bits. When the input groups are isomorphic, their isomorphisms form a torsor for either automorphism group, so this order gives the requested count.

The ordinary \(p\)-group theorem already gives the nilpotent case and its same-prime queries. Babai and Qiao give polynomial-time isomorphism-coset computation for groups with abelian Sylow towers, and Skresanov gives an isomorphism algorithm for solvable groups with abelian Sylow subgroups. The new result is a reduction allowing arbitrary nonabelian Sylow factors in a normal tower. It uses independent simple complement modules to mark each Sylow factor and a solvable transporter calculation to keep the possible complement maps coupled to the cohomological lift. It applies in particular to all finite supersolvable groups and all groups with a normal Sylow subgroup and nilpotent complement, without bounds on Sylow ranks or classes. The results and proofs above concern the specified tower cases. [Babai–Qiao, abelian Sylow towers](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/papers/stacs12.pdf) · [Skresanov, solvable groups with abelian Sylow subgroups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26748) · [Earlier cohomological framework](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1009767) · [Research context for search and counting](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317v2)

## 5. A bounded-generator terminal quotient

The result also holds when the successive normal Sylow removals stop at a group \(L\) with no nontrivial normal Sylow subgroup, if the terminal group \(L\) is generated by at most a fixed \(b\) elements. The exponent of the polynomial can depend on \(b\). No solvability condition is imposed on \(L\); for example it can be a nonabelian finite simple group. The usual tower case takes \(L=1\).

Choose the prime sequence by the same least-prime algorithm, stopping when there is no normal Sylow subgroup. Leave the terminal group unchanged in the marker construction. There is still a surjection from each enlarged group to its original group, since the projections in the semidirect products commute with their actions. If the enlarged group has a normal Sylow subgroup for some prime, its image is a normal Sylow subgroup of the original group for the same prime. The exhibited first normal Sylow is still present, so at the first layer the least eligible prime stays the same. The same argument in its quotient shows that all choices recur identically, including the terminal group. The marked queries thus stay inside the enlarged class and still have polynomial order. Complement alignment and the extraction proof all continue to work; Schur–Zassenhaus here only needs the normal Hall subgroup to be a \(p\)-group.

At the terminal quotient, find a generating tuple by trying all tuples of length at most \(b\). A homomorphism from a finite group with a given generating tuple is uniquely specified by the images of that tuple. For each target tuple, assign those images, propagate through the Cayley graph of the tuple, then check the resulting full multiplication table map for bijectivity and multiplication preservation. This enumerates all terminal isomorphisms in \(|L|^{b+O(1)}\) time.

For a completed mark list, run the lifting algorithm separately for each candidate terminal map. In this run all group-of-pairs calculations are restricted to automorphisms acting as the identity on the terminal group. At the terminal level the candidate isomorphism set for the chosen map has exactly one member, and its target group is trivial. The induction in Section 3 then uses solvable groups throughout. For each successful terminal map this gives one lift and generators of the actual marked target group acting trivially on its terminal quotient. In self-comparison, the marked automorphism group is generated by that kernel and one representative for each successful terminal automorphism, since these exhaust its image in the terminal automorphism group. This supplies the full-mark stabilizer required in Section 4, with a polynomial bound for fixed \(b\).
