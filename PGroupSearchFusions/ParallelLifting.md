# Parallel lifting, search, and counting for Cayley-table p-groups

This informal proof refines the [same-prime search and counting reduction for all finite p-groups](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GroupIsomorphismAndEpimorphisms/AllPGroupsSearchCounting.md). The new bound comes from doing the transporter computation on a small cohomology quotient, even though its input equations are indexed by the complete Cayley table. The proof also uses the [implicit Cayley and weighted-Gram routines developed for minimum generation](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/LogspaceMinimumGeneration/MinimumGeneratorNumber.md).

## Theorems

Input groups are given by explicit Cayley tables. Write \(n\) for the larger order and allow the input prime \(p\) to vary. Here uniform NC means a logspace-uniform family of bounded-fan-in Boolean circuits of size polynomial in the input length and depth polylogarithmic in that length. Function outputs can be padded to a polynomial bound, with a length or validity indicator.

1. Given an exact linear isomorphism between the Frattini quotients of two finite \(p\)-groups, deciding whether it lifts, constructing a lift if one exists, and counting lifts are in uniform NC. From one group, uniform NC also constructs a generating set for the kernel of its automorphism action on its Frattini quotient. These procedures use no isomorphism oracle.
2. Given a decision oracle for same-prime \(p\)-group isomorphism, search, exact counting, and automorphism generation have polynomial-size, polylogarithmic-depth logspace-uniform oracle circuits. All decision queries use groups of order at most \(n^{28}\). Search and automorphism generation use at most \(O(1+\log_p n)\) adaptive oracle rounds, with polynomially many queries in a round. **Exact counting uses one nonadaptive batch**, including the decision on the original pair. Ordinary oracle gates can be replaced by uniform NC decision circuits if those are available for the query class.
3. As an unconditional consequence, isomorphism decision, search, counting, and automorphism generation are in uniform NC whenever the Frattini rank satisfies \(d(G)^2\le C\log_p n\) for a fixed constant \(C\), and the corresponding inequality holds on the second group. This permits unbounded rank. The same-prime oracle reductions extend componentwise to Cayley-table nilpotent groups, with each query confined to a single Sylow prime.

The bound on the number of oracle rounds treats all gates in one batch as available in parallel. The result gives polynomial work and polylogarithmic parallel time. It does not identify the complexity of the decision oracle.

## 1. Cohomology has only polylogarithmically many coordinates

Let \(Q\) be any group of order \(p^b\). Choose a normal series of length \(b\) whose factors have order \(p\) and are central in the corresponding quotient of \(Q\). Such a series is obtained by selecting a central subgroup of order \(p\) and proceeding in the quotient. Write the series in descending order and choose a representative \(x_i\) for the generator of each successive factor. There is a presentation of \(Q\) with these \(b\) generators, one power relation for each \(x_i\), and one conjugation relation for each pair \(i<j\). The power \(x_i^p\) is specified as a word in later generators. Conjugation of \(x_j\) by \(x_i\) is specified as \(x_j\) times a word in generators after \(x_j\), since that factor is central in the corresponding quotient. To verify the presentation, induct from the last generator. In the presented group the subgroup generated by all generators after \(x_i\) is finite of order at most \(p^{b-i}\) by induction. Conjugation by \(x_i\) takes it into itself by the relations, and bijectively onto itself by finiteness. The power relation then makes its index in the next subgroup at most \(p\). The presented group surjects onto \(Q\) and has order at most \(p^b\), so the presentation is exact.

For a normalized scalar cocycle \(c\) of \(Q\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), write the central extension as \((x,u)(y,v)=(xy,u+v+c(x,y))\). Take lifts \((x_i,0)\). Evaluate all fixed presentation relators at these lifts and record their defects in the kernel. This defines a linear map on the vector space of normalized cocycles, since every fixed-word second coordinate is linear in \(c\). If the defects vanish, the lifts satisfy the presentation and give a homomorphic section, so \(c\) is a coboundary. Thus the kernel of the defect map is contained in the space of coboundaries, giving

\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^2(Q,\mathbb F_p)\le b+\binom b2.
\]

This is a dimension bound. The algorithm may use the usual full cocycle and coboundary matrices, with rows and columns indexed by tuples from \(Q\). Their entries are determined by a bounded number of Cayley-table lookups. Row and column operations yield a basis of cocycles, a basis of coboundaries, and an extending quotient basis. Hence every specified pullback also has an explicit matrix on at most \(O(\log^2 n)\) quotient coordinates.

## 2. Uniform parallel linear algebra and Cayley interfaces

Linear algebra on polynomial-size matrices over \(\mathbb F_p\), for \(p\le n\), is in uniform NC. Here is the rank detail that avoids a cancellation assumption for finite fields. For an \(r\)-by-\(s\) matrix \(M\), the coefficient of \(z^k\) in

\[
\det\bigl(I_s+zD_s(u)M^{\mathsf T}D_r(t)M\bigr),\qquad
D_a(v)=\operatorname{diag}(v,v^2,\ldots,v^a),
\]

is the sum of \(\det(M[I,J])^2 t^{\sum I}u^{\sum J}\) over \(|I|=|J|=k\), by the principal-minor expansion and Cauchy–Binet. At \(k=\operatorname{rank}M\), the lexicographically least feasible exponent pair occurs exactly once. The independent row bases have a unique minimum under distinct index weights by the matroid greedy theorem. For that basis the independent column bases have their own unique minimum. Its coefficient survives in every characteristic. All greater \(k\) have zero coefficients. Compute the determinant as a polynomial in the three variables using the division-free Berkowitz circuit. Degrees and coefficient-array sizes are polynomial, and truncated-array convolution and reduction modulo \(p\) use uniform NC arithmetic. This gives rank. Computing ranks of row and column prefixes in parallel gives canonical pivot sets. Solving, forming kernel bases and quotient bases, and computing coordinates use inverses of nonsingular pivot matrices by minors and determinants. A nonzero scalar inverse may also be obtained by powering to \(p-2\). These operations have uniform NC circuits. [Berkowitz](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(84)90018-8)

Cayley-table lookup, inversion, powers, subgroups given by their list of generators, normal quotients, and elementary abelian coordinates have the required parallel interfaces. In particular, membership in a generated subgroup is undirected reachability in the Cayley graph of the generators and inverses. One can use Reingold's logspace routine, or Boolean matrix repeated squaring for reachability. For a normal quotient use least representatives and parallel table lookups. The greedy basis of an elementary abelian group is obtained by testing each element against the subgroup generated by all its predecessors, then keeping precisely the nonmembers. This uses independent reachability tests before basis compaction. Coordinates on a basis are found by testing its at most \(n\) base-\(p\) coefficient tuples in parallel. [Reingold](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/sl.pdf)

## 3. The p-group subspace transporter in small dimension

The input is a \(p\)-group \(P\) given by generators in a faithful permutation action of degree at most \(n\), a compatible linear action on an \(\mathbb F_p\)-space of dimension \(D=O(\log^2 n)\), and a pair of subspaces. We use the logspace-uniform NC algorithms of Luks and McKenzie for membership and order in solvable permutation groups, which in particular apply to every group in this calculation. The permutation action retains the original group elements even when the separate linear action has a kernel. [Luks–McKenzie, Sections 2 and 5](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/002200008890044X)

For vector transport, compute a common invariant flag with one-dimensional factors and trivial factor action. A nonzero common fixed vector exists in any nonzero \(\mathbb F_p\)-representation of a \(p\)-group: all its orbits on vectors except the fixed points have size divisible by \(p\). Pick a line in the joint kernel of the generator differences and recurse on the quotient. There are \(D\) steps, each an NC linear calculation. Match the vector along this flag, starting modulo the whole space. At a step the subgroup preserving the already matched quotient has a homomorphism to the next additive line, given by its change to the target there. The factor action is trivial. The image is either zero or all of \(\mathbb F_p\). In the latter case the powers of one generator of nonzero image supply all \(p\) representatives, and powering permutations and matrices is in NC by repeated squaring. Choose the representative with the needed change. Schreier's formula gives the next kernel generators using the old generators and the \(p\) representatives. This keeps the moved vector matched in the new quotient. An absent value certifies failure. The final subgroup is the target vector stabilizer. Affine actions are handled as linear actions on one additional coordinate fixed to one.

After each generator-producing operation, compact a polynomial list in parallel. Retain an entry exactly when it is outside the subgroup generated by its whole raw prefix. The retained elements generate the same subgroup, by induction on the prefix. Each retained entry at least doubles the order of the group generated so far. Thus there are at most \(\log_2(n!)\) retained entries. Membership tests for raw prefixes run in parallel by the cited theorem. To implement fixed-size parallel arrays, use prefix sums of the retention bits to locate the surviving entries and fill the unused positions with identity permutations and identity matrices. All operations on the linear action follow the same Schreier generator products as on the permutations.

For subspace transport, take a common fixed line \(L\) and recurse in the quotient. This returns a projected transporter and the stabilizer of the common target projection \(U\), as original permutations. After moving the source by this transporter, reject if just one subspace contains \(L\). If both contain it they equal the full inverse image of \(U\). If neither contains it they are graphs \(U\to L\) in a chosen splitting. An element of the projected stabilizer acts in that splitting by \((x,a)\mapsto(Ax,a+Bx)\), so the graph coordinate transforms by \(\lambda\mapsto(\lambda+B)A^{-1}\). Use the affine vector procedure to finish. At most one quotient subspace call is required at each level. To return a stabilizer even when the pair has no match, one can propagate the target stabilizer alone on the failing branch and run the same local step on the target with itself. The recursion uses at most \(O((D+1)^2)\) sequential stages of NC work. Generator lists are compacted before each new stage. Thus subspace transport with a witness and target stabilizer is in NC for the dimension above. This refinement uses the polynomial-time transporter mechanism from the [same-prime search proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GroupIsomorphismAndEpimorphisms/AllPGroupsSearchCounting.md#2-subspace-transport-by-a-p-group-acting-in-characteristic-p), related to [Luks–Miyazaki, Proposition 3.17](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/531/download).

## 4. Lift through the characteristic central series

Compute the characteristic lower exponent-\(p\) central series \(P_1=G\), \(P_{j+1}=P_j^p[P_j,G]\). It has \(O(\log_p n)\) strict layers and \(P_2=\Phi(G)\). To see strictness, the conjugation action on the dual of \(P_j/\Phi(P_j)\), when \(P_j\ne1\), has a nonzero fixed vector by the same fixed-vector argument as above. Its functional kills \(P_j^p[P_j,G]\). Compute each successive subgroup by membership on the listed commutators and powers. Quotients and elementary abelian layer coordinates use Section 2. If source and target layer sizes disagree, reject.

Suppose the current quotients match by some \(f_0\) inducing the prescribed Frattini map. Let \(B\) be the kernel of the target quotient's automorphism action on its Frattini quotient, with generators maintained by the induction. All possible present quotient maps are \(g\circ f_0\) for \(g\in B\). The group \(B\) is a \(p\)-group because it acts freely on the tuples obtained by lifting one fixed Frattini basis, and every such tuple generates. This set has cardinality a power of \(p\).

At the next step write \(Q\) for a quotient and \(E\) for the central elementary abelian kernel of the next extension. From any section compute its cocycle \(c\). The map \(E^*\to H^2(Q,\mathbb F_p)\) given by \(\lambda\mapsto[\lambda c]\) is injective. A nonzero functional in its kernel would give a split quotient extension by a central group of order \(p\), while the kernel is still in the Frattini subgroup. A nontrivial direct factor of order \(p\) cannot lie there. A quotient isomorphism lifts exactly when pullback identifies the two images in cohomology. Indeed image equality makes the induced isomorphism on the central kernels unique by injectivity, and the difference of their transformed cocycles is a coboundary. Solve the coboundary equations to obtain the lift. The kernel of the action on the present quotient is the group of linear-coordinate corrections from \(\operatorname{Hom}(Q,E)\). [Grochow–Qiao on extension and cohomology algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1009767)

Use Section 3 to align the cohomology subspaces under the pullback action of \(B\) (using inverse pullback for a left action). This is the step for which the polylogarithmic dimension bound in Section 1 is essential. Also compute the stabilizer for the target cohomology subspace, lift each of its generators, and adjoin the corrections from \(\operatorname{Hom}(Q,E)\). These generate the new Frattini-action kernel, since the stabilizer is exactly its quotient image. The elementary abelian base has trivial such kernel. Run this computation layer by layer. Every layer consists of polynomial-size NC subroutines, polylogarithmically many within the transporter, and the number of layers is polylogarithmic. All loops can be padded to their deterministic bounds and halted by a validity bit, so composition gives uniform NC. The kernel computation can be run on the target alone if the prescribed map fails. A nonempty lift set is a coset of the final kernel, whose order is computed with the NC permutation-group order algorithm. This establishes Theorem 1 and counts the lifts.

## 5. Oracle rounds and unbounded-rank consequences

Use the [ordered same-prime Frattini-covector marker](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GroupIsomorphismAndEpimorphisms/SamePrimePGroupMarkers.md). Its table compiler is a logspace transducer given independent covectors in standard coordinates. The Frattini subgroup is generated by all commutators and \(p\)-th powers. Membership by implicit undirected Cayley reachability gives a least-representative quotient interface. Its greedy basis is the prefix-membership basis above, which can be enumerated in logspace by recomputation. Coefficients are read by scanning word codes of length at most \(\log_p n\), each encoded by a single base-\(p\) counter bounded by \(n\). The ordered marker graph is a tree with private leaves or a fixed small star union. Its labels and incidence are computed from the indices. In the marker group's multiplication rule each output coordinate uses just the corresponding edge, one linear covector evaluation and the original group table. All tuple labels and counters fit in logarithmic space because the order is at most \(n^{28}\). Thus the entire query table is logspace output by scanning entries, independently of how its actual prefix marks were obtained.

For search, test all extensions of the next dual basis line in parallel, fix the first successful candidate, and proceed to the next line. There are at most \(n\) candidates per round and at most \(\log_p n\) line positions. After finding an entire projective frame, try all at most \((p-1)^{d(G)}\le n\) scalar assignments in parallel, using the NC full-map lift. One succeeds if the prefix was positive. This supplies an actual transporter. To produce generators, compute one such transporter for every point in every stabilizer orbit, starting with that point's required prefix. Run all those searches in parallel, then add the final diagonal subgroup lifts and the Frattini-action kernel generators. The stabilizer chain implies that this list generates all automorphisms.

For exact counting, all stabilizer-chain orbit queries are known in advance. The prefix for stage \(i\) fixes the first \(i-1\) basis lines to themselves. For every candidate line for position \(i\), ask the corresponding self-comparison query. These are all in one nonadaptive batch together with the original-pair decision. For a positive pair, multiply the successful orbit sizes, the number of successful exact diagonal maps (parallel NC lift tests), and the final kernel order. This counts all isomorphisms by orbit-stabilizer and the torsor formula. All integer outputs have polynomial bit length. Products of polynomially many such integers are in uniform NC by a balanced tree and standard parallel arithmetic.

For the bounded-rank consequence replace the projective oracle search by a parallel enumeration of all Frattini linear maps. There are at most \(p^{d(G)^2}\le n^C\), and Gaussian elimination discards the singular ones. Exact lifting now yields decision, a witness, one lift per successful automorphism image, and the kernel generators, all in NC. The orders of the image and kernel give the count. Finally the Sylow subgroups of a nilpotent table group are characteristic and consist of the elements of the corresponding prime-power orders. Those orders and all relevant primes can be computed by parallel scans over integers up to \(n\). Apply the oracle result independently to the Sylow groups and combine by direct product.

The principal comparison is [Grochow–Qiao, Part IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317v2), which gives polynomial-time Cayley-table search-to-decision and counting-to-decision for class-two groups of odd exponent \(p\). The earlier MathProofs result extends the allowed input groups to all \(p\)-groups. The new statements are uniform parallel lifting and restricted adaptivity, together with the oracle-free parallel result for an unbounded Frattini rank range. They concern the Cayley-table model in [Arvind–Torán, Problem 16](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.190/Mitarbeiter/toran/beatcs/column86.pdf). Publication priority remains unverified.
