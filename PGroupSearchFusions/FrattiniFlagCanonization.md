# Canonical Cayley tables from Frattini flags

This informal result strengthens the cohomological lifting method in [same-prime search and counting for finite p-groups](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GroupIsomorphismAndEpimorphisms/AllPGroupsSearchCounting.md). It outputs a single canonical labelled group and a witness from each input in the promised model. It does not query an isomorphism oracle.

## The theorem

Let a finite \(p\)-group \(G\) of order \(N\) be given by its Cayley table. The prime may vary, including \(p=2\). Write \(V_G=G/\Phi(G)\), and let \(d=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}V_G\). Suppose we mark an ordered flag
\[
0=F_0<F_1<\cdots<F_t=V_G,\qquad b_i=\dim(F_i/F_{i-1}).
\]
Isomorphisms of marked groups must carry each \(F_i\) to its counterpart. A deterministic algorithm produces a canonical labelled Cayley table with the flag and an explicit isomorphism to it. It also gives generators and the order of the flag-preserving automorphism group. Its bit time is
\[
N^{O(1)}\prod_{i=1}^t[\operatorname{GL}_{b_i}(p):U_{b_i}(p)]
\ \le\ N^{O(1)}p^{\sum_i\binom{b_i}{2}},
\tag{1}
\]
where \(U_b(p)\) is the upper unitriangular subgroup and the \(O(1)\) is absolute. In particular full flags and flags with bounded \(b_i\) give polynomial bit time. Taking just \(0<V_G\) gives an unconditional unmarked bound \(N^{O(1)}p^{\binom d2}\) for arbitrary finite \(p\)-groups.

There is a uniform, raw-input application. For each \(j\ge0\) use the image in \(V_G\) of the characteristic subgroup
\[
\Omega_j(G)=\langle g\in G:g^{p^j}=1\rangle.
\]
Omit repeated images as \(j\) increases. Equation (1) applies to this intrinsic flag without supplied marks. It yields a polynomial-time canonical form for every fixed bound on the consecutive dimension jumps. Even the width-two family contains examples with unbounded generator rank and unbounded nilpotency class simultaneously.

## 1. Canonical representatives for subspaces under a known p-group

Let \(P\) be a \(p\)-group given by generators as permutations on an explicitly listed set, with a specified linear action on a based vector space \(D\) over \(k=\mathbb F_p\). The linear action need not be faithful. A subspace \(S\subseteq D\) can be put into an orbit-canonical position in time polynomial in the permutation degree, \(p\), \(\dim D\), and the generator data. A witness and generators for the stabilizer of the returned subspace are computed in the original permutation group. The representative depends on the fixed coordinates and the acting group, and is independent of the generating set used to specify it.

We first give the vector algorithm, which also works for an affine action after adjoining one constant coordinate. A nonzero linear \(p\)-group module has a nonzero fixed vector: the orbits of the action on its elements other than fixed orbits have sizes divisible by \(p\), whereas the zero vector is a fixed point. A canonical nonzero vector in the fixed space is chosen using its reduced row-echelon basis in the fixed ambient coordinates. Apply the same process in the quotient by its span, using quotient coordinates chosen by Gaussian elimination. Induction gives a full invariant flag \(0=D_0<\cdots<D_m=D\) and a specified coordinate basis respecting it. The group acts trivially on each one-dimensional quotient, since \(\mathbb F_p^\times\) has order coprime to \(p\). All flag choices here depend on fixed spaces, so are independent of the generators used to compute them.

Starting from a vector \(x\), canonize its coordinates successively from the top of this flag down. At a step suppose the chosen coordinates modulo \(D_j\) are already fixed, and let \(P_j\) be their stabilizer. For the current vector \(x'\) the map
\[
P_j\longrightarrow D_j/D_{j-1},\quad g\longmapsto (gx'-x')\bmod D_{j-1}
\]
is an additive homomorphism. Indeed \(g\) acts trivially on the layer \(D_j/D_{j-1}\), and \(gx'-x'\in D_j\). This homomorphism only depends on \(x'\bmod D_j\). Its generated image has at most \(p\) elements. Compute their permutation representatives using an additive scan of the images of the generators. Choose the least attainable coordinate in a fixed ordering of \(k\), move \(x'\) by the corresponding representative, and let \(P_{j-1}\) be the kernel, generated by Schreier's lemma. This group is the stabilizer of the extended prefix regardless of the representative used. Starting from any vector in the same orbit gives the same sequence of least attainable coordinates. The final group is the stabilizer of the returned vector. In the affine case work in the invariant hyperplane with extra coordinate (1) and apply the same linear algorithm to its vectors. Whenever Schreier's lemma creates a longer list, compress it by deterministic permutation group membership, retaining a proposed generator exactly when it is outside the subgroup generated so far. Every retention at least doubles the generated order, so a group acting faithfully on degree \(n'\) needs at most \(\log_2(n'!)\) retained generators. [Furst–Hopcroft–Luks](https://doi.org/10.1109/SFCS.1980.34)

For a subspace, choose the canonical fixed line \(L\subset D\) by the same fixed-space rule. Recursively canonize the projection of \(S\) in \(D/L\), getting a representative \(U\), one witness, and its projected stabilizer as a subgroup of \(P\). Move \(S\) by that witness. If \(S\) contains \(L\), it is now the full inverse image of \(U\) and already canonical. Otherwise \(S\) is the graph of a linear map \(U\to L\), using a fixed linear splitting of the inverse image of \(U\). The stabilizer of \(U\) in \(P\) acts on these section maps by an explicitly computable affine action. In coordinates a group element acts as \((x,a)\mapsto(Ax,a+Bx)\), with \(A\in\operatorname{GL}(U)\), since it fixes \(L\) pointwise. Its action on a section \(\lambda\) is \(\lambda\mapsto(\lambda+B)A^{-1}\). This is an affine \(p\)-group action. Canonize its graph parameter using the vector algorithm. Any two witnesses bringing the projection to \(U\) differ by its stabilizer, so they produce the same final result. The vector stabilizer is exactly the subspace stabilizer in the projected stabilizer. The lift and stabilizer throughout the recursion are actual permutations, including when the action has a kernel. Dimension zero is immediate. There is one recursive call on a quotient line at each level and a polynomial amount of work beside that call. This proves the asserted time bound.

Canonicalizing under unipotent actions has a precedent in the subspace standardization of Eick, Leedham-Green and O’Brien. Sun’s affine vector transporter over \(\mathbb Z/p^\tau\mathbb Z\) also uses a prime-order flag and a Schreier kernel at each step. The construction above spells out the section-graph reduction, its use for orbit representatives, and the bit-time bound on the Cayley-table scale with variable \(p\). [Eick–Leedham-Green–O’Brien, *Constructing automorphism groups of p-groups*, §5.2](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~obrien/research/autpgroup.pdf) · [Sun, *Faster Isomorphism for Groups of Class 2*, Lemma A.24](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920v1)

## 2. Canonical cohomological lifting with a p-group of allowed frame changes

Fix a based elementary abelian group \(k^d\) and a specified \(p\)-subgroup \(P_0\le\operatorname{GL}_d(k)\), given by generators. At first mark \(V_G\) by an identification \(V_G\simeq k^d\) considered up to postcomposition by \(P_0\). We produce a canonical table and its witness in \(N^{O(1)}\) time for these marked groups. The construction also produces generators for its group of allowed automorphisms.

Use the characteristic lower exponent-\(p\) central series \(P_1(G)=G, P_{i+1}(G)=P_i(G)^p[P_i(G),G]\). Its second group is \(\Phi(G)\). At each nontrivial \(P_i(G)\) the dual of \(P_i(G)/\Phi(P_i(G))\) has a nonzero fixed vector for the conjugation action of \(G\), by the fixed-vector argument above. The resulting functional kills \(P_i(G)^p[P_i(G),G]\), proving strict decrease. Thus there are at most \(\log_2 N\) steps and elementary central kernels. Both series and quotient tables can be produced by table arithmetic and subgroup generation. The first quotient is \(k^d\), ordered by its coordinates. Its allowed automorphism subgroup is \(P_0\).

At an inductive step write \(X\twoheadrightarrow Q\) for the next group over the preceding quotient and \(E\) for its central elementary kernel. Always \(E\subseteq\Phi(X)\), since both quotients come from terms inside \(\Phi(G)\). Use the already computed canonical table of \(Q\), a witness from the input quotient, and generators for its corresponding allowed automorphism group \(A_Q\). This group is a \(p\)-group: the automorphisms acting trivially on \(V_G\) act freely on the set of all lifts of a fixed ordered basis of \(V_G\), since every such tuple generates \(Q\). Hence the order of this kernel divides a power of \(p\), and the image is a subgroup of \(P_0\). The same reasoning applies at every subsequent layer.

Choose any section of \(X\to Q\) taking identity to identity and any elementary coordinates on \(E\). The law is given by an \(E\)-valued normalized cocycle \(c(x,y)\) on the canonical quotient \(Q\). Compute \(H^2(Q,k)\) by Gaussian elimination on the cocycle and coboundary equations with a fixed ordering on the cochain coordinates. Choose a standard representative cocycle for every cohomology coordinate using a fixed linear section of the cocycle-to-cohomology map. All these choices depend only on the canonical quotient table. The dimensions and numbers of explicitly written equations are polynomial in \(N\).

The map
\[
 E^*\longrightarrow H^2(Q,k),\qquad \lambda\longmapsto[\lambda c]
\]
is injective. A nonzero functional in its kernel would split the extension after quotienting by \(\ker\lambda\). The resulting central order-\(p\) factor is a direct factor in the split group and does not lie in its Frattini subgroup. But the image of \(E\subseteq\Phi(X)\) lies in that subgroup, by \(\Phi(X)=X^p[X,X]\). This contradiction gives injectivity. Its image \(S\) is independent of the section and the coordinates on \(E\).

The group \(A_Q\) acts on \(H^2(Q,k)\) by inverse pullback, computed by permuting the cochain coordinates and then reducing modulo coboundaries. Two extensions of the canonical quotient are equivalent with an allowed quotient action exactly when their image subspaces \(S\) belong to the same orbit. Necessity follows by applying the dual of the new kernel map to the cocycle equation. For sufficiency the required kernel map is uniquely determined by equality of the two image subspaces after applying the chosen quotient action, by injectivity. The linear coboundary equations then give the correction to the sections, and therefore an explicit extension isomorphism. These are the extension coordinates used in the same-prime lifting theorem. [MathProofs lifting argument](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GroupIsomorphismAndEpimorphisms/AllPGroupsSearchCounting.md#3-lifting-a-specified-frattini-quotient-isomorphism) · [Grochow–Qiao on group extensions and cohomology](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1009767)

Now use Section 1 to canonize \(S\) under \(A_Q\), giving \(S_0\), one quotient action, and generators for its stabilizer. The reduced row-echelon basis of \(S_0\) is canonical in the cohomology coordinates. Use its standard cocycle representatives as the \(E\)-coordinate entries of a canonical cocycle, and output the extension table on \(Q\times k^{\dim S_0}\) in fixed lexicographic order. It represents the input extension, with the isomorphism obtained by the explicit coboundary solve just described. Two isomorphic marked inputs produce the same table at this level because their image subspaces have the same \(A_Q\)-orbit on the same canonical quotient. The canonical output carries its original quotient marking through the table maps. The same induction proves canonicity of the full output.

To continue the induction, lift each generator of the stabilizer of \(S_0\) along the computed cocycle equation. Adjoin generators for the automorphisms inducing the identity on \(Q\). Injectivity forces such automorphisms to fix \(E\) pointwise, so this kernel acts by translation of the section by homomorphisms \(Q\to E\). A basis follows from the linear equations \(h(xy)=h(x)+h(y)\), with values in \(E\). These two types of generators give all allowed automorphisms. Use the action on the elements of the canonical group as the faithful permutation action for the next round. The only cohomology calculations are polynomial-size linear systems and pullback matrices. In the subspace subroutine \(p\le N\) for a nontrivial group, so its uniform polynomial dependence on \(p\) is polynomial in the explicit table input. The trivial input is immediate. This proves the relative canonical labeling theorem and witness bound.

## 3. Refining the marked flag and removing the refinements

For each marked block \(F_i/F_{i-1}\), enumerate full flags and one nonzero vector in each of their successive one-dimensional quotients. A refinement together with those vectors is the same data as an ordered basis of the block modulo its upper unitriangular group. There are exactly \([\operatorname{GL}_{b_i}(p):U_{b_i}(p)]\) choices in block \(i\). Enumerate flags by recursively listing projective points in each quotient, then list the scalar choices on each one-dimensional step. The quotient spaces have at most \(p^d\le N\) points, so enumeration has only polynomial overhead per output.

Using a lift of each chosen vector, obtain any basis of \(V_G\) realizing the full refined flag and its specified vectors. Two such adapted bases differ by a full upper unitriangular matrix in \(\operatorname{GL}_d(k)\), including entries between different original blocks. This group is a \(p\)-group, with order \(p^{\binom d2}\). Therefore Section 2 canonizes the group with the full refined data without enumerating the adapted bases. The coarse flag is remembered as the designated prefix dimensions of the standard full flag. For two isomorphic coarse-flagged groups their isomorphism bijects the refinement data and hence the resulting list of canonical representatives. Conversely an equal table with the equal recorded coarse flag exhibits an isomorphism carrying that flag. Take the lexicographic least output with its witness. This gives the claimed canonical form and the exact product bound in (1).

For the automorphism generators, take a refinement \(r_0\) giving the minimum output. The refinements giving exactly that output are one orbit under the coarse-flag stabilizer, since equality of the normalized marked tables is equivalent to equivalence with the full refined data. If \(f_r\) is the computed isomorphism to that table for a matching refinement \(r\), then \(f_r^{-1}f_{r_0}\) sends \(r_0\) to \(r\). Their union with the stabilizer generators of \(r_0\) generates the full coarse-flag stabilizer by orbit–stabilizer. Its order is the number of matching refinements times the stabilizer order, the latter computed by permutation group algorithms. A second enumeration after finding the minimum suffices to recover these generators with the same asymptotic time. Thus two inputs also admit exact counting of the marked isomorphisms, since any nonempty set of isomorphisms has the size of the automorphism group of its source.

The inequalities follow from
\[
[\operatorname{GL}_b(p):U_b(p)]=\prod_{j=1}^b(p^j-1)\le p^{\binom b2+b}
\]
and \(p^{\sum b_i}=p^d\le N\). If each block size is at most \(B\), then \(\sum_i\binom{b_i}{2}\le (B-1)d/2\), giving \(N^{(B-1)/2+O(1)}\). The runtime depends on the Frattini rank even in the unmarked case, with a gain when that rank is significantly smaller than \(\log_p N\).

## 4. A canonical flag in the raw input

Each \(\Omega_j(G)\) is characteristic because automorphisms preserve the equation \(g^{p^j}=1\). Its image in the Frattini quotient is the span of the images of precisely the elements killed by that power, so no lift of the full subgroup is necessary for the flag. Scan the table and take the ranks of these spans for successive \(j\le\log_p N\). They form an increasing flag from zero to all of \(V_G\), including after removing redundant repetitions. Every group isomorphism carries the flag to its counterpart. Applying (1) gives the raw canonical form and isomorphism witness. The original indices or jump dimensions can also be retained as part of the canonical output.

Here are nonabelian width-two families with no constant bound on the number of generators or the class. For each sufficiently large positive integer \(i\), take
\[
H_i=C_{p^i}\rtimes C_{p^i},\qquad b a b^{-1}=a^{1+p^e},
\]
where \(e=1\) for odd \(p\) and \(e=2\) for \(p=2\). The action is well-defined since \(1+p^e\) has multiplicative order dividing \(p^i\) modulo \(p^i\), by binomial expansion. The Frattini quotient has basis given by \(a,b\) modulo powers and commutators. For an element \(a^x b^y\) with \(y\) a unit modulo \(p\), its image in the second cyclic quotient has order \(p^i\). If \(x\) is a unit modulo \(p\), write the first coordinate of its \(p^j\)-th power as \(x S_{p^j}(t)\), where \(t=(1+p^e)^y\) and \(S_m(t)=1+t+\cdots+t^{m-1}\). In the integers \(v_p(S_{p^j}(t))=j\): iteratively factor out \(S_p(t^{p^u})\) for \(0\le u<j\). For odd \(p\) each factor is \(p\bmod p^2\) by expansion at \(t^{p^u}\equiv1\bmod p\). For \(p=2\) it is \(2\bmod 4\). Thus such an element is not killed by any \(p^j\) with \(j<i\). The two basis classes are represented by the generators of order \(p^i\), so the intrinsic flag on \(H_i\) jumps from zero to dimension two at index \(i\). Commuting the first cyclic subgroup with \(b\) raises it to \(p^e\)-th powers at each step, so the nilpotency classes are unbounded as \(i\) grows. Take products of increasing numbers of different \(H_i\) with distinct \(i\). Their Frattini quotients are the direct sums of the individual ones, and projection to each factor gives the asserted jump of dimension two in the product. Thus these products have unbounded class and Frattini rank, and meet the raw criterion with \(B=2\).

## Relation to prior work

The output is a canonical table and a transporter to it, in the sense of the canonical-form versus complete-invariant distinction made by Fortnow–Grochow. The canonical presentation algorithms of O’Brien and Barrera Acevedo–Dietrich–Horn provide the prior central-extension framework. The latter explicitly separate existence of an algorithmic canonical labeling from a polynomial running-time result for all groups. Here the novelty claim is the parameter-dependent, uniform bit-time bound obtained by restricting each cohomology orbit computation to a \(p\)-group, followed by refinement removal, and the intrinsic bounded-width application. Rosenbaum gives an order-dependent halving bound for \(p\)-group canonization. The present unmarked bound uses the Frattini rank, and the characteristic flag can reduce the exponent further. Publication priority for these refinements has not been independently established. [Fortnow–Grochow](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4775) · [Barrera Acevedo–Dietrich–Horn, §§5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26030v1) · [Rosenbaum, Theorem 5.6 and Corollary 5.5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2015.05.036)
