# A determinant-character reduction for tensors of unbounded order

Fix $F=\mathbb F_3$, and let $H$ assert $\mathsf{TI}_{\rm SL}(F)\le_m^{\rm P}\mathsf{TI}(F)$. The following is an additional equivalent assertion.

> For $d\ge2$ supplied as part of the input, let $T,S:V_1\times\cdots\times V_d\to W$ be dense multilinear maps over an explicitly represented extension $E/F$. Given weights $w_1,\ldots,w_d,w_W\in\{0,1\}$ and a prescribed sign, ordinary $F$-TI can decide whether a transporter satisfies the corresponding weighted product of the $E/F$ determinant norms.

The input arity can grow even when some of the factor dimensions are one. Weights specify one character. The construction below is unconditional as a reduction to three-mode SL-TI over $F$, and it uses only output orientation on the resulting multiplication tables.

## From a chain of tensor products to a triangular algebra

Reject unequal formats. Zero-dimensional formats can be treated directly: every map is then zero and the sign characters on the product of the invertible mode groups are immediately determined by which factors have positive dimension. Assume $d\ge2$ and positive matched dimensions. As usual, if the character is nontrivial on the universal scalars $(s_i I_{V_i},(\prod_i s_i)I_W)$, either sign is possible exactly when ordinary equivalence holds. The trivial character's negative target is impossible. The nontrivial universally correctable case and the ordinary comparison can be treated by the zero-weight construction below. In the remaining case, for every $i$,
$$
w_i\dim_E V_i\equiv w_W\dim_E W\pmod2 .
$$

Use vertices $0,\ldots,d$ with diagonal rings $E[t]/(t^{M_v})$. For $i<j$ let $V_{ij}=V_{i+1}\otimes_E\cdots\otimes_E V_j$, except that $V_{0d}=W$. Put $V_{ij}\otimes_E E[t]/(t^{k_{ij}})$ in corner $ij$. Set $k_{i-1,i}=2+w_i$ on adjacent edges, $k_{ij}=2$ on all other proper intervals, and $k_{0d}=2-w_W$. Diagonal actions are by truncation. Concatenate the tensor factors when multiplying consecutive proper intervals. When the full interval is reached, contract the ordered list of input factors with $T$ to obtain an element of $W$. The products are extended across the indicated truncation. All other products of off-diagonal corners vanish.

These products are associative. Concatenations over a proper interval give the same ordered tensor under either association. The full interval always uses $T$ on that same ordered tensor. Every extension of an interval has truncation length no bigger than the parts used to build it, so the same agreement holds for all polynomial coefficients. Products that have incompatible endpoints are zero for either association. The diagonal actions are the compatible scalar actions of the truncated rings, and the sum of the diagonal units is a unit.

Add the four-line anchor from [the norm construction](UniformNorms.md#the-weighted-triangle-and-its-anchor), sharing vertex $0$. There are no new cross products. Give all vertices distinct diagonal lengths $M_v$, and ensure all are at least the maximum edge length. The coefficient field and all vertices are forced by exactly the same argument as in that construction. Thus after radical-unit conjugation each algebra isomorphism is $E$-linear and carries each corner to its namesake. Equality of the two $t$ actions on each adjacent edge forces a common uniformizer coefficient $\lambda$ on the original chain.

## Determinants and exact reflection

On each original corner, reduction modulo $t$ gives an invertible map $a_{ij}$ on $V_{ij}$. On any proper interval its action is the tensor product of its actions on the adjacent factors. This follows because products of adjacent vectors span the proper interval after reduction modulo $t$. On the full interval write $g_W=a_{0d}$. Multiplication preserves exactly $S(g_1 x_1,\ldots,g_d x_d)=g_WT(x_1,\ldots,x_d)$, where $g_i=a_{i-1,i}$. Conversely a multilinear transporter gives the claimed componentwise maps on that chain and extends using the diagonal identity and the identity on the anchor.

The determinant on the original parts is calculated by their $t$-adic filtrations. For each corner it contributes the norm of $(\det_E a_{ij})^{k_{ij}}\lambda^{(\dim_E V_{ij})\binom{k_{ij}}2}$. Each proper nonadjacent interval has even $k_{ij}$, so its first factor contributes sign one. The remaining determinant terms are exactly the intended sign character times a power of the norm of $\lambda$. At an interior vertex the incoming and outgoing dimensions modulo two of the weighted adjacent edges agree. At vertex $0$ and vertex $d$ they balance against the full interval. All other original corner dimensions are even, and the four-line anchor adds an even amount at vertex $0$. Choose the parity of each $M_v$ so left and right unit multiplications have determinant one. Shift one original $M_v$ by two if needed to zero the total exponent of $\lambda$ modulo two. Further shifts by multiples of four make the lengths distinct, without upsetting either calibration. The auxiliary anchor contributes determinant one. All possible algebra-isomorphism signs are therefore exactly the intended character values.

By unit normalization, the output determinant of a multiplication-tensor transporter is the determinant of its algebra isomorphism in these tables. An algebra isomorphism of determinant one gives three determinant-one mode maps. Negative targets are calibrated by an $F$-linear reflection of the target algebra frame. These facts give one ordinary or SL-constrained multiplication-tensor comparison for each single character, including the mentioned unweighted comparison.

There are $O(d^2)$ corners. Every proper interval dimension is at most the product of all positive input dimensions, bounded by the dense input length. The required lengths at all vertices are $O(d+1)$ and the extension degree multiplies the output dimension over $F$. Thus the construction takes polynomial bit time even at unbounded arity.

Under $H$ the three-mode SL output reduces to ordinary TI. Conversely, apply the asserted weighted-character decision procedure to the eight characters of a three-mode transporter, with $E=F$ and $d=2$. The character coset test and the fixed positive conjunction compiler from [the norm construction](UniformNorms.md#combining-character-and-sign-choices) give $H$.

## Further uniformly transferable problems

Prescribed *individual* determinant norms in all modes reduce to one ordinary comparison under $H$ at every arity. To see this, eliminate each one-dimensional input mode by evaluating it on its displayed basis vector. The transporter on that mode is a scalar, and their product multiplies the output map in the reduced comparison. With prescribed signs on the removed modes, the change in the output sign is the product of those signs raised to $\dim_E W$. This is a known calibration, and norm surjectivity gives a scalar of each prescribed sign. In particular all-one inputs need no sign adjustment. Every remaining input dimension is at least two, so the number of remaining modes is at most logarithmic in the dense input length. Enumerate all characters as before. If only one input mode remains, determine the possible signs from an ordinary rank comparison: on the rank block the two maps are identified by the linear map, and their actions on the kernel and the codomain quotient are independently free. Bases in those spaces give the raw determinant calibration. With no input mode remaining, compare vectors, with determinant freedom on the complement of the vector line when that complement is nonzero.

Even without eliminating one-dimensional modes, an arbitrary rank-$O(\log L)$ binary system of sign equations gives a polynomial reduction. Enumerate dual characters of the image of the binary constraint matrix. Each pullback is a single weighted character, covered by this construction. The product and automaton procedures for independent lists likewise work whenever each local sign query is encoded by one of these character tests, or when the local alphabet enumeration is polynomial.

The equivalence is about dense presentations. Succinct evaluation circuits for the original multilinear map can encode input arrays much larger than the written input, and the interval construction has no polynomial-size promise in that model.
