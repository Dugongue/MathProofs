# Normality and rational singularities under hyperbolic padding

**Theorem.** Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic zero. For a tensor \(T\in \bigotimes_i V_i\), let \(X\) be its ordinary orbit closure, and \(Y\) the orbit closure of its primal image under independent symplectic changes in \(V_i\oplus V_i^*\). Then \(X\) is normal if and only if \(Y\) is normal, and \(X\) has rational singularities if and only if \(Y\) has rational singularities. These are assertions about the whole closures. In addition, the projective incidence model \(\pi:\widetilde Y\to Y\) over the isotropic support Grassmannians satisfies
\[
\pi_*\mathcal O_{\widetilde Y}=\mathcal O_Y,\qquad
R^q\pi_*\mathcal O_{\widetilde Y}=0\quad(q>0).
\]
The same pushforward statement holds for the ordinary incidence model.

**The two incidence models.** Let \(U_i\) be the intrinsic mode support of \(T\), of dimension \(r_i\), and \(Z\) its ordinary orbit closure in \(\bigotimes_i U_i\). The case \(T=0\) is immediate. In the symplectic model, put \(W_i=V_i\oplus V_i^*\) and \(B=\prod_i\operatorname{IGr}(r_i,W_i)\). In the ordinary model use \(W_i=V_i\) and \(B=\prod_i\operatorname{Gr}(r_i,W_i)\). Let \(\mathcal S_i\) denote the tautological bundles. In either case the relative variety \(\widetilde Y\) in the tensor bundle on \(B\) has fiber the frame-transported copy of \(Z\). On a chart trivializing the frames it is the product of that chart and \(Z\). Its projection to the ambient tensor space is projective and gives the relevant orbit closure. It is birational because on the maximal-support stratum the planes are recovered from the mode supports. These properties are the incidence construction from the symplectic threshold theorem. In the ordinary case the same construction uses extension of linear isomorphisms rather than extension of symplectic bases. [Orbit-closure incidence model](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SymplecticTensorFusions/OrbitClosures.md).

**Cohomology.** Write \(\mathcal A_d\) for the degree-\(d\) piece of the relative coordinate algebra of the affine map from the model to \(B\). The maps \(\operatorname{Sym}^d(\bigotimes_i\mathcal S_i^*)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal A_d\) are issued from homogeneous coordinate restriction to the fiber \(Z\). In characteristic zero the Schur–Weyl decomposition, functorial in maps of vector spaces, is
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^d\!\left(\bigotimes_i S_i^*\right)
=\bigoplus_{\lambda_i\vdash d}
\left(\bigotimes_i\mathbb S_{\lambda_i} S_i^*\right)\otimes K_{\boldsymbol\lambda}
\]
with zero terms when a partition is too long. The space \(K_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\) is the invariant subspace for simultaneous permutation of the \(d\) tensor factors in the product of the corresponding symmetric-group representations. It depends only on the partitions and the number of modes. The ideal of \(Z\) in each degree is stable under \(\prod_i\operatorname{GL}(U_i)\). Complete reducibility therefore makes the coordinate algebra quotient replace each multiplicity space \(K_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\) by a quotient \(M_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\). Frame changes act trivially on these multiplicities, so the same decomposition gives \(\mathcal A_d\) on \(B\).

On either Grassmannian \(\mathbb S_\lambda\mathcal S_i^*\), for a partition \(\lambda\) of length at most \(r_i\), has no higher cohomology and has irreducible global sections. These are the dominant weights obtained by appending zeros to \(\lambda\) in Borel–Weil–Bott. The restriction \(\mathbb S_\lambda W_i^*\to H^0(\mathbb S_\lambda\mathcal S_i^*)\) is equivariant and nonzero: its evaluation at any point gives the surjection induced by restricting \(W_i^*\) to the plane. It is therefore surjective. By the product cohomology formula every \(\mathcal A_d\) is acyclic in positive degrees. Functoriality of the displayed Schur decomposition identifies the map from ambient degree-\(d\) polynomials on the \(\boldsymbol\lambda\)-summand with the product of these restriction maps and the quotient \(K_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\twoheadrightarrow M_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\). Thus ambient polynomials surject onto \(H^0(B,\mathcal A_d)\). [Ordinary Grassmannian calculation of Landsberg–Weyman](https://people.tamu.edu/~jml/Bulletin%20of%20London%20Math%20Soc%20-%202007%20-%20Landsberg%20-%20On%20the%20ideals%20and%20singularities%20of%20secant%20varieties%20of%20Segre%20varieties.pdf), [Symplectic Borel–Weil–Bott for tautological bundles](https://www.mathnet.ru/links/82c688a1defca42148cc1801126abc6d/sm9292_eng.pdf).

The map from the model to \(B\) is affine, so these calculations compute its structure sheaf cohomology via the direct sum of the \(\mathcal A_d\). Cohomology commutes with this sum on the finite affine cover of the separated Grassmannian product. The kernel of ambient polynomial restriction is the ideal of the image, since the model and image are reduced and the map is surjective onto the image. The image is affine, and the higher direct image sheaves are quasi-coherent. The cohomology and surjectivity assertions therefore give exactly the displayed identities for \(\pi\).

**Ascent from the intrinsic closure.** If \(Z\) is normal, the model is normal on its frame charts. Its ring of global functions is then integrally closed in its function field, by checking at the normal local rings. The ring equals the coordinate ring of the image, so the image is normal. If \(Z\) has rational singularities then the model does as well, by the frame-chart products with smooth bases. Resolve the model and compose with \(\pi\). The higher direct images of the resolution of the model vanish, and the higher direct images for \(\pi\) vanish by the computation above. Their composite is a resolution of the image with the required pushforward, proving rational singularities.

**Descent to the intrinsic closure.** In the ordinary case choose complements to the \(U_i\) and scale the complementary directions by a common parameter \(t\in\mathbb G_m\), while fixing every \(U_i\). In the symplectic case first extend each \(U_i\) to a primal–dual block \(U_i\oplus U_i^*\) in \(W_i\). On \(W_i\) choose a symplectic one-parameter group with weights \(-1,+1\) on \(U_i,U_i^*\), and weight zero on the symplectic complement. On the ambient tensor space compose its product action with scalar multiplication by \(t^\ell\), where \(\ell\) is the order. The symplectic image is a cone and is preserved by both actions. All ambient weights of the resulting action are nonnegative, and weight zero consists exactly of \(\bigotimes_i U_i\). The analogous statement holds for the ordinary action just described.

In both cases the projection onto this weight-zero space maps the orbit closure onto \(Z\). On its orbit it comes from taking the \(U_i\)-components of the transformed inputs, so the image is obtained from \(T\) by tuples of endomorphisms of the \(U_i\). Such images lie in \(Z\) by density of invertible tuples. Continuity extends inclusion to the closure, and the embedded intrinsic orbit closure is fixed pointwise by the projection. The weight-zero coordinate ring is therefore \(k[Z]\), making \(Z\) the affine torus quotient of either image. An invariant subring of a normal domain is normal, and Boutot's theorem gives rational singularities for the affine quotient of a variety with rational singularities by a reductive group in characteristic zero. Combining descent and ascent proves the two equivalences. [Boutot](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01405091).
