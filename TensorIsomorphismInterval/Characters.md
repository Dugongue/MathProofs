# Determinant-subgroup tensor isomorphism classes

Fix a finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$. For a subgroup $H\leq (F^\times)^3$, let $\operatorname{TI}(H)$ be the language of pairs of cubic tensors $T,S$ of equal format for which some ordinary transporter $(A_1,A_2,A_3)$ has determinant triple in $H$. Its complexity class is its deterministic polynomial-time many-one downward closure. We use the physical covariant convention $(A_1\otimes A_2\otimes A_3)T=S$. The unrestricted subgroup and the trivial subgroup correspond to ordinary TI and SL-TI.

**Theorem.** Over $\mathbb F_4$, let $H$ range over the trivial subgroup, the whole group, the nine lines supported on at most two coordinates, and all thirteen planes of $(\mathbb F_4^\times)^3\simeq\mathbb F_3^3$ in exponent coordinates. For each of these twenty-four subgroups,
$$
 \operatorname{TI}\le_m^p\operatorname{TI}(H)\le_m^p\operatorname{SL\!-\!TI}.
$$
Moreover for two listed subgroups $H\subseteq K$, one has $\operatorname{TI}(K)\le_m^p\operatorname{TI}(H)$. This gives twenty-two interior problem descriptions together with the two endpoints. No strict separation of the downward closures is asserted. In particular permuting the tensor modes identifies many of these descriptions. Over $\mathbb F_3$ all sixteen sign subgroups have the same proved inclusions and order-reversing rule.

## 1. General determinant slack and preservation of square formats

A concise tensor over $F$ has a commutative centroid algebra. Its elements are triples of linear maps whose three separate actions on the tensor coincide. Multiplicative closure follows by moving one action to another mode. Commutativity follows by interchanging two actions on distinct modes, then using injectivity in each mode to conclude that the endomorphisms commute. Primitive centroid idempotents give an intrinsic finest direct-sum decomposition. The verification is elementary: orthogonal centroid projections give disjoint subspaces in each mode and every term has support in matching subspaces, by the equality of the three actions. Conversely a decomposition gives centroid projections. For a direct sum of concise tensors, the centroid is the product of their centroids. To see that the off-diagonal entries of a centroid map are zero, act on a summand in one mode. A nonzero off-diagonal entry produces a component supported in a mixed block with two unchanged modes; the action in another mode cannot have that component. Conciseness on each summand makes the candidate off-diagonal entry zero. Thus every transporter permutes the indecomposable centroid summands. Unique ordered formats identify a distinguished indecomposable summand among others without marking its basis.

Suppose we can add disjoint concise indecomposable tensors $G_j$ with distinct ordered formats, each too large in some coordinate to match an original summand, with known determinant images $D_j$ of their stabilizers. Also allow unused ambient dimensions in selected modes. Add the same gadgets to both tensors. The gadget sectors are preserved, so determinant triples of transporters multiply by elements of $\prod_j D_j$. An unused ambient dimension contributes an arbitrary independent determinant in its mode. When original inputs have deficient supports, the supports are intrinsic and are mapped to each other. Their ordinary transporters extend to the original ambient spaces with arbitrary invertible actions on the unused quotients. In the padded instance the unused quotient dimensions are precisely the original deficiency plus any added slack. Therefore the same product-of-charges rule holds. For clarity on basis factors, choose adapted support and complement bases for the two tensors and extend those bases by the same gadget bases. Regrouping these bases has the same permutation determinant on the two sides, while the ratio of original coordinate determinants occurs before and after gadget installation. Thus the rule concerns original determinants, not orientations in an arbitrary rebasement. Mismatched support dimensions are immediate negative instances.

Let $s=q-1$, and assume $q>2$. All gadgets below have dimensions divisible by $s$. Use $s$ unused coordinates for each desired coordinate line. Starting with cubic inputs, this preserves congruence of the three dimensions modulo $s$. One can then balance to a cubic instance using a determinant-trivial indecomposable gadget of shape $(s,N,N)$ for every sufficiently large multiple $N$ of $s$, with the short mode chosen at will. The next section constructs it. If the current mode sizes are $n_1,n_2,n_3$ and initially were $n$, choose a sufficiently large multiple $C$ of $s$ and put $N_i=C+n_i-n$. Add this gadget with short mode $i$ and long side $N_i$, for each $i$. The final side in mode $i$ is $n_i+s+\sum_{j\ne i}N_j$, independent of $i$. Choose $C$ so that every new long side exceeds all previous sides and exceeds $s$. The three new gadgets are distinguished from one another by their short mode. Their determinant images are trivial. For rectangular endpoints the balancing step is unnecessary.

It follows that adding gadgets with total slack subgroup $K$ and then asking for determinant membership in $H$ is equivalent to asking for original determinant membership in $HK$. With $H\subseteq K$, this is precisely the converse-inclusion rule in the theorem. The upper bound uses $H=\{1\}$ as the question on the resulting tensor and the desired subgroup as slack. The lower bound also follows immediately by adding an unused coordinate in all three modes, since any transporter can then be corrected to determinant one.

## 2. Rigid label coordinates and trivial or inverse-pair slack

Take label dimension $s$, with linear forms
$$
 \ell_1=x_1,\ldots,\ell_s=x_s,\qquad \ell_{s+1}=\sum_{i=1}^s x_i.
$$
Choose positive pairwise distinct multiplicities $m_i$ divisible by $s$. Write $N=\sum_i m_i$. For the determinant-trivial gadget, the matrix pencil is block diagonal with blocks
$$
 M_i(x)=\ell_i(x)I_{m_i}+u(x)J_{m_i},
$$
where $J_{m_i}$ is a single nilpotent Jordan block and $u\ne0$ vanishes on $a=(1,\ldots,1)$. The vector $a$ has all $\ell_i(a)\ne0$, including $\ell_{s+1}(a)=q-1$ in the field. The polynomial determinant is $\prod_i\ell_i^{m_i}$. Under a stabilizer its label action must fix the projective hyperplanes individually, by unique factorization and distinct multiplicities. Fixing the coordinate hyperplanes gives a diagonal map, and fixing the sum hyperplane makes it scalar. The conclusion uses the formal polynomial over $F$.

An automorphism with label fixed is understood as a pair $G,H$ on the two matrix spaces with $H M(x)=M(x)G$. Normalize at $M(a)$, which is invertible. It identifies the two actions and makes the action on the domain commute with all normalized operators. The generalized eigencharacters $\ell_i/\ell_i(a)$ are distinct, so the commuting action preserves each block. More explicitly, between any two distinct blocks one can choose $x$ at which their two normalized eigenvalues differ, and the intertwining equation for that operator makes the off-diagonal block zero. Each block has a normalized operator with nonzero Jordan coefficient, so its commutant is $F[J_{m_i}]$. A unit of it has determinant the $m_i$-th power of its constant term, equal to one. The argument applies simultaneously to both matrix modes. Scalar label actions have determinant one on the label, and can be compensated with a scalar matrix in one matrix mode, whose determinant is $1$ because $s\mid N$. Thus the entire determinant image is trivial. Since arbitrary sufficiently large multiples $N$ of $s$ are a sum of $s+1$ distinct positive multiples of $s$, the shape is flexible as claimed. For example take $m_i=si$ for the first $s$ and take the last much larger than these.

Omitting the $u J_{m_i}$ terms creates the inverse-pair slack. The same eigencharacter argument leaves independent invertible matrices on the blocks. Since the two matrix actions are inverse-transposes in the covariant convention, after the anchor identification, the determinant image is exactly
$$
 \{(1,d,d^{-1}):d\in F^\times\}.
$$
It is onto this subgroup by placing any desired determinant on one block.

These gadgets are concise: the coordinate piece spans the labels and the invertible anchor witnesses conciseness in each matrix mode. The scalar-label property also proves indecomposability. If $e$ is a centroid idempotent, choose $c\in F^\times\setminus\{1\}$ and act by $I+(c-1)e$ in the label, and by the inverse of the corresponding centroid member in one matrix mode. This is a tensor automorphism since the two endomorphism actions on the tensor coincide and commute. Its label action must be scalar. Hence the idempotent in the label is scalar, so is $0$ or $I$. Conciseness forces the other two idempotents to match. The argument holds over every field extension.

## 3. Weighted two-mode slack from two Kronecker blocks

Here is a construction for any residue $r$ modulo $s=q-1$. Start with the regular Jordan blocks in Section 2, and add to that matrix pencil a direct sum of two rectangular blocks sharing the same label space. Write
$$
 K_a(x) = x_1 [I_a\mid 0]+x_2[0\mid I_a],
$$
of size $a\times(a+1)$. Use $K_a(x)$ and $K_b(x)^{\mathsf T}$ with positive $a\equiv r\pmod{s}$ and $b\equiv-r-1\pmod{s}$. The two matrix mode sizes are again equal, and their dimensions are divisible by $s$. The rectangular blocks together have row and column size $a+b+1$ and maximum formal matrix rank $a+b$. The full pencil is concise: the regular piece is label-concise, and each rectangular block has zero joint kernel and its collective images span the output.

The gcd of all maximal nonzero minors of this enlarged pencil is $\prod_i\ell_i^{m_i}$ up to a constant. In any nonzero maximal minor each diagonal block must contribute its full normal rank, because row and column index counts per block must agree and those normal ranks sum to the full rank. The regular blocks thus contribute their determinants. The maximal minors of $K_a$ include $x_1^a,x_2^a$, and those of $K_b^{\mathsf T}$ include $x_1^b,x_2^b$, so each family has gcd one. Every product of a maximal minor from each block appears as a full-pencil maximal minor, giving the asserted gcd. Constant invertible matrix-mode changes preserve the gcd up to scalar by Cauchy–Binet in both directions. Thus every label automorphism is scalar, by the same unique hyperplanes and multiplicities as in Section 2. This also gives indecomposability as a tensor by the centroid argument there, even though the *fixed-label matrix representation* has several summands.

For fixed labels, view the block pencil as a representation of the quiver with two vertices and $s$ arrows. The regular blocks have local endomorphism rings $F[J_{m_i}]$, with mutually distinct normalized eigencharacters. The two Kronecker blocks each have scalar endomorphism. For $K_a$, write $G$ on the $a+1$ columns and $H$ on the $a$ rows. The equations for $x_1$ and $x_2$ say that the first $a$ rows of $G$ are $[H\mid0]$ and the last $a$ rows are $[0\mid H]$. Their overlaps give $H_{i,j}=H_{i-1,j-1}$ for $i,j\ge2$, with zero borders above and below the diagonal. This forces $H$, then $G$, scalar. Transposition gives the same for the other block. The two dimension vectors are unequal to one another and to the square regular dimension vectors, so all listed indecomposables are nonisomorphic.

Their direct-sum endomorphism ring has semisimple quotient the product of their residue fields $F$. Here one can use the standard Krull–Schmidt description of the endomorphism ring. Explicitly, take each diagonal block endomorphism modulo its own radical. Cross terms in products have zero residue, since a composite through a different indecomposable cannot be invertible. This gives a homomorphism onto a product of $F$'s. Its kernel consists of nilpotent endomorphisms, by Fitting's lemma and uniqueness of indecomposable decompositions. Indeed a nonnilpotent kernel element would have an idempotent polynomial with zero constant term projecting to a nonzero invertible Fitting summand, also of zero residue. By Krull–Schmidt that projector is conjugate to a nonzero diagonal summand projector, which has nonzero residue. The resulting kernel therefore affects neither determinant of a unit. Each unit is a product of block scalars and a unit $I+R$ for nilpotent $R$. The regular scalar determinants are trivial; for $K_a$ they contribute exponents $(a,a+1)$ on rows and columns before taking one inverse for the physical tensor convention. Up to multiplying the generator by $-1$, the two-mode determinant exponents are $(r+1,-r)$. The second Kronecker block contributes the opposite generator because $b\equiv-r-1$. Scalar label corrections are trivial on determinants as above. Consequently the determinant image is exactly the cyclic subgroup generated in exponent coordinates by $(0,r+1,-r)$ up to reordering the last two slots. Taking long regular block sizes arbitrarily large makes each gadget different in format from all other blocks being added.

For $F=\mathbb F_4$, put $s=3$. Taking $r=1$ gives the equal-pair line because $(2,-1)=(2,2)$ modulo 3. The inverse-pair line is already available from Section 2. Together with single-coordinate slack this realizes every line on support at most two. Every plane in $\mathbb F_3^3$ has a basis of such vectors: for a normal of support one use the two free coordinates; for support two use a supported relation and the remaining free coordinate; for support three use $\alpha_2e_1-\alpha_1e_2$ and $\alpha_3e_1-\alpha_1e_3$. There are thirteen planes since the nonzero normals are identified up to the two invertible scalars. This proves the twenty-four subgroup claims.

More generally, if $q-1=\ell$ is prime, the same argument gives all two-supported lines. A two-supported vector of nonzero sum can be scaled to $(r+1,-r)$, and a vector of zero sum is covered by the inverse-pair gadget. This yields $3\ell$ lines, all $\ell^2+\ell+1$ planes, and the two endpoints.

## 4. The remaining sign line over F3

For $F=\mathbb F_3$, the foregoing already gives the six lines of support one or two, all seven planes and the endpoints. To obtain the line consisting of $(1,1,1),(-1,-1,-1)$, use the earlier even-side graph pencil with trace color. Here is the specific construction and stabilizer argument, so only its relevant small portion is needed.

Let $E=\mathbb F_9$, with Frobenius $\sigma(z)=z^3$, let $m$ be any even positive integer at least two, and put $K(z)w=zw$. Let $D$ be the adjacency matrix of the directed path on $m$ vertices. Use the $(m+2)$-square pencil
$$
 M(x,z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \mathrm{diag}(x)+\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F}(z)D
     &{\bf1}\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F}\bigl(\sigma(z)\,\cdot\bigr)\\
 \sigma(z){\bf1}^{\mathsf T}&K(z)
 \end{pmatrix}.
$$
The tensor is concise: the $m$ top coordinate slices cover their top input and output lines, and at $z=1$ the quotient multiplication matrix is invertible. The rank-one labels are exactly the displayed $x_i$-lines, as $z\ne0$ would already give an invertible $2\times2$ bottom block. Their images and coimages mark the matching coordinate lines on the matrix modes. A stabilizer hence permutes these lines by one common permutation $\pi$, with top scalings $a_i,b_i\in F^\times$ on the two matrix modes, and $a_i b_i$ in the label under the convention
$$
 M(\varphi(x,z))=hM(x,z)k^*,
$$
where $*$ denotes the adjoint for the trace pairing on $E$ and the standard pairing on the top. The physical determinant triple uses $\varphi^{-\mathsf T},h,k$.

On the three quotient copies of $E$, the maps $A,B,C$ for $h,k,\varphi$ obey $A K(z)B^*=K(Cz)$. At $z=1$, $AB^*=K(c)$ with $c=C1\ne0$. Thus $A K(z)A^{-1}=K(c^{-1}Cz)$. Since conjugation preserves multiplication and the identity, $c^{-1}C=\tau$ is either the identity or $\sigma$. It follows that $A=a\tau$, $B=b\tau$, $C=ab\tau$, for $a,b\in E^\times$ and $ab=c$. The upper-right equation now compares a multiple $b a_i$ of $\sigma(\tau z)$, plus $b\tau(\alpha_i)\tau(z)$ if the corresponding shear of $h$ has row functional $\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha_i\cdot)$, to $\sigma(c)\sigma(\tau z)$. Since $z\mapsto z$ and $z\mapsto z^3$ are $E$-linearly independent, the shear is zero and $b a_i=c^3$; the lower-left equation similarly gives zero shear and $a b_i=c^3$. Thus $a_i b_i=c^5\in F^\times$. The fifth-power map is invertible on $E^\times$, of order eight, so $c\in F^\times$, and then $a,b\in F^\times$, $a_i=a$, $b_i=b$.

The top-left diagonal equation forces the label shear to vanish. The off-diagonal entries then force $\pi$ to preserve the directed path, since the trace color is fixed by $\tau$ and scales by $c$. The path is rigid, so $\pi=1$. Conversely either $\tau$ with the displayed block maps is a stabilizer. Because $m+2$ is even the common scalars give determinant one, and Frobenius on $E/F$ has determinant $-1$; this gives exactly the desired line. With the whole label map fixed, the matrix actions are common scalars. A centroid idempotent acting via $I+e$ in one matrix mode and its corresponding inverse in the other gives such a stabilizer; conciseness then forces the idempotent either zero or identity. Hence this gadget is indecomposable. Take $m+2$ beyond all extant sides.

The use of many-one downward closures follows the notation of [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, §1.5 and Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5). The proof produces intermediate descriptions and mutually comparable reduction classes. It leaves strictness and equality with endpoints open.
