# Structural subclasses of special-linear tensor isomorphism

Fix a finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$ and use deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions. Write $\mathrm{TI}$ and $\mathrm{SL\text{-}TI}$ for the corresponding raw 3-tensor decision problems with unrestricted side dimensions. For a polynomial-time recognizable tensor family $\mathcal P$, write $\mathrm{SL}_{\mathcal P}$ for the decision language that asks for a determinant-one transporter on each mode when both tensors are in $\mathcal P$. It rejects pairs outside that input family. The associated class is the many-one lower closure of this language. In particular a malformed input can be mapped to a fixed negative instance. The following examples use concise tensors throughout their hard embeddings.

1. **Balanced concise SL-TI.** Take $\mathcal P$ to be concise tensors with equal side lengths.
2. **Absolutely indecomposable SL-TI.** Take $\mathcal P$ to be concise tensors with no nontrivial direct summand over an algebraic closure.
3. **Scalar-centroid SL-TI.** Use concise tensors with scalar geometric centroid.
4. **Scalar-adjoint low-max-rank SL-TI.** Use the recognizable alternating suspensions below with $k=2$. The fixed-label pair adjoint is scalar in every choice of label mode, and the square matrix direction has geometric max-rank $O(\sqrt n)$, where $n$ here is the square matrix side.
5. **All-mode square-root max-rank SL-TI.** Use the raw recognizable three-arm family below. These tensors are balanced and absolutely indecomposable, and all three geometric max-ranks are $O(\sqrt n)$.
6. **Scalar-centroid all-mode square-root SL-TI.** Use the mixed alternating and symmetric arm construction below and impose a scalar geometric centroid. This construction is for odd characteristic.
7. **Unequal-format two-summand SL-TI.** Use concise tensors with exactly two absolutely indecomposable geometric direct summands of different formats.
8. **Equal-format two-summand SL-TI.** Use concise tensors with exactly two absolutely indecomposable geometric direct summands of identical formats. Their isomorphism types may differ.

Each associated class is in the closed interval from $\mathsf{TI}$ to $\mathsf{TI}_{\mathrm{SL}}$. The structural definitions differ. Distinctness of their many-one classes is unproved.

## Two standard ingredients

Alternating matrix-space isometry is TI-hard over $F$, by the linear-length reduction from 3-TI. Its output is a subspace of alternating matrices, with congruence as equivalence. We use arbitrary such matrix spaces $\mathcal D$ as sources. Adding a fixed number of zero rows and columns to the congruence space preserves and reflects congruence. Indeed an isometry maps the common radical to the common radical. The forms descend to the quotient, and a quotient isometry yields an isometry after extending by any identification of the radicals of equal dimension. This handles original spaces with nonzero common radical as well. For inequality of dimensions or zero spaces, decide the case directly. Nonzero spaces of dimension bounded by a constant are likewise decidable at fixed $q$. All of the target families contain positive and negative instances under the respective rules, so these cases can map to fixed answers. [Grochow and Qiao, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317v2#S2)

For $B$ carrying such a nonzero space $\mathcal D$, take $A$ of dimension $a>2b$, where $b=\dim B\ge3$. The alternating suspension is the subspace
$$
 S_-(\mathcal D)=
 \left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&C\\-C^{\mathsf T}&D\end{pmatrix}
 : C\in F^{a\times b},\ D\in\mathcal D\right\}.
$$
It is concise as a tensor when described using an independent list of slices. The fixed-label adjoint algebra of these matrices consists of pairs $g,h$, possibly singular, with $hM=Mg$ for all $M$. Computing with the elementary cross forms forces $h,g$ diagonal, since the range planes for three or more coordinate neighbors intersect in the corresponding single coordinate line. The cross equations fix two common diagonal scalars, one on each bipartite part, and a nonzero bottom form makes them equal. This proof holds over every extension. In particular equivalence of these spaces under left-right actions reduces to congruence: if $H M K^{\mathsf T}$ is alternating for every $M$, then $(K^{-1}H)M=M(H^{\mathsf T}K^{-\mathsf T})$, forcing $H$ proportional to $K$. The large common totally isotropic subspace is uniquely $A$. In fact all cross forms vanish on the vectors $(x,y),(x',y')$ exactly when $x\otimes y'=x'\otimes y$. This implies a common isotropic space of dimension at least two is wholly in one of the two parts. The subspace of forms annihilating $A$ descends to $\mathcal D$ on the quotient. Hence the suspension reflects source congruence.

Here is the recognition routine needed for all the suspension variants. Solve the linear system for an output change making the matrices alternating, and check that the solution line is one-dimensional with invertible generator. The scalar adjoint condition forces this exact solution line on every true input. In the recovered alternating space over a polynomial-cardinality extension, iteratively increase the rank of a current form $M$ until its radical is common totally isotropic. If a form $N$ has nonzero restriction to that radical, find two vectors there paired nontrivially by $N$. The restriction of $M+tN$ to these vectors together with a nondegenerate complement to the radical has a determinant with nonzero lowest coefficient of $t^2$. Scan all $t$ over an extension larger than the ambient side dimension. Each success raises rank. On a true suspension every matrix has rank at most $2b$, so the final radical has dimension at least $a-b>b$, lies in $A$, and is nonzero. Its common perpendicular under the whole matrix space is $A$ because of the full cross forms. Check this rational subspace on the base field by requiring its reduced echelon basis to descend. Check its dimension and common isotropy. In an arbitrary complement, verify full cross projection and a nonzero bottom space given by its kernel. The adjustment of every bottom form under complement shear is linear in the shear, so solve to place all bottom forms in that kernel space. Verify the resulting exact template by checking support and dimension. This accepts the whole left-right orbit closure and only that closure. At fixed $q$ constructing an extension of polynomial cardinality can be done by enumerating monic polynomials of logarithmic degree and testing irreducibility. All the other operations are polynomial-time linear algebra.

In odd characteristic the symmetric suspension $S_+(\mathcal D)$, with $+C^{\mathsf T}$ and symmetric bottom forms, has the same dimension, rank bound and scalar fixed-label adjoint. The adjoint and symmetry arguments use transpose instead of skew-transpose. Common isotropic spaces still lie in one part: a vector with both parts nonzero does not have zero value on itself for all cross forms, since $2\ne0$. The raw recognition routine applies with symmetry instead of alternation. A nonzero restriction of a symmetric form to the radical gives a radical vector with nonzero self-value by polarization. Restricting to that one radical vector and the complement produces a nonzero coefficient of $t$, so a rank increase again exists.

## Balanced, indecomposable and square-root targets

Start from the arbitrary hard space $\mathcal D$. Enlarge its common radical if necessary so that $b=\dim B\ge3$ is divisible by $q-1$. Set $a=3b$. Let the small space in each tensor mode be $A\oplus B$, of dimension $a+b$. A large complementary space in each mode has dimension
$$
 m=ab+\dim\mathcal D .
$$
In $U$, put the tensor of the injective slice map $U_1^*\to V_0\otimes W_0$ with image $S_-(\mathcal D)$; put the same arm cyclically on $V_1^*$, $W_1^*$. Keep the core on $U_0\otimes V_0\otimes W_0$ zero on these hard instances. The target recognizable family allows arbitrary core and independent suspension spaces in each arm with the same dimensions. Its iso closure is intended. Every linear contraction has rank at most $2(a+b)$ since the large-by-large block in each contraction vanishes. Since the balanced side length is $n=a+b+m=\Theta(b^2)$, this is $O(\sqrt n)$. Conciseness follows from injectivity on a large label space and the absence of a common radical in each corresponding cross-form system on active small labels. The exponent is the known optimal simultaneous order of max-ranks on concise balanced tensors. [Briët, Christandl, Leigh, Shpilka and Zuiddam, Theorem 1.13](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.01718v4#S1.SS3)

The small spaces are recovered from raw coordinates by fixed-degree minor extraction. On contractions along $U$, form the span of homogeneous degree-$p^j$ monomial multiples of $3\times3$ minors, where $p=\operatorname{char}F$ and $p^j$ is the first power at least three. Using linear algebra on pure powers and inverse scalar Frobenius, find all linear forms with $p^j$-th powers in this span. Their joint kernel $H$ contains every rank-at-most-two label. On the template it lies within $U_1^*$: for each small coordinate $x_i$ pick three elementary cross forms in the $V_1$-arm involving the index $i$ and three distinct indices opposite it, with columns or rows on those three opposite coordinates. Their off-diagonal contraction block gives a diagonal $3\times3$ matrix with diagonal entries $\pm x_i$, hence a pure cube. On the other hand every cross-form label from the $U_1$-arm is rank two and belongs to $H$. The two factor supports of the slices at $H$ thus identify all of $V_0,W_0$. Repeat cyclically. This procedure commutes with basis changes by Cauchy–Binet and invariance of the homogeneous multiplier space. Exact membership is checked using any complements, the indicated support pattern, injectivity of the arms and the individual suspension recognizers. Choice of complements can change only the allowed core.

An ordinary isomorphism preserves the intrinsic small spaces and hence gives a left-right equivalence on an arm, reflecting $\mathcal D$. For absolute indecomposability, the centroid consists of triples $c_U,c_V,c_W$ whose three individual mode actions on the tensor agree. Over the algebraic closure, such a triple preserves the small spaces. For generic $t$, acting by $I+t c_U$ on the first mode and by $(I+t c_V)^{-1}$ on the second fixes the tensor, so both maps preserve the intrinsic small spaces. The same argument covers the third. On each arm, the equalities of the two small-space actions are fixed-label adjoint equations. All three small-space actions must be a common scalar, and injectivity of the arms makes the quotient actions equal to that scalar as well. After subtracting it, each residual map sends its large quotient into its small space and has square zero. Thus the centroid has no nontrivial idempotent. A direct summand would give one by projection, proving the claim.

To get a special-linear isomorphism on a positive hard pair, take a congruence $Y$ on $B$ of the source alternating spaces and take any $X\in\mathrm{GL}(A)$ on the cross-factor auxiliary space. The same small map $X\oplus Y$ is used in each small mode. The induced quotient map is inverse dual to its action on the corresponding matrix space, whose determinant is
$$
 (\det X)^b(\det Y)^a \det\rho_{\mathcal D}(Y),
$$
where $\rho_{\mathcal D}(Y)$ denotes the induced linear congruence map from the ordered slice basis of $\mathcal D$ to the target slice basis. This formula uses independent ordered cross-matrix units and bottom bases. The determinant of each full tensor-mode map is consequently
$$
 (\det X)^{1-b}(\det Y)^{1-a}\bigl(\det\rho_{\mathcal D}(Y)\bigr)^{-1}.
$$
Since $1-b\equiv1\bmod(q-1)$, choose $\det X$ to make this product one. Such $X$ exists by taking a diagonal entry of the desired scalar. The hard embeddings use this choice for all three modes at once. This gives $\mathrm{TI}\le_m^p\mathrm{SL}_{\mathcal P}$ for each of the balanced, absolutely indecomposable and square-root families. All three upper inclusions use recognition followed by the ordinary SL-TI instance.

Absolute indecomposability of an arbitrary concise tensor can be recognized directly without recovering its summands. In bilinear-map convention the centroid equations are $m(c_Ux,y)=m(x,c_Vy)=c_Wm(x,y)$, polynomial-size linear equations. This algebra is commutative: evaluating $m(c_Ux,d_Vy)$ in both orders makes the output actions commute on the full image span, and conciseness then implies commutativity on the two input factors. In the finite commutative algebra $C$, the kernel of $z\mapsto z^{q^j}$ for sufficiently large $q^j$ is the nilradical, and this power map is linear over $F$. The geometric semisimple quotient has as many primitive idempotents as the dimension of the original semisimple quotient over $F$. A nontrivial direct sum corresponds exactly to a nontrivial centroid idempotent, by taking the simultaneous images and kernels in the three modes. Thus the absolute condition is the polynomial-time rank test $\dim(C/\sqrt0)=1$. This also justifies using the wider primitive family in the upper inclusion.

## Scalar-adjoint targets with a low-rank direction

Use the suspension $S_-(\mathcal D)$ itself as a tensor with two square matrix modes and a label mode. After padding, require $b\equiv0\bmod(q-1)$ and at least one new common radical coordinate of $\mathcal D$. Let $a=b^k+2b+1$ for fixed $k\ge1$. The full geometric max-rank in the square matrix direction is at most $2b=O((a+b)^{1/k})$. The alternating symmetry and the proved scalar adjoint in the fixed slice-label view imply scalar adjoints in the two remaining views. Indeed an endomorphism $f$ on the label sort and $h$ on the other matrix sort that commutes with every evaluation satisfies $f(M)=hM$ for all $M$. Since $f(M)$ is alternating, $hM=Mh^{\mathsf T}$; scalar adjoint forces $h$, hence $f$, scalar. The third view follows by transposition. All tests of scalar adjoint are linear kernels with the same dimension after field extension.

A bottom-space congruence $Y$ can be chosen with any prescribed determinant because scaling one new radical coordinate does not change $\rho_{\mathcal D}(Y)$. For the square matrix maps $X\oplus Y$ choose $\det X=(\det Y)^{-1}$. This makes their determinants one. The determinant of the label map is one exactly when
$$
 (\det Y)^{a-b}\det\rho_{\mathcal D}(Y)=1 .
$$
The exponent $a-b=b^k+b+1$ is $1\bmod(q-1)$; the remaining radical scale attains the required value. Ordinary equivalence of suspensions reflects source congruence. Recognition then proves the upper reduction to SL-TI. This also proves the interval claim for the broader scalar-centroid family. Indeed any centroid element supplies a fixed-label pair adjoint in two slots, and injectivity of the remaining flattening forces that slot to have the same scalar action.

## A mixed-sign balanced target with scalar centroid

Assume $q$ is odd. Take the same three-arm pattern but use two alternating suspensions and one symmetric suspension, each recognized on its own arm. Impose the additional scalar centroid condition by linear algebra. The raw recognition and max-rank upper bound apply without change: the pure cubes use cross-form support and only the sign of a diagonal entry varies. The scalar centroid condition implies absolute indecomposability.

For hardness, start from a hard alternating space $\mathcal D_0$ on $B_0$ and pad with zero radical coordinates if necessary. Take $B=B_0\oplus B_0$ with $b\equiv0\bmod(q-1)$, and $A$ of dimension $3b$. For each of the two alternating arms the bottom subspace has forms $\mathrm{diag}(D,0)$. For the symmetric arm use bottom forms
$$
 \begin{pmatrix}0&D\\D^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad D\in\mathcal D_0 .
$$
All three spaces have the same dimension. A congruence $Y_0$ of the input acts by $Y=Y_0\oplus Y_0$ on the small $B$ factors and induces the same coefficient-space determinant on each bottom space. The common $X$ on $A$ can therefore neutralize all three mode determinants by the same formula above. Any target isomorphism reflects $\mathcal D_0$ on an alternating arm, since padding by a common radical preserves and reflects congruence.

It remains to check the scalar centroid on this hard embedding. The centroid argument above makes every map one common scalar modulo its shear. A residual centroid shear must induce the same core tensor when applied to each arm. In the common coordinates of the construction, that core tensor is antisymmetric under the transposition of the two small slots in each of the alternating arms and symmetric under the transposition for the symmetric arm. A transposition of the latter pair is the conjugate of one of the former transpositions by the other. Hence the two antisymmetries also impose antisymmetry in the symmetric pair. The tensor must vanish since the characteristic is odd. Injectivity of the arms then forces all three shears zero. This proves scalar geometric centroid for the hard instances and concludes TI-hardness of the special-linear mixed-sign problem.

## Two geometric summands

A concise tensor decomposes over $\overline F$ into intrinsic indecomposable summands via the primitive centroid idempotents, as proved by the centroid argument above. In particular exactly two summands is equivalent to $\dim_F C/\sqrt0=2$. The $q$-fixed subalgebra of $C$ is computed by solving $x^q=x$ as a linear equation over $F$. Each residue field factor contributes exactly one base-field dimension, and the nilradical has no nonzero solutions. One may see this by the finite decomposition of $C$ into local algebras, lifting each fixed residue by its corresponding scalar times the local identity. Hence the $q$-fixed space has dimension two in the split case and dimension one when one quadratic field factor remains. In the split case its $q^2$ elements can be enumerated to find the two primitive idempotents, and the ranks of their actions in each mode give the two formats. In the nonsplit case Frobenius exchanges the geometric idempotents, so the formats necessarily coincide. Thus both families are raw recognizable in polynomial time without testing whether the summands themselves are isomorphic.

Take the balanced, absolutely indecomposable input model $S(T)$ from the square-root construction. To reduce to the unequal-format target, add a common $1\times1\times1$ rank-one summand. The hard model has side length greater than one. Its summand is distinguished by its format, and this remains true over all extensions. Any ordinary isomorphism between such outputs restricts to an ordinary isomorphism of the two hard models, so it reflects $\mathrm{TI}$. Conversely any ordinary input isomorphism produces determinant-one transporters of the two hard models by the balanced construction. Extend by identity on the rank-one summand, giving determinant one on each ambient sum. For the equal-format target, use $S(T)\oplus S(T)$. An ordinary output isomorphism permutes the two geometric primitive summands, and they are both individually defined over $F$ in this reduction. It therefore reflects ordinary equivalence of the hard models over $F$. A determinant-one transporter on the hard models extends block-diagonally using the same transporter on both copies. This gives the other lower inclusion. In both cases the upper reduction is the original SL-TI instance after the recognition test.

## A genuine constraint on the underlying relation

Over $\mathbb F_3$, the recognizable all-alternating square-root family includes raw ordinarily equivalent pairs with no special-linear transporter. In the zero-core template with each arm using the same suspension take the matrix-space dimension $m$ even, for example $b=4$ and $\dim\mathcal D=2$. The small side $a+b$ is also even. For any automorphism the intrinsic small maps on each pair of modes are proportional by the adjoint argument for left-right equivalence of alternating spaces. Write them as three scalar multiples of a single $g$. Their scalar multiples disappear in the physical determinants on both the small spaces and the large label spaces, because both dimensions are even. The resulting determinant in each mode is $\det(g)/\det((g\otimes g)|S_-(\mathcal D))$, the same in all three modes. A reflection in only one mode gives the asserted pair. The example separates relations on common coordinates; it does not separate many-one complexity classes.

All these conclusions are informal reductions. They refine earlier ordinary tensor recognition and hardness proofs by absorbing determinant corrections within the concise construction.
