# A mode-swap condition between one and two signs

Work over F_3. Write A for one-sign TI in the first mode, B for two-sign TI in the first and second modes, and J for the conjunction of two independent A comparisons. Write O for ordinary TI. All reductions below are deterministic polynomial-time many-one. Sign bits are additive and zero is positive. A swap of the first two ordered tensor modes is denoted by τ. If those dimensions differ, O(T,τT) is false.

There are two natural decision problems using this raw permutation test. The first is a conjunction with an ordinary bypass for one sign,

    G(T,S) = A_x(T,S) AND (A_y(T,S) OR O(T,τT)).

The second is the restriction of shared two-sign comparison to sources admitting that swap,

    C(T,S) = B(T,S) AND O(T,τT).

The results are A ≤ G ≡ J ≤ B and A ≤ C ≤ B. The first equality means G is another complete problem for the existing independent-conjunction degree. The second problem is exactly the earlier correction predicate N in a new structural presentation. Its collapse to either endpoint remains unresolved. Its outer assumptions impose a structural restriction on the source rather than just renaming determinant characters. In a specified affine compiler model the two reverse arrows on either side of C both fail. No unrestricted complexity separation is claimed.

## Turn an ordinary comparison into an available sign correction

Here is the algebra construction needed for the bypass. The earlier one-sign compiler builds a finite-dimensional unital algebra R(X) from a bounded-order tensor system X. Algebra isomorphisms between the compiled structures recover exactly the system transporters, and the determinant of an algebra transporter equals the sign on a designated original role. Use a dummy designated one-dimensional role pinned by a nonzero vector. Consequently every algebra isomorphism between R(X) and R(Y) has determinant one, including relative isomorphisms in their fixed frames. Tensor comparisons are single-system components. Coordinate orders, diagram signatures and dimensions are the same at both endpoints of a same-format comparison. A mismatch can instead be replaced by a fixed ordinary negative pair of matching formats.

The algebra has a finite set of vertex idempotents and tagged diagonal corners F_3[t_v]/(t_v^(N_v)). All tag dimensions in the earlier proof are distinct and are 1 modulo 4. Choose them instead from residues 0 or 1 modulo 4, so that the total dimension of R(X) is odd. Changing one tag suffices. The number of vertices and each off-diagonal dimension depend only on the data format, so make the same choice independently on both objects. An automorphism of a diagonal corner sends t_v to c t_v modulo its square, so its determinant is c^(N_v(N_v−1)/2), also one at residue 0 modulo 4. None of the signs on off-diagonal blocks changes. The idempotent and relative determinant proof of the earlier compiler therefore remains valid. In particular inner alignment of idempotents by a radical unit is unipotent on the filtration and contributes no sign.

Each R(X) is indecomposable as a direct product. A central idempotent commutes with the named vertices, so lies in their diagonal corners. Each diagonal tag ring is local, so the corresponding component of that central idempotent is zero or the entire vertex identity. Commutation with any nonzero off-diagonal corner forces the two endpoint choices to agree. The compiler's arrows connect all vertices through its main vertices O and P. This remains true if an input tensor component is zero because the scalar anchor arrows and projection probes are present independently of tensor values. Hence the central idempotent is zero or one.

Use the multiplication algebra D(X,Y)=R(X)×R(Y). Its central decomposition has exactly the two displayed factors. When O(X,Y) fails, every automorphism fixes both factors and has positive determinant by the dummy-role proof. When O(X,Y) holds, an isomorphism of the factors and its inverse define an automorphism exchanging the equal odd-dimensional spaces. Its determinant is −1, since the permutation of the two full summands contributes −1 and the two relative maps have opposite products equal to one. The determinant image of Aut(D(X,Y)) is therefore {+1} or {+1,−1} according to the ordinary comparison.

For its multiplication tensor, let a,b,c be the maps of an ordinary autotopism, satisfying a(x)b(y)=c(xy). Substitution at the unit gives units u=a(1), v=b(1) and a unital algebra automorphism φ with a=L_uφ, b=R_vφ, c=L_uR_vφ. Thus the product of the three physical signs equals det φ because the unit signs occur twice. Every algebra automorphism extends by u=v=1. The exact linear sign projection from the earlier work turns the self-comparison of that multiplication tensor into an ordinary-positive comparison with only one live sign. The image on that axis is zero when O(X,Y) is false and the entire one-bit space when it is true. This construction uses no ordinary TI oracle. ## The bypass problem and its exact degree

Given T,S, the sign profile of a comparison is the set of determinant tuples of its actual ordinary transporters. Project the profile of T,S to the y-axis. Add the independent correction profile above with X=T and Y=τT. Tagged direct-sum assembly makes this a real one-sign comparison. If the ordinary T,S profile is nonempty, it tests whether y can be zero or the swap test is true. If the ordinary profile is empty, the Minkowski sum is empty. This causes no issue in G because its first clause is A_x(T,S), which already requires an ordinary transporter. Combine the two resulting one-sign comparisons independently. This gives G ≤ J ≤ B. The exact projection to x with y always zero gives A ≤ G.

In fact J reduces back to G. Start with the two independent one-sign input comparisons packed on the two physical axes. Attach a single determinant-neutral indecomposable rectangular multiplication tensor with its format chosen larger than all previous sides in every coordinate, using the same coordinates on both endpoints. Choose its first/second dimension difference to have absolute value larger than the absolute difference of the previous sides. This can be arranged by taking the three even rectangular parameters all large, with the first and last differing by two and the middle sufficiently large. The original axes retain their profiles because the padding block is rigid and the centroid distinguishes it by its format. The enlarged first and second dimensions differ, so its swap guard is false. Its G-test now imposes exactly the original two separate sign constraints on the packed comparison. This proves G ≡ J and avoids presenting it as an extra many-one degree. ## A check that the bypass is genuinely structural

We next show that the swap guard can change the answer even with the same raw format and the same singleton determinant profile. For positive integers p,q,r write M(p,q,r) for the tensor of the rectangular bilinear map Mat_(p×q) × Mat_(q×r) → Mat_(p×r). Its ordered format is (pq,qr,pr). Every such tensor is concise. Its centroid is scalar. To see this, let A,B,C be a centroid triple satisfying C(XY)=A(X)Y=X B(Y). Test X=e_(i,a),Y=e_(b,j). For a≠b, the products are zero, so A(e_(i,a)) has no columns other than a and B(e_(b,j)) has no rows other than b. For a=b the equality forces these remaining vectors to be scalar multiples of the original matrix units. The scalars agree on the paired row and column through each shared a. Finally C(e_(i,j)) must be independent of which a was used to produce that matrix unit, so all scalars agree. This proves the centroid claim and makes each multiplication tensor a primitive direct-sum block.

If p,q,r are all even, its full stabilizer sign image is zero. One can verify this directly using the earlier exact edge-inflation theorem. Treat M(p,q,r) as the scalar tensor inflated along the first/output edge by p, first/second edge by q, and second/output edge by r. In the last enlargement r is even, so all previous second/output signs are erased. Its fixed-label correction algebra is computed from the pencil before that last enlargement. With the first mode as label, the p and q factors give the entire space of rectangular p by q slice matrices between spaces of dimensions q and p. Its fixed-label endomorphism algebra is the single scalar field. Indeed the only pairs satisfying D M = M C for every matrix unit M have D,C equal scalars. The inflated algebra on multiplicities acts with determinant powers q and p on the two enlarged modes, both even. Thus no correction survives in either. The only sign retained from before the last step is the first-mode sign. The preceding even q enlargement erased that sign from the earlier profile, and its fixed-label correction contributes the even exponent p in the first mode. To check this correction, before q the tensor is the identity between first/output spaces of dimension p, with scalar second space. Fixing the output label gives the full row pencil from the first space to the second, whose endomorphism algebra is again scalar. Its q-fold multiplicity action therefore has exponent p in the first space. This establishes zero sign in all three modes. In a direct sum of these blocks a possible exchange of equal-format blocks contributes positive determinants because every block dimension is even. Rigidity is retained. [Edge inflation and exact correction formulas](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorDeterminantFamilies/MatrixFactors.md)

For p=r the first-two-mode swap of M(p,q,p) is ordinary-isomorphic to itself. This is the full three-mode transpose correspondence: after input order is exchanged, transpose maps the new first input from Mat_(q×p) to Mat_(p×q), the new second input in the reverse direction, and the output by square transposition, using (XY)^t=Y^tX^t. This supplies invertible maps in the actual three ordered modes. It does not assert that transposing the output alone or exchanging just the inputs is an autotopism in fixed coordinates.

Take the two rigid sources

    T_sym  = M(4,2,4) ⊕ M(4,2,4),
    T_asym = M(4,2,2) ⊕ M(4,2,6).

Their shared format is (16,16,32). The first source admits its swap by the preceding transpose correspondence on each factor. The second has primitive centroid block formats (8,4,8) and (8,12,24). Swapping first and second changes them to (4,8,8) and (12,8,24). No ordinary transporter can identify these multisets, since a transporter induces a bijection of primitive centroid blocks preserving their ordered formats. Hence the second source does not admit the swap. For each source reflect a single coordinate in only the second mode to obtain its target. All original ordinary stabilizer signs are positive, so both comparisons have sign profile exactly {(0,1,0)}. The G predicate accepts the symmetric case and rejects the asymmetric case. Its behavior cannot be inferred from affine sign profiles and total format alone. The same sources compared to themselves give the analogous fact for C.

## The symmetric-source two-sign restriction

For C ≤ B, absorb the ordinary comparison (T,τT) into a B query using the existing determinant-neutral cube conjunction. On each success the auxiliary ordinary maps have positive signs, so the projected two signs still have to be supplied by one original transporter. A format mismatch in the guard can be mapped to a fixed negative B pair. [Determinant-neutral ordinary conjunction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/NeutralConjunction.md)

For A ≤ C, replace the input pair by the earlier one-axis indecomposable pair U,V of dimensions (b,2b,2b), whose exact profiles are (x,0,0) with x the original sign profile. The one-axis compiler can handle empty ordinary profiles and does not require a supplied transporter. Use as the C comparison

    U ⊕ τU     versus     V ⊕ τU.

The source admits its swap, which simply exchanges the two named summands after swapping the physical modes. The summands have distinct ordered formats because b is positive. Their primitive centroid idempotents force any relative transporter to use the displayed distinct formats. The first block contributes (x,0,0), and the second self-comparison contributes only in the second coordinate and always permits the identity. Therefore a transporter positive in both modes exists exactly when x can be zero in the original pair. This proves the lower bound. [One-axis indecomposable compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/CastlingAndConjunction.md)

The defining source restriction has a further concrete consequence. If an ordinary tensor admits a swap of its first two modes, its projected stabilizer sign subgroup is invariant under exchanging its two coordinates. In fact conjugating the ordered-mode automorphism group by any fixed swap transporter implements this exchange on determinant signs. The conjugating coordinates cancel from determinant products. The only invariant projected subgroups are zero, the diagonal line, and the whole two-dimensional space. In particular a one-axis correction subgroup is impossible on a valid C source. That is an intrinsic difference from arbitrary two-sign comparison.

Here is a separation within the one-shot affine readout model. Focus on source/target reflection families parametrized by a nonempty projected profile a+H in the two-bit space. The model compiles it to a nonempty projected profile L a+b+K_H for a fixed linear L and offset b, with K_H a correction group independent of a and containing L H. The output source must not depend on a, so any ordinary swap guard on that source is constant as the target is reflected. The linear maps here are those for the modes whose signs are tested after compilation; fixed filler and extra subgroup choices are included in K_H. Exact projections and independent witness sums satisfy the containment of L H in that model. This does not encompass arbitrary pair-dependent reductions.

Suppose such a compiler reduced B to C. On a rigid H=0, correctness for a=0 makes the new source swap-symmetric. The guard is then true for every target reflection. Correctness says exactly one offset is accepted. The output can accept one offset only if L has rank two and K_0=0. Now let H be any line. Correctness at a=0 again makes the output guard true for all offsets of this reflection family. Exactly two must be accepted. With L invertible, its output K_H must therefore be a line. Since it contains L H, it equals L H. Swap-symmetry forces this line to be the unique invariant diagonal line. Two distinct input lines cannot both map to that line under invertible L. That contradiction proves the restricted lower statement. All source profiles used here are realizable by actual tensors by exact sign projection and reorientation, for example starting with the full sign group of the zero tensor and projecting to a desired subgroup. If an output has an independent third bit it plays no role in the tested projection. The restricted reverse C ≤ A also fails on a rigid symmetric source, such as T_sym above. Reflections in the two tested modes give four offsets, only one of which is accepted by C, while a single affine sign equation can accept either zero, two, or four of those offsets. These arguments prove that C has genuine intermediate behavior in this model. They give no unrestricted reversal or separation.

The construction, mode permutation argument, and affine compiler analysis are informal. Existing neutral conjunction, sign projection, and block assembly proofs are reused. Whether C collapses to an existing unrestricted degree remains open here.

## Exact identification with an earlier intermediate predicate

The structural restriction C in fact has an exact profile description. Let N_τ on a nonempty two-bit profile D=a+H accept if 0 belongs to D or if H is one of the two coordinate axes. Empty profiles reject. Equivalently, N_τ accepts when 0 belongs to D+τH, where τ exchanges two coordinates. When H is zero, diagonal, or full, the latter sum is D. When H is horizontal or vertical, adding τH gives the whole plane.

First, C reduces to N_τ. Absorb its ordinary swap guard into a determinant-neutral conjunct. On guard failure the whole comparison then has empty profile. If the guard succeeds, the original projected H is swap-invariant. All original signs survive the neutral conjunct, and N_τ therefore tests precisely whether the original comparison is positive in both modes.

Conversely, to reduce N_τ to C we need an indecomposable asymmetric-format pair realizing both given bits. The prior two-sign-preserving compiler provides one minor variant with this property. In its notation choose a as the role reading x and b as the direct sum of the two tested roles, linked through its canonical inclusions and projections, so d_a=x and d_b=x+y. Choose diagonal tags using the both-sided projective parity choice proved for the three-live-sign compiler. This makes every left unit action on the regular algebra determinant-positive, while preserving det φ=d_a for algebra isomorphisms. In the two-readout module compiler M=R⊕L, the identity unit determinant then gives physical sign bits (d_a,d_a+d_b,d_a+d_b), namely (x,y,y), before any sign-projection step. Here L is the nonzero left readout module. Thus the ordinary action tensor R×M→M has dimensions (dim R,dim M,dim M) with dim R<dim M, and realizes the exact projected pair (x,y).

This action tensor is concise by faithfulness and the action of the unit. Its centroid is the center of R. To check that fact, write the centroid maps as C_R,C_in,C_out on R and its two module copies. The equations at the unit force C_in=C_out=ρ(C_R(1)), where ρ is the faithful action. The equations at every label r then give ρ(C_R(r))=ρ(r)ρ(C_R(1))=ρ(C_R(1))ρ(r). Faithfulness proves that C_R(1) is central and determines all maps. Conversely any central element gives those maps. The quiver algebra R has no nontrivial central idempotents by the connectivity argument above. Hence each tensor from this pair is an indecomposable centroid block. [Prior module readout compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignChain/RetainedSigns.md)

Denote these two action tensors by U,V. The comparison U⊕τU versus V⊕τU therefore forces the indicated block matching by the two distinct primitive ordered formats, and its source admits its swap. Its two-bit profile is D+τH, since the second summand is an independent self-comparison of τU. Thus its C verdict is precisely N_τ. This proves C ≡ N_τ even in the presence of ordinarily negative input comparisons.

Finally N_τ is the existing correction predicate N up to a fixed invertible linear change of the two signs. Recall that N accepts when 0 belongs to D+QH for Q(x,y)=(0,x). It waives both equations on horizontal and diagonal H, retains the original condition on vertical H, and has the same behavior as N_τ on zero and full H. The invertible map (x,y)↦(x+y,y) sends the distinguished diagonal line of N_τ to the distinguished vertical line of N, and permutes the other two lines. Applying the exact sign projections implements the change in either direction. Hence C is a new structural characterization of the earlier N, not a new unrestricted many-one degree. In particular M ≤ C and C ≤ B follow using the previous intermediate chain. The two nonreductions in the stated affine model also follow directly for N_τ by the same offset and invariant-line arguments.
