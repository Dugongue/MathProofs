# Three-direction sign correction between independent and shared tests

Work over F₃. Let A be one-sign TI, J the conjunction of two independent A comparisons, and B two-sign TI on one transporter. For a raw tensor comparison let D=a+H be its nonempty transporter sign profile in F₂³. H is the sign image of the source stabilizer. Empty profiles reject. Define P by

    0 ∈ π(D) + Q(H), where π(x,y,z)=(x,y) and Q(x,y,z)=(z,x).

Then J ≤ₘᵖ P ≤ₘᵖ B. The upper reduction uses one projected relative comparison and one projected self-comparison, combined by exact tagged summation. The construction uses raw tensors throughout. [Prior sign projections](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/SignProjections.md)

For a nonzero line generator h=(x,y,z), the directions π(h) and Q(h) span the whole sign plane exactly when x²−yz is nonzero. The three exceptions are 001, 010 and 111. Their generators are independent in the input space and leave three distinct output lines, respectively horizontal, vertical and diagonal. Every subspace of dimension at least two contains a nonexceptional line, so its correction spans the whole plane.

For J ≤ₘᵖ P, independently place the two one-bit profiles on the input lines generated by 010 and 111, using the exact sign compiler. Their projected directions are independent. If neither input sign is flexible, P accepts precisely the two zero signs. If one is flexible, its direction is unchanged by correction and the other sign must be zero. If both are flexible, P accepts. An ordinary negative in either comparison still rejects.

## Reverse comparisons in the affine-copy model

A branch with fixed affine offset readout L a+b has output direction K_H independent of a and containing L H. This includes fixed projections and sums of independent relative and self witnesses.

One branch cannot reduce P to J. On H=0, acceptance requires x=y=0. The two J readouts must therefore be independent and both binding. For the three exceptional lines, the accepted offsets are y=0, x=0 and x+y=0, respectively. Each such hyperplane must be enforced by one of the two fixed readouts, which cannot supply three distinct normals.

Two J branches combined by OR suffice. The first reads (x+y,x) on the relative profile. The second reads y on the relative profile and the independent bit profile 1+(x+z)(H). On H=0 and the 010 and 111 lines the first is exact and the second rejects. On the 001 line the second accepts exactly y=0 and covers all positives. On the 100 and 101 lines the first accepts all offsets. On the 110 and 011 lines the second accepts all offsets. Both branch verdicts are monotone as H grows at fixed a, so these checks also cover every higher-dimensional direction.

Even a finite disjunction of affine-copy branches cannot reduce B to P. A sound branch accepting the rigid zero offset must have πL invertible on the two-dimensional input and πK₀+QK₀=0, because only the zero rigid offset may pass. For each of the three input lines, the image of its generator under L must be exceptional. Otherwise the output accepts every offset on that line, including an offset rejected by B. The image of a plane under L cannot contain the three independent exceptional generators. Hence no sound branch covers the rigid zero offset.

## Scope of the correction construction

For an arbitrary Q(v,t)=Mv+tb with v in F₂², the case b=0 depends, up to coordinates, only on how many of the three lines M preserves. These are the earlier zero-, one-, two- and three-line predicates. If b≠0, the kernel of π is an exceptional line with output direction b. Over each nonzero direction other than b exactly one lift is exceptional, since adding b toggles the transverse bit. If Mb is not parallel to b, neither lift over b is exceptional. The three exceptional generators then form a basis and give exactly the predicate studied above after a coordinate change. If Mb is parallel to b, both lifts over b are exceptional. The two exceptional lifts over the other directions span a plane whose every line is exceptional and projects injectively. Restricting the input to that plane gives a reduction from B. Thus this last case is many-one equivalent to B.

These are informal proofs. The forward reductions apply to arbitrary explicit tensor pairs over F₃. The reverse exclusions apply to the specified affine-copy model.
