# Deterministic transporters with low-dimensional parameter groups

Assume complete univariate factorization over every explicitly represented finite field \(\mathbb F_q\) takes deterministic time \(d^{O(\log(d+1))}(\log q)^{O(1)}\) in degree \(d\). This is the sole factoring premise. Let \(N\ge2\) denote the combinatorial and matrix input size, excluding coefficient field bit length, and take the field in an explicit polynomial basis.

**Theorem.** Under this premise the following transporter problems have deterministic algorithms, returning an actual transporter or deciding that none exists under their stated hypotheses.

1. Projective isomorphism of based representations of any finite named quiver, allowing independent nonzero scalars on its arrows, in
   \[
   N^{O((\beta+1)^4\log N)}(\log q)^{O(1)}
   \]
   bit time, where \(\beta\) is the cycle rank of its undirected multigraph. This includes the ordinary multiplication-tensor instances compiled from these diagrams when the presentations are supplied. The bound remains quasipolynomial for any fixed polylogarithmic bound on \(\beta\).
2. Ordinary tensor isomorphism and transporter search for tensors of format \(a\times b\times k\), with all three basis changes unrestricted, in
   \[
   N^{O((k+1)^6\log N)}(\log q)^{O(1)}
   \]
   bit time, on the promise that the reduced connected component of the projection of the source stabilizer to the \(k\)-mode is diagonal in the given basis. The promise concerns the algebraic group over the algebraic closure. Disconnected components can act nondiagonally. In particular the theorem applies if that projected stabilizer is finite modulo scalars. For this last case the bound improves to \(N^{O((k+1)^4\log N)}(\log q)^{O(1)}\).

For quivers the previous one-cycle argument used univariate specialization of one scalar. Here a higher-dimensional coset is cut to a finite system by a deterministically enumerable family of monomial slices. For ordinary tensors, the prior small-mode search used random charts and affine slices. The theorem selects slices preserving rational transporters for the stated split-torus case. It needs no factorization of multivariate polynomials and no search for a point on a general positive-dimensional variety.

## 1. Written upper-rank equations without random compression

Let \(H(w)\) be a matrix with polynomial entries in a short parameter tuple \(w\). Suppose we wish to test \(\operatorname{rank}H(w)\le r\). If \(r+1\) is larger than a matrix dimension no equations are needed. Otherwise put \(t=r+1\), and let \(H\) have \(m\) rows and \(n\) columns. We construct polynomially many \(t\)-by-\(t\) compressed determinants whose vanishing is equivalent to the desired rank bound over every field extension.

For the row compression choose \(\omega\in k^\times\) such that its first \(\max(m,n)\) nonnegative powers are distinct. Such an element can be found by testing a set of \(1+\sum_{i=1}^{\max(m,n)}i\) distinct nonzero elements when the field is large enough, since the excluded orders are roots of the polynomials \(X^i-1\). No factorization of \(q-1\) is used. Define \(U_a\) with entries \((a\omega^i)^j\) for \(0\le i<t,\ 0\le j<m\). For any \(t\) independent vectors of length \(m\) over an extension field, view them as coefficient vectors of polynomials \(f_1,\ldots,f_t\). An invertible change of their basis makes their leading degrees distinct. The determinant of \((f_j(a\omega^i))_{ij}\) then has a nonzero leading coefficient, the product of the leading coefficients times the Vandermonde determinant in the distinct powers of \(\omega\). Its degree in \(a\) is at most \(t(m-1)\). Thus some \(a\) in any list of \(t(m-1)+1\) distinct base-field elements preserves their rank. Apply the same argument to the transpose for the column compression \(V_b\) and a list of \(t(n-1)+1\) elements. If \(H\) has rank at least \(t\), choose first \(a\) preserving \(t\) independent columns and then \(b\) preserving the full row rank of \(U_aH\). Hence exactly the determinants of \(U_aHV_b\) give the claimed equations. Their number and their degrees are polynomial in the supplied matrix bounds. In small fields the applications below instead enumerate the short parameter space. [Folded Wronskians and rank condensers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.7455).

## 2. Isolated rational points in a mixed-dimensional system

We use the following elementary form of polynomial perturbation. Given \(h_1,\ldots,h_\ell\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_u]\) of degrees at most \(D\ge2\), a deterministic procedure lists a finite set containing every isolated \(k\)-point of their geometric zero set, with each reported point verified against the input equations. Other verified points may occur. If \(q>1+\ell D^u\), the procedure uses \((\ell D)^{O((u+1)^2)}\) field operations apart from polynomially many, in the same bound, factorizations of degrees at most \((D+1)^u\). If the inequality on \(q\) fails, direct enumeration of the \(u\) coordinates has the same type of bound.

Choose a list \(\Gamma\subset k\) of \(1+\ell D^u\) distinct elements and enumerate \(u\)-tuples \(c\) from it, substituting the square subsystem
\[
g_i=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} c_i^{\,j-1} h_j,\qquad 1\le i\le u .
\]
Every geometrically isolated target point is isolated in at least one such square subsystem. To see this, construct the rows successively for that point. Each positive-dimensional component through the point in the current system has at least one \(h_j\) nonzero on it, since the target point is isolated in their common zero set. The combined row polynomial is nonzero on that component except for at most \(\ell-1\) choices of \(c_i\), by treating its coefficients as elements of the function field of the component. There are at most \(D^u\) components at each step by the geometric Bézout bound. Thus a list element cutting all the relevant components exists. Their local dimensions decrease at each step, so \(u\) choices suffice.

For a square subsystem put \(L=D+1\) and deform the equations to \(g_i+\varepsilon x_i^L=0\). Over \(k(\varepsilon)\) the normalized polynomials have leading monomials \(x_i^L\) in a graded monomial order. These are pairwise coprime, so the polynomials form a Gröbner basis. Their quotient has the basis of monomials with each exponent \(<L\). Multiplication by each coordinate on it has characteristic polynomial with \(T\)-degree \(L^u\), and coefficients in \(k[\varepsilon^{-1}]\). The multiplication matrices can be computed by replacing \(x_i^L\) using the normalized equations. Each replacement decreases total degree and increases the degree in \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) by at most one. Memoization on the at most \((O(L))^u\) monomials of degree at most \(u(L-1)+1\) bounds the arithmetic, and determinant computation gives polynomial cost in these sizes. Clear powers of \(\varepsilon\) from each characteristic polynomial, remove its maximal common \(\varepsilon\) factor, and specialize to \(\varepsilon=0\). This gives a nonzero polynomial \(P_j(T)\) for each coordinate, of degree at most \(L^u\).

Every coordinate of an isolated point \(x\) of the square system is a root of the corresponding \(P_j\). Indeed every irreducible component of the total deformation through \((x,0)\) has dimension at least one, by the height bound for \(u\) equations in \(u+1\) variables. Such a component cannot be contained in the special fiber, since \(x\) is isolated there. On its dense open part where \(\varepsilon\ne0\), the characteristic polynomial annihilates the coordinate by Cayley–Hamilton. That identity, after clearing and removing powers of \(\varepsilon\), continues to hold on its closure, giving the claim at \(x\). Factor the \(P_j\) over \(k\), enumerate the Cartesian products of their base-field roots, and check the original equations. There are at most \(L^{u^2}\) candidates per square system. This is a Canny-type isolated-point computation with a separate coordinate characteristic polynomial and no generic separating form. [Canny's generalized characteristic polynomials](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1988/6040.html).

## 3. A bounded monomial slice through rational torus cosets

A connected reduced subgroup \(H\) of the split torus \(\mathbb G_m^b\) is itself a split torus, with character quotient \(\mathbb Z^b/M\) for a saturated sublattice \(M\). Suppose a coset \(aH\) is an irreducible component of the common zero set, inside the torus, of polynomials of total degree at most \(D\). Then \(M\) is the saturation of a lattice generated by vectors of infinity norm at most \(D\).

Indeed, each defining polynomial vanishes on \(aH\). Group its monomials by their exponent classes modulo \(M\). Characters on \(H\) are linearly independent, so the coefficient sum after inserting \(a\) vanishes separately in every group. Let \(\Lambda\) be generated by all differences of exponents in the same group in each polynomial. These differences lie in \(M\), with the required norm bound. On the possibly larger coset of the torus defined by the saturation of \(\Lambda\), the same grouped cancellations still hold. This larger coset would contradict the component hypothesis if it had larger dimension. Thus \(\Lambda\) has the rank of \(M\) and its saturation is \(M\). The argument applies also when the defining polynomials are restricted from a larger ambient coordinate space by replacing the coordinates with scalar multiples of the \(b\) torus coordinates.

We next bound representatives of a basis of \(\mathbb Z^b/M\). If \(M\) has rank \(c>0\), choose \(c\) independent short generating columns for its rational span, assemble them into \(V\), and choose an invertible \(c\)-row block \(B\). Set \(\Delta=|\det B|\le c!D^c\). The quotient embeds by
\[
x\longmapsto x_{B^c}-V_{B^c}B^{-1}x_B .
\]
Its image contains \(\mathbb Z^{b-c}\) and lies in \(\Delta^{-1}\mathbb Z^{b-c}\). After multiplying by \(\Delta\), take the reduced Hermite basis of the image lattice. Its entries have absolute value at most \(\Delta\), because the sublattice contains \(\Delta\mathbb Z^{b-c}\). For each basis element divided by \(\Delta\), choose a preimage whose \(B\)-coordinates are reduced modulo \(\Delta\). The complementary coordinates are integers and are bounded by the adjugate formula. In particular the representatives can be chosen with infinity norm at most
\[
(b+1)!(D+1)^b .
\]
For \(c=0\) use the coordinate basis. Rank \(c=b\) needs no representatives.

Consequently a finite explicitly enumerable family of cuts suffices. Enumerate \(d\) from zero through \(b\), and \(d\)-tuples of integer exponent vectors of the indicated norm bound. For such a tuple impose
\[
t^{w_i^+}-t^{w_i^-}=0\qquad(1\le i\le d),
\]
where the two superscripts give the positive and negative parts. One such tuple gives a character basis on \(H\). With that tuple the slice meets each translate of \(H\) exactly once. If a translate has a rational point, its intersection point is rational: starting from that point, the change in \(H\) is uniquely determined by a monomial isomorphism of split tori over the base field. We need neither the character lattice as input nor a rational point on the original coset to form the list. The character facts used here are the split-torus case of the anti-equivalence between groups of multiplicative type and character modules. [Groups of multiplicative type, SGA 3](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~benoist/refs/SGA3-2.pdf).

## 4. Applying the slices to actual transporter spaces

For each of the two problems, the parameter fixes the rescalings or the small-mode change. With that parameter, the remaining maps are an isomorphism between two linear diagrams of the same dimension vector. The isomorphism equations are linear Hom equations in the remaining maps. Here are the two instances.

- In the projective quiver case, a spanning forest gauges the scalars on forest edges to one. More explicitly, multiply each vertex map by a scalar propagated from its component root so that the new edge scale on every tree edge is 1. The remaining \(\beta\) scalars on chords give an action of the split torus \(\mathbb G_m^\beta\) on the labelled arrow matrices. At a fixed parameter, use the ordinary Hom equations from the first representation to the rescaled second one. Recover a projective diagram witness by using the named remaining maps and the scales on the chords, with scales one on the forest. The ordinary multiplication-tensor section is the earlier no-ruler algebra construction. [Projective diagram compilation](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/OrdinaryTensorTransferFallout/Certificates.md).
- In the ordinary tensor case, an invertible \(k\)-mode map \(z\) mixes the \(k\) matrix slices. Compare that tuple to the second tensor's slice tuple as two representations of the quiver with \(k\) parallel arrows between two vertices. The other two TI maps are the two node maps, with the inverse transpose on the input mode according to the ordinary tensor-action convention. All entries of the Hom matrix are affine in the small parameters. No decomposition into blocks is imposed on any tensor witness.

Write \(\mathcal T\) for the set of geometric parameters for which the named diagram isomorphism exists. In both cases there is a fixed number \(r\) equal to the rank of the Hom matrix at every geometric transporter. Namely the Hom kernel there is linearly isomorphic to the endomorphism space of the fixed comparison diagram. Compute \(r\) using that fixed endomorphism problem and linear algebra. Let \(X\) be the rank-at-most-\(r\) locus inside the parameter torus or general linear group. Use §1 to describe it by polynomially many explicit equations of polynomial degree. The transporter is open in \(X\). At a point of \(\mathcal T\) choose constant row and column selectors \(L,R\) for a nonzero \(r\)-minor and fix an invertible Hom solution \(v\) there. On the open pivot chart the rank is exactly \(r\), because all \((r+1)\)-minors vanish. Put \(B=LHR\), \(\Delta=\det B\) and
\[
P=\Delta I-R\operatorname{adj}(B)LH.
\]
Here \(L\) is injective on \(\operatorname{im}H\). Since \(LHP=0\) by the adjugate identity, \(P\) lands in \(\ker H\). At the chosen point \(Pv=\Delta v\) is invertible in every node block. The nonzero pivot and invertibility of those blocks then give an open neighborhood in \(X\) of valid parameters. For \(r=0\) the upper-rank equations give \(H=0\) and the identity suffices. Lower-rank degenerations are outside the positive-size pivot chart. This argument uses no constancy of the Hom rank away from transporters. Separately, the transporter is closed: if it is nonempty, it is a translate of the image of an algebraic stabilizer subgroup by a group projection, and the constructible image of an algebraic group homomorphism is a closed subgroup. For quivers the group action is the chord torus together with vertex basis changes. For ordinary TI it is the product of the three general linear groups. This proves that \(\mathcal T\) is open and closed in \(X\), and every irreducible component of \(\mathcal T\) is an irreducible component of \(X\). [Closed images of group homomorphisms, Milne](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/iAG2022.pdf), [Earlier one-Hom transporter locus](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ExactMatrixProofDegrees/OneHomSearch.md).

At a rational point of the geometric transporter locus, the fixed-parameter diagrams are already isomorphic over the base field. Indeed an isomorphism over the algebraic closure is defined over some finite extension \(E/k\). Restriction of scalars gives an isomorphism of \([E:k]\) copies of the two original diagrams over \(k\). Finite-dimensional quiver representations have Krull–Schmidt cancellation, so the original diagrams are isomorphic. This is the Noether–Deuring argument for the two fixed-parameter comparisons.

For the quiver application every geometric component is a coset of a single connected split subtorus. Use §3 on \(X\) inside \(\mathbb G_m^\beta\), and enumerate the proposed monomial slices. In each slice add one variable \(u\) and equation \(u\prod_j t_j=1\) to retain the open parameter torus. If a rational transporter exists, a correct slice has a rational point of \(\mathcal T\) isolated in the sliced rank system, since \(\mathcal T\) was open there. Recover it by §2. Test all returned parameter tuples by ordinary module isomorphism over \(\mathbb F_q\) and verify a recovered projective transporter directly. Extraneous candidates are discarded by these tests.

For the ordinary tensor case assume its connected projected stabilizer \(H\), with reduced structure, is diagonal in the supplied basis. The algorithm uses this as a promise and does not compute \(H\). Each geometric component of \(\mathcal T\) is of the form \(aH\), with \(H\) acting by column scalings. Choose one pivot entry in every column of the small-mode matrix, and retain the open chart where all chosen entries are nonzero. Enumerate all \(k^k\) choices of pivot positions. On a coset in such a chart, the column ratios are fixed, so the pivot entries identify the coset with a coset of a subtorus in \(\mathbb G_m^k\). For a component containing a rational point, restrict the rank equations to its fixed column ratios. The component remains an irreducible component of the restricted system, since its full matrix locus is open and closed in \(X\). This restriction substitutes scalar multiples of the \(k\) pivot variables and preserves the degree bound. The short character-basis argument now applies. Use the monomial cuts on the pivot entries as equations in the original \(k^2\) entries of \(z\). A single auxiliary inverse equation inverts the product of the pivot entries and \(\det z\). A correct cut meets every transporter component in the chart in a single geometric point and meets the chosen rational component rationally. Thus its point is isolated in the cut rank system and is listed by §2. Test every candidate by ordinary module isomorphism and verify the original three tensor equations after constructing the modes.

If the projected stabilizer is finite modulo scalars, its connected part is the scalar torus. It contains the full scalar torus because scaling the small mode can always be compensated in another mode. In this case use the simpler cuts \(z_{ij}=1\) for each of the \(k^2\) positions. Any nonzero position permits rational scaling, and the cut meets each transporter component either in a point or in the empty set. This replaces the general lattice enumeration. [Deterministic module isomorphism and transporter search](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf).

## 5. Cost

The rank equations have polynomially bounded count and degree in \(N\). They can be represented densely in the short parameter list in time polynomial in the corresponding monomial count. The torus exponent bound is \(N^{O(b+1)}\) for \(b=\beta\) or \(b=k\), so the slice count is \(N^{O((b+1)^3)}\) and the slice equation degrees are \(N^{O(b+1)}\). The solver uses \(u=\beta+1\) variables for the quiver application and \(u=k^2+1\) for the ordinary tensor application, counting the open-chart inverse. Therefore its largest factorization degrees are \(N^{O((\beta+1)^2)}\) and \(N^{O((k+1)^3)}\), respectively. The factorization premise and the polynomial bounds of §2 give the displayed running times. In the scalar-only case the slice equation degrees are polynomial with an absolute exponent, so the factorization degrees fall to \(N^{O((k+1)^2)}\).

For fields too small for the explicit lists, the thresholds needed for the relevant algorithm are at most a polynomial in the equation count times the degree raised to \(O(u)\), and at most a polynomial in the Hom matrix dimensions for §1. Direct enumeration of the original short parameter list then stays below the same time bounds. Elements of the explicit field can be generated in their coordinate order. All field arithmetic and the checks take bit time polynomial in \(\log q\) per operation.

This is a written conditional proof with a fixed factorization premise. Exact symbolic controls expanded the coordinate multiplication-matrix determinants in 32 mixed-dimensional examples over the fields of orders 2, 3, 5, 7 and 11, and checked 64 coordinate specializations. The cases included isolated points with a nonreduced scheme structure. For general small-mode tensor inputs, random affine slicing can find a rational point on a positive-dimensional non-toral transporter component. The deterministic algorithm above applies to the diagonal connected-stabilizer hypothesis, which is what makes the monomial cuts preserve a rational point. [Prior small-mode randomized search](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ExactMatrixProofDegrees/OneHomSearch.md).
