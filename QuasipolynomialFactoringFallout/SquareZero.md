# Recognizing associative multiplication tensors and classifying those with square-zero radical

Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) be explicitly presented. The algorithmic input in this note is **QPF**, deterministic complete factorization in \(k[X]\), uniformly in the explicit finite field, with bit cost \((d^{\log d}\log q)^{O(1)}\) for degree \(d\). All reductions below use ordered tensor isomorphism. For \(T:U\times V\to W\) an isomorphism to \(T'\) is a triple of invertible linear maps \((f,g,h)\) satisfying \(T'(f(x),g(y))=h(T(x,y))\). We work with positive-dimensional unital algebras. If the zero algebra is admitted, its single empty transporter can be handled separately.

## 1. Recognition and descent of the associative locus

**Theorem 1.** Over any field, one can recognize whether an ordinary bilinear tensor is isomorphic to multiplication in a unital associative algebra, recover such an algebra, and give the tensor isomorphism, deterministically in polynomially many field operations. The property descends along every field extension. The finite-field bit cost is polynomial in the tensor dimensions and \(\log q\).

**Proof.** The three dimensions must be equal. Let \(\mathcal L\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(V,W)\) be the space of left slices and let
\[
D_{\mathcal L}=\{d\in\operatorname{End}(W):d\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal L\}.
\]
Calculate the slices, \(D_{\mathcal L}\), and its actions on \(\mathcal L\) and on \(W\) by linear algebra. Require that \(U\to\mathcal L\) be injective. Use constructive module isomorphism to find a \(D_{\mathcal L}\)-linear isomorphism \(j\colon\mathcal L\to W\). Solve the linear equations \(j(L)=L(b)\) for \(b\in V\), using a basis of \(\mathcal L\). If successful, \(\alpha(x)=T(x,b)\) is invertible. Apply the same procedure to the right slices, interchanging \(U,V\), to obtain \(a\in U\) with \(\beta(y)=T(a,y)\) invertible. A failed step yields a negative answer.

To see that a positive input passes these steps, work first in multiplication coordinates on a unital associative algebra \(C\). The left-slice space consists of the left multiplications \(L_x\). An endomorphism that preserves this space by postcomposition belongs to it because \(L_1=I\). Thus \(D_{\mathcal L}\) is the left regular algebra. Any module map \(j\) satisfies
\[
j(L_x)=j(L_xL_1)=L_xj(L_1).
\]
It is evaluation at \(j(L_1)\). Evaluation at \(1\) is an isomorphism. Changes of bases transport both statements to the given coordinates, and the right-slice argument uses the opposite algebra.

Define a product on \(W\) by
\[
x*y=T(\alpha^{-1}x,\beta^{-1}y).
\]
Its unit is \(T(a,b)\), by the defining equations of \(\alpha,\beta\). Test associativity on basis triples. This validates every accepting output, with tensor isomorphism \((\alpha,\beta,I)\). In multiplication coordinates a positive input has \(\alpha=R_b,\ \beta=L_a\), and \(a,b\) are units. In any finite-dimensional unital algebra, a one-sided inverse in an associative product suffices for a two-sided inverse, by the corresponding regular representation. The normalized product is \(x*y=x(ab)^{-1}y\) and is associative.

For descent, the slice spaces and their postcomposition algebras commute with scalar extension because their constructions use kernels and images of linear maps. If \(T\) becomes a multiplication tensor over \(K/k\), the two module isomorphisms exist over \(K\). The Noether–Deuring theorem gives both over \(k\). Evaluation equations for these particular maps have solutions over \(K\) by the computation with \(L_1\), so they have solutions over \(k\). The product obtained over \(k\) is associative since its extension to \(K\) is associative. Deterministic constructive module isomorphism uses the Brooksbank–Luks algorithm. [Brooksbank–Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=c2002ca40d487b7c4f73b14bee95eb06736531ba&repid=rep1&type=pdf) · [Noether–Deuring, stated in Benson–Reichstein, Theorem 2.2](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~reichst/benson-reichstein-v2.pdf)

**Normalization of witnesses.** For unital associative \(A,B\), every isotopy satisfies \(a=f(1)\), \(b=g(1)\), \(h(x)=f(x)b=a g(x)\). Invertibility gives \(a,b\in B^\times\). Set \(\phi(x)=a^{-1}h(x)b^{-1}\). Then \(f(x)=a\phi(x)\), \(g(x)=\phi(x)b\), and cancelling from the isotopy equation gives \(\phi(xy)=\phi(x)\phi(y)\). Conversely each \(\phi:A\simeq B\) and \(a,b\in B^\times\) give a unique triple. In particular the tensor transporter has cardinality \(|B^\times|^2|\operatorname{Iso}_{k\text{-alg}}(A,B)|\).

## 2. A complete graph invariant in the nonsplit square-zero case

Let \(A\) be unital and associative, with \(J=\operatorname{rad}A\) and \(J^2=0\). Write \(S=A/J=\prod_i M_{t_i}(E_i)\) for finite fields \(E_i/k\), and \(d_i=[E_i:k]\). Since \(S\) is separable over \(k\), the Wedderburn–Malcev theorem gives \(A\simeq S\ltimes J\), with product
\[
(s,x)(t,y)=(st,sy+xt).
\]
The \(S\)-bimodule structure on \(J\) is intrinsic, since changing a lift of \(s\) by an element of \(J\) has no effect. A multiplicative section can be found via linear algebra. Start with a linear section \(s_0:S\to A\) and solve for a linear correction \(u:S\to J\) so that \((s_0+u)(1)=1\) and \((s_0+u)(xy)=(s_0+u)(x)(s_0+u)(y)\) on basis pairs. The correction equations are linear because \(J^2=0\), and existence follows from Wedderburn–Malcev.

Define \(\Gamma(A)\) as follows. Its vertices are pairs \(v=(i,\sigma)\) where \(\sigma:E_i\hookrightarrow\bar k\). The vertex color is \(t_i\). There is a distinguished directed permutation relation \(F\) sending \((i,\sigma)\) to \((i,\operatorname{Frob}_q\circ\sigma)\). Write \(e_v\) for the primitive central idempotent in \(S_{\bar k}\) attached to \(v\). Give every ordered pair \((v,w)\) weight
\[
m_{vw}=\frac{\dim_{\bar k}(e_v J_{\bar k}e_w)}{t(v)t(w)}.
\]
This is a nonnegative integer because the indicated corner is a module over \(M_{t(v)t(w)}(\bar k)\). Dimensions are Frobenius invariant.

**Theorem 2.** Two square-zero algebras are \(k\)-isomorphic if and only if their weighted graphs with the distinguished Frobenius permutation are isomorphic. Every graph isomorphism lifts to a \(k\)-algebra isomorphism. Assuming QPF, graphs and lifts are computable in \((N^{\log N})^{O(1)}(\log q)^{O(1)}\) time with a graph isomorphism witness provided for the lift, where \(N\) bounds the algebra dimensions. With Babai's algorithm, decision, witness search, and a complete canonical invariant for the ordinary multiplication tensors are deterministic quasipolynomial in \(N\) and polynomial in \(\log q\). The same holds for unmarked ordinary tensors in the corresponding orbits.

**Proof of classification.** An algebra isomorphism induces an isomorphism of the quotient, and then preserves the corners of the transported radical. Conversely, a permutation of the vertices commuting with Frobenius is precisely a permutation of the center factors together with \(k\)-field isomorphisms on them, using the contravariant action of the center on embeddings. If the colors match, these center maps lift entrywise to an isomorphism \(\alpha:S_A\to S_B\). For a Frobenius orbit \(\mathcal O\) of ordered vertex pairs, the corresponding factor of \(S_A\otimes S_A^{\rm op}\) is a full matrix algebra over \(\mathbb F_{q^{|\mathcal O|}}\) of size \(t(v)t(w)\). In fact \(E_i\otimes_k E_j\) is finite étale, with geometric embeddings indexed by those pairs and field factors indexed by their Frobenius orbits. The corresponding factors of the matrix algebras are full matrices, by base change and \(M_s^{\rm op}\cong M_s\). A module over any one such factor is determined by its multiplicity of the standard simple module. Extending scalars to \(\bar k\) reads this multiplicity as \(m_{vw}\). Thus matching weights imply that \(J_A\) and \(J_B\), with the second action pulled back by \(\alpha\), are isomorphic as bimodules. A bimodule isomorphism \(\beta\) gives \((s,x)\mapsto(\alpha(s),\beta(x))\) on the split presentations.

**Computed data and coherent embeddings.** Compute the radical by the deterministic finite-field algorithm for the Jacobson radical, take the quotient, and compute its center by commutation equations. Split this finite étale center using QPF. One explicit procedure starts with \(k\) embedded diagonally in a center summand \(C\) and repeatedly adjoins a basis element outside the current embedded field \(H\subseteq C\). The minimal polynomial of that element over \(H\) is squarefree, as \(C\) is a product of finite separable fields. If it splits into multiple irreducible factors, its Chinese remainder idempotents give smaller summands. If it is irreducible, the embedded field grows, and one repeats until it equals \(C\). All degrees, dimensions and numbers of branches are polynomial in \(N\). The factor \(M_{t_i}(E_i)\) is recovered by its central idempotent, and \(t_i\) follows from its dimension. For lifts, an explicit matrix model of each factor can be obtained within \(t_i^{O(\log t_i)}\operatorname{poly}(\log |E_i|)\) by Ivanyos–Karpinski–Rónyai–Saxena, Theorem 1.8. [Rónyai, Computing the structure of finite algebras](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(08)80017-X) · [Ivanyos–Karpinski–Rónyai–Saxena, Theorem 1.8](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-2011-02505-6)

Here is a way to compute the embeddings without constructing a possibly exponential-degree common splitting field. For each rational prime \(\ell\) dividing a center degree, pick one available center field with maximal \(\ell\)-adic valuation \(a\). In that field obtain nested representatives \(H_{\ell,b}\) of degree \(\ell^b\), \(0\le b\le a\), as kernels of \(q^{\ell^b}\)-Frobenius minus identity. Model the degree-\(d\) field, whenever \(d\) is needed, as
\[
H(d)=\bigotimes_{\ell\mid d}H_{\ell,v_\ell(d)}
\]
over \(k\). Coprime finite extension degrees make this tensor product a field, and inclusions for divisibility are coherent by construction. A polynomial presentation of \(H(d)\) can be obtained by summing an element from each prime-power factor outside its unique maximal proper subfield. The sum is outside every maximal proper subfield of \(H(d)\), since the corresponding Frobenius power fixes the other summands and moves that one. The same subfield-kernel argument identifies a generator for each \(E_i\). Polynomials and linear maps are obtained from the powers of the generators.

Choose one embedding of \(E_i\) into \(H(d_i)\) by factoring its generator's minimal polynomial there. Index the other embeddings by its \(q\)-power conjugates. Embed both field models \(H(d_i),H(d_j)\) into \(H(\operatorname{lcm}(d_i,d_j))\), of degree at most \(N^2\). In that field, find the simultaneous eigenspaces on \(e_iJe_j\) for left multiplication by a generator of \(E_i\) and right multiplication by a generator of \(E_j\). The eigenvalues are the images of the generators under the chosen pair of embeddings. These dimensions give the weights, since over this field the two centers split. The prime-power towers admit compatible embeddings into \(\bar k\) by taking an embedding of each maximal tower first and then its restrictions. The computed data therefore label the intrinsic graph even when different inputs choose different towers. For witness search build both graph encodings in towers chosen from their joint list of center fields at the start. The matched vertices determine an explicit center isomorphism by composing their embeddings into \(H(d_i)\). Compute a bimodule isomorphism by constructive module isomorphism, use the computed sections, and verify the resulting matrix on basis products. If an oracle requires a polynomial presentation over the prime field, apply the prime-power subfield generator argument with \(p\)-Frobenius to the absolute field degrees. These degrees are at most \(N^2\log_2 q\), so the conversion is still polynomial in the bit parameters. Zero radical and one-vertex cases follow from the same procedure.

**Graph algorithm.** The graph has at most \(N\) vertices before relation encoding. The colors are integers bounded by \(N\), and the distinguished successor relation is a separate directed edge color. Encode directed weighted relations by one vertex per relation entry with two role-colored incidence vertices. Include all original colors as deterministic color labels. This costs polynomial size and preserves graph isomorphisms in both directions. Standard vertex individualization gives a witness using polynomially many graph decision queries, and Babai's canonical labeling gives a complete canonical graph invariant in quasipolynomial time. The tensor normalization in §1 allows recognition and use without marked units or aligned modes. [Babai, Graph isomorphism in quasipolynomial time](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03547) · [Babai, Canonical form for graphs in quasipolynomial time](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313276.3316356)

### Exact transporter counts and extension decisions

Put \(J^S=\{x\in J:sx=xs\text{ for all }s\in S\}\). For zero multiplicities use \(|\mathrm{GL}_0|=1\). The algebra automorphism count is
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}_k(A)|=
|\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(A))|\,
q^{\dim_k J-\dim_k J^S}
\prod_i|\mathrm{PGL}_{t_i}(\mathbb F_{q^{d_i}})|
\prod_{\mathcal O}|\mathrm{GL}_{m_{\mathcal O}}(\mathbb F_{q^{|\mathcal O|}})|.
\]
Indeed an automorphism induces a quotient map and a compatible bimodule map, and every such pair lifts by the split presentation. The kernel consists of derivations \(S\to J\). Separability of \(S\) makes these derivations inner. For example, if \(\sum a_r\otimes b_r\) is a separability element, set \(z=\sum a_r d(b_r)\) and apply the derivation identity to its commutation relation with \(s\). This gives \(d(s)=sz-zs\), with kernel on \(z\) exactly \(J^S\). For each graph automorphism the quotient-map choices form a coset of the product of the inner automorphism groups of the matrix factors, by Skolem–Noether. For each quotient map the bimodule choices form a torsor for the indicated product of general linear groups, because each simple module has endomorphism field \(\mathbb F_{q^{|\mathcal O|}}\). This proves the count. Also \(|A^\times|=q^{\dim J}\prod_i|\mathrm{GL}_{t_i}(\mathbb F_{q^{d_i}})|\). Thus one obtains the ordinary TI transporter count from §1, using graph automorphism counting by the orbit-stabilizer chain and colored graph isomorphism queries. If there is no graph isomorphism the count is zero.

For extension degree \(e\) in binary, replacing the distinguished permutations by \(F_A^e,F_B^e\) on the same weighted geometric graphs is necessary and sufficient for isomorphism over \(\mathbb F_{q^e}\). Indeed \(\operatorname{rad}(A_{\mathbb F_{q^e}})=J_{\mathbb F_{q^e}}\) because the quotient remains semisimple, and the new Frobenius is the \(e\)-th power of the old one. The graph can be reused, even for exponentially large \(e\). The exact condition depends only on \(\gcd(e,L)\), where \(L\) is the least common multiple of the two Frobenius permutation orders. For \(r=\gcd(e,L)\) there is an integer \(u\) with \(eu\equiv r\pmod L\) and \(r\mid e\). Intertwining the \(e\)-th powers implies intertwining the \(r\)-th powers by raising to \(u\), and the converse follows by raising to \(e/r\). This gives a compact exact extension decision algorithm without constructing the extension field.

## 3. Factoring is exactly promised split étale tensor witness search

**Theorem 3.** Uniform finite-field polynomial factorization and witness search for ordinary tensor isomorphism of unital commutative semisimple multiplication tensors are polynomial-time Turing equivalent. The hard direction uses one call for a squarefree input, and the call is a promised positive instance between two split étale algebras, with one side the standard \(\mathbb F_q^r\). On the entire étale class, decision and transporter counting are already deterministic polynomial-time. Therefore QPF yields a deterministic quasipolynomial restricted search-to-decision reduction, and a polynomial-time search-to-decision reduction within this class exists if and only if finite-field factoring is deterministic polynomial-time.

**Proof.** Given a squarefree univariate input \(f\), compute \(C=k[X]/(f)\) and its Berlekamp subalgebra \(B=\ker(\operatorname{Frob}_q-I:C\to C)\) by modular powers and linear algebra. If \(f=\prod_i f_i\) is its unknown irreducible factorization, the Chinese remainder theorem identifies \(C\) with \(\prod_i k[X]/(f_i)\). Its fixed algebra \(B\) is \(\prod_i k\). Set \(r=\dim B\) and ask for any ordinary tensor isomorphism from \(\mu_{k^r}\) to \(\mu_B\). Normalization in §1 converts the answer to a unital algebra isomorphism. The images \(e_i\) of the standard idempotents are the primitive factor selectors of \(C\), so \(\gcd(f,1-e_i)\), using representatives of \(e_i\), gives all irreducible factors. Squarefree decomposition before this step handles multiplicities. The underlying fixed-algebra method is Berlekamp's. [Berlekamp, Factoring polynomials over large finite fields](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1970-0276200-X)

Conversely, complete factoring separates the field factors of any given étale algebra by the center-splitting procedure in §2. Match equal degrees. Factor a source generator's minimal polynomial in the corresponding target field to get the map on each field factor, then assemble an algebra isomorphism and its diagonal tensor witness. All factorizations have degrees at most the algebra dimension over finite fields with logarithmic size at most \(N\log q\).

For decision, let \(c_d\) count the field factors of degree \(d\). From multiplication tables and fast powering, compute the linear \(q\)-Frobenius map and
\[
b_e=\dim_k\ker(F^e-I)=\sum_d c_d\gcd(d,e),\qquad 1\le e\le N.
\]
Since \(\gcd(d,e)=\sum_{s\mid d,\ s\mid e}\varphi(s)\), two successive divisor-sum inversions recover all \(c_d\), with ordinary integer arithmetic. The same sequence matches exactly the isomorphism type. A positive pair has \(\prod_d d^{c_d}c_d!\) algebra isomorphisms, and \(|A^\times|=\prod_d(q^d-1)^{c_d}\). Apply §1 for the tensor count. If only a tensor is given, the polynomial recognition algorithm recovers a multiplication algebra and the promise of being étale can be checked by commutativity and zero radical. Thus a decision oracle restricted to the étale class is itself polynomial-time computable. The equivalence concerns this restricted oracle.

## 4. The change at radical cube zero

Even over split quotient \(k^3\), \(J^3=0\) already permits a linear-dimension reduction of general ordinary TI to multiplication tensor isomorphism. Compress the output of each bilinear map to its image and separately compare the original dimensions and output ranks. Output isomorphisms between the images extend to the original equal-dimensional output spaces. The zero maps can be decided directly. For a nonzero compressed \(T:U\times V\to W\), take the vector space \(k^3\oplus U\oplus V\oplus W\) with the diagonal algebra \(k^3\), put \(U,V,W\) in Peirce positions \((1,2),(2,3),(1,3)\), respectively, and use \(uv=T(u,v)\) as the sole possibly nonzero product of two radical pieces. Balancing by the middle diagonal scalar gives associativity, and all triple radical products vanish. The radical is \(J=U\oplus V\oplus W\), with \(J^2=W\), because \(W\) is the span of the products.

An algebra isomorphism preserves the quotient bimodule \(J/J^2\), whose directed support is a two-edge path with uniquely identified source, middle and sink. It therefore induces separate maps on \(U,V,W\), and the intrinsic product \((J/J^2)\times(J/J^2)\to J^2\) recovers the original tensor isomorphism on its only potentially nonzero corner. Conversely any tensor isomorphism extends to an algebra isomorphism by the identity on the three diagonal idempotents. Use §1 to pass from algebra to multiplication tensor.

For \(J^2=0\), the split graph construction \(S=k^{V(G)}\), with a one-dimensional radical corner for each oriented edge of an undirected graph, gives the reverse reduction from graph isomorphism. This is the classical Grigoriev construction. Grigoriev's stated equivalence uses a commutative quotient over an algebraically closed field. The graph above adds the finite-field Frobenius relation and allows arbitrary matrix factors in the quotient. [Grigoriev, original article](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/znsl3395) · [Grochow–Qiao I, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

## 5. Prescribed determinants and their exact witness counts

**Theorem 4.** For unmarked ordinary tensors isotopic to multiplication in unital associative algebras with square-zero radical, QPF and colored graph isomorphism yield a deterministic quasipolynomial algorithm that tests any prescribed nonzero determinant triple, returns a transporter with that triple when one exists, and computes the exact number of transporters with the triple. The field can vary. The explicit QPF calls have polynomial degree and logarithmic field size polynomial in \(N\) and \(\log q\). The algorithm uses additive linear algebra and bounded-degree root extraction for the multiplicative corrections.

**Proof.** The only arithmetic search beyond the explicit algebra decompositions is the following norm computation. For an explicit extension \(E/k\) of degree \(d\) and \(c\in k^\times\), form the cyclic algebra on \(\bigoplus_{j=0}^{d-1}E u^j\) with \(u x=x^q u\) and \(u^d=c\). These structure constants are computable by field operations. Over \(\bar k\), \(E\otimes_k\bar k\) acts as the diagonal algebra on \(d\) coordinates and \(u\) acts as a cyclic shift with wrap entry \(c\). The diagonal projectors and powers of this invertible shift give the matrix units, so the algebra is central simple over \(k\) and split by the finite-field case of Wedderburn. Use the cited constructive matrix splitting algorithm with complexity \(d^{O(\log d)}\operatorname{poly}(\log q)\). In its standard \(d\)-dimensional module choose a nonzero \(v\). The map \(E\to k^d,\ x\mapsto xv\), is a linear isomorphism since \(x\ne0\) acts invertibly. Write \(uv=bv\) in these coordinates. Iterating \(d\) times gives \(N_{E/k}(b)=c\), with \(b\) recovered by a linear solve.

Use the normalized algebras \(A,B\) and let \(\delta_1=\det(\alpha_A)/\det(\alpha_B)\), \(\delta_2=\det(\beta_A)/\det(\beta_B)\). Build a reference algebra isomorphism \(\phi_0\) using §2, or return zero if there is none. For \(\phi:A\to B\), set \(r=\det\phi\), \(u_1=\det L_a\), and \(u_2=\det R_b\), where \(a,b\in B^\times\). Section 1 gives the raw determinants
\[
(\delta_1 u_1r,\ \delta_2 u_2r,\ u_1u_2r).
\]
For desired determinants \((D_1,D_2,D_3)\), the unique required values are
\[
r=\frac{D_1D_2}{D_3\delta_1\delta_2},\qquad
u_1=\frac{D_3\delta_2}{D_2},\qquad u_2=\frac{D_3\delta_1}{D_1}.
\]

Compute the simple multiplicities \(\lambda_i,\rho_i\) of the left and right \(S_B\)-actions on \(S_B\oplus J_B\). For example \(\lambda_i=\dim_k(e_i(S_B\oplus J_B))/(d_it_i)\) and the right formula uses \((S_B\oplus J_B)e_i\). The determinant maps on units have the forms
\[
\det L_a=\prod_i N_{E_i/k}(\det a_i)^{\lambda_i},\qquad
\det R_a=\prod_i N_{E_i/k}(\det a_i)^{\rho_i}.
\]
Indeed the determinant multiplies over the radical filtration, and on a simple constituent is the norm of the residue matrix determinant. The radical-unit contribution is one. Thus both images are explicit power subgroups of \(k^\times\). A product of norm determinants with weights \(w_i\) has image \((k^\times)^g\) where \(g=\gcd(q-1,w_1,\ldots,w_s)\). Test membership by raising to \((q-1)/g\). If feasible, find a \(g\)-th root by factoring a degree-\(g\) polynomial and use Bézout coefficients to prescribe the individual norm values. The norm algorithm realizes each value by a diagonal residue matrix and the multiplicative section lifts it. For these unit actions \(g\le N\).

It remains to construct \(r/\det\phi_0\) as a determinant in \(\operatorname{Aut}(B)\). First compute the determinant image \(H_0\) of the kernel of \(\operatorname{Aut}(B)\to\operatorname{Aut}\Gamma(B)\). Its subgroup of derivations is unipotent. Every remaining element of the kernel is a product of a conjugation by a unit of \(S_B\) and an automorphism fixing \(S_B\) pointwise. The conjugation contributes the exponents \(\lambda_i-\rho_i\), by the two unit formulas. The second part acts only by an \(S_B\)-bimodule automorphism of \(J_B\). In an orbit \(\mathcal O\) of ordered vertex pairs with positive multiplicity it contributes the norm of a multiplicity matrix determinant raised to \(t(v)t(w)\), the dimension of the simple module over its own field. Consequently \(H_0=(k^\times)^{g_0}\) where \(g_0\) is the gcd of \(q-1\) with all these exponents. If \(J_B\ne0\), at least one positive \(t(v)t(w)\) appears, and \(g_0\le N\). Corrections within \(H_0\) are constructed by the same root, Bézout and norm routine. To realize a norm value on an individual positive multiplicity space, explicitly split its matrix-algebra factor in \(S_B\otimes S_B^{\rm op}\), restrict to a first diagonal matrix unit and use the remaining matrix units to extend a diagonal linear map on that multiplicity space to the full module. These operations have dimension polynomial in \(N\).

Obtain generators of the finite graph automorphism group via individualization and a stabilizer chain, and lift each one as in §2. Their determinants generate the remainder of the determinant image modulo \(H_0\). In the case \(J_B\ne0\), use \(z^{(q-1)/g_0}\) as the key of a coset. A breadth-first traversal has at most \(g_0\le N\) vertices and records a word in the lifted generators for each reachable key. If the desired determinant ratio has a reached key, compose the corresponding lifted word and make the remaining \(H_0\) correction. If \(J_B=0\), \(H_0=1\), and the graph lift determinants are signs. For a Frobenius automorphism this follows from a normal basis of its center and the entrywise action. Permutations of equal-size blocks also have determinant a sign in consistent block bases, and inner conjugation has determinant one. The traversal in the semisimple case has at most two keys. This constructs \(\phi\) of determinant \(r\) and hence the raw transporter by the first formula.

For the count, put \(H=\det(\operatorname{Aut}B)\) and \(U_1=\det L(B^\times)\), \(U_2=\det R(B^\times)\). They have orders known from the traversal and the two gcds. If any prescribed requirement is infeasible, the count is zero. Otherwise the formula in §1 gives the exact count
\[
\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}B|}{|H|}
\cdot\frac{|B^\times|}{|U_1|}
\cdot\frac{|B^\times|}{|U_2|}.
\]
Each quotient counts a nonempty fiber of a group homomorphism, so it holds for any prescribed feasible triple. Section 2 computes both numerators. The data that distinguish the fixed-field case from this uniform algorithm are the small quotient traversal, field-size independent norm preimages, and arithmetic corrections from bounded-degree factor calls.

## Status

The reductions and classification are written proofs. The quasipolynomial statements use QPF as the specified input, together with the cited graph and algebra algorithms. Recognition and descent, factoring equivalence, and the cube-zero construction are unconditional mathematical reductions and statements. Section 2 gives graph canonization as a complete invariant. The certificate of an isomorphism also includes the lifted linear maps in the original input bases.
