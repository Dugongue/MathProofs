# Two hypoworlds for special-linear tensor isomorphism

For a fixed finite field $F$, write $H(F)$ for the deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction from special-linear to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $F$. Ordinary TI always reduces in the other direction by unused mode lines. The principal question is $H=H(\mathbb F_3)$. The new proofs give exact consequences of either answer and concrete comparisons between models. They give no contradiction in the unrestricted explicit-input problem. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

## New links between the equality and inequality worlds

| Established theorem | If $H$ holds | If $H$ fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Finite extension reflection.** For fixed finite fields $F\subseteq K$, $H(K)\Rightarrow H(F)$. Frobenius constraints preserve the original base-field maps and their chosen determinant character. | $\mathbb F_3$ equality is necessary for equality over every fixed positive degree extension in characteristic three. The converse field-transfer implication remains open here. | The same-field equality fails over **every** $\mathbb F_{3^r}$. A proof over any one such field would contradict this branch. |
| **Commutative cube-zero rings.** $H(F)$ is equivalent to reducing even or determinant-one isomorphism to ordinary isomorphism on the explicit scalar-residue local class with $J^3=0$, $\operatorname{Ann}_J(J)=J^2$, and the stated layer congruences. | The orientation constraint can be removed already on these rings. | In this class ordinary isomorphism and determinant-one isomorphism have strictly different many-one degrees. Radical square zero remains elementary. |
| **Relative norm orientation.** A uniform norm-one reduction over explicit extensions follows from $H(F)$. For any fixed extension of degree coprime to $|F^\times|$, the norm-one assertion is equivalent to $H(F)$. | Over every extension, arbitrary explicit degree is allowed for the norm-one comparison into ordinary TI over the base. | For $\mathbb F_3$, norm-one comparison is strictly harder than ordinary TI over every fixed extension of odd degree. The determinant-one conclusion applies to all degrees. |
| **Limited randomness.** A bounded-error randomized many-one reduction using $O(\log L)$ bits exists exactly when $H$ holds. | The ordinary and signed decisions admit this randomized reduction and all equivalent positive logarithmic-depth truth-table reductions. | Even logarithmic randomness and polynomially many positive queries with logarithmic-depth decoding cannot remove the determinant constraint. |
| **Short adaptive query trees.** Ordinary-TI complement closure and a deterministic $O(\log L)$-query TI reduction for signed TI together imply $H$. | One query already suffices. | At least one of these two routes must fail. Conditional on complement closure, every deterministic TI-oracle algorithm for the signed language exceeds a logarithmic query bound. |
| **Perfect-zero-knowledge benchmark.** Both tensor languages lie in $\mathrm{NP}\cap\mathrm{PZK}\cap\mathrm{coHVPZK}$ in the stated protocol model. | The tensor degree equality does not settle that class separation. | $\mathrm P$ is strictly below this intersection. In particular the negative branch separates $\mathrm P$ from $\mathrm{NP}$. |
| **Polynomial randomness and advice.** Any randomized polynomial-time many-one reduction can be compiled to a deterministic nonuniform one. Its existence is also equivalent to a deterministic external-oracle many-one reduction for almost every independent random oracle, and to such a reduction on a positive-measure set. | All these weaker reduction statements hold. | The branch can still admit a randomized many-one reduction, but then polynomial advice removes the separation. If randomized many-one fails too, almost every independent random oracle preserves the fixed-language separation. |
| **Geometric decision plus arithmetic descent.** A rational signed comparison can be split into a geometric signed comparison and the promised problem of descending a geometric transporter. Their ordinary reductions combine by AND. | Promised rational descent reduces by restriction of rational signed TI. Reduction of the general geometric task is a separate conditional problem. | The two tasks cannot both have ordinary many-one reductions. The geometric condition alone loses necessary arithmetic data. |

The precise scope and the proofs are in [Cube-zero rings and extension fields](CubeZeroAndExtensions.md), [Complexity worlds](ComplexityWorlds.md), and [Geometry and arithmetic descent](GeometryAndDescent.md), and [Relative norms](NormExtensionComparison.md).

The fixed fields form a divisibility constraint. Among degrees $r\ge1$ for which $H(\mathbb F_{3^r})$ holds, every divisor of any member is also a member. The complementary degrees are upward closed under taking multiples. The identity $\mathrm{GL}=\mathrm{SL}$ over $\mathbb F_2$ is in a different characteristic and gives no implication of this kind for $\mathbb F_3$.

## Which apparent contradictions survive?

1. **Opaque failure versus explicit success.** The same fixed-$\mathbb F_3$ input family takes $\Omega(3^{m/2}/m)$ ordinary opaque queries in the specified model, yet its explicit coefficients give a polynomial-time decision by a cross-ratio square test. The restrictions concern what data the reduction can use. Thus an opaque query lower bound cannot contradict the proposed explicit-input reduction. [Proof](FixedFieldOpaqueSeparation.md)
2. **All geometric invariants versus rational orientation.** Closed geometric SL orbits can be equal and the rational ordinary transporter can be supplied, while rational signed equivalence depends on an arbitrarily large finite extension. The field-natural obstruction applies to targets whose geometric stabilizers have too few components. An arbitrary same-field many-one reduction is free to change the presentation and need not commute with field extension. [Proof](GeometryAndDescent.md)
3. **Equal worst-case upper bounds versus different degrees.** Ordinary and signed full-coset problems already have finite-index reductions with explicit generators. The subexponential upper bounds inherited from matrix-space equivalence therefore hold in either world. Turning a decision reduction into an algorithm supplying those generators requires another argument. [Proof](ComplexityWorlds.md)
4. **General complexity separations versus tensor inequality.** A recursive external oracle makes the fixed tensor languages equivalent while $\mathrm P^\Omega$ is strictly below $\mathrm{UP}^\Omega\cap\mathrm{coUP}^\Omega\cap\mathrm{SZK}^\Omega$. This is a coexistence result in that oracle model. It excludes an argument that uses only the relativized class separation to infer tensor inequality. [Proof](ComplexityWorlds.md)
5. **Positive local algebra classes versus cube-zero hardness.** The cube-zero determinant compiler is scalar-residue, its radical annihilator has no detachable quotient, and its grade-scaling determinant is trivial. Thus the earlier corrections from matrix multiplicities, detachable summands, and scalar gradings do not decide the encoded character. A reduction for the whole new class would settle the equality. [Proof](CubeZeroAndExtensions.md)

## Two further algorithmic connections

- Signed and ordinary TI both have bounded-error randomized nonadaptive reductions to MKTP through the entropy difference between an ordered pair of orbit samples and a randomly swapped pair. Those reductions do not identify the two deterministic many-one degrees. A nonuniform inequality would force both MKTP and MCSP outside $\mathrm{P/poly}$, while the uniform inequality needs derandomization assumptions to imply the corresponding exclusion from $\mathrm P$. [Proof](ComplexityWorlds.md)
- The geometry examples produce genuine extension-degree obstructions before any complexity assumption. An extension-compatible reduction needs to preserve the full pattern of rational comparability, including delayed signed equivalence on explicitly describable inputs. This gives falsifiers for candidate field-natural compilers. [Proof](GeometryAndDescent.md)
