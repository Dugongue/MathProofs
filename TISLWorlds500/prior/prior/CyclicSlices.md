# Cyclic scalar slices in the square-zero orientation problem

[Earlier square-zero equivalence](earlier/SquareZeroClosure.md) · [Prior weighted carrier](earlier/baseline/baseline/earlier/CubeZeroAndExtensions.md) · [Earlier ordinary compilation](earlier/baseline/baseline/LinearSwitchCompiler.md)

Work over \(F=\mathbb F_3\), with all vector spaces framed. Write \(H\) for the full deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction \(\mathsf{TI}_{SL}(F)\le_m\mathsf{TI}(F)\). I use the exact reflection of the square-zero matrix spaces in *SquareZeroClosure.md*: for the displayed carrier \(\mathcal S(A)\), the conjugator signs are exactly the determinants of \(F\)-algebra isomorphisms of \(A\). In the cube-zero commutative local algebra \(A=F1\oplus X\oplus Z\), these determinants are precisely the total signs of the transporters of its radical product \(\beta:X\times X\to Z\). This follows since every algebra isomorphism induces the two radical-grade maps and every compatible pair lifts. The unit and all shears contribute determinant one.

Two results narrow this exact missing lemma. The first gives a one-query ordinary compiler on a recognizable subclass with unbounded ambient and output dimensions. The second shows that the same cyclic-slice model, with a non-Frobenius slice algebra, still has the full difficulty of \(H\). In that second model, the ambient dimension is exactly twice the output dimension, all scalar forms have split nonsingular quotient, and all operator ratios to a nonsingular slice commute. The proofs are informal.

## 1. Cyclic scalar slices and an actual odd correction

Write \(S=\beta^*(Z^*)\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_F(X,X^*)\), using the scalar slices as symmetric matrices. Define the right idealizer
\[
 C=\{T\in\operatorname{End}_F(X):S T\subseteq S\}.
\]
A change of basis carrying one form space to another conjugates their idealizers. Assume that \(S\) contains a nonsingular slice \(B\) and \(S=B C\). Every element of \(C\) is self-adjoint for \(B\) because \(BT\) is symmetric. The products \(TU\in C\) are also self-adjoint, so \(TU=UT\). Consequently \(C=\prod_i C_i\) is a finite-dimensional commutative algebra written as a product of local factors. Its central idempotents split \(X=\bigoplus_i X_i\) into a \(B\)-orthogonal direct sum, and \(X_i\) is a faithful \(C_i\)-module. Since \(B\) is invertible, \(\dim_F C=\dim_F S=\dim_F Z\).

**Theorem 1.** If some \(C_i\) is Frobenius of odd dimension over \(F\), the pointwise isometry group of \(S\) contains a determinant-\(-1\) involution. Thus every ordinary isomorphism instance in the square-zero normal form on this subclass is even. There is a deterministic polynomial-time many-one map to one ordinary TI comparison for the subclass, with or without the ordinary-conjugate promise. In particular this holds when \(C\) is Frobenius, since \(\dim_F Z\) is odd.

Here Frobenius for a local commutative algebra means that its socle is one-dimensional over the residue field. Every nonzero ideal contains that socle. Indeed, apply successive powers of the radical to a nonzero ideal until the last nonzero subspace. That subspace is annihilated by the radical, so it is a nonzero part of the one-dimensional socle.

Choose nonzero \(s\) in that socle. It acts nontrivially on \(X_i\) by faithfulness. The bilinear form \((x,y)\mapsto B(sx,y)\) on \(X_i\) is symmetric and nonzero, so there is \(v\) with \(B(sv,v)\ne0\), by polarization in odd characteristic. The annihilator of \(v\) is an ideal of \(C_i\) missing \(s\), hence vanishes. Thus \(P=C_i v\) is the regular \(C_i\)-module. The restriction of \(B\) to \(P\) pairs \(av,bv\) by \(B(abv,v)\). For nonzero \(a\), the ideal \(a C_i\) contains \(s\), so choosing \(b\) with \(ab=s\) proves that this restriction is nondegenerate. Both \(P\) and its orthogonal complement are \(C\)-stable. The linear map equal to \(-I\) on \(P\) and \(I\) on its orthogonal complement is a \(B\)-isometry commuting with \(C\). It therefore fixes every form \(B c\). Its determinant is \((-1)^{\dim_F C_i}=-1\). The induced cube-zero algebra automorphism acts by this reflection on \(X\) and by the identity on the unit and \(Z\); it lifts to an odd normalizer of the square-zero carrier. Compose with an ordinary conjugator of either sign to achieve plus.

The hypothesis is recognizable from the form matrices, with no given nonsingular slice. Compute \(C\) by linear equations and test commutativity. Use finite-algebra decomposition to obtain the local idempotents and radicals. On each \(X_i=e_iX\), the block \(S_i=S e_i\) is supported on \(X_i\times X_i\), by symmetry. Test whether \(S_i\) is isomorphic as a \(C_i\)-module to its regular module and find the image \(B_i\) of the unit. This uses constructive module isomorphism. Alternatively, test the one-dimensionality of \(S_i/\operatorname{rad}(C_i)S_i\) over the residue field, compare the total dimensions, and choose a lift of a generator by Nakayama's lemma. Check that \(B_i\) is nondegenerate on \(X_i\); any other generator differs by multiplication by a unit and has the same property. The sum of these forms gives \(B\) and certifies \(S=B C\). Compute the socle of each factor by its radical annihilator and check its dimension over the residue field. A vector \(v\) in the correction proof can be found by testing basis vectors and their pairwise sums. All computations are polynomial in written coefficients. On the recognized domain, return the ordinary TI comparison of the two multiplication tensors of \(A\) and \(A'\). On the ordinary-conjugate promise this comparison is always YES. No oriented subroutine is used. [Brooksbank and Luks, constructive module isomorphism](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf) · [Ivanyos and Qiao, finite-algebra algorithms, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

This applies to arbitrary cyclic étale slice spaces, and includes cyclic local slice algebras with radical of unbounded length. It does not assume that \(X\) is free over \(C\). The existence of the odd sign is shown by an actual reflection on an explicitly recovered summand, so no witness of the ordinary comparison is needed to build the query.

## 2. The hyperbolic regular-plus-dual construction

Let \(C\) be any local finite-dimensional commutative \(F\)-algebra, with arbitrary ordered basis. Set
\[
 X=C\oplus C^*,\qquad Z=C^*,\qquad
 \beta_C((a,f),(b,g))(c)=f(cb)+g(ca).
\]
The unit of \(C\) supplies a nonsingular scalar slice \(B_1\) which is the standard split hyperbolic form on \(C\oplus C^*\). The span of scalar slices is \(S_C=B_1\rho(C)\), where
\(\rho(c)=L_c\oplus L_c^*\) is the faithful regular-plus-dual action and \(L_c^*\) is the dual of \(L_c\). In particular its right idealizer is exactly \(\rho(C)\): a right multiplier sends the invertible \(B_1\) into \(B_1\rho(C)\), hence is in \(\rho(C)\). Distinct \(c\) give distinct scalar slices, so the bracket spans \(Z\); \(B_1\) shows its common radical is zero. Every scalar form has block matrix \(\begin{psmallmatrix}0&M\\M^{\mathsf T}&0\end{psmallmatrix}\). After quotienting its common kernel on the two halves, the surviving form is a split perfect pairing. Neither half nor \(B_1\) is marked for isomorphisms.

**Theorem 2.** For local non-Frobenius \(C,C'\) of equal odd dimension, the exact total determinant set of \(\beta_C\to\beta_{C'}\) equals
\[
 \{\det_F\phi:\phi:C\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}C'\},
\]
with the displayed bases and dual bases. Consequently the same is true of the cube-zero algebra \(A(C)=F1\oplus X\oplus Z\) with radical product \(\beta_C\), and of its square-zero matrix-space conjugators. The cube-zero algebra satisfies every parity and annihilator condition of the missing lemma: \(\dim_F X=2\dim_F Z\), \(\dim_F Z=\dim_F C\) is odd, and \(\operatorname{Ann}_{J}(J)=J^2=Z\).

Proof. An ordinary bimap transporter \((p,h)\) pulls back \(S_{C'}\) to \(S_C\). Taking right idealizers shows that \(p\) conjugates \(\rho(C)\) onto \(\rho(C')\), giving an actual algebra isomorphism \(\phi:C\to C'\). The canonical bimap transporter induced by \(\phi\) is \(\phi\oplus\phi^{-\mathsf T}\) on \(X\) and \(\phi^{-\mathsf T}\) on \(Z\), as evaluation on \(c'\) verifies. Its determinant on \(X\) is one and its determinant on \(Z\) is \(\det\phi\) in \(F^\times=\{\pm1\}\).

After removing this lift, \(p\) is \(C\)-linear. Its pullback takes \(B_1\) to \(B_t\) for a unit \(t\in C\), since a slice \(B_t=B_1\rho(t)\) is nonsingular exactly when \(t\) is a unit. The \(C\)-linear block map \(D_t=L_t\oplus I\) on \(X\) induces this pullback, and transforms the form \(B_c\) to \(B_{tc}\). The two determinants on \(X,Z\) of this transporter are both \(\det_F L_t\) (one is inverse if the other tensor convention is chosen). Their product is one. Removing \(D_t\) leaves a \(C\)-linear \(B_1\)-isometry, which acts trivially on \(Z\).

For completeness, every such pointwise isometry is even. The modules \(C\) and \(C^*\) are indecomposable, with local endomorphism rings both isomorphic to \(C\); the dual assertion follows from finite-dimensional vector-space duality. They are nonisomorphic because the socle of \(C^*\) is \((C/\operatorname{rad} C)^*\), one-dimensional over the residue field, while the socle of \(C\) has larger dimension. Write \(E=\operatorname{End}_C(C\oplus C^*)\). Its semisimple quotient is \(K\times K\), where \(K=C/\operatorname{rad} C\), given by the two diagonal residues. In detail, any composite through the other indecomposable is a nonunit on the first: an invertible composite would exhibit an isomorphic direct summand and the two modules have the same dimension. Thus those composites lie in the diagonal radicals, and taking diagonal residues is multiplicative. An element in its kernel has \(I-u\) invertible by the block Schur complement, since the diagonal blocks differ from identity by radicals and the off-diagonal composite lies in the second radical. The same test after multiplying \(u\) by any element of \(E\) shows that the kernel is the Jacobson radical, as the quotient is already semisimple.

The \(B_1\)-adjoint involution on \(E\) swaps the two diagonal residues, identifying their scalar by the duality action. A unitary therefore has quotient residues \((s,s^{-1})\). The \(F\)-determinant of these regular and dual actions is \(N_{K/F}(s)^{\dim_F C/[K:F]}N_{K/F}(s^{-1})^{\dim_F C/[K:F]}=1\), by the radical filtration on \(C\) or its dual. Any lift differs from these diagonal actions by a unit \(1+r\) with \(r\) in the radical of \(E\), and is unipotent in this faithful linear representation. This proves the pointwise assertion. Combining the three normalized factors gives total sign exactly \(\det\phi\). The canonical lift of every algebra isomorphism gives the other direction. [Standard adjoint-algebra language and radical decomposition](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

## 3. The full difficulty survives in the cyclic non-Frobenius subclass

**Corollary.** The missing lemma is equivalent to \(H\) even on the constructed subclass of Section 2 with \(C\) scalar-residue, local, non-Frobenius and \((\operatorname{rad}C)^3=0\). This includes the ordinary-conjugate promise with no conjugator supplied. Ordinary comparison of this subclass is TI-complete.

For the lower direction use the earlier symmetric weighted cube-zero compiler for any specified character of the three determinant signs. One can choose the output algebra \(C\) to have odd dimension while preserving its exact algebra-isomorphism determinant set. In the compiler's notation, the main role dimension \(D\), output dimension \(Q\) of the main symmetric product, and trace-cycle marker count \(b\) are all made even. Add a further pure pin with its compulsory separate output line if necessary to adjust \(D\), and use the available reserve products to adjust \(Q\). Choose an even marker count \(b\ge4\) with enough marker roles. All trace-edge input blocks have dimensions \(4r_e\). Every original determinant-cancellation equation still holds: the main scalar contributes \(\tau^{2Q}\), and common marker scalar contributes \(\alpha^{D+b}\), both one. The old choice \(D\) odd was only used to make the dimension even and eliminate unit determinants in the multiplication tensor. Here the source concerns algebra determinants directly. The dimension \(1+2D+Q+b+\sum_e4r_e\) is odd, and the radical square, equal to its annihilator, has dimension \(Q+b\ge4\). Thus \(C\) is local non-Frobenius with scalar residue and cube-zero radical, with exactly the original prescribed character image. No square-zero matrix claim here uses unit determinants of this intermediate odd-dimensional \(C\).

Apply Theorem 2 and then the original square-zero carrier to \(A(C)\). This final algebra has even dimension \(1+3\dim C\), so the determinant-neutral unit calculation in the square-zero lemma applies. The total orientation in the final carrier is the original determinant character. With the trivial character this proves ordinary TI hardness. For the full three-sign test use the seven nontrivial characters and the ordinary test, conjoining the constant collection of ordinary comparisons after applying the proposed missing-lemma compiler. This uses the coset separation argument from the original lemma and preserves its promise by separately conjoining the ordinary comparison. Conversely \(H\) solves every signed comparison on this subclass via the ordinary square-zero reflection and the even-dimensional multiplication table of \(A(C)\). The statement can use canonical frames computed from \(C\), or supplied arbitrary frames with their explicit determinant calibration. [Earlier weighted cube-zero character compiler](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2) (the ordinary tensor-system step is Theorem 4.1; the weighted construction is the staged atlas proof).

This supplies a concrete limitation of a closed-slice strategy: having a commuting algebra of ratios to a split scalar form, or even having every scalar form split modulo its radical, still leaves the full determinant problem. The Frobenius correction of Theorem 1 cannot remove this case.

## 4. Explicit sign-rigid ordinary pair with no Frobenius correction

For \(n\ge4\) divisible by four, let \(C=F[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(x_1,\ldots,x_n)^3\). It has odd dimension \(1+n+\binom{n+1}{2}\), and its socle \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(F^n)\) has dimension greater than one. Every algebra automorphism acts on its two radical grades by \(P\in\operatorname{GL}_n(F)\) and \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 P\), with shears having determinant one. Hence every algebra automorphism determinant is \((\det P)^{n+2}=1\). Choose a coordinate reflection \(D\) on the full ordered table and form \(C'\) with multiplication \(D\,\mu_C(D^{-1}-,D^{-1}-)\). The table has the same abstract algebra and every relative algebra isomorphism \(C\to C'\) has determinant \(-1\). Theorem 2 gives ordinary-conjugate square-zero matrix spaces \(\mathcal S(A(C)),\mathcal S(A(C'))\) with no even conjugator, each satisfying the preceding commuting-ratio and split-slice properties. The construction does not merely exhibit even pointwise isometries, since it controls every ordinary bimap transporter and the entire square-zero normalizer.

The result is a positive ordinary reduction for the recognizable Frobenius subclass and a precise equivalence for the non-Frobenius cyclic model. It gives no compiler for all non-Frobenius cyclic models, and no proof of \(H\).


## 5. Recognizing the subclass directly in ambient coordinates

The Frobenius compiler needs no supplied basis identifying the square-zero carrier with \(A^2\oplus A\), provided the input is promised to be a conjugate of such a carrier. Compute \(W=\bigcap_{N\in\mathcal S}\ker N=\sum_{N\in\mathcal S}\operatorname{im}N\), and set \(U=V/W\). View the space as rectangular maps \(U\to W\) and compute its left idealizer by linear equations. On the promise this represented algebra acts regularly on \(W\) and on the rectangular space as two copies of its regular module. Construct these module isomorphisms by constructive module isomorphism. In the second identification take the images \(m_1,m_2\) of the two regular generators. The map \(U\to W^2\), \(u\mapsto(m_1u,m_2u)\), is invertible: on a hidden presentation it is a change of \(A\)-basis on the row module \(A^2\). This gives an explicit normalization to the original square-zero carrier. Choosing lifts of the quotient basis and adjoining a basis of \(W\) also computes the individual ambient determinant calibration if required. The Frobenius subclass does not need that calibration, since the actual odd normalizer supplies both signs. Then compute \(J/J^2,J^2\) in the recovered \(A\) and apply the slice recognition tests of Section 1. [Constructive module isomorphism](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)


## 6. Bounded checks

Over \(F[t]/(t^3)\), take \(s=t^2\) and \(v=(1,\lambda)\), where \(\lambda\) extracts the \(t^2\) coordinate. The correction is the reflection in the graph of the nonsingular trace-pairing matrix. Its three-dimensional negative space gives sign \(-1\), and all 108 scalar basis-pair identities across the three slices hold. Over the non-Frobenius \(F[x,y]/(x,y)^2\), direct substitution verified 648 basis-pair equations for all 18 unit-scaling lifts and 1,728 equations for all 48 canonical lifts of \(\operatorname{GL}_2(F)\) on the radical. These checks supplement the general arguments; they do not enumerate the orientation problem over unbounded inputs.
