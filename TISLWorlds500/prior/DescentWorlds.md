Write \(A=\mathsf{TI}_{\mathrm{SL}}(\mathbb F_3)\), \(B=\mathsf{TI}(\mathbb F_3)\), and \(H\) for \(A\le_m^{\mathrm P} B\). A clash below means a stated theorem would imply \(H\) and hence conflicts with the hypothesis \(\neg H\). Field-natural means that the same output data have the stated correctness after every finite scalar extension. This is a stronger requirement than a reduction on the fixed \(\mathbb F_3\) input language. The earlier atlas supplies its ordinary conjunction construction, weighted cube-zero carriers, and exact Frobenius component criterion.

### W01. Nilpotent components give one exact extension period

Let two rational tensors over \(K=\mathbb F_q\) lie in one geometric orbit for the tested action, and let \(\Gamma\) be the component group of the reduced geometric stabilizer. If \(\Gamma\) is nilpotent, their successful extension degrees are precisely the multiples of one integer \(m\) dividing \(|\Gamma|\). In particular equivalence over any extensions of collectively coprime degrees implies equivalence over \(K\), even for nonabelian nilpotent \(\Gamma\) and without a rational ordinary transporter.

By Lang, rational forms and their extension maps are the twisted classes in \(\Gamma\) and the ordered Frobenius products, as in atlas entries 183–184. Factor \(\Gamma=\prod_p\Gamma_p\) into its characteristic Sylow subgroups. Both twisted conjugacy and the restriction of each class split into the corresponding factors. In one factor \(\Gamma_p\), a successful extension of degree \(p^u v\) with \(p\nmid v\) already succeeds over the degree-\(p^u\) intermediate field by coprime restriction injectivity for twisted classes. The success spectrum for that factor is therefore the multiples of the least successful \(p\)-power. Existence and the bound \(p^u\le|\Gamma_p|\) follow by viewing its equation as fixed-point search under iterates of a permutation of the \(|\Gamma_p|\) possible component alignments. These alignments are acted on by \(x\mapsto b\sigma(x)a^{-1}\) in suitable conventions for the two twists, with the iterates giving the corresponding ordered products. A nonempty finite permutation has a cycle of length at most its size. The product of the resulting coprime prime powers is \(m\) and divides \(|\Gamma|\). [Lang, 1956](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Lang-Algebraic_Groups_Over_Finite_Fields.pdf)

### W02. A size-independent obstruction to nilpotent-component target worlds

A field-natural exact transformation of signed tensor comparison cannot have nilpotent reduced stabilizer component group on every output, regardless of its output size. The old closed signed multiplication-tensor examples over \(\mathbb F_3\) succeed exactly at extension degrees divisible by one of a chosen list of distinct odd primes. Taking two primes gives a spectrum containing both prime degrees but excluding degree one. Any field-natural correct output would be geometrically equivalent and have the same spectrum, contrary to W01 if its component group were nilpotent. This argument concerns an extension requirement and does not prohibit a transformation whose correctness is asserted only over \(\mathbb F_3\). It strengthens the earlier connected-component scope from connectedness to arbitrary nilpotent component groups.

### W03. Exact restriction period for a cyclic component action

Suppose the relevant finite component group is cyclic \(C_m\), with Frobenius acting by multiplication by a positive unit representative \(r\) modulo \(m\). Let \(u\) be the difference of two base twists in its additive coordinates. For a prime \(\ell\mid m\) with \(v_\ell(u)<\min(v_\ell(r-1),v_\ell(m))\), successful restriction at degree \(k\) requires
\[
v_\ell(1+r+\cdots+r^{k-1})\ge v_\ell(m)-v_\ell(u).
\]
There is no condition from any other prime. In fact the exact restriction condition in entry 185 is \(\gcd(m,r^k-1)\mid u(1+r+\cdots+r^{k-1})\). Factoring \(r^k-1\) gives the stated prime-by-prime cancellation. For odd active \(\ell\) the left side of the valuation inequality is \(v_\ell(k)\). For active \(\ell=2\) it is zero at odd \(k\) and \(v_2(r+1)+v_2(k)-1\) at even \(k\), by the geometric-sum valuation formula. The same formula holds when \(r=1\), using \(v_\ell(0)=\infty\). Thus the least restriction degree is explicit from the prime powers and the action. Its size can exceed the order of the original coinvariant class substantially: take \(C_{\ell^a}\) with odd \(\ell\), \(r=1+\ell\), \(u=1\). The class has order \(\ell\) in the base coinvariants, and the minimum successful degree is \(\ell^a\).

### W04. Free integral typed diagrams have one free integral tensor comparison

A polynomial list of bilinear maps, linear maps and marked vectors between named finite free \(\mathbb Z\)-modules, with all repeated occurrences required to have the same maps, reduces deterministically to one ordinary free integral three-tensor comparison. The following direct intermediate construction works over any fixed characteristic-zero domain with effective arithmetic and polynomial coefficient operations, as a reduction to algebra isomorphism there.

Use one master slot per named module and private copies per occurrence, and let \(W\) be their direct sum. Form a possibly nonassociative algebra \(R e_1\oplus R e_2\oplus W\), with \(e_i e_j=\delta_{ij}e_i\), all right products \(w e_i=0\), and left products \(e_i w=L_iw\). Let \(L_1\) be an integer scalar at least two on each slot, distinct for distinct slots. The operator \(L_2\) contains the identity links from each master to its private copies, and every prescribed linear map in a distinct private source-to-output block. This directed operator is nilpotent, with paths of length at most two. Products inside \(W\) are the prescribed bilinear maps on their private slots and zero elsewhere. They all have output in slots with no further outgoing slot edges, so \(W^3=0\) and \(L_2\) vanishes on their output space \(W_1\). A marked vector is a linear map out of a scalar line whose map is pinned by writing its own bilinear multiplication in private copies.

The ideal \(W\) is the largest nilpotent ideal since the quotient \(R^2\) has no nonzero nilpotent ideal. Hence isomorphisms permute the two quotient idempotents. Each has exactly one idempotent lift: writing it \(e_i+w\) gives \((I-L_i)w=w^2\in W_1\). The induced \(I-L_i\) is injective after passage to the fraction field on \(W/W_1\), so \(w\in W_1\), then \(w^2=0\) and \(w=0\). The positive integer trace of \(L_1\) and zero trace of \(L_2\) distinguish the two labels. Preserving \(L_1\) fixes each slot, because differences of its distinct integer eigenvalues are nonzero in the domain. The \(L_2\) and product equations are then precisely the original linked equations. Conversely any linked transporter lifts by the assigned maps on the private slots. The sizes and marker coefficient bit lengths are polynomial. Over \(\mathbb Z\), apply the action \(5\)-to-\(1\) reduction of Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun to the resulting explicit algebra tables. This produces one ordinary free three-tensor comparison. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S1)

### W05. Signed ternary TI reduces to ordinary free integral TI

This unconditional many-one bridge concerns ordinary integral targets. Start from a ternary bimap \(T:X\times Y\to Z\) in fixed bases, replace its role spaces by free integer lattices of the same dimensions, and write \(\widetilde T:X\otimes_{\mathbb Z}Y\to Z\) for an arbitrary coefficient lift. Include the tensor-product bimap into a named lattice \(P=X\otimes_{\mathbb Z}Y\), linking its map to the product of the maps on \(X,Y\). Include a named injective map into \(P\oplus Z\) whose image is
\[
L_T=\{(p,z):z\equiv\widetilde T(p)\pmod3\}.
\]
A basis matrix is \(\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\\widetilde T&3I\end{pmatrix}\). Inclusions into the direct sum force the map there to be the block sum of the prescribed maps. Preservation of this image is equivalent to the tensor equation modulo three, since the image is the full inverse image of its finite graph. An allowed block map preserving the image uniquely determines the isomorphism of its free lattice domain.

In each mode \(V\), name another copy \(V_*\) with its coordinate basis pinned pointwise. Add the lattice \(\{(v,w)\in V\oplus V_*:v\equiv w\bmod5\}\) by its analogous explicit graph matrix. Its preservation forces \(g_V\equiv I\bmod5\). An invertible integral matrix has determinant \(1\) or \(-1\), so this congruence forces \(1\). Conversely every determinant-one map over \(\mathbb F_3\) lifts to an integral determinant-one matrix congruent to the identity modulo five: write it as a product of elementary matrices and lift each off-diagonal coefficient using the Chinese remainder theorem. In rank one the allowed map is already the identity. Thus the ordinary integral diagrams are isomorphic exactly for the original signed ternary YES instances. W04 gives one ordinary free integral TI instance. Under \(\neg H\), even the resulting restricted ordinary integral tensor family cannot reduce back to \(B\).

### W06. A congruence-general integral transfer

W05 works uniformly starting with signed TI over \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\), with \(m\ge2\) written in binary. Use the mod-\(m\) graph lattice and congruence pins modulo a coprime odd modulus, for example \(2m+1\). The reduction \(\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb Z)\to\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\) is onto by elementary generation over the latter ring. To see this, split the ring into its local prime-power factors for the proof. Over each factor any unimodular column has a unit entry, so elementary row and column operations reduce a determinant-one matrix to the identity, leaving only diagonal unit pairs \(\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})\), which themselves are elementary products. A global elementary list is obtained by giving coefficients zero on all other factors via CRT. For the integral lift choose each elementary coefficient zero modulo \(2m+1\) and equal to the required coefficient modulo \(m\). The algorithm writes the input graph matrices directly and need not factor \(m\). All written coefficients are polynomial-bit integers. This yields a single ordinary free integral TI target through W04 even when the congruence ring is variable.

### W07. Integral genus cannot determine ordinary algebra isomorphism in these families

For the integral diagrams in W05, at \(\mathbb Z_{(3)}\) the mod-five pin lattices become entire free modules, and the mod-three graph still imposes the ordinary ternary equation. Every invertible ternary matrix lifts to \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z_{(3)})\). At \(\mathbb Z_{(5)}\) the mod-three graph becomes the entire free module and the identity on the role maps satisfies all remaining pins. At every other prime both lattices become entire free modules. Over \(\mathbb Q\) and \(\mathbb R\) there are no remaining congruence conditions. These assertions are about ordinary comparison and thus also hold over all corresponding completions. The explicit algebra carrier in W04 commutes with these base changes, and its correctness holds over each of the characteristic-zero domains just considered. In particular any ordinary YES but signed NO ternary pair gives integral algebras that are isomorphic rationally and over every prime localization, but fail ordinary integral isomorphism. They are also isomorphic modulo every positive integer by reducing the local isomorphisms modulo each prime power and combining them with CRT. One explicit ternary pair is \(A(x,y)=\operatorname{diag}(xI_3,yI_4,(x+y)I_5)\), \(B(x,y)=A(-x,y)\): the label reflection is an ordinary transporter, while its determinant can only be repaired by a scalar whose square is \(-1\), because the distinct weight factors force the source label stabilizer to be scalar. This proves a local-global failure for the actual integral algebras, independently of \(H\).

### W08. Number rings with infinite unit group still admit a residue-sign transfer

Fix a number ring \(R=\mathcal O_K\) with a prime \(\mathfrak m\) of residue field \(\mathbb F_3\). There is an auxiliary ideal \(\mathfrak n\) coprime to \(\mathfrak m\) such that every unit reducing to one modulo \(\mathfrak n\) reduces to one modulo \(\mathfrak m\). To construct it, the finite group \(R^\times/(R^\times)^2\) has finitely many classes reducing to \(-1\) at \(\mathfrak m\), by Dirichlet's theorem and the fact that squares reduce to \(1\). For every such class choose a representative \(\eta\). It is a nonsquare in \(K\): if \(\eta=b^2\) in \(K\), all finite valuations of \(b\) are zero, making \(b\in R^\times\). Chebotarev in \(K(\sqrt{\eta})/K\) supplies an odd unramified prime different from \(\mathfrak m\) where \(\eta\) is nonsquare in the residue field. Multiply these finitely many chosen primes. A unit congruent to one modulo that product cannot lie in any of the selected square classes. These are fixed ring constants.

Use the mod-\(\mathfrak m\) graph and mod-\(\mathfrak n\) pins from W05, with the principality-free encoding in W09. An invertible role map preserving the pins has determinant congruent to one modulo \(\mathfrak n\), hence its residual determinant at \(\mathfrak m\) is one. Conversely CRT lifts the required determinant-one maps by elementary matrices with coefficients zero modulo \(\mathfrak n\). This yields an exact family of ordinary algebra comparisons over \(R\), including rings of positive unit rank. For these rings this conclusion concerns the explicit finite free algebra tables and uses no general tensor-encoding theorem over \(R\). [Dirichlet unit theorem in the algebraic number theory notes of Stein](https://wstein.org/books/ant/ant.pdf), [Chebotarev density theorem](https://websites.math.leidenuniv.nl/algebra/chebotarev.pdf)

### W09. Nonprincipal congruence graphs still have exact free typed diagrams

Let \(R\) be a domain, \(I=(s_1,\ldots,s_t)\) an explicitly generated ideal, and \(M:P\to Z\) a map between free modules. The graph of \(M\) modulo \(I\) can be imposed without a free basis for the graph lattice. Name the free map
\[
(p,v_1,\ldots,v_t)\longmapsto
\Big(p,Mp+\sum_j s_jv_j\Big)
\]
into \(P\oplus Z\). Its image is the full inverse image of that graph. If \(G_P,G_Z\) satisfy \(G_ZM-M'G_P\equiv0\bmod I\), write the difference as \(\sum s_jD_j\), entrywise. The block triangular free domain map \((p,(v_j))\mapsto(G_Pp,(G_Zv_j+D_jp))\) is invertible and gives exact commutation. Conversely an invertible domain map implies equality of the images and hence the congruence. The same argument applies to the pin lattice with the identity map and its input coordinates fixed. Consequently both congruences of W08 are free typed diagrams, and W04 encodes them into finite free algebra tables over any fixed number ring meeting W08. This claim does not require a computation of generators of projective kernels or a principal presentation of either ideal.

### W10. A Hasse principle with a disconnected stabilizer

Let \(K\) be a number field and let \(X\) be the nonempty geometric transporter scheme of two \(K\)-tensors for either tested action. Suppose its source stabilizer \(G\) has a split extension \(G=G^\circ\rtimes\Gamma\) with \(\Gamma\) a constant finite group and \(G^\circ\) semisimple simply connected. If \(X(K_v)\ne\varnothing\) for every place \(v\), then \(X(K)\ne\varnothing\). Its component scheme is a torsor under the constant \(\Gamma\). Local triviality at all places makes the corresponding continuous homomorphism \(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline K/K)\to\Gamma\) trivial: a nonidentity element in its finite image occurs as an unramified Frobenius conjugacy class by Chebotarev and acts without a fixed point on the regular torsor. Choose a rational component. The split section of \(\Gamma\) translates every local point into that chosen component. It is a \(G^\circ\)-torsor, so the Kneser–Harder–Chernousov Hasse principle gives a rational point there. This extends the earlier connected version while requiring a particular split finite quotient. [Chernousov, final \(E_8\) case](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=dan&option_lang=eng&paperid=7092&wshow=paper), [Chebotarev](https://websites.math.leidenuniv.nl/algebra/chebotarev.pdf)

### W11. Exact separation persists on socle tensors over a fixed local lift

Let \(R\) be an explicitly tabulated finite commutative local ring with residue \(\mathbb F_3\), maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\), and choose a fixed nonzero socle element \(s\) killed by \(\mathfrak m\). Then \(\operatorname{Ann}_R(s)=\mathfrak m\), since an annihilator outside the maximal ideal would contain a unit. Write a ternary tensor \(T\) as the \(R\)-tensor \(s\widetilde T\) on the same free format, independently of the chosen coefficient lifts. For either action, two such tensors are equivalent exactly when the corresponding ternary tensors are equivalent. Indeed every \(R\)-transporter reduces to one over the residue field by the annihilator identity. Any ordinary residue transporter lifts to invertible matrices over the local ring. For a signed residue transporter divide one column of each arbitrary lift by its unit determinant, which is congruent to one, to obtain determinant one without changing the reduction. The entire equation on socle coefficients sees only the reduction. Recover the residue entries from the stated ideal \(sR\cong\mathbb F_3\), so the reverse reductions are also exact. Thus in the \(\neg H\) world the gap persists even on this restricted free-module family over such \(R\), including \(R=\mathbb Z/3^r\mathbb Z\) for every fixed \(r\). This proof concerns the socle-supported branch and makes no claim about unconstrained ordinary TI over every finite ring.

### W12. A CRT product of extension fields cannot erase the gap

Let \(R=\prod_{i=1}^t\mathbb F_{3^{d_i}}\) be fixed with \(t\ge1\). Under \(\neg H\) signed free-module TI over \(R\) has no deterministic polynomial many-one reduction to its ordinary counterpart. In both actions, free tensors and the transporter conditions split into the field factors. If there were an ordinary compiler over \(R\), test any signed field-factor pair by placing it there and using equal zero tensors of the same formats elsewhere. An output ordinary \(R\)-pair is just the fixed conjunction of ordinary comparisons in its fields. Each reduces to \(B\) by marking coefficient multiplication in the ordinary restriction of scalars, and the conjunction is one \(B\)-query. The existing fixed-extension reflection reduces \(A\) to signed comparison in the chosen factor. Conversely \(B\) reduces into the ordinary product problem by the earlier fixed-extension ordinary compiler and zero tensors elsewhere. This separates a same-characteristic product from an arbitrary cross-characteristic product, whose extra ordinary factors need not have a proven reduction to \(B\).

### W13. The negative world persists for a concrete projective \(\operatorname{PSL}\) comparison

Use the cube-format multiplication tensors of the scalar-residue cube-zero algebras of even dimension from the square-zero construction. Test their projective points under the product of the three rational projective special-linear groups. For every nonzero tensor whose format has an even side over \(\mathbb F_3\), determinant-one equivalence of the tensors is equivalent to equivalence of their projective points under those groups. Projective representatives lift to determinant-one matrices by the definition of \(\operatorname{PSL}\). Their extra scalar on the tensor can be absorbed by \(-I\) on any even side, which is determinant one. Projective equivalence under the full rational projective general-linear groups is equivalent to ordinary TI by independent scalar multiplication. On the specified cube-zero family, a compiler from this projective \(\operatorname{PSL}\) comparison to ordinary TI therefore implies \(H\): apply it to the seven weighted orientation tests, then take one ordinary conjunction, as in the square-zero closure. The reverse implication uses \(H\) on the corresponding signed multiplication-tensor comparison. Thus the projective presentation keeps exactly the full unresolved compiler question on that explicit family.

### W14. Exact scalar ambiguity in the projective comparison

For arbitrary finite \(F=\mathbb F_q\), only the factor
\[
C=\prod_{i=1}^3\{\lambda\in F^\times:\lambda^{n_i}=1\}
\]
can be altered by choosing different determinant-one representatives of fixed projective maps. This follows because every pair of lifts of a fixed projective map differs by a scalar matrix. Thus every projective special-linear transporter can be normalized to an exact tensor transporter by changing only its representatives whenever \(\operatorname{lcm}_i\gcd(n_i,q-1)=q-1\), the criterion \(C=F^\times\). The even-side hypothesis in W13 is substantive. Over \(\mathbb F_3\), take the field multiplication tensor of \(E=\mathbb F_{3^n}\) with \(n\) odd, and compare it to its negative. They give the same projective point, while signed equivalence would require a field automorphism with determinant \(-1\). In the multiplication autotopism formula the determinant tuple is \((ad,bd,abd)\), where \(a,b\) are unit norms and \(d\) the automorphism determinant. The demanded correction \((1,1,-1)\) gives \(d=-1\), but Frobenius on an odd-degree field always has determinant \(1\). This failure occurs on a full-support tensor.

### W15. The top Frobenius trace alone detects rational tensor equivalence

Let \(X\) be the reduced transporter of any two finite-field tensors for either action, and suppose it is geometrically nonempty. Its geometric components are smooth and have a common dimension \(d\), since after choosing a geometric transporter it is isomorphic to the reduced stabilizer over the algebraic closure. For \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}K\), smooth duality identifies \(H^{2d}_c(X_{\overline K},\mathbb Q_\ell)(d)\) with the Frobenius permutation module of its geometric components. Indeed each connected geometric component contributes one top fundamental class, and the Tate twist removes \(q^d\). Thus the trace on this single normalized cohomology space is the number of Frobenius-fixed components. Lang makes it positive exactly for rational equivalence. In the empty geometric case set the trace to zero. A deterministic polynomial algorithm computing this trace on arbitrary signed ternary tensor transporters, including handling the empty case, would decide \(A\) in polynomial time and therefore imply \(H\). The cohomological identification by itself says nothing about the complexity of computing it. [SGA 4, Exposé XVIII, §3](https://grothendiecksga.com/read/sga4/en/XVIII-3.html), [Lang](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Lang-Algebraic_Groups_Over_Finite_Fields.pdf)

### W16. An explicit zeta-factor formula for polynomial-count identity components

Suppose a nonempty geometric transporter over \(F_q\) has connected reduced source stabilizer \(J\) with \(|J(F_{q^k})|=P(q^k)\) for an integer polynomial \(P(t)=\sum_j b_jt^j\). This holds, for example, for split tori, general linear groups, and split unipotent groups. Write \(a_s\) for the number of length-\(s\) Frobenius cycles on the transporter components. The zeta function is
\[
Z_X(t)=\prod_{s,j}(1-q^{js}t^s)^{-a_s b_j}.
\]
Indeed Lang gives \(|X(F_{q^k})|=P(q^k)\sum_{s\mid k}s a_s\); substituting in the exponential generating definition of \(Z_X\) and summing each geometric logarithm gives the formula. The factors for distinct \(j\) have disjoint complex root radii. With \(P\) given, the largest \(j\) block of poles recovers the component-cycle product, from which the individual \(a_s\) are recovered by logarithms and Möbius inversion. This is an exact positive description under the displayed polynomial-count hypothesis, and does not supply the Frobenius cycles from arbitrary signed input tensors.

### W17. Dual-number lifting has an exact normal-quotient criterion

Let \(\varepsilon^2=0\) over a finite field \(F\), and write two covariant tensors as \(T+\varepsilon t\) and \(S+\varepsilon s\). A prescribed determinant-one base transporter \(g\) lifts to one over \(F[\varepsilon]\) if and only if
\[
s-g t\in \sum_i \mathfrak{sl}(V_i)\cdot S .
\]
Any lift is uniquely \((I+\varepsilon X)g\) per factor, and determinant one is equivalent to \(\operatorname{tr}X=0\) since \(\varepsilon^2=0\). Expanding the action gives the stated linear test and produces a lift by solving it. The analogous ordinary criterion replaces \(\mathfrak{sl}\) by full matrix algebras. In particular if a base transporter is supplied there is a deterministic polynomial exact first-order test. If the original task is existence of some base transporter whose normal class matches, this theorem locates the extra orbit condition in the quotient by the tangent image, rather than supplying a base signed witness.

### W18. Surjective orbit derivative gives an effective complete local lift

For tensors over a complete discrete valuation ring \(R\) with finite residue \(F\), assume an ordinary or signed transporter \(\bar g\) exists modulo its uniformizer. In the tested action suppose the orbit derivative at the target residue tensor maps the corresponding full-matrix or trace-zero Lie algebra onto the whole tensor space over \(F\). Then \(\bar g\) lifts to a transporter over \(R\). At precision \(j\), take the residual error divided by the \(j\)-th power of the uniformizer and solve the derivative equation over \(F\). Correct an arbitrary lift by \(I+\pi^j X\) in each mode. In the signed case the trace-zero condition means its determinant is one modulo \(\pi^{j+1}\), and one column can be divided by the exact unit determinant without changing this correction modulo \(\pi^{j+1}\). These steps iterate from any initial lift with determinant adjusted. Corrections tend to zero in the complete topology, so the limit is the desired transporter. Where residue arithmetic and finite-precision lifts are explicit, a prescribed initial transporter yields a polynomial number of finite-field linear operations and ring operations per requested precision. The derivative assumption concerns the orbit map, not just smoothness of an individual endpoint stabilizer.

### W19. All congruence tests characterize the completed local world

For any fixed tensors over \(\mathbb Z_p\), in either action equivalence over \(\mathbb Z_p\) holds if and only if they are equivalent modulo \(p^k\) for every \(k\ge1\). The transporter conditions modulo successive powers define nested closed subsets of the compact product of the corresponding \(\mathbb Z_p\) matrix groups. Each finite-field or finite-ring solution lifts to matrices in those groups, adjusting a column by a congruent-to-one determinant in the signed case. Nonemptiness of every subset therefore gives a point of their intersection. A bound on the required precision cannot depend only on the formats and individual smoothness: in format \(1\times1\times1\), the signed group is trivial and the two unit scalars \(1,1+p^N\) agree modulo \(p^N\) yet have no signed comparison over the completion. Their stabilizers are trivial smooth groups. W07 shows the further jump from every completed local ordinary comparison to a single global integral one can fail on an explicit family.
