# Coefficient-ring worlds and the special-linear reduction

Write \(H(F)\) for determinant-one dense three-tensor isomorphism over a fixed finite field \(F\) reducing to ordinary TI by a deterministic polynomial-time many-one map. For a finite commutative ring \(R\), write \(H(R)\) for the same comparison on explicitly framed **free** \(R\)-modules, with coefficient-linear changes on all three modes. Tensor entries in an explicitly varying characteristic-\(p\) ring are represented by an \(\mathbb F_p\)-basis and multiplication table. The field and ring problems in this note use exact determinants in the respective unit groups. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Open Question 1.9 and Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2)

## 1. An unconditional ordinary compiler for the principal determinant

Let \(R\) be finite commutative local of characteristic \(p\), with radical \(J\), residue field \(E\), and \(J^k=0\). Its canonical coefficient field is the stable image of Frobenius. That image is reduced by Frobenius injectivity, maps onto the residue field by surjectivity of field Frobenius, and has zero intersection with \(J\). Identify it with \(E\). For \(G\in\operatorname{GL}_n(R)\) write \(g=\bar G\) for its residue matrix, and set
\[
 \rho(G)=\det_R(G)/\det_E(g)\ \in\ 1+J .
\]
The denominator is taken in the coefficient field. This is a homomorphism. Tests of \(\rho(G)\) are different from the residue determinant tests.

**Theorem 1.** For fixed \(k\), comparison of two three-tensors over \(R\) by ordinary transporters satisfying any prescribed \(\rho(G_i)=u_i\in1+J\) in specified modes reduces to **one ordinary TI instance over \(E\)**. The construction is polynomial for explicitly varying \(R\), counting its multiplication table in the input, and uses a constant number of symmetric power degrees depending on \(k\). In particular no assumption \(H(E)\) is needed for a principal-unit test.

Here is the construction for one mode. Let \(V=R^n\), \(V_0=V/JV\). Include these \(E\)-vector spaces, the residue map, the action of \(R\), and the original tensor. The coefficient space \(R\) is fixed pointwise by a shift of an ordered basis together with a mark on its first vector. This makes ring-linearity an ordinary tensor-system constraint.

In the displayed residue frame let \(W\) be the space of \(E\)-linear maps \(S:V_0\to V\) whose reduction is \(cI\) for some \(c\in E\). The inclusions and evaluation map \(W\times V_0\to V\) intrinsically impose the action \(S\mapsto G S g^{-1}\) of an original transporter on the two framed copies of \(W\). It preserves the displayed coordinate \(c\). Each such matrix has a unique form \(cI+N\) in the fixed \(R\)-basis, with \(N\) over \(J\). Write \(r=\min(n,k-1)\), and let \(e_j(N)\) denote the sum of principal \(j\)-row minors. Define the \(R\)-valued homogeneous formal polynomial
\[
 P(c,N)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}c^{r-j}e_j(N),\qquad e_0=1.
\]
All contributions of degree at least \(k\) in entries of \(N\) are zero by \(J^k=0\). Consequently
\[
 \det_R(cI+N)=c^{n-r}P(c,N).
\]
Both equalities are identities over a formal polynomial ring over \(R\), using an \(E\)-basis of the independent \(N\) entries. The determinant of \(G S g^{-1}\) equals \(\rho(G)\det_R S\). Since \(c\) is preserved, and multiplication by a monomial in the independent indeterminate \(c\) is injective even over a ring with zero divisors, cancellation gives
\[
 P(G Sg^{-1})=\rho(G)P(S).
\]

Present the formal polynomial as a vector in \(R\otimes_E\operatorname{Sym}^r(W^*)\), where \(\operatorname{Sym}^r(W^*)\) is the degree-\(r\) coordinate polynomial space. No polarization of the polynomial or division by \(r!\) is used. Present evaluation and all symmetric multiplication maps through degree \(r\) as order-at-most-three constraints on named spaces. These force the induced action on formal polynomial coefficients. Mark the two endpoint polynomials with the prescribed factor \(u_i\), so that their compatibility is exactly \(P(G Sg^{-1})=u_iP(S)\). The equality implies \(\rho(G)=u_i\) by the value \(P(I)=1\). Conversely any such transporter extends along all displayed maps. For \(r=0\), \(P=1\) and the principal subgroup is trivial; a claimed nontrivial value can be rejected.

The dimension of \(W\) is at most \(1+n^2\dim_E J\). The monomials through degree \(r\) and the principal-minor sums can be generated by explicit loops of polynomial length for fixed \(k\). Pinning three modes increases the number of constraints polynomially. The existing polynomial packing of named tensor systems into a constant number of order-at-most-three tensors gives one ordinary target. [Ordinary typed packing](prior/earlier/baseline/baseline/LinearSwitchCompiler.md)

For \(J^2=0\) the gadget uses just \(P(c,N)=c+\operatorname{tr} N\) when \(n>0\). Thus principal-unit orientation in the complete dual-number family, including arbitrarily wide square-zero radicals, uses a linear coefficient mark. No condition on the full residue determinant is implicit in that result.

## 2. Exact transfer of the collapse across fixed local rings

**Theorem 2.** For every fixed finite commutative local ring \(R\) of characteristic \(p\) with residue field \(E\),
\[
       H(R)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ H(E).
\]
This proves an equivalence of deterministic many-one degree assertions for fixed rings, and applies even when the principal-unit group is nontrivial.

Ordinary TI over \(R\) and over \(E\) are unconditionally equivalent. Constant table entries lift over the coefficient field and reflect by reduction. In the other direction restrict scalars to \(E\), retaining a pointwise fixed \(R\) role and the module actions. The ordinary packing gives one ordinary \(E\)-tensor comparison with precisely the required linear transporters. The same coefficient-field lift and reduction preserve determinant-one between constant tensors. Hence \(H(R)\) implies \(H(E)\).

For the converse use Theorem 1 with all principal values one, retaining the three residue spaces and their actual induced maps. The remaining unknown signs here are the three values \(\det_E g_i\). Their triples form the empty set or a coset of the determinant image of the stabilizer of the structured comparison, since the principal-unit restrictions with value one define a tensor system whose solutions are transporters. The identity triple lies in the coset if and only if every multiplicative character of \((E^\times)^3\) attains the identity there. If there is no transporter every local test fails. This is character separation in a finite abelian quotient. It is important to test every character rather than independently check the three individual coordinate constraints on possibly different witnesses.

For clarity, a determinant character on any given polynomial-size ordinary typed system can be exported to a single weighted determinant of a **fixed-signature** typed system. Use the ordinary packing with masters, private occurrences, pinned tags and fixed scalar line. A master map \(g_i\) acts on its own block and on every attached copy, by \(g_i\) or \(g_i^{-\mathsf T}\) as prescribed. Determine its resulting exponent in the determinant of the packed total space. Adding at most \(|E^\times|-1\) more private, positively linked copies of that master adjusts the exponent to any desired residue modulo \(|E^\times|\). These copies are forced to follow the master and add no predicate. The two packed ambient roles and the number of tensor components are constant. Split their role occurrences a constant number of times and link by identities if needed for the fixed-signature weighted cube-zero compiler. The earlier exact character compiler now expresses the weighted test by a signed \(E\)-tensor comparison. Apply \(H(E)\) to each of the fixed finite list of characters, and conjoin the resulting ordinary comparisons. The single query obtained this way checks all three original ring determinants on the same transporter. [Exact finite-field character compiler](prior/earlier/baseline/baseline/earlier/CubeZeroAndExtensions.md) · [Ordinary conjunction](prior/earlier/baseline/baseline/LinearSwitchCompiler.md)

Thus, under \(\neg H(\mathbb F_3)\), signed TI is strictly harder than ordinary TI over **every fixed finite commutative characteristic-three local ring**. Indeed \(H(E)\) would reflect to \(H(\mathbb F_3)\) by the fixed-field transfer. The ordinary comparison over \(R\) reduces to ordinary comparison over \(E\), then to the signed comparison over \(E\) by unused-line padding, and then to the signed comparison over \(R\) by constant lift. In particular \(\mathrm{SL}\) versus \(\mathrm{GL}\) over \(R\) is a strict many-one comparison on the stated domains if the base world is strict. [Fixed-field reflection and ordinary comparisons](prior/earlier/baseline/baseline/earlier/CubeZeroAndExtensions.md)

**Product variant.** A fixed finite commutative characteristic-\(p\) ring is the product of its local factors. For linear comparisons over that fixed ring, each primitive ring idempotent is fixed, so the matrix group, tensor equations and determinant tests are componentwise. Ordinary comparison over the product is the conjunction of the ordinary local comparisons. For a fixed residue field \(E_i\), ordinary TI over \(E_i\) is equivalent to ordinary TI over the prime field, by ring-action restriction in one direction and coefficient Frobenius constraints on matrix entries in the other. One ordinary prime-field output returns to ordinary comparison over \(R\) by first applying the prime-field ordinary Frobenius-anchored reduction to a chosen \(E_i\), lifting those coefficients to its local factor, and putting zero tensors in the other factors. Thus \(H(R)\) holds exactly when every \(H(E_i)\) holds. For the reverse implication of this statement, plant a residue-field comparison in one component and use identical zero tensors in every other component; doing so preserves the componentwise signed condition because transporters can be identities at all other components. This does not use a component-permuting form of equivalence. Over characteristic three, \(\neg H(\mathbb F_3)\) excludes collapse over every such fixed nonzero product.

**Uniform bounded-depth variant.** For explicitly varying local \(R\) with residue \(\mathbb F_3\) and fixed nilpotence bound \(k\), a uniform reduction from signed TI over these rings to ordinary \(\mathbb F_3\)-TI exists iff \(H(\mathbb F_3)\). The upper construction uses the seven ternary residue characters and a bounded number of polynomial degrees. The converse uses constant lifts, even if one restricts to any particular fixed ring of the indicated class. By decomposing the coefficient algebra using Frobenius-fixed idempotents, this also works uniformly for finite products of these local rings, testing all component determinants separately. Each factor yields one ordinary output under \(H(\mathbb F_3)\), and polynomially many factors can be conjoined by an ordinary balanced Boolean construction.

## 3. Further exact worlds and separators

- **Characteristic two with nilpotents.** For every fixed finite commutative characteristic-two ring with all residue fields \(\mathbb F_2\), the signed and ordinary problems collapse, unconditionally. The exact same statement is uniform on explicitly tabulated scalar-residue rings of bounded nilpotence depth and their products. Each residual determinant over \(\mathbb F_2\) is one, so Theorem 1 handles the entire determinant condition over the ring. This solves a world with nontrivial principal units, such as dual numbers over \(\mathbb F_2\), without comparing different characteristics.

- **Coefficient-ring maps allowed to vary.** For local characteristic-\(p\) rings \(R,R'\) with scalar residue \(\mathbb F_p\), one can add an unknown coefficient-ring isomorphism \(\sigma\) and use mode maps semilinear for that same \(\sigma\), with matrices \(G\) in the displayed free frames. The principal-unit-one condition still compiles to ordinary TI at fixed nilpotence depth. Use the multiplication and action constraints for the unknown \(\sigma\) and do not pin the coefficient basis. Its action on scalar residue is the identity. The actual section action on matrices is \(G\,\sigma(S)g^{-1}\), so the identity above reads \(P(G\sigma(S)g^{-1})=\rho(G)\sigma(P(S))\). Presenting \(P\) as the same coefficient-valued tensor now compares it by the induced \(\sigma\)-action. Evaluation at \(I\) forces \(\rho=1\). The same residue-character comparison finishes under \(H(\mathbb F_p)\), and over \(\mathbb F_2\) it is unnecessary.

- **Coupled principal corrections.** With a fixed number of modes and bounded nilpotence, one can impose any specified product of powers \(\prod_i \rho(G_i)^{w_i}=u\) by a single ordinary tensor-system compilation over \(E\). Apply the same construction to the formal polynomial \(\prod_iP_i^{w_i}\) on the direct sum of the section spaces, with fixed pointwise coefficient ring. Reduce the exponents modulo a power \(p^a\ge k\), since \((1+j)^{p^a}=1\) for all \(j\in J\). The polynomial degree stays bounded. Its value at all identity sections is one, so covariance reflects the specified product exactly. This is a test on a common actual transporter with compatible principal corrections and needs no separate residue calculation.

- **Underlying determinant norm.** For an \(R\)-linear invertible matrix over any fixed or explicitly presented local characteristic-\(p\) \(R\), let \(N_{E/\mathbb F_p}\) denote the finite-field norm and \(\ell=\dim_E R\). Then
  \[
    \det_{\mathbb F_p}(G)
       =N_{E/\mathbb F_p}(\det_E\bar G)^\ell .
  \]
  The filtration by \(J^r R^n\) computes the determinant: on each associated quotient the matrix is \(\bar G\) on each residue-composition copy, and there are \(\ell\) copies. This also proves the identity with the regular norm of \(\det_R G\). Principal units act unipotently. Thus in the ternary case a local ring of even \(\ell\) imposes no constraint at all if the tested determinant is only the underlying \(\mathbb F_3\)-determinant. For odd \(\ell\), that test is precisely the norm-to-\(\mathbb F_3\) test of the residue determinant. Constant lift and reduction transfer equivalence of the norm-only degrees between that fixed ring and its residue field.

- **An exact even-width contrast.** For \(R=\mathbb F_3[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\), every invertible \(R\)-linear matrix has underlying \(\mathbb F_3\)-determinant \(+1\). Hence the norm-only problem agrees with ordinary TI unconditionally in this ring, while the full determinant-one collapse over this same ring is equivalent to \(H(\mathbb F_3)\). For scalar triples \(1\) and \(-1\) the norm-only test accepts and the determinant-one test rejects. The same occurs for \(1\) and \(1+\epsilon\), demonstrating that the lost information also includes genuine principal units. The automorphisms of a single rank-one free module of determinant exactly one are just the identity.

- **Odd-width and odd-residue transfer.** In characteristic three, if \(\ell\) is odd and \([E: \mathbb F_3]\) is odd, the fixed-ring norm-only collapse is equivalent to \(H(\mathbb F_3)\), by its equality with the residue norm-only world and the earlier odd-degree norm reflection. This explains precisely which parity kills this particular implication when the ambient determinant is used. [Prior relative norm theorem](prior/earlier/baseline/baseline/earlier/NormExtensionComparison.md)

- **Uniform norm-only world.** With arbitrary explicit commutative finite-dimensional characteristic-three algebras as coefficients, the existence of a single uniform reduction from testing every underlying \(\mathbb F_3\)-determinant to ordinary TI over \(\mathbb F_3\) is equivalent to \(H(\mathbb F_3)\). Restrict scalars to \(\mathbb F_3\) and remember the ring action, then compile the finitely many determinant characters of the three actual underlying maps by the weighted packing argument. This does not need a radical nilpotence bound. The reverse direction specializes to the prime field. It concerns restriction-of-scalars norms, not exact \(R\)-unit determinants.

## 4. The size bound and the remaining distinction

For an explicitly varying nilpotence bound \(k\), the degree and variable count of the principal polynomial give an \(L^{O(k)}\) construction in coefficient input length \(L\). The theorem therefore supplies polynomial size for fixed \(k\) and quasipolynomial size when \(k=O(\log L)\). It gives no polynomial guarantee for arbitrary growing radical length. That distinction does not affect any fixed-ring equivalence above. Mixed characteristic is a separate issue because the coefficient-field section and linear restriction of scalars used here need not exist.

The principal determinant, the residue determinant and the determinant norm are three different tests. For \(\mathbb F_3\)-scalar residue and fixed bounded nilpotence the first is now an unconditional ordinary test. The exact sign on the residue is the part still equivalent to the original unresolved ternary reduction.
