# Complexity and proof worlds for strict ternary tensor isomorphism

Let \(A=\mathrm{TI}_{\mathrm{SL}}(\mathbf F_3)\), \(B=\mathrm{TI}(\mathbf F_3)\), and \(H\) denote \(A\le_m^P B\). The tensor factors act independently. Dimensions are explicit except where the succinct model is named. The unconditional reverse reduction pads by zero lines. Write \(H_R\) for bounded-error randomized one-output reducibility and \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) for deterministic polynomial-size circuit many-one reducibility. Results are written proofs. A comparison labeled **H / strict** states exactly what changes between \(H\) and \(\neg H\), including any additional premises. Relative alternatives explicitly name the oracle.

## Low-depth circuit bounds and succinct input

### C01. Deterministic branching programs project to corank-one tensor pairs

Let a polynomial-size acyclic branching program select at most one outgoing edge from each vertex, using one input bit at each branch. Give it starting vertex \(s\) and accepting sink \(t\). The adjacency matrix \(U\) in topological order has entries \(0,1,x_i,1-x_i\), after harmless vertex splitting if needed. Thus \(K=I-U\) is unitriangular and \((K^{-1})_{s,t}\) is 1 precisely on acceptance. The bordered matrix
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}K&e_t\\e_s^T&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
has rank \(\dim K+1\) on acceptance and \(\dim K\) otherwise. Hence nonacceptance projects to both tensor languages by comparing the one-slice \(M\) to \(\operatorname{diag}(I_{\dim K},0)\). Equivalently use the rejecting sink to project acceptance for total programs. Rank equality between two deficient square matrices lifts to determinant-one left and right multipliers: alter each determinant by scaling independent null directions in each mode. The slice mode acts as the identity. All positions are projections or fixed symbols, even after zero-padding to balanced dimensions.

### C02. Path counting modulo three projects to the same matrix promise

Allow several live outgoing arcs in C01, with each arc guarded by a literal or constant. Time-unroll and split parallel arcs through fresh vertices. Then \(K^{-1}=I+U+\cdots+U^{\dim K-1}\) counts directed paths modulo three. Equality to any prescribed residue \(a\) is obtained by putting \(a\) in the bottom-right entry so that the Schur complement is \(a-(K^{-1})_{s,t}\). In particular, a polynomial-time unrolled nondeterministic logspace machine gives a logspace reduction from zero modulo three of its accepting path count to \(A\) and \(B\). Ordinary matching is still exactly rank on every target considered. Nonzero modulo three can be implemented as the OR of the residue-one and residue-two tests (the previously proved positive ordinary-TI compiler, followed by zero-padding for \(A\)).

### C03. Prime-power-modulus circuit lower bounds for both decision languages

For every fixed integer \(r\ge 2\), take the branching program with time layer \(i\) and state \(\sum_{j\le i} x_j\bmod r\). The path to state zero tests \(\mathrm{MOD}_r\). Comparing to the full-rank target for \(B\) is possible, but for \(A\) use a deficient target and select nonzero residues as the accepting sinks of a single deterministic program. Then equal rank means \(\mathrm{MOD}_r\). For every fixed prime power \(p^a\), choose any distinct prime \(r\ne p\). The Razborov–Smolensky lower bound for this modulo function transfers through the projections. Thus for every fixed depth the lower bound is exponential with a positive depth-dependent exponent in the tensor input length along a polynomially stretched sequence of lengths. It applies to both decision languages even on these one-slice subfamilies, before any \(H\) assumption. For fixed \(a\), a gate checking divisibility by \(p^a\) can be simulated at constant depth by \(\mathrm{MOD}_p\) gates: Lucas's theorem gives the \(j\)-th base-\(p\) digit of the Hamming weight \(w\) as \(\binom{w}{p^j}\bmod p\), and each of these counts is a sum of polynomially many constant-size conjunctions. The theorem concerns a single prime-power modulus basis. [Razborov, Boolean functions in the basis with modular gates](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/mz4883), [Smolensky, algebraic methods](https://doi.org/10.1145/28395.28404)

### C04. Nonuniform \(\mathrm{NC}^1\) embeds by projections

Barrington's theorem gives a polynomial-length width-five deterministic branching program for every polynomial-size logarithmic-depth bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit family. C01 compiles that program by projections into one-slice signed or ordinary inputs. In particular, the lower bounds of C03 already occur in a subproblem that can be implemented by the elementary rank algorithm. **H / strict.** A polynomial-time \(H\) reduction can coexist with the same lower bounds on both decision languages. If \(H\) fails, these rank copies still yield identical paired decision predicates on the projected inputs. Thus this circuit obstruction has constructive matching \(A\) and \(B\) restrictions in both branches. [Barrington, bounded-width branching programs](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(89)90037-8)

### C05. Both languages nevertheless admit constant-depth modular witness verification

A trit can be encoded with two Boolean flags with validity checked locally. Addition of polynomially many trits uses \(\mathrm{MOD}_3\) gates with at most two copies per summand. Ordinary tensor action and inverse equations each have bounded total degree, so their coordinate equations are uniform polynomial-size constant-depth circuits with \(\mathrm{MOD}_3\). For signed TI use the elementary-shear certificates from the preceding cross-world atlas. Each gate update is quadratic, the action cubic, and all equations have polynomial total length. Therefore both languages are projections, by existential witness bits, of uniform \(\mathrm{AC}^0[3]\) relations. C03 shows that eliminating the existential quantification without increasing depth or enriching this modulus basis already fails on an explicit one-slice family. This proof separates witness-verification complexity from decision complexity, irrespective of \(H\).

### C06. Succinct one-slice PSPACE-completeness with a promised bordering format

Supply an acyclic, topologically numbered graph on exponentially many vertices by an adjacency circuit, and give \(s,t\) and the matrix format in C01. That is a succinct representation of a promised subproblem of both tensor languages. Its rank ambiguity is exactly whether the path count is zero modulo three. Membership in PSPACE follows by recursively computing entries of powers of \(U\) modulo three using \( (U^{a+b})_{ij}=\sum_k (U^a)_{ik}(U^b)_{kj}\). The recursion depth is logarithmic in the number of vertices, the sum is streamed, and all indices have polynomial bit length. Sum powers through the number of vertices minus one for the inverse entry.

For hardness, time-unroll a deterministic polynomial-space machine for its exponential upper bound on halting time, padding using absorbing accept/reject configurations. Both the time and configuration labels use polynomial bits. Adjacency has a polynomial-size circuit checking one step. The terminal configuration is linked to the sink on acceptance (or use the reject condition to target zero). There is at most one source-to-sink path, so reduction modulo three loses no information. This gives PSPACE-completeness by polynomial-time many-one reductions in the specific succinct format.

### C07. Succinct padding and the locality needed for a forward transfer

The unconditional reverse reduction lifts to circuit-succinct tensors by increasing each address length by one and specifying a zero slice when the new high bit is set. Independent determinant corrections remain valid. Conversely a forward explicit reduction whose format and each output entry are computable in polylogarithmic time using random access to the explicit input entries lifts to a uniform polynomial-time reduction on circuit-succinct inputs: simulate the entry transducer as a Boolean circuit and replace reads by calls to the supplied input circuit. Over an explicit input of length \(N\), a polynomial-time \(H\) reduction with unrestricted access does not supply this locality bound. **H / strict.** The local condition is sufficient for both explicit forward \(H\) (enumerate outputs) and succinct forward reducibility. Under strictness it fails even if the full succinct languages turn out to be equivalent. C06 independently gives an exact shared PSPACE-complete succinct subproblem.

## Complementary query compilation

A complementary pair supplies two explicit ordinary TI instances of which exactly one is YES. The prior linear-switch compiler takes polynomial lists of cyclic-group variables over unary moduli and enforces linear equations, enabling a variable only when its attached ordinary TI instance is YES. In particular, the prior compiler's exact modular guarded-path recurrence uses both rails of every tested edge. We use its unrestricted modulus- and list-size bounds as proved in the 234-result atlas.

### C08. One ordinary test for the sign of a guarded integer path sum

Given complementary pairs and a polynomial-size acyclic branching program with edges guarded by those rails or constants, assign polynomial-bit signed integer weights to edges. Let \(x\) be the sum of products of the weights along active source-to-sink paths. There is a deterministic one-output ordinary TI compilation for \(x>0\), including when the given program represents a difference of two nonnegative path sums. Attach a fixed bit bound to \(x\) from the number of edges and maximal weight length.

Choose \(L\) polynomial so \(M=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,L)>2|x|+2\) using the bound. For example \(M\ge\binom{L}{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}\) by prime valuations. Use as pairwise coprime moduli \(p_i\) the maximal prime powers at most \(L\). For each \(p_i\), the guarded linear recurrence computes every residue-equality rail \(x\bmod p_i=r\) using one TI comparison. Its complementary rail is the OR of all other residue tests. Thus all binary bits of \(r_i=x\bmod p_i\) and their complements are available as ordinary comparisons.

Let \(u_i<M\) be the CRT multiplier for coordinate \(i\), with residue 1 there and 0 at all other moduli. The integer \(S=\sum_i r_i u_i\) is congruent to \(x\bmod M\) and \(S/M\) has only polynomially many possible integral parts because \(r_i<p_i\le L\). Test whether \(S\) lies in the union of the intervals \(jM+[1,\lfloor(M-1)/2\rfloor]\). This exactly tests \(x>0\). The sum of the bit-selected shifted constants \(u_i\) uses polynomial-size logarithmic-depth Boolean addition by a carry-save tree and a final carry-lookahead adder. Comparison to constants and the polynomial disjunction preserve logarithmic depth. Unfold to a polynomial-size Boolean formula, push negations to the rails, and use the existing positive TI formula compiler. This gives one query and avoids reconstructing the integer with an ordinary decision oracle.

### C09. One ordinary query for uniform PL decoders

A uniform PL decoder is a polynomial-time logspace probabilistic computation whose answer is the sign of the difference between accepting and rejecting path counts. Time-unroll its configurations, with the supplied input bits read as complementary TI rails. A dummy sink with weights \(+1,-1\) on the final edges implements that difference. C08 compiles the decoder into one ordinary query. This also covers BPL and NL decoders with complementary rails, using the probabilistic version or a positive accepting path count respectively. **H / strict.** If \(\overline B\le_m^P B\), all ordinary lists can be given complementary rails. A uniform PL postprocessor for signed decision then implies \(H\), and under strictness no such postprocessor is possible. The unconditional promise version operates even if the ambient complement reduction fails.

### C10. Fixed-depth nesting of PL decoders retains one ordinary query

Both sign rails of an integer expression \(x\) can be compiled: \(x>0\) and \(1-x>0\) are complementary. Apply C08 from the bottom of any fixed-depth circuit of polynomially many explicit uniform PL decoders on complementary TI inputs. The input for each layer can consist of compiled queries from the preceding layer, including repeated uses. Each layer has polynomial blowup, so any fixed number of layers has polynomial blowup and gives a single final query. Unlike composition of bounded-fan-in formulas, this permits each decoder gate to accept a polynomial-length list. **H / strict.** With the global complement premise of C09 this supplies a sufficient compiler in the equality branch and excludes a deterministic reduction by these nested decoders in the strict branch. The bound assumes a fixed number of layers.

### C11. Guarded exact absorption probabilities compile by determinant signs

Let a promised finite absorbing rational Markov chain have transitions expressed by polynomial-bit rationals selected from complementary ordinary-query rails. Bound the state set and total representation polynomially, and assume absorption is almost sure from every transient state. The probability \(h_s\) of absorption in a distinguished set satisfies \((I-Q)h=b\), with \(Q\) the transient submatrix. The matrix \(I-Q\) is invertible and has positive determinant: all eigenvalues of \(Q\) have absolute value less than one, so real factors \(1-\lambda\) are positive and complex factors pair to positive products. Multiply all coefficients by a positive common denominator obtained as the product of all input denominators. Cramer's rule transforms \(h_s>r\) into the sign of an integer difference of two determinants with polynomial-bit coefficients. A determinant is a polynomial-size signed arithmetic branching program by the clow-sequence construction. Replace a selected coefficient by parallel guarded terms of constant weights, with multiedges if needed. C08 therefore gives one ordinary test for the threshold. [Mahajan–Vinay, arithmetic branching program for determinant](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/1997/036/)

### C12. Guarded comparisons of stationary probabilities need no positivity certificate for the denominator

For an irreducible finite chain given by complementary-rail transitions, impose the stationary linear equations and replace one redundant equation by normalization. This is an invertible square system because the eigenvalue 1 has a one-dimensional left eigenspace and the rows of the unnormalized system sum to zero. Let \(D\ne0\) be its cleared-denominator determinant and \(D_i,D_j\) the Cramer numerators. Then \(\pi_i>\pi_j\) exactly when \(D(D_i-D_j)>0\). The product of arithmetic branching program polynomials is implemented by connecting the sink of the first graph to the source of the second. C08 therefore yields a one-query comparison in ordinary TI, independent of the sign convention for the determinant.

### C13. Guarded rational matrix rank thresholds compile in one query

Consider an integer or rational matrix \(M\) with entries selected via complementary ordinary rails and coefficients of polynomial bit length. Clear denominators once by a fixed positive product. For \(k\ge1\), \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf Q} M\ge k\) iff the coefficient of \(t^k\) in \(\det(I+tM^TM)\) is positive. Cauchy–Binet identifies the coefficient as a sum of squares of \(k\times k\) minors. Construct the determinant branching program and replace each quadratic entry by a polynomial-size two-step guarded branching computation, with constant coefficient pieces and a possible power of \(t\). Carry an extra exponent state from 0 to \(k\), discarding terms exceeding \(k\) and requiring final exponent \(k\). The resulting integer path expression permits C08. The argument uses rational rank and positivity in \(\mathbb Z\), even though the final tensors are ternary.

### C14. Known sign-profile size gives complementary rails from positive filters

For an ordinary isomorphic pair of ternary tensors let \(C\subseteq\{\pm1\}^3\) be its attained determinant profile and assume \(|C|=k\) is supplied correctly. If local single-sign feasibility is compiled to ordinary TI, the other rail of a sign \(v\) is a positive threshold on the seven complementary profiles: \(v\notin C\) iff at least \(k\) profiles among the other seven are feasible. This also handles \(C=\varnothing\) when \(k=0\). The fixed-size positive threshold compiles to ordinary TI. **H / strict.** Under \(H\), this combines arbitrary rank-known signed predicates with C08–C13 without a complement assumption for either ambient language. Under strictness the same implication survives on each subfamily with its own proven signed compiler. A one-query compiler for all unrestricted profile bits would imply \(H\) by selecting the all-positive bit.

### C15. Positivity of an arithmetic formula with query-dependent leaves

A polynomial-size arithmetic formula using addition, subtraction, and multiplication on polynomial-bit integer constants chosen by complementary query rails embeds into a polynomial-size weighted branching program: use disjoint unions for sums, attach a negative unit edge for negations, and concatenate for products. A formula, unlike a general arithmetic circuit, has an explicit occurrence tree, so total graph size is polynomial in that representation. Every path product and the total signed sum have polynomial bit length. C08 gives a one-query ordinary TI compiler for positivity. **H / strict.** Under \(H\) and with known profile cardinalities this also tests arithmetic inequalities on signed-feasibility bits by C14. Under strictness this closure still holds for independently compilable signed subfamilies and places no formula-model restriction on extracting a single unknown signed bit from a raw ordinary instance.



## Sparse-oracle worlds

Fix a PSPACE-complete language \(D\). Let \(S\) be a sparse set, meaning its total membership up to size \(m\) is bounded above by a polynomial \(K(m)\ge1\). The algorithms may hardcode a polynomial bound.

### C16. Heavy-query trimming for sparse oracle access

Consider a fixed polynomial-time computation with access to \(D\oplus S\), on an arbitrary fixed input, with a specified uniformly random polynomial-length tape. It may also take uniformly random data as part of the same tape. Start with a table \(T\) of known positive \(S\)-queries and simulate \(S\) by \(T\), leaving access to \(D\) exact. If the maximum query length is \(m\), find every query word whose probability of ever being asked in the simulation is at least \(\delta/K(m)\). The list has polynomial length for inverse-polynomial \(\delta\), since the sum of these probabilities is at most the maximum query count. A PSPACE computation can count all tapes exactly for a proposed word and can decide whether any heavy word extends a given prefix. Recover the list by prefix traversal using polynomially many calls to \(D\), and ask \(S\) about the candidates. Add every newly discovered positive and repeat.

There are at most \(K(m)\) rounds with discoveries. At stabilization, the simulated computation touches an undiscovered genuine positive with probability at most \(\delta\), by the union bound over accessible positives. Until such a first query the real and simulated executions on the same tape coincide. Hence their output distributions are within \(\delta\) in total variation. The trimmed table is computed in uniform polynomial time with access to \(D\oplus S\).

### C17. Deterministic approximation of randomized acceptance after a sparse PSPACE join

Let \(\Omega=D\oplus S\). For each clocked randomized polynomial-time oracle computation, C16 followed by exact counting with the trimmed table computes its acceptance probability to inverse-polynomial additive error deterministically in polynomial time with \(\Omega\). Exact counting is within PSPACE by enumeration of tapes and an exact accumulator. In particular \(\mathrm P^\Omega=\mathrm{BPP}^\Omega\) for every sparse \(S\), unlike the almost-sure fresh-randomness argument for fair external oracles. Also \(H^\Omega\) holds because the fixed yes/no languages are unrelativized NP languages and can be answered by \(D\). The two conclusions have independent proofs using the sparsity premise only for the first.

### C18. No classical relative one-way functions after a sparse PSPACE join

For a polynomial-time oracle function \(f^\Omega\), apply C16 to evaluating \(f^\Omega\) on a uniformly random \(n\)-bit input. The resulting trimmed function \(\widetilde f\) differs on at most a \(\delta\) fraction of inputs. Given \(y\), compute preimage counts for \(\widetilde f(x)=y\) through the fixed PSPACE oracle, obtaining an exact count of at most \(2^n\) by prefix counts. Sample a nearly uniform preimage, using the counts to unrank a randomly chosen index, with a bounded-time rejection cap. On a trimmed challenge \(\widetilde f(X)\), the resampled preimage \(X'\) is itself uniform marginally. The real challenge equals the trimmed one except with probability \(\delta\), and \(f^\Omega(X')\ne\widetilde f(X')\) has marginal probability at most \(\delta\). This gives inversion success at least \(1-2\delta-o(1)\). Thus no relative classical polynomial-time function is one-way, even against classical PPT adversaries. The result allows arbitrary sparse \(S\), including sparse sets whose strings have no uniform polynomial-time enumeration.

### C19. Explicit collision finding for compressed hash families in the same world

For a fixed public key, apply C16 to \(f_k^\Omega:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}^m\) with \(m<n\). Sample \(X\) uniformly and sample \(X'\) uniformly among its other trimmed preimages if possible, using the prefix counts from C18. On a singleton fiber retain \(X'=X\). This transition preserves the uniform marginal on inputs, because the different-point transition within each nonsingleton fiber is doubly stochastic. The singleton-fiber probability is at most \(2^{m-n}\), and the trimming failure probability on either marginal is at most \(2\delta\), plus sampling abort. Therefore the algorithm explicitly finds a genuine collision with probability at least \(1-2^{m-n}-2\delta-o(1)\), uniformly in the public key. This proves the failure of relative classical collision resistance with genuine length compression in this oracle world.

### C20. Randomized verifiable search also derandomizes uniformly

Let the relation verifier and the randomized search algorithm be polynomial-time with \(D\oplus S\), and suppose the latter produces a verifiable answer with probability at least \(2/3\) on its promised YES inputs. Apply C16 to their combined computation. With \(\delta<1/3\), the trimmed verifier has some accepting tape on every YES input. Locate such a tape with a PSPACE prefix test. Run both trimmed and real computations on that tape. If the real output fails verification, the first discrepancy reveals a previously unknown positive \(S\)-query. Add it to the table, run the heavy-trimming loop again, and retry. Each failed trial adds one of at most \(K(m)\) words. Thus polynomial time suffices and every returned witness is checked against the real oracle. On an input with no valid witness the algorithm can reject when there is no accepting trimmed tape; in any event the same polynomial bound prohibits infinite retries.

### C21. Equality and classical derandomization can coexist with unambiguous search hardness and no one-way functions

Take \(S\) to contain one independent uniform word \(w_n\) of each positive length \(n\). The language \(\{(1^n,i):(w_n)_i=1\}\) belongs to \(\mathrm{UP}^\Omega\cap\mathrm{coUP}^\Omega\), since the unique word serves for either answer. If it were in \(\mathrm P^\Omega\), polynomially many bit queries would recover \(w_n\). Conditional on the other blocks, a \(q\)-query machine has mean recovery probability at most \((q+1)/2^n\), using the transcript with no positive reply and the final guess. Markov's inequality and summability show almost sure failure of every fixed uniform polynomial-time machine on sufficiently large lengths. Intersect over the countable list. Hence the inclusion of P in UP intersect coUP is strict in this world, while \(H^\Omega\), \(\mathrm P^\Omega=\mathrm{BPP}^\Omega\), and absence of relative OWFs follow from C17–C18. The prior no-OWF world with just PSPACE has equality of P and NP. The new world gives explicit separation without average-case inversion hardness.

### C22. The sparse oracle separation persists against quantum algorithms

For C21, compare an oracle with the length-\(n\) word planted to the oracle with that whole block empty. In the unplanted computation the expected query mass of a uniformly random \(w_n\) is at most \(2^{-n}\) per query. The query hybrid and Cauchy–Schwarz give expected squared final-state distance at most \(4q^2/2^n\). Measurement then bounds expected recovery by \((2+8q^2)/2^n\). Conditioning on all other blocks and the fixed PSPACE component preserves the argument, even with their coherent access. Uniform quantum algorithms therefore cannot recover \(w_n\), almost surely, by Markov and summability. If the bit language were in BQP, independent amplified bit decisions would recover it. Thus even \(\mathrm{UP}^\Omega\cap\mathrm{coUP}^\Omega\) has a language outside \(\mathrm{BQP}^\Omega\), despite the no-OWF result. [Bennett–Bernstein–Brassard–Vazirani query hybrid](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9701001)

### C23. Uniform separation and a relativized Karp–Lipton collapse in the sparse world

For every sparse \(S\), a computation in \(\mathrm{NP}^\Omega\) on length \(n\) uses only polynomial-size \(S\)-queries. As polynomial advice supply their entire accessible positive table. Its verifier can now be evaluated with \(D\) alone and the NP search performed by the same PSPACE oracle. Thus \(\mathrm{NP}^\Omega\subseteq\mathrm{P/poly}^\Omega\) (polynomial circuits with oracle gates). The Karp–Lipton collapse works with these gates, by applying its circuit-certification argument to the self-reducible satisfiability language for Boolean circuits with \(\Omega\)-gates. Consequently \(\mathrm{PH}^\Omega=\Sigma_2^{P,\Omega}\). For the singleton oracle C21 still separates \(\mathrm P^\Omega\) from \(\mathrm{NP}^\Omega\). This witnesses the difference between polynomial-size decision circuits and uniform polynomial decision in a world with fixed tensor equality and no OWFs. [Karp–Lipton](https://doi.org/10.1145/800141.804678)

## Advice erasers and class transfers

### C24. A uniform reduction relative to a small-circuit language is exactly an advice threshold

Any oracle \(D'\in\mathrm{P/poly}\) that erases the gap gives \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\). For each input length insert circuits for all oracle-query lengths bounded by the polynomial clock into a circuit simulation of the adaptive reduction. The aggregate size is polynomial. Conversely, an advice reduction can be implemented with a tally oracle storing one chosen advice bit per address length. This gives an iff for erasure by an oracle in \(\mathrm{P/poly}\), allowing dense oracles as well as sparse ones. **H / strict.** When \(H\) holds every external oracle preserves reducibility. Under strictness with failure of \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\), every small-circuit oracle preserves the gap. Under strictness with \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\), some small-circuit oracle erases it and no oracle in \(\mathrm P\) does.

### C25. P-selective oracles realize precisely the same advice threshold

Every P-selective language is in \(\mathrm{P/poly}\). For each length form the tournament on its YES strings in which a vertex wins if chosen by the selector. A set of \(O(n)\) YES strings suffices so every YES string wins against or equals some member: iteratively choose a vertex whose own singleton together with the vertices beating it covers at least half the remaining vertices. A NO string loses against every member. This proves the advice bound. Conversely take the binary sequence \(\alpha\) holding successive polynomial-length blocks of good reduction advice with noneventually constant guard bits. The language of dyadic rationals \(0.x\) strictly below \(\alpha\) is P-selective by choosing the smaller rational. Its prefix bits are recovered one at a time by oracle comparisons to thresholds with the already recovered prefix and a trial 1. Entry C24 applies. **H / strict.** Equality always persists with such oracles, whereas under strictness the existence of *any* P-selective eraser is equivalent to \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\).

### C26. Good advice has an \(E\)-computable tally eraser

If \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) holds, choose the lexicographically first correct circuit of a fixed polynomial bound per length. It is feasible to find each binary description by exhaustive circuit enumeration and to test it by checking all inputs of the length. Deciding the fixed tensor languages on an explicitly encoded query can be done by exhaustive enumeration of all matrix tuples, in time \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(n)}\) for inputs and outputs of polynomial length. Encode length and advice index into a tally address \(1^M\) with \(M=n^C+\) a polynomial address offset, choosing \(C\) larger than the address-offset exponent by at least two to separate the ranges. Pick \(C\) at least the exponent of the total search bound. Oracle membership then runs in \(2^{O(M)}\), and the reducer uses only polynomial query lengths. Conversely any tally oracle gives polynomial advice. **H / strict.** This precisely characterizes \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) by erasure with a tally set in \(E\). Under \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\land\neg H\) that eraser is an explicit language in \(E\setminus\mathrm P\) defined by the least correct reductions.

### C27. Failure of nonuniform reduction lifts to uniform advice-class separations with certificates

Suppose \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) fails and signed nonisomorphism has a polynomially bounded polynomial-time proof system. Then \(A\in\mathrm{NP}\cap\mathrm{coNP}\) and \(A\notin\mathrm{P/poly}\) by the fixed YES/NO target argument. Hence \(\mathrm{NP}\cap\mathrm{coNP}\nsubseteq\mathrm{P/poly}\). **H / strict.** Under \(H\) a certificate system with a polynomial length bound pulls back from ordinary nonisomorphism and conversely by unconditional padding. Under \(\neg H\) a signed polynomial bound alone already separates \(\mathrm P\) from \(\mathrm{NP}\cap\mathrm{coNP}\). The stronger circuit separation specifically needs failure of \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\).

### C28. If NP has bounded-error polynomial algorithms, strictness entails a randomized-class separation

If \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{BPP}\), then \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\): solve SAT by prefix search using its bounded-error algorithm, amplify every step and verify the final witness. It follows that \(A\in\mathrm{RP}\) and \(H_R\) holds using constant ordinary outputs. **H / strict.** Under equality this assumption gives polynomial-time deterministic equivalence and one-sided randomized algorithms for both. Under strictness it forces \(A\in\mathrm{RP}\setminus\mathrm P\), so \(\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{RP}\) and \(\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{BPP}\). In particular ordinary (non-oracle) exponential circuit hardness in \(E\) cannot hold in this joint strict world by Impagliazzo–Wigderson. This use of plain Boolean hardness requires the additional NP-to-BPP assumption. [Impagliazzo–Wigderson](https://doi.org/10.1145/258533.258590)

### C29. Unambiguous preprocessing enforces a class collapse only with a total verifier promise

On tensor subfamilies described by efficiently checkable syntactic restrictions and admitting a uniform NP verifier with at most one accepting witness on every input, signed decision on the subfamily lies in UP. **H / strict.** Under \(H\), any such subfamily can use the ordinary target directly. Under strictness, a polynomial-time reduction from *unrestricted* signed decision into a total UP subfamily, plus \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{UP}\), would give a contradiction. For a merely promised unique-transporter subclass, the sufficient premise is polynomial algorithms for the corresponding PromiseUP problems with correctness on both promised answers. Total \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{UP}\) only eliminates verifiers unambiguous on every input string, which need not include malformed or out-of-promise tensor encodings. This exact distinction also applies to any isolation construction used as a target.

### C30. P-selectivity of the decision languages follows the correct reduction direction

A polynomial-time many-one preimage of a P-selective set is P-selective. On \(x,y\), run the target selector on their images and choose a corresponding original string, using either original if the images coincide. If at least one original is YES, at least one image is YES, so the selected original is YES. **H / strict.** Under \(H\), \(A\) is P-selective iff \(B\) is P-selective. The implication from signed to ordinary P-selectivity is unconditional. If \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) fails, then \(A\notin\mathrm{P/poly}\), so \(A\) cannot be P-selective by the tournament argument in C25 even if \(B\) is P-selective. Mere strictness of the uniform reductions supplies no such advice lower bound. This concerns selectivity of the original decision sets, as distinguished from the choice of an external selective eraser.

## Proof-system and counting worlds

Proof systems here are polynomial-time surjections onto the indicated language, with arbitrary proof lengths. Lower bounds of the earlier atlas for the field-axiom Polynomial Calculus certificate presentation are independent of the semantic comparisons in this section.

### C31. p-optimal proof systems transfer across the many-one degree

If \(L\le_m^P M\) by \(f\), a p-optimal proof system \(\Pi_M\) pulls back to a p-optimal system for \(L\). Define a proof of \(x\) to be \((x,z)\) with \(\Pi_M(z)=f(x)\), using a fixed valid output to totalize invalid proof strings. For any other source proof system \(P_L\), the map \(w\mapsto f(P_L(w))\) is a polynomial-time partial-prefix proof system for \(M\), made surjective by an independent branch with any complete proof system for \(M\). Its proof translation into \(\Pi_M\) yields a polynomial-time translation into the pullback after adding \(P_L(w)\). Apply to complements, using the same reductions. **H / strict.** Under \(H\), signed and ordinary nonisomorphism admit p-optimal systems simultaneously. Without \(H\) the unconditional padding direction still transfers p-optimality from signed to ordinary nonisomorphism. Standard p-optimality for TAUT would independently supply both, since both complements reduce to TAUT. [Cook–Reckhow, proof systems and simulations](https://www.cs.toronto.ca/~sacook/homepage/cook_reckhow.pdf)

### C32. Proof search automatizability survives pullback to the signed language

For a fixed target proof system with a polynomial-time automatizer given a claimed proof-size bound \(s\), the C31 pullback has an automatizer: map \(x\) to \(f(x)\), run the target proof search with bound \(s\), and prefix the found proof with \(x\). Apart from the finitely specified fallback output used for invalid encodings, which admits a hardcoded proof, any pullback proof of length at most \(s\) contains a target proof of length at most \(s\). This is a statement about a named pullback system, even if it is not p-optimal. **H / strict.** Under \(H\) proof-search systems for either nonisomorphism problem can be pulled back to the other. Under strictness, if \(B\in\mathrm P\) its nonisomorphism has a polynomially bounded automatizable system, while signed nonisomorphism cannot have one. Without a proof-size bound this argument supplies proof search rather than a decision algorithm.

### C33. Polynomially bounded and automatizable nonisomorphism systems decide the language

For any of the two nonisomorphism languages, a proof system that is both polynomially bounded and polynomially automatizable gives a deterministic polynomial decision algorithm. Run the automatizer at the uniform proof-length bound and verify the returned proof. The converse uses the input as its own verified proof if the problem is in P. **H / strict.** If \(H\) holds, the existence of this combination for either orientation transfers to the other by C32. Under strictness the combination is excluded for signed nonisomorphism even if nonisomorphism has polynomial-size certificates. This gives a proof-search separation from any efficiently decidable ordinary target without relying on a lower bound for PC or on known circuit lower bounds.

### C34. Fixed domain sign split gives an explicit feasible-interpolation route

Let \(P(x)\) be a uniform polynomial-time syntactic condition, and assume on its accepted inputs the signed profile contains at most one of \(0,e_1\). Form circuits \(F_n(x,u)\) and \(G_n(x,v)\) checking \(P\) and respective transporter signs \(0,e_1\). Use separate witness and gate variables. Their conjunction is unsatisfiable. Feasible interpolation turns a refutation into a circuit separating the two sign languages: \(F_n\) satisfiable for \(x\) forces circuit output 1, and \(G_n\) satisfiable forces 0. Small refutations yield polynomial-size separator circuits; polynomial-time constructible refutations with uniform interpolation yield P separation. **H / strict.** If there is also a deterministic preprocessing map from arbitrary signed instances into this syntactic domain whose promised outputs have exactly one of the two signs and the correct sign marks the original answer, uniform short interpolating refutations imply \(H\). Under strictness this joint route fails, whereas the separator theorem holds on every independently split domain. [Krajíček, feasible interpolation and disjoint NP sets](https://www.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~krajicek/ds1.pdf)

### C35. The canonical verifier has a parsimonious width-three CNF

Guess exactly the trit entries in the three matrices. Use a deterministic verifier for action and inverses or determinants. Arithmetize each deterministic gate via its fixed finite Boolean truth table and use an AND/NOT circuit basis. Encode each gate by clauses of width at most three, imposing that its output bit equals the prescribed function of its input bits. Thus every valid transporter extends to exactly one satisfying assignment, and every invalid guess has none. When inverse matrices are represented, their entries are uniquely determined by the matrix witnesses. This gives parsimonious reductions of the ordinary and signed transporter counting problems to #3SAT. In particular it avoids the multiple shear-word multiplicity arising if one counts proofs of determinant-one membership. It also supports arbitrary explicit prefix constraints and prescribed determinant profiles without introducing another counted witness.

### C36. A polynomial list of possible stabilizer orders supplies exact gap normalization

Suppose an applicable tensor subfamily comes with a uniformly computable polynomial-size list \(s_1,\ldots,s_\ell\) of positive integers of polynomial bit length containing every possible positive witness count for a pair. For the signed problem such a list can be built from a list of ordinary stabilizer sizes by adjoining their integer quotients by \(1,2,4,8\). The signed stabilizer is the determinant kernel inside the ordinary one, so a nonempty signed transporter coset has one of these counts.

Let \(c(x)\) be the \(\#\mathrm P\) transporter count and \(U(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(s_i-t)\). The GapP function \(U(0)-U(c(x))\) is zero on NO inputs and identically the explicitly computable positive integer \(U(0)\) on YES inputs. GapP is closed under polynomially many uniform products by enumerating the independently tagged computation paths and recording sign parity. Thus a recognizable promised class yields a WPP language upon rejecting outside the class. With a length-only list it yields LWPP. **H / strict.** A deterministic collapse for the specified total counting class decides these fragments in either branch. If the count list were available for every signed tensor at every length, such a collapse would imply \(H\), hence cannot coexist with strictness and that global list premise. A promise lacking an efficient recognition procedure only yields the analogous promise characterization.

### C37. Prime-to-\(\ell\) stabilizers put the corresponding fragment in modular counting

For a fixed odd prime \(\ell\), if the ordinary stabilizer order on a subfamily is prime to \(\ell\), then the signed stabilizer order is prime to \(\ell\) as well. Counts are zero on NO and nonzero modulo \(\ell\) on YES, so C35 gives a \(\mathrm{Mod}_\ell\mathrm P\) decision on this fragment. For \(\ell=2\), one may first quotient by the known universal scalar kernel \(K\) of the acting group: cross each matrix tuple with the at most four scalar moves and count exactly the lexicographically least representative. If \(|\operatorname{Stab}(T)|/|K|\) is odd, the resulting parity decides the fragment. These are modular witness-count criteria rather than determinant-image order guesses. For total efficiently recognizable domains an assumed equality of the corresponding modular class with P yields deterministic decision independently of \(H\).

### C38. Deterministic polynomial-list isolation when there are only polynomially many transporters

If at most \(k(n)\) valid transporter encodings occur and a polynomial bound \(k\) is supplied, deterministically produce polynomially many width-three CNFs whose satisfiable members all retain transporter witnesses and among which at least one is uniquely satisfiable for every YES. Interpret each \(L\)-bit transporter encoding as an integer. A nonzero difference between two such integers has at most \(L\) distinct prime divisors. Thus at most \(L\binom{k}{2}\) primes are bad for the whole witness set. Enumerate more small primes than that bound, and all their residue buckets. Polynomial bounds on the prime sizes follow, for example, from \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,m)\ge2^m/(m+1)\) and the product of maximal prime powers at most \(m\). At least one prime separates all valid encodings. Use C35 with the bucket predicate to ensure unique gate extensions. This deterministic isolation uses a bound on witness multiplicity, unlike Valiant–Vazirani for arbitrary witness sets.

### C39. Parity queries enumerate every transporter on bounded-count fragments

Under C38, a parity-SAT oracle recovers all transporter encodings with polynomially many queries. For every residue bucket, query the accepting witness parity and, when it is odd, parity after imposing each possible witness bit to be 1. The recovered bit string is checked in the real transporter verifier. Every singleton bucket gives its valid witness, and extra buckets can only yield additional checked witnesses. At least one prime makes every witness singleton in its own bucket, so no valid witness is lost. **H / strict.** This is a deterministic full search on a bounded-stabilizer promise if parity-SAT is in P, with no implication to full \(H\) without a reduction from unrestricted signed inputs to that promise. If such a polynomial-time reduction preserving searched witnesses existed, it and parity-SAT in P would contradict strictness. This separates exact witness enumeration from mere promise decision or randomized isolation.

## A single directional signed gap

Write \(r_i x\) for reflecting one target coordinate in mode \(i\) of a tensor pair. Write \(A_v(x)\) for feasibility at sign \(v\in(\mathbf F_2)^3\). Thus \(A_v(x)=A(r_vx)\) in membership-bit notation. Ordinary NO inputs have empty profile.

### C40. One fixed directional difference captures a hard promise under strictness

Set \(D_1(x)=A_{e_1}(x)\wedge\neg A_0(x)\). The other differences \(D_i\) are obtained by permuting modes. Assume a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for \(D_1\) only on the promise \(x\in B\). On all eight reflections of \(x\), estimate \(D_i\) for all modes. These reveal the labeled boundary in the sign cube: for an edge an oriented positive answer specifies that its start is outside the profile and its end inside, and two zero answers signify equal labels. If a boundary edge is present, propagate labels along paths in the connected cube. If no boundary edge is present, assume the profile full, as it is nonempty on the promise. Output \(x\) itself as an ordinary instance exactly when the decoded bit for \(0\) is 1, and otherwise output one fixed ordinary NO instance. Every off-promise input gives a final NO regardless of earlier answers. **H / strict.** Under \(H\), \(D_1\) is decidable with two nonadaptive ordinary TI queries and a fixed difference decoder. Under strictness the same fixed directional difference is outside PromiseP on ordinary-positive pairs. As a total language \(D_1\in\mathrm{DP}\cap\mathrm{SZK}\), using the paired NP tests and Boolean closure of SZK. [Sahai–Vadhan, complete characterization of SZK](https://doi.org/10.1145/636865.636868)

### C41. The directional promise separates the randomized and advice branches too

The reconstruction in C40 makes only a constant number of oriented-gap tests. A bounded-error promise algorithm can be amplified sufficiently to make them all correct with probability at least \(2/3\); the same B-positive filter protects every ordinary-NO input. A promise algorithm using polynomial-size circuits with advice can also be composed with the constant number of fixed transformations, taking advice for all resulting query lengths up to a polynomial bound. Therefore \(\neg H_R\) excludes PromiseBPP for the fixed directional gap, and \(\neg H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) excludes its polynomial-size promise circuits. **H / strict.** If ordinary TI lies in BPP or P/poly and \(H\) holds, C40 supplies the respective algorithms for this promise. Deterministic strictness alone yields the PromiseP exclusion of C40 and leaves both stronger separations open.

### C42. Affine profiles reduce the directional checks and reveal determinant rank

If \(C=c+S\) is a nonempty ternary profile, then \(D_i(r_vx)=1\) iff \(v+e_i\in C\) and \(e_i\notin S\). If \(e_i\in S\), a shift never changes membership. Otherwise \(C\) and its shift by \(e_i\) are disjoint, and exactly \(|C|\) of the eight directional tests for this axis are positive. Every proper \(C\) has some \(e_i\notin S\). Thus all three orientations taken over the eight cube vertices recover \(C\) as the union of inward endpoints, and any nonzero per-axis count gives its exact size. No positives means empty or full; the ordinary answer resolves the distinction. **H / strict.** Under \(H\) this is a deterministic constant-query reconstruction of the entire profile and stabilizer rank. Under strictness a deterministic ordinary-positive-promise method producing these directional bits without ordinary oracle access cannot exist. The formula for the rank itself is unconditional.

### C43. One directional language is equivalent to signed reducibility with an explicit complement hypothesis

Assume \(\overline B\le_m^P B\). If \(H\) holds, implement \(D_1=A_{e_1}\wedge\neg A_0\) by two mapped ordinary queries and the ordinary AND compiler, using the complement reduction on the second. Conversely if \(D_1\le_m^P B\), compile each directional boundary query in C40 and add the original \(B\) query. The output is now one ordinary query by the existing fixed-size Boolean formula compiler on complementary rails. **H / strict.** Under this explicit complement hypothesis, \(H\) holds exactly when the *single specified* total directional language \(D_1\) reduces many-one to ordinary TI. In particular neither orientation sampling nor case distinctions among all fixed profile subsets are additional assumptions in this equivalence.

### C44. Logspace count-sign postprocessing suffices to make a target NP-hard

If SAT maps uniformly to a polynomial batch of ordinary TI comparisons with complementary rails, followed by a uniform PL decoder, C09 shows \(\mathrm{SAT}\le_m^P B\). Since signed TI is in NP this implies \(H\). The same follows for fixed-depth nesting of such decoders by C10. **H / strict.** If \(B\) is NP-complete and its complement reduces to \(B\), a single paired comparison already supplies the stated compiler. Strictness rules out even the multiquery PL form of NP-hardness. This condition is stronger than unconstrained truth-table or adaptive Cook hardness because it specifies the decoder and supplies the complementary rails.

### C45. OR-distillation compares the two many-one degrees without confusing aggregation and compression

An OR-distillation for \(L\) takes an arbitrary list of strings of maximum length \(n\) and outputs an equivalent OR instance of length \(\operatorname{poly}(n)\), in total polynomial time, with any fixed target language. This property pulls back through polynomial-time many-one reductions by transforming every list member. **H / strict.** It is equivalent for \(A,B\) under \(H\). Without \(H\), distillation of \(A\) implies distillation of \(B\) by padding; distillation of \(B\) alone has no converse supplied by this argument. If either source is NP-hard and has a deterministic OR-distillation, Fortnow–Santhanam gives \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{coNP/poly}\). Strictness excludes NP-hardness of \(B\) but leaves this obstruction meaningful for a hypothetical NP-hard \(A\). This distinguishes a polynomial total-size ordinary OR compiler, which is already known, from an actual compression theorem. [Fortnow–Santhanam](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcss.2010.06.007)

### C46. AND-distillation has a different conditional obstruction

AND-distillation, defined with the AND answer replacing OR, transfers both ways under \(H\) by pulling back every list member. Complementing its output interpretation gives an OR-distillation of the complement language. There is also an obstruction for NP-hardness of the *original* language: if SAT reduces to that language, the same compression would solve AND(SAT) with output bounded by \(\operatorname{poly}(n)\) for clauses of individual input length at most \(n\). Choose a polynomial input count \(t(n)\) large enough that this bound is below \(t(n)/2-1\). Drucker's deterministic AND-compression theorem then implies \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{coNP/poly}\). **H / strict.** Under equality this conditional obstruction transfers between the orientations. Negative uniform strictness excludes NP-hardness of \(B\), while an NP-hard signed language in that branch would still face the AND-compression obstruction. [Drucker, Theorem 7.4](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/130927115)

## Arithmetic circuits and current time bounds

### C47. A noncancelling witness polynomial gives an arithmetic decision criterion

For a tensor pair \(x\) with transporter encodings of length \(d\) trits, define
\[
 W_x(Z)=\sum_{g\text{ valid}}\ \prod_{j=1}^{d} Z_{j,g_j}.
\]
Use the exact signed relation or the ordinary one, according to the problem considered. Distinct witnesses give distinct monomials with coefficient 1, so the polynomial is nonzero exactly on YES over every coefficient characteristic, even when the total transporter count vanishes in that characteristic. If \(W_x\) has a uniform polynomial-time circuit constructor over \(\mathbf F_3\), Schwartz–Zippel over an extension of polynomial cardinality yields RP decision. For nonuniform consequences, require a polynomial-size circuit family on symbolic input bits as well as \(Z\), specializing correctly for every pair \(x\) at each length. After amplification and fixing coins, this gives a P/poly algorithm. **H / strict.** For the signed polynomial, this would imply \(H_R\) in the uniform case and \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) in the stated nonuniform case, using fixed ordinary outputs. The corresponding stronger negative branch refutes each respective circuit hypothesis. An arbitrary small circuit selected separately for each fixed \(x\) would not supply that inference.

### C48. Identity testing for restrictions of the symbolic witness polynomial yields full lexicographic search

Fixing a proposed trit \(g_j=c\) corresponds to setting \(Z_{j,b}=0\) for every \(b\ne c\). The restricted polynomial is nonzero exactly when the assigned prefix has a valid extension. If signed \(W_x\) has the uniform circuit constructor of C47 and these restrictions admit polynomial-time polynomial identity testing, greedy trit fixing gives the lexicographically least signed witness on every YES. The test need only work for the stated restricted circuit family. Random evaluation gives a polynomial-time randomized witness algorithm with verification by amplifying over all prefix queries, even without deterministic PIT. **H / strict.** On the full signed language, the deterministic premises imply \(A\in\mathrm P\) and hence \(H\), whereas on a separately specified subfamily they give deterministic witness search even in the strict branch. This is distinct from evaluating the signed witness count at all-ones weights, which can lose every witness through cancellation.

### C49. Ordinary symbolic witness circuits already test the randomized signed branch

Suppose the ordinary \(W_x\) has the uniform constructor of C47. The randomized search in C48, now applied to ordinary witnesses, gives an arbitrary verified ordinary transporter on YES with high probability. Privately multiply the target by a uniformly random ordinary ambient group element \(h\). Calling a fixed ordinary selector on the randomized target and undoing \(h\) gives a uniform original transporter: for each randomized target the possible \(h\) form an equal-size coset and the selected output is fixed before undoing \(h\). Fix a successful random tape for the selector conditionally or average over its independent tapes; its failure depends only on the randomized pair, so successful outputs are still uniform. A nonempty signed fiber accounts for at least one eighth of a uniform ordinary coset. Polynomially many independent trials therefore give RP signed decision. The sampler can use rejection with negligible abort and all returned witnesses verify. **H / strict.** Under strictness the ordinary constructor forces \(\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{RP}\). Under \(\neg H_R\) the constructor is impossible outright. A shared nonuniform circuit family instead forces \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) by seed fixing on these verifiable searches.

### C50. November 2026 preprint gives a single-exponential signed tensor bound

Using Qiao–Zhang's November 2026 Theorem 8.1 as stated, the entire determinant-one coset over \(\mathbf F_3\) can be computed in deterministic time \(3^{O(n_1+n_2+n_3)}\operatorname{poly}(|x|)\), whether or not \(H\) holds. The theorem permits generators for a restricted ambient subgroup and outputs a coset representative with stabilizer generators. Supply the three determinant-one groups using their standard elementary transvections. Equivalently compute the ordinary coset and pass to its index-at-most-eight determinant kernel. The manuscript is a recent preprint; its authors report that Lean verification is in progress. The older published \(\widetilde O(n^{3/2})\) exponent remains a separate available bound. [Qiao–Zhang, Theorem 8.1](https://group-iso.github.io/main.pdf), [Authors’ verification status](https://group-iso.github.io/), [Ivanyos–Mendoza–Qiao–Sun–Zhang, FOCS 2024](https://chuanqizhang.com/assets/pdf/pdf/focs24.pdf)

### C51. The same preprint yields the signed coset over variable explicit finite fields

For \(\mathbf F_q=\mathbf F_{p^e}\) given with an additive basis, supply transvections with coefficients in the additive basis and all their coordinate pairs. Addition of coefficients corresponds to multiplication of same-position transvections, so this polynomially sized generating set generates all elementary matrices and hence \(\mathrm{SL}\) in every dimension (dimension one is trivial). Thus the Theorem 8.1 consequence in C50 extends to exact \(\prod_i\mathrm{SL}(n_i,q)\) coset computation in \(q^{O(n_1+n_2+n_3)}\operatorname{poly}(\mathrm{InSize})\). Prescribed nonzero determinant values can be shifted to one by known diagonal matrices on the target. This direct subgroup use requires no factorization of \(q-1\) and no discrete logarithms. It is a runtime consequence of the same named recent preprint, not a polynomial fixed-field many-one construction.

### C52. Even Cayley-table group isomorphism follows from the claimed full-coset theorem

Take Qiao–Zhang's Theorem 9.1 for explicit finite multiplication tables as an imported premise. It gives either no ordinary isomorphism or a representative \(g\) and generators \(S\) of the source automorphism group. Evaluate permutation signs with respect to the table orders. If \(g\) is even, return it; if \(g\) is odd, an even isomorphism exists exactly when some element of \(S\) is odd, and its product with \(g\) is the desired witness. This adds polynomial time and works for every finite group, including the special ternary groups previously bounded with a larger power of the order. Converting a compact presentation of dimension \(d\) to its explicit table still costs exponential size in \(d\). **H / strict.** This solves the full Cayley-table parity question in both branches subject to the named theorem; it settles neither the compact parity problem nor its fixed-field tensor degree. [Qiao–Zhang, Theorem 9.1](https://group-iso.github.io/main.pdf)

### C53. Multiple intrinsic table-parity constraints reduce to one linear system after ordinary coset recovery

Suppose a list of polynomially many intrinsic strata of a group given by its table can be computed in polynomial time from the input, and all isomorphisms preserve their named correspondence. Give each stratum the ordering induced by the table order. Restriction gives a vector of parity bits for each source automorphism. The vector is a homomorphism, including when strata overlap, because each restriction composes on its own invariant set. Evaluate it on the source generators, correct by the vector for one representative, and solve arbitrary affine equations on these bits by Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbf F_2\). A combination of source generators lifts any solution. This gives an even stronger polynomial-time witness test under the ordinary full-coset premise in C52. It describes signed restrictions for a polynomial number of subsets without enumerating an exponentially large parity-image group.

### C54. A strongly dimension-efficient Circuit-SAT reduction would give a Williams lower bound

Assume there is a uniform deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction from Circuit-SAT to ordinary TI such that, for every \(k\), inputs on \(u\) variables and at most \(u^k\) gates produce maximum tensor dimension \(O_k(u^\gamma)\) for some fixed \(\gamma<2/3\). The published FOCS 2024 bound then solves those circuits in \(2^{o(u)}\) time. Williams's Theorem 1.1 yields \(\mathrm{NEXP}\nsubseteq\mathrm{P/poly}\), since the speedup beats brute force by a superpolynomial factor for every polynomial gate bound. If the runtime theorem of C50 is accepted, the dimensional premise relaxes to \(\gamma<1\). **H / strict.** The same reduction family implies \(H\), because it includes circuits large enough to encode arbitrary SAT by a polynomial padding of the variable count. Under strictness no such reduction family exists, regardless of which runtime premise one accepts. Williams's consequence concerns arbitrary polynomial-gate Circuit-SAT; a corresponding fixed-width-CNF reduction cannot directly be substituted into that theorem. [Williams, Theorem 1.1](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/10080703X)

### C55. The relevant remaining size-exponent premise is Weak ETH

Let Weak ETH mean that no deterministic \(O(2^{u^{1-\varepsilon}})L^{O(1)}\)-time algorithm solves 3SAT for any constant \(\varepsilon>0\), where \(u\) is the number of variables and \(L\) the encoding length. The October 2026 fixed-width counting result gives \(2^{o(u)}L^{O(1)}\) and refutes classical ETH, but that bound alone falls short of a fixed-power improvement. Under Weak ETH, no polynomial-time reduction from 3SAT to \(A\) or \(B\) outputs maximum tensor dimension \(O(u^\gamma)\) for a fixed \(\gamma<2/3\), by the published tensor bound. Using the recent preprint bound would replace \(2/3\) by 1. **H / strict.** The obstruction applies in both branches and reflects a dimension exponent; it does not rule out general polynomial-size reductions under equality. Strictness additionally rules out all polynomial-time reductions from 3SAT to \(B\) because \(A\in\mathrm{NP}\). [OpenAI fixed-width counting formalization scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/378.md), [Weak ETH formulation and SH comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02650)

## Scope of these comparisons

Each implication invoking an algorithm, proof-system conjecture, oracle, circuit family, or Weak ETH states that premise locally. The imported November 2026 group/tensor results are identified as preprint-dependent. Lower bounds C03 and the new compilers and oracle constructions are written proofs with their specified premises and models. Neither the strict branch nor equality is settled here.
