# Ordinary Polynomial Calculus for each matrix-conjugacy instance

Let $A,B$ be nonconjugate $n\times n$ matrices over a field $F$. Use the original conjugacy equations
$$
 XA-BX=0,\qquad XY-I_n=0,\qquad YX-I_n=0 .
$$
Polynomial Calculus (PC) uses ordinary commutative polynomials, scalar linear combinations, and multiplication by a variable. There are no field or Boolean axioms and no extra variables. Sparse size counts nonzero monomial occurrences over all proof lines. [Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She, Open Question 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

Choose a splitting field. For each eigenvalue and exact Jordan block length $\tau$, let $a_\tau,b_\tau$ be the two multiplicities. For $r\ne s$ let $d_F(r,s)$ and $L_F(r,s)$ be the minimum PC degree and sparse size of the rectangular inverse system
$$
 PQ=I_s,\qquad QP=I_r,\qquad
 P\in\operatorname{Mat}_{s,r},\quad Q\in\operatorname{Mat}_{r,s}.
$$
If one dimension is zero, the system includes a nonzero constant. Define $d_*=\min_{\tau:a_\tau\ne b_\tau}d_F(a_\tau,b_\tau)$.

**Theorem.** The conjugacy-system degree is exactly 2 if there are constant matrices $C,D$ with $\operatorname{tr}(C)\ne\operatorname{tr}(D)$ and $XC=DX$ for every $X$ satisfying $XA=BX$. Otherwise the degree is exactly $\max(3,d_*)$. The trace test is explicit linear algebra over $F$.

**Sparse-size theorem.** If the displayed input matrices are already in Jordan form over $F$, let $L_*=\min_{\tau:a_\tau\ne b_\tau}L_F(a_\tau,b_\tau)$. If $L$ is the conjugacy-system sparse size, then
$$
 L_*\le O((L+1)^3),\qquad L\le L_*+\operatorname{poly}(n).
$$
The lower bound controls proofs of arbitrary degree. A dense change of coordinates can expand individual lines, so this sparse statement is for the displayed Jordan presentations. The degree theorem is coordinate-independent.

## Proof complexity of disjoint systems

Let $\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_t$ be systems in mutually disjoint sets of variables. If their union has a PC refutation of degree $D$, at least one system has a PC refutation of degree at most $D$. If their union has a sparse PC refutation of size $L$, at least one has one of size $O((L+1)^3)$.

For the degree claim, let $W_i$ be the space of local polynomials of degree at most $D$ and $V_i$ the space of consequences of $\Gamma_i$ derivable at degree at most $D$. Choose a basis for $W_i$ adapted to both its degree filtration and $V_i$. To construct one, at each degree first extend the previous basis for the intersection with $V_i$, and then extend to the whole filtered step. Give each basis element its actual polynomial degree. The resulting tensor basis of the truncated global space consists of tensor products of weight at most $D$, where weights add. Independence of their leading forms at each weight follows by tensoring the bases for the associated graded spaces. Let $K$ be the span of the tensor basis products with at least one factor in a selected $V_i$ basis. It contains every input usable at degree $D$. Its intersection with the space of degree at most $D-1$ has the same basis with weight at most $D-1$. To multiply such a product by a variable in block $i$, either its selected factor lies elsewhere and remains unchanged, or the $i$ factor is in $V_i$ and its permissible variable multiple stays there. Thus all proof lines belong to $K$. If no $V_i$ contains 1, the basis in each block can include 1 as a complementary element of weight zero, and the tensor basis element 1 lies outside $K$. This proves the degree claim, including after cancellations that lower the degree of a line.

For the size claim, fix one global proof and replace $W_i$ by the span of the local monomial factors in its lines, together with 1. Its dimension is at most $L+1$. Let $V_i\subseteq W_i$ be generated by the used local axioms and the closure rule
$$
 u\in V_i,\quad u\in H_i(x):=\{w\in W_i:xw\in W_i\}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad xu\in V_i
$$
for each local variable $x$ used in a multiplication step. Let $W_{-i}$ be the tensor product of the other spaces and let $K_{-i}$ be the sum of their tensor subspaces with a selected local $V_j$ factor. In the full tensor product use $K=V_i\otimes W_{-i}+W_i\otimes K_{-i}$, the same subspace for every choice of $i$. If a line $u$ is multiplied by $x$ in block $i$, both $u$ and $xu$ are in the full tensor product by definition of the monomial spaces. Expanding in a basis of $W_{-i}$ shows that $u\in H_i(x)\otimes W_{-i}$. The intersection with $K$ is exactly
$$
 (V_i\cap H_i(x))\otimes W_{-i}+H_i(x)\otimes K_{-i}.
$$
Indeed, this is the kernel on $H_i(x)\otimes W_{-i}$ of the quotient map to $(W_i/V_i)\otimes (W_{-i}/K_{-i})$. Multiplication by $x$ sends both summands back into $K$. It follows by induction on the given proof that 1 belongs to $K$, so some $V_i$ contains 1. To recover a short local proof, maintain a basis of that $V_i$ constructed by saturation. Each enlargement uses a combination of at most $L+1$ earlier basis lines, then one multiplication. At most $L+1$ enlargements are needed. Each intermediate line is in $W_i$ and has at most $L+1$ monomials. Include the initial independent input lines and the final linear combination for 1. This costs $O((L+1)^3)$ monomial occurrences. The construction does not require enumerating the global tensor basis.

## The lower and upper transfers

After changing constant bases to Jordan form, specialize $X$ and $Y$ separately on each type $\tau$ to $P_\tau\otimes I_\ell$ and $Q_\tau\otimes I_\ell$, where $\ell$ is the block length. Put all cross-type entries to zero. For equal multiplicities take identities, and for discrepant types use mutually disjoint rectangular variables. The intertwining equations become zero because each image uses the same Jordan operator in both source and target. Each inverse entry becomes zero or one rectangular inverse equation, with the same equations possibly repeated on several layers. Each variable maps to a variable, zero, or one. In Jordan coordinates this substitution does not increase monomial counts. By the disjoint-system result the conjugacy degree is at least $d_*$, and the size of the easiest rectangle is at most cubic in the conjugacy sparse size.

For the converse, fix an eigenvalue $\lambda$ and a block length $\ell$, and put $N=A-\lambda I$ or $B-\lambda I$. The functor
$$
 \ker N^\ell\big/\big(\ker N^{\ell-1}+N\ker N^{\ell+1}\big)
$$
picks one vector from precisely each block of exact length $\ell$ at that eigenvalue. On such a block it retains the highest surviving chain vector. A shorter block is in the first denominator term, and on a longer block the second denominator term fills the numerator. On other primary components $N$ is invertible. Thus the source and target dimensions are exactly $a_\tau,b_\tau$.

The reverse linear intertwining equation follows in PC degree 3 from the identity
$$
 AY-YB=Y(XA-BX)Y+(I-YX)AY+YB(XY-I).
$$
Project $X,Y$ independently onto the two actual linear intertwiner spaces, writing the projections as $\widehat X,\widehat Y$. Their differences from $X,Y$ are linear combinations of the corresponding linear equations. Thus the projected inverse equations follow by expanding
$$
 \widehat X\widehat Y-I=(XY-I)+(\widehat X-X)Y+\widehat X(\widehat Y-Y)
$$
and the reverse product. On each linear endomorphism space take the linear map induced by the quotient and extend it linearly to the space of all matrices. Apply its coordinate functionals to the derived projected inverse equations. Functoriality on the projected intertwiners identifies the resulting entries with both rectangular inverse equations. Once the reverse linear lines are available, every subsequent correction to a quadratic line uses degree at most 2. Each step uses only a polynomial number of expanded monomial occurrences, since it is linear algebra on explicit spaces and only constant-degree polynomial lines are handled. In a displayed Jordan presentation the induced entries can be chosen to be individual entries of $X,Y$, by extracting the highest chain coordinates between the blocks of that exact type. If their projected versions were used in the derivation, replacing each by the original selected entry changes it by an available linear equation. Therefore a proof for that rectangle replays without sparse expansion of its high-degree lines. This proves the sparse upper bound and the degree upper bound $\max(3,d_*)$. [Prior Jordan-quotient derivation](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/MatrixIsomorphismPC/PROOF.md)

This degree comparison applies to every field. A constant change of matrix bases induces invertible linear substitutions for $X,Y$, with input equations replaced by scalar combinations of equations in the new bases. Multiplication by a linear form is simulated by a scalar combination of variable multiplications, preserving degree. For scalar descent, the bounded-degree closure starts with the low-degree input span and repeatedly adds variable multiples of its intersection with the preceding degree space. Field extension commutes with these spans and intersections, so existence of a proof at fixed degree does not change. Each rectangular system is defined over the prime field, hence its degree over the splitting field is the same as over $F$. Nonconjugacy is preserved on extension by rational canonical form.

## The degree-two decision

Take the span of the inverse equations, the linear intertwiner equations, and all single-variable multiples of the linear intertwiner equations. Unless it already contains a nonzero constant, this span contains every degree-two derivable line. Indeed its components have bidegrees $(1,1)$ with $(0,0)$, $(2,0)$, and $(1,0)$ in $(X,Y)$. If the span has no nonzero constant, cancellation can leave a linear line only in the original intertwiner span. Its permissible multiplication steps are then already present.

Write a scalar combination of the inverse axioms as
$$
 \operatorname{tr}\big(C(YX-I)\big)-\operatorname{tr}\big(D(XY-I)\big).
$$
After restriction to the linear space of intertwiners its bilinear part is $\operatorname{tr}(Y(XC-DX))$. This vanishes for all $Y$ precisely when $XC=DX$ throughout that space. Conversely, if it vanishes there, each coefficient of $Y$ is a linear combination of the input intertwining equations and can be canceled in degree 2. The constant survives exactly when the listed traces differ. Degree 1 cannot produce a constant because the usable input equations at that degree are homogeneous linear. The trace criterion therefore completes the exact degree assertion. The prior four-dimensional nilpotent example already used this trace criterion separately. [Degree-two obstruction in prior work](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/MatrixIsomorphismPC/TRACE-CERTIFICATE.md)

## Quantitative consequences in positive characteristic

For a discrepant type put $r=\min(a_\tau,b_\tau)$, $s=\max(a_\tau,b_\tau)$ and $q=p^{v_p(s-r)}$, with $p=\operatorname{char} F$. Define
$$
 h=\min_{\tau:a_\tau\ne b_\tau}\min(r+1,q).
$$
In every positive characteristic the actual conjugacy degree is between $\max(2,\lceil h/2\rceil)$ and $\max(3,2h)$, simultaneously for every instance. In characteristic zero take $h=1$. For the local lower bound with $r>0$, substitute $P$ and $Q$ by a Boolean matching matrix and its transpose in Riis's explicit rectangular bijection encoding. Each projected diagonal entry is the row or column totality plus Boolean equations, and each off-diagonal entry is a sum of collision equations. They have degree-two derivations. Riis's Lemma 14.1 applies at $D=\min(\lfloor r/2\rfloor,q-1)$ when $D\ge2$, and for $D<2$ the degree-two input bound suffices. Thus the rectangular degree is at least $\min(\lfloor r/2\rfloor+1,q)$. For the upper bound, the first differing binomial coefficient in $(1+t)^s-(1+t)^r$ occurs at degree $q$ in characteristic $p$. The elementary coefficient identity $e_q(PQ)=e_q(QP)$ from Cauchy–Binet and factor replacement under the inverse equations give degree at most $2q$. The cofactor descent in prior work gives degree at most $r+2$: each signed cofactor row of degree $k$ annihilates $P$ once the $(k+1)$-minors vanish, and multiplication by $Q$ plus $PQ=I$ then kills the cofactor row in degree $k+2$. Start at $k=r$ and descend to the empty determinant. When $r=0$ the original conjugacy system still has degree at least two and the universal upper from the quotient is 3. [Riis, Lemma 14.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07280v1#S14) · [Prior conjugacy lower and upper constructions](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSparsePCBounds/Proof.md)

In particular, for every $n\ge4$ the worst-case degree over a field is exactly
$$
 \max\left(3,\ \max_{\substack{0\le r<s\\r+s\le n}} d_F(r,s)\right).
$$
For the upper bound, there is a positively discrepant type and a negatively discrepant type because the weighted sums of multiplicities are both $n$. For these two types the sums of their two multiplicities add to at most $2n$, so one local rectangle has $r+s\le n$ and $d_*$ is no larger than its degree. For the lower bound, the pair $\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0_s)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(I_s,0_r)$ specializes to the same rectangle in both complementary blocks. Pad with an identical zero block and specialize the corresponding extra maps to identities if $r+s<n$. This preserves the lower bound. Degree 3 is realized over every field by the known pair $J_2(0)\oplus J_2(0)$ and $J_2(0)\oplus J_1(0)\oplus J_1(0)$ and its identity-block proof specialization after padding. Consequently every degree above three is dominated by a two-eigenvalue idempotent pair with no larger dimension. [Prior exact degree-three pair](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/MatrixIsomorphismPC/CONJUGACY-SHARPNESS.md)

The exact worst-case degree formula and the local factor-two bounds yield a numerical envelope uniform in the characteristic. For positive characteristic $p$ and $n\ge4$, the worst-case degree is within absolute constant factors of
$$
 1+\max_{p^a\le n}\min\big(p^a,1+\lfloor(n-p^a)/2\rfloor\big).
$$
For one value $q=p^a$, the smallest positive gap of that valuation is $q$ itself. It leaves the largest possible smaller side, $\lfloor(n-q)/2\rfloor$. Use that rectangle for the lower bound when its smaller side is positive and the fixed degree-three pair for the bounded cases.

Let $L_J(n,F)$ be the largest of the minimum sparse proof sizes for displayed Jordan pairs of dimension $n$, and let $L_R(n,F)=\max_{0\le r<s,\,r+s\le n}L_F(r,s)$. Then $L_R(n,F)\le L_J(n,F)\le L_R(n,F)+\operatorname{poly}(n)$. The upper bound uses a discrepant type with sum at most $n$, as in the degree argument. For the lower bound the padded complementary idempotents specialize directly to one shared rectangle. This operation never expands a line, so no cubic loss is needed for the worst-case comparison.

The parameter also gives a sparse size lower bound $\exp(\Omega(h))/n^{O(1)}$ for displayed Jordan pairs, with absolute constants. This extends the earlier fixed-ratio matching restriction to every rectangle with $r$ and $q$ large. In the matching encoding choose $t=\lfloor r/3\rfloor$ rows uniformly and assign them to uniformly chosen distinct columns, set those entries to 1, and set all other entries incident to the selected rows and columns to zero. The residual matching rectangle has dimensions $r-t,s-t$, the same gap, and by Riis has no degree-$D$ refutation for $D=\min(\lfloor(r-t)/2\rfloor,q-1)$. Given a collision-free monomial support of $k$ distinct edges, let $J$ be the number of selected rows meeting it. Conditional on those rows, survival requires their $J$ prescribed columns and has probability at most $a^J$, where $a=1/(s-t+1)$. Expanding $\prod(a+(1-a)1_{\text{row unselected}})$ over the support and using sampling without replacement gives mean at most $(1-(t/r)(1-a))^k$. For $r\ge6$ this is at most $(8/9)^k$. Collision monomials are zero after normalization modulo the Boolean and collision axioms.

If a sparse proof has fewer than $(9/8)^D$ monomial occurrences, some restriction kills all normalized supports of size at least $D$, by the union bound. Its normal-form lines have degree at most $D-1$. Replay as ordinary PC by using Boolean reductions and collision axioms to clean each single multiplication, with degree at most $D$. This is a contradiction. For a transfer from rectangular inverse to matching, each used inverse axiom simulates in degree 2 with sparse cost polynomial in $r+s$, so the rectangle needs $\exp(\Omega(\min(r+1,q)))/(r+s)^{O(1)}$ monomial occurrences. Bounded values of that minimum are included by adjusting constants. The cubic-loss disjoint-system theorem now gives the claimed bound for the original Jordan conjugacy proof. The corresponding degree upper bound gives sparse size $n^{O(h)}$ by proof-emitting closure. [Prior fixed-ratio restriction argument](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSparsePCBounds/Proof.md)

## Contribution and limits

The quotient extraction, degree-two trace test, Riis lower bound, cofactor descent, and special-case restriction have prior proofs. The new comparisons are the independent-system theorem for degree and unrestricted sparse size, the exact instance-wise Jordan reduction, and the uniform rectangular parameter bounds. This gives the degree for each conjugacy instance in terms of its independent rectangular-inverse systems, and locates the worst cases among idempotents and a fixed degree-three example. The exact numerical degree of every rectangle and the remaining logarithmic gap in the sparse exponent are open in this analysis. The numerical size lower bound is for the stated displayed coordinates. Exact finite checks covered 56 distinct pairs of low-dimensional binary Jordan forms, the sharp degree-three nilpotent example, 600 bounded-degree disjoint closure instances and 63 matching-restriction cases. The theorem is a written proof without a Lean certificate.
