# Factor profiles in a good-prime tensor genus

Let $d_1,\ldots,d_m$ be positive nonsquare integers representing distinct rational squareclasses, given in binary. Put $A=\prod_{i=1}^m\mathbb Q(\sqrt{d_i})$. Define the good-prime genus using ordinary multiplication tensor equivalence over $\mathbb Q_p$ for all but finitely many rational primes $p$. For these unramified fibres this is equivalently equality of the finite étale reductions up to isomorphism.

**Theorem.** Given $0\le t\le\lfloor m/3\rfloor$, the number of global classes in this genus with $t$ quartic field factors, $m-3t$ quadratic field factors, and $2t$ rational factors equals the number of ways to choose $t$ mutually disjoint triples of indices $\{i,j,k\}$ for which $d_i d_j d_k$ is a square. These counts include no ordering of the triples. In particular existence for a specified profile is NP-complete. This holds even when every $d_i$ is the product of two distinct primes.

## Exact correspondence

The splitting field of $A$ is multiquadratic. Let $G$ be its Galois group and $\lambda_i$ the nontrivial quadratic character for the subfield defined by $d_i$. These characters are distinct. The permutation character of $A$ is $m\cdot 1+\sum_i\lambda_i$.

An algebra in the good-prime genus has the same character by Chebotarev in a common splitting field and has the same minimal splitting field by taking the kernel of the embedding action. This also holds for a tensor representative, since local ordinary equivalence to a geometrically diagonal multiplication tensor makes it geometrically diagonal, and an isotope with invertible slices identifies it with an étale algebra. Tensor isomorphism for these multiplication maps is precisely algebra isomorphism by normalization at the unit. [Proof of the tensor and character reductions](GENUS.md)

Since the common splitting group has exponent two, every quartic field factor is biquadratic. Its permutation character has one copy of the trivial character and one copy of each of its three nontrivial characters. Those three form a triple $\lambda_i,\lambda_j,\lambda_k$ with product equal to the trivial character. They must be drawn from the $\lambda_i$, and each distinct character can be used at most once, because its multiplicity in the entire target character is exactly one. By quadratic Kummer theory their product is trivial exactly when $d_i d_j d_k$ is a square.

Conversely, a selected triple defines the unique biquadratic field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{d_i},\sqrt{d_j})$, also containing $\sqrt{d_k}$. This choice is independent of the order within the triple. Take the product of these fields, the quadratic fields for the unselected indices, and $2t$ copies of $\mathbb Q$. Its nontrivial character support and multiplicities match those of $A$, and the multiplicity at the trivial character is $t+(m-3t)+2t=m$. Thus at every common unramified prime the two étale algebras agree. In fact equality of characters gives equality of all powers of every Frobenius character value, hence of the full cycle type. Different sets of triples give different multisets of field factors, because an individual quartic factor determines its three quadratic subfields. This proves both directions and the count.

## Computational complexity

A certificate lists the $t$ disjoint triples, whose product-square tests use deterministic integer square roots in polynomial bit time. Nonsquareness of each $d_i$ and distinctness of its squareclass from the others can also be tested by integer square roots, using that $d_i$ and $d_j$ have the same squareclass exactly when $d_i d_j$ is a square.

For hardness start with a simple graph whose edges are to be partitioned into triangles. Assign different primes $p_v$ to the vertices and take one integer $p_up_v$ for each edge $\{u,v\}$. The first polynomially many primes have polynomial size and can be generated by sieving. For three distinct edges the product is a square precisely when every vertex has even degree in the subgraph of those edges. A simple graph of three edges in which every degree is even is exactly a triangle. Thus a partition into triangles is exactly a witness with $t=m/3$, where $m$ is the number of edges. Inputs with $m$ not divisible by three can be mapped to a fixed no-instance. Holyer's edge-partition theorem gives NP-hardness. The reduction identifies every edge-triangle decomposition with a distinct global class of the specified profile. [Holyer, The NP-Completeness of Some Edge-Partition Problems](https://doi.org/10.1137/0210054)

The result is about existence and counting within a specified fibre of anonymous prime data. Ramified completions are outside the good-prime condition. The proof is informal.
