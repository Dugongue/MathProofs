# Sharp joint-prime threshold for equal-degree field factors

Fix a number field $F$, a rational prime $p$, and $k\ge1$. Consider degree-$n$ étale $F$-algebras whose field factors all have degree $d=p^k$ and whose common splitting field is abelian over $F$. For a set of good primes, count the total degree of those original field factors in which every prime of the set splits completely. This is a joint statistic on the original factors.

**Theorem.** The counts on all sets of at most $k$ good primes determine the algebra, hence its ordinary multiplication tensor. The first side admitting two different algebras with equal counts on all sets of at most $k-1$ good primes is
$$
n=p^k\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(1+p^i).
$$
The bound is attained over every number field, already with a common elementary abelian splitting field all of whose decomposition groups are cyclic. In particular the extremizers have the same local genus when $k\ge2$. Attainment extends to each larger multiple of $p^k$ by adjoining identical degree-$p^k$ factors.

The finite-geometric extremum is the minimum-volume subspace trade of type with strength one less than the block dimension. The dual polar construction and minimum volume are due to Krotov, Mogilnykh and Potapov, with later extensions by Krotov. We give a direct proof to track repeated field factors and abelian quotients whose annihilators need not be elementary. The contribution here is the exact transfer to joint prime measurements of tensor equivalence and to fields with cyclic local decomposition groups. ## Fourier coefficients give subspace incidence

Work in a common abelian Galois extension with group $G$. A field factor fixed by $H\le G$ gives orbit $G/H$ of size $d$. Put $W=H^\perp\le\widehat G$ in the complex character group. The field factor is specified by $W$, which has order $d$. A prime splits completely in this field precisely when its Frobenius lies in $H$. For a tuple $g_1,\ldots,g_j\in G$, its contribution to the joint count is $d\prod_{i=1}^j{\bf1}_H(g_i)$. Chebotarev realizes arbitrary tuples by good primes because $G$ is abelian. For the empty tuple the count is $n$.

The finite Fourier formula ${\bf1}_H(g)=d^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in W}\lambda(g)$ shows that the coefficient indexed by $(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_j)$ equals $d^{1-j}$ times the number of factor occurrences whose annihilator contains $\langle\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_j\rangle$. This includes the empty span for $j=0$. Thus tests of order at most $j$ are equivalent to these containment counts for all character subgroups generated by at most $j$ elements. Each order-$p^k$ subgroup is generated by at most $k$ elements, and no distinct order-$p^k$ subgroup can contain it. This recovers all factor multiplicities at order $k$. [Étale algebras as Galois sets](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~mmwood/Publications/ArithStats.pdf)

## Lower bound on a collision

Let $\delta_{W'}$ be the signed multiplicity difference of the factor indexed by $W'$. Suppose that all orders at most $k-1$ agree and some $\delta_W=t\ne0$. If $W$ were generated by $k-1$ elements, the counts would recover its multiplicity directly. Therefore $W$ needs $k$ generators. An abelian group of order $p^k$ needing $k$ generators is elementary abelian. Identify $W$ with a $k$-dimensional space over $\mathbb F_p$.

For each subspace $U\le W$, put $b_U=\sum_{W'\cap W=U}\delta_{W'}$. The containment sum over $U\ge L$ is zero for every proper $L<W$, by the preceding Fourier formula. The sum for $L=W$ equals $t$. Möbius inversion on the subspace lattice gives $b_U=t\mu(U,W)$. In codimension $j$ the Möbius value is $(-1)^j p^{j(j-1)/2}$, as follows from the Gaussian binomial product at $-1$. At $1$ that product gives
$$
\sum_{U\le W}|\mu(U,W)|=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(1+p^i).
$$
Since each side has $n/d$ factors, grouping by intersections bounds $\sum_U |b_U|$ by $\sum_{W'}|\delta_{W'}|\le2n/d$. Using $|t|\ge1$ yields the claimed first possible side.

## Equality from the two systems of maximal singular subspaces

Let $V=L\oplus L^*$ with $L=\mathbb F_p^k$, and use $V$ as the character group. The split quadratic form is $q(x,\ell)=\ell(x)$. Divide its $k$-dimensional totally singular subspaces into two collections by the parity of the dimension of their intersection with $L$. For each collection take one orbit of degree $d$ for each member, using it as the character annihilator.

Every totally singular $(k-1)$-space $S$ lies in exactly two maximal totally singular spaces, one of each parity. To see this, write $t=\dim(S\cap L)$. The projection of $S$ onto $L^*$ has dimension $k-1-t$, so $L\cap S^\perp$ has dimension $t+1$. Adding a vector from this space outside $S$ gives one totally singular extension, meeting $L$ in dimension $t+1$. The nonsingular quadratic space $S^\perp/S$ has dimension two and is split because it contains this singular line. It has exactly two singular lines, over every $\mathbb F_p$ including characteristic two. Only the first comes from $L\cap S^\perp$, so the other extension meets $L$ in dimension $t$.

For a lower-dimensional totally singular subspace $R$, count pairs consisting of a $(k-1)$-space containing $R$ and a member of a collection containing that $(k-1)$-space. Each $(k-1)$-space contributes one on each side. Each member containing $R$ contributes the same positive number, determined only by dimensions. Thus the collections have equal containment counts at every dimension below $k$. A subspace that is not totally singular contributes zero. Their order-$(k-1)$ joint data agree, and the collections are distinct.

The total number $N_k$ of maximal totally singular subspaces satisfies $N_k=(p^{k-1}+1)N_{k-1}$, with $N_0=1$. Indeed the number of nonzero singular vectors is $(p^k-1)(p^{k-1}+1)$, by first choosing $x$ and then a functional vanishing at $x$. For a singular projective point $P$, the quotient $P^\perp/P$ is split of dimension $2k-2$, so $N_{k-1}$ members contain it. It is split because $P$ extends to a maximal totally singular space by taking its sum with $L\cap P^\perp$. Divide the count of pairs of points and members by the number $(p^k-1)/(p-1)$ of points in each member. The two collections have the same size by the preceding incidence argument at $R=0$. Hence each has $\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(1+p^i)$ members. This proves equality in the size bound.

## Realization with cyclic local decomposition groups

Over $\mathbb Q$, construct $2k$ independent cyclic degree-$p$ fields $K_i$, each inside $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{\ell_i})$ for a distinct rational prime $\ell_i\equiv1\bmod p$, unramified in $F$. At step $i$, choose $\ell_i$ to split completely in the compositum of the earlier $K_j$, $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2p})$, and the splitting fields of $X^p-\ell_j$ for all $j<i$. Chebotarev applied to the identity class supplies such a prime avoiding all the finitely many bad primes. Take the unique degree-$p$ subfield $K_i$ of the prime cyclotomic field. The earlier $\ell_j$ are $p$-th powers modulo $\ell_i$, so they split completely in $K_i$. The new $\ell_i$ splits in every earlier $K_j$ by construction. The condition from $\zeta_{2p}$ also makes the quadratic case totally real, and the odd-degree factors are automatically totally real.

Each factor $K_i$ is totally ramified at its own $\ell_i$ and unramified elsewhere. This proves independence from the earlier factors. At each chosen prime the other factors split. Thus all decomposition groups in the resulting elementary abelian compositum $K_0$ are cyclic, including the unramified and real cases. The inertia group at $\ell_i$ generates the corresponding coordinate of the Galois group, so every nontrivial intermediate field of $K_0/\mathbb Q$ ramifies at some $\ell_i$. Since $F$ is unramified there, $F\cap K_0=\mathbb Q$. The extension $FK_0/F$ has the required group and cyclic decomposition groups. Apply the construction of orbits to the fixed fields in that extension. If $k\ge2$, the equality includes singleton counts, hence full characters, and their restrictions to all cyclic decomposition groups give locally isomorphic algebras. The result concerns the fixed common field-factor degree profile and counts that follow the original factors across the queried primes. The proof uses unconditional Chebotarev for prime realization and specifies no effective prime cutoff. The finite-group extremal theorem and the hyperbolic constructions are independent of number-field hypotheses. The proof is informal and extends the arbitrary-profile abelian joint-prime hierarchy.
