# Prescribing the factor degrees in a rational tensor genus is NP-complete

Given a list of distinct positive nonsquare integer squareclasses $a_1,\ldots,a_m$, form the étale algebra $A=\prod_i\mathbb Q(\sqrt{a_i})$. Decide whether there is an algebra $B$ consisting of $m/3$ biquadratic fields and $2m/3$ rational factors for which $A\otimes\mathbb Q_v\cong B\otimes\mathbb Q_v$ at **every** place $v$ of $\mathbb Q$. Reject when $3\nmid m$. This problem is NP-complete under deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions. Each accepting certificate identifies the global isomorphism class of $B$ uniquely, up to reordering its factors. On the hard inputs each $a_i$ is the product of two distinct primes. With Dirichlet GRH those primes can all be chosen $O(t^2\log^2(2t))$ for a reduction graph on $t$ vertices.

The same statement holds for ordinary equivalence of the multiplication tensors in this input presentation. For unital commutative algebras any isotopy $R(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$ gives the algebra isomorphism $x\mapsto R(x)/(P(1)Q(1))$. Both divisors are units by invertibility of the three linear maps. The tensor dimensions here are $2m$.

## An exact certificate

At unramified primes local isomorphism implies equality of the global permutation characters by Chebotarev in a common multiquadratic extension. A quadratic field contributes the trivial character and its defining quadratic character. A biquadratic field contributes the trivial character and the three nontrivial characters of one binary plane. Uniqueness of the character expansion forces a partition of the given squareclasses into triples $\{a,b,ab\}$ in the rational squareclass group. The trivial multiplicities already match. Conversely such a partition specifies the candidate factors $\mathbb Q(\sqrt a,\sqrt b)$.

There is exactly one further test. In every triple, at every place, the images of $a,b$ in the local squareclass group must span dimension at most one. At rank zero both sides of the local trade are split. At rank one exactly two of the three quadratic factors become the same field $E$ of degree two, and the third becomes two copies of the base. The biquadratic algebra becomes two copies of $E$, with two base factors supplied by $B$. At rank two the biquadratic remains a local field of degree four, whereas every simple local factor of $A$ has degree at most two. Other triples cannot remove such a factor. These cases prove necessity and sufficiency.

It suffices to check 2, infinity and primes dividing the radicands. At all other primes the extensions are unramified and have cyclic decomposition group. For an odd prime, square testing uses the parity of the valuation and the residue symbol of the unit part. At 2 an odd unit is a square exactly when it is $1\bmod 8$. Over the reals use the sign. Local rank is at most one exactly when one of $a,b,ab$ is a local square. Integer factorizations may accompany the partition as certificates. Prime verification, exact integer products, valuations and modular exponentiation are polynomial-time operations in the input bit length. This proves membership in NP even if the input radicands are given unfactored.

## The reduction

Holyer's reduction from 3-SAT to edge decomposition into triangles gives simple graphs of maximum degree at most 18. Here the degree bound follows directly from the construction. Each graph $H_{3,p}$ used for a variable or clause piece is a degree-six triangular torus. The designated patches within each copy have mutual distance at least ten. A patch is identified with one other patch in a variable join or with two others in a clause join. Therefore any resulting vertex has at most three original preimages, each of degree six. Edge deletion in the clause join can only decrease degrees. [Holyer, proof and Figures 1–3](https://doi.org/10.1137/0210054)

Give vertex $u$ a distinct prime $p_u\equiv1\bmod8$, arranging $(p_u/p_v)=(p_v/p_u)=1$ on graph edges. A polynomial-time construction is proved below. Give edge $uv$ the radicand $p_up_v$. Three distinct edge radicands multiply to a square precisely when the three edges form a triangle. Indeed every vertex in the three-edge subgraph must have even degree, so its nonisolated vertices form a 3-cycle. The converse follows by multiplying around the cycle. The accompanying Lean theorem proves this incidence equivalence for arbitrarily labeled simple graphs using lexicographically ordered edges.

For each triangle $uvw$ only its three vertex primes could be odd ramified primes for its trade. At $p_u$ the opposite radicand $p_vp_w$ is a square by the edge conditions and Hensel's lemma. The other two vertices give the same conclusion. All three radicands are positive and $1\bmod8$. Thus every triangle passes every local test, independently of the behavior at primes attached to other vertices. The exact certificate gives a bijection between edge-triangle decompositions and global classes of algebras $B$ of the specified shape in the local genus of $A$. Holyer's decision theorem proves NP-hardness. No counting-hardness premise is needed.

## Constructing the labels

For an unconditional polynomial bound, greedily color the maximum-degree-$\Delta$ graph in at most $\Delta+1$ colors ($\Delta=18$ suffices). Process whole colors in order. Choose an auxiliary prime $T>t+1$ of size $O(t+1)$, and give the vertices different residues in $\{1,\dots,t\}$ modulo $T$. For each vertex require its assigned residue modulo $T$, and residue 1 modulo 8 times the product of its earlier neighbors' primes. These congruences specify a reduced class by CRT. No prior label equals $T$, and the distinct residues enforce distinct labels. Linnik's theorem bounds its least prime by a fixed power of the modulus. If earlier labels are at most $(2t)^C$, each new modulus is $O(t(2t)^{C\Delta})$. The exponent remains constant after at most 19 color rounds. Deterministic enumeration with primality testing is therefore polynomial, and quadratic reciprocity gives both directions of each edge condition. [Linnik bound and references](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.3595)

Dirichlet GRH gives smaller labels. Now process vertices in any order and set $M=8\prod p_j$ over **all** previous labels. Require residue 1 modulo 8 and Legendre symbol $+1$ at just the earlier neighbors. These conditions give a subgroup of the units modulo $M$ of index at most $4\cdot2^\Delta$. A prime in the subgroup is automatically distinct from every previous label. The least-prime-in-a-coset theorem gives $p\ll_\Delta\log^2(2M)$. If $P$ is the largest label through the first $t$ vertices, then $\sqrt P\ll_\Delta 1+t\log P$, which implies $P\ll_\Delta t^2\log^2(2t)$. This is deterministic by scanning primes. Only Dirichlet characters over $\mathbb Q$ enter the bound. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.3595) · [Dirichlet GRH verification](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

This is a decision problem about the existence of a prescribed global factorization inside one genus. The ordinary isomorphism problem with two fixed input algebras has different quantifiers. The number-theoretic reduction and complexity classification are written proofs.
