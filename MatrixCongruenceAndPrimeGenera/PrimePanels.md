# Two-prime reconstruction in large tensor genera

In the rational lower-bound family of the [genus theorem](GENUS.md), there is a single list of fewer than $4n$ good primes that works for every member. Every one-prime local algebra is identical throughout the family. Nevertheless all pairwise counts on this list determine the individual rational algebra, including which biquadratic fields occur. Under Dirichlet GRH the primes have size $O(n^2\log^4(2n))$. An adaptive procedure that only asks for one integer joint count at a time needs at least $(1/4-o(1))n$ answers in the worst case for this family. Any fixed panel of primes from which only singleton and pair counts are read needs at least $(1/\sqrt{2}-o(1))\sqrt n$ primes.

Here a joint count sums the degrees of those **same global factors** which split completely at both selected primes. Reading the two separate local algebras at those primes does not supply this extra observable.

**Proof.** In the common multiquadratic splitting field let $G$ be the Galois group and let $V$ be its group of binary characters. The construction gives $|G|<4n$ and a common abelian conductor dividing $M$ with $\log(2M)=O(\log^2(2n))$. Select one good prime for each $g\in G$ by its character signs. Each sign prescription is a coset of index $|G|$ modulo $M$. The least-prime-in-a-coset estimate gives $p\ll |G|^2\log^2(2M)$, after enlarging the absolute constant for the small moduli. The bound follows also by averaging the smooth character prime sum in the genus proof with coefficients equal to the prescribed signs. The list is independent of which algebra is presented. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.3595)

For a biquadratic factor let $W\subseteq V$ be its two-dimensional character space. Its contribution to $J(g,h)$ is $4\,1_{W^\perp}(g)1_{W^\perp}(h)$. Normalize the Fourier transform on $G\times G$ by $|G|^{-2}$. At a pair of independent characters $u,v$ this contribution has Fourier coefficient $1/4$ if $u,v\in W$, and zero otherwise, by character orthogonality in each coordinate. Rational factors contribute zero at these frequencies. Thus $4\widehat J(u,v)$ is exactly the multiplicity of the field with character group $\langle u,v\rangle$. The remaining degree determines the rational factors. This computation also works with repeated fields.

For the lower bounds let $N$ be the number of constructed classes. The genus construction gives $\log N\ge(n/4)\log n-O(n)$. Each query answer lies in $\{0,\ldots,n\}$. A correct deterministic decision tree of worst-case depth $d$ has at most $(n+1)^d$ leaves, so $d\ge\log N/\log(n+1)$. A fixed panel of $k$ primes gives at most $k(k+1)/2$ unordered pair counts including the diagonal. Combining these inequalities proves both bounds. The lower bounds concern the specified observations on a hidden member of a known family, with no supplementary input revealing its factors.

## Joint modular profiles for matrix proofs

The short certificates below can also be chosen Hilbert-like, linear in the axiom placeholders. For completeness, evaluate every gate $U$ of a division-free IPS circuit at placeholders equal to the input equations $F$ and at zero. Keep circuits for the coefficients of $U(F)-U(0)=\sum_i F_i Q_i$. The input gates initialize this representation, sums add the coefficient vectors, and for a product $UV$ use $U(F)(V(F)-V(0))+V(0)(U(F)-U(0))$. Sharing the gate computations costs polynomial overhead. At the output the difference equals one, so $\sum_i y_i Q_i$ is the desired certificate. This is the known circuit simulation and applies also to the general odd-field upper bound. [Forbes, Shpilka, Tzameret and Wigderson](https://toc.cs.uchicago.edu/articles/v017a010/v017a010.pdf)

For nonzero rational $d_i=a_i/b_i$ in lowest terms, put $M=4\prod_i |a_i b_i|$ and let $r$ be their rank in the rational squareclass group. At all odd primes $p\nmid M$ record the Legendre signs of the $d_i$. Exactly $2^r$ sign vectors occur. They are all sign prescriptions compatible with the multiplicative squareclass relations. Each has relative natural density $2^{-r}$ among primes and each has a representative $p\ll 4^r\log^2(2M)$ under Dirichlet GRH.

Indeed the multiquadratic group is dual to the span of the squareclasses and every compatible evaluation occurs, by elementary Kummer theory. The corresponding map on units modulo $M$ is onto, by the independence of the associated quadratic Dirichlet characters and character duality. Conductors divide $M$, including the conductor at 2. Each fiber is a coset of index $2^r$, so the prime number theorem in progressions gives the density and the coset bound gives the least prime. For $r=0$ choose any good prime; for instance the least prime outside the divisors of $M$ is $O(\log(2M))$ by the primorial bound.

For the rational matrices $I_n,\operatorname{diag}(d_i,1,\ldots,1)$ these cosets prescribe **which reductions have solutions and which require $n^{\Omega(n)}$ sparse Polynomial Calculus size** with the prime-field equations. A positive sign gives a one-entry diagonal square-root witness. For a negative sign the [congruence separation](PROOF.md) applies with constants uniform in the odd prime, along the expander dimensions. Every negative also has a circuit IPS certificate of size polynomial in $n,\log p$. For independent $d_i$ every subset is realized simultaneously at one prime. The smaller list that spans the signatures in the congruence paper suffices for testing dependencies; realizing a prescribed signature is a different query with the explicit index term above. [Dirichlet GRH verification](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)
