# Matrix proof complexity and joint prime tests for tensor genera

**The top-ten nomination is odd finite-field matrix congruence.** For every fixed odd prime power $q$, including singular and nonsymmetric matrices, the worst-case ordinary Polynomial Calculus degree is $\Theta_q(n)$ and the worst-case expanded sparse size is $n^{\Theta_q(n)}$ when the coordinate field equations are included. Every negative instance has a circuit Ideal Proof System certificate of size polynomial in $n$ and $\log q$ in the field-operation model. The simple diagonal pair $I_n,\operatorname{diag}(\nu,1,\ldots,1)$, for any nonsquare $\nu$, gives the lower bound. The finite-field result follows independently of GRH. Dirichlet GRH bounds a prime at which a prescribed combination of such hard and solvable reductions occurs. [Lower bound](PROOF.md) · [All-matrix upper bound](ALL-ODD.md) · [Joint prime bounds](PrimePanels.md)

**The closest result on Grochow's literal equations is for conjugacy.** Over a field of positive characteristic $p$, for each mismatched exact Jordan type let $a,b$ be its multiplicities after passing to a splitting field. Minimize $\min(1+\min(a,b),p^{v_p(|a-b|)})$ over the mismatches, and call the answer $h$. Every nonconjugate instance has minimum PC degree $\Theta(h)$ with absolute constants. The paper also gives an exact comparison to the independent rectangular-inverse degrees, a linear-algebra test for degree two, and bounds on sparse size in displayed Jordan coordinates. The degree theorem holds in the input coordinates. The literal two-part question asks for size and degree for both conjugacy and congruence. Its displayed congruence equations permit algebraic-closure solutions for the hard diagonal pair. The complete question remains open. [Instance-wise degree proof](JordanProofComplexity.md) · [Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She, Question 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

**Tensor genus and joint primes.** Over each number field, let $M_F(n)$ be the maximum number of global classes of abelian-split, geometrically diagonal side-$n$ tensors that agree at every completion. Then $\log M_F(n)=(n/4)\log n+O(n)$, with an absolute implied constant. The examples have varying biquadratic factors. Over the rationals a common list of fewer than $4n$ primes recovers every member of the constructed family from pairwise counts of simultaneous splitting in the same factor. Dirichlet GRH bounds each prime by $O(n^2\log^4(2n))$. Deciding whether a product of quadratic fields has a member of a prescribed biquadratic and rational factorization in its **all-place** genus is NP-complete, even when every supplied radicand is a product of two primes. These are quantitative forms results related to Grochow–Qiao's field-change question. ## Proof details and verification

When every field factor has degree $p^k$ and the common splitting group is abelian, the first side at which all tests on at most $k-1$ good primes can agree on different algebras is exactly $p^k\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(1+p^i)$. Thus for biquadratic factors the first one-prime collision is at side 12; for elementary degree-eight factors the first two-prime collision is at side 120. Extremizers exist over every number field. Minimum volumes and the polar-space construction for finite subspace trades have prior proofs. The extension here identifies the arithmetic statistic, handles arbitrary abelian splitting groups in the bound, and realizes the examples with cyclic decomposition groups at every place. [Equal-degree threshold](JOINT-UNIFORM-DEGREE.md) · [Krotov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.02592) · [Krotov, Mogilnykh and Potapov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.3792)

The congruence lower bound maps a bounded-degree expander parity contradiction into the entries of a sparse invertible matrix by local two-coordinate isometries. A random common permutation of its columns and the inverse matrix's rows then forces a high-support monomial of any putative proof to survive an event of probability $n^{-\Omega(n)}$. For the all-ones charge convention in the cited theorem, take a connected odd-order expander. The difference from a single designated charge has even parity and is the boundary of a subset of edges, by leaf elimination on a spanning tree. Flipping those edge signs transports the equations in both directions with no change in degree. The proof of the source theorem uses the edge boundary estimate in Proposition 11. Bounded-degree expanders with uniform positive edge expansion exist at each sufficiently large admissible order, for example for fixed even degree. For an even target dimension, a proof specializes to the preceding odd dimension on setting $X,Y$ to block diagonals with a final identity entry. The specialization preserves degree and cannot increase monomial counts. Hence the asymptotic worst cases hold in every sufficiently large dimension. [Buss, Grigoriev, Impagliazzo and Pitassi, Theorem 10](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/lineargaps/journalfinal.pdf) · [Random regular graph expansion](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.6555)

The positive-characteristic numerical conjugacy lower bound uses Riis's rectangular matching lemma. The proof of the genus lower bound uses the Eberhard–Manners–Mrazović count for complete mappings.
