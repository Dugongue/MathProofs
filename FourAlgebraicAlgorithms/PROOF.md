# Four algorithms and reductions around Grochow's questions

These are informal mathematical arguments. The four conclusions concern distinct problems. The cited papers identify their context. No claim of publication priority is made.

## 1. Exact epimorphism counting for bounded-generator metabelian targets

Fix \(d\). Given Cayley tables of any finite group \(G\) and any metabelian group \(H\) generated by at most \(d\) elements, one can count all epimorphisms \(G\twoheadrightarrow H\) and return one if the count is positive, in deterministic polynomial bit time. Neither a splitting of \(H\) nor semisimplicity of any module is required. This answers the metabelian-target case of Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar's Questions 2 and 3(2). [Original questions, §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)

Choose a generating tuple \(h_1,\ldots,h_d\) by enumerating tuples in the target, padding with identities if necessary. Set \(A=[H,H]\) and \(Q=H/A\). Both are abelian. The number of possible maps \(G\to Q\) is at most \(|G|^d\). Indeed \(Q\) is a product of at most \(d\) cyclic groups and, for finite abelian \(B\), \(|\mathrm{Hom}(B,C_m)|\le |B|\). In a cyclic decomposition of \(B=G_{\rm ab}\), a factor of order \(k\) gives precisely \(\gcd(k,m)\) choices. This argument lists all the maps within the same bound times polynomial overhead. Discard the nonsurjections. All abelian decompositions needed here can be obtained by integer Smith normal form from the table presentation with one generator per element and one additive relation per product.

For each remaining \(\psi:G\twoheadrightarrow Q\), choose a section \(s:Q\to H\) with \(s(1)=1\). Write \(A\) additively. The action of \(q\) on \(A\) is conjugation by \(s(q)\), independent of the section value within its coset. Put \(c(q,r)=s(q)s(r)s(qr)^{-1}\). All lifts of \(\psi\) are exactly the maps \(f(g)=a_gs(\psi(g))\) satisfying

\[
a_{gu}=a_g+\psi(g)a_u+c(\psi(g),\psi(u))\qquad(g,u\in G).
\]

Enumerate \(x_1,\ldots,x_d\in G\) with \(\psi(x_i)=h_i A\), adding the equations \(a_{x_i}=h_i s(\psi(x_i))^{-1}\). Every solution is onto because its image contains the generating tuple. Count the solutions to each resulting affine system. Explicitly, the left sides define a homomorphism \(M:A^{|G|}\to B'\), with \(B'\) a product of copies of \(A\). Lattice membership modulo the cyclic moduli of \(B'\) tests whether the offset belongs to the image. Smith form gives \(|\operatorname{im} M|\) from the index of the lattice generated by the columns and the output moduli. A nonempty fiber has size \(|A|^{|G|}/|\operatorname{im} M|\), and an integer preimage gives one solution. The matrices, entry bit lengths and output count bit lengths are polynomial in the table sizes.

If \(|H|\nmid|G|\), return zero first. Otherwise, every epimorphism \(f\) occurs at its unique \(\psi\), for exactly \((|G|/|H|)^d\) tuples of preimages of the \(h_i\). Repeated target entries do not affect that product. Divide the sum of the affine counts by this integer. Any nonempty system also supplies the requested epimorphism. There are at most \(|G|^{2d}\) systems. The proof treats all source groups and all derived-subgroup ranks. It leaves general nonmetabelian bounded-generator targets open.

## 2. Tensor isomorphism survives hyperbolic doubling with a fixed simple Levi factor

Let \(F\) be a field of characteristic different from two. There is a polynomial-size reduction from ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over \(F\) to conjugacy of explicitly given Lie subalgebras of a split symplectic Lie algebra by a symplectic matrix. The very same matrix lists work for conjugacy by a split special orthogonal matrix. The images are perfect, centerless, have square-zero associative radical products, and have Levi factor \(\mathfrak{sl}_2(F)\). On the constructed pairs even allowing every invertible conjugator gives exactly the same yes-instances. This strengthens the repository's fixed-Levi reduction in the setting of Grochow's ambient matrix Lie algebra conjugacy question. The ordinary abelian-radical hardness already follows from Grochow–Qiao. [Grochow, Question VII.1](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~billey/classes/lie/bulletins/grochow.2012.pdf#page=10) · [Grochow–Qiao, Part I](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full)

Represent a tensor by its matrix slice space \(M\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(V,U)\), comparing pairs of equal original mode dimensions. Left-right equivalence of slice spaces is equivalent to tensor isomorphism because the maps from the dual third mode onto the slice spaces have equally dimensional kernels whenever the slice ranks agree. An image equivalence lifts by choosing complements to the kernels. Restrict \(U\) to the sum of the images and quotient \(V\) by the common kernel. These dimensions are invariants and equivalences of the resulting spaces extend by independent basis completions. Handle zero tensors, rank or dimension mismatches by fixed yes- or no-pairs. For a fixed concise no-pair of equal dimensions one may use the two-dimensional matrix spaces in dimension two spanned respectively by \(\{I,E_{12}\}\) and \(\{E_{11},E_{22}\}\). They have respectively one and two lines of singular matrices.

Let \(W=F^2,\ P=W\otimes U,\ E=P\oplus V\), and use the Lie algebra

\[
L(M)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}s\otimes I_U&r\\0&0\end{pmatrix}:
s\in\mathfrak{sl}(W),\quad r\in W\otimes M\right\}.
\]

Its upper-right ideal \(R\) has zero products. Its quotient is simple and nonabelian, so \(R\) is its solvable radical. The standard module has no invariants and \(\mathfrak{sl}(W)W=W\); the algebra is centerless and perfect. Now act on \(X=E\oplus E^*\) by \(a\mapsto \operatorname{diag}(a,-a^{\mathsf T})\), and write \(L,R\) also for their faithful images. These matrices are skew-adjoint for both standard hyperbolic forms, with block matrices having off-diagonal blocks \(I,I\) and \(I,-I\), respectively. If \(a\mapsto gag^{-1}\) transports the original algebras, \(\operatorname{diag}(g,g^{-\mathsf T})\) transports their doubles, preserves both forms and has determinant one. In particular a left-right equivalence \(M\to M'\) gives both requested conjugators.

The converse must exclude a change of basis that interchanges the information in \(E\) and \(E^*\). It suffices to recover the original rectangular matrix space from the doubled Lie action. Write \(R X\) and \(L X\) for the spans of all images. Conciseness gives

\[
 R X=P\oplus V^*,\qquad (R X)^L=V^*,\qquad
 L X=P\oplus P^*\oplus V^*.
\]

Indeed, radical elements take \(V\) into \(P\) with spanning images and take \(P^*\) into \(V^*\) with spanning images, the latter by the common-kernel condition. The Levi matrices have full image span on \(P,P^*\), have zero fixed space on \(P\), and kill \(V^*\). Finally \(R(L X)\subseteq V^*\). Consequently each \(r\in R\) gives an intrinsic map

\[
 X/L X\longrightarrow R X/(R X)^L .
\]

These spaces identify with \(V,P\) respectively, and the recovered space of maps is exactly \(W\otimes M\). On the recovered output space the induced Lie algebra of operators is exactly \(\mathfrak{sl}(W)\otimes I_U\).

Any invertible conjugator therefore induces \(B:V\to V'\) and \(D:P\to P'\). The latter intertwines the standard Levi action up to a single Lie automorphism. In bases of \(U,U'\), every block of \(D\) intertwines the standard module with the same twist. A nonzero block \(C\) is invertible by irreducibility. Multiplying every block by \(C^{-1}\) on the left gives scalar matrices, since the off-diagonal matrix units generate \(\operatorname{End}(W)\) as an associative algebra. Thus \(D=C\otimes A\) with \(A\) invertible. It follows that \(W\otimes (AMB^{-1})=W\otimes M'\), hence \(AMB^{-1}=M'\). This proves reflection even for unrestricted conjugators. The matrix construction and basis operations have polynomial exact arithmetic cost, and polynomial bit cost over fixed finite fields and the rationals with the usual matrix encodings. This theorem concerns split forms; the real positive-definite and compact unitary reductions in TI Question 1.8 remain separate.

## 3. A deterministic translator for function-field IPS proofs in characteristic zero

Fix a finitely generated field \(K/\mathbb Q\), given by a fixed finite multiplication basis over \(\mathbb Q(t_1,\ldots,t_d)\) beginning with 1. Basis coordinates of scalar inputs are sparse rational functions with binary exponents. Consider circuit IPS allowing inverses of legal nonzero syntactically constant circuits. There is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time translator taking any valid \(K\)-IPS proof for equations over \(\mathbb Q\) to a \(\mathbb Q\)-IPS proof of the same rational identity in this circuit model. Circuit size counts a shared subcircuit once. Large constants produced by arithmetic gates are charged by their circuit encoding. This improves the earlier existential specialization in this model to an explicit translation. [Grochow, §5 and §7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.02184v1#S7.SS2)

Expand each gate in basis coordinates using the fixed multiplication table. For a constant inverse, take the inverse of the matrix of multiplication by its operand on the extension field and apply it to the coordinate vector of 1. Adjugate over determinant suffices since the degree is fixed. Keep integer polynomial numerator-denominator pairs over the base, removing rational input coefficients by pairing their integer numerators and denominators. Binary powers use repeated squaring. Addition and multiplication on pairs preserve circuit sharing. Denominators depend only on \(t\) because all inversions are syntactically constant. In handling a constant inverse, any numerator used as a new denominator is nonzero by validity. All denominator polynomials that must survive thus occur as outputs of division-free integer circuits of polynomial total size.

Here is an explicit point avoiding all of them simultaneously, without testing a polynomial identity. Let \(m\ge2\) bound the number of gates of the resulting integer circuits, and \(b\ge1\) bound the bit length of their integer leaves. Every gate has total degree at most \(2^m\). For the coefficient \(\ell_1\)-norm, start with the upper bound \(2^{b+1}\). Each gate increases the current common bound \(B\ge 2\) to at most \(B^2\), by submultiplicativity and \(2B\le B^2\). Thus the norm of every denominator is at most \(2^{(b+1)2^m}\). Set

\[
 D=2^m+1,\qquad H=2^{(b+2)2^m},\qquad t_i=H^{D^{i-1}}\quad(1\le i\le d).
\]

The base-\(D\) exponent substitution first sends distinct monomials to distinct powers of one variable. In a resulting nonzero polynomial of degree \(j>0\), the absolute leading contribution at \(H\) is at least \(H^j\). All lower contributions together have absolute value at most \(\|p\|_1 H^{j-1}<H^j\). Nonzero constants survive as well. This proof covers every denominator regardless of cancellations at intermediate gates.

The translator emits circuits for these integers. The exponent \((b+2)2^m\) has \(O(m+\log(b+2))\) bits, and \(D^{i-1}\) has \(O(1+i m)\) bits. Repeated squaring therefore emits all constants in polynomial time, without expanding their values. Substitute the point in the output coordinate at 1, dividing its numerator by its circuit denominator via a legal constant inverse. The two IPS identities survive specialization in the localization inverting the finite list of denominators. Taking the unit coordinate commutes with substitution of equations over \(\mathbb Q\). In particular \(C(x,0)=0\) and \(C(x,P(x))=1\) become the required rational identities. The same reasoning applies to rational target polynomials in place of 1.

The translator assumes its input is a valid proof, as a proof translation should. It supplies neither an efficient validity test nor a polynomial-bit expanded fractional value at every constant gate. The open rational Boolean IPS/PIT equivalence is a different assertion.

## 4. Exact coarse-graining to a finite target with a quasigroup term

Let \(S\) and \(B\) be finite nonempty algebras of the same fixed finite signature and bounded arity, given by their operation tables. Suppose that, on \(B\), a binary term using named constants of \(B\) defines a quasigroup multiplication. All surjective homomorphisms \(S\twoheadrightarrow B\) can be counted, and one returned if it exists, in deterministic time polynomial in \(|S|\) for fixed \(B\). The proof works even when no term given by the same expression on the raw source is cancellative. This extends the earlier binary and semilattice-target coarse-graining results in a different direction. [Song–Grochow, fixed-block question](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12153) · [Barto–DeMeo–Mottet, homomorphisms into finite algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04958)

Write \(m=|S|,\ b=|B|\). If \(m<b\), the answer is zero. One may find a term by closing the two projections and the \(b\) constant binary tables under pointwise basic operations on \(B\). Store one straight-line program per distinct table, stopping when all rows and columns of a binary table are permutations. The finite closure is exactly the set of binary term operations with constants, by induction on terms. With fixed maximum arity there are at most \(b^{b^2}\) tables and the time is \(b^{O(b^2)}\) for \(b\ge2\). The singleton case is immediate.

For each ordered choice of preimages for the named constants used by that term, interpret the same program on \(S\), obtaining a binary operation \(*\). Force a quotient by the following finite process. If \(a*x=a*y\) in the current quotient, identify \(x,y\); if \(x*a=y*a\), likewise identify \(x,y\). Take the congruence closure under every original operation after each identification. The term, with its pinned constants, descends through each quotient. Every homomorphism compatible with the pins factors through every identification, by left and right cancellation in \(B\). Every strict step decreases the cardinality. When the process terminates, all left and right translations are injective on the finite quotient \(S_0\), hence bijective. Taking congruence closure by table scanning and repeating costs polynomial time in \(m\) for fixed signature, apart from the stated term construction cost.

Find multiplication generators of \((S_0,*)\) greedily. Every proper nonempty product-closed subset \(Y\) is a subquasigroup, since its translations restricted to \(Y\) are injections of \(Y\) into itself. If \(u\notin Y\), the sets \(Y\) and \(u*Y\) are disjoint. Otherwise \(u*y\in Y\) for some \(y\in Y\), whose right translation already maps \(Y\) onto \(Y\), contradicting injectivity. Adjoining \(u\) thus at least doubles the generated set. Starting with one element gives at most \(1+\lfloor\log_2 m\rfloor\) generators. No divisions are needed to recover a full multiplication table homomorphism from its values on these generators.

Try the \(b^{1+\lfloor\log_2 m\rfloor}\) possible generator image assignments, propagating products on the quotient and checking consistency. Verify each resulting map against every original operation, against the pin assignments, and for surjectivity. To count each map once, retain it only when its chosen lift of each named constant was the least original preimage in a fixed ordering of \(S\). Each surjection has precisely one such pin assignment and determines all its generator images uniquely. These arguments bound total time by \(b^{O(b^2)}m^{b+O(1)}b^{1+\lfloor\log_2 m\rfloor}\), with constants for the fixed signature. The count has polynomially many bits in the explicit table size.

For the ternary block operation of a one-dimensional radius-one cellular automaton with source alphabet size \(a\) and block size \(N\), \(m=a^N\). Constructing a table entry uses the local rule for \(N\) time steps on three adjacent blocks. Thus every fixed target with the stated term has deterministic \(2^{O(N\log a)}\) time for exact counting and witness construction (plus polynomial input-reading cost and the elementary simulation overhead, with \(a\ge2\)). Variable explicitly tabulated targets of this type still give an EXP upper bound for unary \(N\). A finite group multiplication already gives an example outside the earlier semilattice mechanism: on any nontrivial finite abelian group every binary group term with constants is affine, and a commutative idempotent associative affine binary operation would require its common linear part \(A\) to satisfy \(A^2=A\) and \(2A=I\), which is impossible. One ternary cellular rule realizing this example is \(g(x,y,z)=x+y\). Arbitrary target algebras remain outside the conclusion.
