# Linear polynomial-calculus degree for matrix congruence in positive characteristic

Fix a prime \(p\) and an algebraically closed field \(K\) of characteristic \(p\). For symmetric \(N\times N\) matrices the worst-case ordinary polynomial-calculus degree of refuting
\[
 XMX^\top=M',\qquad XY=YX=I
\]
is \(\Theta_p(N)\), with no finite-field equations. In odd characteristic the same sharp order holds even when the matrices and their lists are unrestricted, for one simultaneous congruence. This answers the positive-characteristic symmetric-congruence branch of Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She Open Question 7.3, and the odd-characteristic version for arbitrary matrices. The upper bound here does not cover arbitrary nonsymmetric matrices in characteristic two. [Open Question 7.3 and its equations](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

## Lower bound for every fixed positive characteristic

Use the rectangular Boolean bijection system on \(b\) rows and \(a\) columns. Its axioms prescribe a single selected entry in each row and column by the corresponding linear totals, exclude collisions separately in each row and column by quadratic products, and impose \(u_{ij}^2=u_{ij}\). Fix \(q=p^j\), take \(a=2q\), \(b=3q\), and put \(U=(u_{ij})\). Riis's rectangle lemma gives ordinary-PC degree at least \(q\) for this system. Its hypotheses hold with smaller side \(2q\), degree \(q-1\), and side difference divisible by \(q\). [Riis, Lemma 14.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07280#S14)

Let \(M=\operatorname{diag}(I_a,0_b)\) and \(M'=\operatorname{diag}(I_b,0_a)\). Partition the columns of \(X\) into lengths \(a,b\) and its rows into lengths \(b,a\). Substitute \(X=\operatorname{diag}(U,U^\top)\) under these partitions, and \(Y=\operatorname{diag}(U^\top,U)\) with the opposite row and column partitions. Then the nonzero substituted congruence equations are the entries of \(UU^\top-I_b\). The inverse equations specialize to entries of that same matrix and \(U^\top U-I_a\). Every off-diagonal entry is a sum of collision axioms. Every diagonal entry is a linear combination of a totality axiom and Boolean axioms. The substitutions preserve proof degree. Extra identical zero blocks are handled by using the identity on that coordinate block in \(X,Y\). Thus dimension \(5q\) and every larger dimension admit examples requiring degree at least \(q\). Choosing the largest \(q=p^j\) with \(5q\le N\) gives a lower bound \(N/(5p)\) for sufficiently large \(N\).

## Symmetric upper bound in every characteristic

In a forward congruence equation, a constant change of variables on each ambient side permits chosen bases for each form. It preserves proof degree and inverse equations, with the inverse variable changed accordingly.

If \(\operatorname{rank} M=a<\operatorname{rank}M'=b\), factor \(M=L R\) with \(L\) of column width \(a\) and \(R\) of row height \(a\). Choose a nonsingular \(b\times b\) minor of \(M'\). The corresponding rows and columns of \(XL R X^\top\), followed by multiplication by the constant inverse of that minor, give a rectangular rank-principle system \(UV=I_b\) with inner side \(a\), where \(U,V\) are linear in \(X\). This system has an elementary \(O(a+1)\)-degree refutation. Starting with the identically zero \((a+1)\)-minors of \(U\), expand a determinant whose last column is a column of \(UV\) as a sum of the known minors times entries of \(V\). Substitute the corresponding identity column using the quadratic equations. A suitable last column yields any requested minor one size smaller. Descend to the empty minor. The largest degree used is \(a+2\). [Detailed descending-minor derivation](complexity.md)

If \(\operatorname{rank} M>\operatorname{rank}M'\), first derive \(YM'Y^\top=M\) in degree at most four. Multiply \(XMX^\top-M'\) on both sides by \(Y,Y^\top\) and replace \(YX\) by \(I\), replacing one quadratic entry at a time. Then apply the foregoing rank argument to the reverse equation.

In odd characteristic over \(K\), symmetric forms of equal rank are congruent by diagonalization and square roots. In characteristic two, after removing the radical, the alternating forms have one class in each admissible dimension and nonalternating symmetric forms have one class in each dimension. One way to see the latter is to split off the line of a vector of self-pairing one. If the remaining nonzero space becomes alternating, a unit line together with an alternating hyperbolic plane has an orthonormal basis. In coordinates \(u,e,f\) of this summand with \(b(u,u)=b(e,f)=1\), the independent vectors \(u+e,u+f,u+e+f\) are an orthonormal basis. This allows the rest of the alternating planes to be absorbed too. Thus in characteristic two the only remaining obstruction at equal rank is different alternation type. An alternating source makes all diagonal action polynomials identically zero, which immediately contradicts a nonalternating target. Use the degree-four reverse equation when their order is reversed. This proves the asserted linear upper bound for symmetric matrices.

## Arbitrary congruence in odd characteristic

For each pair of matrices \(M_i,M_i'\), form block matrices of order \(2N\) with sole nonzero block \(M_i\) and \(M_i^\top\), respectively, in the upper-right position, and include the common diagonal block projector \(\operatorname{diag}(I,0)\) in the tuples. Simultaneous similarity of these two block tuples is the same as strict equivalence of the paired lists \((M_i,M_i^\top)_i\). It is equivalent to congruence in the stipulated field. In fact a block intertwiner gives \(P M_i Q^{-1}=M_i'\) and \(P M_i^\top Q^{-1}=(M_i')^\top\). Putting \(C=Q^\top P\) gives \(CM_i=M_i C^\top\) for all \(i\). There is a square root \(S\) polynomial in \(C\): choose roots of its nonzero eigenvalues, lift through the primary powers in its minimal polynomial by dividing by twice a nonzero root, and use the Chinese remainder theorem. Then \(SM_i=M_iS^\top\), and \(Q^{-\top}S\) gives simultaneous congruence. The converse follows directly from the same block construction.

The polynomial-calculus transfer has degree at most three. Right multiplication by \(Y^\top\) in the congruence equations and their transposes gives \(XM_i=M_i'Y^\top\) and \(XM_i^\top=(M_i')^\top Y^\top\) using the inverse axioms. These are the simultaneous intertwiner equations after substituting \(\operatorname{diag}(X,Y^\top)\) and its formal inverse \(\operatorname{diag}(Y,X^\top)\). The inverse identities for these blocks follow from the original inverse equations.

For completeness, the \(O(N)\) simultaneous-conjugacy upper bound uses opposite rectangular corners of the semisimple quotient of the endomorphism algebra of the two combined tuple modules. The images of the modules in each isotypic multiplicity quotient have dimensions equal to the corresponding indecomposable multiplicities, by Krull–Schmidt. The coefficient maps on intertwiners are linear and respect composition and identities. Derive the reverse intertwiner and project the formal variables onto the linear intertwiner spaces in degree three. Apply the quotient maps to the formal inverse products. In one quotient the multiplicities differ, otherwise the original modules would be isomorphic. The resulting two rectangular inverse identities give a refutation in degree \(O(N)\), by the descending-minor argument. Linear substitution through the block tuples gives the claimed congruence bound. These are paper proofs.
