# Multiplicity separation for self-complementary symmetry-characterized graphs

Let \(G,G'\) be nonisomorphic, self-complementary, symmetry-characterized simple graphs on the same number of vertices. Their full automorphism groups have different cycle indices. Thus the multiplicity obstructions in Grochow–Urisman's sense distinguish the entire self-complementary portion of GP070. [Grochow–Urisman, Theorem 1.5 and Open Question 1.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26244v2#S1.SS3)

A nontrivial self-complementary graph with \(N\) unordered pairs has \(N/2\) edges. By the [orbital criterion](invariants.md), a symmetry-characterized graph with three unordered pair orbits has one or two thirds of all pairs as edges. Hence the self-complementary graph has exactly two orbits, consisting of its edges and nonedges. An intransitive symmetry-characterized graph with two unordered orbitals is a star or its complement. Indeed two vertex orbits of size at least two already give three different pair types, as do three or more vertex orbits. If one vertex is fixed and the remaining vertices form an orbit, the two pair types are the edges of the star and its complement. Neither of the resulting nontrivial graphs is self-complementary: when both have the same number of edges, the star is connected and its complement is disconnected. Thus the full automorphism group \(H=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) is transitive on vertices.

Take a complementing permutation \(\tau\), so \(\tau\) normalizes \(H\) and \(\tau^2\in H\). If \(|H|\) were odd, then \(\tau^2\) would have odd order \(b\), and \(\tau^b\) would be a complementing involution. A nonidentity involution fixes its swapped unordered pair setwise, which is impossible for a complementing permutation. Thus \(H\) has even order. An involution of \(H\) reverses some pair. Conjugating it by \(\tau\) gives an involution reversing a pair of the other type. Since \(H\) is transitive on each unordered pair type, it follows that it is transitive on the corresponding ordered pairs as well. In particular \(G\) is arc-transitive. Conversely every nontrivial self-complementary arc-transitive graph is symmetry-characterized: a complementing permutation normalizes its automorphism group, so the automorphism group is transitive on nonedges as well as edges.

Peisert's classification gives the Paley graphs, ordinary Peisert graphs, and one sporadic Peisert graph of order \(529=23^2\). For \(q=p^e\), the Paley group has order \(e q(q-1)/2\), while the ordinary Peisert group has order \(e q(q-1)/4\) except for \(q=9,49,81\), where the latter expression is multiplied by \(2,3,6\), respectively. The ordinary Peisert graph at \(q=9\) is isomorphic to the Paley graph. The only order tie between different graph types is the ordinary and sporadic Peisert pair at \(q=529\). [Peisert's classification](https://doi.org/10.1006/JABR.2000.8714) · [Brouwer–Van Maldeghem, §§7.3.6, 7.4.4, 10.70](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/math/srg/rk3/srgw.pdf)

For the tied pair, scalar multiplication by a fourth power of a generator of \(\mathbb F_{529}^{\times}\) is an automorphism of the ordinary Peisert graph, of order \(132\). The sporadic graph has full affine automorphism group with translation kernel of order \(23^2\) and point stabilizer \(C_{11}\times\mathrm{SL}_2(3)\). The group \(\mathrm{SL}_2(3)\) has no element of order \(12\). A split characteristic polynomial permits only eigenvalues in \(\{1,-1\}\), and any nontrivial Jordan part has order three. A nonsplit characteristic polynomial has roots of norm one from \(\mathbb F_9\), so their order divides four. The split case therefore has orders at most six, and the nonsplit case has orders dividing four. Consequently the sporadic stabilizer has no element of order \(132\). Since \(132\) is prime to \(23\), an element of that order in the full affine group would project to an element of the same order in its point stabilizer quotient. Thus the sporadic automorphism group has no element of order \(132\), and the two cycle indices differ. The result is a paper proof. [Brouwer–Van Maldeghem, §10.70 and the rank-three tables](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/math/srg/rk3/srgw.pdf)
