# Graph isomorphism, group isomorphism, and the determinant TI chain

Use the fixed field F₃ for the tensor chain. O, A, B, and S denote ordinary, one-sign, two-sign and SL tensor isomorphism. All unqualified reductions here are deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions. Abstract isomorphism of groups means an isomorphism of the groups themselves. For permutation-generator inputs it need not arise by conjugating their displayed permutation actions.

**Ordinary TI is many-one equivalent to abstract isomorphism of class-two 3-groups of exponent dividing nine given by permutation generators.** The hard instances have orbit size at most 81. For the general reverse direction, recover the Lazard Lie ring and compile its additive group and bracket over F₃ by using bounded-degree augmentation data. The signed correspondence below is on an explicit recognizable subfamily. [General ordinary reverse](BoundedExponent.md)

## The map across the whole chain

1. **Ordinary GI and full-table GpI lie below every rung.** Full Cayley-table group isomorphism reduces to colored graph isomorphism. Use element vertices and a record for each true multiplication triple, with three different slot colors. Any color-preserving graph isomorphism preserves precisely the multiplication table and the input element colors. Vertex colors can be encoded in ordinary GI. Ordinary GI reduces to O by the published graph-to-tensor reduction. Both reductions compose with every forward arrow along the chain. The same multiplication-record construction preserves the sign on any marked union of input colors, giving the marked Cayley-to-marked-graph arrow. [Grochow and Qiao, Tensor Isomorphism-Completeness, Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)
2. **The bottom through the marked ternary-code rung has an ordinary permutation-group upper bound.** The individual marked group, graph, graphic-matroid and ternary-code problems reduce to ordinary abstract isomorphism of groups generated by permutations of bounded orbit size. The earlier graphic-matroid reduction preserves every marked real-edge action. For the lower rungs that also require an independent ordinary tensor comparison, use the direct product construction below. [Permutation and code bridge](CodeGroupBridge.md) · [Marked graphic matroids](ReverseReductions.md)
3. **The whole chain has exact group counterparts with determinant tests.** Replace O by plain abstract isomorphism in the recovered family of exponent-nine 3-groups. Replace each tensor sign by the determinant on the corresponding characteristic group section. Fixed relative comparisons, independent conjunctions and self-comparisons used for stabilizer repairs are preserved, with the whole attainable sign set on each comparison. For a lower rung with a subspace, graph, code, or form input, retain that companion structured problem and replace its O component. Alternatively its known upper reduction to A gives one signed group comparison. The abstract group algorithms use only permutation generators, with the orientation frames supplied where a sign is read. [Characteristic group sections and exact tensor reconstruction](PermutationGroups.md)
4. **There are two genuine unsigned reductions on structured group and graph inputs.** Graph parity systems with logarithmic active color classes reduce to one ordinary GI instance, with exact witness restrictions. The Cayley-table reduction erases parity on a coset of a 3-subgroup, on selected Frattini layers, and on polynomially listed Grassmannians of elementary abelian 3-sections. It permits arbitrary external colors in the input group table. [Graph proof](GraphParity.md) · [Cayley-table proof](CayleyTables.md)

In (3) the result does not identify plain general GpI, plain Cayley-table GpI, or permutation conjugacy with the signed classes. In particular the faithful actions of the constructed 3-groups can have exponentially many elements while having polynomial permutation degree. A Cayley-table expansion or a conjugacy test on those actions would impose a different reduction contract. Neither the unrestricted signed-tier collapse nor the assertion that arbitrary Cayley-table 3-group markings reduce to O is needed for the proved arrows.

## Preserve the independent ordinary TI condition in the lower rungs

Let P,P′ be the bounded-orbit 3-groups encoding an ordinary tensor comparison, and let H,H′ be the groups from the marked-code construction, contained in Aut(S)^m with S^m as commutator subgroup for S=PSL₂(64). Form permutation generators of P×H and P′×H′ acting on disjoint unions of the two original sets. This adds their degrees and keeps all orbits bounded.

The solvable radical of H is trivial. A solvable normal subgroup N meets the product S^m trivially: since it is normal there, a nontrivial intersection would contain a nonabelian simple factor. Then [N,S^m] is trivial because the commutator belongs to their intersection. The centralizer of S^m in Aut(S)^m is trivial because each S is centerless and the conjugation action identifies it with the inner automorphisms. This forces N=1. Thus the solvable radical of P×H is exactly P, since P is solvable and every solvable normal subgroup has trivial image in H. Abstract isomorphism of the product identifies the two radicals and the two corresponding quotients. Conversely independent isomorphisms of the factors give an isomorphism of the product. This proves ordinary abstract group isomorphism on generators is an upper bound for the entire rungs O∧marked Cayley GpI, O∧marked GI, and O∧marked ternary code equivalence, with the graphic-matroid branch included. These are single output group comparisons. The graph/code analysis here gives no reduction of the higher subspace/monomial or signed rungs to unsigned abstract permutation-group isomorphism.
