# Tensor isomorphism inside class-two permutation groups

Work over F₃. Use explicit dense tensors as bilinear maps and use permutation generators for groups, with permutation degree as part of the written input. The construction prints faithful permutations with orbit sizes at most 81. There is a deterministically polynomial-time recognizable family of class-two groups of exponent at most nine such that their ordinary abstract isomorphism problem is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism. For each group there are three ordered characteristic sections. With bases supplied for these sections, the correspondence preserves the **entire** three-bit determinant profile, including emptiness. Thus all tensor decision problems expressed on this profile have group counterparts of exactly the same deterministic polynomial-time many-one degree. This includes the one-sign, two-sign and SL endpoints and the relative stabilizer-correction comparisons.

## 1. Encode the kernel in the center

Let $\mu:X\times Y\to W$, with $\dim X=a,\dim Y=b$ both positive. Put $A=X\oplus Y$, $E=X\otimes Y$, $K=\ker(\widetilde\mu:E\to W)$, and $I=\operatorname{im}\widetilde\mu$. Let $Q$ be any chosen complement to $I$ in $W$. Use bases from Gaussian elimination for $K$ and a complement $J$ of $K$ in $E$; identify $J$ with $I$. Take the abelian group
$$
 C=(\mathbb Z/9)^{\dim K}\oplus J\oplus C_Y\oplus Q,
$$
where the short summands are elementary abelian and $C_Y$ is a copy of $Y$. Embed $E$ as $3(\mathbb Z/9)^{\dim K}\oplus J$. In this embedding $3C=K$. Use the alternating pairing
$$
 b((x,y),(x',y'))=x\otimes y'-x'\otimes y\in E\subset C.
$$
For each coordinate $y_j$, choose representatives $0,1,2$ and let $\operatorname{car}(y,y')$ have $j$-th $C_Y$-coordinate $\lfloor(y_j+y'_j)/3\rfloor$. The group is the set $A\times C$ with product
$$
 (v,c)(v',c')=(v+v',c+c'+\tfrac12 b(v,v')+\operatorname{car}(y,y')).
$$
The bilinear term is a central cocycle and the carry identity follows by adding three integer representatives in either order. Their antisymmetric difference is $b$. Since $a,b>0$, its radical is zero by testing against $X$ and $Y$ separately, so $C=Z(G)$. Simple tensors span $E=[G,G]$. The cube of the chosen lift of $(x,y)$ is $y$ in $C_Y$: the bilinear term vanishes on multiples of one vector and the three additions have total carry $y$. Central lifts change the cube by $3C=K$. This also shows exponent at most nine.

Write $q:A\to C/3C$ for the cube modulo $3C$. It is a homomorphism because the commutators have order dividing three. The three characteristic sections are
$$
 X=\ker q,\qquad Y_{\rm gr}=A/X,\qquad
 W_{\rm gr}=\big(C[3]/3C\big)/q(A).
$$
Indeed $C$ has exponent at most nine, so $3C\subseteq C[3]$, and cubes lie in $C[3]$. In the constructed group $Y_{\rm gr}=Y$ and $W_{\rm gr}=(E/K)\oplus Q$ is identified with $W=I\oplus Q$. Give these sections the input $X,Y,W$ bases under the identifications. The complement in $W$ allows arbitrary deficient output support. Deficient support in $X$ or $Y$ needs no changes because the commutator before projecting to $E/K$ is the full cross product.

## 2. Exact lifting

Every group isomorphism induces section maps preserving $\mu$. In fact, for $x\in X$ and $\bar y\in A/X$, the pairing $b(x,y)$ is independent of the choice of $y$, because $b(X,X)=0$. Its map into $C[3]/3C$ followed by quotient by $q(A)$ is exactly the original bilinear map. This also recovers its image section $E/K$.

Conversely let $f:X\to X',g:Y\to Y',h:W\to W'$ preserve the bilinear map. The map $u=f\otimes g:E\to E'$ preserves $K$, and its action on $E/K$ is $h|_I$. Give $A$ the block map $f\oplus g$. On the long central generators use any lift modulo nine of the matrix $u|_K$; this induces the specified map on $K=3C$. On the short $J$ generators use $u|_J$ into $K'\oplus J'$. On $Q$ use lifts in $J'\oplus Q'$ of the indicated map $h|_Q$, and on $C_Y$ use $g$. This map of abelian centers is invertible because its long and short diagonal reductions modulo three are invertible. It has the exact required action on $W_{\rm gr}$ and respects $b,q$.

For a pair of maps on $A,C$ preserving $b,q$, one can lift each basis vector of $A$ to a target element. The specified central map of its cube and the actual cube differ by $3C'$, so multiply the lift by a central element correcting that difference. The adjusted lifts have the correct cubes and all commutators are already correct. With the cyclic relations of $C$, these are defining relations: collection gives at most $|A||C|$ normal forms, and the constructed group has precisely that many. Hence the adjusted lifts give a homomorphism, invertible because it is invertible on $C$ and $A$. This proves that every tensor transporter lifts, giving equality of the relative sign profiles and of the self-comparison profiles. The case $a=0$ or $b=0$ is a zero tensor and any sign convention on its nonzero roles is decidable directly; for the individual decision reductions use fixed positive and negative outputs in the family. For simultaneously compiling exact profiles also on these degenerate pairs one can use the prior exact concise normalization.

## 3. Polynomial-degree faithful permutation generators

The group order may be exponential in the tensor size. Use many small quotient actions to output permutation generators compactly. Take all coordinate functionals $\ell_{ij}$ of the original tensor-product basis of $E$. For each extend it to a central homomorphism $\lambda:C\to \mathbb Z/9$, with $\lambda(e)=3\ell_{ij}(e)$ for $e\in E$, killing $C_Y$ and $Q$. On a long generator choose a representative of the value of $\ell_{ij}$ on its triple. On $J$ send $j$ to $3\ell_{ij}(j)$. Let $D$ be the cyclic image of $\lambda$, of size at most nine. The map on $A$ retains just $x_i,y_j$; its kernel is the radical of the projected pairing. Since the cube carry takes values in $C_Y$, the map
$$
 (v,c)\longmapsto ((x_i,y_j),\lambda(c))
$$
is a surjective homomorphism to a group on $\mathbb F_3^2\times D$ with the alternating half-pairing as its central cocycle. Its order is at most $81$. Take its regular permutation action.

For each $C_Y$ coordinate take the homomorphism to $\mathbb Z/9$ taking $(x,y,c)$ to the representative of $y_j$ plus $3c_{Y,j}$. The carry makes this additive and the commutator part vanishes on $C_Y$. For each coordinate of $Q$ take the projection to its cyclic order-three group. Use their regular actions as well. These maps jointly separate the order-three elements of the center: the $\ell_{ij}$ separate $E$ and the remaining maps separate $C_Y$ and $Q$. Multiplying a hypothetical common kernel element of order nine by three shows the combined action is faithful on the center. An element of the kernel outside the center would have a nontrivial commutator with some group element, giving a nontrivial kernel element in the center. Thus the combined permutation action is faithful. It uses at most $81ab+9b+3\dim Q$ points, and the images of the standard $A,C$ generators are computed directly in the constant-order factors. Consequently the reduction outputs permutation generators with polynomial degree and bit length without tabulating $G$. The center calculation and constant quotient construction also work over any fixed odd prime, with factor order at most $p^4$.

## 4. Uniform recovery from bare permutation generators

Here is an intrinsic description of the target family. The input group has class at most two and exponent at most nine, with $A=G/C$ and $E=[G,G]$ elementary abelian, $K=3C\subseteq E$, and $q(A)$ linearly disjoint from $E/K$ inside $C[3]/3C$. Put $X=\ker q$; require $X$ and $A/X$ positive dimensional and $b(X,X)=0$. Require an isotropic complement of $X$ in $A$, and require the canonical linear map $X\otimes(A/X)\to E$ to be an isomorphism. These are intrinsic checks. For the complement test start with any linear section $j:A/X\to A$. Replacing $j$ by $j+t$, where $t$ has values in $X$, changes the pairing on the candidate complement by $b(j,t)+b(t,j)$. The quadratic term vanishes by isotropy of $X$. Solve these equations exactly as a linear system.

All data for these checks are computable from the permutation generators using standard polynomial-time permutation-group constructive membership, which returns straight-line expressions in the specified generators. The following describes the necessary group computations. Check that every input cube has cube one, and each pairwise commutator is central and has cube one. Let $D_0$ be the elementary abelian group generated by the input cubes and pairwise commutators. It is central because $[s^3,t]=[s,t]^3=1$. Then $G/D_0$ is elementary abelian, and the cube identity with a central order-three commutator verifies the exponent bound on every element. Choose a basis of $D_0$ by successive subgroup membership. Choose representatives of a basis of $G/D_0$ by extending the generators of $D_0$ successively with input generators lying outside the current subgroup. Read all elementary coordinates by evaluating straight-line expressions modulo three in these bases, assigning coordinate zero to generators of $D_0$ when working in $G/D_0$. The pairing on $G/D_0$ has values in $D_0$; its radical lifts to $C$ and its image spans $E$. Explicit radical lifts together with $D_0$ generate $C$.

In $C$, choose independent cubes of the displayed generators forming a basis of $3C$, and keep their corresponding generators of order nine. After quotienting by $3C$, extend their classes to an elementary basis. Correct each additional short generator by a combination of the long generators to make its cube zero. This gives a cyclic central basis and all needed maps involving $C/3C,C[3]$, and $E$, by elementary coordinate or membership calculations. The same procedure computes $A$, $q$, and the chosen section bases. Generators of every subgroup used in construction have polynomial word/straight-line length and bit complexity. In the signed version, supply the three section bases as group words or straight-line expressions. Verify them in the computed quotient coordinates, and output the recovered bilinear map in exactly those frames.

To prove recognition suffices for the reverse, choose an isotropic complement $Y$ and choose cyclic central generators so that the cubes of the long generators form a basis of $K$, the short generators in $E$ complement $K$, and the short generators representing $q(Y)$ and $Q$ complete the center. Such coordinates exist by the section disjointness condition. The cross-pairing identifies $E$ with $X\otimes Y$ and discloses $K$ as the kernel of the recovered $\mu$. The displayed cube data and commutators then put the group in the construction of Section 1 up to isomorphism, by the lifting argument. This proves that extraction preserves ordinary feasibility and the same three physical determinant bits. Additional recognizable tests on tensor formats can be carried over by this extraction when desired.

The result concerns **abstract group isomorphism** of permutation-generator inputs. The permutation action itself is used for a concise encoding and is allowed to change under the abstract isomorphism. It is a different input model from either Cayley-table isomorphism or permutation conjugacy. In particular the polynomial permutation degree does not turn the group order into a polynomial parameter.
