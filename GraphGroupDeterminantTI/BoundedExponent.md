# Ordinary tensor equivalence for all class-two exponent-nine permutation groups

Use explicit permutation generators, with the action allowed to change under group isomorphism. For the class of groups of nilpotency class at most two and exponent dividing nine, ordinary abstract group isomorphism is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to ordinary TI over F₃. The lower bound is the [bounded-orbit construction](PermutationGroups.md). This note proves the general upper bound by applying the earlier fixed-prime-power residue encoding to the bracket of the Lazard Lie ring. It is an informal proof.

## Recover additive coordinates and the bracket

From generating permutations $g_1,\ldots,g_s$, check $g_i^9=1$ and that each $[g_i,g_j]$ commutes with all generators and has order dividing nine. If these tests pass the commutators generate the central derived group $D$. The class-two identity $(xy)^9=x^9y^9[y,x]^{36}$ proves the exponent promise on the entire group; conversely any input in the family passes.

For any abelian subgroup presented by a list of generating permutations and known to have exponent at most nine, obtain cyclic coordinates as follows. Adjoin generators successively, using permutation-group membership on the existing subgroup. At a nonredundant step find the relative order 3 or 9 by testing powers in the previous subgroup. Constructive permutation-group membership expresses that power by a straight-line expression in older generators. Reading exponent sums modulo nine gives one triangular additive relation. The relations are complete because their finite presentation has exactly the product of the relative orders by the usual ordered collection, and it surjects onto the subgroup of that order. Smith normal form supplies cyclic coordinates and the corresponding words. The same constructive membership in the original generating list gives coordinates for any displayed subgroup element. This uses standard polynomial-time permutation-group membership and its constructive version, with straight-line expressions. [Seress, *Permutation Group Algorithms*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511546549)

Apply this to $D$. Consider the homomorphism $G\to D^s$, $x\mapsto([x,g_i])_i$, whose kernel is exactly $C=Z(G)$. It is a homomorphism since all commutators are central. Its image is generated by known columns, computed in cyclic coordinates of $D^s$. Smith operations give cyclic coordinates and lifts for $A=G/C$ and the kernel of the linear map $(\mathbb Z/9)^s\to D^s$ defined by those columns. For each generator of that linear kernel take the ordered product of the corresponding powers of the input permutations. These elements and the generators of $D$ generate $C$: collection modulo $D$ writes any element by an exponent vector, and a central element has exponent vector in the linear kernel. Products chosen for a relation can differ from the ordered product of their sum only by elements of $D$. Thus cyclic coordinates of $C$ are also computable by the preceding abelian procedure.

Let $L$ be the additive group on the same underlying set as $G$, with $x+y=xy[y,x]^5$, and bracket $[x,y]_L=[x,y]_G$. The exponent $5$ is the inverse of two modulo nine. In class two the bracket commutes with every element, so the commutator identities show that the new sum is associative and commutative and the bracket is additive in both variables. The original product is recovered as $x+y+5[x,y]_L$. In particular group isomorphisms are exactly the additive isomorphisms preserving this bracket.

The additive group $L$ has a compact presentation. Keep the cyclic generators of $C$; for each cyclic generator of $A$ choose the previously found lift $x_i$. If its relative order is $m_i$, impose the additive relation $m_i x_i=x_i^{m_i}$ written in the central cyclic coordinates. Repeated addition of one element agrees with its group powers. These relations present the full additive group by collection and the order $|A||C|$. Smith normal form gives cyclic coordinates of $L$, with exponent at most nine. The bracket on the original lifted generators is given by their pairwise commutators, with zero brackets for the central generators. Transform this biadditive table into the computed cyclic coordinates by the Smith basis change. All operations and bit lengths are polynomial in the explicit permutation input.

## Compile this bracket into one F₃ tensor

For $M$ an abelian 3-group of exponent at most nine, use the earlier residue-encoding structure on
$$
  R_M= \mathbb F_3[M]/I^4,\qquad
  D_M=(R_M\otimes R_M)/\sum_{i+j\ge4} I^i\otimes I^j,
$$
where $I$ denotes the augmentation ideal or its image. Include multiplication, the augmentation, the unit, the bilinear tensor map $\pi:R_M\times R_M\to D_M$, and the diagonal $\Delta([x])=[x]\otimes[x]$ in the truncated target. In cyclic generators put $z_i=[e_i]-1$; bases are monomials of total degree at most three, with $z_i^3=0$ if $e_i$ has order three. The images of group elements are distinct, since their least nonzero base-three digit gives a nonzero lowest term in degrees 1 or 3 after translation to the identity.

These images are exactly the augmentation-one elements satisfying $\Delta(u)=\pi(u,u)$. For the reverse, subtract the identity and compare the least positive homogeneous degree. The leading term must be primitive for $z_i\mapsto z_i\otimes1+1\otimes z_i$. In degrees at most three the primitive terms are linear forms in the degree-one generators and linear combinations of the permitted $z_i^3$; mixed-variable monomials have a nonzero split unique to their exponent pattern, and $z_i^2$ has split coefficient two. Multiply by the inverse of a true group image with that leading digit to remove the term while preserving the group-like equation. Induction on the degree expresses $u$ as a true group image. It follows that isomorphisms of these marked structures are exactly additive group isomorphisms; every such isomorphism extends by functoriality.

Now put $M=L$, and include the bracket extension taking $([x],[y])$ to $[[x,y]_L]$ before truncation. For either fixed group basis input this is induced by an additive homomorphism in the other argument, so it kills fourth augmentation powers after truncation. By linearity it descends to a bilinear map $R_L\times R_L\to R_L$. The map can be tabulated by expanding each degree-at-most-three monomial, with a constant number of terms per basis monomial, and evaluating the biadditive table in the cyclic coordinates. Preservation holds exactly when the recovered additive group maps preserve the bracket.

All marks use a fixed finite number of linear and bilinear maps in polynomial dimension. Use the tensor-system compiler to obtain one ordinary three-tensor comparison over F₃. It follows that arbitrary class-two exponent-dividing-nine permutation-group isomorphism reduces to ordinary TI in one polynomial-time instance, and the degree is exactly TI. The same proof works for every fixed odd prime and fixed exponent bound $p^e$ with $e\ge2$; use the augmentation cutoff $p^{e-1}+1$ from the earlier fixed-prime-power construction, and the same exponent-$p^2$ lower bound. [Futorny, Grochow and Sergeichuk, *Wildness for tensors*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219)

The signed problem in the companion note is on the recognizable family specified there. Here the upper bound concerns the ordinary group problem on its entire stated exponent-bounded class.
