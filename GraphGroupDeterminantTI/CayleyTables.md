# Cayley tables and determinant restrictions over the field of three elements

All reductions here are deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions on the input model stated in each theorem. The arguments are informal.

## Signed Cayley-table comparisons that reduce to ordinary TI

The input is two explicit Cayley tables G and G′ with arbitrary explicit element colors. An isomorphism is required to preserve the colors. Over the field of three elements, the following conditions reduce to **one ordinary three-tensor isomorphism instance**.

1. The marked elements form one coset of a 3-subgroup, and the induced permutation between the two ordered marked cosets must be even. The ambient groups are unrestricted. Taking the marked set to be the whole group covers all Cayley-table 3-groups.
2. In a 3-group, the ordered marked set is any union of layers of its iterated Frattini series, and the induced permutation must be even.
3. A marked set consists of all k-dimensional subspaces of a specified elementary abelian 3-section carried by the group isomorphism. Its ordering is supplied explicitly or computed from the listed table. The induced permutation on the subspaces must be even. A fixed k or fixed codimension suffices for listing the entire family in polynomial size. In particular this covers parity on the maximal subgroups of a Cayley-table 3-group.

One can also impose a fixed number of these conditions simultaneously on the same isomorphism. Different list orders for the same marked set merely change a computable bit in the reduction.

### Sign on a coset and on Frattini layers

For a finite 3-group H, set H₀=H and Hᵢ₊₁=Hᵢ³[Hᵢ,Hᵢ]. These are the iterated Frattini subgroups. Every positive-order nontrivial term has a proper next term, and each quotient Aᵢ=Hᵢ/Hᵢ₊₁ is elementary abelian. The series is characteristic in H, and every term, quotient, and basis can be constructed by subgroup closure and elementary linear algebra with the Cayley table. For each i choose an arbitrary ordered basis, enumerate Aᵢ in lexicographic coordinates, and choose one representative rᵢ(q) for each coset. Do this independently at the two endpoints. A corresponding isomorphism f:H→H′ gives a linear map gᵢ:Aᵢ→Aᵢ′.

An isomorphism out of Hᵢ, in coordinates rᵢ(q)h with h in Hᵢ₊₁, permutes the coset blocks by gᵢ. Within the matched block it acts by f restricted to Hᵢ₊₁, followed by a left translation in Hᵢ₊₁′. That translation is even because it has odd order. For the entire Hᵢ, the internal sign appears 3^dim(Aᵢ) times, and the block sign is raised to |Hᵢ₊₁|. Both exponents are odd. Recursing with adapted orders shows that the sign of f on H is the product of det(gᵢ). To see the determinant identity, elementary transvections have order 3 and hence act evenly on the entire vector space. A diagonal matrix with one entry −1 swaps 3^(dim(Aᵢ)−1) pairs, an odd number. Transvections and coordinate reflections generate every invertible matrix over F₃, so the sign on the vector space equals its determinant.

For the layer Hᵢ\Hᵢ₊₁, restrict the same coset argument to the nonzero quotient indices. The internal sign appears 3^dim(Aᵢ)−1 times and cancels. The parity of the block permutation on the nonzero indices still equals det(gᵢ), because zero is fixed. Thus a marked union of layers, ordered in consecutive layer blocks with adapted coset orders, has sign equal to the product of the determinants of its selected quotient maps. Color-preserving group isomorphisms preserve the marked set as required. If the raw ordered mark is one of these unions, the selected indices can be found by checking equality of the given mark with the union of the layers it intersects. The identity, if included, is a separate singleton block.

For a marked left coset M=rH in an arbitrary ambient group, recover H=M⁻¹M. A color-preserving map sending the marked set to the target coset takes H to H′; within the two coset frames it acts as f|H followed by left translation by an element of H′. Its sign is therefore the same product. Right cosets are also left cosets of a conjugate subgroup, so the same result applies. A singleton has sign one. An empty marked set requires no orientation condition. For all these choices, compute from each input the sign of changing from its supplied marked order to its adapted order. Their product times the displayed determinant product gives the sign in the original decision problem.

### Sign on the derived subspace set

Let V=F₃^d, and put G_k(V) equal to the set of its k-dimensional subspaces. In the orders obtained from independently chosen bases, the sign of the action on G_k(V) is

    det(g)^((d−k)·binom(d−1,k−1))       for 1≤k≤d.

The two trivial Grassmannians, k=0 and k=d, have sign one. To prove the formula, the sign is automatically one for every transvection, by its order. Take the remaining generator g=diag(−1,1,…,1) and write V=F₃e⊕K. Subspaces inside K or containing e are fixed. Every other k-subspace equals D+F₃(e+v), for D of dimension k−1 in K and a unique nonzero v in K/D. The map swaps v with −v. For each D it makes (3^(d−k)−1)/2 transpositions. The parity of this number is d−k modulo 2. The number of D is the Gaussian binomial with parameter 3, which is congruent modulo 2 to the usual binomial by the Gaussian binomial recurrence. This proves the formula for the generators and thus all g. A raw ordering can again be calibrated by deterministic lexicographic enumeration from an ordered quotient basis. If k is fixed, enumerate all ordered k-tuples of basis vectors in the sense of candidate vectors from V and retain canonical row-reduced bases of dimension k. There are at most |V|^k candidates. For fixed codimension use dual annihilators. If the input lists the entire marked family in arbitrary dimension, the same calibration can instead sort those listed canonical row-reduced bases in their ambient quotient coordinates. Full coverage of the family can be checked by comparing the count with the Gaussian binomial after validating the listed subspaces and checking distinctness. For maximal subgroups in a finite 3-group the relevant vector space is G/Φ(G). The action sign is det(g)^(d−1).

### One ordinary tensor comparison and the size bound

Take E=F₃^G with basis e_g. Use two products on E, the coordinatewise multiplication and the bilinear map sending (e_g,e_h) to e_gh. The first forces every isomorphism to permute the coordinate idempotents. The second forces exactly group isomorphism. If there are r explicit colors, use the split coordinate algebra F₃^r and the linear map e_g to the idempotent of its color. Include on the color algebra the linear shift that takes each idempotent to the next in a fixed ordered list and kills the last. Its commuting algebra automorphism is the identity, since the last idempotent is fixed and every predecessor is then fixed. This implements named colors with a constant number of maps. An explicit marked set or a chosen subgroup can be included as one extra bit of color.

For the Frattini quotients include the sum of copies of the coordinate spans of Hᵢ. Link this sum to E by the sum of the inclusions, and to the direct sum of the Aᵢ by the map sending each coordinate basis vector to its element of the indicated quotient. A tagged summand cannot mix with another summand, so on that summand injection into E forces the coordinate permutation induced by the same group isomorphism. Its quotient map, which is surjective, forces exactly gᵢ on Aᵢ. A single chosen elementary abelian section H/K is handled by the identical construction on the span of H, with the named subgroups included when needed.

For each Aᵢ attach its exterior algebra ΛAᵢ with its multiplication and degree-one inclusion. The automorphism on it is forced by the degree-one action because ΛAᵢ is generated in degree one as a unital algebra, and gᵢ extends through this multiplication. Attach the top line using its inclusion. The induced scalar on that line in the chosen wedge basis is det(gᵢ), as required. For the selected top lines, introduce consecutive one-dimensional prefix spaces and bilinear maps multiplying each successive scalar with the preceding prefix. These are maps from the direct sum of lines and the direct sum of prefix spaces to the latter direct sum, with other entries zero. Pin the first prefix to its basis vector and the last prefix to the calibrated requested sign times its basis vector. Equivalently the target endpoint marks one of the two vectors on that line, according to the product of the two endpoint order calibrations. An isomorphism on the earlier data extends over the prefix spaces precisely when the required product holds. With no selected indices the constant equation is checked directly.

All growing lists of spaces in this proof are packed as direct sums. For tags use one split coordinate algebra with its directed shift, just as for colors. Represent the projections by its action on each of the indicated direct sums, with one data map for each displayed type of inclusion or projection. Exterior multiplication is componentwise on the exterior direct sum. The marked first and last prefix vectors are two tensors. Consequently the construction uses a constant number of order-at-most-three tensors. FGS as stated in Theorem 4.1 by Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang applies with this fixed bound, even though the count of quotient summands and of colors can grow. This detail prevents the exponential dependence of that theorem on the number of components from becoming a hidden cost. The explicit group products have O(N³) field entries, where N is the table group order. The total exterior algebra dimension is at most (log₃ N) · N^(log₃2), since dim(Aᵢ)≤log₃ N and ΛAᵢ has dimension 2^dim(Aᵢ). All tag, prefix, span and quotient data have polynomial size. All negative recognition cases, mismatched signatures, or unequal obvious invariant counts can be mapped to a fixed negative ordinary TI instance. This proves the single-instance reductions. [Ordinary tensor-system compiler, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

These results give an actual one-instance ordinary-TI upper bound for structural marked parity in the Cayley-table branch of the determinant chain. The arbitrary marked color-union problem permits marks with none of these structures.

## A table expansion with the exact three determinant signs

For a bilinear tensor b:U×V→W over F₃, form the group on U⊕V⊕W with multiplication

    (u,v,w)(u′,v′,w′)=(u+u′,v+v′,w+w′+b(u,v′)).

This is associative by bilinearity and has class at most two and exponent dividing three. Include the three distinguished axis subgroups U₀,V₀,W₀, identifiable by coloring axis nonidentity elements with three different colors. From the formula the commutator of the U and V elements is b(u,v) in W₀. Any axis-preserving group isomorphism therefore induces an isotopism of b on its three linear coordinate groups. It is uniquely determined by the three maps, since every element is a product of axis elements. Conversely any isotopism extends by the coordinate formula. Using the above sign identity on each axis in its lexicographic coordinates shows that the attainable permutations on these three named sets carry exactly the original three determinant bits. Removing the identity from each axis does not change parity. Thus the one-sign, two-sign and all-three-sign tests, and the profile correction rules between them, have exactly corresponding group tests on this image. This also works for zero and nonconcise input tensors, since no structural recovery of the axes is needed from their marked group encoding.

If the format is (a,b,c), the resulting group has 3^(a+b+c) elements. Writing the whole Cayley table takes 3^(2(a+b+c)) entries. Dense tensor inputs contain a·b·c field entries. The construction therefore gives an exponential-size expansion rather than a polynomial many-one reduction between those two input models. One may instead give the axis bases and commutator structure constants by a compact power-commutator presentation, in which case the expansion is polynomial and the same sign proof applies. For comparisons originally given by a **full value table** of the bilinear map, which also lists the carrier spaces, this group construction is polynomial in the expanded input size whenever |U||V||W| is counted as a parameter. In particular, its order is exactly that parameter. Compact and full group representations should remain explicit in every comparison.

## What the November group claim gives

Zhang, Wang and Zhuang, arXiv:2611.18084v1, invoke Qiao and Zhang, Theorem 9.1, for a polynomial-time algorithm on whole multiplication tables. The invoked output is a representative isomorphism and generators of the complete source automorphism group, or the empty set. Their Lemma 2.6 restricts that coset by an **arbitrary list of pointwise correspondence requirements**, using orbit and point-stabilizer computations one mark at a time. This is a pointwise marking theorem. Preserving arbitrary colors or named subsets setwise is a different coset problem. Their Corollary 4.3 concerns an exponent in the side lengths for dense tensors. Its polynomial-in-order statement concerns explicit tables. The original manuscript is cited through a STOC submissions release asset, and these notes have not independently verified that manuscript's algorithm. [Application preprint, §§2.2 and 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18084v1) · [Cited manuscript](https://github.com/sigact-stoc/stoc2027/releases/download/papers-2/1723.pdf)

Conditionally on the cited coset algorithm, arbitrary whole-group parity with any polynomial list of pointwise correspondence requirements is polynomial time. First impose the point requirements by Lemma 2.6. If a representative f has odd permutation sign, a suitable even representative exists exactly when some generator of the remaining source stabilizer has odd sign. The same sign-vector test works on a fixed number of **characteristic** named sets with chosen orders, by checking whether the desired correction belongs to the binary span of the sign vectors of the generators. This gives no polynomial algorithm for the arbitrary-colored Cayley-table rung, since one still needs to impose its color-set constraints. For arbitrary relative bilinear maps over a fixed finite field, the cited full-table application can compute a representative and stabilizer generators and hence the entire attainable determinant profile, at an expense exponential in a+b+c. These conditional algorithmic consequences are independent of the unconditional ordinary reductions proved above.
