# Ring quotients and nonreduced genus two

**Theorem.** Let $F=\mathbb F_p$ with $p$ odd. For a commutative finite local $F$-algebra $C$, define the alternating bimap $\beta_C:C^3\times C^3\to C^2$ by
$$
 \beta_C((x,y,z),(a,b,c))=(xb-ya,xc-za).
$$
The group on $C^3\times C^2$ with multiplication given by addition plus half of $\beta_C$ has genus exactly two under Brooksbank–Maglione–Wilson Definition 3.2. For finite local $F$-algebras $R,S$, this group for $R$ surjects onto this group for $S$ if and only if there is a unital algebra surjection $R\to S$. There is a deterministic polynomial Cayley-table algorithm when $S$ has a bounded number of $F$-algebra generators and bounded socle dimension over its residue field. In particular this applies to every monogenic $S$, with no bound on its nilpotence index.

## Recovering the local ring from the flat bimap

Each coordinate of $C^2$ is obtained by pairing a multiple of the first standard basis vector with one other standard basis vector. The common radical is zero, by testing against the three standard basis vectors. The bimap therefore gives a class-two group whose center equals its derived subgroup. Its input and output are the central quotient and the derived group.

Every $F$-linear self-adjoint operator $T$ of $\beta_C$ is $C$-linear. For each $a\in C$ and any standard basis vectors $e_i,e_j$,
$$
  \beta_C(T(ae_i),e_j)=\beta_C(ae_i,Te_j)=a\beta_C(Te_i,e_j).
$$
The three coordinate tests force $T(ae_i)=aTe_i$. Since $p$ is odd, alternation and self-adjointness give $\beta_C(Te_i,e_i)=0$. For $i=1$ this makes $Te_1$ a scalar multiple of $e_1$. For $i=2,3$ it makes the first coordinate of $Te_i$ zero. Pairing these latter vectors with $e_1$ gives $Te_i=a e_i$ for the same scalar $a$ as in $Te_1$. All scalars are centroid operators, and the output action is forced because the bimap spans $C^2$. Thus the centroid is exactly $C$. It is local and $C^2$ has residue-field rank two. This is the literal genus under the cited definition. [Brooksbank–Maglione–Wilson, Definition 3.2 and §3.2(c)](https://joshmaglione.com/papers/Genus2.pdf)

For an epimorphism of the two groups, let $A:R^3\to S^3$ and $B:R^2\to S^2$ be its induced maps. These are surjective and satisfy $B\beta_R=\beta_S(A,A)$. Hence $\ker A=\operatorname{rad}(B\beta_R)$, which is invariant under multiplication by $R$. Descending multiplication along $A$ defines a unital action of $R$ by self-adjoint operators on $S^3$. The preceding calculation identifies this with a ring homomorphism $\alpha:R\to S$. Applying the bimap equation to scalar multiples and using that $\beta_R$ spans its output gives $B(rw)=\alpha(r)B(w)$. The two columns $B(1,0),B(0,1)$ therefore span $S^2$ over $S$, because $B$ is surjective. They form an invertible matrix, and applying its inverse to $B(R^2)=S^2$ gives $\alpha(R)^2=S^2$. Thus $\alpha$ is surjective. Conversely a ring surjection extends to the groups coordinatewise. The same argument shows that having a surjective pair between these bimaps is equivalent to having a group epimorphism.

## Listing surjections onto targets of bounded generator and socle ranks

The following algorithm takes finite commutative $F$-algebras $R,S$, with $S$ local. Put $k=S/J(S)$ and $b=\dim_k\operatorname{Soc}(S)$, where $\operatorname{Soc}(S)=\operatorname{Ann}_S J(S)$. Assume $S$ has $c$ algebra generators. We list all unital algebra surjections $R\to S$ in $|R|^{c+b}\operatorname{poly}(\log|R|+\log|S|)$ time given explicit bases, structure constants, and a generating list. Finding a generating list for fixed $c$ takes at most $|S|^c$ algebra-closure computations. The resulting bound gives polynomial time in the table sizes for fixed $b,c$.

Enumerate all $c$-tuples $r_i\in R$ as prospective preimages of the displayed generators $s_i$ of $S$. Construct a presentation of $S$ on these generators of polynomial length in its multiplication table in basis coordinates. Take basis words $w_j(s)$ including $1$, obtained by closing the span under multiplying by generators. Impose all relations of the form $s_i w_j=\sum_\ell a_{ij\ell}w_\ell$ that hold in $S$. They present $S$ because they reduce every word to the span of the basis words, while the actual evaluations of those words are independent. In $R$, evaluate those relations at the $r_i$ and take the ideal they generate. The resulting quotient $R'$ receives a unital map $S\to R'$. A ring epimorphism $R\to S$ sending $r_i$ to $s_i$ must factor through a unital algebra retraction of this map. Conversely any such retraction gives a ring epimorphism. Search among $S$-linear maps $R'\to S$, checking multiplication on a basis and checking that the composition with $S\to R'$ is the identity.

The number of linear maps is small because $S$ is controlled by its socle. Choose $k$-linear coordinates on its socle and compose each with the finite-field trace $k\to F$, giving $b$ linear functionals on the socle. Extend them to linear functionals $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_b$ on $S$. The map
$$
 S\longrightarrow (S^*)^b,\qquad a\longmapsto ((x\mapsto \ell_i(ax)))_{i=1}^b,
$$
where $S^*=\operatorname{Hom}_F(S,F)$ has the multiplication action, is $S$-linear and injective. For a nonzero $a$, the nonzero ideal $aS$ contains a nonzero socle element, by taking the last nonzero product with powers of $J(S)$. Some chosen coordinate of that element is nonzero. Multiplying by a scalar lift in $S$ can make its trace nonzero by nondegeneracy of the trace pairing. This proves injectivity. For any $S$-module $M$, sending an $S$-linear map $M\to S^*$ to its evaluation at $1$ gives a bijection with $\operatorname{Hom}_F(M,F)$, with inverse $g\mapsto(m\mapsto (s\mapsto g(sm)))$. Therefore there are at most $|R'|^b$ candidate $S$-linear maps to $S$. Solve their equations by Gaussian elimination and enumerate the resulting linear space. This proves the bound.

If $S=F[t]/(f)$ is local and monogenic, a candidate $r$ gives $R'=R/(f(r))$. The pairing sending $(u,v)$ to the coefficient of $t^{\deg f-1}$ in $uv$ modulo $f$ is nondegenerate: for a nonzero polynomial representative of degree $d$, multiply by $t^{\deg f-1-d}$. Thus $S\cong S^*$ as $S$-modules and exactly $|R'|$ $S$-linear maps $R'\to S$ occur. The target generator can be found by enumerating the elements of $S$ and testing their power spans. This gives the monogenic algorithm directly, without inspecting any nilpotence layers.

## A group-theoretic extension

Suppose $G,H$ are class-two groups of exponent an odd prime $p$. Assume that each local centroid factor of the commutator bimap of $G$ has output generated by at most two elements as a module over its centroid. Assume the target commutator bimap has local centroid $S$, every self-adjoint input operator belongs to that centroid, and its output is $S$-free of rank two. If $S$ has bounded generator and socle ranks, deciding whether $G$ surjects onto $H$ is in deterministic polynomial time from the two tables. This applies in particular to the group constructed from $\beta_S$ for every monogenic finite local $S$.

To see this, first consider one source centroid factor. Its centroid action descends to a ring map $\alpha:R\to S$ under any bimap epimorphism, and the two generators of the source output module have images forming an invertible pair of columns over $S$. The proof for $\beta_R$ therefore still forces $\alpha$ to be surjective. The preceding algorithm lists all these $\alpha$. The centroid rings can be computed by linear equations. Each acts faithfully on an output module generated by two elements, so its cardinality is at most the square of the cardinality of that output. Enumerate two proposed images of the source module generators in the target output. For a fixed $\alpha$, verify these extend to an $\alpha$-semilinear surjection $B$ by ordinary linear algebra on the module relations. If they do, the only possible source kernel on central quotients is $\operatorname{rad}(B\beta_G)$. Test ordered alternating tuple isometry between its quotient bimap and the target bimap in the basis of the fixed target output. The finite-field tuple isometry method can be made deterministic at polynomial Cayley-table cost: characteristic-dependent algebra decomposition is deterministic, and the extension fields used to check simple components have cardinality bounded by the order of the faithful ambient vector space. Their square-root and norm searches can be done by enumerating field elements. [Ivanyos–Qiao, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03495) · [Brooksbank–Wilson, Remark 2.1](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-2011-05388-2)

If the source has several local centroid factors, their input spaces are a direct orthogonal sum and their output spaces are a direct sum. Their images under an onto input map form a direct orthogonal decomposition of the target. Indeed an element in one image and the sum of the others pairs to zero with every image, so it is zero. The target has only trivial self-adjoint idempotents because every self-adjoint operator comes from the local ring $S$. Thus exactly one nonzero source input factor surjects onto the target, and trying the factors suffices. Fullness forces its induced output map to be onto. All factorizations into centroid factors can be computed by finding the primitive idempotents of the commutative centroid. Finally a surjective bimap morphism lifts to a group epimorphism exactly when
$$
 \dim_F\ker A+\dim_F Z(G)/G'\ \ge\ \dim_F Z(H)/H'.
$$
Indeed the half-bracket law identifies each group with its commutator bimap plus a freely split central complement. The target central complement must be filled from $\ker A$ and the source central complement, and any linear surjection from that space to the target complement extends to a group map. Since $A$ is onto, the dimensions in this comparison are determined by the two groups alone.

## Source and reuse audit

The original question asks for Cayley-table polynomial time. The literal cited definition uses $\dim_{C/J(C)}W/J(C)W$. The proof of the isomorphism algorithm in the same cited paper instead tests $\dim_K W$ over a coefficient-field complement $K$. These conditions differ on the nonreduced groups above. The companion main proof handles bounded coefficient-field dimension by enumerating field actions; the present argument instead preserves the nonreduced ring via self-adjoint operators. [Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar, Open Question 3(3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7) · [Brooksbank–Maglione–Wilson, Definition 3.2 and §7](https://joshmaglione.com/papers/Genus2.pdf)

The local DAG and Lean survey recovered the adjoint equations and their basis tests in `ManinBilinearAdjoints`, ordered commutant stability in `Problem18FullCommutantAdjoint20260930`, Kronecker simultaneous-equivalence and cancellation in `QuiverRepKronecker20260902`, and the earlier table epimorphism proofs for prescribed bimap quotient and Heisenberg trace transfer. They are relevant to the individual linear-algebra operations. No retrieved formal declaration establishes the nonreduced ring quotient algorithm or the exact-genus rank-marker reduction. This companion is an informal proof.
