# Independent audit of the graph functor boundary

I checked the proof in [GraphFunctorBoundary.md](GraphFunctorBoundary.md). Both directions pass under the stated source categories, fixed tensor order, and polynomial output-dimension bound. The argument is informal.

## Obstruction for ordinary tensor targets

If $S_i(T)$ is the minimal support in mode $i$, then an all-covariant tensor morphism $f:T\to T'$ gives $S_i(T')\subseteq f_i(S_i(T))$. Arrows each way imply equality of support dimensions. The restriction of $f_i$ to the support is then bijective onto the target support, and their tensor product still sends $T$ to $T'$. Thus the concise tensors are isomorphic. Ordinary tensor isomorphism for the original spaces additionally needs only the ambient dimension tuple, since support isomorphisms extend along arbitrary unused complements. The same extension uses one common factor map in each group of repeated modes of the shared-factor types, because the relevant repeated supports agree.

Every bipartite graph with an edge has a homomorphism to $K_2$ and receives one from $K_2$, so all such graphs must map into one concise tensor isomorphism class. At fixed order and polynomially bounded dimensions, the number of possible ambient dimension tuples is polynomial. A balanced bipartition yields $2^{\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor}-1$ labeled nonempty edge sets, and division by $n!$ leaves $2^{\Omega(n^2)}$ isomorphism classes. This proves the obstruction independently of the arrow map's computational cost. Section 2.3 of the definitive paper uses exactly the all-covariant convention, and Appendix B supplies the graph-isomorphism direction to which the obstruction applies. [Grochow–Qiao, §2.3, Question 7.5, and Appendix B](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full)

## Positive graph-to-algebra map

Any graph homomorphism sends the basis generators of the diagonal relation to diagonal generators, and the ordered edge generators to edge generators. This remains true when the graph map identifies nonadjacent vertices. Tensoring and taking the two induced quotient maps respect composition. The maps on each duplicated pair of vertex spaces coincide, so they preserve the shared image of multiplication by $e$. The provided leaf maps are functorial by direct sums and tensor products. The zero-vertex input is distinguished by its dimension, and the morphisms from it obey the same formulas.

The designated degree-two space is exactly the span of products. The dimension of the degree-one quotient determines the total semantic dimension. A pure leaf's multiplication factors through a space of that dimension. A nonzero semantic component has strictly larger multiplication rank on its private leaf by the injective tensor product with its nonzero component. This detects the pure leaf subspace. The maximal proper annihilators of its elements in the semantic quotient are precisely the complements of individual nonzero named summands. Their leaf-annihilator dimensions distinguish the tags, and the intersection of the other complements recovers the named summand. All five semantics are nonzero when the graph has at least one vertex. Factoring out the products with leaves recovers the original degree-two map independently of the chosen representatives.

In the recovered core an isomorphism scales $e$ by one nonzero scalar. Comparison of the two products with $e$ identifies the maps on each duplicated pair. Pulling back the two quotient products now shows that the corresponding factor maps carry $D_G$ and $E_G$ to $D_{G'}$ and $E_{G'}$. Every nonzero decomposable vector in $D_G$ lies on exactly one diagonal coordinate line. This can be seen by viewing a diagonal element as a matrix whose rank is its number of nonzero diagonal coordinates. Therefore the factor maps are monomial with the same vertex permutation. On the edge relation the scalings are nonzero, so equality of the subspaces is equivalent to preserving and reflecting edges. This proves isomorphism reflection over every field.

The central and semantic spaces have polynomial size in the vertex count. The product is associative since every triple product vanishes. In the unitalization the original ideal is nilpotent and the quotient is the field, so that ideal is the Jacobson radical. A unital isomorphism preserves it, and a graph map extends by the identity on the scalar factor.

## Consequence and scope

Composing the graph-to-unital-algebra functor with a hypothetical polynomial-dimension functorial reduction to the ordinary fixed-order tensor category would contradict the graph obstruction. Thus the algebra-to-tensor obstruction already holds for the stated cube-zero unitizations. These statements concern all graph or unital algebra homomorphisms and all-covariant tensor morphisms. They settle the specified functoriality directions while the other questions in Question 7.5 require separate arguments.
