# Polynomial Calculus degree for geometric matrix nonisomorphism

## The classification

Let $M,N$ be two explicitly given representations of the same finite quiver with the same dimension vector over a field $F$ of characteristic $p>0$. The ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) system is the linear arrow-intertwining equations for a forward map and the quadratic two-sided inverse equations for the vertex maps. Suppose $M,N$ are nonisomorphic over $\overline F$. For each indecomposable isomorphism type $W$ over $\overline F$, write $a_W,b_W$ for its multiplicities in $M,N$, respectively. Set

$$
 h=\min_{W\mid a_W\ne b_W}
       \min\{\min(a_W,b_W)+1,\ p^{v_p(|a_W-b_W|)}\}.
$$

**Theorem.** The minimum ordinary-PC refutation degree is between $h/2$ and $\max(3,2h)$. The upper bound gives expanded size $L^{O(h)}$, where $L$ counts the explicit input and its standard polynomial-system encoding in field elements. These statements include simultaneous matrix conjugacy for every field of positive characteristic. The same bounds hold for quiver representations over finite fields when all field equations are supplied.

For simultaneous congruence of arbitrary labelled matrix tuples over any field of odd characteristic, compute $h$ from the two-vertex representations with arrows $A_i,A_i^{\mathsf T}$ and $B_i,B_i^{\mathsf T}$. On geometrically noncongruent pairs the minimum degree is between $h/4$ and $\max(3,2h)$, again with expanded size $L^{O(h)}$ for the upper bound. In characteristic zero, the same upper argument gives degree at most three for both problems. These statements use geometric nonisomorphism and geometric noncongruence, the convention of Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She. They settle the degree part, up to constant factors, of their Question 7.3 for matrix conjugacy in every positive characteristic and for matrix congruence in odd characteristic. [Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She, Open Question 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

On the quiver systems, there is also a polynomial expanded-size degree-three PC+INV refutation over the input field using at most **one** inversion with linear matrix entries. This also applies to geometric matrix-tuple noncongruence in characteristic other than two. The size bound charges the inversion entries and its premise references.

## 1. Upper bounds from multiplicity blocks

Write $S_v,T_v$ for the forward and reverse maps at vertex $v$, with $T_vS_v=I$ and $S_vT_v=I$. For an arrow $a$ with tail $u$, head $v$, and matrices $A,B$, the forward equation is $S_vA-BS_u=0$. Multiplying on the left by $T_v$ and the right by $T_u$ and using the inverse equations gives $AT_u-T_vB=0$ in degree at most three. Each matrix product here expands into a sum of permitted variable multiplications and linear combinations.

Let $P,Q$ be linear projections from the ambient spaces of all vertexwise maps to $\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)$, $\operatorname{Hom}(N,M)$. A linear functional vanishing on the kernel of a linear map belongs to its row span. Accordingly, the entries of $S-P(S)$ and $T-Q(T)$ are linear combinations of the corresponding intertwiner equations. Multiplying those available linear consequences by linear entries and using the inverse equations gives

$$
 Q(T)P(S)=I_M,\qquad P(S)Q(T)=I_N .
$$

Work over the base field for the moment. Put $E=\operatorname{End}(M\oplus N)$ and $J=\operatorname{rad}(E)$. Its quotient is a product of matrix rings $\operatorname{Mat}_{a+b}(D)$, one for each base-field indecomposable type $W$, with $D=\operatorname{End}(W)/\operatorname{rad}(\operatorname{End}(W))$, and $a,b$ the corresponding multiplicities. This is the standard endomorphism-ring form of Krull–Schmidt. The idempotents onto $M,N$ have complementary ranks $a,b$ in that factor. The displayed inverse lines are polynomial identities with coefficients in the appropriate block of $E$, so applying the linear quotient map to each coefficient gives inverse identities there. Denote the two rectangular blocks by $U,V$. All entries, when written in scalar coordinates, are linear forms in the original variables.

For the ordinary-PC upper bound first work over $\overline F$, where each $D=\overline F$. If $a>b$, take the first $b+1$ rows and corresponding columns of the identity $UV=I_a$. This gives a square identity of size $b+1$, expressed as the product of a $(b+1)$-by-$b$ matrix and a $b$-by-$(b+1)$ matrix. Its determinant is identically zero by Cauchy–Binet. For a matrix $C$ of quadratic polynomial entries, the formula for $\det C-\det I$ telescopes in each permutation term, expressing the difference as a sum of multiples of the entries of $C-I$ with multipliers of degree at most $2b$. Ordinary PC derives the contradiction in degree at most $2(b+1)$. Swapping $a,b$ treats $a<b$.

For the $p$-adic bound, let $r=p^{v_p(|a-b|)}$. The sums of principal $r$-minors of $UV$ and $VU$ are equal by Cauchy–Binet, including when one of those sums is empty. Apply the same telescoping to each minor. This gives the constant $\binom{a}{r}-\binom{b}{r}$ in degree at most $2r$, in addition to the degree-three preparation. The constant is nonzero by the base-$p$ binomial identity. In characteristic zero the traces already differ. [MathProofs, earlier principal-minor upper bound](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorAndAlgebraOpenQuestions/Positive-Characteristic-PC.md)

The ordinary-PC degree bound descends to $F$. In a fixed degree cap $D_0$, the space of derivable lines is the least subspace of polynomials of degree at most $D_0$ containing the eligible axioms and closed under multiplying its intersection with degree at most $D_0-1$ by any variable. The saturation uses sums and intersections of finite-dimensional spaces, which commute with field extension. Moreover, performing the saturation by keeping a derivable basis costs a polynomial in the number of axioms and in the number of ambient monomials. At $D_0=\max(3,2h)$ this gives expanded size $L^{O(h)}$.

For the PC+INV bound, choose a differing type over $F$, which exists by Krull–Schmidt since the pair is geometrically nonisomorphic. Keep the identities $UV=I_a$ and $VU=I_b$ over $D$. Express the action on column vectors over $D$ in coordinates over $F$ to get rectangular products over $F$. Assume $a>b$ by exchanging the two names if needed. Extend the product $UV=I_{a[D:F]}$ by zero columns in the first factor and zero rows in the second to obtain square matrices. The inverted product has a zero diagonal position on the padding, so its identity line gives $-1=0$. All entries are linear or zero. The total size of each square matrix is polynomial: $a[D:F]\le a\dim_F W$, since the filtration by powers of the radical of $\operatorname{End}(W)$ on $W$ has a nonzero $D$-module quotient. The operations before the inversion have degree at most three and polynomial expanded size. [Inversion rule](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S2.SS3)

## 2. Degree bounds for disjoint axiom families

For disjoint variable sets, the minimum ordinary-PC degree to refute the union of systems is the minimum over the individual systems. Interpret a satisfiable component as having infinite refutation degree.

The upper bound is inclusion. For the lower bound, suppose no individual system refutes in degree at most $D_0$. Let $V_i$ be the space of polynomials up to degree $D_0$ in variable block $i$, and $S_i\subset V_i$ the span of lines derivable in this degree from its axioms. The quotient $V_i/S_i$ has the filtration whose level $k$ is the image of polynomials of degree at most $k$. Multiplication by a variable in block $i$ induces a map from level $k$ to level $k+1$ for $k<D_0$. Indeed, representatives in degree at most $k$ differ by a derivable line of degree at most $k$, and its product by the variable is still derivable within $D_0$.

Take the tensor product of these quotient vector spaces with its total-degree filtration. Send a monomial to the tensor of the classes of its variable-block factors, and extend linearly. Multiplication by any variable on a polynomial of degree at most $D_0-1$ agrees with the induced multiplication on the corresponding tensor factor. Therefore linear combinations and permitted multiplications send prior zero images to zero. Axioms of degree at most $D_0$ have zero image. But the image of $1$ is a tensor of nonzero vectors, hence nonzero. This argument includes degree falls, because the induced multiplication only requires the current line to belong to the lower filtration level.

## 3. Lower bounds for arbitrary quiver pairs

Work over $\overline F$. Take common bases for each indecomposable type in decompositions of $M,N$. Each arrow is now a direct sum of matrices $I_a\otimes W(a)$, with the multiplicities and matrices interpreted at the appropriate vertices. On a type with equal multiplicities use the identity for both hypothetical intertwiners. On a type with unequal multiplicities, introduce a matrix $X_W$ of fresh distinct variables of shape $b_W$-by-$a_W$. Substitute $X_W\otimes I_{W_v}$ for the corresponding forward block at vertex $v$ and $X_W^{\mathsf T}\otimes I_{W_v}$ for its reverse. Set all between-type blocks to zero.

The arrow equations are identities under this substitution. Two-sided inverse equations become entries of $X_W^{\mathsf T}X_W-I$ or $X_W X_W^{\mathsf T}-I$, possibly repeated. The rectangular bijection axioms give these in degree at most two. An off-diagonal entry is a sum of collisions. A diagonal entry follows from a row or column sum minus one and the equations $x^2-x=0$. The inverse basis changes back to raw coordinates are linear substitutions and preserve degree in PC. A multiplication by a linear form is simulated by individual variable multiplications and linear combinations.

Write $c_W=\min(a_W,b_W)$ and $r_W=p^{v_p(|a_W-b_W|)}$. Riis’s rectangle lemma applies in every degree $D_0$ with $2D_0\le c_W$ and $D_0<r_W$, provided $c_W\ge1$. Whenever these inequalities hold for every differing type, the disjoint-system argument excludes a refutation after substitution. In particular the refutation degree is at least

$$
 \min_{W\mid a_W\ne b_W}\min\{\lfloor c_W/2\rfloor+1,r_W\}.
$$

If this expression is at most two, the degree lower bound of two already follows from the homogeneous linear arrow equations and quadratic inverse axioms. This takes care also of a type with multiplicity zero. The expression is at least $h/2$. An $F$-proof would also be a proof over $\overline F$, which gives the lower bound over $F$. [Riis, rectangle obstruction, Lemma 14.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07280v1#S14)

Over finite $F$, the lower bound with field equations uses decompositions over $F$ itself. The geometric multiplicity data are obtained by splitting each matrix factor over its finite division field into a product of matrix factors over $\overline F$, with the same two idempotent ranks in each factor. Indeed the old radical remains nilpotent after extension, and the extended quotient is semisimple by separability of finite fields. Thus its radical is exactly the extended radical. Each original field equation $y^{|F|}-y=0$ maps under the linear substitutions to a scalar linear combination of the rectangle variables’ field equations. Over $F$ each of those follows from $x^2-x=0$ in degree at most $|F|$. When the degree cap is smaller, no original field axiom is used. The same lower bound follows.

## 4. Matrix congruence in odd characteristic

Use the quiver with two vertices and arrows labelled $A_i,A_i^{\mathsf T}$, before quotienting by any change of arrow coordinates. The transpose-pair involution pairs the arrows, exchanges the vertices, and takes vector-space duals. This defines a contravariant duality $X\mapsto X^*$ on the representation category. In coordinates, a symmetric isomorphism $j:X\to X^*$ has two vertex maps that are dual to one another and identifies the two vertex spaces as duals. In dual bases this gives a matrix tuple of the displayed transpose-pair form. Isometries of the symmetric isomorphisms correspond exactly to tuple congruences.

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two, two symmetric isomorphisms $j_1,j_2:X\to X^*$ on the same representation are isometric. The map $G=j_1^{-1}j_2$ is invertible and selfadjoint for $j_1$. Choose a polynomial square root $H$ of $G$. This exists by lifting a chosen square root on each nonzero eigenvalue in the primary quotient of the minimal polynomial, solving each successive coefficient by division by twice the chosen root, then combining by Chinese remaindering. The polynomial $H$ stays selfadjoint and $H^*j_1 H=j_2$. Hence ordinary isomorphism of the augmented quiver implies congruence over the algebraic closure. This is also the transpose-pair criterion of Belitskii–Futorny–Muzychuk–Sergeichuk. [Matrix tuple congruence and equivalence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13894)

For the upper bound, a formal congruence $XA_iX^{\mathsf T}=B_i$ with inverse $Y$ gives the two linear quiver intertwiners in degree at most three. Multiply the congruence lines and their transposes on the right by $Y^{\mathsf T}$ and use $X^{\mathsf T}Y^{\mathsf T}=I$. The forward vertex maps are $Y^{\mathsf T},X$ in tail, head order, and the reverse maps are $X^{\mathsf T},Y$. These satisfy the original inverse lines. The quiver upper proof therefore translates with its maximum degree preserved.

For the lower bound one needs actual direct sums of bilinear tuples, so we describe the decomposition over the algebraic closure. Group the indecomposables of the underlying representation into orbits by $W\mapsto W^*$.

- If $W\not\cong W^*$, their multiplicities agree because $X\cong X^*$. There is a symmetric form on the pair $W\oplus W^*$ given by the hyperbolic exchange.
- If $W\cong W^*$, choose an isomorphism $u:W\to W^*$. Its symmetric and skew parts sum to $u$. After comparison with $u$, locality of $\operatorname{End}(W)$ implies at least one part is invertible. Both signs cannot occur as invertible forms: comparing an invertible skew form to an invertible symmetric form gives a skew-adjoint unit in $\operatorname{End}(W)$. The adjoint induces the identity on the scalar residue $\overline F$. Such a unit would have zero residue since the characteristic is odd. For the symmetric type use a single copy with its symmetric form.
- For a skew type choose an invertible skew isomorphism $j_W$. The multiplicity of $W$ in $X$ is even. Use the semisimple quotient of the additive category on all the chosen summands, killing maps between distinct types and the radicals within a type, and take its $W$-part. Identify each copy of $W^*$ in that part with $W$ via $j_W$. The symmetry identity $j=j^*$ gives a skew matrix on the multiplicity space because the transpose on each scalar residue fixes scalars and $j_W^*=-j_W$. It is invertible because $j$ is invertible. Its dimension is even. A symmetric form exists on $W\oplus W$, with off-diagonal entries $j_W,-j_W$, and zero diagonal entries.

The corresponding orthogonal sum of these blocks is a symmetric form on the original underlying quiver object. The polynomial square-root argument shows it is isometric to the given tuple. Each block contributes the indicated geometric multiplicity $a_W$ or $a_W/2$ copies, and every block has equal dimensions on the two vertices.

Now put the input tuples in these orthogonal decompositions and repeat the rectangle projection, with one rectangular matrix on the multiplicity indices of each differing block type. Its tensor product with the identity on the block coordinates is a single proposed congruence block. All projected congruence equations follow from the products $XX^{\mathsf T}=I$ on the multiplicity blocks in degree two, and the inverse equations follow as before. Coordinate changes for the two input tuples are linear substitutions on the witness and inverse variables. The smaller multiplicity per skew block loses at most a factor two, and the valuation $v_p(a_W-b_W)$ is unchanged by division by two. The same projection and disjoint-system proof therefore give the lower bound $h/4$.

## 5. Scope and attribution

The characteristic-independent additive-category argument underlies the upper bound with one inversion. The ordinary-PC classification combines the earlier MathProofs principal-minor upper bound, Riis’s rectangle obstruction, a degree-fall-compatible argument for disjoint systems, and an indecomposable multiplicity projection on arbitrary inputs. This explains both hard regimes and low-degree cases without assuming the input is diagonal or semisimple. The characteristic-two congruence extension and the sharp sparse-size bound remain separate questions. This is an informal proof with a bounded prior-work check.
