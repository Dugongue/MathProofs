# Four theorems on isomorphism, specialization, and proof complexity

These are informal mathematical proofs. The exact arithmetic controls test specified parts of the constructions. Publication priority is unestablished.

| Result | Main theorem and scope |
| --- | --- |
| [Smooth Fano specialization and tensor degeneration](Geometry.md) | The Picard-rank-two rational Fano construction reflects abstract smooth projective specialization over $k[[t]]$ exactly as tensor degeneration over $k((t))$. Over $\mathbb Q$ the specialization decision on canonical minrank presentations is polynomial-time equivalent to Hilbert's tenth problem. The reduction itself is unconditional. |
| [Polynomial Calculus degree for matrix nonisomorphism](AlgebraicComplexity.md) | Characterizes ordinary Polynomial Calculus refutation degree up to absolute factors from indecomposable multiplicities and their $p$-adic differences. It covers all geometrically nonisomorphic quiver representations in positive characteristic and geometrically noncongruent matrix tuples in odd characteristic. A companion bound uses one linear-entry inversion. |
| [Sharp-exponent symmetric quartic compression](BalancedQuarticCompression.md) | One rational affine map preserves and reflects exact orbits, directed orbit-closure containment and orbit-closure intersection for fixed mixed-tensor signatures. It achieves side length $O_{t,d}(n^{\lceil d/4\rceil})$ and a matching exponent when $4\mid d$ for objectwise algebraic reductions. [Exact marker control](check_quartic.py) |
| [Exact epimorphism counts from Sylow towers](GroupAlgorithms.md) | Deterministic polynomial-time decision, construction and counting from Cayley tables for arbitrary-length elementary abelian Sylow towers, even with a prescribed normal kernel bound. The first layer may have unbounded abelian exponent under the explicit kernel-bound condition. |

The proof-degree theorem addresses the degree part of Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She Question 7.3 in the stated characteristics and geometric model. The epimorphism theorem covers a subclass of Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar Question 3(4) and the corresponding counting case of their Question 2.
