# Sharp-exponent compression to one symmetric quartic

**Theorem.** Fix a signature of $t\ge1$ ordered mixed-tensor slots, each of order at most $d\ge1$, for a product of general linear groups. Suppose each named mode space has dimension at most $n$, with $n\ge 2$. There is a rational affine map from these tensor tuples to single tensors in $\operatorname{Sym}^2 W\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2 W^*$, with $\dim W=O_{t,d}(n^{\lceil d/4\rceil})$. The map preserves and reflects complex orbit equality, directed Zariski orbit-closure containment, and orbit-closure intersection. Exact orbit equality is also preserved and reflected over every characteristic-zero field. On explicitly listed rational coordinates the map takes polynomial bit time. When $4\mid d$ the exponent is optimal in the worst case for objectwise algebraic reductions, already for orbit-closure intersection. For these orders the output has $O_{t,d}(n^d)$ scalar coordinates.

The construction combines balanced product trees for tensor isomorphism with invariant recovery by internal partial-trace markers. The proof is informal.

## 1. Bundled data and fixed tensors

Write the source group as $G=\prod_j\mathrm{GL}(V_j)$. Construct a space $E$ as a direct sum of named spaces. Include a scalar line $L$ with a chosen $\ell$ and dual $\ell^*$ satisfying $\ell^*(\ell)=1$. For each original mode include separate summands $V_j$ and $V_j^*$, both as actual vector summands of $E$. The intended $G$ actions on them are the natural action and its dual. Connect each pair by an evaluation tensor with two dual slots and two copies of $\ell$ in the vector slots.

For each input tensor, partition its ordered list of slots into four consecutive chunks of sizes at most $k=\lceil d/4\rceil$. For each nonempty chunk form its product space $C$ using a binary tree with the original slot spaces as leaves. Include each internal product $C=A\otimes B$ as a new summand. A fixed tensor in $A\otimes B\otimes C^*\otimes L^*$ is the canonical splitting map $C\to A\otimes B$ tensored with $\ell^*$. The two child slots are ordered even when their named summands coincide. Include a distinct dual partner $D=C^*$ for each nonempty chunk assigned to a dual slot of the final data tensor. Include both $L^{\otimes2}\otimes C^*\otimes D^*$ and $C\otimes D\otimes(L^*)^{\otimes2}$ with the canonical evaluation and coevaluation coefficients and the chosen scalar vectors.

Reshape the input into a balanced quartic on $E$. Put the first two chunk indices in vector slots and the last two in covector slots, identifying the vector space $C$ of a covector chunk with $D^*$. Fill empty chunks with $\ell$ or $\ell^*$ as appropriate. Call this quartic $Z_i$. Finally include the quartic $P_B\otimes I_E$, with the two identity-map pairs reordered into the four slots, for every named summand $B$. Here $P_B$ is its coordinate projection and $I_E$ is the identity. Let $A_0$ denote the ordered list of fixed quartics. The intermediate output is $(A_0,(Z_i)_i)$, with one $\mathrm{GL}(E)$ acting on every component.

The original group embeds by $\beta$, acting on every summand by its declared tensor product or dual action and trivially on $L$. Under $\beta$ the fixed quartics are invariant and the data quartics transform as the inputs do. There are $O(t(d+1))$ fixed and variable components after omitting unused group factors. Since every intermediate product uses a subset of the indices in one chunk, $\dim E=O(t(d+1)n^k)$. One may omit source slots using a zero-dimensional factor, whose tensor is necessarily zero. If all source components are scalar or zero, only the scalar line is needed.

## 2. The three relations in the marked system

**Exact orbits.** Let $h$ fix $A_0$. Taking the partial trace of each projection tensor gives $(\dim E)P_B$, so $h$ preserves every named summand. Write $u$ for its action on $L$ and $g_B=u^{-1}h|_B$ for its normalized action on a named summand. The evaluation tensors force $g_{V_j^*}=g_{V_j}^{-T}$ and $g_D=g_C^{-T}$. Each splitting tensor gives $g_C=g_A\otimes g_B$ for its two child spaces. Thus $h=u\beta(g)$ for some $g\in G$. Conversely these maps fix the anchors. The common scalar acts trivially on balanced quartics, so the exact orbit relation is reflected. This uses only linear algebra over the coefficient field.

**Directed containment.** For a linear action of a complex algebraic group $H$, fix an anchor tuple $a$. If $(a,y)\in\overline{H(a,x)}$, take a sequence $h_\nu(a,x)\to(a,y)$ in the Euclidean topology. Such a sequence exists since the orbit is constructible and its Euclidean and Zariski closures coincide over $\mathbb C$. The orbit of $a$ is a smooth locally closed submanifold and $H\to Ha$ is a submersion. A local section at $a$ gives $u_\nu\to 1$ with $u_\nu a=h_\nu a$. Thus $u_\nu^{-1}h_\nu$ fixes $a$ and sends $x$ to a sequence converging to $y$. Hence $y\in\overline{H_a x}$. Apply this using the exact stabilizer computed above. Equivariance gives the reverse implication.

**Closure intersection.** Every polynomial invariant of the source is a linear combination of complete contractions of copies of input slots. This is the mixed-tensor first fundamental theorem for products of general linear groups. Each such contraction lifts to an invariant of the marked quartic tuple as follows.

1. Use a copy of a $Z_i$ for each occurrence of an input tensor. For each covector chunk, pair the $D^*$ slot with the $D$ slot of its coevaluation tensor. This transports the chunk to a vector slot on $C$.
2. For each non-leaf chunk, feed its vector into the $C^*$ slot of its splitting tensor and repeat on each child. The exposed leaves lie in the declared copies of $V_j$ or $V_j^*$.
3. For each original contraction edge, feed its two leaves into the two dual slots of their evaluation tensor. All ports still free are on the scalar line and its dual. Pair them in any order. Their counts agree because every quartic contributes two slots of each variance and every edge already used removes one of each.

This is one complete contraction on copies of components of the marked tuple. It is a polynomial invariant on arbitrary tuples. On the image the splitting and coevaluation maps undo the grouping and each scalar edge contributes one, so the lifted invariant equals the original. Equality of target invariants forces equality of source invariants, hence intersection of source orbit closures. Equivariance proves preservation.

## 3. Packing the whole tuple into one symmetric quartic

This step applies to any $s$ labeled balanced quartics on one space $E$. Set $r=4\lceil s^{1/4}\rceil$ and partition the colors into four equally sized sets. Take $W=\bigoplus_{a=1}^r E_a$ with every $E_a$ a copy of $E$. Assign the components distinct ordered four-color signatures by choosing one color from each set in order. Embed each component in its block and independently symmetrize the two vector slots and the two dual slots by the sum of their swaps. Because the four colors are distinct, the chosen ordered block has the original entries and the partial trace is zero. The two unordered color pairs distinguish every data component.

Write $P_a$ for the color projections, $E_{ba}$ for identity transfer from $E_a$ to $E_b$ extended by zero, and $N=\dim E$. Let $S$ denote the unnormalized symmetrization and $\tau$ the trace over the second vector–covector pair. Add the following markers to the data sum:
$$
\sum_a\frac{a}{2(N+1)}S(P_a\otimes P_a)
+\sum_{a\ne b}\frac{S(E_{ba}\otimes P_{c_{ab}})-S(E_{ba}\otimes P_{e_{ab}})}{N},
$$
where $c_{ab},e_{ab}$ are two distinct colors outside $\{a,b\}$. Reorder endomorphism tensor products into the four slots before applying $S$. Of the four symmetrization terms for $P_a\otimes P_a$, two contribute $NP_a$ under $\tau$, and the other two contribute $P_a$. For $E_{ba}\otimes P_c$ with all three colors distinct, only its ordered block $(b,c;a,c)$ survives the trace and contributes $NE_{ba}$. In particular the two spectator terms in each difference have canceling traces. The output $Y$ satisfies $\tau(Y)=\sum_a aP_a$.

The spectral projector polynomials of $\tau(Y)$ recover every $P_a$, using its fixed distinct eigenvalues $1,\ldots,r$. Projecting to the ordered block $(b,c_{ab};a,c_{ab})$ and applying $\tau$ recovers $E_{ba}$. No data term has a shared color between the two variance sets, and the transfer block has exactly the shared color $c_{ab}$. The remaining ordered colors fix the transfer direction. These are polynomial covariants on the whole target space. Their common stabilizer at an encoded tensor is the diagonal copy of $\mathrm{GL}(E)$, since preservation of all projectors makes a transporter block diagonal and preservation of the identity transfers equates all its blocks.

For exact orbits, apply the readouts to any transporter and extract the ordered data blocks. For directed containment, apply the readouts along any converging sequence from one output orbit to another output. Use the local section for the orbit of the matrix-unit tuple to align the readouts, then extract the corresponding limits of the data blocks. For closure intersection lift each contraction of the labeled input tuple by projecting its vector slots with the color projections and its covector slots with the dual projections. On an edge from color $a$ to color $b$ use the identity-transfer covariant before evaluation, using $P_a$ when $a=b$. The resulting polynomial is invariant by equivariance of each readout and naturality of evaluation. Its value on the image is the original invariant. This proves reflection for all three relations. Preservation follows by affinity and equivariance under the diagonal embedding.

Here $s=O(t(d+1))$ and $\dim W=r\dim E=O_{t,d}(n^k)$. The target uses $\binom{\dim W+1}{2}^2$ scalar coordinates. Its entries come from fixed placements, explicit identity tensors, and rational dimension factors with logarithmically many bits. The actual product dimension of any chunk is at most the number of dense coordinates of its source component. At fixed signature, the construction and writing the full output therefore take polynomial time in the input bits. The map is rational affine in the input entries.

## 4. Matching exponent for orders divisible by four

Use the one-group source $\operatorname{End}((\mathbb C^n)^{\otimes d/2})$, with $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb C)$ acting through its tensor power by conjugation. Its Hermitian real subspace has dimension $n^d$. Each Hermitian operator minimizes the Frobenius norm under conjugation, even by the general linear group on the full tensor product, by Schur triangularization and the lower bound given by the sum of squared eigenvalue moduli. Thus it also minimizes the norm under the smaller tensor-power group. Kempf–Ness gives a closed orbit and says that two minimum-norm points in the same orbit differ by a member of $\mathrm U(n)$.

Let $\alpha$ be an objectwise polynomial OCI reduction to one symmetric quartic of side $m$. Hermitian points in one fiber of $\alpha$ have intersecting source closures, so the fiber lies in a single $\mathrm U(n)$ orbit of real dimension at most $n^2$. At a point of maximal real differential rank on the Hermitian subspace, the constant rank theorem gives local fiber dimension at least $n^d-2\binom{m+1}{2}^2$. Therefore
$$
2\binom{m+1}{2}^2\ge n^d-n^2,
$$
which proves $m=\Omega(n^{d/4})$. This applies in particular to reductions required to preserve all three relations. It applies to objectwise algebraic maps as in Lysikov and Walter, Definition III.3.
