# Smooth Fano specialization is tensor degeneration

## Theorem

Fix three positive mode dimensions and let $A,B$ be ordered three-tensors of that format over a field $k$. There is a polynomial-size construction of smooth, geometrically integral, rational Fano varieties $X_A,X_B$ of geometric Picard rank two with the following equivalence. Write $R=k[[t]]$ and $K=k((t))$.

1. There are invertible mode maps over $K$ sending $A$ to a tensor over $R$ with reduction $B$.
2. There is a smooth projective $R$-scheme with generic fiber isomorphic to $(X_A)_K$ and special fiber isomorphic to $X_B$.
3. There is a smooth projective family over $\mathbb A^1_k$ with generic fiber isomorphic to $(X_A)_{k(t)}$ and fiber at zero isomorphic to $X_B$.

The family in condition 2 may have any projective presentation. The same construction preserves and reflects ordinary isomorphism. Over an algebraically closed field it therefore embeds the full directed tensor orbit-closure order into abstract smooth projective specialization. Deciding this specialization relation is NP-hard over every fixed finite field and over $\mathbb C$. Over $\mathbb Q$, on the canonical Fano presentations arising from the minrank construction below, it is polynomial-time equivalent to Hilbert's tenth problem over $\mathbb Q$. All outputs of one format have the same integral Chow ring, anticanonical class, and Tate Chow motive. Over finite fields they also have the same point counts over every finite extension.

This is an informal proof. The construction comes from the earlier MathProofs isomorphism theorem. The new step is reflection of specialization, including arbitrary smooth projective families. [Earlier isomorphism and forms theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorIsomorphismAdvances/GEOMETRY.md)

## Quadratic systems and the two intrinsic rulings

Write the tensor as a linear family $A(z)$ of matrices, with $z$ in the label space $Z$. Use six disjoint pairs of blocks of linear forms. Three pairs carry copies of $A(z)$ and three carry the universal row $F(z)$, the linear functional on $Z^*$ given by evaluation at $z$. Independently pad the twelve individual blocks to distinct dimensions at least two, in a fixed order depending only on the input format. Write $E$ for the direct sum of the twelve spaces of linear forms $E_i$, with $m=\dim E$. Let $D=\bigoplus_i\operatorname{Sym}^2 E_i$ and let $B_A(z)$ be the direct sum of the six mixed components. Set

$$S_A=D\oplus B_A(Z)\ \subseteq\ \operatorname{Sym}^2 E,\qquad r=\dim S_A.$$

The three row copies make $B_A$ injective. The left–right matrix of every nonzero $B_A(z)$ has rank at least three. Here the left space is the sum of the first blocks of the six pairs, and the right space is the sum of the second blocks. For any product $\ell\ell'$ of two linear forms, its left–right matrix has rank at most two. Consequently if $\ell\ell'\in S_A$, it belongs to $D$. In particular, for a nonzero $\ell\in E_i$, the subspace

$$C_A(\ell)=\{\ell'\in E\mid\ell\ell'\in S_A\}$$

is exactly $E_i$. If $\ell$ has at least two nonzero block components then $C_A(\ell)$ has dimension at most one. Indeed, the mixed tensor equation for any pair of occupied blocks forces the corresponding components of $\ell'$ to be scalar multiples of the components of $\ell$, with opposite coefficients. Components outside the occupied blocks vanish. Thus the set where $\dim C_A(\ell)\ge2$ is the union of the $E_i$. The distinct dimensions label these blocks intrinsically. This argument is valid in characteristic two.

Define $X_A$ by $q(x)=0$ in $\mathbf P(E^*)\times\mathbf P(S_A)$, with coordinates $(x,[q])$. The system contains all squares of the chosen coordinate forms, so evaluation $S_A\otimes\mathcal O\to\mathcal O(2)$ is surjective. The first projection is the projective bundle of the kernel. This proves smoothness, geometric integrality and rationality. Writing $H_x,H_y$ for the two restricted hyperplane classes, the projective-bundle formula gives $\operatorname{Pic}(X_A)=\mathbb ZH_x\oplus\mathbb ZH_y$, also after field extension. Adjunction gives

$$-K_{X_A}=(m-2)H_x+(r-1)H_y.$$

Both coefficients are positive. The Chern class of the kernel is $(1+2H_x)^{-1}$, which gives the stated Chow ring. The projective-bundle motive and point counts depend only on $m,r$. [Stacks Project, projective-bundle formula](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02TX)

The nef cone is the nonnegative span of $H_x,H_y$. Both are globally generated. A line in a fiber of the first projection tests the $H_y$ coefficient. A curve in a geometric quadric fiber of the second projection tests the $H_x$ coefficient. We have $m\ge4$ and $r>m$. The restriction sequence from the product gives

$$h^0(H_x)=m,\qquad h^0(H_y)=r,\qquad h^0(H_x+H_y)=mr,\qquad H^1(X_A,H_x)=H^1(X_A,H_y)=0.$$

For these three classes the kernel terms in the restriction sequences have bidegrees $(-1,-1)$, $(-2,0)$, and $(-1,0)$, respectively, so the cohomology claims follow from the cohomology of projective space. Any abstract isomorphism sends the two primitive nef rays to their counterparts because their section dimensions differ. The two complete linear systems give the original embedding into the product. Hence abstract isomorphisms induce congruences of the quadratic systems. Conversely these congruences induce isomorphisms. They are block diagonal by the intrinsic $E_i$ characterization. The universal rows identify one common invertible label substitution and the matrix components recover the tensor orbit, by restricting to the used supports and extending the resulting maps. Any tensor transporter extends to the padded blocks by acting on the three matrix copies, acting contragrediently on the row label spaces, and fixing the auxiliary coordinates. [Earlier orbit argument and polynomial presentation](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorIsomorphismAdvances/GEOMETRY.md)

## Quadratic-system specialization reflects tensor degeneration

Suppose a direct summand $\mathcal S\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2 E_R$ has special fiber $S_B$ and generic fiber $gS_A$ for $g\in\mathrm{GL}(E_K)$. For each $i$, intersect the subspace $gE_{i,K}$ with $E_R$ to obtain a saturated lattice $U_i$, which is a direct summand over the DVR. Then $\operatorname{Sym}^2 U_i\subseteq\mathcal S$ by saturation. Every nonzero element of the reduction $\overline U_i$ has $C_B$ of dimension at least two. Apply the union-of-blocks characterization after extending the residue field to an algebraic closure. A vector subspace contained in a finite union of linear spaces over an infinite field lies in one member, so $\overline U_i\subseteq E_j$ for some $j$ and $\dim E_i\le\dim E_j$.

Process the original blocks in decreasing dimension. The largest specializes to itself by dimension. Once a block specializes to itself, a regular change of basis reducing to the identity can make it constant while fixing the preceding constant blocks. To do this, lift the prescribed special bases to the lattice summands and extend them to a basis of $E_R$.

It remains to rule out a collision into a preceding constant block $E_j$. Suppose $\overline U_i\subset E_j$ with $U_j=E_{j,R}$. Write $E_R=E_{j,R}\oplus E_{\mathrm{rest},R}$. The generic subspaces $U_{i,K}$ and $E_{j,K}$ are disjoint, so there is a finite smallest valuation $b\ge1$ of a nonzero outside component of an element of $U_i$. Choose $w=u+t^bv$ in $U_i$ attaining this valuation. The inside component $u$ and outside component $v$ both have nonzero reductions. Choose $w'=u'+t^bv'$ in $U_i$ with $\bar u'$ linearly independent of $\bar u$, which is possible since the reduction of $U_i$ has dimension at least two and lies in $E_j$. Both $ww'$ and $uu'$ belong to $\mathcal S$. Saturation gives an element $t^{-b}(ww'-uu')\in\mathcal S$ whose reduction is

$$\bar u\,\bar v'+\bar u'\,\bar v.$$

This element is nonzero by independence of $\bar u,\bar u'$ and $\bar v\ne0$. It is entirely off diagonal with respect to the twelve blocks. Its left–right matrix has rank at most two. Such an element cannot belong to $S_B$, since its $B_B(Z)$ component would have rank at least three if nonzero and its $D$ component would be diagonal. This contradiction excludes the collision in every characteristic. By induction all reduced blocks have their original labels, and we can assume all the $U_i$ equal $E_{i,R}$, with $S_B$ unchanged.

Now $g$ is block diagonal over $K$. The submodule $\mathcal S$ contains $D_R$ and decomposes into $D_R$ plus a rank-$\dim Z$ mixed summand. Projection to the original row coordinates in one universal-row pair identifies this mixed summand with $Z_R$. It is an isomorphism because its reduction is the original row identification at $S_B$. In these label coordinates project to the first matrix pair. The result is a padded tensor over $R$, reducing to padded $B$ and generically a translate of padded $A$, with one invertible substitution of the labels.

Padding can be removed over the DVR. The images of the two original unpadded mode spaces under the generic block maps extend to saturated direct summands by intersection with the respective mode lattices. The padded coefficient tensor lies in their product over $R$, since these are direct summands and containment holds generically. Their reductions contain the corresponding supports of padded $B$. A constant invertible map sends each limiting summand to the prescribed unpadded mode space and fixes the support of padded $B$ pointwise. Such a map extends an isomorphism between the two subspaces that is the identity on the common support. Use bases lifting the new limiting summands to make them the prescribed unpadded mode spaces over $R$ by a regular change of basis reducing to the identity. Restricting to those spaces produces a tensor over $R$ with generic fiber isomorphic to $A$ and reduction $B$.

## Abstract smooth families yield quadratic-system specializations

Let $\mathcal X$ be a smooth projective $R$-scheme with the two abstract fibers in condition 2. It is regular and integral, since it is smooth over a regular base with an integral generic fiber and flatness excludes components supported in the special fiber. On $X_B$, the projective-bundle presentation gives $H^1(\mathcal O)=H^2(\mathcal O)=0$. Consequently the reduction maps on Picard groups for successive thickenings are bijective by the units sequence. Grothendieck existence algebraizes the compatible invertible sheaves, so $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal X)\to\operatorname{Pic}(X_B)$ is an isomorphism. Restriction $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal X)\to\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal X_K)$ is also an isomorphism. A generic divisor extends by closure and is Cartier by regularity. The kernel is generated by vertical prime divisors, and the only such divisor is the integral special fiber, which is principal. [Stacks Project, Grothendieck existence](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/087V)

Lift the two special ruling line bundles to $\mathcal L_x,\mathcal L_y$ on $\mathcal X$. Their special $H^1$ vanishes, so cohomology and base change gives free modules of global sections of ranks $m,r$, respectively, with sections commuting with base change. Evaluation is surjective on the special fiber, and properness implies global generation throughout $\mathcal X$. Thus the two generic classes are nef and form an integral basis. Express them in the intrinsic basis of the generic $X_A$. Any nonnegative combination with positive $H_y$ coefficient has at least $r$ sections, by multiplication of sections of $H_y$ by a nonzero section of the other factor. The class of $\mathcal L_x$ therefore has $H_y$ coefficient zero. Primitivity now forces $\mathcal L_x|_K=H_x$. The basis determinant forces the $H_y$ coefficient of $\mathcal L_y|_K$ to be one. If this class also had positive $H_x$ coefficient it would have at least $mr$ sections by multiplication of sections of $H_x+H_y$. Thus $\mathcal L_y|_K=H_y$.

The two complete linear systems now give a map $\mathcal X\to\mathbf P_R^{m-1}\times\mathbf P_R^{r-1}$ that is a closed immersion on both fibers. Properness and fiberwise quasi-finiteness give finiteness. The map on structure sheaves for the finite morphism is surjective by checking the fibers and applying Nakayama, so the map is a closed immersion. Its image is an integral effective Cartier divisor in the regular product. The line bundle of this divisor has bidegree $(2,1)$, read from the special fiber using the Picard group of the projective-space product over $R$. Its equation is therefore a tuple of $r$ quadrics in the first variables. A minor witnessing independence in the special fiber is a unit, so their span is a direct summand $\mathcal S$ over $R$. Choosing bases that match the special ruling coordinates gives special system $S_B$. The intrinsic generic rulings give congruence of the generic system to $S_A$. The preceding reflection proof gives condition 1.

Conversely, given condition 1, truncate the three Laurent series matrices above a sufficiently large common exponent. There are finitely many entries and a finite minimum valuation among all entries. By taking the exponent large enough, the change in their action on $A$ has valuation at least one in every coordinate, and the leading coefficient of each matrix determinant is unchanged. The truncated action therefore gives a polynomial tensor over $k[t]$ with value $B$ at zero, and the truncated matrices are invertible over $k(t)$. Apply the quadratic-system construction over $k[t]$. The universal row makes the system free and the fixed coordinate squares make evaluation surjective throughout the base. The resulting incidence scheme is a projective bundle over $\mathbf P^{m-1}_{k[t]}$ and gives condition 3. Base change from $k[t]$ to $R$ gives condition 2.

## Exact arithmetic complexity on canonical source–target pairs

For a $d$-tuple $A_1,\ldots,A_d$ of $n$ by $n$ matrices over $k$, call its minrank at most one if some nonzero $k$-linear combination has rank at most one. Here the coefficient vector must be nonzero, as in the usual tensor minrank definition, so a dependent tuple has minrank zero. Put $s=1+(d-1)n$. In format $d$ by $s$ by $s$, take a universal source having slices $D_1,\ldots,D_d$, where the $D_i$ are identity blocks on pairwise disjoint coordinate sets of sizes $1,n,\ldots,n$. For $d=1$ take $s=\max(1,n)$ and use only a rank-one block. The target is the original tuple padded by zeros to $s$ by $s$. Then

$$
\text{the universal source Laurent-series degenerates to the target}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{the target tuple has minrank at most one over }k.
$$

For the forward implication, the generic first slice after arbitrary mode maps means there is a nonzero slice parameter over $K$ giving rank at most one. Multiply the parameter by a power of $t$ to obtain integral coordinates with at least one unit. The two-by-two minors then vanish over $R$ and over its residue field, where the parameter stays nonzero. Conversely choose a slice basis putting the rank-at-most-one slice first. Factor every padded slice as $P_i Q_i$ using inner dimensions $1,n,\ldots,n$, permitting zero columns and rows. A single $P$ and a single $Q$ obtained by concatenating these columns and stacking these rows satisfy $P D_i Q=P_i Q_i$ for every $i$. Extend these maps by zeros on unused coordinates when needed. The matrices $P+tI$ and $Q+tI$ are invertible over $K$ since their determinants are monic polynomials. They carry the universal source to a tensor regular at $t=0$ with the desired reduction, after undoing the slice basis change. This is the universal source of the minrank orbit-closure reduction equipped with an exact argument over the base field. [Bläser et al., Sections 2 and 4](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3129310/)

A homogeneous system $q_j(x)=0$ of quadrics in $n$ variables reduces to minrank at most one by linear algebra. In the vector space of symmetric $n$ by $n$ matrices put one homogeneous linear equation for each $q_j$, replacing $x_i x_l$ by the entry at $(i,l)$. A basis of the common kernel gives the tuple of slices. A nonzero common zero yields the nonzero rank-one matrix $xx^{\mathsf T}$ in the kernel. Conversely, a nonzero symmetric rank-one matrix is of the form $cxx^{\mathsf T}$ with $c\in k^\times$, in every characteristic, so it gives a common zero. For a zero-dimensional kernel the answer is negative and one can use the single slice $I_2$ as a fixed negative instance. Bläser et al., Theorem 2.2, gives NP-hardness of homogeneous quadratic feasibility over every field via a single three-colorability construction using coefficients in $\{-1,0,1\}$. Together with the Fano reduction this gives the all-fixed-finite-field hardness claim, with arithmetic and linear algebra polynomial in the ordinary input size. [Bläser et al., Theorems 2.2 and 2.3](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3129310/)

Over $\mathbb Q$ one can reduce arbitrary polynomial solvability to homogeneous quadratic feasibility without introducing false solutions at infinity. First express a rational polynomial equation by a system $G_j(z)=0$ of affine quadratic equations for its arithmetic gates. For each equation use its homogeneous quadratic form $G_j^{\mathrm{hom}}(x,b)=b^2G_j(x/b)$ with homogenizing variable $b$. Impose

$$
G_j^{\mathrm{hom}}(x,b)=0,\qquad b h=8u^2-h^2,\qquad
\sum_i x_i^2+b^2+u^2+\sum_{a=1}^4w_a^2=h^2 .
$$

A nonzero rational solution has $h\ne0$ by the last equation and can be normalized to $h=1$. Then $b\ne0$ since $1/8$ is not a rational square, and $z=x/b$ solves the original system. In the other direction, given any rational solution $z$, choose $u\in\mathbb Q$ sufficiently close to $1/\sqrt8$, and put $h=1$, $b=8u^2-1$, $x=bz$. Then $\sum_i x_i^2+b^2+u^2<1$. The positive rational slack is a sum of four rational squares, by clearing a square denominator and applying Lagrange's theorem. This supplies the $w_a$. This homogenization adapts the compactification used in the MathProofs rational Nash reduction. [Earlier compactification](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TernaryRationalNash/PROOF.md)

Composing gives a polynomial-time many-one reduction from $\mathrm{H10}(\mathbb Q)$ to existence of a smooth projective $\mathbb Q[[t]]$ specialization between these rational Fano varieties. The reverse reduction on this canonical promise family reads the target slice matrices in their designated blocks. It asks for a nonzero rational slice parameter with every two-by-two minor zero. Nonzeroness can be written as $\sum_i v_i y_i=1$ with additional variables, and finitely many equations over $\mathbb Q$ can be combined by a sum of squares. This proves the unconditional equivalence with $\mathrm{H10}(\mathbb Q)$ and isolates its exact premise if one invokes an external undecidability theorem over $\mathbb Q$.

## Geometric and real closure consequences

Over an algebraically closed field, membership in an orbit closure is equivalent to the existence of a Laurent-series degeneration. For the forward direction take a curve through the limit meeting the orbit, normalize and complete it. The generic orbit point lifts to a group element after a finite extension of the fraction field. Complete its valuation ring, which still has residue field $k$, and choose its uniformizer. The reverse direction follows by specialization of the orbit closure equations. Thus the theorem transfers the NP-hardness of directed three-tensor orbit containment in the closure established by Bläser, Ikenmeyer, Lysikov, Pandey and Schreyer. Their complexity result over $\mathbb C$ uses finite input coefficients. The Fano reduction copies the tensor entries and uses fixed coefficients $0,1$, with $m$ linear and $r$ quadratic in the largest input mode dimension. [Bläser et al., Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 3.1](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3129310/)

Over $\mathbb R$ the real Laurent-series formulation is equivalent to Euclidean closure of the real tensor orbit. Semialgebraic curve selection and choice give a real Puiseux path of transporters tending to any limit, and reparametrization makes the exponents integral. Conversely sufficiently high Laurent truncations of formal transporters give real invertible maps for small positive $t$, with the required tensor limit. Therefore smooth projective specialization over $\mathbb R[[t]]$ on the constructed rational Fano inputs is $\exists\mathbb R$-hard by the real three-tensor version of their Theorem 1.2. On these canonical presentations the reduction is reversible in polynomial time by reading the entries in the designated mixed quadrics, so the corresponding promised decision problem is $\exists\mathbb R$-complete. [Bläser et al., Theorem 1.2 and Section 3.1](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3129310/)
