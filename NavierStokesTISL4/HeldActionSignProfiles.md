# Determinant signs of filtered action tensors over the ternary field

Let $F=\mathbb F_3$. Write an action tensor in normalized form as $A\times M\to M$, $(a,v)\mapsto av$, where $A$ is a nonzero finite-dimensional unital associative $F$-algebra and $M$ a faithful left module. The raw input can use three independent bases. Write $P_j$ for ternary 3-tensor isomorphism with $j$ indicated determinant-one constraints. Each theorem below is a deterministic polynomial-time reduction on its recognizable input family.

**Theorem 1.** Suppose $A/J$, for $J=\operatorname{rad}(A)$, is split over $F$. In every positive radical grade, suppose that every nonzero simple bimodule type has an even matrix size on at least one of its two sides. Suppose the odd-size simple factors of $A/J$ have distinct fingerprints as defined below. Then $P_3$ reduces many-one to $P_1$ on raw action tensors of this kind. A computable additional correction test reduces it directly to $P_0$ and determines the entire sign profile whenever an ordinary transporter exists.

**Theorem 2.** There is a recognizable, ordinary-TI-complete subclass of faithful action tensors over $F$ with scalar centroids, even dimensions in all three roles, and fixed-label indecomposable multiplicity gcd one in each role. A single ordinary orbit of any member contains exactly four $P_2$ classes when both module determinants are tested. The full $P_3$ problem on the family reduces many-one to ordinary TI. The construction works for unbounded nilpotence length and absent structural coordinates.

The Navier–Stokes certificate computes its unresolved multipliers by testing the held equation against adjoint directions after the controlled correction has been separated. It also projects onto labeled harmonic sectors, so local components can be compared and assembled. Here holding one input at the unit isolates exact algebra and module semilinearity. On each radical grade the semisimple matrix-unit projections separate the two endpoint actions from the unknown multiplicity map. Summing the signs of the grades leaves at most one unresolved bit in Theorem 1. Theorem 2 has an additional projective corner calculation that cancels that bit. This uses finite algebraic identities, with no analytic hypothesis. ## 1. Normalize the raw action

For $T:X\times Y\to Z$, require $\dim Y=\dim Z$ and injectivity of the slice map from $X$. Compute the slice space $\mathcal S\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(Y,Z)$ and its left idealizer $A=\{a\in\operatorname{End}(Z):a\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal S\}$ by linear equations. Find a cyclic generator $s$ of the left $A$-module $\mathcal S$ and test its invertibility. To find one, take $\mathcal S/J\mathcal S$. In the factor $M_d(E)$ of the semisimple quotient, the component is cyclic precisely when the multiplicity $t$ of its natural simple module is at most $d$. If $w_1,\ldots,w_t$ are a basis over $E$ in the first matrix-unit image, then $\sum_{i=1}^t e_{i1} w_i$ is a generator. Sum the generators and lift. Its generated submodule is all of $\mathcal S$ because the cokernel equals its product with the nilpotent radical. Semisimple decomposition and matrix coordinates are deterministic polynomial time over $F$.

If $s$ is invertible, changing the second raw role by $s$ and the first by $x\mapsto T(x,-)s^{-1}$ gives the faithful action $(A,Z)$. Conversely, in an action isotope $\mathcal S=A s_0$ for an invertible $s_0$ and the idealizer is exactly $A$. Every cyclic generator $s$ is invertible since $s_0=a s$ for some $a$. Record determinants of all frame changes. This is the existing action recognition construction. [MathProofs, action recognition and the transporter formula](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/84b007e1048cdf24df3ff66050cd5cc33bf979c7/DeterminantTensorRoutes/GeometryRoute.md) · [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

An ordinary transporter $(g,f,h)$ satisfies $g(a)f=ha$ on the module for every $a\in A$. Holding $a=1$ gives $u=g(1)=hf^{-1}$, a unit of the target algebra. Invertibility on the faithful module already gives an inverse in the algebra by Cayley–Hamilton. Therefore
$$
 g=L_u\phi,\qquad h=uf,\qquad \phi(a)=f a f^{-1}.
$$
Conversely any algebra isomorphism $\phi$, invertible $\phi$-semilinear module map $f$, and target unit $u$ define a transporter by those equations. We use three binary sign coordinates in this order. Inverting signs for the covariant tensor convention has no effect over the ternary field. A raw requirement becomes a normalized target sign vector by changing frames in both endpoints.

## 2. The exact correction group and an affine sign envelope

Write the simple factors of $A/J$ as $M_{d_i}(E_i)$. Set $\ell_i,r_i,m_i$ to the multiplicities modulo two of the $i$-th natural simple over $E_i$ in the regular left module, regular right module and $M$, respectively, summed over their radical grades. They are computed by matrix ranks on the indicated filtered spaces. A unit with residue matrix $a_i$ has determinant on any such module equal to the product of $\mathrm N_{E_i/F}(\det_{E_i}a_i)$ raised to these multiplicities. This follows by restriction of scalars and triangularity in a filtration basis. Every quotient unit lifts through $J$ and every norm determinant value is attainable. Likewise compute $H_M\le\mathbb F_2$, the determinant sign image of the module centralizer $\operatorname{End}_A(M)^\times$, by the same construction for that algebra.

The computable group $K$ of actual sign corrections used below is spanned by
$$
 (\ell_i,0,m_i),\qquad (\ell_i+r_i,m_i,m_i),\qquad
 (0,c,c)\quad(c\in H_M).
$$
The first comes from varying $u$. For the second take $\phi=\operatorname{Inn}_a$, $f=\rho(a)$ and $u=1$ for a unit $a$. The third comes from module-linear automorphisms. Every vector in $K$ is the sign of an actual stabilizer, not just a solution of linearized stabilizer equations. On an ordinarily equivalent input pair, both endpoints give the same group $K$. Hence the nonempty normalized sign profile is invariant under translation by $K$.

We use the following elementary consequence. Suppose a computed affine space $s_*+W$ contains every possible normalized sign and $K\subset W$. Reject if the target lies outside this space. For a target in the space, choose $\dim(W/K)$ independent linear sign probes separating $W/K$ and annihilating $K$. A transporter matching the target on those probes differs from it by an element of $K$, so a stabilizer correction makes the actual sign exact. The converse follows because an exact sign certainly matches the probes. The previously proved sign-projection compiler makes this a single instance of $P_{\dim(W/K)}$; when the dimension is zero, the original ordinary comparison suffices. This works even when the ordinary transporter set is empty. [Exact ternary sign projections](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/SignProjections.md)

## 3. Signs from even bimodule grades

Assume first all $E_i=F$. An intrinsic fingerprint for each factor is its matrix size together with the arrays of simple multiplicities on the left and right regular grades and on the module grades for that factor. Include zero multiplicities explicitly. Factor fingerprints are invariant under any algebra and module isomorphism. A mismatch in multisets therefore rejects. Choose one common ordering by fingerprint, with arbitrary orders of ties. The uniqueness hypothesis only concerns odd matrix sizes, so every quotient isomorphism has positive factor-permutation sign on $A/J$ in these frames. A block swap of dimension $d_i^2$ has sign $(-1)^{d_i^2}$, and permutations of odd blocks between distinct fingerprints cannot occur. On the individual matrix algebra a quotient isomorphism is inner and has determinant one.

For each positive grade $J^s/J^{s+1}$, use its standard direct-sum bases over the natural simple $M_{d_i}(F)$–$M_{d_j}(F)$ bimodules. A basis for the multiplicity space is obtained from the first diagonal matrix unit on each side. The other copies are obtained with the matrix units. Because the action on a grade is independent of radical lifts, this construction is Gaussian elimination. Component dimensions and correspondence are intrinsic. Lift all the grade bases to an ordinary ordered filtration basis of $A$. For a transporter, normalize the quotient isomorphism in those frames by writing it as the factor matching followed by conjugations by matrices $B_i$. On a $(i,j)$ component the grade map is
$$
 B_i\otimes B_j^{-t}\otimes U_{s,ij}
$$
for some invertible multiplicity map $U_{s,ij}$. All possible component swaps have even dimension and cause positive sign. Each $U_{s,ij}$ occurs $d_i d_j$ times in the determinant formula, so it also has positive sign by the even-size hypothesis. The remaining exponent of $\det B_i$ is $\ell_i+r_i$ modulo two: left multiplication on the $(i,j)$ grade contributes its multiplicity times $d_j$, and right multiplication contributes its multiplicity times the opposite side width. The semisimple grade's left and right multiplicities contribute twice $d_i$ and cancel.

It follows that the sign of $\phi$ is exactly the sign of conjugation by any target unit lifting the quotient tuple $(B_i)_i$. Its module sign counterpart is computed by $m_i$, and that simultaneous inner transformation is an actual correction in $K$. After removing the arbitrary unit and this inner correction, every remaining normalized transporter has sign in $\langle(0,1,1)\rangle$. We have proved the certified enclosure
$$
  W=K+\langle(0,1,1)\rangle.
$$
All tests that established the enclosure are computed from the given raw tensors. Apply the projection argument in Section 2. If $(0,1,1)\in K$ the profile is exactly $K$ whenever nonempty, and $P_0$ suffices. Otherwise one sign suffices. This proves Theorem 1 without assuming availability of any relative transporter or that the recognized inner and centralizer corrections exhaust the full group. The uniqueness condition can also be omitted when $(1,0,0)\in K$ because any residual odd quotient permutation contributes precisely that vector before the final module diagonal sign.

## 4. An independent projective enclosure

Suppose instead that $M$ is projective, with no restriction on the radical or on residue field extensions. Let $e_{i,r}$ be lifted matrix units on the diagonal of the $i$-th quotient factor; use $P_i=Ae_{i,1}$. As a left module $A\simeq\bigoplus_i P_i^{d_i}$. Let $b_i$ be the multiplicity of the $i$-th simple in $M/JM$. Choose lifts of top vectors in $e_{i,1}M$. Each choice gives a map $P_i\to M$ by multiplication, so the chosen generator maps give an epimorphism $Q=\bigoplus_iP_i^{b_i}\to M$ by the radical argument. Check equality of dimensions. If it holds this map is an isomorphism and gives a projective frame. If $M$ were projective the epimorphism would split and the kernel would have zero top, hence be zero by nilpotence. Thus this is an exact projectivity and frame test.

For completeness, lift orthogonal primitive idempotents successively in complementary corners by taking a sufficiently high power of three of any quotient-idempotent lift. It becomes idempotent since the nilpotent $x^2-x$ commutes with $x$. For primitive idempotents in the same matrix factor, lift off-diagonal units in the corresponding corners. Their products are units in those corners since they are units modulo their nilpotent radicals. Correct one of the lifts by the product inverse so that the two lifts are mutually inverse between the two corner identities. Their composites give the full set of matrix units. All lifts, powers and inverses are polynomial-time by linear algebra and bounded radical nilpotence.

For a feasible transporter, factor out a unit as in Section 1. Match quotient factors by their intrinsic types $(d_i,[E_i:F],b_i,\dim P_i)$ and order frames by these types. The quotient isomorphism is inner combined with an automorphism of the coefficient field and the factor matching. The field automorphism fixes the standard matrix units. Remove the inner part by conjugation by a lifted unit, with its simultaneous known effect on the module. Two systems of lifted matrix units now agree modulo the radical after matching indices. If they are called $e_{i,rs}$ and $f_{i,rs}$, then
$$
 v=\sum_{i,r} f_{i,r1}e_{i,1r}
$$
satisfies $f_{i,ab}v=v e_{i,ab}$ and equals one modulo the radical. Thus a second conjugation makes the isomorphism carry the exact selected matrix units to their counterparts. Its simultaneous changes lie in $K$.

The remaining algebra isomorphism on the $d_i$ regular copies of $P_i$ is the same map on each copy, because the fixed matrix units identify them. Its remaining semilinear module map is, up to a module-centralizer action, this same map on the $b_i$ copies of $P_i$. The sign of either full-block permutation is the permutation sign raised to its respective block dimensions $d_i\dim P_i$ and $b_i\dim P_i$. These permutations act only on equal-type blocks in the common order. Consequently the three residual signs lie in the span of
$$
      (d_i,b_i,b_i)\pmod 2.
$$
The projection argument gives one $P_r$ instance for $r$ equal to the rank of those rows modulo $K$. If $b_i\equiv d_i\pmod 2$ for every $i$, then $r\le 1$ for arbitrary radicals and residue fields. On inputs where Section 3 also applies, its calibrated enclosure can be intersected with this one. Convert both affine spaces to the original frames before intersecting. Their intersection is either empty, in which case there is no ordinary transporter, or a coset of a subspace containing $K$, so the same projection argument applies.

## 5. An indecomposable family attaining all four $P_2$ classes

Take any finite-dimensional local $F$-algebra $B$ with residue $F$ and even dimension $b>0$. Put $D=F\times M_3(F)$, $C=M_2(B)$, and
$$
 N=\bigoplus_{d\in\{1,3\}}\operatorname{Mat}_{d,2}(B)^2,\qquad
 A_B=\begin{pmatrix}D&N\\0&C\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The top $d$-block acts on the rows of its two corresponding rectangles, and $C$ acts by right multiplication. Let $e$ be the first diagonal matrix unit of $C$ and $M_B=A_Be$. The algebra acts faithfully. If an element annihilates $A_Be$, apply it to the arbitrary bottom columns of $C e$ to see that its bottom and upper-right parts are zero. Apply it to all upper columns in $N e$ to see that its top part is zero. The dimensions of $A_B$ and $M_B$ are $10+20b$ and $10b$, respectively. Its center is scalar: a central element is block diagonal and has the same scalar on each top factor and on the bottom because the $N$-actions are faithful and commute with that element. The action tensor's centroid equals the algebra center by substituting the unit in the centroid equations. The same center calculation works after field extension.

This family is recognizable. Normalize the raw action first. The quotient must have split factors of widths $1,2,3$. Choose lifted matrix units as in Section 4. Check that all off-diagonal corners except the two top-to-bottom ones vanish, and that the two top diagonal corners have dimensions $1,9$. The bottom corner is automatically $M_2(B)$ by its matrix units. The primitive corner $B=e A_Be$ has scalar residue and its dimension can be tested. In each top-to-bottom corner take the left and right first matrix-unit images. Check that this is the free right $B$-module $B^2$. This check uses two top generators and equality of dimensions, by the projective frame test for right modules. Recover all other coordinates from the units. Finally test that $M_B$ is one copy of the bottom primitive projective using its top and dimensions. Every test follows from the displayed model, and success reconstructs that model including its multiplication and all three frame determinants.

Ordinary equivalence of two action tensors in these frames holds exactly when their $B$'s are isomorphic. Indeed an algebra isomorphism of the triangular algebras preserves the bottom quotient factor, and the image of its primitive idempotent is conjugate to the bottom primitive idempotent in the other algebra by the matrix-unit argument. Thus it restricts, after adjustment, to an isomorphism of the $B$ corners. Conversely every isomorphism of $B$'s extends entrywise to the triangular algebra and to its primitive projective. The module requirement is automatic because the projective is modeled by that same corner.

We next prove that every ordinary transporter has positive signs on the two standardized module roles. A left unit acts positively on the module. Its regular simple multiplicities in the module, for the two top factors and bottom factor, are $2b,2b,b$, respectively, all even. One can also compute them from the dimensions of the lifted idempotent projections, since along each module grade the sum of the dimensions of their projected subspaces telescopes to the dimension on the original module. Consequently inner conjugations, when implemented on the module by the corresponding unit, are also positive.

Remove those inner changes to align the exact chosen matrix units. On the bottom corner the algebra isomorphism acts entrywise by an underlying $\psi:B\to B'$. On the top it fixes the matrix coordinates in the standard models. After removing $\psi$ entrywise, the only remaining transformations are automorphisms of the two $D$–$C$ bimodules in $N$. At their first corners they are right $B$-linear automorphisms of $B^2$. On the projective, each of these maps acts on its first-column section repeated $d$ times. Its determinant on the $F$-space $B^2$ is $\det_F(\overline U)^b$, where $\overline U\in\operatorname{GL}_2(F)$ is its residue matrix, by applying the radical filtration of $B$ entrywise. This is positive. The removed $\psi$ acts on $M_B$ in $10$ copies, hence positively as well. Finally a module-centralizer unit acts by right multiplication in $B^\mathrm{op}$ because $\operatorname{End}_{A_B}(A_B e)\simeq(eA_Be)^\mathrm{op}$. This is right multiplication on $10$ copies of $B$, with positive determinant. This exhausts all module intertwiners. Both $f$ and $h=uf$ are therefore positive in standard frames.

The first role can attain either sign. A unit that is $-1$ on the one-dimensional top factor and the identity on every other diagonal factor has negative determinant on the regular algebra module, since its contribution is repeated $1+4b$ times. Its action on the held module is positive. Thus the normalized profile for every ordinarily isomorphic pair is exactly $\{(0,0,0),(1,0,0)\}$. In raw frames this is an explicitly known translate. Reject an inequivalent family type or a sign outside that translate, and return an ordinary TI instance otherwise. Since both module signs can take four independent external basis twists within a common ordinary orbit, and its stabilizers preserve each sign, there are exactly four $P_2$ classes for that choice of retained modes.

There is no even matrix amplification hidden in the example. The fixed-label paired adjoint algebra on the module sides is $\operatorname{End}_{A_B}(M_B)$ with one-dimensional split residue. With one module role held, the paired adjoint algebra is the left or right regular unit algebra of $A_B$: the equation at $1$ identifies the adjustment as multiplication by its value there, and faithfulness gives the regular action on the label. Its quotient is a product with matrix widths $1,3,2$. Thus in each role the gcd of the indecomposable multiplicities is one by the fixed-label algebra criterion. [Prior matrix-factor criterion](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/84b007e1048cdf24df3ff66050cd5cc33bf979c7/SLMatchingDeterminantFactors/Proof.md)

For TI-hardness, use the local algebra
$$
 B(T)=F1\oplus U\oplus V\oplus W\oplus tF[t]/(t^L)
$$
for a nonzero concise tensor $T:U\times V\to W$. The product of the old radical spaces is only $UV\to W$ via $T$, all mixed products with $t$ vanish, and the $t$-branch has its usual product. Choose $L\ge4$ so that $b=\dim U+\dim V+\dim W+L$ is even. In the first radical grade the kernel of cubing into the third grade is the old $U\oplus V$: the $t$ coordinate is detected by its cube since $t^3\ne0$ in $J^3/J^4$. Multiplication among the recovered spaces into the second grade recovers $W$ and the two annihilator roles by conciseness. Thus $B(T)\cong B(T')$ exactly when the original tensors are ordinarily equivalent, and every ordinary transporter extends with the $t$-branch fixed. Equal support formats permit a common $L$. Inputs with unequal support formats and zero tensors can be handled by fixed yes/no comparisons from the family. This is an explicit polynomial reduction and $L$ can grow with the input. ## Verification scope

The proofs establish sufficient reductions for the stated recognizable classes. The four-class construction provides an exact and ordinary-TI-hard case with a nontrivial radical and a faithful indecomposable module. They do not assert a universal reduction for arbitrary raw three-tensors. The full algebraic proofs here are informal. The action normal form, finite algebra decomposition, local incidence encoding and linear sign-projection compiler are prior ingredients. The even-bimodule and projective sign enclosures, their joint use, and the triangular four-class family are the additions here.
