# Even radical bimodules with nonsplit residue fields

This extends the filtered action-tensor reduction to finite field extensions in the Wedderburn quotient. Retain the notation and construction of the actual correction group $K$ in [the main proof](HeldActionSignProfiles.md). Write $A/J=\prod_i M_{d_i}(E_i)$, where $E_i=\mathbb F_{3^{e_i}}$. A **positive radical component** means a nonzero summand of $J^s/J^{s+1}$ for $s\ge1$ under the left $i$-th and right $j$-th central idempotents.

**Theorem.** Require $d_i d_j$ even at every positive radical component. In addition require either of the following two conditions.

- The odd-size matrix factors all have odd $e_i$, and the fingerprints of these factors are pairwise distinct.
- $(1,0,0)\in K$.

Then the same exact affine enclosure $K+\langle(0,1,1)\rangle$ holds after deterministic raw-frame calibration. Thus one $P_1$ instance suffices, and ordinary TI suffices whenever $(0,1,1)\in K$. The ranks, correcting groups, and comparisons are computable in polynomial time. Field degrees and radical length may grow.

A fingerprint includes $(d_i,e_i)$ and the arrays of multiplicities of the natural simple modules in each left regular, right regular, and module radical grade. These are intrinsic and computable. Sort the quotient blocks by fingerprint and use one field model for every degree, the same at both endpoints. Explicit finite-field isomorphisms and Wedderburn maps can be constructed in polynomial time at fixed characteristic. For the local basis step, choose matrix units and a basis of each first diagonal matrix-unit image over its explicit coefficient field.

The positive-grade calculation employs the full pair of commuting actions. On a left $i$, right $j$ component the field algebra $E_i\otimes_F E_j$ splits into finite fields $L$, and the natural bimodule term in each such field has matrix width $d_i d_j$ over $L$. This follows by multiplying the left and right matrix units. They serve as the matricial part of $M_{d_i d_j}(L)$. The multiplicity module is the image under both first diagonal matrix units and the given field factor's idempotent. This is a polynomial-size matrix algebra over $F$. One can compute its semisimple coordinates directly or by decomposing the commutative field tensor product and using specialized Gaussian elimination. The actual field degree of every term is at most $e_i e_j$, which is polynomial in the explicit input dimension.

After matching and removing the quotient inner factors, a semilinear map can permute these field components, apply the induced $F$-isomorphism of the field factors on every coordinate, and apply an arbitrary $L$-linear map on the multiplicity part. A field component has even dimension $d_i d_j[L:F] t$ where $t$ is its multiplicity, so moving such blocks makes no sign contribution. Any determinant of a field isomorphism is repeated $d_i d_j t$ times, hence likewise disappears. A multiplicity determinant is repeated $d_i d_j$ times and has positive sign. The inner actions have exactly the determinant character of actual lifted inner conjugation on the algebra. For example, the contribution from a left matrix $B_i$ on this term is the norm of $\det_{E_i}B_i$ to $F$ raised to $d_j t[L:E_i]$, by the tower formula for field norms. This exponent is the left simple multiplicity. The corresponding right formula gives the right simple multiplicity with inverse determinant, which is the same sign over $F$.

The semisimple grade has one further potential sign. Coefficient-field Frobenius on a factor, in matched standard models, has determinant $(-1)^{e_i-1}$ on its field, by a normal basis or by its cyclic permutation of the geometric embeddings. This is repeated $d_i^2$ times on matrix entries. Under the first hypothesis it is positive, and odd-dimensional block permutations are excluded by the distinct fingerprints. Under the second hypothesis any sign from this grade contributes only $(1,0,0)$, which is an available actual correction. Positive radical grades have already been controlled under either hypothesis. No unknown permutation or field isomorphism has to be recovered from the transporter.

As in the split proof, remove the free unit and the known inner correction. The only remaining possible sign is common to the two module maps. The main proof's projection argument now applies. This is a written extension using the same finite-sector NS transfer. The proof requires only fixed-characteristic finite algebra structure algorithms, explicit field isomorphisms, and the previously established ternary sign-projection compiler.
