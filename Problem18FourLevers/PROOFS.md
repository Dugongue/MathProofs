# Four extensions and limits of the finite-field tensor isomorphism recognizer

These notes investigate the graph, cryptographic, general-linear, and mechanized-composition directions starting from [MathProofs Problem 1.8](../Problem18/README.md). Sections 1–3 are informal. Section 4 has a separately checked Lean assembly lemma. The cryptographic analysis concerns mathematical key distributions and synthetic tests.

## 1. Graphs can be encoded with deliberately uninformative marginals

Over any fixed odd finite field, graph isomorphism reduces to standard orthogonal three-tensor isomorphism with output dimensions $n,n,O(n)$. On the images of strongly regular graphs, the first two Gram operators are zero and the third has minimal polynomial of degree at most four. There is also a polynomial-time isomorphism algorithm on an explicit rooted spanning promise below. These statements do not settle isomorphism of arbitrary strongly regular graphs.

Let $A$ be the zero-diagonal adjacency matrix of a simple graph. Use a hyperbolic space with paired basis $u_i,v_i$, and put $x_i=u_i+\frac12\sum_j A_{ji}v_j$. Then the $x_i$ are independent and their Gram matrix is $A$. Form
$$
 T_A=\sum_i e_i\otimes e_i\otimes x_i.
$$
One may add an unused nondegenerate complement so that the hyperbolic space sits in the standard dot-product space with $O(n)$ dimensions. For example use a split hyperbolic space with an even number of planes, so its determinant is a square and its form is equivalent to the standard form over a finite field of odd characteristic. For a fixed field the basis conversion can be fixed on four-dimensional blocks.

A graph permutation lifts to its two permutations on the first modes and to the corresponding simultaneous permutation of the paired hyperbolic vectors. Conversely the first-two-mode support is the diagonal matrix space. Its only decomposable lines are the coordinate rank-one lines $e_i\otimes e_i$. Thus an isomorphism of tensors pairs these lines by a single permutation, and the third isometry carries each $x_i$ to a nonzero scalar multiple of its matched line. Gram zero versus nonzero is unchanged by such scalings, so that permutation preserves graph adjacency.

The first two Gram operators vanish because each $x_i$ has norm zero and the first two coordinate factors are orthonormal. With $V$ the column matrix of the $x_i$, the third Gram operator is $K=VV^*$, and $V^*V=A$. In the basis $(x_i,v_i)$ it has matrix $\begin{pmatrix}A&I\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$ on the original hyperbolic summand. For strongly regular parameters $(n,k,\lambda,\mu)$, $A^2=(k-\mu)I+(\lambda-\mu)A+\mu J$ and $AJ=kJ$. Hence a cubic annihilates $A$ and its product with $x$ annihilates $K$. The two classical strongly regular graphs with parameters $(16,6,2,2)$, the rook and Shrikhande graphs, have identical Gram similarity types in this encoding over $\mathbb F_5$. Their adjacency eigenvalues $6,2,-2$ stay distinct modulo 5, with multiplicities $1,6,9$, so they are similar and the displayed block matrices are similar. The checker verifies the parameters and that only the rook graph has a four-clique. Thus the three Gram similarity types alone cannot decide even on this explicit family of tensor encodings.

Here is a sufficient algorithmic condition beyond comparing spectra. Individualize an ordered tuple of $k$ vertices. Use the adjacency matrix and the diagonal projections onto the binary adjacency profiles to the marked vertices as labelled operators on the vertex space. Start with the standard vectors at the marks, and grow their joint invariant span by applying all labelled operators to the current basis and retaining independent results. Use a fixed order for this procedure. If the span is the whole vertex space, comparison against a target with specified marks is polynomial: apply the same generator words to the target marks. A graph permutation respecting the marks must transport these basis columns to those columns. If they fail to form a basis, reject. Otherwise compute the unique candidate map and check that it is a permutation preserving adjacency. Try all target ordered tuples. For any fixed $k$, recognition and search for a source tuple also take polynomial time by trying all source tuples. The condition is a rooted version of the walk-matrix approach with several operators. This gives an exact algorithm on a checkable promise and no claim of coverage for all strongly regular graphs.

The general reduction from graphs to classical-group tensor isomorphism is already in [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Part III](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135). The contribution here is the explicit control and collision of the three marginals, together with the stated rooted sufficient condition. Average-case isomorphism of uniform random graphs already has the classical [Babai–Erdős–Selkow algorithm](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1980-35.pdf). For the antecedent to the rooted condition see [Godsil, Controllable Subsets in Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3231).

## 2. Exact search for orthogonally masked alternating forms

Let $q$ be odd. An alternating three-form is encoded by independent coefficients $t_{ijk}$ for $i<j<k$ and extended by the signs of permutations. Work with the usual dot product to identify covariant and contravariant coordinates when applying orthogonal maps. Let $G(T)=T_{(1)}T_{(1)}^{\mathsf T}$. Alternation makes the three mode marginals equal. For a simultaneous orthogonal action $g^{\otimes3}$, the operator $G$ is transported by conjugation.

**Search theorem.** If the characteristic polynomial of $G(T)$ is squarefree, simultaneous orthogonal equivalence of $T$ and any alternating $T'$ can be decided and an exact transporter produced on a YES instance in expected polynomial bit time. For every fixed odd $q$, the fraction of uniform alternating $T$ satisfying this promise tends to
$$
 \left(1-\frac1q\right)\prod_{j\geq1}(1-q^{-2j})>0.
$$

For the algorithm, reject differing characteristic polynomials. In cyclic bases with common algebra $K=\mathbb F_q[z]/(f)$, each form-preserving intertwiner is multiplication by a unit of $K$. Self-adjointness gives the two transported bilinear forms as $(x,y)\mapsto\ell(xy)$ and $(x,y)\mapsto\ell'(xy)$ on $K$. There is a unique $h$ with $\ell(x)=\ell'(hx)$, by the nondegenerate pairing for $\ell'$. It is a unit by nondegeneracy of the other form. Thus the isometry multipliers are exactly the roots of $b^2=h$, found in the field factors of $K$ by finite-field factoring. If there is no root, reject. Use one root to align the Gram spaces. The remaining isometries are signs independently on each field factor. They act on a block indexed by three factor labels by the product of its three signs, counting repeated labels. Matching zero blocks impose no constraints. Each remaining block must agree up to a sign, which is a linear equation over $\mathbb F_2$ in the sign bits. Solve these equations and assemble the resulting simultaneous transporter. Factoring is Las Vegas and all vector-space dimensions and field bit lengths are polynomial in the input. This is the simultaneous-action version of the [nonsplit Gram transporter](../TensorAndAlgebraOpenQuestions/Finite-Field-Orthogonal-TI.md) and does not require any relation between independent mode transporters.

The density needs a separate argument because the entries of an alternating tensor are correlated. Let $S$ be a symmetric matrix. On $\Lambda^3 V$ write $D_S$ for the derivation that applies $S$ to each slot and adds the results. In the ordered exterior basis, direct expansion gives
$$
 \operatorname{tr}(SG(T))=2\langle t,D_S t\rangle.
$$
The factor 2 counts the two orders of the other two indices in the flattening. In particular the identity remains valid in characteristic 3. The matrix $D_S$ is symmetric. Thus the squared absolute value of the additive Fourier coefficient of the Gram law for $S$ is at most $q^{-\operatorname{rank}D_S}$, by the quadratic character-sum bound obtained by squaring and summing over differences. In characteristic 3 the law lives on the trace-zero hyperplane, with scalar $S$ giving the trivial character. In other odd characteristics nonzero scalar $S$ gives rank $\binom n3$.

Here are the rank bounds needed for all other characters. For nonscalar $S$, a vector $v$ and $Sv$ are independent for some $v$. If $\min_\lambda\operatorname{rank}(S-\lambda I)\geq2$ over an algebraic closure, there is a two-space $U$ with $U\cap SU=0$ and $\dim SU=2$. To prove this, fix $v,Sv$ and put $P=\langle v,Sv\rangle$. If no $w$ makes $v,Sv,w,Sw$ independent, then the images of $w,Sw$ modulo $P$ are proportional for all $w$ outside $P$. Varying $w$ by elements of $P$ and using two independent quotient directions shows $S(P)\subseteq P$. The quotient map preserves every line, hence equals $\lambda I$. Now $S-\lambda I$ has rank exactly two and image $P$. Choose a two-space transverse to $P$ and to the kernel of $S-\lambda I$. Both conditions are nonempty open determinant conditions on ordered pairs over an infinite field, so a simultaneous choice exists. Its vectors and their $S$ images are independent, as required.

With $r=\dim U$ equal to 1 or 2 in the two cases, choose a complement $L$ to $U\oplus SU$. Apply $D_S$ to $U\wedge\Lambda^2L$ and project modulo $U$. Only the terms where $S$ acts on $U$ survive, giving an injection onto $SU\wedge\Lambda^2L$. The rank lower bounds are therefore $\binom{n-2}{2}$ and $2\binom{n-4}{2}$ respectively. Ranks are unchanged by extension.

There are at most $q^{2n+1}$ matrices that are scalar plus rank at most one. The exceptional scalar is unique for $n>2$ and belongs to the base field by Frobenius. For the higher-rank case there are at most $q^{n(n+1)/2}$ symmetric matrices. Summing the squared Fourier bounds and applying Plancherel shows that the law of $G(T)$ is exponentially close in $n^2$ to uniform symmetric, or uniform trace-zero symmetric in characteristic 3. The terms in the squared bound have exponents at most
$$
 \frac{n(n+1)}2-2\binom{n-4}{2},\qquad
 2n+1-\binom{n-2}{2},\qquad 1-\binom n3 .
$$
The final term is needed only outside characteristic 3.

The squarefree probability under either uniform law tends to the stated constant. For a squarefree monic polynomial with $r$ irreducible factors there are $2^{r-1}$ square-class choices of nondegenerate invariant form giving the fixed determinant type, and each orthogonal centralizer has size $2^r$. Thus the number of symmetric matrices with that polynomial is $|O_n(q)|/2$. The count of squarefree monic polynomials with any fixed trace is $q^{n-1}(1-1/q)+O(q^{n/2})$, by polynomial Möbius inversion. The standard orthogonal group order divided by $2q^{n(n-1)/2}$ tends to $\prod_{j\geq1}(1-q^{-2j})$. These are the same squarefree counts used in the [earlier nonsplit-spectrum proof](../TensorAndAlgebraOpenQuestions/Finite-Field-Orthogonal-TI.md). The exterior-power Fourier estimate is the new distribution step.

There is also a direct decision transfer from the original independent-mode orthogonal problem when the alternating source has scalar centroid. If $(g_1\otimes g_2\otimes g_3)T=T'$, swapping the first two slots and using alternation on both sides gives the same equation with $g_1,g_2$ interchanged. It implies $(h\otimes h^{-1}\otimes I)T=T$ for $h=g_2^{-1}g_1$, hence $h$ has the same action on the first two slots. Alternation gives the same action on the third. Thus $h$ is a scalar by the centroid hypothesis. All three $g_i$ are therefore proportional, with proportionality factors signs because they are orthogonal. Their product of signs can be absorbed into one simultaneous map since cubing a sign fixes it. So a successful independent-mode decision on this scalar-centroid promise decides the simultaneous orthogonal orbit. The direct search proof above works without the centroid hypothesis.

**What this says about ALTEQ.** Under Algorithm 1 of the [ALTEQ specification](https://csrc.nist.gov/csrc/media/Projects/pqc-dig-sig/documents/round-1/spec-files/ALTEQ-Spec-web.pdf), a base alternating form is uniform and a mask is uniform in $\mathrm{GL}_n(q)$. This is a different action from the orthogonal variant above. In fact for $n\geq4$ and odd $q$, a uniform pair produced this way has equal exterior norms with probability at most
$$
 \frac2q+\frac{3|O_n(q)|}{|\mathrm{GL}_n(q)|}.
$$
For a fixed $g$, exterior norm preservation for all vectors is equivalent to $\Lambda^3(g^{\mathsf T}g)=I$. If $\Lambda^3 H=I$, the operator $H$ preserves every three-plane because it fixes their decomposable exterior lines. Intersecting such planes shows it preserves every line, so $H=\lambda I$ with $\lambda^3=1$. For each admissible $\lambda$, the solutions of $g^{\mathsf T}g=\lambda I$ are empty or one orthogonal coset. For every other $g$, norm equality at a uniformly sampled exterior vector is a nonzero degree-two equation, with probability at most $2/q$. This proves the bound, whose second term is exponentially small in $n^2$ for fixed $q$. Orthogonal equivalence would imply norm equality. Thus the search theorem applies to an explicitly different mask distribution, and supplies no key-recovery claim for published ALTEQ.

## 3. Ordinary TI survives inside orthogonal TI with identical invertible Grams

Over every fixed odd finite field, arbitrary ordinary three-tensor isomorphism has a deterministic affine-linear objectwise reduction to standard orthogonal three-tensor isomorphism with linear side blowup. Every output of a given side has the same three invertible Gram operators, each with one eigenvalue of multiplicity one and one of multiplicity equal to the remaining dimension. Thus even the promise of identical nonsingular Gram marginals does not reduce the worst-case difficulty of the ordinary action.

Use a hyperbolic space $W_0$ with three disjoint hyperbolic pairs $U_i\oplus U_i^*$, each $U_i$ of dimension $n$. Pad by at most a constant number of hyperbolic planes so that the total number is even. Add an orthogonal norm-one vector $e$. Then $W=W_0\perp Fe$ has standard determinant type and is isometric to the dot product over the finite field. Let $B$ be the inverse-pairing tensor on $W_0$, so $(h\otimes h)B=B$ for an isometry $h$. With $D=\dim W_0$, choose $\alpha$ so $D+\alpha^2$ is different from both 0 and 2. In characteristic at least 5 the squares of $0,1,2$ give three choices. In characteristic 3, if $D=2$ in the field, add two hyperbolic planes, and then choose $\alpha$ from $0,1$. The padding preserves evenness of the plane count. Let
$$
 C=B_{12}e_3+B_{13}e_2+B_{23}e_1+\alpha e_1e_2e_3,\qquad
 \Phi(X)=C+X_{U_1U_2U_3}.
$$
For the Gram in each mode the carrier contributes $2I$ on $W_0$ and $D+\alpha^2$ on $Fe$. Terms in the carrier have disjoint orthogonal supports in the two contracted slots. The data term has zero self-contraction, since the data slots are isotropic. A mixed carrier–data contraction either meets an $e$ in a contracted slot or pairs two distinct $U_i$ through $B$, so it also vanishes. This proves the claimed Gram formula, including invertibility.

A source transporter lifts to the same isometry on all three copies of $W$, acting by $g_i$ and its inverse transpose on the paired summands and trivially on the rest. Conversely each output isometry preserves $W_0$ because it intertwines the displayed two-eigenvalue Gram. Projecting all three slots to $W_0$ removes the carrier. The resulting isomorphism of the embedded data restricts to its singleton supports, and these maps extend to the equal-dimensional original source factors. Hence the reduction preserves and reflects ordinary isomorphism.

There is also a unitary version over $\mathbb F_{q^2}$ for odd $q$ and for even $q\geq8$. Use a hyperbolic Hermitian form and retain only the pairing terms on modes 12 and 13. Use coefficients with base-field norms $a,b$ and a scalar $e^{\otimes3}$ term with coefficient of norm $c$. The carrier Gram on $W_0$ is respectively $a+b,a,b$, and on $Fe$ it is $c,Db+c,Da+c$ in the three modes. The cross terms vanish by the same support and isotropy argument. For odd $q\geq5$, choose $a=b=1$ and $c$ outside $\{0,2,-D,1-D\}$. For $q=3$ arrange $D=1$ in the field by padding, and take $a=b=c=1$. For even $q\geq8$, choose distinct nonzero $a,b$, so $a+b\ne0$. Here $D=0$ in the field, and one can take $c$ outside $\{0,a,b,a+b\}$. All required norms exist. To preserve the pair tensors use $h$ in mode 1 and $\overline h$ in modes 2 and 3, choosing the blocks of $h$ as $g_1,\overline g_2,\overline g_3$ and their Hermitian duals. The reflection proof is the same Gram projection. All nondegenerate Hermitian forms of the target dimension are isometric to the standard form.

This strengthens the simple isotropic-embedding reduction by making every Gram nonsingular and fixing its entire matrix across all instances of the same size. It does not prove that these outputs pass the published Problem 1.8 recognizer or that an all-input ordinary algorithm follows from its uniform-input density guarantee.

## 4. A checked composition rule for unordered blocks

Suppose intrinsic decomposition has already reduced a pair's equivalence to a perfect matching between two lists of blocks. For each pair of blocks a local zero-error algorithm returns yes, no, or unknown. Put the confirmed yes edges into a first bipartite graph. Put yes and unknown edges into a second. A perfect matching in the first certifies yes. No perfect matching in the second certifies no. Otherwise abstain. This is more permissive than requiring every local comparison to finish. The two extreme completions of the unknown entries give opposite answers when the rule abstains, if the only information available is the independent edge answers. Additional relations among the edges may allow stronger inference.

The companion [Lean file](BlockOracle.lean) proves soundness and the exact abstention criterion of this assembly, given a correct Boolean perfect-matching procedure. It also proves totality if all edges are decided. A concrete polynomial-time application can instantiate the matcher with a standard bipartite matching algorithm and needs only polynomially many block comparisons. A tensor application still needs an intrinsic block decomposition and a proof that tensor equivalence is exactly that matching problem. The Lean interface checks the assembly theorem, rather than tensor extraction, matching runtime, or the new informal results above. The unchanged Problem 1.8 theorem and its kernel audit are documented in the repository.

Publication priority of these extensions is unestablished. The graph and carrier controls are finite diagnostics.
