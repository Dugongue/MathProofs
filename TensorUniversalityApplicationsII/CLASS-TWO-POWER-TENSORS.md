# A free tensor encoding of finite class-two groups

**Theorem.** Suppose two finite groups of nilpotency class at most two are specified by central presentations with their characteristic derived subgroups $C=[G,G]$ and abelian quotients $A=G/C$ in cyclic coordinates. Write $r$ for the larger number of cyclic generators of the two quotients, and $s$ for the larger number for the two derived groups. Let $k$ be the number of distinct nontrivial cyclic orders of the quotients. Take $M$ to be the least common multiple of the cyclic orders of both $A$ and both $C$, or any common multiple at least two. Group isomorphism reduces deterministically to ordinary free 3-tensor isomorphism over $R=\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$, with maximum side $O((k+1)^2(r^2+s+1))$. The same integer outputs give an exact reduction over $\mathbb Z$. The construction is polynomial in the binary length of the explicit central presentation. In particular for a $2$-group with $A,C$ killed by $2^e$, the target ring is $\mathbb Z/2^e\mathbb Z$ itself. A tensor witness yields a group witness in polynomial time in the explicit bit lengths of the witness and input.

The input presentation gives the orders of the cyclic generators of $C$ and $A$, coordinates of the commutators of chosen quotient lifts $x_i$, and the central values $x_i^{n_i}$, where the $i$-th quotient generator has order $n_i$. The cyclic orders themselves are supplied in binary. The assertion also gives a polynomial-time reduction from class-two groups of order $N$ in the Cayley-table model, with maximum side $O((1+\log N)^3)$. The modulus uses only the exponents of $A,C$, and the construction works directly with composite moduli without factoring them.

The result extends the group-to-tensor direction of Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun's Part V, §5.1, from odd-order class-two groups to even order. The new part at $2$ retains the power corrections in addition to the commutator. The ordinary free target uses the earlier presented-module reduction and the low-arity forcing construction from Open Question 7.4. The algorithmic assertion bounds the reduction to free tensor isomorphism. [Part V, §5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S5.SS1) · [Sun, *Faster Isomorphism for Groups of Class 2*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05920)

## 1. The commutator and power records

Write the abelian groups additively. The commutator is the well-defined alternating bilinear map $\beta\colon A\times A\to C$, using $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$. For a positive integer $m$, set $A_m=A[m]$ and $C_m=C/mC$. Define
$$
q_m\colon A_m\longrightarrow C_m,\qquad q_m(v)=\widetilde v^{\,m}\bmod mC.
$$
Here $\widetilde v$ is any lift in $G$. Its $m$-th power is in $C$. Changing the lift by a central element changes the power by a member of $mC$. Each $q_m$ obeys $q_m(tv)=tq_m(v)$ for every integer $t$. Collection in a group of class at most two gives
$$
q_m(v+w)-q_m(v)-q_m(w)=-\binom m2\beta(v,w)\pmod {mC}.
$$
Indeed $(xy)^m=x^my^m[y,x]^{\binom m2}$, since the commutator is central and moving each new $x$ past the $j$ preceding copies of $y$ gives $j$ commutator factors. The right side is bilinear. It is symmetric after taking the quotient, since twice its scalar coefficient is divisible by $m$, and it vanishes on the diagonal. For odd $m$ it vanishes identically. For a common record signature, use the union of the cyclic orders in the two presentations of $A$ and $A'$. This includes every cyclic generator order needed for sufficiency, even if the two presentations use different decompositions of the same abelian group.

Two such groups are isomorphic if and only if there are isomorphisms $u\colon A\to A'$ and $v\colon C\to C'$ preserving $\beta$ and every indicated $q_m$, using the restrictions to $A_m$ and the induced maps on $C_m$. For necessity use characteristicity of $C$ and apply the isomorphism to commutators and powers. For sufficiency choose any target lift $y_i$ of $u(\bar x_i)$. Agreement of $q_{n_i}$ means that $y_i^{n_i}$ differs from $v(x_i^{n_i})$ by a member of $n_i C'$. Adjust $y_i$ by a suitable central element to eliminate that difference. Agreement of $\beta$ gives the prescribed commutators of all adjusted lifts. The source is presented by the relations of $C$, its centrality, those commutators, and the prescribed powers. These relations give at most $|A||C|$ ordered normal forms by collection, and they hold in the actual group of that order. The map on adjusted lifts and on $C$ therefore defines a homomorphism. It is surjective since the maps on $A,C$ are surjective. Equal group orders make it an isomorphism.

For a fixed preserving pair $(u,v)$ the choices of the adjusted $y_i$ are independent. Once one choice is made, it can be multiplied by any element of $C'[n_i]$. Thus precisely $|\operatorname{Hom}(A,C')|$ group isomorphisms lift each preserving pair. This count concerns recovery from the quotient records.

To compute the records, the cyclic generator of $A_m$ in coordinate $i$ is $b_i=(n_i/d_i)\bar x_i$, of order $d_i=\gcd(n_i,m)$, with order-one factors omitted. Its $q_m$ value is $(m/d_i)$ times the additive coordinate vector for $x_i^{n_i}$. The polar coefficients come from the displayed formula for $q_m$ and the input commutator matrix. The inclusion $A_m\to A$ and the quotient $C\to C_m$ have diagonal coordinate descriptions. These operations use polynomial-time arithmetic in the explicit cyclic orders and the given coefficients. From a Cayley table one computes $[G,G]$ by generating the subgroup of commutators and computes $A$ by cosets. Cyclic coordinates on these finite abelian tables can be obtained from Smith normal form of the presentation with one additive generator $e_x$ per element and relations $e_x+e_y=e_{xy}$. Tracking the generator changes gives representatives, and the table computes the required commutators and powers. This has polynomial cost in the table size. [Kannan and Bachem on Smith form with multipliers](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

## 2. A quadratic record on a free cover

Let $F=\mathbb Z^d$ with ordered basis $e_i$. Let $J(F)$ be free on $E_i$, $D_i$ and $T_{ij}$ for $i<j$. Define the integer quadratic map
$$
\eta_F(x)=\sum_i x_i E_i+\sum_i\binom{x_i}{2}D_i+\sum_{i<j}x_ix_jT_{ij},\qquad x\in F.
$$
The formula applies to negative coordinates using $\binom z2=z(z-1)/2$. Its cross-effect is bilinear and $\eta_F(F)$ spans $J(F)$, since values at $e_i,2e_i,e_i+e_j$ generate the displayed basis. A function $q\colon F\to M$ to any abelian group with $q(0)=0$ and bilinear cross-effect $B_q(x,y)=q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)$ factors uniquely as $L_q\eta_F$ for a linear map $L_q\colon J(F)\to M$. The coefficients are $q(e_i)$ on $E_i$, $B_q(e_i,e_i)$ on $D_i$, and $B_q(e_i,e_j)$ on $T_{ij}$. The formula follows by expanding a sum with the cross-effect and using $q(ne_i)=nq(e_i)+\binom n2 B_q(e_i,e_i)$, obtained by the recurrence for consecutive integers. If $q(nx)=nq(x)$, then $L_q(D_i)=0$.

Choose the following linear map into a tensor product using the full ordered tensor coordinates:
$$
\begin{aligned}
j_F\colon J(F)&\longrightarrow F\oplus(F\otimes F),\\
E_i&\longmapsto(e_i,e_i\otimes e_i),\\
D_i&\longmapsto(0,2e_i\otimes e_i),\\
T_{ij}&\longmapsto(0,e_i\otimes e_j+e_j\otimes e_i).
\end{aligned}
$$
Then $j_F\eta_F(x)=(x,x\otimes x)$. Over the integers this map is injective. For any commutative base ring after applying its coordinate matrix, its kernel is exactly the combinations $\sum_i d_iD_i$ with $2d_i=0$. Compare first the coordinates in $F$ and then one coordinate of each off-diagonal pair in $F\otimes F$. In particular every linearization of a power map vanishes on this kernel, even in characteristic two. The use of $F\otimes F$ here distinguishes both off-diagonal coordinates. Passing to the symmetric quotient would multiply those coordinates by two and would lose this property in characteristic two.

Every integral linear map $g\colon F\to F'$ induces a linear map $J(g)$ sending $\eta_F(x)$ to $\eta_{F'}(gx)$. The factorization of quadratic maps just proved gives this map, and then
$$
j_{F'}J(g)=(g\oplus(g\otimes g))j_F
$$
by checking on the spanning $\eta_F(x)$. If $g$ is invertible over the integers, functoriality on these generators gives inverse $J(g^{-1})$. If the coordinate matrix of $g$ is square and invertible modulo $M$, the reduction of $J(g)$ is invertible modulo $M$ as well. For this last claim restrict the codomain of $j_F$ to $F$ plus the symmetric tensors, with basis the diagonal terms and the sums of off-diagonal pairs. This lattice is preserved by $g\otimes g$. Over the rationals $j_F$ gives a change of basis with this restricted codomain, so the determinant of $J(g)$ equals the determinant of $g$ times the determinant of the map on symmetric tensors. Modulo each prime dividing $M$, the inverse of $g$ induces the inverse on symmetric tensors. In every characteristic this invariant submodule has the same displayed basis, as symmetry is the equality of the two off-diagonal coordinates. Hence the determinant of $J(g)$ is a unit modulo $M$. This argument does not require computing the primes dividing $M$.

Suppose work is over $R=\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$, an invertible $R$-linear map $\bar g$ is prescribed on the free cover, and an invertible $R$-linear map $H$ on $J$ respects the displayed embedding records with $\bar g$ and $\bar g\otimes\bar g$. Take any integral matrix lift $g$ of $\bar g$. The two maps $H$ and $J(g)\bmod M$ have the same composite into the target of $j_{F'}$. Their difference therefore lands in the diagonal part of the kernel where every power-map linearization is zero. It follows that after applying any target power record the two maps agree, including when $j_F$ acquires a kernel after reduction.

## 3. Compile the marked system to regular free tensors

Either work over the integers or reduce the construction modulo $M$. All presented quotient modules have the same interpretation in both cases, since they are killed by $M$. Compare the abelian types of $A,C$ by Smith normal form of the cyclic-order diagonals. For differing types output a fixed no-instance. For equal types choose free covers for $A,C$ and each $A_m$, with the other input's generator count adjoined as zero coordinates. The two stabilized cover ranks are equal for each sort. This is the stabilized presentation construction in the [companion module proof, §1](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorUniversalityApplications/PRESENTED-MODULE-TENSOR-ISOMORPHISM.md). Any quotient isomorphism lifts to an invertible change on these stabilized covers, over either ring. Each cover kernel has its supplied generating list, including the zero-coordinate basis vectors. For $C_m$ use the same cover for $C$ and enlarge its kernel by $m$ times the cover. Preserving the kernel for $C$ automatically preserves this enlarged kernel. Choose integer basis lifts for $\beta$, the maps $A_m\to A$, and the values of the linearizations in the preceding section. Reduce these entries for finite ring output.

Here is the needed marked construction for a map specified modulo its output kernel. Let $X_1,\ldots,X_t,Y$ be free and let $U=X_1\otimes\cdots\otimes X_t$, where $t=1$ or $2$. Given $f\colon U\to Y$ and a marked submodule $K\subseteq Y$, temporarily replace the output by a private sort $Y\oplus U$. Mark $Y\oplus0$ and $K\oplus U$. Tie the change of basis on $Y\oplus0$ to the original $Y$ via its inclusion. Use the map $z\mapsto(fz,z)$ as a linear record for $t=1$ or a bilinear record on pure products for $t=2$. An invertible map on the augmented output preserving its marks has blocks $\begin{pmatrix}A&S\\0&D\end{pmatrix}$ with $AK=K'$ and $S(U)\subseteq K'$. Preserving the graph record forces $D=B=\bigotimes_i g_i$ and $S=f'B-Af$. Conversely whenever this residual lands in $K'$, these blocks define the required invertible augmented map. This is the linear and bilinear form of the earlier coevaluation correction. A mark given by a generator list is itself expressed as a linear map from a free sort. Adjoining the other input's list as zero generators makes its domain rank equal on the two sides and permits every isomorphism of the marked image to lift by the same stabilized presentation argument. Thus even dependent relation columns give valid marks.

The system now consists of the following named records.

- Every cover kernel, together with the map for $A_m\to A$ modulo the kernel of the cover of $A$.
- The bilinear commutator record on the cover of $A$, modulo the kernel of the cover of $C$.
- For each $m$, the product map $F_m\times F_m\to F_m\otimes F_m$ and the two components of $j_{F_m}$, all preserved exactly.
- For each $m$, the map $L_{q_m\pi_m}\colon J(F_m)\to C_m$, linearized after precomposing with the projection $\pi_m\colon F_m\to A_m$. Use its chosen lift into the cover of $C$ modulo the enlarged kernel for $C_m$.

This system uses only linear and bilinear maps between free sorts. Its isomorphisms induce invertible maps on $A,C$, and the marked inclusions force the map on $A_m$ to be the restriction of that on $A$. The paired product and embedding records force the action on $J(F_m)$ over the integers and force it modulo the invisible diagonal kernel over the finite ring. Either way, the preceding section implies that preservation of the last record yields preservation of $q_m$ by the induced quotient maps. Hence those maps preserve the complete group data. Conversely lifts of a preserving pair on $A,C$ and the restricted maps on $A_m$ satisfy all quotient equations. Use $J(g_m)$ for any integral representative of the selected cover lift, and use the graph-map construction to realize all residuals in the private outputs. All records then have a simultaneous witness. The graph-map corrections are independent for distinct records.

Erase the labels using the fixed-type arbitrary-ring compiler of the [companion module proof, §3](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorUniversalityApplications/PRESENTED-MODULE-TENSOR-ISOMORPHISM.md). The same construction is polynomial even with a variable number of named records. Each occurrence is copied to separate slots linked to its reference by scalar multiplication, and primal and dual slots are linked by evaluation. Each slot of either input mode is marked with one projection into its free complementary quotient. An associative triangular algebra records these projections and the typed multiplication. To recover its diagonal vertices, attach one private incoming corner of distinct free rank to each vertex, using ranks larger than every unmarked corner. The off-diagonal ideal is the commutator ideal. In its quotient by its square, the marking corners survive with their full ranks over each residue field and identify all vertices. Orthogonal diagonal idempotents that agree modulo the nilpotent ideal can be conjugated to the standard frame using a unit equal to one modulo the ideal. Taking the projection annihilators recovers the named slots. Passing to the free multiplication tensor preserves equivalence because isotopies of associative unital algebras normalize to isomorphisms. These arguments work over the integers and over each specified finite ring.

For the size bound, let $L$ be the number of named slots and records, and $D$ the sum of their free side lengths counted with occurrences. The compiler has $O(L)$ vertices and at most $O(L)$ nonzero corner slots, each of rank $O(D+L)$ including the distinguishing ranks. Its algebra and hence its ordinary tensor have side $O(L(D+L))$. Here $L=O(k+1)$, and $D=O((k+1)(r^2+s+1))$, since $J(F_m)$ and the commutator product cover are quadratic in $r$, and all cover kernels have lists linear in the corresponding cover ranks. Thus the asserted bound includes the unmarked output. The coefficients come from the typed records and the marking constants. In the Cayley-table algorithm, use the divisibility-ordered Smith cyclic factors. If their distinct nontrivial values are $m_1<\cdots<m_k$, then $m_j\ge 2^j$. Hence $2^{k(k+1)/2}\le |A|$ and $k=O(\sqrt{1+\log N})$. Also $r+s\le\log_2 N$ on a pair with equal abelian types. Substitution gives the cubic logarithmic bound. For $p$-groups the same argument uses $p^j$ and $\log_p N$.

Over the integers tensor witness recovery uses matrix inverses of unital multiplication operators, finitely many algebra operations, and extraction of the recovered blocks. Over $\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$ the inverses can use integer adjugates and the extended Euclidean algorithm with $M$. Once the quotient maps are recovered, collection and linear congruences find the central lift corrections from Section 1 in polynomial time.

## Verification and prior scope

The central power functions are quadratic maps in the sense of Passi. The free lattice written above gives their explicit quadratic linearization and its transformation law. A prior MathProofs construction uses truncated modular augmentation algebras with bounds depending on a fixed exponent. Here the second difference of each power record is bilinear, and the size bound is polynomial in the succinct input for variable cyclic orders. The reduction and its correctness have written proofs above. The Lean companion checks the central-commutator power formula and the invisibility of the embedding kernel for records that ignore the diagonal polar coefficients. The finite replay checks the power records and their quadratic linearization in small mixed-exponent groups, including the two nonabelian groups of order eight.
