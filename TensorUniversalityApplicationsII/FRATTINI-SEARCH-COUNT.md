# From decision to witnesses and counts for groups of Frattini class two

**Theorem.** Let $G,H$ be finite $p$-groups given by Cayley tables, with central elementary abelian Frattini subgroups. Write $|G|=|H|=N$, $n=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}G/\Phi(G)$ and $m=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\Phi(G)$. There are deterministic polynomial-time reductions from finding an isomorphism, counting isomorphisms, and finding automorphism generators to ordinary group isomorphism decision in the same class. Search and counting use at most $1+np^n$ queries each. Every query group has order $p^{O(n+m)}$ with an absolute constant. For odd $p$ all nontrivial queries may be restricted to groups of exponent $p$ and nilpotency class at most two.

In particular, for 2-groups this extends the $N^{O(\sqrt{\log N})}$ decision bound of Grochow–Ivanyos–Qiao–Sun to finding an isomorphism, counting isomorphisms, and giving automorphism generators. Grochow–Qiao previously answered Arvind and Tóran’s search- and counting-to-decision questions for class-two exponent-$p$ groups given by Cayley tables. The main extension here is to retain the full square-map data of 2-groups of Frattini class two. The odd-prime result also covers every linked commutator and power map. [Grochow–Qiao, Part IV, Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S1.SS2) · [Grochow–Ivanyos–Qiao–Sun, Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00874v1)

## 1. The full group data

Put $V=G/\Phi(G)$ and $W=\Phi(G)$, in additive coordinates over $K=\mathbb F_p$. Let $b\colon\Lambda^2 V\to W$ be the commutator map, with $[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$. Let $q(x)$ be the $p$th power in $W$ of any lift of $x\in V$. Centrality and exponent $p$ of $W$ make both definitions independent of the lifts. The class-two power identity implies that for odd $p$, $q$ is linear. For $p=2$, $q$ is quadratic with polar form $b$. Indeed $q(x+y)=q(x)+q(y)+b(x,y)$ in this case. These formulas also follow from collecting a word into ordered generator powers, since the commutators are central. In both cases the values of $q$ and $b$ span $W$, by $\Phi(G)=G^p[G,G]$. For $p=2$ the values of $q$ alone span $W$ by polarization.

In characteristic two, set $\mathcal D_G$ to the image of $W^*$ in $\operatorname{Sym}^2(V^*)$ under $f\mapsto f\circ q$. Write the polynomial in the standard monomial basis of total degree two. Over $\mathbb F_2$ those monomials are linearly independent as functions, since evaluation at basis vectors detects the squares and evaluation at sums of distinct basis vectors then detects the mixed terms. For odd $p$ use instead the image in $\Lambda^2(V^*)\oplus V^*$ under $f\mapsto (f\circ b,f\circ q)$. In both cases it has dimension $m$. Under a map $g:V_G\to V_H$ the action on the covector spaces from source to target is $P=(g^{-1})^*$. A linear isomorphism $P$ transports $\mathcal D_G$ onto $\mathcal D_H$ exactly when $g$ extends to the full Frattini data. To see this, the transport of the images defines an isomorphism of the spaces $W^*$ via their injective coordinate maps. Taking its inverse dual gives the unique $h:W_G\to W_H$ compatible with $g$ and the records. The converse follows by applying covectors to the compatibility equations.

For completeness these linear data give actual group maps, with a precise lift count. Fix a basis $(e_i)$ of $V$ and generator lifts $(x_i)$. Every group element is uniquely $x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}z$ with $0\le a_i<p$ and $z\in W$. In these coordinates the product adds residues in $V$ and adds a central factor
$$
C(a,b)=\sum_i \left\lfloor(a_i+b_i)/p\right\rfloor q(e_i)+\sum_{j>i}a_j b_i[x_j,x_i].
$$
The first term is the carry on each factor. The second term collects the generator of lower index passing the higher-index generator. Given a compatible $(g,h)$, choose any lift of each $g(e_i)$ in $H$ and use $h$ on $W_G$. The commutator and power equations on those chosen generators agree with the displayed collecting law, so this defines a group homomorphism. It is bijective because its induced maps on the central subgroup and quotient are bijective. Every other lift has its generator images shifted by elements of $W_H$. All these choices are independent, with exactly $p^{nm}$ resulting isomorphisms over each valid $P$.

The same formula constructs a Cayley table from a homogeneous quadratic space over $\mathbb F_2$: take its basis as scalar coordinates of $q$, use polarization for the commutators, and take independent central coordinate positions. It also constructs a Cayley table of exponent $p$ for odd $p$ from an alternating space by setting all $q(e_i)=0$. The carry cocycle identity follows by writing both sides as the carry of three residues, and the second summand is a bilinear cocycle. Thus the product is associative, with identity $(0,0)$ and inverses from the quotient and central coordinates. Group commutators and powers give back the supplied data by the same collecting formula. Independence of the displayed forms means their joint values span the central coordinates. Thus the Frattini subgroup of each constructed group is exactly that central space. This gives ordinary query groups and retains the intended data at their characteristic Frattini subgroups. The characteristic-two criterion is the standard square-map description, and the odd-prime mixed space is the inhomogeneous alternating space from the earlier Frattini-class-two algorithm. [Higman criterion as presented by Grochow–Ivanyos–Qiao–Sun, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00874v1#S3) · [Ivanyos–Mendoza–Qiao–Sun–Zhang, §V](https://chuanqizhang.com/assets/pdf/pdf/focs24.pdf)

## 2. Exact prefixes as linear-size cross-block records

Work over any field and take an independent frame $u_1,\ldots,u_k$ in a carrier $A$. Its line list is constructed as in the prior [linear-order alternating-space reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorUniversalityApplications/LINEAR-SIDE-AMSI.md). For $k=1$ take just its line. For $2\le k\le 6$ take $i$ copies of each line $L_i=\langle u_i\rangle$ and $k+1$ copies of the sum line $L_*=\langle u_1+\cdots+u_k\rangle$. For $k\ge7$ take one copy of each $L_i$, two copies of $L_*$, and one plane $L_i+L_j$ per edge of the tree with center 1 and arms $2$, $3,4$, and $5,6,\ldots,k$. The arms have distinct lengths $1,2,k-4$. In every case the resulting list of subspaces $(U_j)$ has size $t\le5k$, with each $U_j$ of dimension at most two.

Introduce a carrier $D$ with separate basis vectors $w_j$ and take the cross-block subspace $C_u=\bigoplus_j U_j\otimes K w_j\subset A\otimes D$. Contracting with $\alpha\in D^*$ gives $\sum_{\alpha(w_j)\ne0} U_j$. For a subspace $B\subset A$, the dimension of the space of $\alpha$ whose contractions lie in $B$ is therefore the number of slots with $U_j\subset B$. Both the contraction images and this dimension function are invariant under block transport. The one-dimensional contraction images are exactly the recorded lines. For $k\le6$ multiplicities identify them. For $k\ge7$ the sum line has multiplicity two, and the dimension function on $L_i+L_j$ takes value $2+1$ on edges and $2$ elsewhere. The resulting tree fixes the center, each branch and each vertex in order. This recovers every line with its label. Preservation of the sum line forces a common nonzero multiplier $\lambda$ in $P u_i=\lambda v_i$, by independence. This condition for $k=1$ follows from line preservation itself. Conversely every such $P$ transports the cross-block record by taking the identity on slot coordinates.

The record may also include one additional slot for a distinguished subspace $A_0\subset A$ of dimension at least three. The rank-one and rank-two tests just described are unaffected. After recovering the ordinary line and plane list, subtract its known incidence counts from the dimension function. The difference is exactly the indicator of $A_0\subset B$, so it recovers $A_0$ itself. The extra slot requires one new coordinate in $D$ and $\dim A_0$ basis records.

## 3. Forgetting the carrier labels without losing square terms

Use either of the homogeneous degree-two functors $\mathcal F(A)=\Lambda^2 A$ over a field or $\mathcal F(A)=\operatorname{Sym}^2 A$ in characteristic two. In the latter case always retain the quadratic polynomials themselves. Define the polar matrix of a polynomial as the alternating matrix of its polarization on $A^*$. In the exterior case use the bivector matrix, defined by $a\wedge b\mapsto ab^{\mathsf T}-ba^{\mathsf T}$. In both cases these matrices transform by congruence under linear variable maps. The cross-block map $A\otimes D\to\mathcal F(A\oplus D)$ sends $a\otimes d$ to $a\wedge d$ or $ad$, respectively. It is injective, and its polar or bivector matrix is the usual alternating cross block. Given data $\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal F(A)$, let $S=\mathcal Q\oplus C_u$ inside $\mathcal F(A\oplus D)$.

A quadratic polynomial has an intrinsic minimal supporting variable space. For any subspaces $X,Y\subset A\oplus D$, the identity $\operatorname{Sym}^2X\cap\operatorname{Sym}^2Y=\operatorname{Sym}^2(X\cap Y)$ follows by choosing a basis of the intersection and separate complementary bases inside $X$ and $Y$, then extending to the full ambient space. The degree-two monomials in the two sets of variables coincide exactly on the common variables. The corresponding statement for exterior powers follows using squarefree degree-two monomials. Iterating the intersection in finite dimension gives the minimal supporting space, which is equivariant under change of variables. The support of a space of records is the sum of the supports of its elements. The column space of each polar matrix lies in its polynomial support, since a polynomial supported on a subspace has its polar contraction there. In particular a nonzero cross-block product or bivector of independent vectors supports exactly those two lines, by its polar image. This notion records the support of a pure square even when its polar matrix is zero.

Write $c=\dim A+\dim D$. Enlarge $A\oplus D$ by paired copies $A',D'$ and two heavy blocks of dimensions $2(c+1)$ and $4(c+1)$. In the first heavy block use a standard full-rank alternating bivector, or the quadratic polynomial $\sum_j x_j y_j$ with that polar matrix. Do the same independently in the second heavy block. Add two records $F_A,F_D$, each the heavy form on its block plus the paired cross record from $A,A'$ or $D,D'$ respectively. Each paired cross record uses the identity coefficient matrix. Let $S^+=S+KF_A+KF_D$.

The polar rank of $M+\alpha F_A+\beta F_D$ lies in
$$
\big[ 2(c+1)h,\ 2(c+1)h+2c\big],\qquad h=\mathbf 1_{\alpha\ne0}+2\mathbf 1_{\beta\ne0},
$$
because the heavy blocks are disjoint and the remaining coordinates have dimension $2c$. These four intervals are disjoint. Every transporter of the ordinary spaces $S^+$ recovers $S$ as the records of polar rank at most $2c$ and preserves each marker up to a nonzero scalar and a term in $S$.

The intrinsic joint support $Z$ of $S$ is $A_1\oplus D$, where $A_1$ is the sum of the original data support and all slot subspaces. Every slot is nonzero and its basis cross records contribute the corresponding line of $D$. Write primes on $Z,A_1$ for the corresponding target spaces in the following intersection argument. The polar image of $F_A$ intersects $Z$ in $A_1$. In the target, the polar image of $\alpha F_A+M$ intersects $Z'$ in exactly $A'_1$ for every $M\in S'$ and every $\alpha\ne0$. An input giving zero output in the paired copy of $A$ has zero covector on $A$. The remaining columns land in $A$ on the core, since the $D$-by-$D$ block of every $M$ is zero. The paired-copy columns produce all of $A$, independently of those contributions. Thus the transporter preserves the two old $A_1$ subspaces.

The polar image of $F_D$ intersects $Z$ in $D$. In the target the polar image of $\beta F_D+M$ intersects $Z'$ in $D\oplus\operatorname{im}(M_{AA})$. In this case zero output in the paired copy of $D$ forces zero covector on $D$, and the paired-copy columns produce all of $D$. Every $AA$ polar image of $M$ lies in $A'_1$. Preservation of intersection dimension forces $\operatorname{im}(M_{AA})=0$ for the image of $F_D$, so the transporter preserves $D$ as well. This deduction uses the full support $Z$, including pure squares, although the marker tests use only polar matrices.

The recovered maps on $A_1,D$ transport the original data and frame records. To see this, extend the map on $A_1$ to any invertible map on $A$ by completing bases. All original records are supported on $A_1\oplus D$, so preservation on this support gives preservation of $S$. The block map preserves the intersections of $S$ with the pure-$A$ and cross blocks, which are exactly $\mathcal Q$ and $C_u$. Conversely every block transporter of these two parts extends to $S^+$ using inverse transposes on the paired copies and identities on the heavy blocks. The total carrier dimension is $8c+6$, and the dimension of the form space is $\dim \mathcal Q+\sum_j\dim U_j+2$. No rank-degeneracy assumption on $\mathcal Q$ was used.

## 4. Compiling a prefix to an ordinary group query

In characteristic two take $A=V^*$ and $\mathcal Q=\mathcal D_G$ in the preceding construction. Carry the target frame similarly. Since $\mathbb F_2^*=\{1\}$, frame recovery is exact. The resulting ordinary quadratic spaces compile by Section 1 to Frattini-class-two groups. This retains the original square and commutator coordinates while using only the polar ranks of the marker records for recovery. It works even when the original space has zero polarization.

For odd $p$, take $A=V^*\oplus Kz$ and homogenize the linked data to the subspace with elements $B+z\wedge\ell$ for $(B,\ell)\in\mathcal D_G$. Use the frame $(z,u_1,\ldots,u_k)$ and the extra slot for $V^*\subset A$. For $n\le2$ the group data problem can be solved directly by enumerating at most $p^4$ mode matrices, so assume $n\ge3$. The recovered $A$ map preserves $V^*$ and sends $z$ to $\lambda z$. Its restriction $R$ to $V^*$ satisfies $R u_i=\lambda v_i$. The homogeneous action gives
$$
(\Lambda^2 R)B+\lambda z\wedge R\ell
=\lambda^2\big((\Lambda^2(R/\lambda))B+z\wedge (R/\lambda)\ell\big).
$$
Since multiplication by $\lambda^2$ preserves a subspace, $R/\lambda$ transports the original linked data and satisfies the exact prefix. Conversely any exact prefix transporter extends by the identity on $z$ and the slot coordinates, then by the carrier markers. Thus the homogenized group query is exact, using only ordinary alternating spaces and therefore exponent-$p$ groups. The construction also works for the empty prefix using the one-vector frame $(z)$, so an initial decision needs no original exponent-$p^2$ group oracle. The homogenization itself is established in the earlier odd-prime algorithm. The new prefix and extra slot make the normalization compatible with prescribed vectors. [Ivanyos–Mendoza–Qiao–Sun–Zhang, Lemma V.3](https://chuanqizhang.com/assets/pdf/pdf/focs24.pdf)

For a prefix of at most $n$ vectors, the carrier sizes and number of additional forms are $O(n)$, because each original slot has dimension at most two and the one extra slot has dimension $n$. Thus the group query has $p^{O(n+m)}$ elements and its entire Cayley table can be written in $N^{O(1)}$ bit operations. The prime can be variable. Computing $V,W$ and the input data from the initial table also takes polynomial time in $N$, using $G^p[G,G]$ and elementary abelian linear coordinates.

## 5. Search, counting and generators

Compare group size, prime and Frattini dimensions first. If the instance is positive, successively choose images of a standard basis of $V_G^*$ in $V_H^*$. For each candidate vector, discard a dependent prefix and query whether it is extendible using Section 4. The current positive prefix always has a positive extension. At most $p^n$ candidates per step and one initial decision suffice. The completed basis map recovers the quotient and Frattini maps, and any consistent choices of lifts then give an explicit group isomorphism by Section 1.

Let $L$ be the stabilizer of $\mathcal D_G$ on $V_G^*$. To count it, for each $i$ enumerate the images of the $i$th basis vector under the subgroup fixing the previous $i-1$ basis vectors pointwise. Each orbit membership is the same exact-prefix query with both endpoints $G$. The product of the successive orbit sizes equals $|L|$, since the last pointwise stabilizer is trivial. The automorphism count is $p^{nm}|L|$. For an isomorphic second input the number of isomorphisms is the same, since the transporter is a coset. For a negative instance it is zero. The count has polynomially many bits in $\log N$.

To output automorphism generators, complete one representative for every successful orbit image in the preceding chain, continuing the same prefix search while keeping the preceding basis vectors fixed. Representatives for a subgroup's cosets together with generators of the next pointwise stabilizer generate that subgroup. Descending from the trivial final stabilizer yields generators of $L$. Lift these generators to group automorphisms and add the elementary central shifts of each generator lift by each basis element of $W$. Those shifts generate the kernel $\operatorname{Hom}_K(V,W)$, as seen directly from the canonical group form. The result generates the entire automorphism group. At most polynomially many additional searches and query tables suffice. For a 2-group, every $N^{O(1)}$-order query and every polynomial number of queries fits the $N^{O(\sqrt{\log N})}$ decision bound cited above. This is the deterministic reduction overhead; the composed algorithm uses the decision algorithm with its stated computational guarantees.

## Verification and scope

The group reductions and their equivalences are proved in writing. The accompanying Lean source proves the carry cocycle, the bilinear cocycle and associativity of central multiplication under the cocycle identity. The finite checks verify 63,362 associativity triples in actual central tables and compare 59 exact transporter counts with an independent enumeration of generator images. They also check 22,464 anchor normalizations, the characteristic-two pure-square case, the dihedral and quaternion distinction, and the hyperplane incidence marker. The reductions apply when the Frattini subgroup is central elementary abelian. Historical priority and external review remain open.
