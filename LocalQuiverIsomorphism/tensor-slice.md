# Exact tensor slices and graph isomorphism over finite local rings

## Theorems

Let $R$ be a commutative local ring with nilpotent maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, and write $k=R/\mathfrak m$. An identity arm of size $d\ge2$ is the tensor of the identity pairing on two free rank-$d$ modules, times a generator of a rank-one third module, with the line in any of the three positions. Consider tensors whose residue is isomorphic to a direct sum of such arms. The residue decomposition need not be part of the input.

If all arm sizes are units in $R$, then every tensor reducing to a fixed sum of identity arms has an exact linear slice at every nilpotent order. A polynomial number of linear coordinate operations per nilpotence layer normalizes the tensor. After normalization, every transporter lies in the explicitly described stabilizer of the base tensor, acting linearly on the slice. If $\mathfrak m^2=0$, the canonical tangent quotient gives an exact slice on isomorphism classes and an affine description of all transporters for every arm size, including those divisible by the residue characteristic.

For each fixed finite local nonfield $R$, ordinary three-tensor isomorphism on the recognizable class above is GI-complete under polynomial many-one reductions if each arm has size at most $C\log_2(L+2)$, where $C$ is any fixed positive constant and $L$ is the sum of the three tensor side lengths. Use the unit-size condition for general $R$, and allow all sizes when $\mathfrak m^2=0$. GI-hardness already holds with all arms of the same constant size and identical residue tensors for a given format. A GI decision oracle also gives search and exact counting on these promises.

## 1. Recognizing and aligning the base tensors

Label the arms $j$. Write $V_{\ell j}$ for the corresponding block of mode $\ell$, and $V_\ell=\bigoplus_j V_{\ell j}$. The base tensor $T_0$ is supported on the label triples $(j,j,j)$. In each such cell it is an identity tensor between the two rank-$d_j$ factors times a generator of the rank-one line. All contractions have full mode support.

The centroid here is the space of triples of endomorphisms for which their respective single-mode insertions give the same tensor. For $T_0$ it is exactly the product $R^s$, where $s$ is the number of arms. Indeed an off-diagonal block of one mode, from $j$ to $i$, contributes to a cell with labels $j,j,i$ in the corresponding order. Comparison with insertion in a different mode makes this contribution zero. Every entry of the offending block is recovered by contraction with the two other factors of arm $j$, so the block vanishes. On each diagonal arm the insertion on its line is scalar. Comparison with the other two insertions forces both corresponding endomorphisms to be that same scalar identity.

Consequently the stabilizer $H$ of $T_0$ permutes the arms of each profile $(\operatorname{rank}V_{1j},\operatorname{rank}V_{2j},\operatorname{rank}V_{3j})$. Here the base ring is local, so automorphisms of $R^s$ permute its coordinate idempotents. With arms fixed, choose $g_j$ freely on one big mode, a unit $\lambda_j$ on the line, and use $\lambda_j^{-1}g_j^{-T}$ on the other big mode. This describes every stabilizer element. The description applies over the local quotients $R/\mathfrak m^i$ as well. In particular stabilizers lift between these rings. Permutations of arms of the same profile are realized by literal basis permutations.

For raw input over a fixed finite local ring, compute the residue centroid by linear equations. Test whether it is a unital commutative subalgebra of the product of the three endomorphism algebras. Test whether left multiplication by each element of a computed basis is annihilated by $x^{|k|}-x$ on the centroid. This polynomial has distinct roots, all in $k$. The multiplication operators commute, so one can split into common eigenspaces by repeatedly intersecting with eigenspaces over the fixed field. A commutative unital algebra with this property is a product of copies of $k$: its regular representation is faithful and identifies it with a subalgebra of diagonal matrices, and distinct characters can be separated by multiplying elements that distinguish their values. Thus its primitive idempotents are found from the common eigenspaces by scaling nonzero vectors to square to themselves. One may check directly that the resulting idempotents form an orthogonal basis and sum to the unit.

Read their images in the three modes, and test if the resulting decomposition has the prescribed arm profiles. Check the tensor has only diagonal-arm components and that each is a full-rank pairing times a line. This exactly recognizes the promised residues. The identity pairing bases can be extracted by inverting the corresponding paired coefficient matrix. Match profiles on the two sides, lift the resulting bases invertibly to $R$, and use a common $T_0$. The choices of bases can differ by the stated stabilizer action and higher-order original coordinate changes. The comparison methods below retain all those choices.

## 2. The normal module over arbitrary nilpotent order

Write $\mathcal V=V_1\otimes_R V_2\otimes_R V_3$. The differential $d$ of the action at $T_0$ sends a triple $X$ of endomorphisms to the sum of the three single-mode insertions of $X$ in $T_0$. Its image $D$ has the following description cell by cell.

- In an all-equal cell $(j,j,j)$, the image is the entire cell. Varying an endomorphism in just one of the two big modes gives every paired matrix.
- In a cell with exactly two labels $j$ and a different label $i$, only an off-diagonal block from $j$ to $i$ in the changed position contributes. If that changed position was a big mode in arm $j$, the image is the entire cell. The unchanged factors are a big module and a line, and the pairing identifies the available map entries with all coordinates of the cell.
- If the changed position was the line of arm $j$, the image is $V_{\ell i}\otimes R\,1_j$ in the cell $V_{\ell i}\otimes\operatorname{End}(R^{d_j})$, using the original pairing to identify the other two factors. Here $1_j$ denotes the identity matrix.
- All-distinct cells have zero image.

Assume first that all $d_j$ are units. In the third case the projection onto the image is $M\mapsto(\operatorname{tr}(M)/d_j)1_j$ on the matrix factor. Choose the trace-zero complement in those cells. Keep all all-distinct cells, and keep none of the cells where the image is entire. This gives $\mathcal V=D\oplus N$. The decomposition is $H$-equivariant. On the paired factors the action is conjugation times a unit, and arm permutations carry the indicated cell types and traces to one another.

There is an explicit free direct summand $K$ of $\bigoplus_\ell\operatorname{End}(V_\ell)$ on which $d$ induces an isomorphism to $D$. Take every off-diagonal block and, within each arm, the endomorphisms of just one chosen big mode. The cell distinctions show that these images are independent and exhaust $D$. Choose the same big position for arms of the same profile. This makes $K$ stable under conjugation by $H$.

For any $T\equiv T_0\pmod{\mathfrak m}$ there is a unique pair
$$
 X\in\mathfrak m K,\qquad Z\in\mathfrak m N
 \quad\text{with}\quad
 T=(1+X)\cdot (T_0+Z).
$$
The notation $1+X$ denotes a triple of invertible matrices, since $\mathfrak m$ is nilpotent. To prove the claim solve modulo successive powers of $\mathfrak m$. At level $i\ge1$, changing $X,Z$ by terms in $\mathfrak m^i$ changes the right side modulo $\mathfrak m^{i+1}$ by $d(\Delta X)+\Delta Z$. Every cross term has an additional factor in $\mathfrak m$. The displayed direct decompositions make this a unique correction at each level. Nilpotence finishes both existence and uniqueness. Arithmetic with coordinate matrices implements each correction. In particular the algorithm uses polynomial bit time for the standard $\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$ and $k[t]/(t^e)$ representations, with uniform dependence on $e$ once a residue arm frame is given.

An isomorphism $g$ between slice points $T_0+Z,T_0+Z'$ reduces to $H(k)$. Suppose it agrees with a stabilizer element modulo $\mathfrak m^i$, and lift that element to $h\in H(R)$. Write $g=h(1+Y)$ with $Y$ in $\mathfrak m^i$ times the endomorphism module. Then
$$
 d(Y)\equiv h^{-1}Z'-Z \pmod{\mathfrak m^{i+1}} .
$$
The left side is in $D$, the right side in $N$, so $d(Y)$ vanishes on the layer. Thus $1+Y$ fixes $T_0$ modulo $\mathfrak m^{i+1}$, giving the next step. At the end $g\in H(R)$. Conversely the action of $H(R)$ on the slice exactly gives the transporters with this property. Multiplying by the known normalizers on the two sides gives a bijection of the normalized and original transporter sets.

## 3. A square-zero slice in every characteristic

Suppose now $\mathfrak m^2=0$, with all $d_j\ge2$ allowed. The canonical payload is the class of $\Delta=T-T_0$ in $(\mathcal V_k/D_k)\otimes_k\mathfrak m$, where $D_k$ is the action differential over $k$. Here $\mathfrak m$ is a $k$-vector space, and the labels of $T_0$ give the description of $D_k$ from Section 2 independently of any trace splitting. A residue map $h\in H(k)$ lifts to a stabilizer $\widetilde h$ of $T_0$. All its possible lifts as original base changes have the form $\widetilde h(1+X)$ with entries of $X$ in $\mathfrak m$. The condition for such a lift to transport $T$ to $T'$ is exactly
$$
 d(X)=\widetilde h^{-1}\Delta'-\Delta .
$$
Its solvability is therefore equivalent to equality of the payload classes under $h$. Whenever solvable the solution set is an affine space, and is generated by one solution and the kernel of the differential. Each residue transporter gives exactly $|\mathfrak m|^{\sum_j(d_j^2+1)}$ original transporters, since the described map from the Lie algebra of the stabilizer identifies that kernel with the direct sum of one matrix space and one scalar per arm, tensored with $\mathfrak m$.

There is an efficient relational test for equality of the quotient payload classes that uses vectors in a single block at a time. Ignore cells where the tangent image is full. In an all-distinct cell compare the values on each triple of dual block vectors. In a two-equal cell with the changed line of arm $j$, test the values on triples whose two unchanged dual block vectors pair to zero under the arm's identity pairing. That zero condition is preserved by $H(k)$. These tests detect the entire quotient by $V_{\ell i}\otimes k\,1_j$. Indeed, for the matrix factor, the rank-one functionals from pairs $\phi,v$ with $\phi(v)=0$ span the annihilator of $1_j$. They include the off-diagonal coordinate tests and generate each difference of two diagonal tests by using $(e_i^*+e_j^*,e_i-e_j)$ and subtracting the off-diagonal tests. The argument works in characteristic two and with values in the $k$-vector space $\mathfrak m$. Pairing with every dual vector in the changed block detects all its coordinates. Thus the tests are equivalent to the tangent-quotient equality, even when the scalar direction has no trace-zero complement.

## 4. Exact reduction to graph isomorphism and hardness

For the all-orders case with unit sizes, take one relational domain consisting of the disjoint unions of the full dual modules of the three modes' blocks. Include an arm vertex for each arm, attach all its block vectors to it by mode-labelled membership, and mark the profile and modes. On each block record the addition ternary relation and the scalar multiplication relations for the fixed ring $R$. A relational isomorphism therefore gives a simultaneous permutation of arms and an invertible $R$-linear map on each block. Record evaluations of $T_0$ and $Z$ on all triples of block vectors by their values, in separate labelled relations. These guard the same change of basis, so the transporter set of these structures is exactly the block transporters for $T_0,Z$. The primal maps are the inverse duals of the extracted dual maps.

For a square-zero ring use the dual blocks over $k$ instead. Record $T_0$ over $k$ and the restricted evaluation tests from Section 3. The domains of the restricted tests are invariant under the certified base-tensor transporters. Values in $\mathfrak m$ are used as fixed labels. This gives exactly the residue transporters of the tangent-quotient classes, followed by the explicit affine lifting test above.

These relational structures have bounded arity and polynomial size for fixed $R$ and arm sizes $O(\log(L+2))$. The domain size is at most a constant times the number of arms times $|R|$ to the largest arm size, and the evaluation tables are cubic in that bound. Convert them to colored graphs by one tuple vertex per relation tuple with its positions individually labelled and connected to their entries. With repetitions in a tuple allowed, use its separately labelled position vertices. Each relational transporter has a unique extension to this graph because each ordered tuple of each relation occurs once. Colored graphs reduce to ordinary GI by standard individualization gadgets. The resulting color-preserving search, automorphism orders and generators reduce to GI decision by successive individualization and orbit–stabilizer. Use the known normalizers for the first case, and multiply the residue count by the affine fiber size in the square-zero case.

For hardness fix a nonzero $t\in\mathfrak m$ of the finite local nonfield $R$. Choose one constant $d\ge2$ nonzero in $k$. Give every vertex of a simple graph a size-$d$ arm with its line in mode three. All arms have the same profile, so $H$ allows any common vertex permutation. For each oriented edge $j\to i$ of the symmetric directed graph of the input, put $tE_{12}$ in cell $(j,j,i)$, using its line basis and its identified matrix coordinates. Set the other payload cells to zero. These matrices are trace zero. Under an element of $H(R)$, the corresponding cells transform by conjugation and multiplication by units and move by the common arm permutation. Both operations preserve whether the cell is zero. Thus a tensor isomorphism induces a graph isomorphism. Conversely any graph isomorphism lifts by the basis permutation on arms with the internal maps and unit parameters all equal to identity. This reduction has linear side lengths and gives a common residue tensor for each graph order. For each fixed positive $C$, the arm-size bound holds at all sufficiently large graph orders. The finitely many smaller orders can be decided directly and sent to fixed positive or negative pairs meeting the promise. It lies in the promise with unit sizes, even when the square-zero enlargement permits nonunit sizes.

## 5. A graph-isomorphism class of genuinely obstructed lifts

The four-vertex construction in the companion proof also accepts a predecessor whose tensor is a sum of any number of identity arms. Its Peirce normalization is independent of the choice of predecessor tensor. For fixed $e\ge3$ over either finite chain ring in that proof, use the predecessor with both divided tensor products equal to such a $T_0$. The residue centroid recognizes the arm format, and the explicit stabilizer lifts between all the quotients. The alignment procedure for the predecessor therefore applies using these recovered arms. The last-step data are $([F],E)$, where $[F]$ is the tangent-quotient class from Section 3 and $E$ is the obstruction tensor. A lift is genuinely obstructed exactly when $E\ne0$.

For logarithmic arm size, the relational encoding of Section 4 applies to these data over $k$. Add a second labelled set of evaluations, testing $E$ on every triple of dual block vectors. The same relational witness must transport the base tensor, the quotient class and $E$. This yields a polynomial reduction from raw algebra comparison to GI. It requires no division by any of the arm sizes, since only the last-step tangent quotient is used.

For the reverse reduction, use common constant-size arms with their lines in mode three, as in the graph construction above. Put $F=0$ and let $E$ equal $T_0$ on all-equal cells. On cell $(j,j,i)$ with $j\ne i$, put $E_{12}$ for each directed edge $j\to i$ and zero otherwise. The fixed all-equal part makes the obstruction nonzero even for an edgeless graph with at least one vertex. Arm permutation and invertibility preserve the zero pattern of the edge cells, and every graph isomorphism lifts by its arm permutation. Therefore genuinely obstructed lifts on this recognizable small-arm predecessor class are GI-complete, with linear algebra dimension in the hardness direction. ## Provenance

This is an AI-assisted informal proof. The tensor centroid and its direct-sum interpretation have earlier precedents. The coordinate calculation above makes a local-slice comparison effective over the indicated finite rings and leads to a graph algorithm for raw, unframed inputs with logarithmic-size arms. The explicit tangent splitting avoids relying on a general slice theorem for a reductive stabilizer in positive characteristic. The earlier MathProofs construction treated a specially placed all-distinct payload on three arms. Here the normal form or the canonical quotient retains all perturbation directions at the stated orders, including the two-equal directions used for graph hardness.
