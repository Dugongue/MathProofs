# Moving-slice tensor isomorphism over truncated local rings

## Theorem

Fix the number $d\ge2$ of slices. Let $R=\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^e)$ or $R=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$, where $e\ge1$, $\pi=t$ or $p$, and $k=R/\pi R$ has characteristic $p$. Use dense coefficient arrays in the equal-characteristic ring, and give the integer modulus in binary in the mixed-characteristic ring. An input is a square matrix pencil with $d$ parameters $A(x)=\sum_{i=1}^d x_i A_i$, where each $A_i$ is $n$ by $n$. Define the residue determinant $f_A(x)=\det \overline A(x)$. Assume $f_A\ne0$. In the dual projective space over $\overline k$, form the set of points represented by the homogeneous linear factors of $f_A$ whose multiplicities are nonzero modulo $p$. The promise is that these points contain a projective frame.

A Las Vegas algorithm recognizes this promise in expected polynomial binary time. On the promise it finds one transporter when one exists and counts all transporters in expected polynomial binary time. Mismatched formats give zero transporters. It allows a specified triple of determinants for the three mode maps. A transporter in the convention of this proof satisfies
$$
 P A(Sx)=B(x)Q
$$
with $P,Q\in\mathrm{GL}_n(R)$, $S\in\mathrm{GL}_d(R)$. The corresponding determinant-one case is invariant under inverse or transpose changes of conventions. The geometric frame is recovered from the raw tensors and need not consist of $k$-rational points. Repeated linear factors, additional nonlinear factors, arbitrary radical behavior of the strict module and arbitrary nilpotence length are allowed. Even a residue determinant vanishing at every $k$-rational slice is allowed.

## 1. Intrinsic anchors and residue projectivities

The determinant is a homogeneous form of degree $n$. It can be computed over $R$ using division-free determinants. Over a fixed $d$, the polynomial coefficient arrays of all intermediate degrees up to $n$ have polynomial size. For example the Berkowitz characteristic-polynomial algorithm uses products of homogeneous coefficient blocks and bounds the degree by $n$. Reduction of its output gives $f_A$.

For a form over $k$, each geometric linear factor has Frobenius orbit length at most $n$. Indeed its orbit consists of pairwise nonproportional factors and the product over the orbit divides the form over the algebraic closure. Choose normalized representatives by setting the first nonzero coefficient to one. A frame consists of $d+1$ points, the first $d$ linearly independent and with the last having nonzero coordinates in all those directions. Therefore if a frame exists it is defined over an extension whose degree is the least common multiple of $d+1$ integers in $[1,n]$.

Enumerate these degree tuples. Each candidate extension $K=\mathbb F_{q^\ell}$ has degree $\ell\le n^{d+1}$, hence polynomial bit complexity at fixed $d$. Arithmetic extension construction by random irreducibles and multivariate polynomial factorization over finite fields are Las Vegas polynomial time in the dense setting. Over $K$ retain the linear factors with the designated multiplicities and test their ordered $(d+1)$-tuples for a frame. This is a recognition algorithm as well as a frame search. A factor that is linear over $K$ represents exactly a geometric linear factor already defined over $K$, with the same multiplicity. One never constructs a splitting field for the whole form. [Von zur Gathen–Kaltofen, polynomial-time multivariate factorization over finite fields](https://users.cs.duke.edu/~elk27/bibliography/85/GaKa85_mathcomp.pdf)

Fix one recovered frame for $A$ in one such $K$. Every projective slice map over $k$ carrying the determinant line of $A$ to that of $B$ sends this frame to a frame of eligible $K$-rational factors of $f_B$. Enumerate the target frames in $K$. Two ordered projective frames determine a unique projectivity from one to the other, computable by solving for the change of basis and its diagonal scaling using the last point. For our pullback convention invert the projectivity if necessary. To test descent to $k$, normalize the resulting matrix by its first nonzero entry and check that every entry belongs to $k$. Check the full coefficient equation $f_A(S_0 x)=\lambda_0f_B(x)$ with $\lambda_0\in k^\times$ and deduplicate. This gives every possible residue projectivity. The number of candidates is at most $n^{d+1}$.

An additional interpretation applies if there is an invertible residue slice $A(s)$. The unital algebra generated by $N(x)=\overline A(x)\overline A(s)^{-1}$ is independent of the choice of invertible slice. For a second choice $s'$ one has $N'(x)=N(x)N(s')^{-1}$, and $N(s')^{-1}$ is in the original unital algebra by Cayley–Hamilton. Interchanging $s,s'$ gives equality. A geometric algebra character $\chi$ gives the covector $\chi(N(x))$, whose projective line is chart independent, since another chart divides its value by the nonzero $\chi(N(s'))$. These are exactly the determinant factors contributed by one-dimensional composition factors in the normalized module. Their character covectors can alternatively be extracted over $K$ by computing common eigenfunctionals of left multiplication by the generators on the dual of the normalized algebra. Any such nonzero functional takes a nonzero value on the identity, since otherwise it vanishes on every generating word. Normalizing at one gives exactly the associated algebra character. The determinant method permits additional anchors from higher-dimensional strict constituents and works without a nonsingular rational evaluation.

## 2. The normal equation forces a unique lift of each moving mode

The reason for the multiplicity condition is infinitesimal. Write the residue form over $\overline k$ as $f=f_A=\prod_j f_j^{m_j}$ up to scalar. For a $d$ by $d$ matrix $D$, let $\partial_D f$ be the first-order variation under substitution by $(1+\epsilon D)x$, with $\epsilon^2=0$. If $\partial_D f=\xi f$, the logarithmic differential identity is
$$
 \sum_j m_j\frac{\partial_D f_j}{f_j}=\xi .
$$
At an eligible linear factor $\ell$, all other denominators are units in the localization at $(\ell)$. Multiplying by $\ell$ and reducing modulo $\ell$ gives $m_\ell\ell(Dx)=0$ there. Since $m_\ell\ne0$ in $k$, the covector $\ell$ is an eigenline for the dual of $D$. The $d$ independent covectors in the frame diagonalize the dual map, and the last frame member forces all diagonal entries equal. Thus $D$ is scalar. This argument holds even if the scalar infinitesimal action on the form vanishes because $p\mid n$.

Fix a normalized residue pair $S_0,\lambda_0$ from Section 1. Choose the first nonzero entry of $S_0$ as its anchor entry and insist that it equal one over $R$. Suppose $(S,\lambda)$ satisfies the determinant coefficient equation modulo $\pi^j$. Lift its coordinates arbitrarily modulo $\pi^{j+1}$ subject to the anchor normalization. The next corrections have form $(\pi^j X,\pi^j\eta)$, where $X,\eta$ are over $k$ and the anchored entry of $X$ is zero. Compute the current error divided by $\pi^j$ and solve the linear coefficient equation for $X,\eta$ over $k$. This is exact because terms with two corrections vanish modulo $\pi^{j+1}$.

The homogeneous correction system has trivial kernel. In fact set $D=X S_0^{-1}$. A homogeneous correction is an infinitesimal perturbation by $D$ on the $A$ side after reverting the $S_0$ substitution. The preceding logarithmic differential argument makes $D$ scalar. The anchored entry of $X$ now forces $D=0$, then the nonzero determinant polynomial forces $\eta=0$. Thus each residue projectivity has either zero lifts or one projective lift satisfying determinant equivalence modulo $\pi^e$. The process finds that lift, or proves its absence. Since the determinant of a unit matrix reduces to a unit, all successively lifted slice matrices are invertible.

Every tensor transporter satisfies this determinant equation. The calculation determines its moving slice projectivity before comparing the other two modes. In particular it does not try to lift a chosen strict residue transporter. Different strict witnesses need not lift one another.

## 3. Solving the remaining strict isomorphism problem over $R$

For a resulting slice matrix $S$, compare the two fixed-label $d$-arrow Kronecker representations $M=(A(Sx))$ and $N=(B(x))$ over $R$. The two free vertex modules have rank $n$ each. Let $F=\operatorname{End}_R(M\oplus N)$ be the algebra of pairs of vertex endomorphisms intertwining every arrow. Its defining equations are linear over $R$. Compute the kernel as an $R$-module with generators by Smith reduction over the principal local ring. Such reduction is polynomial in the coefficient sizes and $e$: choose a nonzero pivot of smallest valuation, normalize its unit factor, and clear the respective row and column. The kernel, its reductions and preimages of specified linear combinations are then explicit.

Let $\bar F$ be the image of $F$ in the endomorphisms of the residue vertex spaces, not the full endomorphism algebra of the reduced tuple. It is a $k$-algebra and its multiplication table is obtained by linear algebra on matrices. The kernel of $F\to\bar F$ is nilpotent, since all its entries lie in $\pi R$. The two named summands have idempotents $e_M,e_N\in F$. A strict isomorphism exists exactly when the images of these idempotents have the same matrix ranks in every simple component of $\bar F/\operatorname{Jac}(\bar F)$. Here are both directions. An isomorphism yields inverse corner maps displaying equivalence of the two idempotents and gives the rank equality in the quotient. Conversely rank equality in every matrix algebra yields inverse corner maps in the semisimple quotient by ordinary linear algebra. Lift them to $F$ and project onto the corresponding corners with $e_M,e_N$. Their composites differ from the respective corner units by elements of a nilpotent ideal. Those composites are invertible, so the lifted corner maps are isomorphisms of representations. Thus we obtain an actual strict transporter. The semisimple decomposition used here is the classical randomized polynomial-time finite-algebra algorithm. [Rónyai, computing the structure of finite algebras](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S074771710880017X)

For counting and determinant adjustment, let $E=\operatorname{End}_R(N)$, let $\bar E$ be its residue image, and let $L$ denote the inverse image of $\operatorname{Jac}(\bar E)$ inside $E$. The ideal $L$ is nilpotent. Indeed some power maps to zero in $\bar E$, and the kernel of reduction has nilpotence index at most $e$. We have the exact unit count
$$
 |E^\times|=|L|\prod_i |\operatorname{GL}_{r_i}(k_i)|,\qquad
 E/L\simeq\prod_i\operatorname{Mat}_{r_i}(k_i),
$$
where $k_i$ are finite extensions of $k$. A unit of the quotient lifts to a unit by the nilpotent-ideal inverse formula, and the fiber over the identity is $1+L$. The cardinality of $L$ is computed from its finite $R$-module invariant factors. For any positive strict instance its transporter set is a torsor for $E^\times$. Every unit scalar multiple $uS$ has the same strict count, because scaling $S$ by $u$ scales the second strict map by $u$. This proves the unrestricted exact count by summing over the computed projective lifts.

## 4. Computing the determinant image of the strict automorphism group

Every strict automorphism $(P,Q)$ of $N$ has $\det P=\det Q$. Taking the determinant of its intertwining equation gives the equality times $\det B(x)$, and at least one coefficient of that polynomial is a unit because $f_B\ne0$. Let $H\le R^\times$ be the resulting subgroup of possible determinants.

First compute its residue image. Consider the residue output vertex space as a module for $\bar E$. Let $\nu_i$ be the multiplicity of the simple module for the $i$-th component $\operatorname{Mat}_{r_i}(k_i)$ in a composition series. To compute $\nu_i$, lift the central idempotent of this component to any element $b_i$ of $\bar E$ and compute the rank of its $n$-th power on the output vertex space. This rank is the sum of the dimensions of the factors on which $b_i$ is the identity. One proof uses the Fitting decomposition into the zero and nonzero generalized eigenvalues of $b_i$, whose characteristic polynomial has only roots zero and one by the composition series. Dividing the rank by $r_i[k_i:k]$ gives $\nu_i$.

A unit of $E/L$ contributes determinant $\prod_i N_{k_i/k}(\det U_i)^{\nu_i}$ on the residue vertex space. This follows by the block triangular determinant product through a composition series. Elements of the nilpotent ideal act trivially on the simple factors. Finite-field norms are surjective, so the residue image is the subgroup of $h$-th powers in $k^\times$, where $h=\gcd(q-1,\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_s)$. Test membership by powering. The value $h$ is at most $n$ since some $\nu_i$ is positive.

For the principal-unit part of $H$, generate $1+L$ by explicit elements $1+x$ for lifts $x$ of prime-field bases of $L^j/L^{j+1}$, for all $j\ge1$. Multiplication in each successive quotient is addition. The quotients are $k$-vector spaces because $\pi E\subset L$, and the powers and bases are computable with $R$-module linear algebra. The nilpotence bound is polynomial in the matrix dimensions and $e$. In addition, lift elementary transvections for each matrix factor of $E/L$, for matrix entries from a prime-field basis of $k_i$, leaving all other components the identity. These suffice to lift the elementary subgroup, because transvections at a fixed position add their coefficients. The determinants of such lifts are principal units by the residue determinant formula. The remaining generators of the quotient can be chosen among diagonal elements of the factors, which have order prime to $p$. For any lift $u$ of such an element, raising $u$ to its quotient order gives an element of $1+L$. Its determinant's principal-unit part thus lies in the subgroup generated by determinants from $1+L$, because raising to an integer prime to $p$ is an automorphism of any finite abelian $p$-group and of every subgroup. Consequently the determinant images of our explicit lifts generate exactly the principal-unit part $H\cap(1+\pi R)$.

Here is a polynomial presentation for all principal units. For each $1\le j<e$ and each basis vector $\gamma_\ell$ of $k$ over $\mathbb F_p$, choose $g_{j,\ell}=1+\pi^j\widetilde\gamma_\ell$. Successive reductions modulo $1+\pi^{j+1}R$ express any principal unit uniquely as an ordered product of these generators with exponents in $[0,p-1]$. Because the group is abelian and $g_{j,\ell}^p\in1+\pi^{j+1}R$, the product expressions for their $p$-th powers give a triangular presentation. The presentation has order $q^{e-1}$, the size of the principal-unit group, so it is exact. Integer Smith normal form in this presentation handles membership, orders and shared power equations in polynomial time. This also returns an expression in the strict determinant generators when membership holds. The projection of $R^\times$ onto the principal units is computable as the power $a\mapsto a^{(q-1)c}$, where $c(q-1)\equiv1\bmod q^{e-1}$. The complementary factor maps isomorphically to $k^\times$.

One can construct a strict unit witnessing any determinant in $H$. For its residue determinant $r$, choose an $h$-th root of $r$ over $k$ by factoring a polynomial of degree at most $n$. A Bézout relation among the $\nu_i$ and $q-1$ gives prescribed norm values in $k^\times$ whose weighted product is $r$. Construct the finite-field norm preimages as follows. For $a\in k^\times$ and degree $s=[k_i:k]$, the norm fiber has $(q^s-1)/(q-1)$ elements. If $q=2$ or $s=1$, use the immediate solution. Otherwise the elements in a maximal proper subfield $k_{s/\ell}$, for a prime $\ell\mid s$, satisfy $N_{k_{s/\ell}/k}(x)^\ell=a$. Their proportion in the full fiber is at most $\ell(q^{s/\ell}-1)/(q^s-1)$. For $s=2$ the bound is at most $2/(q+1)\le1/2$, and for $s=3$ it is at most $3/q^2\le1/3$. For $s\ge4$, the sum is at most $s q^{-s/2}\le4/9$, using that the sum of the distinct prime divisors is at most $s$. At least half the full fiber therefore has degree $s$ over $k$. Sampling monic degree-$s$ polynomials with fixed constant coefficient $(-1)^s a$ gives an irreducible polynomial in expected at most $2s$ trials. Find a root in $k_i$ by finite-field factoring. It has norm $a$. Put each preimage in a diagonal position of its factor of $E/L$, lift the resulting unit, and correct its determinant to the desired one using the computed principal-unit subgroup generators. All exponents used for correction have polynomial bit length.

## 5. Prescribed moving and strict determinants

For each exact normalized slice projectivity, choose a strict witness $(P_0,Q_0)$ for $S$. The full triples above this projectivity have moving mode $uS$, and strict determinants
$$
 (\det P_0\, z,\ u^n\det Q_0\, z)
 \quad\text{for }z\in H,\ u\in R^\times .
$$
This is because strict transporters differ by target strict automorphisms and scaling $S$ scales the second strict witness by $u$. Thus for the requested determinant tuple $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ first test $z=\alpha/\det P_0\in H$. The scalar $u$ must solve the simultaneous equations
$$
 u^d=\gamma/\det S,\qquad
 u^n=\beta/(\det Q_0\,z).
$$
On $k^\times$ enumerate the roots of the first equation by a polynomial factorization of degree $d$ and test the second on each. On principal units solve both simultaneous equations in the exact presentation in Section 4, which gives their count as well as a witness. The two factors of $R^\times$ split, so their counts multiply. For each eligible scalar $u$, the number of strict witnesses is $|E^\times|/|H|$. Construct one with the committed determinant adjustment above, and sum the exact counts over all projective candidates. There is no enumeration of the potentially many roots over the entire local ring.

## 6. Exact local normal space

Let $M$ be the residue $d$-arrow Kronecker representation. The strict orbit tangent map is
$$
 \operatorname{End}_k(k^n)\oplus\operatorname{End}_k(k^n)
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_k(k^n,k^n)^d,\qquad
 (X,Y)\longmapsto (X A_i-A_iY)_i.
$$
Its cokernel is $\operatorname{Ext}^1_Q(M,M)$. Indeed a self-extension splits as vector spaces at both vertices, and its upper off-diagonal arrow blocks describe all choices of extensions. Changing the splittings changes those arrow blocks by the displayed strict tangent map. The moving slice tangent map from $\mathfrak{gl}_d(k)$ sends $D$ to the coefficient tuple of $A(Dx)$. If this is a strict tangent direction, then the first variation of the determinant is a scalar times $f_A$, as seen by multiplying $A(x)$ on both sides by the infinitesimal strict maps and taking determinants. Section 2 forces $D$ to be scalar, and conversely any scalar direction is strict. Thus there is an exact sequence
$$
 0\longrightarrow \mathfrak{pgl}_d(k)
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Ext}^1_Q(M,M)
 \longrightarrow N_{\mathrm{TI},M}\longrightarrow 0 ,
$$
where $\mathfrak{pgl}_d(k)$ denotes $\mathfrak{gl}_d(k)/kI_d$ and $N_{\mathrm{TI},M}$ is the cokernel of the combined strict and moving tangent actions. This explicitly separates the moving directions inside the extension space. The determinant variation detects them even in small characteristic when the stated frame is present.

## 7. Hardness with a growing number of slices

If $d$ is allowed to grow, the framed promise already contains a linear-side reduction from Graph Isomorphism over every field. Here is a construction using the earlier MathProofs equal-cup projective configuration. Subdivide every edge of a simple graph once and attach two private five-cycles at each original vertex, letting each cycle meet its original vertex in exactly one vertex. In the resulting graph $G$, all vertices have degree at least two, and the high-degree vertices recover the originals. Two originals are adjacent in the input precisely when they share a neighbor in $G$. The graph $G$ is triangle-free.

For $d=|V(G)|$, take the following covectors in $k^d$:
$$
 e_i,\quad e_i+e_j\quad(ij\in E(G)),\qquad \sum_i e_i .
$$
They are pairwise projectively distinct. The projective lines containing three of these points contain precisely $e_i,e_j,e_i+e_j$ for an edge. Indeed the final point has support larger than four and lies on none of these secants. For the remaining points, disjoint supports can give a third point only from two coordinate points. Nested distinct supports give a coordinate pair and its edge. Two overlapping edge supports would need cancellation of their shared coordinate and a third edge to yield a third point, contradicting triangle-freeness. Consequently coordinate points lie on at least two such lines, edge points on exactly one and the full-support point on none. Projective equivalence of configurations therefore recovers the graph, while a graph isomorphism permutes the displayed covectors exactly.

Make the matrix pencil diagonal with these linear forms as its diagonal entries. Each occurs with multiplicity one in its determinant, and the coordinate points together with the full-support point form a projective frame. Any pencil isomorphism takes the determinant factors to one another and gives the projective equivalence above. Conversely permuting the covectors up to nonzero constants gives a strict transporter using the diagonal-line permutation and scalings. Shapes that already differ can be handled before writing a fixed negative pair satisfying the same promise, and trivial empty graphs can use a fixed positive pair. Dimensions of the nontrivial output are $O(|V|+|E|)$. This direction only needs a field. Hence allowing the slice dimension to grow presents an isomorphism challenge even for completely diagonal tensors whose intrinsic moving tangent map is injective modulo scalars.



## Scope and relation to local quivers

This algorithm uses the strict quiver module only after recovering the moving slice projectivity. At fixed labels, comparing representations over the truncated rings is an idempotent-equivalence problem in the residue image of the actual intertwiner algebra. For the moving mode, the determinant hyperplanes and their multiplicities supply a zero-dimensional effective local comparison after fixing the scalar gauge. The proof gives explicit linear equations for every infinitesimal layer. This is the feature suggested by the local-quiver approach. It uses the full operator/algebra data to certify isomorphism rather than identifying an orbit from its determinant.

The determinant multiplicity requirement is genuine for this route. If $\ell$ occurs with multiplicity divisible by $p$, then its contribution vanishes in the logarithmic differential. Formal deformation theory and classical versal deformations of pencils supply the general infinitesimal setting, while the homogeneous projective frame supplies the input-specific rigidity here. The previously established rational binary-pencil argument treats moving projective roots in dimension two. This result uses any fixed number of slice parameters, possibly nonsplit frames, and arbitrary jet length over the displayed finite local rings. Local slice theory by itself does not identify the correct residue image of an intertwiner algebra over a thickening. This manuscript is an informal proof and publication priority for the complete algorithm remains unverified.
