# Polynomial Calculus reductions among the five tensor actions

## Statement

Let $k$ be any field. For each fixed type of a system of tensors of order at most three on named spaces, with primal and dual occurrences and repeated names, there are deterministic linear-side-length encodings into each of the following.

1. Ordinary three-tensor isomorphism.
2. Matrix-space isometry on independent symmetric or alternating slices.
3. Matrix-space conjugacy.
4. Symmetric or alternating trilinear-form equivalence.
5. Unital associative algebra isomorphism on split basic algebras with a fixed number of simple modules and radical cube zero.

The same construction gives formal cubic-form equivalence over every field. All forward and backward simulations use ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) with only the coefficient equations for the displayed actions and two-sided witness inverse equations. For each encoding there is a constant $C$, depending only on the type, with the following properties.

- A PC refutation of the encoded instance of degree $D$ and sparse size $S$ gives a PC refutation of the source of degree $\max(D,C)$ and sparse size $S+\operatorname{poly}(N)$.
- A PC refutation of the source of degree $D$ gives a PC refutation of the encoded instance of degree at most $\max(CD,C)$. At each fixed degree cutoff its sparse size is polynomial in the input size and original proof size.

Here $N$ is the sum of named space dimensions and the bounds on refutations do not include field equations. The encoding uses $O(N+1)$ side lengths, including when the data are nonconcise. The reverse proof is a genuine bounded-degree substitution. In particular, it does not use a general low-degree inference that two vector spaces of different positive dimensions cannot be isomorphic.

This strengthens polynomial-time and linear-length reductions among these actions in Grochow–Qiao Parts I and IV for the indicated encoding. It yields linear PC degree lower bounds in every target from the published all-field tensor lower bound. The sparse-size lower bounds of MathProofs also transfer to all the targets by the size bound above. Our reductions concern the constructed encodings. They do not identify the exact Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk output for the routes listed in Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She Question 7.5. ## 1. Proof conventions and the typed bilinear map

A source instance has one witness matrix and one inverse matrix for each named space. Dual positions use the transpose inverse. Action equations are coordinate equations on the tensor or bilinear map. Multiplication isotopy uses $H(xy)=F(x)G(y)$. Algebra isomorphism uses a single map in the three roles, with a unit equation optionally included. Trilinear equivalence uses pullback by the witness. Matrix-space witnesses include the invertible basis-change map on the independent slices. Equal dimensions are assumed in the displays. Format mismatches can be decided before compiling.

In the proofs below matrix identities are shorthand for entrywise polynomial operations. A bounded number of multiplications and additions of matrices of polynomial dimensions gives a bounded-degree PC derivation of any displayed replacement. In particular, replacing a factor in a product uses its difference line multiplied by the remaining factors, expanded as monomials, one variable at a time. The number of factors and degree of all witness substitutions below are bounded independently of $N$. When a constant linear system defines a subspace, one may choose a linear coordinate extractor and section by Gaussian elimination on constants. If a polynomial matrix $P$ satisfies the defining linear equations in PC, then the equality of $P$ with its extracted and re-embedded coordinates follows by linear combination with constant coefficients. This applies equally to quotient, corner, and centroid coordinates.

Use the three-copy bilinear construction of the earlier MathProofs linear-length compiler. In detail, replace each order-three tensor by a bilinear map, taking the first two inputs dual to their respective slots. Pad lower-order components by a fixed scalar sort and include scalar multiplication on that sort. Give each named space and its dual separate reference indices. Give each of the three data occurrences their own indices, with maps $r(u)$ to their corresponding reference types. For each reference or occurrence $i$, form copies $X_i,Y_i,Z_i$ of that space. Add the scalar copies $X_0,Y_0,Z_0$ and a private right scalar slot $Y_{b(u)}$ for each occurrence. Form one bilinear map $M:X\times Y\to Z$ on these direct sums with precisely the following blocks.

- Scalar multiplication $X_0\times Y_0\to Z_0$.
- The scalar actions $X_i\times Y_0\to Z_i$ and $X_0\times Y_i\to Z_i$.
- Scalar multiplication $X_0\times Y_{b(u)}\to Z_0$, and the copy block $X_u\times Y_{b(u)}\to Z_{r(u)}$.
- The evaluation block $X_{i^+}\times Y_{i^-}\to Z_0$ on each primal and dual reference pair.
- Each data bilinear map on its private triple of occurrence indices.

Every type uses a fixed number of blocks. All indicated scalar and copy maps have standard coordinate matrices. Basis vectors in $Z$ are products via the scalar action blocks, independently of the data. A source witness acts as a direct sum of copies of its maps and transpose inverses on the three copies. Its scalar slots are fixed. This gives a coordinate-or-constant substitution for a simultaneously summand-preserving isomorphism of the bilinear map. Its inverse identities and transport equations are the source identities and fixed scalar or evaluation identities, at bounded degree.

Conversely, suppose the three maps of an $M$-isomorphism and their inverses preserve the $X,Y$ summands. The $Z_i$ and $Z_0$ also are preserved, by writing their basis vectors as products via $Y_0$. Let $a,b$ denote the maps on $X_0,Y_0$, with their supplied scalar inverses. The equations on the scalar copy maps give some invertible $g_i$ with transformations $ag_i,bg_i,abg_i$ on $X_i,Y_i,Z_i$, respectively. One can use $g_i=a^{-1}h_{X_i}$ and inverse $a h_{X_i}^{-1}$ as explicit substitutions. The private right scalars have map $b$, and the copy equations then give $g_u=g_{r(u)}$. Evaluation gives $g_{i^-}=g_{i^+}^{-T}$ by multiplying the evaluation equation by the transpose inverse. Scalar multiplication in any padding sort gives $\lambda^2=\lambda$ and $\lambda^{-1}\lambda=1$, hence $\lambda=1$. The data equations give the original action after cancelling $ab$. All cancellations here use the displayed inverses and bounded-degree identities.

## 2. Support labels for triangular algebras

Make the triangular unital algebra $A$ with diagonal copies $ke_v$, three main vertices $0,1,2$, and strict corners $e_0Ae_1=X,e_1Ae_2=Y,e_0Ae_2=Z$, composed by $M$. Add a private sink $s_i$ per summand $X_i$, with corners $e_1Ae_{s_i}=k$ and $e_0Ae_{s_i}=X/X_i$, composed by the projection $X\to X/X_i$ times the scalar. Add a private source $t_j$ per $Y$-summand, with corners $e_{t_j}Ae_1=k$ and $e_{t_j}Ae_2=Y/Y_j$, using the analogous projection. An element $x\in X$ is in $X_i$ exactly when its product with the scalar basis arrow toward $s_i$ vanishes. The analogous condition on the left recovers each $Y_j$. The unintended two-step products are zero. Call all these vertices old, ordered $v_1,\dots,v_h$. To $v_i$ add $L_i=(2h+2)i$ private source vertices, one outgoing arrow of dimension one from each to $v_i$, and let all products of two strict corner elements involving these arrows vanish. All other strict products are zero. These additions keep the algebra associative. The only nonzero products of two strict corners in the old subgraph go from an initial old vertex to a terminal old vertex. Any triple involving a new arrow has both parenthesizations zero, by the definitions and by the fact that a new arrow cannot itself be a strict product. Diagonal factors satisfy associativity by the endpoint rules.

Let $J$ be the direct sum of strict corners. Both the commutator span and the Jacobson radical equal $J$, since $[e_i,x]=x$ for $x\in e_iAe_j$ with $i\ne j$, $J^3=0$, and $A/J=k^{h+\sum L_i}$. In fact $J^2$ is the direct sum of the $Z$ corner and projection-output corners, by the surjective scalar-copy and projection products. Thus the support of $J/J^2$ is a fixed graph for each input format, with $L_i$ pendant incoming arrows to $v_i$. The old in-degrees are distinct and positive. In the graph obtained by making every edge bidirectional, the old out-degrees are distinct and larger than one. Within each of these graphs every automorphism therefore fixes all the old vertices. The individual private sources within a fan may be permuted.

The following extraction yields constant-degree PC lines from algebra isomorphism equations for $A,A'$. It applies also to their doubled algebras of Section 4.

**Preservation of characteristic spaces.** Write $F,G$ for an algebra map and its inverse. The multiplicativity equations for $G$ follow by applying $G$ to those for $F$ evaluated on $Gx,Gy$, and using both inverse identities. Preservation of the units follows from multiplicativity at $x=1,y=G1$ and its reverse. Express each strict basis element as the commutator above. Its image belongs to $J'$, since the quotient by $J'$ is commutative. As each basis element of $J^2$ has a fixed expression as a product of strict elements, its image belongs to $(J')^2$. The same arguments hold for $G$. This yields two-sided inverse maps on the explicitly given quotient spaces $A/J$ and $J/J^2$, including the inverse identity on each quotient.

**Extracting support from quotient idempotents.** Let $P_{ai}$ be the $a$-th diagonal coordinate of $F(e_i)$ and let $Q$ be the induced inverse. Multiplicativity and the unit equation give each row's sum-one, idempotence and pairwise collision equations on $P$, with $QP=I$. For every function $\tau$ from target rows to source columns define $w_\tau=\prod_a P_{a,\tau(a)}$. Row sums give $\sum_\tau w_\tau=1$ by expansion. Collision and idempotence give $w_\tau P_{ai}=\boldsymbol 1_{i=\tau(a)}w_\tau$. If $\tau$ misses a column, multiply the respective entry of $QP=I$ by $w_\tau$ to derive $w_\tau=0$. The other functions are permutations. For a permutation $\tau$ put $\sigma=\tau^{-1}$. For a basis vector $x$ in the $(i,j)$ corner of $J/J^2$, multiplicativity on $x=e_i x e_j$ gives, modulo $(J')^2$,
$$
w_\tau F(x)=w_\tau e_{\sigma(i)}F(x)e_{\sigma(j)}.
$$
Indeed after quotienting by $(J')^2$ only the diagonal components of $F(e_i),F(e_j)$ can multiply the strict component of $F(x)$, and the row-selector equations fix each diagonal coefficient on multiplying by $w_\tau$. If the target corner on the right is absent, the weighted image is zero. Applying the quotient inverse implies $w_\tau=0$, by the $x$-coordinate. Thus $w_\tau$ can survive only for permutations sending support edges to support edges. Because both graphs have exactly the same support pattern, these are automorphisms of the support graph. They fix the old vertices. Summing the row selector identities over $\tau$ now proves $F(e_i)\equiv e_i\bmod J'$ for each old $i$. Every expansion here has bounded degree because the total number of vertices is fixed by the tensor type.

**Normalizing the old corners.** Let $e_*$ be the sum of private source idempotents, and let $\mathcal E$ consist of the old idempotents and $e_*$. Put $u=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E} F(e)e'$, where $e'$ denotes the matching target idempotent. The quotient conclusions and the unit equation give $u=1+j$ and $j\in J'$. Thus $u^{-1}=1-j+j^2$ is a polynomial substitution and both inverse identities follow from $(J')^3=0$. Orthogonality of the $F(e)$ gives $F(e)u=ue'$, so the normalized map $F_0(x)=u^{-1}F(x)u$ preserves every old corner. Its inverse is $G_0(y)=G(uyu^{-1})$. To extract $X_i$, apply $F_0$ to each relation $xq_i=0$ for its basis elements $x$, where $q_i$ generates the one-dimensional scalar arrow for the projection onto $X/X_i$. Its target scalar has inverse given by the restriction of $G_0$ to that arrow, so one can cancel it and derive that the $X/X_i$ component of $F_0(x)$ vanishes. The products on the other side give $Y_j$ in the same manner. Applying this reasoning to the inverse gives both directions. Thus the maps on the three main corners reduce to the summand-preserving $M$-isomorphism of Section 1.

Conversely a source witness extends to every old corner of $A$, to the induced quotient blocks on projection outputs, and by the identity on diagonals and all scalar and private-label arrows. The projections are coordinate deletions and the summands are preserved by the block diagonal source maps, so all entries of the forward witness and inverse are coordinates or constants. This gives both PC reductions between the typed systems and the linear-size associative algebra tables.


## 3. Regular matrices and ordinary multiplication tensors

For a unital associative algebra $A$, let $L_a$ be the left multiplication operator. Its span is a matrix space with independent basis obtained from any basis of $A$, since $L_a(1)=a$. A conjugacy witness with slice map $U$ and basis matrix $P$ gives $P L_aP^{-1}=L'_{Ua}$ in the coefficient equations. Multiplying two such identities and using $L_aL_b=L_{ab}$ gives $L'_{U(ab)}=L'_{Ua\cdot Ub}$. Evaluate at the target unit to get multiplicativity of $U$. The two-sided inverses for $U$ are already axioms. A forward algebra isomorphism $F$ gives the regular-space conjugacy with $U=P=F$, providing coordinate substitutions in the other direction.

For ordinary three-tensor isomorphism, take the multiplication tensor of $A$. Two inverse-transpose relabelings when using tensor conventions express its equations as isotopy $H(xy)=F(x)G(y)$, with all three maps invertible. Equivalently, one can derive this isotopy convention from the tensor action by substituting the two inverse maps in the bilinear inputs, and recover the tensor equations by substituting the forward maps. To normalize isotopy within PC, put
$$
a=F(1),\quad b=G(1),\quad c=G(H^{-1}1),\quad d=F(H^{-1}1).
$$
The identities $H=L_aG=R_b F$ and the supplied inverses give two-sided inverse matrices $GH^{-1}$ for $L_a$ and $FH^{-1}$ for $R_b$. Here $R_b$ is right multiplication. Therefore $ac=1$ and $db=1$. Derive $ca=1$ by applying the inverse of $L_a$ to $a(ca-1)=0$, and $bd=1$ by applying the inverse of $R_b$ to $(bd-1)b=0$. The two substitutions
$$
K(x)=dcH(x),\qquad K^{-1}(y)=H^{-1}(ab\,y)
$$
satisfy the inverse equations. They give an algebra isomorphism by $H(xy)=H(x)dcH(y)$, obtained by substituting $F(x)=H(x)d$ and $G(y)=cH(y)$. They preserve the units by $H(1)=ab$. This supplies bounded-degree PC derivations of the algebra equations. An algebra isomorphism gives an isotopy by using the same map and inverse in all positions. The latter substitutions are coordinate identifications.

## 4. Symmetric and alternating matrix spaces

On $A\oplus A^*$ define the matrix space with slices
$$
J_a=\begin{pmatrix}0&L_a^T\\ \varepsilon L_a&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}.
$$
Its slice map $a\mapsto J_a$ is injective, and $\varepsilon$ gives the symmetric and alternating choices. In characteristic two the matrices have zero diagonal and the alternating condition still holds. An algebra isomorphism $F$ gives a congruence using the block change $F\oplus F^{-T}$ and slice change $a\mapsto F(a)$. The forward entries again are coordinates. Use the congruence convention $P^TJ'_{Ua}P=J_a$.

We derive the reverse by extracting a multiplication algebra from ratios of slices. With $b=U(1)$, the matrix $P J_1^{-1}P^T$ is an inverse to $J'_b$, by the congruence at $1$ and the inverse equations for $P$. From its blocks one obtains an inverse matrix for $L'_b$. Evaluating at the target unit gives $b^{-1}$ as a bounded-degree polynomial, using the same associativity and inverse argument as for $a,c$ in Section 3. In lowercase letters, $b^{-1}$ always denotes this explicit substitution. Ratio transport then reads
$$
 P\operatorname{diag}(L_a,L_a^T)P^{-1}
 =\operatorname{diag}(L'_{b^{-1}Ua},(L'_{(Ua)b^{-1}})^T).
$$
The equality follows by multiplying the two displayed slice conjugacy equations, using the known inverse of $J'_b$.

Write $D(A)=A\times_{A/J} A^{\mathrm{op}}$ for the algebra of pairs with a shared quotient image. It has the faithful linear coordinate representation $(u,v^{\mathrm{op}})\mapsto \operatorname{diag}(L_u,L_v^T)$. The ratio on the right belongs to $D(A')$, as its two entries agree modulo $J'$ by commutativity of $A'/J'$. The algebra $D(A)$ is linearly spanned by words of length at most two in the diagonal pairs $\Delta a=(a,a^{\mathrm{op}})$. Indeed, if $x$ is a basis vector in $e_iJe_j$ with $i\ne j$, then
$$
 \Delta e_i\cdot\Delta x-\Delta x=(0,-x^{\mathrm{op}}).
$$
The diagonal pairs and these pure second components span the fiber product. All these identities hold formally over every field.

Conjugation by $P$ thus maps $D(A)$ into $D(A')$ by bounded-degree PC lines. To see each coordinate equation, first use the ratio identity on the diagonal pairs and then use the length-two expressions to obtain the lines on all remaining basis vectors. The reverse congruence, with $U^{-1}$ and $P^{-1}$, gives the reverse containment. Projecting to fixed coordinates of $D(A')$ and $D(A)$ defines the two substitutions, whose re-embedded values are the corresponding conjugations. They are inverse and multiplicative by the inverse equations for $P$.

The commutator span in $D(A)$ is $J\times J^{\mathrm{op}}$. Each strict basis vector is a commutator with the shared diagonal idempotents, and the quotient is commutative. The support of this span modulo its square is the doubled Peirce support from Section 2, with one edge in each direction for each original edge. Thus the old vertices are fixed by the same row selector argument, now using the distinct old out-degrees in the doubled graph. Normalizing with $u$ and its polynomial inverse makes the old corners preserved. The old forward corners of $D(A)$ are precisely the old forward corners in $A$, since $A$ has no backward corners. Their restricted products are unchanged, so the old $X,Y,Z$, scalar projection arrows, and projection quotient corners all admit the same extraction of a typed isomorphism. No recovery of a permutation of the private sources is required.

## 5. Symmetric and alternating forms and cubic polynomials

Use the symmetric choice of Section 4 with $W=A\oplus A^*$, and let $E=A^*$. Put $\beta(w,w')(a)=J_a(w,w')$, a symmetric $E$-valued bilinear map. Form the associative commutative algebra
$$
 C=k\oplus(W\oplus D)\oplus E
$$
where $D=0$ or a one-dimensional dummy annihilator. The ideal $K=W\oplus D\oplus E$ has all products determined by $\beta$, extended by zero on $D$. In particular $K^2=E$ because $\beta((1,0),(0,\phi))=\phi$, and $K^3=0$. Choose $D=0$ unless $1+3\dim_k A=0$ in $k$, in which case $\dim D=1$. Then $\dim C$ is nonzero in $k$. A symmetric matrix-space congruence lifts to an algebra isomorphism of these $C$'s by using $P$ on $W$, identity on $D$ and the inverse transpose of the slice change on $E$. It fixes the scalar. For the constructed forward witnesses these entries are again coordinates or constants.

Conversely let $H$ be a witness for $C\to C'$. The regular space identity $H L_x H^{-1}=L'_{Hx}$ follows from multiplicativity. For every $x\in C$, the trace of $L_x$ equals $(\dim C)$ times the scalar coordinate of $x$. This follows entrywise from the displayed graded multiplication, since left multiplication by any basis element of $K$ strictly increases the grades $k,W\oplus D,E$. Taking the trace of the regular space identity and using the known inverse matrices, we derive that $H$ preserves scalar coordinates. Thus $H,H^{-1}$ preserve $K,K'$ and their squares, with the latter obtained by fixed products as in Section 2. Let $P$ be the induced map on $K/K^2=W\oplus D$ and $U$ the inverse transpose of the map on $E$. The products on $K$ give congruence of the symmetric matrix spaces extended by zero on $D$, using the supplied inverses and coefficient equations. This congruence preserves $D$ by simple matrix identities. For $x\in D$, use the congruence and $P^{-1}$ to derive $J'_{Ua}(Px,z)=0$ for all basis vectors $z$. Substitute $a=U^{-1}1$ to obtain $J'_1(Px,z)=0$. The matrix $J'_1$ is invertible on $W'$, so $Px$ has $W'$-component zero. The reverse argument gives the inverse, and quotienting by $D$ recovers the original symmetric matrix-space congruence. The trace division was by a fixed nonzero field element, made available by at most one dimension of dummy padding.

On $C\oplus C\oplus C^*$ form the trilinear tensor $T$ given on the three different summands, in their fixed order, by $\phi(xy)$. Extend it to the other orders by symmetry or signed alternation, and set it to zero on triples with a repeated summand. For the formal cubic representation use $f(x,y,\phi)=\phi(xy)$. Its full mixed polarization is the symmetric $T$ in every characteristic, including two and three. An algebra isomorphism of the $C$'s induces the forward form equivalences using copies of the map and its transpose inverse.

For the reverse, the centroid of $T$ consists of endomorphisms $c$ that give the same result when inserted in any one of the arguments. It is given by constant linear equations. We recall its coordinates. For a block from summand $i$ to another summand $j$, test the centroid relation by vectors from the summands $i,i,k$ where $k$ is the remaining one. Inserting $c$ on the $k$-vector gives zero because the first two input vectors are from the same summand. Inserting it on the first vector makes only the block from $i$ into $j$ contribute. The unit and free dual separate the resulting pairings, so this block is zero. For the three diagonal blocks $P_1,P_2,P_3$ test an ordered triple of distinct summands to get
$$
 \phi((P_1x)y)=\phi(x(P_2y))=(P_3\phi)(xy).
$$
Setting $x$ or $y$ to the unit shows that $P_1,P_2$ are multiplication by a common element of $C$, and $P_3$ is its dual multiplication. Conversely these endomorphisms are in the centroid. Composition therefore identifies the centroid with $C$ as a unital algebra.

A form equivalence with matrix $S$ and inverse $S^{-1}$ transports any fixed centroid operator by conjugation $S c S^{-1}$. To get each target centroid equation, substitute $S^{-1}$ in the three input slots of the action equation, use $SS^{-1}=I$, and substitute the source centroid equation with these same inputs. This produces bounded-degree lines for every coordinate equation of the target centroid. The reverse use of $S^{-1}$ gives the other map. Apply the constant coordinate extractors for the two centroids as described in Section 1. Multiplicativity and inverses follow from matrix identities of conjugation, producing the isomorphism equations of the $C$'s. For formal cubic input to this construction, simply take full mixed polarization of the cubic coefficient equations. That is a constant linear combination of equations, so it attains the same degree bounds over every field.

## 6. Proof-size accounting and consequences

The number of blocks is fixed by the type. Private source fans add a fixed number of dimensions. All quotient projections in the triangular algebra have coordinate descriptions. The regular-matrix and form representations use constant multiples of the algebra dimension, and the dummy consumes at most one dimension. Thus the tensors and algebra tables have the stated linear side lengths and polynomial explicit output size.

For the forward reductions each encoded variable has a coordinate-or-constant image in the source witnesses. Before translating a proof, derive all substituted encoded axioms by the bounded-degree identities above. This costs polynomial sparse size because the degrees are constant and the number of variables is polynomial in $N$. Thereafter the substitutions preserve linear combinations and take a variable multiplication to a variable multiplication, an identity, or zero. No proof line grows in monomial support. For the reverse reductions every substituted source variable has degree at most a type-dependent constant. A multiplication by such a substituted variable can be simulated using its monomial expansion and linear combinations. The same bounded-degree identities derive the substituted axioms. Hence the degree scales by a constant, and for fixed degree cutoff the expansions of all lines have polynomial size. Exponential sparse lower bounds use the coordinate-or-constant direction only.

Taking a single ordinary TI tensor as the source transfers the Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She lower bound to each explicit output family of size linear in the original side length. Taking the earlier MathProofs all-characteristic sparse PC families transfers their exponential size lower bounds as well. The supported conclusion is linear worst-case degree lower bounds, and exponential sparse size from those source proofs, for structured instances of every action listed above. The corresponding upper bounds and the exact originally named graph reductions in Question 7.5 are separate. The constant-degree equivalences here are a property of the concrete compiler, including its support-based labels and the trace normalization. ## 7. Nullstellensatz and Sum-of-Squares

The same substitutions preserve bounded degree for Nullstellensatz over any field and Sum-of-Squares over the reals. This follows from a stronger property of the displayed algebraic derivations. Each substituted axiom is a polynomial combination of the receiving axioms with a degree bound on every summand depending only on the type. To verify this directly, replace a used difference by its polynomial combination and distribute through the product displaying the difference. The derivations above have bounded multiplication depth: they compose the fixed number of stages, multiply through constant-length words and fixed-size row selectors, and use only constant linear combinations for projections and coefficient comparisons. The loops in basis dimensions only add parallel terms. Thus iterating the replacement does not accumulate a degree depending on $N$. Alternatively over the reals, one can use the standard simulation of degree-$d$ PC derivations by degree at most $2d$ Sum-of-Squares, deriving equality in both directions.

A Nullstellensatz refutation $\sum_i h_i f_i=1$ translates by substituting in all terms, then replacing each $f_i$ by its bounded-degree combination. A real Sum-of-Squares refutation translates in the same way since a substituted square remains a square. Both directions therefore increase degree by at most a type-dependent constant factor and additive constant. The published linear Sum-of-Squares lower bound for ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over the reals transfers in particular to the formal cubic, alternating-form, matrix-space conjugacy, and split basic associative algebra families above.
