# Central group quotients and sparse constraints on the factor matching

Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar Question 3(5) asks for a polynomial-time epimorphism test for Cayley-table groups with central elementary abelian radicals and semisimple quotients that are products of nonabelian simple groups. We give an additional deterministic polynomial-time regime on the binary central products of double covers of the alternating group on five letters. The full question stays open. [Question 3(5)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7) · [Earlier factor-center criterion, Section 4](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FactorCenterEpimorphisms/PROOF.md)

## A binary fiber that can be computed exactly

Put U = SL(2,5) and identify its center with the binary additive group. For binary length-k codes C,D, form G(C) = U^k/C, using the inclusion of C in the center. Both groups have quotient A5^k by their center. A quotient transporter permutes these simple factors and acts by an automorphism on each survivor. The induced coordinate permutation sigma lifts to an epimorphism exactly when sigma(C) is contained in D. In detail, each source factor must cover its matched target factor and has central image on other factors, where perfection kills the image. The unique element of order two in the source factor center either goes to the corresponding center element or dies if that center was killed. Hence all relations on source centers must be target relations. Conversely every coordinate permutation satisfying containment lifts by permuting the universal U factors. The earlier criterion recovers a parity-check matrix of C from a raw promised table by taking as columns the center elements from its identified factor covers, with zero for a split-off A5. The same works for D. Thus k has logarithmic size relative to the group order.

Fix a constant w at least two. From a code X construct a graph H(X) on its coordinates. Join two positions if a codeword of weight at most w contains both. Enumerating supports and checking linear membership costs k to the power O(w). For every containment witness, sigma takes H(C) into H(D) as a spanning subgraph. Reverse reflection for this graph map alone need not hold. We test all source generator rows against D before accepting any permutation.

**Theorem.** Suppose H(D) has m edges. Suppose H(C) has a spanning forest with c components and maximum degree d. There is a deterministic decision and witness algorithm of cost
$$
2^{O(k+m)} k^c (d!)^k k^{O(w)}
$$
for fixed d (and the same parameter dependence when a forest is supplied). In particular, if m is O(k), d is bounded, and c is O(k/log(k+1)), then the binary subcode problem takes singly exponential time and the raw promised group problem takes polynomial time. A second version only requires H(D) to have bounded maximum degree and the component bound. The codewords of weight at most w need not generate C. The remaining dense constraints are checked exactly at the leaves.

To find a suitable forest, use the connected components of H(C). In each store for every subset and candidate root whether there is a rooted tree with all nonroot degrees at most d and root with at most b children. Strip one child subtree, guessing its subset, its root adjacent to the old root, and the remainder. The child root gets budget d-1 and the old root budget b-1. Singletons have zero children. Disjoint subset splits sum to at most 3^k times a polynomial. Use budget d at the root of a component and store choices to reconstruct. A spanning forest with more components can be handled by the same enumeration of component partitions, or simply remove edges and use the subset dynamic program for a smallest number of degree-bounded trees. For this latter variant, allow a component to be any feasible subset and split that subset from its complement in the covered set. This minimizes c in singly exponential time. Use d at least one; the empty and singleton inputs are direct.

For each edge subset of H(D), inspect the forest it defines on all vertices. Enumerate isomorphisms of the chosen source forest onto this forest by matching roots, then children. It suffices to guess the c root images in at most k^c ways. A vertex gives at most d! child matchings. Reject on degrees, repeated targets, or incorrect adjacencies of forest edges. Every containment witness appears when its own image forest is enumerated. Finally verify the generator matrix of C under the proposed permutation by linear algebra against D. This checks containment completely, even when dense source relations cross between forest components. Returning the first successful permutation also constructs a group epimorphism on the quotient presentations. For bounded target maximum degree one may instead map each tree outward, trying at most that maximum number of images for each new vertex. Sum only over root guesses and these local choices. This proves the second version without a source degree promise. All tables and local bounds are effectively available. This is a special-purpose use of ordinary subgraph search, and the literature already uses low-weight words in code equivalence. [Sendrier on support splitting](https://www.rocq.inria.fr/secret/PUBLICATIONS/RESUMES/Sendrier00.html) · [Santini–Baldi–Chiaraluce on subcode equivalence](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2023.3323068)

## A second exact description, including repeated coordinates

Let the columns of a generator matrix of C be u_i and those of a parity-check matrix of D be v_j. Group identical columns on each side, writing s_a and t_b for multiplicities of the distinct column types u_a and v_b. There is a containment witness precisely when a binary matrix E on the pairs of types satisfies the three tests below.

1. The row sums as integers are at most s_a and congruent to s_a modulo two.
2. The column sums as integers are at most t_b and congruent to t_b modulo two.
3. The binary matrix sum of E_ab times the outer product u_a v_b^T is zero.

Indeed, count how many positions of each source type are matched to each target type. Reduction of these counts modulo two gives E and preserves the required orthogonality equations. Conversely subtract the row and column sums of E from the required margins and divide the leftovers by two. These nonnegative integer margins have equal totals. Fill them by a nonnegative integral transportation table (greedy pairing of remaining units suffices), double that table and add E. The resulting counts realize a permutation by arbitrary bijections of the allocated positions. This gives both a uniform decision criterion and an explicit decoder. For square-free multiplicities on both sides the margin conditions once again describe arbitrary permutations. An unrestricted singly exponential solver for the parity-and-capacity problem has not been derived here.

## What the scope buys

A weight-three example uses coordinates indexed by the edges of a wheel with a rim cycle of length k' at least seven. Take C to be its cycle code. Its triangles are exactly the k' rim-edge triangles, each sharing a spoke with each neighbor. Their coordinate graph has bounded degree and is connected, even though the wheel has a hub of unbounded degree. There are 2k' coordinates. We may enlarge C by a linear space of dimension proportional to k' without changing any short words: in the quotient of dimension k', choose a uniform subspace of dimension floor(k'/2). The probability it contains any specified nonzero coset is (2^floor(k'/2)-1)/(2^k'-1). The union bound over the O(k'^3) supports of size at most three is less than one for large k'. Its preimage D has the same triangle graph and a linearly larger dimension. Arbitrary relabeling preserves the promise. The proof gives an infinite class; it makes no claim of a worst-case single-exponential algorithm for all binary codes.
## Enumerating the image subcode before its lifts

There is a second algorithm that removes both the connectivity and the bounded-degree conditions when the source relations are generated by a small collection of target-compatible weights. Let W be any specified set of weights. Let a be the dimension of the span of source words of those weights, let M count the nonzero target words of those weights, let s = dim C minus a, and let g = dim D minus dim C. Reject if g is negative. In deterministic time
$$
2^{O(k)} {M\choose a}\, 2^{sg}
$$
when M is at least a, one can decide permutation containment. When M is smaller than a there is no containment. This also applies to the dual reversed pair since coordinate permutations commute with orthogonal complements.

Enumerate the two codes in time singly exponential in k. A basis of the selected source words must map to a set of a linearly independent selected target words. Enumerate subsets of a distinct target words, discard dependent choices, and call their span U. For each U, enumerate all s-dimensional subspaces of D/U and pull them back. There are at most four times two to the power sg by the binary Gaussian coefficient for dimension s inside s+g. Reduced row echelon bases list them with polynomial overhead per output (choose pivots and fill the free entries). Every pulled-back space X is contained in D and has dimension dim C. Apply the known singly exponential algorithm for permutation equivalence to X and C. A positive answer suffices, regardless of why this X was tried. Conversely every successful permutation supplies one enumerated U and one X, its actual full image. This establishes the bound with no shared-permutation assumption between the two search stages. [Babai–Codenotti–Grochow–Qiao, Code Equivalence and Group Isomorphism](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/1.9781611973082.107)

In particular if M is O(k) and sg is O(k), the group decision algorithm is polynomial on the raw tables. This includes an unbounded number of disconnected short-word components, and even unbounded local degrees. The wheel construction above has a basis consisting of its rim triangles (independence follows from their distinct rim edges), M = k' at weight three, s = 0, and g grows linearly. Here the new search enumerates exactly one candidate quotient span. The equality test cannot simply be applied to the two original codes, since their dimensions differ. No publication priority for these parameter bounds is asserted.
