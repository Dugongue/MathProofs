# Epimorphisms onto bounded-generator groups with low-rank derived Fitting layers

Informal proof. Fix three constants $d,t,r$. Let $G,H$ be given by complete Cayley tables. Assume $H$ can be generated by at most $d$ elements. Write $F=\operatorname{Fit}(H)$ for its Fitting subgroup and $B=F'$. Assume $B$ has derived length at most $t$, and every primary component of each abelian layer $B^{(i)}/B^{(i+1)}$ can be generated by at most $r$ elements. There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm counting all epimorphisms $G\twoheadrightarrow H$ and constructing one when it exists. The algorithm can also impose any supplied point-value constraints.

More generally, if $|H|\mid|G|$, set $e=|G|/|H|$ and let $R_p$ be the sum of the ranks of the $p$-primary parts of the derived layers of $F'$. The exact algorithm takes $(|G|+|H|)^{O(d+1)}\prod_{p\mid e} e_p^{R_p}$ bit time. In particular the problem is polynomial for a bounded-generator target whenever $\gcd(e,|F'|)=1$, without restrictions on the ranks or derived length of $F'$.

There is no order bound on the derived Fitting subgroup, no nilpotency-class bound, and no bound on the rank of $F/F'$. The action need not be coprime or semisimple and the extension need not split. This gives a new case of Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar Open Question 3(2), on bounded-generator targets, and of their counting Open Question 2. The arbitrary bounded-generator clause remains open here. [Original questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)

## 1. Bounded generators for an action on a bounded-rank primary module

Let $A$ be a finite abelian $p$-group generated by at most $r$ elements, and let $P\le \operatorname{Aut}(A)$ be a $p$-subgroup. Then $P$ is generated by at most
$$
c(r)=2r^2+r(r-1)/2
$$
elements, independently of the exponent and of $p$.

To prove this, write $A=\bigoplus_{j=1}^s\mathbb Z/p^{a_j}\mathbb Z$, with $s\le r$, let $a=\max a_j$, and present $A$ as a quotient of $(\mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z)^s$ by its diagonal kernel $D$. Every automorphism of $A$ lifts to a matrix preserving $D$: lift each image of a standard generator to a column. Since $D$ lies in the submodule of multiples of $p$, the reduction of this matrix modulo $p$ induces the invertible map on $A/pA$. Thus the matrix is invertible. It sends $D$ into $D$ and hence onto $D$ by finiteness. In the preimage of $P$ in this matrix stabilizer, any Sylow $p$-subgroup surjects onto $P$. It suffices to bound the number of generators of any $p$-subgroup $U$ of the general linear group over $\mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z$.

For $i\ge1$, the congruence subgroups $U_i=U\cap(1+p^i\operatorname{Mat}_s)$ have quotients $U_i/U_{i+1}$ identified with subspaces $V_i$ of $\operatorname{Mat}_s(\mathbb F_p)$ by leading coefficient. Starting at $i=1$ for odd $p$ and $i=2$ for $p=2$, the identity
$$
(1+p^iX)^p\equiv 1+p^{i+1}X\pmod {p^{i+2}}
$$
gives $V_i\subseteq V_{i+1}$ whenever the next grade is present. Select representatives for each new basis direction when it first appears. There are at most $s^2$ such directions in total. The appropriate powers of these representatives give a spanning set at each subsequent grade. Descending through the quotients then shows that the selected representatives generate the corresponding first congruence subgroup. In characteristic two the possible grade $U_1/U_2$ costs a further $s^2$ generators. Finally the image modulo $p$ is a $p$-subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}_s(\mathbb F_p)$, of order at most $p^{s(s-1)/2}$, so needs at most $s(s-1)/2$ generators. This proves the claimed bound, including when some congruence levels are absent.

## 2. Polynomial enumeration of homomorphisms into $B$

Let $X,B$ be finite Cayley-table groups, with $B$ nilpotent and satisfying the stated bounds $t,r$ on its derived layers. All homomorphisms $X\to B$ can be enumerated in polynomial time. The source $X$ is arbitrary.

Lift along the derived series of $B$. In one step, let $E$ be the next target, let $A\unlhd E$ be its abelian derived layer, and fix a homomorphism $\psi:X\to E/A$. A table section gives the action and factor set in the usual extension equations. The set of lifts of $\psi$ is empty or is an affine translate of $Z^1(X,A)$, the group of crossed homomorphisms for the induced action. The equations
$$
z(xy)=z(x)+x\cdot z(y)
$$
for the homogeneous part are additive congruences in explicit cyclic coordinates. Smith normal form supplies a particular solution of a nonempty affine system and a cyclic presentation of the homogeneous solution group. Iterating through the cyclic parameters lists the solutions with polynomial overhead per map.

Here is the bound on the number of those parameters. For a primary subgroup $A_p$ let $P$ be the image of the action of $X$ on $A_p$, and $L$ its kernel. Because $E$ is nilpotent and its Sylow subgroups commute, only its $p$-part can act on $A_p$. Consequently $P$ is a $p$-group, so it is generated by at most $c(r)$ elements by Section 1. Restriction of a crossed homomorphism to $L$ is an ordinary homomorphism. The number of possible restrictions is at most $|X|_p^r$. Indeed $A_p$ is a direct product of at most $r$ cyclic $p$-groups, and maps from a finite abelian $p$-group to one cyclic group number no more than the order of the source. The $p$-primary abelianization of $L$ has order at most $|X|_p$. If two crossed homomorphisms have the same restriction, their difference vanishes on $L$ and factors through $X/L=P$. Its values at generators of $P$ determine it, so each restriction has at most $|A_p|^{c(r)}$ completions. It follows that
$$
|Z^1(X,A)|\le \prod_p |X|_p^r |A_p|^{c(r)}
\le |X|^r |A|^{c(r)}.
$$
This product bound has no dependence on the number of distinct primes. Inductively the number of homomorphisms into the full $B$ is at most $|X|^{rt}|B|^{c(r)}$. All intermediate lifts also obey this bound. The argument uses linear congruences in the raw table model, with polynomial bit complexity and at most polynomially many solutions for fixed $r,t$. [Kannan–Bachem on polynomial-time Smith and Hermite normal forms](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

## 3. Applying the enumeration to full epimorphisms

Use the earlier Fitting quotient and commutator-recovery procedure. Let $Q=H/F$. For clarity, its input to Section 2 and its exact final counting stage are as follows. [Earlier Fitting-subgroup epimorphism proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FittingSubgroupEpimorphisms/PROOF.md)

Choose a chief series of $G$. The preimage $L$ of $F$ under any epimorphism $G\to H$ is the intersection of the centralizers of the surviving chief sections. The chosen source chief series maps to a target chief series after repeated terms are removed, and a surviving section is equivariantly isomorphic to its original section, by minimal normality. The Fitting subgroup of a finite group is the intersection of the centralizers of its chief sections. To see this, the intersection inherits a central series from the chief series. Conversely, any normal nilpotent subgroup has trivial intersection with a chief section and commutes with it in the corresponding quotient, or contains that section and centralizes it by minimality and the fact that a nontrivial normal subgroup of a nilpotent group meets the center. Since there are at most $\log_2|G|$ chief sections, there are at most $|G|$ candidate intersections. Deduplicate them and enumerate isomorphisms $G/L\to Q$ by scanning images of the images of the fixed $d$ generators of $H$. This produces a polynomial-size set of all possible quotient maps $\psi:G\twoheadrightarrow Q$, possibly including extra candidates.

Fix a generating tuple $h_1,\ldots,h_d$ of $H$, padding with identities if needed, and choose prospective preimages $x_i\in\psi^{-1}(h_iF)$. Pick generators $n_j$ of $F$ and write them as words in the $h_i$. Evaluate these words at the $x_i$ to obtain $u_j\in L$. A compatible full map $f$ has image $F$ on $L$, so it gives a map $b=f|_{L'}:L'\to F'$. Enumerate all such candidate homomorphisms using Section 2. For each $l\in L$ scan $a\in F$ imposing
$$
[a,n_j]=b([l,u_j])\quad\text{for all }j.
$$
If a solution exists, it determines one coset of $Z(F)$, since it specifies the action by conjugation on generators of $F$. This recovers the image of $l$ modulo $Z(F)$ for every true lift. For any $g\in G$, choose a word $w$ in the $x_i$ with $\psi(w)=\psi(g)$. Use the recovered coset for $gw^{-1}\in L$ and the specified image of $w$ to determine the image of $g$ modulo $Z(F)$. Test the full table equations for the resulting map $G\to H/Z(F)$, and reject inconsistent candidates.

The remaining lift through $Z(F)$ is an affine congruence system over a finite abelian group. Include the specified $f(x_i)=h_i$, the entire restriction $f|_{L'}=b$, and any additional point values. Every solution is onto because it contains the chosen generating tuple in its image. Smith form returns the exact count and one such solution if the fiber is nonempty. For fixed anchors each true $f$ determines its unique $(\psi,b)$ and its computed map modulo $Z(F)$, so there is no duplicate within that branch. If $|H|\mid |G|$, each epimorphism occurs in exactly $(|G|/|H|)^d$ anchor branches. Divide by this factor after summing. If divisibility fails the count is zero. All table operations and affine systems are polynomial size. Neither the enumeration of $b$ nor the final affine count assumes a bounded rank for $F/F'$.

## 4. A stronger bound when the full generator anchors are used

Let $N=|G|+|H|$, assume $|H|\mid|G|$, and put $e=|G|/|H|$. Let $e_p$ be the $p$-part of $e$. Write $r_{i,p}$ for the minimum number of generators of the $p$-primary part of $B^{(i)}/B^{(i+1)}$, and set $R_p=\sum_i r_{i,p}$. The full algorithm in fact runs in
$$
N^{O(d+1)}\,\prod_{p\mid e}e_p^{R_p}.
$$
In particular no generator or derived-length bound on $F'$ is needed when $\gcd(e,|F'|)=1$. This permits overlap with primes dividing the size of $F/F'$ or the order of $H/F$. More generally let $\Omega(n)$ denote the total number of prime factors counted with multiplicity. As $R_p\le v_p(|F'|)$, for each $p$ we have
$$
e_p^{R_p}\le N^{\min\{v_p(e),v_p(|F'|)\}},
$$
because both $e_p$ and $|F'|_p$ are at most $N$. The algorithm therefore also takes $N^{O(d+1+\Omega(\gcd(e,|F'|)))}$ time. In particular polynomial time extends to any bounded total multiplicity of common primes, even if the common primes themselves grow.

Fix an anchor tuple $x_i$, and let $K=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_d\rangle$. Its restriction $f_K:K\to H$ is determined by the required values. Find it by word propagation and check all table products. Reject an inconsistent tuple. The consistent map is onto because its image contains the $h_i$. For each candidate $\psi:G\to H/F$ verify that it agrees with $f_K$ modulo $F$ on the anchors. This also ensures $f_K(K\cap L)=F$ for $L=\ker\psi$. Set $X=L'$ and $J=K\cap X$. Reject the branch if $f_K(J)$ is not contained in $F'$, since every compatible full map sends $L'$ into $F'$. In the remaining branches
$$
f_K(J)=F',
$$
since $(K\cap L)'$ is contained in $J$ and already maps to $F'$. Also $[G:K]\mid e$, because $f_K$ is onto, and $[X:J]=[XK:K]\mid [G:K]$ since $X\unlhd G$.

We therefore need to enumerate only those $b:X\to B$ extending the fixed epimorphism $f_K|_J:J\to B$. In Section 2, keep its projected values at every step. Given a candidate lift to a quotient $P_i$ of $B$, let $D_i\unlhd X$ be its kernel and let $A_i$ be the abelian kernel of the next target quotient map. The current map sends $J$ onto $P_i$, so $X=D_iJ$. The difference of any two next lifts is a crossed homomorphism vanishing on $J$. It is determined by its restriction to $D_i$, which is an ordinary homomorphism into $A_i$ vanishing on $D_i\cap J$. Therefore it factors through the quotient of $D_i$ by the normal closure of $D_i\cap J$. The order of this quotient divides
$$
[D_i:D_i\cap J]=[X:J]\mid e.
$$
For each primary component of $A_i$ there are at most $e_p^{r_{i,p}}$ such homomorphisms, by cyclic abelian coordinates. It follows that the list of all admissible $b$'s has size at most $\prod_p e_p^{R_p}$. The same bound applies to intermediate lists. Each layer is still an affine system with the prescribed values on $J$, so Smith form enumerates exactly those lists in time polynomial per feasible or tested solution. No action-group generator bound is needed in this version. All other steps and the count normalization from Section 3 still apply.

For a broader time bound, suppose $r_{i,p}\le r$ for a constant $r$ but allow unrestricted derived length. If $B\ne1$ has derived length $t$, then $B^{(t-1)}\ne1$ is contained in its lower central term of index $2^{t-1}$, using the lower-central commutator inclusion. A finite nilpotent group of class $c$ has order at least $2^c$, since each proper lower central drop has index at least two. Thus $t\le 1+\log_2\log_2 |B|$. The anchored algorithm takes $N^{O(\log\log(N+4))}$ time for fixed $d,r$ and is polynomial when $\log e\log\log(N+4)=O(\log N)$. These bounds come from the actual index of the generator anchor and do not require enumerating the unrestricted homomorphism space from Section 2.

## 5. An explicit family outside the previous hypotheses

For $w\ge 3$, let $K=D_{2^w}$ be the dihedral group of order $2^w$, and put $P_w=K\wr C_2$, with the two copies of $K$ interchanged. This group is generated by at most three elements. Its derived subgroup projects onto $K$ under either coordinate of the base, as the elements $(x,x^{-1})$ occur as commutators with the interchange. It contains $K'\times K'$, which is abelian of generator rank two. Modulo $K'\times K'$, its derived subgroup is the diagonal in $K_{\mathrm{ab}}\times K_{\mathrm{ab}}$, of order four. Hence $P_w'$ is nonabelian of unbounded class and order as $w$ grows, and it has derived length two with both abelian derived layers of generator rank at most four.

Choose $m\ge2$ and $2^k\ge m$. Let $Q_m=(A_5)^{C_{2^k}^2}\rtimes C_{2^k}^2$, with regular translation on the base coordinates. This group is generated by at most four elements and is Fitting-free. A nilpotent normal subgroup intersects the base trivially, because the intersection would be a normal subgroup of a direct product of nonabelian simple groups. It therefore centralizes the base, whose centralizer is trivial by faithful permutation action and centerlessness. Let $V_m$ be the additive group of $\mathbb F_2[x,y]/(x,y)^m$, acted upon through the complement quotient by multiplication by $1+x$ and $1+y$. These units have orders dividing $2^k$, and $V_m$ is cyclic as a module. Its subspace $(x,y)^{m-1}$ is fixed by the whole $Q_m$ and has dimension $m$.

Finally put $H=P_w\times(V_m\rtimes Q_m)$. Then $d(H)\le 8$, its Fitting subgroup is $F=P_w\times V_m$, and $F'=P_w'$. The Fitting subgroup is not Hall and the trivial simple has socle multiplicity at least $m$ in the conjugation module $F/F'$. This family is outside the earlier $F'$-abelian hypotheses and cannot use the earlier bounded-socle estimate for the first derived Fitting layer with a uniform constant. It is covered by the new polynomial algorithm. ## Scope of the original question

The structural work changes the enumeration from generator images in a large nonabelian $F'$ to a bounded number of affine fibers with provably small action-group generator sets. The source paper leaves the full bounded-generator clause open. The nearby retraction reduction in the existing research also does not give full closure: one can guess the preimages of fixed target generators, form their subgroup and its forced surjection, and quotient out the normal closure of that surjection's kernel. A compatible epimorphism then exists exactly when the embedded target copy is a retract. This reduction imposes no uniform bound on the derived layers of $F'$, and solving the resulting unrestricted retraction problem would require a further theorem. The cited literature establishes the cohomological and finite-matrix tools. Priority for this particular enumeration and its application was not established by the search.
