# Prior results and audit for the fixed-vertex affine margin

The two-vertex Frobenius-norm margin and its coarse optimal flags have exact prior art. The comparison for a bounded number of vertices concerns an affine special-linear optimization with simultaneous vertex trace constraints and exact rational output. A fixed-vertex solution follows by combining the height-shift support function with a finite price-chamber search and established Harder–Narasimhan algorithms.

## Which instability is being computed

Let $A\ne0$ be a rational quiver representation with heights $h_t-h_s=1$ along every arrow. For the action $A_a\mapsto g_t A_a g_s^{-1}$ of $G=\prod_v\mathrm{SL}(V_v)$, write $w_{v,i}$ for integral cocharacter weights with $\sum_i w_{v,i}=0$. The order of approach to zero, when the limit exists and equals zero, is

$$
\operatorname{ord}_0(w\cdot A)=
\min\{w_{t,j}-w_{s,i}:(A_a)_{ji}\ne0\}.
$$

The order follows by pulling back the ideal generated by the arrow coordinates to the one-parameter curve. The positive maximum of this order divided by $(\sum_{v,i}w_{v,i}^2)^{1/2}$ is the affine Kempf index with the convention of maximizing positive orders. The associated weighted flags are the usual optimal weighted flags. Kempf gives the optimal indivisible cocharacters, their common parabolic and conjugacy under its unipotent radical. The conjugating elements preserve the filtrations, so the relative weights are fixed when normalized to order one. For rational data the canonical geometric flags descend to $\mathbb Q$. A basis adapted to rational flags gives a rational splitting and an integral cocharacter after clearing weight denominators. Existence, uniqueness in this sense, and rationality are classical parts of the result. [Kempf, *Instability in Invariant Theory*, §§3–4](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1978/108-2/p04).

King character instability uses a different objective on the product of general linear groups. It tests cocharacters for which the limit exists, then evaluates a determinant character on the cocharacter. The limit can be nonzero. The numerator is linear in all vertex weight sums. On the product of the special linear groups this determinant character is trivial. Zamora and Hoskins identify the corresponding character instability flags or strata with Harder–Narasimhan filtrations. Cheng's explicit optimum in §§4.3–4.4 maximizes the character pairing divided by the norm among cocharacters with a limit. The general Kempf theorem covers both constructions with their respective objectives. [Zamora](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.5475) · [Hoskins](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.6811) · [Cheng, Theorem 4.6 and (4.4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.06428#S4).

## Exact prior art for the two-vertex formula

Hirai and Sakabe, Theorem 4.24 and equation (4.33), compute the optimal Frobenius-normalized recession direction in the rectangular matrix-tuple case and give the squared affine margin

$$
\frac{1}{C_A}-\frac1m-\frac1n,\qquad
C_A=\sum_i\frac{m_i n_i}{m_i+n_i},
$$

where $(m_i,n_i)$ are the coarse noncommutative Dulmage–Mendelsohn block dimensions. The matrix-tuple left-right action in their analysis uses an adjoint on the right. Pass to the inverse on the source in the cocharacter description by reversing the source sign. Their cocharacter directions and coarse flags give the same affine optimum after that identification. The normalized recession objective is explicitly the extremal support weight in (4.20), and (4.29)–(4.32) give its weights and subspaces. Iwamasa, Oki and Soma identify the coarse decomposition with a generalized Kronecker Harder–Narasimhan filtration in §4.3 and give a polynomial algorithm for computing it. [Hirai and Sakabe, Theorems 4.20 and 4.24](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.09746) · [Iwamasa, Oki and Soma, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06493#S4.SS3).

To verify the identity with the principal-slope formula, put $D=m+n$, $C=mn/D$, and $b_i=m_i/(m_i+n_i)-m/D$. The ratio $m_i/(m_i+n_i)$ is a monotone reparametrization of the source/target dimension ratio. It gives the same block ordering, with the endpoint ratios allowed for zero one-sided block dimensions. In the price notation $B=\sum_i (m_i+n_i)b_i^2$, so

$$
B=\sum_i\frac{m_i^2}{m_i+n_i}-\frac{m^2}{D},
\qquad C-B=C_A,\qquad
\frac{B}{C(C-B)}=\frac1{C_A}-\frac1m-\frac1n.
$$

Thus the two-vertex numerical formula and optimizer should be attributed to Hirai and Sakabe. This also places the two-vertex exact rational computation within the combination of that formula and exact coarse decomposition algorithms. The matrix-tuple score's invariance under slice mixing is immediate because it uses the common matrix space. A separation example using this score has a separate priority question from the score itself.

## Prior algorithms for larger quivers

- Iwamasa, Oki and Soma, Theorems 4.3 and 4.5, identify the principal partition with a Harder–Narasimhan filtration and compute the filtration for acyclic quivers with Gaussian integer entries. The time bound is polynomial in the input bit length, dimensions, quiver size and **absolute values** of the integral slope weights. This makes them suitable for the polynomial-magnitude representative prices described below. For rational input, denominators can be cleared in each arrow independently because scalar multiplication by a nonzero rational preserves every subrepresentation. [Full paper, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06493#S4).
- Cheng, Theorems 3.5 and 3.7, constructs discrepancy witnesses and Harder–Narasimhan flags by an algebraic route. Its stated field-operation bound is polynomial in weight magnitudes, total dimension and the number of paths. If the vertex count $k$ is fixed, every path in an acyclic quiver uses at most $k-1$ arrows, including when the input has parallel arrows. Ivanyos, Qiao and Subrahmanyam give a polynomial **bit** bound over $\mathbb Q$ for the constituent noncommutative-rank and shrunk-subspace computation. Cheng's claim over an infinite field is a field-operation bound, so the rational bit bound is a distinct part of an implementation. [Cheng, §3 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.06428) · [Ivanyos, Qiao and Subrahmanyam](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03531).
- John Maar, *On the Gap of Quiver Representations*, considers the same product of special linear groups and the same affine null cone. His inverse-gap bounds give polynomial-time **membership** for type $A$, affine type $A$, and uniform-dimension tree quivers. The bounds and their geodesic optimization application address a representation-dependent condition number. The algorithmic output is a membership decision. [Maar, Theorems 1.9–1.11](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02805).
- The general complex moment–weight duality identifies the optimal numerical instability with the minimum moment-map norm on the projective orbit closure in the unstable case. Hirai and Sakabe also establish convergence of a gradient method to a maximally destabilizing direction. This supplies a general variational description and limiting computation. Their concrete exact block formula is for matrix tuples. [Georgoulas, Robbin and Salamon, *The Moment-Weight Inequality and the Hilbert–Mumford Criterion*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.0410) · [Hirai and Sakabe, Theorems 4.5, 4.7 and 4.14](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.09746).

## Exact price-chamber audit

Let $d=(d_v)_v$, $D=\sum_v d_v$, $c_v=\sum_u d_u h_u/D-h_v$, $r_v=d_v c_v$, and $C=\sum_v d_v c_v^2$. With a least arrow exponent normalized to one, the shift $q_{v,i}=w_{v,i}+c_v$ converts the inequalities to preservation of all weighted superlevel subrepresentations. Write $m(q)$ for the vector of vertex sums. The determinant constraints then say $m(q)=r$, and $\|w\|^2=\|q\|^2-C$. A vertex incident only to zero maps can be assigned zero cocharacter weights and removed from the computation. This lets an implementation on dense rational arrow matrices bound $D$ by the input length on the remaining vertices. The nonzero arrow and its unit height jump imply $r\ne0$.

For a price $\lambda\in\mathbb R^k$, let $\beta_\lambda$ assign the slope $\lambda\cdot a/|a|$ to each graded block of dimension vector $a$ of the Harder–Narasimhan filtration. The principal-slope support identity gives

$$
\tfrac12\|\beta_\lambda\|^2
=\max_q\{\lambda\cdot m(q)-\tfrac12\|q\|^2\},
\qquad
\lambda\cdot m(\beta_\lambda)=\|\beta_\lambda\|^2.
$$

The maximum is over admissible weighted flags and is attained by $\beta_\lambda$. For two prices, take a common adapted basis for their maximizing flags. In its coordinate description the two maximizers are Euclidean projections of the lifted prices, constant $\lambda_v$ and $\eta_v$ on vertex coordinates, onto the same cone of weak arrow inequalities. Projection is nonexpansive, so $m(\beta_\lambda)$ is Lipschitz with constant at most $\max_vd_v$. The two supporting inequalities for the displayed maximum then show that its gradient is $m(\beta_\lambda)$.

Build the homogeneous arrangement of nonzero normals $|b|a-|a|b$ over $0<a,b\le d$, where inequalities between vectors are componentwise and $0<a$ means nonzero. These normals decide all slope comparisons in all subquotients. Their entries have magnitude $O(D^2)$. There are $D^{O(k)}$ normals and $D^{O(k^2)}$ chambers. A full-dimensional chamber has an integer representative of magnitude $D^{O(k)}$: maximize a uniform signed slack $\epsilon$ in the unit box, choose an optimal vertex with $\epsilon>0$, and clear denominators by its basis determinant. The arrangement can be enumerated by incremental exact linear-feasibility tests, with a polynomial number of cells at every step for fixed $k$.

The entire Harder–Narasimhan chain is constant on each open chamber. The set of maximal-slope subrepresentations is unchanged by a price change within a chamber, as is the largest one among them. Repeat this argument on the one fixed quotient at each step. For its graded block dimensions $a_i$, the objective on the chamber is

$$
\|\beta_\lambda\|^2=\lambda^\mathsf{T}
\left(\sum_i\frac{a_i a_i^\mathsf{T}}{|a_i|}\right)\lambda.
$$

This quadratic remains valid on the closed chamber. The filtration at a boundary price is evaluated from the stored generic chain by assigning its limiting slopes and merging equal consecutive grades. The chain stays admissible, and its value agrees with the continuous support function. Consequently a potentially large-denominator optimizer price never needs a fresh pseudopolynomial Harder–Narasimhan call.

Minimize the quadratic over each closed chamber with $\lambda\cdot r=1$. A feasible piece has a minimum because the image of a closed polyhedron under the linear square-root map for the quadratic is a closed polyhedron. One exact fixed-dimensional implementation enumerates independent active normals together with the affine equality, imposes the linear stationarity equations along that affine face, and tests rational feasibility in the closed chamber. A minimizer lies in the relative interior of its smallest face and is stationary along it. All coefficients have polynomial bit size, including the sum of fractions defining the Hessian. If the global optimum is $\delta>0$, differentiability and stationarity give $m(\beta_\lambda)=\delta r$, using $\lambda\cdot m(\beta_\lambda)=\delta$. Thus $q=\beta_\lambda/\delta$ attains the trace constraints, and the support bound proves it has least norm $1/\sqrt{\delta}$. The squared affine margin is $1/(1/\delta-C)$. If $\delta=0$, the price with $\lambda\cdot r=1$ and zero support value excludes every flag with sums $r$. This returns margin zero.

An implementation can use only the original quiver in each rational filtration call. For the integer chamber representative and each candidate slope $p/q$, optimize the linear subrepresentation weight $q\lambda-p\mathbf1$, refined by giving the largest maximizer priority. Multiply the original integer weight by $D+1$ and add $\mathbf1$ for this refinement. Maximizers of the original modular objective are closed under joins, and the greatest one uniquely maximizes total dimension. If applying Cheng's zero-total discrepancy theorem, append an isolated one-dimensional vertex with opposite total weight and restrict the output back. All candidate slope numerators and denominators and the enlarged weights have polynomial magnitude for fixed $k$. The discrepancy construction on the explicitly enumerated path matrices then uses the rational bit complexity of the shrunk-subspace algorithm. This provides an algebraic implementation without relying on a floating-point scaling tolerance.

The verified overlap is exact for the two-vertex formula, classical for optimality and rational descent, and algorithmic for the fixed-price Harder–Narasimhan oracle. The specific bounded-vertex construction adds simultaneous vertex traces, a full search over price chambers, and exact reconstruction of rational optimal flags at boundary prices. Publication priority for that combined construction remains unconfirmed after the targeted primary-source search. The audit is a written argument.
