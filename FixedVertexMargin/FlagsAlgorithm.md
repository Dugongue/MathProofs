# A fixed-vertex algorithm for the exact affine margin

Let a layered quiver representation be given by explicit rational arrow matrices $A_a:V_s\to V_t$ and rational vertex heights with $h_t-h_s=1$ on every arrow. Suppose at least one map is nonzero. The group is the product of the vertex special linear groups. For a cocharacter, use the Euclidean norm of all its eigenweights and let its least arrow exponent be the least difference of target and source weights on a nonzero entry in adapted bases.

**Theorem.** For each fixed number $k$ of vertices, there is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time algorithm over $\mathbb Q$ that returns the exact square of the maximal positive normalized least arrow exponent, or zero if no positive exponent is possible. In the positive case it returns all vertex flags, rational eigenweights normalized to least arrow exponent one, and rational bases splitting the flags. The algorithm recovers the principal flag on every price chamber and every tie face. All principal threshold queries are made on the original representation.

Write $d_v=\dim V_v$ and $D=\sum_v d_v$. Vertices incident to no nonzero map can be deleted during the computation, since their optimal weight is zero and they impose no arrow condition. Restore their zero-weight whole-space flags at the end, with the standard basis specified implicitly when an isolated dimension was encoded in binary. For the remaining vertices $D$ is bounded by the input size of the explicit matrices. The estimates below are polynomial in those dimensions and in the bit size of all input rationals. These flags extend the earlier numerical characterization of the affine origin margin. [Parametric subrepresentation flags](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ParametricConvexFour/Flags.md) · [Kempf, instability](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1978/108-2/p04)

## 1. An exact rational subrepresentation oracle

For an integer weight vector $\theta$, the object to maximize is $\theta\cdot\dim U$ over subrepresentations $U$. We use a path-space form of the reduction of Iwamasa, Oki and Soma. This also specifies a rational constructive implementation and verifies the field of its answer. [Iwamasa–Oki–Soma, Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06493)

Make $\theta_s$ copies of each positive vertex space in a domain and $-\theta_t$ copies of each negative vertex space in a codomain. For each positive $s$ and negative $t$, use the products of arrows along directed $s$-to-$t$ paths. For every pair of copies and for every such path product, add a matrix supported in that one block. Let $\mathcal A$ be their span. The graph is acyclic and paths have length at most $k-1$, so at fixed $k$ the path list is polynomial. One can also compute just a basis of each path span by propagating earlier basis products along arrows in topological order and retaining independent products. Each path product has polynomial bit size. Clearing each arrow's denominators separately shows this, since the path length is bounded and multiplying a path matrix by a nonzero integer does not affect its span.

For any subrepresentation $W$, take $X$ in the domain to be the sum of the positive copies of $W_s$. The image $\mathcal A X$ lies in the sum of the negative copies of $W_t$, so $\dim X-\dim\mathcal A X\ge\theta\cdot\dim W$. Conversely, given a domain subspace $X$, let $U_s$ be the sum of its projections to all copies of vertex $s$, identified in $V_s$. Close this tuple under paths, including the length-zero identities at positive vertices, to form a subrepresentation $W$. Every negative copy of $W_t$ occurs exactly as the corresponding summand of $\mathcal A X$, because each generator is supported in just one block. At positive vertices $W_s$ contains $U_s$, and $\sum_s \theta_s\dim U_s\ge\dim X$. Therefore $\theta\cdot\dim W\ge\dim X-\dim\mathcal A X$. This proves equality of the two maxima and constructs an optimizer from an optimal defect subspace.

Use constructive noncommutative rank over $\mathbb Q$ to obtain the defect subspace. For a rectangular space, pad with zero rows or zero columns to make a square. Zero rows leave the defect unchanged. Adding zero columns increases maximal defect by exactly their number, and projecting an optimal subspace to the original domain recovers an optimum there. If the input weight has no positive coordinates, zero is an optimizer. If it has no negative coordinates, the whole representation is an optimizer. The constructive algorithm supplies a matching blow-up rank certificate over $\mathbb Q$. This rank remains over every field extension $K/\mathbb Q$. Every matrix in blow-up size $d_0$ maps $X\otimes K^{d_0}$ into $\mathcal A X\otimes K^{d_0}$. Thus a defect subspace of defect $\delta$ bounds blow-up rank above by $d_0(n-\delta)$ for a square space of size $n$. Thus the rational optimum found here is also the geometric optimum. Its bit complexity is polynomial in the input and the **magnitudes** of the integer weights, as reflected by the copy counts. [Ivanyos–Qiao–Subrahmanyam, ITCS Theorem 7](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol067-itcs2017/LIPIcs.ITCS.2017.55/LIPIcs.ITCS.2017.55.pdf)

To select the largest maximizer of an integer objective $\sigma$, run this oracle with $\theta=(D+1)\sigma+\mathbf 1$. The integer objective dominates the added dimension term, which selects the largest dimension among its maximizers. The original maximizers are closed under sums and intersections by modularity. Hence the maximum-dimension one is unique and contains all others. This treats ties over the original rational representation and over its scalar extension.

## 2. Recover all price flags with bounded integer prices

For a price $\lambda\in\mathbb R^k$, the slope of a nonzero dimension vector $a$ is $\lambda\cdot a/|a|$, where $|a|=\sum_v a_v$. The principal filtration takes the largest maximum-slope subrepresentation and repeats in the quotient. Two maximal-slope subrepresentations have a sum of the same slope by modularity of dimension and price, so the largest is unique. The quotient slopes strictly decrease, since a next slope at least as large would give a larger preimage of at least the old maximal slope. For computation on the original representation, consider all possible thresholds $\alpha=\lambda\cdot a/|a|$ with integral $0\le a_v\le d_v$, $a\ne0$. At each threshold find the largest optimizer of
$$
 (\lambda-\alpha\mathbf1)\cdot\dim U .
$$
The distinct answers, in decreasing threshold order, give precisely the successive steps of the principal filtration. In detail, if the principal graded slopes are $b_1>\cdots>b_m$, intersect $U$ with each filtration step. Each induced subobject of a graded block has slope at most its block slope. Its contribution to the threshold objective is therefore at most $(b_i-\alpha)$ times its dimension. The largest sum fills the positive blocks, discards the negative blocks, and may include the zero block. The largest maximizer fills the zero block as well, giving the corresponding principal step. The threshold list includes all its slopes. At a bounded integer $\lambda$, use the integer vector $\sigma=|a|\lambda-(\lambda\cdot a)\mathbf1$ in the oracle of Section 1. [Iwamasa–Oki–Soma, Algorithm 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.06493#S4.SS2)

To handle all prices, form the homogeneous hyperplane arrangement of equal slopes between any two of these candidate vectors. Its nonzero integer normals are
$$
 |b|a-|a|b
$$
and have coordinates of absolute value at most $2D^2$. On each realized sign pattern, including patterns with ties, the principal filtration is constant as an unweighted flag. At the first step the complete ordering of the possible slopes determines the same maximizing subrepresentations and the same largest one. Induction applies the same argument inside the successive quotients. This proves constancy, even though the algorithm itself uses no quotient matrices.

Every realized sign pattern has an integer sample $\lambda$ with $\|\lambda\|_\infty\le B$, where
$$
 B=(k+1)!\,\max(1,2D^2)^{k+1}.
$$
To prove this, set normals with sign zero to equality, and orient the strict normals so their desired signs are positive. In the box $|\lambda_i|\le1$, maximize a common slack $\eta\in[0,1]$ for the strict inequalities. Scaling a realization into the box gives positive feasibility, so an optimal vertex has positive slack. The determinant bound for an independent set of active rows gives a common denominator at most $B$ for its coordinates. Scaling the vertex by this denominator gives the desired sample. If there are no strict signs use zero. Thus enumerating the integer box and retaining one representative per sign key suffices. The box and hyperplane list have size $D^{O(k^2)}$, and the integer oracle weights from the threshold and tie perturbation have polynomial magnitude for fixed $k$. An arbitrarily large rational price can subsequently be handled by locating its key and evaluating its actual slopes on the stored flag.

## 3. Exact minimization on closed cones

If a principal step has dimension increment $a_i$ and size $|a_i|$, assign $\lambda\cdot a_i/|a_i|$ to its graded block. Denote the resulting admissible weighted flag by $\beta_\lambda$. Here an admissible weighted flag has every superlevel tuple a subrepresentation, or equivalently has nondecreasing weights along nonzero arrow entries in adapted bases. Let $m(q)_v$ be the sum of the weights at vertex $v$.

For use in the optimization, the support value for price $\lambda$ on admissible flags of norm at most one is $\|\beta_\lambda\|$. Indeed every subrepresentation of total dimension $s$ has price at most the sum of the $s$ largest entries of $\beta_\lambda$, by intersecting it with the principal steps and using their slope bounds. Write the price pairing with an arbitrary admissible flag $q$ in terms of successive differences of its sorted weights and apply these bounds to the corresponding superlevel subrepresentations. Since the bound at total dimension $D$ is an equality, this gives $\lambda\cdot m(q)\le\|\beta_\lambda\|\|q\|$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. For $\beta_\lambda$ the price pairing equals its squared norm, giving attainment.

The set of vertex sums of admissible unit-ball flags is compact and convex. Compactness follows by taking the union of the unit-ball feasible weight sets over the finitely many binary arrow supports obtainable in adapted bases, then mapping to their vertex sums. For convexity, two flags at a given vertex admit a common adapted basis. For example, choose complements in the intersections of their steps modulo the two preceding intersections and use the resulting bases. Interpolation in the common bases preserves the arrow inequalities and the unit norm bound. It also interpolates the vertex sums.

Put
$$
 c_v=D^{-1}\sum_u d_uh_u-h_v,\qquad r_v=d_vc_v,\qquad C=\sum_vd_vc_v^2.
$$
Shifting every weight at vertex $v$ by $c_v$ converts the least-arrow-exponent constraint $\mu\ge1$ to admissibility and converts tracelessness to $m(q)=r$. The original squared norm is $\|q\|^2-C$. By separation and the support formula, the least admissible norm $\Gamma$ at these vertex sums satisfies exactly the inequalities $\lambda\cdot r\le\Gamma\|\beta_\lambda\|$ for all $\lambda$. If no such norm is finite, there is no positive margin.

On the closure of a price cell, the squared support function equals the rational quadratic form
$$
 q_{\mathcal C}(\lambda)=\sum_i(\lambda\cdot a_i)^2/|a_i|
$$
computed with its sampled flag. The formula holds on the cell by construction and extends to its closure by continuity of a support function of a compact set. This argument covers slopes tied at the boundary. Minimize $q_{\mathcal C}$ on the closed cone with $\lambda\cdot r=1$, and take the smallest result $v$ over the feasible cells. There is a feasible cell since the heights differ on a nonzero arrow, so $r\ne0$. Positive prices can be scaled to this slice, and nonpositive pairings impose no lower bound on $\Gamma$. It follows that $v=0$ gives infinite $\Gamma$ and zero margin, and otherwise $\Gamma^2=1/v$.

These are exact rational optimizations even when a quadratic form is singular or a sliced cone is unbounded. The infimum on each nonempty slice is attained, since the image of a polyhedron by a linear map is a closed polyhedron and a positive-semidefinite quadratic form is a squared norm of such an image. Enumerate sets of at most $k$ inequality rows to declare tight. Keep the original constraints and solve the additional linear equations that the gradient belongs to the span of the equality normals and the selected tight rows. A true minimizer occurs in one system, since the gradient vanishes on all directions tangent to its active constraints and at most $k$ rows span the added normals. Every solution of one such system is stationary on that affine space, so all its solutions have the same quadratic value. Rational linear feasibility gives a rational candidate from each system, and taking their minimum is exact. The number of systems and the bit sizes of all coefficients are polynomial at fixed $k$.

For a positive result, let $\lambda_*$ be the rational winning price with $\lambda_*\cdot r=1$, and look up its actual sign key to recover its flag with all ties. The minimum-norm admissible flag with sums $r$ has norm $\Gamma=1/\sqrt v$ and attains the support bound at $\lambda_*$. The admissible flag $\beta_{\lambda_*}/v$ has the same norm and attains the same linear objective. Two different weighted flags with both properties would have an average of strictly smaller norm in common adapted bases and unchanged price pairing, contradicting the support bound. Hence the required flag is $\beta_{\lambda_*}/v$. Subtract $c_v$ from its weights at each vertex to obtain the rational optimal cocharacter weights. Their least exponent is one because any larger exponent would allow a smaller norm after rescaling. Their squared norm is $1/v-C>0$, so the exact squared margin is $v/(1-Cv)$. Ordinary rational elimination constructs adapted bases, and one can clear weight denominators to produce an integral cocharacter.

## Controls and scope

A single rank-one arrow from dimension one to dimension two gives $v=3/8$, $C=2/3$, and squared margin $1/2$, with weights $0$ at the source and $1,-1$ at the target. Three one-dimensional vertices with a nonzero chain have no positive margin. The three-layer example of the preceding flags paper has a supporting price $(2,-1,-1)/6$ with ties, $v=1/6$, and squared margin $1/2$.

In a separate exact control, all 256 coordinate supports on three layers of dimension two were crossed with the 27 prices in $\{-1,0,1\}^3$. The threshold result matched the iterated maximal-slope construction for all 6,912 principal flags. For all 57,600 tested thresholds, selecting closure of positive-source subsets using the perturbed objective agreed with the largest original maximizer, including ties.

The numerical-characterization and principal-partition ingredients have prior proofs. The new step is the fixed-vertex compilation of every price and tie face to bounded integer objectives, followed by exact optimization and recovery of the rational optimal flags. The bound permits its polynomial degree to depend on $k$. The proof is in prose and publication priority remains unverified.
