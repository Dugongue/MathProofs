# Removing matrix factors and recovering nonsplit monomial kernels

Work over \(F=\mathbb F_3\) in based coordinates. For a spanned bilinear map \(U\otimes V\to W\) let \(R\) be its kernel and let \(A,B\) be its two one-sided idealizers from the accompanying proof. Every unit of \(A\) on the left preserves \(R\); every unit of \(B\) on the right does likewise. Any input transporter conjugates these algebras to their corresponding target algebras.

## A descending induction with three sign bits

**Theorem.** If \(A/J(A)\simeq M_r(F)\), \(r\ge2\), recover a smaller based tensor \(U_0\otimes V\to W_0\) with \(\dim U=r u,\ \dim W=r w\), \(u=\dim U_0,w=\dim W_0\). After computable endpoint basis corrections, the full determinant image is obtained from that of the core by
\[
  (\alpha^u X^r,\ Y,\ \alpha^w Z^r),
  \qquad \alpha=\pm1,\quad (X,Y,Z)\ \text{any core transporter signature}.
\]
The other endpoint must have the same split simple quotient and matching dimensions. Its failure to do so excludes ordinary equivalence. No structure hypothesis is made on the core's kernel.

Over the fixed field, compute the radical, a full system of matrix units of the split quotient and a semisimple complement in the idealizer. These are polynomial-time associative-algebra operations at fixed field size. One explicit way to lift the complement is successive lifting through \(J^k/J^{k+1}\). For a linear unital lift, its multiplicativity defects are corrected by a linear system on the corrections to its basis images, because the kernel is square-zero. Existence at each step follows from Wedderburn–Malcev for separable semisimple algebras over a perfect field: a complement exists one level higher and its image is conjugate to the prescribed lower complement by a unit congruent to 1 modulo the radical, which in turn lifts to a unit. There are at most \(\dim A\) steps. [Rónyai, *Computing the structure of finite algebras*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S074771710880017X)

The complement matrix units give \(U=F^r\otimes U_0\) explicitly: take the image of the first diagonal unit for \(U_0\) and use the first column units on its basis. Invariance under every matrix unit gives \(R=F^r\otimes R_0\), since each component can be extracted and placed in any of the \(r\) slots. On the quotient this gives the claimed \(W=F^r\otimes W_0\). These are based identifications obtained by linear algebra.

A transporter may send the chosen complement to a different complement at the target. By Wedderburn–Malcev they are conjugate by \(1+j\) with \(j\in J(A')\). This unit preserves the target relation space and is unipotent on the left mode, hence on the ambient tensor product and its output quotient. Correcting by it leaves every prescribed sign unchanged. A transporter respecting the matrix algebras acts on the left as \(a\otimes P_0\), because every automorphism of the split matrix algebra is inner and the commutant acts on the multiplicity space. Its right component is \(Q\) and its output action is \(a\otimes S_0\). Conversely these maps work for every core transporter and arbitrary \(a\in{\rm GL}_r(F)\). Taking determinants gives the formula with \(\alpha=\det a\). For raw bases multiply by the three ratios of target and source identification determinants, or compute them by composing the actual frame maps.

For successive compressions store the desired signs as an affine subset \(C\subseteq\mathbb F_2^3\), beginning with a singleton for the requested triple. Use additive parity. After accounting for the based offset \(b\), the new allowed set is
\[
 \{(x,y,z)\mid \exists e\in\mathbb F_2,\quad
          b+(u e+r x,\ y,\ w e+r z)\in C\}.
\]
This is an affine projection or is empty, and can be computed by just checking eight candidates. The fixed state space prevents branching exponentially with the number of factors. The original instance has a witness exactly when the reduced tensor has an allowed signature. The two affected dimensions strictly decrease and no dimension grows. Either input mode can play the left role. Thus there are polynomially many steps, and a witness at the last step lifts back through all recorded frames.

**A one-instance consequence for arbitrary cores.** If \(r\) is even and \(\dim V\) is odd, the first and third required signs need only be \(\alpha^u,\alpha^w\) for one \(\alpha\) after calibration. If this fails emit a fixed NO. If it succeeds emit **one ordinary TI instance for the recovered core**. Indeed multiplying the core right and output maps by \(-1\) flips the second sign while its effect on the amplified output determinant is raised to the even power \(r\). Thus every ordinary core transporter can be corrected. This is also an example of stopping the sign induction before solving the smaller tensor.

For an explicit nonmonomial example let \(U_0=V=F^n\), odd \(n\ge3\), \(R_0\) the line spanned by \(\sum e_i\otimes e_i\), and \(r\) positive even. The core left idealizer is scalar because left multiplication of the identity matrix into its span requires a scalar. Amplification gives left idealizer exactly \(M_r(F)\) by checking each matrix entry on \(R_0\), and \(w=n^2-1\) is even. Every nonzero relation of the amplified tensor has matrix rank \(n\), even over the closure. It therefore admits no rectangular monomial kernel basis. An output reflection gives an ordinary isomorphism with third determinant necessarily negative in the reflected bases. The first example has dimensions \((6,3,16)\).

## Recognition of geometrically monomial rectangular kernels

Call \(R\subseteq U\otimes V\) geometrically monomial when over the algebraic closure it is spanned by a subset of the products of two bases, one per input mode. This section recognizes that property in deterministic polynomial time and computes its orientation problem with a GI oracle. No symmetrizer or field labels are required.

For either idealizer \(A\), compute a Wedderburn complement \(S_A\), its center \(C_A\), and test the equality \(S_A={\rm End}_{C_A}(U)\) using a linear centralizer system. Do the same on the right. The choices of complements do not affect either test by unipotent conjugacy. When both tests pass, over the closure the central idempotents decompose each mode into spaces on which the full matrix algebras act independently. Between any two such spaces all elementary left and right matrix units act on their product. Each component of \(R\) is either the whole product or zero, as any nonzero coefficient can be extracted and placed anywhere. Thus it is monomial in product bases.

Conversely fix monomial bases over the closure and view relation monomials as edges of a bipartite graph. In the left idealizer the coefficient \(E_{ki}\) is allowed exactly when \(N(i)\subseteq N(k)\), by checking each basis relation; there is no cancellation between different replacements. Equality of neighborhoods gives full diagonal matrix blocks on the classes; strict containments give a nilpotent ideal with semisimple quotient the product of those full matrix blocks. The block diagonal is a complement and acts with each natural module occurring once. The same holds on the right. Scalar extension commutes with the idealizer linear equations; over the perfect ground field the radical extends to the radical, since its quotient remains semisimple. The extended ground complement is conjugate to the displayed geometric one. Its full-centralizer test therefore passes after scalar extension and hence over the base. This proves the recognition equivalence.

Write each left center factor as \(K_i\), \(U_i=K_i^{s_i}\), and each right one as \(L_j\), \(V_j=L_j^{t_j}\). Extract the primitive rational central idempotents, explicitly if needed by splitting the Frobenius-fixed center with the three indicators \(1-(v-c)^2,\ c\in F\), for each fixed-space basis vector \(v\). In the product of copies of \(F\) these basis vectors distinguish all coordinates. Compute module bases by extending independent field vectors. Use one reference field per degree occurring at either endpoint and explicit field isomorphisms. [Lenstra, constructive finite-field isomorphism](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

Correcting by a target radical unit on each side aligns complements with all three signs unchanged, as in the induction. Every remaining transporter on a side is a permutation of equal-degree, equal-rank factors followed by field isomorphisms and invertible field-linear matrices on the blocks. All these block linear groups preserve \(R\) independently, being units of the complements. Thus the only compatibility question for ordinary transport concerns permutations of the factors and powers of Frobenius. All quotient spaces \(Z_{ij}=(U_i\otimes V_j)/(R\cap(U_i\otimes V_j))\) can be computed on the ground field.

**One graph for the compatibility problem.** For each field factor of degree \(d\) create \(d\) vertices, one for each reference-field identification of the factor, linked as a directed successor cycle under Frobenius. Color by side, field degree and module rank. Give each ordered left-right pair of vertices a color equal to the canonical row-reduced basis of its relation space pulled back into the two reference modules through those identifications (acting coordinatewise on module bases). Use identical conventions and common color names at the endpoints. The graph uses polynomially many vertices, arcs and field entries. Directed edges and colors admit the usual polynomial incidence encoding for colored GI. A cycle-preserving graph map makes one factor permutation and one Frobenius shift per factor. Pullback colors match exactly when these maps carry every component of \(R\) to its target. Necessity holds for every frame pair; for sufficiency one frame pair per component already suffices. This represents all normalized factor permutations and phases without constructing their common splitting field.

A GI decision oracle yields one map and generators of the source automorphism group by vertex individualization: find possible images with earlier vertices fixed, recover a map for each attainable image by successive individualization, and use the resulting stabilizer-chain transversals as generators. The query count is polynomial. Given the normalized maps, compute their actual signs on the two inputs and the based quotient by linear algebra. The remaining block linear maps give the following sign columns. Put
\[
       c_i={\sum_j\dim Z_{ij}\over \dim_F U_i},
       \qquad e_j={\sum_i\dim Z_{ij}\over \dim_F V_j}.
\]
For each geometric vertex of field orbit \(i\) the number of surviving right basis coordinates is the same by Frobenius transitivity and equals \(c_i\). This proves integrality. A field-linear left matrix with \(z=N_{K_i/F}(\det_{K_i} M)\) acts on the two inputs and quotient with signature \((z,1,z^{c_i})\), by the determinant of a tensor product on every surviving geometric block. Similarly right matrices contribute \((1,z,z^{e_j})\). These norms are independent and surjective. To construct a lift, put a preimage of the prescribed norm in one diagonal entry; the earlier explicit field-norm algorithm applies at 3. Combine the generator signatures, norm columns, and based transporter offset in the sign group of order eight. This gives exact decision and witnesses. [Effective finite-field norm lifting](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/5d2a1a370c051bbdcb0406ecce525b2125b730e0/DirichletGrochowPrimeTests/OrientedTransport.md)

Babai's algorithm makes the unconditional running time quasipolynomial on the whole geometrically monomial class. If each factor label (side, degree, module rank, optionally any polynomially computable intrinsic refinement) occurs at most a fixed \(b\) times, the algorithm is polynomial even with unboundedly many ambiguous labels. On the frame tokens the ambient group is the product of wreath products \(C_d\wr S_h\) with \(h\le b\). On tokens and token pairs it has polynomial permutation degree and all its nonabelian composition factors embed in \(S_b\). Encode the same row-reduced data as a string on these tokens and pairs. Luks's bounded-composition-factor color transporter gives the required coset and generators. The same frame construction works in the earlier maximal étale **symmetric** class, using unary colors for the within-factor symmetric squares, ordered pair colors for distinct factors, and its scalar symmetrizer correction for independent modes. This replaces the previous bound on the product of all factorials by a bound on each multiplicity. [Luks–Miyazaki, Theorem 3.7](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/polytimenormalizers.pdf) · [Babai, graph and string isomorphism](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03547)

**GI lower bound.** Encode a simple graph by a bipartite incidence graph with a row for each vertex and a column for each edge, plus one private singleton column for every vertex. The neighborhoods are distinct on each side. Replace every row and column by a two-dimensional coordinate block. In the tensor send active incidence cells identically onto separate four-dimensional output blocks, and kill the other cells. A graph isomorphism induces block permutations in even dimensions on all three modes, so all signs are positive. Conversely an ordinary tensor transporter aligns the geometric diagonal complements up to radical units, as proved above. Twin classes are precisely the displayed two-dimensional blocks, so the induced block permutations give a bipartite graph isomorphism and identify the original degree-two edge columns. Empty or mismatching sizes are dealt with directly. This is a polynomial many-one GI lower bound even for the all-positive sign target. Combined with the graph algorithm it gives GI equivalence under polynomial **Turing** reductions, without assuming a single-query reduction for the unrestricted multiplicity case.

The size descent requires a proper matrix factor, the flag induction requires bounded local orbits, and the graph theorem requires a geometrically monomial kernel. For example the left and right idealizers of the exterior product \(U\times U\to\bigwedge^2U\) are scalars when \(\dim U\ge3\): applying a candidate left operator to the relation \(x\otimes x\) forces \(Ax\wedge x=0\) for every \(x\), hence scalarity. This example explains why a universally available matrix factor cannot be inferred from the normalizer argument. It makes no hardness claim about the exterior product.
