# Induction for determinant-constrained tensor isomorphism over the ternary field

Let \(F=\mathbb F_3\). A based tensor is a bilinear map \(T:U\times V\to W\). A transporter satisfies \(S T(x,y)=T'(Px,Qy)\). Signs are the three determinants in the supplied bases, with \(2=-1\). The results here concern ordinary isomorphism and special-linear isomorphism of tensors over this same field. The unrestricted polynomial-time many-one reduction from special-linear TI to ordinary TI remains open in this work. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

| Proved case | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Two intrinsic input flags with \(3^{(ab)^2}\) polynomial in dense input size, where \(a,b\) are their maximum grade dimensions | Deterministic polynomial-time decision and a witness for three prescribed signs; unrestricted output dimension and extension coefficients. |
| A terminal quadratic multiplication law on a filtered local algebra | An exact based determinant formula and constructive algorithm with independently unbounded filtration length and first-layer dimension. The first orientation obstruction can occur strictly after every proper truncation. |
| A split matrix factor acting on one input | Exact size-decreasing induction carrying one affine subset of three sign bits. An even factor and an odd opposite input suffice to reduce to one ordinary TI instance on the smaller, otherwise arbitrary core. |
| Rectangular kernels that become monomial in product bases over the algebraic closure | Polynomial-time recognition. Prescribed-sign TI here is GI-hard under many-one reductions and equivalent to GI under polynomial-time Turing reductions. Bounded multiplicities of intrinsic field labels give a deterministic polynomial algorithm with witnesses. |

The first theorem needs no symmetrizer. The last two explicitly remove nilpotent changes of coordinates without changing determinant signs. The earlier Frobenius and norm proof supplies the construction of norm lifts on finite fields. The prior three-mode parabolic theorem uses flags on all three modes; the two-mode proof below keeps the output unconstrained until its determinant is computed on the quotient. The recent rank-drop recovery gives abstract scheme isomorphism for its constructed pencils. Extending orientation through a concrete tensor still requires compatible linear lifts. ## Two narrow input flags suffice

Suppose \(b:U\otimes V\to W\) spans the output. Put \(H=U\otimes V\), \(R=\ker b\). Suppose ordered flags in \(U,V\) are recovered on both endpoints by the same deterministic intrinsic rule. A transporter must respect them. If the grades differ, declare NO. Let \(a,b_1\) be their maximum dimensions and \(k=ab_1\). For any fixed \(C\), the recognized pairs with \(3^{k^2}\le L^C\), for dense input length \(L\), admit a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm returning a transporter of any feasible sign triple. This yields a single ordinary TI instance by emitting the scalar pair \((1,1)\) or \((1,0)\) after decision. The running time before imposing that bound is polynomial in \(L\) and \(3^{k^2}\).

Choose adapted bases, with each flag a chain of suffixes. Order the tensor-product block pairs lexicographically, so the parabolic product preserves the descending suffix flag \(H=F_0\supset\cdots\supset F_s=0\). Each grade has dimension at most \(k\). Write \(R_i=R\cap F_i\), and use primes at the target. An unconstrained output transporter is uniquely determined by input maps carrying \(R\) onto \(R'\). In adapted coordinates start with one representative of the initially allowed maps (identity) and the entire product of the two block lower triangular groups. These groups have explicit generators: elementary additions between allowable coordinates, diagonal signs, and additions in both directions within diagonal blocks; these generate by elimination. Work with transporter cosets \(g_0G\), where \(G\) is the exact source stabilizer of all constraints already processed.

Proceed from deepest layer to shallowest. After matching \(R_{i+1}\), compare the images of \(R_i\) in \(F_i/(F_j+R_{i+1})\) for \(j=i+1,\ldots,s\), in that order. These are actual images of the input relation spaces. Their common dimension is \(\dim R_i-\dim R_{i+1}=d_i\le k\), because already the first map into \(F_i/F_{i+1}\) is injective on that quotient. At the first level there are at most \(3^{k d_i}\) possible subspaces (count ordered lists spanning them). At each later level the subspace must project isomorphically onto the preceding one. After choosing one lift, all lifts are graphs of maps from a \(d_i\)-space to a kernel of dimension at most \(k\). Thus the orbit at each step has size at most \(3^{k^2}\). The full preimage used for computation is \(R_i+F_j\), since \(R_{i+1}\subseteq R_i\). At the last level it is \(R_i\).

Compute each actual source orbit by breadth-first search. Test whether it contains the target pulled back by \(g_0^{-1}\). If the test succeeds with element \(h\), replace \(g_0\) by \(g_0h\) and \(G\) by the stabilizer of the source object. Schreier's lemma gives the generators of that stabilizer from the orbit representatives. On failure no map extending the prefix exists. This proves both directions by induction, with no splitting assumption on \(R\). There are at most \(s(s+1)/2\) comparisons.

Generator counts must also be controlled. A faithful stabilizer chain for each parabolic acts on each standard basis vector viewed successively modulo each suffix of the mode flag, from shortest prefix to full vector, then moves to the next vector. After its preceding image is fixed, the next image lies in a fiber of size at most \(3^{\max(a,b_1)}\). The entire chain has polynomial length; fixing every full column forces the identity. Explicitly maintain transversals and strong generators by sifting. When a residual misses the transversal at level \(j\), insert it there and recompute the basic orbits. The earlier points are fixed, no orbit decreases, and the orbit at \(j\) strictly grows. Close under Schreier generators at each level until every one sifts to identity. The sum of the basic orbit bounds limits the number of insertions. Each sweep uses polynomially many matrix operations in these bounds. After each outer orbit step, compress its generator list this way before the next comparison. The included implementation uses explicit matrix representatives. [Implementation](flag_transport.py)

After the last comparison, compute \(S\) on the quotient via \(Sb=b'(P\otimes Q)\). Compute the three signs of \(g_0\) and of each source stabilizer generator with its induced output action. Their image lies in a group of order eight; multiplying generator images and retaining a lifting word finds the requested correction or proves it infeasible. Restore the input bases, including their determinant ratios. The output matrix is in the original presented basis. For unspanned output one may first restrict to the output image. With matching positive codimension, a free complementary map realizes either total output determinant, so the full predicate is recovered by ignoring that character on the image. The supplied code implements spanned output.

For the symmetric congruence problem the same proof acts by \(g\otimes g\) on \(U\otimes U\), with one intrinsic tangent flag of width \(a\) and \(k=a^2\). Use a single copy of the parabolic and test \(\det g\det S\) after the orbit computation. This gives the even-conjugator decision on the ternary square-zero subfamily covered by the existing based reconstruction, after its endpoint orientation correction. [Square-zero reconstruction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/5d2a1a370c051bbdcb0406ecce525b2125b730e0/EvenConjugatorF3/FilteredRelations.md)

## Recovering flags from the raw tables

For the first mode compute the one-sided idealizer \(E=\{A\in{\rm End}(U)\mid (A\otimes1)R\subseteq R\}\) by linear equations; use the other side for \(V\). It is an associative algebra. The powers \(I^jU\) of any functorially defined two-sided ideal \(I\) yield an intrinsic chain. Keep strict inclusions, ending at zero if a nonzero term stabilizes. Two explicit choices suffice for the implementation. For commutative \(E\) use the kernel of \(x\mapsto x^{3^h}\) for \(3^h\ge\dim U\); Frobenius is linear here and this kills exactly the nilpotent matrices. Otherwise use the kernel of \((a,b)\mapsto{\rm Tr}_U(ab)\) in the first slot. It is a two-sided ideal by cyclicity of trace. These choices require no algebra factoring. More generally the Jacobson radical gives another polynomial-time intrinsic rule over this fixed field. [Friedl–Rónyai, radical algorithms](https://www.cs.uoregon.edu/Reports/TR-1985-003.pdf)

For an explicit family with a wide output let \(U=\bigoplus_{i=1}^t U_i,\ V=\bigoplus_{j=1}^t V_j\), with every summand of dimension two. Take \(R=\bigoplus_{i+j\le t}U_i\otimes V_j\). Use the quotient as output. The forbidden neighborhoods are strictly nested on each side. A matrix coefficient from block \(i\) to block \(k\) is allowed exactly when all forbidden neighbors of \(i\) are forbidden for \(k\); testing a pure product proves necessity and sufficiency. Thus each idealizer is a full block triangular algebra and its trace kernel is exactly the strict block triangular ideal (the trace pairing on each diagonal matrix block is nondegenerate). Its powers recover width-two flags. In the intrinsic input flags the output filtration by surviving antidiagonals can have grades of dimension \(4t\). There is no requirement on output flags in the algorithm.

All ordinary automorphisms act on the surviving product grades by \(P_i\otimes Q_j\). Their determinants are \((\det P_i)^2(\det Q_j)^2=1\). This calculation applies to every automorphism because the input flags are intrinsic. Hence reflecting one output coordinate gives ordinary YES and special-linear NO in dimensions \((2t,2t,2t(t+1))\) for every \(t\ge2\). A symmetrizer cannot be invertible: for \(x\) in the last input block, its row has no kernel; symmetry against \(y\) in any earlier block equates outputs in disjoint row summands, forcing \(J y=0\).
