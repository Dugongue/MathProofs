# When a conjugate-dual tensor pair detects finite unitary isomorphism

**Theorem.** Let $K=\mathbb F_{q^2}$ be a presented finite field. Pair each three-tensor $T$ over $K$ with the coefficientwise conjugate $T^{[q]}$ in the coordinate dual. Let $H$ be the geometric stabilizer of this pair and $\Pi=H/H^\circ$ its group of connected components. Set $\Theta(g_i)=(g_i^{[q]})^{-T}$ on the three general linear factors and $D=\Pi^{\Theta^2}$. In the ordinary $K$-linear orbit of the pair, the pairs of the form $(T',T'^{[q]})$ split into unitary orbits indexed by the $D$-conjugacy classes of order-two elements of $D\theta$ in $D\rtimes\langle\theta\rangle$, where $\theta$ has order two and acts on $D$ by $\Theta$.

If $|D|$ is odd, the orbit of this pair therefore contains exactly one unitary orbit. On this promise one ordinary three-tensor isomorphism instance over $K$ decides unitary isomorphism. Its side lengths are linear in the sum of the input side lengths and its construction has deterministic polynomial bit cost uniformly in $q$. The procedure requires no computation of $D$. The promise applies only to the source. This strengthens the finite-field clause of Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang's Open Question 1.10 on the stated class of tensors. The prior MathProofs reduction allows arbitrary tensors for each fixed characteristic, with a polynomial exponent depending on that characteristic. [Open Question 1.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5) · [Previous fixed-characteristic reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs)

## The unitary points of the pair space

Work first over $k=\overline{\mathbb F_q}$. Write $G=\prod_i\operatorname{GL}_{n_i}(k)$, $X=\bigotimes_i k^{n_i}$ and $X^*$ for the coordinate dual. Define $\Theta_X(x,y)=(y^{[q]},x^{[q]})$ on $X\oplus X^*$, using the designated bases to place the outputs in the primal and dual spaces. This is compatible with the $G$-action in the sense that $\Theta_X(gz)=\Theta(g)\Theta_X(z)$. Indeed the dual action applies inverse transpose in all three modes, which exactly accounts for the inverse transposes in $\Theta$. The fixed pairs are precisely $z=(T,T^{[q]})$ with $T$ over $K$. Also $\Theta^2(g)=g^{[q^2]}$, so $G^{\Theta^2}$ is the product of the general linear groups over $K$. The fixed group $G^\Theta$ is the product of the standard unitary groups by the equation $(g_i^{[q]})^Tg_i=I$.

Fix a pair $z$ in this locus and let $H$ be its reduced geometric stabilizer. It is $\Theta$-stable by compatibility. Its identity component $H^\circ$ is a connected smooth algebraic group over $k$. The Lang–Steinberg theorem says that $u\mapsto u^{-1}\Psi(u)$ is surjective for a surjective endomorphism $\Psi$ of a connected algebraic group with a finite fixed-point group. The connected restrictions here satisfy these conditions since $\Psi^2$ will be a Frobenius. The reversed form $u\mapsto u\Psi(u)^{-1}$ is surjective as well by applying the same map to $u^{-1}$. [Steinberg, Endomorphisms of Linear Algebraic Groups](https://doi.org/10.1090/memo/0080)

## Reduction to the finite component group

Use a subscript $K$ for $K$-points. If $g\in G_K$ and $gz$ is $\Theta_X$-fixed, then $a=g^{-1}\Theta(g)$ lies in $H_K$ and $a\Theta(a)=1$. Right multiplication of $g$ by $h\in H_K$ replaces $a$ by $h^{-1}a\Theta(h)$. Left multiplication by $u\in G^\Theta$ leaves $a$ unchanged. Conversely Lang–Steinberg on $G$ gives $g$ with $g^{-1}\Theta(g)=a$ for each such $a$. Then $\Theta^2(g)=g a\Theta(a)=g$, so $g\in G_K$ and $gz$ is a fixed pair. If $g$ and $g'$ give the same $a$, then $g'g^{-1}\in G^\Theta$. With right multiplication by $H_K$, this gives a bijection between unitary orbits and the twisted-conjugacy classes of $a$'s in $H_K$.

The map $H_K\to D=(H/H^\circ)^{\Theta^2}$ is onto. If $b\in H$ represents a component fixed by $\Theta^2$, solve $b^{-1}\Theta^2(b)=u\Theta^2(u)^{-1}$ in $H^\circ$ by Lang–Steinberg. Then $bu\in H_K$ represents the same component.

Every $c\in D$ with $c\Theta(c)=1$ lifts to an $a\in H_K$ with $a\Theta(a)=1$. Pick any representative $b\in H$ of $c$. Choose $g\in G$ with $g^{-1}\Theta(g)=b$. This gives $\Theta^2(g)=gw$, with $w=b\Theta(b)\in H^\circ$. Solve $w=u\Theta^2(u)^{-1}$ in $H^\circ$. Thus $g'=gu$ lies in $G_K$ and $a=g'^{-1}\Theta(g')=u^{-1}b\Theta(u)$ lies in $H_K$ in the desired component. Since $g'\in G_K$ this $a$ has $a\Theta(a)=1$.

Twisted-conjugacy classes in $H_K$ correspond exactly to the resulting classes in $D$. For injectivity, lift a conjugating element of $D$ to $H_K$ and first use it to move the two lifts into the same component. Write those lifts $a,b$, let $d=ba^{-1}\in H^\circ$, and let $\Psi=\operatorname{Int}(a)\Theta$ on $H^\circ$. The relations $a\Theta(a)=b\Theta(b)=1$ give $\Psi^2=\Theta^2$ and $d\Psi(d)=1$. Solve $d=u^{-1}\Psi(u)$ in $H^\circ$ by Lang–Steinberg. The relation $d\Psi(d)=1$ now gives $\Theta^2(u)=u$, so $u\in H_K$. It follows that $b=u^{-1}a\Theta(u)$.

On $D$, $\Theta^2$ acts trivially. The condition $c\Theta(c)=1$ is exactly the condition that $c\theta$ has order two in the semidirect product. Twisted conjugacy is ordinary conjugacy by $D$ in that coset. When $|D|$ is odd, every such order-two subgroup is a Sylow $2$-subgroup. All are conjugate to $\langle\theta\rangle$. The conjugating element can be taken in $D$ because right multiplication by $\theta$ does not change its conjugation of $\theta$. In general the criterion for the direct pair test to work for every target of the given source is precisely that all coset involutions are $D$-conjugate.

## One ordinary TI instance, uniformly in the field

Start with a partitioned tensor, where every marked coordinate block is preserved individually. In each of the three modes put a plus and minus copy of every $V_i$, with each minus copy using the dual coordinates, and put a one-dimensional anchor with basis vector $e_j$ in mode $j$. Place the input tensor in the three corresponding plus copies and its conjugate tensor in the three corresponding minus copies. Add $e_1\otimes e_2\otimes e_3$. For every $i$ and every ordered pair of distinct modes $a,b$, add the identity tensor on the plus copy of $V_i$ in mode $a$ and the minus copy in mode $b$, multiplied by the anchor in the remaining mode. The terms occupy distinct block triples.

Suppose the anchor multipliers are $t_1,t_2,t_3$. Their product is one by the anchor tensor. Divide each other block action in mode $j$ by $t_j$. The identity tensors then impose $P_a^+(P_b^-)^T=I$ on the normalized actions. For each $i$ these equations form a six-cycle between the plus and minus copies. All plus actions of that $i$ therefore equal a single $g_i$, and all minus actions equal $g_i^{-T}$. The data equations use exactly these normalized actions since the anchor multipliers cancel. Conversely any transporter of the pair extends using all anchor multipliers one. Thus this partitioned tensor isomorphism query decides the ordinary orbit of the pair.

The three modes have seven blocks each and side lengths $2\sum_i n_i+1$. Applying the arbitrary-field linear-length partition removal yields one ordinary TI query. Coefficient conjugation takes $O(\log q)$ multiplications per entry by binary powering. With standard presented finite fields, the explicit output and all field arithmetic have deterministic polynomial bit cost, uniformly in the characteristic. The proof of correctness uses Lang–Steinberg without computing its witnesses. A matching format with a zero mode is a fixed YES case, while a format mismatch is a fixed NO case. [Grochow–Qiao Part IV, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S5)

## Unbounded splitting on the diagonal tensor

Let $T=\sum_{i=1}^n e_i\otimes e_i\otimes e_i$. In each mode the nonzero rank-one contractions occur exactly at the coordinate covector lines, because contraction yields a diagonal matrix with the chosen coefficients. A stabilizer triple therefore acts by monomial matrices. The support of $T$ forces a common permutation, and the scalar products along each matched coordinate equal one. Those triples also stabilize the dual conjugate: inverse transpose keeps the permutation and inverts the scalar product in each coordinate. The kernel of the action on the coordinates is the connected torus $(k^\times)^{2n}$, so the component group is $S_n$. The action of $\Theta$ on this quotient is trivial since permutation matrices are fixed by inverse transpose and Frobenius.

Thus one ordinary orbit of the pair contains exactly $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1$ unitary orbits of fixed pairs. The count is one per possible number of transpositions in an involution, including the identity in $S_n$ because it corresponds to $\theta$ in the coset. Dimension two already gives false positives for the unrestricted direct pair test in every characteristic.

For an explicit example over $\mathbb F_4$, choose $\omega$ with $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$ and apply
$$
    g=\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega\\1&\omega^2\end{pmatrix}
$$
in all three modes of the two-dimensional diagonal tensor. The matrix $g^*g$ is the swap matrix, with $g^*=(g^{[2]})^T$. Consequently $g^{-1}\Theta(g)$ is also the swap matrix, making the transported pair a fixed pair of the required form. The two primal support lines obtained from rank-one contractions in another mode are the column lines of $g$, both isotropic. For the source they are the coordinate lines, both nonisotropic. This supplies a direct check of failure without consulting the component classification.

## Scope and antecedents

Lysikov–Walter prove the complex conjugate-dual pair reduction using Kempf–Ness. Guralnick uses the Frobenius–inverse-transpose fixed-point method and connected stabilizers for finite-field matrix pairs. The identity-component argument is finite-field descent. Here the calculation gives the exact splitting for rational paired three-tensor orbits, the explicit unbounded family of failures, and a linear-length uniform reduction on the odd-component promise. The proof is informal, and publication priority for this tensor specialization is unverified. [Lysikov–Walter, Theorems V.30–31](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04639v2) · [Guralnick, Corollary 4.2 and §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.06999)
