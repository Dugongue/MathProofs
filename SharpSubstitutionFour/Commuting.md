# Exact thresholds for commuting coefficient matrices

Fix a field $k$, $h\ge2$, and two marked pencils of the same format from the [matrix-substitution construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SliceSimilarityFour/Tensor.md). Write $m=h+1$. The centered slice $N_u$ on $V\oplus W$ takes $(v,w)$ to $(0,\beta_M(u,v))$, where $\beta_M(u,v)$ is the image of $uv$ modulo the quadratic relation space $H_M$, included in padded $W$. For distinct input matrix spaces $M,M'$ set
$$
t=m\min_{B\in M\triangle M'}\operatorname{rank}B.
$$

**Theorem.** The first matching substitution with different rational canonical forms has size $t+1$ when the scalar and all centered coefficient matrices commute. If the algebra generated by these matrices acts on the coefficient space with a cyclic vector, the first size is $t+2$. If all coefficients are polynomials in a single matrix, every matching substitution is similar by a conjugator commuting with that matrix on the coefficient factor, at any size. For $M=M'$ all matching substitutions agree.

In particular the rank-one versus rank-two pair has successive exact sizes $h+1$, $h+2$ and $h+3$ for arbitrary, commuting and cyclic commuting coefficient probes. These statements concern marked comparisons and hold over every field. The global tensor-isomorphism reduction is inherited from the original pencils.

## Compressed rank

An evaluation is
$$
E=\begin{pmatrix}S\otimes I_V&0\\ C_M&S\otimes I_W\end{pmatrix},
\qquad (C_M)_{ij}(v)=\beta_M(u_{ij},v).
$$
Independent row and column changes reduce $S$ to an identity block plus a zero block. Eliminating at the identity blocks gives
$$
\operatorname{rank}E=(\dim V+\dim W)\operatorname{rank}S+
\operatorname{rank}\bigl(\ker S\otimes V\xrightarrow{\overline C_M}
\operatorname{coker}S\otimes W\bigr).
\tag{1}
$$
The off-diagonal blocks of any polynomial in $E$ have the same form. A product with two centered factors vanishes even with scalar factors between them. The new coefficients are independent of $M$ and belong to the algebra generated by the original coefficients.

Two facts from the original construction are used. Over every extension of $k$, $\beta_M(u,-)$ is injective when $u\ne0$ because $H_M$ contains no nonzero product of linear forms. Over $k$, if $\dim U<t$ then $H_M\cap UV=H_{M'}\cap UV$. A relation in $UV$ has polarization rank at most $2\dim U$. A differing relation is a replicated zero-anchor data matrix and has polarization rank at least $2t$. The [marked width theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SliceSimilarityFour/Algorithms.md) gives a subspace $U$ of dimension exactly $t$ where the two intersection dimensions differ. Its two-copy construction isolates a minimum-rank member of $M\triangle M'$ over $k$.

A rectangular direction array with fewer than $t$ coefficient columns has matching rank. The directions in each row span a space $U_i$ of dimension less than $t$, so the two quotient maps on $U_iV$ have the same kernel. Match their images by one isomorphism of the padded $W$ spaces in each row. The same argument works whenever each row separately uses fewer than $t$ directions.

## Commuting coefficients

Let $A$ be the unital algebra of coefficient matrices. As it is commutative and finite-dimensional, the coefficient module decomposes into summands for the local factors of $A$. This decomposition applies to any polynomial in $E$. On a local summand of dimension $b\le t$, if $S\ne0$ then the final map in (1) has at most $b-1<t$ coefficient columns.

Suppose $S=0$. All simple factors of a composition series of this coefficient module are the residue field $L$ of its local algebra. If the residue centered direction $u\in L\otimes_k V$ is nonzero, the diagonal maps on that series are $\beta_{M,L}(u,-)$ and are injective. The full centered map is injective by induction on the series. If $u=0$, the coefficient matrices are strictly block upper triangular in a $k$-basis adapted to the series. The first $[L:k]$ columns vanish, so each row uses at most $b-[L:k]<t$ directions. All comparisons involving the threshold $t$ take place over $k$, regardless of the residue field.

Ranks of all polynomials determine the rational canonical form. For sharpness take a basis $u_1,\ldots,u_t$ of the distinguishing $U$. At size $t+1$ give $u_i$ the coefficient $E_{0i}$ with indices $0,\ldots,t$, and set $S=0$. Products of these coefficient matrices vanish. Their single nonzero output row has image $\beta_M(U,V)$ of dimension $\dim UV-\dim(H_M\cap UV)$, giving different ranks.

## Commuting coefficients with a cyclic vector

A cyclic vector identifies the coefficient module with the regular module $A$. Indeed $A$ acts faithfully, and any element killing a cyclic vector kills the entire module. Polynomials in the evaluated matrix still give coefficients in the same $A$. At total size at most $t+1$, local summands of dimension at most $t$ are covered above. The remaining case is a regular local algebra $A$ of dimension $t+1$, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak n$ and residue field $L$.

Write the scalar part as multiplication by $a\in A$. If its rank is at least two, the compressed array in (1) has at most $t-1$ coefficient columns. If its rank is one, $a$ is a nonunit. The line $aA$ is stable under the coefficients, and $a^2=0$ since multiplication by the nilpotent $a$ acts as zero on this line. Thus $aA\subseteq\ker S$, and the compressed centered map sends $aA\otimes V$ to zero in $(A/aA)\otimes W$. Factoring out this input line again leaves $t-1$ coefficient columns.

Suppose $a=0$. A nonzero residue direction gives injectivity by the composition-series argument. If all centered coefficients lie in $\mathfrak n$ and the combined direction is nonzero, choose $j$ maximal with the combined direction in $\mathfrak n^j\otimes_k V$. Project the centered map to $(\mathfrak n^j/\mathfrak n^{j+1})\otimes_k W$. On input this factors through $L\otimes_k V$. Composing with a suitable $L$-linear functional on $\mathfrak n^j/\mathfrak n^{j+1}$ gives $\beta_{M,L}(u,-)$ with $u\ne0$. Every kernel vector therefore belongs to $\mathfrak n\otimes_k V$. Restrict to that subspace. The restriction factors by the last nonzero power of $\mathfrak n$ on input because every centered coefficient kills it. At most $(t+1)-[L:k]-1\le t-1$ coefficient columns remain. The restricted ranks agree, hence the full kernel dimensions agree. An identically zero direction gives the zero map. This proves equality for every polynomial.

For sharpness use the commutative local algebra with basis $1,z,p_1,\ldots,p_t$, products $p_ip_j=\delta_{ij}z$ and $z\mathfrak n=0$ for $\mathfrak n=\langle z,p_1,\ldots,p_t\rangle$. Associativity follows from the vanishing of triple products in $\mathfrak n$. Take $S=0$ and give $u_i$ the coefficient matrix of multiplication by $p_i$. These multipliers generate the algebra and act with cyclic vector $1$. The unit input feeds a $\dim V$-dimensional image in the $p_i$ rows by injectivity of $\beta_M(u_i,-)$. The $p_i$ inputs feed the disjoint $z$ row with image $\beta_M(U,V)$. This gives different ranks at size $t+2$.

## Polynomials in a single matrix

Suppose all coefficients are polynomials in $T$. Decompose its coefficient space into rational cyclic summands $B=k[x]/(g^e)$ for monic irreducibles $g$. The centered map on a summand is the $B$-linear map $\beta_M(u,-)$ for a common $u\in B\otimes_k V$. If $u=g^j u_*\ne0$ where $u_*$ has a unit coordinate, reduction modulo $g$ gives an injective map $\beta_M(u_*,-)$. A full-size unit minor gives a split embedding whose image basis extends to a basis of $B\otimes_k W$, by elementary elimination over $B$. Match these bases for $M$ and $M'$ to match $\beta_M(u_*,-)$ and then $\beta_M(u,-)$. For $u=0$ any identification of the padded spaces works. Take the identity on $B\otimes_k V$. This gives a $B$-linear similarity preserving the scalar term and commuting with $T$.

## The normalized last-two-mode symmetric carrier

The doubled evaluation is the sum of the ordinary evaluation and the transpose of the ordinary evaluation at transposed coefficients. This gives the commuting lower bound through size $t$ and the all-size single-matrix theorem on both summands. At the commuting row witness the transpose term has rank $\dim V$ in both instances, since its ordinary centered map is a column feeding several rows. The rank difference survives.

For cyclic commuting probes of the doubled carrier the exact threshold is $t+1$. Use the regular module of the square-zero algebra with basis $1,p_1,\ldots,p_t$ and $p_ip_j=0$, assigning multiplication by $p_i$ to $u_i$. The ordinary summand has the constant rank $\dim V$ of a column probe. The other summand has the differing rank of the row probe. If the coefficient module and its dual are both cyclic over the generated algebra, the exact threshold for the doubled carrier is $t+2$. The ordinary lower proof applies on both sides. The size-$t+2$ algebra above has a perfect pairing that takes the $z$ coefficient of a product, identifying the regular module with its dual and giving the same separating rank difference in both summands.
