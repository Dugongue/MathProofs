# Determinant cosets from marked slice modules

Informal mathematics. Fix $F=\mathbb F_q$ of characteristic $p$. Consider two pencils $M_i:U_i\to\operatorname{Hom}_F(X_i,Y_i)$, with transporter convention $M_B(fu)=hM_A(u)g^{-1}$. The inputs include a prescribed isomorphism $W_A\to W_B$ of marked label subspaces and a prescribed isomorphism $U_A/W_A\to U_B/W_B$. The maps on complementary lifts are allowed arbitrary shears into $W_B$. The task tests a prescribed value $\eta$ of a determinant character $\chi(f,g,h)=(\det f)^{a_U}(\det g)^{a_X}(\det h)^{a_Y}$. This setting allows rectangular slices, singular anchors, arbitrary basis changes and nonsemisimple module algebras. It is a branch theorem with prescribed subspace and quotient maps.

Two computable subgroups control the test. The exact subgroup $H\le F^\times$ comes from automorphisms that fix the full ordered slice list. A second group $H_{\rm rel}$ comes from a polynomial-time relaxation described below. If they coincide, the test reduces to one ordinary TI instance with no bound on any matrix factor size. In general the construction requires exterior algebras only for the matrix factors still contributing after taking the power $|H|$. The new relaxation uses whole slices, so it sees information above the first symbols of radical filtrations. Sections 5 and 6 give a separate width-free, oracle-free algorithm whenever the only freedom in the normalized slices is to add scalar matrices.

## 1. The exact kernel already gives every determinant correction

Let $G$ denote the source stabilizer fixing $W_A$ pointwise and acting identically on $U_A/W_A$. Its image on $U_A$ lies in the additive group $\operatorname{Hom}_F(U_A/W_A,W_A)$, acting by $u\mapsto u+s(u+W_A)$. The kernel $K$ consists of the pairs of matrix maps that fix every ordered slice. In particular $K$ is the unit group of
$$
 C=\{(g,h):hM_A(u)=M_A(u)g\text{ for every }u\in U_A\},
$$
where endomorphisms need not be invertible in the algebra. The quotient $G/K$ is a $p$-group. The quotient $\chi(G)/\chi(K)$ is both an image of that $p$-group and of order dividing $q-1$. Therefore $\chi(G)=\chi(K)=H$. The same argument gives the joint determinant-triple subgroup in $(F^\times)^3$. The determinant on the label map of $G$ is always one.

The group $H$ can be computed deterministically in polynomial time without finding the group $G$. Compute $C$ by linear equations, its radical $J$, and its explicit semisimple quotient $\prod_t\operatorname{Mat}_{e_t}(E_t)$. On each of $X_A,Y_A$ filter by $J^r$ and count the multiplicities $m_{X,t},m_{Y,t}$ of the natural simple modules on the grades. For each simple component the determinant contributes the norm of the simple determinant, raised to the corresponding multiplicity. This follows by multiplying determinants on the filtration and restricting scalars. The quotient units lift, since the radical is nilpotent. Every norm value in $F^\times$ is realized independently on each simple component. Thus $H$ is generated by the $(a_X m_{X,t}+a_Y m_{Y,t})$-th powers of any fixed generator of $F^\times$. This takes polynomial time at fixed $q$. The construction uses the finite-field algebra decomposition and module algorithms of [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18) and [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski](https://doi.org/10.1145/258726.258751).

The actual branch transporters form either the empty set or a coset of $G$. This uses both prescribed label maps: dividing two transporters cancels them on $W_A$ and $U_A/W_A$. Consequently their character values are empty or a single coset of $H$. For any actual branch transporter its character lies in $\eta H$ exactly when
$$
  \chi(f,g,h)^{|H|}=\eta^{|H|}.
$$
If that condition holds, composition with an element of $K$ obtains character exactly $\eta$. A joint determinant-triple target can be tested by the constant number of characters of $(F^\times)^3$ that kill the computed joint subgroup. They separate its cosets. All the corrections keep the marked subspace and quotient maps prescribed.

## 2. Linearize the allowed difference by a bimodule

First find an isomorphism $(g_0,h_0)$ of the two ordered anchor representations using constructive module isomorphism, or reject. Align the target matrix spaces by this reference. The marks now have the same slice values on both sides. Let $A$ be their common algebra of endomorphism pairs. Its unit group gives exactly the aligned matrix maps satisfying the anchor equations. Put $W=M_A(W_A)=M_B(W_B)$ in the aligned coordinates, where the second equality refers to the target after alignment. Every $S\in W$ obeys $a_Y S=S a_X$ for all $a\in A$. Form the subspace
$$
 I=\operatorname{span}_F\{S a_X:S\in W,\ a\in A\}\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_F(X,Y).
$$
It contains $W$ and is closed under both left multiplication by $a_Y$ and right multiplication by $a_X$. For example $b_Y S a_X=S b_X a_X$. It is computed from the products of bases.

Choose source lift vectors of a quotient basis, and target lifts of its prescribed image. Denote the resulting slices by $D_{A,k}$ and $D_{B,k}$ in the aligned coordinates. A genuine transporter with matrix pair $c\in A^\times$ satisfies
$$
 c_Y D_{A,k} - D_{B,k}c_X \in I.
$$
Indeed the difference is an allowed shear slice times $c_X$. Replacing the shear condition by this linear condition defines the relaxation. It is immaterial which quotient lifts were used, since changing a lift adds a member of $W$ and the difference lies in $I$.

For $i,j\in\{A,B\}$ define the morphism space
$$
 B_{ij}=\{c\in A:c_YD_{j,k}-D_{i,k}c_X\in I\text{ for every }k\}.
$$
The indexed block matrix space $B_{\rm both}=(B_{ij})_{i,j\in\{A,B\}}$ is a unital algebra, by the bimodule property. In fact for composable $a,b$,
$$
 (ab)_Y D_{\ell,k}-D_{i,k}(ab)_X
 =a_Y(b_YD_{\ell,k}-D_{j,k}b_X)
   +(a_YD_{j,k}-D_{i,k}a_X)b_X.
$$
The two source and target summand projections are idempotents of this algebra. An isomorphism of their objects means inverse off-diagonal corner elements. To find one, compare the ranks of their images in every matrix factor of the semisimple quotient. If those ranks agree, construct inverse corner elements there and lift them. The lifted products equal the respective identities modulo the radical, so they are units of the two corners. Correct one lift by the inverse of a product to get genuine inverse corner elements. If the ranks disagree, no relaxed isomorphism exists and a genuine transporter is impossible. All steps are linear or finite-algebra computations of polynomial size.

Write $c_0$ for the relaxed reference and $B=B_{AA}$. Every relaxed relative matrix pair is exactly $c_0 B^\times$ in the aligned frames. In fact a member of $B_{ij}$ invertible as a pair has inverse in $B_{ji}$, by multiplying its defining differences on both sides by the inverse and using stability of $I$. Products give the asserted coset. The group $H_{\rm rel}$ is the image of $B^\times$ under $(g,h)\mapsto(\det g)^{a_X}(\det h)^{a_Y}$. Compute it using the same radical multiplicity and norm formula as for $H$. Exact source kernel units belong to $B^\times$, so $H\le H_{\rm rel}$.

## 3. The exact ordinary TI reduction

The determinant of the label map is known from the prescribed maps on $W_A$ and $U_A/W_A$, including the determinants of the chosen raw basis changes. The first and second matrix references contribute known scalars to the weighted character.

If $H=H_{\rm rel}$, all genuine branch transporters, if any, have character in the coset of $H$ determined by the references. Compare that coset with $\eta H$. Return a fixed NO instance if unequal. If equal, output the ordinary TI test of the original tensors with their marked subspace and quotient maps. This is exact by Section 1: even if a surviving transporter has a different value in the same coset, a complete ordered-slice automorphism corrects it. Thus the final instance contains no exterior algebras on this branch.

More generally, write $B/\operatorname{rad}B=\prod_t\operatorname{Mat}_{b_t}(K_t)$ and compute its character exponents $\alpha_t=a_Xm_{X,t}+a_Ym_{Y,t}$. For a genuine transporter the power $\chi^{|H|}$ is the known calibration times
$$
 \prod_t \mathrm N_{K_t/F}(\det_{K_t} v_t)^{|H|\alpha_t},
$$
where $v_t$ is the quotient matrix of the action corrected by the two references. Attach radical grades and one simple copy for each factor with $|H|\alpha_t\ne0\bmod(q-1)$, using source grades and copies carried to the target by the references. Inclusions and quotient maps force these to use the actual matrix transporters. The simple copy has a $K_t$-exterior algebra whose top line gives the determinant multiplier. Field multiplication and a repeated-power chain with exponent $(q^{[K_t:F]}-1)/(q-1)$ give its norm on an $F$-line. Multiply the weighted factors and pin the result at the value for $\eta^{|H|}$ after calibration. Other grades do not require exterior algebras. If all the powers vanish, this is precisely the previous equality-of-images case.

The original tensor and its prescribed label maps stay in the instance. Every qualified original transporter has induced maps on the displayed grades and lines, and every accepted system isomorphism induces the original transporter with the required powered character. Section 1 gives the exact $\eta$ test. The cost is polynomial in the input length and $2^b$, where $b$ is the maximum of $b_t$ over the surviving factors, or zero when there are none. In particular a logarithmic bound on this new width suffices. The variable lists of spaces and maps are represented by labeled direct sums. Pinned label idempotents and linked copies of each operand force each local map separately, so the signature is fixed. The tensor-system compiler then gives one same-field ordinary TI instance. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

If $I=W$, the relaxation itself is exact: right multiplication of a difference by $c_X^{-1}$ keeps it in $W$, so each difference becomes a genuine allowed shear slice. Choose a preimage in the marked label space to construct each shear; injectivity on that space is unnecessary. In this case all determinant profiles are computable in polynomial time without TI queries. The known anchor and quotient maps ensure that the constructed label map is invertible.

## 4. Strict examples with unbounded previous width

For the first example work over $F=\mathbb F_3$, put $d=2s$, and take two square matrix modes $F^3\otimes F^d$. Let the anchors be $I_{3d}$ and $N=J_3\otimes I_d$, with $J_3$ the upper nilpotent shift. Take two other labeled slices $D_1=E_{12}\otimes P$, $D_2=E_{12}\otimes Q$, where $P=E_{12}\otimes I_s$ and $Q=E_{21}\otimes I_s$ on $F^d$. Prescribe both label maps to be identities in the displayed markings. The anchor algebra consists of identical domain and range maps, with matrix in the upper block Toeplitz form
$$
 c=\begin{pmatrix}G&U&V\\0&G&U\\0&0&G\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The identity slice gives $I=A$ in Section 2 under this identification: the other anchor $N$ is also an element of $A$, so $N A\subseteq A$.

Use the character $\det g$. The first semisimple quotient is $\operatorname{Mat}_d(F)$, with multiplicity three. The two unmarked slices strictly raise the radical filtration, so their degree-zero symbols vanish. The previous construction using the capped radical symbols, scalar-shift powers and their commutators therefore leaves the whole active width $d$. In the new relaxation the first superdiagonal blocks of $cD_1-D_1c$ are $GP-PG$ and zero. Membership in the Toeplitz algebra forces $GP=PG$. The corresponding equation for $D_2$ gives $GQ=QG$, and no higher block condition changes these necessary equalities. Conversely every such $G$ occurs in the diagonal repeated map with $U,V=0$. The common commutant is $I_2\otimes\operatorname{Mat}_s(F)$. Hence every relaxed unit has determinant $(\det R)^6=1$ with $R\in\operatorname{GL}_s(F)$. It follows that $H=H_{\rm rel}=\{1\}$, and the new test has width zero. Independently twisting the target domain basis by a determinant $-1$ shifts the resulting coset, so the calibration detects both signs on ordinarily equivalent pairs. The family grows with $s$.

For a different way the width reduction occurs, use $F=\mathbb F_5$, anchors $I_{2d}$ and $J_2\otimes I_d$, and one other slice $I_2\otimes P$ for a nontrivial coordinate projection $P$. Again use the character $\det g$. The entire relaxed algebra here is the original block Toeplitz algebra, since the third slice is itself in that algebra and $I=A$. Its active width before quotienting by $H$ is $d$, with exponent two. Exact ordered-slice automorphisms include repeated diagonal actions of all maps preserving the image and kernel of $P$. Their determinants realize every square, and all anchor-respecting determinants are squares. Thus $H=H_{\rm rel}=(F^\times)^2$, so raising to $|H|=2$ cancels the width. Frame twists can change the coset from squares to nonsquares. These two examples separate the use of full slice linear equations from the use of the exact determinant correction subgroup.

## 5. Eliminate a scalar shear without any width bound

There is an unconditional deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for this marked problem when the allowed shear changes every normalized slice by a scalar matrix. The underlying module result is stronger. For two ordered lists of matrices over $F$, consider simultaneous conjugacy when one may add an independent scalar multiple of the identity to each designated matrix. All other matrices are fixed as labeled constraints. In polynomial time one can compute the entire set of permissible shift vectors as an affine subspace over $\mathbb F_p$, or determine that it is empty. This yields a transporter and its exact determinant coset, with no TI calls. Additional explicit affine $\mathbb F_p$-linear constraints on the shifts can be intersected with this space. The proof uses a translation-invariant free algebra to recognize the orbits of the indecomposable summands.

### The invariant algebra and its computation

Let $A_{\rm free}$ be the free unital algebra over $F$ on generators $x_1,\ldots,x_m,y_1,\ldots,y_t$. The $x_i$ correspond to shifted matrices and the $y_j$ are fixed. The group $\Gamma=F^m$ acts on the algebra by $x_i\mapsto x_i+s_i$ for $s=(s_1,\ldots,s_m)$, with the $y_j$ fixed. Let $R$ be the unital subalgebra generated by all the $y_j$, the commutators $[x_i,x_j]$, the powers $x_i^q-x_i$, and all their iterated commutators with the $x_i$. Iterated commutators with an invariant element are invariant, because the scalar changes in an $x_i$ commute with everything. The rule $[x,ab]=[x,a]b+a[x,b]$ shows that $R$ is stable under every $[x_i,-]$.

As a right $R$-module the free algebra has basis the ordered monomials $x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_m^{a_m}$ with $0\le a_i<q$. For spanning, move coefficients in $R$ to the right using stability under commutators. Exchange adjacent $x_i$'s using their commutators, and reduce each $q$-th power by $x_i^q=x_i+(x_i^q-x_i)$. The correction terms either shorten the number of free letters or are pure coefficients. At a fixed length the exchanges reduce the number of inversions. This proves spanning by induction on length and inversions, including words in the $y_j$ since those are coefficients. For independence, suppose $\sum_a x^a r_a=0$ for $r_a\in R$. Translate by all $s\in F^m$. This gives a polynomial in the central variables $s_i$ of individual degrees at most $q-1$, with coefficients in the underlying $F$-vector space of the free algebra, vanishing on the full grid. Such a polynomial has all zero coefficients by iterated univariate interpolation. For a nonzero $r_a$ of maximum total $\sum_i a_i$, the coefficient at $s^a$ is exactly $r_a$. This is a contradiction. The same reasoning applied to a translation-invariant element shows that its basis expansion can have only its constant coefficient, so $R=A_{\rm free}^{\Gamma}$.

The exponential basis size is used only in this proof. Given explicit matrix representations, compute the action of $R$ by linear span closure of the displayed generators under products and commutation with the matrices of the $x_i$. For a pair, close the span of paired evaluations, equivalently in the block diagonal matrices on the direct sum. This space has dimension at most the sum of the squares of the two original matrix dimensions. Each addition to its basis increases its dimension, so the closure computation and the test of isomorphism of the two restrictions to $R$ take polynomial time. The same invariant-algebra argument works when just a suffix or any chosen subset of the $x_i$ can be shifted. Treat all other generators as fixed $y_j$.

### Match indecomposable modules and compute their shift sets

If $V$ is an $A_{\rm free}$-module, denote by $V^s$ its twist via translation by $s$. The restriction/induction identity is
$$
 A_{\rm free}\otimes_R V|_R \ \cong\ \bigoplus_{s\in\Gamma} V^s .
$$
To verify it, send $a\otimes v$ to $(\rho(\gamma_s(a))v)_s$, where $\rho$ is the action on $V$. It is well-defined because translations fix $R$, and it is $A_{\rm free}$-linear by the respective twisted actions. With the free right basis above, its kernel corresponds to relations $\sum_a\rho(\gamma_s(x^a))v_a=0$ for all $s$. These again form an interpolation problem of degree less than $q$ in each variable. A coefficient of highest total degree is the corresponding $v_a$, so every $v_a$ vanishes. Both sides have the same finite dimension. This proves the identity without constructing either large module computationally.

For indecomposable $V,W$, their restrictions to $R$ are isomorphic exactly when $V\cong W^s$ for some $s$. One implication holds because translations fix $R$. The other follows by induction and Krull–Schmidt: $V=V^0$ is an indecomposable summand of the induced left side and therefore occurs among the twists of $W$. These modules have finite length, and Krull–Schmidt applies to them, for example over the common finite-dimensional matrix algebra generated by the action on their direct sum. This yields a polynomial test for a shift on an indecomposable pair. The test still works after prescribing selected coordinates of the shift: apply the prescribed part to one tuple, and use the invariant algebra for the remaining coordinates. A shift representative is found by choosing each subsequent coordinate from its $q$ possibilities, retaining a value with an exact feasible remaining-shift test.

For a reference indecomposable $V$, the stabilizer $K_V=\{s:V\cong V^s\}$ is an additive $\mathbb F_p$-subspace of $\Gamma$. Compute a generating list as follows. For each coordinate $i$ and each field element $c$, test whether there is an element of $K_V$ whose first $i-1$ coordinates are zero and whose $i$-th coordinate is $c$. If so, find a representative by the same successive coordinates method. These representatives generate the subgroup. Indeed subtract the representative of the first nonzero coordinate to obtain an element in the next prefix kernel, and continue. Their $\mathbb F_p$-span is a polynomial-size explicit description of $K_V$.

### Recover the simultaneous shift for arbitrary tuples

Decompose both matrix representations into indecomposable summands. This uses the endomorphism algebra of each ordered tuple, its semisimple quotient and primitive diagonal idempotents lifted in successive complementary corners. The image of each lift is indecomposable, and the idempotents sum to the identity since the omitted idempotent would lie in the nilpotent radical. These are deterministic polynomial-time finite-algebra computations at fixed $q$.

Partition the combined list of summands by equivalence up to shift, using the indecomposable test. In one class choose a reference $V$ and compute $K_V$. For every summand in that class find one shift relating it to $V$. Its ordinary isomorphism type is now exactly a point of $\Gamma/K_V$, represented in $\mathbb F_p$-coordinates. Form the two multiplicity lists at these points. A global shift $s$ carries the second list to the first exactly when translation by its class in $\Gamma/K_V$ carries one weighted finite set to the other. Choose one point of the second list and try its image at each point of the first. This enumerates at most the number of summands of candidate translations, and explicit point and multiplicity comparisons verify each. If either list is empty in a nonempty class, or no translation works, the input has no shift. Otherwise the valid translations form a coset of the stabilizer of either translated weighted list. Compute it by taking one surviving point and the $\mathbb F_p$-span of all differences with it. Lift this coset to $\Gamma$ by adding $K_V$. Intersect these affine spaces over all classes. This intersection is precisely the full set of shifts, by Krull–Schmidt and preservation of the twist classes by every translation.

Every step has a polynomial bound in the input size at fixed $F$. There are at most the total matrix dimension many summands. Testing a suffix shift and finding a representative require polynomially many module computations, and the subgroup generation has at most $mq$ prefix cases per reference. All final affine spaces have dimension at most $m[F : \mathbb F_p]$. No enumeration of the full group $\Gamma$ is used.

For a chosen allowed shift, constructive module isomorphism gives one conjugator. The group of conjugators that send the source tuple to one of its translates maps to the additive group $\Gamma$, with kernel the ordinary centralizer unit group of the tuple. The shifts of a conjugator are unique when the matrix dimension is positive. Its determinant image is therefore exactly the image of the centralizer unit group, as in Section 1. This group is computed by the radical norm formula. In particular the exact determinant coset is the same for all allowed shift representatives of a pair. If the matrix dimension is zero the sole conjugator has determinant one and all scalar-shift equations have zero matrices, which is decided directly.

## 6. Consequence for tensor branches and an iterative refinement

If the only marked label direction is an invertible slice, fixing it and the label quotient makes the normalized residual tuple a scalar-shift problem. Explicitly, let the anchored slices be $S_A,S_B$, and pair lift matrices $D_{A,i},D_{B,i}$ by the prescribed quotient map. Then $h=S_B g S_A^{-1}$. With $S_A^{-1}D_{A,i}$ and $S_B^{-1}D_{B,i}$ as the normalized tuples, the remaining condition is simultaneous conjugacy up to scalar shifts. The label determinant is fixed and $\det h/\det g=\det S_B/\det S_A$. Section 5 gives the full joint determinant profile for this entire marked class. The same argument permits additional fixed normalized slices as exact conjugacy constraints while the allowed shears remain scalar. The shift solution space can also be intersected with affine $\mathbb F_p$-linear constraints.

The bimodule method also admits a useful iterative strengthening for general marked branches. After finding a relaxed reference $c_0$, replace the current algebra $A$ by $B_{AA}$ and pull the target lift slices back through $c_0$. The marks still agree, since $c_0$ obeys the exact anchor equations. Every true relative pair, corrected by $c_0$, is now a unit of $B_{AA}$. Recompute $I=W B_{AA}$, build the same linear corner algebra and repeat. Exact source kernel units stay in every relaxed automorphism algebra. A strict refinement decreases the dimension over $F$, and if the algebra stays the same then the subspace $I=WA$ and the source equations stabilize. At most the starting dimension plus one rounds are needed. Accumulate the reference determinants along the way. The space used to read the final powered character is now the smallest relaxed algebra in this iteration. Since each prior step was a necessary relaxation, keeping the full original tensor in the final branch gives the same exact test as in Section 3.

For a strict two-round example, over $F_3$ use the same three-level anchor shift $N=J_3\otimes I_{2s}$ and identity slice as in Section 4. On the $2s$-dimensional block take
$$
 P=E_{11}\otimes I_s,\qquad Q=(E_{12}+E_{22})\otimes I_s.
$$
The two other slices are $E_{12}\otimes P$ and $I_3\otimes Q$. The first relaxed algebra is still upper block Toeplitz, with the restriction that its repeated diagonal $G$ commutes with $P$. The commutator with $I_3\otimes Q$ is automatically Toeplitz so imposes no first-round condition. Both anchors still belong to the first relaxed algebra, so the next subspace $I$ is that algebra itself. In the second round $[G,Q]$ must commute with $P$. For $G=\operatorname{diag}(G_1,G_2)$ this commutator has only the cross block $G_1-G_2$, so it is zero and $G_1=G_2$. Thus for the domain determinant the first relaxed image is all of $F_3^\times$ and the second is trivial. The exact ordered-slice image is also trivial. The previous capped symbols again make no refinement: the first slice has zero degree-zero symbols, and $Q$ is idempotent so $Q^3-Q=0$. This gives an unbounded family on which the second relaxation removes the whole active width.

## Scope

The determinant kernel formula and the iterative relaxation apply to every prescribed subspace and quotient branch. Their general polynomial bound still depends on the final surviving width or the equality of the two determinant images. The scalar-shift problem of Section 5 and its single invertible marked direction consequence have no width hypothesis. Computing compatible subspace and quotient data for every raw tensor, and handling arbitrary multidimensional nonscalar shears without a width bound, remain open. All proofs are informal. Publication priority is unverified.
