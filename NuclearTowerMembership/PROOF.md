# Logspace membership across nuclear extensions of finite loops

Collins, Grochow, Levet, and Weiß ask whether membership in the subquasigroup generated by a supplied subset of an arbitrary Cayley table is in deterministic logspace. The theorem below gives a recognizable class of nonassociative loops for which the answer is affirmative. The unrestricted question remains open. [Original Question 8.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00133#S8)

**Theorem.** Fix a nonnegative constant $d$. Consider finite loops that admit a tower of at most $d$ successive quotients by normal subloops contained in the nucleus. The terminal loop may be Moufang, uniquely two-divisible left Bol with the automorphic inverse property, or uniquely two-divisible automorphic with an abelian associated Bruck loop. From a raw multiplication table, recognition of this class and membership in the subloop generated by any supplied subset take deterministic logarithmic workspace. On acceptance the algorithm can also stream a polynomial-length, constant-register program over the supplied generators, the identity, multiplication, and the two divisions that produces the target.

The statement about generated subquasigroups uses the convention that the empty set generates the empty subquasigroup. Handle that case before running the algorithm. In the subloop formulation the empty set generates the singleton identity subloop. All other generated subquasigroups of a finite loop are subloops. Indeed every finite nonempty product-closed subset is closed under both divisions, and $x\backslash x=e$.

## 1. A logspace path can be streamed

Reingold's theorem decides undirected connectivity in deterministic logarithmic workspace. For completeness, an oracle call to this theorem also suffices to stream a path through any polynomial-size graph whose adjacency predicate is in L. Put a fixed total order on the undirected edges. The canonical Kruskal forest includes an edge $uv$ exactly when $u$ and $v$ are disconnected using only edges preceding $uv$. Thus membership in the forest is computable in L. Given connected vertices $u$ and $t$, the first edge from $u$ on the unique forest path to $t$ is the neighbor edge $uv$ for which $v$ is connected to $t$ in the forest with $uv$ removed. Scan the possibilities and repeat at the new vertex. The path has length at most the graph's vertex count. The fixed number of compositions of logspace graph predicates uses logarithmic workspace. [Reingold](https://doi.org/10.1145/1391289.1391291)

An implicit graph may have several available labels for the same edge. A logspace scan can choose the first valid label for the observed step. A deterministic logspace transducer supplying a polynomial-length output string can be simulated as a read-only virtual string by rerunning it to the requested position. We use this rule for quotient tables and for generated-element programs.

## 2. The translation-orbit base cases

The left and right maps $L_s(x)=sx$ and $R_s(x)=xs$ are permutations of the loop. Draw their edges for seed elements $s$ and allow each edge to be traversed either way. When the loop is Moufang, Nagy's translation-generation lemma applied to the generated subloop shows that the orbit of its identity under these generators is the entire generated subloop. Restrictions of the ambient seed permutations to the subloop are its seed permutations, so the orbit computation may run directly on the ambient raw table. [Nagy, _Moufang and Bol loops of small exponent_, Lemma 2](https://www.acta.hu/download.phtml?id=2632#page=4)

For the uniquely two-divisible left Bol case with the automorphic inverse property, use only the left edges. In the left translation group with inversion automorphism $J$, the map $a\mapsto L_a$ identifies the loop with the anti-fixed elements, on which the product is $(x y^2 x)^{1/2}$ in group notation. Each anti-fixed element has odd order. The group generated by the seed translations consequently contains $L_a$ for every generated loop element $a$. Indeed the subset of labels $a$ whose $L_a$ are in that group contains the seeds, and it is closed under this multiplication since the square root of an odd-order element of the group is a power of its square. The orbit of $e$ is again the generated subloop. [Nagy, definition and Lemma 1](https://www.acta.hu/download.phtml?id=2632#page=3)

These arguments give logspace membership by Reingold. They also give the base case of the constructive assertion, since each step along a streamed path is multiplication or division by a supplied generator on the appropriate side. Recognizing the two loop identities and, in the second case, bijectivity of the squaring map requires only bounded numbers of table-element counters.

There is a third base case via Lie rings. First take any finite abelian group $V$, supplied endomorphisms $D_s$ of its additive group, and seed vectors $s$. The least additive subgroup $W$ containing the seeds and invariant under every $D_s$ is an undirected orbit calculation on $V^2$. Generate permutations of $V^2$ by the translations $(x,y)\mapsto(x+s,y)$, the swap $(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)$, and the shears $(x,y)\mapsto(x+D_s(y),y)$. The orbit $O$ of $(0,0)$ is an additive subgroup. To see this, take the subgroup of automorphisms generated by swaps and shears, conjugate each seed translation by all these automorphisms, and observe that all resulting translations generate the orbit. It lies in $W^2$. If $(z,0)\in O$, swap to $(0,z)$ and shear to $(D_s(z),z)$, then subtract $(0,z)$ to obtain $(D_s(z),0)\in O$. Closure under addition and swap now gives $O=W^2$.

For a finite Lie ring with addition and bracket tables, take $D_s(z)=[s,z]$. The subgroup $W$ just constructed equals the generated Lie subring. Indeed let $P$ consist of the elements $a$ of $W$ for which $[a,W]\subseteq W$. It is an additive subgroup containing the seeds. For $a,b\in P$, the element $[a,b]$ lies in $W$ and its adjoint is the commutator of the two adjoints by Jacobi. Thus $[a,b]\in P$. The Lie subring generated by the seeds is contained in $P\subseteq W$, and it contains $W$ by the defining stability of $W$. This gives deterministic logspace membership for every finite Lie ring in the full-table model, including streamed witnesses using the two current coordinates and a fixed number of scratch registers.

In a uniquely two-divisible automorphic loop with abelian associated Bruck loop, the correspondence of Jedlička, Kinyon, and Vojtěchovský recovers such a Lie ring on the same elements. Its addition is the Bruck product $x\circ y=[x^{-1}\backslash(y^2x)]^{1/2}$ and its bracket is $x\circ y\circ(xy)^{-1}$, with inverses taken in the original loop. In their correspondence, each Lie subring is a subloop. Conversely, the two formulas take any subloop into itself because a finite subloop is closed under the globally injective squaring map, which restricts to a bijection there. Hence generated subsets agree. To get a program using original operations, implement a square root of $u$ by the power $u^{(m+1)/2}$, with $m$ its order. The cited power-associativity theorem makes the order computable by iteration with a counter, and squaring on the cyclic subloop shows that $m$ is odd. Each addition, subtraction, and bracket call in a graph step can therefore be streamed in the loop operations using constant registers. [Jedlička, Kinyon, and Vojtěchovský, Proposition 4.2 and Theorem 5.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.1642)

Recognition of this third base case is also in L. For each table label $x$ and each generator $\gamma$ of the multiplication permutation group or its inverse, test that $L_{\gamma(x)}^{-1}\gamma L_x$ preserves the product on all pairs. These permutations generate the identity stabilizer by Schreier's argument with $L_x(e)=x$, and a loop is automorphic exactly when that stabilizer consists of automorphisms. Test unique two-divisibility, then test associativity and commutativity of the implicit Bruck operation by bounded scans.

## 3. Recovering a normal nuclear kernel from the table

Let $Q$ be the given loop with identity $e$. Its nucleus $N$ consists of the elements that associate when placed in each of the three positions of a product. It is a group. For example, closure under products follows by applying the three associativity equations twice, and finite closure under both divisions follows by injectivity of translations. Left translation identifies $N$ with a subgroup $L_N$ of the permutation group of $Q$, since $L_{ab}=L_aL_b$ for $a,b\in N$.

Let $M$ be generated by every left and right translation of $Q$. We can test whether $L_a$ lies in the normal core of $L_N$ in $M$, without building $M$. Use a graph with one vertex for each $a\in N$ and an extra vertex $\bot$. For every ambient translation or inverse $\gamma$, add an edge from $a$ to $b$ if $\gamma L_a\gamma^{-1}=L_b$ for a $b\in N$, or from $a$ to $\bot$ if no such $b$ exists. Each test uses only scans and table lookups, including evaluation of inverse translations by scanning a row or column. A vertex $a$ is disconnected from $\bot$ exactly when every $M$-conjugate of $L_a$ belongs to $L_N$. To prove this, consider the first conjugation step by a generator that leaves $L_N$. Inverse generators allow all internal edges to be traversed backwards. Reingold therefore computes membership in the core.

Write $A$ for the set of labels whose left translations belong to this core. Its orbit relation on $Q$ is invariant under all left and right translations by normality of the core. Thus multiplication preserves the relation in both coordinates, and translations of the quotient are bijections. The quotient is a loop, its identity class is $A$, and the relation is also preserved by both divisions. In particular $A$ is a normal subloop contained in the nucleus.

Conversely, any normal subloop $U\subseteq N$ arises inside $A$. If $u\in U$ and $x\in Q$, normality gives $xu=u'x$ for a unique $u'\in U$. Nuclearity yields
$$
L_x L_u=L_{xu}=L_{u'x}=L_{u'} L_x.
$$
Also $L_u$ commutes with every $R_x$ by the left nucleus identity. Hence $L_U$ is normal in $M$ and lies in the core. This proves maximality of $A$.

Every quotient class can be represented by its least table label. To find the representative of $x$, scan $y$ and test membership of $y/x$ in $A$, where $y/x$ is right division, the unique $a$ with $ax=y$. Thus tables of the quotient and the quotient map are virtual logspace data. The use of normal permutation operators to recover a congruence is the same mechanism as in our quasigroup direct-product work. [MathProofs, direct-product algorithm](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/CayleyCircuitsAndDecompositions/QuasigroupDecomposition.md)

## 4. Lifting membership through one nuclear kernel

Let $p:Q\to Q/A$ be a quotient by a normal nuclear subloop. Assume a constructive membership algorithm of the stated kind for $Q/A$. Fix a seed set $S\subset Q$, set $H=\langle S\rangle$, and let $B=\langle p(S)\rangle$ in the quotient. For each $q\in B$, run the quotient's canonical constructive algorithm on $q$. Evaluate its output program in $Q$ by replacing every seed label in $p(S)$ by the least seed that maps to it and using $e$ for the identity. This gives a chosen lift $s(q)\in H$. Use $s(e)=e$. Each lift is obtained in logspace by streaming the constant-register program, and it can itself be streamed as a program over $S$.

For $q,r\in B$ and $x\in S$, put
$$
c(q,r)=\frac{s(q)s(r)}{s(qr)},\qquad
\delta(x)=\frac{x}{s(p(x))},\qquad
\phi_q(a)=\frac{s(q)a}{s(q)} \quad (a\in A).
$$
The first two expressions belong to $H\cap A$. Normality gives $\phi_q(A)=A$ by finiteness and cancellation. Each $\phi_q$ is a group automorphism of $A$. To check multiplicativity, transport $s(q)(ab)$ across the fiber using the nuclear identities for $a,b,\phi_q(a),\phi_q(b)$. This gives
$$
s(q)(ab)=(s(q)a)b
=(\phi_q(a)s(q))b
=(\phi_q(a)\phi_q(b))s(q).
$$

Let $K$ be the smallest subgroup of $A$ containing every defect $c(q,r)$ and $\delta(x)$ and invariant under the $\phi_q$. Since $H$ is closed under division and products, $K\subseteq H\cap A$. Conversely, nuclearity gives the exact coordinate identity
$$
(a s(q))(b s(r))=\bigl(a\,\phi_q(b)\,c(q,r)\bigr)s(qr) \qquad(a,b\in A).
$$
For example, first move the leftmost $a$ outside and reassociate $s(q)(b s(r))$ as $(s(q)b)s(r)$. Replace $s(q)b$ by $\phi_q(b)s(q)$ and move $\phi_q(b)$ outside. All these rebracketings use nuclear elements. Substitute $s(q)s(r)=c(q,r)s(qr)$ to get the formula. Hence the union of the fibers $K\,s(q)$ for $q\in B$ contains $S$ and is closed under multiplication. Finiteness makes it a subloop, and every element of this union is in $H$. This proves
$$
H=\bigcup_{q\in B} K\,s(q).
$$

To compute $K$, take a graph on $A$ with edges $a\leftrightarrow\phi_q(a)$ for all $q\in B$, and $a\leftrightarrow a u$ for every defect $u$. The orbit of $e$ lies in $K$. Conversely, conjugating the permutation $a\mapsto au$ by any product $\psi$ of the $\phi_q$ and their inverses gives $a\mapsto a\psi(u)$. Thus the orbit of $e$ contains the subgroup generated by all such images $\psi(u)$, which equals $K$. Adjacency is decidable by scanning candidate indices. Each candidate lift and defect is obtained in logarithmic space, and the graph has at most $|Q|$ vertices. Reingold determines whether $t/s(p(t))$ belongs to $K$ whenever $p(t)$ belongs to $B$.

For a constructive witness, stream an edge-labeled path of the kernel graph from $e$ to $t/s(p(t))$. In addition to the register holding the current kernel element, use a fixed number of scratch registers to replay the quotient lift programs in each edge label. Right multiplication by a defect uses that defect's displayed expression, or its right division in the inverse direction. The map $\phi_q$ uses the expression above and its inverse is $a\mapsto s(q)\backslash(a\,s(q))$. Finish by multiplying the kernel result by $s(p(t))$. The number of registers increases by a constant in one lifting step and the output length stays polynomial through any fixed number of steps.

## 5. Why the canonical test finds any tower of the stated length

The class of loops admitting a tower of at most $d$ normal nuclear quotients terminating at a base case is closed under quotients. The base identities are inherited by quotients. Unique two-divisibility is inherited by finite quotients because the squaring map descends and remains surjective. The Schreier generators in §2 descend along a quotient homomorphism and every generator downstairs is obtained in this way, so the automorphic condition is inherited. The Bruck product formula also descends because both divisions and unique square roots are respected. For the inductive step, the image of a nuclear subloop lies in the nucleus by surjectivity, and the image of a normal subloop is normal. The latter assertion follows from congruence permutability. In a loop the term $m(x,y,z)=(x/y)z$ obeys $m(x,y,y)=x$ and $m(x,x,y)=y$. If $x\mathrel{\alpha} y\mathrel{\beta} z$ for two congruences, then $w=m(x,y,z)$ satisfies $w\mathrel{\alpha} z$ and $w\mathrel{\beta} x$. Thus the two congruences permute, and their join induces a congruence on the quotient by $\beta$ whose identity class is the image of the identity class of $\alpha$. Its next quotient is an image of the chosen earlier quotient. This proves the inheritance of the tower by induction.

If a tower exists and the input is not yet a base case, its first kernel lies inside the maximal kernel from §3. The quotient by the maximal kernel is a quotient of the first table in that tower and therefore still admits a tower of length at most $d-1$. Conversely, every successful canonical test produces a tower of the required form. Use §3 recursively at most $d$ times, then apply §4 upwards from the base. The number of levels is fixed, so logspace composition and the constant-register bound prove the theorem.

For an explicit non-Moufang example, let $V=\mathbb F_2^2$ with basis $a,b$ and take $Q=\mathbb F_2\times V$ with
$$
(u,x)*(v,y)=(u+v+f(x,y),x+y),
$$
where $f(a,b)=1$ and $f$ vanishes on other ordered pairs. The first coordinate subgroup is central and nuclear and the quotient is $V$. For $X=(0,a)$ and $Y=(0,b)$, the first coordinates of $(X*Y)*X$ and $X*(Y*X)$ are respectively $1$ and $0$. So even the one-layer transfer applies well beyond Moufang loops.

## Scope of the open question

A one-register seed-translation algorithm cannot handle all loops. In the XOR table of $\mathbb F_2^4$ with labels $0$ through $15$, switch the intercalate on rows $3,11$ and columns $5,13$, exchanging symbols $6$ and $14$ at those four positions. The result remains a loop because no entry in row or column $0$ changed. For $S=\{1,2,4\}$, every left and right translation by a seed is unchanged. Its orbit of $0$ remains $\{0,\ldots,7\}$. Those eight elements are still generated, but $3*5=14$ is now outside this orbit. Any product-closed set containing the orbit and $14$ must be the whole loop, since a proper subquasigroup has size at most half the ambient order. The latter fact follows by translating the subquasigroup by an outside element to obtain a disjoint set of equal size. This example also leaves every table entry with a seed as its symbol unchanged. The unrestricted Question 8.2 calls for a further idea beyond this seed-orbit construction and beyond fixed-depth nuclear quotient towers.
