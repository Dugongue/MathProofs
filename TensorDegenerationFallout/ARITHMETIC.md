# Finite arithmetic in strict tensor degeneration

**Theorem.** Fix a prime \(p\). Ordinary 3-tensor isomorphism over a presented finite field \(\mathbb F_{p^a}\) reduces in deterministic polynomial time to directional degeneration of ordinary three-tensors over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\). There are prime-field outputs of side \(O_p((an+1)^p)\), uniformly in \(a\), where \(n\) is the largest input side. Retaining coefficients in the presented field gives side \(O_p(a(n+1)^p)\). The two output tensors can be required to be concise, of equal format, and geometrically nonisomorphic. In that construction each affirmative target has one prescribed local type. The reduced source orbit closure at the target is smoothly equivalent to the \(2\times2\) rank-one cone at zero. A second choice instead gives smooth strict degenerations.

The result extends to fixed-signature explicit mixed linear structures over the input field, finitely presented tensors over any fixed finite commutative ring of characteristic \(p^e\), and finite \(p\)-groups given by straight-line relators with fixed class and exponent bounds. For graphs and multiplication-table groups it holds in every characteristic, with integral output coordinates and side linear in the universe size. Without the extra record imposing strictness, prime-field outputs also preserve exact transporter counts up to an explicit format factor, with the ordinary target count taken over any finite extension of \(\mathbb F_p\).

## 1. Arithmetic constraints in a geometric directional comparison

Write \(k_0=\mathbb F_p\), \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p\) and \(K=k_0[\theta]=\mathbb F_{p^a}\). First regard each vector space over \(K\) as a \(k_0\)-space and include multiplication by \(\theta\) as a named endomorphism. Commuting with this map is equivalent to \(K\)-linearity. Express an ordinary tensor as a bilinear map from the two dual modes to the third mode, and write that map over \(k_0\). The resulting transporter condition is exactly the ordinary tensor action. This restriction is bijective on transporters. [Same-characteristic field comparison](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorUniversalityApplications/SAME-CHARACTERISTIC-TENSOR-ISOMORPHISM.md)

For general mixed structures, record separate \(K\)-dual sorts and their evaluations into a named copy of \(K\), all restricted to the prime field. Give the scalar copy its multiplication and distinguished element \(\theta\), forcing its map to be the identity. The evaluation maps force the inverse-transpose actions on the duals. A nonempty tensor word gives a multilinear map by contracting every position but one. Its coefficients can be written over \(k_0\). An empty word is a marked element of \(K\). A relation subspace is recorded by its inclusion from a basis sort, with multiplication by \(\theta\) on that sort too. Mapping the actual relation fixes the necessary basis change uniquely. These constructions add only a fixed number of records, with polynomial cost on dense input.

Impose one Frobenius guard on each resulting \(k_0\)-space \(E\). Over \(k\) add the spaces \(S_r=\operatorname{Sym}^r E\) with \(S_1=E\), the successive homogeneous products \(E\times S_{r-1}\to S_r\) for \(2\le r\le p\), and the linear map \(J:E\to S_p\) sending each basis vector \(e_i\) to \(e_i^p\). The maps on the \(S_r\) are forced to be \(\operatorname{Sym}^r(g)\) for the map \(g\) on \(E\), since products of basis elements span each next space. On the pure powers \(\operatorname{Sym}^p(g)\) acts by the entrywise power \(g^{[p]}\). The link equation is therefore \(g^{[p]}=g\). Its allowed invertible matrices are exactly \(\operatorname{GL}(E)(k_0)\), and all these matrices have unique extensions through the guard.

All marked outputs of one format have finite geometric stabilizers. Their mixed orbits therefore have the same dimension, namely the dimension of the acting product of general linear groups. An orbit is open and dense in its closure, and every boundary orbit has smaller dimension. Thus one guarded output is in the closure of another exactly when they lie in the same orbit, which is precisely isomorphism over the original field. This argument uses full general linear groups and requires no global closedness of their orbits. It avoids the determinant records and Lang step used when asking for geometrically closed special-linear orbits. [Earlier finite-field closed-orbit construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorUniversalityApplicationsII/CLOSED-ORBITS-FINITE-FIELD-TI.md)

Apply the linear-width directional compiler to these mixed records. For ordinary input each record has at most three slots, and the named dimension sum is \(O_p((an+1)^p)\). The output is a bare multiplication tensor of a unital algebra, hence has full mode supports. For the other width bound use the direct cyclic guard over \(K\) from the same-characteristic comparison. There are \(a\) levels, with aggregate spaces of each degree and fixed endomorphisms having distinct eigenvalues on the levels. The multiplication records and cyclic pure-power link force \(g_{i+1}=g_i^{[p]}\) and \(g_0^{[p^a]}=g_0\). Thus the same dimension argument applies. The sum of the aggregate dimensions is \(O_p(a(n+1)^p)\), with a fixed number of low-order records. Coefficients now lie in the given \(K\), embedded in \(k\). All degree and count bounds here fix \(p\), while \(a\) can grow.

## 2. Strictness and prescribed singularities

Add an independent \(2\times2\) matrix record before compilation, with its own two general linear factors. Put \(E_{11}\) in that record on the source and the zero matrix on the target. The mixed source orbit closure is the product of the arithmetic orbit closure and the rank-at-most-one determinantal variety, since the two orbit factors are independent and their product is dense in the product of the two closures. Membership of the target is exactly arithmetic isomorphism. The extra matrix ranks differ, so exact isomorphism of the two outputs is impossible by orbit reflection.

In an affirmative case the arithmetic point is in its open orbit and is therefore smooth. Consequently the mixed product germ is smoothly equivalent to the germ of \(ad-bc=0\) at the zero matrix. This is a reduced, singular three-dimensional cone, with tangent dimension four at zero in every characteristic. The linear-width compiler transports that smooth equivalence to the ordinary target point. To obtain a smooth strict degeneration instead, take two independent lines and a nonzero scalar matrix degenerating to zero.

One can also use a rectangular source matrix of rank \(r\) and target matrix of rank \(t<r\). In the invertible \(t\times t\) pivot chart, the Schur complement gives a residual rank-at-most-\(r-t\) locus at zero, with smooth factors from the other blocks. Thus any such determinantal germ can be included with a fixed number of extra records. These constructions use different extra constants on the two inputs, into a common format. Format mismatches can be handled by a fixed negative pair, obtained by comparing two different marked scalar values with the same strictness construction.

In place of the matrix record one can use any fixed tuple with a specified strict degeneration over the query coefficient field. The same product argument then prescribes its local singularity at every affirmative instance. For example, use Zwara's two Kronecker-quiver matrices in Theorem 1, taking \(\lambda_1=0,\lambda_2=1\). Their coefficients lie in the prime field and the theorem applies in every characteristic. The source second matrix has rank two, while with these choices the target second matrix has rank one, so the auxiliary degeneration is strict. Its reduced germ is the isolated intersection of two planes. This gives the arithmetic hardness reduction with a fixed non-unibranch local type as well. [Zwara, Theorem 1](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/87496)

Using the companion near-minimal-rank compiler instead of the linear-width compiler gives the same arithmetic and local-type comparisons with ordinary rank at most \((1+\varepsilon)n_{\rm out}\), or at most \((3/2+\varepsilon)n_{\rm out}\) for indecomposable split basic multiplication tensors, for fixed \(\varepsilon>0\). These are separate polynomial-width variants. The linear side bounds above use the linear-width compiler.

## 3. Further input types

**Finite rings.** The existing additive jet construction records multiplication, augmentation, coproduct and the total-filtration projection on fixed-depth augmentation quotients. The group-like points over \(\mathbb F_p\) recover the original additive groups. Named scalar actions require \(R\)-linearity, and the pure tensor maps and marked tensor element recover each finitely presented tensor. The construction over any fixed finite commutative ring \(R\) of characteristic \(p^e\) has polynomial size and a fixed signature. Its mixed stage is bijective on transporters. Feeding those records directly to the prime-field guards proves the ring assertion, including nonfree modules and finite chain rings. For a fixed ring with several residue characteristics, primary decomposition gives a fixed conjunction of queries over the respective characteristics. [Fixed finite ring compiler, §§2–6](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/PrimePowerTensorIsomorphism/PROOF.md)

**Presented groups.** For fixed \(p,c,e\), the augmented group-algebra jet \(\mathbb F_p[G]/I^{cp^{e-1}+1}\) with the coproduct into its total-filtered tensor square recovers finite \(p\)-groups of class at most \(c\) and exponent dividing \(p^e\). Its group-like points over \(\mathbb F_p\) are exactly the input group, by the Jennings–Quillen filtration argument, and the points span the algebra. The marked linear or bilinear maps of the jet give bijections on group isomorphisms. The jet is computable from straight-line relators without expanding the multiplication table, using a truncated free associative algebra of fixed depth and linear elimination. Passing directly through the prime-field guards proves the asserted reductions. [Presented-group jets](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FourTensorAndGroupIsomorphismResults/GROUP-JETS.md)

**Relational structures in any characteristic.** Let \(X\) be a finite set. Record the coordinatewise multiplication of \(k^X\) in its basis \((e_x)_{x\in X}\). Its primitive idempotents are exactly those basis vectors, over every field. For a relation \(R\subseteq X^d\), include the incidence tensor
\[
\sum_{(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\in R}e_{x_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{x_d}.
\]
An invertible linear map preserving multiplication is an algebra isomorphism and hence permutes the idempotents. Preservation of the incidence tensors is precisely preservation of the relations. The geometric stabilizer is finite, so the orbit-dimension argument applies without Frobenius. In particular, graph adjacency and the ternary graph of group multiplication give record sizes of order at most three and one base space of dimension \(|X|\). The linear-width compiler gives both strict degeneration variants with linear side lengths and integral coordinates, in every characteristic. This is a claim about directional membership with controlled local geometry. [For comparison, graph-isomorphism hardness for orbit-closure intersection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04639)

## 4. Exact finite-field fibers through the linear-width compiler

For ordinary comparison, omit the strictness record or choose the same one on the two sides. At the mixed stage of the prime-field reduction, the transporter set over every finite extension \(L/\mathbb F_p\) is in bijection with the original transporter set over \(\mathbb F_{p^a}\). The field guard forces the matrices on all initial records to have prime-field entries, and the remaining maps are determined. The same statement applies to the fixed ring and group encodings, with their original transporters in place of the finite-field ones.

Use the Peirce and three-copy compiler in Sections 1–3 of the linear-width proof. Set \(Q=|L|\), let \(v\) be the number of vertices of the resulting algebra, \(j=\dim J\) for its strict off-diagonal ideal, and \(b\) the number of scalar arrows for the summand projections. Write \(L_u\) for the rank-label arrow widths. These are determined by the format. The exact factor for this compiler is
\[
F(Q)=Q^{3j}(Q-1)^{\,2v+b+2}
     \prod_u |\operatorname{GL}_{L_u}(L)|.
\]
Here is the accounting, including the steps that are not one-to-one.

- In the balanced step every source transporter has one lift through the forced product maps and dual evaluations. A relation frame map is likewise uniquely determined.
- In the three-copy bilinear system the two scalar inputs have independently chosen unit maps. Their copy equations force all other maps for a specified typed transporter. This contributes \((Q-1)^2\).
- Every algebra transporter fixes the quotient vertex labels, and the ordered actual vertex lifts are conjugate by \(1+J\). Since \(J\) consists only of off-diagonal corners, the conjugator in \(1+J\) is unique. This contributes \(Q^j\). With the vertices fixed, each of the \(b\) scalar arrows can be scaled freely, and the corresponding projection-quotient map is forced by that scale and the main corner map. The rank-label arrows contribute the independent factors \(|\operatorname{GL}_{L_u}(L)|\).
- Every transporter of the ordinary multiplication tensor is an algebra transporter together with two independent target units. In the isotopy equation \(H(xy)=P(x)Q_1(y)\), their values are \(P(1)\) and \(Q_1(1)\). Left and right multiplication by those elements are invertible, and dividing them out makes \(H\) an algebra map. The algebra unit group has \((Q-1)^vQ^j\) elements because the quotient is \(L^v\) with nilpotent ideal \(J\). This contributes its square.

The same algebra fiber calculation occurs in the presented-module counting proof. The new use here is its compatibility with arithmetic field descent and directional degeneration. All factors are computable in polynomial bit time from the explicit format and \(\log Q\). Thus one exact ordinary tensor count over any \(\mathbb F_{p^b}\), divided by \(F(p^b)\), gives the original arithmetic count. The result includes automorphism-group orders by comparing an object to itself. An affirmative strict pair has no exact transporter, as required by the rank difference, so the count statement uses matching auxiliary records.

## Scope

These are written reductions. The smooth comparison is at encoded points of reduced orbit closures. The finite-field complexity fixes the characteristic, and the ring and succinct-group variants fix their respective precision or structural bounds. No comparison between different characteristics for ordinary finite-field tensor isomorphism is used.
