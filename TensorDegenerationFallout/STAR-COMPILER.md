# Directional degeneration universality for mixed tensor records

## Statement

Fix finitely many named vector spaces and a finite list of ordered words of bounded length in their primal and dual occurrences. A tensor record consists of one tensor in each specified word. The group is the product of the general linear groups of the named spaces, with one shared map per name and the inverse transpose in each dual occurrence.

For each dimension format there is a single affine-linear, polynomial-size map \(\Phi\) from the space of records to ordinary three-tensors. Its construction is defined by integral constants. Over every algebraically closed field \(k\), for any records \(x,y\) of that format,
\[
y\in\overline{Gx}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Phi(y)\in\overline{(\operatorname{GL}\times\operatorname{GL}\times\operatorname{GL})\Phi(x)} .
\]
The closures are Zariski closures and the three mode maps on the right are independent. At the indicated points the two **reduced orbit closures are smoothly equivalent**. This means that a variety with a distinguished point admits smooth maps to neighborhoods of the two indicated points, sending that point to both of them. The same map preserves and reflects exact isomorphism over the input field, which need not be algebraically closed.

Linear maps between given word spaces are included by writing their coefficients in the corresponding mixed tensors. For fixed-dimensional relation subspaces with supplied bases, the analogous theorem uses the product of Grassmannians on the source. The construction is polynomial time on those presentations, and is affine-linear in the frame coefficients before taking the Grassmannian quotient. It preserves and reflects directional specialization and smooth equivalence at encoded points for each fixed dimension format. Changing the supplied basis of a relation changes the target within its ordinary tensor orbit.

The bounds treat the signature as fixed and charge the sum of named dimensions and the explicit coordinates. A zero-dimensional factor makes its tensor space zero. Such components and then unused names can be discarded. Empty words are allowed and have scalar coefficients. This is a written proof. The characteristic is arbitrary.

## 1. Pure records with independent named spaces

First take covariant words \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\) in positive-dimensional named spaces \(E_s\). Use a quiver with a hub \(h\) and one leaf for each name \(s\). The arrows from \(h\) to leaf \(s\) form a space with chosen basis \(E_s\), and the arrows back form a line with chosen generator. The hub-to-hub cycle through leaf \(s\) has path space \(E_s\). Add three further leaves, each with a pair of scalar arrows. Denote the resulting scalar cycles by \(A,B,C\). All references to a word in cycles mean the ordered product of its paths, with composition in traversal order.

Set \(D=\max(\{1\}\cup\{|w_i|:1\le i\le m\})\) and \(N=2(D+m+1)\). Retain only paths of length at most \(N\). Put \(t_i=A^iBA^{m-i}\) for \(0\le i\le m\), and let
\[
p_0=C^D t_0,\qquad
r_x=p_0+\sum_{i=1}^m x_i\, C^{D-|w_i|}t_i .
\]
The term containing \(x_i\) means the linear combination of paths in word \(w_i\) with coefficient coordinates \(x_i\). All terms have length \(N\) and endpoints \(h,h\). The channels for distinct \(i\) are disjoint, because their last \(m+1\) cycles have different tag words. The coefficient of \(p_0\) is one. Quotient by the relation \(r_x=0\).

To label the core vertices, for each core vertex \(u\) add a fresh source \(m_u\) and an arrow space of dimension \(L_u\) from \(m_u\) to \(u\). Choose pairwise distinct \(L_u\) greater than the number of all core paths of length at most \(N\) before quotienting. Impose the additional relations that any positive-length continuation of a marker arrow is zero. Marker sources have no incoming arrows. Denote the resulting finite-dimensional associative unital algebra by \(A(x)\). A basis is given by the old vertices, the marker vertices and arrows, and all positive core paths up to length \(N\) except \(p_0\). Indeed the two-sided ideal generated by \(r_x\) in the truncated core algebra is just its span, since its product with any positive path is zero on either side. Marker paths of length at least two are deleted independently. Multiplication in the chosen basis is affine-linear in \(x\), using \(r_x\) only to replace a product equal to \(p_0\). The unit is a fixed sum of vertex idempotents. The path count and multiplication table size are polynomial for fixed signature.

Let \(J\) be the two-sided ideal generated by all commutators. On \(A(x)\) this is the positive-path ideal. The quotient by that ideal is split commutative, and every arrow \(a\) with source \(u\) and target \(v\ne u\) equals \([e_u,a]\). All positive paths are products of arrows. This argument uses the ideal generated by commutators. The span of commutators can be smaller when cycles are present. The \(J^d\) are the parts spanned by paths of length at least \(d\), with \(J^{N+1}=0\). All their dimensions are constant in \(x\), as are all Peirce corner dimensions and the dimensions of the corners of \(J/J^2\). A Peirce corner is \(e_u A e_v\) for an ordered orthogonal idempotent system.

## 2. Recovering the orbit from the top-degree relation

An isomorphism of two encoded algebras sends \(J\) to \(J\). Modulo \(J\), it permutes the coordinate vertex idempotents. After that permutation, complete idempotent systems with the same residues are conjugate by a unit in \(1+J\). In detail, if \(f_u,e_u\) are the two systems in matching order, then \(z=\sum_u e_u f_u\) lies in \(1+J\) and satisfies \(zf_u=e_u z\). The corner of dimension \(L_u\) is unique, at \((m_u,u)\). Thus corner dimensions force both its endpoints and determine all vertex labels. The induced map on \(J/J^2\) is an independent invertible map on each arrow space.

An ordered path of length \(N\) can be multiplied using any lifts of its arrows from \(J/J^2\): changing one lift by an element of \(J^2\) changes the product by an element of \(J^{N+1}=0\). On the space of length-\(N\) core paths from \(h\) to \(h\), the multiplication map therefore has kernel exactly the line \(k r_x\). With \(h_s\) the outgoing map on \(E_s\), \(\rho_s\) the return scalar for \(s\), and \(a,b,c\) the full scalar maps of the cycles \(A,B,C\), the reference path scales by \(c^D a^m b\). Each \(t_i\) scales by \(a^m b\). After normalizing the reference coefficient, the map on a data channel is precisely the tensor word in the independent maps \(g_s=(\rho_s/c)h_s\). The original tuple is thus transported under the stated product of general linear groups. Conversely each tuple transporter extends by using \(g_s\) on its outgoing arrow, and fixing the scalar arrows, vertex idempotents, and markers. Extending it to paths sends the top line to the top line and gives an algebra isomorphism. This proves exact orbit equivalence over every field, including when data tensors vanish.

## 3. A smooth comparison of reduced orbit closures

Fix \(x\). Let \(Z\) be the reduced orbit closure of \(A(x)\) in the affine variety of associative multiplication laws with a specified unit on the fixed underlying vector space \(M\). The group \(\operatorname{GL}(M)\) transports both. The polynomial map \(x\mapsto A(x)\) sends \(\overline{Gx}\) into \(Z\), by the forward implication of Section 2.

Fix an encoded point \(A(y)\) in \(Z\). On a neighborhood in \(Z\) the powers of the commutator ideal are vector subbundles of constant ranks as follows. The commutator ideal is the image of the map \(r\otimes a\otimes b\otimes s\mapsto r[a,b]s\). Products of \(d\) copies give polynomial bundle maps with image \(J^d\). Larger minors vanish on the dense orbit and therefore on reduced \(Z\). For each \(d\), the union of the nonzero maximal-minor charts of the encoded rank is the intrinsic open where that rank is attained. Intersect these general-linear-stable opens for the finitely many \(d\). The identity \(J^{N+1}=0\) holds on all of \(Z\). The quotient by \(J\) is a finite locally free commutative algebra. Its trace pairing is invertible at \(A(y)\), since the quotient there is a product of copies of \(k\). Restrict to this open so that the quotient is finite étale. The scheme of ordered coordinate idempotents of this quotient is a finite étale cover of the resulting open.

Over that cover choose actual ordered complete orthogonal idempotents lifting the quotient ones. This choice is smooth over the cover. Indeed on an affine open the constant quotient system lifts to \(e_u\) by successive nilpotent lifting, working inside complementary corners. For an individual lift, the update \(e\mapsto e-(2e-1)^{-1}(e^2-e)\) squares the error when the error is nilpotent, including in characteristic two. Once the \(e_u\) are fixed, any other lift system \(f_u\) is conjugated to it by a unit \(z\in1+J\) as above. Multiplication of \(z\) on the left by diagonal units makes \(e_u z e_u=e_u\) for all \(u\). This normalized \(z\) is unique: two conjugators differ on the left by a block-diagonal unit, and their diagonal entries are both the identity. Conversely every element of \(1+\bigoplus_{u\ne v} e_u J e_v\) gives such a system. This identifies the lift choices locally with a vector bundle. The corner ranks are locally constant, so restrict to the open and closed rank conditions of the standard labelled system, including marker widths.

Now choose bases of the arrow corners of \(J/J^2\) and actual lifts in their \(J\) corners. These are successive general-linear frame bundles and affine bundles. The quotient \(J/J^2\) is locally free because the graded subbundles have constant ranks and the inclusion of \(J^2\) is an inclusion of subbundles. The resulting parameter space of laws and choices is smooth over the specified open of \(Z\), and consequently reduced. Retain the open chart \(S\) where the retained paths, evaluated on these lifts, form a basis of \(M\). It contains the canonical frame over \(A(y)\). Its inverse image of the dense orbit in \(Z\) is dense in \(S\): a smooth map is open, and any open part of \(S\) maps to an open meeting that orbit.

On \(S\), evaluate \(p_0\) and express it in the retained path basis. On the dense inverse image of the orbit, Section 2 shows that its coefficients are zero outside the designated data channels. That argument applies to every allowed frame because corner widths force the labels, and changes of lifts cannot affect products of length \(N\). These coefficient equations are regular, so they hold on the reduced chart. The negative of the data coefficients defines a regular recovered tuple \(x'\). It lies in \(\overline{Gx}\), because this closed condition holds on the same dense inverse image. The retained path basis defines a regular matrix \(g\in\operatorname{GL}(M)\).

These readouts identify \(S\) with \(\overline{Gx}\times \operatorname{GL}(M)\). One direction sends a framed algebra to \((x',g)\). In the other direction transport the canonical framed \(A(x')\) by \(g\). It lies in \(S\) by construction and specializes to the chosen frame at \((y,I)\). The two maps are inverse on the dense orbit with frames, by evaluating all path products and the top relation. Equivalently on the whole chart, products concatenate, incompatible endpoints multiply to zero, length greater than \(N\) vanishes, marker continuations vanish by density, and the one removed path is recovered by its readout. Thus all multiplication coordinates are those of \(A(x')\) in the retained path basis. The lifted arrows, idempotents, and quotient labels are also recovered, since they are among that basis and its residues. The regular inverses give the product chart as reduced varieties. The smooth map \(S\to Z\) and the projection to \(\overline{Gx}\) give the smooth equivalence at the chosen points. This also proves reflection of directional specialization: applying the same recovery to the canonical frame of \(A(y)\in Z\) gives \(y\in\overline{Gx}\).

## 4. From unital algebras to ordinary three-tensors

Take the bare multiplication tensor of \(A(x)\), viewing multiplication as a bilinear map \(M\times M\to M\). Independent general linear changes act on the two inputs and the output. This is the ordinary three-mode action after using the dual spaces for the inputs.

Here is the local comparison, which also proves reflection at the orbit-closure level. Let \(e\) be the common unit vector at the encoded laws, fix \(\ell\in M^*\) with \(\ell(e)=1\), and for a bilinear map \(m\) put \(R_e(x)=m(x,e)\), \(L_e(y)=m(e,y)\), and \(u=m(e,e)\). Take the open where \(R_e,L_e\) are invertible and \(\ell(u)\ne0\). Set \(h=I+(u-e)\ell\), so \(h(e)=u\) and \(h\) is invertible. Then
\[
\nu(x,y)=h^{-1}m(R_e^{-1}h x,L_e^{-1}h y),\qquad
s=h^{-1}R_e,\qquad t=h^{-1}L_e
\]
give a regular product chart. The law \(\nu\) has unit \(e\), the linear maps \(s,t\) fix \(e\), and the inverse is \(m(x,y)=h\nu(sx,ty)\). The auxiliary factors are the open \(\ell(u)\ne0\) in \(M\) and two copies of the general-linear stabilizer of \(e\); they are smooth.

If \(m=P\mu(Q^{-1}-,S^{-1}-)\) for a unital associative \(\mu\), invertibility of \(R_e,L_e\) forces \(b=S^{-1}e\) and \(a=Q^{-1}e\) to be units in \(\mu\). Indeed the respective right and left multiplications are bijective, and multiplication on the opposite side of a one-sided inverse verifies a two-sided inverse by injectivity. The law obtained before the change by \(h\) is \(P((P^{-1}x)b^{-1}a^{-1}(P^{-1}y))\), isomorphic to \(\mu\) via \(z\mapsto P(abz)\). Thus the ordinary tensor orbit cuts out exactly the product of the fixed-unit algebra orbit with the auxiliary factors in this chart. Taking reduced closures in the chart preserves this product. For the marked-unit algebra orbit there is likewise a product chart over \(\ell(u)\ne0\), transporting the unit by the same \(h\). Its fixed-unit factor is the same one. This gives ordinary-tensor directional equivalence and smooth equivalence at encoded points. The formula works over the input field and proves the exact isomorphism reflection too. This recovers the directional result for unital algebras of Bläser–Lysikov and gives the local form required here.

## 5. Shared primal and dual actions, maps, and relations

Replace each dual named space by an independently acted coordinate space. For every name add a fixed coevaluation tensor in its primal space tensored with this new space. The stabilizer of the coevaluation has exactly the action \((g,g^{-T})\). The orbit of the coevaluation under the two independent groups is the open set of invertible pairings. There is a regular section of the orbit map on this open: act by the matrix of the pairing in one factor and by the identity in the other. Apply this simultaneously to all added pairings. Over this open the marked tuple orbit is a product of the pairing open and the desired mixed orbit by that section, so its closure on the open is the corresponding product of the mixed closure. This proves the exact, directional and smooth-equivalence claims for signed records. The extra tensors are constant and available in every characteristic.

Express a linear map by its tensor in the output word tensored with the dual of the input word, with a fixed reordering of slots. For a relation of fixed rank \(r\), include the tensor of a frame \(k^r\hookrightarrow W\) and let an additional independent factor \(\operatorname{GL}_r\) change its basis. Rank zero contributes no frame. The full-rank frame space maps smoothly onto the Grassmannian with fibers the invertible frame changes and standard local sections. The preimage of an orbit of the subspace data (together with the other tensor data) is exactly the joint orbit of a frame under the additional factors. On a local trivialization, the closure within the full-rank locus is therefore the product of the Grassmannian orbit closure with the frame group. Since the full-rank locus is open, closure there is the intersection with the ambient framed closure. This proves the claimed directional and smooth-equivalence conclusions for relation structures.

## Scope and attribution

This concerns directional containment at encoded points. It uses reduced orbit closures and smooth equivalence at those points. It makes no assertion that the entire boundary of the target closure consists of encodings. Unrestricted affine orbit-closure intersection under the three independent general linear groups is a different relation, since every ordinary-tensor orbit closure contains zero. The size proof uses a fixed signature, unlike the broader dense-input decision result.
