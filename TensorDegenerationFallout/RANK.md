# Directional tensor degeneration at near-minimal rank

**Theorem.** Fix a finite mixed tensor signature of bounded arity, with the same meaning of directional specialization as in the companion directional universality proof. Fix a rational $\varepsilon>0$. The basis-explicit problem admits an affine-linear, polynomial-size reduction to concise ordinary $n\times n\times n$ tensors of ordinary rank at most $(1+\varepsilon)n$, over every algebraically closed field. A second reduction has rank at most $(3/2+\varepsilon)n$ and uses multiplication tensors of indecomposable split basic associative unital algebras. Both preserve and reflect directional orbit-closure membership, and identify the reduced orbit-closure singularities up to smooth equivalence at the encoded points. Both preserve and reflect exact ordinary tensor isomorphism over the input field. Their side lengths are at most $O_\sigma(1+\varepsilon^{-1})$ times the dimension of the companion path algebra. Explicit decompositions certify each asserted rank upper bound.

For any fixed $0<\alpha<1$, the two reductions can instead achieve $n+n^\alpha$ and $3n/2+n^\alpha$, respectively, with polynomial side length. The coefficient ratios 1 and $3/2$ are asymptotically optimal for concise tensors and for the stated indecomposable algebra class, respectively. The source may include maps and fixed-rank relation subspaces as in the companion proof, with affine-linearity in chosen frames for the relations.

## 1. A linear rank bound for the path compiler

In the companion mixed-record compiler, first replace dual occurrences by separately named spaces and fix their actions by adding the standard coevaluation records. Functions are coefficient tensors. Fixed-rank relations use frames as in that compiler. The resulting pure records are encoded by a unital associative algebra $A(x)$. Its vertices and arrows form a quiver with a hub, core leaves and rank-label arrow spaces. Core paths longer than a constant $D$, depending only on the signature, are killed. A single relation among top-length core paths has a distinguished basis path $p_0$ with coefficient 1, so $p_0$ is removed from the multiplication basis. The other terms in that relation carry the input data. Rank-label arrows have no permitted positive-length continuation. Write $m=\dim A(x)$, constant over the source format.

There are at most $m+1$ legal basis paths before deleting $p_0$, including length-zero paths. For any legal labelled path of length $j$, at most $j+1\le D+1$ ordered pairs of basis inputs concatenate to that path. Nonzero basis products occur only among these pairs. When the concatenation is $p_0$, the product is the prescribed linear combination of retained paths. For each pair $a,b$, the term $a^*\otimes b^*\otimes (ab)$ is still a single pure term or zero. Therefore the multiplication tensor $T(A(x))$ satisfies
$
R(T(A(x)))\le (D+1)(m+1)\le C_\sigma m
$
with $C_\sigma=2(D+1)$. This decomposition can be generated in polynomial time. The possibly many coefficients of the output vector $ab$ do not multiply its contribution to the rank bound.

The multiplication tensor of an $m$-dimensional unital algebra is concise. A vector acting as zero in either input would act as zero on the unit, which forces that vector to vanish. Surjectivity to the output follows because multiplication by the unit fixes every vector.

## 2. Scalar padding with exact directional cancellation

For an arbitrary unital associative algebra $A$, put $A^{[s]}=A\times k^s$, where $s$ is a fixed nonnegative integer. For equal-dimensional $A,B$, exact algebra isomorphism and directional algebra degeneration are preserved and reflected by this operation. The reduced orbit closures at the encoded points are smoothly equivalent. Here the variety of laws includes the chosen unit, and the general linear group transports the law and unit together.

For exact isomorphism, every rank-one central idempotent splits off one scalar algebra. In a direct product of finite-dimensional unital algebras an idempotent of this kind is supported in exactly one factor, since its image ideal is one-dimensional. Removing an ordered list of such pairwise orthogonal idempotents is independent up to isomorphism of the choices: the one-dimensional central primitive factors, wherever they occur, are all copies of $k$. Thus an isomorphism of $A\times k^s$ with $B\times k^s$ yields $A\cong B$.

For directional reflection and smooth equivalence, let $Y$ be the reduced orbit closure of $A\times k^s$ and assume $B\times k^s\in Y$. At this point mark the $s$ scalar idempotents. In a family of algebras, an idempotent satisfies $e^2-e=0$. The relative derivative in direction $v$ is $ev+ve-v$. For central $e$ this acts as $+1$ on $eA$ and $-1$ on $(1-e)A$, and is invertible in every characteristic. The idempotent scheme is therefore étale in a neighborhood of such a point by the relative Jacobian criterion. Centrality persists on an open neighborhood in this chart: the two off-diagonal Peirce summands are images of the commuting idempotent projections $L_e(1-R_e)$ and $(1-L_e)R_e$, and their ranks are locally constant and zero at this point. The same argument shows the diagonal summand $eA$ stays of rank one. At each step use the entire intrinsic rank-one central locus and repeat in the complementary algebra to mark an ordered system of orthogonal scalar idempotents, then choose a basis of the final complement. The result is smooth over a neighborhood in $Y$.

The complement has dimension $m=\dim A$, and reading its law in this frame is regular. On the dense inverse image of the orbit in $Y$, it is isomorphic to $A$ by scalar cancellation. The inverse image is dense because the frame chart is open over $Y$. Thus the recovered complement everywhere in the reduced chart lies in the reduced orbit closure $X$ of $A$, in particular giving $B\in X$. Conversely the affine map $C\mapsto C\times k^s$ carries $X$ into $Y$ by continuity and equivariance with respect to block diagonal maps. Taking a basis of the complement together with the ordered scalar idempotents gives a basis of the whole algebra. In this frame the whole law, unit and all marked idempotents are reconstructed by transporting the canonical framed product of the recovered law with $k^s$. The reconstruction and readout give mutually inverse regular maps between the framed splitting chart and $X\times\operatorname{GL}_{m+s}$. This supplies the common smooth neighborhood and proves the claimed comparison.

Apply scalar padding to $A(x)$ and take its bare multiplication tensor. The companion unital isotope chart identifies directional ordinary tensor degeneration and its local singularity type at unital points with the corresponding algebra comparison, up to smooth factors. The orbit-containment statement is also the unital specialization theorem of Bläser and Lysikov. Exact tensor isotopy of these algebras gives algebra isomorphism over the input field. [Bläser–Lysikov, Theorem 3.2](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.19)

The padded tensor has side $n=m+s$ and explicit rank at most $C_\sigma m+s$, since the new factors contribute one coordinate product each. Choosing $s=\lceil (C_\sigma-1)m/\varepsilon\rceil$ gives $R\le(1+\varepsilon)n$. Every concise cubic-format tensor has rank at least $n$ by flattening. Thus the ratio approaches the smallest possible coefficient.

## 3. A connected variant

Instead of taking a direct product, attach $s$ new source vertices $v_i$ to the distinguished hub $h$ in the original path construction. Give each new vertex exactly one arrow $a_i:v_i\to h$, and impose $a_i b=0$ for every positive-length continuation $b$. There are no arrows into the new vertices. Keep the old labels and the top path relation exactly as before. The new algebra $A_s^\star(x)$ has dimension $n=m+2s$, and the only added nonzero basis products are
$
e_{v_i}e_{v_i}=e_{v_i},\qquad e_{v_i}a_i=a_i,\qquad a_i e_h=a_i.
$
Thus $R(T(A_s^\star(x)))\le C_\sigma m+3s$. The new law is affine-linear in the same original coefficients.

The previous directional and smooth comparison via frames applies to this augmented quiver. Here are the changes needed in its proof. The commutator ideal is still the positive-path ideal since every arrow joins distinct vertices. The ranks of its powers and of the Peirce corners of its first quotient are constant over the source family. The original distinct large rank-label arrows still identify every original vertex by their widths and endpoints. None of the new width-one arrows can be confused with one of those labels. Once the original vertices have been recovered, any permutation of the new vertices is harmless, and no new arrow belongs to a top-length path used by the data relation. The orbit comparison thus still reads precisely the original top relation on the named core and reflects exact isomorphism.

In the local variety of framed laws, allow all ordered lifts of the quotient idempotents in the specified rank pattern. Ordering the new vertices is an auxiliary finite étale choice, with their order fixed in the reference presentation. Frame each new one-dimensional arrow corner as usual. On the dense orbit the new corners have exactly the listed products, and the vanishing of all positive continuations persists on the reduced chart. Read the original top relation in the framed core basis. The recovered tuple and the matrix of the full retained path basis still reconstruct the law and its frame uniquely. The new idempotents and arrows are simply additional entries of that basis, their product table being prescribed. Hence the framed product chart and the unchanged unital isotope chart give directional reflection and smooth equivalence with the same source. All these arguments use arbitrary characteristic.

The augmented algebra is indecomposable and split basic. Its radical is the nilpotent positive-path ideal, with quotient a product of $k$'s indexed by the vertices. The graph of its arrows is connected. A central idempotent reduces to a zero-or-one value at each vertex. Commuting with any nonzero arrow modulo the square of the radical forces equal values at its endpoints. Connectedness makes all values equal. An idempotent in the nilpotent radical is zero, so the only central idempotents are 0 and 1. This proves indecomposability. The multiplication tensor is also indecomposable under ordinary tensor direct sum. Indeed, if such a multiplication is isomorphic to a direct sum tensor, transport the six input and output subspaces back along those invertible linear maps so mixed block products vanish. Writing the unit in the second-mode block decomposition shows each first-mode block lies in the corresponding output block. Writing it in the first-mode block decomposition gives the analogous statement for the second-mode blocks. The respective decompositions all span the algebra, so inclusions are equalities. This would make the blocks nonzero two-sided ideals forming an algebra direct product.

Choosing $s=\lceil C_\sigma m/\varepsilon\rceil$ gives $R\le(3/2+\varepsilon)n$. To see sharpness of the leading coefficient in this algebra class, let an arbitrary indecomposable split basic unital algebra of dimension $n$ have radical $J$ and let $t=\dim(A/J)$. Its Gabriel graph is connected. Otherwise components give central idempotents, since products of lifts of $J/J^2$ generate the higher radical layers and cannot cross different components. Thus at least $t-1$ arrow spaces are nonzero, so $n\ge t+\dim(J/J^2)\ge2t-1$. There are exactly $t$ maximal two-sided ideals since they contain $J$ and $A/J\cong k^t$. The Alder–Strassen lower bound gives
$
R(T(A))\ge2n-t\ge(3n-1)/2.
$
This is a lower bound on ordinary rank in this algebra class. [Alder–Strassen, On the algorithmic complexity of associative algebras](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(81)90070-0)

For either construction, to obtain the asserted $n^\alpha$ surplus use $s=(C_\sigma m)^b$ for a fixed integer $b\ge1/\alpha$. The base dimension and the padding dimension are polynomial in the basis-explicit input size. The same decomposition yields the same upper bound for border rank. The sharpness assertion at coefficient $3/2$ concerns ordinary rank.

## 4. An example of singularities under the rank bounds

For example, the two parallel-arrow Kronecker quiver gives a fixed mixed signature of two maps between the same two named spaces. Zwara exhibited a representation whose orbit closure has a point smoothly equivalent to the isolated intersection of two planes. Each compiler here carries that singularity type to an ordinary three-tensor orbit closure at an encoded point, with the stated rank guarantees at both encoded endpoints. In particular, the connected variant gives non-unibranch orbit closures within the stated exact rank bounds and among indecomposable multiplication tensors. This uses the cited singularity rather than asserting a classification of possible singularities. [Zwara, An orbit closure for a representation of the Kronecker quiver with bad singularities](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/87496)

The results are written proofs. The rank certificates are constructive decompositions. The statements concern orbit closure membership at encoded points. An exact minimum border rank statement would require additional structure, since a unital tensor of minimum border rank is the multiplication tensor of a smoothable algebra by Bläser–Lysikov, Corollary 3.3. The constructions above allow noncommutative algebras.
