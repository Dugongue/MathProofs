# Combining the reductions

## Strict arithmetic degenerations near minimum rank

Apply the arithmetic marker construction and append the directional comparison \(E_{11}\rightsquigarrow 0\) of independent \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrices. Feed these mixed records into the near-minimum-rank compiler. For each fixed prime \(p\), this reduces tensor isomorphism over explicitly presented fields \(\mathbb F_{p^a}\) to strict ordinary tensor degeneration over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\). The target pair has polynomial side length, is concise, and has ordinary rank at most \((1+\varepsilon)N\) for every fixed rational \(\varepsilon>0\). Every affirmative target germ is smoothly equivalent to the rank-at-most-one \(2\)-by-\(2\) determinantal cone at zero. The same holds with connected indecomposable split basic multiplication tensors and rank upper bound \((3/2+\varepsilon)N\). Strictness follows from exact orbit reflection and the different ranks in the appended matrix slot. The ring and group specializations of the arithmetic proof carry through this composition with their original input and characteristic restrictions.

## Exponential branching at near-minimum rank

For the explicit \(29s\)-dimensional algebras in the singularity theorem, the two multiplication tensors have at most \(74s\) and \(72s\) nonzero coordinates, respectively. Each coordinate is a rank-one term. Pad the two algebras by \(k^r\) as in the rank proof, with \(r=\lceil45s/\varepsilon\rceil\). The rank bound is \(74s+r\le(1+\varepsilon)(29s+r)\), and the target side is \(N=29s+r=O_\varepsilon(s)\). The smooth scalar cancellation keeps all \(2^s\) branches, the multiplicity \(2^s\), and the Cohen–Macaulay defect \(s\). The resulting arrays still have binary entries and only \(O_\varepsilon(s)\) nonzero coordinates, over every algebraically closed field.

A binary connected variant can be built directly from the \(29\)-dimensional blocks. Add one source hub \(h\), with a width-one arrow to the sink \(2\) in each block and to each of five new dummy sinks. Every positive continuation of a new arrow is zero. There are no other new products. Its side length is \(30s+11\) and its rank is at most \(76s+16\), since each new arrow contributes two identity actions and each new vertex contributes one diagonal product. Attach \(L\) additional source vertices with one arrow each into \(h\), again killing all positive continuations. The side increases by \(2L\) and the rank upper bound by \(3L\).

The hub is uniquely determined by its outgoing Peirce degree \(s+5>4\), even after padding. Removing it leaves the \(s\) six-vertex components and isolated vertices. The orientation of the arrows incident to the hub distinguishes dummy sinks from padding sources. Within each six-vertex component the same Peirce pattern as in the singularity proof orders its vertices. In the rank-open and finite étale frame chart of that proof, order the \(s\) components and frame each new arrow line. All new products are determined by the labelled endpoints and the zero continuation equations. Those equations hold throughout the reduced chart by density. On the dense orbit, reading the \(s\) pencils gives \(s\) independent points of the same source pencil orbit, even if the components were permuted. The recovered tuple of pencils and the full basis matrix have a regular inverse given by transporting the canonical framed algebra, now with all hub and padding arrows. Conversely every point of the product of the \(s\) source pencil closures gives a degeneration by the polynomial table map. Thus the same smooth comparison proves the \(s\)-fold product germ for this connected construction. The ordinary tensor comparison applies because these are unital associative algebras.

The arrow graph is connected, so this is again an indecomposable split basic algebra and an indecomposable multiplication tensor. Taking \(L=\lceil(76s+16)/(2\varepsilon)\rceil\) makes its rank at most \((3/2+\varepsilon)N\), with \(N=30s+11+2L=O_\varepsilon(s)\). It retains the \(2^s\) branches, multiplicity \(2^s\), and Cohen–Macaulay defect \(s\). Both tensors are binary and sparse over every algebraically closed field.
