# Exponentially many branches in sparse ordinary tensor orbit closures

For every integer \(s\ge 1\) there are explicit \(0/1\) tensors \(T_s,U_s\) of format \(29s\times29s\times29s\), each with \(O(s)\) nonzero entries, with the following properties over every algebraically closed field. The tensor \(U_s\) lies in the closure of the ordinary three-mode orbit of \(T_s\). At \(U_s\), the reduced orbit closure is smoothly equivalent to the \(s\)-fold product of two affine planes meeting at their origins. It has exactly \(2^s\) geometric branches, Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity \(2^s\), and Cohen–Macaulay defect \(s\). In particular it is neither normal nor unibranch there. Both tensors have invertible unit contractions in either input mode.

The construction combines a small labelled path algebra for an ordered matrix pencil, an amplification argument using central idempotents, and the local smooth comparison for a unital associative multiplication tensor. The seed singularity is Zwara’s theorem. [Zwara, Theorem 1](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/87496) · [Bläser–Lysikov on unital multiplication tensor degenerations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04253)

## 1. A \(29\)-dimensional algebra for an ordered pencil

Write \(X=(X_a,X_b)\), where \(X_i:U\to V\) and \(U,V\) are both three-dimensional. Use vertices \(0,1,a,b,2,w\). The arrows are the two space-valued arrows \(U:0\to1\), \(V:0\to2\), and the scalar arrows
\[
r:w\to1,\quad t:w\to2,\quad
p_i:1\to i,\quad q_i:i\to2\quad(i=a,b),\quad \eta:a\to b .
\]
Use traversal order for path products. Every path containing \(\eta\) and at least one other arrow is zero. For the other paths impose
\[
u p_iq_i=X_i(u)\text{ in }V,\qquad rp_iq_i=t,\qquad i=a,b .
\]
These are all the relations. The terms of each relation have endpoints at source \(0\) or \(w\) and sink \(2\), and cannot be extended by positive paths. Thus replacing each length-three path causes no further relation among shorter paths. The algebra \(A(X)\) has the basis of six vertices, thirteen arrows, and ten surviving length-two paths. The latter are \(u_jp_i\), \(rp_i\), and \(p_iq_i\) for \(i=a,b\), where \(u_j\) runs through the chosen basis of \(U\). There are no retained paths of length three. This constructs \(A(X)\) as a quotient of a path algebra, proving associativity and the unit. All constants are \(0,1\) or entries of \(X_i\).

Let \(J\) be the two-sided ideal generated by commutators. The positive-path span is an ideal with commutative quotient \(k^6\). Every arrow has unequal endpoints and is its commutator with its source idempotent. Thus \(J\) is exactly the positive-path ideal and \(J^4=0\). Its dimension is constantly \(23\), regardless of the ranks of \(X_a,X_b\). The off-diagonal Peirce support has row outdegrees \(4,4,3,2,1,0\), respectively at \(0,w,1,a,b,2\). The nonzero widths in the row at \(0\) are all three and those in the row at \(w\) are all one. Hence its labelled width pattern has no permutation symmetries. The marker \(\eta\) is the arrow distinguishing \(a\) and \(b\).

An algebra isomorphism between two such algebras preserves \(J\) and permutes the primitive idempotents modulo \(J\). Two complete lists of orthogonal idempotents with matching residues are conjugate by a unit of \(1+J\): the unit \(\sum_i e_if_i\) intertwines the ordered lists \(f_i,e_i\). The Peirce pattern thus fixes every vertex. On the arrow spaces \(U,V\) the vertex-normalized isomorphism acts by \(P,H\). Let \(\rho_r,\rho_t,\alpha_i,\beta_i\) denote the scalars on \(r,t,p_i,q_i\). The relation \(rp_iq_i=t\) gives \(\rho_r\alpha_i\beta_i=\rho_t\), so \(\alpha_i\beta_i\) is independent of \(i\). The relations in \(V\) give
\[
H X_i=(\rho_t/\rho_r) X_i' P .
\]
After rescaling \(H\) by the common scalar, this is an isomorphism of the ordered pencils. Conversely any such pencil isomorphism extends with all scalar arrows fixed. This calculation also identifies the recovered pencil when arbitrary Peirce bases are used and normalized by the relations \(rp_iq_i=t\).

## 2. Smooth local recovery of the pencil

Consider reduced orbit closures in the space of associative laws with their unit marked. Let \(Z\) be the closure of the orbit of \(A(X)\), and suppose \(A(Y)\in Z\). On the rank-\(23\) open containing this point, the universal ideal \(J\) is a subbundle. Indeed it is the image of the bundle map \((b,x,y,c)\mapsto b[x,y]c\), written as a linear map on the fourth tensor power. Higher rank minors vanish on the dense orbit, hence on reduced \(Z\). Inverting any full-rank pivot gives an image direct summand; take the union of the pivot opens. The polynomial identity \(J^4=0\) holds on \(Z\). The commutative locally free quotient has nondegenerate trace pairing near \(A(Y)\), where it is \(k^6\), so it is finite étale there. Pass to the étale cover of its ordered coordinate idempotents. [Trace discriminant and finite étaleness](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BVH)

Lift the labelled idempotents to the universal law. This is a smooth choice. Locally one lift \(e_i\) exists by successive nilpotent lifting. For any other lift \(f_i\), normalize the conjugator \(\sum_i e_if_i\) by left multiplication by diagonal units so that all its diagonal blocks equal \(e_i\). The normalized conjugator is unique since two conjugators differ by a block-diagonal unit. The free off-diagonal entries of this element of \(1+J\) form a vector bundle. Restrict to the locally constant Peirce widths of \(A(Y)\).

Choose frames for \(U,V\) in their full Peirce corners and generators for \(r,t,p_a,p_b,\eta\). These frame choices are smooth. On the open where \(q\mapsto rp_iq\) is an isomorphism of the one-dimensional \(q_i\) and \(t\) corners, choose the unique \(q_i\) making \(rp_iq_i=t\). This open contains the canonical frame of \(A(Y)\). The retained products \(up_i,rp_i,p_iq_i\), together with the chosen arrows and vertices, form a basis on another open containing that frame. Use this basis to read out the matrices \(X_i'\) from \(up_iq_i\).

The inverse image of the dense orbit is dense on the framed chart because the frame map is smooth and open. On that inverse image the readout lies in the pencil orbit of \(X\), by the vertex and scalar normalization calculation. On the reduced chart the readout therefore lands in the reduced pencil closure. The relations killing the marker products also hold everywhere on the chart, by the same density argument applied to coordinate functions. All remaining path products are those of \(A(X')\), since the displayed algebra relations hold and the retained basis is a basis. The basis-change matrix and \(X'\) recover the framed law, with regular inverse obtained by transporting the canonical frame. Thus near the canonical frame the chart is an open of the pencil closure times \(\operatorname{GL}_{29}\). Its smooth projection to \(Z\) proves smooth equivalence and also reflects the directional comparison. Preservation follows directly from the polynomial construction and extension of isomorphisms.

## 3. Independent amplification at linear total side length

The center of \(A(X)\) is \(k\), including for zero pencils. Commuting with vertex idempotents forces a central element into the sum of the diagonal corners. The underlying graph is acyclic, so each diagonal corner is \(ke_i\). Commuting with each arrow forces its endpoint coefficients to agree. The graph is connected.

The following amplification works for any two equal-dimensional unital algebras \(A,A'\) with center \(k\), with \(A'\) in the reduced orbit closure \(Z\) of \(A\). Put \(B=A^{\times s}\), \(B'=(A')^{\times s}\). Direct sum of laws shows \(B'\in\overline{\mathcal O_B}\), because the product of \(s\) copies of \(\mathcal O_A\) is dense in \(Z^s\) and its direct sums lie in \(\mathcal O_B\). In the reduced orbit closure of \(B\), restrict to the open where the universal center has dimension \(s\) and nondegenerate trace pairing. It contains \(B'\), whose center is \(k^s\). To obtain the center subbundle, use the matrix of commutation with all basis elements and the constant-rank argument. This open has finite étale center, so pass to its étale cover of ordered coordinate central idempotents. Their images on the universal algebra give orthogonal algebra blocks. Near \(B'\) each block has dimension \(\dim A\) by the rank of its idempotent projection. Choose a basis of each block, a smooth frame choice.

Read out the \(s\) block laws on this reduced framed chart. They are each isomorphic to \(A\) on the dense preimage of the original orbit, because each is one of its central factors. By density they belong to \(Z\) everywhere. Conversely \(s\) laws from the open \(Z^\circ\subset Z\) with centers of dimension one give a direct-sum law with the chosen central ordering. The assembled basis-change matrix identifies the framed chart with an open of \((Z^\circ)^s\times\operatorname{GL}_{s\dim A}\), with regular inverse obtained by transporting the canonical ordered frame. Hence the pointed germ at \(B'\) is smoothly equivalent to the \(s\)-fold product of \((Z,A')\). This proof uses central idempotents to retain independent blocks. Direct summation of an unmarked pencil alone does not supply this argument.

To pass to the ordinary three-mode multiplication tensor, fix the unit \(e\) at the endpoint and let \(L_e,R_e\) be the two slice maps of a nearby bilinear law \(m\). In the open where they are invertible, set \(u=m(e,e)\) and normalize
\[
\nu_0(x,y)=m(R_e^{-1}x,L_e^{-1}y).
\]
This law has unit \(u\). Where a fixed functional \(\ell\) satisfies \(\ell(e)=1\), \(\ell(u)\ne0\), the matrix \(h_u=I+(u-e)\ell\) sends \(e\) to \(u\). The normalized fixed-unit law \(h_u^{-1}\cdot\nu_0\), the vector \(u\), and the operators \(h_u^{-1}R_e,h_u^{-1}L_e\) fixing \(e\) give a product chart. Its inverse is \(m(x,y)=h_u\,\nu(h_u^{-1}R_e x,h_u^{-1}L_e y)\), with \(\nu=h_u^{-1}\cdot\nu_0\). On the tensor orbit of an associative unital algebra, the normalized law is isomorphic to that algebra. In its multiplication coordinates the normalized law before taking \(h_u^{-1}\) is a principal isotope \(xcy\) with \(c\) invertible, obtained by the two invertible slice maps. It is isomorphic to the original law by left multiplication by \(c^{-1}\). Conversely reconstructing with any of the auxiliary factors gives a point in the original tensor orbit. The tensor closure on this chart is consequently the fixed-unit algebra closure times the smooth factors. The varying marked unit in the algebra-law variety is itself a smooth factor using the same \(h_u\). This proves the needed local smooth comparison for the bare tensor closure.

## 4. Explicit seeds and exact invariants

Let \(E_{ij}\) denote the elementary three-by-three matrix with a one in row \(i\), column \(j\). Take
\[
X_a=Y_a=E_{21}+E_{32},\qquad X_b=E_{11}+E_{33},\qquad Y_b=E_{32}.
\]
These are Zwara’s matrices in Theorem 1, with \(\lambda_1=0,\lambda_2=1\). Thus \(Y\) is in the ordered pencil orbit closure of \(X\), and its pointed smooth type is the reduced crossing
\[
C=\operatorname{Spec} k[a,b,c,d]/(ac,ad,bc,bd)
\]
at the origin. Set \(T_s\) and \(U_s\) to the multiplication tensors of \(A(X)^{\times s}\) and \(A(Y)^{\times s}\) in their retained bases. Sections 1–3 prove the tensor format, coefficient bounds, directional membership and smooth type \(C^s\). All multiplication coefficients are \(0/1\), so the same arrays work in every characteristic. The multiplication units also establish the invertible slice assertion.

The completed local ring of \(C^s\) at the origin is a power-series ring in \(4s\) variables modulo \(a_ic_i,a_id_i,b_ic_i,b_id_i\) for \(1\le i\le s\). Its minimal primes are obtained by independently choosing \((a_i,b_i)\) or \((c_i,d_i)\) in each block. This gives \(2^s\) branches, all smooth of dimension \(2s\) before adjoining smooth variables. The minimal-prime description also follows by intersecting these ideals monomial by monomial. The single factor has graded Hilbert series
\[
\frac{2}{(1-z)^2}-1=\frac{1+2z-z^2}{(1-z)^2}.
\]
Multiplying the series gives Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity \(2^s\) at the origin. The depth of the single crossing is one by the depth lemma applied to the exact sequence given by its two planes and their origin. Its minimal free resolution over the four-variable regular local ring has length three by Auslander–Buchsbaum. Tensor the corresponding minimal graded resolutions over \(k\) in disjoint variables and complete. The differentials still have entries in the maximal ideal, so the resulting minimal resolution has length \(3s\). The depth of \(C^s\) at the origin is therefore \(s\) and its dimension is \(2s\). Smooth comparison preserves multiplicity, geometric branch count and dimension minus depth: at rational points a smooth map yields a power-series extension on completed local rings. The geometric branch calculation agrees with that of the completion for these excellent rings. [Branches and completion](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C27) · [Smooth invariance of geometric branches](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0DQ1)

The single crossing is already present in an ordinary \(3\times3\times2\) pencil tensor. In the source \(X\), split input as a \(1\)-space plus a \(2\)-space and output as a \(2\)-space plus a \(1\)-space. The two slices then give a basis pair of vectors and a basis pair of covectors, respectively. Any invertible mixing of the two slices can be absorbed by changes of these two bases. Thus the extra \(\operatorname{GL}_2\) action does not change that particular source orbit. The role of the marked construction here is the exact independent amplification with linear side length.

## Scope for other singularity transfers

Any orbit closure of representations of a fixed quiver embeds via one mixed linear-map slot per arrow, with the vertex changes shared. Polynomial equations from fixed relations are closed and invariant, so the closure of a representation satisfying them agrees with its closure inside that representation subvariety. Consequently the mixed compiler transfers every pointed singularity established for such a representation orbit closure, including for fixed bound quivers. It transfers questions about local normality, the Cohen–Macaulay property and branch count on promised containment instances with the same encoded points. A particular complexity lower bound requires a lower bound for the corresponding source promise problem or a separate reduction. Universality of quiver Grassmannians is about subrepresentation parameter spaces and implies no unrestricted realization claim for orbit closures here.

This is an informal proof. The multiplicity and branch counts concern a designated tensor in the reduced closure of a specified source orbit.
