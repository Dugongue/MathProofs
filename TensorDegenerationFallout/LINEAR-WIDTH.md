# Linear-width compilation of mixed tensor degeneration

## Theorem

Fix a basis-explicit signature \(\sigma\) of ordered tensor records on named vector spaces, with repeated primal and dual slots. Write \(n\ge 1\) for the largest named dimension, and \(d\ge 1\) for the largest number of slots in one record. There is one affine-linear map, defined by integer coordinates for each dimension format, into ordinary three-tensors. The target is the multiplication tensor of a split basic unital associative algebra with radical cube zero over fields. All three sides have length
\[
O_\sigma(n^{\lceil d/3\rceil}).
\]
Over any algebraically closed field it preserves and reflects directional orbit-closure containment, and the reduced source and target orbit closures are smoothly equivalent at corresponding encoded points. It also preserves and reflects exact orbit equality over the input field. For order at most three the side bound is linear in the sum of named dimensions, at fixed signature. The size bound applies to a function by counting its input and output slots, and to a relation subspace by counting its basis-frame sort as an extra slot and including its dimension in the maximum \(n\). The subspace version interprets source closure in the fixed-rank Grassmannian.

This improves the size bound of the earlier truncated-star degeneration compiler and strengthens the linear-length decision and Polynomial Calculus encodings to one-sided orbit-closure comparison and local geometry. It is a written proof, with no characteristic assumption.

## 1. A linear-size algebra for a block-partitioned bilinear map

Let \(X=\bigoplus_i X_i\) and \(Y=\bigoplus_j Y_j\) have ordered positive-dimensional summands, and let \(Z\) be a positive-dimensional output space. An input is an arbitrary bilinear map \(\mu:X\times Y\to Z\). Write \(H\) for the independent general linear maps on the \(X_i,Y_j\), and on \(Z\).

Make a diagonal algebra with vertices \(0,1,2\), a private sink \(s_i\) for each \(X_i\), and a private source \(t_j\) for each \(Y_j\). Add the following strict Peirce corners, with paths written in traversal order:
- \(0\to 1\) is \(X\), \(1\to 2\) is \(Y\), and \(0\to 2\) is \(Z\). Composition of the first two is \(\mu\).
- \(1\to s_i\) is the line \(k a_i\), and \(0\to s_i\) is \(X/X_i\). Set \(x a_i=\pi_i(x)\), the quotient.
- \(t_j\to 1\) is the line \(k b_j\), and \(t_j\to 2\) is \(Y/Y_j\). Set \(b_j y=\pi_j(y)\).
- For each of these vertices \(u\), add a private source \(m_u\) with a corner \(m_u\to u\) of dimension \(L_u\). Choose distinct \(L_u\), larger than every old corner dimension. Every positive continuation of a marker arrow is zero.

All other strict products are zero. In particular \(b_j a_i=0\). The old products end at sinks and all products of three strict elements vanish. Associativity follows by this vanishing on strict triples and the endpoint rules on triples containing an idempotent. The strict ideal \(J\) is the two-sided ideal of commutators, because the quotient is diagonal and every element of a strict corner equals its commutator with the source idempotent. Thus \(J^3=0\) and the diagonal quotient is a product of fields. The underlying basis and all corner dimensions are independent of \(\mu\), and the table is affine-linear in \(\mu\). One may choose \(L_u=1+\dim X+\dim Y+\dim Z+i(u)\), using distinct small positive integer indices \(i(u)\). The algebra's dimension is \(O_b(\dim X+\dim Y+\dim Z+1)\) for a fixed number \(b\) of parts.

The argument works even if a projection quotient is zero. Its kernel is then the whole \(X\) or \(Y\), which occurs when that side has one part. The scalar marker line remains part of the format.

## 2. Exact and smooth recovery of the marked bilinear map

Modulo the commutator ideal an isomorphism permutes the diagonal idempotents. Align their lifts by \(z=\sum_u e_u f_u\in1+J\), where \(f_u,e_u\) are ordered systems with the same residues. The identity \(zf_u=e_uz\) gives the alignment. Each dimension \(L_u\) occurs in precisely one ordered corner and pins both its endpoints. The remaining normalized maps on \(X,Y\) preserve \(\ker(-\,a_i)=X_i\), \(\ker(b_j\, -)=Y_j\), because the lines \(k a_i,k b_j\) are intrinsic. The map on \(Z\) is free except for its requirement to transport \(\mu\). Conversely an \(H\)-transporter extends by the induced quotient maps and identities on all scalar and label corners. This proves the exact orbit statement over every field.

We prove the corresponding smooth comparison with full details of the frame choice. Fix \(\mu\), and let \(\mathcal C\) be the reduced change-of-basis orbit closure of its algebra in the variety of associative laws with specified unit. At an encoded law of the same format, restrict to the invariant open of \(\mathcal C\) where the rank of the linear commutator map \(A\otimes A\to A\) is its encoded value. Larger minors vanish on the dense orbit. The image \(J\) is a vector subbundle there. It remains an ideal with \(J^3=0\) by density, as does equality with the ideal generated by commutators. The trace discriminant of the bundle \(A/J\) is a unit at an encoded law. Restrict to its nonzero open, so this commutative algebra bundle is finite étale. Frame its ordered quotient idempotents on the associated finite étale cover.

The lift space of complete orthogonal idempotent systems is smooth. Locally choose one lift system by lifting through the nilpotent ideal in successive complementary corners. The idempotent correction \(e\mapsto e-(2e-1)^{-1}(e^2-e)\) squares the error and works in every characteristic. Each other lift system \(f_u\) is obtained by conjugation as above. Left multiplication of the conjugator by a diagonal unit makes its diagonal entries equal to the \(e_u\). That normalized conjugator is unique because a map centralizing every \(e_u\) is block diagonal. The normalized choices are \(1+\bigoplus_{u\ne v}e_uJe_v\), giving a vector bundle locally. The dimensions of the labelled Peirce corners are locally constant. Select the ordered format using these dimensions, including the unique widths \(L_u\).

Over this smooth cover, choose nonzero generators for all \(a_i,b_j\) lines. The maps \(x\mapsto x a_i\) and \(y\mapsto b_j y\) have full quotient rank on the dense orbit and at the encoded point. Restrict to that open. Their kernels are subbundles. The sums of the \(X\) kernels and of the \(Y\) kernels are direct and exhaustive where the natural maps from their direct sums to \(X,Y\) are invertible. This is an open condition containing the encoded point and every correctly labelled orbit point. Now frame each kernel, frame \(Z\), and frame each private rank-marker corner. For the projection-output corners use the images of the already framed complementary kernel bases. This gives bases because multiplication by the scalar generator induces the quotient isomorphism. Together with the selected idempotents, these choices are a basis of the full algebra. All the independent choices just made are smooth frame or line bundles.

Call the resulting framed space \(\mathcal S\). It is smooth over an open neighborhood of the encoded point (allowing the intermediate étale cover), and it is reduced. Multiplication \(X\times Y\to Z\) in the selected coordinates gives a regular map \(\mathcal S\to\overline{H\mu}\). To verify this containment, the inverse image of the original orbit is dense since smooth maps are open. On that inverse image, the labels and kernel conditions identify the input up to \(H\) by the exact recovery argument. Thus the closed containment holds throughout \(\mathcal S\). Likewise every zero block relation and every other specified product is the prescribed one in the selected frames. This holds on the dense orbit inverse image, then on the whole reduced space. The quotient products also follow directly from the definition of their frames.

The framed algebra is recovered from its readout \(\mu'\), together with the general-linear matrix \(g\) whose columns are its frame. Conversely transport the displayed algebra and frame of any \(\mu'\in\overline{H\mu}\) by \(g\). This gives precisely the chosen frame conditions, which are intrinsic and therefore allow arbitrary \(g\). These regular maps are inverse and exhibit \(\mathcal S\simeq\overline{H\mu}\times\operatorname{GL}(A)\). At any encoded point of the target closure the readout is the encoded \(\mu'\), proving reflection. The two smooth maps from this framed space prove smooth equivalence at the encoded points.

## 3. Three copies and a regular normalization of the fixed blocks

First consider a tuple of data bilinear maps between named spaces. This is equivalent to a mixed trilinear tensor signature by dualizing its two input indices. Lower-order tensors can be padded with a one-dimensional sort pinned by one multiplication law. Give every occurrence of every named input or output space its own index \(u\), with \(r(u)\) the associated reference index. Make separate reference indices for primal and dual sorts. Make three copies \(X_i,Y_i,Z_i\) of each reference or occurrence space. Add scalar copies \(X_0,Y_0,Z_0\), and private right scalar lines \(Y_{b(u)}\). Take the direct sums as the spaces \(X,Y,Z\) in Section 1, with their indicated summands on \(X,Y\).

The following blocks of one bilinear map are fixed:
- \(X_0\times Y_0\to Z_0\) is scalar multiplication.
- \(X_i\times Y_0\to Z_i\) and \(X_0\times Y_i\to Z_i\) are scalar actions.
- \(X_0\times Y_{b(u)}\to Z_0\) is scalar multiplication and \(X_u\times Y_{b(u)}\to Z_{r(u)}\) is the copy map.
- \(X_{i^+}\times Y_{i^-}\to Z_0\) is the evaluation on each reference dual pair.

Every variable map occupies its private occurrence input pair, with value in its corresponding output copy. These pairs are disjoint from the fixed pairs and from one another. All remaining blocks are zero.

In the ambient space of bilinear maps, projection to the fixed input pairs is \(H\)-equivariant. Let \(a\) be the tuple of prescribed fixed restrictions, initially with the whole \(Z\) available as the codomain of each restriction. The stabilizer of \(a\) has the following form. The two maps on \(X_0,Y_0\) are scalar multiples \(s,t\). The images of \(X_i\times Y_0\) recover \(Z_i\), including \(Z_0\). The copy equations then force actions \(s g_i,t g_i,st g_i\) on \(X_i,Y_i,Z_i\). The private scalar map has factor \(t\); its copy block forces \(g_u=g_{r(u)}\). Evaluation forces \(g_{i^-}=g_{i^+}^{-T}\). Conversely these conditions suffice. On the variable data, the factors \(s,t\) cancel, leaving exactly the desired action.

The orbit of \(a\) admits a regular section. Here are explicit choices, valid in all characteristics. At a translated anchor tuple use the fixed standard generators \(x_0,y_0\) of the input lines. The maps \(R_i:x\mapsto a'(x,y_0)\) identify \(X_i\) with the translated \(Z_i\), and their sum is invertible. The maps \(L_i:y\mapsto a'(x_0,y)\) identify \(Y_i\) with the same translated \(Z_i\). The vector \(z'_0=a'(x_0,y_0)\) is nonzero. For each private line take its unique vector \(y_{b(u)}'\) with \(a'(x_0,y_{b(u)}')=z'_0\). For each primal reference use its standard \(X_i\) basis. For its dual reference choose an \(X_{i^-}\) basis so that evaluating on \(Y_{i^-}\) after the identification \(L_{i^-}^{-1}R_{i^-}\) gives the standard pairing in the \(z'_0\) coordinate. The coefficient pairing is invertible, so this choice is regular. Transport the selected reference bases to their \(Z_i\) by \(R_i\). On each occurrence \(X_u\) choose the basis that the translated copy map \(x\mapsto a'(x,y_{b(u)}')\) sends to the already chosen basis of \(Z_{r(u)}\). Transport each occurrence basis to \(Z_u\) by \(R_u\), and now determine all \(Y_i\) bases by \(L_i^{-1}\). This gives a group element taking the fixed tuple to the translated one. Regularity uses inversion of invertible matrix maps between the indicated blocks. Their coordinates can be taken in the \(R_i\)-bases, so the section is regular on the orbit.

For clarity, the geometry of using this section is as follows. Given a projection to an anchor module and a fixed anchor \(a\), the projected orbit lies in the locally closed orbit \(H a\). Its closure inside the total orbit closure restricts to the open preimage of \(H a\), since the latter is open in its own closure. A regular section identifies the preimage of \(H a\) with \(H a\) times the variable-data space by applying the inverse of the chosen transporter. Under that identification the total orbit and its relative reduced closure are the products of \(H a\) with the stabilizer orbit and closure. The embedded variable-data subspace at anchor \(a\) is closed and stabilizer invariant. The orbit \(H a\) is smooth as a reduced orbit over the algebraically closed field. This proves the exact directional and smooth comparison for the trilinear records. The number of copies and scalar lines depends only on the signature, so Section 1 gives linear side bound in their total dimension.

## 4. Balanced products and relation subspaces

Split each ordered record into at most three consecutive chunks, with at most \(\lceil d/3\rceil\) slots in each. Use named physical primal and dual copies of the original spaces and a pinned scalar line. Add evaluation maps for the dual coupling. For each nontrivial chunk create a binary product tree and include the canonical isomorphism \(U\otimes V\to W\) at every internal node. These are trilinear coefficients on three named spaces. The reshaped payload has at most three slots on the chunk outputs, and lower-order payloads can be padded on the pinned line. Include scalar multiplication to pin its map. All of these are a fixed signature of the order-at-most-three type of Section 3.

With independent groups at the auxiliary named spaces, the structural anchor orbit consists precisely of nonzero scalar multiplication, invertible evaluation pairings and invertible product isomorphisms in the respective slots. The orbit map has a regular section: fix the primal bases, normalize the scalar and dual bases using their nonzero or invertible maps, and choose the product-space transforms in tree order by the unique required inverses or forward maps. Empty chunks are pinned scalars. The structural stabilizer on the payload is precisely the original mixed action. Apply the orbit-with-section argument of Section 3. Each auxiliary space has dimension at most \(n^{\lceil d/3\rceil}\), and the number of auxiliary spaces is fixed. Dimensions zero can be removed along with the forced zero components. This yields the claimed width.

For relations include their injective basis matrices as linear maps from additional named frame spaces. The full-rank frame space is a general-linear frame bundle over the fixed-dimension Grassmannian. The inverse image of a subspace orbit is the joint orbit of any frame under the extra frame group. A local trivialization shows that the inverse image of the subspace orbit closure equals the closure relative to the full-rank frame open, and compares their smooth germs. This allows the same construction on relation presentations. No geometric assertion is needed about a rank-losing frame boundary.

## 5. Ordinary multiplication tensors and the quantitative bound

Take the bare multiplication tensor of the algebra of Section 1. The following local chart gives the relevant geometry of the independent action. Fix the encoded unit vector \(e\) and a functional \(\ell\) with \(\ell(e)=1\). For a bilinear law \(m\) take the open where \(R_e:x\mapsto m(x,e)\) and \(L_e:y\mapsto m(e,y)\) are invertible, and \(\ell(u)\ne0\) for \(u=m(e,e)\). Normalize by
\[
N(m)(x,y)=m(R_e^{-1}x,L_e^{-1}y),\qquad h=I+(u-e)\ell .
\]
Then \(N(m)\) has unit \(u\). The map to the fixed-unit law \(h^{-1}\cdot N(m)\) and the auxiliary variables \((u,h^{-1}R_e,h^{-1}L_e)\) is a regular product chart, with inverse \(m(x,y)=h\,\nu(sx,ty)\) for the fixed-unit law \(\nu\) and transforms \(s,t\) fixing \(e\). If \(m=P\mu(Q^{-1}-,S^{-1}-)\) is in the ordinary orbit of an associative unital multiplication \(\mu\), the elements \(a=Q^{-1}e,b=S^{-1}e\) are units since the left and right partial evaluations are invertible. The normalized isotope is
\[
N(m)(x,y)=P((P^{-1}x)b^{-1}a^{-1}(P^{-1}y)).
\]
It is isomorphic to \(\mu\) by \(z\mapsto P(abz)\). Thus the ordinary orbit inside the chart is exactly the fixed-unit algebra orbit times the smooth auxiliary factors, and the same is true for the reduced closures in the chart. Marked-unit algebra laws likewise have a local smooth unit chart via \(h\). This proves the final ordinary-tensor comparison.

All constructions copy coordinates and insert fixed integer entries into explicit polynomial-size tables. If \(3\mid d\) the exponent is optimal among algebraic orbit-reflecting maps, already for independent \(d\)-tensors with side \(n\). An exact fiber lies in a source orbit of dimension at most \(dn^2\); hence the image of the \(n^d\)-dimensional tensor space has dimension at least \(n^d-dn^2\). A three-tensor with sides at most \(N\) has at most \(N^3\) coordinates. This gives \(N=\Omega(n^{d/3})\) for \(d\ge3\). Smooth equivalence here is pointed equivalence of reduced orbit closures. It composes along the smooth frame comparisons by taking pointed fiber products.
