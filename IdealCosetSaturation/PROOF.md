# When polynomially bounded ideal cosets have saturated circuit degrees

Both universal assertions in Grochow's Open Question 6.10 fail for proper nonzero principal-ideal cosets in fixed ambient rings, even when representatives have arbitrary degrees. In the p-bounded, family-oracle model there is also an exact criterion for the existence of saturated degrees. When it holds, every realized degree lies below a saturated degree. If the ideal has a polynomial-size nonzero member on the indices used for the criterion, every realized degree is saturated. This includes the usual growing-arity cosets of polynomially bounded Ideal Proof System certificates. [Grochow, Open Question 6.10 and Proposition 6.9](https://eatcs.org/beatcs/index.php/beatcs/article/download/620/633#page=19) · [Grochow–Pitassi, General Question 7.4](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~toni/Papers/jacm-gp.pdf#page=41)

## Counterexample with arbitrary polynomial degrees

The following argument uses formal arithmetic oracle circuits over $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, with polynomial size and ordinary scalar constants. Start with the proper coset $C_j=1+(x_1)$ inside $R_j=F[x_1,\dots,x_{m(j)}]$. We may take $m(j)=1$ at every index. If oracle indices are polynomially bounded, we may instead take $m(j)=\lceil\log_2(j+1)\rceil$, so the ambient arity is unbounded. The ideals are nonzero in both examples.

For any family $f\in (C_j)$, form an upper oracle family

$$
g_n(x,y,z)=f_n(x)+zG_n(y),\qquad
G_n(y)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]} a_{n,S}\prod_{i\in S}y_i ,
$$

where the $2^n$ coefficients $a_{n,S}$ are algebraically independent over $\mathbb Q$. The two sets of variables are disjoint. Setting $z=0$ gives $f_n$. The difference of the $z=1$ and $z=0$ evaluations, with $x$ fixed, gives $G_n$. Both use only the single index $n$. If the universe consists only of p-bounded families and $f$ is p-bounded, then so is $g$.

Suppose an oracle family $h\in(C_j)$ computed $G_n$ with polynomial-size circuits. Evaluate a proposed circuit formally modulo the ideal generated by all monomials in $y$ of total degree above $n$. Its final output would still be $G_n$. At one oracle gate, write its input tuple as $c+u$, where $c$ is its vector of constant terms and $u$ has zero constant terms. To compute $h_j(c+u)$ in the truncated ring, only the coefficients of total degree at most $n$ of the translated polynomial $h_j(c+t)$ can contribute. The number of these scalar coefficients is at most $(n+1)^{m(j)}$. The argument takes them at their actual values and allows them as independent scalar parameters. It makes no assumption on the degree or circuit complexity of $h_j$ itself.

Induct over the circuit. The coefficients of every intermediate truncated polynomial belong to the field generated over $\mathbb Q$ by the supplied circuit constants and the collected scalar coefficients of the translated oracle polynomials at the gates used so far. At arithmetic gates this follows by closure under sums and products. At an oracle gate expand the translated polynomial and discard terms above degree $n$, which vanish after substitution of $u$. Thus all $2^n$ coefficients of the output would belong to a field generated by $\operatorname{poly}(n)$ scalars for $m(j)=1$, or by $2^{O((\log n)^2)}$ scalars for $m(j)=O(\log j)$ and $j\le \operatorname{poly}(n)$. Both counts are smaller than $2^n$ for large $n$, contradicting algebraic independence. The univariate case does not require any bound on the indices of the oracles used.

Therefore $G$ and hence $g$ cannot circuit-reduce to any representative of the coset. For every realized degree we have found a degree above it missing from the coset. No realized degree is saturated, and therefore no realized degree lies below a saturated degree in the coset. This proves the negative answer in either of the paper's family-oracle or single-index conventions. The coset is fixed inside its stated rings. Extending those ideals to larger rings before asking for representatives changes the coset. [Grochow, §2.1 and Question 6.10](https://eatcs.org/beatcs/index.php/beatcs/article/download/620/633) · [Grochow–Pitassi, footnote 8 and General Question 7.4](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~toni/Papers/jacm-gp.pdf#page=40)

The same counterexample works over every field by choosing $G_n$ through a dimension argument. Allow $n^{\lceil\log_2 n\rceil}$ gates and, in the logarithmic-arity case, oracle indices up to that same bound. For each circuit topology, give each oracle invocation independent scalar parameters for its order-$n$ Taylor coefficients. Treat the original circuit constants as scalar parameters too. There are $2^{O((\log n)^3)}$ parameters. The $2^n$ squarefree output coefficients of the truncated circuit are polynomial functions of those parameters, by the same gate-by-gate calculation. The translated coefficients are ordinary coefficients in $h_j(c+t)$, which requires no division in any characteristic. For an infinite field each topology's image is contained in the zero set of a nonzero polynomial, since the source dimension is smaller than $2^n$. There are finitely many topologies, so their union omits a point of $F^{2^n}$. Over a finite field of order $q$, each topology gives at most $q^{2^{O((\log n)^3)}}$ coefficient vectors. The number of ordered circuit descriptions is at most $2^{2^{O((\log n)^2)}}$, even allowing all indicated oracle index labels and scalar slots. Their total still falls below $q^{2^n}$. Choose $G_n$ outside the resulting set. With univariate oracles the index labels can be erased from the topology count, since each invocation has completely free coefficients through order $n$. All polynomial-size reductions are eventually within the allowed size bound.

## A wide counterexample for the single-index convention

There is a separate failure of saturation in the exact single-index convention, with $j+1$ ambient variables and a degree-one ideal generator. Work over $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$ and fix multilinear $p_j(x_1,\ldots,x_j)$ whose coefficients, over all $j$, are jointly algebraically independent over $\mathbb Q$. Take $C_j=p_j+(x_0)$ inside $F[x_0,\ldots,x_j]$.

For any representative family $f$ choose multilinear $G_n$ with all coefficients jointly algebraically independent over the countable field generated by all coefficients of $f$ and $p$. Form the interleaved oracle family $g_{2n}=f_n$, $g_{2n+1}=G_n$. Then $f\le_c g$ even under the single-index restriction. If $f$ is p-bounded, $g$ is as well.

Suppose $h\in(C_j)$ and $h\le_c g$ under the single-index restriction. For all sufficiently large $j$ its chosen index must be even. Otherwise set $x_0=0$ in the circuit for $h_j$ to get $p_j$ from one of the $G$ oracles. The $2^j$ coefficients of $p_j$ are algebraically independent over the field of all $G$ coefficients, whereas the circuit supplies only polynomially many additional constants. This is impossible.

If also $g\le_c h$, a circuit for $g_{2n+1}=G_n$ queries just one $h$ index, polynomially bounded in $n$. For large queried indices, inline the circuit for that $h$ member, which accesses only an even $g$ member and thus only an $f$ polynomial. Even with a finite set of exceptional small indices, there are at most finitely many extra oracle coefficients from those exceptions. The inlined circuits use polynomially many scalar constants. Their outputs lie in a field of transcendence degree polynomial in $n$ over the field of all coefficients of $f$. They cannot output all $2^n$ independent coefficients of $G_n$. This excludes an equivalent representative $h$ for every $f$, and thus excludes saturated degrees. The p-bounded family-oracle criterion below gives the opposite saturation conclusion for this same wide coset under the more permissive reductions. [Single-index convention, Grochow–Pitassi, footnote 8](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~toni/Papers/jacm-gp.pdf#page=40)

## Exact criterion for polynomially bounded families

Work over $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$. A polynomial family is p-bounded if its arity and degree are bounded by powers of the size index. A circuit reduction has polynomially many arithmetic gates and oracle wires, and may query members of the oracle family with indices bounded by a polynomial in the input index. Multiple such members may be used in a single circuit. The gates compute formal polynomials. A degree here means equivalence under this circuit reduction, among p-bounded families. A degree realized in a coset is saturated when every p-bounded degree above it is also realized there.

Fix the rings $R_j=F[x_1,\ldots,x_{m(j)}]$, with polynomially bounded $m(j)$. A representative of the coset at level $j$ must belong to that ring. Let $C_j=c_j+I_j$ for an ideal $I_j\subseteq R_j$, and assume that the family has at least one p-bounded representative. Set

$$
\nu(j)=\min\{\deg b: 0\ne b\in I_j\},\qquad
W_D(N)=\max\bigl(\{m(j):1\le j\le N,\ \nu(j)\le (j+1)^D\}\cup\{0\}\bigr),
$$

with $\nu(j)=\infty$ for the zero ideal.

**Theorem.** A saturated degree exists in the family $(C_j)$ if and only if there are fixed positive integers $D,K$ such that $W_D(n^K)\ge n$ for all sufficiently large $n$. If so, every realized degree lies below a saturated degree realized in the coset. If the low-degree nonzero ideal members witnessing the condition can be chosen as a VP family (allowed to be zero at unused indices), every realized degree is saturated.

## Extracting tagged polynomials by oracle circuits

Two elementary operations suffice. First, a formal polynomial of bounded individual degree $E$ in its variable $x_1$ can be decomposed into its $x_1$ coefficients with $E+1$ oracle evaluations. Substitute distinct field constants for $x_1$ and apply Lagrange interpolation to the resulting polynomials in the other variables. Thus one can encode many polynomials in disjoint intervals of the $x_1$ degree without introducing a new variable. Removing the known $x_1$ exponent shift amounts to changing the powers used in recombination.

Second, if $a\ne0$, $b=ag$, and all three degrees are polynomially bounded, then $g$ is obtainable by a polynomial-size circuit from oracles for $a$ and $b$. Choose a constant point $u$ where $a(u)\ne0$. Make the substitution $x=u+t(x-u)$ and interpolate in $t$. Invert $a(u+t(x-u))$ as a truncated power series to order $d=\deg g$. Its constant coefficient $a(u)$ has a constant inverse. If the coefficients of the denominator are $a_0,a_1,\ldots$, the first $d+1$ inverse coefficients are obtained by the recurrence $r_0=a_0^{-1}$ and

$$
r_\ell=-a_0^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell} a_i r_{\ell-i}.
$$

Multiply by the numerator in the truncated series ring. The result is $g(u+t(x-u))$ exactly to order $d$, which is its entire expansion. Set $t=1$. This is a division-free circuit with field constants. The numerator and its divisor are both supplied through the tagged encoding below.

## Proof of sufficiency

Fix any p-bounded family $f$ in the coset. Choose $b_j$ in $I_j$ of degree at most $(j+1)^D$, nonzero at every eligible index of positive arity. Set $b_j=0$ at the other indices. At indices of positive arity encode $f_j$ and $b_j$ by $s_j=f_j+x_1^{L_j}b_j$, with $L_j$ larger than the degree bound. When the arity is zero take $s_j=f_j$. These are coset representatives. Both $f$ and $b$ reduce to $s$ by coefficient extraction.

Let $g$ be any p-bounded family with $s\le_c g$. At index $j$ of positive arity, use the polynomial

$$
h_j=f_j+x_1^{H_j}b_j+
\sum_{\substack{i\le j\\ \operatorname{arity}(g_i)\le m(j)}} x_1^{(i+1)H_j} b_jg_i .
$$

Plug the variables of each eligible $g_i$ into the first slots of $R_j$. Take $H_j$ strictly larger than $\deg f_j$ and $\deg b_j+\max_{i\le j}\deg g_i$. At zero arity take $h_j=f_j$. The polynomial is a coset representative: every term added to $f_j$ is a multiple of $b_j$. It is p-bounded, since it uses at most $j$ summands, polynomially large integer shifts, and at most $m(j)$ variables. Because $f,b\le_c g$ and there are at most $j$ additional oracle members in the sum, $h\le_c g$.

To recover $g_i$, fix a constant $a$ sufficiently large that $i^a$ exceeds its arity and exceeds $\max_{j\le i}m(j)$ for large $i$. The condition gives a $j\le i^{aK}$ with $m(j)\ge i^a$ and $\nu(j)\le (j+1)^D$. In particular $j>i$, and $b_j\ne0$. The two tagged bands of $h_j$ supply $b_j$ and $b_jg_i$ with polynomially many oracle gates in $i$. The series calculation recovers $g_i$ in the first slots. The finite exceptions do not affect a circuit-reduction degree, since a constant number of fixed polynomials have fixed circuits. Thus $g\le_c h$. The degree of $s$ is saturated and lies above that of $f$.

If the chosen $b$ is in VP, the same construction works directly for every upper $g\ge_c f$. In that case $b\le_c g$ by its VP circuits and the intermediate family $s$ is unnecessary.

## Proof of necessity

Let $f$ be any p-bounded representative. Let $k$ be the field generated over $\mathbb Q$ by all coefficients of all $f_j$, a countable subfield. For every $n$ choose a multilinear polynomial $G_n$ on $n$ variables with $2^n$ coefficients algebraically independent over $k$. This is possible in $\mathbb R$ and $\mathbb C$. On separate variable sets form

$$
g_n(x,y,z)=f_n(x)+zG_n(y).
$$

It is p-bounded, and $f\le_c g$ and $G\le_c g$ by specialization and subtraction.

Suppose some p-bounded $h\in (C_j)$ satisfies $g\le_c h$. Then $G\le_c h$. Its circuits query indices at most $n^B$ and use at most $\operatorname{poly}(n)$ scalar constants, for some fixed integer $B$. Pick $D$ bounding the degrees of $h_j-f_j$ by $(j+1)^D$ for all $j$. The hypothesis that the width criterion fails gives arbitrarily large $t$ with $W_D(t^{2B})<t$. Work at $n=t^2$.

For each queried index $j$ we have $h_j-f_j\in I_j$. If this difference is nonzero, $j$ is eligible for $W_D$ and $m(j)<t=\sqrt n$. Its degree is at most $(n^B+1)^D$. It has at most $(1+(n^B+1)^D)^{\sqrt n}$ coefficients. Otherwise the difference is zero. Counting all indices $j\le n^B$, the total number of coefficients needed to specify all nonzero differences is $2^{O(\sqrt n\log n)}$.

Every coefficient of the formal output of an oracle circuit over $h$ lies in the field generated by $k$, the used difference coefficients, and the circuit's scalar constants. This follows by induction on arithmetic and composition gates. This field has transcendence degree at most $2^{O(\sqrt n\log n)}+\operatorname{poly}(n)$ over $k$, which is strictly below $2^n$ for large $n$. It cannot contain the coefficients of $G_n$. Thus no p-bounded representative is equivalent to $g$, so the degree of $f$ is unsaturated. The choice of $f$ was arbitrary.

## Consequences and model comparisons

1. **Both unrestricted assertions fail in the fixed-ring p-bounded model.** Set $m(j)=\lceil\log_2(j+1)\rceil$ and $C_j=1+(x_1)$ in $R_j$. These are proper cosets of nonzero ideals at every index and their arity is unbounded. At polynomially accessible indices the width is $O(\log n)$, so the theorem rules out every saturated degree. If exactly $j$ available variables are required, $C_j=1+(x_1^{2^j})$ is a nonzero alternative: the admissible p-bounded representatives eventually equal 1.
2. **Standard growing-arity IPS cosets have saturation at every realized p-bounded degree.** In the usual IPS setting the translation ideal is $(y_1,\ldots,y_m)\cap(y_1-F_1(x),\ldots,y_m-F_m(x))$. The nonzero polynomial $y_1(y_1-F_1(x))$ is in this ideal and is in VP when the given equations have polynomially bounded degree and circuit size. This yields the result whenever the ambient slots grow at least as a fixed positive power of the index and there is a p-bounded certificate. This consequence also works directly in the full family-oracle model without a Factor Conjecture assumption.

The width criterion concerns p-bounded representatives. Its sufficiency construction uses multiple indices, or one index when the upper oracle family admits polynomial padding of all smaller members. Its necessity proof still applies with one polynomially bounded index. The counterexample at the start of the note applies even with arbitrary degrees and with either oracle convention.
