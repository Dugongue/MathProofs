# Dimension-free prime signatures for abelian étale tensor orbits

Let $A,B$ be nonzero étale $\mathbb Q$-algebras whose field factors are abelian extensions of $\mathbb Q$. The input consists of rational multiplication tables. Write $\mathcal O_A,\mathcal O_B$ for full-rank orders and put $D=|\operatorname{disc}(\mathcal O_A)\operatorname{disc}(\mathcal O_B)|$. A good prime means $p\nmid D$. The tensor of multiplication is compared using three independent invertible maps, as in ordinary tensor isomorphism.

Assume Dirichlet GRH, in the exact form specified in Section 8. There is an absolute effective constant $C$ with the following properties.

1. **Unmarked reductions.** For equal-dimensional inputs, the permutation characters of the field factors agree if their numbers of degree-one factors agree at every good prime $p\le C\log^2(2D)$. Character agreement gives equality of all unmarked local multiplication-tensor orbits at every good prime. For cyclic compositum, this decides rational tensor equivalence. The cutoff has no extra dimension factor.
2. **Component-marked reductions.** Keeping the primitive rational idempotents identifies the multiplication subtensors belonging to the individual fields. For each such field, retain its dimension and one bit per good prime below the same cutoff. The bit records whether the local subtensor is a split multiplication tensor of that dimension. Then $T_A$ and $T_B$ are equivalent over $\mathbb Q$ if and only if their multisets of marked profiles agree. This handles all abelian field factors and composita of arbitrarily large degree.
3. **Batch compression.** For a finite batch with product of order discriminants $D$, let $k$ be the number of distinct isomorphism types of constituent fields, $b$ the number of distinct field degrees, and $m_0$ the largest field degree. One can deterministically select a common panel of $O(\min\{k-b,m_0\log(2k)\})$ primes below the same cutoff. Degree and the selected split bits distinguish all constituent field isomorphism types.

The computations use polynomial bit time in the input length, including construction of the orders and primitive idempotents. The analytic use of Dirichlet GRH is the effective polynomial bound on primes in these tests.

## 1. Tensor equivalence and the modular records

For finite-dimensional unital associative algebras over a field, a multiplication-tensor transporter is a triple $P,Q,U$ with $U(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$. Put $a=P(1)$ and $b=Q(1)$. Then $U(x)=P(x)b=aQ(x)$, so right multiplication by $b$ and left multiplication by $a$ are bijective. In a finite-dimensional unital algebra this makes both elements units. Substitution gives
$$
U(xy)=U(x)U(1)^{-1}U(y).
$$
The map $x\mapsto U(1)^{-1}U(x)$ is a unital algebra isomorphism. Conversely an algebra isomorphism supplies all three maps. Thus existence of a rational tensor transporter is equivalent to equality of multisets of field isomorphism types.

Given a rational table, solve for $1$ and let $m$ clear the denominators of the multiplication constants in the displayed basis $e_i$. The additive lattice generated by $1$ and $me_i$ is multiplicatively closed since $(me_i)(me_j)=\sum_k(mc_{ij}^k)(me_k)$. Compute its basis by clearing denominators and taking Hermite normal form. It is a full-rank order. Its discriminant is the determinant of the multiplication trace form $(x,y)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(L_{xy})$, so its bit length is polynomial by rational linear algebra and determinant bounds. The maximal-order discriminant divides the order discriminant by the index-square formula.

At a good prime the order reduces to a finite étale $\mathbb F_p$-algebra $S$, and Frobenius $F(s)=s^p$ is a linear map on $S$. The number of degree-one factors equals the dimension of the quotient of $S$ by the ideal generated by $F(s)-s$. This ideal is zero in a degree-one field factor and is the whole field in every other factor. Thus in a basis $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ the statistic is
$$
n-\operatorname{rank}\bigl[v_i(v_j^p-v_j)\bigr]_{i,j}.
$$
In a single marked factor of rational dimension $d$, the local multiplication tensor is isomorphic to multiplication on $\mathbb F_p^d$ exactly when $F$ acts as the identity on the entire factor. Both tests use reductions of multiplication tables and repeated squaring. They require no finite-field factorization.

## 2. The analytic estimate with the primitive conductor retained

Choose a common algebraic closure and let $K$ be the compositum of the field factors, $G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)$, and $q$ its cyclotomic conductor. Kronecker–Weber and conductor–discriminant give $q\mid D$. Indeed the conductor of each field is the least common multiple of its character conductors, its discriminant is their product, and the compositum has conductor dividing the least common multiple of the constituent field conductors. Each character of $G$ comes from a primitive Dirichlet character of conductor $q_\chi\mid q$. The primitive conductor of $\chi\overline\psi$ divides $q_\chi q_\psi$.

Fix a nonnegative $C^2$ weight $w$, positive on $(1/2,1)$ and supported on $[1/2,1]$, for instance $w(t)=(t-1/2)^3(1-t)^3$ on that interval and zero outside it. Put $I_w=\int_0^\infty w(t)\,dt>0$. The smoothed explicit formula under Dirichlet GRH gives, for any $\xi\in\widehat G$ and $x\ge4$,
$$
 \sum_{p\nmid D}\xi(\operatorname{Frob}_p)\log p\,w(p/x)
 =\mathbf 1_{\xi=1}I_w x+
 O_w\!\left(\sqrt{x}\log(2q_\xi)+\log(2D)\right).                 \tag{1}
$$
First apply the formula to the $\Lambda(n)$-weighted sum for the primitive inducing character. The Mellin transform $\widehat w(s)=\int w(t)t^{s-1}\,dt$ decays as $(1+|\operatorname{Im}s|)^{-2}$ on the critical line by integration by parts. A nontrivial zero contributes $-x^\rho\widehat w(\rho)$, where GRH gives $|x^\rho|=\sqrt x$. The unit-interval zero bound $O(\log(2q_\xi(2+|\operatorname{Im}\rho|)))$ bounds the total by $O_w(\sqrt x\log(2q_\xi))$. Trivial zeros contribute $O_w(1)$ since the support is above $1/2$ and $x\ge4$. The principal character's pole gives $I_wx$. Higher prime powers cost $O_w(\sqrt x)$ by the Chebyshev estimate $\sum_{a\ge2}\vartheta(x^{1/a})\ll\sqrt x$. Removing $p\mid D$ costs $O_w(\log(2D))$. At all remaining primes the inflated and primitive values agree. This proves (1) with effective constants. [Bach–Sorenson, character-weighted prime sums](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1996-65-216/S0025-5718-96-00763-6/S0025-5718-96-00763-6.pdf)

For $h=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G} c_\chi\chi$, put
$$
M=\sum|c_\chi|^2,\qquad L=\sum|c_\chi|,\qquad
W=\sum|c_\chi|\log(2q_\chi).
$$
Expand $|h|^2$ and apply (1) to each $\chi\overline\psi$. The principal terms are exactly the diagonal $\chi=\psi$. The triangle inequality gives
$$
 \sum_{p\nmid D}|h(\operatorname{Frob}_p)|^2\log p\,w(p/x)
 =I_wxM+O_w\!\left(\sqrt{x}LW+L^2\log(2D)\right).               \tag{2}
$$
For integral coefficients $L\le M$, so the relative error after dividing by $M$ is $O_w(\sqrt x\,W+L\log(2D))$. The mean-square main term survives even when $h$ has mean zero.

## 3. Applying the conductor budget to unmarked tensor orbits

For a field factor $E$, put $H_E=\operatorname{Gal}(K/E)$. The character of $G/H_E$ is the sum of the characters of the quotient $G/H_E$, each once. The sum of their log conductors equals $\log|\operatorname{disc}E|$. Hence for $h=\rho_A-\rho_B$, the difference of total permutation characters, conductor–discriminant gives
$$
\sum_\chi |c_\chi|\log q_\chi
  \le\sum_{E\text{ in }A\text{ or }B}\log|\operatorname{disc}E|
  \le\log D.
$$
The $c_\chi$ are integers. If dimensions agree then $\sum c_\chi=h(1)=0$, so the absolute value of the trivial coefficient is at most the sum of the others. Each nontrivial conductor is at least three. Thus $L\le 2\log D/\log 3$ and $W\ll\log(2D)$, even in the presence of arbitrarily many rational factors. Unequal input dimensions can be rejected immediately.

For $x\ge C\log^2(2D)$, (2) shows that if $h\ne0$, its square mass on good primes in $(x/2,x)$ is at least $I_w xM/2$. At least
$$
 \gg_w \frac{Mx}{L^2\log x}                                      \tag{3}
$$
such primes witness disagreement, because $\max_g|h(g)|\le L$. The unmarked statistic of Section 1 is the Frobenius fixed-point count. Conversely equal characters have equal fixed counts for every power of every Frobenius. Fixed counts of powers determine the cycle type by divisor inversion, and the cycle type determines the local multiplication tensor up to equivalence. For cyclic $G$, the character also recovers the rational factors. Choose a maximal subgroup $H$ with differing transitive multiplicity and evaluate at its generator. That evaluation isolates the discrepancy since only overgroups can contribute, and their differences already vanish.

For noncyclic groups, collisions occur even among products of the same number of fields of the same degree. Take $G=(C_2)^4$ from a multiquadratic extension, and identify $\widehat G$ with $\mathbb F_4^2$ additively. The five $\mathbb F_4$-lines partition its nonzero vectors. The invertible $\mathbb F_2$-linear map $(a,b)\mapsto(a,b+a^2)$ gives a different partition into $\mathbb F_2$-planes, since the line $b=0$ becomes a plane that is no longer an $\mathbb F_4$-line. For each plane use the quartic field fixed by its annihilator. Each product of five fields has coefficient five at the trivial character and coefficient one at all others. Their rational field multisets differ, whereas their unmarked good-prime tensor orbits agree everywhere.

## 4. Primitive rational marks give a full orbit test

Compute the primitive idempotents $e_j$ of each rational algebra by deterministic polynomial-time rational decomposition. On an étale algebra their images $E_j=e_j A$ are the field factors. [Lenstra–Silverberg, *Algorithms for commutative algebras over the rational numbers*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.08843)

For an order $\mathcal O$, the product of projected orders $e_j\mathcal O$ contains $\mathcal O$ with finite index. By the index-square formula this index is invertible at every good prime. The primitive rational idempotents reduce to distinct nonzero central projectors there. Equivalently, compute the individual projected orders by rational linear algebra and Hermite normal form and work in them directly. Each projector is retained across the entire prime list, so the modular data refer to the same field component at every prime.

Two abelian fields of the same degree $m$ are isomorphic precisely when their subfields inside the common algebraic closure agree, since each is normal. If $E\ne E'$, the transitive character difference $h=m(\mathbf 1_{H_E}-\mathbf 1_{H_{E'}})$ is nonzero. At a good prime, a difference in $h$ is exactly a difference in the complete-splitting bits. Apply Sections 2–3 to the two fields with the larger global $D$. Every distinct same-degree pair has a witness below $C\log^2(2D)$. Isomorphic fields have identical bits at all good primes. This proves both implications of the component-marked comparison.

Enumerating primes up to the effective cutoff and testing the bits takes polynomial time in the input length. The conductor $q$, Fourier table, compositum, and maximal orders are only used in the correctness argument. A mismatch of marked multisets over any set of good primes gives an unconditional certificate of inequivalence. The hypothesis supplies the bounded scan and hence its completeness.

## 5. Short simultaneous panels

Survey all constituent fields in a batch at all good primes below the cutoff for the product $D$ of all its order discriminants. The survey partitions the factors into exactly the $k$ field isomorphism types. Starting with the $b$ degree classes, greedily add a prime separating the largest number of as-yet-unseparated pairs of distinct surveyed classes. Each step refines some block, so at most $k-b$ primes are required.

For a distinct same-degree pair all $c_\chi$ lie in $\{0,1,-1\}$. Thus $M=L\le2m$. Taking $x=C\log^2(2D)$, (3) gives $\gg_w x/(m\log x)$ distinguishing primes in $(x/2,x)$. There are $O(x/\log x)$ primes in this interval by Chebyshev. Averaging over the good primes shows that some prime separates a fraction $\gg 1/m_0$ of the remaining pairs. From at most $k(k-1)/2$ initial pairs, the deterministic greedy routine terminates in $O(m_0\log(2k))$ steps. It can use only the upper interval since that interval already separates each necessary pair. A single panel works for all comparisons in the batch.

## 6. Directed field-inclusion probes

For explicit abelian field factors $E,E'$, let $m=[E : \mathbb Q]$, $m'=[E':\mathbb Q]$, and let $\Delta_E,\Delta_{E'}$ be the absolute discriminants of their projected orders. Then $E'\subseteq E$ if and only if every good prime below
$$
 C\left(m+\log(2\Delta_E)+\frac{m}{m'}\log(2\Delta_{E'})
            +\sqrt{m\log(2D)}\right)^2
 \tag{4}
$$
that splits completely in $E$ also splits completely in $E'$.

Containment gives $H_E\subseteq H_{E'}$, so the implication holds at every good prime. Otherwise $k(g)=\mathbf 1_{H_E}(g)(1-\mathbf 1_{H_{E'}}(g))$ has mean $1/m-1/[EE':\mathbb Q]\ge1/(2m)$. Expand the first term as an average of quotient characters and the intersection term as a product of two such averages. The mean log conductor over the quotient characters attached to $E$ is $\log|\operatorname{disc}E|/m$, and a product conductor is bounded by the product of the original conductors. Thus from (1) the total error is
$$
 O_w\left(\sqrt{x}\left(1+\frac{\log \Delta_E}{m}+
                    \frac{\log\Delta_{E'}}{m'}\right)+\log(2D)\right).
$$
The bound (4) makes the positive main term dominate this error. A good prime where $k$ is nonzero witnesses failure of containment. Applying the criterion to all ordered pairs recovers the inclusion poset on any batch of constituent fields in polynomial bit time.

## 7. Simultaneously marked field elements

The construction also handles multiplication tensors with a distinguished unit and an ordered tuple of elements, using a common change of basis that carries the tuple. In each primitive rational field $E$, compute a basis of the subfield generated by the projected tuple. Start with $1$. Multiply the accepted monomials by the marks in a fixed order and enqueue a monomial only if it enlarges the span. When this stops, the span is the generated unital subalgebra, which is a field since it lies in $E$. There are polynomially many trials and monomial lengths are at most $[E : \mathbb Q]$. Record the ordered list of accepted words and the matrices of multiplication by the marks in this basis.

These word profiles coincide exactly when an isomorphism of the generated subfields carries the marked tuples. Equal profiles identify the monomial bases, preserve $1$, and intertwine all the marked multiplications. The multiplication operators generate the regular representation of the subfield, so this intertwiner is the required isomorphism. Conversely an isomorphism preserves the prescribed ordering, every dependence test, and the operator matrices. If the ambient abelian fields are isomorphic, they are equal as subfields of a common algebraic closure. An embedding of a generated subfield extends to an embedding of the ambient field, whose image is the same field by normality. Hence the ambient field type and rational word profile jointly classify marked factors. Their multiset classifies the decorated tensor.

This comparison can use only modular profiles once exceptional primes have been recorded. Scale the tuple on both sides by one common integer clearing all projected-order denominators and include that integer in the exceptional product. For each component choose a nonzero full-size pivot minor of the accepted monomial basis in its projected order. Include these minors and the common denominators of the recorded matrices. For each pair of components with equal accepted word lists and different matrices include the numerator of one nonzero entry difference. Multiply their absolute values and $D$ to obtain $D_*$. The added integers have polynomial bit length: there are only polynomially many relevant words and pairs, the words have polynomial length in fixed multiplication tables, and their rational coordinates and pivot minors are computed by polynomial-bit linear algebra.

At primes outside $D_*$, the accepted word basis stays independent. For each discarded trial, its coefficient column in the final full basis occurs in one of the recorded marked multiplication matrices and has zeros beyond the earlier accepted span. The same column preserves the dependence after reduction. Thus the word process returns precisely the reduced rational profile. Distinct profiles stay distinct at every such prime. Appending the local word records to the complete-splitting bits and comparing their correlated multisets at good primes below $C\log^2(2D_*)$ gives exact marked rational isomorphism. The field profile comparison uses the same mean-square proof with the enlarged prime exclusions. There is a good prime below that bound by the principal case of (1) with a sufficiently large effective $C$. This tests the joint action on the ordered tuple on a single matched field factor.
