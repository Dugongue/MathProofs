# Certificates and exact scalar descent for projective diagrams

Let $Q$ be a finite named quiver, with loops and parallel edges allowed, and let $X,Y$ be representations over a field $k$ with the same positive vertex dimensions. A projective diagram witness consists of invertible vertex maps $G_v$ and units $\theta_a$ satisfying
$$
G_vX_a=\theta_a Y_aG_u\quad(a:u\to v).
$$
The arrow scalar is independent for each arrow, even if that arrow is zero. Let $\beta=|Q_1|-|Q_0|+\#\pi_0(Q)$, with the underlying undirected multigraph used to count components. The applications here use $\beta\le 1$. There is no bound on the number or dimensions of vertices and on the three modes of the compiled ordinary tensors.

**Theorem 1 (one-cycle compilation).** There is an explicit pair of ordinary multiplication tensors of unital associative algebras for $X,Y$ whose witnesses reduce to projective diagram witnesses over every commutative $k$-algebra, and each such diagram witness lifts back by a natural section. The output side length is linear in the number of vertices and arrows and the sum of the data dimensions copied at the arrow endpoints.

For $\beta\le 1$, every geometrically negative pair in this constructed family has a polynomial-size Hilbert-like IPS refutation of polynomial total-product degree, over the original field, including imperfect fields. Consequently ordinary PC refutation degree is polynomial. No coordinate field equations are used. The proof also gives an exact finite scalar test for witnesses over field extensions, and polynomial-time decision and search from the diagram presentations over $\mathbb Q$. Over finite fields the latter takes randomized polynomial bit time.

The input to these algorithmic assertions includes the diagram presentations. The certificate concerns the literal ordinary TI axioms of the resulting tensors, with two-sided inverse matrix variables. The bounded-mode IPS results in the previous portfolio concern unrestricted tensors with one small mode. The present family has all three modes equal and potentially arbitrarily large. Its restriction is on the number of independent arrow scalings. [Prior bounded-mode certificates and search](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/ExactMatrixProofDegrees), [Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She, Questions 7.1 and 7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7).

## 1. Projective arrows in ordinary bilinear multiplication

Use the ordered split algebra of the previous quiver compilation, omitting its ruler anchor and ruler lines. For clarity, make source and target copies $s_v,t_v$ for each vertex $v$, and $s_a,t_a$ for each arrow $a:u\to v$, with dimensions $\dim X_u,\dim X_v$ at the arrow copies. Use the distinct links
$$
s_v\to t_v\text{ by }1,\quad
s_a\to t_u\text{ by }1,\quad
s_v\to t_a\text{ by }1,\quad
s_a\to t_a\text{ by }X_a.
$$
Include a single first anchor $d$, then all sources and then all targets in a fixed total order. Let the algebra be $k^m\oplus M\oplus W$, with $k^m$ acting by coordinate idempotents and with the same ordered corners. In $M$ put each data source in $e_sMe_d$, each link on its own line $e_tMe_s$, and an inert marker line in each empty consecutive forward corner. In $W$ put each data target in $e_tWe_d$. The only strict products are $u_\lambda x=X_\lambda x$ for a link basis vector $u_\lambda$ acting on its data source. They respect endpoints and vanish on triples of strict elements, proving associativity. Every vector in $M\oplus W$ is the commutator with its left idempotent. The strict ideal is therefore the commutator span and its square is $W$, by the supplied identity links. The consecutive forward edges and the absence of backward edges in $M$ force an algebra isomorphism to fix the ordered quotient after every base change.

The usual normalization of a tensor witness reduces it to a unital algebra isomorphism. Its maps on the intrinsic source and target data are $G_s,H_t$, and its scale on a link is $\eta_\lambda$. Products give $H_t X_\lambda=\eta_\lambda Y_\lambda G_s$. Let $\eta_v$ denote the scale on $s_v\to t_v$, and for $a:u\to v$ denote the remaining three scales by $\eta_{a,1},\eta_{a,2},\eta_{a,3}$ in the displayed order. The three identity links eliminate the copied data maps, giving
$$
G_{s_v}X_a=\theta_aY_aG_{s_u},
\qquad
\theta_a=\eta_{a,3}\eta_u\eta_{a,1}^{-1}\eta_{a,2}^{-1}.
$$
Conversely use the vertex maps on all of their copies and set the identity-link and marker scales to 1 and the data-link scales to $\theta_a$. These formulas give the claimed section. The scalar inverses can be read from the inverse algebra map, or the designated inverse variables in the polynomial system.

Here and below an ideal consequence with polynomial circuit coefficients means an explicit expression as a sum of products with the original axioms. The two directions above use bounded-degree substitutions and polynomial-size ideal preparation before forest normalization. In particular, unit extraction uses $a=P(1)$, $b=Q(1)$, $a^{-1}=QT^{-1}(1)$, $b^{-1}=PT^{-1}(1)$ in the bilinear witness convention $T(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$. The identities $T=L_aQ=R_bP$ verify the named units and give the normalized invertible algebra map $x\mapsto a^{-1}T(x)b^{-1}$. Projecting the commutator and identity-link products gives preservation of the strict ideal and its square. The same ordered quotient calculation as in the previous quiver gadget then applies. Its equations are described explicitly in the next paragraph to specify the certificate interface.

On the split quotient the induced map and its inverse have matrices $\alpha,\gamma$ with orthogonal-idempotent rows summing to 1. Multiplying the diagonal inverse-coordinate identity by $\alpha_{ij}$, and doing the reverse calculation, gives $\alpha_{ij}=\gamma_{ji}$. Thus the same partition identities hold in columns. If $i\to i+1$ is a consecutive forward corner, apply the quotient actions to a basis vector there. Multiplication by $\alpha_{bi}\alpha_{a,i+1}$, for $b\ge a$, kills its image in every other corner by column orthogonality, while the desired corner itself is zero. Applying the inverse on $M$ gives $\alpha_{bi}\alpha_{a,i+1}=0$. For $i<j$ extend this relation by inserting the column sum at $j-1$. Each summand contains an already forbidden pair. In increasing row order, multiplication by the column sum at $i$ now forces the entries of row $i$ above the diagonal to vanish, and orthogonality with the earlier columns kills those below. The row sum gives the diagonal entry 1. There are polynomially many such consequences, each using bounded-degree multiplication of previously derived equations. Storing shared circuit coefficient lists at each step therefore gives polynomial circuit preparation. Alternatively the ordered-path formula certificate gives preparations of uniformly bounded coefficient degrees by telescoping the same column-sum exclusions. We give an explicit polynomial degree accounting below, without requiring a constant total-product degree for this interface.

All normalized equations on a given corner follow by projection after this step. Substitution of the extracted vertex maps and scales into the projective diagram axioms consequently gives ideal consequences in the original ordinary tensor variables. In this forward extraction only polynomially many algebraic degree layers are needed even for static certificates. For instance, the incompatibility for $i<j$ multiplies earlier coefficient lists by one quotient entry at each step, so increases coefficient degree by $O(m)$ from the adjacent identities. The row induction increases it by at most another $O(m)$. Every remaining projection or correction multiplies previously prepared coefficients by polynomials of bounded degree. The total-product degree is therefore polynomial in the compiled side, and circuits retain sharing.

## 2. Forest normalization and a one-parameter certificate

Choose a spanning forest of the underlying undirected multigraph. At each root set $c_v=1$, and propagate along forest edges so $c_v\theta_a c_u^{-1}=1$ when $a:u\to v$ is in the forest. Propagation in either direction uses the corresponding unit or its named inverse. Then $F_v=c_vG_v$ satisfies the ordinary labelled intertwiner equations on the forest, and leaves one free unit parameter $t_a=c_v\theta_a c_u^{-1}$ for each chord. Conversely setting all forest scales to 1 and the chord scales to the indicated parameters lifts to projective witnesses. The substitutions use products of length at most linear in the graph size, with inverses obtained by reversing factors. They give polynomial circuit representations of the resulting equations from the original tensor ideal, by the same multiplication identities. Their total-product degree is polynomial.

We use a general one-parameter lemma. Let $t$ be a unit parameter with equation $t t'-1=0$, $U$ a block diagonal candidate square matrix of total size $n$, and $V$ its designated block diagonal inverse. Write the homogeneous linear equations in the entries $u$ of $U$ as $M(t)u=0$, where entries of $M$ have $t$-degree at most $\delta$. If this system with inverse variables has no geometric solution, then it has a Hilbert-like IPS refutation whose circuit size and total-product degree are polynomial in the matrix dimensions, number of equations and $\delta+1$. No factorization algorithm is needed for the existence assertion. The same claim without $t$ is the special case of a fixed coefficient matrix.

Choose a maximal-size nonzero minor $\Delta(t)=\det(LM(t)R)$ of $M(t)$, for constant coordinate selectors $L,R$. The empty minor is 1 and its correction term below is zero. Put
$$
z=\Delta u-R\operatorname{adj}(LMR)LMu.
$$
Over the field $k(t)$ this lies in the kernel of $M$. Indeed $LMR$ is invertible, so $L$ is injective on $\operatorname{im}M$, and it annihilates the image of $z$ by the adjugate identity. Let $Z$ be the block matrix corresponding to $z$. The element $\det Z$ is identically zero. Otherwise evaluation in the rational function field of the independent coordinates $u$ would yield an invertible kernel matrix at nonzero $t$, giving a point over an extension of $k$. Such a point would give a geometric point by the weak Nullstellensatz on the original finite polynomial system. Since $\Delta U-Z$ is linear in the entries of $M(t)u$, a determinant difference gives $\Delta^n\det U$ in the input ideal with a polynomial-size certificate. Correcting $\det U\det V$ to 1 by the inverse equations then places $\Delta^n$ in the same ideal.

For precision on the circuit and degree claims, if two $n$-by-$n$ matrices differ by known entrywise ideal consequences, replace one column at a time in their determinants. Expand the replaced-column difference along that column. Each coefficient is a cofactor of a mixed-column matrix, and cofactors have polynomial-size division-free determinant circuits over any field. If each entry degree is bounded by $b$, these cofactors have degree at most $(n-1)b$. The same argument applied to $UV$ and $I$, with determinant multiplicativity, gives the correction above. It accounts for the total degree of products with the input equations. The rank $r$ of $M(t)$ bounds $\deg_t\Delta$ by $r\delta$, and $z$ has coordinates of $t$-degree at most $r\delta$ and linear in $u$. The entire first stage therefore has polynomial circuit size and degree.

If $\Delta$ is constant this already gives the refutation. Otherwise factor it over $k$ as $\lambda\prod_i g_i^{e_i}$. For $g_i\ne t$, work over $K_i=k[t]/(g_i)$. Its image of $t$ is nonzero, and the specialized family is again geometrically infeasible. The same projection argument, now with constant coefficients and a nonzero scalar pivot, gives a polynomial-size certificate of 1 from the specialized linear and inverse equations. Its total-product degree in node variables is $O(n+1)$. This uses no perfectness or separability of $K_i/k$.

Write the certificate coefficients over the power basis of $K_i$ and lift each multiplier to a polynomial of $t$-degree below $\deg g_i$ with coefficients that are $k$-circuits in the node variables. Running the original circuit in the power-basis coefficient ring costs polynomial size using the multiplication table of $K_i$. The difference between 1 and the resulting combination of original equations has polynomial $t$-degree and is divisible by $g_i$. Monic polynomial division in $t$, with the coefficient circuits as entries, gives its quotient $v_i$ with polynomial size and degree. This proves $1-v_i g_i$ in the input ideal. For $g_i=t$, the inverse axiom gives the same statement with $v_i=t'$. Finally put $B=\prod_i (v_i g_i)^{ne_i}$. Telescoping products and powers shows that $1-B$ lies in the input ideal with polynomial circuit size and degree. The other term $B$ lies in the ideal by $\Delta^n$, since $B=\lambda^{-n}\Delta^n\prod_i v_i^{ne_i}$. The sum of all exponents is at most $n\deg\Delta$, so this step retains the asserted polynomial degree. This proves the one-parameter lemma.

Apply the lemma to the forest-normalized Hom constraints, with $\delta\le1$ and $n=\sum_v\dim X_v$. When $\beta=0$ use its no-parameter form. Substituting the tensor-to-diagram witness polynomials into all coefficient circuits and using the prepared ideal representations gives the claimed Hilbert-like IPS certificate in the original ordinary TI equations. The total-product degree remains polynomial. Expanding a Hilbert-like certificate of bounded total-product degree gives a PC proof in that degree by summing the monomial multiples of the axioms. [Division-free determinant circuits](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(84)90018-8).

## 3. The exact scalar test over extension fields

Assume $\beta=1$. Test the unscaled named diagram comparison first. If it succeeds, every field extension has a witness. If it fails, compute the nonzero maximal Hom minor $\Delta(t)$ as above. Every admissible scalar in any extension field is a root of $\Delta$. In particular its degree is at most the number of node-map coordinates, since $\delta\le1$. To prove this, work over $\bar k$ first. Outside the zeros of $\Delta$, the projector above maps onto the full Hom space after rescaling by $\Delta^{-1}$. If any scalar there has an invertible intertwiner, choose fixed coordinates $u_0$ for which $\det Z(t,u_0)$ is a nonzero polynomial. This gives an invertible intertwiner for every scalar in a nonempty open subset of $\bar k^\times$. The set of working scalars, when nonempty, is a coset of the subgroup of $\bar k^\times$ stabilizing the named diagram $Y$ up to ordinary isomorphism under rescaling its chord. A subgroup containing a nonempty open subset is all of $\bar k^\times$: the open subset meets each of its nonzero translates, so their ratios give all scalars. Then $t=1$ would give an isomorphism over $\bar k$. Named diagram isomorphism descends from a field extension by Noether–Deuring. In fact one may take a finite extension carrying the isomorphism by applying the weak Nullstellensatz to the finite system, then restrict scalars and cancel common positive multiplicities by Krull–Schmidt. This contradicts the preliminary test. Over any other extension, the same maximal minor excludes a solution outside its zero set, since $\det Z$ is now identically zero.

For each distinct irreducible factor $g_i\ne t$ of $\Delta$, test ordinary diagram isomorphism with the chord rescaled by the residue class of $t$ over $k_i=k[t]/(g_i)$. Retain precisely the successful $g_i$. The ordinary compiled tensors have a witness over a field $E/k$ exactly when some retained factor has a root in $E$, equivalently an embedding $k_i\hookrightarrow E$. Indeed an accepted residue-field witness extends under any such embedding, and a witness with such a scalar descends to its residue subfield by the same cancellation argument. The finite candidate set and the algorithm work even if some factors are inseparable. Over number fields and finite fields the possible extension degrees are therefore exactly the union of the positive multiples of the retained factor degrees, with all degrees allowed if the unscaled comparison succeeds. For number fields each multiple can be realized over the relevant field by Eisenstein extension. For finite fields existence at each multiple follows from their field lattice.

Over $\mathbb Q$, one may compute the maximal minor by rational-function elimination with polynomial bit complexity, factor it over $\mathbb Q$, and test and recover an ordinary module isomorphism at each rational root. Over an explicitly represented finite field, factorization is randomized polynomial in the input bit length and the degree of $\Delta$, and the same module step applies at each root. The module input is explicit, using the vertex idempotents and the arrow matrices on the direct sum of the vertex spaces. The spanning-forest gauge and algebra section turn success into a verifiable ordinary tensor witness. [Polynomial-time module isomorphism](https://doi.org/10.1145/258726.258751), [Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf).

An arithmetic example has one vertex and one loop. Let $X$ and $Y$ be the companion matrices of $z^m-a$ and $z^m-1$ for $a\in k^\times$. The scaled matrix $tY$ is cyclic for $t\ne0$ and has characteristic polynomial $z^m-t^m$. Thus these projective diagrams, and their ordinary tensor outputs, admit a witness over $E/k$ exactly when $t^m=a$ has a solution in $E$. Over $\mathbb F_p(s)$ use $m=p,a=s$. There is a geometric witness and no witness over any separable extension. Over $\mathbb Q$ with $a$ a rational prime, the least witness degree is exactly $m$ by Eisenstein and the full degree spectrum is its positive multiples. For fixed one-loop quiver the compiled side length is $O(m)$.

These are prose proofs. The geometric certificate theorem applies only when the literal equations are geometrically inconsistent. The scalar test also treats arithmetic negatives whose equations are geometrically consistent.

## 4. Any fixed cycle count for geometric certificates

**Theorem 2.** For any fixed $\beta$, every geometrically negative compiled pair has a polynomial-size Hilbert-like IPS refutation of polynomial total-product degree over its coefficient field. The bound does not require a fixed number of vertices or bounded vertex dimensions. The same result holds if only the maximum cycle count per connected component is fixed, even when the total cycle count grows.

Use the forest-normalized system with $\beta$ scalar parameters $t$ and their designated inverses $t'$. Let $M(w)$ be the matrix of Hom equations from $X$ to the diagram with $Y$'s chords rescaled by the dummy forward parameters $w_0$. Write $w=(w_0,w_1)$ with $2\beta$ entries. Let $M_0$ be the fixed matrix of Hom equations from $X$ to itself, with rank $r$. Write $F$ for the actual tuple of node maps, $V$ for its designated inverse block matrix, and $f$ for the coordinate vector of $F$. The vertex maps from an isomorphism give square changes $D$ on Hom inputs and $E$ on arrow errors, with
$$
M(t)D=E M_0 .
$$
Specifically $D$ left multiplies each node map by its $F_v$, and $E$ left multiplies the error in each arrow by the map at its target. Direct expansion proves the identity modulo the actual linear intertwiner equations. Their polynomial inverses use the supplied inverse node variables. It follows that every $(r+1)$-minor of $M(t)$ is an ideal consequence with polynomial circuit size and degree. Compare each minor with the same minor of $E M_0 D^{-1}$, which is identically zero by rank factorization through an $r$-dimensional space, and use the determinant-difference formula.

In the dummy parameters let $I$ be generated by $w_{0,i}w_{1,i}-1$ and a basis of the $k$-linear span of all $(r+1)$-minors of $M(w)$, selected from those minors. Their degrees are polynomial, so the number chosen is polynomial for fixed $2\beta$. Choose scalar row and column selectors $L_0,R_0$ with $b_*=\det(L_0 M_0 R_0)\ne0$ and put $L=L_0 E^{-1}, R=D R_0$. Set
$$
b(w)=\det(LM(w)R),\qquad
J(w)=b(w)I-R\operatorname{adj}(LM(w)R)LM(w).
$$
For $r=0$ use $b=1,J=I$. With $\operatorname{blk}$ reshaping node coordinates to a block diagonal matrix and $n$ its side length, define
$$
C(w)=b_*^{-n-1}b(w)\det\bigl(\operatorname{blk}(J(w)f)\bigr)\det V .
$$
At $w=(t,t')$, one has $b(w)\equiv b_*$ by the change identities and $J(w)f\equiv b(w)f$ by the actual Hom equations. Hence $C(t,t')\equiv 1$ with polynomial-size ideal preparations. For any geometric point of $I$, the polynomial $C(w)$ in all actual node variables is identically zero. At a node-variable evaluation with $b(w)=0$ this is immediate from the leading factor. If $b(w)\ne0$, then $M(w)$ has rank $r$, the projector $J(w)$ lands in its kernel, and geometric infeasibility implies that the projected block matrix is singular. This uses geometric infeasibility of the original system, because the dummy torus equations make every forward $w_{0,i}$ invertible.

Here is the bounded-variable circuit compression, included to specify the coefficient-field and degree scope. Take the span, as polynomials in $w$, of the coefficients of $C(w)$ expanded in all actual node variables. It has dimension polynomial in $n$ and the input size since the number of dummy variables is fixed and their degrees are polynomial. Each element vanishes on the zero set of $I$. For a basis $h_i$, the effective Nullstellensatz in the dummy variables and one Rabinowitsch variable gives
$$
h_i^{e}=\sum_j a_{ij}(w) I_j(w)
$$
with a common exponent $e$ and total-product degrees polynomial for fixed $\beta$. The coefficient solution can be taken over $k$ by solving its finite linear system, and the degree bounds are independent of characteristic. If the span is zero, $C(t,t')\equiv1$ already refutes the system. Otherwise, for a basis of length $l$ put $m=(e-1)l+1$. The coefficients of $C(w)^m$ lie in the span of the length-$m$ products of the $h_i$, and every such product contains some factor $h_i^e$. Take a basis of this span from the products, each with the preceding ideal representation. To express $C(w)^m$ in this basis with circuits in the actual node variables, evaluate it in the ring truncated above its polynomial dummy degree and apply the fixed linear coefficient functionals for the selected basis. The truncated ring has polynomial dimension over the actual-variable polynomial ring. This transfers multiplication to polynomial-size circuit computation, with polynomial semantic degree. It expresses $C(w)^m$ with polynomial-size, polynomial-degree ideal multipliers of $I$. Substitute $w=(t,t')$ and use $C(t,t')\equiv1$ and the prepared minors to obtain a Hilbert-like certificate. The preceding forest and tensor substitutions give the result for the original tensor equations. [Prior one-Hom projector and coefficient compression](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/ExactMatrixProofDegrees/OneHomSearch.md), [Kollár's effective Nullstellensatz](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Kollar/kollarnullstellen.pdf).

For the component assertion, the named projective system splits into independent variable sets indexed by connected components. If every component system has a geometric witness, the choices combine over the algebraic closure, and lift to a tensor witness. Thus a negative instance has a negative component, whose certificate can be substituted into the full ordinary equations. The analogous one-cycle decision and exact scalar tests run separately on components when each has at most one cycle. For finite base fields the exact accepting extension-degree set in that case is the intersection of the component unions of positive multiples.
