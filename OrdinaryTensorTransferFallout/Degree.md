# Prime-selective raw polynomial calculus hardness in ordinary tensors

Fix a nonempty finite set $\mathcal P$ of primes. For each $p\in\mathcal P$ choose a power $b_p=p^{h_p}$, allowing $h_p=0$, and a positive integer $r_p\ge (p+1)b_p$. There is one pair of ordinary $0,1$ multiplication tensors, independent of the field, with common side length
$$
 N=\Theta\Big(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}(r_p+b_p)\Big).
$$
Each endpoint has tensor rank between $N$ and $C N$ over every field. The pair is isomorphic in characteristic zero or prime characteristic outside $\mathcal P$, and geometrically nonisomorphic in each characteristic $p\in\mathcal P$.

**Theorem.** At characteristic $p\in\mathcal P$, the raw ordinary Polynomial Calculus refutation degree is $\Theta(b_p)$, with absolute constants. The minimum expanded sparse size $S$ satisfies
$$
 S+N^{C}\ \ge\ \left(\frac{r_p}{C_p b_p}\right)^{2b_p},
 \qquad
 S\le N^{C(b_p+1)}.
$$
The constants $C$ are absolute and $C_p$ depends only on $p$. The degree and size bounds also hold with the field equations over every finite field of characteristic $p$, with the same constants. The lower bound has content for all sufficiently large $r_p/b_p$, even when the proof has unrestricted degree.

Thus one integer tensor pair can be hard at any prescribed finite set of characteristics and have actual witnesses elsewhere. For fixed $\mathcal P$, take $b_p$ of common order $m$ and $r_p=K_p b_p$ where the fixed $K_p>C_p$ are sufficiently large. Then every negative probe needs degree $\Theta(N)$ and size $\exp(\Omega(N))$. For any fixed $\epsilon\in(0,1)$, take $r_p$ of common order $m$ and $b_p$ of order $m^{1-\epsilon}$. Every negative probe has size $N^{\Theta(N^{1-\epsilon})}$ and degree $\Theta(N^{1-\epsilon})$. Replacing every $b_p$ by $1$ preserves the characteristic decision pattern while giving constant-degree and polynomial-size negative certificates. Restricting the statements to geometrically negative ordinary tensor pairs on each selected probe places them in the setting of Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She Questions 7.1 and 7.2. [Published questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7)

## 1. A $0,1$ switch for a single prime

Let $d=p+1$ and
$$
 D_p=\begin{pmatrix}I_p&\mathbf 1\\ \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Subtracting the first $p$ rows from the last and then the first $p$ columns from the last gives $I_p\oplus[-p]$. Keep $b=b_p,r=r_p$, and on a common space of dimension $2r+b$ form the two square matrices
$$
 A= I_{r-pb}\oplus 0_r\oplus I_{db},
 \qquad
 B= I_{r-pb}\oplus 0_r\oplus D_p^{\oplus b}.
$$
On a doubled space use $L_A=\begin{pmatrix}0&A\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, and similarly $L_B$. Away from $p$, $A,B$ are equivalent by independent changes on their row and column spaces, giving conjugacy of $L_A,L_B$. Over characteristic $p$, both loops are square-zero, the source has $r+b$ Jordan blocks of length two and $2r$ of length one, and the target has $r$ blocks of length two and $2(r+b)$ of length one. This holds over every extension. The elementary changes to Jordan coordinates are signed row or column shears within the individual $d$-blocks and coordinate permutations. Their matrices and inverses have at most $O_p(1)$ nonzero entries per row and column, with entries in the prime field.

In positive characteristic $p$, the exact raw two-sided inverse PC degree of a rectangular $a$-by-$c$ board is
$$
 \min(a+2,2p^{v_p(c-a)})\qquad (0<a<c).
$$
The full matrix-conjugacy formula specializes, for equal minimal polynomials and multiplicity differences zero as field elements, to the minimum of these degrees across mismatched Jordan types, with a degree-three preparation floor. Here the two gaps are $b$ and $2b$. For $b>1$ the formula gives degree $2b$, since $r\ge(p+1)b$; for $b=1$ a multiplicity differs as a field element and the full formula gives degree two. Ordinary PC is invariant in degree under invertible linear coordinate changes. Indeed a multiplication by a replaced variable can be realized by individual variable multiplications and scalar additions, with the parent degree strictly below the original product degree. The constant coefficient maps are invertible in both directions. Thus this formula applies also in the literal $0,1$ coordinates. [Exact matrix conjugacy and rectangular inverse degree](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/ExactMatrixProofDegrees/ConjugacyDegree.md)

## 2. Sparse lower bound in literal coordinates

Use a Boolean rectangular matching board of dimensions $r$ by $r+b$, with row and column sums one, Boolean equations, and collision products between distinct edges incident to a shared vertex. Write $P$ for the rectangular matrix of edge variables. In Jordan coordinates substitute $P\otimes I_2$ on the length-two blocks of the forward witness and $P^{\mathsf T}\otimes I_2$ on the length-one blocks, and transpose these for the supplied inverse. The length-two part intertwines $I\otimes J_2$ identically, and the length-one part intertwines zero. Under Boolean matching normalization the inverse equations become row totalities or column totalities, repeated in the appropriate diagonal entries; the cross entries vanish. Normalize by the Boolean and collision axioms, so the monomial basis consists of partial matchings and variable powers are reduced. Under the bounded-block return to literal coordinates each transporter entry is a linear form in at most $L_p=O_p(1)$ edge variables, or zero. Each edge occurs in the images of at most $L_p$ transporter variables, by the column and row bounds on both coordinate changes and the bounded repetition of each edge in $P\otimes I_2$, its transpose, and the corresponding inverse witness. The products of the basis changes with their inverses cancel identically, so each original inverse axiom maps to a constant linear combination of normalized totalities. Each original intertwiner axiom maps to zero. Since the coefficients of the substitutions lie in the prime field, all coordinate field equations also map to zero after normalization, over every finite extension of that prime field.

For the probability estimate take $b\ge2$ and $r\ge 8b$. In the excluded cases the displayed ordinary size bound follows from the additive polynomial term by increasing $C,C_p$. Keep independently uniform sets of $2b$ rows and $3b$ columns live, and match their complements by a uniform bijection pinned to one. Other nonlive edges become zero. Each totality maps to a live totality or zero. The live board has normalized PC degree at least $b$. Here we use existence of normalized rectangular designs of order $b-1$ and their order-$(b-1)$ extensions from order $b-2$ after deleting one row and column of the live board; its smaller side is then $2b-1$, and the gap is still $b$. To recall the argument for immediate normalization, each totality pairs to zero with every such design. When multiplying by one edge, force that edge, keep the design values on its disjoint board up to order $b-2$, and extend there to order $b-1$. On forcing the edge this new design evaluates the parent line as the old design evaluates its normalized product. A degree-$(b-1)$ parent term disjoint from the forced edge cannot contribute in this equality, because the resulting degree-$b$ term could only arise from that term itself and hence has zero coefficient. This proves closure under the allowed multiplications, and the design value of the empty matching is one. [Riis, Section 14](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07280v1#S14) · [Earlier dense-board restriction proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/SparseMatrixProofBounds/SparseConjugacy.md)

For each restriction, a projected proof must consequently contain some surviving matching term of degree at least $b$ in the image of an original proof-monomial occurrence. Indeed, if all normalized lines have degree at most $b-1$, multiplication by an original variable is a sum of multiplications by live edges and a possible constant. Each separate edge product also remains in degree $b-1$: the original ordinary parent has degree at most its ordinary proof cutoff minus one when proving a degree bound. For the unrestricted-size argument, where only the line images are known at degree $b-1$, an individual edge product's top term is itself contributed by some original monomial of that multiplication line, so it also cannot have degree $b$ if no original occurrence produces a surviving term of that degree. More explicitly the final product line is obtained by multiplying each nonzero parent occurrence by the same literal witness variable. The candidate support argument below counts every compatible choice of a constituent for each factor before all cancellations. Thus the probability estimate guarantees the degree cutoff separately for each summand in every replayed multiplication. The normalization of input axioms has already been verified. Applying this reasoning without random restriction to the line-degree bound on the same projection gives ordinary degree at least $b$ even when field axioms are included.

For the sparse count fix a single original monomial, with arbitrary powers and with duplicate occurrences of the same original variable combined. Its candidate edge sets are the supports of the projected variables. A term that survives the pinning and Boolean normalization has selected a compatible set of edges, selecting at least one edge from the candidate set of each original variable in its support. A clique collision kills a term, and powers can repeat an edge without creating extra distinct edges. We bound the probability that **any compatible choice** survives with at least $b$ live edges.

If there are fewer than $2b(L_p^2+1)$ distinct variables, all candidate sets together have $O_p(b)$ edges. Choose $b$ distinct live edges in at most $C_p^b$ ways. If compatible, they have distinct row and column endpoints, and the probability all endpoints are live is at most $(2b/r)^b(3b/(r+b))^b$. If there are at least $2b(L_p^2+1)$ variables, greedily choose $2b$ whose candidate sets are pairwise disjoint; each chosen variable excludes at most $L_p^2$ others since each edge is used at most $L_p$ times. Survival of any term entails $2b$ distinct compatible edges, one from each chosen set. The union over these choices costs at most $L_p^{2b}$. For a fixed compatible choice and any specification of $j$ live rows, the necessary probability is at most
$$
 (2b/r)^j/(r-2b)_{2b-j}.
$$
The denominator is the probability cost of the specified pinned pairs conditional on the live set. Discarding column-status requirements only enlarges the probability. There are at most $2^{2b}$ specifications. Since $r-4b\ge r/2$, the two cases both give probability at most $(C_p b/r)^{2b}$. A union bound over original monomial occurrences completes the lower bound. This argument handles arbitrary degrees and powers in the literal non-Jordan coordinates.

## 3. Compiling the independent switches

For each $p$ make a labelled block system using a left copy $V_p$ of the loop space and a line $Fx_p$, right lines $Fy_{p,0},Fy_{p,1}$, and output blocks $V_p,Fz_p$. Its products are
$$
 v y_{p,0}=v,\qquad v y_{p,1}=L v,\qquad
 x_p y_{p,0}=x_p y_{p,1}=z_p
$$
on the $p$-component, with all other cross-component products zero. Here $L$ is that side's loop. The scalar equations force equality of the two right scalars, because the left scalar has a supplied inverse. The identity product then makes the output $V_p$-witness the left $V_p$-witness times this right scalar. The remaining product therefore gives the desired $p$-loop conjugacy. The converse lift uses the same loop witness on the two copies and fixes all scalars, a coordinate-or-constant substitution. This gives constant-degree PC preparation of the respective axiom images. It preserves sparse size from a labelled proof to a loop proof up to additive preparation, and allows either raw or finite-field equations.

Apply the uniform ordered-path block erasure to obtain ordinary multiplication tensors of unital associative algebras. This has linear side overhead in the sum of block dimensions, constant-degree readout and sparse-nonincreasing witness substitution apart from one-time polynomial preparation in the direction from ordinary refutations to labelled refutations. The construction uses $0,1$ structure constants here. The lower rank bound $N$ comes from the full output flattening since $1\cdot u=u$. For the upper rank bound, all vertex, path, and projection products use $O(N)$ elementary rank-one terms. For the data products the identity, scalar and $L$ slices require at most the sum of their matrix ranks, also $O(N)$. The ordered-path algebra step is the only use of an erasure theorem. [Ordered-path erasure and its proof transfer](Background/MarkerProof.md)

At a characteristic outside $\mathcal P$ every loop has a witness over the probe's own field, so the ordinary pair has one. At a selected prime, all other components have witnesses and specialize to constants. This keeps the degree and preserves sparse supports, including when the probe includes its coordinate field equations. The bad component then supplies the raw lower bound and the normalized finite-field lower bound. Conversely its raw degree-$2b_p$ refutation transfers to an ordinary raw proof of degree $O(b_p)$, also valid with added field equations. A degree-$D$ PC proof in $t=O(N^2)$ variables can be compressed to sparse size polynomial in $\binom{t+D}{D}$ by retaining independent lines and reconstructing omitted parents from the retained basis. This gives the displayed sparse upper bound. All constants in the compiler degree bounds are independent of the label count and the input primes. $\square$

These are written proofs. They give an exact chosen set of bad characteristics, including after algebraic closure, and jointly prescribe asymptotic negative proof resources. For a degree linear in $N$, the raw sparse lower bound proved here is exponential in $N$; the upper bound is factorial-scale $N^{O(N)}$. The gap at proportional multiplicity discrepancy remains.


## 4. One all-field ordinary family separating expanded PC from Hilbert-like IPS

**Theorem.** There are $0,1$ tensor pairs with equal side length $N$ and endpoint ranks $\Theta(N)$ over every field, such that the same pair is geometrically nonisomorphic in every characteristic. Over every field it requires raw PC degree $\Theta(N)$ and raw expanded sparse PC size $2^{\Omega(N)}$, and admits a polynomial-size Hilbert-like Ideal Proof System (IPS) circuit. The raw upper bounds and both lower bounds continue to hold with the coefficient field equations at any finite field probe. The family is constructed in polynomial time from fixed-degree expanding graphs with order $n\equiv1\bmod6$. No finite-field axioms occur in the IPS certificates.

### The local equation systems

Let $G$ be an $n$-vertex $d$-regular expander for fixed even $d$. Such graphs with bounded expansion constant away from zero exist along $n\equiv1\bmod 6$. Use both orientations of each edge. For $\ell=2,3$ introduce one-hot values $x_{e,a}$ for every directed edge $e$ and $a\in\mathbb Z/\ell$. Use sum-one, Boolean and pairwise collision equations in each edge block. Include forbidden products for incompatible assignments: $x_{e,a}x_{\bar e,b}$ for $a+b\ne0$, and $\prod_{e\ {\rm out\ of}\ v}x_{e,a_e}$ for $\sum_e a_e\ne1$. The underlying values are inconsistent, since the sum of charges is $n\equiv1\bmod\ell$. [Friedman, regular random graph expansion](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0405020)

If the proof field has characteristic different from $\ell$, the PCR system with complementary literals for these equations requires degree $\Omega(n)$ and sparse size $2^{\Omega(n)}$. For the degree assertion, extend the field to contain a primitive $\ell$-th root $\zeta$ and substitute
$$
 x_{e,a}\ \longmapsto\
 \frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\zeta^{-aj}t_e^j
$$
and the complement to one minus this polynomial. Modulo $t_e^\ell-1$, these are the orthogonal Fourier selectors. Every forbidden product becomes a constant-degree consequence of the relevant root-of-unity clause equation: multiply that clause by the product of selectors and reduce in the local cyclic quotient, giving a nonzero constant times the forbidden product. The complement, Boolean and collision images are zero in the same local quotient. All arities are bounded, so this sends a degree-$D$ proof to one of degree $O(D+1)$, and the root-of-unity Tseitin lower bound applies. For $\ell=2$ one may identify the opposite oriented variables after applying the two-way clauses, or project from undirected signs by setting both oriented variables for an edge to its single sign. For $\ell=3$ use the two-direction theorem. The PCR size-degree theorem applied to the $O(n)$ Boolean literals turns the degree lower bound into the stated sparse lower bound. A base-field proof also works over the extension, so the conclusions hold over the original field. [Buss–Grigoriev–Impagliazzo–Pitassi, Theorems 10 and 15](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/lineargaps/journalfinal.pdf) · [Alekhnovich–Ben-Sasson–Razborov–Wigderson, PCR size-degree](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539701389944)

Every one-hot instance in this construction has a polynomial-size Hilbert-like IPS certificate over every field, including in characteristic $\ell$. Work in the free constant-dimensional group algebra $F[z]/(z^\ell-1)$ and put $A_e=\sum_a x_{e,a}z^a$. For the edge pair, express
$$
 A_eA_{\bar e}-1
  =\sum_{a+b\ne0}(z^{a+b}-1)x_{e,a}x_{\bar e,b}
    +\left(\sum_a x_{e,a}\right)\left(\sum_b x_{\bar e,b}\right)-1 .
$$
For the vertex, write
$$
 \prod_{e\ {\rm out\ of}\ v}A_e-z
  =\sum_{\sum a_e\ne1}(z^{\sum a_e}-z)\prod_e x_{e,a_e}
    +z\left(\prod_e\sum_a x_{e,a}-1\right).
$$
The final terms telescope against the sum-one axioms. Multiplying all $A_e$'s and telescoping by edge pairs yields equality to $1$ with these ideal generators. Grouping instead by vertices yields equality to $z^n=z$. Subtract the two ideal expressions and extract the coefficient of $z$. This derives $1$ using polynomial-size circuits for the multipliers and summands. Indeed group algebra operations are on $\ell\le3$ coordinates and take a bounded number of ring operations. All terms have polynomial degree $O(n)$, and expanded ordinary PC can replay the identities in degree $O(n)$. The coefficient extraction uses the basis $1,z,\dots,z^{\ell-1}$ and needs neither a root of unity nor division.

### Encoding the local systems into labelled records

For each $\ell$, define two records $R_\ell^0,R_\ell^1$ of split diagonal algebras and named linear maps. At each directed edge put the coordinate algebra with basis $u_a$, $a\in\mathbb Z/\ell$, with product $u_a u_b=\delta_{ab}u_a$. Add the fixed cycle shift $u_a\mapsto u_{a+1}$, and the edge-reversal map $u_a\mapsto u_{-a}$ into the opposite edge's algebra. At each vertex put the coordinate algebra with basis $u_\beta$ indexed by the outgoing $d$-tuples of sum $j$, for record $R_\ell^j$. Its dimensions $\ell^{d-1}$ are independent of $j$. Add each coordinate projection $u_\beta\mapsto u_{\beta_e}$ into its edge algebra. Transporters preserve every name, each multiplication and every named map, with witnessed inverses.

The record equations yield the one-hot source at bounded PC degree and polynomial sparse preparation. Multiplicativity and two-sided inverse equations give row and column sums, idempotence and collisions, as for the diagonal quotient extraction in the ordered-path theorem. The shift ensures that the orbit of each matrix position is indexed by the difference of its row and column, so each directed-edge witness has entries $x_{e,c-a}$. Reversal changes the difference to its negative and thus gives the two-edge clauses. Fix the all-zero source tuple in a vertex block. For target tuple $\gamma$ of sum one, incidence identifies the sum of matrix entries in the corresponding source column with prescribed $e$-coordinate $a$ as $x_{e,a}$. Multiply this sum equation by the entry $P_v(\gamma,0)$ and use column collisions. This yields $P_v(\gamma,0)x_{e,a}=0$ whenever $\gamma_e\ne a$. For any forbidden tuple $a$, every $\gamma$ differs at some coordinate. Multiply those equations by the other $x$'s and sum over $\gamma$, whose column sum is one. This gives the vertex forbidden product. All products have bounded length.

In the other direction substitute each edge entry $x_{e,c-a}$ and each vertex entry $\prod_e x_{e,\gamma_e-\beta_e}$ in the forward witnesses, and the transposes in the inverse witnesses. The row and column sums give one by expanding products of edge sums, with terms of forbidden charge vanishing. Distinct entries in a common row or column collide at a differing edge value, and individual entries are idempotent. These give the inverse and diagonal multiplication equations. In the map equations for each incidence, sum the entries over all tuples with the designated edge value. Expanding unconstrained edge sums and removing forbidden products gives the required $x_{e,c-a}$. The edge-reversal map follows from $x_{\bar e,-a}=x_{e,a}$, derived by multiplying each block's sum-one equation by the opposite indicator and using the forbidden pair products. Thus image axioms have constant-degree, polynomial-size PC derivations. Each image variable is a single bounded-degree monomial, zero or one, so a proof in the record variables replays with at most a constant factor increase in expanded sparse size and proof degree, apart from polynomial preparation. Over any finite probe its field axioms also become polynomial consequences of Boolean axioms. They can be treated as zero after squarefree Boolean normalization, so no dependence of the lower bound on the order of the field is needed. Equivalently, choose a fixed probe first and use the bounded-cost preparation of the resulting field axiom images. For the degree bound uniformly over probes, normalize each projected product in the Boolean quotient, where the image degree is at most a fixed times the original degree, and reintroduce the Boolean equations with at most fixed additive degree overhead per multiplication.

For clarity, these records with an unbounded number of named algebras and maps fit the linear-side erasure as follows. For each named space $W$ use a copy in each mode, and scalar lines $X_0,Y_0,Z_0$, with the two scalar actions $X_WY_0\to Z_W$, $X_0Y_W\to Z_W$, and $X_0Y_0\to Z_0$. The multiplication of a named algebra is its $X_WY_W\to Z_W$ entry. For each named map $f:W\to U$ add a private scalar line $Y_f$, let $X_WY_f\to Z_U$ be $f$, and use $X_0Y_f\to Z_0$ as an additional scalar multiplication. The anchor and scalar products show that all the right scalar witnesses equal a common $b$, and the three copies of $W$ have maps $a g_W,bg_W,abg_W$ for the left scalar $a$. Cancel these scalars using their witnessed inverses to recover exactly the records. The reverse lift uses $g_W$ at all three copies and scalar identities. All entries are then coordinate or constant, and the mode dimensions and the number of elementary product terms are linear in $n$. The ordered-path erasure gives a triangular associative algebra $U_{ij}$ for the joined record $(R_2^i,R_3^j)$, with the same connected strict support path and common format for all $i,j\in\{0,1\}$. All structure constants are $0,1$. Its center is precisely the scalar line: commuting with every diagonal vertex idempotent kills all off-diagonal coordinates, and commuting with the consecutive path arrows equates every diagonal entry.

### Two alternatives with a fixed ordinary tensor pair

Take the multiplication tensors of
$$
 U_{00}\times U_{11}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 U_{01}\times U_{10}.
$$
An isotopy normalizes to an algebra isomorphism using the unit identities of the ordered-path theorem. On the two-dimensional centers an algebra isomorphism either keeps or swaps the two factors. Keeping the factors would require a transporter for the mod-3 records; swapping would require a transporter for the mod-2 records. Both are impossible over any algebraic closure. The tensors have side $\Theta(n)$. Their rank is at least the side by unitality and at most a fixed multiple of the side by the linear number of elementary products. Rank is unchanged at this scale across all characteristics.

For the lower bounds, if the field has characteristic different from two, match factors by the swap so that all mod-3 records agree. In characteristic two, keep the factors and all mod-2 records agree. On the differing records use the monomial projections above, forward on one factor and inverse on the other. On agreeing records use identities. The substitutions for the algebra and isotopy witnesses are coordinate or constant in these projected record variables. Each original ordinary tensor axiom has a bounded-degree polynomial-size proof after substitution. This follows from the linear-size erasure's coordinate lift and the fact that the fixed factor permutation gives a direct-product isomorphism whenever the two chosen block maps are isomorphisms. The final substitution is monomial of bounded degree, so it transfers the degree and sparse lower bounds. If finite-field axioms are present their images are zero in the Boolean quotient. Subsequent multiplications by any projected variable use only its bounded monomial number of factors and Boolean normalization. This preserves the degree estimate with absolute constants. For sparse size with any fixed finite field, their monomial images derive from the Boolean equations with $O(q^2)$ preparation per used coordinate. The same sparse lower bound is uniform in the field order by replacing every field-axiom line by zero in the Boolean quotient and expanding each normalized multiplication: each projected proof monomial normalizes to zero or one monomial, reduction of repeated variables in a multiplication uses only boundedly many new repeated factors, and the only initial images that need preparation are the raw action and inverse lines.

For the short upper certificates, use $K,L$ for the normalized algebra isomorphism and its inverse. To derive preservation of the center in PC, apply multiplicativity to each equation $c L(y)=L(y)c$ for a source central basis element $c$, and use the inverse equations. The defining linear center equations then give the center coordinates precisely. Both center maps have inverse and multiplication equations. Their two-by-two diagonal-coordinate matrices have row and column sums one and idempotent entries. Thus the forward one has the form
$$
 \begin{pmatrix}s&1-s\\1-s&s\end{pmatrix},\qquad s^2=s.
$$
These equations have bounded-degree derivations with polynomial sparse size. For the branch $s=1$, adjoin the temporary input $1-s=0$. In that system the block restrictions of $K,L$ give two algebra isomorphisms between identically placed factors: multiply any $x=e_i x$ by the transported central idempotent, and use preservation of multiplication. The block restrictions inherit the inverse equations since the off-factor entries vanish. Apply the ordered-path readout inside either factor, then the record readout, to derive the mod-3 one-hot equations. For the branch $s=0$, adjoin $s=0$ and use exactly this argument with the factors swapped, deriving the mod-2 equations. All branch preparations have a polynomial number of bounded-degree PC lines. Their equations are in the ideal of the original equations and the one temporary branch equation, with polynomial-size multiplier circuits: record each PC line as a vector of circuit coefficients in the given input equations, reusing earlier gates at every addition or multiplication. Substituting these expressions into the group-algebra certificates gives Hilbert-like IPS circuits of polynomial size on both branches. Multiply the first certificate by $s$, the second by $1-s$, and add. The terms using the temporary equations have a factor $s(1-s)$, which is itself a polynomial-size ideal consequence of the original equations. This eliminates both temporary inputs and gives the claimed ordinary-tensor IPS circuit.

The same argument gives PC degree $O(n)$. On each branch replay the degree-$O(n)$ group-ring calculation with the bounded-degree readout substitutions, expanding products as needed. To remove the temporary axiom, multiply every line in the first expanded branch proof by $s$, and every line in the second by $1-s$. This preserves linear combinations and variable multiplications with a degree increase bounded by the degrees of these two polynomials. The extra input in each weighted proof is eliminated by the bounded-degree derivation of $s(1-s)=0$. Add the final weighted constants to derive one. This gives the matching upper bound on degree and completes the proof. $\square$

The published ordinary TI lower bound already has linear side-scale degree over every field, and the earlier characteristic-zero sparse construction already has exponential size over fields of characteristic different from two. The theorem here combines both characteristic classes in a single $0,1$ pair, keeps tensor rank linear, and supplies polynomial-size Hilbert-like IPS circuits uniformly over all coefficient fields. This strengthens the raw size comparison for a structured family and leaves the general worst-case exponent in the ambient proof-variable count open.
