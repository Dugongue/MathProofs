# Parallel solvable radicals of quotient permutation groups

**Theorem.** Let $K\unlhd G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(n)$ be given by lists of generating permutations. There is a deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$ algorithm that returns generators for the preimage of $\operatorname{Rad}(G/K)$, the largest solvable normal subgroup of $G/K$. It also constructs a faithful permutation representation of $(G/K)/\operatorname{Rad}(G/K)$ of polynomial degree, given by the images of the supplied generators of $G$.

**Corollary.** If $G/K$ is Fitting-free, meaning it has no nontrivial abelian normal subgroup, there is a deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$ algorithm returning generators for the preimage in $G$ of $\operatorname{Soc}(G/K)$. This answers Problem 12.4 exactly under its Fitting-free hypothesis. The radical theorem also answers the open $\mathsf{NC}$ solvable radical question noted in the same paper's introduction. [Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar, §§1 and 12](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039v4)

## The representation kernel

Use the $\mathsf{NC}$ good-series algorithm for $G$ above $K$ to obtain a series of $G$-normal subgroups $K=N_0\leq\cdots\leq N_r=G$. Each layer $L_i=N_i/N_{i-1}$ is abelian or a product of nonabelian finite simple groups. In the latter case the algorithm also returns generators for the preimages $T\leq N_i$ of the simple factors and explicit permutation representations of the individual factors of polynomial degree. These are homomorphisms on $T$, with kernel $N_{i-1}$, from which we may compute the images of the supplied generators of $T$. [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Lemma 3.14](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf)

Write $\bar G=G/K$ and $\bar N_i=N_i/K$. Let $U\leq\bar G$ be the intersection of the kernels of its conjugation actions on all nonabelian $L_i$. We claim $U=\operatorname{Rad}(\bar G)$. If $R\unlhd\bar G$ is solvable, then the image of $R\cap\bar N_i$ in a nonabelian layer is a solvable normal subgroup of a product of nonabelian simple groups, and therefore trivial. As $[R,\bar N_i]\subseteq R\cap\bar N_i$, this proves $R\subseteq U$. Conversely, every element of $U\cap\bar N_i$ acts trivially by conjugation on $L_i$ when the latter is nonabelian and therefore projects to the center of $L_i$, which is trivial. The series obtained by intersecting $U$ with the $\bar N_i$ thus has trivial or abelian layers. Hence $U$ is solvable and normal in $\bar G$, proving the claim.

We construct an explicit permutation action of $G$ with polynomial degree whose kernel is the preimage of $U$. It is enough to construct one such action per nonabelian layer that detects exactly its conjugation action. Fix a layer $L=N_i/N_{i-1}$. The natural conjugation action by $G$ on $L$ permutes its simple factors. Make the following marked objects for each factor $S=T/N_{i-1}$ using its faithful permutation representation of degree $m=\operatorname{poly}(n)$.

- If $|S|\leq m$, enumerate the entire represented group using repeated products, duplicate removal and compaction, with $\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$ rounds. For each element choose a preimage in $T$ by the published $\mathsf{NC}$ homomorphism-lifting algorithm. The marked objects are the resulting cosets of $N_{i-1}$. Each nonidentity object's orbit under $G$ is contained in the union of the isomorphic factor groups as sets and hence has polynomial size. This works also if the given representation is regular.
- If $|S|>m$, choose a moved point in the representation and let $H<S$ be its stabilizer. Its orbit length is greater than one and at most $m$, so $H$ is proper and nontrivial. Its preimage $\widetilde H\leq T$ is computable in $\mathsf{NC}$ as the point stabilizer of the action of $T$ through the given representation. Use $\widetilde H$ as the marked object. Its conjugates in $G$ lie over conjugate images of $H$ in the various isomorphic simple factors. The orbit within one factor has size at most $[S:N_S(H)]\,|\operatorname{Out}(S)|$, by breaking $\operatorname{Aut}(S)$ into outer cosets of $\operatorname{Inn}(S)$. This is at most $m\cdot 2\log_2|S|$ by the outer-order bound. Since $|S|\leq n!$ and the factor count is at most $\log_2(n!)$, the orbit has polynomial size.

The small-group operations are the published parallel enumeration and lifting procedures. For the first bullet, an enumeration by products includes the inverse generators and identity, and all shortest Cayley paths have length below $|S|$. In the second bullet, equivalently compute the stabilizer in the represented group and pull it back, using LIFT on the supplied stabilizer generators and adding the denominator generators. [Kantor–Luks–Mark, §§2.2, 3.2 and 7.2](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf) · [Kohl, Theorem 1](https://stefan-kohl.github.io/preprints/outbound.pdf)

## Listing the marked orbits and computing their action

Here is an explicit construction for the orbit of any marked object $x$ above, when given a polynomial upper bound $B$ on its orbit length. Add an identity generator if needed so the generator list is nonempty. Attach paired generator and inverse ports to each vertex of the orbit, where the port for a generator is paired with the port for its inverse. These ports give a connected regular undirected Schreier multigraph in which the reverse port is determined by the port alone. This is the consistent-labeling case of Reingold's universal traversal construction. For each possible size $1\leq b\leq B$, compute the universal traversal word for size $b$ with this port pairing. The word has polynomial length in unary $b$ and the generator count and is logspace-constructible. For the singleton case the identity suffices. Evaluate every prefix on the input generators with balanced permutation-product circuits. Apply each prefix permutation by conjugation to $x$. The candidates stay in the orbit, and the prefix list at the correct orbit size visits all of it. The construction does not need a list of the vertices or the true orbit size in advance. [Reingold, Theorem 5.4](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/sl.pdf#page=15)

Remove duplicates by checking every pair of marks in parallel. Two subgroups given by permutation generators are equal exactly when each one's generators belong to the other. Two object cosets are equal exactly when their representative difference belongs to $N_{i-1}$. These are standard parallel membership tests on actual permutations. Retain the first candidate for each distinct object and compact by parallel prefix sums. For each input generator and each retained object, conjugate and find its uniquely equal object in the list. This is an explicit action by permutations on the orbit. Over all marked objects use a disjoint union of the resulting orbit actions, allowing independent copies of the same orbit. The index bound $B$ can be one fixed polynomial in the original input length, because the degree bounds of the published factor representations are uniform. For variable-length lists use padded arrays and validity flags. The universal words are logspace-computable from their parameters, so evaluating all possible parameter choices in parallel and selecting the required ones also gives uniform $\mathsf{NC}$. [Babai–Luks–Seress, Theorem 1.2](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf)

The action on these marks detects an automorphism of the layer exactly. Trivial action on the layer fixes every mark by definition. Conversely an automorphism of $L$ that fixes every mark must preserve each simple factor, since each contains a marked nonidentity coset or a marked nontrivial subgroup, and different factors intersect trivially. For a small factor, all its elements are among the marks, so the restriction is the identity. For a large factor, the marks include all $S$-conjugates of the chosen proper nontrivial $H$, since $S$ is represented by elements of $G$ acting as inner automorphisms on that factor. Conjugation by $S$ on these conjugates is faithful. Its kernel is normal in the simple $S$, and its action is nontrivial because $H$ is not normal. Write this faithful action as $\rho$. If $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)$ fixes each conjugate of $H$, the permutation it induces on this set is the identity. The compatibility of conjugation with $\alpha$ then gives $\rho(\alpha(s))=\rho(s)$ for each $s\in S$, whence $\alpha(s)=s$. This proves that the constructed permutation representation for a layer has exactly the kernel of conjugation on the layer.

Finally use another disjoint union over the nonabelian layers. Elements of $K$ act trivially on all marks because each denominator contains $K$. The kernel in $G$ of the union action is the preimage of $U=\operatorname{Rad}(G/K)$. Recover its permutation generators by the parallel kernel algorithm. If every layer is abelian, take the trivial action and return $G$ as kernel. The degree of the union remains polynomial, and the construction has polynomial size and polylogarithmic depth. As in the companion ordinary permutation-group proof, logspace-uniform circuit composition can be made DLOGTIME-uniform by evaluating the wiring predicate in uniform $\mathsf{NC}^2$ and then the gates in parallel for polylogarithmically many rounds. [Babai–Luks–Seress, Theorem 7.1](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf#page=9)

If $G/K$ is Fitting-free, it has trivial solvable radical. A nontrivial finite solvable normal subgroup would contain a nontrivial abelian characteristic subgroup given by its last nontrivial derived subgroup, itself normal in $G/K$. Hence the constructed permutation group is an explicit faithful copy of $G/K$. Apply the known parallel socle algorithm to this Fitting-free permutation group. Pull the output generators back to $G$ by the parallel homomorphism-lifting algorithm and include the input generators of $K$. This computes the full preimage of the quotient socle. [Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar, Proposition 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039v4#S3) · [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Lemma 3.3](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf)

## Scope and provenance

The proof is informal. The solvable radical theorem covers arbitrary quotients of explicitly generated permutation groups, and the quotient socle corollary uses precisely the Fitting-free hypothesis of numbered Problem 12.4. The companion proof treats arbitrary ordinary permutation groups, including ones with abelian normal subgroups. Kantor and Luks gave sequential polynomial-time solutions and previously highlighted the general advantage of explicit permutation representations over quotient names. The contribution here is the parallel construction of a faithful permutation image modulo the solvable radical, using polynomially many conjugate marks and the universal traversal of a Schreier graph that need not be presented explicitly. A targeted literature check found the cited sequential and restricted parallel methods, and did not establish earlier publication of the stated parallel quotient result. [Kantor–Luks, *Computing in Quotient Groups*](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/quotient.pdf)

## Minimum faithful degree, optimal representations and normal centralizers

The explicit faithful model for Fitting-free $G/K$ lets one apply Theorem 1.2(b) of Levet, Srivastava and Thakkar. Computing the minimum faithful degree is therefore in deterministic $\mathsf{NC}$, and comparison with the input threshold answers their Problem 12.1.

The search part of Theorem 1.2(b) also becomes deterministic. The source identifies the transversal computation as its sole randomized operation, and uses it for the normalizers of simple direct factors in §10. The index of each such normalizer is the length of the orbit of that factor, at most the number of factors in the socle. For any $H\leq P$ given by permutation generators with a known polynomial bound on $[P:H]$, take the same pool of universal traversal prefixes described above, now acting by right multiplication on the right cosets of $H$. The paired generator and inverse ports again give the required consistent labeling. Each prefix is an actual permutation in $P$, and the family contains a representative for every right coset. Two representatives $u,v$ give the same right coset exactly when $uv^{-1}\in H$, an ordinary permutation-group membership test. Retain the first representative of each coset. This is the deterministic $\mathsf{NC}$ transversal computation used in our earlier proof, and supplies Problem 12.6 and the precise subroutine needed to derandomize Theorem 1.2(b). Applying the resulting search algorithm to the explicit faithful model of $G/K$ and composing the two representations returns the image of each input generator of $G$ in a minimum faithful permutation representation of $G/K$. [Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar, Theorem 1.2(b) and §§1, 10, 12](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039v4)

There is also a restricted form of Problem 12.2. Let $N/K\unlhd G/K$ be normal and Fitting-free, while $G/K$ is unrestricted. Write $R=\operatorname{Rad}(G/K)$. The intersection $R\cap (N/K)$ is a solvable normal subgroup of $N/K$, so is trivial. An element of $G/K$ centralizes $N/K$ exactly when its image in $(G/K)/R$ centralizes the image of $N/K$, since each relevant commutator already lies in $N/K$. The latter is the centralizer of a normal subgroup in the explicit permutation model constructed above. Compute it by the ordinary parallel centralizer algorithm, lift its generating permutations, and add the generators of the kernel. This covers all instances with Fitting-free ambient quotient, as a normal subgroup of a Fitting-free group is Fitting-free. The general Problem 12.2 still permits normal subgroups with nontrivial solvable radical. [Babai–Luks–Seress, Theorem 7.1(4)](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf#page=9) · [Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar, Problem 12.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039v4#S12)

The same transversal algorithm answers the Shonan quotient-transversal question. Given generators of $\overline H\le G/K$ by their permutation representatives, take $H$ generated by those representatives and generators of $K$. Then $H$ is the full preimage of $\overline H$ and $[G:H]=[G/K\colon\overline H]$. A transversal for $H$ in $G$ supplies a quotient transversal via those same representatives. The promise of polynomial index is preserved. [Shonan Meeting 246, printed p. 16](https://shonan.nii.ac.jp/docs/No.246.pdf#page=17)

## Nonabelian socle in the unrestricted quotient model

For arbitrary $Q=G/K$, write $R=\operatorname{Rad}(Q)$ and $P=Q/R$. The construction above gives $P$ explicitly as a Fitting-free permutation group. Apply Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar Proposition 3.1 to decompose $\operatorname{Soc}(P)$ into its nonabelian simple direct factors. For each factor $S$, use LIFT to compute its full preimage $\widetilde E\leq G$, by lifting generating permutations of $S$ and including the kernel generators of $G\to P$. Let $E=\widetilde E/K$. Compute $\widetilde D=K\cdot\widetilde E^{(\infty)}$, where the superscript denotes the last subgroup of the derived series, which is computable in $\mathsf{NC}$. Its image $D=\widetilde D/K$ is the last subgroup of the derived series of $E$, because all derived subgroup operations commute with surjective homomorphisms. The group $D$ maps onto the perfect group $S$. Keep it exactly when $|\widetilde D|=|K|\,|S|$. [Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar, Proposition 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039v4#S3) · [Babai–Luks–Seress, Theorem 7.1(3)](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf#page=9)

For each retained $D$, the projection $D\to S$ is an isomorphism. Within the preimage of $\operatorname{Soc}(P)$, each $E$ is normal and its last derived subgroup is characteristic in $E$, so the retained groups are normal in that preimage. Distinct retained groups have trivial intersection since they inject into distinct factors, and hence commute. Their product is direct since its projection is coordinatewise injective. Conjugation by $Q$ permutes the retained groups, and each product over a single orbit is therefore minimal normal in $Q$.

Conversely, a nonabelian minimal normal subgroup $M$ of $Q$ intersects $R$ trivially and commutes with it. It injects into $P$ with minimal normal image, since a proper nontrivial $P$-normal subgroup of its image would lift isomorphically inside $M$ to a proper nontrivial $Q$-normal subgroup. Its image is a product of simple factors of $\operatorname{Soc}(P)$. For each such factor $S$, the lift of $S$ inside $M$ is a simple group $S'$ and $E=R\times S'$. The last derived subgroup of this direct product is $S'$, since $R$ is solvable and $S'$ is perfect. Thus all factors generating $M$ are retained. Taking the union of their generating lists and the kernel generators gives the preimage in $G$ of the nonabelian socle of arbitrary $Q$, in deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$.
