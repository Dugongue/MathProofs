# Cubic equivalence with prescribed separable and inseparable extension requirements

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic three. Let $E,E'$ be finite étale $K$-algebras of the same positive dimension $s$, given by bases, and $t_1,\ldots,t_r\in K$. There are explicit formal cubics $f,g$ on $3s+r+1$ variables and ordinary multiplication tensors of split local commutative algebras such that over **every field extension** $L/K$, each of the following is equivalent to the other two.

1. The cubics are linearly equivalent over $L$.
2. Their multiplication tensors are ordinarily isomorphic over $L$.
3. $E\otimes_K L\simeq E'\otimes_K L$ as $L$-algebras, and $t_i\in L^3$ for every $i$.

The construction takes polynomially many base-field operations in the dense input and recovers witnesses for the cubic and algebra equivalences. Over $K=\mathbb F_q(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$ with $q$ a power of three and indeterminates $t_i$, the smallest extension can be forced to have a prescribed separable degree $m$ and purely inseparable degree $3^r$. For every $m\ge2$ this takes only $3\sum_{\ell\mid m}\ell^{v_\ell(m)}+r+1$ variables, where the sum runs over distinct prime divisors $\ell$. The total first extension degree is $m3^r$. The proof also works for infinite extension fields.

## Recover the étale algebra from polarization

Use three copies of $E$ with coordinates $x,y,z$ and set $C_E(x,y,z)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}(xyz)$. Over a separable splitting field with its primitive idempotent basis the cubic is $\sum_{j=1}^s x_jy_jz_j$. Its full polarization, defined by the coefficient of $uvw$ in the cubic evaluated on a sum of three vectors with coefficients $u,v,w$, is a direct sum of $s$ trilinear forms on three-dimensional spaces. Each summand is one on permutations of the three distinct basis vectors and zero elsewhere. The contraction radical is zero.

The centroid of a trilinear form $T$ on one space $W$ is the algebra of operators $D$ with $T(Da,b,c)=T(a,Db,c)=T(a,b,Dc)$. For the displayed direct sum, its centroid consists of independent scalars on the $s$ summands. To remove an off-diagonal block from summand $i$ to summand $j\ne i$, put $a$ in $i$ and $b,c$ in $j$. Then $T(a,Db,c)=0$ regardless of $D$, and conciseness of the $j$th form forces the block to vanish. In one summand test $a=c=e_i$, $b=e_j$ with $j\ne i$. The middle insertion vanishes, while the first insertion is the $e_k$ coefficient of $De_i$ for the third index $k$. This kills every off-diagonal entry. On a triple of distinct basis vectors the tests identify all diagonal entries. Over $K$, multiplication by any element of $E$ in all three copies gives a centroid operator, since the trace is balanced in the three product arguments of $C_E$. The defining equations are linear and commute with field extension, so the split calculation proves that $E$ is the full centroid over every extension of $K$.

An equivalence of the polarized trilinear forms conjugates their centroids. This recovers an $L$-algebra isomorphism $E_L\simeq E'_L$. Conversely an algebra isomorphism applied in all three copies preserves $C_E$, by invariance of the trace of multiplication under conjugacy. This proves exact reflection and lifting of the étale test.

## Add independent cube-root conditions

Introduce radical coordinates $u_0,\ldots,u_r$, with
$$
f=C_E+u_0^3+\sum_{i=1}^r t_i u_i^3,\qquad
g=C_{E'}+u_0^3.
$$
In characteristic three the polarizations forget every displayed pure cube. Their radicals are exactly the spaces of the $u_i$, and quotienting by the radicals gives the preceding test. Any cubic equivalence restricts to an equivalence on the radicals. In $g$ the restriction is a single cube. Under substitution, a cube has all its coefficients in $L^3$. Hence every $t_i$ must be a cube. Conversely if $t_i=b_i^3$ in $L$, the invertible shear $u_0\mapsto u_0+\sum b_i u_i$ carries the one cube to the desired radical polynomial. Combine it with the étale isomorphism on the quotient, applied independently of the radical. This proves the cubic criterion with explicit lifts and shows that an arbitrary mixed map on the full variables cannot bypass either test.

## Pass the two requirements through the relation $a^3=f$

For a cubic $h$ on based variables $V$, let $A_h$ be the symmetric algebra on $V$ and one new variable $a$, truncated above degree three, with $aV=0$ and $a^3=h$. Its multiplication is written on the fixed basis of the monomials in $V$ of degrees up to three and $a,a^2$. Its construction commutes with base change. For every field extension, its unique maximal ideal is the positive-degree ideal. In the associated graded algebra, multiplication from degree one into degree two has rank one exactly on the nonzero vectors of $La$ when $\dim V\ge2$. The annihilator of this line in degree one is $V$. An isomorphism therefore induces $S$ on $V$ and a scalar $\alpha$ on $a$. The last relation gives $S h=\alpha^3 h'$, so $\alpha^{-1}S$ transports $h$ to $h'$. The converse extends any cubic equivalence by the identity on $a$. No choice of cube root enters this equivalence between the cubic and the algebra. A three-map isotopy between the multiplication tensors of unital associative algebras normalizes to an algebra isomorphism by multiplication by the inverse units obtained from the two input images of $1$. Thus ordinary three-tensor equivalence gives the same criterion, even though the three mode actions are independent. ## Exact mixed degrees and Galois patterns

For $K=\mathbb F_q(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$ and each prime power $\ell^{v_\ell(m)}$ dividing $m$ maximally, put one copy of $\mathbb F_{q^{\ell^{v_\ell(m)}}}\otimes_{\mathbb F_q}K$ in $E$. Take $E'=K^s$ for the sum $s$ of these degrees. The constant extensions are fields because the $t_i$ are algebraically independent. Splitting $E$ over $L$ is equivalent to containing $\mathbb F_{q^m}K$, since the compositum of the prescribed finite constant fields has degree the least common multiple. The $t_i$ generate a purely inseparable field $I=K(t_1^{1/3},\ldots,t_r^{1/3})$ of degree $3^r$. For example, this degree follows by grouping polynomials in the $r$ independent cube roots according to exponents modulo three, which makes the $3^r$ residue monomials linearly independent over $K$. Their span is closed under multiplication and is a finite-dimensional domain, hence a field. The separable constant field and $I$ are linearly disjoint. Thus an extension works exactly when it contains their compositum, and that compositum is the least such extension, with the stated separable and purely inseparable degrees. The exponent $3^r$ already occurs with $s=1$ and no separable requirement by taking $E=E'=K$.

For $m=12$ and $r=2$, the construction uses $(4+3)\cdot3+3=24$ variables. Over $\mathbb F_3(t_1,t_2)$ the least extension is $\mathbb F_{3^{12}}(t_1^{1/3},t_2^{1/3})$, of degree $108$. The separable closure alone still fails to supply either cube root.

More generally take a finite Galois extension $M/K$ and a conjugation-invariant subgroup downset containing the trivial group. The previous finite Galois set construction gives equally sized étale algebras whose equivalence over any extension $L$ in a fixed algebraic closure is precisely membership of $\operatorname{Gal}(ML/L)$ in the downset under restriction to $M$. Applying the trace cubic construction and adjoining the radical cube terms gives the conjunction of that arbitrary separable condition with containment of $I$. The earlier diagonal trace-cubic method for the Galois pattern used characteristic different from two and three. The three independent trace input blocks allow centroid recovery here in characteristic three. Matching field type and extracting cube conditions do separate jobs in this proof. For every odd prime $p$, the same proof in degree $p$ gives the $p$th-power conditions and purely inseparable degree $p^r$. Replace the trace product by the trace of the product of $p$ independent arguments, and use the degree-$p$ relation $a^p=h$. In split coordinates, a polar summand is one on permutations of the $p$ distinct coordinate vectors. To kill a matrix entry from coordinate $i$ to coordinate $k\ne i$, test two occurrences of $i$ and omit $k$. Compare an insertion at the first $i$ with an insertion at an index different from $i,k$, which exists for $p\ge3$. The latter still has repeated $i$ and vanishes. This proves the same scalar-centroid calculation, and the Frobenius and field arguments proceed with $p$ in place of three.

The claims are written proofs. They concern simultaneous exact equivalence over each extension, with no bound on the field arithmetic for finding cube roots or solving the étale comparison when extension fields are presented computationally.
