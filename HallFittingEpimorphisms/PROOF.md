# Exact epimorphism counting along the Fitting derived series

Let \(G,H\) be given by Cayley tables, let \(H\) have at most \(d\) generators for fixed \(d\), and put \(F=F(H)\). Write \(F_i=F^{(i)}\), with \(F_\ell=1\), and let \(A_i=F_i/F_{i+1}\) as a module for \(P_i=H/F_i\) by conjugation. In each primary component of each nonfinal \(A_i\), assume every simple \(T\) in the socle occurs at most \(c_i\dim_{\operatorname{End}_{P_i}(T)}T\) times. The final abelian layer has no such hypothesis. If \(e=|G|/|H|\) is integral, the number of epimorphisms and a witness can be computed in time
\[
\operatorname{poly}(|G|+|H|)\,e^{\sum_{i<\ell-1}c_i}.
\]
Extra specified point values are allowed. In particular this is polynomial when the derived length and the \(c_i\)'s are bounded. A new unrestricted-final-layer case follows: the problem is in polynomial time for bounded-generator \(H\) whose Fitting subgroup is metabelian and a Hall subgroup. There is no restriction on the commutator structure or nilpotence class of that Fitting subgroup. This combines chief masks, marked affine lifting, and coinduced bounds.

Enumerate the quotient epimorphisms \(\psi:G\to H/F\) as in the earlier Fitting proof. The preimage of \(F\) is the intersection of the centralizers of the surviving chief sections, so at most \(|G|\) candidate kernels suffice. Isomorphisms of each quotient with \(H/F\) are found by guessing images of a fixed-length generating tuple. Fix generators \(h_j\) of \(H\) and enumerate their prospective preimage tuple \(x_j\) in \(G\), writing \(K=\langle x_j\rangle\). If \([G:K]>e\), reject the tuple, since \(f(K)=H\) for every compatible epimorphism. If \(|H|\) does not divide \(|G|\), return zero.

Given an onto map \(\phi:G\to P_i\) matching the anchors, write \(L=\ker\phi\). Its lifts to \(H/F_{i+1}\) form an affine system over \(A_i\), with the generator values and any specified values included. The homogeneous differences vanish on \(K\), and \(G=LK\). Their restrictions to \(L\) are equivariant homomorphisms and determine the differences everywhere. They factor through
\[
M_i=L/([L,L]\langle L\cap K\rangle^G).
\]
All the indicated conjugates are killed by equivariance. In particular \(|M_i|\le [L:L\cap K]=[G:K]\le e\).

For a finite primary component of \(A_i\) of exponent \(p^a\), put \(R=(\mathbb Z/p^a)[P_i]\). The module \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z/p^a}(R,\mathbb Z/p^a)\) is injective among finite modules of this exponent by its adjunction to character extension. Each simple \(T\) occurs in its socle with multiplicity \(\dim_{\operatorname{End}(T)}T\), by evaluation of homomorphisms at the identity. The socle hypothesis embeds the target component in \(c_i\) copies of this coinduced module: first embed its socle and extend by injectivity, with zero kernel because any nonzero kernel contains a simple submodule. The number of equivariant maps from the corresponding source component is therefore at most the \(c_i\)-th power of its order. Multiplying over primes gives at most \(e^{c_i}\) anchored affine lifts at stage \(i\). List them by Smith form, checking input promises or capping the enumeration at the theorem bound when needed. At the final layer count the whole affine solution set directly by Smith form without listing it. All lifts that reach the end are onto by the anchors. Divide the sum by \(e^d\) for a length-\(d\) tuple, since each actual epimorphism determines its quotient chain and occurs for exactly \(e^d\) tuples. When \(F=1\), the quotient maps are already the full maps and may be counted directly.

## Automatic module bound for metabelian Hall Fitting subgroups

Schur–Zassenhaus gives a complement \(Q=H/F\). Write the chosen generators of \(H\) as \(f_jq_j\) in that decomposition. The subgroup of \(F\) generated by the \(Q\)-conjugates of the \(f_j\) is \(Q\)-stable, and with \(Q\) it contains the generators of \(H\). It is therefore all of \(F\). Consequently \(A=F/F'\) is \(d\)-generated as a \(\mathbb Z[Q]\)-module. For every \(p\), \(A_p/pA_p\) is a quotient of \(d\) copies of the regular \(\mathbb F_p[Q]\)-module. It and \(A_p[p]\) are semisimple by coprimality, and have the same composition factors by the two exact sequences for multiplication by \(p\). The regular module has the coinduced multiplicity bound, so the nonfinal layer has \(c_0=d\). The final layer is \(F'\), which is abelian by the metabelian hypothesis and is counted directly. If \(F\) is abelian there is just the final layer. More generally the same argument works at every nonfinal layer if its primary module heads have at most \(c_i\) generators over their corresponding group rings and the action orders are coprime to the primary orders. This is an informal proof.
