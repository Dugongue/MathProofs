# Sharp cone counts and arithmetic on fixed hyperbolic three-spaces

The minimum positive number of projective-plane cone points is computable exactly for large families of product groups. In particular one fixed two-cone hyperbolic orbifold already supports arbitrary computably enumerable relations on integral winding numbers through extension of maps from closed hyperbolic three-manifolds. Products permit independent input coordinates and equality probes that synchronize their arithmetic without changing the optimal cone count.

## 1. Exact cone counts for products

**Theorem 1.** Let \(N_1,\ldots,N_t\) be closed connected three-manifolds and let \(\Gamma_i=\pi_1(N_i)\). Suppose that a field \(F\) of characteristic different from two satisfies
\[
\dim_F H_1(\Gamma_i;F)=\dim_F H_2(\Gamma_i;F)=d_i
\]
and that \(\Gamma_i\) can be generated by \(d_i\) elements. Among closed three-dimensional pseudomanifolds with fundamental group \(\prod_i\Gamma_i\) and a positive number of nonmanifold points, all with link \(\mathbb{RP}^2\), the minimum number of such points is
\[
2+2\sum_{i<j}d_i d_j.
\]
The same minimum is attained by the underlying space of a compact hyperbolic three-orbifold. For characteristic two the hypotheses instead give the same upper bound and the lower bound \(2\max(1,\sum_{i<j}d_i d_j)\).

**Proof.** Write \(s=\sum_{i<j}d_i d_j\). The field Künneth formula for classifying spaces gives \(b_1=\sum_i d_i\) and \(b_2=\sum_i d_i+s\) for the product group. The cone bound gives \(2k\ge 2(1+s)\) in characteristic different from two and \(2k\ge2s\) in characteristic two. Our minimum is over positive numbers, so always \(k\ge1\). [Prior cone bound, Theorem 1](TwoConePoints.md)

Take the connected sum \(N_1\#\cdots\# N_t\). Its fundamental group is the free product. Choose the generating set in each factor and impose that each generator of \(\Gamma_i\) commute with each generator of \(\Gamma_j\) for \(i<j\). Exactly \(s\) relators are used. The quotient is the direct product, by its universal property. The hyperbolic realization with \(s\) added relators of a closed three-manifold group uses exactly \(2(s+1)\) cone points. This attains the asserted upper bound. \(\square\) [Prior hyperbolic realization, Theorem 2](TwoConePoints.md)

Let \(H\) denote the integral upper unitriangular \(3\times3\) group. Its real counterpart is diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb R^3\), and \(H\) is a cocompact lattice, as is seen by reducing the three coordinates to a bounded fundamental box. The quotient \(N_H\) is a closed oriented aspherical three-manifold, with \(H\) generated by \(a=I+E_{12}\) and \(b=I+E_{23}\). Its abelianization is \(\mathbb Z^2\). Thus \(d=2\) over every field, by Poincaré duality and asphericity. For every \(r\ge1\) the sharp positive cone count for \(H^r\) is
\[
2+8\binom r2=4r(r-1)+2.
\]
More generally, if \(q_1,\ldots,q_s\) share an odd prime divisor \(p\), use lens spaces and \(F=\mathbb F_p\) to get the sharp count
\[
c(H^r\times C_{q_1}\times\cdots\times C_{q_s})
=2+8\binom r2+4rs+2\binom s2.
\]
Indeed each cyclic group \(C_{q_i}\) is one-generated, and its standard alternating cyclic resolution gives dimension one in homological degrees one and two over \(\mathbb F_p\). A trivial factor has \(d_i=0\) and leaves the formula unchanged. A nontrivial finite group with vanishing first homology over the chosen field does not satisfy the generator hypothesis.

## 2. Realizing group probes by one fixed hyperbolic source

**Lemma 2.** Let \(Y\) be a connected based finite CW complex whose fundamental group \(G\) is nilpotent. Fix a finite tuple of words in formal generators \(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_e\). There is a closed orientable hyperbolic three-manifold \(M\) and a fixed piecewise-linear embedded bouquet \(C\subset M\) with one circle for every word in the tuple, such that:

1. Every based map \(M\to Y\) induces a unique assignment in \(G^e\). Its values on the based circles in \(C\) are the evaluations of the fixed words.
2. Every assignment in \(G^e\) comes from some based map. On a subbouquet \(A\subset C\), this map can be chosen to agree exactly with any given based \(f:A\to Y\) having the indicated word values.
3. After postcomposition with the classifying map \(Y\to K(G,1)\), the based relative homotopy classes represented by extensions of \(f\) correspond bijectively to the assignments satisfying those values. Extra conditions comparing specified based-circle values inside classifying spaces can be imposed on either side of the bijection.

**Proof.** Apply the perfect-kernel hyperbolic replacement to \(N=\#^{\,e}(S^2\times S^1)\). It gives a continuous map from a closed orientable hyperbolic \(M\) to \(N\) inducing a surjection to \(F_e\) with perfect kernel. Compose with the classifying map \(N\to K(F_e,1)=\bigvee^e S^1\), giving \(p:M\to\bigvee^e S^1\). The image of a perfect group under a homomorphism is perfect, and any perfect subgroup of a nilpotent group is trivial, as its lower central series would otherwise be stationary and nonzero. Thus every \(G\)-valued homomorphism factors uniquely through \(p_*\). Choose a lift of every word to \(\pi_1(M)\). Representatives defining a map of a finite bouquet to \(M\) can be perturbed, fixing the vertex, to a piecewise-linear embedding by general position for a graph in a three-manifold. The perturbation preserves the based loop classes. Repetition of word values is allowed. [Perfect-kernel hyperbolic replacement, Theorem 1](DerivedHyperbolicTransfer.md)

Conversely, represent the assigned elements of \(G\) by based loops in \(Y\). The induced map from the wedge composed with \(p\) realizes the assignment. Its restriction to \(A\) is based homotopic to \(f\), because \(A\) is a bouquet and the loops give the same elements of \(G\). The homotopy extension property for the embedded subcomplex \(A\subset M\) gives exact agreement with \(f\). Finally, for connected based \(M\) and \(A\) the based mapping spaces into \(K(G,1)\) have contractible components, with components indexed by the homomorphisms on fundamental groups. This follows by lifting a based homotopy problem to the contractible universal cover and applying obstruction theory. Thus the relative based extension space to a specified map on \(A\) has one component per compatible homomorphism. All such homomorphisms here are realized by extensions into \(Y\), by the previous argument. Identical constraints on circle values carve out identical sets on both sides. \(\square\)

## 3. Fixed two-cone targets and independent input winding numbers

**Theorem 3.** Fix \(r\ge1\), and choose once and for all a compact hyperbolic three-orbifold \(O_r\) with group \(H^r\) for its underlying space \(Y_r=|O_r|\), attaining the sharp positive cone count of Theorem 1. For every computably enumerable relation \(R\subseteq\mathbb Z^r\), there is a closed orientable hyperbolic three-manifold \(M\) with a fixed based embedded subbouquet \(A\), and \(r\) marked circles in \(A\), so that \(R\) is exactly a constrained extension relation for maps \(A\to Y_r\). Only the maps on these \(r\) circles vary. On circle \(i\), the input integer \(n_i\) is a winding number around a fixed loop supported in factor \(i\) on fundamental groups. The constraints consist of a fixed finite list of equalities of based loop classes after fixed maps \(Y_r\to N_H\). With these probes, the based extension classes after the first Postnikov truncation are in bijection with the Diophantine witnesses for a fixed equation defining \(R\).

For \(r=1\) the constraints may be discarded, leaving just the ordinary extension problem for the fixed pair \((M,A)\) and the fixed two-cone target \(Y_1\). Choosing \(R\) computably enumerable complete gives an ordinary computably enumerable complete extension problem in which only the winding number of one fixed circle varies.

**Proof.** Put \(z=[a,b]=I+E_{13}\), with \([x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}\). Direct matrix multiplication gives
\[
[a^u,b^v]=z^{uv},\qquad z^u z^v=z^{u+v}.
\]
Commuting with both \(a\) and \(b\) is exactly the condition of being a power of \(z\). Thus a wire of an arithmetic circuit is represented by \(z^w\). To implement multiplication without requiring initially that the lift variables be normalized, take \(U,V\in H\) and impose
\[
[U,a]=[V,b]=1,\qquad [U,b]=z^u,\qquad [a,V]=z^v,\qquad [U,V]=z^w .
\]
The first four conditions mean \(U=a^u z^\alpha\), \(V=b^v z^\beta\), for arbitrary integers \(\alpha,\beta\). The last holds exactly when \(w=uv\). Addition and named integer constants use the first two identities directly. This is the rank-one integral commutator realization of the bilinear pairing used in Manin's arithmetic reduction. The equation interpretation works integrally because there is a primitive central generator \(z\).

By the Matiyasevich–Robinson–Davis–Putnam theorem there is one integral polynomial \(P(n_1,\ldots,n_r,x_1,\ldots,x_\ell)\) such that \(\mathbf n\in R\) if and only if \(P(\mathbf n,\mathbf x)=0\) for some \(\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z^\ell\). For the signed version, apply the theorem to the computably enumerable relation on pairs of nonnegative integers \((u_i,v_i)\) given by \((u_i-v_i)_i\in R\). Replace each nonnegative variable by a sum of four integer squares, adjoin \(n_i=u_i-v_i\), and combine the equations by a sum of squares. Compile \(P\) to a fixed list of integer addition and multiplication gates, with wire variables including the unknowns, and impose that the output be zero.

All arithmetic takes place in factor 1. For every formal generator used for a wire or auxiliary there, impose that its projections to all other factors be the identity. Such constraints can be read topologically. For each \(i\) the abstract projection \(p_i:H^r\to H\) is induced by a fixed based map \(Y_r\to N_H=K(H,1)\). So are the homomorphisms \(\alpha_i,\beta_i:H^r\to H\) obtained by projecting to factor \(i\) and then retracting \(H\) to \(\langle a\rangle\), respectively \(\langle b\rangle\), by killing the other generator and \(z\). For each multiplication auxiliary impose \(p_1(U)=\alpha_1(U)\) and \(p_1(V)=\beta_1(V)\). Consequently \(U=a^u\) and \(V=b^v\) in factor 1, with no residual central shifts. For input \(i\), include a formal generator \(T_i\) fixed by a boundary circle to \(z_i^{n_i}\), where \(z_i\) is the copy of \(z\) supported in factor \(i\). Give the factor-1 input wire \(S_i\) the constraint \(p_1(S_i)=p_i(T_i)\). Every comparison just listed is between based loop classes in the single fixed aspherical target \(N_H\).

Use Lemma 2 with one formal generator for each marked frame element, external input, internal circuit wire and multiplication auxiliary. Its word tuple contains the named generator loops, all the group-equation words in the circuit, and the generator loops needed for the projected comparisons. Place in \(A\) the loops pinning the frame and the raw inputs, and the loops for the circuit equations. On equation loops prescribe the constant map to the base point. Use fixed loops representing the other named values. The \(i\)-th raw input loop is mapped by an \(n_i\)-fold winding around a fixed representative of \(z_i\). The relation \(P=0\) is now equivalent to the existence of an extension satisfying the projected comparisons. With the normalized auxiliaries, every group-generator image is forced once \(\mathbf x\) is supplied, and every witnessing \(\mathbf x\) supplies a unique assignment. Lemma 2 identifies these assignments exactly with the first-stage relative classes of the extensions. The proof uses the actual topological map extension to \(Y_r\), while the stated bijection concerns its first Postnikov truncation.

For \(r=1\) use the input as the circuit wire directly. There are no off-factor restrictions or synchronization; omit the two retraction comparisons on each pair of multiplication auxiliaries. Their central shifts do not affect satisfiability. Thus ordinary extensions exist exactly for the integers in \(R\), and their first-stage relative classes have precisely the extra free central shifts described above. All spaces, embeddings and constant loop maps are fixed. In the piecewise-linear input model one gives the boundary map by a finite loop word, or subdivides the input circle to represent that word simplicially. The map \(n\mapsto f_n\) is computable, and membership in the ordinary extension problem is semidecidable by enumerating assignments in \(H^e\) and testing the finite word equations. This yields the stated completeness. \(\square\)

The same gate construction compiles any finite system for any integral bilinear map \(B\) by implementing the sums of coordinatewise products and the fixed integral linear combinations of the coefficients of \(B\). Gate equations are exact in both directions, and the auxiliary central coordinates can again be normalized by the projections. In this uniform variant the number of variables and the marked source pair can depend on the input circuit, while the two-cone target is fixed.

There is an exact distinction between varying one circle in a larger marked graph and using a graph consisting of just one circle. For \(r=1\), if the entire prescribed graph \(A\) is one circle, Lemma 2 gives a single equation in \(H\), with an arbitrary input constant as its right side. That problem is decidable by Duchin–Liang–Shapiro. Their theorem applies even when the source word is allowed to vary. The hard family above retains additional circles with fixed images. [Duchin–Liang–Shapiro, *Equations in nilpotent groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.2471)

## 4. Complete undecidability with uniformly solvable rational and finite probes

**Theorem 4.** There exist a fixed closed orientable hyperbolic three-manifold \(M\), a fixed embedded based bouquet \(A\subset M\), and a fixed compact hyperbolic three-orbifold with underlying space \(Y\) and exactly two projective-plane cone points, such that the following hold. Only the winding of one marked circle varies in the input map \(f_n:A\to Y\), for \(n\in\mathbb N\).

* The set of \(n\) admitting an extension \(M\to Y\) is computably enumerable complete.
* For **every** input the map extends after postcomposition with the classifying probe of the rational Mal'cev completion of \(\pi_1(Y)\).
* For **every** input there is one compatible assignment of extensions at all finite quotient probes of \(\pi_1(Y)\). More precisely, all the finite-quotient extensions are obtained, up to based relative homotopy after fixing their boundary maps, from a single extension into the classifying space of the profinite completion considered as an abstract group.

**Proof.** Use \(Y=Y_1\) and pick a fixed integral polynomial \(P(n,\mathbf x)\) for a computably enumerable complete set. Set
\[
Q(t)=(2t-1)(t^2-13)(t^2-17)(t^2-221),\qquad
D(n,\mathbf x,t)=P(n,\mathbf x)Q(t).
\]
The polynomial \(Q\) has no integer root. Its rational root \(1/2\) is explicit. It has a root in \(\mathbb Z_p\) for every prime \(p\). When \(p\) is odd and different from \(13,17\), the elements \(13,17,221\) are units. At least one is a quadratic residue modulo \(p\), since the product of two nonresidues is a residue. A square root of such a unit is simple modulo \(p\) and lifts to \(\mathbb Z_p\). At \(p=13\) use \(17\equiv 2^2\pmod {13}\). At \(p=17\) use \(13\equiv 8^2\pmod {17}\). At \(p=2\) the integer \(17\) is a square in \(\mathbb Z_2\), since \(17\equiv1\pmod8\), or by the valuation form of Hensel's lemma applied at \(1\). Thus a single \(t\in\prod_p\mathbb Z_p=\widehat{\mathbb Z}\) satisfies \(Q(t)=0\). This \(t\) is independent of \(n\). The product of the three quadratic factors is a standard intersective polynomial. [Lê–Spencer, §2](https://home.olemiss.edu/~leth/papers/intersective_polynomials_II_2.pdf)

Use the ordinary, rank-one construction of Theorem 3, now for the circuit \(D=0\) rather than \(P=0\). Over \(\mathbb Z\) it has solutions exactly when \(P(n,\mathbf x)=0\), since \(Q\) never vanishes there and the integers form an integral domain. The group identities implementing the circuit hold over every commutative coefficient ring \(S\) in the unitriangular group \(H(S)\). In fact write \(a^u=I+uE_{12}\), \(b^v=I+vE_{23}\), \(z^w=I+wE_{13}\) for \(u,v,w\in S\). Thus every ring solution gives a compatible group assignment directly, regardless of extra group solutions that might occur over some rings. For every \(n\) there is a ring solution over \(\mathbb Q\) with \(t=1/2\) and one over \(\widehat{\mathbb Z}\) with the chosen \(t\). In both choose \(\mathbf x=0\).

The uniquely divisible group \(H(\mathbb Q)\) is the rational Mal'cev completion of \(H\). In upper-entry coordinates \((x,y,z)\), its power map is \((x,y,z)^m=(mx,my,mz+\binom m2xy)\), which has a unique inverse for positive \(m\) over \(\mathbb Q\). A sufficiently divisible power of every triple of rationals has all entries integral. The profinite completion of \(H\) is \(H(\widehat{\mathbb Z})\). To see this, a map to any finite quotient kills \(a^m,b^m,z^m\) for one \(m>0\). These powers generate the coordinate congruence kernel of \(H\to H(\mathbb Z/m)\), by the normal form \(a^x b^y z^{z'-xy}\) for a matrix with coordinates \((x,y,z')\). Congruence quotients are therefore cofinal among all finite quotients, and their inverse limit is \(H(\widehat{\mathbb Z})\).

The appropriate classifying probes \(Y\to K(H(\mathbb Q),1)\) and \(Y\to K(H(\widehat{\mathbb Z}),1)\) are induced by the two group embeddings. For the second probe use the underlying abstract group to define the classifying space. Composing an assignment from the coordinate circuit with \(p_*:\pi_1 M\to F_e\) gives a homomorphism that matches the probed boundary values. Because \(A\) is a bouquet and hence a cofibration in \(M\), an induced map to the classifying space can be chosen to extend the prescribed boundary map exactly, by the argument in Lemma 2. The profinite assignment descends along each canonical finite quotient. Choosing the finite probe maps as these composites gives compatible relative homotopy classes, and arbitrary homotopic choices of the probes give the corresponding classes by the homotopy extension property. This proves all three assertions. \(\square\)

**Exact compatibility criterion.** For the same geometry, an actual extension exists if and only if there are rational and profinite probe extensions that induce the same based fundamental-group homomorphism after passage to \(H(\mathbb A_f)\), where \(\mathbb A_f\) is the finite adele ring. In fact \(\mathbb Q\) and \(\widehat{\mathbb Z}\) are subrings of \(\mathbb A_f\) with intersection precisely \(\mathbb Z\). Entrywise comparison gives \(H(\mathbb Q)\cap H(\widehat{\mathbb Z})=H\) inside \(H(\mathbb A_f)\). Thus a compatible pair has one integral group homomorphism and it matches the original boundary values. Lemma 2 realizes it by an actual extension. The reverse direction follows by composing any actual extension with both probes. In particular it is computably enumerable complete to decide whether the two kinds of solutions can be chosen to agree, even under the promise that both kinds of solutions exist separately.

**Scope and precedents.** Known arithmetic in Heisenberg groups supplies the elementary commutator mechanism. The conclusions here use the sharp product cone count and simultaneous fixation of the closed hyperbolic source, embedded pair, and hyperbolic orbifold target while only integer winding probes vary. The nilpotent hard target in the two-cone case is the underlying topological space of the orbifold. A smooth closed hyperbolic three-manifold target is aspherical with word-hyperbolic fundamental group, so its extension problem for the same graph-boundary input model reduces to decidable equations over a hyperbolic group. Allowing cyclic isotropy in an orbifold without projective-plane links is a different geometric condition. The proofs above are informal.
