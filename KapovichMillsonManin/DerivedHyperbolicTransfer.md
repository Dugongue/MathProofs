# Hyperbolic three-manifolds and the full homotopy theory of local systems

The perfect-kernel filling used for Kapovich–Millson Question 8.2 is induced by a degree-one map in the orientable case. It preserves homology with all local coefficients pulled back from the target. This gives an open-and-closed transfer of the entire derived Betti moduli stack and preserves its shifted symplectic form in the orientable case.

## 1. Hyperbolic replacement by a map preserving local coefficients

**Theorem 1.** Let \(N\) be any closed connected three-manifold. There are a closed hyperbolic three-manifold \(M\) of the same orientability type and a map \(f\colon M\to N\) such that:

1. \(f_*\) is onto on fundamental groups with perfect kernel normally generated by one element.
2. Write \(\Gamma=\pi_1(N)\), let \(U\to N\) be the universal cover, and let \(\widehat M\to M\) be the regular cover pulled back from \(U\to N\). The induced \(\Gamma\)-equivariant chain map \(C_*(\widehat M;\mathbb Z)\to C_*(U;\mathbb Z)\) is a chain homotopy equivalence over \(\mathbb Z[\Gamma]\). In particular \(f\) gives isomorphisms on homology and cohomology with every local system of abelian groups on \(N\).
3. If \(N\) is orientable, orient \(M\) compatibly on the common knot exterior in the construction. Then \(f\) has degree \(+1\).

**Proof.** Start with a standard unknot \(J\) in a ball in \(N\). Myers provides an excellent knot \(K\) ribbon concordant to \(J\), including for nonorientable \(N\). Both knots are nullhomotopic; their tubular neighborhoods are solid tori. Let \(E\) be the compact exterior of \(K\), \(\mu\) its meridian, and \(L=\ker(\pi_1(E)\to\Gamma)\). This is the normal closure of \(\mu\). Let \(\widehat E\subseteq U\) be the associated cover. We choose a framing longitude \(\lambda\) of \(K\) whose lift is zero in \(H_1(\widehat E;\mathbb Z)\).

Indeed, the concordance lifts into \(U\times[0,1]\) as a disjoint locally finite family of annuli, indexed by \(\Gamma\), since its core has trivial image in \(\Gamma\). Let \(\widehat W\) be the exterior of these annuli. The inclusions of the two lifted end exteriors induce isomorphisms on \(H_1\): compare the exact sequences of the ambient and exterior pairs. The ambient \(H_2\) maps are isomorphisms because the end inclusion into \(U\times[0,1]\) is a homotopy equivalence. Each relative \(H_2\) is a direct sum of copies of \(\mathbb Z\) on the transverse disks, with end maps matching the summands. This follows by tubular excision and the rank-two Thom isomorphism. The ambient \(H_1\) groups vanish. The resulting cokernel isomorphisms prove the claim. Extend the unknot's disk framing through the annulus, whose normal plane bundle is trivial because the core is nullhomotopic. The parallel annulus in \(\widehat W\) identifies the top longitude with the disk longitude of a lifted unknot. This proves \([\widehat\lambda]=0\) in \(H_1(\widehat E;\mathbb Z)\), equivalently \(\lambda\in[L,L]\).

For an integer \(n\) let \(M_n=E(\mu\lambda^n)\), the primitive filling of this slope. For all sufficiently large \(|n|\), \(M_n\) is closed hyperbolic by hyperbolic Dehn filling. If \(E\) is nonorientable, its orientation cover has two tori over the peripheral torus. Fill both lifted slopes, apply the orientable filling theorem, and use equivariant geometrization to give the extended free deck involution an invariant hyperbolic metric. The quotient is \(M_n\). Take such \(M=M_n\). The common exterior carries the same orientation character before and after filling because the peripheral subgroup maps trivially to the orientation character. [Myers, Theorem 1.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-8641(93)90038-F) · [Thurston, Chapter 5](https://library.slmath.org/books/gt3m/) · [Dinkelbach–Leeb, Theorem H](https://msp.org/gt/2009/13-2/gt-v13-n2-p15-p.pdf)

Here is an explicit map \(f\). It is the identity on \(E\). Write \(V\) for the old solid torus around \(K\), \(V'\) for the new filling solid torus, and identify their boundaries using the common peripheral torus in \(E\). On the first half of a collar of \(\partial V'\), use the boundary identification and radially contract the meridian circles of \(V\) until the image reaches its core \(K\). On the second half continue with a fixed nullhomotopy of \(K\) in \(N\), independent of the meridional coordinate of \(\partial V\). Set \(f\) constant on the rest of \(V'\). A piecewise-linear nullhomotopy has image contained in a two-dimensional polyhedron, and such a nullhomotopy can be used here. This defines a continuous map of pairs \((M,E)\to(N,E)\). On fundamental groups it induces the prescribed epimorphism, since it extends the quotient map on \(\pi_1(E)\). Its kernel is the quotient of \(L\) by the normal closure in \(\pi_1(E)\) of \(\mu\lambda^n\). In this quotient the image of \(\mu\) equals the inverse \(n\)-th power of an element in \([L,L]\), so it lies in the commutator subgroup of the kernel. The kernel is normally generated by the image of \(\mu\), which proves perfectness.

All the filling tori lift to separate copies indexed by \(\Gamma\) in \(\widehat M\) and \(U\), since the core of each filling torus has trivial image in \(\Gamma\). The lift of \(f\) is the identity on their common \(\widehat E\). Compare the two relative homology groups of these pairs. Excision identifies them with the direct sums over the tori of \(H_i(S^1\times D^2,S^1\times S^1;\mathbb Z)\), nonzero only in degrees \(2\) and \(3\).

In degree \(2\), a meridian disk of a new cap has boundary \([\widehat\mu]+n[\widehat\lambda]=[\widehat\mu]\). The classes of all lifted old meridians are linearly independent in \(H_1(\widehat E;\mathbb Z)\). To see this, use the same end-exterior isomorphisms from the concordance, which identify these classes with the meridians of separate unknots in lifted disjoint balls. For the bottom exterior they form a free basis by the relative pair sequence. The map from ambient \(H_2(U)\) to relative \(H_2\) is zero there, since its coordinates are intersection numbers with the nullhomologous lifted unknots. Thus the target boundary map in relative degree \(2\) is injective, and \(f\) identifies the individual classes of meridian disks.

In degree \(3\), choose a point of the interior of \(V\) away from its core and away from the image of the nullhomotopy. Its lifts detect the generators of the old relative groups by excision at these points. The only preimage of each such lift under \(\widehat f\) is in the first part of the corresponding new cap collar. On this region the radial construction is a homeomorphism onto the old solid torus minus the core and boundary. Its local degree is a unit, with sign \(+1\) when the ambient orientations agree on the exterior. To check the sign, the boundary map is the identity in coordinates from the common exterior and the radial parameter on the collar points inward in both caps. The remaining parts of all new caps avoid the chosen points by the choice of nullhomotopy image. Consequently in degree \(3\) the relative map identifies the corresponding free summands up to their orientation signs. In the orientable case the same point computes the ordinary degree \(+1\) of \(f\).

The homology sequences of the pairs now give that \(\widehat f\) is an integral homology equivalence. Its equivariant chain map is between bounded-below complexes of free \(\mathbb Z[\Gamma]\)-modules, since deck action on singular simplices is free. Its mapping cone is an acyclic bounded-below complex of projectives over this ring, hence contractible: split the surjection onto the bottom term and then split successively onto the cycles, which are projective by induction. This supplies the claimed chain homotopy equivalence. Tensoring with, or taking equivariant Hom into, any coefficient module proves the statement for local coefficients. \(\square\)

For orientable \(N\), degree-one hyperbolic imitations with homology isomorphisms on pullback covers have classical precedents. The argument above obtains the local-coefficient equivalence for the same knot filling used to transfer representation schemes, and applies to both orientability types. [Kawauchi, Corollary 4.3](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/8213/ojm26_04_02.pdf) · [Boileau–Wang method as presented by Cochran–Masters, Proposition 1.2](https://math.rice.edu/~cochran/virtualbetti8_8.pdf)

## 2. The universal property on mapping spaces

**Theorem 2.** For the map \(f\) of Theorem 1 and every target space \(T\), the map
\[
f^*\colon\operatorname{Map}(N,T)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Map}(M,T)
\]
is an equivalence onto the union of components whose induced fundamental-group homomorphism kills \(\ker f_*\). Here \(\operatorname{Map}\) denotes the derived mapping space of spaces. The statement includes nonconnected targets.

**Proof.** The domain manifolds are connected, so it suffices to fix one component of \(T\). For the first Postnikov stage \(B\pi_1(T)\), maps form the groupoid of homomorphisms with conjugation. The surjection \(f_*\) identifies homomorphisms from \(\pi_1(N)\) with exactly those from \(\pi_1(M)\) killing the kernel. Conjugation acts on these two sets in the same way. This proves the equivalence for stage one.

Induct along the Postnikov tower of \(T\). Above any map into stage \(j-1\), the obstruction to lifting to stage \(j\) is a class in degree \(j+1\) with the pulled-back local coefficients \(\pi_j(T)\). The class is natural under \(f\), and vanishing is equivalent because the relevant cohomology map is an isomorphism. If lifts exist, choose one. The homotopy groups of the space of lifts relative to that choice are the local-coefficient groups \(H^{j-i}(-;\pi_j(T))\) in homotopy degree \(i\), including the torsor description in degree zero. These descriptions follow from the twisted Eilenberg–Mac Lane fiber in the Postnikov fibration. They identify the two lift spaces under \(f^*\) by Theorem 1. Fiberwise equivalence over the preceding stage gives the result at stage \(j\). Finally, \(T\) is the homotopy limit of its Postnikov tower and mapping spaces into it commute with this limit. The selected union of components is defined at stage one, so the tower argument proves the asserted equivalence onto it. \(\square\)

## 3. Derived representation schemes and their forms

**Corollary.** Let \(G\) be a smooth affine complex algebraic group with a faithful finite-dimensional algebraic representation. The pullback
\[
\mathbf{Loc}_G(N)=\operatorname{Map}(N,BG)\longrightarrow
\mathbf{Loc}_G(M)=\operatorname{Map}(M,BG)
\]
identifies the source with an open-and-closed derived substack. It preserves all deformation and obstruction data there. If \(N\) is aspherical, these are the derived local systems of the classifying spaces of the fundamental groups. When \(N\) is oriented, the equivalence preserves the \((-1)\)-shifted symplectic structures transgressed from any chosen \(2\)-shifted symplectic form on \(BG\). For reductive \(G\) this includes the forms obtained from nondegenerate invariant symmetric bilinear forms on its Lie algebra.

On each test algebra, apply Theorem 2 to the space of points of \(BG\), including its components of torsors. The cotensor defining \(\operatorname{Map}(N,BG)\) is evaluated objectwise. The condition on fundamental groups is local on the test algebra and may be checked after trivializing the torsor. For a connective derived test algebra \(A\) the condition then says that the representation in \(G(\pi_0 A)\) kills the kernel. Here \(\pi_0 G(A)=G(\pi_0 A)\) by smoothness. On the classical representation scheme the condition defines an open-and-closed subscheme: its ideal \(I\) is generated by the entries of a matrix for the normal generator minus the identity, and the perfect kernel maps trivially into the abelian matrix congruence group modulo \(I^2\). Thus \(I=I^2\), so its vanishing locus is open as well as closed by finite generation and Nakayama. The invariant condition descends to the classical quotient stack. Open-and-closed derived substacks are obtained from these classical opens, since an open substack is specified by a condition on the truncation of test points. Theorem 2 identifies the derived points of this substack on every test algebra.

For the form, apply the mapping-stack construction of Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi. The form is obtained by pulling back the \(2\)-shifted form on \(BG\) by evaluation and integrating against the fundamental class of the manifold. On the identified substack, evaluation on \(M\) is evaluation on \(N\) composed with \(f\) in the manifold variable. Integration then agrees because \(f_*[M]=[N]\). This applies to the closed shifted form, as integration is defined from the orientation functional on cochains and is natural under the indicated pullback. [Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi, Theorem 2.5 and the Betti local-systems example](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.3209)

The derived statement concerns the Betti homotopy type of \(N\), including its higher cells. When \(N\) is not aspherical, replacing that Betti source by \(B\pi_1(N)\) changes the derived problem, though their classical representation stacks agree.
