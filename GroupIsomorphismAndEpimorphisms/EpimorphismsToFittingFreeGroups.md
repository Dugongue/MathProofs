# Polynomial-time epimorphism testing with a Fitting-free target

**Theorem.** Given Cayley tables of a finite group $G$ and a Fitting-free finite group $H$, one can decide whether $G$ surjects onto $H$ in deterministic polynomial time and in logspace-uniform $\mathsf{AC}^{3}$. One can also return an epimorphism and count all epimorphisms in polynomial time. The source $G$ is arbitrary. This answers Open Question 3(1) of Grochow, Srivastava, and Thakkar, which asks for a polynomial-time test when both groups are Fitting-free. [*Algorithms for Finite Group Epimorphism Testing*, §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)

A finite group is Fitting-free if it has no nonidentity abelian normal subgroup. This is equivalent to having trivial solvable radical, since the last nonidentity term in the derived series of a solvable normal subgroup is an abelian normal subgroup. In particular every normal subgroup of a Fitting-free group has trivial center, since its center is characteristic in it and hence normal in the ambient group.

## An exact list of all Fitting-free quotient kernels

Fix a chief series $1=G_0<G_1<\cdots<G_t=G$, a maximal normal series with all terms normal in $G$. For each nonabelian chief factor $N_i=G_i/G_{i-1}$, put
$$
 C_i=\{g\in G:[g,G_i]\subseteq G_{i-1}\}.
$$
This is the kernel of the conjugation action of $G$ on $N_i$. The normal subgroups $K$ for which $G/K$ is Fitting-free are exactly the intersections
$$
 K_S=\bigcap_{i\in S}C_i
$$
for subsets $S$ of the nonabelian chief-factor indices. The empty intersection is $G$. Different subsets may determine the same kernel.

For completeness of this list, take a normal $K$ with $H_0=G/K$ Fitting-free. The images of the $G_i$ in $H_0$, omitting repetitions, form a chief series. In detail, each $G_i/G_{i-1}$ maps onto the section of consecutive images with kernel normal in $G/G_{i-1}$. Minimal normality makes that map injective or trivial. Each surviving section is minimal normal in the corresponding quotient of $H_0$, since any normal subgroup lying strictly between the image terms would pull back to a $G$-invariant proper nontrivial subgroup of the original chief factor.

Let $S$ consist of the indices of the surviving nonabelian factors. The action of $G$ on each $N_i$ for $i\in S$ then factors through $H_0$, and agrees with the action of $H_0$ on the corresponding surviving section under the induced group isomorphism. In particular $K\subseteq K_S$. Now $K_S/K$ is a normal subgroup of $H_0$ centralizing every nonabelian chief factor of this series. It is solvable. Indeed, intersect it with the terms of the series. If a resulting section at a nonabelian chief factor is nontrivial, its image is the entire chief factor by minimality. The normal subgroup would then include representatives of all elements of that factor, so centralizing the factor would force the factor to be abelian. Thus all nontrivial sections of the restricted series are abelian, which gives solvability. As $H_0$ is Fitting-free, $K_S/K=1$ and $K=K_S$. The radical-as-intersection characterization at the heart of this argument also appears in recent work on Graph Isomorphism. [Hajiaghayi–JafariRaviz, §A.6.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01399v1)

For soundness, $N_i$ is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups, and the conjugation image $G/C_i$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(N_i)$ contains $\operatorname{Inn}(N_i)$. The latter has trivial centralizer in $\operatorname{Aut}(N_i)$. Indeed, if an automorphism $\alpha$ commutes with all inner automorphisms then conjugation by $\alpha(x)$ equals conjugation by $x$ for every $x\in N_i$. Triviality of the center gives $\alpha(x)=x$. A solvable normal subgroup of $G/C_i$ has trivial intersection with $\operatorname{Inn}(N_i)$, since a direct product of nonabelian simple groups has trivial solvable radical. The two normal subgroups commute when their intersection is trivial. Hence that solvable normal subgroup is trivial, so $G/C_i$ is Fitting-free. Finally $G/K_S$ embeds in the product of the $G/C_i$ for $i\in S$, surjectively onto each coordinate. A solvable normal subgroup of $G/K_S$ would have trivial image in each coordinate and would therefore be trivial. Thus every $K_S$ on the list gives a Fitting-free quotient.

## Algorithm and exact complexity

Each chief factor has size at least two, so $t\leq\log_2 |G|$. To find the chief series, start with $G_0=1$. In the current quotient $G/G_{i-1}$, compute the normal closure of each nonidentity element and choose one of smallest order. It is minimal normal, since a strictly smaller nontrivial normal subgroup would contain an element with smaller normal closure. Its full inverse image is $G_i$. Quotient tables and normal closures are computable in polynomial time from the Cayley table. For a normal closure, enumerate conjugates and close the resulting set under products and inverses until no new element appears.

Compute each $C_i$ by table scans. Enumerate all at most $2^{t_{\rm na}}\leq 2^t\leq |G|$ subsets, where $t_{\rm na}$ counts the nonabelian factors. Deduplicate the resulting membership vectors to get every Fitting-free quotient kernel exactly once. Construct the quotient table for each and compare it with $H$, first checking equality of orders. Use the polynomial Cayley-table isomorphism algorithm of Babai, Codenotti, and Qiao on each such pair. A successful isomorphism certifies an epimorphism by composing it with the quotient map. Conversely each epimorphism has a kernel on the list. [Babai–Codenotti–Qiao](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/28941)

## Constructing and counting the epimorphisms

The constructive Fitting-free isomorphism algorithm of Grochow, Johnson, and Levet uses a fixed diagonal product in the source socle and polynomially many possible image diagonal products in the target socle. For each such image it computes the twisted-code equivalences as a coset representative and subgroup generators. Each equivalence lifts uniquely to a group isomorphism. These are the outputs of their Theorem 4.3 and Lemma 7.14, assembled in Section 7.5. Taking a representative of any nonempty coset gives an explicit isomorphism. [Grochow–Johnson–Levet, Theorem 4.3 and §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19777v1#S7)

For $H$ against itself, the sets arising from distinct image diagonal products are disjoint, since each automorphism maps the fixed source diagonal product to a unique image. For each nonempty coset compute the order of its subgroup by the polynomial-time permutation-group order algorithm, using the generators output by twisted-code equivalence. The coset has that same cardinality, and unique lifting preserves it. Summing those cardinalities gives $|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|$ in polynomial time. Equivalently the representatives and the lifted coset-subgroup generators together generate $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$, after which one may compute its order directly as a permutation group on $H$.

For each successful kernel $K$, the epimorphisms $G\to H$ with kernel $K$ are in bijection with $\operatorname{Iso}(G/K,H)$, a torsor for $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$. Thus the total count is
$$
 \#\operatorname{Epi}(G,H)
 = |\{K\trianglelefteq G:G/K\cong H\}|\cdot|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|.
$$
The possible kernels are visited once each. The answer has polynomial bit length, because even the number of functions from $G$ to $H$ is at most $|H|^{|G|}$. This counting result concerns Fitting-free targets.

## Parallel decision

Write $n=|G|$. An input with $|H|>n$ can immediately be rejected. Represent every subgroup of $G$ by an $n$-bit membership vector. The chief series can be computed in depth $O(\log^2 n)$ and polynomial size with unbounded fan-in AND and OR gates. To find the next term above $G_{i-1}$, form the normal closures of $G_{i-1}\cup\{g\}$ for all $g\notin G_{i-1}$ in parallel. For each $g$, form the set consisting of $G_{i-1}$, all conjugates of $g$ and $g^{-1}$, and the identity. Repeatedly replace the set by its product with itself. Each replacement uses constant depth, and $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ repetitions suffice because the Cayley graph of the generated subgroup has diameter at most $n-1$. Pick an inclusion-minimal strictly larger closure, breaking ties by the least table label $g$. This test uses only comparisons of membership vectors and hence constant depth. Inclusion minimality gives a chief factor by the same argument as minimal order. Repeat for at most $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ stages, padding once the entire group has been reached.

For each $i$, check whether some two elements of $G_i$ have commutator outside $G_{i-1}$. This is a constant-depth test for the factor being nonabelian, and it excludes the padded repetitions. In constant depth compute $C_i$ by checking the commutator condition for every $g\in G$. For each $t$-bit subset indicator, compute $K_S$ by intersecting the selected $C_i$ and ignoring indicated abelian factors. All candidates can be computed in parallel in constant depth once the chief series is available. A separate constant-depth test verifies that $H$ is Fitting-free if its type is unpromised. Namely, it checks that no nonidentity element of $H$ has all its conjugates commuting pairwise. Such an element exists exactly when $H$ has a nontrivial abelian normal subgroup, because a set of pairwise commuting conjugates generates an abelian normal subgroup.

For each $K_S$, the least element in each coset gives a transversal by a constant-depth test. Obtain consecutive labels for those elements by their ranks in the transversal. Binary prefix sums of the membership bits are computable by parallel balanced addition in depth $O(\log n)$ with unbounded fan-in, since each binary addition on $O(\log n)$ bits uses constant-depth carry gates. This also checks that the number of representatives is $|H|$. Table lookup, taking the product representative, and converting its label to the new rank give a Cayley table on that many labels. Feed these tables and $H$ to the uniform $\mathsf{AC}^3$ Fitting-free isomorphism test of Grochow, Johnson, and Levet, whose circuit uniformity convention is DLOGTIME. Parallel OR of the order-matched accepted candidates decides epimorphism. All the additional gates can be indexed by stage counters, subset bits, and a constant number of element labels, with their connections generated in logarithmic space. The total is therefore logspace-uniform $\mathsf{AC}^3$. [Grochow–Johnson–Levet, Theorem 1.1 and §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19777v1#S1)

The original paper gives a polynomial-time algorithm for Fitting-free targets when the source has bounded abelian width as in its Corollary 2. Its concluding question specifically leaves arbitrary Fitting-free sources open. The intersections of chief-factor centralizers give the entire kernel list without a bound on the source's abelian width. [Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar, Corollary 2 and §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)
