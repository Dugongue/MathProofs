# Search and counting for all finite p-groups via Group Isomorphism decision

**Theorem.** For every prime $p$, given the Cayley tables of two finite $p$-groups, one can find an isomorphism, count all isomorphisms, and generate the automorphism group in deterministic polynomial time with an oracle for $p$-group isomorphism at that prime. The prime can vary. Every oracle query is on groups of polynomial order. The same statements hold for arbitrary finite nilpotent input groups, using only prime-power-order queries.

This solves the $p$-group and nilpotent-input cases of the search and counting questions posed by Arvind and Torán, using a same-prime decision oracle. Grochow and Qiao obtained polynomial reductions staying inside the class-two, odd-exponent-$p$ family. The extension here uses a same-prime marking construction and a lift algorithm that works through an arbitrary number of central layers. [Arvind–Torán, Problem 16](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.190/Mitarbeiter/toran/beatcs/column86.pdf) · [Grochow–Qiao, *On the Complexity of Isomorphism Problems for Tensors, Groups, and Polynomials IV*](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317v2)

## 1. Marking a list in the dual of the Frattini quotient

Let $G$ have order $n=p^b>1$, and put $V_G=G/\Phi(G)$. For any independent list of one-dimensional subspaces of $V_G^*$, its prescribed transport by a group isomorphism reduces to unmarked isomorphism of $p$-groups of order at most $n^{28}$. The reduction builds an elementary abelian module on which $G$ acts through $V_G$, then adds one cyclic generator of high order. The elements killed by a fixed power and their relative centralizers identify the original Frattini quotient and its mixed commutator with the module. That intrinsic commutator carries the marked lines. An isomorphism of the enlarged groups induces an actual isomorphism of the input groups with the prescribed action on the lines. Conversely, every such isomorphism extends to the enlarged groups. The accompanying proof gives the construction and the polynomial bound for all primes. [Same-prime marking theorem](SamePrimePGroupMarkers.md)

We use line marks when querying the oracle, then recover the exact scaling of a full basis. There are at most $(p-1)^{\dim V_G}\le n$ possible scales to try.

## 2. Subspace transport by a p-group acting in characteristic p

We use a deterministic transporter algorithm. Let $P$ be a $p$-group given as a faithful permutation group of degree at most $n$, and suppose its generators act linearly on an explicitly represented $\mathbb F_p$-space $D$. Given subspaces $S,T\subseteq D$, find an element of $P$ mapping $S$ onto $T$ or certify that none exists, and compute generators for the stabilizer of $S$. The time is polynomial in $n,p,\dim D$ and the representation length. The action on $D$ need not be faithful. The permutation representation keeps track of group elements throughout. This is a special case of Luks and Miyazaki's subspace transporter theorem. The following proof exploits the characteristic-$p$ action to give a direct construction. [Luks–Miyazaki, Proposition 3.17, Problem 22](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/531/download)

First consider the transporter of two vectors. A linear action of a $p$-group on a nonzero $\mathbb F_p$-space has a nonzero common fixed vector. Indeed the orbits of nonfixed vectors have size divisible by $p$, and zero is fixed, so the number of fixed vectors is a positive multiple of $p$. Intersect the fixed spaces of the generators to find a fixed line, repeat in the quotient, and obtain a complete invariant flag $0=F_0<F_1<\cdots<F_d=D$. The action on every one-dimensional successive quotient of this flag is trivial. Keep the current vector $x'$ equal to the target $y$ modulo $F_i$, and maintain the subgroup $P_i$ stabilizing $y$ modulo $F_i$. Beginning at $i=d$, the difference map
$$
 P_i\longrightarrow F_i/F_{i-1},\qquad g\longmapsto gy-y\bmod F_{i-1}
$$
is a homomorphism to the additive group. This follows from triviality of the action on $F_i/F_{i-1}$. Because $gx'-gy\equiv x'-y\pmod {F_{i-1}}$, matching the next quotient amounts to finding one additive value of this homomorphism. There are at most $p$ values. A scan of the generated image finds a representative for each value. If the needed value is absent, the transporter is empty. Otherwise move the vector and replace the subgroup by the kernel, for which Schreier's lemma gives generators using these representatives. Continuing gives a vector transporter and the stabilizer of $y$. The stabilizer of the source is its conjugate, and can in all cases be computed by the same routine with source and target equal. For an affine action on an $\mathbb F_p$-space, adjoin a constant coordinate $1$ and use the linear action on one extra dimension. After each Schreier step the generator list can be compressed using polynomial-time permutation group membership. Each retained generator at least doubles the order of the subgroup generated so far, and the ambient permutation group has order at most $n!$.

For subspaces, take a common fixed line $L\subset D$ and solve the transporter and stabilizer problem recursively in $D/L$. This recursion still returns original permutation group elements. If the projected subspaces cannot be matched, there is no transporter. Otherwise move the source by a projected transporter and restrict to the stabilizer of the common projection $U$. If exactly one subspace contains $L$, no match exists. If both do, each equals the full inverse image of $U$, so they already agree and the projected stabilizer is the full stabilizer. In the remaining case the two subspaces are graphs of linear maps $U\to L$ in an arbitrary splitting of its inverse image. Write one such graph as $x\mapsto x+\lambda(x)$. An element of the projected stabilizer acts on coordinates $(x,a)$ by $(A x,a+B x)$, because it fixes $L$ pointwise. Thus the action on the graph parameter is the affine action $\lambda\mapsto(\lambda+B)A^{-1}$. The vector algorithm decides the final match and computes its stabilizer. To compute a stabilizer alone, use the recursion with $S=T$. The subspace procedure can return the stabilizer of the source along with the transporter by obtaining the target stabilizer at the same recursive call and conjugating. Only one projected recursive call is required at each level, and the other operations there are a vector transporter and polynomial linear and permutation-group operations. The zero space serves as the base case. This proves the claimed polynomial bound. Standard permutation group membership and order here refer to deterministic algorithms on explicit permutations. [Furst–Hopcroft–Luks, *Polynomial-time algorithms for permutation groups*](https://doi.org/10.1109/SFCS.1980.34)

## 3. Lifting a specified Frattini quotient isomorphism

Given $G,H$ and an exact linear isomorphism $a:V_G\to V_H$, we can determine in deterministic polynomial time whether $a$ lifts to a group isomorphism, and find a lift when it does. We also compute, without any oracle, generators of the group of automorphisms of any given $p$-group acting trivially on its Frattini quotient.

Use the characteristic lower exponent-$p$ central series $P_1(G)=G$ and $P_{i+1}(G)=P_i(G)^p[P_i(G),G]$. It has $P_2(G)=\Phi(G)$. Each successive layer is elementary abelian and central in the corresponding quotient. The series strictly decreases until it reaches the identity. Indeed, if $P_i(G)$ is nontrivial, the dual of the nonzero space $P_i(G)/\Phi(P_i(G))$ has a nonzero vector fixed by conjugation by $G$, by the $p$-group argument above. The corresponding functional kills $P_i(G)^p[P_i(G),G]$ and proves strict decrease. There are at most $\log_2 n$ layers. Compute them by table scans and subgroup generation. If the layer orders differ between the two groups, no lift exists.

Work up the quotients from $G/P_2(G)$ and $H/P_2(H)$, starting with $a$. At one step write $X$ for the next quotient of one group, $Q$ for its preceding quotient, and $E$ for the central elementary abelian kernel of $X\to Q$. Here $E\subseteq\Phi(X)$, since each kernel is the image of a subgroup of $P_2(G)$ or $P_2(H)$. Choose a section $s:Q\to X$ with $s(1)=1$. Write the resulting extension as $(x,u)(y,v)=(xy,u+v+c(x,y))$ in $Q\times E$. The cocycle condition on $c$ follows from associativity. For scalar values in $\mathbb F_p$, compute $H^2(Q,\mathbb F_p)$ by linear algebra on all functions on $Q^2$, with one cocycle equation per triple and quotient by the coboundaries of functions on $Q$. All dimensions are polynomial in $n$. The scalar extension components give a linear map
$$
 \theta_X:E^*\longrightarrow H^2(Q,\mathbb F_p),\qquad \lambda\longmapsto[\lambda c].
$$
It is injective. If $\lambda\ne0$ lay in its kernel, passing to $X/\ker\lambda$ would split the central extension of $Q$ by a group of order $p$. The latter central kernel would then be a nontrivial direct factor and would not lie in the Frattini subgroup of the split group. But the image of $E\subseteq\Phi(X)$ lies in the Frattini subgroup of the quotient, by the powers-and-commutators formula. This is a contradiction. Write $S_X=\operatorname{im}\theta_X$.

For an isomorphism $f:Q_G\to Q_H$ of the preceding quotients, a lift $X_G\to X_H$ exists exactly when $f^*S_{X_H}=S_{X_G}$. Indeed, a lift needs an invertible map $j:E_G\to E_H$ satisfying $\theta_{X_G}j^*=f^*\theta_{X_H}$. Equality of spaces makes $j$ exist uniquely by injectivity, and the resulting cocycles agree up to a coboundary. Solve the corresponding scalar coboundary equations in each output coordinate and use the solution as the correction to the sections in the formula for the lift. This is explicit linear algebra. Conversely any lift gives those cocycle equations from preservation of products. The automorphisms inducing the identity on $Q$ automatically induce the identity on $E$ and are precisely the corrections by homomorphisms $Q\to E$. Their generators are obtained by solving the linear equations $h(xy)=h(x)+h(y)$ in the coordinates of $E$. This is the standard central-extension calculation, with injectivity used to eliminate the separate action on the kernel. [Grochow–Qiao, *Algorithms for Group Isomorphism via Group Extensions and Cohomology*](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1009767)

Suppose we have one isomorphism $f_0:Q_G\to Q_H$ inducing $a$ and generators for the group $B$ of automorphisms of $Q_H$ acting trivially on its Frattini quotient. These quotient maps indeed induce $a$ on the same Frattini layers because all intermediate kernels lie inside $P_2$. All candidates inducing $a$ are $g\circ f_0$ with $g\in B$. The group $B$ is a $p$-group. For an ordered basis of the Frattini quotient, $B$ acts freely on the tuple of all possible lifts of its basis elements, since every such tuple generates the group. That last fact follows from the definition of the Frattini subgroup as the intersection of all maximal proper subgroups. The freely acted on set has size a power of $p$, so $|B|$ divides that power.

Apply Section 2 to the action of $B$ on $H^2(Q_H,\mathbb F_p)$ by inverse pullback. Under this action, testing whether some $g^*$ takes $S_{X_H}$ onto $(f_0^{-1})^*S_{X_G}$ is a subspace transporter query. The cohomology actions are computed by permuting cocycle variables and expressing the result in the quotient basis. The faithful permutation action is on the elements of $Q_H$. The resulting transporter, inverted when necessary to use pullback, produces a candidate whose lift exists. Also compute the stabilizer of $S_{X_H}$ under $B$. This subgroup is exactly the image of the automorphisms of $X_H$ which act trivially on the Frattini quotient. Lift its generators using the coboundary equations, and adjoin generators for the kernel $\operatorname{Hom}(Q_H,E_H)$. This gives the required generator list at the new layer. The base at the Frattini quotient has the identity subgroup of automorphisms fixing the quotient. Thus the process yields the desired lift after at most $\log_2 n$ steps, or certifies nonexistence. Run it with identical inputs and $a=1$ to compute generators of the Frattini-action kernel for any input group.

## 4. Search, counting and automorphism generators

With a positive unmarked oracle answer, choose a basis of $V_H^*$ and find the pullback images of its lines under an isomorphism one at a time. At each step enumerate the lines of $V_G^*$ outside the already specified span and use Section 1 to test the whole prefix of constraints. There are at most $p^{\dim V_G}\le n$ candidates at each step. A positive prefix guarantees at least one viable next member. Once all lines are fixed, choose one nonzero representative on each and enumerate the at most $(p-1)^{\dim V_G}\le n$ assignments of nonzero scales. Duality gives a candidate exact linear map on Frattini quotients for each choice. Section 3 tests each candidate and returns a lifting isomorphism for the first successful choice. Some choice must succeed by the prefix invariant. An initial negative answer gives count zero. Groups of different orders or primes can be rejected directly, and trivial groups are immediate.

For a group compared with itself, let $A$ be the image of its automorphism group on the dual Frattini quotient, using inverse pullback to make the action a homomorphism. Fix the lines of a basis of that quotient. For each successive line, use Section 1 to count its orbit under the pointwise stabilizer of the preceding lines. Each orbit has at most $n$ members. The final stabilizer consists of allowed diagonal maps in this basis, so enumerate its at most $(p-1)^{\dim V_G}\le n$ candidates and test each exact action by Section 3. Multiply the number of successful diagonal maps by the orbit sizes to obtain $|A|$. Section 3 also gives generators of the automorphism kernel, whose order is computed by permutation group algorithms on the group elements. Multiplying the two orders yields the order of the full automorphism group. This counts isomorphisms between isomorphic inputs, since the isomorphisms form a torsor for either automorphism group. The numbers have polynomial bit length.

For generators of the full group, obtain one transporter for each member of each successive line orbit by completing its specified prefix with the search procedure. At the final stabilizer, use the lifts of the enumerated successful diagonal maps. These transporters and final lifts, together with the generators of the kernel, generate the full automorphism group by the stabilizer chain.

Finally a finite nilpotent group is the direct product of its characteristic Sylow subgroups. Their tables are recoverable by selecting the elements of prime-power order for each prime, and trial division suffices to find the primes in polynomial time in the input order. Isomorphisms restrict to the prime-by-prime isomorphisms and conversely assemble from them. Counts multiply, and each Sylow automorphism generator extends by the identity in the other factors. This proves the nilpotent statement.

The proof gives both a marked decision reduction and an oracle-free lift for a prescribed Frattini quotient map. It is informal. The corresponding questions for arbitrary finite groups as input remain open here. The companion Frattini-class-two reduction additionally retains that bound on the class of the query groups.
