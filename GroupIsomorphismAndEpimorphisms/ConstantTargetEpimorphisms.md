# Epimorphisms to a constant-size group in logspace

Fix an integer $M\geq 1$. For finite groups $G,H$ given by their Cayley tables, with $|H|\leq M$, deciding whether $G$ has an epimorphism onto $H$ is in deterministic logspace. A logspace transducer can output an epimorphism when one exists and compute the number of epimorphisms. This answers the constant-size target clause of Grochow, Srivastava and Thakkar, Open Question 3(2), which asks for polynomial time. Their other clause asks about targets generated by a constant number of elements. [*Algorithms for Finite Group Epimorphism Testing*, §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7).

## An implicit generating list

Let $G$ have order $n$ and use the element labels in its input table. Scan them in label order. An element enters the list $s_1,\ldots,s_k$ when it is outside the subgroup generated by all earlier labels. This is equivalent to adjoining it when it strictly enlarges the subgroup generated by the earlier list members. Inductively the two subgroups are the same after each step. At each addition the subgroup order at least doubles, so $k\leq\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$. The labels left out were already generated by their preceding labels, so the list generates $G$.

Both $k$ and a requested entry $s_i$ can be computed in logspace without storing the list. For any label $x$, test whether it is reachable from the identity in the undirected Cayley graph whose allowed generators are all labels less than $x$. Adjacency is evaluated by a scan of that prefix and table lookups. Allow the inverse edges by testing multiplication in either direction. This tests precisely membership in the subgroup generated by the prefix. Deterministic undirected connectivity is in logspace by Reingold. Scanning labels with this test and a counter gives either the total or the requested entry. [Reingold, *Undirected connectivity in log-space*](https://doi.org/10.1145/1391289.1391291).

## Test one assignment of images

Write $m=|H|$ and enumerate all assignments $(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\in H^k$ to the selected generators. Handle $m=1$ by returning the unique homomorphism to the trivial group. Otherwise assign one counter the base-$m$ encoding of the entire tuple. The counter uses at most $k\lceil\log_2 m\rceil=O_M(\log n)$ bits. Its digits are available on demand.

For one assignment let
$$
R=\langle(s_i,t_i):1\leq i\leq k\rangle\leq G\times H.
$$
The first projection of $R$ is all of $G$. The assignment extends to a homomorphism if and only if every first-coordinate fiber of $R$ has size one. In one direction, if the assignment extends, $R$ is contained in the graph of its homomorphism and projects onto all of $G$. In the other direction, singleton fibers give a uniquely defined map with graph $R$, and the subgroup product rule proves that it is a homomorphism. The resulting homomorphism is onto exactly when the second projection is all of $H$.

Membership of a pair in $R$ is another undirected Cayley graph connectivity query. The vertex set $G\times H$ has $nm$ elements and adjacency is tested using the paired generators and the two tables. Retrieve $s_i$ by the previous procedure and $t_i$ from the counter. This is a composition of a fixed number of logspace procedures. The singleton and surjectivity conditions are checked by scanning the pairs and counting or recording their relevant projections with label counters.

## Decision, search and count

Accept precisely when one assignment passes. A homomorphism is determined by its values on $s_1,\ldots,s_k$, so distinct successful assignments give distinct epimorphisms and every epimorphism is found. To output one, take the first successful assignment and stream its value at each $g\in G$ by finding the unique $h\in H$ with $(g,h)\in R$. To count, increment one counter for each successful assignment. There are at most $m^k\leq n^{\log_2 M}$ assignments, so the count and all counters fit in logarithmic space for fixed $M$. The simulations are deterministic and polynomial-time for fixed $M$. This is an informal proof.
