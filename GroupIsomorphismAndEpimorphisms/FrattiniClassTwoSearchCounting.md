# Search and counting reduce to decision for groups of Frattini class two

**Theorem.** The following problems have deterministic polynomial-time Turing reductions to Group Isomorphism within the class of finite $p$-groups of Frattini class at most two, given by Cayley tables: finding an isomorphism, counting all isomorphisms, and finding generators of the automorphism group. The prime is allowed to vary, including $p=2$. Every nontrivial oracle query stays in the same prime and has group order polynomial in the original group order.

Arvind and Torán's Problem 16 asks about such reductions for general Group Isomorphism. Grochow and Qiao proved the polynomial reductions for class-two groups of exponent $p$. The theorem here covers the entire Frattini-class-two family. The original question for arbitrary groups remains open. [Arvind–Torán, Problem 16](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.190/Mitarbeiter/toran/beatcs/column86.pdf) · [Grochow–Qiao, Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317v2)

## 1. The linear data of a Frattini-class-two group

Let $G$ have Frattini class at most two. Put $W=\Phi(G)$ and $V=G/\Phi(G)$. Both groups are elementary Abelian and $W$ is central. We view them as vector spaces over $\mathbb F_p$. The information on which isomorphism depends can be given by the commutator map $\beta:V\times V\to W$ and the power map $q:V\to W$, where $q(v)=g^p$ for a representative $g$ of $v$. These maps are independent of the representative because $W$ is central of exponent $p$. The commutator map is alternating and bilinear. For odd $p$ the power map is linear since the exponent of a commutator divides $p$ and $p$ divides $\binom p2$. For $p=2$ the power map is quadratic with polar form $\beta$.

Choose a basis of $W^*$. At $p=2$ the coordinate polynomials $q_1,\ldots,q_m$ span a subspace $\mathcal Q$ of homogeneous quadratic forms on $V$. At odd $p$ the pairs $(\beta_i,q_i)$ span a subspace $\mathcal Q$ of $\Lambda^2 V^*\oplus V^*$. In either case the indicated $m$ coordinate objects are independent. Indeed, the images of the commutator and power maps together generate $\Phi(G)=G^p[G,G]$. At $p=2$ the polar form is a linear combination of values of $q$, so the linear span of its values alone is enough. A functional vanishing on all these data is zero.

For completeness, choose a basis of $V$, take representative generators $g_1,\ldots,g_n$, and choose a basis of $W$. The powers $g_i^p$, the commutators $[g_i,g_j]$, and the centrality and exponent-$p$ relations of the $W$ basis describe the group. These relations put every word into a normal form $g_1^{a_1}\cdots g_n^{a_n}w$ with $0\le a_i<p$ and $w\in W$. The $p^{n+m}$ normal forms are distinct in $G$ by first mapping to $V$ and then to $W$. Conversely any of the indicated alternating and power data on elementary spaces give such a group. One can use the normal form set $V\times W$ and a central cocycle. For odd $p$ use $\tfrac12\beta(v,v')$ plus the sum of coordinate carries $\lfloor(a_i+a_i')/p\rfloor\,q(e_i)$ with representatives $a_i,a_i'\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$. The carry identity follows by adding three integers in either order. For $p=2$ use the bilinear cocycle
$$
 c(v,v')=\sum_i a_i a_i' q(e_i)+\sum_{i<j}a_j a_i'\beta(e_i,e_j).
$$
Its diagonal is $q$ and its polar commutator is $\beta$. The group law is $(v,w)(v',w')=(v+v',w+w'+c(v,v'))$. For odd $p$, the cocycle likewise has the required commutator and $p$-power data. If the coordinate objects are independent, powers and commutators span $W$ and the constructed group's Frattini subgroup is exactly $W$.

It follows that the group-isomorphism condition is a linear map $A:V_G\to V_H$ whose pullback maps $\mathcal Q_H$ onto $\mathcal Q_G$. The induced map on $W$ is determined uniquely, since the two spans have dimension $m$. Every such $A$ lifts to a group isomorphism. Choose in $H$ any lifts of the images of the chosen generators of $V_G$, and map the $W_G$ basis according to the unique induced map. The power and commutator relations then hold and the induced maps on both layers are invertible. All lifts of a given $A$ differ by choices in $W_H$ for the $n$ generators, so there are exactly $p^{nm}$ of them. This is the same commutator and power information as in the constructive Frattini-class-two classification. [Ivanyos–Mendoza–Qiao–Sun–Zhang, §V](https://chuanqizhang.com/assets/pdf/pdf/focs24.pdf) · [Grochow–Ivanyos–Qiao–Sun, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00874v1#S3)

All these objects are available in polynomial time from a Cayley table. Find $\Phi(G)$ by generating the subgroup of powers and commutators, form its quotient, and construct bases by adjoining elements outside the generated span. One can enumerate the elementary layers explicitly since each has at most $|G|$ elements. This also provides coordinate lifting, field operations, and exact element labels. The formulas above construct the entire Cayley table of a new group in time polynomial in its order and the encoded dimension.

## 2. Pin prescribed covector lines using linearly many coordinates

We first give a bipartite graph $\Gamma_k$ on $k$ left vertices and $O(k)$ right vertices, with $O(k)$ edges. The graph has no isolated vertices. Every left vertex is fixed by every bipartition-preserving automorphism, and no left neighborhood contains another.

For $k\ge7$, form the tree on $k$ vertices with one degree-three vertex and three branches of lengths $1,2,k-4$. Those branch lengths differ, so the tree has no nontrivial automorphism. Its vertices are the left part. On the right use one vertex adjacent to the two endpoints of each tree edge and one private degree-one neighbor for each left vertex. Right degrees distinguish tree edges from private neighbors, so a bipartition-preserving automorphism fixes the left part pointwise. The private neighbors give the neighborhood noncontainment. For $1\le k\le6$, use disjoint stars with the $k$ left degrees $1,\ldots,k$, so these properties still hold and the size bound holds with an absolute constant. For $k=0$ the marker is empty.

Let $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_k$ be independent covectors in $V^*$ and let $z_j$ be the coordinate basis of an auxiliary dual space $Z^*$ indexed by the right vertices. Consider the subspace of mixed tensors
$$
 M(\ell)=\operatorname{span}\{\ell_i\otimes z_j:(i,j)\in E(\Gamma_k)\}
 \subseteq V^*\otimes Z^*.
$$
The spans of its left and right contractions, equivalently its collective supports in the two factors, are $\operatorname{span}\{\ell_i\}$ and all of $Z^*$ because there are no isolated vertices. Any two independent changes of coordinates carrying one such tensor space to another consequently restrict to invertible matrices $R,S$ on these two supports.

In the marked covector and right-vertex bases, the space is spanned by the elementary matrices $E_{ij}$ on the edges. If $(i,j)$ is an edge and $R_{a i}S_{b j}\neq0$, then $(a,b)$ must be an edge because $RE_{ij}S^{\mathsf T}$ belongs to the same coordinate subspace. Choose determinant matchings $\sigma,\tau$ in $R,S$, so that $R_{\sigma(i),i}$ and $S_{\tau(j),j}$ are nonzero for every $i,j$. The two permutations take edges to edges, hence give an automorphism of the finite bipartite graph. Therefore $\sigma$ is the identity. If $R_{a i}\neq0$, the same support property with $S_{\tau(j),j}$ says $\tau(N(i))\subseteq N(a)$. Since $\tau(N(i))=N(i)$, the neighborhood condition forces $a=i$. Thus $R$ is diagonal. Each marked line is preserved individually, and over $\mathbb F_2$ so is each marked covector. Conversely a map carrying the specified lines individually gives an isomorphism of these tensor spaces with the identity on the right part, since scaling a generator preserves its span. The support argument is the one used for diagonal restrictions by Grochow and Qiao. The graph above makes it deterministic and uses only linear size. [Grochow–Qiao, Lemma 4.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317v2#S4.SS5)

## 3. Put the marking inside the unmarked group instance

Enlarge the variable space to
$$
 X=V\oplus A_0\oplus\mathbb F_p b\oplus Z,\qquad \dim A_0=m .
$$
Use coordinates $a_i$ on $A_0$, $b$ on its one-dimensional companion, and $z_j$ on $Z$. Write $U=A_0\oplus\mathbb F_p b\oplus Z$, so $X=V\oplus U$.

For $p=2$, take the new space $\widetilde{\mathcal Q}$ of quadratic forms on $X$ spanned by the following family.

- The $m$ lifts $q_i+a_i b$.
- The squares of every coordinate covector on $U$.
- One form $\ell_i z_j$ for each edge $(i,j)$ of $\Gamma_k$.

For odd $p$, take the corresponding subspace of $\Lambda^2 X^*\oplus X^*$. Replace the three families, respectively, by $(\beta_i+a_i\wedge b,q_i)$, $(0,u)$ for each coordinate covector $u$ on $U$, and $(\ell_i\wedge z_j,0)$ for each edge.

In either case let the alternating component mean polarization when $p=2$, and the first component of a pair for odd $p$. The lifts have independent $A_0$-by-$b$ entries in their alternating components. Markers have independent $V$-by-$Z$ entries. The kernel of the alternating-component map on $\widetilde{\mathcal Q}$ is therefore precisely the auxiliary square or pure-power subspace. In characteristic two, its common zero set is $V$, since $u(x)^2=0$ exactly when $u(x)=0$. For odd $p$, its common zero set in the power component is $V$. This proves that $V$ is intrinsically identifiable under every congruence of the enlarged space. It also proves that the displayed families form a basis of $\widetilde{\mathcal Q}$, since the remaining pure-power coordinates are independent.

Restricting the whole enlarged space to $V$ yields precisely the original $\mathcal Q$. Let $J$ be the kernel of this restriction map, on both components in odd characteristic. Independence of the original basis implies that $J$ is exactly the span of pure-power coordinates and the edge markers. Its alternating components vanish on $V\times V$. Consequently they define the intrinsic mixed bilinear space on $V\times(X/V)$, with value at $(v,[x])$ computed by any representative $x$. The resulting space is exactly $M(\ell)$ extended by zero on the auxiliary quotient coordinates outside $Z$. Thus an isomorphism of the enlarged spaces restricts to an original isomorphism respecting every marked line. The argument uses the induced map on the quotient $X/V$, so no assumption about preserving the chosen complement $U$ is needed.

Conversely suppose $A:V_G\to V_H$ maps the original spaces by pullback and carries the specified covector lines as prescribed. Write the invertible $m$-by-$m$ coefficient matrix of the pullback in the given original bases. Extend $A$ by an invertible map on $A_0$ whose coordinate pullback is that same coefficient matrix, and by identity in the $b,Z$ coordinates. Then each lifted form or pair pulls back to the corresponding linear combination of the lifted originals. The auxiliary square or pure-power subspace is preserved, and each marker pulls back to a nonzero scalar multiple of its designated marker. Thus the enlarged spaces are isomorphic exactly when there exists a line-respecting original isomorphism.

By Section 1, the new spaces give unmarked Frattini-class-at-most-two groups. Their coordinate objects are independent, so the new abstract $W$ is again their actual Frattini subgroup. In the original groups $|G|=p^{n+m}$, while the new variable and output dimensions are $n+O(n+m+1)$ and $m+O(n+m+1)$ because $k\le n$ and the marker graph has linear size. The corresponding order is $|G|^{O(1)}$ for nontrivial inputs, uniformly in $p$. The auxiliary square and power features are needed to make the original domain intrinsic when the original commutator and power data do not split.

## 4. Recover a witness, count, and generate automorphisms

For a search query, fix a basis of $V_H^*$. Find its images under $A^*$ one line at a time, enumerating projective points in $V_G^*$ outside the span of the previously chosen lines. Match source and target marked lists in Section 3 and query the decision oracle for each candidate. A positive decision on the unmarked instance initializes the process. At each stage a positive candidate exists by the previous query, and the marker tests the new constraint while retaining all previous ones. There are at most $p^n\le |G|$ candidates at any stage. After all lines are matched, the possible maps $A^*$ differ only by their nonzero scales in these basis images. Enumerate the at most $(p-1)^n\le|G|$ scale assignments and test the original linear pullback condition by exact linear algebra. This finds a full witness, which lifts to a group isomorphism by Section 1. At $p=2$ no scale enumeration is needed.

For counting, first check whether the groups are isomorphic. If they are, the number of isomorphisms equals the number of automorphisms of either group. Let $B$ be the group of allowed $A^*$ on a fixed domain, and let $B_i$ be its pointwise stabilizer of the first $i$ basis lines in projective space. For each $i$, the index $[B_i:B_{i+1}]$ is the number of projective points in the orbit of the next line under $B_i$. Enumerate the candidate lines and test membership in this orbit by the unmarked-group marker query. The group $B_n$ is diagonal in the fixed basis, so its order is found by enumerating and checking at most $(p-1)^n$ maps. Multiplication of these indices gives $|B|$. Multiply by $p^{nm}$ for the lift multiplicity. All resulting integers have polynomial bit length since the automorphism group is a subgroup of the permutations of the input set.

For generators, for each successful line in each stabilizer orbit construct a transporter by the same search, starting with the line constraints for that orbit. These transporters give coset representatives for $B_i/B_{i+1}$ in the sense of a transversal, and their union with the enumerated $B_n$ generates $B$. Lift those generators to automorphisms of the input group and adjoin the lifts of the elementary basis maps $V\to W$ that induce the identity on $V$ and $W$. These latter automorphisms generate the kernel of the induced action, since an automorphism acting trivially on $V,W$ maps $g$ to $g\,\delta([g])$ with $\delta:V\to W$ linear. This gives generators of the full automorphism group. All candidate scans, oracle calls, and lifts are polynomial in the original table size.

The prior decision running times therefore also give search, counting and automorphism-generation algorithms for this family of the same asymptotic exponent in $\log |G|$, since every oracle-instance order is $|G|^{O(1)}$. The reductions themselves are deterministic polynomial-time statements independent of those decision methods. A targeted search found no prior reductions covering all Frattini-class-two groups in this form. This is an informal proof.
