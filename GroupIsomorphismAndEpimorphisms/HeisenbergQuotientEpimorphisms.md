# Polynomial-time epimorphisms from generalized Heisenberg quotients

**Theorem.** Suppose $G$ is a quotient of an odd-order generalized Heisenberg group over a finite field, in the sense of Lewis and Wilson. Given the Cayley tables of $G$ and any finite group $H$, one deterministic polynomial-bit-time algorithm decides whether $G$ maps onto $H$, finds an epimorphism when one exists, and counts all epimorphisms. It needs no cover, field model, section, or generators supplied. The algorithm also recognizes whether a given Cayley table belongs to the source class. This answers the genus-one quotient part of Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar Open Question 3(3) for the family cited in their question. Their genus-two question is separate. [Open Question 3(3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7) · [Lewis–Wilson, Theorem 1.3 and §1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.5466)

## From a table to a space of alternating forms

Write $F=\mathbb F_p$ for the prime field, with $p$ odd. The generalized Heisenberg group over a finite extension $L/F$ has underlying set $L^{2a}\times L$ for some $a\ge 1$, with multiplication
$$
(u,z)(v,w)=(u+v,z+w+\tfrac12 b(u,v)),
$$
where $b$ is a nondegenerate alternating $L$-bilinear form. Its commutator is $b$, its center is its derived group, and each noncentral element commutes with a subgroup of index $|L|$: for $u\ne 0$, $b(u,-)$ is onto $L$. A normal subgroup containing a noncentral element therefore contains the entire derived group. Thus each nonabelian quotient has kernel inside $L$ and commutator $\pi b$ for a surjective $F$-linear map $\pi:L\to W$. Its center and derived subgroup are both $W$, and the map $h\mapsto[g,h]$ is onto $W$ whenever $g$ is noncentral. The abelian quotients are elementary abelian. All groups here have exponent dividing $p$.

Given a nonabelian group of class two and exponent $p$ with $W_G=G'=Z(G)$, construct $V_G=G/Z(G)$ and its commutator $b_G:V_G\times V_G\to W_G$. Bases for both elementary abelian groups can be chosen from their tables by adjoining elements and enumerating their spans. Let $S_G\subseteq\operatorname{Alt}(V_G)$ be the image of $W_G^*$ under $\lambda\mapsto\lambda b_G$. The map is injective since the commutators span $W_G$. Centers, derived subgroups, quotients, bases, and the matrices of these forms all take polynomial time to compute from the table. Use identical notation for $H$.

For a nonabelian Heisenberg quotient every form in $S_G$ other than zero is nonsingular. Indeed, every $F$-linear functional on $L$ has the form $z\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{L/F}(c z)$ for a unique $c\in L$. A nonzero functional composed with $b(u,-)$ is nonzero whenever $u\ne 0$. Choose any $A_0\in S_G\setminus\{0\}$. Define $R_G=A_0^{-1}S_G$ as a subspace of $\operatorname{End}_F(V_G)$, and let $K_G$ be the unital $F$-algebra it generates. In the Heisenberg coordinates, if $A_0=\operatorname{Tr}_{L/F}(c_0b)$ and $A=\operatorname{Tr}_{L/F}(cb)$, the endomorphism $A_0^{-1}A$ is multiplication by $c/c_0$. Therefore $K_G$ is a field for every choice of $A_0$. All these statements refer to linear algebra on the table-derived form space.

## Normalizing a form over its ratio field

Let $V$ be an $F$-space with a nonsingular alternating form $A_0$ and let $K\subseteq\operatorname{End}_F(V)$ be a field extension of $F$ consisting of $A_0$-self-adjoint operators. The trace pairing of $K/F$ is nondegenerate. It gives a unique $K$-valued form $a$ with
$$
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(c\, a(u,v))=A_0(cu,v)\qquad(c\in K).
$$
For multiplication by $d\in K$, the equation for $a(du,v)$ follows by replacing $c$ with $cd$. Self-adjointness gives the same result for $a(u,dv)$. For each $c\in K$ the identities $A_0(cu,u)=A_0(u,cu)=-A_0(cu,u)$ imply $A_0(cu,u)=0$ since $p$ is odd. Hence $a$ is alternating. Taking $c=1$ proves nondegeneracy. Solving the trace-pairing linear system computes $a$. In the ratio construction, every element of $R_G$ is self-adjoint because $A_0$ and the corresponding $A$ are alternating. Their generated algebra is a field, and commuting self-adjoint operators have self-adjoint products. This proves the claim for $K_G$ and for each subfield of $K_G$.

Conversely, if a nonabelian group has class two, exponent $p$, $G'=Z(G)$, and a nonsingular anchor $A_0$ whose ratio algebra is a field, the construction recognizes a Heisenberg quotient. To see this, identify $V_G$ as a $K_G$-space and build the nondegenerate alternating $K_G$-valued form $a$. For each element $T\in R_G\subseteq K_G$, the scalar form $A_0T$ is $\operatorname{Tr}_{K_G/F}(T a)$. The commutator map is thus the composite of $a$ with the surjection $K_G\to R_G^*$ obtained by dualizing the inclusion $R_G\subseteq K_G$ and using the trace pairing. This group is the quotient by the kernel of that surjection. For the passage between a commutator and a group law, define addition by $x+y=xy[x,y]^{-1/2}$. In class two the central commutator rules show this is associative and commutative, and exponent $p$ makes the resulting additive group an $F$-vector space. On a linear splitting of $G\to V_G$ the original product is exactly the product displayed above with $b_G$ in place of $b$. Any map preserving the commutator and linear for this addition is a group homomorphism.

This yields an explicit recognition procedure. The abelian groups on the list are precisely the elementary abelian groups at odd primes, together with the trivial group. Otherwise test the class, exponent, center and derived conditions, choose any nonzero form as anchor and reject if it is singular. Generate its ratio algebra by linear closure under multiplication. Check that every nonzero element is invertible as an operator. This needs enumeration only if the algebra has $F$-dimension at most $\dim_F V_G$, a necessary condition for a field acting on $V_G$. If an algebra of that bounded dimension has no singular nonzero operator, the map of multiplication in the algebra by any nonzero element is injective and thus surjective. It is then a finite division ring and hence a field. Equivalently one can first check commutativity on a basis and then use this invertibility test. The enumeration has at most $p^{\dim_F V_G}\le |G|$ elements.

## Reducing quotient testing to inclusion under a field embedding

If $H$ is trivial there is one epimorphism. For nontrivial abelian $H$, an epimorphism factors through $G/G'$ and is precisely a surjection of elementary abelian groups at the same prime. These are decided and counted by ordinary linear algebra. If $H$ is nonabelian, reject unless recognized by the above procedure at the prime of $G$. This is valid because a quotient of a member of the source class is still a member. An abelian $G$ has no nonabelian quotients.

Consider nonabelian $G,H$ of the class, and let $f:G\to H$ be an epimorphism. If some noncentral $g$ maps to the center of $H$, then $f$ kills all of $G'$, since $[g,G]=G'$. This contradicts the noncommutativity of $H$. Thus $f^{-1}(Z(H))=Z(G)$, so $f$ induces an isomorphism $\alpha:V_H\to V_G$ in the inverse direction and a surjection $\delta:W_G\to W_H$. A pair $(\alpha,\delta)$ gives the required commutator relation exactly when
$$
\alpha^*(\delta^* B)=B\qquad(B\in S_H),
$$
where $\alpha^*$ denotes pullback on forms and $\delta^*$ denotes the linear inclusion $S_H\hookrightarrow S_G$ using the identifications with $W_H^*$ and $W_G^*$. Equivalently $\alpha$ is invertible and $(\alpha^{-1})^* S_H\subseteq S_G$. If the dimensions of $V_H,V_G$ differ, no epimorphism exists.

Fix a nonzero anchor $B_0\in S_H$. For each nonzero $A_0\in S_G$, form $(R_H,K_H)$ using $B_0$ and $(R_G,K_G)$ using $A_0$. We claim that an invertible map $\alpha:V_H\to V_G$ with $\alpha^* A_0=B_0$ and $(\alpha^{-1})^* S_H\subseteq S_G$ exists exactly when there is an $F$-field embedding $\kappa:K_H\hookrightarrow K_G$ with $\kappa(R_H)\subseteq R_G$. In one direction, conjugation by $\alpha$ maps $B_0^{-1}B$ to the ratio $A_0^{-1}((\alpha^{-1})^* B)$, and therefore gives the required field embedding.

For the other direction, regard $V_G$ as a $K_H$-space using $\kappa$. Over $K_H$, lift $A_0$ by the trace construction using this subfield. Lift $B_0$ in the same way. The spaces have equal dimensions over $K_H$, and the lifts are nondegenerate alternating forms on them. Symplectic basis construction gives a $K_H$-linear isometry $\alpha$ from the lift of $B_0$ to the lift of $A_0$. Taking traces yields $\alpha^*A_0=B_0$. For $T\in R_H$, $K_H$-linearity gives $\alpha T=\kappa(T)\alpha$. Consequently $\alpha^*(A_0\kappa(T))=B_0T$, and $A_0\kappa(T)$ lies in $S_G$. This proves the claim. The field embedding determines the inclusion of form spaces by $B_0T\mapsto A_0\kappa(T)$. Dualize it to recover $\delta$ and take any linear splitting of the additive presentations to build a map $G\to H$; it is onto since both induced maps are onto.

Each anchor can be enumerated in time polynomial in $|G|$. Finite field embeddings can be computed by finding a generator of $K_H/F$ by enumeration, computing its minimal polynomial, and testing every element of $K_G$ as a possible image. The fields each have at most as many elements as their input group. All matrix spans and each field containment test use polynomial-size linear algebra. The symplectic isometry and the table of the epimorphism can likewise be computed in polynomial bit time. For an arbitrary input pair we may also verify the outgoing witness on every product in the table.

## Counting epimorphisms

In the nonabelian case each epimorphism determines a unique $(A_0,\kappa)$: its inverse map on central quotients determines the inclusion of alternating form spaces, and conjugation on $R_H$ determines the field map on $K_H$ because $R_H$ generates that field. Conversely, for a qualifying pair $(A_0,\kappa)$, all allowed maps $\alpha$ are precisely the symplectic isometries between the two trace lifts over $K_H$. Indeed, $K_H$-linearity and equality of trace forms imply equality of the original $K_H$-valued forms by testing against every scalar. Thus their number is $|\operatorname{Sp}_{2r}(q)|$ for $q=|K_H|$ and $2r=\dim_{K_H} V_H$. Its value is $q^{r^2}\prod_{j=1}^r(q^{2j}-1)$. With $\alpha,\delta$ fixed, in linear splittings every group epimorphism differs by an arbitrary linear map $V_G\to W_H$. This gives the constant multiplier $p^{(\dim_F V_G)(\dim_F W_H)}$. Summing these integers over the qualifying pairs counts all epimorphisms. For an elementary abelian target of rank $b$ and source abelianization of rank $a$ the count is $\prod_{j=0}^{b-1}(p^a-p^j)$ if $b\le a$, and zero otherwise. Counts have polynomial bit length since there are at most $|H|^{|G|}$ set maps.

Lewis and Wilson used adjoints and finite-field subspaces to recognize this family and solve its isomorphism problem. Here the two ratio fields can have different degrees. The quotient step uses inclusion under a field embedding and changes the acting field for only one of the two symplectic spaces. This is an informal proof.
