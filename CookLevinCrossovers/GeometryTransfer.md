# Exact affine universality and quantitative transfer for marked three-manifolds

These are informal proofs. The first strengthens the affine-scheme and fixed three-manifold results. The second strengthens the finite-target extension and counting branches. The last two use the epoch decomposition and Boolean tensor contraction bounds of Strassle and Subercaseaux.

## 1. Stable affine universality with one marked circle

**Theorem 1.** Let \(X\) be a finitely presented affine \(\mathbb Z\)-scheme. There are a closed orientable hyperbolic three-manifold \(M\) with torsion-free integral homology, a based embedded circle \(\gamma\) representing a commutator in \(\pi_1(M)\), and an integer \(D\) such that the affine scheme of extensions of the fixed based map \(\gamma\to BH\) with central value \((0,0,1)\) is
\[
X\times_{\mathbb Z}\mathrm{SL}_{2,\mathbb Z}\times_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb A^D_{\mathbb Z}.
\tag{1}
\]
Here the functor on a commutative ring \(R\) consists of relative homotopy classes of based maps into \(BH(R)\), with \(H(R)\) the discrete group of upper unitriangular three-by-three matrices over \(R\). If \(X\) is presented by an arithmetic circuit with \(s\) scalar wires and \(m\) tests in the construction below, then \(b_1(M)=2+2s+m\) and \(D=2+3m\). The same product works over any parameter space cut out by designated input coordinates, with the parameter values imposed by additional central commutator marks. Over every finite commutative ring the number of marked extensions is
\[
|X(R)|\,|\mathrm{SL}_2(R)|\,|R|^D .
\tag{2}
\]

**Proof.** Write \((p,q,z)(p',q',z')=(p+p',q+q',z+z'+pq')\) in \(H(R)\). The commutator is \((0,0,pq'-p'q)\). Use an integer arithmetic circuit built from addition, multiplication, zero and one, with signs represented by addition to zero. Let \(W\) have integral basis \(a,b,P_t,Q_t\), with two labels \(P_t,Q_t\) per scalar wire \(t\). For characters \(u,v\) let \([i,j]\) denote \(u_iv_j-u_jv_i\). When the columns on \(a,b\) are \((1,0),(0,1)\), take the tests
\[
[a,P_t]=0,\quad [Q_t,b]=0,\quad [P_t,b]-[a,Q_t]=0.
\]
These force the columns on \(P_t,Q_t\) to be \((t,0),(0,t)\). To set a wire to 0 or 1 use the test \([P_t,b]\) or \([P_t,b]-[a,b]\). To impose \(r=t+t'\) use \([P_t,b]+[P_{t'},b]-[P_r,b]\), and to impose \(r=tt'\) use \([P_t,Q_{t'}]-[P_r,b]\). The output constraints are zero tests. These alternating bilinear test forms \(\beta_j\) on \(W\) have zero locus, on normalized columns, naturally identified with \(X(R)\). Every actual arithmetic gate has a fresh output, so filling the circuit introduces no extra choices. If needed, constraints \(u=-v\) are represented by the linear addition test with output the zero wire, which determines the value of \(u\) uniquely.

With additional basis labels \(r_1,\ldots,r_m\) put \(V=W\oplus\mathbb Z^m\) and \(\mu=\sum_j\beta_j\wedge r_j^*\). Realize this as the triple cup form of a closed orientable hyperbolic three-manifold \(M\) with \(H^1(M;\mathbb Z)=V\) and free integral homology. To do this, start with one connected summand \(S^1\times S^2\) per label and one oriented \(T^3\) summand per signed unit of a triple coefficient, then impose three homology identifications per torus by surgery. The independent relation classes are jointly primitive because each contains its own torus axis with coefficient one. Dual integral two-cycles show that the corresponding meridians vanish in the exterior's first homology, so surgery kills precisely the primitive relation subgroup. On the resulting cobordism the surviving degree-one classes extend across the trace. This preserves their triple evaluations on the incoming and outgoing fundamental classes and gives \(\mu\). Apply Ruberman's integral homology cobordism theorem to make \(M\) hyperbolic without changing the form. [Sullivan's realization theorem](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~dennis/publications/PDF/DS-pub-0019.pdf) · [Ruberman, Theorem 2.6](https://msp.org/pjm/1990/145-1/pjm-v145-n1-p05-s.pdf)

Choose based loops \(g_i\) projecting to the labeled integral homology basis. A based PL loop can be made embedded in dimension three, so mark \([g_a,g_b]\) with value \((0,0,1)\). A finite collection of marks can similarly be made into an embedded bouquet. The commutator condition says the column matrix on \(a,b\) has determinant one. Multiplication of all columns on the left by its inverse normalizes them and preserves all alternating minors. The class of the central group extension of \(H(R)\) is the cup cocycle \(u(g)v(h)\), so a character pair lifts exactly when its cup product vanishes. This can be tested in \(H^2(M;R)\). For a general connected CW source the map \(H^2(\pi_1;R)\to H^2(M;R)\) is injective, by attaching cells of dimensions at least three to obtain a classifying space. Thus the same contention applies to the pre-hyperbolization source. Over \(R\), the degree-one cup form is alternating, since the integral basis is pulled back from circles and the free integral homology allows coefficient change. Poincaré duality tests cup-product vanishing via \(\mu(u,v,-)=0\).

Testing on \(r_j\) gives \(\beta_j(u_W,v_W)=0\), hence a point of \(X(R)\). Write \(A_j=u_{r_j}\), \(B_j=v_{r_j}\). The remaining tests, on \(w\in W\otimes R\), impose vanishing of
\[
L(A,B)(w)=\sum_j \bigl(A_j\beta_j(v_W,w)-B_j\beta_j(u_W,w)\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]
This functional annihilates both \(u_W\) and \(v_W\), by the \(\beta_j\) equations. Its full value is determined by its values on the wire basis labels \(P_t,Q_t\), since \(u_W,v_W\) replace \(a,b\) in a basis over \(R\). For the selector \([a,P_t]\), the coordinate \(B_j\) contributes \(-w_{P_t}+t w_a\). For the selector \([Q_t,b]\), the coordinate \(A_j\) contributes \(-w_{Q_t}+t w_b\). Therefore these \(2s\) selected auxiliary coordinates give a signed identity on all \(2s\) wire tests. Assign the other \(2m-2s\) auxiliary coordinates arbitrarily and solve uniquely for the selected ones. This describes the kernel naturally as \(R^{2m-2s}\) over every \(X(R)\) and remains valid in rings with nilpotents or zero divisors.

At every cup-zero pair the lifts form a torsor under \(H^1(M;R)\). The central coordinates on the \(g_i\) trivialize the torsor because their images in the free abelianization form a basis. Their values can be chosen arbitrarily, and any two lifts with the same such central coordinates are equal. This gives another affine factor of dimension \(2+2s+m\). Combining the arbitrary normalization matrix, the split tester coordinates and the free central coordinates gives (1), functorially in \(R\), so the natural bijection is an affine scheme isomorphism. Relative homotopy classes are the prescribed based homomorphisms, by lifting to the contractible universal cover of \(BH(R)\) and extending equivariantly over remaining cells and the relative homotopy cylinder. Finally the mark \([g_{P_t},g_b]\) reads the wire \(t\) independently of all three free factors, proving the parameter assertion and (2). \(\square\)

In particular, over any field every affine finite-type singularity defined over that field by base change from this presentation occurs with an explicitly smooth product factor. For a Boolean circuit use equations \(x_i^2-x_i=0\), \(x\wedge y=xy\), \(\neg x=1-x\), and an output check equal to one. The Boolean circuit scheme over \(\mathbb Z\) is a disjoint union of one copy of \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\) per satisfying bit string: the input idempotent equations give the product of \(2^n\) copies, gate equations give graphs, and the final check selects factors. Thus its entire marked representation scheme is a disjoint union of known identical smooth schemes, and for each finite field of order \(q\) its marked extension count is exactly the satisfying-assignment count times \(q^{D+1}(q^2-1)\). This is a global scheme statement. Earlier universality results for representations of three-manifold groups include complex local germs into different targets. [Kapovich–Millson](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.2347)

## 2. A single mark makes finite nilpotent extension NP-complete

**Theorem 2.** Fix a positive integer \(c\ge2\). Given an arithmetic presentation over \(\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z\), a polynomial-time algorithm produces a closed orientable hyperbolic triangulated three-manifold and one marked commutator whose extension scheme over \(\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z\) is exactly the product in (1), after changing base to \(\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z\). The polynomial bound uses the size of the arithmetic presentation with its gate list and constants written in the fixed coefficient ring.

Consequently, fix any finite field \(k\), including \(\mathbb F_2\), where \(H(k)\) is the dihedral group of order eight. For inputs promised to be closed oriented triangulated hyperbolic three-manifolds with a based embedded circle representing a commutator, extension of the prescribed central-value-one map to \(BH(k)\) is NP-complete on the promise. Counting relative homotopy classes is \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete there under polynomial-time reductions which divide the output by an explicit positive integer depending only on the reduction's construction. For the unrestricted language given by all closed oriented triangulated three-manifolds with a marked based circle, existence and counting have the same completeness statements. Starting from a Boolean circuit with \(s\) variables and gates in total, the reduction maintains \(b_1(M;k)=O(s)\). Every extension is onto \(H(k)\) when \(k\) is a prime field.

**Proof.** Lift coefficients of the arithmetic presentation to integers in \([0,c-1]\) and compile the resulting circuit as in Theorem 1. With \(c\) fixed there are \(O(s)\) labels, tests, signed unit triple coefficients and primitive surgery relations for an input of size \(s\). Compute the preliminary marked manifold by a polynomial-size surgery diagram. Present the initial connected sum by split zero-framed unknots and zero-framed Borromean links. Represent each primitive relation \(\text{axis}-\text{label}\) by a band sum of two meridians, with the second meridian reversed. Give separate meridian parallels to the occurrences of each basis label and choose all bands disjoint as embedded bands in the link exterior. Place the fixed summand diagrams in disjoint boxes, allocate disjoint small meridian arcs in the boxes, and connect their pairs by polygonal projected paths at distinct heights outside the boxes. Within each box short parallel paths from a collar of its component reach the allocated ports. Generic projected crossings of \(O(s)\) strands and the fixed local arcs give at most polynomially many crossings. Every new framing can be the diagram framing, which is an integral longitude surgery. The original meridian loops representing \(a,b\) can be retained as marked arcs outside the surgery neighborhoods. A triangulation of the marked exterior with polynomially many tetrahedra follows by thickening the diagram and separating over- and under-crossings in a fixed local pattern. The slopes for filling have length polynomial in the diagram size, and the solid tori can be filled with polynomially many layers, as in the surgery-to-triangulation procedure used by Galindo. This outputs a polynomial-length based word for \([g_a,g_b]\) in the edge generators. The product (1) applies to this preliminary manifold by the injectivity and duality arguments in Theorem 1. [Galindo, Proposition 4.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06984)

We give the marked form of efficient hyperbolization, since the mark has to survive. Take the triangulation's Heegaard splitting obtained from a neighborhood of the primal one-skeleton and its dual handlebody. The mark is a word in the first handlebody spine. In particular its word, and the corresponding free generators, have polynomial total size. Use dense curve encodings as in Ennes–Maria and stabilize away from the marking word. Their hyperbolization proof, with fixed distance parameter \(k_0\ge4\), changes only the second handlebody attaching curves by a sequence of Dehn twists whose powers are multiples of a freely chosen fixed integer. Choose that integer to be \(2c\). For every \(\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z\)-algebra \(R\), the group \(H(R)\) has exponent dividing \(2c\): the first two coordinates of the \(n\)-th power are \(np,nq\) and the last is \(nz+\binom{n}{2}pq\). Such a twist acts trivially on every based surface homomorphism into \(H(R)\). Take the twist fixing a basepoint away from the twist annulus, split a loop at its intersections with the annulus, and each inserted power has image \(1\). Therefore the set of surface homomorphisms killing both attaching sets is literally unchanged. Van Kampen gives a bijection on the based manifold homomorphisms, commuting with evaluation at the mark in the common first handlebody. It is natural in \(R\), so it preserves the full affine scheme over the specified coefficient ring. The same argument works for homomorphisms into each additive-prime-field subgroup, which shows that the dimension of mod-characteristic first cohomology is preserved. [Ennes–Maria, Proposition 4.4 and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.02814v3) · [Samperton–Weiß, Theorem 3.3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.02885)

For a single guaranteed hyperbolic output, make the two case choices in the proof of Ennes–Maria Proposition 4.4 by testing whether a specified curve is diskbusting in a specified handlebody. The test is polynomial in dense input size. Indeed, read the cyclic word of the curve in the free basis dual to the given meridian disks. A simple boundary curve fails to be diskbusting exactly when its conjugacy class belongs to a proper free factor, by Stallings' geometric Whitehead criterion. Apply the variable-rank polynomial Whitehead minimization algorithm to its cyclic word. For completeness, one can then test the proper-factor condition by checking whether the minimized word omits a basis generator. The cyclic Whitehead graph of a minimal nonempty word has no cut vertex within a nontrivial component. A component separated from the inverse of the cut vertex gives a strictly shortening Whitehead move. Each nontrivial component is closed under inversion, because otherwise choosing it as a cut set for a vertex whose inverse lies outside again gives a shortening move. Consecutive letters in the cyclic word therefore all belong to one component. If a generator is omitted, a proper factor is explicit. If no generator is omitted, the graph is connected without a cut vertex and Whitehead's lemma rules out belonging to a proper free factor. The empty word is separately recognized. Hence the curve choices throughout Proposition 4.4 can be made in polynomial time and an actual hyperbolic diagram produced. [Stallings, Theorem 3.2](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110806861.317) · [Roig–Ventura–Weil](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608779)

To triangulate this diagram while keeping the mark, first refine the boundary triangulation of the first handlebody to display the second attaching curves and their annular neighborhoods. The dense encodings ensure polynomially many segments. Extend this boundary refinement across a product collar of the original triangulation. Attach triangulated two-handles along those annuli and cone off the resulting sphere boundary. In the interior of the first handlebody put the marked word on parallel segments near its spine, separating repeated passages at vertices by general position with polynomially many local segments and subdivisions. This produces an embedded edge loop in the desired based class. The same operation embeds a fixed finite family of words as a marked bouquet whenever additional parameter marks are wanted.

On the resulting triangulation, a based homomorphism is encoded uniquely by its values on the oriented edges outside a fixed maximal tree. Face relations and the commutator value can be checked in polynomial time. This gives NP and \(\#\mathrm P\) membership. Formula (2) gives the reductions from SAT and \(\#\mathrm{SAT}\). Finally, for \(k=\mathbb F_p\), the two anchor images project to a basis of \(\mathbb F_p^2\), and their commutator generates the additive center, giving surjectivity. \(\square\)

The marked decision result applies to \(D_8\) despite a simple unmarked existence problem for nontrivial maps to that group. For odd \(p\) it also contrasts with the unmarked surjection criterion in the prior three-manifold cup-product work. Earlier counting hardness includes unmarked nonabelian targets and marked conjugacy constraints for other classes of targets. The precise assertion here is decision hardness for a constant central value on a single commutator and the multiplicative counting reduction to this marked fiber. [Samperton–Weiß](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02885) · [Kuperberg–Samperton](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.03811) · [Galindo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06984)

## 3. A topological form of generalized Cook–Levin

**Theorem 3.** Fix a nondeterministic multitape machine of time \(t(n)\ge n\), and a finite field \(k\). For every block size \(2\le b\le t=t(n)\), one can produce a list of \(2^{O(t/b)}\) single-mark hyperbolic three-manifold instances as in Theorem 2, in time \(2^{O(t/b)}\operatorname{poly}(t)\). The machine accepts exactly when some instance extends. Every instance has \(b_1(M;k)=O(t\log b)\) and its explicit reduction presentation specifies \(O(t)\) Boolean interface degrees of freedom, in \(O(t/b)\) blocks each on at most \(O(b)\) interface bits. Each interface bit occurs in at most two blocks. The extension count of every instance is computable from its supplied reduction presentation in time
\[
2^{O(t/\sqrt{\log t})}\operatorname{poly}(t)
\]
whenever \(b\le t^{4/5}\). Existence on the whole list can therefore be decided in time \(2^{O(t/b+t/\sqrt{\log t})}\operatorname{poly}(t)\). This is an algorithm on the reduction family with its supplied interface decomposition.

**Proof.** Use Strassle–Subercaseaux, Lemma 2.1. Each guessed head pattern gives a circuit-CSP with \(O(t)\) variables, \(O(t/b)\) local circuits of arity \(O(b)\) and size \(O(b\log b)\), and each variable occurs at most twice by the epoch data-flow construction. Arithmeticize the local circuits together with the Boolean idempotence constraints. Keep distinct local gate outputs and reuse the interface wires across circuits. Compiling this combined system as in Theorem 2 gives a closed hyperbolic marked manifold whose first Betti number over \(k\) is \(O(t\log b)\), with exactly the prescribed Boolean points. The manifold exists uniformly and its marked extension count is a known constant times the circuit-CSP count, by (2) and the marked hyperbolization. Since \(O(t^{4/5})\le O(t)^{9/10}\) for large \(t\), Fact 1.3 and its exact-counting proof apply to each interface description, after padding by fixed dummy bits if necessary. This gives the stated bound with no charge in the exponential for internal gate wires. Summing the per-pattern time proves the decision bound. Each raw count is computed individually, so this proof makes no assumption about duplications of accepting paths within a chosen block-respecting simulation. \(\square\) [Strassle–Subercaseaux, Lemma 2.1, Fact 1.3 and Section 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11832)

The direction of transfer also gives a future-solver consequence. Suppose marked extension on these three-manifolds can be decided from the dense manifold presentation in \(2^{O(N/\log^\alpha(N+2))}\operatorname{poly}(\text{input length})\) time, where \(N=b_1(M;k)\) and \(\alpha>0\) is fixed. Use block size \(\lceil\log^\alpha(t+2)\rceil\), truncated at \(t\). As \(N=O(t\log\log(t+4))\), the bound for every instance is at most \(2^{O(t\log\log(t+4)/\log^\alpha(t+2))}\operatorname{poly}(t)\), by eventual monotonicity of \(N/\log^\alpha(N+2)\). The number of instances contributes only \(2^{O(t/\log^\alpha(t+2))}\). Thus the same geometric solver would simulate arbitrary nondeterministic multitape time with the same logarithmic saving up to one iterated logarithm.

## 4. Boolean tensor contraction by gluing three-manifolds along tori

**Theorem 4.** Let \(\Gamma\) be a finite connected tensor network with Boolean edge indices. At each vertex \(v\), suppose \(C_v(x,w)\) is a Boolean circuit in the incident edge labels \(x\) and private bits \(w\), and let \(T_v(x)\) count its accepting private inputs. There is a closed connected orientable three-manifold \(N\) built by gluing finite-volume hyperbolic three-manifold pieces along tori, with additional based marked circles prescribed central values in \(D_8=H(\mathbb F_2)\), such that its number of marked extensions into \(BD_8\) is
\[
8^{b_1(\Gamma)} \prod_v (6\cdot 2^{D_v})
\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{E(\Gamma)}}\prod_v T_v(x).
\tag{4}
\]
Each \(D_v\) is the explicit integer from Theorem 1 applied to \(C_v\). A circuit may have no private bits. When the input includes the network reduction presentation, the contraction and marked extension count can be computed in time \(2^{O(E/\sqrt{\log(E+2)})}\operatorname{poly}(L)\) for \(E=|E(\Gamma)|\) and circuit description length \(L\), provided each degree is \(O((E+2)^{4/5})\) and the sum of private bit counts is \(O(E)\), with each individual private bit count \(O((E+2)^{4/5})\).

**Proof.** For each \(v\), use the marked closed hyperbolic manifold of Theorem 1 for the accepted-assignment scheme of \(C_v\). In addition to its normalization mark, choose one input-reading commutator for each incident half-edge as in Theorem 1, using a distinct edge-input wire for every incidence before gluing. Make these input-reading commutators into disjoint knots in the same free homotopy classes, with excellent link exterior by Myers. These knots can be chosen without a disjointness condition on the normalization word, since that word can subsequently be represented by a loop in the exterior and its free homotopy class is all that matters for any specified central value. Choose the meridian-longitude pair on each resulting torus using the solid torus removed around the knot. Along each edge glue the corresponding tori by the orientation-reversing map identifying longitudes and reversing meridians. Each piece is oriented, so \(N\) is oriented and has the asserted geometric decomposition. The required marks send each normalization word to the central generator and each glued seam meridian to the identity. These marks can be represented by circles with a shared basepoint, using conjugates and general position. [Myers, Theorem 1.1](https://openresearch.okstate.edu/bitstreams/b948a900-c087-4041-a3c5-8ccea8ee57fe/download)

Imposing a seam meridian value of one makes every piece homomorphism factor uniquely through the original closed vertex manifold, by van Kampen applied to its meridional fillings. There the marked longitude values are the chosen central bit values and the remaining marked count, for specified incident bits, is \(T_v(x)\cdot 6\cdot 2^{D_v}\), by (2). A maximal tree in \(\Gamma\) gives a graph-of-spaces presentation of \(\pi_1(N)\). Along a tree edge the two longitudes must agree. Each edge outside the tree contributes an arbitrary stable letter with the relation that conjugation identifies the longitude images. These images are central, so the relation again asks for equality and places no restriction on the stable letter. The meridian relation is automatic after imposing the marked value of one. Vertex homomorphisms and the stable letters are otherwise independent. This proves (4), counting based relative homotopy classes as before. The formula also holds for a single isolated vertex by taking its original closed manifold, with empty product of edge constraints.

For the algorithm, a tensor entry \(T_v(x)\) can be evaluated by exhaustive enumeration in \(2^{O((E+2)^{4/5})}\operatorname{poly}(L)\) time and polynomial space. Subdivide each edge by a two-input equality tensor to make the contraction network bipartite. This preserves its value, including for a loop with two incidences at one vertex. The contraction is at most \(2^{E+\sum_v |w_v|}\), so its residue modulo \(2^{E+\sum_v|w_v|+1}\) determines it exactly. For large \(E\), the degree and tensor-entry computation bounds fit the degree and oracle-time hypotheses of the Boolean tensor contraction theorem stated as Theorem 4 of Strassle–Subercaseaux, with \(\sigma\) a sufficiently large fixed multiple of \(E\). Connectedness gives at most \(E+1\) original vertices. For bounded \(E\) use exhaustive edge enumeration, including the single isolated-vertex case. Multiplying the known factors produces the exact marked count. The geometry construction in this theorem is existential, and the algorithm uses the supplied network presentation. \(\square\) [Strassle–Subercaseaux, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11832)
