# Sharp field dependence and fast simulation for weighted affine and cut polynomials

These are informal proofs. Only the fast running-time theorem and its weighted simulation consequence use the subexponential Boolean tensor contraction bound from the OpenAI ETH preprint as stated by Strassle and Subercaseaux.

## 1. The ordinary cut polynomial over different fields

For a finite graph \(G\) with a designated vertex \(o\), use the pinned cut polynomial
\[
 C_{G,o}(X)=\sum_{s:V(G)\to\{0,1\},\,s(o)=0}
   \prod_{ij\in E(G):\,s(i)\ne s(j)}X_{ij}.
\]
Write \(C_n\) for the complete graph on \(n\) vertices with one pin. An edge of activity \(u\) contributes 1 for equal spins and \(u\) for unequal spins.

**Theorem 1.** Over every field other than \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5\), \((C_n)\) is VNP-complete under p-projections. There is a single family of subcubic bipartite graphs, independent of the coefficient field, whose pinned cut polynomials are VNP-complete over those same fields. Over the three exceptional fields both families have polynomial-time point evaluation. Under \(\mathrm{Mod}_p\mathrm P\not\subseteq\mathrm P/\mathrm{poly}\), both families are VNP-intermediate over the corresponding exceptional field, even for oracle-circuit reductions. Here \(p\) is the characteristic.

**Local construction.** Let \(R\) be any commutative ring with a unit \(w\) such that \(b=w^4-1\) is a unit. This covers every field in the completeness claim. A field with more than five elements has more than four nonzero elements, and \(\mathbb F_4^\times\) has order 3. Set \(a=w^2-1\), also a unit because \(b=(w^2-1)(w^2+1)\). The integer identity
\[
 2z(x+y)=x+y+2z-(z\oplus x)-(z\oplus y)
\]
for bits \(x,y,z\) gives a boundary signature with table \((0,a,a,b)\). Use a fresh spin \(z\), join it to both \(x,y\) by activity \(w^{-1}\), give \(x,y\) unary factors \(w^x,w^y\), and give \(z\) the weight table \((-1,w^2)\) in the order \(0,1\). Summing \(z\) gives \(w^{2(x+y)}-1\). Row scales \((1,a/b)\) and column scales \((b/a^2,1/a)\) make the three surviving entries 1. The resulting signature is OR. Flipping both external bits gives NAND. A flip swaps unary entries and converts an incident edge factor \(u^{(1-x)\oplus z}\) into \(u(u^{-1})^{x\oplus z}\), so the same model realizes it.

For each Boolean variable introduce literal spins \(x,\bar x\). NAND excludes both being 1. Also join a fresh spin \(h\) to both by NAND, with unary \((1,-1)\) on \(h\). Summing \(h\) contributes 0 when both are 0 and 1 otherwise. Thus exactly one literal spin is true. For each clause attach its own spin of unary weight \((1,-1)\) to every literal spin in the clause by NAND. The sum is 0 if all literals are false by cancellation, and 1 otherwise because the clause spin is forced to 0. Duplicate literals can be removed. The empty clause yields the zero polynomial. Ordinary gate-by-gate Tseitin clauses then implement any bounded-fan-in circuit, with exactly one consistent gate assignment per input. This weighted boundary computation is valid in characteristic two as well.

Every constant unary table \((c,d)\) used here has \(c\) a unit. Replace it by the scalar \(c\) times a single edge of activity \(d/c\) to the pinned vertex. Combine multiple constant tables at a spin. A separate symbolic unary \((1,Y)\) at the same spin uses its own clone joined by activity 0, which forces equality. Two formal weights \((Y_0,Y_1)\) can be placed at the complement and positive literal spins as \((1,Y_0)\) and \((1,Y_1)\), respectively. A scalar \(c\) is itself a cut sum on an extra leaf whose edge to the pin has activity \(c-1\). All substitutions therefore use individual indeterminates or constants.

Split any high-degree vertex into a path of equality clones, placing at most one old edge on each clone and retaining one pin if the original vertex was pinned. This gives maximum degree three and one extension to the clones of any nonzero-weight assignment. Subdivide each edge by inserting an equality edge followed by the old activity. Now every old vertex is on one side of a bipartition and every subdivision vertex is on the other. The size remains linear. A projection to the complete graph is obtained by filling absent edges with activity 1.

The full compiler uses \(O(N+g)\) vertices and edges for \(N\) named inputs and \(g\) bounded-fan-in gates, with \(O(N+g)\) labeled unary factors. Each accepted input contributes exactly its weight product, and all other inputs contribute 0. Apply it to a polynomial-size Boolean verifier of whether edge indicators describe one directed Hamiltonian cycle on \(m\) vertices. Such a verifier can test the degrees and follow the successor of vertex 1. Give the positive indicator for arc \(ij\) weight \((1,Y_{ij})\). Valiant's Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial results exactly. This establishes hardness over each stated field with the same graph family. The graph polynomials belong to VNP because a spin witness contributes the product over edges of \(1+(s_i+s_j-2s_is_j)(X_{ij}-1)\), omitting the pinned bit from the witness. [Valiant, Completeness classes in algebra](https://doi.org/10.1145/800135.804419)

**Exceptional fields.** The algorithm applies more generally to \(\sum_{x:Ax=d}\prod_i\lambda_i(x_i)\), with \(Ax=d\) a Boolean affine system over \(\mathbb F_2\) and weights in the coefficient field. A zero unary entry forces the other allowed bit and two zero entries make the sum zero. Include these pins in Gaussian elimination. Each remaining coordinate is affine in free bits and every unary ratio is nonzero. In \(\mathbb F_2\) the answer is the affine solution count modulo 2. In \(\mathbb F_3\) the ratios are signs, so the product is a constant times a parity sign, summing to zero or a signed power of 2.

In \(\mathbb F_5\) write the ratios as powers of \(i=2\), where \(i^2=-1\). The XOR expansion modulo 4 of any number of bits is their sum minus twice the sum of their pairwise products. Complementing a parity replaces it by one minus itself. Consequently the contraction is a scalar times
\[
 \sum_{u\in\{0,1\}^k} i^{Q(u)},\qquad
 Q(u)=c+\sum_j l_j u_j+2\sum_{j<h}b_{jh}u_j u_h\pmod 4.
\]
To eliminate a bit, write its part in the exponent as \(l u+2uL(v)\), where \(L\) is a linear parity of the remaining variables \(v\). For odd \(l\) set \(r=i^l\). The summation factor is \(1+r(-1)^{L(v)}=(1+r)(-r)^{L(v)}\), using \(r^2=-1\). The scalar \(1+r\) is nonzero and the new parity phase preserves the displayed quadratic form. For even \(l\) the summation factor equals 2 under the affine condition \(L(v)=l/2\bmod 2\), and zero otherwise. Solve that condition and substitute any affine parameterization. The phase keeps the same form by the XOR expansion and the fact that the even quadratic terms only need their coefficients modulo 2. Maintaining the \(O(k^2)\) coefficients takes polynomial time. Cut polynomial evaluation is an instance of this algorithm via the extra affine coordinates \(s_i\oplus s_j\).

Over a fixed finite field of characteristic \(p\), VP=VNP implies \(\mathrm{Mod}_p\mathrm P\subseteq\mathrm P/\mathrm{poly}\). To see this, build the VNP polynomial that outputs the accepting count modulo \(p\) on Boolean formula encodings. For each input length use a bounded-fan-in Boolean verifier and multiply one constant-degree gate truth polynomial per gate. Sum over witness bits and gate-value bits, which have exactly one extension per accepting witness. Small field circuits yield small Boolean circuits on those encodings. Similarly, c-completeness of a family admitting polynomial-time point evaluation implies the containment by evaluating the c-reduction. Conversely, a VP circuit for either cut family over \(\mathbb F_q\), \(q=2,3,5\), would give a VP circuit over \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\) for the same polynomial. Its proved VNP-completeness there again implies the containment. This proves the three conditional intermediate regimes.

Bürgisser's exponentiated cut enumerator uses \(X_e^{q-1}\) over \(\mathbb F_q\), so its point evaluation sees only zero versus nonzero activities. De Rugy-Altherre established rational-field cut hardness. Van den Nest, Dür and Briegel established spin model universality at complex Ising parameters. The new claims concern the unpowered multivariate polynomial, its precise field dependence, and the sparse linear-size compiler. [Bürgisser](https://emis.de/ft/51738) · [de Rugy-Altherre](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.7641) · [Van den Nest, Dür and Briegel](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.2275)

## 2. A single multiplication relation over a fixed finite group

A multiplication CSP over a fixed group \(G\) uses equations \(xy=z\). Its solution enumerator is
\[
 P(X)=\sum_{\sigma:\,\mathrm{equations\ hold}}\prod_v X_{v,\sigma(v)}.
\]
Substituting 0 provides unary lists, and substituting 1 gives auxiliary variables neutral weight.

**Theorem 2.** For every fixed finite group of order at least three, multiplication CSP solution polynomials have a family VNP-complete under p-projections over every coefficient field. Every variable can be restricted to at most three multiplication equations, and the compilation of bounded-fan-in circuits has linear size. For the order-two group there is a single family with the exact three-field exception and conditional intermediate status of Theorem 1. For the trivial group the solution polynomials are monomials.

**Proof.** Work additively in an abelian subgroup. If \(g\) has order at least three, use the Boolean list \(\{0,g\}\). The three sums \(0,g,2g\) are distinct. For inputs \(x,y\) and AND output \(z\) enforce
\[
 x+\bar x=g,\quad p_{00}+p_{01}=\bar x,\quad
 p_{10}+p_{11}=x,\quad p_{01}+p_{11}=y,\quad z=p_{11}
\]
where all the letters on the left and right, apart from the constant \(g\), take Boolean values. Exactly one \(p\) takes the value \(g\), in row \(x\) and column \(y\), and \(z\) follows. The extension is unique. Negation uses \(x+\bar x=g\). Constants are pinned and equality uses an already pinned identity.

Otherwise every nonidentity element has order two, so \(G\) is abelian by \((ab)^{-1}=ba=ab\). If \(G\) has more than two elements it contains \(H=\{0,e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2\}\). A Boolean bit of type \(e\ne0\) takes values in \(\{0,e\}\). For distinct types \(e,f\), implementing equal logical bits amounts to enforcing \(u+v=h\) with \(h\) listed in \(\{0,e+f\}\). Its extension to \(h\) is uniquely determined. For AND give \(x,y,z\) types \(e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2\). Requiring \(x+z\) and \(y+z\) to lie in \(H\setminus\{e_1+e_2\}\) enforces respectively \(z\le x\) and \(z\le y\) on logical bits. Compute \(u=x+y\) in all of \(H\), and require \(u+z\in H\setminus\{e_1+e_2\}\). Among triples satisfying the first two conditions this excludes precisely the logical triple \(x=y=1,z=0\). Every auxiliary sum is uniquely determined. Equal-type negation uses \(u+v=e\).

In either case wire these gadgets into a verifier circuit and split shared variables into equality paths. Each copy lies in one original atom and at most two equality atoms. Use a separate constant variable pinned to the identity for each equality. Gate encodings are parsimonious under their lists, so the Hamiltonian-cycle verifier gives VNP-hardness over every field. VNP membership follows by a fixed-domain Boolean encoding, taking a product of constant-size truth-table polynomials and summing over valid values. If \(G\) is abelian, the unweighted counts are polynomial-time computable by linear algebra in the fixed cyclic decomposition of \(G\).

For order two, implement each edge parity by \(s_i+s_j=u_{ij}\), set \(s_o=0\) by a unary list, and give \(u_{ij}\) weight \((1,X_{ij})\). Use the fixed subcubic graph family of Theorem 1. Its hard fields transfer under projection. Its intermediate fields transfer because the source system is Boolean affine and point evaluation is polynomial there, while small VP circuits would imply small circuits for the cut family. In the trivial group there is one assignment before any specialization. This argument upgrades the additive template case of the algebraic CSP question to all coefficient fields and handles the order-two extension-field dependence.

## 3. Weighted block Cook–Levin via sparse cut polynomials

**Theorem 3.** Fix a nondeterministic multitape machine with a known time-constructible bound \(t=t(n)\ge n\). Give each transition at time \(i\) a formal weight \(W_{i,a}\) according to its finite transition label \(a\). For a block size \(1\le b\le t\) as in the block Cook–Levin construction, there is a deterministic reduction of the accepting-path weight polynomial to a sum of at most \(2^{O(t/b)}\) projections of cut polynomials on subcubic bipartite graphs with \(O(t\log(2b))\) vertices and edges each. Each graph is given in \(O(t/b)\) pieces with \(O(b)\) unpinned interface bits per piece, total unpinned interface incidence \(O(t)\), and \(O(b\log(2b))\) local vertices per piece. The coefficient ring may be any commutative ring with a unit \(w\) such that \(w^4-1\) is a unit. Alternatively use fixed-group solution polynomials of Theorem 2 for a group of order at least three, over any commutative coefficient ring.

**Proof.** Use the parsimonious three-block window implementation of the epoch decomposition in [the affine transfer](AffineTransfer.md). The fixed input and blank positions are constants. Each computation step in a block has a constant-length transition code. Impose the canonical code after halting, with weight 1. Each accepting computation uniquely determines its head pattern, transition codes and all carried states, offsets and tape contents. For each step \(i\), add constant-size Boolean gates indicating each possible valid transition code \(a\); give its indicator spin unary weight \((1,W_{i,a})\). On a valid computation the resulting product is its path monomial. Gate and boundary sizes are unchanged up to constants.

Compile each local Boolean circuit before gluing, using the exact boundary signature of Theorem 1. Each shared bit occurs at most twice at the epoch level in Lemma 2.1, so one linking equality edge between local clones connects those occurrences. Choose pinned-spin clones within each epoch and connect them by a chain of equality edges to the global pin. Their spin 0 is known on every surviving term. Splitting and subdividing preserve the \(O(b\log(2b))\) local size. Any linking subdivision can be folded into one adjacent piece or the equality edge. The free inter-epoch incidence is \(O(t)\). The graph construction uses polynomial time per pattern. The group version instead compiles each gate with unique extensions by Theorem 2 and glues by group equality. In either construction, summing the resulting projections over all patterns counts each weighted path exactly once. [Strassle–Subercaseaux, Lemma 2.1 and §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11832)

## 4. Fast contractions over finite free coefficient algebras

**Theorem 4.** Assume the Boolean tensor contraction bound stated as Theorem 4 of Strassle–Subercaseaux. In its setting of \(O(\sigma)\) edges and precision \(r=O(\sigma)\), the same \(2^{O(\sigma/\sqrt{\log\sigma})}\operatorname{poly}(\sigma+L)\) bound holds over any fixed-rank finite free commutative \(\mathbb Z/2^r\mathbb Z\)-algebra, represented by structure constants in a basis of rank independent of \(\sigma\). It also holds over every fixed finite commutative ring. An entry oracle supplies the coordinate tuple of the tensor entry, with the resource bounds of the original theorem.

**Proof.** Compute and remove isolated-vertex scalar factors. There are at most \(D=O(\sigma)\) remaining vertices. In a basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_d\), replace each entry by the linear polynomial \(\sum_{j=1}^d c_jZ_j\), where \(c_j\) are integer representatives of its coordinates. The contraction gives an integer polynomial \(F(Z)\), of degree at most \(D\) in each variable. Substituting \(Z_j=e_j\) computes the desired ring contraction after reducing the coefficients modulo \(2^r\).

Use the base tensor algorithm on the grid \(\{0,\ldots,D\}^d\) at precision \(r'=r+d\,\nu_2(D!)=O(\sigma)\). Each evaluated entry is a linear combination of the supplied coordinates. The rank is fixed, so the grid is polynomial-size and meets the original oracle resource bounds. In the multivariate falling-factorial basis the coefficient at \(\prod_j (Z_j)_{k_j}\) equals
\[
 \frac{\Delta_1^{k_1}\cdots\Delta_d^{k_d}F(0)}{\prod_j k_j!}.
\]
The quotient is an integer by the integral unit-triangular change from the monomial basis in each coordinate. Divide the numerator residue by the known power of 2, which costs at most \(d\nu_2(D!)\) bits of precision, and invert the odd part to recover the quotient modulo \(2^r\). This recovers the wanted coefficients and avoids division in the given coefficient algebra. Restore the isolated-vertex product.

For any fixed finite ring use generators of the cyclic summands in its finite additive group, with bounded nonnegative integer coordinate representatives. The same polynomial has coefficients bounded by \(2^{|E|}C^D\) for a constant \(C\) depending only on the ring. Choose \(r=O(\sigma)\) large enough to reconstruct these coefficients over the integers before evaluating in the ring. [Strassle–Subercaseaux, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11832) · [OpenAI, fast tensor contraction](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026)

**Weighted simulation consequence.** For \(r=O(t)\), the weighted accepting-path sum over \(\mathcal R_r=(\mathbb Z/2^r\mathbb Z)[w]/(w^2+w+1)\) can be evaluated in \(2^{O(t/\sqrt{\log t})}\operatorname{poly}(t+L)\) deterministic time. So can the weighted sum over any fixed finite ring using the epoch circuit signatures directly. In particular, when the sum of the per-step maximum bit lengths of signed integer transition weights is \(O(t)\), the integer path sum is computable exactly within this bound.

Indeed \(w\) is a unit and \((w^4-1)(w+2)=-3\) is a unit in \(\mathcal R_r\). Take \(b=\lceil\sqrt{\log(2+t)}\rceil\), restricted to \(b\le t\). Enumerate \(O(b\log(2b))\) local variables to evaluate any piece's conditioned sum, or directly evaluate the original epoch circuit times its prescribed unary weights. A variable with a single occurrence can be summed within its epoch in the same local entry bound. The number of interface edges is \(O(t)\), and the contraction is bipartite after subdividing every interface edge by a two-input equality tensor. The degree and entry time are within the hypotheses of Theorem 4 with \(\sigma\) a constant multiple of \(t\). Summing up to \(2^{O(t/b)}\) contractions preserves the claimed exponent. Under the signed bit-length bound the total magnitude is at most \(2^{O(t)}\), by the path count bound and the product of maximum transition magnitudes. The integer follows from its residue modulo \(2^r\) with sufficiently large \(r=O(t)\). The cited paper already discusses unweighted accepting-path counts in Remark 1.4. The result here preserves transition weights and supports nontrivial coefficient algebras.
