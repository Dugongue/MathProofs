# Collinearity isomorphism on a fixed cubic is GI-complete

**Theorem.** Given two finite sets of rational points on the fixed smooth cubic $E:y^2=x^3-2$, deciding whether a bijection preserves collinearity of every distinct triple is GI-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. The result also holds for the fixed cubic $y=x^3$ over the rationals. In the latter construction the abscissae are integers of magnitude $O(n^2)$ for an $n$-vertex graph, and the reduction works unchanged over a prime field of order $O(n^2)$ chosen from the graph order. For any fixed elliptic curve over a fixed finite field, the construction can also be realized over extensions of cardinality $O(n^4)$.

Here isomorphism concerns the incidence structure, equivalently the simple rank-three matroid. It allows every bijection of the given point sets that preserves collinear triples in both directions. The proof is informal.

## Procedure for making the point sets

Given a simple graph $G$ on $n$ vertices, form $J$ by adding three universal vertices, with edges between them as well. Graph isomorphism of $G$ extends to $J$. Conversely, the added vertices lie in the set of all universal vertices of $J$. Any three universal vertices can be removed to obtain a graph isomorphic to $G$, since every permutation of the universal vertices is an automorphism. Thus an isomorphism between two such graphs $J$ implies an isomorphism of their original graphs. Every vertex of $J$ has degree at least two. For an input pair with different orders we can return a fixed pair with different cardinalities.

Write $N=n+3$, and choose a prime $p$ with $N<p<2N$. This can be done in polynomial time in $N$ by trial primality tests, using Bertrand's postulate. Index the vertices by $i$ from $0$ through $N-1$ and use integers
$$
 s_i=2pi+(i^2\bmod p).
$$
The residue in this formula lies in $[0,p-1]$. All unordered pair sums $s_i+s_j$, even with repetitions, are distinct unless the index pairs are equal as multisets. Indeed, the low part in a sum is less than $2p$. Equality of sums therefore gives equality of the integer index sums and of the sums of square residues modulo $p$. Since $p$ is odd, the index products are equal modulo $p$ as well. The index pairs are the roots of the same monic quadratic over $\mathbb F_p$, and each index is less than $p$.

Take $L=2p^2$ and $C=L+1$. Give vertex $i$ the integer $v_i=C+s_i$, and give edge $\{i,j\}$ the integer $e_{ij}=-v_i-v_j$. The $v_i$ lie in $[C,2C)$, while $e_{ij}$ are negative with magnitudes in $[2C,4C)$. The pair-sum property gives distinct edge labels, and no two of the labels are negatives of one another. Three distinct labels sum to zero precisely when they are $v_i,v_j,e_{ij}$ for an edge. All-one-sign triples and triples with two negatives have nonzero sums by the bounds. For the remaining sign pattern the pair-sum property identifies the endpoints. All labels have magnitude less than $4C$, and every three-label sum has magnitude less than $12C$.

## Realize the same ternary relation in three ways

1. On the rational cusp $y=x^3$, map label $t$ to $(t,t^3)$. The affine collinearity determinant for $t,u,v$ is, up to sign, the product $(u-t)(v-t)(v-u)(t+u+v)$. The factors other than the sum are nonzero on distinct labels. For the finite-field version choose a prime $P$ with $12C<P<24C$ and reduce coordinates modulo $P$. No differences or nonzero triple sums can vanish on reduction, so the same ternary relation holds. Both graphs of equal order use the same prime.
2. On $E:y^2=x^3-2$ over $\mathbb Q$, use $A=(3,5)$. This is a nonsingular cubic and $A$ has infinite order. The Nagell–Lutz theorem in short integral Weierstrass form requires any integral torsion point with nonzero second coordinate to have $y^2$ dividing $4a^3+27b^2$. Here $25$ does not divide $108$. Send label $t$ to $[t]A$. Three distinct points of a smooth plane Weierstrass cubic are collinear exactly when their sum in the elliptic-curve group is the identity. Since $A$ has infinite order, this is exactly the integer sum condition. Every label is nonzero, so these points are affine. Opposite labels are absent, so the points also have distinct abscissae. To compute them, use doubling and addition with $O(\log |t|)$ group operations. Each group operation uses a bounded number of rational operations, treating the identity and inverse cases separately. The maximum bit length after an operation grows by at most a fixed constant factor plus a fixed constant. This gives polynomial bit lengths and running time because $|t|=O(n^2)$. [Silverman and Tate, *Rational Points on Elliptic Curves*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-18588-0)
3. For any fixed elliptic curve over $\mathbb F_{q_0}$, take an extension of size $Q=q_0^f$ with $f$ the first positive integer for which $\sqrt Q-1>12C$. It has $Q=O(n^4)$ for fixed $q_0$. Hasse's bound gives $\#E(\mathbb F_Q)\ge(\sqrt Q-1)^2$, and the finite elliptic-curve group has at most two cyclic factors. Its exponent is at least the square root of its order, and is realized as the order of a point. So a point $A$ with order greater than $12C$ exists. Construct the field by searching for an irreducible polynomial of degree $f$ and find $A$ by enumeration and exact order tests. This is polynomial in $n$ because $Q$ is polynomial in $n$. Send $t$ to $[t]A$. Distinctness and the sum test hold by the magnitude bounds. [Sutherland, MIT elliptic curves notes](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2022/lectures.html)

## Recover the graph from the whole incidence relation

For any of the three realizations the collinear triples are exactly the edges of the hypergraph with triples $\{v_i,v_j,e_{ij}\}$. Every edge point belongs to exactly one triple. A vertex point belongs to at least two. Consequently the vertex points are intrinsically recognizable. Joining two of them when they occur together in a triple recovers $J$. An isomorphism of $J$ also extends to a collinearity isomorphism by mapping each edge point to the point for the image edge. This proves both directions. There are $N+|E(J)|$ points and the three or more vertex points include a noncollinear triple, so the matroid has rank three.

For membership in GI, compute the collinearity hypergraph by one determinant per distinct triple and turn it into a two-coloured graph with a node for each point and a node for each triple, joined by incidence. Colour-preserving graph isomorphism reduces to ordinary graph isomorphism in polynomial time.

## Relation to Ley and to known reductions

The earlier Ley work computed ternary collinearity on polynomial graphs. The earlier permanent encoding used designated parts of a cubic incidence relation. Here the Sidon labels and sign separation ensure that the **entire uncoloured** relation has just the desired triples. The identified graph structure survives replacing the cusp by one fixed smooth elliptic curve. Rao and Sarma already give GI-completeness for bounded-rank linearly represented matroids. The restriction here is that all points lie on one fixed cubic. The recent elliptic-matroid literature uses the zero-sum relation on finite cyclic groups. It supplies close geometric context. Priority for the fixed-curve restriction remains unverified. [Rao–Sarma, *On the Complexity of Matroid Isomorphism Problem*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.3859) · [*Elliptic matroids and modular curves*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05299)
