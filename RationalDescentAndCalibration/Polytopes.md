# Rational realizability of a convex 4-polytope with four supplied realizations

**Theorem.** Rational polynomial feasibility reduces effectively to rational realizability of labelled convex 4-polytopes whose normalized real realization space is contractible. The input can include four explicit labelled realizations, one over each of the fixed real fields
$$\mathbb Q(\sqrt{13}),\quad\mathbb Q(\sqrt{17}),\quad\mathbb Q(\sqrt{221}),\quad\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{2}).$$
Thus the least degree of a real number field of realization is one or two. Under the [Diophantine premise](Results.md), deciding degree one on these promised inputs is halting-complete.

**Proof.** For an integer polynomial $f(x)$ let
$$F(x,t)=f(x)(t^2-13)(t^2-17)(t^2-221).$$
Use the primary real algebraic set defined by $y^3=2F(x,t)^3$. On real points this is the graph of $y=\sqrt[3]{2}F(x,t)$, homeomorphic to Euclidean space under projection. A rational point requires $F=0$, since otherwise $y/F$ would be a rational cube root of two. None of the three quadratic factors has a rational zero. Conversely a rational zero of $f$ gives a rational point. Each quadratic field has a point with $x=0$, $y=0$ and $t$ the appropriate square root; the cubic field has a point with $x=t=0$. These are claims of separate realizability over each individual field.

Apply the effective 4-polytope universality theorem of Richter-Gebert, in the formulation of Richter-Gebert and Ziegler. It produces a face lattice whose normalized realization space is stably equivalent to this primary set. Their Lemma 1 preserves homotopy type and nonemptiness over each real algebraic subfield, including the rationals. This proves the contractibility and field promises for the same polytope. The theorem produces the face lattice effectively. To provide actual coordinates as part of the input, enumerate labelled vertex tuples in each of the four computable ordered number fields and check the face lattice by exact arithmetic. All four searches terminate; there is no asserted polynomial-time bound for this witness production. Rational search likewise semidecides the positive case. [Universality theorem and Lemma 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9510217)

**Local incidence and rank-two completion.** The three quadratic fields have pairwise intersection $\mathbb Q$. For each prime $p$ at least one embeds in $\mathbb Q_p$: away from $2,13,17$ one of $13,17,221$ has quadratic character one, and Hensel lifting applies. At 2 use $17\equiv1\pmod8$, at 13 use $17\equiv4$, and at 17 use $13\equiv8^2$. Thus vertex–facet incidence and nonincidence can also be realized over each completion (transfer facet equations via an embedding); convex inequalities are asserted over real fields only. The repository's field-faithful rank-two construction applied to the same equation also gives binary partial matrices whose rational rank-two completability detects a rational zero of $f$, with completions over all four indicated fields. This additional consequence uses the earlier informal completion theorem. [Rank-two geometric universality](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/RankTwoGeometricUniversality/PROOF.md)

Arithmetic universality for 4-polytopes is classical. This note singles out the simultaneous promises of contractibility, supplied field witnesses of coprime degrees, and a one-versus-two degree decision.
