# Exact rational attainment changes at the second use of a bias

A finite acyclic binary experiment has rational constants and adjustable independent Bernoulli trials with shared bias labels. A fresh toss is made on every visit. The adjustable biases range over a closed box with rational endpoints. Specified target probabilities are rational.

**One use per path.** The acceptance probability is multi-affine since each path probability is a product in which each adjustable label occurs at most once. Its extreme values occur at vertices of the box and can be computed by finite enumeration. If the desired target lies between them, take an edge path from a minimizing to a maximizing vertex. Some edge straddles the target; there rational affine interpolation gives an exact rational calibration, including endpoint and constant cases. This result is unconditional.

**Two uses per path.** Under the premise in [Results](Results.md), there exists a single fixed experiment with one supplied rational bias and a fixed number of adjustable biases. Every path uses at most four labelled tosses, at most twice of any individual label, plus fixed fair coins. Every branch bias can be kept in $[1/4,3/4]$. The real maximum is always $1/2$; the rational supremum is always $1/2$ with effective approximations. Whether rational settings *attain* it is computably enumerable complete.

Start from a fixed quadratic arithmetic-gate system $E_i(x)=0$ with $x_0$ the input register. For input $n\ge0$ supply $\tau=1/(n+2)$ and enforce $\tau(x_0+2)=1$. Introduce bounded coordinates $z,s,h$ in $[-1,1]$. Homogenize every gate to degree two as $s^2 E_i(z/s)$, interpreted polynomially, impose $\tau z_0+2\tau s-s=0$, and set $s=2h^2-1$. For rational $h$, $s$ cannot vanish. Conversely for any rational solution $x$, choose rational $h$ sufficiently close to $1/\sqrt2$, then take $z=sx$ to fit the box. Over the reals $h=1/\sqrt2,z=s=0$ always solves the equations, for every supplied parameter.

For any adjustable variable appearing squared in an equation, introduce a duplicate, replace the square by the product with its duplicate and impose equality by a separate linear equation. Thus every residual is multi-affine of total degree at most two, and their sum of squares $P$ has total degree at most four and each individual degree at most two. Encode the bounded variables, and the supplied $\tau$, by rational affine transformations of biases in $[1/4,3/4]$. For instance use $x=4p-2$ for a coordinate in $[-1,1]$. Expand a positive integer multiple of $P$ in the coin means $2p-1$, obtaining integer coefficients $c_\alpha$. Fix a power of two $M\ge\sum|c_\alpha|$. A fair selector allocates $|c_\alpha|$ out of $M$ leaves to each monomial. On such a leaf toss its indicated coins with fresh randomness and accept if the product of their signs equals $-\operatorname{sign}(c_\alpha)$. Unallocated leaves accept fairly. Acceptance is $1/2-\sum c_\alpha(2p-1)^\alpha/(2M)$. Thus exact attainment detects a simultaneous zero. The repetition and horizon bounds follow from the degrees.

The exhibited real setting can be approximated by rational settings effectively and independently of existence of an exact rational solution. Enumeration semidecides exact attainment. The same experiment is a fixed finite-horizon partially observable process: reveal each adjustable label as an observation, use two actions for the two toss outcomes, and share one stationary randomized behavioral policy across occurrences of that observation. To allow unrestricted policy probabilities, the environment can mix each policy action with fixed fair noise to implement the smaller bias box.

The repository uses shared rational mixed strategies in equilibrium constructions. Existing work analyzes reachability in real-parametric Markov chains. The restrictions here concern repeated use of an individual parameter and exact attainment with rational parameters.
