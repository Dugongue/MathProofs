# One changing matrix entry and the uniqueness of a common rational invariant plane

**Theorem, conditional on the arithmetic premise.** There exist fixed $N,k$, an integer diagonal matrix $D\in\operatorname{GL}_N(\mathbb Q)$, fixed integer matrices $B_0,E$ with just one nonzero entry in $E$, and a known fixed rational $k$-plane $W_*$, with the following properties. All $B(n)=B_0+nE$ are rationally conjugate to the same finite-order signed permutation matrix. Both $D$ and $B(n)$ preserve $W_*$. For each nonnegative integer $n$ their common rational $k$-planes consist either of $W_*$ alone or of infinitely many planes. Deciding the second alternative is halting-complete. No hypothesis about solvability of ordinary tensor or matrix isomorphism is needed.

**Projective quadrics with a marked point.** Take the fixed quadratic gate system from the arithmetic premise, with $x_0=n$ the only input assignment. Introduce projective coordinates $w,s,h,z_i,v_1,\ldots,v_4$. Use $s^2 E_i(z/s)=0$ for each fixed quadratic gate (including lower-degree gates after homogenization), and impose
$$wz_0-nws=0,\qquad ws=2h^2-w^2,\qquad \sum_i z_i^2+s^2+h^2+\sum_{j=1}^4v_j^2=4w^2.$$
All these equations are homogeneous quadrics with integer coefficients after fixed clearing of denominators. A rational point must have $w\ne0$ by the last equation. Normalize $w=1$. Then $s\ne0$ since $2h^2=1$ has no rational solution. Thus $x=z/s$ satisfies the original system. Conversely fix any rational solution $x$ and vary $h$ through rational numbers sufficiently close to $1/\sqrt2$. With $s=2h^2-1$ and $z=sx$, the remaining right side in the four-square equation is positive rational. Every positive rational $a/b$ is a sum of four rational squares by representing $ab$ and dividing the four integers by $b$. Infinitely many normalized points result. Finally add a coordinate $p$ and equations $py=0$ for each old coordinate $y$. This adds exactly one disjoint rational point, $[p:y]=[1:0]$, to every fiber. The sole dependence on $n$ is the coefficient of $ws$ in one quadric.

**Turn points into pairs of lines.** Let $d$ be the number of coordinates including $p$, $U$ the space of symmetric $d$-square matrices, and $V=\mathbb Q^d\oplus\mathbb Q$. Use arrows $A_j:U\to V$ giving the columns of $X\in U$ in the first summand. For each quadric use one additional arrow with value in the last summand, given by its linearization on symmetric matrices, replacing $x_i x_j$ by $X_{ij}$ once per monomial. A pair of lines $L\subset U$, $M\subset V$ obeys $A_jL\subset M$ for all $j$ exactly when it comes from a projective point of the quadrics. In fact a nonzero $X$ on $L$ has a nonzero column, forcing $M$ into the first summand. All columns belong to $M$, making $X$ rank one; a symmetric rank-one rational matrix is $cxx^t$ for a nonzero rational $x$ and nonzero rational scalar $c$, with no square root required. The scalar-valued arrows now say precisely that $x$ is a point. Conversely such a point supplies both lines uniquely.

**Force that dimension vector with two matrices.** Choose $m$ strictly larger than $\dim U$ and at least the number of arrows. Pad the list with zero arrows to length $m$. On $U\oplus V^{\oplus m}$ let $D$ have distinct positive integer eigenvalues on the $m+1$ displayed blocks and act scalarly on each. Write $C$ for the cyclic shift on the $V$ blocks with a minus sign on the wraparound, so $C^m=-I$. Put
$$B(n)=\begin{pmatrix} I & 0 \\ (A_j(n))_{j=1}^{m} & C\end{pmatrix}.$$
Only one matrix entry varies: the coefficient on $X_{ws}$ in a scalar-valued arrow. Since $I-C$ is rationally invertible, conjugation by a rational lower unitriangular block matrix eliminates the off-diagonal column. Thus all $B(n)$ are conjugate to $\operatorname{diag}(I,C)$ and have finite order.

A $D$-invariant rational subspace splits across the displayed blocks by rational polynomial spectral projections. Invariance under $B(n)$ forces the $m$ subspaces in the copies of $V$ to agree (each shift inclusion is followed cyclically by its reverse dimension inequality). It also imposes exactly the arrow inclusions from its $U$-subspace to the common $V$-subspace. For total dimension $k=1+m$, if these two dimensions are $a,b$, then $a+mb=1+m$, with $0\le a\le\dim U<m$, so $a=b=1$. The desired planes are consequently in bijection with the projective rational points, and the added coordinate point gives the same explicitly known plane for every $n$. Enumeration of rational bases semidecides existence of a second plane.

The repository's marked-point construction and its quiver work motivate the two ends of this argument. Projective universality of quiver Grassmannians is classical. The direct construction above checks rationality and the fixed single-entry, finite-order and known-plane restrictions explicitly.
