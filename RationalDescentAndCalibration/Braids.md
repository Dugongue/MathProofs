# Arithmetic universality in linear slices of a single Yang–Baxter orbit

**Geometric theorem.** Every affine scheme of finite presentation over $\mathbb Q$ is isomorphic over $\mathbb Q$ to an affine-linear section of the braid equations on involutions. All points of the linear family are isometries for the product of a fixed split symmetric form with itself. All solutions over any characteristic-zero extension field belong to one local split-orthogonal orbit with a rational representative depending only on the construction format. The ambient dimension can depend on the input presentation in this first assertion.

**Quadratics as a composition.** Replace polynomial evaluations by gate variables and their graph equations, giving a quadratic presentation $q_j(x)=0$ without altering the coordinate ring. Write each $q_j$ as a sum of products of affine linear forms. For each equation use one basis index in a space $V_0$ and one in $V_2$, and for each of its terms use a separate index in $V_1$. Put the two linear forms of that term on successive arrows $V_0\xrightarrow{B}V_1\xrightarrow{A}V_2$. With other entries zero, $AB$ is the diagonal matrix with entries $q_j$. Copy each coordinate into an additional private column of $A$ with its row of $B$ zero, if needed, so the parameter map is an affine-linear embedding. Include dummy zero rows/columns to avoid zero-dimensional marker blocks. Replace the middle space by $V_1\oplus V_2\oplus V_0$ and the two matrices by $[A,I,0]$ and $[B;0;I]$ (the latter is a block column). The composition stays unchanged and the arrows now always have full surjective and injective rank, respectively. Write them again as $A,B$. Extended by zero to the full graded space, the only potentially nonzero product of two arrows is $AB$.

**The braid equation reads the entries of that composition.** Adjoin two disjoint two-dimensional blocks carrying single matrix units $e,f$, each square-zero. All products involving a marker and any other listed factor vanish. For $u\wedge v=u\otimes v-v\otimes u$ set $r=e\wedge A+B\wedge f$ and $X=P(I+r)$ with $P$ the swap. Then $r^2=0$ and $r_{21}=-r$, so $X^2=I$ at every parameter. The braid equation for $X$ is the unbraided Yang–Baxter equation for $I+r$. Its linear terms cancel. Its quadratic terms are the three placement commutators $[r_{12},r_{13}]+[r_{12},r_{23}]+[r_{13},r_{23}]$. In these, the sole surviving product of leg factors is $AB$; the six terms place $AB,e,f$ in the six orders with alternating signs. For instance the placement with $e$ first and $f$ third has middle factor $AB$ from $[r_{12},r_{23}]$. The cubic terms vanish because every selected edge term contains a marker on a leg that must be multiplied by another factor. Disjoint supports show that entries of this defect are linear combinations of the $q_j$ and, conversely, projections onto a prescribed marker entry on each outside leg read every $q_j$. Therefore the ideal of the pulled-back equations is the defining ideal, including its nilpotents. Contraction against the two distinct markers recovers the arrow matrices, preserving the affine embedding.

**Isometry and a single orbit.** Double the space to $H_0\oplus H_0^*$ with the hyperbolic rational symmetric form. Put all previous factors on the original half, and set $\widehat r=r-r^\dagger$ with the adjoint for the product form. Its two halves have disjoint supports, $\widehat r^\dagger=-\widehat r$, $\widehat r^2=0$, and $\widehat r_{21}=-\widehat r$. Thus $P(I+\widehat r)$ is a split-orthogonal involution even away from the braid locus. Projecting the defect to the original half still reads all equations. Conversely overlapping factors from opposite halves have zero product, and in the dual half the only possible nonzero products are transposes of the original composition, so the scheme-level ideal remains unchanged.

At $AB=0$, choose the images of the basis of $V_0$ under $B$ first in a basis of the middle space, complete to the kernel of $A$, then adjoin lifts of the basis of $V_2$. This puts every solution over the ground field into the same model complex with ranks fixed. The changes on $H_0$ fixing markers extend by inverse transpose to isometries on the double. The model is defined over $\mathbb Q$ even if the slice itself has no rational solution. All strand representations at a solution are conjugate isometrically to flips: take $s=e\otimes A+B\otimes f$ on the original half, replace it by $s-s^\dagger$ on the double, and apply the commuting product of strand intertwiners from the earlier braid construction. **Fixed arithmetic problem.** Under the premise in [Results](Results.md), let $F(t,x)=0$ be a fixed rational equation for a halting-complete set on nonnegative integer inputs. Introduce a separate variable $z$ and multiply $F$ by $(z^2-13)(z^2-17)(z^2-221)$. No factor has a rational root. Over the reals and over every $\mathbb Q_p$ at least one factor has a root: for odd unramified primes use the quadratic characters of 13, 17 and their product; 17 is a square at 2 and 13, and 13 is a square at 17. Convert once to gates, treating the input as a separate coordinate, and impose its value by a separate linear equation. Put that equation into a private two-arrow path with one factor constant and the other of the form $x_0-n$. The affine slices therefore have a common direction with the external input changing only the translation. The split-orthogonal orbit and dimension are fixed. Every slice meets the orbit over every completion; meeting the rational orbit is halting-complete. Rational hits are enumerable by testing rational parameters in the braid equation. This is a constrained intersection problem. It leaves equivalence testing of two supplied rational operators untouched.
