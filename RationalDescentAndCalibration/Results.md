# Four rational descent and exact calibration results

**Premise and attribution.** Assume that a fixed integer polynomial defines the integers existentially over the rationals, as proposed in [Elliot Glazer's manuscript and review archive](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q). The archive describes partial Lean work and an incomplete main formalization. These notes do not verify the number-theoretic premise. They are informal reductions; priority for their combined promises is unverified.

By the Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich theorem, a particular computably enumerable complete subset of the natural numbers has a fixed Diophantine representation over the integers. Nonnegative witnesses can be expressed by four squares. Under the premise, attach the fixed rational integrality predicate separately to each integer witness. Encode evaluation with new gate coordinates and combine equations by a sum of squares over the rationals. This produces a fixed finite system, with one integer input, whose rational feasibility is equivalent to halting. Conversely rational witnesses to finite polynomial equations can be enumerated and checked. Throughout, “halting-complete” refers to computable many-one reductions. The sizes of the fixed constructions may be enormous. [Universal Diophantine equations](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-symbolic-logic/article/abs/universal-diophantine-equation/113EBF3D928DFC6011C52BECAE81A087)

* [One use versus two uses of a shared probability](Calibration.md). A sharp rational-attainment boundary in acyclic stochastic experiments and stationary randomized control.
* [Rational descent of convex 4-polytopes](Polytopes.md). Contractibility and supplied realizations over fixed fields of coprime degrees coexist with undecidable rational realizability.
* [Affine sections of the braid equation](Braids.md). A scheme-level encoding into split-orthogonal involutions gives a fixed orbit whose rational slice-intersection problem is halting-complete under an everywhere-local promise.
* [Invariant subspaces of two rational matrices](InvariantPlanes.md). For a fixed diagonal matrix and a rank-one family of finite-order matrices in a single conjugacy class, a fixed common rational plane can be unique or have infinitely many companions, with a halting-complete distinction.
