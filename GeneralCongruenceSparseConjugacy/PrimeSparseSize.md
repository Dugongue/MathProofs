# A common prime preserving optimal sparse proofs

Fix the displayed rational input coordinates and use ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC), with a binary scalar-linear-combination rule and single-variable multiplication. Count every monomial occurrence in every nonzero line actually used, including axioms and the final one. For a finite list of rational matrix conjugacy and congruence instances of total dense bit length $L$ there is a computable positive integer $D$ with
$$
 \log(2D)\le 2^{L^{O(1)}}
$$
such that at every prime away from $D$ the **exact minimum sparse size** and the **exact minimum degree** of each instance over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ agree with their values over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$. Both are infinite for a geometrically satisfiable instance. All input entries reduce. For any prescribed coset of a subgroup of index $h$ in $(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times$, Dirichlet GRH gives such a common prime
$$
 p\ll h^2\left(\log(2m)+2^{L^{O(1)}}\right)^2 .
$$
In particular its bit length is polynomial in $L+\log(2m)$. The proof gives an existence bound on the prime and a finite algorithm for $D$. Finding an optimal sparse proof efficiently would be a different conclusion. These statements compare algebraically closed coefficient fields; over a particular prime field a minimum sparse proof might require additional lines.

**A bounded sparse proof as a system of quadratics.** First clear denominators of the coefficients in the equations (or in the coefficient comparisons below), excluding primes in the denominators. For charging the exact sparse optimum, keep each original rational axiom unchanged as a named input during these comparisons. Suppose the axiom degrees are at most two, there are $v$ variables, and we seek size at most $s\ge1$. Delete zero lines: a linear combination using a zero parent can be implemented by scalar multiplication of its other parent, using that same parent twice, and a multiplication of zero produces zero. A nonzero proof now has at most $s$ lines and every one of its monomials has degree at most $s+2$ by induction on the lines.

Enumerate the following finite descriptions. Choose at most $s$ nonempty support lists, with at most $s$ monomials total and exponents of the bounded degrees above. For each line choose a named axiom, two earlier lines for a scalar combination, or an earlier line and a variable for multiplication. The last line must be one. A coefficient variable is assigned to each monomial on a list and two scalars to each combination. Impose the selected equations by comparing polynomial coefficients, including all monomials occurring on either side. These are equations of degree at most two in at most $3s$ scalar unknowns. Coefficients assigned to listed monomials may be zero. A solution gives an actual proof of size at most the declared cost after deleting zero lines. Conversely any proof within budget fits such lists. A degree cutoff $d$ can be imposed by limiting the allowed line supports to degree $d$ while still testing any larger monomials on the right of a rule. The descriptions include parent reuse and all intermediate cancellations.

For $v$, input equation count, coefficient bit length and $s$ bounded by a polynomial in $L$, the logarithm of the number of descriptions is polynomial in $L$. Indeed the logarithm of the number of possible monomials per slot is at most $(s+2)\log(v+1)+O(s)$ using sequences of at most $s+2$ variable names. The coefficient equations per description have polynomial cardinality, polynomially many integer bits per coefficient, and degree at most two. The same assertions hold if we include several budgets or degree cutoffs up to polynomial size.

**Preserving feasibility of each description.** Over any algebraically closed field the effective weak Nullstellensatz for bounded-degree generators in $b$ variables gives an identity for one in the inconsistent case, with multiplier degrees at most $T=2^{O(b+1)}$ for quadratics. One can use the uniform estimate with degrees padded in the bound to at least three. Assemble an integer matrix for the linear map that sends multipliers through degree $T$ to the sum of the generator products, using all output monomials through degree $T+2$. Adjoining the column representing one does not increase its rank precisely in the inconsistent case. The scalar-extension invariance of this linear test lets us take ranks over the prime subfield. The two matrix dimensions are at most $2^{L^{O(1)}}$ when $b$ and the input bounds are polynomial in $L$. For each matrix retain one nonzero maximal minor over the rationals, taking value one in rank zero. At every prime not dividing their product, the two ranks remain unchanged, since larger integer minors already vanish. Hadamard's inequality bounds the logarithm of the product by $2^{L^{O(1)}}$. The number of descriptions has that same singly exponential form, so multiplying all these nonzero integers gives the claimed bound on $\log D$. [Kollár, effective Nullstellensatz](https://doi.org/10.2307/1990996) · [Sombra, bounds covering quadratics in arbitrary characteristic](https://www.maia.ub.es/~sombra/publications/sparse-nss/sparse4.pdf)

**From finite budgets to the exact optima.** In characteristic zero every geometrically negative matrix conjugacy or congruence instance has a refutation of degree at most three by the respective formulas. In $v$ variables the degree-three polynomial space has dimension $B=\binom{v+3}{3}$. Closure at that degree yields an actual proof with polynomially many monomial occurrences: maintain a basis of the reachable space, enlarge by variable multiples of its intersection with degree at most two, and keep just independent new vectors. There are at most $B$ enlargements. Each needed vector in the intersection can be generated by at most $B$ binary combinations of already recorded lines, each of sparsity at most $B$. This argument at any of the cutoffs zero through three gives a common polynomial size budget $S$ for deriving one whenever it is derivable at that cutoff. Preserve all size budgets up to $S$, and the tests of size at most $S$ at each of the four degree cutoffs. Also preserve the geometric consistency of the original system itself by the same maximal-minor method on its original quadratics. Finally, if a modular system acquired a refutation of degree at most three, the identical dimension argument over its own field bounds the required size by $S$. Thus a smaller modular degree cannot evade the tests via a longer proof. This proves both exact comparisons, including the infinite case. Lifting the prescribed coset by inverse image from modulus $m$ to modulus $mD$ preserves its index. A prime in the lifted coset avoids every excluded prime. Apply the least-prime-in-a-coset estimate from Dirichlet GRH; for index one the ordinary primorial estimate already supplies a prime avoiding $mD$. This last step is the only analytic input.
