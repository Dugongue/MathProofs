# Sharp conjugacy exponents beyond diagonal coordinates

Let a split diagonal family have a fixed nonempty list of mismatched multiplicity gaps and write $q=1$ in characteristic zero and $q=p^{v_p(G)}$ in characteristic $p$, where $G$ is the gcd of those gaps. Suppose every eigenvalue occurring in either matrix has multiplicity at least $cn$ in **both** matrices, for a constant $c>0$. This permits additional matched blocks. Apply arbitrary independent similarities to the source and target, requiring that every row and column of each change of basis and its inverse have at most $w$ nonzero entries for a fixed $w$. The resulting original-coordinate systems still have minimum sparse size between $\Omega_{c,w,g}(n^{2q})$ and $O_{c,w,g}(n^{2q}\log(2n))$. This includes unbounded families of nondiagonal presentations. The lower bound covers refutations of unrestricted degree. [Diagonal theorem and matching designs](SparseConjugacy.md)

**Lower bound.** Map unknown forward and reverse matrices to the diagonal coordinates by the two linear basis changes. Within a mismatched eigenvalue block use the matching matrix and its transpose, leave a random constant-size rectangle live, match the complements uniformly, and put all other positions equal to zero as in the diagonal theorem. On a matched eigenvalue choose a uniformly random full permutation, independently, with no live entries. Before these random choices each original variable is a linear form in at most $W=w^2$ candidate matching edges. There is no constant term. Each edge occurs in at most $2W$ candidate forms, counting both directions: for the forward change this follows from the column bound on the left matrix and the row bound on the right, and similarly for the reverse. We may combine repeated terms and discard zero coefficients.

If a proof line is mapped to the Boolean collision quotient, its normal form is a linear combination of products of partial matchings on the remaining boards. A linear rule preserves this representation. For multiplication by a literal variable, the multiplier is now a linear combination of edges and a possible pinned constant. Here an additional argument is essential because mixed-edge terms can cancel at the top degree. Instead apply the change of coordinates *termwise to the whole PC derivation*, simulating multiplication by each summand and summing, before the final normalization. If a product creates forbidden live degree at an intermediate step, its monomials still come from the expansion of a monomial occurrence in the multiplied target line: multiplication in the original polynomial ring by one literal variable is injective on monomials, so each parent monomial has exactly one child in that line. For a bound on the probability, count as a witness any occurrence that admits a choice of its candidate edges, one per factor with multiplicity, whose product survives and uses at least $b_i=p^{v_p(|g_i|)}$ live edges on one board (take $b_i=1$ in characteristic zero). We count choices even when their coefficients eventually cancel.

If there is no such occurrence, every intermediate normalized line in this simulation lies below every board cutoff $b_i$. This includes a single-edge product and partial sums at each simulated multiplication, since every potentially offending term would give a witness at its child occurrence. Images of conjugacy linear axioms vanish. An inverse axiom maps to a scalar combination of diagonal-coordinate inverse axioms; after normalization these are zero or single row or column totalities. When a mismatched board has $b_i=1$ there is a small point concerning inputs: a nonzero coefficient on a live edge in this normalized axiom would already constitute a witness. Equivalently for this board, an inverse input is a sum of row totalities only, or of column totalities only, because the change of basis conjugates $XY-I$ and $YX-I$ separately. In a fixed eigenvalue block distinct rows (respectively columns) have disjoint edge supports, and the live board has both sides positive. If a coefficient on such a totality is nonzero, some live edge occurs. Thus all contributions from cutoffs zero vanish in the absence of a witness. On boards with $b_i>1$ the designs annihilate all these inputs. Pinned components have zero contribution. The matching-design and forced-edge extension argument of the diagonal theorem therefore excludes the constant one. Every random restriction must give a witness.

For a fixed original monomial let $m$ be its number of distinct variable names. If any variable has empty candidate set the monomial cannot witness. A candidate set intersects those of at most $2W^2$ other variables. When $m\ge 2q(2W^2+1)$, greedily choose $2q$ different variables with pairwise disjoint candidate sets. Survival requires some choice of one edge from each that is a matching on every board, since all $2q$ chosen edges are distinct. There are at most $W^{2q}$ such choices. For any prescribed matching of $t\le2q$ edges in a board, the probability that they all survive is $O(n^{-t})$: specify which rows are live, an event costing $O(n^{-1})$ per required live row, and require prescribed partners in the random bijection on the complement for each other row. All live sides have bounded size, and all complement sides are at least $cn-O(1)$. In a matched board the full random permutation has the same estimate. Independence across boards proves $O(n^{-2q})$ overall.

For smaller $m$ the union of candidate sets has bounded size, regardless of powers of the variables. A witness needs at least $q$ distinct live edges on some board. There are only boundedly many choices. Both endpoints of every chosen edge must be live, which for a matching has probability $O(n^{-2q})$ by independent choices of live rows and columns. The union bound over monomial occurrences now proves the lower estimate.

**Upper bound.** The displayed diagonal proof multiplies only up to degree $2q$. Substitute the corresponding linear changes back. Every degree-$d$ monomial expands to at most $W^d$ terms, and multiplication by a replaced variable has a simulation with at most $W$ summands. For fixed $w,q$ this increases the replay size by only a constant factor. Each diagonal-coordinate intertwiner axiom used is a linear combination of at most $W$ actual intertwiner entries of bounded sparsity, since the two transformed input matrices have bounded row and column sparsity. There are at most $O(n^2)$ such inputs. Each used diagonal-coordinate inverse entry needs at most $W$ actual inverse entries, each of size $O(n)$. For $q=1$ the diagonal proof needs only $O(n)$ diagonal inverse entries. For $q>1$ even $O(n^2)$ inverse entries cost just $O(n^3)$ additional monomials. This proves the asserted upper bound. The argument uses bounded support of both the transformations and their inverses in the input coordinates.
