# General congruence degree and sparse conjugacy proofs

**The written degree formula now covers every matrix in Question 7.3.** For the congruence equations $XAX^T=B$, $XY=YX=I$, it gives the exact minimum ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) degree over every field, for singular and nonsymmetric inputs as well as symmetric ones. In combination with the earlier conjugacy formula, this answers the degree part for both systems in the published question. Sparse size is more sensitive to the coordinates. The results below give sharp exponents in several regimes; the unrestricted sparse-size question stays open. [Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She, Open Question 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3) · [Conjugacy degree](ConjugacyDegree.md) · [General congruence](GeneralCongruenceDegree.md) · [Characteristic two](BinaryCongruence.md)

### Reading the degree formula

First check for a displayed nonzero constant axiom, which gives degree zero. Apart from that case, degree two occurs exactly when $B$ is outside the linear span of the matrices $XAX^T$ over the algebraic closure. A geometrically congruent pair admits no raw refutation. For the remaining cases decompose the two forms into normalized canonical blocks over the closure. If a type is missing, or if the two positive multiplicity spaces at a characteristic-two type have opposite alternating flags, the degree is three. Otherwise for each mismatched type let $r$ be its smaller count and let $g$ be the absolute difference of its counts. A doubled alternating block counts as one block. In characteristic $p>0$ assign the type the number $\min(r+2,2p^{v_p(g)})$. Take the minimum over the mismatches, then the maximum with three. In characteristic zero every remaining refutation has degree three.

The upper bound extracts inverse equations on multiplicity spaces in degree three. For alternating spaces the proof uses all divided symplectic contractions. An integral inclusion-exclusion identity isolates the required minor even when a factorial vanishes in the field. The lower bound specializes the original variables to signed partial matchings. For a concrete example, a nonsymmetric invertible pair of side 22 has minimum degree six in characteristic three and three in every other odd characteristic. A separate construction gives the exact least side at which any finite list of prescribed degrees can occur under reduction at specified primes, including two. [Exact example](GeneralCongruenceDegree.md) · [Sharp simultaneous thresholds](SimultaneousThresholds.md) · [Canonical forms in characteristic two](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.0565)

### Sharp sparse-size exponents for conjugacy

* **Displayed diagonal families.** Assume a fixed list of nonzero eigenvalue multiplicity gaps, each with smaller multiplicity at least $cn$. Let $G$ be the gcd of the gaps, and take $q=p^{v_p(G)}$ in characteristic $p$, or $q=1$ in characteristic zero. Minimum sparse size is between $\Omega(n^{2q})$ and $O(n^{2q}\log(2n))$, even allowing proofs of unrestricted degree in the lower bound. For rank gap four, complementary diagonal projections have exponent **eight in characteristic two and two in all other characteristics**. The result extends to nondiagonal inputs under bounded row and column supports for the independent basis changes and their inverses, when all eigenvalue multiplicities on each side are at least $cn$. [Diagonal proof and growing-gap bounds](SparseConjugacy.md) · [Nondiagonal extension](SparseBasis.md)
* **Arbitrary input coordinates with unequal traces.** The worst-case sparse size has exponent **three over every field**. The bounds are $\Omega(n^3)$ and $O(n^3\log(2n))$. A dense integral presentation of rank-gap-one idempotents attains the lower bound, while diagonal presentations of the same similarity types have exponent two. The trace-identity upper bound applies to every pair with unequal traces, in its given coordinates. [Coordinate dependence and proof](DenseConjugacy.md)
* **Transfer to nonsymmetric congruence.** If the two spectra have no reciprocal pair across the inputs, passing from $M$ to the form with off-diagonal blocks $I,M$ changes optimal sparse size by at most an additive polynomial in the side. A common scalar shift enforces the hypothesis over large fields. A fixed companion-matrix construction transfers superpolynomial rank-gap conjugacy lower bounds to nonsingular, nonsymmetric congruence over every field. [Explicit substitutions](SparseTransfer.md)

* **An exact count in every semisimple family.** If the matrices are diagonalizable over the algebraic closure and $m$ is the smallest multiplicity on either side of a discrepant eigenvalue, every proof in any basis must use at least $(m+1)(n-m)$ forward equations. A proof using exactly that many exists in displayed diagonal coordinates. With $m$ proportional to $n$ this also gives the sharp exponent two in diagonal coordinates whenever a multiplicity gap is nonzero in the field, including growing gaps. [Input-count theorem](SemisimpleInputs.md) · [Exhaustive Hall-rectangle check](check_semisimple_inputs.py)

For characteristic $p$ and complementary idempotents with gap $q=p^a\le r^{1-\varepsilon}$ and smaller rank $r$, the bounds already give $r^{\Theta_\varepsilon(q)}$. When the prime-power gap and the two multiplicities grow proportionally, the current general estimates leave an exponential-to-$n^{O(n)}$ gap.

### What Dirichlet GRH contributes

For any finite list of rational conjugacy and congruence inputs there is one prime in any prescribed residue coset at which **the exact minimum sparse size and degree of every instance are preserved**, comparing proofs over the two algebraic closures. The prime has polynomial bit length in the dense input length and the bit length of the modulus. The proof encodes bounded sparse derivations and their cancellations as quadratic systems; Dirichlet GRH bounds the prime needed to avoid the exceptional characteristics. The theorem is existential and permits the optimal coefficients to lie in an extension of the prime field.
