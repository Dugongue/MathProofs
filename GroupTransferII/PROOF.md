# Exact counting through abelian kernels with ordered simple heads

Given Cayley-table groups \(G,H\), a specified abelian normal subgroup \(A\) of \(H\), and an onto quotient map \(\psi:G\to P=H/A\), consider epimorphic lifts with arbitrary prescribed point values. Suppose each primary \(P\)-module \(A_p\) has a direct-sum decomposition into modules \(A_i\) of simple head \(T_i\). Assume that individual scalar endomorphisms of \(T_i\) lift to \(A_i\). Assume further that for any two summands of the same head type at least one direction \(A_i\to A_j\) has a map nonzero on heads. Then exact epimorphic lift counting and witness construction take polynomial time in the table lengths. The condition is recognizable within the same bound. The proof combines the earlier semisimple-head algorithm with nested row counting, removing the requirement that all head endomorphisms lift.

One consistent lift and its affine variations are found by the section-based additive equations for lifting through \(A\), including the prescribed values. Let \(W\) be the group of homogeneous variations, and \(L=\ker\psi\). Record their restrictions along the projections \(L_{\rm ab}\to A_p\to\bigoplus_i T_i\), producing a homomorphism \(\rho\) on \(W\). Each head restriction is \(P\)-linear on \(L_{\rm ab}/pL_{\rm ab}\), since conjugation on the abelianization is unchanged by the choice of a preimage of a quotient element. The lift is onto precisely when its maps to these heads are onto. This follows because the lift already covers \(P\), its kernel restriction supplies its intersection with \(A\), and Nakayama tests surjectivity onto \(A_p\) on its head.

The individual projections onto \(A_i\) make \(\rho(W)\) the product of its individual row variations. For rows of common type \(T\), write \(D=\operatorname{End}_P(T)\), a finite field, and identify their variation spaces as \(V_i\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_P(L_{\rm ab}/pL_{\rm ab},T)\). The scalar-lifting hypothesis makes each \(V_i\) a \(D\)-space. A map \(A_i\to A_j\) nonzero on heads gives \(V_i\subseteq V_j\): postcompose the variation with that map, then divide by its nonzero head scalar. The relation that such a map exists is reflexive and transitive, the latter because nonzero maps of the simple heads compose to nonzero maps. Thus the comparability hypothesis gives an order with \(V_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq V_b\) within each type. Independence of the rows over \(D\) is equivalent to surjectivity onto \(T^b\), since every proper submodule of this semisimple target is annihilated by a nonzero map to \(T\).

At row \(i\), suppose the earlier rows are independent and span \(S\). Let \(a_i\) be the offset of this row. The projection of \(S\) modulo \(V_i\) is the fixed span of the earlier offsets modulo \(V_i\), because their variations are contained in \(V_i\). Write \(s_i\) for the dimension of that projected span. If \(a_i+V_i\) misses the span of the projected offsets, there are \(|V_i|\) choices. Otherwise the forbidden choices for row \(i\) form a fiber inside \(S\) of size \(|D|^{i-1-s_i}\), so subtract that number. These counts depend only on the offsets and subspaces, and may thus be multiplied across the rows. Multiply across the types and primes and also by \(|\ker\rho|\) for the full affine lifts. A witness can be chosen greedily: take \(a_i\) if possible, otherwise add a basis vector of \(V_i\) outside \(S\), then solve the lift equations with the chosen rows.

To recognize the decomposition, enumerate cyclic submodules of each primary kernel by scanning its elements. Any module with simple head is cyclic by Nakayama and indecomposable since any nonzero direct summand has nonzero head. Test whether a cyclic submodule has simple head and splits by solving for an equivariant retraction, and recur on its complement. Under the promise any simple-head direct summand removed this way leaves a decomposition of the same allowed types by Krull–Schmidt. To obtain the head, compute simple composition factors by least nonzero cyclic submodules, then intersect kernels of a basis of the maps to each simple type. All endomorphisms, induced scalar spaces, and comparisons of maps on heads are found by equivariant linear congruences and Smith form. The arithmetic in \(D\) can be performed through its action on \(T\). This gives polynomial total cost in the full table model.

For a strict extension of the earlier head-endomorphism condition, take \(P=C_p\) and a kernel that is a sum of modules \(\mathbb F_p[t]/(t^j)\), \(1\le j\le p\), with the generator acting by \(1+t\). Each head is trivial, scalars lift, and longer modules map onto their shorter quotients. A map from a shorter summand to a longer one lands in the latter's \(t\)-multiple submodule, since its image is annihilated by the shorter power of \(t\). It therefore acts by zero on the head. Two distinct lengths prevent surjectivity on the full head endomorphism algebra, while the new ordered row calculation still applies. This is an informal proof.

## Coprime actions and a modular cyclic corollary

If \(p\nmid |P|\), the ordered hypothesis holds for every finite \(A_p\). For a simple \(T\) over \(\mathbb F_p[P]\), choose a primitive idempotent representing it as a projective left ideal by Maschke's theorem. Lift that idempotent modulo the nilpotent ideal \(pR_e\), for \(R_e=(\mathbb Z/p^e)[P]\), to obtain a projective \(R_e\)-module \(L_e(T)\) with head \(T\). It is free as a module over \(\mathbb Z/p^e\), since it is a projective summand over this local ring. If \(p^e\) is the largest exponent in \(A_p\), choose a simple in \(p^{e-1}A_p\). The map \(L_e(T)\to p^{e-1}A_p\) given by this embedding lifts through multiplication by \(p^{e-1}\). The lift is injective on \(L_e(T)[p]=p^{e-1}L_e(T)\), hence injective. Its image splits as an additive module since free modules over \(\mathbb Z/p^e\) are injective in this finite exponent category, by extension of additive characters coordinate by coordinate. Average the retraction over \(P\), dividing by \(|P|\), to make it equivariant. Repetition produces a sum of the indicated \(L_j(T)\). Projectivity lifts every head scalar, and for the same \(T\) gives a map nonzero on heads from any longer lift to any shorter one. This proves the claim.

If \(P=C_m\) and \(A_p\) is elementary abelian for each \(p\mid m\), those modular primary summands have the required form as well. The primary cyclic decomposition over \(\mathbb F_p[x]\), with \(x\) acting by a generator of \(P\), gives blocks \(\mathbb F_p[x]/(g^j)\); scalar head maps lift by multiplication and longer blocks for a given \(g\) quotient onto shorter ones. The other primes use the coprime argument. This gives exact polynomial-time epimorphism counting for any raw target \(H\) admitting such an abelian normal kernel and cyclic quotient. To recover one such kernel, for each divisor \(m\) of \(|H|\) enumerate the maps \(H_{\rm ab}\to C_m\). There are at most \(|H_{\rm ab}|\) maps per \(m\) by the cyclic-factor gcd count. Require the map to be onto and check the kernel condition. Choose any one qualifying kernel and enumerate the at most \(|G|\) quotient epimorphisms from \(G\) in the same way, then sum the disjoint exact lift counts. This includes nonsplit modular extensions with unbounded generator numbers.
