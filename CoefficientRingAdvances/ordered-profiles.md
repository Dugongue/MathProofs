# Exact joint determinant profiles for ordered matrix systems

**Theorem.** Fix a residue characteristic \(p\). Let \(R\) be any finite commutative local ring with residue field \(k=\mathbb F_q\) of characteristic \(p\) and arbitrary residue degree, presented by additive invariant factors and a multiplication table on the additive generators. Given two representations of the same labelled quiver on explicitly based finite free \(R\)-modules, compute the joint determinant coset of all transporters in deterministic polynomial bit time. The number of vertices and arrows may vary, and arrows may repeat, form cycles, or be singular. For any prescribed determinant tuple, compute the exact number of transporters with those values. The residue degree, radical breadth, radical length, and characteristic exponent may all vary at fixed residue characteristic. If the prime also varies, the finite-algebra step gives an exact Las Vegas polynomial-time algorithm.

A determinant coset here is represented by a basepoint, an integer exponent matrix for the residue subgroup, and concrete generators for the principal subgroup. The two-vertex case includes all ordered rectangular matrix slice systems. The proof extends the existing prime-power-ring slice result using the full nilpotent unit filtration. [Earlier ordered-slice result](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowLiteratureTransfers/PrescribedDeterminants.md).

**Additive computations and ordinary comparison.** The additive group of \(R\) is a \(p\)-group for \(p=\operatorname{char}k\). The quotient \(R/pR\) is a finite \(\mathbb F_p\)-algebra. Its nilradical is the kernel of a sufficiently high Frobenius iterate, which is computed by linear algebra over \(\mathbb F_p\). The preimage is the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) of \(R\), since \(pR\) is nilpotent. Its powers and all required additive subquotients are computable by integer linear algebra on additive presentations. For example, products of additive generators suffice to generate any displayed ideal product. Each nontrivial element of the decreasing ideal filtration shortens the logarithm of the order by at least one.

Find one ordinary transporter or certify absence by the deterministic finite-ring module-isomorphism algorithm. One explicit finite ring for applying it is the subring of endomorphisms of the direct sum of the two displayed representations generated by the vertex projections, the arrow operators, and the scalar generators of \(R\). Regard the two representations as modules by the two block restrictions. Compute the additive generators of the acting ring by multiplication closure inside its displayed finite additive ambient group. Each strict addition of a generated subgroup increases the logarithm of its order, so this is polynomial time. An isomorphism of these modules is exactly an \(R\)-linear list of vertex maps intertwining all arrows. [Ciocănea-Teodorescu, Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08365).

**Work in the endomorphism ring of the actual input.** For the first representation let \(C\) be its endomorphism ring, defined by the linear arrow intertwining equations, and let \(\overline C\) be the image under reduction of the vertex matrices modulo \(\mathfrak m\). Compute \(C\) by solving the linear equations on the additive presentation, including its ring multiplication, and compute \(\overline C\) from its generators. One must take the actual reduction image, since solving only the reduced equations may give more endomorphisms.

Put \(L\) equal to the preimage in \(C\) of \(\operatorname{rad}(\overline C)\). The kernel of reduction consists of matrices with entries in \(\mathfrak m\), hence is nilpotent. This shows \(L\) is nilpotent and
\[
C/L\simeq\prod_j M_{d_j}(k_j)
\]
is semisimple, where the \(k_j\) are finite extensions of \(k\). The radical and an explicit semisimple decomposition are computable deterministically in polynomial time at fixed \(p\) by the finite-algebra structure algorithms. They reduce to polynomial factoring over the prime field, which also gives the Las Vegas bound for a varying prime. Units of the quotient lift to units of \(C\) by nilpotence, and the kernel is \(1+L\). [Rónyai, *Computing the structure of finite algebras*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(88)80017-X), [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, Section 4](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf).

**Prime-to-\(p\) determinants.** At vertex \(v\), use the filtration of its residue space by the powers of \(\operatorname{rad}(\overline C)\). Let \(m_{vj}\) be the total multiplicity of the simple column module of the \(j\)-th factor on the associated quotients. Compute it from the dimensions of the corresponding idempotent images. The determinant of each residue action is computed by multiplying determinants on the filtration, using the field norm on each \(k_j\) column block. The joint residue subgroup is the product of the images of the tuples
\[
\bigl(z^{m_{vj}}\bigr)_v,\qquad z\in k^\times,
\]
for each \(j\), by surjectivity of the determinant and of the finite-field norm.

A generator of \(k^\times\) is unnecessary even when its order is large. Write \(M=(m_{vj})\) for the integer multiplicity matrix and compute its integer Smith form \(UMV=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_\ell,0,\ldots)\) with unimodular transforms. An integer matrix acts on tuples of units by taking the corresponding products of powers. In particular the unimodular transforms act invertibly on tuples, and the image of the residue map is, after the transform \(U\), a product of \(a_i\)-power subgroups in the first \(\ell\) coordinates and identity coordinates thereafter. With \(Q=q-1\), the \(i\)-th subgroup has order \(Q/\gcd(Q,a_i)\). Membership is the test \(z_i^{Q/\gcd(Q,a_i)}=1\) in that coordinate. This computes exact membership and the image order without discrete logarithms, finding a primitive unit, or factoring \(Q\). The matrix \(M\), with a canonical lift applied to the tuples it specifies, represents the whole prime-to-\(p\) subgroup.

The unique prime-to-\(p\) lift of any residue unit is obtained by raising an arbitrary unit lift to an exponent divisible by \(p^{\log_p|R|}\) and congruent to \(1\bmod(q-1)\). The extended Euclidean algorithm constructs the exponent. For a prescribed unit tuple, its residue part is tested as above and its principal part is found by dividing by those lifts.

**Principal determinants.** Take the inverse image in \(C^\times\) of the product of upper unitriangular subgroups of the factors \(\mathrm{GL}_{d_j}(k_j)\). This is a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup, because \(1+L\) is a \(p\)-group. A generating list consists of unit lifts of transvections with entries in a prime-field basis of each \(k_j\), and \(1+x\) for lifted prime-field bases of the layers \(L^i/L^{i+1}\). The latter generate \(1+L\) by induction along its filtration. Their quotients are elementary abelian since \(p1\in L\). Lifts of the transvections are obtained by linear preimage computation and are units. These groups map onto the \(p\)-part of the joint determinant image, by the Sylow theorem for the image of a homomorphism. Compute the determinant tuple of every generator by local pivot elimination. Each of these tuples consists of principal units.

The required abelian calculations do not enumerate \(1+\mathfrak m\). Choose units \(u_j=1+x_j\) for an ordered list of lifted \(\mathbb F_p\)-bases of \(\mathfrak m^i/\mathfrak m^{i+1}\), starting at the outermost nonzero layer. For any principal unit, use successive quotient coordinates and multiply by inverse powers of the \(u_j\) to obtain its unique coordinates with exponents \(0,\ldots,p-1\). Each \(u_j^p\) lies in a later layer. Its coordinates give an integer relation \(p e_j-\sum_{\ell>j}c_{j\ell}e_\ell\) for the ambient unit group. These relations are complete: their square triangular matrix has determinant \(p^{\#\{j\}}\), which is the exact order of the principal-unit group from the filtration. Use a direct sum of the relations across vertices. Integer Smith form computes membership and order for the principal subgroup, which together with the residue calculation gives the same operations on the full subgroup \(D\) and on any given translate for membership.

**Counts.** If \(t\) is the ordinary transporter, the full set of transporters is \(tC^\times\). Hence its determinant coset is \(\det(t)D\). Every tuple in that coset has the same fiber size
\[
\frac{|L|\prod_j|\mathrm{GL}_{d_j}(k_j)|}{|D|}.
\]
A tuple outside the coset has no transporters. The formula uses the standard general linear group cardinalities and the order of the additive group of \(L\), with polynomial-size integers. All auxiliary spaces, additive linear systems, and subgroup presentations have polynomial size in the explicitly presented coefficients and based modules. This also gives the joint profile and counting result for finite products of such local rings when their product presentations are supplied, by taking componentwise transporters.
