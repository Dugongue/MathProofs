# Polynomial exact three-mode lifting for residue \(p\)-groups

**Theorem.** Let \(R\) be a finite commutative local ring with residue field \(k\) of characteristic \(p\), presented by additive invariant factors and a multiplication table. Given dense free three-tensors \(T,T'\), three unit determinant targets, and a coset \(\bar g\,P_0\) in \(\prod_i\mathrm{GL}_{n_i}(k)\) with \(P_0\) a supplied, generator-defined \(p\)-group, one can in deterministic polynomial bit time find and exactly count all transporters over \(R\) with those determinants and with residue in the coset. The time is uniform in \(p\), the residue field degree, the radical depth and all three mode ranks. This includes a single supplied residue transporter, by taking \(P_0=1\), and allows all three mode maps to vary jointly inside the specified congruence restriction.

The proof keeps the tensor displacement and all determinant conditions in the same orbit problem, as in the local lifting equation. It uses only \(p\)-group operations on explicit filtrations, with no search over residue flags or choices of lifts. [Earlier joint tangent quotient](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/LocalDeterminantTransfers/DetLift.md).

**Normalize the coset and obtain flags.** Reduce the ring and compute its maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) via the nilradical of \(R/pR\) using the kernel of a high Frobenius iterate. Lift \(\bar g\) to any invertible tuple \(g\), which exists by local elimination or nilpotent inversion. Apply \(g^{-1}\) to the target and divide the determinant targets by those of \(g\). We now need residues in \(P_0\). Each of its three linear representations on \(k^{n_i}\) has a full invariant flag with trivial action on its factors. A finite \(p\)-group acting on a nonzero vector space in characteristic \(p\) fixes a nonzero vector: count orbit sizes modulo \(p\), noting that zero is fixed. The fixed space of a generating set is computed by linear equations. Repeat on the quotient by a selected fixed line. Lift the resulting residue bases to bases over \(R\).

In these bases every allowed residue matrix is upper unitriangular. The full preimage of the product of the upper unitriangular groups is \(Q=\prod_i(1+N_i)\), where \(N_i\) is the additive algebra of matrices that are strictly upper triangular modulo \(\mathfrak m\). It is nilpotent, because \(N_i^{n_i}\subseteq \operatorname{Mat}_{n_i}(\mathfrak m)\). The quotients \(N_i^a/N_i^{a+1}\) are elementary abelian since \(p1_{n_i}\in N_i\). Also \([1+N_i^a,1+N_i^b]\subseteq1+N_i^{a+b}\). Thus the filtration by these powers, refined by prime-field basis flags in each layer, is an explicit central series of \(Q\) with order-\(p\) factors. Its arithmetic, bases and quotient coordinates come from additive linear algebra. All lengths and dimensions are bounded by \(\log_p|Q|\).

Let \(P\) be the preimage of \(P_0\) in \(Q\). Generate it by arbitrary invertible lifts of the supplied generators and generators of the congruence kernel, found from the layers of \(1+\operatorname{Mat}_{n_i}(\mathfrak m)\). For each layer, \(1+x\) for an additive basis of its quotient generates that layer modulo the next. Induction gives the full kernel. This proves that the constructed \(P\) gives precisely the allowed residue tuples.

**Polynomial kernels inside \(Q\).** Here are explicit group operations sufficient for the lifting. Order the consecutive cyclic factors of the central series from outermost to innermost. An element is sifted using a sequence with at most one normalized pivot at each factor, by clearing the successive quotient coordinates with powers of those pivots. A missing pivot and a nonzero coordinate supply a new pivot by raising the residual element to the inverse of that coordinate in \(\mathbb F_p\). Given subgroup generators, sift them and the \(p\)-th powers and pairwise commutators of all current pivots. Recheck after any pivot insertion. There are at most \(\log_p|Q|\) insertions. When all the tests sift to identity, the ordered pivot products with exponents \(0,\ldots,p-1\) form the subgroup. Indeed the \(p\)-th power of each pivot and its commutators with later pivots lie deeper in the central series and have expressions in the deeper pivots. Induction from the deepest factor shows that adjoining one pivot at a time gives its \(p\) cosets of the deeper subgroup, which it normalizes. Distinct exponent vectors give distinct products by their first differing quotient coordinate. This gives exact membership and order.

To compute the kernel of a homomorphism \(\phi:H\to(\mathbb F_p,+)\) on a subgroup \(H\), return \(H\) if all generators have zero image. Otherwise choose a normalized \(x\in H\) with \(\phi(x)=1\). The kernel is the normal closure in \(H\) of \(x^p\) and \(y x^{-\phi(y)}\) for the subgroup generators \(y\). These elements lie in the kernel, and killing their normal closure makes the quotient generated by \(x\) of order at most \(p\). For the closure, sift conjugates of the current pivot generators by the generators of \(H\), inserting any new elements and repeating. A strict subgroup increase can occur at most \(\log_p|Q|\) times. On termination every generator normalizes the resulting finite subgroup, so it is the desired normal closure. Powers throughout use repeated squaring with exponent bit length \(O(\log p)\). No list of \(p\) elements is needed.

**The joint module orbit.** Use the additive \(R\)-module
\[
M=\bigl(R^{n_1}\otimes_R R^{n_2}\otimes_R R^{n_3}\bigr)\oplus R^3
\]
with the action of a tuple \(h\) on the first component by tensor multiplication and on the three other coordinates by \(\det_R(h_i)\). We compare \((T,1,1,1)\) with the normalized \((T',d_1,d_2,d_3)\). This is a linear action on \(M\) for each fixed \(h\). On each \(\mathfrak m^a M/\mathfrak m^{a+1}M\) the tensor action is upper unitriangular in the lexicographically ordered tensor basis on each copy of the residue tensor space. The determinant coordinates have trivial action. Refining this filtration by coordinate flags and prime-field bases gives an invariant chain \(M=M_0\supset M_1\supset\cdots\supset M_b=0\) with trivial action and order \(p\) on every successive quotient. This is computable by linear algebra. The ambient tensor action is applied directly to its dense coefficient array.

Starting with all of \(P\), maintain a coset \(g_0H\) taking the source \(v\) to the target modulo \(M_j\). Here \(H\) fixes \(v\) modulo \(M_j\). The displacement \(h\mapsto hv-v\bmod M_{j+1}\), taken in \(M_j/M_{j+1}\), is a homomorphism on \(H\) because all of \(P\) acts trivially on that layer. Since \(g_0\) also acts trivially there, the next comparison requires this homomorphism to take the value of the target minus \(g_0v\) on that same quotient. Use the subgroup operations above to find a representative or detect an empty layer. If a representative exists, multiply \(g_0\) by it and replace \(H\) by the kernel. At the last layer the coset is exactly the set requested. Its size is the group order from the pivot sequence. Multiply the final representative by the initial lift to return to the original residue coset.

All linear operations use the additive coordinates of the ring and field. The numbers of quotient factors, generator insertions and matrix operations are polynomial in the explicitly charged input sizes, and integer orders require only polynomially many bits. A polynomially sized explicitly supplied list of residue \(p\)-group cosets can be tested with the same method one at a time. For disjoint cosets the exact counts add.
