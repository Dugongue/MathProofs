# Even conjugators with large relation spaces over the ternary field

There are deterministic reductions to one ordinary tensor-isomorphism instance for the following families. They allow the output map and a large relation space to vary. The unrestricted even-conjugator lemma remains open. No reduction here assumes access to coTI.

| Structure found on the input | What the reduction does |
| --- | --- |
| A relation space is monomial in a hidden tangent basis | Recover a graph in polynomial time. Its edge permutation and the computed endpoint frame signs give the exact remaining parity problem. Odd correction, fiber parity, or bounded quotient valence as specified in the proof gives one ordinary TI. [Graph recovery and sufficient criteria](GradeGroups.md) |
| Many permuted relation blocks have logarithmic dimension and an intrinsic connected graph of logarithmic maximum degree | An incidence gadget couples local determinant bits and the permutation of odd-dimensional blocks to the very same transporter. It produces a single ordinary TI pair even when there are exponentially many block permutations. [Parity gadget](Configuration.md) |
| Distinct matrix factors are recovered from quadratic derivations | Split the symmetric square into symmetric and alternating tensor factors and solve parity equations on their determinants. A family of spaces of two-by-two minors has full free output dimension in the earlier linear tests and admits odd-only partners. [Hidden tensor factors](HiddenFactors.md) |
| An intrinsic alternating pencil on the relation grade has a nonzero complementary Pfaffian of fixed corank and bounded reduced ternary degree, or satisfies the stated multiplicity criterion | Encode the cofactor function in a polynomial-size space. The marking imposes the determinant on the full grade. Bounded reduced degree is a promise unless independently certified. [Pfaffian sign test](RelationForms.md) |

For the executable raw example, \(X\) has dimension 14 and the quotient \(Z\) has dimension 91. The earlier output-subspace equations allow all \(91^2=8{,}281\) coefficients of an output map. The compiler recovers the two hidden cycles from the input table, determines the sign and emits \(1,1\) or \(1,0\) as two one-entry tensors. It works on disjoint cycles of pairwise distinct lengths congruent to one modulo four, subject to the dimension conditions in the code. The more general incidence construction retains TI as its final decision step. [Raw cycle compiler](CycleCompiler.py) · [Frame-checked minor compiler](segre.py)

## Input and frame conventions

Each bimap table represents a surjection from \(\operatorname{Sym}^2X\) to \(Z\) in the stated tangent and output bases. Write \(R\) for its kernel. For a transporter \(h\beta=\beta'(g,g)\), the determinant identity for a short exact sequence and \(\det(\operatorname{Sym}^2 g)=(\det g)^{\dim X+1}\) transfer \(\det g\det h\) to the determinant on \(R\) when \(\dim X\) is even. Include the determinants of the two endpoint matrices whose columns are the chosen relation basis and lifts of the displayed quotient basis. Ignoring these factors can reverse the answer even on two presentations of the same bimap.

For a raw conjugate of the promised square-zero matrix space, take its common kernel \(W\), its quotient \(U\), and the resulting maps \(M\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(U,W)\). The left idealizer \(A\) of \(M\) is the regular local algebra: in model coordinates \(M\) has rows \([L_a,L_b]\), so applying the idealizer to a coordinate projection identifies it. Its radical \(J\) gives \(X=J/J^2\) and \(Z=J^2\). For each \(a\in A\), the equations \(\ell T_a=a\ell\) for every \(\ell\in M\) recover the module action on \(U\) uniquely. Lifts of bases modulo \(J\) provide the two free \(A\)-generators of \(U\) and one of \(W\). Use these and an adapted \(A\)-basis to recover the model frame in the original space. Multiply the requested sign by the ratio of these frame determinants. Between model frames the diagonal blocks left after removing the triple lift of an algebra transporter are a unit of \(A\) and a matrix in \(\operatorname{GL}_2(A)\). Both ground determinants are residue determinants raised to the even exponent \(\dim A\). The triple lift therefore has the same sign as the bimap transporter and realizes every such sign. [Square-zero normal form](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TISLSquareZeroDirect/earlier/SquareZeroClosure.md)

The incidence theorem alone is Lean-verified. It states that a vertex function on a finite connected graph is the boundary of edge weights exactly when its sum is zero, over any additive commutative group. The TI reductions have written proofs.
