# Pfaffian cofactor functions of low ternary degree

Let \(F=\mathbf F_3\), and let \(T\) be a computable intrinsic relation grade of a based symmetric bimap. Write \(t=\dim T\). On the even-dimensional input space the square-zero conjugator sign transfers to the determinant on its quadratic relations, with a computable endpoint frame correction. The grading and sign assembly use the earlier construction. [Relation transfer and graded assembly](../EvenConjugatorF3/FilteredRelations.md)

**Theorem.** Let \(K\subseteq\Lambda^2 T^*\) be an intrinsically computed space of alternating forms. Fix nonnegative integers \(c,D\), with \(0\le c\le t\) and \(t-c\) even. In a based \(T\), form the vector of signed complementary \((t-c)\)-Pfaffians \(P(\omega)\in\Lambda^c T\) of \(\omega\in K\). Assume this function is nonzero and that its reduced polynomial over \(F\) has total degree at most \(D\). There is a deterministic polynomial-size tensor diagram giving the exact determinant on \(T\) for every original transporter. It therefore compiles the desired sign to one ordinary TI pair whenever all the contributing grades have this certificate, a small exterior line, or one of the earlier exact determinant lines. The number of parameters in the output map and the dimension of \(K\) are unrestricted. The degree assumption is a promise in this statement. A computable sufficient test for full Pfaffians and an explicit cofactor family appear below.

For example, compute the Lie algebra of derivations \(A\in\operatorname{End}X\) whose symmetric-square derivation preserves \(R=\ker(\operatorname{Sym}^2 X\to Z)\) by linear equations. Its derived algebra acts on \(R\) and on the grades it preserves. On such a grade solve \(A^{\mathsf T}\omega+\omega A=0\) for all those induced operators, or impose an invariant linear restriction on the alternating forms using an intrinsic subspace. These rules give spaces \(K\) transported exactly by the original maps. Small lists of invariant forms and alternating-form transporters have been used previously. [Earlier alternating forms](../OneSignTensorComparison/AlternatingForms.md)

## Exact character and a polynomial-size representation

Use the ordered basis volume \(\nu_T\). If \(t-c=2r\), the divided exterior power \(\omega^{[r]}\) is the alternating \(2r\)-form whose minor coordinates are Pfaffians. It is defined over the integers by sums over pair partitions. In characteristic zero it equals \(\omega^r/r!\), and its integral polynomial covariance therefore holds in characteristic three even when the factorial vanishes. Define \(P(\omega)\) by contraction into \(\nu_T\) to give \(\omega^{[r]}\), with a fixed contraction convention. If \(g:T\to T'\) and \(u:K\to K'\) are induced by a transporter, then in the displayed bases
\[
 P'(u\omega)=(\det g)^{-1}(\Lambda^c g)P(\omega).
\]
This follows by pulling back \(\nu_{T'}\) to \((\det g)\nu_T\) and using naturality of the divided power and contraction. Thus on the line spanned by the based coefficient tensor, the natural action in the two endpoint frames is multiplication by \(\det g\). This also handles odd-dimensional \(T\) with \(c=1\).

All scalar functions on \(F^k\) have unique reduced polynomials with each coordinate exponent at most two, via the relations \(x_i^3=x_i\). Their degree-at-most-\(D\) space \(V_D(K)\) is invariant under arbitrary linear substitutions, which preserve the degree bound before reduction. For fixed \(D\) it has polynomial dimension in \(k=\dim K\). A polynomial of this degree is recovered by evaluating it on all vectors of support at most \(D\) in one coordinate system. For each variable subset \(J\) of size at most \(D\), interpolate the restriction to \(F^J\) by the tensor product of the three-point univariate interpolation matrices, then read the monomials with support exactly \(J\). This determines all coefficients. Bases can be chosen independently because the recovered functions and their natural action are basis-independent. Evaluating the \(\binom tc\) complementary Pfaffians at every interpolation point costs polynomial time. A degree bound on the formal generic Pfaffian is unnecessary.

Adjoin the line spanned by \(P\) with its based generator, as a subspace of \(V_D(K)\otimes\Lambda^cT\). To force the action on its ambient space, use evaluation to link duals, the inclusion of \(K\) into the alternating forms, and degree-by-degree alternating multiplication through degree \(c\). For the polynomial-function spaces include the constant 1, the inclusions \(V_{j-1}\to V_j\), and multiplication by linear functions \(K^*\times V_{j-1}\to V_j\), reducing powers by \(x_i^3=x_i\). Induction on monomials forces precisely the action by precomposition with \(u^{-1}\). Use tensor products to link the resulting coefficient tensor. Use the cofactor line in the chain of determinant lines and mark only the calibrated generator of the final product, so individual signs remain available to cancel. The number and sizes of the sorts and their structure coefficients are polynomial in the dense input length for fixed \(c,D\). Existing tensor-system packing gives one ordinary TI pair over the same field. [Grochow–Qiao, Theorem 7.3](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

## A computable low-degree certificate for the full Pfaffian

For \(c=0\), try regular \(\omega_0\in K\) on the same list of vectors of support at most \(D\). If the nonzero low-degree promise holds, interpolation guarantees this search finds a regular form. Any regular choice serves to compute the following sufficient certificate. Let \(E\) be the associative algebra generated by \(\omega_0^{-1}K\). On \(T\) write \(m_i\) for the composition multiplicity of each simple \(E\)-module and \(w_i\) for its dimension over \(F\). Compute these using the semisimple quotient and the radical layers. Let \(s(0)=0\), \(s(a)=1\) for positive odd \(a\), and \(s(a)=2\) for positive even \(a\). Then the full Pfaffian's reduced degree is at most
\[
 D_0=\sum_i w_i\,s(\lfloor m_i/2\rfloor)
       +\tfrac12\sum_{m_i\ {\rm odd}}w_i .
\]
The algebra and all its multiplicities are unchanged on replacing the regular slice: if another choice is \(\omega_0 b\), then \(b\in E^\times\), and the inclusions of generated algebras follow in both directions. Thus \(D_0\le D\) is an intrinsic sufficient bound. Search failure certifies that this sufficient regular low-degree branch cannot apply.

Indeed for the generic form \(\omega\), the determinant of the operator \(\omega_0^{-1}\omega\) on the \(i\)-th simple module gives a homogeneous polynomial \(f_i\) of degree \(w_i\), with \(f_i(\omega_0)=1\). On a composition series the full determinant is \(\prod_i f_i^{m_i}\). It equals the square of the normalized generic Pfaffian \(p\). The polynomial ring is a unique factorization domain, so \(\prod_i f_i^{\lfloor m_i/2\rfloor}\) divides \(p\) even when different \(f_i\) share factors. The quotient has square \(\prod_{i\mid m_i\ {\rm odd}} f_i\), hence degree exactly the second summand above. As functions on ternary points, \(f_i^a=f_i^{s(a)}\), which proves the bound. The nilpotent radical, the original Pfaffian degree and the number of directions in \(K\) can be large. [Ivanyos–Qiao, finite-field algebra decomposition, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

## A scalar-centroid family with many actual output maps

Take \(d\ge4\) with \(d\equiv4\pmod {12}\). Let \(X=U\oplus W\oplus E_0\) where \(\dim U=d\), \(\dim W=\dim E_0=2\), and write \(e,f\) for a basis of \(E_0\). In \(\operatorname{Sym}^2X\) choose the relation subspace
\[
 R=UW\oplus F(Q_U+e^2),\qquad Q_U=\sum_{j=1}^d u_j^2 .
\]
Let the symmetric bimap be the product into \(\operatorname{Sym}^2X/R\). Its input dimension is \(d+4\), divisible by four, and its output dimension is \((d+4)(d+5)/2-(2d+1)\), odd. The diagonal scalar form with entries \(+1\) on \(U,W\) and \(-1\) on \(e,f\) kills the relations since \(d\equiv1\bmod3\). It is nonsingular and split. In fact it has square determinant in a dimension divisible by four, the split class of a ternary symmetric form. Surjectivity is built into the table and this slice gives zero common radical.

All mixed scalar forms \(\ell t+t\ell\) for \(0\ne\ell\in(U\oplus W)^*\) and \(0\ne t\in E_0^*\) survive. A centroid endomorphism of \(X\) is self-adjoint for all of them. Its dual therefore preserves every plane \(\langle\ell,t\rangle\), the annihilator of the corresponding radical. Intersecting these planes with \(\ell\) or \(t\) fixed shows that every line in each of the two dual blocks is preserved. Hence it acts by one scalar on each block, and a mixed form equates the two scalars. The output map is determined by surjectivity. Thus the centroid is scalar.

Recover \(T=UW\) intrinsically as \([\mathfrak d,\mathfrak d]R\) using the derivation algebra above. To check this, differentiate \(uw\) for \(u\in U,w\in W\). Its projection into \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 U\) has rank at most two and lies in \(FQ_U\), so that component vanishes. The \(W^2,WE_0,UE_0\) components must also vanish. It follows that each derivation preserves \(U,W\). Differentiating \(Q_U+e^2\) now forces \(Ae=t e\), \(A_U+A_U^{\mathsf T}=2tI\), while \(A_W\) is arbitrary. Conversely all these choices occur (take \(Af=0\)). The induced derived action on \(UW=U\otimes W\) is \(\mathfrak{so}_d\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_2\); it annihilates the displayed relation \(Q_U+e^2\) and spans \(UW\) through its action. Here skew matrix units are commutators of skew units through a third index, and the usual elementary matrices give the derived algebra on the two-dimensional slot.

The invariant alternating forms on \(T\) under this derived action are a single nonsingular line, generated in the displayed bases by \(I_U\otimes J_W\) for the standard alternating \(J_W\). Indeed the skew units on \(U\) generate all matrix units as an associative algebra (use products through a third index). The two off-diagonal units on \(W\) generate its full matrix algebra. The common commutant on \(U\otimes W\) is therefore scalar. Comparing any invariant bilinear form to the displayed nonsingular one proves uniqueness. Take \(K\) on **the full relation space \(R\)** to be the inverse image of this invariant line under restriction \(\Lambda^2R^*\to\Lambda^2T^*\). This raw-input rule gives \(\dim K=2d+1\), and the grade on which we compute the sign is \(R\) of the same unbounded dimension.

In a basis adapted to \(T\), each form has blocks \(aJ,\ v,\ -v^{\mathsf T},\ 0\), with \(J\) nonsingular alternating. Its complementary principal Pfaffians for \(c=1\) are a nonzero constant times \(a^d\) in the last coordinate and \(a^{d-1}\) times linear forms in \(v\) in all others, by expansion along the last row. The reduced ternary degree is at most three independently of \(d\). Thus the polynomial cofactor diagram handles these inputs with \(c=1,D=3\). Every \(K\)-form on this odd-dimensional space is singular, while at least one cofactor is nonzero.

Independent sign changes on the \(d\) coordinates of \(U\) preserve the relations. Their actions on the surviving products \(u_i f\) distinguish at least \(2^d\) actual output automorphisms; their linear span after restriction to these coordinates has dimension \(d\). Nevertheless all combined automorphism signs are positive. On \(T\) its nonsingular alternating line gives determinant equal to the multiplier to power \(d\), which is even. The support of \(T\) is \(U\oplus W\), hence intrinsic. The image of \(R\) in \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(X/(U\oplus W))\) is the square line of \(e\), naturally isomorphic to \(R/T\); its induced scalar is the square of a nonzero ternary scalar and equals one. This proves the assertion for \(R\), hence for the combined sign. Reflecting the output basis gives an ordinarily isomorphic pair whose every transporter is odd. This family exhibits the large relation space, unbounded output freedom and sign sensitivity simultaneously. It does not imply the degree bound on all promised inputs.

The cofactor reduction and this family have written proofs.

## Removing all multiplicity-four factors before interpolation

Here is a second deterministic sufficient criterion. Suppose \(K\) contains a regular alternating form, whose coordinates need never be supplied or preserved. With \(m_i,w_i\) as above, put
\[
 \delta=\tfrac12\sum_i w_i(m_i\bmod 4).
\]
If \(\delta\) is bounded by a fixed constant, one ordinary tensor diagram carries \(\det g_T\). This bound permits arbitrarily many arbitrarily large simple factors of multiplicity divisible by four. It can apply even when the preceding bound on the algebraic degree of the original Pfaffian grows. The following construction never selects a target by guessing the image of a reference form.

**The square divisor.** First suppose a regular alternating reference \(C\in K\) is available at each endpoint. Let \(f_i(v)\) be the determinant of \(C^{-1}v\) on the \(i\)-th simple \(E\)-module. View the original Pfaffian in the supplied \(T\)-frame, without normalizing its scalar. Define
\[
 H(v)=\prod_i f_i(v)^{2\lfloor m_i/4\rfloor},\qquad Q(v)=\operatorname{Pf}(v)/H(v).
\]
Polynomial divisibility follows from \(\operatorname{Pf}(v)^2=\det C\prod_i f_i(v)^{m_i}\) in a unique factorization domain. The quotient has formal degree \(\delta\). For the transported argument \(v'=g^{-{\mathsf T}}vg^{-1}\), the normalized target operator is \(C'^{-1}g^{-{\mathsf T}}Cg^{-1}\) times \(g(C^{-1}v)g^{-1}\). The first factor is a fixed unit of the target algebra. On matched simple modules, determinants \(f_i\) thus change by fixed nonzero ternary scalars, and the factors are permuted preserving \(w_i,m_i\). Every exponent in \(H\) is even, so \(H'(v')=H(v)\) identically. Consequently \(Q'(v')=(\det g)^{-1}Q(v)\) as a polynomial identity. Cancellation takes place in the polynomial ring before any evaluation at a singular point.

To evaluate \(Q(v)\), compute univariate Pfaffian and determinant polynomials along \(v+s C\). The divisor polynomial is nonzero since each \(f_i\) is monic along this line. Perform exact univariate division and evaluate the quotient at zero. Polynomial-matrix elimination or division-free polynomial algorithms work in polynomial time in \(t\) for these univariate computations; equivalently evaluate over a polynomial-size extension and interpolate in one variable. The resulting function is nonzero at \(C\). Interpolation on sparse ternary points now encodes it in \(V_\delta\), with its based generator carrying the sign.

**Constructing an alternating reference on an intrinsic larger space.** The regular-member promise on \(K\) suffices even when finding such a scalar member directly would be difficult. Compute \(E=\langle C^{-1}K\rangle_{\rm alg}\) without \(C\) using the block-matrix noncommutative-rank construction from the earlier flag proof. To recall why, a polynomial-size invertible witness \(W\in M_b(K)\) exists and is found deterministically in characteristic three. The generated algebra of \(W^{-1}M_b(K)\) is independent of the invertible choice since the ratio of two choices and its inverse belong to the generated algebra by Cayley–Hamilton. Substituting the existential witness \(I_b\otimes C\) in the proof therefore identifies this algebra with \(M_b(E)\). The generating space contains the matrix units tensored with the identity and the repeated operators \(I_b\otimes C^{-1}v\). Read \(E\) by projecting to the first diagonal block. [Ivanyos–Qiao–Subrahmanyam, Corollary 3](https://www.cmi.ac.in/~kv/IQS2.pdf) · [Earlier raw ratio-algebra construction](../EvenConjugatorF3/ProbeGroups.md)

Now compute \(M=K E\), the linear span of the products, and \(\widehat K=\{m\in M:m^{\mathsf T}=-m\}\). For any underlying regular \(C\in K\) we have \(M=CE\). Each generator \(C^{-1}v\) is self-adjoint for \(C\), so \(E\) is stable under its adjoint involution, transposition preserves \(M\), and transposition preserves \(M J\) for \(J=\operatorname{rad}E\). These identities also show that every transport of \(K\) conjugates \(E\) and carries \(M,\widehat K\) to the target by the usual form congruence. Constructive right-module isomorphism \(E_E\cong M_E\), using right multiplication by a basis of \(E\), supplies a generator \(B\) of \(M\) as the image of 1. Since \(M=CE\), this generator is invertible on \(T\). It need not be alternating. [Constructive module isomorphism and finite algebra algorithms, Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorems 12 and 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

Here are details for producing a regular alternating \(\widehat C\in\widehat K\) deterministically. Use \(B\) to identify \(M/MJ\) with the regular module of \(E/J\). Transposition becomes the linear involution \(\tau(a)=\theta(a)c_0\), where \(\theta(a)=B^{-1}a^{\mathsf T}B\) is an anti-automorphism and \(c_0=B^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}\) a unit. These formulas descend to the quotient. In an exchanged pair of simple coordinates choose the identity on one member and the negative of its transpose-image on the other. This gives a skew element with both coordinates invertible.

On a fixed coordinate \(M_s(k)\), let \(\sigma\) be the induced center automorphism. The square of \(\theta\) is inner so \(\sigma^2=1\). Compute an invertible \(Y\) with \(\theta(a)=Y^{-1}a^{\sigma{\mathsf T}}Y\). The intertwining equations are linear over \(k\) once \(\sigma\) is known. Skolem–Noether applied to the composition of the two anti-automorphisms supplies an invertible solution; every nonzero solution is invertible by simplicity of the natural module. Change vector-space coordinates to \(P=Ya\). In these coordinates \(\tau\) is \(P\mapsto P^{\sigma{\mathsf T}}H_0\) with \(H_0=(Y^{-1})^{\sigma{\mathsf T}}Y c_0\) in that factor. The identity \(\tau^2(P)=P\) for every \(P\) forces \(H_0=hI\) with \(h\,\sigma(h)=1\). We require an invertible \(P=-hP^{\sigma{\mathsf T}}\). For \(\sigma=1,h=-1\) take \(I\). For \(\sigma=1,h=1\) take the standard alternating matrix; the degree \(s\) is even here because the promised \(C\) already has an invertible skew coordinate. Otherwise choose a nonzero scalar \(a\) solving \(a=-h\sigma(a)\) by base-field linear equations and take \(aI\). Existence follows by applying \(z\mapsto z-h\sigma(z)\), which has nonzero image when \(\sigma\ne1\) and lands in the stated solution space.

Assemble the fixed and exchanged choices, lift the resulting skew class to \(m\in M\) and put \(\widehat C=(m-m^{\mathsf T})/2\). Its class is the same and is a unit in the \(B\)-coordinates modulo the radical, hence \(\widehat C\) is invertible. All steps take polynomial time over the fixed field. The complete space \(\widehat K\) is intrinsic. On it, for any computed regular reference \(\widehat C\), we have \(\widehat C^{-1}\widehat K\subseteq E\) and \(\widehat C^{-1}M=E\). Use the same \(E\) and its multiplicities in the divisor construction, now for \(v\in\widehat K\), using \(\widehat C\) for the perturbation and normalization. Two independently computed references differ under transport by a unit of \(E'\) because both generate the corresponding \(M'\). The divisor covariance proof consequently applies on the entire enlarged space, while \(Q(\widehat C)\ne0\) guarantees a nonzero orientation tensor. Link \(\widehat K\) to the original \(T\) by its natural inclusion into the alternating forms as above. The polynomial class construction uses only the promised existence of a regular form in \(K\), never a supplied transporter or an oracle to recover its action.
