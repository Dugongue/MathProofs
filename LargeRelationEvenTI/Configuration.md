# Multiplying signs on permuted relation blocks

Let \(F=\mathbf F_3\). Write \(R=\ker(\operatorname{Sym}^2 X\to Z)\) for a based surjective symmetric bimap with \(\dim X\) even. The determinant exact sequence identifies \(\det g\,\det h\) with the character of the induced action on \(R\), up to computable endpoint frame signs. We give a polynomial-size ordinary TI construction for an unbounded relation grade split into many permuted blocks. A family below meets all the scalar-centroid, split nonsingular slice and parity promises. Its unique unrefined relation grade has dimension linear in the tangent dimension. [Relation determinant and raw-frame correction](../EvenConjugatorF3/FilteredRelations.md)

## An incidence gadget for ternary signs

Suppose a connected finite simple graph \(G\) indexes two-element sets \(T_v\), each with its fixed-point-free involution, attached to a linear structure under comparison. A transporter induces a graph isomorphism \(\pi\) and bijections of these sets. Choose one element \(t_v\) in each set at each endpoint. In additive bit notation define \(b_v=0\) if the transporter takes \(t_v\) to \(t'_{\pi(v)}\) and \(b_v=1\) otherwise. There is a finite extension of the structure through which this transporter lifts exactly when \(\sum_v b_v=0\) in \(\mathbf F_2\).

For each edge \(e\) attach an unordered pair of new elements \(p_e^0,p_e^1\). The superscripts specify the construction and are never marked in the resulting structure. For every vertex \(v\) create a separate element for each assignment \(a:E(v)\to\{0,1\}\), where \(E(v)\) is its incident edge set. Link this element to \(v\), to each selected \(p_e^{a(e)}\), and to the element of \(T_v\) obtained by flipping \(t_v\) exactly \(\sum_{e\ni v}a(e)\) times. Use distinct sorts or relations for each role.

An extension must carry each edge pair by flipping some bit \(x_e\) relative to the target labels. The incidence links then force the image of every assignment element. Its torsor link agrees with the prescribed transporter exactly when \(\sum_{e\ni v}x_e=b_v\). Every column of this system has two ones. Conversely, if \(\sum b_v=0\), choose a spanning tree, set off-tree bits to zero, and solve leaf to root. All equations hold, including at the root. At a single isolated vertex this simply enforces \(b_v=0\). The tree is used in the proof and is never marked. Replacing one \(t_v\) by its other element changes the tested total parity by one while allowing arbitrary permutations of the vertices. This is the parity mechanism of the Cai–Fürer–Immerman construction, here linked to signs of a linear transporter. [Cai, Fürer and Immerman](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~immerman/pub/opt.pdf)

## Determinant with permuted blocks

Suppose a deterministic polynomial-time rule recovers a direct sum \(R=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R_i\) together with a connected simple graph \(H\) on \(I\). Every bimap transporter must carry this collection and graph to the corresponding target collection and graph. The concrete raw rule below requires no supplied labeling. If the original data instead include these as marks, the statement applies to the marked transporter problem. Suppose \(\max_i\dim R_i\le c\log_2 L\) and \(\Delta(H)\le c\log_2 L\) for fixed \(c\) and dense input length \(L\). Then the signed comparison reduces deterministically to **one ordinary TI instance** over \(F\). The action on the blocks and the output map may both be unknown.

Use the graph \(G\) whose vertices are the unordered two-element multisets of \(I\), with repetitions allowed. Join two sites when moving one token along an edge of \(H\) changes one into the other. This graph is connected since both tokens can be moved successively to any fixed vertex, and its maximum degree is at most \(2\Delta(H)\). At the diagonal site \(ii\) use the two nonzero elements of \(\det R_i\) as \(T_{ii}\). At an off-diagonal site \(ij\) with both block dimensions odd use the two orders \((i,j),(j,i)\) as \(T_{ij}\), linked to their first and second blocks. At remaining sites take a two-point set with one of its points structurally fixed relative to the site. Use identical dimensions across matched blocks. With an arbitrary ordering of the blocks and ordered bases within them, select the positive local volume and the increasing order at the relevant sites. The total bit is the sum of the local determinant bits and the inversion count on the odd blocks. An exchange of blocks of sizes \(a,b\) contributes \((-1)^{ab}\) in the exterior product of a concatenated basis. Thus the total sign is the determinant on \(R\) in these frames. Multiply in the two computable frame signs for the concatenated basis and the original bimap determinant exact sequence, and the requested raw sign, by flipping a single chosen reference point at an endpoint when needed. This flip changes the assignment links but adds no marked site.

All record sizes are polynomial. There are at most \(|I|(|I|+1)/2\) sites and at most \(2^{2\Delta(H)}\) assignments per site. To obtain a fixed linear signature, give each finite sort \(S\) its coordinate algebra \(F^S\), whose primitive idempotents are permuted exactly as the elements of \(S\). Maps between finite point sets use the linear maps on primitive idempotents, and arbitrary incidence relations use a finite sort of true tuples and their coordinate projections. Finite sorts may equivalently be empty, using the zero vector space. Link the primitive idempotents of \(F^I\) to their block projections by its action on \(R\). Include the original bimap, symmetric square, kernels and inclusion maps. Build the direct sum of exterior algebras of the \(R_i\) with componentwise multiplication, its \(F^I\)-action, units and linked degree-one inclusions. Multiplication forces the exterior map on every block. The inclusion of the two elements of each volume torsor into this algebra is represented by the linear map sending its coordinate idempotents to the actual top vectors. No independent choice of a sign on a volume line is left. The algebra dimension is at most \(|I|2^{c\log_2L}\). The finite incidence sorts and named operations above have bounded arities and a constant number of types. The linear-system isomorphism theorem therefore compiles them into one ordinary three-tensor pair. This is a direct gadget reduction, even if there are exponentially many induced permutations. The same construction applies to a permutation-equivariant direct sum on one associated grade, and for a fixed number of characters one can enumerate their compatible sign assignments. In each branch attach all character gadgets to one shared original bimap, so every sign refers to the same transporter. Apply the established positive disjunction only across the resulting whole branches. [Grochow–Qiao, Theorem 7.3](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

## A raw recognition rule and a sign-rigid family

Compute by linear equations
\[
 \mathfrak d=\{D\in\operatorname{End} X:(Du)v+u(Dv)\text{ induces an operator carrying }R\text{ into }R\},
\]
where the displayed formula defines the operator on products \(uv\). Test that a basis of \(\mathfrak d\) commutes. Refine \(X\) successively by its intersections with the three eigenspaces of each basis element. Check that the resulting simultaneous eigenspaces are lines whose direct sum is \(X\). All are obtained without guessing coordinates. On choosing any nonzero vector in each line, test whether \(R\) is the span of a subset of the pairwise *distinct-coordinate* monomials. If it is, draw a simple graph \(K\) on these tangent lines whose edges are precisely the relation monomials. On connected \(K\) with at least one edge and maximum degree \(O(\log L)\), the relation blocks are the edge lines and their carrier \(H\) is the line graph of \(K\), of degree at most \(2\Delta(K)-2\). Change of tangent frame permutes vertices and preserves the monomial membership tests. Thus all graphs and the required one-dimensional blocks are recovered on raw bimap input. The determinant probe and the raw output map are unused.

For each \(n=4k+2\ge18\) take tangent coordinates \(x_j\) indexed by \(\mathbb Z/n\), and let \(K\) have all edges of cyclic distances 1 and 3. It is 4-regular, with \(2n\) edges. Set \(R\) equal to the span of their monomials. The output dimension \(n(n-3)/2\) is odd. A diagonal form with \(n/2\) pairs of entries \(1,-1\) is a split nonsingular slice. All diagonal squares survive and the surviving off-diagonal graph (the complement of \(K\)) is connected. Indeed it contains every edge within each parity class as well as all distance-5 edges between the classes. With the identity as a slice, its ratio algebra contains every diagonal matrix unit and, for every surviving pair, the sum of the corresponding two matrix units. Multiplication by diagonal projectors and along paths generates the full matrix algebra. Its commutant is scalar. In particular the simultaneous adjoint and the centroid are scalar. The radical is zero.

For any nonzero \(u\in X\), if two or more coordinates are nonzero then the diagonal terms of \(\beta(u,-)\) already have rank at least two. If only one is nonzero, a surviving mixed product and its diagonal square give rank at least two. Thus there is no rank-one output line. The original ratio and adjoint radical flags have a single grade, and their natural output image of \(X\times X\) is all of \(Z\). The ground-field determinant of the slice pencil also fails to split into linear factors. In fact the determinant of a generic symmetric matrix on a connected off-diagonal graph with separate diagonal variables is irreducible. It is affine-linear in each diagonal variable, so any factorization partitions those variables between the factors. For an edge across the partition, specialize every other off-diagonal variable to zero. The specialized determinant is \((z_i z_j-w^2)\prod_{k\ne i,j}z_k\). The irreducible factor \(z_i z_j-w^2\) depends on both \(z_i,z_j\), contradicting the partition. Connectedness leaves all diagonal variables in one factor, and the coefficient one of their product forces the remaining factor to be constant.

The computed derivation algebra consists of the diagonal matrices. To see this, suppose \(D_{ji}\ne0\) for \(j\ne i\). For each \(a\in N(i)\), differentiating \(x_i x_a\) produces the term \(D_{ji}x_jx_a\), which cannot cancel with any term involving \(x_i\). Thus \(N(i)\subseteq N(j)\), including the exclusion \(a=j\) because squares are not relations. Equal degrees give equality. Equality of neighborhoods would give a nonidentity translation stabilizing the 4-set \(\{-3,-1,1,3\}\). Its order must divide both 4 and \(n\), hence it must be translation by \(n/2\), which does not preserve this set for \(n\ge18\). Conversely all diagonal endomorphisms preserve the monomial relations. Therefore all automorphisms of the bimap have monomial tangent actions.

Counts of length-three walks between the endpoints of a distance-1 and a distance-3 edge are respectively 12 and 10. They are coefficients at powers 1 and 3 in \((x^{-3}+x^{-1}+x+x^3)^3\), with no wraparound for \(n\ge18\). Every graph automorphism therefore preserves the distance-1 cycle, hence is dihedral. On the two classes of edges every rotation gives the same permutation sign twice. For a reflection \(x\mapsto a-x\), the fixed \(j\)-edges, indexed as \(\{x,x+j\}\), solve \(2x=a-j\) for \(j=1,3\). The two fixed-edge counts are equal, so its permutation signs on these two equally sized classes also agree. Every graph automorphism therefore acts evenly on the union of all edges. Each tangent line scaling enters the edge action to its fourth power. All bimap automorphisms consequently have positive combined sign. Comparing the bimap to itself is affirmative, and composing its output with a single reflection gives an ordinary-isomorphic pair admitting only negative transporters. Both the relation dimension and the output dimension in this family grow without bound.

These are written proofs. The unrestricted square-zero case remains open.
