# Hidden tensor factors and orientation of quadratic relations

Over \(F=\mathbf F_3\), let \(b\colon \operatorname{Sym}^2X\twoheadrightarrow Z\) be the multiplication table recovered from the promised square-zero carrier. Put \(R=\ker b\). All signs below refer to the supplied bases. When \(n=\dim X\) is even the sign \(\det g\,\det h\) transfers to the sign on \(R\) with an endpoint basis correction. The prior proof gives this correction and deterministic recovery from raw conjugated square-zero matrix spaces. [Frame recovery and relation transfer](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/EvenConjugatorF3/FilteredRelations.md)

## A test on large relation grades

Suppose the following raw calculation at both ends succeeds. It is one explicit way to recognize the tensor factors used below.

1. Compute the linear space of endomorphisms \(D\) of \(X\) whose derivative \(xy\mapsto (Dx)y+x(Dy)\) preserves \(R\). Compute its derived Lie algebra \(L=[D,D]\), meaning the span of commutators. Compute the centroid of the Lie bracket on \(L\) by linear equations.
2. Test that this centroid under composition is a split product \(F^s\), and take its primitive idempotents. They give Lie ideals \(L_i\). Compute the unital associative algebras \(A_i\) they generate on \(X\). Test that these algebras commute pairwise, that each is \(M_{d_i}(F)\) with \(d_i\ge2\), that their products span \(\operatorname{End}(X)\), and that \(\prod_i d_i=n\). In this criterion require the \(d_i\) to be pairwise distinct and order the factors by size. Other polynomially computed invariant labels distinguishing the factors also suffice.
3. Take matrix units in each \(A_i\). The tensor product of these matrix algebras acts as the full endomorphism algebra. Their diagonal first idempotents have a common image of dimension one, since their product is a primitive matrix unit of this full algebra. Choose a nonzero vector there and apply the products of the \((j,1)\) units to it. The resulting basis gives a tensor identification \(X=\bigotimes_i U_i\), where \(\dim U_i=d_i\). All tests and constructions take deterministic polynomial time by linear algebra and explicit finite-field associative algebra decomposition at fixed characteristic. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

These identifications may be chosen independently at the endpoints. Any transporter \(g\) maps each intrinsic algebra to its counterpart. In the two tensor identifications, the induced automorphism of each matrix algebra is inner. Removing the product of those inner changes leaves an operator commuting with the full matrix algebra, hence a scalar. Thus every original transporter has \(g=\bigotimes_i p_i\) in these frames, with the scalar absorbed into one \(p_i\). Conversely any such tuple preserving the original table is admissible. Choices of lifts introduce no restriction. The calculation also applies with tensor factors obtained by other specified intrinsic algorithms that establish this normalizer property.

In \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 X\) there is a direct sum of spaces \(H_I\), indexed by even-cardinality subsets \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}\). In position \(i\), take \(\Lambda^2 U_i\) for \(i\in I\) and \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 U_i\) otherwise, then tensor the factors. Indeed, inside the tensor square of \(X\), the swaps of the two copies of each \(U_i\) commute and have eigenvalues \(1,-1\). The full swap is their product. Its \(+1\)-space gives precisely these simultaneous eigenspaces in characteristic three. Construct them by eigenspace intersections or directly by tensoring the symmetric and alternating bases. The number of subsets is at most \(n\). The sum of their dimensions is \(n(n+1)/2\).

Order these spaces by their subset labels. Filter \(R\) by its intersections with their successive partial sums. The new subspace contributed at \(I\), denoted \(T_I\), embeds by projection in \(H_I\), and let \(Q_I=H_I/T_I\). This works even if \(R\) does not split across the summands. Compare all dimensions at both endpoints, rejecting mismatches.

**Sufficient reduction.** Fix \(k=\lfloor c\log_2 L_{\rm in}\rfloor\) for constant \(c\) and dense input length \(L_{\rm in}\ge2\). At each \(I\) choose either \(T_I\) or \(Q_I\) of dimension at most \(k\). If \(Q_I\) is chosen, add to the exponent of factor \(i\) the integer
\[
 e_{iI}=(d_i-1\ \text{if }i\in I,\ \ d_i+1\ \text{otherwise})\prod_{j\ne i}\dim H_{I,j},
\]
where \(H_{I,j}\) is the symmetric or exterior square in position \(j\). There is one ordinary TI instance for the signed comparison whenever these choices can give even total exponents for every \(d_i>k\). The choices are found by linear equations over \(\mathbf F_2\). Both \(R\) and every large tangent factor can exceed logarithmic dimension.

For a transporter the determinant on \(H_I\) is \(\prod_i(\det p_i)^{e_{iI}}\) by the tensor-product and symmetric/exterior-square determinant formulas. If \(Q_I\) is retained, the character on \(T_I\) is the ambient character divided by that on \(Q_I\), and the two sign operations multiplication and division agree in \(F^\times\). Multiplication over grades proves that the surviving character is the product on the retained small spaces and the small factor spaces with odd total exponent. This argument holds for every lift \((p_i)\), since its initial expression is the intrinsic determinant on \(R\). It also works on a large relation grade with other already encoded sign lines by tensoring the resulting lines before marking their product.

Attach the \(U_i\) and the bilinear tensor-product chain ending in the computed identification with \(X\). Attach \(R\), the partial sums, and the kernels and quotients just used through their inclusion and quotient maps. Attach the small exterior algebras and take the tensor product of the top lines. To fix its generator, start in the displayed frames of \(X,Z\) and use the exact-sequence determinant rule, comparing concatenated bases of a kernel and lifts of a quotient with the displayed basis. In each \(H_I\) use the fixed tensor ordering and the symmetric or alternating basis from the factors. The actual \(+\) and \(-\) eigenbasis embeddings are part of the frame computation. All changes are determinants of polynomial-size matrices. Pin the generator at the two endpoints with the desired relative sign. A transporter extends exactly when its combined determinant has that sign. Polynomially many arrows can be packed in one fixed linear signature by the ordered coordinate-algebra construction in the previous filtered reduction. Applying the tensor-system compiler gives one ordinary ternary TI pair. [Grochow–Qiao, Theorem 7.3](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

## A family where the old output test leaves every parameter free

Choose \(a=12i+2\) and \(b=12j+5\), for nonnegative \(i,j\). Let \(U,V\) have dimensions \(a,b\), \(X=U\otimes V\), and take the surjection
\[
 (u\otimes v)(u'\otimes v')\longmapsto (uu')\otimes(vv')
 \quad\text{onto}\quad Z=\operatorname{Sym}^2U\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2V .
\]
The kernel is \(R=\Lambda^2U\otimes\Lambda^2V\), spanned by the \(2\)-by-\(2\) minors of the generic \(a\)-by-\(b\) matrix of tangent variables. This follows either from the swap decomposition or by the straightening relation equating the two products with the same row and column multisets. The tangent dimension is even and the output dimension is odd. A split nondegenerate scalar form on \(U\) tensored with a nondegenerate scalar form on \(V\) is a split nonsingular slice on \(X\).

Here the raw derivation calculation in the theorem recovers the two factors. To verify it, dualize the action on equations. Infinitesimal preservation of the minors implies that the associated operator on \(U^*\otimes V^*\) takes every rank-one point \(u\otimes v\) into \(u\otimes V^*+U^*\otimes v\), with \(u,v\) in the dual factors. At a coordinate point this follows by linearizing minors involving its nonzero entry. It holds at every rank-one point by separate basis changes. An operator satisfying this condition has the form \(P\otimes 1+1\otimes Q\). On a basis element \(e_i\otimes f_j\) only its row and column can occur. Testing \(e_i\otimes(f_j+f_l)\) makes each off-diagonal change of row independent of the column. The analogous test interchanging the factors makes each off-diagonal change of column independent of the row. Subtract these two operators. Testing \((e_i+e_k)\otimes(f_j+f_l)\) forces the four diagonal coefficients on each rectangle to have zero alternating sum. They are therefore a row coefficient plus a column coefficient. Conversely separate row and column infinitesimal changes preserve the span of minors by differentiation. Dualizing back gives the claimed Lie algebra on \(X\).

Its commutator is the direct sum of the two special linear Lie algebras. Both sizes are prime to three. For such a size \(d\ge2\), the centroid of \(\mathfrak{sl}_d(F)\) is scalar: commuting with brackets by trace-zero diagonals preserves the distinct off-diagonal root lines. The roots remain distinct on the trace-zero diagonals since their differences have coordinate sum zero and \(3\nmid d\). Brackets of opposite units tie their two scalars and the diagonal differences, and brackets along triples tie the remaining units. These algebras are perfect, so there are no cross maps in the centroid of their direct sum. The two associative envelopes are the full factor algebras, generated by their matrix units. This proves the raw recognition, including when \(a=2\).

The character on the entire large relation space is
\[
 (\det p)^{(a-1)\binom b2}\,(\det q)^{(b-1)\binom a2}=1 ,
\]
as \(b\equiv1\bmod4\). Since \(a\ne b\), there is no factor exchange. Every automorphism is even, while an odd reflection of the output gives an ordinarily isomorphic pair with no even transporter. Independently chosen tensor frames produce a transporter by aligning the product frames and their straightened quotient bases. Comparing the two frame determinants thus decides the answer directly. On this family one may emit a pair of one-entry tensors, \(1,1\) or \(1,0\).

The ratio algebra of the raw scalar slices is \(\operatorname{End}(X)\): the identity is a slice, every diagonal coordinate projector is a slice, and slices contain the symmetric edge matrices for the connected grid joining two entries in a common row or column. Products with the diagonal projectors give all matrix units. The simultaneous adjoint is its scalar commutant and the centroid is scalar. The quotient is the bidegree \((2,2)\) part of the polynomial ring on two independent sets of variables, with \(X\) its bidegree \((1,1)\) part. Multiplication by any nonzero tangent vector on \(X\) is injective, so there are no rank-one output directions. The ratio and adjoint radical flags have one grade, and the earlier output-subspace constraints leave \((\dim Z)^2\) linear parameters. The determinant of the scalar pencil does not split into linear factors: on the slice family \(S\otimes I_V\) it restricts to \(\det(S)^b\); further specializing \(S\) to one generic symmetric two-by-two corner and an identity block leaves \((xy-z^2)^b\), with irreducible quadratic factor. Both the relation and its quotient grow beyond logarithmic dimension. The smallest displayed member has \((\dim X,\dim R,\dim Z)=(10,10,45)\).
