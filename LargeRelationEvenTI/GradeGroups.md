# Recovering a graph from quadratic relations and transferring its sign

Let \(F=\mathbf F_3\), let \(n=\dim X\) be even, and let \(R=\ker(\beta:\operatorname{Sym}^2 X\to Z)\) for a based surjective symmetric bimap. Call \(R\) monomial if it is spanned by a subset of the quadratic monomials in some basis of \(X\). The basis and the subset are absent from the input.

**Theorem.** There is a deterministic polynomial-time recognition and recovery algorithm for monomial \(R\). In this class the signed transporter question reduces exactly to a graph-isomorphism question with one edge-permutation sign, including a computable endpoint correction. One polynomial-size ordinary TI instance suffices in any of the following cases.

- A recovered vertex has odd off-loop degree, or a transposition inside a class of twins induces an odd permutation of the relation monomials.
- The fiber-size and transpose parity tests in Section 3 hold. The quotient graph can have arbitrary valence, size, and automorphism group.
- The quotient by twins, with the colors specified below, has maximum valence bounded by a fixed constant, or its complement does.

The hypotheses are decidable on the dense input and involve neither the number of output-map parameters nor the dimension of the relation grade. The examples in Section 4 are inside the scalar-centroid, split nonsingular slice promise. The algorithms retain the signs in the original bases.

## 1. A hidden monomial frame

Compute the linear space \(D=\{a\in\operatorname{End}(X):\partial_a R\subseteq R\}\), with \(\partial_a(xy)=(ax)y+x(ay)\). If \(R\) is monomial, form its graph \(H\) in a monomial basis, putting an edge \(ij\) for \(x_i x_j\in R\), allowing a loop for \(i=j\). Write \(N_i=\{j:ij\in H\}\), counting a loop as membership of \(i\). In this basis
\[
             D=\operatorname{span}\{E_{ki}:N_i\subseteq N_k\}.
\]
Indeed apply the derivative to each relation monomial. In a nonsquare the coefficient of any forbidden replacement is a single off-diagonal matrix entry. In a square the coefficient is twice this entry, and 2 is invertible. Conversely the indicated replacements preserve the monomial span. Thus \(D\) is an associative pattern algebra. Equal neighborhoods are its diagonal matrix blocks and strict containments give its nilpotent ideal. The quotient is the product of full matrix algebras on the equal-neighborhood classes. Every unit of \(D\) preserves \(R\). To see this, block triangular elimination generates the units by invertible diagonal scalings and transvections \(I+tE_{ki}\) with the indicated containments. For an allowed replacement on \(x_i^2\), the term \(x_i x_k\) is allowed, and then \(k\in N_i\subseteq N_k\) makes \(x_k^2\) allowed too. Each generator and its inverse preserve \(R\).

Test associativity of the matrix space \(D\) by products of a basis. For an associative \(D\), compute its radical \(J\) and an explicit product decomposition of \(D/J\). Reject monomiality if a center field differs from \(F\). Lift the diagonal matrix units of these split matrix factors to a complete set of orthogonal idempotents in \(D\). For explicit lifting, work successively in the remaining corner \(pDp\), starting with \(p=1\). Choose a preimage of the next diagonal idempotent, corner it by \(p\), and raise it to a power \(3^s\) at least the nilpotency index of \(J\). Since \(a^2-a\in J\), the result is idempotent with the same reduction. Subtract it from \(p\) and proceed. The last unused corner is zero once its quotient is zero, because it is a nilpotent idempotent. These computations are polynomial over \(F\). [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

The method accepts if each lifted idempotent has rank one on \(X\). Their images give a basis of lines. The derivative of each idempotent acts diagonally on the monomial basis of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 X\). The simultaneous weight vectors distinguish all monomials, since squares have weight 2 in one coordinate and other products have weight 1 in each of two coordinates. Each weight projection is a product of univariate interpolation polynomials in these diagonal derivations. Hence every invariant subspace, in particular \(R\), is spanned by monomials. The graph follows by membership tests. For square-free monomial ideals, reconstruction of the underlying complex from the associated ring has classical precedents. Here the linear equations recover a monomial presentation from the raw quadratic subspace, and the remaining calculation retains the sign of a transporter. [Reconstruction of simplicial complexes from related algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0702618)

For completeness, two complete systems obtained by lifting diagonal primitive idempotents of the split quotient are conjugate by a unit of \(D\), up to reindexing. In the quotient use a basis change in each matrix factor, lift that unit to a unit, and conjugate. Once corresponding idempotents \(f_i,e_i\) agree modulo \(J\), the element \(\sum_i f_i e_i\) is a unit congruent to 1, and multiplying by \(e_i\) or \(f_i\) on the appropriate side gives the same result. Thus on an actual monomial input all lifted idempotents have rank one, since the original coordinate idempotents do. The same argument shows that every linear transporter of two relation spaces can be followed by a target unit preserving the relation space so that it carries the recovered lines to the recovered target lines. Conversely every graph isomorphism lifts by the corresponding basis permutation. This proves ordinary equivalence with graph isomorphism, even when the input basis completely mixes the vertices.

## 2. The sign in recovered coordinates

For an actual transporter the based square-zero orientation is \(\det g\det h\). The exact sequence for \(\beta\) and \(\det(\operatorname{Sym}^2g)=(\det g)^{n+1}\) identify this bit with the determinant on \(R\), up to a computable ratio of endpoint signs. For an explicit convention use the original monomial basis, then form the square matrix with columns the recovered relation basis in the chosen order, followed by arbitrary lifts of the displayed output basis. Let \(c\) be its determinant. If a transporter has relation determinant \(r\) in the two recovered relation bases, its original sign is \((c'/c)r\). Indeed \(c'\,r\,\det h/c=(\det g)^{n+1}\), and every nonzero ternary scalar is its own inverse. The same computation incorporates prescribed raw matrix-space frame offsets when passing from the square-zero operator model. [Grade and raw frame transfer](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TISLSquareZeroDirect/earlier/SquareZeroClosure.md)

An aligned transporter in recovered frames consists of a graph isomorphism and one ternary sign per vertex. Its relation determinant equals the permutation sign on the chosen ordered lists of monomials, times \(\prod_i s_i^{\deg_0(i)}\), where \(\deg_0(i)\) counts the nonloop neighbors. A loop contributes the square of \(s_i\). The omitted alignment units contribute no additional character: transvections have order three and hence positive determinant on \(R\), and the diagonal generators give exactly these degree signs. If some vertex has odd off-loop degree, any graph isomorphism realizes both signs. Otherwise the entire problem is edge-permutation parity for the recovered graphs with the endpoint factors. This conclusion does not require an ordinary isomorphism as auxiliary input.

## 3. Two reductions on the graph

Call \(v,w\) twins when the transposition interchanging them is an automorphism of the graph with loops. Together with equality this is an equivalence relation, by conjugating transpositions. Between two distinct classes there is either a complete bipartite graph or no edge. Within a class every vertex has the same loop bit \(\ell\), and every pair of distinct vertices has the same adjacency bit \(e\). Use \(e=0\) for classes of size one. Color a quotient vertex by \((s,\ell,e)\), where \(s\) is class size. Every color-preserving quotient isomorphism lifts by arbitrary within-class bijections, and all graph isomorphisms arise this way. Adjacent transpositions inside classes generate the remaining permutation freedom. If any of them has odd induced sign on the edges, ordinary equivalence already suffices for the signed comparison.

Hereafter suppose the two local correction tests found no odd action. For arbitrarily chosen orderings inside classes, lift a quotient isomorphism by matching vertex indices in corresponding classes. Its edge sign can be computed through the following fibers.

1. A vertex class contributes \(\ell s+e\binom{s}{2}\) local monomials. Order these in a uniform order of loops and local pairs. Permuting classes of one color contributes the block-permutation sign raised to that common fiber size.
2. Each quotient edge contributes \(s_i s_j\) monomials. Use a common ordering of color pairs, and orient an edge from smaller to larger color when these differ. Within each fiber use the lexicographic ordering of the two local indices. Permuting quotient edges of a fixed unordered color pair has its sign raised to \(s_i s_j\).
3. A quotient edge with equal endpoint colors may reverse relative to the arbitrary local ordering of its two endpoints. The transpose on \(s\times s\) ordered pairs contributes \((-1)^{\binom{s}{2}}\), since it interchanges \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\) for each distinct unordered pair.

Choose the relation-basis order grouped by these colors and fiber types at both endpoints before evaluating \(c\). If the two color and fiber counts differ, return ordinary NO. Otherwise the following polynomial tests force sign \(+1\) for every such index-preserving lift. A local fiber size must be even whenever at least two quotient vertices have that color. A mixed fiber size must be even whenever at least two quotient edges have that unordered color pair. Every existing equal-color quotient edge must have \(\binom{s}{2}\) even. When the tests pass, compare \(c'/c\) with the requested sign. Return a fixed NO on mismatch; on agreement return one ordinary tensor instance for isomorphism of the original unsigned bimap. The latter is the fixed-signature bilinear-model construction. The same ordinary pair suffices without calibration whenever the local sign is free. [Grochow–Qiao, Theorem 7.3](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

Independently, if the quotient or its complement has bounded maximum degree, the colored bounded-valence algorithm gives a quotient isomorphism and generators for its automorphism group in deterministic polynomial time. One can also obtain generators by individualizing an ordered vertex sequence and using colored bounded-degree isomorphism to compute a transversal for each step of the stabilizer chain. Lift the isomorphism and generators as above and include the within-class transpositions. Evaluate their induced edge signs directly. The attainable sign set is the representative's sign times the subgroup of the two signs generated by the stabilizer signs, with the endpoint correction from Section 2. Emit a pair of identical nonzero one-entry tensors for YES, or a zero and a nonzero one-entry tensor for NO. The original graph can have unbounded degree. [Luks, bounded-valence graph isomorphism](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/iso.pdf)

## 4. Two sign-rigid families with large unresolved grades in the earlier tests

Take any loopless graph \(G\) and replace each vertex with an independent set of four coordinates. Replace a base edge by all 16 cross monomials between the corresponding sets, and add two more isolated relation coordinates. Let \(R\) be the span of these edges, let \(n=4|V(G)|+2\), and let \(\beta\) be the universal symmetric product modulo \(R\). The relation graph's nonisolated twin classes are independent sets of size divisible by four. Indeed two vertices in adjacent four-sets cannot be twins, as a third vertex in the first set distinguishes them. Isolates form a separate class without edges. No relation loops occur. All off-loop degrees are multiples of four. A transposition inside a nonisolated twin class swaps a pair of edges for each neighbor, so its sign is positive. Every nonempty mixed fiber has even size and same-color transposes have even parity. The parity tests apply to arbitrary \(G\), with no bound on its degree. Every automorphism is even. A single reflection on the output produces an ordinary-isomorphic pair admitting only odd transporters.

Here \(Z\) has odd dimension, since \(n\equiv2\bmod4\) and \(\dim R=16|E(G)|\). Every diagonal scalar form survives, as do the off-diagonal scalar forms associated to the complement edges. The diagonal form with equally many \(+1\) and \(-1\) entries is nonsingular and split. The normalized slice algebra with the identity slice contains all \(E_{ii}\) and \(E_{ij}+E_{ji}\) along the complement edges. The complement is connected through the two isolated relation vertices, hence multiplication gives the full matrix algebra. Its commutant is scalar and a centroid map must belong to that commutant. Surjectivity then makes the output centroid map the same scalar. Zero common radical follows from the nonsingular slice.

For a nonzero \(x\), choose a nonzero coordinate \(x_i\) and a distinct complement neighbor \(j\) of \(i\). The value \(\beta(x,x_i^{\rm bas})\), where \(x_i^{\rm bas}\) denotes the \(i\)th basis vector, has a nonzero \(i\)-square coordinate. The value at the \(j\)th basis vector has zero \(i\)-square coordinate and a nonzero \(ij\)-coordinate. Thus no nonzero vector has rank-one evaluation image. Both previously used radical flags are trivial and the normalized algebra has just one simple factor. Its radical-layer output subspaces and the rank-one-output rule impose no restrictions on the \(m^2\) linear parameters of the output map, where \(m=\dim Z\). Taking a growing twin-free base cycle gives unbounded \(\dim R\) and unbounded codimension in the symmetric square of its support, even after removing the two isolates. More general base graphs retain the arbitrary quotient-isomorphism comparison.

For a family with full relation support, instead let \(H\) be the disjoint union of cycles of lengths 5 and \(4k+1\), \(k\ge2\). Now \(n=4k+6\), \(\dim R=n\), and \(m=n(n-1)/2\) is odd. Support is all of \(X\), the complement graph is connected, and the same matrix-algebra, centroid, split-slice and rank-one computations apply. The graph is twin-free with valence two. Its two components have different lengths. Every rotation on an odd cycle induces an even permutation of the edges, and each reflection on a cycle of length \(1\bmod4\) swaps an even number of edge pairs. Thus all stabilizers are even, and an output reflection again gives an odd-only input.

For both families the determinant of the scalar slice pencil is irreducible as a polynomial in its independent surviving coordinates. For any connected complement graph the generic symmetric matrix has independent diagonal variables \(d_i\) and independent edge variables \(y_{ij}\). The determinant is multiaffine in the \(d_i\), with full diagonal coefficient 1. In a factorization over the base polynomial ring in the \(y\)'s, each \(d_i\) must belong to exactly one factor. A factor involving none divides that leading coefficient. If two nonconstant factors split the diagonals, connectedness gives a crossing edge \(ij\). Taking the coefficient of all other diagonals leaves \(d_i d_j-y_{ij}^2\), while the factorization gives a polynomial in \(d_i\) times a polynomial in \(d_j\), with unit leading coefficients. Comparing the two linear coefficients forces both constant terms to vanish, contradicting the nonzero constant coefficient. The rational-factor output detector therefore also gives no linear factors on these examples.

All results here are written proofs. General relation spaces outside this class, and monomial graphs failing the stated sign or quotient hypotheses, receive no polynomial-time signed compiler from this argument.
