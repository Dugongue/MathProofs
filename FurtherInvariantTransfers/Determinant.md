# Determinant-one monomial tensor isomorphism over Galois rings

Let $R=(\mathbb Z/p^a)[X]/(f)$, where $p$ is prime, $a\geq1$, and $f$ is monic of degree $\ell$ and irreducible modulo $p$. Use explicit power-basis residues with $a\log p$ bits per coefficient. For arbitrary dense three-way arrays over $R$, deciding whether an all-mode monomial transporter has a prescribed determinant triple in $(R^\times)^3$ is deterministically polynomial-time Turing reducible to ordinary free 3-tensor isomorphism over the same ring. No hypothesis is placed on the support or on which entries are units. The theorem includes finite fields of varying order and determinant one in each mode. The action is relative to the supplied coordinate bases.

## 1. Recover only the discrete permutation image

First reduce marked monomial transport to ordinary TI. For each mode $V_i=R^{n_i}$ include the coordinate algebra $E_i=R^{n_i}$ with multiplication and its coordinatewise action on $V_i$. The primitive idempotents of $E_i$ are exactly the coordinate idempotents, because a local ring has only zero and one as idempotents. An algebra isomorphism permutes them, and intertwining the action on $V_i$ then forces a monomial map with exactly that permutation. In the other direction, any monomial map intertwines the action with this algebra permutation. To enforce a partial list of coordinate correspondences in each mode, add a split algebra $C_i$ whose primitive idempotents index the marked labels and a common unmarked label. Include the linear endomorphism of $C_i$ given by the directed path on this list. Its centralizer among permutations is trivial. Include a map $E_i\to C_i$ sending each coordinate idempotent to its label. This leaves the desired marked pairs and permits any permutation of the unmarked ones.

Here is the compilation of this system. A bilinear map is a tensor with two dual inputs and a covariant output, and a linear map gets one scalar slot. Include the data tensor as another relation. For every module sort use a covariant and a dual core block in each of three modes. Reserve a scalar anchor line in each mode, with their triple tensor equal to one. Link each covariant core block to the dual core blocks of its sort in the other two modes using identity tensors, each padded with the remaining mode's anchor. The resulting six-cycle equates all normalized covariant actions and makes all normalized dual actions inverse transposes. Here normalization divides each mode's block matrices by its anchor action, whose three-way product is one. For every relation slot of positive arity allocate its own module copy, link that copy to an opposite-variance core block of the same sort in another mode by a padded identity, and place the relation in its designated block triple. The non-anchor slot blocks of different relations are disjoint. The identity triples also differ since they specify both a covariant and a dual block. All constraints are thus respected exactly by the resulting partitioned tensor action. Conversely, module actions satisfying all constraints extend with anchors one.

There are constantly many blocks, since each marker list is encoded in one map and its label algebra. Remove the three mode partitions successively by the constant-part construction in Part V, Theorem 3.12. In its forward direction the extensions on the two other modes are block diagonal between data and auxiliary coordinates, as in lines 831–837. In the reverse direction those two extracted actions are the data diagonal blocks, as in lines 823–829. Thus previously imposed restrictions on the other modes can be carried through a step by appending one auxiliary block there and restricting back to the data blocks. All three uses have constant part counts. Skip any one-part partition and reorder the two unpartitioned modes as needed for the size convention. To justify the rank test over rings with zero divisors, put $r=n+1$, where $n$ is the smaller side of a data slice. A nonzero coefficient of the auxiliary pivot slice in an old output slice gives a nonzero scalar multiple of an identity of size at least $(2^L-1)r$ on an off-diagonal submatrix, where $L$ is the part count. Its inner rank by diagonal fullness exceeds the old output bound $2^{L-1}r+n$ for $L\geq2$. Once that coefficient vanishes, the active parts contribute exactly the sum of their weights $2^{i-1}r$ to auxiliary inner rank. The lower bound is a diagonal pivot submatrix and the upper bound is row support. The additional data rank is at most $n$. The weight intervals are disjoint and binary subset sums unique. Thus each old output slice uses just its original part. The pivot coefficient is now a unit by invertibility, and the pivot is invertible on its auxiliary block by the two unit off-diagonal identities. The hypothesis of diagonal fullness holds by Proposition 3.6 since $R$ is a local principal ideal ring. [Grochow, Qiao, Stange and Sun, Part V](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S3.SS3.SSS2)

Order all $N=\sum_i n_i$ coordinate positions, keeping their modes distinct. Standard pointwise-prefix individualization now finds the permutation of one transporter, or rejects: for each next position try candidate images while preserving all previously selected pairs. Each successful final list has compatible diagonal entries by the oracle semantics. Likewise compute generators of the image $P$ of the source stabilizer in $\prod_i S_{n_i}$. At each prefix level try each possible image of the next fixed-source coordinate, and for every success finish one witness permutation with that prefix. One such transversal at each stabilizer-chain level generates $P$. This uses only polynomially many marked queries and recovers no hidden scalings.

## 2. Separate the exact precisions and the two kinds of units

For a permutation already certified feasible, write the monomial action as diagonal factors on source positions followed by that permutation. Each nonzero entry $x$ has a valuation $v<a$, computable from the power-basis coefficients. Match it to the target entry $x'$ at its image position. The equality of the nonzero entries is equivalent to
$$
 \prod_{j\in e} z_j=(x'/p^v)/(x/p^v)\quad\text{in }R_{b_e}^{\times},\qquad b_e=a-v,
$$
where $e$ is the tuple of source coordinates, $R_b=(\mathbb Z/p^b)[X]/(f\bmod p^b)$, and $z_j\in R^\times$. Zeros impose no equations after feasibility. Division by $p^v$ here means dividing representatives and reducing to precision $b_e$. For the prescribed determinant values, include the signs of the permutations.

The unit group of every $R_b$ splits into its prime-to-$p$ part and $U_b=1+pR_b$. Write $q=p^\ell$. Reduction identifies the prime-to-$p$ part with $\mathbb F_q^\times$. The exponent of $U_b$ divides $p^{b-1}$, by binomial expansion on the filtration $1+p^jR_b$. Raising a unit to $p^{k\ell}$ with $k\ell\geq a-1$ gives its prime-to-$p$ part, since this exponent is one modulo $q-1$ and kills all the principal parts. The splitting respects every precision reduction. Split permutation signs and target determinants in the same way.

The principal parts have explicit additive presentations even when $p=2$. For each $1\le j<b$ and $0\le i<\ell$ let $g_{ji}=1+p^jX^i$. At depth $j$, read the power-basis coefficients of $(u-1)/p^j$ modulo $p$ and remove the corresponding product of powers of the $g_{ji}$. The remainder lies at depth $j+1$. This yields digits for every unit in $U_b$ with binary exponentiation and no enumeration through $p$. Express each $g_{ji}^p$ by its higher-depth digits and take these as the relations for $p[g_{ji}]$. The relations give a triangular integer matrix with all diagonal entries $p$. The presented abelian group maps onto $U_b$, and its order by the triangular determinant is $p^{\ell(b-1)}=|U_b|$. Hence these are exact presentations. Reduction maps are computed by digit reading on each reduced generator. Kernel, image, quotient and linear equations in these presentations use integer normal forms with transformations. [Kannan–Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

## 3. Evaluate determinant classes without extracting field roots

Let $B$ be the support incidence matrix with rows indexed by nonzero positions and columns by coordinates of all modes. Let $D$ be the mode incidence matrix with three rows, summing coordinates in each mode. On the principal parts use $B$ with the precision reduction in each row to define a homomorphism $B_U\colon U_a^N\to\prod_e U_{b_e}$. The determinant class lies in
$$
 Q_U=U_a^3/D(\ker B_U).
$$
For a feasible permutation solve the inhomogeneous system for one principal-part solution using the presentations. Apply $D$, insert the principal parts of the permutation signs, and reduce to $Q_U$. Changing the chosen solution leaves the class invariant.

For the residue parts put $M=q-1$ and formally identify the cyclic group $\mathbb F_q^\times$ with $\mathbb Z/M$. The identification is used in the proof alone. The quotient of interest is $Q_F=(\mathbb Z/M)^3/D(\ker B)$. Its dual is the set of $h\in(\mathbb Z/M)^3$ such that $D^\top h=B^\top w$ for some $w$. This follows by dualizing the map $B$, or directly by Smith reduction of $B$: the annihilator of $\ker B$ is the row module of $B$ modulo $M$. Compute generators of the module of pairs $(h,w)$ by integer normal form. These characters jointly separate all classes in $Q_F$ by the perfect duality of finite cyclic products. In field coordinates, given the residue ratios $r_e$ and the permutation signs $\sigma_i$, the coordinate for a pair is
$$
  \prod_i\sigma_i^{h_i}\prod_e r_e^{w_e}.
$$
The formula is valid because substituting any feasible scaling solution gives its determinant character. It also computes the target's coordinates by powering the target determinant entries with $h_i$. The case $M=1$ has trivial residue part.

## 4. Solve one determinant coset membership problem

The subgroup of diagonals in the source stabilizer has determinant image $D(\ker B_U)\times D(\ker B)$ under the unit splitting. Modulo this image, taking determinants of a lift defines a homomorphism $P\to Q_U\times Q_F$. Two lifts differ by a diagonal stabilizer, and the ordinary determinant is multiplicative. If $g_0$ is any transporter, all transporters are $g_0$ times the source stabilizer. Thus, after evaluating the class of $g_0$ by its permutation, the target class divided by it must belong to the subgroup generated by the classes of the computed generators of $P$. Membership also suffices: correct a chosen lift by a diagonal stabilizer with the requisite determinant ratio.

Every element of $P$ has order dividing $\Lambda=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\max_i n_i)$, whose known prime factors are at most $N$. The $Q_U$ condition is a linear congruence system in explicit normal-form coordinates. In the residue coordinates the equation asks whether given field units equal products of supplied field units of order dividing $\Lambda$, with the same exponents across all coordinates. Test that each target unit has order dividing $\Lambda$ by exponentiation. Isolate each prime-primary part by CRT powers modulo $\Lambda$. For each prime $s$ and coordinate select a supplied projected generator of largest order. Since the field unit group is cyclic, this generator spans the entire projected coordinate subgroup. Test the target order against that largest order, and if successful compute digits of its exponent and of each supplied unit in this base. For a base $b$ of order $s^h$, test digit $j$ of $\log_b z$ by raising $zb^{-c-ds^j}$ to $s^{h-1-j}$, where $c$ collects previous digits and $d$ ranges from zero to $s-1$. Each such test yields one linear congruence modulo $s^h$. The trivial coordinate only requires equality to one. Solve these congruences jointly with the principal-part congruences using integer normal forms. The number of tests, the bit lengths, the ring computations and the oracle queries are polynomial in the input. [Pohlig–Hellman](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1978.1055817)

The Rune idea from the early invariant notes suggested replacing a global coefficient modulus by the actual precision at each coordinate. The Sigil idea suggested keeping the exact coordinate orbit and quotienting scalings rather than choosing arbitrary representatives. Ward and Trellis suggested retaining each support incidence and using common exponents throughout the final test. Earlier determinant proofs required additional hypotheses for the permutation stage. Here the marked TI queries recover its full image, and the local-ring unit presentation treats arbitrary nonzero valuations.

## 5. Extension to explicit finite chain rings

The same result holds for any finite commutative local principal ideal ring supplied by additive invariant factors and multiplication structure constants with identity. This includes ramified finite chain rings. The compiler still applies. For the arithmetic, let $J$ be the maximal ideal and $p$ the residue characteristic. The characteristic is a power of $p$ since $p$ lies in the nilpotent ideal $J$. Recover $p$, if needed, by exact integer root tests on the additive order of the identity. Compute $J$ as the preimage of the kernel of a sufficiently high Frobenius power on $R/pR$. Indeed, the kernel of $x\mapsto x^{p^k}$ on this finite-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$-algebra equals its nilradical for $p^k\geq\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(R/pR)$. This is a linear map computed on a basis, and $pR$ is nilpotent. Successive powers of $J$ follow by additive spans of products of generators. Each strict step shrinks the order by a factor of at least two.

For a matched nonzero entry $x$ replace its precision ring by $R/\operatorname{ann}(x)$. The annihilator is the additive kernel of multiplication by $x$. Solve $xc=x'$ in additive coordinates to compute the exact ratio in this quotient. All solutions give the same class, and a solution is a unit there by permutation feasibility. Every such quotient has the same residue field $R/J$. In each required ring, including $R$, lift a basis of each layer $J^j/J^{j+1}$ over $\mathbb F_p$ to a list $t_{ji}$. Use $1+t_{ji}$ as the digit generators. Multiplication on the layer is addition, and the $p$-th power of each generator lies one layer deeper by binomial expansion and $p\in J$. The triangular presentation therefore has exactly the order of $1+J$, and digit reading gives the reduction maps as before. For the unit splitting raise to a sufficiently large integer power of $|R/J|$, which kills the principal parts and is one modulo $|R/J|-1$. Additive operations and the field arithmetic in $R/J$ use explicit quotients, with bit lengths polynomial in the input. Sections 3 and 4 now apply unchanged. [Hofmann, unit groups of finite rings, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00266)
This is an informal decision reduction in the displayed monomial actions. Ordinary TI on the right is an oracle, and the construction makes polynomially many queries. Recovering coordinate lines from arbitrary tensors, obtaining a single query, and finding actual determinant-one scalings are separate questions. Related monomial tensor algorithms use the supplied bases as well. [D’Alconzo, Monomial isomorphism for tensors](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-024-01375-0)
