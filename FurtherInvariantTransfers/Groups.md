# Parallel subgroup descent in arbitrary permutation-group quotients

Let $K\unlhd G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(n)$ be given by generating permutations, and write $Q=G/K$. All input and output subgroups of $Q$ are specified by generators of their full preimages. The following constructions are deterministic uniform $\mathsf{NC}$.

1. Given any $H\leq Q$, compute every term of $T_0=Q$, $T_{i+1}=\langle H^{T_i}\rangle$ through its first stable term. In particular, decide subnormality and compute the minimum subnormal defect when $H$ is subnormal.
2. Given $N\unlhd Q$ and any indexed list $B_0,\ldots,B_{m-1}\unlhd Q$, compute all terms of $T_0=N$, $T_{i+1}=[T_i,B_i]$ for $0\le i<m$. This includes the full relative lower central series of a normal subgroup in an arbitrary ambient quotient.
3. Given $N\unlhd Q$ and any explicit list of positive binary integers $e_0,\ldots,e_{m-1}$, compute all terms of $P_0=N$, $P_{i+1}=P_i^{e_i}[P_i,Q]$. Here $P_i^{e_i}$ means the subgroup generated by all $e_i$-th powers in $P_i$. With $N=Q$ and $e_i=p$ fixed, this computes the lower exponent-$p$ central series through its stable term by choosing the same index bound as in the first theorem.

The first theorem extends the solvable-only normal-closure tower algorithm in *Parallel normal cores and subnormality in permutation-group quotients*. The second extends the relative commutator algorithm in *Parallel computation of the lower central series*, which treated solvable ambient quotients and the fixed second argument $Q$. The older permutation-group literature includes algorithms for one normal closure, and sequential iteration appears in Seress. It does not provide the parallel bound used here for a chain of possibly polynomial length. [Babai–Luks–Seress, Theorems 1.1 and 7.1](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf) · [Luks–McKenzie](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(88)90044-X) · [Seress, §6.1.5](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511546549)

## Quotient operations and normal layers

Use the standard parallel permutation-group algorithms for constructive membership, normal closure, order and intersections of subgroups when one normalizes the other. These also apply in the quotient model by appending the denominator to subgroup generators. A constructive membership program computed on full preimages evaluates in the quotient after replacing denominator symbols with the identity. To lift a subgroup of an image through a homomorphism, lift its generators by constructive membership and append generators of the kernel. [Babai–Luks–Seress, Theorems 1.1 and 7.1](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf) · [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf)

We use our parallel quotient normal-centralizer theorem from *Parallel normal centralizers and socles in quotient permutation groups*. It applies to $C_X(V)$ whenever $V\unlhd X$ and $X$ is supplied in the quotient representation. In this proof the argument $V$ is always a normal product of nonabelian simple factors. The appendix gives the needed marked conjugation action for this case.

The permutation-group construction of Babai–Luks–Seress and Kantor–Luks–Mark supplies a normal series of polylogarithmic length with semisimple layers and generators for their nonabelian simple factors. Take its images in $Q$ and discard trivial layers. A normal image of a direct product of nonabelian simple groups is obtained by deleting factors, and a mixed semisimple layer can be split into its abelian and nonabelian parts. Abelian layers may be refined by simultaneous powers with exponent the product of the primes at most $n$, so that each is a product of elementary abelian primary components. The exponent of the $p$-primary part of any permutation-group section divides the largest $p$-power at most $n$. Hence this abelian refinement increases the length by at most a logarithmic factor. The resulting series and its factor data are computed in parallel. [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Lemma 3.14 and its proof](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf#page=20)

## Lifting through a centerless semisimple layer

Suppose $A=\prod_\alpha S_\alpha\unlhd X$ is the bottom layer being added, with all $S_\alpha$ nonabelian simple. Let $\pi:X\to X/A$. We have already computed the generators of every image $\overline T_i=\pi(T_i)$. Conjugation by $X$ permutes the factor labels, and the action factors through $X/A$, since $A$ preserves each factor. On a supplied list of quotient representatives the permutation action is explicit. Select any nonidentity image of a supplied generator of one factor and test its conjugate for membership in each listed factor. Exactly one test succeeds.

For the normal-closure tower, set $U_i=T_i\cap A$. We have $U_0=A$ and $U_{i+1}\unlhd U_i$, since $T_{i+1}\unlhd T_i$. Every normal subgroup of a direct product of nonabelian simple groups is a product of factors, so induction gives that each $U_i$ is such a product. Moreover, $T_i$ centralizes every factor outside $U_i$. Indeed, if $S_\alpha$ was removed at step $j\leq i$, then $[T_j,S_\alpha]\leq U_j$ by normality of $T_j$ in $T_{j-1}$. For $x\in T_j$, the component of $[x,s]$ in $S_\alpha$, as $s$ ranges over $S_\alpha$, rules out $x$ moving $S_\alpha$ to a different factor. When $x$ preserves $S_\alpha$, the commutator lies in $S_\alpha\cap U_j=1$. Thus $T_j$ centralizes $S_\alpha$, and nesting gives the assertion for $T_i$. Conversely $T_i$ cannot centralize a factor contained in $U_i$, because that factor is nonabelian and itself lies in $T_i$.

Mark a factor precisely when $H$ does not centralize it. One can obtain the marks by pairwise commutation tests on the given generators of $H$ and the factor. For $i\geq 1$ the factor belongs to $U_i$ exactly when its $\overline T_{i-1}$-orbit contains a marked factor. This follows because the generators of $T_i=\langle H^{T_{i-1}}\rangle$ centralize the factor exactly when $H$ centralizes all its $T_{i-1}$-conjugates. Conjugation by $A$ leaves each factor label in place, so the orbit is indeed computed from $\overline T_{i-1}$. These indexed orbit computations all run independently.

For the commutator tower let $U_i=T_i\cap A$ and $R_i=B_i\cap A$. These are products of factors by normality in $X$. Then
$$
 U_{i+1}=U_i\cap R_i .
$$
The inclusion into the intersection follows because $[T_i,B_i]\leq T_i\cap B_i$. For the reverse inclusion each common simple factor $S_\alpha$ is perfect, so $S_\alpha=[S_\alpha,S_\alpha]\leq[T_i,B_i]$. Therefore every $U_i$ is given by parallel prefix intersections of the factor sets in $U_0,R_0,\ldots,R_{i-1}$. These initial sets are obtained by testing inclusion of a factor generator list into $N$ or the given $B_j$. Also $T_i$ centralizes every factor outside $U_i$, since $[T_i,S_\alpha]\leq U_i$ by normality and the same component argument above applies.

In either tower let $V_i$ be the product of the factors outside $U_i$ and set $X_i=\pi^{-1}(\overline T_i)$. We have the identity
$$
 T_i=C_{X_i}(V_i).
$$
Indeed $T_i$ centralizes $V_i$. Also $X_i=T_iA$, since $T_i$ maps onto $\overline T_i$. Consequently $V_i\unlhd X_i$, and $C_A(V_i)=U_i$ by the direct product description and the trivial centers of the individual factors. An element of $C_{X_i}(V_i)$ can be written $ta$, with $t\in T_i$ and $a\in A$. Then $a\in U_i\leq T_i$, proving equality. Thus the quotient normal-centralizer routine recovers all generator lists from the indexed factors, without evaluating formal words over this semisimple extension.

## Recovering word witnesses with all gate values

The abelian lift below needs a single polynomial-size formal group program for all terms of the old tower, together with the old value of **every gate** of this program. After an arbitrary layer has been solved, both can be recovered from the now-known actual generator lists. This is what allows the order of abelian and nonabelian layers to be arbitrary.

For the normal-closure tower, take a free group on the ambient input symbols and the supplied $H$ symbols. The corresponding formal terms are obtained by iterated normal closure of the subgroup generated by the $H$ symbols. For each $i+1$, independently, form the subgroup of $X\times X$ generated by $(t,t)$ for generators $t$ of the actual $T_i$, and $(h,1)$ for input generators $h$ of $H$. This is the set of pairs $(xy,y)$ with $x\in T_{i+1}$ and $y\in T_i$. For every known generator $u$ of $T_{i+1}$, obtain a constructive membership program for $(u,1)$, and output the first coordinate multiplied by the inverse of the second. Formally substitute the corresponding previous generator output in place of each $t$ symbol. The result belongs to the formal $(i+1)$-st term, by the same diagonal identity in the free group. Take the zeroth generator list to be the ambient inputs. Include the $H$ symbols in every generator list.

For the commutator tower, use dedicated symbols for the ambient, $N$, and each $B_i$ generator list. Start from the formal normal closure of the $N$ symbols and take commutators with the corresponding formal normal closures of the $B_i$ symbols. These project onto the actual terms. For the local witness at $i+1$, use ambient diagonal generators in $X\times X$, and use $([t,b],1)$ for $t$ a known generator of $T_i$, $b$ an input generator of $B_i$. These commutators normally generate $[T_i,B_i]$ in $X$. Indeed, after killing them, the images of the generating lists commute and hence the images of the two subgroups commute. Recover each generator by paired membership and a coordinate difference as before. The formal difference belongs to the formal commutator term. The zeroth generator list here is the supplied $N$ list.

Each local membership program has polynomial size and polylogarithmic depth. Expand its integer-power gates into multiplication and inversion by repeated squaring. The membership exponents have polynomial bit length, so this expansion keeps local depth polylogarithmic. The separate input power operators below can have arbitrary binary length and are handled by computing their old gate values directly. Its actual input permutations or quotient representatives are known already. Evaluate **every gate** of each program independently on those actual local inputs, in parallel across tower indices and output generators. Then splice the formal programs by referring to previous output indices. Keep every locally evaluated value with its gate. The same output index may be referenced many times, so the globally spliced program has polynomial size. Its global depth may be polynomial, but no evaluation of that deep program in a nonsolvable group takes place. Quotient denominator symbols in local membership programs are evaluated as identity and omitted from the formal words.

## Lifting through an abelian layer

Suppose the next layer $A\unlhd X$ is squarefree abelian. Lift the spliced formal program of the old quotient simultaneously. Given a representative $r_g$ for the old value of gate $g$, write its new value as $a_gr_g$, $a_g\in A$. For a multiplication gate $g=(h,k)$,
$$
 a_g=a_h+\rho(r_h)a_k+(r_hr_kr_g^{-1}),
$$
where the final term is interpreted in $A$ and $\rho$ is conjugation on $A$. For an inverse gate $g=h^{-1}$ the equation is $a_g=-\rho(r_h^{-1})a_h+(r_h^{-1}r_g^{-1})$, again interpreting the final term in $A$. In each primary component the resulting system is triangular with identity diagonal. Solve it in parallel by finite-field linear algebra. This step only uses abelian corrections and the supplied old values, so it applies whether or not $X/A$ is solvable. [Kantor–Luks–Mark, Lemma 3.6 and Proposition 3.7](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/klmFINAL.pdf#page=12) · [Mulmuley, parallel rank](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579205)

The correction coordinates are computable directly from the quotient permutation generators. In an elementary abelian primary component, keep a supplied generator when simultaneous prefix membership shows it lies outside the span of its predecessors. For any input element and each basis direction, try all $p\leq n$ coefficients and test whether removing that power leaves an element in the subgroup generated by the other directions. This computes the coordinate because the representation in the basis is unique. Use products of powers for the inverse conversion. The primary projections of the squarefree layer are obtained by Chinese remainder exponents from its order. The prescribed input gates contribute their stated values and the identity gates their fixed identity values.

Let $S_i$ be the resulting lifted $i$-th generator list, $P_i=\langle S_i\rangle$, and $U_i=T_i\cap A$. The formal word invariant ensures $S_i\subseteq T_i$. The image of the list generates $\overline T_i$, so $T_i=P_iU_i$. The starting subspace $U_0$ is $A$ in the normal-closure tower and $N\cap A$ in the commutator tower. Work independently over each primary field.

For the normal-closure tower define $L_i=\langle H^{P_i}\rangle$ and $D_i=L_i\cap A$. Let $W_i$ be the linear span of the conjugation operators of $P_i$ on $A$, and let $M_i$ be the span of the products $W(\rho(h)-I)$, with $W\in W_i$ and $h$ an input generator of $H$. The list $S_i$ contains all those $h$. The product $M_iU_i$ is normalized by $P_i$, and is centralized by $U_i$ because $A$ is abelian. Its generators lie in $T_{i+1}$ by the normal-closure definition and the formula for commutation with elements of $A$. Modulo $M_iU_i$, the image of $U_i$ centralizes $H$, and hence centralizes every $P_i$-conjugate of $H$ because $U_i\unlhd T_i$. Thus $L_i(M_iU_i)$ is normal in $T_i$ and contains $H$. It follows that
$$
 T_{i+1}=L_i(M_iU_i),\qquad U_{i+1}=D_i+M_iU_i .
$$
Compute $W_i$ by doubling the word-length spans of the generator matrices and their inverses, with identity included. Spans stabilize in at most the matrix-space dimension many strict steps, so polynomial word lengths and logarithmically many doublings suffice.

For the commutator tower let $L_i$ be the ambient normal closure of all $[s,b]$ with $s\in S_i$ and $b$ a given generator of $B_i$. Put $D_i=L_i\cap A$ and let $M_i$ be the span of $\rho(b)-I$ for these $b$. Because $U_i$ is ambient-normal, $[U_i,B_i]=M_iU_i$ by the usual product and inverse identities for $\rho(b)-I$ acting on an invariant subgroup. Killing $L_i[U_i,B_i]$ makes every generator of $T_i=\langle S_i,U_i\rangle$ commute with every generator of $B_i$, so the same two displayed identities hold.

In both cases the entire affine recurrence is evaluated by expanding
$$
 U_i=M_{i-1}\cdots M_0U_0+
 \sum_{0\leq j<i}M_{i-1}\cdots M_{j+1}D_j.
$$
An empty operator product is the identity. Compute the exact span of every interval product by balanced pairwise matrix multiplication and rank-based basis selection. All $D_j$ and $M_j$ are available before this calculation, and all intervals and output indices are processed in parallel. The generator list for $T_i$ is $S_i$ joined with the coordinate lifts of a primary basis of $U_i$. Regenerate and evaluate all local witness programs directly from these lists whenever another layer follows.

## Adding the power operators

For the lower exponent-central series every $P_i$ is ambient-normal. In a centerless semisimple layer $A$, its intersection $U_i=P_i\cap A$ is a product of factors. The set of these factors is constant, since $U_{i+1}\leq U_i$, while each simple factor of $U_i$ is perfect and belongs to $[P_i,Q]$. Thus its semisimple lift is exactly the centralizer construction above with this constant retained factor set.

In an elementary abelian primary component of an abelian layer, lift $P_i$ using the same formal list $S_i$ and unknown $U_i$. Take $L_i$ to be the ambient normal closure of $[s,g]$ and $s^{e_i}$, for $s\in S_i$ and all ambient generators $g$, and put $D_i=L_i\cap A$. The operator space $M_i$ is now spanned by the conjugation differences $\rho(g)-I$ and the scalar operator $e_i I$. Indeed, upon killing $L_i(M_i U_i)$, all generators of $P_i$ become central and have trivial $e_i$-th power. Therefore every $e_i$-th power in $P_i$ becomes trivial, because the image of $P_i$ is abelian. The generators of $L_i(M_i U_i)$ lie in $P_i^{e_i}[P_i,Q]$ by construction. Hence the same formula $U_{i+1}=D_i+M_iU_i$ applies. Products and inverse identities for the action matrices justify this argument even when the abelian primary characteristic divides $e_i$.

For the local witness programs, use ambient diagonals and the pairs $([t,g],1)$, $(t^{e_i},1)$ on the known $P_i$ generators $t$. Their normally generated first-coordinate subgroup is $P_{i+1}$, by the same centrality and power calculation. Formal witness terms are defined by the corresponding power subgroup and commutator in the free group, so both kinds of pair preserve the witness invariant.

Permutation powers with a binary exponent of arbitrary input length are in uniform $\mathsf{NC}$. For each point, determine its cycle length, at most $n$, by testing the first $n$ powers in parallel. Compute the remainder of the binary exponent modulo that length by a balanced tree of modular sums of its bit contributions, with $2^j$ reduced modulo the length by binary powering on $O(\log n)$-bit residues. The required output is the power indexed by that remainder on the point. This evaluates quotient representatives as well. When storing a formal repeated-squaring power program, each intermediate multiplication gate computes a power with an explicitly known exponent, of bit length at most the input exponent length plus one. Compute every such old gate value in parallel by the same permutation power procedure. Its formal dependence on previous generator outputs is retained for the later abelian correction. This preserves both polynomial size and polylogarithmic depth when an exponent has more than polylogarithmically many bits.

## Size, scope and provenance

For the normal-closure tower use $2+n^2$ indices, enough because each strict subgroup descent at least halves the order and an equality of successive terms is permanent. Compare subgroup orders and return the list through its first stable term. Any subnormal chain from $Q$ to $H$ contains $T_i$ at its $i$-th position, by induction using the universal property of normal closure. Thus its length is at least the first index for which $T_i=H$. Conversely the computed tower itself is a subnormal chain if it reaches $H$. For the other two theorems, $m$ is part of the explicit input. All subgroup lists may be compressed with parallel prefix membership, and product permutation domains have polynomial degree. At each layer the programs have polynomial total size and all operations use polylogarithmic depth. There are polylogarithmically many layers. Include $K$ in every final full preimage.

The retained indexing in the Ward and Trellis design experiments suggested the use of the ordered table $(i,\alpha)$ of factor labels at each descent index. Here it is an analogy and a design choice. The actual theorem uses the exact identities on those labels, the normal layer structure, and the deliberate retention of the old value of every local witness gate. The semisimple factor masks and the local gate reconstruction are the added arguments beyond the earlier solvable proof. The literature on the lower exponent-central series uses it in sequential quotient algorithms. [GAP ANUPQ, Chapter 2](https://gap-packages.github.io/anupq/doc/chap2.html)

The descent algorithms are informal. They compute the stated descending subgroup chains. The intermediate-generator assertion of Babai–Luks–Seress §10, Problem 4 remains open here. [Babai–Luks–Seress, §10](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/NC.pdf#page=11)

## Appendix. The needed semisimple centralizer algorithm

The following is the marked-action argument from our previous quotient centralizer proof, included here as a dependency. Apply it with the full preimage of the current ambient group and the selected product of simple factors. The faithful representations of the factors can be inherited from the full semisimple layer.

Let $A=M/L$ be a direct product of nonabelian simple groups, where $M,L$ are normal in an explicitly given permutation group $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(n)$. Assume the simple factor preimages and their faithful permutation representations of degree polynomial in the input length are given, as they are for each nonabelian layer of the good series. We construct in $\mathsf{NC}$ a polynomial-degree permutation action of $G$ with exactly the kernel of conjugation on $A$.

For each simple factor $S$ with a supplied faithful representation of degree $b$, take marks as follows. If $|S|\leq b$, take each individual element, represented by a lift modulo $L$. List the represented group by repeated pairwise products with deduplication, which takes $O(\log b)$ iterations for a group of order at most $b$, and obtain a preimage of each element by LIFT. Otherwise choose a moved point and let $T<S$ be its point stabilizer. Its index is in $[2,b]$, so $T$ is proper and nontrivial. As a mark use its preimage over $L$, computed by lifting a generating list for the represented point stabilizer and including the generators of $L$. Conjugation can send individual factor marks to only isomorphic factors. The full automorphism orbit of $T$ inside one factor has size at most $[S:N_S(T)]|\operatorname{Out}(S)|\leq 2b\log_2|S|$, by the outer-order bound and the size of the inner-conjugacy class. There are at most $\log_2(n!)$ factors, so all marked $G$-orbits have polynomial size. [Kohl, Theorem 1](https://stefan-kohl.github.io/preprints/outbound.pdf)

To enumerate the orbit of any mark, its generated Schreier graph admits paired ports for each generator of $G$ and its inverse. The reverse port depends only on the original port, including at repeated edges and loops. Reingold's theorem for consistently labeled graphs constructs a universal traversal word in logspace for each given graph size and port pairing. Use the words for all sizes from one through the known polynomial orbit-size bound. In parallel evaluate their prefixes as permutation products and conjugate the mark. The correct size's prefix list visits every orbit point. Each candidate belongs to the orbit regardless of size. Deduplicate cosets by membership of the representative difference in $L$, and subgroups by testing mutual membership of all supplied generators. The action of any input permutation on this list is another conjugation and matching operation. The words have polynomial length and can be wired by a logspace uniformity machine, and the products have balanced trees. The disjoint union of all the resulting orbit actions is a polynomial-degree permutation representation. [Reingold, Theorem 5.4](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/sl.pdf#page=15)

Any automorphism fixing every mark preserves each simple factor, since the factors have trivial mutual intersection and each has nontrivial marks. Its action is trivial on a factor marked by all elements. For a factor marked by a nontrivial proper $T$, the action of $S$ on the inner-conjugates of $T$ is faithful, since its kernel is normal and its action is nontrivial by simplicity. If $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)$ fixes all those conjugates, that faithful action assigns the same permutation to $s$ and $\alpha(s)$, by compatibility of conjugation with $\alpha$. Thus $\alpha(s)=s$ for all $s$. This proves equality of the two kernels. Apply the ordinary parallel permutation-action kernel algorithm to compute their generators. The same statement holds with a trivial group of factors, using the trivial action.
