# A raw determinant test from two quadratic factors of one pencil

Fix $F=\mathbb F_3$ and constants $d,r$. Let $A,B$ be $n\times n\times n$ tensors with odd $n$, given in arbitrary bases. Express their first-mode slice maps as $M_A(x),M_B(x)$. Suppose $\det M_A(x)$ is nonzero and has at most $r$ distinct geometric factors, each of degree at most $d$ and definable over $F$. Allow arbitrary multiplicities. Suppose two distinct quadratic factors have symmetric matrices $S_1,S_2$, with $S_2$ invertible and $S_2^{-1}S_1$ of squarefree characteristic polynomial. There is a deterministic polynomial-time pair-level many-one reduction of SL-TI on this promise to ordinary 3-TI over $F$. Only the first input needs the promise. An isomorphic second input inherits it.

This result uses the determinant factors without generating paired slices. The factor dimensions may be as large as the full mode dimensions. The last section gives unbounded examples with scalar stabilizer and scalar pair adjoints.

## 1. Recover bounded-degree absolute factors without an essential-variable bound

We prove the following elementary algorithmic fact used here. Let $P(x)$ be a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree $n$ in $N$ variables, accessible by polynomial-time evaluation and known to have all distinct absolute factors of degree at most a fixed $d$, defined over the fixed base field $F$. Then all its normalized factors, their multiplicities and the overall scalar can be found deterministically in time polynomial in $n+N$. Evaluation over extensions of polynomial bit length is assumed. A determinant of a matrix pencil has this property.

**Deterministic restriction set.** For fixed $D$, choose positive integer weights $w_1,\ldots,w_k$ of size $k^{O(D^2)}$ such that monomials of total degree at most $D$ have distinct weights. One explicit choice is $w_i=\sum_{j=0}^{D}i^j b^j$ with $b>Dk^D$. Equality of weighted sums gives equality of the cardinality and the power sums of the index multisets by no-carry arithmetic. Newton identities over the rational numbers determine the multiset. Consequently a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $D$ remains nonzero under the substitution $z_i=t^{w_i}$. Take an extension $E_0/F$ of polynomial cardinality exceeding all degree bounds below. Such a field can be constructed by enumerating irreducibles of degree $O(\log(n+N))$. The curve evaluated at all $t\in E_0$ is a polynomial-size hitting set. For a product of up to polynomially many bounded-degree polynomials use the same substitution on every factor and choose the field larger than the sum of the resulting degree bounds. The argument works for coefficients in any extension of $E_0$.

**Find a good line.** Apply the curve to the $2N$ entries of $v,u$ and try each line $x=v+tu$, with a fresh parameter $t$. A good line has the following properties. The top homogeneous part of each distinct factor is nonzero at $u$, each restricted factor has distinct roots over the algebraic closure, and different factors have disjoint root sets. These properties are nonvanishing of polynomially many bounded-degree coefficient, discriminant and resultant polynomials in $u,v$. None vanishes identically. For discriminants, choose a direction with nonzero directional derivative of the factor and nonzero leading coefficient. An absolutely irreducible polynomial has a nonzero derivative in some direction because the algebraic closure is perfect. Its directional derivative has smaller degree, giving coprimality over the rational function field of offsets normal to the direction. For a pair of distinct factors, choose a direction where both leading coefficients are nonzero. In coordinates with that direction as one axis, the two polynomials are coprime over the rational function field of the other coordinates. These observations show the requisite univariate resultants are nonzero polynomials. Thus the curve contains a good line. Skip degree-dropping lines and choose the line with the maximum number of distinct roots. This finds a good line since the number is bounded by the sum of the degrees of the distinct factors, and equality implies precisely the stated properties.

Interpolate each line restriction from oracle evaluations. All its roots lie in a common extension $K/E_0$ of degree $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,d)$, since all underlying factors are defined over $E_0$ and have degree at most $d$. This field also has polynomial cardinality, so enumeration finds the roots and their multiplicities. Use compatible field representations by constructing $K$ over $E_0$ via a monic irreducible of the specified constant degree.

**Recover the local factors, including repeated roots.** Fix the good line $(u,v)$, complete $u$ to a basis by the columns of $C$, and write $P(v+tu+Cz)/P(u)$. It is monic of degree $n$ in $t$. For each root $a$ of the good restriction, let $e$ be its multiplicity. Use directional specializations $z=s\beta$, where $\beta$ ranges over a curve as above. The bivariate polynomial in $t,s$ is obtained by interpolation. Lift the coprime factorization with first factor $(t-a)^e$ modulo $s$ by monic Hensel lifting, to precision $s^{d3^{\nu_3(e)}+1}$. Each lift uses polynomially many operations in $K$ because the degree and precision are $O_d(n)$. For example, each new coefficient is found from the product equation by solving the Bézout equations with the two coprime initial factors, keeping the corrections of degrees below the factor degrees.

In the true factor supported at this root the lift is $(t-a_\beta(s))^e$, where $a_\beta(s)$ is the formal simple-root lift for the corresponding reduced factor. Put $e=3^j e'$ with $3\nmid e'$. The coefficient of $t^{e-3^j}$ is $-e'a_\beta(s)^{3^j}$. Taking the inverse Frobenius coefficientwise, at exponents divisible by $3^j$, recovers $a_\beta(s)$ through degree $d$.

For every nonempty subset $I$ of at most $d$ roots, multiply its factors $t-a_\beta(s)$ modulo $s^{d+1}$. The coefficient of $s^i$ is the evaluation at $\beta$ of a homogeneous polynomial of degree $i$ in $z$. This follows by substituting $z=s\beta$ in the unique multivariate simple-root lifts. Interpolate all these homogeneous polynomials using the weights with distinct monomial exponents. There are polynomially many roots, subsets and coefficients at fixed $d$. Restore $z$ to get a candidate $G_I(t,z)$, and discard candidates of total degree above $d$. The subset of the roots of any actual distinct factor recovers that factor exactly after making it monic in $t$, since its total degree is at most $d$.

**Certify without testing a high-degree multivariate remainder.** Convert candidates back to the original coordinates. Retain candidates whose univariate restriction divides the restriction of $P$ on every line of a deterministic curve test set, skipping zero restrictions of $P$. Actual factors pass. A candidate coprime to all true factors fails on a test line where its leading coefficient and all the bounded-degree pairwise resultants with the true factors are nonzero, and $P$ has full degree. Such a line is in the hitting set by the same coprimality and degree argument. A candidate sharing a true factor has that factor's good-line root subset inside its own subset. The latter follows by specializing the divisibility in the adapted coordinates at $z=0$, where both polynomials have their specified monic initial factors. Keep inclusion-minimal surviving root subsets. Each gives a true factor, because its subset contains one for a true factor, and equality of subsets gives equal $t$-degree and monicity, hence equality of the polynomials if one divides the other. Normalize the recovered factors over $F$. Read their multiplicities from any of their good-line roots and compute the overall scalar by evaluation at $u$. This proves the factor algorithm. It extends the bounded essential-variable strategy of the earlier MathProofs determinant-factor notes by using directional interpolation and bounded-degree resultant tests.

## 2. Read the exact character from the recovered factors

Write $P_X=c_X\prod_i Q_{X,i}^{a_i}$, where all $Q_{X,i}$ are normalized, for $X=A,B$. Keep the original tensor equation. In pencil conventions a transporter consists of $f,g,h$ satisfying
$
M_B(fx)=hM_A(x)g^{-1}.
$$
The target total determinant in $F^\times=\{1,-1\}$ is $\det f\,\det h/\det g$, since inversion coincides with identity on this group. It suffices to impose this total determinant. The central scalar triples of product one realize every triple of determinant signs whose product is one, because $n$ is odd.

Try all bijections of the factor lists preserving degrees and multiplicities, a constant number at fixed $r$. For each bijection, write its matched target factors as $Q_{B,i}$. Attach each factor line with its inclusion in the symmetric power of the first dual mode. Build the symmetric powers by multiplication maps from degree one, which determine the higher actions since the products span. This argument works when characteristic divides a degree. A branch transporter therefore satisfies $Q_{B,i}(fx)=\lambda_i Q_{A,i}(x)$, and the factor line has scalar $\lambda_i^{-1}=\lambda_i$ over $F$. Comparing determinant pencils gives
$
\det h/\det g=(c_B/c_A)\prod_i \lambda_i^{a_i}.
$$

Under this matching define $T_X=S_{X,2}^{-1}S_{X,1}$ on both sides, using the two selected matched quadratic factors. Their covariance gives $T_B f=\rho f T_A$, with $\rho=\lambda_1/\lambda_2$. Try both $\rho\in F^\times$, and attach this intertwining equation as a linear-map condition on the same first-mode action. Discard a branch if the second quadratic is singular or if $T_A,\rho^{-1}T_B$ have different characteristic polynomials. Let $E=F[t]/(\operatorname{charpol}(T_A))$. This algebra is étale of dimension $n$. Its action on each label mode is regular of rank one. For explicit module coordinates choose a nonzero vector in each primary component and sum them. The cyclic bases obtained from these vectors provide linear identifications $\psi_A,\psi_B$ from $E$ to the mode spaces. On each field factor these identifications use $T_A$ and $\rho^{-1}T_B$, respectively. The fixed intertwining equation makes the allowed mode map take the form
$
f=\psi_B m_b\psi_A^{-1},\qquad b\in E^\times,\qquad
\det f=(\det\psi_B/\det\psi_A)\operatorname{Norm}_{E/F}(b).
$$
Here $m_b$ is multiplication by $b$. Computing the primary components uses polynomial-time univariate factoring over the fixed field.

The norm is a polynomial-size typed linear structure tied to this particular mode map. On each field factor $K_0$ of $E$, attach its copy via $\psi_A,\psi_B$ and link its Frobenius-conjugate multiplier copies by the linear powers of Frobenius on $K_0$. Multiplication diagrams between these copies force the induced map on their product copy to be multiplication by $\prod_{\sigma}\sigma(b)=\operatorname{Norm}_{K_0/F}(b)$. A base-field line included there reads that scalar. These diagrams involve only linear and bilinear maps. Multiply these lines and the quadratic and other factor lines with their corresponding multiplicities. After the known calibrations $c_B/c_A$ and $\det\psi_B/\det\psi_A$, pin the resulting total line to impose total determinant one.

Every branch map projects to an actual transporter with the required character because the original tensor and all attachments use the same original map. Every determinant-one transporter matches the factors and picks one $\rho$, and induces all auxiliary maps in its branch. Fixed-signature packing and positive OR packing from the earlier MathProofs constructions now produce a single ordinary TI instance. The [tensor-system compiler](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4) applies because the attachments use order at most three, and a polynomial list of norm and graded records can be encoded with a fixed signature. The computations on the second tensor may return no factor data outside the promise. A genuinely isomorphic second tensor always satisfies the promise and is processed correctly. An ordinarily nonisomorphic tensor cannot pass an attached branch.

## 3. Unbounded scalar-stabilizer examples

Let $m\ge 3$, $n=2m+1$, and define the label mode as $V=E_0\oplus F$ for $E_0=F_{3^{2m}}$. Choose $\theta\in E_0$ of degree $2m$ and nonzero trace. Such a choice exists because the number of elements of nonzero trace is $2\cdot3^{2m-1}$, larger than the union bound $\sum_{j=1}^{m}3^j$ for proper subfields. Choose $a\in E_0^\times$ such that $\operatorname{Tr}(a\theta x^2)$ on $E_0$ is split. This is possible because multiplying the trace form parameter by a field element multiplies its Gram determinant by that element's norm, and both square classes occur. Choose any $c\in F^\times$, and set
$
 Q_1(x,t)=\operatorname{Tr}(a\theta x^2),\qquad
 Q_2(x,t)=\operatorname{Tr}(a x^2)+ct^2.
$$
Then $Q_1$ has rank $n-1$, $Q_2$ is nonsingular, and their operator ratio is multiplication by $\theta$ on $E_0$ and zero on $F$. It has squarefree characteristic polynomial and nonzero trace.

Choose an isotropic dual covector $\ell_2$ for $Q_2$ with both coordinates nonzero in $E_0^*\oplus F^*$. The nondegenerate even-dimensional dual trace form represents every element of $F$ since it contains a hyperbolic plane, so its value can cancel the nonzero value of the chosen $F$-coordinate. Complete $\ell_2$ to a hyperbolic pair and then a Witt basis. There is a rational $v_0=(x,t)$ with $Q_1(v_0)Q_2(v_0)\ell_2(v_0)\ne0$. Choose $x$ with $\ell_{2,E_0}(x)\ne0$ and $Q_1(x)\ne0$. The restriction of the nonzero homogeneous quadratic to the affine hyperplane $\ell_{2,E_0}=1$ is a nonzero polynomial of individual degrees below three and cannot vanish at every rational point. If $Q_2(x,0)\ne0$, take $t=0$. Otherwise both $t=\pm1$ give $Q_2(x,t)\ne0$, and at most one of these two choices makes $\ell_2(x,t)=0$. Choose a split isotropic dual covector $\ell_1$ of $Q_1|_{E_0}$ that is nonzero at $x$. Such a choice exists because isotropic dual covectors span a split space. Extend it by zero on the $F$-summand. Its hyperbolic pair and Witt basis give
$
 Q_1=a_1\ell_1-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}u_{1,i}v_{1,i},\qquad
 Q_2=a_2\ell_2-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}u_{2,i}v_{2,i}-\kappa z^2
$$
in labeled coordinates, for some $\kappa\ne0$. These identities use the standard classification of nondegenerate quadratic forms over odd finite fields.

Realize each expression by the determinant of the block
$
 M_i(x)=\begin{pmatrix}a_i & u_i^{T}\\ v_i & \ell_i I\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The blocks have sizes $m,m+1$. In the larger block the extra row and column coordinates are $z,\kappa z$. Put them on the diagonal of an upper block triangular pencil, with cross block $R(x)$ to be chosen and zero lower block. Its determinant is
$
\ell_1^{m-2}\ell_2^{m-1}Q_1Q_2.
$$
The quadratics of rank at least four are absolutely irreducible, and $\ell_1,\ell_2$ are distinct because $\ell_1$ vanishes on $F$ and $\ell_2$ does not. In particular this family has a determinant factor of full essential dimension.

We can make the simultaneous commutant of the normalized pencil scalar. Set $R(v_0)=0$ and normalize by $M(v_0)^{-1}$. The normalized diagonal blocks $N_1,N_2$ act irreducibly over the algebraic closure. To see this, let $L_i=M_i(v_0)^{-1}$. The arrowhead span contains $e_1 e_j^T,e_j e_1^T$ for all standard basis vectors, except that the last two off-diagonal directions of the second block appear as $e_1e_j^T+\kappa e_je_1^T$. A nonzero invariant subspace for the left-normalized span contains $L_i e_1$: apply a suitable $L_i e_1e_j^T$, or the combined last generator to a vector supported on its last coordinate. The first coordinate of $L_i e_1$ is nonzero since its inverse cofactor is a power of $\ell_i(v_0)$. Applying the other generators now produces every $L_i e_j$, including the last by subtraction of a multiple of $L_i e_1$. This proves irreducibility. The modules have distinct dimensions and scalar endomorphism rings.

The extension of the two diagonal modules is split precisely when the normalized cross block has the form $H N_2(x)-N_1(x)H$ for a fixed rectangular matrix $H$. This follows by writing a complementary invariant subspace as a graph. The space of possible cross blocks normalized to vanish at $v_0$ has dimension $(n-1)m(m+1)$. The space of the displayed coboundaries has dimension at most $m(m+1)$. Choose a cross block outside it by Gaussian elimination. The resulting nonsplit extension of distinct simple modules has scalar endomorphisms. The lower-dimensional submodule is unique, as any other simple submodule would split the extension. An endomorphism induces a scalar on each simple factor, the two scalars must coincide by the nonsplit condition, and the remaining off-diagonal map is an intertwiner between distinct simple modules and vanishes. Thus the pair adjoint on the matrix modes is scalar.

An ordinary rational autotopism must send each quadratic factor to a scalar multiple of itself because their ranks differ. It must fix each linear factor line because their multiplicities differ. If $f$ is the label-mode map, its conjugation on the operator ratio scales it by $\rho=\lambda_1/\lambda_2$. The nonzero trace forces $\rho=1$. Hence $f$ is multiplication on $E_0\oplus F$, and each multiplication map is self-adjoint for $Q_2$. The equation $f^TS_2 f=\lambda_2 S_2$ says $f^2=\lambda_2 I$. On the $F$-summand $\lambda_2=1$, so there are two signs on the two summands. Preservation of the line of $\ell_2$, with both components nonzero, forces the signs to agree. Thus $f$ is scalar. The matrix-mode maps are then scalar as well, since the matrix-mode commutant is scalar. Conciseness holds since the second block includes all $n$ label coordinates and $M(v_0)$ is invertible.

For the two remaining pair-adjoint algebras, an element has a label-mode component $Y$ with $M(Yx)=M(x)Z$ or $M(Yx)=Z M(x)$ for a matrix $Z$. Over $F(s)$, $I+sY$ therefore preserves the determinant polynomial up to scalar, and preserves each quadratic factor line by rank and irreducibility. The same trace argument makes $I+sY$ commute with the operator ratio. It acts as $1+sb$ on $E_0$ and $1+su$ on $F$, for some $b\in E_0,u\in F$. The second quadratic identity gives $(1+sb)^2=(1+su)^2$; comparing linear coefficients gives $b=u$. Thus $Y$ and $Z$ are scalar. This certifies scalar pair adjoints without an enumeration of automorphisms.

Any negative-total-sign twist of one of these examples is ordinarily isomorphic and fails the special-linear test. The construction resolves this explicit unbounded part of the scalar-adjoint benchmark. General scalar-adjoint tensors can have determinant factors of unbounded degree or no usable quadratic pair, so universal coverage remains open. The proofs here are informal and publication priority is unestablished.


## 4. Arbitrarily many factors and a fixed extension of definition

The same raw theorem holds with arbitrarily many distinct factors, provided all geometric factors have bounded degree and are definable over a single fixed extension $K/F$. The distinguished quadratic pair and its squarefree ratio can be over $K$. This includes the nonsplit case of a constant bound $r$ on the number of factors, because Frobenius permutes the factors and fixes their monically normalized coefficients over $\mathbb F_{3^{\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,r)}}$.

Run the factor extraction above over $K$. Its counting and curve bounds depend on the number of factors only through the total degree. Its common splitting extension for the line restrictions still has fixed relative degree because each factor has bounded degree and is defined over $K$. Enumerate only the matches of the two chosen quadratics. There are at most $n^2$ ordered choices in the target. Enumerate $\rho,\lambda\in K^\times$, where an allowed map $f_K$ satisfies
$
T_B f_K=\rho f_K T_A,\qquad
f_K^{\mathsf T}S_{B,2}f_K=\lambda S_{A,2}.
$$
These are necessary for a genuine transporter in its matching branch. They are directly imposed on the attached systems.

As before identify the two label spaces over $K$ with $E=K[t]/(\operatorname{charpol}(T_A))$, with actions $T_A,\rho^{-1}T_B$. Construct compatible cyclic bases. The second form on either source or target is $B_X(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}(a_Xxy)$ in these bases for a unique $a_X\in E^\times$. Indeed, the respective operator is self-adjoint for its second quadratic, so every multiplication operator from $E$ is self-adjoint. Hence $B_X(x,y)=B_X(xy,1)$, and perfectness of the étale trace pairing gives $a_X$. Nondegeneracy makes it a unit. Solve
$
b^2=\lambda a_A/a_B
$$
in the simple field factors of $E$, or reject the branch. This step is deterministic polynomial time. For example, on a field $U$ and nonzero right-hand side $v$, form $C=U[z]/(z^2-v)$. Compute the kernel of the $\mathbb F_3$-linear map $x\mapsto x^3-x$ in $C$. Its dimension is one when $C$ is a field and two when $C=U\times U$. In the latter case a fixed element outside the base scalar line has unequal coordinates, and the polynomial $1-(x-j)^2$ for a suitable $j\in\mathbb F_3$ gives a primitive idempotent $e$. Linear algebra in the image $eC=eU$ recovers the corresponding root from $ez$.

Choose one such $b$, and let $f_0$ be the resulting $K$-linear map between the two label spaces. Every allowed map now has the form $f_K=f_0 m_s$ in the source coordinates, with $m_s$ acting on each field block $E_j$ as a sign $s_j\in\{1,-1\}$. This follows by dividing the quadratic equations by the one for $b$, since the roots of $s_j^2=1$ in a field of odd characteristic are exactly the two signs. An admissible $f_K$ coming from a base-field transporter is subject to the same description, even when $f_0$ itself is defined only over $K$.

Choose a basis of the source label space consisting of consecutive bases of the $E_j$ in their cyclic coordinates. Compute the leading monomial and its nonzero coefficient for $P_A(x)=\det M_A(x)$ and $P_B(f_0x)=\det M_B(f_0x)$ in these source coordinates, using any fixed monomial order. Transform their recovered factors by the relevant linear changes of variables. Expanding a single factor costs polynomial time because its degree is bounded. The leading monomial of a product is the product of the leading monomials, with the product of their coefficients. Thus this computation never expands the full determinant polynomial.

If the leading exponents disagree, reject the branch. For an allowed transporter the identity $P_B(f_Kx)=\mu P_A(x)$ holds with $\mu=\det h/\det g$ in the base field. Changing $f_0$ to $f_0m_s$ rescales coefficients and never changes support, so a mismatch would be impossible. Otherwise let $p_A,p_B$ be the two leading coefficients, and let $\alpha_j$ be the sum of the common leading monomial's exponents in block $E_j$. Then
$
\mu=(p_B/p_A)\prod_j s_j^{\alpha_j},
\qquad
\det f_K=\det f_0\prod_j s_j^{\dim_K E_j}.
$$
All raw determinant coordinates for base-field transporters embed unchanged in $K$, so the exact product-one condition is
$
(\det f_0)(p_B/p_A)
\prod_j s_j^{\dim_K E_j+\alpha_j}=1\quad\text{in }K.
$$

The dimensions of the $E_j$ do not affect the line encoding. Choose a nonzero vector in each source block, include its $K$-line in the extended label space, and use the line spanned by its $f_0$-image in the target. The induced map on that line is precisely multiplication by $s_j$, using the chosen representative vectors on the two sides. The known exponent mod two tells whether to use this line in the product. Multiply the chosen $K$-lines by bilinear maps and impose the scalar equation above.

All auxiliary sorts are encoded over $F$ by restriction of scalars. Keep $K$ pointwise pinned through a named $F$-basis and its multiplication table. The fixed scalar extension map $K\times V\to K\otimes_F V$ ties the extended action to the actual base-field transporter, and multiplication of $K$ with each $K$-module sort makes all needed auxiliary maps $K$-linear. The explicit norm factors of Section 2 and the additional factor-line matching are unnecessary in this stronger construction. Its branch constraints, line conditions and product use linear and bilinear maps, so fixed-signature and OR packing again give one ordinary TI instance over $F$.

This removes the permutation assembly for the complete determinant factor list. Two matched quadratics reduce the unknown label map to independent field-block signs, and one leading monomial suffices to read the shared determinant factor of the two matrix modes. Large essential-variable spaces, repeated factors, and unbounded numbers of geometric factors are permitted when their degrees and common definition-field degree are bounded. General scalar-adjoint pencils can fall outside these hypotheses.
