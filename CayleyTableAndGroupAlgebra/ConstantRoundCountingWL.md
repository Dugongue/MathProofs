# Constant-round counting Weisfeiler–Leman identifies products of bounded-generator direct factors

Let $d\geq 1$ be fixed. Write $\mathcal D_d$ for the class of finite groups that admit a fully refined internal direct product decomposition with every factor generated by at most $d$ elements. Counting Version II Weisfeiler–Leman of dimension bounded in $d$ identifies every $G\in\mathcal D_d$ in a number of rounds bounded in $d$, against every finite comparison group. This answers Question 7.2 affirmatively. [Johnson, Levet, Vojtěchovský, and Widholm, *On the Parallel Complexity of Identifying Groups and Quasigroups via Decompositions*, §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06478v3#S7).

## Version II formulas

Use first-order formulas with unary counting quantifiers and with the following atomic tests on currently pebbled elements: equalities of fixed group words and the marked isomorphism type of a subgroup generated by an ordered tuple. Version II initial colors distinguish marked generated subgroups. Thus they determine every fixed-word test, including arbitrarily large fixed powers. Counting Version II with a bounded number of pebbles and rounds preserves the truth of formulas of bounded variable width and quantifier rank in this expanded language. To see this in the bijective pebble game, a new pebble and Duplicator's bijection match the truth of the subformula on all one-element extensions by induction on quantifier rank. The bijection therefore preserves the number of extensions satisfying that subformula. Boolean operations preserve equivalence without using rounds. Extra pebbles can be added for the variable-width convention in the chosen definition of WL. [Johnson et al., §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06478v3#S2).

For comparison groups of size at most $N$, the predicate $x\in\langle\bar a\rangle$ is a quantifier-free disjunction over words of length at most $N$ in $\bar a$ and their inverses. Every element of the generated subgroup has a word of that length by shortest paths in its Cayley graph. The marked type of a fixed group $T$ with a specified $d$-tuple of generators is a test on the $d$-tuple alone. Explicitly, choose one word for every element of $T$, require these words to take distinct values, require their values to satisfy the multiplication table of $T$, and compare the values representing the marked generators with the supplied tuple. The image of the table then contains the supplied generators and consists entirely of words in them. Width and rank are uniform in $N$ even though formula length may depend on the comparison.

In the rest of the proof $G\in\mathcal D_d$ is fixed, $H$ is the comparison group, and $N=\max(|G|,|H|)$. Each test is used on a group $X$ of size at most $N$. Formulas may depend on $G$ and $N$, and their variable width and quantifier rank will depend only on $d$.

## Uniform derived-subgroup tests

Nikolov and Segal proved that for each $d$ there is $c=c(d)\geq 1$ such that every element of the derived subgroup of every finite $d$-generated group is a product of $c$ commutators. Use Theorem 1.7 with its normal subgroup equal to the ambient group. The identity is a commutator, so padding allows exactly $c$ commutators. [Nikolov and Segal, *On finitely generated profinite groups, I: strong completeness and uniform bounds*, Theorem 1.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0604399#page=5).

Given a subgroup $Y$ of $X$ with a bounded-width and bounded-rank membership test, let $R_Y(z)$ say that $z$ is a product of $c$ commutators of elements of $Y$. The equality with that word is atomic, and the $2c$ factors are quantified. Add the guard that $R_Y$ is closed under multiplication. The set defined by $R_Y$ lies in $Y'$, contains the identity and every commutator of $Y$, and is a subgroup when the guard holds, since $Y$ is finite. The guard therefore makes $R_Y(z)$ equivalent to $z\in Y'$.

For a marked tuple $\bar a$ of length $d$, write $P=\langle\bar a\rangle$ and $C=C_X(P)$. Membership in $C$ means commuting with every entry of $\bar a$. Say that the tuple satisfies the centralizer-product condition when $X=PC$. This asserts that each element of $X$ is a product of one element of each subgroup, using bounded width and rank. Include the guard for $R_X$, and for every marked $d$-tuple satisfying the centralizer-product condition include the guard for $R_C$. Quantifying over those tuples adds only $d$ to the quantifier rank.

All guards hold in $G$. Indeed, if $G=\prod_i Q_i$ is the prescribed decomposition, a coordinatewise product of $c$ commutators realizes every element of $G'$. For a tuple with $G=PC_G(P)$, let $P_i$ be its projection to $Q_i$ and let $C_i=C_{Q_i}(P_i)$. Then $C_G(P)=\prod_i C_i$ and $Q_i=P_iC_i$. The commuting product makes $P_i$ normal in $Q_i$, and
$$
D_i=P_i\cap C_i=Z(P_i)\leq Z(C_i),\qquad C_i/D_i\simeq Q_i/P_i.
$$
The last quotient is generated by at most $d$ elements. Choose at most $d$ lifts in $C_i$, and let $L_i$ be their generated subgroup. Then $C_i=L_iD_i$ with $D_i$ central, so $C_i'=L_i'$. Nikolov–Segal applies to $L_i$. Taking the product over coordinates proves the guard for $C_G(P)$ for each applicable tuple.

## Recognizing direct factors and counting central summands

For a finite abelian group $B$, written additively for this paragraph, set
$$
W_{p,k}(B)=B[p]\cap p^{\,k-1}B,\qquad
U_{p,k}(B)=W_{p,k}(B)/W_{p,k+1}(B).
$$
A homomorphism $f:A\to B$ embeds $A$ as a direct summand if and only if every induced $\mathbb F_p$-linear map $U_{p,k}(f)$ is injective. A split injection has injective maps on these grades. Conversely, choose cyclic power-of-$p$ generators of $A$ for each prime and exponent. The order-$p$ elements at the bottom of the order-$p^k$ cycles give a basis of $U_{p,k}(A)$. Under the grade condition, their images extend to a basis of $U_{p,k}(B)$. Lift each added basis element to an element of order $p^k$ in $B$, keeping the images of the original cyclic generators. The bottom elements of all the lifted cycles form a basis of $B[p]$, by the filtration $W_{p,k}$. The map from the product of the chosen cyclic groups into the $p$-primary part of $B$ is injective because any nontrivial kernel in a finite abelian $p$-group contains a nontrivial element of order $p$. The cyclic decomposition of $B$ shows that the dimension of each grade is exactly the multiplicity of the corresponding cyclic factor. The product of the lifted cycles therefore has the order of the whole $p$-primary part. Taking the product over primes gives the asserted splitting.

Suppose $X=PC$ with $P=\langle\bar a\rangle$ and $C=C_X(P)$. Set $D=P\cap C=Z(P)$. If $X=P\times B$, then $C=D\times B$. If $C=D\times B$, then $X=P\times B$. As $D$ is central in $C$, its splitting is equivalent to the natural map $j:D\to C_{\mathrm{ab}}$ embedding $D$ as a direct summand. Indeed, splitting in $C$ induces splitting in $C_{\mathrm{ab}}$. A retraction of $j$ from $C_{\mathrm{ab}}$ back to $D$ gives a retraction on $C$ with a kernel commuting with $D$.

Check grade injectivity of $j$ by a conjunction over all $p,k$ with $p$ prime, $k\geq 1$, and $p^k\le N$. In multiplicative notation, for every $z\in D$ such that $z^p=1$ and $a^{p^{k-1}}=z$ for some $a\in D$, impose
$$
\bigl(\exists u\in C\;\;R_C(u^{p^k}z^{-1})\bigr)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\bigl(\exists b\in D\;\;b^{p^k}=z\bigr).
$$
Here $R_C$ on a word means that this word equals a product of $c$ commutators in $C$. The first condition places $j(z)$ in $W_{p,k+1}$ since $j(z)$ already has order dividing $p$. The conclusion places $z$ in $W_{p,k+1}$, exactly the zero-kernel condition on the grade. Larger exponents have zero domain grade. All powers and word equalities use atoms. Together with the centralizer-product condition and the guards, this tests exactly whether the marked subgroup $P$ is an internal direct factor.

For abelian factor multiplicities, apply the same grades to $f:Z(X)\to X_{\mathrm{ab}}$, the natural map. For each $p,k$, define a set of elements $W=W_{p,k}(Z(X))$ using a $p$-torsion test and a $p^{k-1}$-power witness in $Z(X)$. Define $V\subseteq W$ by the additional condition that $u^{p^k}z^{-1}\in X'$ for some $u\in X$, using $R_X$. These are bounded-width and bounded-rank unary predicates. The quotient $W/V$ is isomorphic to the image of the induced map on the $p,k$ grade. Its order is $|W|/|V|$.

To identify the rank of that image, take any fully refined decomposition $X=A\times J$ in which $A$ is the product of the abelian factors and $J$ has no abelian direct factor. The natural map is the product of the identity on $A$ and $Z(J)\to J_{\mathrm{ab}}$. If the latter map has nonzero $p,k$ grade image, let $z$ be a bottom element whose image is nonzero in that grade and choose $a\in Z(J)$ with $a^{p^{k-1}}=z$. The order of $a$ is $p^k$. The map $\langle a\rangle\to J_{\mathrm{ab}}$ is injective on all grades since its only nonzero domain grade is $p,k$. It splits by the abelian criterion. This gives a retraction $J\to\langle a\rangle$ with central image, and hence an abelian direct factor of $J$, a contradiction. Thus the rank of the image for $X$ is exactly the multiplicity of the cyclic factor of order $p^k$ in $A$. Matching the two unary counts $|W|,|V|$ to their values in $G$ matches all abelian multiplicities.

## Counting nonabelian factors by centralizers

Use the central-automorphism form of Krull–Remak–Schmidt: any two fully refined decompositions of a finite group into directly indecomposable factors are related, after matching coordinates, by an automorphism acting trivially on the quotient by the center. [Johnson et al., Theorem 3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06478v3#S3).

List the distinct nonabelian directly indecomposable isomorphism types $T$ that occur in the prescribed decomposition of $G$, and fix a $d$-element generating tuple for each, padding with identities. A tuple $\bar a$ is a candidate of type $T$ if it has that marked type and its generated subgroup is an internal direct factor, as tested above. Each such $P=\langle\bar a\rangle$ is indecomposable, and each direct factor of the listed type has such a marking. For any group $X$ satisfying the guards, choose a fully refined decomposition $X=\prod_i Q_i$ for this argument. Extend $P$ to a fully refined decomposition by taking a fully refined decomposition of a complement. The central-automorphism theorem gives a coordinate $Q_i\simeq P$ with
$$
PZ(X)/Z(X)=Q_iZ(X)/Z(X),\qquad C_X(P)=C_X(Q_i).
$$
The second equality holds because multiplying an element by a central element preserves its commutation relations.

For each listed type $T$, define $Z_T(X)$ to be the intersection of the centralizers of all candidates of that type. This is a bounded-width, bounded-rank unary predicate: the element commutes with the entries of every marked $d$-tuple that is a candidate of that type. Every reference factor $Q_i$ of type $T$ has a marked candidate tuple. The centralizer equality shows that no other subgroups affect the intersection. In the chosen direct product of $X$ we obtain
$$
Z_T(X)=
\prod_{i: \, Q_i\simeq T}Z(Q_i)\ \times\
\prod_{i: \, Q_i\not\simeq T}Q_i,
\qquad
[X:Z_T(X)]=[T:Z(T)]^{m_T(X)},
$$
where $m_T(X)$ is the number of factors of type $T$. Because $T$ is nonabelian, the base in the last power is bigger than one. Matching $|X|$ and the unary count $|Z_T(X)|$ to their values in $G$ matches $m_T(X)$ exactly. Exact counts are expressible by conjoining a lower counting threshold with the negation of the next threshold.

Include also the sentence that every element centralizing all candidate tuples is in $Z(X)$. Its bounded rank follows from quantifying over $d$ entries and using a disjunction over the type list. This sentence holds in $G$, since the nonabelian factors together with central abelian factors generate $G$. If $X$ has a nonabelian $Q_i$ of unlisted type, any noncentral element of $Q_i$ centralizes all candidates by the centralizer equalities, while it fails to lie in $Z(X)$. The sentence therefore makes the list exhaustive for the comparison group as well.

## Identification

Take the conjunction of the derived-subgroup guards, the assertion that the nonabelian type list is exhaustive, the ambient order and $Z_T$ counts, and the unary count assertions that match the abelian multiplicities. Conjunctions indexed by the prime-power list or type list can reuse the same variable symbols. There is a fixed bound in $d$ on the width and quantifier rank, since word relations are atomic and all counting quantifiers here count single elements.

The reference group $G$ satisfies this sentence. Every $H$ satisfying it has, in any fully refined decomposition, exactly the nonabelian factors tallied by the centralizer indices and exactly the abelian factors tallied by the graded images. Matching the factors gives $H\simeq G$. The guarded derived-subgroup tests and the exhaustiveness sentence establish this implication for arbitrary finite comparison groups, including groups outside the initial decomposition class. Version II counting Weisfeiler–Leman with the bounded dimension and round count associated with the sentence therefore identifies $G$.
