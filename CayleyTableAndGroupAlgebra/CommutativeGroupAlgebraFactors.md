# Commutative tensor factors of modular group algebras over arbitrary fields

**Theorem.** Let $p$ be a prime, $k$ any field of characteristic $p$, and $G$ a finite $p$-group. If $kG\cong A\otimes_k B$ as unital $k$-algebras and $B$ is commutative, then there are subgroups $U,T\leq G$ with $G=U\times T$, $T$ abelian, $A\cong kU$, and $B\cong kT$. This answers Remark 7 of García-Lucas, del Río and Sakurai, which asks whether their Theorem 2 extends from the prime field to arbitrary coefficient fields of characteristic $p$. As a corollary it gives a second proof of García-Lucas's Question D, which Sakurai previously answered. [Theorem 2 and Remark 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09036v1) · [Question D](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00009-023-02557-1)

## 1. The power filtration on the augmentation tangent space

For an augmented finite-dimensional $k$-algebra $R$, write $J_R$ for the kernel of its augmentation. We use algebras where $J_R$ is nilpotent. In particular, for a finite $p$-group $H$ this holds for $kH$. One proof is induction on $|H|$, using a central element $z$ of order $p$ and the quotient $kH/(z-1)\cong k[H/\langle z\rangle]$. A sufficiently high power of the augmentation ideal lies in the ideal $(z-1)$, whose $p$-th power is zero. This also proves that $J_{kH}$ is the unique maximal ideal and that the $k$-augmentation is intrinsic. An algebra factor in an isomorphism $R=A\otimes B\cong kH$ inherits an augmentation by restriction, with nilpotent augmentation ideal.

We will use the following elementary algebra observation. Elements of $J_R$ whose classes span $J_R/J_R^2$ generate $R$ as a unital algebra. In fact the images of length-$i$ products of those elements span $J_R^i/J_R^{i+1}$. Successively lifting through the powers of the nilpotent ideal expresses every element of $R$ in the generated algebra. In particular, an augmentation-preserving endomorphism inducing an invertible tangent map is an automorphism.

When $R$ is commutative, let
$$
 T_r(R)=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\{x\in J_R:x^{p^r}=0\}\longrightarrow J_R/J_R^2\bigr)
 \qquad(r\geq 0).
$$
These are $k$-linear subspaces, since taking $p^r$-th powers is additive and sends a scalar multiple to its $p^r$-th scalar multiple. They form an increasing filtration with $T_0=0$. This definition works over imperfect fields as well.

For a finite abelian $p$-group $D=\prod_{i=1}^m\langle d_i\rangle$, $|d_i|=p^{s_i}$, write $x_i=d_i-1$. There is an isomorphism
$$
 kD\cong k[x_1,\ldots,x_m]/(x_i^{p^{s_i}}:1\leq i\leq m).
$$
Each $x_i$ with $s_i\leq r$ has $p^r$-th power zero. Conversely, if the coefficient of $x_i$ in the tangent class of $x$ is nonzero and $s_i>r$, then $x^{p^r}$ has a nonzero coefficient of $x_i^{p^r}$. Frobenius acts termwise on the monomial expansion, and no other monomial can produce that surviving monomial. Thus $T_r(kD)$ is exactly the span of the tangent coordinates of lengths $\leq r$.

Now let $R,S$ be commutative augmented finite-dimensional algebras with nilpotent augmentation ideals. The tangent space of $R\otimes S$ is the direct sum of the two tangent spaces. This follows by expanding each factor as $k\oplus J$ and observing that $J_R\otimes J_S$ lies in the square of the tensor-product augmentation ideal. The augmentations give retractions from $R\otimes S$ to both factors. Applying these retractions to nilpotent lifts, and using the sum of a pair of nilpotent lifts for the other inclusion, gives the exact identity
$$
 T_r(R\otimes S)=T_r(R)\oplus T_r(S).
$$
In each factor choose a basis of its tangent space adapted to the filtration. For each new basis vector at level $r$, choose a lift with $p^r$-th power zero. The lifts generate and give a surjection from a truncated polynomial algebra with one variable of length $r$ for each such basis vector. If $R\otimes S\cong kD$, the additivity of the filtration shows that the two lists of lengths together are precisely the list $(s_i)$ for $D$. The product of the dimensions of the two truncated polynomial sources is therefore $|D|=\dim R\dim S$. Both maps are surjections, so dimension forces each to be an isomorphism. In particular both factors are abelian group algebras.

For an associative algebra $E$, let $E^{\rm ab}$ denote its quotient by the two-sided ideal generated by commutators. The commutative quotient of $kG$ is $k[G/G']$. This follows directly from $gh-hg=hg([g,h]-1)$ with a suitable commutator convention, or from the universal property of the group abelianization. Since $B$ is commutative, $(A\otimes B)^{\rm ab}=A^{\rm ab}\otimes B$, as is seen by generating the commutator ideal with commutators from $A$. Applying the preceding argument to $A^{\rm ab}\otimes B\cong k[G/G']$ shows that $B$ is a truncated polynomial algebra with variables having lengths $r_1,\ldots,r_t$.

## 2. Turning a central algebra variable into a group direct factor

Suppose $u\in Z(kG)\cap J_{kG}$ satisfies $u^{p^r}=0$, where $r\geq1$. Assume its tangent class in $k[G/G']$ lies outside $T_{r-1}(k[G/G'])$. We show that
$$
 G=\langle z\rangle\times U,\quad |z|=p^r,
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad kG/(u)\cong kU .
$$

Let $\operatorname{Br}:Z(kG)\to kZ(G)$ keep the coefficients of group elements in $Z(G)$ and discard the rest. This is multiplicative. For each central basis element $c$, the coefficient of $c$ in a product of two central algebra elements is a sum over pairs $(a,b)\in G^2$ with $ab=c$. Simultaneous conjugation by $G$ preserves the summands and the set of pairs. Every non-singleton orbit has size divisible by $p$, and the singleton orbits have both $a$ and $b$ central. Consequently only the latter pairs contribute, proving multiplicativity. Also, any noncentral conjugacy class sum maps to zero in $k[G/G']$, because each conjugacy class collapses to a single coset and its size is divisible by $p$. Thus $u$ and $a=\operatorname{Br}(u)$ have the same image in $k[G/G']$.

Since $a^{p^r}=0$, a direct calculation in the commutative group algebra $kZ(G)$ places $a$ in the ideal generated by $h-1$ for $h\in\Omega_r(Z(G))=\{h\in Z(G):h^{p^r}=1\}$. Indeed $a^{p^r}$ is the sum of its coefficients raised to the $p^r$-th power and grouped by the map $h\mapsto h^{p^r}$. Fibers are cosets of $\Omega_r(Z(G))$. For each coset, the sum of coefficients is zero because Frobenius is injective in $k$. This is precisely the kernel of the group-algebra map to $k[Z(G)/\Omega_r(Z(G))]$. It follows that the tangent class of $u$ in $k[G/G']$ is a linear combination of the classes $h-1$ for $h\in\Omega_r(Z(G))$. A group element multiplying such a generator has augmentation 1 and therefore does not change its tangent class.

Fix any cyclic decomposition of $G/G'$. A tangent coordinate of exact length $r$ detects $u$. Indeed its class is in $T_r$, since $u^{p^r}=0$, and lies outside $T_{r-1}$ by hypothesis. Write $\pi:G\to C_{p^r}$ for the projection onto one such coordinate through the abelianization. As the tangent class of $u$ is a linear combination of classes from $\Omega_r(Z(G))$, some $z$ in that subgroup must have $\pi(z)$ a generator of $C_{p^r}$. To see this, for a generator $c$ of $C_{p^r}$ the class of $c^j-1$ is $j(c-1)$ in the tangent space. Thus $\langle z\rangle$ is central of order $p^r$ and $\pi$ restricts to an isomorphism on it. It follows that $G=\ker(\pi)\times\langle z\rangle$.

Put $U=\ker(\pi)$ and $y=z-1$, so that $kG=kU[y]/(y^{p^r})$ with central variable $y$. Substituting $u$ for $y$ while fixing $kU$ gives an algebra endomorphism, because $u$ is central and satisfies the variable relation. It preserves the augmentation. Its tangent map is invertible. It fixes the tangent directions from $kU$, and the $y$-coefficient of $u$ is nonzero since it is detected by the algebra map induced by $\pi$. The observation in Section 1 therefore makes this endomorphism an automorphism, so the quotients by $u$ and by $y$ are isomorphic. This proves the claim.

## 3. Iteration and the cancellation corollary

If $B\ne k$, choose one of its truncated polynomial variables, of length $r$, and let $u$ be its image in $kG$. This element is central and $u^{p^r}=0$. Its class in the tangent space of $k[G/G']$ lies outside $T_{r-1}$. The augmentation of $A^{\rm ab}$ gives a map $A^{\rm ab}\otimes B\to B$ sending this class to the original variable class, which is outside $T_{r-1}(B)$ by Section 1. This map preserves $T_{r-1}$, proving the assertion.

Section 2 splits a central $C_{p^r}$ off $G$ and identifies the complement's group algebra with the quotient by $u$. This quotient is $A\otimes B_0$, where $B_0$ is the truncated polynomial algebra in the remaining variables. Repeat for every variable of $B$. The product $T$ of the extracted central cyclic factors is abelian, $G=U\times T$, and the remaining group algebra is $kU\cong A$. The list of cyclic orders in $T$ is the list of exponents defining $B$, so $kT\cong B$. The case $B=k$ uses $T=1$ and $U=G$. This proves the theorem and answers Remark 7.

For the cancellation corollary, write $G=G_0\times G_1$ and $H=H_0\times H_1$ with maximal abelian direct factors $G_1,H_1$, and complements $G_0,H_0$. Given $kG\cong kH$, apply the theorem to $kH\cong kG_0\otimes kG_1$. This gives $H=U\times T$ with $kU\cong kG_0$ and $kT\cong kG_1$. Thus $|G_1|=|T|\leq|H_1|$. Applying the theorem in the reverse direction yields $|H_1|\leq|G_1|$. Hence $T$ is itself maximal. Krull–Remak–Schmidt then gives $U\cong H_0$, so $k[\operatorname{NAb}(G)]\cong k[\operatorname{NAb}(H)]$. Sakurai previously proved this conclusion in Theorem C using uniqueness of irreducible tensor factorization of split local algebras. The group direct factor in our theorem is obtained by the central-variable substitution of Section 2. [Sakurai, *Tensor factorization of local algebras*, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38323v1#A1)

## 4. Tensor indecomposability over arbitrary coefficient fields

The theorem also extends the two cases in Corollary 3 of García-Lucas, del Río and Sakurai to every field of characteristic $p$. If $G$ is a directly indecomposable finite $p$-group and either $G$ is generated by at most three elements or $G'$ is cyclic, then $kG$ has no nontrivial tensor product factorization. [Prime-field cases and Carlson–Kovács Question 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09036v1)

For the first case, if $g_1,g_2,g_3$ generate $G$, then $g_i-1$ generate $kG$ as a unital algebra, since inverses of the $g_i$ are positive powers. Their classes span its augmentation tangent space. In a nontrivial tensor factorization the tangent spaces of the two factors add, and each is nonzero by nilpotence. At least one therefore has dimension one, and Section 1 shows that its algebra is generated by one element and hence commutative. The theorem would split $G$ nontrivially.

For the second case, write $\mathcal C(X)$ for the two-sided commutator ideal of an augmented algebra $X$. If $G'$ is cyclic then $\mathcal C(kG)$ is generated as a two-sided ideal by $z-1$, for a generator $z$ of $G'$, because $kG/\mathcal C(kG)=k[G/G']$. Thus $\mathcal C(kG)/(J\mathcal C(kG)+\mathcal C(kG)J)$ has dimension at most one, where $J=J_{kG}$. Suppose $kG\cong A\otimes B$. The retractions to $A$ and $B$ induce a surjection from this quotient onto
$$
 \mathcal C(A)/(J_A\mathcal C(A)+\mathcal C(A)J_A)
 \ \oplus\
 \mathcal C(B)/(J_B\mathcal C(B)+\mathcal C(B)J_B).
$$
Indeed, the embeddings of the factor commutator ideals give lifts of the two target summands, and each lifts to zero under the opposite retraction. A factor's displayed quotient is nonzero whenever its commutator ideal is nonzero. If $\mathcal C(X)=J_X\mathcal C(X)+\mathcal C(X)J_X$, repeated substitution gives a sum of terms $J_X^i\mathcal C(X)J_X^{s-i}$ for arbitrarily large $s$, and nilpotence forces $\mathcal C(X)=0$. Hence the dimension bound ensures at least one factor is commutative. Applying the theorem again contradicts direct indecomposability if both factors were nontrivial.

## Prior work

This is an informal proof. García-Lucas, del Río and Sakurai proved the commutative-factor theorem over the prime field and explicitly asked for arbitrary coefficient fields in Remark 7. Sakurai's subsequent tensor-factorization theorem proves abstract cancellation over split local algebras and expressly answers Question D. The targeted literature search found no prior arbitrary-field answer to Remark 7, which requires that a commutative algebra factor be realized by a direct factor of the given group. The general Carlson–Kovács question also permits both tensor factors to be noncommutative. [García-Lucas, del Río and Sakurai](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10468-026-10379-4) · [Sakurai](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38323)
