# Determinant cosets from castling and normalized matrix pencils

We give two informal algorithms over \(\mathbb F_3\) for computing all determinant triples of transporters between two 3-tensors. The first is a polynomial ordinary-TI decision reduction on raw inputs with a flattening of logarithmic rank or codimension. The second handles normalized matrix pencils with an intrinsic invertible slice line and squarefree trace operator. It uses one TI decision query, or none when the trace operator has irreducible characteristic polynomial. Both allow arbitrary stabilizers.

## 1. Raw tensors with a small flattening image or annihilator

Let \(L\) denote the explicit input length. Write the selected flattenings as \(f_X:V^*\to M=U\otimes W\), where \(X=A,B\). Reject if their ranks differ. Let \(r\) be the common rank, \(I_X=\operatorname{im} f_X\), and \(K_X=\ker f_X\). A basis of \(I_X\) gives a tensor with third dimension \(r\). Alternatively a basis of \(I_X^\perp\subset M^*\) gives a tensor with third dimension \(\dim M-r\). Choose the smaller dimension \(d\). Bases, lifts and annihilators are computed by Gaussian elimination.

For original mode maps \(P,Q,R\), put \(h=P\otimes Q\). The original tensor equation is \(f_B R^{-T}=h f_A\). A transporter of the image tensors gives \(P,Q\) and the matrix \(D\) of \(h:I_A\to I_B\). A transporter of the annihilator tensors gives \(P^{-T},Q^{-T}\) and the matrix \(D\) of \(h^{-T}:I_A^\perp\to I_B^\perp\). In both conventions \(D^{-T}\) is the small third-mode matrix. Every small transporter lifts to the original. In bases adapted to the kernels, let \(R^{-T}\) have any invertible kernel block and the required quotient block induced by \(h\). Conversely an original transporter induces a small one. If the small third dimension is zero, the empty ordered tuple puts no constraint on the two remaining matrices.

For determinant calibration choose bases on \(V^*\) consisting of a basis of \(K_X\) followed by lifts of the chosen image basis. Write \(b_X\) for the determinant of that basis matrix in the original coordinates. Let \(k\) be the determinant of the kernel block in these bases. It is \(1\) for zero-dimensional kernels and either sign independently otherwise. With \(H\) the matrix of \(h:I_A\to I_B\) we obtain \((\det R)^{-1}=(b_B/b_A)k\det H\). In the image case \(H=D\). For the annihilator case, complete the image basis to a basis of \(M\) using representatives dual to the chosen annihilator basis, and let its determinant be \(c_X\). The quotient map then has matrix \(D^{-T}\), so
\[
\det H=(c_A/c_B)(\det P)^{\dim W}(\det Q)^{\dim U}\det D.
\]
Consequently the determinant coset of the small tensor determines the original coset exactly. In particular an unused direction gives a free sign on that mode, without a premise about the other two modes.

### Small-mode queries and the ordered-slice kernel

For tensors with one mode of dimension \(d\), an ordinary TI query can require a partial list of exact vector images on that mode. Use one linear map from a selector space whose basis vectors map to the prescribed vectors on each side. Include a fixed shift on the selector and mark its first basis vector, which is cyclic for the shift. This forces the selector action to be the identity. Thus a constant-signature linear system comprising the original tensor, linear maps and a vector suffices, even for a long list of marks. The typed-system reduction compiles this into ordinary TI of polynomial size. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

After checking feasibility, greedily choose the next small-mode basis image among at most \(3^d\) vectors, keeping all previous pins, until one full feasible map is known. For the source stabilizer, obtain generators of its image on the small mode by the pointwise stabilizer chain. At each level pin the earlier basis vectors and test every possible image of the next one. Complete each feasible choice to a full map by the same greedy procedure. These transversals generate the image because dividing by a representative having the same next image moves an element to the next pointwise stabilizer. This takes \(3^{O(d)}\operatorname{poly}(L)\) time and queries. Only the small mode is searched.

A fixed small-mode map leaves simultaneous left-right equivalence of two ordered matrix tuples. Write the equations as \(P M_j=N_j Q^{-T}\). This is an isomorphism of two explicit representations of the two-vertex quiver with one arrow per slice. Construct an intertwiner by deterministic polynomial-time module isomorphism, which lifts the small-mode map. [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski](https://doi.org/10.1145/258726.258751)

The kernel of the small-mode action is the unit group of the source quiver endomorphism algebra. Solve for the algebra by linear equations, and compute its radical \(J\) and semisimple quotient \(\prod_i M_{a_i}(E_i)\) over \(\mathbb F_3\). On each vertex space, take its filtration by powers of \(J\). A quotient unit has determinant on that vertex equal to the product of the norms of its matrix determinants, each raised to its total multiplicity of the corresponding simple column module. Compute those multiplicities from central-idempotent images on the radical layers. Units congruent to one modulo the radical have determinant one, and every quotient unit lifts. Since each field norm surjects onto \(\mathbb F_3^\times\), the kernel sign image is generated by one pair per simple factor, whose two entries are \((-1)\) to the two multiplicities. This needs no search for a norm preimage. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03495)

The kernel signs together with the signs of the chosen transversal lifts generate all stabilizer signs. Multiplying this subgroup by the signs of the one relative lift yields the exact transporter coset of the small tensor. The basis calibrations above recover the original coset. Test any prescribed product of signs or the simultaneous triple \((1,1,1)\) in it. In particular the algorithm is polynomial whenever \(d=O(\log L)\).

### Iterating and replacing the pencil endpoint

The determinant calibration holds without a size promise at an intermediate step. One may trim a mode to its image when it is deficient, or cast to its annihilator when the latter is smaller than the current mode. One can reorder modes between steps. The sum of positive mode dimensions decreases, so these transforms take polynomial total time. Store the basis factors and propagate determinant cosets back through the sequence. Zero tensors can be handled directly. Any decreasing sequence reaching a logarithmically small image or annihilator gives a polynomial-time algorithm for the original pair.

All \(2\times a\times b\) endpoints admit an oracle-free algorithm by enumerating the constant number of maps on the first mode and applying the ordered-slice calculation. Thus the squarefree regular-pencil endpoint used in earlier castling work can be replaced by an arbitrary pencil, including singular pencils and repeated factors. The transform also handles rank-deficient intermediate flattenings, though decreasing steps need not reach a pencil. [Background on castling transforms](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/maximum-likelihood-estimation-for-tensor-normal-models-via-castling-transforms/FF03C608CF421F399C16127F1476538F)

## 2. An invertible slice line and an irreducible trace operator

Here the two matrix modes have dimension \(n\), and the third flattening is injective of dimension \(\ell\). Assume an invertible slice line can be found in polynomial time from each raw tensor and every ordinary transporter takes the first line to the second. The proof also works for arbitrary supplied lines if the target question requires a transporter carrying one line to the other. Let \(A_0,B_0\) be chosen nonzero slices on the lines. Normalize the matrix spaces by right multiplication by \(A_0^{-1},B_0^{-1}\). Call the normalized spaces \(X_A,X_B\), each containing \(I\). Assume the pairing \((Y,Z)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(YZ)\) on each space is nondegenerate. For any trace-dual pair of bases \((X_i),(Y_i)\) define \(J_X=\sum_i X_iY_i\). The dual-basis tensor is basis independent, so \(J_X\) is conjugacy-covariant. Assume \(J_{X_A}\) has irreducible characteristic polynomial over \(\mathbb F_3\). If those of the two operators differ, reject.

Write the slices as \(A(x), B(y)\) for dual third-mode coordinates. If \(R^T\xi_B=t\xi_A\) for the line representatives, with \(t\in\{\pm1\}\), then
\[
B_0=tPA_0Q^T,\qquad
B(y)B_0^{-1}=t^{-1}P\big(A(R^Ty)A_0^{-1}\big)P^{-1}.
\]
Thus a transporter is specified by a conjugation \(P:X_A\to X_B\) and \(t\). Conversely, these determine \(Q\) by the first equation and \(R\) by the second, and the resulting maps carry the specified line to its mate. In fact both normalized line vectors are \(I\), so the second identity forces the required line mapping.

Put \(E=\mathbb F_3[z]/(f)\), where \(f\) is the common irreducible characteristic polynomial of \(J_X\). Choose nonzero eigenvectors of the two operators for \(z\) over \(E\). Their Frobenius iterates give eigenbases \((v_i),(w_i)\) for \(0\le i<n\), and the map \(P_0:v_i\mapsto w_i\) descends to \(\mathbb F_3\) because it commutes with Frobenius. Every rational intertwiner \(P\) of the two operators has the form \(v_i\mapsto u^{3^i}w_i\) with \(u\in E^\times\). Thus in matrix vectorization coordinates \((i,j)\) for these two bases the corresponding conjugacy map rescales coordinate \((i,j)\) by \(m_{ij}=u^{3^i-3^j}\).

Take row matrices \(Z_A,Z_B\) for the original normalized slice bases in these vectorization coordinates. Row-reduce over \(E\). Diagonal scaling preserves the ordered pivot set \(I\). If the pivot sets or reduced-row-echelon supports differ, there is no such conjugacy. For common pivot list \(I_1,\ldots,I_\ell\), each common nonzero in row \(k\), column \(l\) gives the equation \(m_l/m_{I_k}=\operatorname{RREF}(Z_B)_{kl}/\operatorname{RREF}(Z_A)_{kl}\). These equations are equivalent to carrying the whole subspace to its mate. This follows by rescaling the pivot in each row back to one after diagonal scaling.

The equation in raw slice coordinates is \(Z_B=t^{-1}RZ_A\operatorname{diag}(m)\). Restrict to the pivot columns and take determinants to obtain
\[
\det R=t^\ell\frac{\det Z_B[:,I]}{\det Z_A[:,I]}\left(\prod_{j\in I}m_j\right)^{-1}.
\]
Also \(\det P=\det(P_0)u^{(3^n-1)/2}\) and \(\det Q=t^{-n}(\det B_0/\det A_0)(\det P)^{-1}\). These formulas calibrate all three signs in the original coordinates. Although the pivot calculations take place in \(E\), the induced maps are rational over \(\mathbb F_3\) because the original subspaces, \(P\), and \(t\) are rational. Thus the computed determinants are the actual base-field determinants.

For each choice of \(t\) and prescribed sign triple, all conditions are power equations \(u^{e_i}=b_i\) in \(E^\times\). Let \(m=3^n-1\) and \(g=\gcd(m,e_1,\ldots,e_s)\). Compute coefficients with \(g=a_0m+\sum_i a_ie_i\) and set \(b=\prod_i b_i^{a_i}\). A solution exists exactly when \(b^{m/g}=1\) and \(b^{e_i/g}=b_i\) for every \(i\). Necessity follows from \(u^g=b\). For sufficiency, cyclicity gives a \(g\)-th root of \(b\) in \(E^\times\) and any such root satisfies the equations. All exponents have polynomial bit length and nothing requires taking discrete logarithms or finding the root. Testing the eight triples returns the entire determinant coset.

This pencil method does not bound the three tensor dimensions. Its hypotheses require an intrinsic invertible line to be computable and the normalized operator to satisfy the irreducibility test. No general line-extraction theorem is used here.

### Squarefree characteristic polynomial with one decision query

The preceding algorithm uses no TI queries in the irreducible case. With ordinary TI decision we can replace irreducibility by squarefreeness, including many distinct factors with a large common splitting field. First use TI to check feasibility on the line-marked pair, or directly on the pair if the lines are intrinsic. A yes answer supplies existence of a conjugacy of normalized spaces. Use rational canonical bases to align the two trace operators by a known reference map \(P_0\). Work in aligned coordinates on the first mode and with the resulting normalized slice spaces. Write \(E=\mathbb F_3[J]\). Squarefreeness gives an étale algebra of dimension \(n\), and the common \(n\)-dimensional space is free of rank one over \(E\).

Put \(B=E\otimes_{\mathbb F_3}E\), acting on matrix space by \((a\otimes b)X=aXb\). This is the regular \(B\)-module, as follows from the trace identification of the dual of \(E\) with \(E\). Conjugation by a residual map of the first mode is the action of \(u\otimes u^{-1}\) for \(u\in E^\times\). Compute by linear equations
\[
C=\{b\in B:bX_A\subseteq X_A\},\qquad H=\{b\in B:bX_A\subseteq X_B\}
\]
in the aligned coordinates. The feasibility answer implies \(H=b_0 C\) for some unit \(b_0=u_0\otimes u_0^{-1}\). Indeed multiplying the defining inclusion by \(b_0^{-1}\) gives the condition defining \(C\). The algebra \(C\) is a unital subalgebra of an étale algebra, hence étale over the finite field. Decompose \(C\) into its field factors, and choose a nonzero vector in the part of \(H\) attached to each factor idempotent. Their sum \(b_*\) generates \(H\) as a \(C\)-module, because it equals \(b_0 c_0\) for a unit \(c_0\) of \(C\). In particular \(b_*\) is invertible on the ambient matrix space. Every residual conjugacy comes from a solution
\[
 u\otimes u^{-1}=b_*c,\qquad u\in E^\times,\quad c\in C^\times.
\]

Factoring these explicit finite-dimensional commutative algebras and evaluating the two embeddings into the field factors of \(B\) turns the identity into a system of multiplicative equations between field factors. They have polynomial degree over \(\mathbb F_3\) individually because \(\dim B=n^2\). The determinant of the first map is \(\det(P_0)\) times the product of the norms of the components of \(u\). The second determinant follows from \(B_0=tPA_0Q^T\). The determinant of the normalized slice-space map is the known determinant of \(b_*\), measured from the aligned source slice basis to the target slice basis, times the determinant of \(c\) on \(X_A\). The latter is a product of the norms of the components of \(c\), each raised to its \(C\)-module multiplicity in \(X_A\). Invert this slice-space determinant and multiply by \(t^\ell\) to obtain \(\det R\), by the change of basis in the preceding proof.

All sign characters kill elements of odd order. Project each multiplicative equation to the Sylow-2 part of its field's unit group. A degree-\(e\) field has Sylow-2 order \(2\) for odd \(e\) and \(4\cdot 2^{v_2(e)}\) for even \(e\), at most \(4e\). These groups can be listed in polynomial time by starting at \(-1\) and repeatedly replacing the current element by a square root until no root exists. This produces a generator of maximal 2-power order; the quadratic root computations use fixed-characteristic finite-field factoring. Projecting a given element to its 2-part uses powering by an exponent that is zero modulo the odd cofactor and one modulo the 2-power order. In these listed groups, each field embedding and each norm becomes a known linear exponent map. Thus the equations and prescribed sign constraints are modular linear equations, decidable by integer Smith normal form. An unconstrained solution is known to exist from the TI query. Any consistent 2-primary solution can therefore be combined with the odd parts of one unconstrained solution. This proves sufficiency without computing those odd parts. Trying the two anchor scales and all eight signs gives the full determinant coset.

## 3. Decision-only transport of narrow field blocks

This gives a sufficient version of the weight-block step in the finite-field roadmap. Fix one product of the three signs. In every mode appearing in that product, suppose a polynomial-time procedure computes a commutative étale subalgebra of its endomorphism algebra. Assume every transporter between the two inputs conjugates these subalgebras, and each field block has dimension \(O(\log L)\) as a module over its own field. Field degrees themselves are unrestricted. Then the set of attainable values of this product is decidable in polynomial time with ordinary TI alone.

Decompose each algebra into field factors. Use the possible images of one field generator on each factor as labels, including all its Frobenius conjugates. The total number of labels is at most the total dimension of the algebras. Use typed diagrams marking these algebras as subspaces of the endomorphism spaces. Pinning a label is a condition on the conjugation of an endomorphism. These conditions give ordinary TI queries for partial label maps by the fixed-signature linear-structure compiler. Polynomial-time finite-field isomorphism and factorization in fixed characteristic compute all label candidates. Find one feasible full relative label map and generators of the source image on the label set by the pointwise chain from Section 1. That search needs polynomially many queries since the label set is explicitly polynomial. [Lenstra, finite-field isomorphisms](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

For a fixed full label map, choose explicit semilinear reference identifications \(f_i^0\) between the field blocks in compatible bases. These are local module identifications. The actual mode map on that label branch has the form \(f_i^0\) followed on its source coordinates by a blockwise field-linear action. The sign on mode \(i\) is
\[
\det(f_i^0)\prod_j \operatorname{Norm}_{E_{ij}/\mathbb F_3}(\det_{E_{ij}} U_{ij}),
\]
where \(U_{ij}\) is the remaining field-linear action on a block. Compute the reference signs as determinants of the actual base-field matrices, thereby including block-permutation and Frobenius signs.

Keep the original tensor in one typed instance. For each remaining field-linear action add its exterior powers over its field and the bilinear wedge maps. The last exterior line acts by \(\det_{E_{ij}} U_{ij}\), and the exterior chain has polynomial size by the module-dimension bound. To take the norm of its multiplier \(u\), use the field multiplication map \(E\times E\to E\) between copies of that line. If the two input actions are multiplication by \(a,b\), the required output action is multiplication by \(ab\), as follows by applying the multiplication identity with one argument equal to \(1\). A binary powering chain for the exponent \((3^{[E: \mathbb F_3]}-1)/2\) uses linearly many steps in the field degree. The result acts on the embedded base line \(\mathbb F_3\cdot 1\) by the norm. Use binary multiplication on the base lines and pin one vector to impose the desired product after multiplying in the reference signs. This is one ordinary TI test for the branch and desired sign. To keep the signature fixed, pack all the named spaces as separate summands using the module action of the coordinatewise algebra on the labels, fixing its standard basis with a shift and cyclic vector. Pack the linear and bilinear maps in distinct occurrence slots, tying copies of each named space by included identity maps. All packs have polynomial dimension and arity at most three.

Query the identity label map to obtain the sign image of the kernel. For each label generator choose one feasible value from its tested signs. The kernel image and these values generate the stabilizer sign image. One feasible sign for the relative label map supplies the representative of the transporter sign coset. Every step uses decision queries only. If the required subalgebras occur in all modes, the same proof imposes joint triples by pinning the three final lines. This construction reuses the determinant-line and norm diagrams of the MathProofs multiplicity arguments. Its input condition concerns the algebras actually recovered, and does not assume that a maximal torus or its rational field blocks can always be extracted from raw tensors.

## Scope

In a balanced concise \(n\times n\times n\) tensor the small dimension in Section 1 is \(n\) for every flattening, and neither trim nor cast decreases the dimensions. Section 2 supplies a different sufficient condition, but no theorem yet proves it covers arbitrary raw scalar-stabilizer pairs. The balanced scalar-stabilizer case and unrestricted SL-TI containment remain open. Publication priority for these restricted algorithms has not been established. [Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)
