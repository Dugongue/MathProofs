# Reading an orthogonal determinant from a trace Gram form

Let F be a fixed finite field of odd order q. Suppose the source and target each consist of explicit tensors of order at most three on named vector spaces. The two systems may include the raw three-tensor and its previously selected marks. Both systems have a named residual space R with a nondegenerate symmetric form B. An inclusion or quotient map can tie R to an existing role. Thus a system isomorphism has an induced residual isometry f, from the source form to the target form.

Fix any further determinant characters to be tested on this same transporter. For example, these can include determinants of raw tensor modes. Suppose the subgroup of target-system automorphisms on which all these characters equal one induces all of SO(R) on the target residual space. Automorphisms of this subgroup preserve all the existing marks. This condition is a promise about actual system automorphisms, so surjectivity of an ambient isometry group alone gives no such promise.

There is a deterministic polynomial-size attachment of tensors of order at most three that reads the determinant on R on a scalar line. Every transporter before attachment can be corrected by one of the promised automorphisms and then extended, with all designated determinants preserved, including the determinant on R. Every isomorphism of the enlarged systems projects to an original transporter and acts on the new line by the calibrated determinant. In particular, a prescribed value of this determinant can be imposed by a line mark. Its scalar can also be multiplied with determinants already read by other attachments. A polynomial list of such determinant tests on one transporter gives one ordinary three-tensor isomorphism instance if the other tests already have ordinary tensor attachments and the stated normalization condition holds.

## 1. A trace Gram model for the residual form

For m at least one let E have q^(2m) elements and K have q^m elements, with K the corresponding subfield of E. Denote q-Frobenius by φ and set σ = φ^m. Use the symmetric form
$$
H_m(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F}\big(\sigma(x)y\big)
$$
on E over F. This is the trace Gram construction of the Alpöge release, with one Hermitian coordinate and Hermitian coefficient 1. Symmetry follows from invariance of trace under σ. The ordinary trace pairing is nonsingular because E/F is separable. Applying the invertible F-linear map σ in the first argument therefore gives a nonsingular form. The release gives precisely the matrix of this scalar form in an F-basis under the name traceGram. [Alpöge's release announcement](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf), source file CompanionTheory/SpectralGeometry/LocalGlobalProofs/AdelicAnalysis/TraceGramDetNeZero.lean.

For the signed discriminant, choose the same F-basis b_1,...,b_(2m) in both arguments and let M be the matrix with entries M_(i,j) = φ^i(b_j), for i from 0 to 2m−1. The embedding matrix M is invertible. For a power basis this is the Vandermonde determinant on the conjugates of a generator, and changing basis preserves invertibility. The Gram matrix of the ordinary trace pairing is M^T M. The Frobenius φ cycles the rows of M, so if D = det M, then D^q = −D. Consequently D² is an element of F that is nonsquare there. Any square root of D² in E is D or −D, and neither belongs to F. The linear map σ in embedding coordinates is a shift by m on the 2m coordinates. Hence det_F σ = (−1)^m. It follows that (−1)^m det H_m has nonsquare class.

A nonsingular symmetric form over F is determined up to isometry by its dimension and determinant squareclass. One can compute a common standard basis model and the isometries in deterministic polynomial time at fixed q. Diagonalize by symmetric elimination, using a basis vector with nonzero self-pairing or a sum of two basis vectors with nonzero mutual pairing. Scale each diagonal coefficient to 1 or to a fixed nonsquare ν, by testing the finitely many field scalars. Replace each pair of ν coefficients by two coefficients 1. To do so, choose u,v in F with u²+v²=ν^(−1) by testing the q² pairs. Such a pair exists since each of the two sets of possible values of u² and of ν^(−1)−v² has (q+1)/2 elements. The two vectors (u,v) and (−v,u) then have self-pairing 1, mutual pairing zero, and form a basis. This constructs the claimed isometries as well as proving the classification used here.

For a residual dimension d of at least three, choose a common form model as follows.

- For d = 2m+1, use H_m and one orthogonal F-line. Take its nonzero coefficient so that the combined determinant squareclass matches the input.
- For d = 2m and nonsquare class of (−1)^m det B, use H_m.
- For d = 2m and square class of (−1)^m det B, use the orthogonal sum H_(m−1) and H_1. Here m is at least two. The combined signed discriminant is a product of two nonsquare classes, hence square.

The two input form matrices must have the same dimension and determinant squareclass for any isometry to exist. A mismatch gives a fixed negative ordinary instance. In the matching case use the above algorithm to construct form isometries P_X and P_Y from the same model to source and target, respectively, in their original coordinates. Use the same field model for each given degree on both sides and choose the block order once in the common model, including when there are two identical blocks. Deterministic construction of the fields takes time polynomial in their degrees at fixed q. Trace, involution, field multiplication, and the Gram matrices are computed in those explicit fields. [Shoup, New Algorithms for Finding Irreducible Polynomials over Finite Fields](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

In dimension zero the determinant is one. In dimensions one and two an ordinary exterior algebra with its degree-one embedding and top line reads the determinant in the original coordinates, with at most four basis elements. These dimensions need no normalization hypothesis.

## 2. Force semilinearity by marking the regular field actions

For each field block E in the common model, include an F-algebra role for E with its multiplication, a module role for its copy of E in the model, the multiplication action, and the inclusion of this module role in R via P_X or P_Y. For the possible remaining orthogonal line, include its own role and inclusion in R. All these components have order at most three. Each algebra and module role is separately named. The target attaches the same ordered blocks through its own model isometry.

An isomorphism matching these components must induce an F-algebra automorphism φ^j of each common field model. On the corresponding module its map in model coordinates is
$$
x \longmapsto \lambda\,\phi^j(x)
$$
for some λ in E^×. Indeed its value at 1 is λ and compatibility with the multiplication action gives the formula. The map is a model trace-form isometry because the residual map preserves B. Thus for every z in E its effect on the trace pairing yields
$$
\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F}\big((\lambda\sigma(\lambda)-1)z\big)=0 .
$$
One can obtain every z as σ(φ^j(x))φ^j(y) by taking x=1 and varying y, and $\operatorname{Tr}(\phi^j(w))=\operatorname{Tr}(w)$. Nondegeneracy of the ordinary trace pairing forces λσ(λ)=1. Multiplication by λ has F-determinant N_(E/F)(λ)=N_(K/F)(λσ(λ))=1. Meanwhile det_F φ^j = (−1)^j, because φ cycles the even number of embedding coordinates. The determinant on the field block is exactly (−1)^j.

This scalar is read by one linear inclusion. In the common E model, take the trace-zero F-line in the unique quadratic subfield and include it as a new role in the algebra role E. This subfield is the kernel of φ²−1. Its trace-zero line is the kernel of φ+1 on that subfield, so it has a computable basis. The induced map on it is multiplication by (−1)^j, which is the required determinant. The optional orthogonal line already provides its own determinant. Use the same basis conventions on both copies of each model line.

In these coordinates, multiplication of the model scalar contributions from the at most two field blocks and the possible orthogonal line gives a single scalar line with multiplier s. One implements this multiplication by nonzero bilinear maps between one-dimensional roles, whose preservation forces the product of the input multipliers on the output. Let r_X=det P_X and r_Y=det P_Y in the original residual coordinates and the common model coordinates. The raw determinant on R is (r_Y/r_X)s. To read it as the matrix of the multiplier line map, use basis r_X^(−1) times the model scalar basis on the source line and r_Y^(−1) times the model scalar basis on the target line, and express the attachment maps in those bases. For a prescribed raw determinant δ, mark the source basis vector against δ times the target basis vector. All quantities used for this calibration are computed separately on the two input forms and the shared chosen model.

## 3. Preserve actual transporter profiles

The new choice of ordered decomposition and regular field actions may require changing the original witness within its coset. Given an original residual isometry f, write u=P_Y^(−1) f P_X for its isometry of the common model. If det u=1, put a=u^(−1). If det u=−1, choose v to act by φ on one field block and by the identity elsewhere, and put a=v u^(−1). The Frobenius action preserves H_m and has determinant −1. Also it normalizes that block's multiplication action by its algebra automorphism φ. Thus in both cases a is a model isometry of determinant one, and au is either the identity or the specified field Frobenius on the marked model. Each model in dimension at least three has a field block.

By the stated promise, the target isometry P_Y a P_Y^(−1) is induced by a target-system automorphism that preserves all old marks and whose other tested determinants equal one. Compose the original transporter with this automorphism. The composition preserves the original determinant values, and its marked residual action extends to the new field, module, line and multiplier roles. Conversely any isomorphism after attachment preserves the old system and provides its actual calibrated residual determinant by the preceding calculation. The proof concerns the existence of a transporter with the desired profile, while each marked transporter supplies its own determinant.

Several residual marks can be made together, for example if for each residual the neutral target automorphisms that act identically on all the other selected residuals still induce its full SO group. Then successive corrections preserve all previously imposed residual markings and all original determinants. If the same correction changes another installed residual action, individual SO surjectivity without the simultaneous preservation property does not justify joint normalization.

## 4. Size and ordinary compilation

All spaces and tables in the attachment have explicit polynomial size in the input dimensions. Field and module roles together have dimension linear in d. Their multiplication, action and inclusion arrays can be written densely in polynomial time, and Gaussian elimination computes the needed subfields and frames. No large exterior algebra is used in dimensions at least three.

For one selected residual the number of new tensor components is bounded. If the entire typed system has a polynomial list of components, pack it into a fixed signature before invoking the usual ordinary three-tensor compiler. For completeness, pad each component to order three with a marked trivial line. Make one separate occurrence space for each slot, as well as one for each original role. Attach an algebra F^ell with one primitive idempotent per label, and a module of respective multiplicities 1 to ell. This freezes the labels by their distinct multiplicities. Its actions on the direct sums of original roles and slot roles force every summand to be individually preserved. A linear map from the slot direct sum to the original direct sum uses the identity on each underlying slot copy and forces its transporter map to equal that of its original role. Put the original components in the corresponding slot blocks of eight order-three arrays, one for each primal or dual slot pattern. The packed system has a constant number of components and polynomial dense size. Its isomorphisms restrict to original typed isomorphisms, and each original isomorphism extends by taking the prescribed maps on its copies. Apply the ordinary compiler to the fixed-size list of components to obtain one pair of ordinary three-tensors. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

This is a sufficient reduction for the stated orthogonal residual promise, over every fixed odd field. It uses the explicit trace Gram form from the Riemann proof release. The reduction and frame calculation are written mathematics.

## 5. Normalization by the determinant and spinor kernel

For d at least three the surjectivity assumption in the theorem can be weakened. Write Ω(R) for the common kernel of determinant and spinor norm in O(R). It suffices that every element of Ω(R) lift to an actual target-system automorphism preserving the existing marks and having all the other tested determinants equal to one. This holds, in particular, if the restriction from the whole target-system automorphism group to O(R) is surjective. Dimensions zero through two are treated in Section 1.

Use the spinor norm sp with values in F^×/(F^×)^2, normalized so that reflection in an anisotropic vector w has spinor norm the class of B(w,w). This is a homomorphism. On a nondegenerate orthogonal summand W, the map acting as minus the identity on W and as the identity on its orthogonal complement has spinor norm the Gram determinant of W modulo squares, by expressing it as the product of the reflections in an orthogonal basis of W. [Zywina, Section 2.2 and Lemma 2.1](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~zywina/papers/BigOrthogonal.pdf)

In one of the trace Gram blocks of the model, let the bar denote σ. For μ in E^× define
$$
r_\mu(x)=-\frac{\mu}{\bar\mu}\bar x.
$$
This acts as minus the identity on μK and as the identity on μ times the trace-zero K-line of E/K. These summands are orthogonal since their trace-pairing cross terms have zero trace to K. Both are nondegenerate, as can also be seen by computing the trace form on each line over K. Extend r_μ by the identity on every other block. In the F-basis of μK obtained from a fixed basis of K, its form is the form on K given by
$$
(z,w)\longmapsto 2\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}\big(N_{E/K}(\mu)zw\big).
$$
The ratio of this Gram determinant to the corresponding Gram determinant for K is N_(K/F)(N_(E/K)(μ)). Thus sp(r_μ r_1) is the squareclass of N_(E/F)(μ). Each r_μ acts by minus the identity on an m-dimensional F-space, so r_μ r_1 has determinant one. The composition r_μ r_1 acts on E by multiplication by μ/μ̄, hence it preserves the field-action markings with the identity on the field role. As the finite-field norm is surjective there is such a map of nonsquare spinor norm.

The q-Frobenius on the same block also preserves the marked field actions with its Frobenius map on the field role, and has determinant minus one. Together these maps give a marked model isometry for each of the four possible pairs of determinant and spinor norm. Given any original residual isometry, express it as a model isometry u as in Section 3. Choose a marked model isometry v with the same determinant and spinor norm as u. Then vu^(−1) belongs to Ω in the model. Its conjugate by the target frame can be lifted using the weakened promise, and composition gives exactly the marked residual isometry v without changing any designated determinant. The choice of v is a proof of witness preservation. The reduction itself still builds the same field and scalar attachments, without computing v or an automorphism lift.

Here is the commutator argument for the full-image assertion, including q = 3. Orthogonal isometries are generated by reflections. To see this, for anisotropic x and y of the same norm, the sum of the norms of x−y and x+y is 4B(x,x), so at least one of these two vectors is anisotropic. Reflection in x−y sends x to y when it is anisotropic. Otherwise reflection in x+y followed by reflection in y sends x to y. Given an orthogonal map, compose by such reflections to fix one anisotropic vector and induct on its nondegenerate orthogonal complement. This argument also shows that reflections in vectors of the same norm squareclass are conjugate, by first scaling either vector to match their norms. Thus in the abelianization there are at most two generating images of reflections, each of order dividing two. The commutator subgroup has index at most four. In dimensions d at least three the marked model construction just proved that determinant and spinor norm jointly surject onto a group of order four, so their common kernel is exactly the commutator subgroup.

A surjective restriction map from the actual target-system automorphism group onto O(R) maps its commutator subgroup onto that of O(R). Every determinant character has abelian codomain, so it is trivial on this commutator subgroup. This supplies the neutral lifts of Ω even if individual target-system automorphisms have nontrivial determinants on other roles. For several residuals, simultaneous surjectivity of the restriction onto the product of their O groups suffices by the same argument, because the commutator subgroup of that product is the product of the individual commutator subgroups. Separate surjectivity of each projection does not supply this joint assumption.
