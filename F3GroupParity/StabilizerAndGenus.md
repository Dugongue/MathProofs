# Even isomorphism for special exponent-three groups

## Statements

A compact input consists of an alternating bilinear map $\beta:V\times V\to Z$ over $F=\mathbf F_3$, in ordered bases, with zero common radical and spanning image. The associated special group is $V\oplus Z$ with multiplication
$$
 (v,z)(v',z')=(v+v',z+z'+\tfrac12\beta(v,v')).
$$
An isomorphism induces $(g,h)\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\times\operatorname{GL}(Z)$ preserving the bracket, and every such bracket transporter lifts. An additive central shear has determinant one. Thus the sign of the isomorphism as a permutation of the group elements in the given coordinate order is $\det_F g\,\det_F h$. The sign identity follows on elementary transvections, which have order three, and by counting the $3^{d-1}$ transpositions of pairs of nonzero coordinate values for a single negative scaling in additive dimension $d$. These matrices generate $\operatorname{GL}_d(F)$.

The following are deterministic results.

1. The determinant image of the subgroup fixing $Z$ pointwise can be computed in polynomial time. The determinant image of a second explicit subgroup, obtained from units of the centroid, can also be computed in polynomial time. If either image contains $-1$ at either endpoint, forgetting parity and compiling the bracket comparison gives one ordinary tensor-isomorphism instance over $F$.
2. Prescribing a particular map $h:Z\to Z'$ gives a polynomial-time even-isomorphism test. As a consequence all even comparisons with $\dim_F Z=O(\sqrt{\log L})$ are in deterministic polynomial time for compact input length $L$.
3. If the intrinsic centroid is a field $E$, all even comparisons with $\dim_E Z\le2$ are in deterministic polynomial bit time, including when the extension degree $[E:F]$ grows. This includes all singular alternating pencils in centroid genus two. The resulting many-one reduction can output a fixed affirmative or negative ordinary $S_3$ subgroup-conjugacy instance, or the fixed respective ordinary TI instance.
4. At $\dim_F Z=O(\log L)$, explicit rank profiles give a further sufficient polynomial-time test when the number of possible frame images is polynomial. There is also a polynomial-time adaptive Turing reduction to ordinary TI for the entire logarithmic-center family.

Here $S_N$ subgroup conjugacy means conjugacy of subgroups acting on their written permutation domain. It is a separate target from abstract isomorphism of groups given by permutation generators.

## Strict stabilizer determinants

Let $k$ be a finite field of characteristic three, possibly an extension of $F$. For a full $k$-bimap as above, consider
$$
 {\cal A}=\{(a,b)\in\operatorname{End}_k(V)^2:
                  \beta(ax,y)=\beta(x,by)\text{ for all }x,y\}.
$$
Multiplication is $(a,b)(c,d)=(ac,db)$. Alternation gives the involution $(a,b)^*=(b,a)$. Zero common radical makes the first projection faithful, so identify this algebra with a matrix algebra acting on $V$. A pointwise-output isometry is exactly a unit $a$ with $a^*a=1$. The algebra and involution are computed from linear equations.

Let $J$ be the radical, and write
$$
 {\cal A}/J=\prod_i M_{d_i}(E_i).
$$
At characteristic three this explicit decomposition can be computed in deterministic bit polynomial time, even when $k$ varies. Let $m_i$ be the total multiplicity of the simple $E_i^{d_i}$ on the successive quotients $J^j V/J^{j+1}V$. Multiply by a lift of the $i$-th central idempotent on each quotient and divide its image dimension over $k$ by $d_i[E_i:k]$ to compute these multiplicities. The component dimensions and the powers of $J$ are ordinary linear algebra. For a unit $a$ with image $(a_i)_i$, restriction of scalars and multiplicativity on the filtration give
$$
 \det_F a=\prod_i\operatorname{Nm}_{E_i/F}(\det_{E_i}a_i)^{m_i}.
$$
All units in $1+J$ have determinant one because their first projections are unipotent.

The involution descends, and every unitary element in the quotient lifts to a unitary element of ${\cal A}$. To see this, first lift to an arbitrary unit $a$. Then $s=a^*a$ is self-adjoint and lies in $1+J$. For $3^t$ at least the nilpotence exponent of $J$, one has $s^{3^t}=1$ by the binomial identity for a single element. Choose an integer $u$ with $2u+1\equiv0\bmod 3^t$. The element $a s^u$ is unitary since $(s^u)^*s s^u=s^{2u+1}=1$, and it has the given image.

The determinant image contains $-1$ precisely in either of these cases.

- A pair of simple factors exchanged by $*$ has $m_i+m_j$ odd. A unitary element on this pair is specified by an arbitrary unit on one factor, and the involute of its inverse on the other. The norms of their determinants are inverse because a field isomorphism preserves the norm to $F$. Their resulting product ranges over the $(m_i-m_j)$-th powers of $F^\times$.
- A self-dual factor of orthogonal type has $[E_i:F]m_i$ odd. Its orthogonal group over $E_i$ realizes the determinants $1,-1$, with $\operatorname{Nm}_{E_i/F}(-1)=(-1)^{[E_i:F]}$.

A self-dual symplectic factor has determinant one. In a self-dual unitary factor the determinant has norm one to the fixed subfield under the center involution, and hence norm one to $F$. These cases exhaust the factors by the finite-field classification of involutions on full matrix algebras. The type is computable from the given involution. For a self-dual factor check first whether the involution acts trivially on its center. If it does, the dimensions of the self-adjoint subspace over $E_i$ are $d_i(d_i+1)/2$ in the orthogonal case and $d_i(d_i-1)/2$ in the symplectic case. Indeed the adjoint is given by conjugate transpose by a symmetric or skew-symmetric Gram matrix, and multiplication by that Gram matrix identifies the fixed space with the corresponding symmetric or skew-symmetric matrices. This equips the strict sign test with an explicit polynomial-time implementation. [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18 and Section 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495), [Brooksbank and Wilson, *Computing isometry groups of Hermitian maps*](https://www.math.colostate.edu/~jwilson/products.html).

## Centroid units and one ordinary tensor comparison

Compute the centroid algebra
$$
 C=\{(a,c):\beta(ax,y)=\beta(x,ay)=c\beta(x,y)\}.
$$
The equations are linear. Faithfulness on $V$ follows from the spanning condition, and faithfulness on $Z$ follows from the common radical condition. The algebra is commutative: for two centroid elements, computing $\beta(ax,by)$ by applying their two actions in either order shows their $Z$-actions commute, which forces commutation on $V$ as well. The components of $C/\operatorname{rad} C$ are finite fields. For any unit $(a,c)$ of $C$, the pair $(a,c^2)$ is a bracket pseudo-isometry, since $\beta(ax,ay)=c^2\beta(x,y)$. The factor $c^2$ has determinant one over $F$. By the same radical-filtration computation, the determinants of the actions of $C^\times$ on $V$ contain $-1$ exactly when some field component has odd multiplicity on the associated graded module. Every quotient unit lifts to a unit, and the norms are surjective.

Either of the two polynomially recognizable odd-correction conditions suffices to turn any ordinary witness into an even one, by composing with an appropriate automorphism at that endpoint. The remaining ordinary comparison is one TI instance. One explicit route uses $F1\oplus V\oplus Z$, with $1$ a unit, $xy=\beta(x,y)$ on $V\times V$, and all other products within the radical zero. This is associative, with radical $V\oplus Z$ and its square $Z$. Ordinary algebra isomorphisms are exactly bracket transporters with arbitrary central shears. Isotopies of unital associative multiplication normalize to algebra isomorphisms: for $R(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$, the elements $a=P(1)$ and $b=Q(1)$ are units by surjectivity, and $\phi(x)=a^{-1}R(x)b^{-1}$ is an algebra isomorphism by substitution. Hence the two multiplication tensors give the required ordinary instance.

The centroid test can succeed when the strict test has trivial determinant image. Let $E=\mathbf F_9$, $V=E^3$, $Z=\Lambda_E^2 V$, and use the exterior bracket. Any fixed-output isometry is $E$-linear: for $a\in E$ compare $\beta(g(ax),g(y))$ and $\beta(a g(x),g(y))$ and use zero radical. Then $\Lambda^2_E g=I$, which in dimension three forces $g=\pm I$ from the identity $\Lambda^2 g=(\det g)g^{-\mathsf T}$ in volume-dual coordinates. Both signs have determinant one on the six-dimensional $F$-space. But $aI$ on $V$ and $a^2 I$ on $Z$ gives an odd pseudo-isometry for $\operatorname{Nm}_{E/F}(a)=-1$.

Conversely, the strict test can succeed when the centroid test has trivial determinant image. Take the orthogonal sum of the singular alternating pencil blocks of minimal indices $1$ and $2$, with the same two-dimensional output space $Z$, over $F$. They have dimensions $3,5$ and zero common radical. Any centroid element preserves both summands, since its pairing with a vector in the other summand must be zero. In the first summand use $\beta((u,w),(u',w'))=uw'-u'w$ on $F\oplus F^2$. Pairing the first-coordinate unit with the second summand $F^2$ of this block forces the centroid action on the output to be a scalar. The zero common radical then forces the same scalar on both whole input blocks. Thus the centroid is $F$ and its multiplicity on $V$ is $8$, even. Negating only the dimension-three block is a strict isometry of negative determinant.
Odd strict isometries need not be found among involutions. On $V=E\oplus E^2$ take $Z=E^2$ and $\beta((u,w),(u',w'))=uw'-u'w$. The subspace $0\oplus E^2$ is intrinsic, since its nonzero vectors have adjoint rank one over $E$ (rank two over $F$), and vectors whose first coordinate is nonzero have rank two over $E$. A strict isometry is therefore triangular. Its cross pairing forces $g(u,w)=(au,a^{-1}w+L(u))$ for some $a\in E^\times$, and pairing two first-coordinate vectors gives $L(u)=ub$ for a fixed $b\in E^2$. This parametrizes the strict group. Its determinant over $F$ is $\operatorname{Nm}_{E/F}(a)^{-1}$, so negative determinants do occur. A strict involution must have $a^2=1$, which makes its norm, and hence determinant, positive.

## Fixed center maps and logarithmic-center consequences

For a particular invertible map $h:Z\to Z'$, compose the output coordinates of $\beta$ with $h$. The existence of a compatible $g$ is the ordinary isometry test of two ordered alternating matrix tuples. The algorithm constructs such a $g$ in deterministic bit polynomial time in characteristic three. If it succeeds, all remaining $g$ for that $h$ differ by a strict stabilizer. Thus a candidate $h$ is feasible for the required sign if and only if the desired sign divided by $\det_F g\,\det_F h$ lies in the strict determinant image computed above. When $k$ is an extension and maps are required to be $k$-linear, use the same formula with norms to $F$. [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 7](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495).

Exhausting at most $3^{m^2}$ matrices for $m=\dim_F Z$ proves the claimed polynomial result when $m=O(\sqrt{\log L})$, with no condition on ranks of the contractions. For $m=O(\log L)$, one can instead enumerate the projective center functionals and their ranks, then select a source basis of $m$ projective lines minimizing the product of their rank-class sizes. This is greedy linear matroid optimization with the logarithms of the class sizes as weights. The order of these weights is computable by integer comparisons. Reject any mismatch in the source and target rank-class sizes. For each selected source line of rank-class size $c_i$, a target image has at most $2c_i$ choices as a nonzero vector. Checking all resulting maps on the dual center and using the fixed-center test gives deterministic polynomial time on the recognizable promise that $2^m\prod_i c_i$ is polynomial. This promise allows logarithmically many dimensions. For instance let the bracket be an orthogonal sum of symplectic forms with independent output lines and half-sizes $2^{j+1}-1$. They are superincreasing, so the axes are singled out by ranks. All half-sizes are odd, and every automorphism has total determinant one, as each block's domain determinant is the odd power of its multiplier and its output determinant is that multiplier. A reflected frame then gives an ordinary-isomorphic pair with no even witness.

For a different result at logarithmic $m$, use an ordinary TI decision oracle to ask whether a bracket transporter exists that sends specified center vectors to specified target vectors. Such a pin query is a fixed-signature tensor system. Include the bracket with linked equal input maps and a map from a marked auxiliary $t$-dimensional space to the center listing the $t$ pin vectors. Pin its first vector and its nilpotent shift to force its map to be the identity. These tensors compile to one ordinary TI instance per query. To find a center transporter, extend its restrictions along a basis, trying every one of at most $3^m$ images at each step. For the image of the source automorphism group on the center, use its chain of pointwise stabilizers of basis prefixes. At each step find one representative mapping the next basis vector to each achievable image under that stabilizer, recovering a full center map by the same pinning procedure. These representatives generate the whole image by induction down the chain. There are $O(m^2 3^{2m})$ calls. Lift each recovered center map using the fixed-center algorithm. Together with the strict determinant image their signs generate the sign image of the whole source stabilizer. Test the sign of a recovered transporter modulo that image. This is an adaptive Turing reduction. [Futorny, Grochow and Sergeichuk, *Wildness of the problems of classifying two-dimensional spaces of linear operators and three-dimensional matrices*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219), [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4).

## Genus at most two over the intrinsic centroid field

Suppose the centroid is a field $E$. This is recognizable by the fixed-characteristic algebra algorithm. Every bracket transporter conjugates the source centroid onto the target centroid, so it is semilinear over a field isomorphism between the two centroid fields. Compute the two fields and choose an isomorphism between them in deterministic polynomial bit time. All isomorphisms are then obtained by composing with the $[E:F]$ Frobenius powers. Compare the two relative dimensions and reject mismatches. Choose bases over $E$ by greedily finding a new vector outside the span of the already selected $E$-lines. For each field identification, express the two bimaps over the same field, with the source coordinates conjugated by that identification. The unknown remaining comparison is $E$-linear. Compute the $F$-linear determinants of these two explicit coordinate identifications from the supplied frames. The sign prescribed on the $E$-linear comparison is the original sign multiplied by the ratio of these frame signs. Thus the rest of the proof works over a fixed identified $E$, with $\operatorname{Nm}_{E/F}(\det_E g\det_E h)$ prescribed. [Lenstra, *Finding isomorphisms between finite fields*](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf), [Brooksbank, Maglione and Wilson, Theorem 4.1](https://joshmaglione.com/papers/Genus2.pdf).

For relative genus one the alternating form is nondegenerate. A single symplectic basis comparison gives a congruence at label one. If $n=2r=\dim_E V$, scaling the central dual label by $a$ lifts by multiplying a Lagrangian half by $a^{-1}$ and fixing the other half. The total determinant sign can therefore vary by $\operatorname{Nm}_{E/F}(a^{1-r})$, $a\in E^\times$. This image is computed by the parity of $1-r$. All fixed-label corrections have determinant one by the Pfaffian identity. This solves genus one.

For relative genus two put $q=|E|$, and write $A(w),B(w)$ for the alternating matrix pencils on the dual center. The compatibility equation is $g^{\mathsf T}B(Pw)g=A(w)$, where $P=(h^{\mathsf T})^{-1}$. Therefore the sign to test is $\operatorname{Nm}_{E/F}(\det_E g\det_E P)$, since inversion in the two-element target of the norm has no effect. The classical alternating Kronecker form decomposes each pencil under congruence into hyperbolic blocks
$$
 {\cal H}(M)=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&M\\-M^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
For a regular block $M$ is an ordinary primary regular block. For a singular block of minimal index $j$ one may choose $M$ of size $(j+1)\times j$ given by multiplication by $w$ from binary forms of degree $j-1$ to binary forms of degree $j$, with the evident zero-index convention. Transposing this block only exchanges the two summands in ${\cal H}(M)$, so the chosen orientation is unrestricted. We use the canonical form only to produce lifts and to identify their determinants. The raw fixed-label congruence algorithm still constructs the base comparison. [Brooksbank, Maglione and Wilson, Proposition 3.9 and Section 5.1](https://joshmaglione.com/papers/Genus2.pdf), [Sergeichuk, *Classification problems for systems of forms and linear mappings*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.0823).

A strict pencil map $M(Pw)G=HM(w)$ gives a congruence on the hyperbolic block by $\operatorname{diag}(H^{-\mathsf T},G)$, with determinant $\det G/\det H$. The same equation holds for a pair of different half pencils. Let $J_0$ be the sum of the singular minimal indices of the source, counted with their multiplicities, and let $n_{\mathrm{reg}}$ be the total dimension of its regular half. For multiplication by $w$ on symmetric powers, any $S\in\operatorname{GL}_2(E)$ has a strict lift
$$
 (G,H)=(\operatorname{Sym}^{j-1}S,\operatorname{Sym}^jS).
$$
It follows from multiplication in the binary polynomial algebra that this is a lift. Triangularization over an algebraic closure gives $\det\operatorname{Sym}^j S=(\det S)^{j(j+1)/2}$, so the singular half contributes $(\det S)^{-J_0}$ to $\det g$.

### Finding the required pencil data

If $q\le2n$, where $n=\dim_E V$, real enumeration of all $q^4$ label matrices and the fixed-label test is polynomial in $n$. Otherwise find the generic rank $r$ of each pencil by testing $n+1$ distinct affine labels. A nonzero $r$-minor has degree at most $n$, so some tested rank is generic. Check ranks on $2n+1$ distinct labels to find a direction that is non-drop for both. A common projective reparameterization puts it at infinity. Conjugating both input label coordinates by the same value does not change the determinant test.

Compute one nonzero generic-rank minor as a polynomial in the new affine parameter by interpolation. Factor it deterministically. Its geometric roots contain every rank-drop point. For each distinct irreducible factor pass to the field defined by that factor and check the rank at its root, retaining exactly the factors giving rank drop. The resulting monic squarefree polynomial records the rank-drop set. Characteristic three guarantees polynomial bit factoring, even for the extension fields used here. Compare the two generic ranks and the degrees of these polynomials.

To recover the singular minimal indices, compute the dimension of the polynomial nullspace of $A(z,1)v(z)=0$ for $\deg v\le t$, by coefficient linear algebra for $0\le t\le n+1$. Each singular hyperbolic block of index $j$ contributes $\max(0,t-j+1)$, because its one right nullspace generator has polynomial degree $j$. A right generator is primitive and has consecutive monomial coordinates up to signs. Regular blocks contribute zero. Second differences recover all the singular indices. At a root $\theta$ use the truncated ring $E(\theta)[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^a)$, for $1\le a\le n+1$, and compute the nullity of $A(\theta+\varepsilon,1)$ as a linear map over $E(\theta)$. Subtract $a(n-r)$. This subtracts the singular contribution, since a maximal minor on each singular block is a unit at every finite label. Each regular half-block of length $j$ at this root contributes $2\min(a,j)$ to what remains. Its two copies have an invertible minor of size $j-1$ and last local invariant of order $j$, so this follows by unit elimination in the truncated ring. First differences and then differences of those counts recover the lengths with multiplicities. They also give the dimensions of the regular half supported at the given root or quadratic pair. All these matrices have polynomial size. [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18 and the fixed-characteristic decomposition](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495).

### At least three geometric rank-drop points

Form the tensor product of six root algebras, three from each of the two squarefree rank-drop polynomials. Its dimension is at most $n^6$. Compute its finite-field components deterministically at characteristic three and keep the components in which each ordered triple is distinct. In each component the projective transformation from one triple to the other is unique. Compute its $2\times2$ matrix by sending each ordered triple to $0,1,\infty$, then composing with the inverse on the target. Normalize by its first nonzero entry. The normalized entries lie in $E$ exactly when each is fixed by the $q$-th power map. Retain those rational candidates and express them in $E$ by linear algebra. Every transporter must map three distinct rank-drop points to distinct rank-drop points, so its projective map appears. The process uses no compositum of all the root fields. The fixed-characteristic finite-algebra algorithm applies to the explicit regular multiplication matrices of the root algebra.

For a candidate representative $P_0$ perform the fixed-label congruence test. If it is accepted with witness $g_0$, all scalar multiples $\lambda P_0$ also lift. In the source hyperbolic form use a map multiplying the right half by $\lambda^{-1}$ and fixing the left half. This exactly offsets the scalar multiplying every coefficient of $B(P_0w)$. Its determinant is $\lambda^{-(J_0+n_{\mathrm{reg}})}$. The label determinant also acquires $\lambda^2$, whose norm is one. Consequently all signs over the projective class are obtained from the base sign by the image of this power of $\lambda$, and by the independently computed strict sign group. If $P_0$ failed the congruence test no scalar representative can work, by reversing the same half scaling. Norm surjectivity and exponent parity decide the image.

### At most two geometric rank-drop points

Reject different split types. For each allowable ordered matching choose a base affine label $P_0$ sending the source root or roots to the indicated targets, preserving the common point at infinity. For one rational root use translation, and for a split pair use slope and intercept. For a quadratic-conjugate pair work in a common quadratic extension, with roots $(t,t^q)$ and $(u,u^q)$. The slope $(u-u^q)/(t-t^q)$ and intercept $u-t(u-u^q)/(t-t^q)$ are fixed by Frobenius. Both Frobenius-compatible matchings can be considered, using deterministic fixed-characteristic factoring or an explicit quadratic field isomorphism. With no roots take $P_0=I$. A label with the chosen matching is $P_0 S$ for a source label $S$ fixing each marked root individually. The formulas below give a lift of every such $S$ to the source congruence group. Consequently if any transporter induces the matching then $P_0$ admits a fixed-label witness. Once that witness is obtained all remaining signs are its sign times the image from these residual lifts and the strict subgroup.

For no roots there are only singular blocks and all $S\in\operatorname{GL}_2(E)$ are allowed. Their total sign is $\operatorname{Nm}_{E/F}((\det S)^{1-J_0})$.

For one root put it at $x=0$ in the source canonical block coordinates. The regular half is $xI+yN$ with nilpotent Jordan blocks, and $S=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\b&d\end{pmatrix}$. On a block modeled as $E[t]/(t^\ell)$, substitution $t\mapsto dt/(a+bt)$ gives the domain map $G$; take $H=(aI+bN)G$. Multiplication by the substituted $t$ intertwines with $G$, so these maps satisfy the strict lift equation. Their determinant ratio is $a^{-\ell}$. After summing the block sizes, the total sign is
$$
 \operatorname{Nm}_{E/F}\big((ad)^{1-J_0} a^{-n_{\mathrm{reg}}}\big).
$$
All $a,d\in E^\times$ occur and $b$ has no effect.

For two rational roots put the marked directions on the two axes. The regular half is the sum of $xI+yN_1$ and $yI+xN_2$, and $S=\operatorname{diag}(a,d)$. Applying the preceding lifts to each root gives the total sign
$$
 \operatorname{Nm}_{E/F}\big((ad)^{1-J_0}a^{-n_1}d^{-n_2}\big),
$$
where $n_1,n_2$ are their regular half dimensions. Again both diagonal scalars are free.

For a quadratic-conjugate pair work over $E'/E$ quadratic. Choose the two root axes and regular half bases as conjugate pairs. The nilpotent blocks, and their bases for the second root, are conjugate to those for the first. This is possible by passing to the primary spaces of a regular normalized slice and taking a basis of one and its conjugate in the other. Rational pointwise label maps are precisely $\operatorname{diag}(a,a^q)$ in these axes, $a\in E'^\times$. The two regular lifts above are exchanged by Frobenius and descend. If the regular half dimension at either geometric root is $u=n_{\mathrm{reg}}/2$, their determinant ratio over $E$ is $\operatorname{Nm}_{E'/E}(a)^{-u}$. The total sign is
$$
 \operatorname{Nm}_{E/F}\big(\operatorname{Nm}_{E'/E}(a)^{1-J_0-u}\big).
$$
Surjectivity of finite-field norms and the displayed exponents give the exact image in each case. Congruence changes used for the source canonical form conjugate the automorphism maps, so they do not affect these determinant values in the actual source frame.

Every stage uses polynomially sized data and a polynomial number of field and bit operations. The Frobenius enumeration has size $[E:F]$, the root algebra has degree at most $n^6$ over $E$, and the largest small-field label enumeration has $q^4\le(2n)^4$ elements. This proves the genus-two statement for varying centroid fields and for all regular and singular mixtures. For either polynomial decision subclass, a fixed conjugate pair can be $(1,1)$ as subgroups of $S_3$, and a fixed nonconjugate pair can be $(1,\langle(123)\rangle)$.

## Verification and scope

The determinant image criteria, fixed-label tests, rank-based subclass, and genus-two decision algorithm are established by the informal proofs above. The logarithmic-center reduction using arbitrary center pinning is Turing. The one-query odd-correction result reduces to ordinary tensor or abstract group isomorphism over the stated compact model. The polynomial-time decision families also reduce to ordinary symmetric-group subgroup conjugacy. Direct checks over $\mathbf F_3$ verified the two slice equations and the determinant ratio for 240 singular symmetric-power lifts and 84 nilpotent regular lifts. These supplement the informal proof. The general many-one reduction for unrestricted compact special exponent-three groups remains open in this argument.
