# Polynomial-time even isomorphism for products of groups of relative genus at most two

Let $F=\mathbb F_3$. For a directly indecomposable compact special exponent-three group, let $C$ be the centroid of its alternating commutator map $\beta:X\times X\to Z$. It is local. Let $E$ be its residue field, realized canonically as a subfield of $C$ as proved below. Call $\dim_E Z$ the relative genus of this factor.

**Theorem.** Given two compact special exponent-three groups such that every indecomposable factor has relative genus at most two, even isomorphism is decidable deterministically in polynomial bit time. The result allows arbitrary local centroid radicals, arbitrary extension degrees, and arbitrary repetitions of the factors. It gives a same-field many-one reduction to one ordinary compact group-isomorphism comparison, or one symmetric-group subgroup-conjugacy comparison, by outputting fixed affirmative and negative instances. The promise is recognizable in polynomial time.

For explicit fixed compact answers one can use the ordinary self-comparison of a group of order $3^5$, or compare two groups of the same order with distinct center dimensions. For the latter take a nondegenerate alternating bracket on $F^4$ valued in $F$, and the quotient bracket on $F^3$ valued in $\bigwedge^2 F^3/K$ for a line $K$. In the second case $x\wedge F^3$ has dimension two for every nonzero $x$, so the common radical is zero. The same two pairs give fixed comparisons of the regular subgroups in $S_{243}$.

## Canonical coefficient fields of local centroids

Use the [centroid splitting algorithm](CentroidProductParity.md#intrinsic-factor-recovery) to compute the directly indecomposable factors and the quotient $C/J$ of each local centroid by its nilradical. For a local centroid this quotient is a field of order $q=3^e$. The subset
$$
E_0=\{b\in C:b^q=b\}
$$
is a subring because $C$ is commutative and the $q$th power is a ring endomorphism. The quotient induces an isomorphism $E_0\to C/J$. It is injective because a nilpotent element fixed by the $q$th power is zero. It is surjective because for any lift $a$ of a residue element, $(a^q-a)\in J$. Raising to $q^t$ with $q^t\ge\dim_F C$ shows that $a^{q^t}$ is fixed by the $q$th power and has the same residue. Thus $E_0$ is a field intrinsically defined in $C$. On the explicit $F$-algebra, compute it by taking the kernel of the linear map $b\mapsto b^q-b$. Exponentiation uses polynomial time because $\log q$ is at most a constant times $\dim_F C$. Using basis extension for its actions gives $E_0$-bases on $X,Z$.

The bracket is $E_0$-bilinear by the defining centroid identities. Any bracket isomorphism conjugates the centroid, carries this canonical field onto the canonical field at the other endpoint, and is semilinear for the resulting field identification on both $X$ and $Z$. Conversely every such semilinear bracket transporter is an $F$-linear transporter in the original coordinates. Therefore we may use the [relative-genus-one and relative-genus-two algorithm](StabilizerAndGenus.md#genus-at-most-two-over-the-intrinsic-centroid-field) on these coefficient fields. The algorithm's field hypothesis is used for this semilinear descent. The alternating pencil classification, the fixed-label adjoint calculation, and the determinant-norm computation use the resulting field action and the explicit bracket. They do not require the centroid to have trivial radical. In detail, compare residue degrees, choose one deterministic isomorphism of the finite fields, and enumerate all its Frobenius translates. For each candidate, view the remaining comparison as $E_0$-linear and calibrate the frame signs over $F$ when converting the two original ordered bases. This gives the exact set of attainable signs for one indecomposable pair. Testing the negative sign can equivalently be done by reflecting one target basis vector.

The hypothesis includes cases with nonzero centroid radical. For example, take $R=E[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ for any finite extension $E/F$, put $X=R^{2r}$ and $Z=R$, and use the standard alternating symplectic bracket over $R$. It spans $R$, has zero common radical, and the centroid contains the $R$-action. Its action on $Z=R$ is faithful and commutes with all multiplication operators from $R$. Any endomorphism of the regular module commuting with those operators is multiplication by its value at $1$. Hence the centroid is exactly $R$, which is local with nonzero radical, and the relative genus over its canonical coefficient field is two. The dimensions and number of such factors may grow freely.

## Assembling products

Make a bipartite graph of the two intrinsic factor lists, putting an edge exactly when the relative-genus algorithm finds at least one attainable sign on that pair. Also retain the two possible local signs on every edge. Use polynomial-time bipartite matching. If there is no perfect matching, there is no ordinary isomorphism. Otherwise take a matching and one attainable sign per edge. Their product, the calibrated frame ratio, and the sign of the induced matching on odd-dimensional additive factor blocks give the sign of a reference isomorphism. The factor formula and calibration are given in [the product calculation](CentroidProductParity.md#intrinsic-factor-recovery).

For this ordinary-isomorphic pair, an odd automorphism is available exactly when some factor on the target admits an odd automorphism or two target factors of odd additive dimension are ordinarily isomorphic. The stated conditions give actual automorphisms, by extension of a single factor automorphism or by exchanging two factors via an isomorphism and its inverse. Conversely, if both tests fail, all permutations of target factors permute only even-dimensional factors. Choose reference isomorphisms from a common coordinate frame for each ordinary factor type. Around each cycle of a matching of target factors, the product of local determinants is the determinant of an automorphism of one factor, because the determinant of the successive basis identifications cancels. That determinant is even by assumption. The permutation part is even as well. Hence every target automorphism is even. This proves the exact correction criterion even with repeated even-dimensional factors.

Decide even isomorphism from the sign of the reference isomorphism and this correction criterion. All comparisons, frame calculations and the matching computation are polynomial. The argument requires only compact coefficient tables. It never lists the exponentially many group elements or regular permutation entries.

The same assembly gives a polynomial-time decision for products of any explicitly recognized indecomposable classes with polynomial-time signed factor comparisons. It also lets a one-query comparison for other factors absorb an invariant collection of even-additive-dimension factors with polynomial-time signed comparison. Compute a matching and a sign on this collection, and use it to calibrate the sign target on the remaining factors. If the collection has an odd correction, ordinary isomorphism of the remaining factors suffices. Crossing an even block with any other block has sign one.
