# A common arithmetic language on a density-one set of quadratic probes

The probe rings are $\mathbb Z$ and the rings of integers of all quadratic number fields. Write $F_d=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)$ for nontrivial signed squarefree $d$, and order the real quadratic probes by their squarefree radicands. All density claims below also hold when real quadratic probes are ordered by discriminant, since the discriminant lies between $d$ and $4d$. The proofs use ordinary arithmetic and the cited number-theoretic theorems.

## 1. Two fixed curves give density-one agreement

**Theorem 1.** There is a computable set $S$ of positive squarefree radicands, with complement of natural density zero among those radicands, on which a single finite existential polynomial system over $\mathbb Z$ defines $\mathbb Z$ in every $\mathcal O_{F_d}$. The same system defines $\mathbb Z$ in every imaginary quadratic integer ring and has integer witnesses for every integer argument. Consequently, one fixed polynomial template realizes a many-one-complete c.e. language identically over all these rings and over $\mathbb Z$.

*Proof.* For positive squarefree $n$, let $E_n:y^2=x^3-n^2x$. Both $E_5$ and $E_{219}$ have positive rational rank, because $5$ and $219$ are congruent numbers. A rational right triangle of area $5$ has sides $(9,40,41)/6$. A rational right triangle of area $219$ has sides $(3025,7008,7633)/220$, from the primitive triangle with parameters $73,48$. Explicit points on the curves are $(25/4,75/8)$ and $(5329/16,293095/64)$, respectively. In both cases the nonintegral $x$-coordinate precludes torsion by Nagell–Lutz.

Given $d>1$ squarefree, put $t(d)=219$ for $d\equiv1,3,6\pmod8$, and put $t(d)=5$ for $d\equiv2,5,7\pmod8$. Let $m(d)$ be the squarefree part of $t(d)d$. Removing an odd square leaves the residue modulo $8$ unchanged. Multiplication by $219\equiv3\pmod8$ maps $1,3,6$ to $3,1,2$, and multiplication by $5$ maps $2,5,7$ to $2,1,3$. Thus $m(d)\equiv1,2,3\pmod8$. The quadratic twist of $E_{t(d)}$ by $d$ is isomorphic over $\mathbb Q$ to $E_{m(d)}$.

Define $S$ by the test $L(E_{m(d)},1)\ne0$. Tunnell’s formulas express this test as nonvanishing of a computable integer coefficient of an explicit series. His noncongruence theorem implies $\operatorname{rank}E_{m(d)}(\mathbb Q)=0$ on $S$. Burungale and Tian’s even-parity density theorem, using Smith's Selmer distribution, gives $o(X)$ exceptions to the test $L(E_m,1)\ne0$ among the relevant squarefree $m\le X$. For each fixed $t=5,219$, the operation $d\mapsto\operatorname{sf}(td)$ on positive squarefree integers is an involution and takes $d\le X$ to $m\le tX$. Hence each choice of $t$ contributes $o(X)$ exceptional $d$, giving the claimed density. [Tunnell, original paper](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/EM22/JT1983.pdf) · [Burungale–Tian, Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03465)

For $d\in S$, the conjugation eigenspaces of the rational Mordell–Weil group give
$$
 \operatorname{rank}E_{t(d)}(F_d)
 =\operatorname{rank}E_{t(d)}(\mathbb Q)+\operatorname{rank}E_{m(d)}(\mathbb Q)
 =\operatorname{rank}E_{t(d)}(\mathbb Q)>0.
$$
For each of the two fixed curves, take the Mazur–Rubin–Shlapentokh polynomial system for rank-stable extensions of degree at most two. Both systems accept every rational integer with witnesses in $\mathbb Z$. On each $F_d$ with $d\in S$, the system for the certified curve has soundness. Conjoin these two systems and the integer definition for all imaginary quadratic integer rings from the arithmetic chapter, Lemma 5. The imaginary system also supplies integer witnesses for every integer argument, and provides soundness in each imaginary quadratic. Call the conjunction $\Theta$. [Mazur–Rubin–Shlapentokh, Theorem 3.1 and Remarks 3.2, 3.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.09963#S3) · [Arithmetic profiles, Lemma 5](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/88b34b8d487ca161124f71a628c64dd251e306f8/SimultaneousComputability/ArithmeticProfiles.md)

Apply MRDP to a fixed many-one-complete c.e. language, and constrain every arithmetic witness by $\Theta$. On $\mathbb Z$ and every certified probe, an accepting solution supplies integer arithmetic witnesses, and any genuine integer solution extends to an accepting solution over $\mathbb Z$. Apply the degree-two trace smoothing of the arithmetic chapter to obtain one smooth hypersurface template. [Arithmetic profiles, Theorem 1](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/88b34b8d487ca161124f71a628c64dd251e306f8/SimultaneousComputability/ArithmeticProfiles.md) $\square$

**Corollary 2.** On the full quadratic probe poset, every uniformly c.e. monotone profile has a smooth integral realization whose answers agree with the prescribed profile on $\mathbb Q$, every imaginary quadratic, and $S$. The realization can have false positives only at real probes outside $S$, uniformly in the input. In particular every profile that is always positive on those exceptional fields is realized exactly.

*Proof.* Apply MRDP to the prescribed rational language, use $\Theta$ to constrain its arithmetic witnesses, and disjoin with the exact base-empty quadratic realization of the prescribed quadratic coordinates from the arithmetic chapter, Theorem 8. The second branch already accepts all prescribed quadratic positives. At the certified probes the first branch accepts only rational positives, which monotonicity makes quadratic positives. At $\mathbb Z$ only the first branch can accept, and it accepts exactly the rational language. Both branches have fixed-format systems in the input, so apply trace smoothing to their disjunction. [Arithmetic profiles, Theorems 1 and 8](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/88b34b8d487ca161124f71a628c64dd251e306f8/SimultaneousComputability/ArithmeticProfiles.md) $\square$

## 2. Uniform-degree decreasing refinements

**Theorem 3.** Every uniformly c.e. monotone profile on the full quadratic probe poset is the coordinatewise intersection of a computable decreasing sequence of positive-existential smooth hypersurface profiles. Each profile in the sequence has the correct rational coordinate and the correct coordinates at every imaginary and certified real quadratic. The polynomial degree is bounded independently of the sequence index, and the ambient dimension grows at most linearly in that index. There is a computable stage by which any given quadratic probe is exact on every input.

*Proof.* For a fixed real quadratic $F_d$, specialize the guarded integer definition $\Phi_{\rm R}$ of the arithmetic chapter to $F_d$. Expand each equation in an integral basis of $\mathcal O_{F_d}$, keeping the tested argument $w$ as the scalar $1\otimes w$. This gives an integer-coefficient system $J_d(w)$ interpreting $\Phi_{\rm R}$ in $\mathcal O_{F_d}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}R$ when evaluated on a probe ring $R$. The basis has two elements and a computable multiplication table. The number of equations, arity, and total degrees are bounded uniformly in $d$. For any integer $w$, witnesses in $\mathcal O_{F_d}$ for $\Phi_{\rm R}$ give witnesses for $J_d$ over $\mathbb Z$ by taking their basis coordinates. For $R=\mathcal O_{F_d}$, the multiplication homomorphism $\mathcal O_{F_d}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal O_{F_d}\to\mathcal O_{F_d}$ sends a solution of $J_d(w)$ to a solution of $\Phi_{\rm R}(w)$. Soundness of the latter gives $w\in\mathbb Z$. [Arithmetic profiles, Lemma 7](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/88b34b8d487ca161124f71a628c64dd251e306f8/SimultaneousComputability/ArithmeticProfiles.md)

Start from the two-branch construction in Corollary 2. Enumerate the real quadratic radicands $d_1,d_2,\ldots$ in increasing order. At stage $j$, place a copy of $J_{d_i}$ on each arithmetic witness of the rational branch for $1\le i\le j$, using fresh variables. Write $Q_j=0$ for the single constraint obtained by replacing all these extra equations with the sum of their squares. On a real quadratic probe this is exactly the conjunction. On an imaginary quadratic the original rational branch is already sound, and all its genuine positives still have integer witnesses for every $J_{d_i}$, so the extra constraint leaves their acceptance unchanged. At $\mathbb Z$, the same integer witnesses preserve acceptance. Consequently stage $j$ is a refinement of stage $j-1$ on all probes and agrees exactly at the first $j$ real quadratics as well as the certified class. An effective stage bound is the position of the probe radicand in the enumeration, or $0$ for an imaginary field or the rational base. The exact base-empty quadratic branch is retained at each stage, so every desired positive remains accepted.

The polynomial $Q_j$ has bounded degree, and uses $O(j)$ extra variables for the fixed arithmetic witness count. The disjunction with the base-empty branch uses a binary selector as in the arithmetic chapter, and keeps the total number of equations and maximum degree bounded independently of $j$. Hence the prime for trace smoothing can be the same at every stage, giving the claimed uniform bound on the degree of the final smooth hypersurface equation. [Arithmetic profiles, Theorems 1 and 2](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/88b34b8d487ca161124f71a628c64dd251e306f8/SimultaneousComputability/ArithmeticProfiles.md) $\square$

## 3. One universal block suffices on all probes

**Theorem 4.** The profiles realized on the full quadratic poset by the following form of bounded-format existential-universal integral test are exactly the uniformly c.e. profiles, with arbitrary compatibility between the base and quadratic coordinates. The universal clause can be fixed once for all inputs and all profiles. It is the absence of integral points on one fixed smooth hypersurface.

*Proof.* Use the following positive-existential test $D$, with no free input. One branch requires $s^2+1+\sum_{h=1}^4 v_h^2=0$, which is solvable over every imaginary quadratic integer ring by the four-square theorem and impossible over a real ring. The other branch requires a unit $u$ with $u^{12}\ne1$, using an inverse witness and the uniform polynomial disequality from the arithmetic chapter, Lemma 5. All units of $\mathbb Z$ and of imaginary quadratic integer rings have order dividing $12$. Every real quadratic integer ring has an infinite-order unit. Thus $D$ is true exactly at the nonrational probes. Its existential system can be smoothed to a fixed hypersurface equation $P_D(\mathbf y)=0$. [Arithmetic profiles, Theorems 1 and 6 and Lemma 5](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/88b34b8d487ca161124f71a628c64dd251e306f8/SimultaneousComputability/ArithmeticProfiles.md)

Let $M_e(\mathbf x)=0$ be a fixed MRDP representation of the desired rational language over $\mathbb Z$, and let $B_e(\mathbf z)=0$ be the exact smoothed base-empty branch for the desired quadratic coordinates. Use the test
$$
 \exists \mathbf x,\mathbf z\ \forall\mathbf y\
 \bigl(B_e(\mathbf z)=0\ \lor\
     (M_e(\mathbf x)=0\ \land\ P_D(\mathbf y)\ne0)\bigr).
$$
At the rational probe $B_e$ has no solutions and $P_D$ never vanishes, so the test gives the rational language. At each quadratic there exists a root of $P_D$, so the test gives exactly $B_e$. The existential witnesses for the unused branch may be arbitrary. Conversely, the effective probe label tells which branch to enumerate, so any test in this form gives a uniformly c.e. profile. Taking MRDP for universal enumeration relations as in the arithmetic chapter gives the same fixed-format formula with a profile-program parameter. [Arithmetic profiles, Theorems 4 and 8](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/88b34b8d487ca161124f71a628c64dd251e306f8/SimultaneousComputability/ArithmeticProfiles.md) $\square$

## 4. A sufficient common-language criterion through the multiquadratic tower

**Proposition 5.** Let $M^+$ be the compositum of all real quadratic number fields. Suppose there is a totally real number field $K$ and an abelian variety $A/K$ with positive finite rank over $KM^+$. Then the full quadratic realization holds.

*Proof.* Choose a finite field $L$ between $K$ and $KM^+$ containing enough points to span $A(KM^+)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q$. Its rank is positive, and $A(KM^+)/A(L)$ is torsion. The Mazur–Rubin–Shlapentokh theorem for totally real algebraic extensions defines $\mathcal O_L$ existentially in $\mathcal O_{KM^+}$, with $\mathcal O_L$-witnesses for each element of $\mathcal O_L$. Use any Diophantine integer definition on the fixed number ring $\mathcal O_L$ and constrain its inputs and witnesses by this definition of $\mathcal O_L$. The resulting definition of $\mathbb Z$ in $\mathcal O_{KM^+}$ has coefficients and integer-input witnesses in $\mathcal O_L$. [Mazur–Rubin–Shlapentokh, Theorem 3.5 and Remark 3.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.09963#S3) · [Koymans–Pagano, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01768v3#S1.SS2)

Expand the equations in an integral basis of $\mathcal O_L$ as above. Over $\mathcal O_{F_d}$ for every real quadratic, any solution pushes under $\mathcal O_L\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal O_{F_d}\to\mathcal O_{KM^+}$ to a sound one. Integer-input witnesses lift from $\mathcal O_L$, so the expanded definition also has witnesses in $\mathbb Z$ on integer arguments. Conjoin the fixed imaginary-quadratic definition and use the two-branch construction of Corollary 2. $\square$

The finite-rank hypothesis ranges over the entire compositum. The theorem constructing a positive-rank abelian variety stable for any prescribed quadratic extension gives a variety that can depend on that extension. Sun’s uniform polynomial packing of real-quadratic witness tuples likewise makes rationality of one packed value sufficient, while his real-quadratic Pell coefficient depends on $d$. The constructions here leave the single-template positive-existential realization on the full quadratic poset open. [Alpöge–Bhargava–Ho–Shnidman, Theorem 1.1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00222-025-01392-3) · [Sun, Theorem 3.1 and Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03992v1#S3)
