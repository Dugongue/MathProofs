# Quadratic profiles and integral obstructions

This note records two results from the simultaneous-computability program and the exact scope of the associated open problems. The [first manuscript](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/88b34b8d487ca161124f71a628c64dd251e306f8/SimultaneousComputability/README.md) proves realization on the entire quadratic antichain when every integral test over $\mathbb Q$ is negative.

## Quadratic profiles with an arbitrary rational test

The full conjecture asks for one smooth hypersurface template for each uniformly computably enumerable profile on $\mathbb Q$ and all quadratic fields, subject to the condition that a positive base-field test remains positive in every extension. It is equivalent to an identical realization over every probe of the specific universal c.e. language $U=\{2^p(2e+1):\text{program }p\text{ halts on }e\}$, for a fixed effective enumeration of programs. For the forward direction prescribe $U$ at every probe. For the reverse direction, choose a program $p$ for the desired rational language. The affine input substitution $e\mapsto 2^p(2e+1)$ preserves the polynomial template. Disjoin the common test with the independently prescribed quadratic tests from the original quadratic-antichain theorem and apply trace smoothing. Monotonicity absorbs every positive base test at each quadratic field. An arbitrary many-one-complete language gives the corresponding equivalence for computable families, where the reindexing can be any total computable map.

The [rank-stability construction](RankStability.md) gives one fixed template for any such profile. It gets every prescribed positive, is exact over the base and every imaginary quadratic field, and is exact over a fixed density-one class of real quadratic fields. Effective Pell patches can make any specified finite list of real quadratic fields exact. With an input-dependent finite patch, each fixed field has only finitely many possible mistaken inputs. A separate decreasing refinement keeps the polynomial degree bounded and the ambient dimension grows at most linearly in the patch size.

The [common-language construction](CommonLanguage.md) gives a computable density-one class using central-value tests for congruent-number curves. It also gives an exact realization over the entire quadratic poset if a single fixed negated existential sentence is allowed. That sentence distinguishes $\mathbb Z$ from all quadratic rings of integers. Exact positive-existential realization for an arbitrary profile over the full poset remains open.

## A restricted Hilbert's tenth problem beyond étale–Brauer–Manin

The [external theorem](External.md) constructs, over the ring of integers of each fixed totally real number field, a computable family of smooth affine schemes defined by two equations of degree at most four in a fixed number of variables. Their integral-point existence problem is computably enumerable complete. Every member has integral local points everywhere, and every adelic point passes the full étale–Brauer–Manin test on the generic fiber. All fibers are geometrically integral. The generic fibers are geometrically rational and log rationally connected, with trivial geometric Picard and Brauer groups, and their complex connectivity can exceed any fixed finite bound. A single example has no integral point over any totally real extension of $\mathbb Q$, with the same local solvability and obstruction guarantees under base change.

The arithmetic construction uses a polynomial with roots in every completion and no roots in any totally real number field. An equation in a sum of squares preserves the input Diophantine test over totally real rings of integers. Successive suspensions control the geometry of the same integral model. The construction gives an undecidability statement in a restricted class. Individual integral Brauer–Manin counterexamples were already known. The models with only archimedean places allowed fail the split-compactification hypothesis in Harpaz's Conjecture 1.11. The conjecture concerns a different class and remains open in this analysis. [Harpaz, *Geometry and arithmetic of certain log K3 surfaces*](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.5802/aif.3132/) · [Colliot-Thélène, Wittenberg, *Groupe de Brauer et points entiers de deux familles de surfaces cubiques affines*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.3539).

### Simultaneous consequence

For every uniformly c.e. pattern across the real quadratic fields, there is one fixed-format family of smooth affine schemes, given by two equations of degree at most four, whose integral existence profile is exactly the requested pattern. Its tests over $\mathbb Z$ are always negative. For every input and every finite extension of $\mathbb Q$, the local integral points survive full étale–Brauer–Manin. Prescribe any fixed finite degree of connectivity for the complex fibers in the same construction.

To prove this, use Theorem 8 of the [arithmetic chapter](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/88b34b8d487ca161124f71a628c64dd251e306f8/SimultaneousComputability/ArithmeticProfiles.md) to construct one polynomial template $g_e=0$ with the prescribed quadratic profile and negative base test. Apply the two-quartic construction in [the external theorem](External.md). Its exact preservation of integral existence over all totally real number fields applies at the base and at every real quadratic probe, including simultaneous base change. Its statements about the local points and the generic fiber hold independently of the profile.

There is likewise no total computable upper bound on the height of a smallest integral point for positive instances in the restricted undecidability theorem. Such a bound would make it possible to enumerate a finite set of integral tuples and decide existence on every instance of the family.
