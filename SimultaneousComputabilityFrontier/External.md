# Undecidability beyond integral étale–Brauer–Manin

**Status.** Complete pencil-and-paper proof of the restricted undecidability theorem below. The two factors of the local polynomial were checked for simple roots at the 239 primes up to 1500 as a finite control. The proof covers every prime. Lean verification and an independent priority review remain open. The link to a published open conjecture is developed in §6.

## 1. The result

Fix a totally real number field $K$ and a positive integer $s$. There is a computable family of smooth affine $\mathcal O_K$-schemes $\mathcal X_e$ with geometrically integral fibers, presented by two equations of degree at most four in a fixed number of variables, such that:

1. The set $\{e: \mathcal X_e(\mathcal O_K)\ne\varnothing\}$ is computably enumerable complete. The degree and number of variables can depend on $K$ and $s$.
2. Every $\mathcal X_e$ has points over $\mathcal O_{E,w}$ at every finite place $w$ and over $E_w$ at every infinite place, simultaneously for every number field $E$ containing $K$. Here $\mathcal O_{E,w}$ denotes the completed valuation ring.
3. For every embedding $K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$, the associated complex manifold of the generic fiber $X_e$ is $(2s-1)$-connected. The generic fiber is geometrically rational and log rationally connected, with constant geometric units and zero geometric Picard and Brauer groups.
4. Over every number field $E$ containing $K$, every adelic point of $X_{e,E}$ survives the étale–Brauer–Manin obstruction. In particular the local points in (2) give integral adèles that survive the full obstruction defined using finite étale torsors of the generic fiber.
5. There is also a single fixed example with $\mathcal X(\mathcal O_E)=\varnothing$ for every totally real number field $E$, with all the same local and generic-fiber properties.

Thus one can prescribe any finite degree of complex connectivity and retain Hilbert's tenth problem inside a class for which the étale–Brauer–Manin test accepts all adèles. The original hypersurface model is generically smooth. The second equation gives relative smoothness at every finite prime.

## 2. A local root available at every place

Set
$$
f(t)=(t^3-t-1)(t^2-t+6).
$$
The monic cubic has no rational root and has discriminant $-23$. Its splitting field has group $S_3$, with quadratic subfield $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})$. At every prime $p\ne 23$ this splitting field is unramified and the two factors are separately separable modulo $p$. If Frobenius has a fixed point on the three cubic roots, the cubic has a simple root modulo $p$. Otherwise Frobenius is a 3-cycle, so its sign is trivial and the quadratic has a simple root modulo $p$. Hensel lifting in either case gives a root in $\mathbb Z_p$. At $p=23$, the cubic has the simple root $3$ modulo $23$, since its value there is $23$ and its derivative is $26$. The cubic has a real root, giving roots at the infinite completions as well. Base change gives a local root over every completed integer ring or archimedean field of every number field.

The cubic has exactly one real root, and every field generated by any one of its roots has nonreal embeddings. The quadratic has no real root. Consequently $f$ has no root in any totally real number field. The construction uses the standard $S_3$ subgroup-cover mechanism for intersective polynomials. [Sonn, Two remarks on the inverse Galois problem for intersective polynomials](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.680.pdf).

## 3. Encoding and relative smoothness

Given $g(x)\in\mathcal O_K[x_1,\dots,x_r]$, write $F(x,t)=g(x)f(t)$. Choose effectively an integer $c\ge1$ such that $1/(4c)$ is outside the geometric critical values of $F^2$. There are only finitely many critical values. On each irreducible component of the reduced critical locus the restriction has zero differential at its generic point, hence is constant in characteristic zero. One can test a candidate $c$ by ideal membership of $1$ in the ideal over $K$ generated by $4cF^2-1$ and the partial derivatives of $F^2$. The Nullstellensatz and Gröbner basis algorithm certify eventual termination, including for constant $F$.

With $z=(x,t,y,u_1,\ldots,u_{2s})$, put
$$
h=y(y-1)+c F^2+\sum_{i=1}^{2s}u_i^2.
$$
Let $V_g$ be the generic-fiber hypersurface $h=0$. It is smooth, since at a singular point in characteristic zero one would have $y=1/2$, $u_i=0$, and $F^2=1/(4c)$ with vanishing differential of $F^2$.

Use the following integral model $\mathcal X_g$, with one new variable $\lambda_j$ for each coordinate $z_j$:
$$
h=0,\qquad \sum_j\lambda_j\frac{\partial h}{\partial z_j}=1 .
$$
The projection factors through the union of principal opens on which one of the relative partial derivatives of $h$ is invertible. The hypersurface is smooth on each such open by the Jacobian criterion, and the new equation solves for the corresponding $\lambda_j$, so the projection is locally an affine-space bundle there. This proves relative smoothness over $\mathcal O_K$ at every prime. Over $K$ the partial derivatives generate the unit ideal in the coordinate ring of $V_g$. Choosing a global solution of the linear equation in that coordinate ring identifies the generic fiber $X_g\to V_g$ with the total space of the kernel vector bundle of the unimodular derivative row. Its rank is one less than the number of $z$-coordinates.

For every totally real finite extension $E/K$, any solution over $\mathcal O_E$ has, at each embedding $\sigma:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb R$,
$$
c\sigma(F)^2+\sum_i\sigma(u_i)^2
=\sigma(y)(1-\sigma(y))\le\frac14 .
$$
An algebraic integer whose absolute values at all embeddings are less than $1$ is zero, by its norm. Thus $F=u_1=\cdots=u_{2s}=0$ and $y\in\{0,1\}$. The absence of $f$-roots over $E$ forces $g(x)=0$. Conversely, $g(x)=0$ lifts with arbitrary integral $t$, $y=0$, all $u_i=0$, $\lambda_y=-1$ and the other $\lambda_j=0$, since $\partial h/\partial y=2y-1$. Thus
$$
\mathcal X_g(\mathcal O_E)\ne\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
g=0\text{ has a solution in }\mathcal O_E.
$$
The very same displayed section with $t$ a local root of $f$ and $x=0$ gives all the local points irrespective of $g$. Taking $K=\mathbb Q$ and $g=1$ proves the assertion about simultaneous failure over all totally real fields.

## 4. Geometry and connectivity

All geometric fibers of the integral model are integral. In odd characteristic, one pair of square terms becomes $ab$, and the equation is $ab=H(v)$ with $H\ne0$ because its $y^2$-coefficient is a unit. The polynomial $ab-H(v)$ is primitive and irreducible when considered as linear in $b$ over the unique factorization domain in $a,v$. Its partial derivative in $b$ equals $a$, so the relative smooth locus contains the entire chart $a\ne0$. The derivative-witness model is an affine-space bundle over this nonempty open smooth locus and therefore has integral geometric fiber. In characteristic two, write $w=\sum_i u_i+\sqrt c\,F$, which replaces $u_1$ by an invertible polynomial coordinate change over the algebraic closure. Then $h=y^2+y+w^2$. Setting $v=y+w$ gives $y=v^2$, $w=v^2+v$, so the fiber of the original hypersurface is affine space. The partial derivative in $y$ of the original $h$ equals $1$ in characteristic two, so the derivative-witness model is also affine space there. The quartic version uses $w=\sum_i u_i+\sqrt c\sum_i Q_i$ with the same argument.

Over $\bar K$, each pair of square terms becomes a hyperbolic product after an invertible linear change of coordinates. Thus $V_g$ is the result of $s$ successive suspensions of the smooth nonempty affine hypersurface $D_0:y(y-1)+cF^2=0$. The nonemptiness follows by taking a root of $f$ and $y=0$, and smoothness follows by the same selection of $c$. One suspension of $D:H(v)=0$ in $\mathbb A^m$ is the hypersurface $T:ab=H(v)$ in $\mathbb A^{m+2}$. It is smooth when $D$ is smooth. It is integral since $ab-H(v)$ is a primitive irreducible polynomial linear in $b$ over the unique factorization domain $k[a,v]$. Its chart $a\ne0$ is $\mathbb G_m\times\mathbb A^m$. This proves geometric rationality for $V_g$ and hence for $X_g$, since a vector bundle is trivial over a dense open subset.

For the topology work over $\mathbb C$. The chart $U:a\ne0$ surjects on the fundamental group of $T$, since a loop can be perturbed away from the real codimension-two divisor $Z:a=0$. For any $v_0\in D$, the complex line $(a,0,v_0)$ fills a generator of $\pi_1(U)=\mathbb Z$. Thus $T$ is simply connected. The divisor $Z\cong D\times\mathbb C$ is smooth and its normal complex line is trivialized by $a$. Excision and the Thom isomorphism give
$$
H_i(T,U;\mathbb Z)\cong H_{i-2}(D;\mathbb Z).
$$
The map $H_2(T,U)\to H_1(U)$ is the augmentation $H_0(D)\to\mathbb Z$ because a normal meridian on any component goes once around $a=0$. Since $U$ has the homotopy type of a circle, the long exact sequence yields
$$
\widetilde H_i(T;\mathbb Z)\cong
\widetilde H_{i-2}(D;\mathbb Z)\quad(i\ge2),\qquad H_1(T;\mathbb Z)=0 .
$$
Starting from nonempty $D_0$, it follows by induction that $\widetilde H_i(V_g(\mathbb C);\mathbb Z)=0$ for $i\le 2s-1$. Simple connectivity and Hurewicz now give the asserted homotopy connectivity. The vector-bundle total space $X_g(\mathbb C)$ deformation retracts to its zero section, so it has the same homotopy groups. Riemann existence and characteristic-zero geometric base change give $\pi_1^{\mathrm{ét}}(\overline X_g)=1$. [Stacks Project, Fundamental groups of schemes](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BQ6).

Use at least two suspensions, which gives the assertions for prescribed $s=1$ as well. The closed divisor $a=0$ in the last suspension is then geometrically integral because the preceding suspension is geometrically integral. On $V_g$, the divisor $a=0$ is then principal and reduced. The chart has trivial Picard group and units $\bar K^\times a^{\mathbb Z}$. Divisor localization gives $\operatorname{Pic}(\overline V_g)=0$, and the principal divisor shows that every everywhere-invertible function is constant.

Here is a direct verification for the generic vector-bundle total space. Over the chart $U=\mathbb G_m\times\mathbb A^m$ its coordinate ring is a symmetric algebra of a projective module over the coordinate ring of $U$. Its units pull back from the base, since polynomial units are constant on a dense trivializing open and the zero section evaluates them. Its Picard group over this chart vanishes by the following divisor argument. A vector bundle is a product $\mathbb A^d$ over a dense base open. Over a smooth factorial base, choose such an open to be a principal open $D(a_0)$. Its total space is factorial. Each codimension-one component of the complement is the pullback of an irreducible divisor of $a_0=0$ in the factorial base and is principal. Divisor localization gives a trivial Picard group. Applying localization once more to the principal irreducible divisor over $a=0$ on $V_g$ gives $\operatorname{Pic}(\overline X_g)=0$. The units are constant by the same zero-section argument. The complex singular cohomology $H^2(X_g(\mathbb C),\mathbb Z/n)$ vanishes by $3$-connectivity, and the Kummer sequence and finite-coefficient comparison show $\operatorname{Br}(\overline X_g)[n]=0$ for every $n$. Here $\operatorname{Br}$ is the torsion subgroup of $H^2_{\mathrm{ét}}(-,\mathbb G_m)$. [Stacks Project, Kummer theory](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03PK).

For a separate geometric property, over $\bar K$ any two points of the chart $a\ne0$ with distinct $a$-coordinates are joined by an affine-line morphism into $V_g$. Interpolate $a$ linearly between the given values at times 0 and 1. At the unique root of this linear polynomial choose $v$ to be any point of $H=0$. Interpolate each $v$-coordinate polynomially through these three points. Then $H(v)$ is divisible by the linear $a$, so the quotient supplies the missing coordinate $b$, with the correct endpoint values. One can lift this affine line to $X_g$ through any two chosen points of the vector-bundle fibers over its endpoints. The pullback of the bundle to $\mathbb A^1_{\bar K}$ is free by the principal ideal domain property of $\bar K[t]$, and polynomial interpolation supplies the section. A morphism from $\mathbb A^1$ extends to $\mathbb P^1$ in any smooth proper compactification and meets the boundary only at infinity. This proves log rational connectedness in the sense used by Harpaz. [Zhu, Log rationally connected surfaces, §1](https://math-zhu.github.io/papers/4_logRC.pdf).

## 5. Étale–Brauer–Manin and the reduction

The Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence, using constant geometric units and vanishing geometric Picard and Brauer groups, gives $\operatorname{Br}(X_{g,E})=\operatorname{Br}(E)$ for every finite extension $E/K$. The equality also follows at each finite torsion order using the vanishing geometric Kummer cohomology in degrees 1 and 2. Global reciprocity makes all adèles Brauer-orthogonal.

The arithmetic exact sequence for the étale fundamental group, together with trivial geometric fundamental group, says that all finite étale covers come from the base field. Hence any torsor under a finite étale group scheme over $E$ is the pullback of such a torsor over $E$. Twisting by the class of that field torsor gives a trivial torsor with a global section over $X_{g,E}$. The section lifts every adelic point. Pulling any Brauer class of the twisted torsor back along the section gives a constant class on $X_{g,E}$, so the lift is orthogonal. This proves compatibility with each finite étale torsor. The integral assertion uses the subset
$$
\left(\prod_{w\nmid\infty}\mathcal X_g(\mathcal O_{E,w})
      \times\prod_{w\mid\infty} X_g(E_w)\right)
       \cap X_g(\mathbb A_E)^{\mathrm{\acute et},\operatorname{Br}} .
$$
[Harpaz and Skorobogatov, Singular curves and the étale Brauer–Manin obstruction for surfaces](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.24033/asens.2226/).

For a fixed $K$, combine one fixed MRDP representation of the halting predicate with Diophantine definability of $\mathbb Z$ over $\mathcal O_K$. This gives $g_e(x)=0$ for the halting predicate with a fixed number and maximum degree of $x$-variables. A finite conjunction of equalities is encoded by their sum of squares, using an embedding of $K$ into $\mathbb R$. The integer $e$ and the computed $c$ affect coefficients and leave the number of variables and degree bounded. The equivalence in §3 gives a many-one reduction. Enumeration of tuples in $\mathcal O_K$ verifies computable enumerability. [Jones, Universal diophantine equation](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-symbolic-logic/article/abs/universal-diophantine-equation/113EBF3D928DFC6011C52BECAE81A087). [Koymans and Pagano, Hilbert's tenth problem via additive combinatorics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01768).

## 6. Relation to Harpaz's Conjecture 1.11

Colliot-Thélène and Wittenberg already gave a simply connected, log rationally connected integral Hasse counterexample with no Brauer–Manin obstruction, the affine quadric $2x^2+3y^2+4z^2=1$. Harpaz's Conjecture 1.11 predicts adequacy of integral Brauer–Manin for simply connected, log rationally connected varieties under the additional $S$-split hypothesis of his Definitions 1.6 and 1.8. His Theorem 1.10 settles the weaker Question 1.9 by exhibiting a simply connected $S$-split log K3 counterexample. [Colliot-Thélène and Wittenberg, Example 5.9](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.3539). [Harpaz, Geometry and arithmetic of certain log K3 surfaces](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.5802/aif.3132/).

In the construction above, take $S$ to be the archimedean places. At every real completion the coordinate $y$ is a bounded nonconstant regular function. It is nonconstant because both $y=0$ and $y=1$ occur in the generic fiber over a root of $f$ after base change to $\mathbb R$. A smooth geometrically integral real variety with a bounded nonconstant regular function cannot have a split compactification in Harpaz's sense. In such a compactification every boundary component is real and has Zariski-dense real points, since each is smooth with a real point. Any pole of the function along a boundary component would be visible along a real analytic arc transverse to a generic real point of that component. Thus boundedness excludes all divisorial poles, making the function regular on the smooth proper compactification and hence constant. The $S$-split hypothesis therefore excludes these examples even though the generic fibers meet the conjecture's simple-connectivity and log rational connectedness hypotheses. The new claim concerns the effectiveness question for the restricted geometric class in §1.


## 7. Two quartic equations suffice

The bounded-degree statement in §1 can be strengthened to total degree at most $4$ for both equations. Choose a fixed arithmetic circuit computing $g_e(x)f(t)$, with $e$ appearing only as a coefficient. Add one variable per internal wire and one equation $Q_i=0$ per gate. Each $Q_i$ has degree at most $2$ since a gate is an addition, subtraction, or multiplication. Impose the additional linear equation that the output wire is zero. Over any ring, the gate equations determine the wires uniquely and then the output equation is equivalent to $g_e(x)f(t)=0$.

In §§3–5 replace $F^2$ by the single polynomial $R=\sum_i Q_i^2$, counting the wire variables among the input coordinates. Choose $c$ by the same finite-critical-values test for $R$. Each integral solution over a totally real field has $|\sigma(Q_i)|\le 1/(2\sqrt c)$ at every embedding, so each $Q_i$ vanishes by the norm argument. Local roots of $f$ and circuit evaluation supply local solutions with $R=0$, with the same $y=0,\lambda_y=-1$ prescription. All geometry arguments depend only on $y(y-1)+cR=0$ being smooth and nonempty in characteristic zero, and the same $c$ test ensures this. The new $h$ has degree at most $4$, so the derivative-witness equation also has degree at most $4$. If desired, describing the evaluation circuits for these two quartics by additional graph equations yields an isomorphic smooth affine scheme presented by a fixed number of equations of degree at most $2$.
