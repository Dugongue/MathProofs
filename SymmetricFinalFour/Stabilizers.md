# Stabilizer schemes and determinant-one transport for symmetric final-digit tensors

Fix a field $k$ of positive characteristic $p$. Let $t,t'$ be tensors of the same positive format on three $k$-spaces. Use the symmetric tagged form $C_t$ and the Frobenius tensor $F_t$ of the fully symmetric final-digit construction. Its fixed unit slice is the perfect multiplication pairing. The scalar $N$ used in its trace test is prime to $p$.

**Theorem.** In equal characteristic, let $R_0$ be a finite-dimensional commutative local $k$-algebra with residue $k$ and a chosen nonzero socle element $s$ annihilating its nonzero maximal ideal. For **every** commutative $k$-algebra $S$, regard $F_t,F_{t'}$ as tensors over $R_0\otimes_k S$. Ordinary source transport over $S$, independent $\mathrm{GL}^3$ output transport, independent $\mathrm{SL}^3$ output transport, and diagonal $\mathrm{GL}$ and $\mathrm{SL}$ output transport are equivalent. For concise sources, there is a natural split map of transporter groupoids. The section takes values in diagonal $\mathrm{SL}$, fixes the unit, and preserves its fixed pairing. In particular the source stabilizer scheme is a split quotient of each output stabilizer scheme, including the diagonal determinant-one stabilizer. This identifies the source scheme even on nonreduced test rings.

There is a mixed-characteristic version with $W_e(S)$ for $e\ge2$, using the final Witt coordinate for the encoding. The same natural extraction exists. Every individual source transporter has a diagonal determinant-one output lift after a finite faithfully flat extension of $S$, obtained by adjoining $p^{e-1}$-st roots of its matrix entries. It has such a lift over $S$ itself if Frobenius is surjective on $S$. Over dual numbers the extraction can miss infinitesimal source transporters, so this version specifies the topology needed to lift individual witnesses.

The Frobenius and trace calculations are on the fixed tensor family. The theorem makes no assumptions about smoothness of the stabilizer schemes. [Starting construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FinalDigitFour/Tensor.md)

## 1. The marker flag works on nonreduced rings

Use the starting construction's $L=K\oplus\bigoplus_j k z_j$, $K=\bigoplus_i O_i$, $O_i=X_i\oplus X_i^*\oplus H_i$. Each split $H_i$ has at least two hyperbolic pairs. The trilinear form on $K^3$ is the polarized separate-role input $T_K$. The only other nonzero summand triples are permutations of $(z_i,O_i,O_i)$, with form $\beta_i$, a perfect pairing on $O_i$ extended by zero on other $O_j$. Write $D$ for the contraction radical of $T_K$. Over $k$ it is the direct sum of the three input contraction radicals together with all the $X_i^*,H_i$. This description commutes with extension of scalars, since the contraction linear maps and their kernels are over $k$. Apply the same definitions to $t'$.

Let $g$ be a projective transporter of the tagged forms over $S$, with
$$
 C_{t'}(gx,gy,gz)=\eta C_t(x,y,z),\qquad \eta\in S^\times.
$$
Over every field extension of $k$, the rank of the full slice distinguishes $K$ and the disjoint rank intervals of the restricted slices distinguish the three lines in $L/K$, as in the starting proof. Apply that argument over the residue field at each prime ideal of $S$. The marker coordinates of $g(K)$ and the off-diagonal entries of the marker-to-marker block of $g$ vanish at every prime ideal and hence are nilpotent. They generate a nilpotent ideal $J$, since there are finitely many such entries.

The following argument lifts both conclusions from $S/J^r$ to $S/J^{r+1}$. In this step use $E=J^r/J^{r+1}$, with $E^2=0$, and write bars for reduction modulo $E$. By hypothesis $\bar g$ carries $K$ onto $K'$, hence $D$ onto $D'$. It sends each labeled marker line modulo $K$ to the corresponding line, with an invertible multiplier $a_i$, since the invertible block triangular matrix has invertible diagonal blocks. For $h\in D$ and $x\in K$, evaluate the transporter equation at $z_i,h,x$ modulo $E$. The $K'$-part of $\bar g(z_i)$ contributes zero because $\bar g(h)\in D'$. It follows that
$$
 \beta'_i(\bar g h,\bar g x)=\bar\eta\,a_i^{-1}\beta_i(h,x).
 \tag{1}
$$
In particular $\bar g(H_j)\subseteq O'_j$. For $i\ne j$, (1) is zero for every $x\in K$ and $\beta'_i$ detects the whole $O'_i$ component.

First fix a coordinate basis vector $x\in K$ and an index $i$. Choose a coordinate hyperbolic pair $h,h'$ in $H_i$ which pairs to one and is orthogonal to $x$. There is such a pair since the displayed bases give at most one offending pair. The source value $C_t(x,h,h')$ is zero. Expand the target value by decomposing $g(x),g(h),g(h')$ into their $K'$-parts and marker parts. The term from three $K'$-parts vanishes, since each of the last two parts lies in $D'$ modulo $E$ and $E^2=0$. Terms with two or more marker parts vanish by the definition of $C_{t'}$. Terms with the marker part from $g(h)$ or $g(h')$ also vanish, because its coefficients lie in $E$ and the corresponding pairings modulo $E$ are zero by (1) and the choice of pair. In the term with the marker part from $g(x)$, only marker $i$ can contribute, and its pairing is a unit by (1). Hence the $i$-th marker coordinate of $g(x)$ vanishes. This proves $g(K)=K'$ at the new precision and therefore $g(D)=D'$ there as well.

Second, fix $j\ne i$ and evaluate on $z_j,h,h'$ for a coordinate hyperbolic pair in $H_i$. Since $g(h),g(h')\in D'\subseteq K'$, only the marker part of $g(z_j)$ contributes. For labels other than $i,j$ the coefficient is in $E$ and the pairing vanishes modulo $E$. For label $j$ both arguments have $O'_j$-components in $E$ by the consequence of (1), so that pairing vanishes exactly. For label $i$ the pairing is a unit. The original value is zero, so the $i$-th marker coordinate of $g(z_j)$ is zero. This completes the induction. Starting at $S/J$ and iterating proves the two conclusions over $S$. In particular the proof uses the tensor equations to eliminate nilpotents after the rank tests at field points.

With the exact flag recovered, use the starting proof's pairings on $K\times D$ with $z_i$ to identify the two complementary roles in $K/D$. All kernel identities there hold after base change. Explicitly the left kernel in $O_i$ lies in $X_i^*$, since it annihilates $X_i^*\oplus H_i$; all other $O_j$ lie in that kernel. Its image in $K/D$ is the sum of the two supported roles labeled differently from $i$. Intersect the corresponding two sums to recover each supported role. The maps induced on their quotients satisfy the tensor equation up to $\eta$, which can be canceled in the first role. If the original tensors have contraction radicals, extend the maps by using complements of those radicals and arbitrary isomorphisms between the matching radical dimensions. Those complements and radicals are base changes of $k$-spaces. For concise tensors, no extension choices are needed and the extracted three maps compose: the induced maps on the role subquotients compose and the multipliers multiply.

## 2. Fixed-slice extraction is a functor

Put $\mathcal R(S)=R_0\otimes_k S$ or $W_e(S)$. Write $\rho:\mathcal R(S)\to S$ for the residue map with nilpotent kernel. The map $\iota:S\to\mathcal R(S)$ giving the last ideal is $\iota(u)=su$ in equal characteristic and $\iota(u)=V^{e-1}(u)$ for Witt vectors. It is injective and additive. Write $j=0$ in equal characteristic and $j=e-1$ for Witt vectors. The two cases share the formula
$$
 r\,\iota(u)=\iota\bigl(\rho(r)^{p^j}u\bigr).
 \tag{2}
$$
For Witt vectors this is the projection formula for Verschiebung and Frobenius. We use the convention that a coefficient $c$ of $C_t$ is placed in the last ideal as $V^{e-1}(c)$. This agrees with the $p^{e-1}$-multiple convention on the prime field. [Hazewinkel, *Witt vectors, Part 1*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.3888) · [Bertapelle–González-Avilés on the Greenberg functor](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.0051)

The perfect pairing at the fixed unit identifies the third covariant slot with the dual of the multiplication output. Thus for any independent transporter $(f_1,f_2,f_3)$ of $F_t$ and $F_{t'}$ there is an invertible linear map $h$ satisfying
$$
 h(xy)=f_1(x)f_2(y).
$$
The arrays here are associative, commutative and unital by the product construction. Substitution of the unit gives units $u=f_1(1)$, $v=f_2(1)$: their multiplication operators are invertible, so each multiplier has a two-sided inverse. The normalized algebra isomorphism is
$$
 \varphi(x)=(uv)^{-1}h(x)=u^{-1}f_1(x).
$$
It is multiplicative by the displayed isotopy identity. The second formula makes normalization functorial. If $f'_1=u'\varphi'$ is the first map in another transporter, then $f'_1 f_1=u'\varphi'(u)\,\varphi'\varphi$, and its normalized map is $\varphi'\varphi$. This reasoning uses the fixed perfect pairing over the entire test ring. [Albert on associative isotopy](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968960)

Write $I=L_{\mathcal R}\oplus L_{\mathcal R}^*\oplus Q\oplus\mathcal R z$ for the augmentation ideal of the Frobenius lift. The original triangular trace formula gives $I=\ker(\operatorname{tr}L_x)$ over $\mathcal R(S)$ because $N$ is invertible there. Also $\operatorname{Ann}_I(I)=\mathcal R z$ by the perfect multiplication pairing on $I/\mathcal R z$. Thus $\varphi$ preserves both prescribed submodules and sends $z$ to $u_0 z'$ for an invertible scalar $u_0\in\mathcal R(S)$. For inputs $x,y,v\in I$, the triple product is
$$
 \iota\bigl(C_t(\bar x_L^{[p^j]},\bar y_L^{[p^j]},\bar v_L^{[p^j]})\bigr)\,z ,
$$
where the bars here mean application of $\rho$. The auxiliary pairing $L\times L^*\to\mathcal R z$ is what turns the final-ideal multiplication of two vectors of $L$ into this triple product. Let $M$ be the matrix of $\rho(\varphi|_I)$ in the displayed free residue coordinates. Functoriality of triple products and (2) give a projective equivalence by $M^{[p^j]}$, with multiplier $\rho(u_0)^{p^j}$, of the two padded forms on $I\otimes_{\mathcal R,\rho} S$. The padding radical of this triple product is exactly the complementary coordinates to $L$. This holds over $S$ because $C_t$ is concise over $k$ and its contraction map therefore admits a $k$-linear left inverse. Pass to the quotient to get a projective $C_t$-transporter, then apply Section 1 to recover a source transporter. Each step is compatible with base change; the functor on concise inputs also respects composition.

## 3. Split in equal characteristic, faithfully flat lifting for Witt vectors

A source transporter $(A_1,A_2,A_3)$ over $S$ acts on $L$ by $P$: use $A_i$ on $X_i$, $A_i^{-\mathsf T}$ on $X_i^*$, and the identity on $H_i,z_i$. This is an exact isometry of the tagged forms. Over $R_0\otimes_k S$, extend scalars along the coefficient map $S\to R_0\otimes_k S$. The Frobenius lift uses $P,P^{-\mathsf T}$ on $L,L^*$ and the identity on $1,Q,z$. It intertwines all multiplication rules, preserves the Frobenius functional and its fixed pairing, and has determinant one. The block action respects composition. Section 2 applied to this diagonal transporter returns the original maps, since the socle multiplier is one and the role maps are the stated $A_i$.

For concise $t$, write $\mathcal H_t$ for the ordinary source stabilizer scheme and $\mathcal G_t$ for any one of the four output stabilizer schemes (diagonal or independent, ordinary or determinant-one). These are affine group schemes, using the finite free coefficient algebra to write matrix entries as $k$-coordinates. The natural maps on test rings give a split homomorphism $\mathcal G_t\to\mathcal H_t$. If $\mathcal K_t$ is its kernel, the section gives $\mathcal G_t\simeq\mathcal K_t\rtimes\mathcal H_t$. In particular any failure to lift source stabilizer elements through a nilpotent extension is retained in the target: section followed by a hypothetical target lift and then extraction would lift the original source element.

For example, take the ordinary multiplication tensor of $k[x]/(x^p)$ as the source. It is concise, since multiplication by $1$ supplies all three supports. Its ordinary tensor stabilizer maps onto its algebra automorphism scheme by the normalization above, with a diagonal section. The map $x\mapsto x+\varepsilon$ is an algebra automorphism over $S=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^p)$. It cannot lift over $S'=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^{p+1})$: any lifted image has $p$-th power $\varepsilon^p\ne0$, since a correction in $(\varepsilon^p)$ has zero $p$-th power there. Hence the constructed symmetric final-digit tensor has a nonsmooth diagonal determinant-one stabilizer in every positive characteristic.

For Witt vectors, let $m=p^{e-1}$. Adjoin $m$-th roots of the finitely many entries of each $A_i$ to $S$ by equations $u^m=a$. Each quotient by one of these monic equations is free of rank $m>0$ over its base, so the composite extension is finite faithfully flat. The root matrices are invertible, since their determinant raised to the $m$-th power is the determinant of $A_i$. Make $P_0$ from the root matrices and their inverse transposes by the same block recipe. Its entrywise $m$-th power is $P$. Lift $P_0$ entrywise into $W_e$ and use the inverse transpose of that lift in the dual block. The resulting map fixes the unit and functional and has determinant one. It preserves the final-coordinate product by (2), since it induces $P$ on the final coordinates, and preserves the perfect duality part by construction. Its extracted source transporter is the original one. When Frobenius is surjective, the roots can be taken directly in $S$.

There need not be a lift returning a specified transporter over the original $S$. Over $k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$, every entrywise $p^{e-1}$-power lies in $k$. For a concise tensor, the determinant-one lift test on the source scalar transporter $((1+\varepsilon)I,(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}I,I)$ exhibits the difference: this tuple preserves the source, while every tuple in the image of the Witt extraction has its entries in $k$. For the latter assertion, the extraction after applying the entrywise power and taking the multiplier uses only fixed $k$-linear coordinates on the role subquotients and the inverse of the powered multiplier. Thus an exact comparison on geometric points alone would miss this discrepancy.

## 4. Scope and prior work

The starting symmetric final-digit theorem proved two-way transport on points over the finite coefficient rings. The extra step is the square-zero calculation proving the three labels and support quotients invariant on nonreduced test rings. The equal-characteristic sections from the determinant-one block action then give an actual group-scheme retract. The Witt case uses the same extraction, with the Frobenius twist explicitly retained in lifting. Grochow–Qiao's symmetric tensor reductions and Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun's commutative-ring framework give the underlying complexity context. The proof here is written. Publication priority for this scheme comparison is unconfirmed. [Grochow–Qiao IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317) · [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun V](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1)

The [finite control](Stabilizers.py) verifies the marker flag directly in the linearized projective stabilizer equations for two small forms in characteristics $2,3,5$. It forces all 60 checked off-block coordinates to vanish in each case and verifies the first-order determinant-one section. [FrobeniusSquareZero.lean](FrobeniusSquareZero.lean) independently checks the prime-characteristic identity used to see the loss of square-zero directions in the Witt comparison.
