# Linear-size local algebras for exact trilinear equivalence

The earlier truncated algebra encodes a cubic by keeping every monomial through degree three. The present construction keeps only the contractions a form can see. A two-generator attachment recovers the original scalar from the unmarked algebra even when the compressed degree-two multiplication has rank-one directions.

## Theorem

Over every field $F$, an explicit symmetric $d$-linear form $T$ on an $n$-dimensional vector space, for fixed $d\ge3$, has a finite-dimensional, commutative, associative, unital local algebra $L_T$. Two such algebras are isomorphic if and only if the original forms are linearly isometric. Any algebra isomorphism explicitly yields a form isometry over the same field. The same equivalence holds for ordinary isomorphism of the three-tensors of multiplication.

For $d=3$, writing $c$ for the contraction rank, one has $\dim L_T=n+c+6\le2n+6$. The maximal ideal has fourth power zero and nonzero third power. The socle has dimension $n-c+2$. For nonzero $T$ and general $d$, the algebra has dimension at most
\[
n-c+d+1+\sum_{i=0}^d\binom{c+\min(i,d-i)-1}{\min(i,d-i)}.
\]
Thus the construction improves the exponent of the dimension bound from $d$ to $\lfloor d/2\rfloor$ for this multilinear input. For formal cubic polynomials in characteristic different from $2,3$, apply it to the normalized polarization. It also gives a linear-length reduction for the characteristic-three cubic family with nonzero pure-power restriction to its polarization radical over a perfect field. The short algebra classification has a separate TI-completeness consequence in every characteristic.

## Contract before adding the scalar direction

Let $D=\{v:T(v,-,\ldots,-)=0\}$, let $r=\dim D$, and let $C=V/D$. The induced symmetric form $\bar T$ on $C$ is concise, meaning its contraction radical is zero. On $\operatorname{Sym}(C)$, extend $\bar T$ to the linear functional $\tau:\operatorname{Sym}^d C\to F$ that takes a product of $d$ vectors to the form evaluated there. A monomial maps to its repeated-argument value, without factorial factors. For $0\le i\le d$ set
\[
I_i=\{u\in\operatorname{Sym}^i C\mid \tau(uv)=0\ \forall v\in\operatorname{Sym}^{d-i}C\}.
\]
The sum of the $I_i$ with all degrees above $d$ is an ideal. Multiplying a test against $u$ by a new factor reduces to a test in the original complementary degree. For $T\ne0$, let $B$ be the quotient by this ideal. Its top degree is $Fs$, where $s$ is the unique element with $\tau(s)=1$. The complementary degree pairings are perfect by the definition of their kernels. In particular $B_1=C$, and for a cubic $B_2=C^*$ with multiplication $xy=\bar T(x,y,-)$ and $x\ell=\ell(x)s$. If $T=0$, use $C=0$ and the algebra with degrees $B_0=F,B_d=Fs$ and zero product on $Fs$.

Augment $B$ by the powers $z,z^2,\ldots,z^{d-1}$ and the elements $w,e$, where $w$ has degree one and $e$ has degree two. Glue the top power by $z^d=s$. Put $zw=e$, $w^2=0$, and $z^i w=0$ for $i\ge2$. An element of positive degree in $B$ multiplies $z,w$ to zero. The element $e$ kills all positive degrees. Add an $r$-dimensional space of inert degree-one elements, denoted by $D_0$, with zero product against every positive degree.

These rules yield an associative algebra. One direct construction takes the fiber product over their augmentations of $B$ and $F[z,w]/(z^{d+1},w^2,z^2w)$, and identifies the socle elements $z^d$ and $s$ by quotienting their difference. The inert space then forms a square-zero summand over the augmentation. This also verifies that $s,e,z^2$ have the stated independence whenever their degrees agree. The algebra is generated in degree one, including the zero-form case, since the arm generates $s$. Its positive part is the unique maximal ideal, and its powers are the direct sums of the corresponding and higher degrees. The claimed dimension follows from the complementary rank bounds. A basis and structure constants can be computed by Gaussian elimination in fixed degree. For cubics the Hilbert vector is $(1,c+r+2,c+2,1)$.

## The marker is intrinsic

Any algebra isomorphism preserves the maximal ideal and acts on its associated graded algebra. All steps below take place in that graded object and use only multiplication.

1. The kernel of multiplication from degree one against all of degree one into degree two is $D_0$. Indeed every nonzero $x\in C$ has a nonzero product with some $y\in C$, since the full form is concise. Testing against $z$ detects the $z,w$ components by the independent vectors $z^2,e$.
2. The degree-two annihilator of degree one is exactly $Fe$. Testing against $z$ detects a nonzero $z^2$ coefficient. An element of $B_2$ killing $C=B_1$ pairs to zero against $B_{d-2}$, because that degree is generated by products of $d-2$ elements of $C$. Its complementary pairing is perfect, so it is zero. The zero-form case has $B_2=0$.
3. Modulo $D_0$ in degree one, reduce the multiplication map modulo $Fe$ in degree two. The radical of this reduced map is $Fw$, since $w$ produced only $e$, the $C$ products still have zero radical, and $z^2$ survives.
4. In the degree-one space modulo $D_0$, the annihilator of $Fw$ under the original degree-two product is $C\oplus Fw$. Thus after quotienting by $Fw$ we recover $C$. The reduced product on that final quotient has orthogonal complement to $C$ equal to $Fz$. For $C=0$ this just says that the final quotient is the one-dimensional $z$-space.

Consequently an isomorphism induces a map $S:C\to C'$ and a scalar $a\ne0$ on the reconstructed $z$-line. Lifting to the earlier quotients only introduces terms in $Fw$ and $D_0$. They have no effect on a product of at least three copies of $z$ or on any product of vectors from $C$. In particular $z^d=s$ forces the top scalar to be $a^d$. Preservation of $d$-fold products on $C$ now gives
\[
\bar T'(Sx_1,\ldots,Sx_d)=a^d\bar T(x_1,\ldots,x_d).
\]
The map $a^{-1}S$ is an exact isometry. Lift it using any complements of the contraction radicals and any isomorphism on the radicals. Conversely an exact isometry identifies the ideals $I_i$, preserves the chosen $s$, and extends by fixing the attached generators. All constituents can be extracted from a supplied algebra witness by filtering and linear algebra.

The multiplication tensor of a unital commutative algebra carries the same isomorphism problem. Given an isotopy $R(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$, evaluate both input units. Then $u=P(1)$ and $v=Q(1)$ are units because multiplication by either is surjective. Dividing $P,Q,R$ by $u,v,uv$ yields a common unital algebra isomorphism. An algebra isomorphism conversely acts on all three legs.

For cubic polynomial input in characteristic different from $2,3$, normalized polarization is an equivariant bijection with symmetric trilinear forms. In characteristic three over a perfect field, suppose the polarization radical has positive dimension and the cubic is nonzero on it. The restriction is a pure-cube polynomial. Its cube-root linear functional is nonzero, so any two such restrictions on radicals of the same dimension are equivalent. A quotient polarization isometry leaves a pure-cube discrepancy on a complement. A linear shear into the radical absorbs each coefficient using its unique cube root. Thus the cubic equivalence problem on this promise is decided by the quotient polarization isometry and the radical dimension. The inert coordinates retain that dimension in $L_T$. This uses the Frobenius mechanism of the earlier reduction, and applies in particular to its hard family with one-dimensional nonzero radical.

## A TI-complete Gorenstein class in every characteristic

There is also a linear-length reduction from ordinary three-tensor isomorphism to ordinary isomorphism of commutative local Gorenstein algebras with Hilbert vector $(1,c,c,1)$. Use Grochow–Qiao IV to map TI to symmetric matrix-space isometry and then use the symmetric trilinear gadget of their Section 6. These forms are concise for independent input slices. In the notation of that gadget, the pairing of its $x,u$ coordinate blocks detects the $h$ coordinate of a radical vector. Pairing with $h$ in turn detects the $x,u$ coordinates and the two auxiliary blocks. The remaining $z$ coordinates form a null combination of the input slices and vanish by independence.

Every nonzero entry of the symmetric gadget contains exactly one coordinate in the $z$ block or the $h$ line. Thus scaling those two blocks by any common nonzero scalar multiplies the form by that scalar, over the original field. For the resulting concise forms, the ordinary algebra $B$ above suffices. Its graded multiplication has $xy=T(x,y,-)$ and $x\ell=\ell(x)s$. An algebra isomorphism induces on degree one a *similarity* of the trilinear forms, with multiplier given by its map on $Fs$. The split scalar action of the gadget makes its similarities equivalent to exact isometries. In the other direction an isometry extends to $B$ by inverse duality on degree two and the identity on $Fs$. This gives both reductions, as multiplication already reduces the output algebra problem to ordinary TI. The algebra has dimension $2c+2$, and its maximal ideal has fourth power zero and socle equal to its third power. The unmarked Gorenstein carrier here uses the particular support of Grochow–Qiao's construction. The two-generator attachment is what gives the exact transporter theorem for arbitrary symmetric input. [Grochow–Qiao IV, Theorems 1.10 and 6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S6)

## Exact counting over finite fields for the compressed cubic algebra

Take $d=3$ and $F=\mathbb F_q$. Write $N_{\bar T,\bar T'}$ for the number of exact isometries of the two concise quotient forms. If the contraction ranks or radical dimensions differ then there is no isomorphism. Otherwise put
\[
b=\dim\ker\!\big(Q\mapsto Q+Q^{\mathsf T}\big),\qquad
k=\dim\!\left(\operatorname{span}\{\bar T(-,-,u):u\in C\}
     \cap \operatorname{im}\!\big(Q\mapsto Q+Q^{\mathsf T}\big)\right).
\]
In odd characteristic $b=\binom c2$ and $k=c$. In characteristic two $b=\binom{c+1}{2}$ and $k$ is the dimension of the alternating slice subspace. Then the exact number of ordinary algebra transporters is
\[
(q-1)^2\,|\operatorname{GL}(r,q)|\,
q^{\,r(c+4)+b+k+3c+7}\,N_{\bar T,\bar T'} .
\tag{1}
\]
The factor applies to the zero-form case with $c=b=k=0$.

To prove (1), first count graded maps. Modulo the inert space, $w$ maps to $\beta w'$ and $z$ maps to $az'+\gamma w'$. The image of $x\in C$ could initially have a $w'$ component, but its zero product with $z$ forces that component to vanish since $z'w'=e'\ne0$. Then the map on $C$ is $a$ times an exact isometry. Conversely these are all possible graded maps, including arbitrary radical components on $C,z,w$, and any invertible map on $D_0$. They extend by the product relations and are invertible on every degree. This accounts for $(q-1)^2q|\operatorname{GL}(r,q)|q^{r(c+2)}N_{\bar T,\bar T'}$.

For the kernel on the associated graded, write the degree-one generator images up to degree three as
\[
\begin{aligned}
x&\longmapsto x+Q(x)+\lambda(x)z^2+p(x)e+t(x)s,\\
z&\longmapsto z+U+u_2 z^2+u_e e+u_s s,\\
w&\longmapsto w+V+v_2 z^2+v_e e+v_s s,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $Q(x),U,V$ belong to $C^*$. The relation $xz=0$ forces $\lambda(x)=-U(x)$, and $xw=0$ forces $V=0$. An inert generator may shift by any element of $Fe\oplus Fs$. The shifts on $z^2,e,s$ are forced by products, as $z^2\mapsto z^2+2u_2s$, $e\mapsto e+v_2s$, and $s\mapsto s$. The map on $C^*$ must have the form $\ell\mapsto\ell+\ell(j)s$ for some $j\in C$. Compatibility with $xy=\bar T(x,y,-)$ is exactly
\[
Q(x)(y)+Q(y)(x)=\bar T(x,y,j).
\]
Conciseness makes $j$ unique for each permitted $Q$. This permits a space of dimension $b+k$. All degree-three pairing relations then hold, since all omitted correction products have degree at least four. The displayed choices preserve every product and give maps invertible by the filtration. There are $c$ free entries of $U$, $2c$ of $p,t$, six displayed scalars on $z,w$, and $2r$ inert corrections. Multiplying their count by the graded count proves (1). For ordinary multiplication-tensor transporters there is one further factor $|L_T^\times|^2$, since the two unit choices in the normalization are independent and uniquely recovered.

## Linear refutation degree in the algebra dimension

The proof-degree transfer from the companion note also applies to the linear-size algebras. In particular, there are pairs of the above unmarked Gorenstein algebras of dimension $N$ whose nonisomorphism requires Polynomial Calculus and Nullstellensatz degree $\Omega(N)$ over every field, and Sum-of-Squares degree $\Omega(N)$ over the reals. The same lower bound holds for $L_T$ with socle dimension two. Both assertions use the ungraded unit, multiplication and two-sided inverse equation system.

Take the hard three-tensor instances and the square-free cubics specified in Section 5 of [the proof-degree note](Optimization.md). The forward substitution there has affine-linear transporter and inverse entries, with bounded-degree certificates on the cubic coefficients. Taking the unnormalized polarization is a linear operation on those coefficients and commutes with formal substitutions, so the same proof gives bounded-degree certificates for the isometry equations of the associated symmetric trilinear forms. If the cubic action is on coefficient vectors, use the inverse transpose on arguments; multiplying the forward equations by the relevant inverse entries in three slots and using the supplied inverse equations gives bounded-degree certificates in that convention as well. The conciseness and split scalar property proved above ensure that the two Gorenstein algebras are nonisomorphic over the algebraic closure.

For a proposed isometry $S$ of the trilinear forms with inverse $J$, substitute the block maps $(1,S,J^{\mathsf T},1)$ and $(1,J,S^{\mathsf T},1)$ on the unit, degree-one space, its dual, and the socle. For the attached carrier fix the extra generators and powers. Products between a vector and a dual vector reduce to the supplied inverse equations. For products of two vectors the only additional equations ask that
\[
 T'(Sx,Sy,-)=J^{\mathsf T}T(x,y,-).
\]
For each coordinate this is obtained by taking the isometry equation, multiplying its third input by $J$, and replacing $SJ$ with the identity. For the inverse algebra map use the reverse trilinear equation. Substitute $J$ in all three slots of the forward relation and telescope out the three $SJ$ factors to get it. Thus both unit constraints, both inverse constraints, and both sets of algebra multiplication constraints have constant-degree polynomial identities in the ideal of the original tensor equations. A refutation for the algebra equations pulls back with constant multiplicative degree overhead and an additive absolute constant, by the substitution calculation in the companion note. The Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She hard instances have linear side length in their lower bound parameter, and the present algebra dimension remains linear in it. This proves the asserted linear degree in $N$. [Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She, Theorem 5.2](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2023.4)

## Precedents and scope

Macaulay inverse systems give the association of a short Gorenstein local algebra with a projective class of a cubic in good characteristic. The contraction algebra here is the symmetric multilinear version of that association. The new exactness argument uses the degree-two socle marker to retain a scalar witness after compression. This is needed because a projective multiplier over the input field need not be a cube. The previous workspace Gorenstein carrier used a padded quadratic space and its trivial extension, with quadratic dimension at that stage. Grochow–Qiao IV gives linear-length reductions to general associative algebra isomorphism. Here the symmetric gadget gives linear dimension in the specific local Gorenstein class, and the explicit proof-degree transfer adds the linear lower bound in that dimension. [Elias–Rossi, Theorem 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.3565) · [Grochow–Qiao IV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16317) · [Agrawal–Saxena, Theorem 2.3](https://www.cse.iitk.ac.in/%75sers/nitin/papers/cubic-forms.pdf)

The main claims have written proofs. CompressedAlgebras.py exhaustively enumerates the graded maps and the ungraded kernel for two small algebras over $\mathbb F_2$, finding 2,048 full automorphisms in each, and checks the scalar obstruction on the one-variable pair over $\mathbb F_7$. The old Gorenstein multiplication is also present in the workspace formalization. The present comparison, count and degree claims have not been formalized there. Publication priority remains unverified.
