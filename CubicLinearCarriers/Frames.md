# Linear tensor carriers from spanning subspaces in characteristic three

Let $k$ have characteristic $3$. A cubic means a formal homogeneous degree-three polynomial on a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space $V$. A transporter from $f$ to $g$ is an invertible linear map $S:V\to V'$ satisfying $g(Sv)=f(v)$ as a polynomial identity. An unmarked instance carries only the cubic coefficients.

**Theorem.** Fix $b$. A cubic on $n$ variables equipped with an unordered family of $m$ spanning subspaces of dimensions $1,\ldots,b$ has a single ordinary tensor carrier of side lengths $O_b(n+m)$ over $k$. Spanning means that the sum of the subspaces is all of $V$. The subspaces can overlap, and repeated subspaces are allowed. Two carriers are isomorphic exactly when a cubic transporter sends the first family to the second with multiplicity. Every tensor transporter decodes to such a cubic transporter, and every such cubic transporter lifts. The construction and decoding use polynomially many field operations. In particular, any class with a polynomial-time computable, isomorphism-equivariant family with $m=O(n)$ admits the same linear-side reduction for unmarked cubics.

Over a finite field of characteristic $3$, an unmarked class with such a family consists of the cubics linearly equivalent to
$$
f(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^m y_i x_i^2+h(x_1,\ldots,x_m)+\sum_{i=1}^m b_i y_i^3, \qquad n=2m,
\tag{1}
$$
where $h$ is an arbitrary cubic. Membership, a basis exhibiting (1), and the equivariant family of $m$ two-dimensional subspaces are computable in deterministic polynomial bit time even when the finite field varies. Consequently on this class the reduction to ordinary TI is deterministic polynomial time, and the output stays over the input field.

## 1. Retaining the restrictions that polarization loses

Use unnormalized complete polarization
$$
P_f(u,v,w)=f(u+v+w)-f(u+v)-f(u+w)-f(v+w)+f(u)+f(v)+f(w).
$$
It is trilinear and its formation commutes with substitution. The coefficient of $x_i^2x_j$ is detected with multiplier $2$ by the component $(e_i,e_i,e_j)$, and the coefficient of $x_ix_jx_\ell$ for distinct indices is detected with multiplier $1$. Its kernel is therefore exactly the pure cubes $\sum_i a_i x_i^3$. If a pure cube vanishes on a spanning family, take a basis among the spanning vectors. In that basis it is again a sum of pure cubes by the characteristic-three binomial identity, and each coefficient is its value on a basis vector. All the coefficients vanish. This argument works over imperfect fields.

For each supplied subspace $W_i$ put $f_i=f|_{W_i}$ and form the local graded unital algebra
$$
A_i=\operatorname{Sym}(W_i^*\oplus k z_i)/(\mathfrak m^4,\ z_i W_i^*,\ f_i-z_i^3),
$$
where $\mathfrak m$ in this presentation is the ideal generated by the degree-one variables. The algebra has basis consisting of the monomials of degree at most three on $W_i^*$, together with $z_i,z_i^2$, since the last relation eliminates $z_i^3$. In particular $\operatorname{Sym}^3(W_i^*)$ injects into the degree-three part. Its positive-degree ideal $J_i$ is the unique maximal ideal, and $\dim A_i=\binom{\dim W_i+3}{3}+2$.

Let $A=\prod_i A_i$, $Z=(z_i)_i$, and let $D: \bigoplus_i W_i\to V$ be the sum of the inclusions. Write $I: \bigoplus_i W_i^*\to A$ for the inclusion into the degree-one covectors in each factor. Consider the following metadata on $A,V$:
$$
P_f,\qquad \text{multiplication on }A,\qquad Z\in A,\qquad I D^*:V^*\to A.
\tag{2}
$$
An isomorphism of these data consists of $S:V\to V'$ and an algebra isomorphism $\phi:A\to A'$ with
$$
P_g(Su,Sv,Sw)=P_f(u,v,w),\quad
\phi Z=Z',\quad
\phi I D^*=I'D'^*S^{-\mathsf T}.
\tag{3}
$$

Every transporter of the cubics and their families yields (3). It permutes the factors and induces the covector substitution $(S|_{W_i})^{-\mathsf T}$ on the corresponding factor. Extend that substitution by $z_i\mapsto z'_{\sigma(i)}$. It preserves all defining relations and the covector restrictions in (3).

In the other direction, each local factor has only the idempotents $0,1$, so the primitive idempotents of $A$ are exactly its factor identities. Hence $\phi$ induces factor isomorphisms $\phi_i:A_i\to A'_{\sigma(i)}$. The equality on $Z$ carries $z_i$ to $z'_{\sigma(i)}$ exactly. On $J_i/J_i^2$, the image of an original degree-one covector has zero $z'_{\sigma(i)}$ coefficient. Indeed, multiply its image by $z'_{\sigma(i)}$ and inspect degree two, using $z_iW_i^*=0$ and the nonzero degree-two class of $z_{\sigma(i)}'{}^2$. Thus $\phi_i$ induces an invertible map $L_i:W_i^*\to W'_{\sigma(i)}{}^*$. The incidence equation on degree one gives
$$
L_i(\eta|_{W_i})=(S^{-\mathsf T}\eta)|_{W'_{\sigma(i)}}\qquad(\eta\in V^*).
$$
If $L_i^\vee:W'_{\sigma(i)}\to W_i$ is the dual map, this says that $S^{-1}|_{W'_{\sigma(i)}}=L_i^\vee$ after the inclusions in $V$. Consequently $S(W_i)=W'_{\sigma(i)}$ and $L_i=(S|_{W_i})^{-\mathsf T}$. Comparing the relation $f_i=z_i^3$ in the associated graded algebras gives $(\operatorname{Sym}^3 L_i)(f_i)=g|_{W'_{\sigma(i)}}$, by the injection of original covector cubics into degree three. The polynomial $f-g\circ S$ now has zero polarization and vanishes on every $W_i$. Spanning forces it to be zero.

## 2. A single ordinary tensor with explicit decoding

First make a tensor of trilinear forms with the following direct-sum partitions:
$$
(V_1,V_1^*,A_1,K_1),\qquad
(V_2,V_2^*,A_2,K_2),\qquad
(V_3,A_3^*,K_3).
$$
Subscripts indicate copies in the corresponding mode, and $K_i=k$. Put the scalar product on $K_1,K_2,K_3$. On each triple listed below, put the evaluation of the dual and primal arguments times the scalar argument:
$$
\begin{array}{llll}
(V_1,V_2^*,K_3), &(K_1,V_2^*,V_3),&
(V_1^*,V_2,K_3), &(V_1^*,K_2,V_3),\\
(A_1,K_2,A_3^*), &(K_1,A_2,A_3^*).
\end{array}
$$
The remaining nonzero blocks are the following.

| Blocks | Trilinear form |
| --- | --- |
| $V_1,V_2,V_3$ | $P_f(u,v,w)$ |
| $A_1,A_2,A_3^*$ | $(a,b,\psi)\mapsto\psi(ab)$ |
| $K_1,K_2,A_3^*$ | $(s,t,\psi)\mapsto st\psi(Z)$ |
| $V_1^*,K_2,A_3^*$ | $(\eta,s,\psi)\mapsto s\psi(I D^*\eta)$ |

To decode a partition-preserving transporter, let $\alpha_i$ be its scalar on $K_i$. The scalar block gives $\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3=1$. Equating the four $V$ evaluations forces the $V_i$ maps to be $\alpha_i S$ for a single invertible $S$, with dual maps $\alpha_i S^{-\mathsf T}$. For instance, if $M_1,M_3$ are the maps on $V_1,V_3$ and $B_2$ acts on $V_2^*$, then $\alpha_3 B_2^{\mathsf T}M_1=\alpha_1 B_2^{\mathsf T}M_3=1_V$. Equating the two $A$ evaluations likewise gives the maps $\alpha_1\phi,\alpha_2\phi,\alpha_3\phi^{-\mathsf T}$. The multiplication block forces $\phi(ab)=\phi(a)\phi(b)$, and invertibility then implies unitality. The other blocks impose exactly (3). To lift, take all scalar maps equal to $1$ and use $S,\phi$ and their inverse transposes.

For completeness, the following field construction removes the partitions while retaining decoding. Use coefficient arrays in this paragraph, converting back to maps on arguments by duality. To remove the parts of the first mode $X=\bigoplus_{i=1}^a X_i$, write $p=\dim X$, $d=\dim Y$ for the second mode, and view the third mode as indexing slices. For $a>1$, append two tag slices per $X_i$, with indices $j=1,\ldots,2a$ and indicated part $i(j)$. Each tag has private row spaces $Y_j,I_j$ and column spaces $X'_j,J_j$. Its three identity rectangles are $(Y_j,Y)$, $(X_{i(j)},X'_j)$, and $(I_j,J_j)$; the last one has rank $r_j=(p+d+1)2^{j-1}$, while the first two have ranks $d,\dim X_{i(j)}$. Old slices are supported in $X$ by $Y$. Assign new coordinates of the other modes to their last existing parts. A partition-preserving witness extends by the matching inverse dual maps on the first two identity rectangles and identities on the remaining new coordinates.

For the reverse, the rank of a slice combination is the sum of its active private ranks $r_j$ plus a remainder between $0$ and $p+d$. The quotient after division by $p+d+1$ therefore specifies precisely the active subset of tags. In any output transporter, a target data slice comes from data slices only, and a target tag $j$ comes from a nonzero multiple $\gamma_j$ of its own tag plus an old data rectangle $C_j$. A single row shear adds $-\gamma_j^{-1}C_j$ from $Y_j$ into $X$ for every $j$. This fixes data slices and simultaneously turns all the tag combinations into pure tag multiples. Absorb the inverse shear into the row witness. The row witness now preserves the column spans $X_{i(j)}\oplus Y_j\oplus I_j$, and the column witness preserves the row spans $Y\oplus X'_j\oplus J_j$. Intersecting the two column spans for a given part recovers $X_i$. Intersecting two distinct row spans recovers $Y$. Restricting the row and column witnesses and taking the original slice-map block gives the partitioned witness. The latter block is invertible because the enlarged slice map is block triangular with invertible tag block. This decoding respects the other two partitions. Iterate in the other modes. With fixed part counts all size increases are linear, and the entire proof uses polynomially many field operations.

This step is in the lineage of partitioned tensor isomorphism and the linear-length theorem of Grochow and Qiao. The spanning family and its local cubic data supply the information lost in characteristic-three polarization. The cited paper explains the usual polarization in its §1.3 and proves the linear-length partition removal in Theorem 5.1. [Grochow–Qiao, Part IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317)

## 3. Intrinsic two-planes for the paired-Hessian class

Let $T=P_f$ and $C(v)$ be the matrix of $T(v,-,-)$. In the coordinates (1),
$$
C(v)=
\begin{pmatrix}
H(v)&2\operatorname{diag}(x_i(v))\\
2\operatorname{diag}(x_i(v))&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Expansion along the last $m$ rows gives $\det C(v)=(-1)^m2^{2m}\prod_i x_i(v)^2$. Under a change of basis this determinant is substituted by the same variable change and multiplied by a nonzero scalar. Therefore its $m$ independent factor-normal lines $k\ell_i\subset V^*$ are intrinsic and unordered. Put $Y=\bigcap_i\ker\ell_i$, and let $X_i$ be the line of $V/Y$ annihilated by the other $m-1$ factor normals. Choose representatives $\ell_i$ and lifts $u_i\in V$ with $\ell_j(u_i)=\delta_{ji}$. The functionals $\varphi_i(y)=T(y,u_i,u_i)$ form a basis of $Y^*$ by the displayed matrix.

Define
$$
W_i=\{v\in V\mid v+Y\in X_i,\quad T(v,u_j,u_j)=0\text{ for every }j\ne i\}.
\tag{4}
$$
If $u_j$ is changed by an element of $Y$, then the new terms in the defining equality involve $T(v,u_j,Y)$ and $T(v,Y,Y)$. Both are zero by (1) when $v+Y\in X_i$ and $j\ne i$. Scaling $u_j$ scales its equality by a unit. Thus the family defined by (4) is intrinsic. Each $W_i$ maps onto $X_i$ because the $m-1$ equations are independent on $Y$. Its kernel in $Y$ is the line annihilated by $\varphi_j$ for $j\ne i$. Consequently $\dim W_i=2$ and the $W_i$ sum directly to $V$, by first projecting a linear dependency to $V/Y$ and then to the dual basis lines of $Y$. Their algebra factors in §1 have dimension $12$.

### Deterministic recovery and recognition over finite fields

Assume the input field $k$ is given in an ordinary irreducible-polynomial representation over $\mathbb F_3$. Reject odd $n$ and put $m=n/2$ otherwise. Use $K=k$ if $k$ has more than
$$
B=2+n+(n-1)\max\{m,\tbinom m2\}
$$
elements. Otherwise choose the smallest extension degree $e$ with $|k|^e>B$ and use $K=\mathbb F_{|k|^e}$, represented over $k$. In this latter case $|k|^e\le |k|B\le B^2$, so an irreducible polynomial of degree $e$ can be found by enumerating monic polynomials and testing irreducibility via modular powers and gcds. Thus extension construction takes polynomial time.

Use the moment curve $\mu(t)=(1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1})$ over $K$. A nonzero linear form restricts to a nonzero univariate polynomial of degree at most $n-1$. Scan $(n-1)m+1$ distinct $t$'s for a point $u=\mu(t)$ with $\det C(u)\ne0$. A member of the class always has one. Fix the first such $u$. Now scan $(n-1)\binom m2+1$ distinct $t$'s for a point $v=\mu(t)$ with all ratios $\ell_i(v)/\ell_i(u)$ distinct. A collision is the vanishing of a nonzero linear form on $v$, so the scan length suffices. Test this without knowing the forms by interpolating $p(z)=\det C(v-zu)$. Require that $p$ be proportional to the square of a degree-$m$ polynomial with $m$ distinct roots in $K$. In detail, compute the monic $g=\gcd(p,z^{|K|}-z)$ using modular powers, and check $\deg g=m$ and $p=\operatorname{lc}(p)g^2$. For a member of the class this test is exactly the ratio test.

To recover all the roots of $g$, choose a prime-field basis $\eta_j$ of $K$. Split $g$ and each of its remaining factors by gcds with
$$
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_3}(\eta_j z)-a,\qquad a\in\{0,1,2\}.
$$
The trace polynomials are computed modulo the current factor by repeated cubing. Every distinct pair of roots is eventually separated, since the trace pairing of a finite field is nondegenerate. Indeed the trace polynomial has degree $3^{[K: \mathbb F_3]-1}<|K|$ and is nonzero, so there is an element of nonzero trace. Multiplication by any nonzero root difference is invertible, and the trace is linear, so some basis element separates that pair.

For a root $\rho_i$, put $w=v-\rho_i u$. On a member this point is on exactly one factor hyperplane. The coefficient of $s^2$ in $\det C(w+s e)$, denoted $q_i(e)$, is then a nonzero multiple of $\ell_i(e)^2$, since all other factors are nonzero at $w$. Interpolate the determinant in $s$ to evaluate $q_i$. Take a coordinate vector $e_j$ with $q_i(e_j)\ne0$ and recover the normalized coefficients from
$$
\frac{q_i(e_j+e_h)-q_i(e_j)-q_i(e_h)}{2q_i(e_j)}=\frac{\ell_i(e_h)}{\ell_i(e_j)}.
$$
The normalized coefficients lie in $k$ for a member.

For exact recognition, reject if the searches or reconstruction fail, if the normalized forms do not lie in $k$, or if they are linearly dependent. Use linear algebra over $k$ to construct $Y$, quotient-dual lifts $u_i$, and their associated $\varphi_i$. Reject if these functionals do not form a basis of $Y^*$. Select a basis $\omega_i$ of $Y$ with $\varphi_j(\omega_i)=2\delta_{ij}$. Check the original cubic by substitution into the basis $(u_1,\ldots,u_m,\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_m)$, accepting only the exact coefficient pattern (1). This proves soundness on arbitrary inputs, even if an earlier determinant test happened to succeed. For a genuine member the recovered forms are the true factor lines, and arbitrary quotient-dual lifts change the $y_i$ by multiples of linear forms in the new $x$'s. In the terms $y_i x_i^2$ these changes contribute only to $h$, and a $y_i^3$ contributes only a $y_i^3$ and an $x$-only cubic by characteristic three. The chosen normalization makes the mixed $y_i x_i^2$ coefficient $1$. Thus every member passes. All scans, degrees, linear algebra dimensions and explicit tensor sizes are polynomial in $n$, and the trace and extension operations are polynomial in $\log|k|$. Only determinant preprocessing uses $K$, and the recovered subspaces and the tensor carrier are over $k$.

The class includes concise polar tensors whose centroid has no nontrivial idempotents, even after extending scalars. For example, for $m\ge3$ let the triples with nonzero squarefree coefficients in $h$ connect all the $x_i$'s. To verify the claim, write $x_i,y_i$ also for the coordinate basis vectors. For a centroid endomorphism $E$, equality between moving $E$ across slots of $T(y_i,y_j,x_j)$ shows that $E$ preserves $Y$. Moving $E$ across the first two slots in $T(x_i,x_j,y_j)$ for $i\ne j$ gives a diagonal quotient action on $V/Y$. Using $T(y_i,x_j,x_j)$ shows that the action on $Y$ is diagonal with the same entries. A nonzero squarefree coefficient equates the three corresponding diagonal entries by the centroid identity. The connectivity assumption now makes this diagonal action scalar. Subtract the scalar endomorphism. The remaining map has only a component from the $x$-space to $Y$, and the identity on $T(x_i,x_j,x_j)$, for $i\ne j$, forces that component to be diagonal. These diagonal maps form a square-zero ideal in the centroid and no nontrivial idempotent is possible. Conciseness follows directly from the contraction matrix, since its vanishing first forces all the $x_i(v)=0$ and then all $y_i(v)=0$.

## 4. Exact finite control

The following Python control uses $\mathbb F_3$ with $m=3$, $h=x_1x_2x_3+x_2^3$, and the pure-$y$ part $y_1^3+2y_3^3$. It applies an invertible shear to the ambient coordinates. It checks the determinant formula on all $729$ vectors, checks the three two-planes by full enumeration, and confirms that deleting the pure-$y$ part changes the restriction to the intrinsic $Y$ while leaving polarization intact. The last observation certifies inequivalence of these two forms.

```python
from itertools import product

n = 6
vectors = list(product(range(3), repeat=n))
basis = [tuple(int(i == j) for i in range(n)) for j in range(n)]

def change(v):
    return ((v[0] + v[4]) % 3, (v[1] + v[5]) % 3,
            (v[2] + v[3]) % 3, *v[3:])

def polar(a, b, c):
    return (sum(a[i] * b[j] * c[k] for i, j, k in
                [(0,1,2),(0,2,1),(1,0,2),(1,2,0),(2,0,1),(2,1,0)])
            + 2 * sum(a[i] * b[i] * c[i+3] + a[i] * b[i+3] * c[i]
                      + a[i+3] * b[i] * c[i] for i in range(3))) % 3

def det(matrix):
    a = [row[:] for row in matrix]
    value = 1
    for j in range(n):
        pivot = next((i for i in range(j, n) if a[i][j]), None)
        if pivot is None:
            return 0
        if pivot != j:
            a[pivot], a[j] = a[j], a[pivot]
            value = -value
        d = a[j][j]
        value = value * d % 3
        for i in range(j + 1, n):
            multiplier = a[i][j] * d % 3
            a[i] = [(s - multiplier * t) % 3 for s, t in zip(a[i], a[j])]
    return value

def frame_condition(a, i):
    return all(a[j] == 0 and polar(a, basis[j], basis[j]) == 0
               for j in range(3) if j != i)

def cubic(a, marked):
    value = a[0] * a[1] * a[2] + sum(a[i+3] * a[i]**2 for i in range(3))
    return (value + a[1]**3 + marked * (a[3]**3 + 2 * a[5]**3)) % 3

directions = list(map(change, basis))
for v in vectors:
    a = change(v)
    contraction = [[polar(a, b, c) for c in directions] for b in directions]
    assert det(contraction) == 2 * (a[0] * a[1] * a[2])**2 % 3
for i in range(3):
    got = {change(v) for v in vectors if frame_condition(change(v), i)}
    expected = {a for a in vectors
                if all(not a[j] and not a[j+3] for j in range(3) if j != i)}
    assert got == expected and len(got) == 9
Y = [change(v) for v in vectors if not any(change(v)[:3])]
assert len(Y) == 27
assert {cubic(a, 0) for a in Y} == {0}
assert {cubic(a, 1) for a in Y} == {0, 1, 2}
print("729 determinants; 3 frames of 9 vectors; 27 vectors in Y")
```

The executed output was `729 determinants; 3 frames of 9 vectors; 27 vectors in Y`. The proof is mathematical, with a finite arithmetic control. The general linear-side reduction for unrestricted characteristic-three cubics remains outside the theorem’s scope. Priority for the spanning-subspace construction and the paired-Hessian class is unverified.
