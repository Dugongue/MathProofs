# An ordinary tensor of side n + c + 9 for a marked polarization

Over a field $k$, let $P:V^3\to k$ be symmetric trilinear and $\ell:V\to k$ linear. Put $n=\dim V$, $D=\mathrm{rad}(P)$ and $c=n-\dim D$. There is a unital associative algebra of dimension **$n+c+9$** whose ordinary multiplication tensor encodes exact simultaneous isometry of $(P,\ell)$. Its three matrix legs may be transformed independently. Any witness yields an exact simultaneous isometry in polynomially many field operations. In particular this gives side at most $2n+9$ for *every refinement* of a finite characteristic-three polarization passing the two rank tests in [Fibers.md](Fibers.md).

## The algebra

Start with the commutative local algebra $L$ for a symmetric trilinear form. Its four grades are
$$
 k,\qquad V\oplus kz\oplus kw,\qquad B\oplus ku\oplus ke,\qquad ks,
 \qquad B=\mathrm{span}\,P(V,V,-)=D^\perp\subseteq V^*.
$$
The first grade contains the unit. The positive-grade products are $xy=P(x,y,-)$, $x\lambda=\lambda(x)s$ for $x,y\in V$ and $\lambda\in B$, and $z^2=u$, $zu=s$, $zw=e$. Other basis products vanish apart from commutative reversals. Symmetry of $P$ verifies associativity. All positive grades are generated by degree one, so the radical powers give this associated grading intrinsically. The dimension of $L$ is $n+c+6$. [Earlier compressed algebra and anchor proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowCubicFour/CompressedAlgebras.md)

Let $M=k^2$ with a fixed nonzero operator $N$ satisfying $N^2=0$. For $x+\alpha z+\beta w$ in degree one set $\tau=\ell(x)-\alpha$. Define the $L$ action on $M$ by letting the unit act as identity, degree one act as $\tau N$, and all higher degrees act by zero. This is a module precisely because products of any two positive-degree actions vanish. Form
$$
 H(P,\ell)=\begin{pmatrix}L&M\\0&k\end{pmatrix}
$$
with ordinary upper-triangular multiplication and the usual right scalar action on $M$. It is unital, associative, and has exactly the asserted dimension. The mark is nonzero on $z$ even when $\ell=0$.

## Which data survive an arbitrary transporter?

First suppose we have an algebra isomorphism of the triangular algebras. The linear span of their commutators is exactly $M$, since the diagonal entries commute and $[e_L,m]=m$ for the left corner idempotent $e_L$. The quotient is $L\times k$ and $L$ is its unique local factor of dimension greater than one. Thus we get an algebra isomorphism $\phi:L\to L'$ and an isomorphism on the bimodules. The bimodule actions factor through the quotient, since $M^2=0$. Intertwining the action on the two-dimensional module gives $\tau'\phi=\kappa\tau$ on the first radical quotient for a nonzero scalar $\kappa$. Indeed the annihilator there is the kernel of $\tau$, and both functionals are nonzero.

Here are the intrinsic pieces of the first radical quotient of $L$. Its annihilator against itself under the product into degree two is $D$. The annihilator in degree two against degree one is $ke$. Modulo those two subspaces, the radical of the degree-one multiplication into degree two modulo $ke$ is the line $kw$. Before quotienting the output by $ke$, the annihilator of this $w$ line in degree one modulo $D$ is $(V/D)\oplus kw$. Modulo $kw$, the orthogonal complement of $V/D$ for the product modulo $ke$ is $kz$. These identifications follow by testing products with $z$, $w$, and vectors of $V$; $P$ has zero radical on $V/D$.

It follows that the degree-one map of $\phi$ sends $D$ isomorphically to $D'$, sends $x\in V$ to $Sx$ plus a $w'$ component, and sends $z$ to $az'+d$ plus a $w'$ component, with $a\ne0$, $d\in D'$. The resulting $S:V\to V'$ is invertible because it is invertible on $D$ and on the quotient by $D$. Cubing the image of $z$ and using the triple products on $V$ gives
$$
 P'(Sx,Sy,Sv)=a^3P(x,y,v),\qquad \ell'(Sx)=\kappa\ell(x).
$$
The first equation also holds in characteristic two or three by direct multiplication of the displayed grades. Set $H=S/a$. This already matches $P$ exactly.

If $\ell'$ vanishes on $D'$, evaluating the module character on $z$ gives $\kappa=a$, so $H$ matches the mark. Otherwise take $r\in D'$ with $\ell'(r)=1$, and put
$$
 h(x)=H(x)+r\bigl(\ell(x)-\ell'(Hx)\bigr).
$$
It matches both tensors and is invertible. To see the latter, $\ell'H=(\kappa/a)\ell$, so the rank-one modification of the identity after composition with $H^{-1}$ has determinant $\ell(H^{-1}r)=a/\kappa\ne0$. The formula also permits zero discrepancy, in which case $H$ itself suffices. Conversely any exact pair transporter lifts using its action on $V$, the induced inverse dual on $D^\perp$, identity on the arm and its powers, and identity on the module and scalar corner.

Finally suppose the given witness is an ordinary tensor isotopy $R(xy)=U(x)V(y)$ of the two associative unital algebras. Put $p=U(1),q=V(1)$. The equalities $R(x)=U(x)q=pV(x)$ show that $p,q$ are units by finite dimensionality. Then $\Phi(x)=(pq)^{-1}R(x)$ is a unital algebra isomorphism, by associativity. In coordinates it is enough to solve the linear system $L_{R(1)}\Phi=R$. The induced $\phi$ above is read on the left diagonal corner, and the indicated blocks and one linear radical solve give $h$. This describes extraction even when the supplied three transformations differ.

The implementation emits an ordinary tensor directly with a basis of $B$ found by elimination. For the eight-variable full-rank example it has shape $25\times25\times25$. Tests check all basis pairs of genuine, nonsymmetric isotopies over $\mathbb F_3$ and $\mathbb F_9$, both with full polarization rank and with a nonzero mark on the radical.
