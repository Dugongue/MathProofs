# Intrinsic block restrictions for cubic equivalence in characteristic three

Let $k=\mathbb F_q$ have characteristic three and be given explicitly. There is a deterministic polynomial-time transformation from a cubic form on $n$ variables to one ordinary three-tensor over the same field. The side lengths are
$$
                O\!\left(n+\sum_{i\ {\rm active}}\binom{d_i}{2}\right),
                \tag{1}
$$
where the $d_i$ are defined by a canonical idempotent decomposition of the polarization and an active block has nonzero internal polarization. Two cubic forms are equivalent if and only if their output tensors are equivalent. No factorization or matching of field components is required. In particular, the bound is linear whenever every internal polarization vanishes. This includes $xQ(w)+\ell^3$ for a nonsingular quadratic form $Q$ on at least three variables, even though its polarization has scalar centroid. The argument is for formal polynomials and applies over $\mathbb F_3$ itself.

## A canonical étale algebra from differential symmetries

Write $V=k^n$ and let $P_f$ be the unnormalized symmetric polarization of $f$, defined by the three-variable finite difference. On monomials with distinct variables it records the coefficient through a single entry. On $x_i^2x_j$ it records twice the coefficient through the entry with indices $i,i,j$. It kills $x_i^3$. Consequently its kernel consists exactly of linear cubes over the perfect field $k$. It also satisfies
$$
                  P_f(u,x,x)=2\,df_x(u).
                  \tag{2}
$$
Both assertions can be checked on the three monomial patterns. In particular $P_f(x,x,x)=0$ as a formal polynomial.

Define
$$
 \mathcal L_P=\{D\in\operatorname{End}_k(V): P(Dx,x,x)=0
                          \text{ as a formal polynomial in }x\}.
$$
By (2), it is the linear derivation algebra annihilating $f$. Equivalently, $I+\epsilon D$ preserves $f$ over $k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$. Thus it is closed under commutators. Any transporter of $P$ carries $\mathcal L_P$ by conjugation, since the defining condition uses $P$ alone. This is the formal differential method used for polynomial symmetries. [Grochow, Lie algebra conjugacy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.2012) · [Kayal, Affine projections of polynomials](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/Projection.pdf)

Let $\mathcal A_P$ be the unital associative matrix algebra generated by $\mathcal L_P$, and put $Z=Z(\mathcal A_P)$. Compute $\mathcal L_P$ by equating coefficients in the defining condition. To compute $\mathcal A_P$, repeatedly multiply a basis of the current span on the right by a basis of $\mathcal L_P$ and adjoin the products, beginning with the identity. The dimension is at most $n^2$, and stabilization gives all words in the generators. The equations for $Z$ are linear commutator equations against a basis of $\mathcal A_P$.

For $q^t\ge \dim_k Z$, define
$$
                       E=\operatorname{im}(z\mapsto z^{q^t}:Z\to Z).
                       \tag{3}
$$
The map is a $k$-linear algebra homomorphism because $Z$ is commutative. The nilradical $J$ of $Z$ has nilpotence index at most $\dim_k Z$, so $q^t$th powers kill it. The finite reduced algebra $Z/J$ is a product of finite fields, and its $q^t$th power map is bijective. It follows that $E$ maps bijectively to $Z/J$. This also makes it the unique largest étale subalgebra, since the $q^t$th power map is bijective on every étale subalgebra over $k$. Repeated squaring and Gaussian elimination compute (3) without computing a splitting field. The construction is canonical under conjugation.

After extension to an algebraic closure, write $e_i$ for the primitive idempotents of $E$ and $V_i=e_iV_{\bar k}$. Set $d_i=\dim_{\bar k}V_i$. The decomposition is compatible with all transporters of $P$. It may have cross terms in $P$, so the whole polarization is kept in the reduction.

## Restriction without field factorization

The separability idempotent $\delta\in E\otimes_k E$ is the unique solution of
$$
           \operatorname{mult}(\delta)=1,\qquad
           (a\otimes1)\delta=(1\otimes a)\delta\quad(a\in E).
$$
These are linear equations using the multiplication table, and the split case verifies existence and uniqueness, since there the solution is $\sum_i e_i\otimes e_i$. Define $\Delta=\delta_{12}\delta_{23}\in E^{\otimes_k3}$, acting on $V^{\otimes_k 3}$ by the three matrix actions. After splitting, $\Delta=\sum_i e_i^{\otimes3}$. Thus for an arbitrary ordered trilinear lift $L$ with $L(x,x,x)=f(x)$, the diagonal of $L\circ\Delta$ is the descended cubic
$$
                         f_{\rm int}(x)=\sum_i f(e_i x).
                         \tag{4}
$$
The right side proves independence from the chosen lift. The lift can be obtained by placing each cubic coefficient in one of its ordered triples. This uses neither normalization by six nor an equivariant selection of representatives. [Knus, Merkurjev, Rost and Tignol, separability idempotents of étale algebras, §18](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/involutions.pdf)

Put $P_{\rm int}=P\circ\Delta$. This is the polarization of (4). Define
$$
          I_0=\{a\in E:P_{\rm int}(ax,y,z)=0
                                \text{ for all }x,y,z\in V\}.
$$
After splitting, $I_0$ is the ideal supported on those blocks on which $P|_{V_i^3}=0$. Its identity $e_0$ is obtained by solving $e_0a=a$ in $I_0$ for each basis element $a$ of $I_0$. Take $e_0=0$ for the zero ideal. Set $e_+=1-e_0$ and $\pi=e_+:V\to V_+=e_+V$. The restrictions
$$
               f_0(x)= f_{\rm int}(e_0x),\qquad
               f_+(x)= f_{\rm int}(e_+x)
$$
satisfy $f_{\rm int}=f_0+f_+$. Here $f_0=\ell_0^3$ for a unique $k$-linear form $\ell_0$, obtained by inverse Frobenius on the pure coefficients. The active blocks are exactly the primitive summands supported by $e_+$.

The triple $(P,\ell_0,f_+)$ classifies the cubic under simultaneous changes of variables. The forward implication follows from conjugation equivariance of the constructions and uniqueness of cube roots. For the reverse implication, the common polarization after transport says that the difference of the two cubics is a linear cube. The corresponding $E$ decompositions are the same, because the transported polarizations are the same. Equality of $\ell_0$ and $f_+$ gives equality of $f_{\rm int}$. A linear cube is the sum of its own restrictions to the geometric direct summands. Thus the difference equals its internal restriction and vanishes. This proves the reverse implication over $k$.

## One ordinary tensor and both witness directions

Let
$$
           W=\bigwedge\nolimits^2_{e_+E}V_+,\qquad a=\dim_kW,
$$
taking $W=0$ if $V_+=0$. Compute $W$ by imposing the balancing relations $(b v)\otimes w=v\otimes(bw)$ for $b\in e_+E$ and the alternating relations on $V_+\otimes_k V_+$. After splitting, $W_{\bar k}$ is the direct sum of the exterior squares on the active blocks. Hence $a$ is the sum in (1). Define $\omega(x,y)=\pi x\wedge_{e_+E}\pi y$. Choose as $L_+$ an ordered trilinear lift pulled back from the active part of $L\circ\Delta$, so that $L_+(x,x,x)=f_+(x)$ and its only geometric terms are internal to active blocks.

Use three modes with ordered blocks
$$
  (X\oplus W^*)\oplus V_1\oplus k s,\qquad
  Y\oplus Y'\oplus k b,\qquad
  (Z'\oplus W^*)\oplus V_3\oplus k t .
$$
Here $X,Y,Z',V_1,V_3$ are copies of $V$, and $Y'$ is its dual. Write $u,w$ for the two $W^*$ inputs and $x,y,z,v_1,v_3$ for the indicated $V$ inputs. Link the three blocks $V_1,Y,V_3$ to the same change of basis $A$ and the three singleton blocks to the same nonzero scalar. Leave the other blocks unlinked. The partitioned tensor is the following trilinear expression:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal T_f={}&L_+(x,y,z)+u(\omega(y,z))+w(\omega(x,y))\\
 &+P_f(v_1,y,v_3)+sbt+\ell_0(x)bt\\
 &+t\langle x,y'\rangle+s\langle y',z\rangle+t\langle v_1,y'\rangle .
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
All pairings use the fixed duality of the copies. The two exterior controls share their respective mode blocks with the cores $X$ and $Z'$, so changes within these larger blocks can include shears.

Suppose a linked partition transporter carries the tensor of $g$ back to that of $f$. The $sbt$ block forces the linked scalar to have cube one, hence to equal one in characteristic three. The $t\langle v_1,y'\rangle$ block forces the map on $Y'$ to be the inverse dual of $A$. The two other pairing blocks force the $X$ and $Z'$ quotient maps to be exactly $A$ and force zero maps from either exterior input into its core. Indeed, equality in $t\langle x,y'\rangle$ holds for all $x,u,y'$, so the core projection of the first block map is $A x$, independent of $u$. The same argument applies to the third block. The polar block and the linear-form block give $P_f=P_g\circ A^{\otimes3}$ and $\ell_{0,f}=\ell_{0,g}\circ A$. Evaluating the main data block on $x=y=z$, with input exterior coordinates zero, makes both exterior terms vanish since the three core outputs are equal. Therefore $f_{+,f}=f_{+,g}\circ A$. The reconstruction in (4) gives $f=g\circ A$.

Conversely, suppose $f=g\circ A$. It transports the algebras $E$ and the active projectors, and induces an invertible map $B:W_f\to W_g$ on balanced exterior squares. Compare $L_{+,f}$ with $L_{+,g}\circ A^{\otimes3}$. Their difference has zero diagonal and is supported, geometrically, on individual active components. Such differences are exactly absorbed by the two exterior controls. On each split component, the ordered-basis tensors over a fixed commuting monomial form an orbit under all permutations of three slots. The kernel of the sum of their coefficients is spanned by differences of adjacent interchanges. Differences in slots one and two are alternating in those slots and factor through their exterior square, and likewise for slots two and three. Thus, over an algebraic closure, there are linear maps $M,N:V\to W_f^*$ such that
$$
 L_{+,f}(x,y,z)-L_{+,g}(Ax,Ay,Az)
                 =M(x)(\omega_f(y,z))+N(z)(\omega_f(x,y)).
$$
The equation is a linear system over $k$, so a solution over an extension gives a solution over $k$. Take all three linked core maps to be $A$, all three singleton maps to be the identity, and $Y'$ to transform by the inverse dual. On the larger exterior blocks use
$$
     (x,u)\longmapsto(Ax,B^{-*}(u+M(x))),\qquad
     (z,w)\longmapsto(Az,B^{-*}(w+N(z))),
$$
where $B^{-*}$ is the inverse dual. These are invertible, preserve the pairing blocks, and realize exactly the required lift correction. The other blocks match by functoriality. This supplies the linked transporter.

To erase the links explicitly, append separate blocks $D_1=V^*,k\alpha_1,k\sigma_1$ in mode one, $D_2=V^*,k\alpha_2,k\sigma_2$ in mode two, and $k\sigma_3$ in mode three. With $d_1,d_2$ the inputs in the two dual blocks, add the terms
$$
\begin{aligned}
 &\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3\ +\ \sigma_3\langle v_1,d_2\rangle
     +\sigma_1\langle d_2,v_3\rangle
     +\sigma_3\langle d_1,y\rangle
     +\sigma_2\langle d_1,v_3\rangle \\
 &\hspace{27mm}+\ \sigma_3s\alpha_2+\sigma_1\alpha_2t
                    +\sigma_3\alpha_1 b+\sigma_2\alpha_1t .
\end{aligned}
$$
In a partition transporter of this enlarged tensor, the new anchor triple forces the product of its three singleton scalings to be one. Rescaling all three mode transformations by the respective inverse scalars preserves the entire transported tensor and fixes the new anchors. The first four pairings then identify the maps on $V_1,Y,V_3$, because each dual pairing gives the inverse dual map on its auxiliary block. The last four pairings impose the same equality on $s,b,t$. Conversely, any linked transporter extends by fixing the new anchors and using the appropriate inverse dummy maps on the dual blocks. This is the identity-link construction of Futorny, Grochow and Sergeichuk in this layout. [Futorny, Grochow and Sergeichuk, Wildness for tensors, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219)

The resulting partition counts are $(6,6,4)$ and the side lengths are $(3n+a+3,3n+3,2n+a+2)$. Apply Grochow and Qiao's Theorem 5.1 to remove these remaining partitions over the same field. Its explicit side formulas have first two coefficient rows $(199922,65616)$ and $(12492,4102)$ in $(n,a)$, up to fixed additive constants. Their determinant is $404972$, so output shapes separate different pairs $(n,a)$. This gives one tensor per input, including when the geometric decomposition types differ or their exterior dimensions differ. Isomorphic cubics have the same $a$ by functoriality. The side bound is (1) because all partition counts are fixed. The input with no variables can be assigned the zero tensor of shape $1\times1\times1$, which has a separate layout. All computations before compilation are matrix multiplication, exponentiation with polynomial-length exponents, or polynomial-size linear systems in the dense input dimension. Field operations and the inverse Frobenius are deterministic polynomial in $\log q$ in the explicit finite-field representation. No component permutation is enumerated. [Grochow and Qiao, linear-length reductions, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317)

## Comparison with the polarization centroid

When $P$ has zero radical, let $C_P$ be its centroid, the endomorphisms that may be moved between any of the three arguments without changing $P$. This is a commutative algebra. For $a,b$ in it,
$$
 P(abx,y,z)=P(bx,ay,z)=P(x,ay,bz)=P(ax,y,bz)=P(bax,y,z),
$$
so concision gives $ab=ba$. Moving the products between slots shows closure under multiplication. Let $E_C$ be its étale subalgebra constructed as in (3). The $E_C$-blocks of $P$ have no cross terms, by the centroid identities for idempotents. Hence each $a\in E_C$ lies in $\mathcal L_P$, since in the split basis it multiplies the expression $P(v_i,v_i,v_i)=0$ independently on each block.

Every $D\in\mathcal L_P$ preserves $f$ and therefore $P$ to first order over the dual numbers. Conjugating the centroid by $I+\epsilon D$ shows that $[D,-]$ is a derivation of $C_P$. It annihilates the $q^t$th powers of that commutative algebra. Thus $D$ commutes with $E_C$, for every such $D$. This proves $E_C\subseteq Z(\mathcal A_P)$ and hence $E_C\subseteq E$. The new absolute blocks refine the centroid blocks, and dropping the inactive blocks can only lower the exterior-square contribution. [Huang, Lu, Ye and Zhang, centers of multilinear forms and their decompositions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07915)

## A quadratic block with zero exterior cost

Consider $f=xQ(w)+\ell(x,w)^3$ with $Q$ nonsingular on a space of dimension $m\ge3$. For a matrix $D(x,w)=(a x+\phi(w),b x+B w)$, the formal derivative of the cube is zero, and the derivative of the mixed term is
$$
     (a x+\phi(w))Q(w)+x\,B_Q(w,bx+Bw),
$$
where $B_Q$ is the symmetric polar bilinear form of $Q$. The $x^0$ coefficient forces $\phi=0$ and the $x^2$ coefficient forces $b=0$. The $x^1$ equation then says $B=aI+S$ with $S$ skew with respect to $B_Q$, since $-1/2=1$ in characteristic three. Thus $\mathcal L_P=k I\oplus(0\oplus\mathfrak{so}(Q))$. In a diagonal basis for $Q$ with entries $q_i\ne0$, the skew matrices $X_{ij}=q_jE_{ij}-q_iE_{ji}$ satisfy $X_{ij}X_{jk}=q_jq_kE_{ik}$ for distinct indices. They generate all matrix units on the quadratic block, including diagonal units by products. The unital associative algebra is therefore $k\oplus\operatorname{End}(W_Q)$, acting on the line and the quadratic space $W_Q$. Its center separates those two blocks, and both internal polarizations vanish. Formula (5) has linear side length with $a=0$.

For comparison the polarization of this cubic is concise and its centroid is scalar, also geometrically. Contracting a radical vector against two quadratic-space entries first kills its line coordinate. Contracting against one line entry and one quadratic-space entry then kills its quadratic-space coordinate. With $e$ on the $x$ line, the only nonzero pattern of the polar tensor has one line entry and two quadratic-space entries. Moving a centroid endomorphism between slots in evaluations on $(e,e,w)$ forces $b=0$ by nonsingularity. Evaluating on $(e,w,w')$ forces $B=aI$. On three quadratic-space entries the centroid equation implies $\phi(w)B_Q(w',w'')=\phi(w')B_Q(w,w'')$. If $\phi\ne0$, take $\phi(w)=1$ and a nonzero $w'\in\ker\phi$ to contradict nonsingularity. Thus the center refinement can lower the side bound from quadratic to linear on arbitrarily large concise polarizations.

## Exact controls and scope

Formal derivative kernels, associative closures, and centers were checked over $\mathbb F_3$ for both diagonal discriminant classes in quadratic-space dimensions two through six. For every dimension at least three the computed Lie, associative, and center dimensions were $1+m(m-1)/2$, $1+m^2$, and $2$ respectively. A nonsplit example over $\mathbb F_3$ took $f=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_9/\mathbb F_3}(x^2y)$ on $\mathbb F_9^2$. Its differential algebra and étale center were the two-dimensional multiplication algebra of $\mathbb F_9$. The separability projector fixed all 64 coefficients of the ordered trace lift, and the balanced exterior dimension was two instead of the ordinary exterior dimension six. Exact elimination also checked that the eight balanced diagonal-kernel directions lie in the two-shear span. A separate control checked this ordered-lift correction on all 3,888 pairs of a binary cubic over $\mathbb F_3$ and an invertible binary change of variables.

The bound is controlled by the active block sizes. Publication priority and Lean verification are unestablished.
