# One-sign congruence for independent split Pfaffians

Let $F=\mathbb F_3$. An input is two based alternating matrix spaces $D,D'$ of dimension $m$ on based vector spaces $V,V'$ of dimension $2m$. The sign test asks for a congruence $Y:V\to V'$ such that
$$
\chi(Y)=\det_F(Y)\det_F\bigl(\rho_D(Y):D\to D'\bigr)=+1,
$$
where $\rho_D(Y)(B)=Y^{-\mathsf T} B Y^{-1}$. Both determinants use the supplied bases. Assume that each formal Pfaffian splits over an algebraic closure into $m$ independent linear factors. Independence here means that the factors form a basis of the dual label space. Frobenius orbit degrees and their multiplicities are unrestricted.

**Result.** On this family the sign test has a deterministic polynomial-time Turing reduction to ordinary TI over $F$. It has a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction if a polynomial-time rule supplies a bounded tuple of source labels forming a base for the induced permutation group. A base is a tuple whose pointwise stabilizer has trivial action on all labels. For example, if every nonidentity permutation in the induced group has no fixed label, its first label suffices. The result uses the label action itself, so its kernel on the matrix space may be arbitrarily large. The many-one proof also applies to any polynomially generated set of compatible paired marks such that each viable branch has only one induced label bijection and all input transporters occur in some branch. These are conditions on the ordinary congruences. No sign oracle is needed to choose the marks.

## Recover the labels and a compatible permutation sign

Write $P_D$ for the Pfaffian in the raw $V$ frame. If $x=(1,t,\ldots,t^{m-1})$ in any basis of $D$, each geometric linear factor gives a nonzero univariate polynomial in $t$. Their product has degree at most $m(m-1)$. Evaluate over a field extension of cardinality larger than this bound and at most three times the first sufficient integer threshold, with $F$ itself allowed. This extension can be built by enumerating irreducible polynomial candidates of logarithmic degree. Testing invertibility of $x$ finds an anchor $a$. The extension is only a workspace for polynomially many field operations.

Write $R_x=a^{-1}x$ for $x\in D$ in this extension. The logarithmic second and third derivatives of the Pfaffian at $a$, with signs and constants chosen as below, can be computed as
$$
\beta_a(x,y)=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(R_xR_y),\qquad
\gamma_a(x,y,z)=\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}(R_xR_yR_z+R_xR_zR_y).
$$
Indeed $\log\operatorname{Pf}$ has formal derivative one half that of $\log\det$, interpreted by $dP/P$ at an invertible point. The signs are $-D^2\log P$ and $\tfrac12D^3\log P$, respectively. In geometric factor coordinates $s_i(x)=\ell_i(x)/\ell_i(a)$ these are $\sum_i s_i(x)s_i(y)$ and $\sum_i s_i(x)s_i(y)s_i(z)$. The former is the restriction-of-scalars trace Gram pairing. It is nonsingular by independence of the factors, equivalently by the separable trace pairing. The calculation uses the trace Gram construction from Alpöge's release. [Alpöge's release](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)

Solve $\beta_a(L_x y,z)=\gamma_a(x,y,z)$ for the linear maps $L_x$. Their span $E_D$ in $\operatorname{End}(D)$ is the diagonal algebra in the independent geometric factor coordinates. It is independent of $a$ and Frobenius stable. Compute its rational matrices by intersecting the span over the small extension with $\operatorname{End}_F(D)$ in coordinates. Thus $E_D$ is finite étale and its action makes $D$ a free module of rank one over it. Compute its primitive rational idempotents by splitting the fixed algebra of $z\mapsto z^3$, which is a product of copies of $F$: the simultaneous eigenspaces for multiplication by a basis of that fixed algebra produce the idempotents from the components of $1$. Choose a nonzero vector in each rational summand of $D$ and sum them to obtain a rational anchor $a_D$. Its coordinates are nonzero at every geometric factor. In coordinates $x=z a_D$ for $z\in E_D=\prod_j E_j$ this gives
$$
P_D(z a_D)=P_D(a_D)\prod_j N_{E_j/F}(z_j).
$$

Every congruence induces an isomorphism $\sigma:E_D\to E_{D'}$ by conjugation with its label map $f=\rho_D(Y)$, since the Pfaffian hyperplane sets correspond. In the above module coordinates $f(z)=u\sigma(z)$ for a unit $u$. Let $c=\det \beta_{D'}/\det \beta_D$ for the two module-to-raw frame matrices, and put $\rho=P_{D'}(a_{D'})/P_D(a_D)$. The Pfaffian identity gives $(\det Y)^{-1}=\rho N(u)$, while $\det f=c N(u)\det_F\sigma$. Therefore
$$
\chi(Y)=c\rho^{-1}\det_F\sigma .
$$
In particular every matrix congruence inducing the same $\sigma$ has the same sign. We can reject mismatched multisets of field degrees.

Use one common field model for each degree on both sides and match the explicit factors to it. A fixed finite-field isomorphism algorithm finds these identifications. Choose their bases and a full-degree generator of each model, using a polynomial defining the common model, and choose $1$ in degree one. The ordered labels are pairs $(j,k)$ where $j$ is a field factor and $k$ indexes the distinct $3$-Frobenius conjugates of that generator. The order is any specified concatenation of the factor orders and then the conjugate orders. An algebra isomorphism permutes the factor blocks and cyclically shifts each conjugate list. Its determinant in the concatenated field bases equals the sign of the induced bijection of geometric labels. Indeed a block exchange of dimensions $e,f$ contributes $(-1)^{ef}$ in both computations, and Frobenius on a degree-$e$ field has determinant $(-1)^{e-1}$ by a normal basis. All frame factors in $c$ are computed in the specified field bases. Thus the prescribed sign is a calibrated permutation parity. These computations never construct a simultaneous splitting field of all factors. [Lenstra, Finding Isomorphisms Between Finite Fields](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

## Ordinary TI for a partial label assignment

Keep the raw alternating evaluation $D\times V\times V\to F$, and include multiplication on $E_D$ and its action on $D$. These records force the algebra map $\sigma$ to be the one induced by the same label map that transports the raw alternating evaluation. Since the action is faithful it cannot choose an independent algebra map. Every ordinary congruence extends to these records. A prescribed match $(j,k)\mapsto(j',k')$ is a single vector mark on the algebra role, using the designated generator conjugate supported on its field component. Its nonzero support forces the field match. Its full degree and its actual value force the Frobenius exponent. Multiple records implement a partial bijection in the same branch. The records are tensors of order at most three, with evaluation represented on the appropriate duals. A polynomial list of such records packs into a fixed signature: use named direct sums of the base roles and of their occurrences, a split label algebra rooted by distinct multiplicities in a marker module, and projection maps linking each occurrence to its base role. Store the tagged records in a fixed array for each primal and dual slot pattern. The fixed number of records then invokes the ordinary TI compiler. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

## All multiplicities with adaptive queries

The labels have cardinality $m$. Querying an empty partial assignment first tests ordinary congruence. If positive, greedily complete a label bijection for a transporter. At each step query all candidate next label images until a positive one is found, retaining the previous assignments. The resulting bijection $\pi_0$ is realized by a genuine congruence. Repeat on the self-pair $D,D$ to compute generators of its label image group: fix an initial segment of the label order, try all images of the next label, and greedily complete one permutation for each successful image. At each level these representatives are a transversal for the next point stabilizer inside the current one. The union generates the image group because the final pointwise stabilizer of the image is trivial. The number of polynomial-size queries is polynomial. The sign set is the sign of $\pi_0$ times the sign image of this group, which is generated by the signs of the returned permutations. Comparing with $c\rho^{-1}$ gives the exact test. No raw matrix transporter is computed.

## One output when labels are determined by the marks

Take each of the at most $m^b$ choices for the image of the supplied base of size $b$, where $b$ is fixed. A branch with an ordinary isomorphism has exactly one induced label permutation, since the quotient of any two matching permutations is a self-permutation fixing the base. More generally use the stated polynomial list of compatible marks with the uniqueness and coverage properties. Let $Q(M)$ denote the ordinary TI test after a branch's marks $M$.

For every ordered source pair $i<j$, form two queries using disjunctions of the partial-assignment tests. The first is the disjunction over images $u<v$ of the query extending $M$ by $i\mapsto u$, $j\mapsto v$. The second uses $u>v$. In a viable branch, the uniqueness of the label bijection means that exactly one of these two Boolean values is true, and they give the inversion indicator of that shared bijection. Maintain two positive Boolean formulas for even and odd inversion parity. Combining disjoint groups of indicators with rails $(E_1,O_1)$ and $(E_2,O_2)$ gives
$$
E=(E_1\wedge E_2)\vee(O_1\wedge O_2),\qquad
O=(E_1\wedge O_2)\vee(O_1\wedge E_2).
$$
Use a balanced tree with one leaf per pair. Even after expanding the formulas, there are polynomially many leaves, since at each of logarithmically many levels there are at most four child formulas per formula. Select the required parity rail and conjoin $Q(M)$ to it. Disjoin across the base-image branches. This preserves existence and uses no answers to construct later queries.

The positive formula has logarithmic depth. Binary AND uses the linear-size fixed-block partitioned-TI reduction for two ordered summands. Binary OR can be built at linear side length from that AND operation. Encode each tagged ordered pair $X,Y$ of tensors as a connected algebra $C(X,Y)$ by applying a triangular unital algebra construction to its AND tensor. Specifically, for a tensor $U\times V\to W$, put $U,V,W$ in strict upper positions $12,23,13$ of a three-by-three scalar triangular algebra, with the tensor as the product $12\cdot23$. The radical modulo its square has the chain $1\to2\to3$, which fixes the visible diagonal idempotents modulo the radical. Conjugating their images to the original idempotents is achieved by the unit $\sum_i f_i e_i$. The component maps then give exactly tensor isomorphism. Pad the strict upper spaces to positive dimension if needed. The center is scalar, since commuting with the diagonal idempotents forces diagonality and the chain equates its entries. Compare the two products
$$
C(X,Y)\times C(X',Y') \quad\text{and}\quad C(X,Y')\times C(X',Y).
$$
The two connected factors must match in order or cross, giving $Y\cong Y'$ or $X\cong X'$. Output their multiplication tensors. Equivalence of such unital associative products is algebra isomorphism: if $\alpha(x)\beta(y)=\gamma(xy)$, the two identity images $a,b$ are units and $(ab)^{-1}\gamma$ is an algebra isomorphism. The AND and OR constructions each multiply the maximum side length plus one by a fixed constant. So logarithmic-depth composition has polynomial dense size. This supplies one ordinary TI instance. [Grochow–Qiao IV, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.16317)

## One output for logarithmic field-degree multiplicities

There is a separate many-one reduction when every degree class contains at most $C\log_2 L$ rational field factors, where $L$ is the dense input length and $C$ is fixed. The total number of factors can grow. Group the recovered field factors by degree $e$. The degree keys are invariant and order the groups on both sides. Let $b$ be the number of factors in a group. In that component algebra, the subspace $R$ fixed by the $3$-Frobenius has dimension $b$, with basis the primitive idempotents. Its determinant is the sign of the permutation of the $b$ rational factors. For odd $e$, the contribution of geometric labels is exactly that sign: exchanging two odd-sized blocks contributes $-1$, and internal Frobenius contributes $+1$.

For even $e$, also attach the dimension-$b$ subspace $Q^-$ on which the $3$-Frobenius is minus the identity. This is the direct sum of the trace-zero lines in the factors' quadratic subfields. Use the same reference frames in every copy of the shared field model. Its determinant is the sign of the permutation of the $b$ factors times the product of their internal Frobenius signs. Because the blocks of $e$ geometric labels now have even size, their block-exchange sign is one. Multiplying the determinants of $R$ and $Q^-$ gives exactly the required geometric parity contribution.

Include the actual inclusions of $R,Q^-$ into the linked algebra role. Attach the exterior algebra and top line of each of these marked dimension-$b$ spaces, using the specified bases. One-dimensional multiplication diagrams then multiply their determinant values across all groups. All computations refer to the same raw congruence. Pin the scalar from the formula for $\chi$. The total dense size is polynomial since $2^b\le L^C$ and the number of linked records is polynomial. Uniform typed packing gives one TI instance. This condition allows unbounded total factor counts and does not require a bound on the base size or enumeration of factor matches.

The determinant cancellation, adaptive reduction, and many-one extension are written proofs. The one-output assertions require either the marked-branch hypothesis or the multiplicity bound. The all-input alternating-space problem includes pencils whose Pfaffian does not have independent split factors.
