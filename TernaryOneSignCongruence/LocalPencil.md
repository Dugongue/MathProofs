# One-sign congruence for raw alternating spaces with a truncated local ratio algebra

Fix $F=\mathbb F_3$. The inputs are two based alternating matrix spaces $D,D'$ on based vector spaces $V,V'$, with the same respective dimensions, and a prescribed sign $\epsilon$. A congruence $Y:V\to V'$ satisfies $Y^{\mathsf T}D'Y=D$. Write $\rho_Y:D'\to D$ for its pullback on forms. Since every nonzero scalar of $F$ is its own inverse, its determinant equals the determinant of the induced map in the forward convention. The character is $\chi(Y)=\det_F Y\,\det_F \rho_Y$.

**Theorem.** Suppose $D$ and $D'$ each contain a nondegenerate form $\Omega$ and their ratio algebras $A=\Omega^{-1}D$ and $A'=(\Omega')^{-1}D'$ are of the type
$$
 A\simeq E[t_1,\ldots,t_s]/(t_1,\ldots,t_s)^r
$$
for an extension field $E/F$. Allow $s=0$ or $r=1$ for a field. Write $L$ for the dense input length. If the common algebra type has
$$
 h=\binom{s+r-1}{s+1}\ \ \text{even},\qquad\text{or}\qquad s\le C\log_2 L
$$
for a fixed constant $C$, then testing for a congruence with $\chi(Y)=\epsilon$ reduces in deterministic polynomial time to one ordinary 3-TI instance over $F$. For the field case use $h=0$. The promise is recognizable from the raw matrices. In particular, the result applies to every $E[t]/t^r$ with no restrictions on $r$ or $[E:F]$. It also applies to arbitrary even $s$ at $r=3$, when $h=s+2$.

The determinant formulas use trace restriction, splitting of finite separable field factors, and the linked semilinear norm-line construction. They apply even when the Pfaffian of the input pencil has repeated factors of growing multiplicity. The present reduction and its complexity are written mathematics.

## 1. Recognize the ratio algebra from raw data

For any invertible $\Omega\in D$, the subspace $A=\Omega^{-1}D$ contains the identity. Its matrices are self-adjoint for $\Omega$, because $\Omega a$ is alternating for all $a\in A$. If $A$ is closed under products, then the product of any two of its matrices is self-adjoint; taking adjoints reverses their order, so $A$ is commutative. In that case, for any other invertible $\Omega a\in D$ the matrix $a$ belongs to $A^\times$. Indeed, an invertible matrix has its inverse in its unital matrix algebra by Cayley–Hamilton. Hence $(\Omega a)^{-1}D=a^{-1}A=A$. This shows that the algebra test is independent of which invertible slice is chosen.

Assume the ratio algebra is local. The nonunits are precisely its radical $J$, a proper $F$-subspace. Thus any $F$-basis of $D$ includes an invertible matrix. Try the input basis, reject if every member is singular, and retain the first invertible $\Omega$. The failed case cannot satisfy the theorem's promise. Multiplication closure is checked on basis pairs.

In a finite commutative $F$-algebra, $x\mapsto x^{3^a}$ is $F$-linear. For $3^a\ge\dim_F A$, its kernel is the nilradical and its image is the canonical reduced algebra $\prod E_i$. Indeed the map kills the nilradical, whereas the induced Frobenius power on the finite reduced quotient is invertible. The kernel and image are therefore computed by linear algebra. The reduced algebra is a field exactly when its subspace fixed by $x\mapsto x^3$ is one-dimensional over $F$. When this happens call the field $E$, and the nilradical $J$. Compute $J^i$ iteratively by basis multiplication, to the first $r$ with $J^r=0$. If $J=0$, use the field case. Otherwise let $s=\dim_E(J/J^2)$, and choose lifts $t_i\in J$ of an $E$-basis there. Greedy $F$-linear span tests compute an $E$-basis, using the explicit multiplication action of a fixed $F$-basis of $E$.

The graded algebra is generated by these degree-one classes. In fact, replacing each factor in a product of $i$ elements of $J$ by its $E$-linear expansion in the lifts gives the same class modulo $J^{i+1}$. Check that the distinct degree-$i$ monomials in the lifts form a basis of $J^i/J^{i+1}$ for all $i<r$. Before enumerating any monomials, compare their binomial count with the computed dimension, and reject on any disagreement. The predicted count then bounds the work by $\dim_F A$ for every grade even on a rejected input. A successful test gives the displayed algebra by mapping abstract variables to the lifts, since $J^r=0$ and the graded monomials form bases. If $A$ already has the displayed type, any choice of degree-one basis acts by an invertible linear substitution in the truncated symmetric algebra, so the test succeeds independently of the lifts.

Apply the construction separately. Different dimensions or algebra types rule out congruence. Use one common model for the two copies of $E$, with a fixed $F$-basis. Explicit finite fields of the same size can be identified deterministically in polynomial time. The base field has order three, so an isomorphism of these fields automatically preserves the base. [Lenstra, Finding Isomorphisms Between Finite Fields](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

## 2. Read the character on the original congruence

For a congruence $Y$, define $u=\Omega^{-1}Y^{\mathsf T}\Omega'Y\in A^\times$ and $\sigma(a')=Y^{-1}a'Y$. The intrinsic ratio-algebra argument shows that $\sigma:A'\to A$ is a unital algebra isomorphism. Direct multiplication gives
$$
 \rho_Y(\Omega' a')=\Omega u\,\sigma(a').
$$
Thus in the ratio-algebra coordinates the label transport is $L_u\sigma$, where $L_u$ denotes multiplication by $u$. Its action on the reduced field is $\tau=\sigma|_E$, and the induced action of $\sigma$ on $J/J^2$ is $P\tau$ in the chosen common field coordinates. The action on the input quotient of $D'$ by $\Omega'J'$ is $\bar u\,\tau$. Here $\bar u\in E^\times$ is the residue of $u$.

Put $m=\dim_E V$ and $d=\dim_E A=\binom{s+r-1}{s}$. The integer $m$ is even. To see this, extend scalars to a splitting field for $E$. The $E$-idempotents are self-adjoint for $\Omega$; their images are perpendicular and each has a nondegenerate alternating restriction. Each such image has dimension $m$. In invariant language this is the same trace-restriction principle as the nondegenerate trace Gram construction in the Alpöge release. [Alpöge’s source release](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)

Multiplication by $\bar u$ scales each split alternating block by the corresponding field embedding of $\bar u$. The Pfaffian scaling on that block is its scalar to the power $m/2$. The contribution of $u/\bar u\in 1+J$ is one. Indeed the commutative group $1+J$ has odd order, so it has a square root $w\in1+J$; $w$ is self-adjoint, and $w^{\mathsf T}(\Omega\bar u)w=\Omega u$. The matrix $w$ is unipotent, so its determinant is one. Pfaffians in split adapted frames thus give the raw-coordinate identity
$$
 \det_F(Y)=\frac{\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega)}{\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega')}\,
          N_{E/F}(\bar u)^{m/2}.
$$
The adapted basis determinants cancel in this ratio. The nonzero Pfaffians of the selected forms are computable by alternating Gaussian elimination.

By the radical filtration, $\det_F L_u=N_{E/F}(\bar u)^d$. On graded degree $i$, $\sigma$ has action $\operatorname{Sym}^i(P)\tau$, with the common coordinatewise field automorphism in its $E$-basis. For $s>0$,
$$
 \det_E(\operatorname{Sym}^i(P))=(\det_E P)^{\binom{s+i-1}{s}}.
$$
For example, the product of all degree-$i$ monomial eigenvalues in an upper-triangular model gives this formula, since the total exponent $i\binom{s+i-1}{s-1}$ is spread equally among the $s$ eigenvalues. Triangularization over an algebraic closure proves the identity over $E$ as well. The exponent summed over $0\le i<r$ is $h$ by the binomial sum identity. Consequently
$$
 \det_F\sigma=N_{E/F}(\det_E P)^h(\det_F\tau)^d.
$$
The determinant of the matrix of $\rho_Y$ in the two supplied bases is the determinant of $L_u\sigma$ times $\det(\beta_D)/\det(\beta_{D'})$. Here the columns of $\beta_D$ are the chosen common $F$-basis of field coefficients times the lifted graded monomials, multiplied by $\Omega$ and expressed in the original basis of $D$. Define the nonzero, directly computable constant
$$
 \kappa=\frac{\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega)}{\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega')}
         \frac{\det(\beta_D)}{\det(\beta_{D'})}.
$$
This gives the exact sign formula
$$
 \chi(Y)
 =\kappa\,N_{E/F}(\bar u)^{m/2+d}
            N_{E/F}(\det_E P)^h
            (\det_F\tau)^d.
$$
For the field case delete the factor containing $P$.

## 3. Enforce the sign with linked tensors

Keep the original alternating matrices as an evaluation map $D\times V\times V\to F$ with $F$ pinned. Add the ratio algebra multiplication, its evaluation on $V$, its action on $D$ by $a\cdot B=Ba$, and the coefficient field inclusion. On both sides the representation of $A$ on $V$ is faithful. This forces its transport to be the conjugation $\sigma$, and the linked map on $D$ to be $\rho_Y$. The identities above used pullbacks on $D,A$, while the matching of data can use their forward inverses. All of the terminal $F$-line determinants and norm values are signs, so they are unchanged by this reversal. Include the actual radical inclusions and quotient maps $D/\Omega J$ and $J/J^2$. Every original transporter lifts to these components by its induced actions. They impose no extra choice of a matched invertible slice.

For an $E$-line with map $z\mapsto a\tau(z)$, use copies related by $F$-linear $3^i$-powers for $0\le i<[E:F]$. Each copy necessarily acts as $z\mapsto a^{3^i}\tau(z)$. Link the copies by $E$-multiplication maps. The final line over $E$ carries $N_{E/F}(a)\tau$, and restricting to $F\cdot1$ reads the norm. Apply this first to the quotient $D/\Omega J$ to read $N_{E/F}(\bar u)$. When $h$ is even, omit the second norm. Otherwise assemble the full exterior algebra of the $E$-space $J/J^2$ with its actual wedge and field actions. Its top line reads $(\det_E P)\tau$, so the same norm rule applies. This exterior algebra has $F$-dimension $[E:F]2^s$, which is polynomial in the stated range.

On the fixed field model, $\det_F\tau=1$ when its degree is odd. For even degree include in the field sort the trace-zero line of its unique quadratic subfield. Its $3$-Frobenius acts by $-1$ on the line, and on $E$ it cycles a normal basis with determinant $-1$. Hence its $k$-th power acts on the line by exactly $\det_F(\tau)$. This is the same restricted trace construction used in the earlier trace and norm construction in the Alpöge tensor reductions.

Combine the indicated powers by bilinear multiplication between $F$-lines, and calibrate the last marked vector by $\epsilon/\kappa$. A tensor-system isomorphism enforces precisely the displayed sign. A raw congruence with this sign extends to the marks, and every matching of all components projects to a raw congruence with the sign. Each datum is a tensor of order at most three, using dual slots for multilinear evaluation and linear or bilinear maps. Variable-size typed systems with distinguished sorts can first be packed into a constant signature and then transformed to one pair of ordinary three-tensors. This leaves polynomial dense size because the norm diagrams, the multiplication tables, and all allowed exterior spaces have polynomial size. ## Graded Gorenstein ratio algebras

The same raw reduction applies to a broader class of commutative local ratio algebras. Put $G_i=J^i/J^{i+1}$ for $0\le i\le t$, where $J^{t+1}=0$ and $G_t\ne0$. Suppose $G_t$ is an $E$-line and every multiplication pairing $G_i\times G_{t-i}\to G_t$ is perfect. This includes the field case $t=0$. In the other cases the reduction applies if $t$ is odd, if $e=[E:F]$ is even, or if $t$ is even and the middle rank $k=\dim_E G_{t/2}$ is at most $C\log_2 L$. There is no bound on the number of generators of $J$. Recognition uses the same construction of the ratio algebra, then checks the $E$-dimensions and perfectness of the displayed bilinear maps in place of the monomial check.

Choose a socle basis on each side. For each distinct pair of degrees $i,t-i$ with $i<t-i$, choose bases dual to each other relative to the socle basis. A change of basis in either form-space frame is already included in the determinants of $\beta_D,\beta_{D'}$. If $\sigma$ acts on the socle by $\lambda\tau$ and on the paired grades by $P_i\tau,P_{t-i}\tau$, preservation of multiplication now gives
$$
 P_i^{\mathsf T} P_{t-i}=\lambda I.
$$
For $n_i=\dim_E G_i$ the product of their $F$-determinants is $N_{E/F}(\lambda)^{n_i}$, since the two factors $(\det_F\tau)^{n_i}$ cancel as signs. Thus for $d=\dim_E A$ and odd $t$,
$$
 \det_F \sigma=N_{E/F}(\lambda)^{d/2}.
$$
This needs only the actual socle line. Keep the socle inclusion and induced actions on the radical quotients in the marked structure, and use the norm-line diagram on that socle.

If $t$ is even with middle rank $k$, the nonmiddle grades contribute $N_{E/F}(\lambda)^{(d-k)/2}$. The middle map $P\tau$ contributes $N_{E/F}(\det_E P)(\det_F\tau)^k$. Its sign can be read by the exterior construction on the middle grade when its rank is logarithmic. For even $e$ this exterior construction is unnecessary, including when $k$ grows. Let $B,B'$ be the two middle pairing matrices in fixed $E$-bases against the respective socle frames, with primes denoting the source of the pullback $\sigma$. Then
$$
 P^{\mathsf T} B P = \lambda \tau(B'),\qquad
 (\det_E P)^2 = \lambda^k\,\frac{\tau(\det_E B')}{\det_E B}.
$$
Put $M=3^e-1$, so that $4\mid M$. Raising the second equation to $M/4$ gives the exact norm of the unknown middle determinant, since the norm from $E^\times$ to $F^\times$ has exponent $M/2$. The sign-bearing contribution can therefore be expressed as
$$
 N_{E/F}(\det_E P)
 =\lambda^{kM/4}\frac{\tau\big((\det_E B')^{M/4}\big)}
                        {(\det_E B)^{M/4}}.
$$
This product lies in $F^\times$. To realize it, use the conjugate multiplication construction on the socle line for exponent $kM/4$, expressing the exponent in base three. At most twice the number of digits of that exponent gives the number of copies used. On the resulting $E$-line, include as a distinguished $F$-line the span of $(\det_E B')^{M/4}$ on the primed side and of $(\det_E B)^{M/4}$ on the unprimed side, with those elements as the two line frames. The induced multiplier is the displayed sign. When using the forward direction both sides exchange roles and the sign inverts, which leaves it unchanged. The determinant of the field automorphism still uses the quadratic trace-zero line. This treats every even extension degree with no middle-rank bound. Multiply all grades, residue and ambient factors as above, with the Pfaffian and form-space frame calibrations in $\kappa$.


## Related simplification for independent split Pfaffian factors

In the [independent split-Pfaffian family](SplitLabelParity.md), the canonically recovered parameter algebra is a finite étale $E=\prod E_j$ and its parameter module has rank one. In absolute coordinates choose the rational anchor used in that proof and write the Pfaffian as $\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega)\prod_j N_{E_j/F}(x_j)$. Every congruence induces a semilinear map of this rank-one parameter module, with a coupled algebra isomorphism $\sigma$ on $E$ and scaling unit $u$. Its label determinant is a known frame ratio times $\det_F\sigma\prod_j N_{E_j/F}(u_j)$. Pfaffian congruence identifies its ambient determinant with a known scalar times the same norm product. The norm product therefore cancels from the one-sign character over $F$. The remaining test, after frame calibration, is the determinant of the induced map on the finite étale algebra. Over a splitting field its roots are explicit geometric labels after extension and its determinant is the sign of their permutation, with the known calibration if independent source and target bases were used.

In particular the rational primitive idempotents, or any fixed tuple of paired primitive idempotents, can be enforced by marking the corresponding elements of the recovered algebra sort. This produces ordinary TI queries asking whether an actual raw congruence realizes the stated partial label matching. For general repeated degrees this permits polynomial-time adaptive queries to recover an attainable rational-label bijection and generators of its automorphism permutation group by a stabilizer chain. If the degree factors still have internal Frobenius freedom, fully marking the rational permutation permits the established ordered norm and trace-zero line diagrams to read the sign on an actual common congruence. For each permutation generator, ask which signs are attainable, and query the identity permutation for the kernel sign. Generator lifts together with that kernel determine the whole sign image. This gives a polynomial-time Turing reduction on the independent split-Pfaffian class with no multiplicity bound. The many-one theorem above for truncated local ratio algebras uses no such adaptive queries.
