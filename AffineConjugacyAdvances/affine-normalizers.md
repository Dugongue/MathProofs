# Full affine normalizers, conjugator cosets and exact counts

**Theorem.** Under the hypotheses of the [affine block search theorem](affine-blocks.md), uncoloured conjugacy decision allows deterministic polynomial-time computation of the full permutation normalizer by generators and the full conjugator coset of an input pair. It also gives the exact number of conjugators and an exact uniform sampler with expected polynomial runtime. In particular this gives normalizer generation and exact counting for all of the abelian-complement cases of the original search theorem. The part with the point orbits and quotient action fixed is solved without an oracle.

**Reach the subgroup with only inner quotient maps.** First use orbit marks to construct generators modulo the subgroup fixing every original point orbit setwise. For a given prefix of fixed orbits, try every possible target orbit for the next one. The search theorem with those marks supplies a witness for every feasible choice. The witnesses meet every coset of the next stabilizer. In the case with refined simple quotient factors, perform the same stabilizer-chain procedure to fix each of their kernels after fixing the original point orbits. A single regular quotient orbit mark tests each kernel image; the search algorithm with the supplied marks completes the associated witness. This makes the paired refined maps individually invariant in the remaining subgroup.

Next fix full block-quotient maps up to inner. Use a generator sequence in each block and build the stabilizer chain of the inclusions of the successive generated subgroups, considered up to postcomposition by inner automorphisms. At any stage the normalizers satisfying all prefix conditions form a subgroup. Two induced maps give the same coset of the next subgroup precisely when their next prefix maps are equal up to one inner correction and the earlier conditions agree. Normalize the previous prefix in the current block to be pointwise exact by multiplying by a lift in \(G\). This preserves all earlier up-to-inner conditions. Then one can try every image of the next generator in that block. It suffices to test the full new map by the quotient marker, retaining witnesses from the search theorem with all established conditions. There are polynomially many candidates. This generates the normalizer modulo the subgroup \(N_*\) fixing the required orbit and quotient labels and inducing only inner automorphisms on every block.

Let \(K\) be the subgroup fixing the orbit and the quotient labels and inducing the identity on \(B\). The surjectivity onto the product of the block quotients modulo their centers gives \(N_*=G K\). Indeed any tuple of block inner automorphisms is the action of a single element of \(B\), liftable to \(G\). Removing that permutation leaves an element of \(K\). In the refined case every element of \(G\) preserves each kernel, since those kernels are normal. It remains to compute generators of \(K\).

**Describe the fully pinned kernel.** Work at a prime \(p\) in the abelian translation groups and use the diagram in the search proof with the same group as source and target and the identity quotient map. Let \(E\) be its finite endomorphism ring, computed by additive linear equations. Write \(R_p=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z\) and write \(Q=(V/A)_p\oplus R_p\) for the quotient vertex. An endomorphism has local maps \(U_i\), a terminal scalar \(u\), and a map on \(Q\) of the form
\[
(x,s)\longmapsto(\bar Ux+s\bar t,us).
\]
The coordinate arrows jointly span \((V/A)_p\), so \(\bar U\) is determined by the local maps. Forgetting the shear \(\bar t\) is a ring homomorphism to the product of the local endomorphism rings and \(R_p\). Its kernel squares to zero. Reducing the local maps on the tops \(V_{i,p}/pV_{i,p}\) and the terminal scalar modulo \(p\) gives a larger kernel that is still nilpotent. A product of \(e\) maps in this kernel sends each local module into \(p^e\) times that module and kills the terminal scalar. Such a product can only be a pure shear. The product of two such shears vanishes. Thus the radical and the semisimple quotient of \(E\) are computed by passing to its actual image \(\bar E\) on the tops and discarding the radical there.

**Small residue fields for the division-ring calculation.** The image algebra \(\bar E\) acts faithfully on the direct sum of the local tops and the terminal top. Its simple matrix factors have the form \(M_{d_j}(k_j)\). Each simple type occurs in some radical graded quotient of an individual top or the terminal. To see this, a central idempotent of the semisimple quotient lifts to an idempotent of \(\bar E\), since its radical is nilpotent. If the type were missing throughout all filtrations, that idempotent would carry each radical layer into its successor and would act nilpotently. An idempotent with this property acts as zero, contradicting the faithful action. Hence for some local top, or the terminal top, the corresponding column module of dimension \(d_j[k_j : \mathbb F_p]\) occurs. In particular
\[
|k_j|\le \max_i |V_{i,p}|\ \ \text{or } |k_j|=p.
\]
Every prime used in the diagram is at most \(n\), and each local group \(V_i\) has order equal to its point-orbit size. Thus these fields have polynomial cardinality even if the quotient vertex \(Q\) is large.

Finite-algebra structure algorithms compute the radical of \(\bar E\) and explicit matrix coordinates for its semisimple quotient in time polynomial in the presentation and in \(p\). This is polynomial here since \(p\le n\). The small field bound lets us enumerate field entries and units in each \(k_j\). Generate the quotient unit groups with elementary transvections and a diagonal coordinate for all field units. Lift those generators additively to \(E\); they are units by nilpotence of the radical. Also generate \(1+\operatorname{rad}E\) by \(1+x\) for lifts of bases of the successive powers of \(\operatorname{rad}E\). Those quotients are elementary abelian in characteristic \(p\) because \(p1\in\operatorname{rad}E\). Their dimensions and number are polynomial, and the unit generators just listed generate all of \(E^\times\). [Rónyai, *Computing the structure of finite algebras*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(88)80017-X), [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, Section 4](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf).

**Lift the generators to point permutations.** Taking the terminal unit is a homomorphism on \(E^\times\) with a central section by scalar multiplication. Multiplying a generator by the inverse of its terminal scalar is therefore the value of a homomorphism onto the terminal-one unit subgroup. The normalized generators generate that subgroup. Over all primes the product of these groups is the image of \(K\) under \( (U,t)\mapsto(U,\bar t)\). This map is a homomorphism because the affine composition rule descends when \(U(A)=A\), and it is onto by the reconstruction equations. Its kernel consists exactly of translations by \(A\). Choose any affine representative for each terminal-one generator and append a generating set of \(A\). This yields generators of \(K\), and then of \(N_*\) and of the full normalizer.

On conjugate inputs take the conjugator of the search theorem and multiply it by the source normalizer to obtain the full coset. The normalizer order gives the exact conjugator count, and a stabilizer chain samples uniformly from it by independent uniform indices in its successive transversals. The count is zero on nonconjugate inputs. [Constructive permutation-group algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539794229417).
