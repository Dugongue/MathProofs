# Conjugators and full normalizers for polynomial-order groups

**Theorem.** Fix \(c\). For permutation groups given by generators on \(n\) points with order at most \(n^c\), uncoloured permutation-group conjugacy decision suffices by deterministic polynomial-time Turing reductions for conjugator search, full permutation normalizer generators, and exact conjugator counting. The permutation actions may be intransitive, and there is no hypothesis on abstract group structure. The reduction may query permutation groups outside the promised input class. This is a scoped answer to the search and automorphism-generation questions of Allender, Grochow, van Melkebeek, Moore and Morgan. [Original questions](https://arxiv.org/html/1710.09806#S1.SS4).

**Search.** Enumerate both multiplication tables from the input permutations. Apply the [partial quotient map marker](quotient-marker.md#partial-quotient-maps-in-an-ordinary-conjugacy-query) using the identity quotients \(G,H\). On a positive instance, pick a greedily generated chain \(S_j=\langle g_1,\ldots,g_j\rangle\) in \(G\). Maintain a known injection \(f_j:S_j\to H\) whose restriction is realized up to one inner automorphism by a permutation conjugator. For the next generator try every candidate image in \(H\). From these proposed generator images, enumerate the subgroup map by multiplication from the identity and reject inconsistent or noninjective assignments. Test each remaining map by one graph orbit for its full domain \(S_{j+1}\). Since the preceding condition is realized up to inner, composition of a witness with a group element of \(H\) makes it exact on \(S_j\). At least one trial therefore extends the condition. The resulting map \(f:G\to H\) is realized exactly by an original conjugator after such a composition.

To recover a point permutation from \(f\), treat the target domain as a \(G\)-set via \(f\). For a source orbit with chosen point \(x\), enumerate the candidate points \(y\) in a target orbit. There is an equivariant bijection sending \(x\) to \(y\) exactly when \(f(G_x)=H_y\). In that case \(gx\mapsto f(g)y\) gives the bijection. Group orbits having the same transitive \(G\)-set type can be paired arbitrarily by type, or by bipartite matching using these tests. Equality of multiplicities follows from the retained condition. All lookup, coset and matching operations use polynomial-size data.

This search also handles extra paired point-orbit or quotient-kernel marks and a partially pinned subgroup image up to inner. Composition by an element of \(H\) preserves each orbit as a set and induces an inner map on each quotient. Thus the same procedure can initialize from the subgroup already pinned. When reading the equivariant bijections, reserve marked pairs before matching all unmarked orbits. The same type argument proves completion.

**Normalizer.** Let \(N=N_{S_n}(G)\). For the same chain \(S_j\), define \(N_j\) to be the subgroup of \(N\) whose induced automorphism restricted to \(S_j\) is inner on that subgroup in the sense of agreeing there with some \(\operatorname{inn}(g)\) of \(G\). These form a subgroup chain: compositions and inverses of such restrictions again agree with one inner map on the specified domain. A left coset of \(N_{j+1}\) in \(N_j\) is determined by the restriction on \(S_{j+1}\) up to postcomposition by inner automorphisms of \(G\). Indeed two such restrictions differ by one inner exactly when the quotient in the order \(b^{-1}a\) of the corresponding normalizers lies in \(N_{j+1}\).

A representative of a given coset may be normalized to fix \(S_j\) pointwise on elements by left multiplication by an element of \(G\), which lies in all of the \(N_j\). Try all images of \(g_{j+1}\) under that normalization. As in the search algorithm, test the full new subgroup map up to inner and recover an actual normalizer for each feasible trial. Representatives may be repeated, but they meet every coset. Together with generators of the terminal subgroup they generate \(N\).

The terminal subgroup induces only inner automorphisms on \(G\), so it is \(G\,C_{S_n}(G)\). The centralizer has a direct group-set computation. On every transitive orbit enumerate all \(G\)-equivariant self-bijections by testing the image of one chosen point. For each transitive \(G\)-set type choose a reference orbit and explicit equivariant bijections to all the others. Add swaps between a reference copy and each other copy, transported through those bijections. The self-bijections and swaps generate the entire equivariant automorphism group of the union, hence the whole centralizer. Use these and the original generators of \(G\) as terminal generators.

Finally, for a conjugate pair the full transporter is one coset of \(N\), and its exact cardinality is the order of \(N\). A stabilizer chain computes the order and gives exact uniform sampling by choosing an independent uniform index at each level, using rejection of out-of-range bit strings if needed. For a nonconjugate pair the count is zero. The orbit, order and constructive membership operations are the usual permutation-group algorithms. [Babai–Luks–Seress](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539794229417), [Allender et al., Proposition 4](https://arxiv.org/html/1710.09806).
