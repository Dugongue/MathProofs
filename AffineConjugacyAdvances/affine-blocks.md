# Conjugator search for nonabelian affine quotient blocks

**Theorem.** Let \(G,H\le S_n\) be generator-presented permutation groups. On each nontrivial point orbit \(i\), suppose the faithful local group \(L_i\) has a specified regular abelian normal subgroup \(V_i\) whose specification is invariant under local permutation equivalence. Put \(C_i=L_i/V_i\), and assume \(|C_i|\) is polynomial in \(n\) for a fixed family. The quotient acts faithfully on \(V_i\) by conjugation. Write \(B\le\prod_i C_i\) for the joint quotient image of \(G\).

Suppose the quotient coordinates can be grouped into given blocks, with each projected image \(B_j\) of polynomial order, and the map
\[
B\longrightarrow \prod_j B_j/Z(B_j)
\tag{1}
\]
is onto. These conditions only need to be given and satisfied on one input. Then ordinary uncoloured permutation-group conjugacy decision suffices for finding a conjugator in deterministic polynomial time. The theorem permits different primes and non-elementary finite abelian translation groups. Neither the global projected group nor the original permutation group needs polynomial order. It also works for local quotients \(C_i\) that are direct products of abelian groups and nonabelian simple groups, without a supplied block decomposition.

A regular abelian normal Hall subgroup gives a permissible intrinsic choice. It is the unique normal Hall subgroup with the primes dividing the orbit size. Another instance is the regular abelian socle of a primitive affine group. The polynomial size condition on local quotients is still required. One may instead mark regular kernels by their quotient actions and solve the comparison respecting those marks. This permits noncanonical supplied choices when the intended transporter is required to preserve them. The question and the former abelian-complement theorem are the starting point. [Allender et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1710.09806#S1.SS4), [Abelian-complement search](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightEleventh/groups.md#1-conjugator-search-with-abelian-local-complements).

**Local and global coordinates.** Use orbit marking to find a complete point-orbit matching compatible with some conjugator, or report failure. A matched target inherits the source grouping by orbit. Each local group can be enumerated since \(|L_i|=|V_i||C_i|\) is polynomial. A choice of origin identifies \(L_i\) with the affine group \(V_i\rtimes C_i\) in its displayed action. The point stabilizer maps isomorphically to the quotient by regularity. A point stabilizer element centralizing the translation action fixes every point, so this action is faithful. Any equivariant bijection preserving the designated translation group is necessarily affine: after choosing the image of the origin, its remaining values are determined by the induced additive isomorphism of translation groups.

Represent the global \(B\) as a permutation group acting on the disjoint union of the regular \(C_i\)-sets. This representation is faithful and of polynomial degree. Its kernel as a quotient of the original group is the subgroup \(A\) of joint translations, contained in \(V=\prod_i V_i\). Construct \(A\) by taking the pointwise stabilizer of all extra quotient points in the original action augmented by the quotient action. Construct \(B_j\) by projection and enumeration. Condition (1) can be checked by comparing the order of the image on the union of the regular \(B_j/Z(B_j)\)-sets to the product of their orders. These operations are polynomial by constructive permutation-group algorithms. [Babai–Luks–Seress](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539794229417).

**Pinning quotient maps.** For each block use the [partial quotient marker](quotient-marker.md#partial-quotient-maps-in-an-ordinary-conjugacy-query) to pin the induced homomorphism on a growing subgroup. The existing orbit matching makes each block kernel well defined on both sides. At a step try all elements of the small target block as the image of one new generator and retain an extendible injective map, with one partial graph orbit for its entire current subgroup. Include all previous blocks and marks in each query. When existing data for the current block are feasible up to an inner correction, lift that correction through the target group to make them exact in that block. A target group element preserves every point orbit and acts by inner maps on every block, so the other feasibility conditions remain true. This gives a successful trial at each step on a positive input.

At the end there are full isomorphisms \(\alpha_j:B_j\to B'_j\) consistent with one original conjugator up to independent inner corrections. Condition (1) on the target holds by transport from the source. It says that any tuple of inner corrections can be realized by the conjugation action of a single element of \(B'\), hence by a lift in \(H\). Consequently the pinned full maps are jointly realized **exactly**. In particular they induce a well-defined common quotient isomorphism \(B\to B'\) and induce the corresponding local maps \(C_i\to C'_i\). By the affine group coordinates all their projection and lift computations use generator-presented groups of polynomial permutation degree.

**Construct the affine maps.** After identifying the quotient images by the pinned maps, use additive coordinates \(V_i,V'_i\), joint translation subgroups \(A,A'\), and corresponding generators \(b_j\) in the common quotient. For every source generator \((a_j,b_j)\) find a target lift \((a'_j,b_j)\) by constructive membership. The quotients of \(a_j,a'_j\) in \(V/A,V'/A'\) do not depend on the lift choices. Work prime by prime, using \(R_p=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z\) at each prime, where \(p^e\) kills the primary parts of \(V,V'\).

At this prime, form a diagram with the local translation modules as vertices, a vertex \(Q=(V/A)_p\oplus R_p\), and terminal vertex \(R_p\). Include coordinate arrows \(V_{i,p}\to Q\), the last-coordinate map, and the local action of \(b_j\) at each translation vertex. At \(Q\) include the maps
\[
(x,s)\longmapsto(b_jx+s\bar a_j,s).
\]
Make the target diagram using its data. The action on \(V/A\) is well defined since \(B\) preserves \(A\). Both diagrams are modules over one explicitly presented finite ring: take their direct sum, and generate the subring of its additive endomorphism ring by both sets of corresponding arrows and vertex projectors acting blockwise, along with the scalars. Closing under multiplication on additive generators creates only polynomially many strict subgroup enlargements, bounded by the logarithm of the ambient additive group order. All spaces are finite and given in additive invariant factors. Use deterministic constructive finite-ring module isomorphism. [Ciocănea-Teodorescu, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08365).

A diagram isomorphism has a unit on the terminal. Multiply all its vertex maps by the inverse scalar to fix the terminal. Its local maps \(U_i\) intertwine the local actions. The coordinate arrows determine the induced \(\bar U\) on the quotient and force \(U(A)=A'\). The map on \(Q\) is \((x,s)\mapsto(\bar Ux+s\bar t,s)\). The arrows at \(Q\) impose
\[
U a_j+(1-b_j)t-a'_j\in A',
\]
where \(t\) is any lift of \(\bar t\). These equations are exactly the conditions for \(x\mapsto U_i x+t_i\) to conjugate every source generator into the target with its fixed quotient map. Existence follows from the retained oracle condition and (1). The inclusion is equality by equality of the translation kernels under \(U\) and of the projected groups. Combine the primary maps to construct the original point permutation. This is the additive reconstruction from the earlier theorem, applied with actual nonabelian quotient actions.

**Automatic blocks for products of abelian and simple factors.** First suppose each local \(C_i\) is either abelian or nonabelian simple. The projection of \(B\) to the product of the simple coordinates is a subdirect product of nonabelian simple groups. Such a group is a product of diagonal strips. For an elementary proof, induct by adjoining one simple factor \(S\) to a projection already written as a product of simples. The image in \(S\) of the kernel of projection onto the previous factors is normal in \(S\), hence either full or trivial. In the first case \(S\) is a new independent factor. In the second the new subgroup is the graph of a surjective homomorphism from the previous product to \(S\). The images of its factors are commuting normal subgroups of \(S\), so exactly one factor maps nontrivially, and that map is an isomorphism. This proves the assertion. The diagonal classes are detected by enumerating pairwise projections, which are graphs on the diagonal class and full products between different classes. Use these classes as blocks and each abelian coordinate as its own block. Their central quotients form exactly the product of the simple strips, onto which \(B\) maps. Each block quotient has polynomial order.

If a \(C_i\) is itself a direct product of an abelian group and nonabelian simples, refine it into its center and its nonabelian simple factors. These factors are the nonabelian minimal normal subgroups, recoverable by taking normal closures of elements and retaining the inclusion-minimal nontrivial nonabelian ones. More explicitly, retain minimal nontrivial normal closures first and then select the nonabelian ones. The center and the simple factors give intrinsic coordinate quotient maps, up to permutations of isomorphic simple factors. Match those factor kernels within the corresponding local quotient by greedily adding paired marked regular quotient actions. This tests exactly that the kernel is carried to the specified target kernel, and a feasible prefix always has a completion. With all such maps paired, run the preceding diagonal-strip argument on the refined coordinates. They jointly separate \(B\) and determine every original local quotient map, so the pinning and affine reconstruction apply without change. This proves the stated automatic case.
