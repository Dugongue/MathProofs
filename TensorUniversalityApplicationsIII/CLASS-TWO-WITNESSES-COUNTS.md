# Isomorphisms and exact automorphism counts for all finite class-two groups

**Theorem.** For two finite nilpotent groups of class at most two and order $N$ given by Cayley tables, one can deterministically find an isomorphism or decide there is none, count all isomorphisms exactly, and give generators for their automorphism groups in time
$$
N^{O\left((\log N)^{19/20}(1+\log\log N)^{1/10}\right)}.
$$
This covers $2$-groups of arbitrary exponent. It transfers the tuned class-two decision bound to these three tasks. The method records witnesses in Sun's orbit computation, then augments that computation with the action on the center and its quotient to extract a full automorphism generating set. The underlying restriction and alternating tuple algorithms are Sun's. The prior Frattini-class-two theorem gives the stronger bound on its narrower class of $2$-groups. ## 1. Restricted search

Work first with a finite class-two $p$-group $G$ of order $p^n$. Write $Z=Z(G)$ and $A=G/Z$ additively when discussing maps of abelian groups. A group restriction in Sun's Definition 4.1 fixes independent central generators $z_1,\ldots,z_\zeta$, a complement $Y$ in $Z$, specified lifts $g_1,\ldots,g_\gamma$, and a subgroup $F$ with a label $\rho$ that factors through its quotient by $F\cap Z$. The images of the $g_i$ generate a direct summand complementing $K=FZ/Z$ in $A$. An isomorphism of restrictions respects those data. Assume that the input to the restricted comparison satisfies the initial format, order, and fixed power and commutator tests from §4.1 and §5.5.

Sun's Lemma 5.24 either rejects or outputs matching extended vectors $a,b$ and generators for the normalized automorphisms of the doubled alternating tuple of $a$. Index the extended vector so that its first $\psi$ entries project to a cyclic basis of $Y$, its next $\zeta_0$ entries lie in the specified central subgroup spanned by the $z_i$, and its next $\eta$ entries project to a cyclic basis of $K$. The other entries are auxiliary. Set $t=\psi+\zeta_0$ and write $d_i$ for the order of the quotient image of $a_i$. In particular $d_i=1$ for $i\le t$.

As in Sun's §5.5, take the normalized block-diagonal parts of the doubled tuple generators and adjoin the prescribed elementary central adjustments. This gives a list $\Gamma$ of operations on augmented vectors. The augmented extra coordinates are fixed identities and fixed central elements. An admissible augmented vector is one satisfying the hypotheses of Sun's Lemma 5.25, with first $\phi$ entries matching $a$. The top-power state $\theta_v$ of such a vector records the highest digits of the fixed-$z$ components of $v_i^{d_i}$. All lower digits are fixed by matching. Each operation $P\in\Gamma$ takes an admissible vector $v$ to an admissible vector by ordered products, and on states it gives the affine transition in Equation (12). Denote this transition by $\theta\mapsto\theta\cdot P$.

Perform breadth-first search on these states starting at $\theta_a$ and retain one admissible vector at each state. To reach a new state, form the ordered products on the stored vector at the previous state. Lemma 5.25 proves that the resulting vector has the new state. A match to $\theta_b$ now gives the actual basis images for an isomorphism of restrictions. Specifically map each fixed $z_i$ and $g_i$ to its prescribed counterpart, match the $Y$-components of the first $\psi$ entries, and match the $\eta$ quotient basis lifts. The lower and top power digits and the commutators give all the relations checked by Lemma 4.4. The ordered-normal-form map in its proof outputs a bijection on the table. Each step costs polynomially many table operations beyond those charged to Sun's enumeration. [Sun, Lemmas 4.4, 5.24 and 5.25](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920v1#S5.SS5)

## 2. A second action on abelian basis data

Now compare a restriction of $G$ with itself. Use an extended vector $a$ for that restriction and compute its normalized doubled-tuple group generators as in §5.5. Here no transport to a different group is required. For any admissible vector $v$ there are automorphisms $u_v$ of $A$ and $h_v$ of $Z$ defined as follows. The first fixes each $\bar g_i$ and maps $\bar a_{t+j}$ to $\bar v_{t+j}$ for $1\le j\le\eta$. The second fixes every $z_i$ and sends the $Y$-component of $a_i$ to the $Y$-component of $v_i$ for $1\le i\le\psi$. Both lists are ordered cyclic bases with the same factor orders, by the definition of representative vectors. Denote the pair by $\pi_v$.

In fact $u_v(\bar a_i)=\bar v_i$ for every auxiliary index as well. To see this, the commutator map $A\times A\to Z$ is bilinear and has zero common radical by the definition of $Z$. The matching commutator coordinates imply $[u_v x,u_v y]=h_v[x,y]$ on the quotient basis consisting of $\bar g_i$ and $\bar a_{t+j}$, hence on all of $A$. In those coordinates the distinguished central summand is kept fixed and the new $Y$ coordinates are interpreted in the new $Y$ basis. For any auxiliary $i$, matching says that $\bar v_i$ and $u_v(\bar a_i)$ have identical brackets against each vector of the transported quotient basis. Their difference is therefore zero.

Let $a\cdot P$ denote the actual vector obtained from $a$ by the operation $P$. Write $\pi_P=\pi_{a\cdot P}$. For any admissible vector $v$, applying $P$ gives
$$
\pi_{v\cdot P}=\pi_v\pi_P,
$$
with multiplication in the order of composition of abelian maps. For the quotient coordinates, take the images of the ordered products in $A$. Every old coordinate maps according to $u_v$, including the auxiliary ones by the preceding argument. The appended coordinates are central, so this gives the same result as composing $u_v$ with the action computed at $a$. For the $Y$-coordinates, the first $t$ output entries only use old central entries. For the block-diagonal tuple operations this follows from the vanishing in Sun's Claim 5.22, and for the elementary central operations it follows from their definition. The $Y$-components of the old central entries are carried by $h_v$, with zero $Y$-components on the distinguished central coordinates and the appended coordinates. This proves the rule on $Z$. Each elementary central adjustment itself has pair equal to the identity.

The affine transitions are permutations on their state orbit. Indeed, the matrix of the common linear part for each column of Equation (12) has $(i,j)$ entry $d_i\widehat P[j,i]/d_j$, reduced modulo $p$. All these entries are integers by the compatibility used in §5.5, with $d_i=1$ on dummy and appended coordinates. Before reduction this matrix is a diagonal conjugate of $\widehat P^{\mathsf T}$ over $\mathbb Q$. Its determinant is thus prime to $p$, since $P$ is invertible over $\mathbb Z/p^\tau\mathbb Z$. Translation does not affect bijectivity. Lemma 5.25 gives invariance of the finite orbit, so restricting the transitions to it gives permutations. Consequently the free group on $\Gamma$ acts on the states on the right. Sending a positive generator to $\pi_P$ defines its homomorphism to the abelian automorphism pairs. Formal inverse letters act by inverse pairs and inverse state permutations. For correctness, any such letter can also be realized by a positive power in the finite product of these two permutation actions. The algorithm need not compute that power or enumerate the joint orbit.

## 3. Which pairs give restriction automorphisms

The image of the stabilizer of $\theta_a$ in the abelian pairs is exactly the image of the automorphism group of the restriction.

For one inclusion take a word fixing $\theta_a$, replace inverse letters by positive powers as above, and apply the resulting word to $a$. The final vector is admissible and has equal lower and top power digits. Lemma 4.4 applies to its projected central bases and the quotient basis lifts. The resulting restriction automorphism has exactly the associated pair.

For the other inclusion take a restriction automorphism $f$ and put $c=f(a)$. It is admissible, and $\theta_c=\theta_a$ since $f$ fixes the specified $z_i$, preserves $Y$, and transports all powers. The proof of Sun's Lemma 5.26 produces a normalized block-diagonal solution $Q$ by Lemma 5.12. The ordered products from $Q_1$ give a vector $s$ with the same first $t$ central entries as $c$ and the same quotient images as $c$ in all remaining entries. The latter entries differ from $c$ only by central factors of order dividing $p$, which also disappear after raising to their respective $d_i$.

To relate that solution to the generators used by the algorithm, express its doubling as a word in the normalized tuple generators. In the proof of Lemma 5.26, Equation (17) compares the product of the block-diagonal parts of the word with the block-diagonal part of its product. Their difference has columns in $p$ times the common kernel of the doubled tuple. Equations (15)–(16) there say that on the first-copy extended coordinates those columns are zero on every noncentral index and have exponents annihilating each central entry. Thus the products using projected generators have the same quotient images and the same first $t$ central entries as the vector $s$ from the block-diagonal solution. Reordering successive ordered products causes central commutators only in the noncentral entries. These disappear on passage to the quotient, and the full power agreement is the additional computation in Lemma 5.26. Therefore the word reaches $\theta_c=\theta_a$, with pair exactly the pair of $f$. This establishes the reverse inclusion and explains why a state alone would be insufficient for counting. [Sun, Lemmas 5.12 and 5.26, especially Equations (13)–(17)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920v1#S5.SS5)

## 4. From the state stabilizer to the full automorphism group

Store the abelian pair of each breadth-first path $w_s$ to a reached state $s$. For every edge $s\xrightarrow{P}s'$, form the pair of $w_s P w_{s'}^{-1}$ by multiplying the two stored pairs and the generator pair. These loops generate the state stabilizer in the free group. For a word that is a based loop, insert a representative and its inverse at every intermediate state. Adjacent terms cancel and each resulting factor is an edge loop or its inverse. Thus their pairs generate the image identified in Section 3. A positive path to every state and the forward-edge loops suffice, because each state transition is invertible.

Each pair can be lifted to an actual restriction automorphism in polynomial time in the input table. Fix the prescribed $g_i$ and $z_i$. Use the central map on the $Y$-basis. For each quotient basis vector in $K$, scan $F$ for a lift of its proposed image whose $d_i$-th power is the required image of the corresponding original power. Section 3 gives existence, and changing one such lift by a central factor changes no commutator. The resulting normal-form map is a restriction automorphism by Lemma 4.4. The kernel of the map from restriction automorphisms to abelian pairs is also explicit. An automorphism trivial on center and quotient has the form $x\mapsto x\chi(\bar x)$ for a homomorphism $\chi:A\to Z$. The restriction fixes the declared $g_i$, so $\chi$ vanishes on their quotient summand. It preserves $F$ exactly when $\chi(K)\subset F\cap Z$. Such shifts preserve $\rho$, since $\rho$ factors through the quotient. They form $\operatorname{Hom}(K,F\cap Z)$. To generate it, for each cyclic summand of $K$ of order $d$, independently shift its generator by generators of the subgroup of $F\cap Z$ killed by $d$. These generators can be found by scanning the table and greedily enlarging a generated subgroup. Lifts of the image generators together with these shifts generate the restriction automorphism group.

For the full group use all branches of Sun's reduction on $(G,G)$. The branches cover **every** automorphism. Sun's Theorem 2.3(2) covers each commutator-tensor transporter. The proof of Lemma 4.13 starts with an arbitrary group isomorphism and produces the corresponding transporter. This justifies applying the label compatibility proof of Lemma 4.14 to that isomorphism. In the restriction enumeration of Lemmas 4.15–4.16, transport a source restriction by this particular group isomorphism. It appears among the target restrictions since its defining conditions and labels are transported, and the associated branch admits that isomorphism. For each positive branch take one restriction isomorphism found in Section 1 and all generators of the automorphisms of its source restriction. The isomorphisms in that branch are a coset of the source restriction automorphisms. The union of the resulting lists therefore generates $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ and every listed permutation is an actual automorphism. An isomorphism from $G$ to another input $H$ is found by the same search on their branches. If there is one, the number of such isomorphisms is $|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|$. The order of a group given by generators as permutations of $G$ is computed by the polynomial-time stabilizer-chain algorithm. [Sun, Theorem 2.3(2) and Lemmas 4.13–4.16](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920v1#S4.SS3) · [Furst–Hopcroft–Luks](https://hdl.handle.net/1813/6282)

## 5. Complexity and scope

The extra work per restriction is polynomial in $N$, the number of normalized tuple generators, and the number of states in Sun's power orbit. There is no enumeration of the abelian pairs over a state. The algorithm keeps one pair per breadth-first representative and computes one loop per edge. The homogeneous tuple generator computation uses the same bound as in the decision algorithm, even when a second computation on the source restriction is needed after testing a branch. The number of states is at most $p^{\phi\zeta}=p^{O(n\zeta)}$. Hence each run of the restricted comparison and the enumeration of branches have the same exponent bounds as in the tuned decision algorithm. The final permutation-group computation is polynomial in the input length and in the number of listed generators, which also leaves the asymptotic bound unchanged. A list of generators may be shortened by membership tests.

Use the parameter choice and matrix tuple estimates from the [tuned decision proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowProofTransfersIII/CLASS-TWO-GROUP-ISOMORPHISM.md). In its small-prime range, take $\delta$ maximal subject to $\delta^{20}(1+\lfloor\log_2(n+1)\rfloor)^2\le n$ and $\tau=\max(\delta^2,\delta+2)$. The orbit cost $p^{O(n\zeta)}$, branch count, and matrix output cost all have the bound established there. In the large-prime range $\log_2 p>n^{1/10}$, enumerate the at most $N^n$ possible images of a generating list of length at most $n$. Test candidates against the table and count or list the successful ones. This takes $N^{O((\log N)^{10/11})}$. Bounded $n$ can likewise use direct enumeration. A finite nilpotent group is the direct product of its characteristic Sylow subgroups. For Cayley tables these can be extracted by element orders in polynomial time. Find witnesses and generators on each factor, extend the latter by identity on the other factors, and multiply the counts. This proves the displayed theorem for all finite class-two inputs.

This is an algorithmic extension of Sun's constructive ingredients with the same tuned runtime. Its input is an explicit Cayley table, and its runtime analysis uses the internal witnesses of the stated restriction and tuple algorithms. The full correctness proof uses those interfaces and the classical permutation-group order algorithm. The companion audit covers four algebraic identities for loop labels and central shifts. The finite replay compares projected stabilizer images with exhaustive product actions on small instances, checks 1,620 weighted affine transitions, and checks 335,521 central-shift multiplication cases in actual class-two groups.
