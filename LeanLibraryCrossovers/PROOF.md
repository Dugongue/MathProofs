# Four crossovers from the Lean library and MathProofs

These are informal proofs. We use published problem statements, with priority and scope described separately for each result.

## 1. A two-sided counterexample to Lovett–Zhang’s conjecture

Conjecture 37 of Lovett and Zhang’s ITCS 2023 paper asks for a bound polynomial in the maximum coordinate exit influence on the minimum \(\ell_\infty\) distance from one Boolean set to the convex hull of another. Here an exit influence is the probability, under the uniform distribution inside the set, that flipping the given coordinate leaves the set. We give pairs of sets of constant density for which **every** point in either set has distance exactly \(1/w\) to the other convex hull, although every exit influence is at most \(4\cdot 2^{-w}\). [Lovett–Zhang, Section 5](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol251-itcs2023/LIPIcs.ITCS.2023.84/LIPIcs.ITCS.2023.84.pdf)

Take Tribes with \(b=2^w\) disjoint blocks of width \(w\ge1\). Write \(T(u)=1\) if some block is all ones, put \(p=2^{-w}\), and let \(a=(1-p)^b\). On two independent copies of these coordinates take
\[
 X=\{(u,v):T(u)=1,\ T(v)=0\},\qquad
 Y=\{(u,v):T(u)=0,\ T(v)=1\}.
\]
Both densities are \(a(1-a)\), bounded below by \(e^{-2}(1-e^{-1})\). For any fixed bit the unconditioned mass of a flip that changes \(T\) from 1 to 0 is \(p(1-p)^{b-1}\). The bit must be 1, the rest of its block must be 1, and every other block must fail. The reverse flip has the same mass by the flip bijection. Conditioning on the starting value of \(T\) gives respective exit influences \(p(1-p)^{b-1}/(1-a)\) and \(p/(1-p)\). The other copy is independent, so these are also the influences for \(X,Y\) and they are at most \(4p\).

Fix \(x=(u,v)\in X\), and take an all-ones block of \(u\). Every \(y\in Y\) changes at least one bit in this block. For every \(z\in\operatorname{conv}Y\) the sum of the coordinate discrepancies \(1-z_i\) across the block is at least 1. This gives distance at least \(1/w\). To attain it, independently choose one uniform bit to turn off in each all-ones block of \(u\), and choose one uniform block of \(v\) to fill with ones. Every resulting point belongs to \(Y\). Its expectation has discrepancies at most \(1/w\) on \(u\) and \(1/b\le 1/w\) on \(v\). Swapping the copies proves the reverse statement. For fixed \(C,\alpha>0\), eventually \(1/w>C(4p)^\alpha\). This contradicts the claimed polynomial dependence.

For the connection to fractional packing, fix a Boolean point \(x\notin Y\) and put \(D_y=\{i:x_i\ne y_i\}\). The distance is the minimum, over distributions on \(Y\), of the maximum coordinate load \(\max_i\Pr(i\in D_y)\). This follows since, at a Boolean \(x\), discrepancies in a coordinate all have the same sign. Scaling any distribution by the reciprocal of its maximum load gives a fractional packing of the \(D_y\) with capacity 1 per coordinate. Conversely, normalizing a fractional packing gives a distribution, so the optimum packing value is the reciprocal of the distance. A single complete block here supplies the corresponding fractional cover certificate. This uses the fractional packing viewpoint in MathProofs and the convex-hull formulation revisited by Physical Superintelligence. The Tribes construction is classical. A targeted search located the conjecture and the later all-pairs substitute, with priority for this specific counterexample unestablished. [MathProofs fractional packing](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/5072239cac626156b25b8e37ab6dff94f907184d/FractionalBicliqueSeparation), [PSI’s proof and discussion](https://github.com/physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis/tree/2a6ceaef872ae993708950f951989134084b851a), [O’Donnell, Analysis of Boolean Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10386)

## 2. General d-position on block graphs in polynomial time

Klavžar, Rall and Yero ask for the complexity of general \(d\)-position on block graphs in Section 6.1. Given a block graph and \(d\), one can compute its general \(d\)-position number, an attaining set, and the entire cardinality polynomial in polynomial time. A set is valid if no selected vertex lies strictly between two other selected vertices on a shortest path of length at most \(d\). [Published paper](https://rodin.uca.es/bitstream/handle/10498/26132/2021_901.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)

For each connected component, replace every clique block by a fresh hub and connect it to each original vertex in the block. Keep isolated vertices. The resulting incidence graph is a tree in each component. This follows from the block-cut tree theorem, with noncut vertices attached as leaves. For original vertices \(u,v\), every graph edge can be expanded into two incidence edges, and a tree path between originals can be compressed into one graph edge per traversed hub. Hence the tree distance is exactly twice the original distance. Distance additivity characterizes geodesic betweenness, so it suffices to solve the tree problem with threshold \(D=2d\), allowing selections only at original vertices. Cap \(D\) at the tree diameter. Work componentwise and multiply the cardinality polynomials.

Here is the two-distance tree recurrence, with forbidden hub selection. Root the tree and express depths relative to the current root. In a collection of already merged child subtrees record \(a\), the least depth of a selected vertex, and \(b\), the least depth of a selected vertex with a distinct selected ancestor in the collection. A missing minimum is infinity. At a child-to-parent transition increment each finite depth by one. When joining disjoint child collections with signatures \((a_1,b_1)\) and \((a_2,b_2)\), reject exactly when \(b_1+a_2\le D\) or \(b_2+a_1\le D\). Indeed any violating path between the collections goes through the current root and any selected interior vertex must belong to an endpoint’s collection. Its endpoint there must therefore be a selected vertex with a selected ancestor. The depths for the new collection are coordinatewise minima.

If the root is selected, also require \(b>D\) for each child collection and \(a_1+a_2>D\) at each merge. The first condition excludes a path with selected root endpoint and a selected interior ancestor below it. The second excludes a path with the root as selected interior vertex. These are all new paths containing the selected root. Set the final signature to \((0,a)\) in this branch, where \(a\) is the minimum selected descendant depth before adding the root. The root itself is never the lower vertex of an ancestor pair. For an unselected root use the merged signature unchanged. The empty collection has signature \((\infty,\infty)\). Induction on the tree and on the number of merged children proves the recurrence, including both directions of every rejection test.

Saturate all depths larger than \(D\) to a single symbol. Each table has \(O((D+2)^2)\) entries and each pairwise merge uses \(O((D+2)^4)\) table operations. Store either an optimum cardinality and a backpointer or a polynomial counting each cardinality for a given signature. Add counts for disjoint alternatives and multiply counts across disjoint subtrees, using a factor of \(t\) when the root is selected. Counts have at most \(n+1\) bits for a graph on \(n\) vertices. Clique-block incidence construction is linear in input graph size and all tables and arithmetic are polynomial even when \(d\) is binary encoded.

Pyle’s tree recurrence supplies the two distances and his preprint explicitly leaves the block-graph question open. The new step is doubling the metric through block incidence and restricting eligible vertices. The count version combines the rooted restriction-and-union method in the connected induced subgraphs formalization with the general-position cardinality polynomial in MathProofs. A targeted search located no earlier all-\(d\) block-graph algorithm. [Pyle, Section 7](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00448v1), [MathProofs cardinality-polynomial proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/5072239cac626156b25b8e37ab6dff94f907184d/GeneralPositionPolynomialBounds/PROOF.md)

## 3. Cubic form equivalence reduces to algebra isomorphism over every field

Agrawal and Saxena ask in Section 6 for this reduction over arbitrary fields. Grochow and Qiao give it in characteristic zero and characteristic greater than three. The following reduction also works in characteristics two and three, including imperfect fields. The target is isomorphism as \(K\)-algebras of commutative associative unital algebras. The source uses formal polynomials with expanded coefficients and invertible \(K\)-linear substitutions. [Agrawal–Saxena, Section 6](https://www.cse.iitk.ac.in/%75sers/nitin/papers/cubic-forms.pdf), [Grochow–Qiao, Remark 1.5](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full)

Let \(V\) be the space of linear forms. Use four labeled spaces \(V,S_2=\operatorname{Sym}^2 V,S_3=\operatorname{Sym}^3 V,L=K\). Construct the following four bilinear maps:
\[
 V\times V\to S_2,\quad V\times S_2\to S_3,\quad
 L\times L\to L,\quad L\times L\to S_3 .
\]
The first three are multiplication. The last sends \((a,b)\) to \(abf\), for the input cubic \(f\in S_3\). An isomorphism of systems acts by one linear map per labeled space, shared across its occurrences. The invertible map on \(L\) is multiplication by a nonzero scalar \(c\). Preserving multiplication gives \(c=c^2\), so \(c=1\). The products spanning \(S_2\) and \(S_3\) force the two induced maps to be \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 A,\operatorname{Sym}^3 A\), where \(A\) is the map on \(V\). Preserving the last map now says exactly \(\operatorname{Sym}^3 A(f)=g\). Conversely such an \(A\) extends to a system isomorphism by these induced maps and the identity on \(L\). This argument uses the symmetric algebra and keeps every monomial in characteristics two and three.

Each structure map is a mixed tensor of order three, with its two inputs dualized. Use the all-field system-to-3TI construction of Futorny, Grochow and Sergeichuk, Theorem 1.1, or the corresponding ordinary tensor compiler in MathProofs. The fixed system has spaces of dimension \(O((n+1)^3)\), uses monomial bases and contains only copied coefficients and \(0,1\). The compiler has polynomial output size for this fixed system. Next use Grochow–Qiao Proposition 5.1 and Corollary 5.2 to reduce 3TI to symmetric matrix-space isometry, and Corollary 6.5(1) to reduce that to commutative unital associative \(K\)-algebra isomorphism. These constructions apply over arbitrary fields. The latter interprets independent symmetric matrices as a commutative product with image equal to its annihilator, and adjoins an identity. The symmetric construction uses no division by 2. Thus composition gives a deterministic polynomial-size many-one reduction over every field, in the field-operation model and with the usual polynomial bit encodings over finite fields and the rationals. Their Corollary 6.6 gives the reverse direction via the known cubic-form construction. The new step here is the characteristic-independent embedding of the form as a distinguished element of the symmetric algebra via multiplication tensors. The same construction, with the successive multiplication maps up to degree \(d\), puts every fixed-degree form-equivalence problem in TI. The recent Stacks grading and Physlib degree-one generation declarations supply the structural counterparts in the local Lean catalog. [Stacks symmetric grading](https://github.com/AxiomMath/Stacks/blob/5ed82d14634e8d9576bb844c7bd4e3da66d450e7/StacksFrontier/Algebra/Category/ModuleCat/Sheaf/SymmetricAlgebraGrading.lean), [Physlib degree-one generation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/physlib/blob/4e2182f2b6f0a54207a2fa62002c264217f875d6/PhyslibAlpha/Mathematics/SymmetricAlgebra.lean). [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219), [MathProofs mixed-system reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/5072239cac626156b25b8e37ab6dff94f907184d/LinearStructureUniversality/PROOF.md), [Grochow–Qiao, Sections 5 and 6](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full)

## 4. Cody–Moore’s k-general d-position problem is NP-complete

Conjecture 7.6 asks for NP-completeness when the graph \(G\), \(k\ge2\), \(d\ge1\), and a positive integer threshold \(r\) are supplied. The question is whether \(G\) has more than \(r\) selected vertices with no \(k\) of them on a common geodesic of length at most \(d\). [Cody–Moore, Section 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05644v1#S7)

For membership in NP, guess the selected set \(S\) and check its cardinality. For every source \(s\), compute BFS distances in its component. Keep directed edges \(u\to v\) for graph edges with \(\operatorname{dist}(s,v)=\operatorname{dist}(s,u)+1\). The resulting DAG has as its directed paths from \(s\) precisely the geodesics starting at \(s\). Give each vertex weight \(\mathbf 1_{v\in S}\). In increasing BFS layer order, compute the maximum vertex-weight sum of a directed path from \(s\) to each \(v\), including both endpoints in the sum. Reject if any \(v\) at distance at most \(d\) has value at least \(k\). Any violating geodesic is covered when its first vertex is the source. Conversely a rejecting DP entry comes from an actual geodesic witnessing the violation. The check takes \(O(|V|(|V|+|E|))\) graph operations, plus reading the thresholds. NP-hardness follows from the general \(d\)-position problem of Klavžar, Rall and Yero at fixed \(d=2\), by taking \(k=3\). For their weak threshold \(\ge t\), use \(r=t-1\) when \(t\ge2\). The trivial smaller thresholds can be decided directly and mapped to fixed yes or no instances with \(r\ge1\). There is also a direct Clique reduction. For an \(n\)-vertex graph \(H\) with \(n\ge1\), join \(H\) with a new clique \(K_n\). A valid set that includes a new vertex can select only a clique from \(H\), because a nonadjacent pair in \(H\) makes a length-two geodesic through that vertex. Without a new vertex the set has size at most \(n\). Taking all new vertices and a maximum clique of \(H\) attains \(n+\omega(H)\). Thus the general \(2\)-position optimum is exactly \(n+\omega(H)\). [Klavžar–Rall–Yero, Theorem 2.2](https://rodin.uca.es/bitstream/handle/10498/26132/2021_901.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)

## Additional result. Exact scope and a repair for degree padding in form equivalence

Grochow and Qiao ask whether padding a homogeneous form \(f\) of degree \(r\) by a new variable power \(z^k\) gives a reduction to degree \(r+k\) form equivalence. For every field \(K\), \(r\ge1\), and \(k\ge1\), the exact criterion is
\[
 z^k f\sim_{\mathrm{GL}} z^k g
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 f\sim_{\mathrm{GL}}\lambda^k g
 \text{ for some }\lambda\in K^\times.
\]
In particular the padding is universally valid exactly when \((K^\times)^k\subseteq (K^\times)^r\). Over \(\mathbb Q\), \(r=3,k=1,f=x^3,g=2x^3\) refutes the proposed map. [Grochow–Qiao, Appendix A after Proposition A.1](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full#A1)

We use the essential variable space \(E(H)\) of a polynomial \(H\), the least linear subspace of the linear forms whose symmetric algebra contains \(H\). It exists because the intersection of the symmetric algebras of two subspaces is the symmetric algebra of their intersection, as seen by extending a basis of the intersection to adapted bases. An invertible linear substitution carries essential spaces to essential spaces. Equivalence of polynomials living in equal-dimensional variable spaces can consequently be checked by an isomorphism of their essential spaces, extended to the ambient spaces. Also, if a nonzero product lies in the symmetric algebra of a subspace then each factor lies there. View the factors as polynomials in the complementary variables and use additivity of degree in those variables.

It suffices to consider \(f,g\ne0\). A substitution on the padded spaces gives \(z^k f=b^k g'\), where \(g'\) lies in the symmetric algebra of a complement to the span of \(b\), and is an invertible substitution of \(g\) into that complement. If \(b=\lambda z\), division gives \(f=\lambda^k g'\) and descent to essential spaces proves the claim. Otherwise \(z,b\) are independent linear forms. Unique factorization gives \(f=b^k h\) and \(g'=z^k h\). The factor property gives \(E(h)\subseteq E(f)\cap E(g')\) and \(b\in E(f)\), \(z\in E(g')\). Since \(z\notin E(f)\) and \(b\notin E(g')\), \(b,z\) are independent modulo \(E(h)\). Swap \(b,z\) while fixing \(E(h)\) to identify \(f\) and \(g'\), then descend to essential spaces. The reverse implication scales the new variable by \(\lambda\). The power inclusion suffices because an \(r\)-th power is absorbed by a scalar substitution. It is necessary by the one-variable monomial example for any \(k\)-th power that fails to be an \(r\)-th power.

A repaired reduction over characteristic zero or characteristic \(>r\) adds only three variables:
\[
 f(x)\longmapsto z^k(f(x)+u^r+v^r+z^r),\qquad r\ge2.
\]
The inner form has no linear divisor, even over an algebraic closure. Restrict \(x=0\) and use the smooth Fermat plane curve \(u^r+v^r+z^r=0\). A linear divisor of the unrestricted form would restrict to a nonzero linear divisor of that curve. A line component meets the other factor over the algebraic closure, giving a singularity, and a repeated line likewise gives singular points. Smoothness follows from \(r\ne0\) in the field.

Thus any equivalence of the padded forms sends \(z\) to \(\lambda z\) and sends \(w=(x,u,v)\) to \(Aw+bz\) with \(A\) invertible. Write \(H_f=f+u^r+v^r\), and use \(H_g\) similarly. Cancelling \(z^k\) gives
\[
 H_f(w)+z^r=\lambda^k(H_g(Aw+bz)+\lambda^r z^r).
\]
At \(z=0\) this identifies \(H_f(w)=\lambda^k H_g(Aw)\). Substitute \(w=tA^{-1}b\) into the full equation and compare \(t^{r-1}z\). It follows that \(H_g(b)=0\), so the coefficient of \(z^r\) yields \(\lambda^{k+r}=1\). Hence \(\lambda^k=\lambda^{-r}\) is absorbed by a scalar map on \(w\).

To cancel the common \(u^r+v^r\), for \(r=2\) first split off radicals and apply Witt cancellation, then restore the equal-dimensional radicals. For \(r\ge3\) polarize each form. The polarization is reversible in these characteristics. Work modulo the radical of the symmetric \(r\)-linear form, the subspace whose insertion always yields zero. On the quotient the centroid consists of endomorphisms that can be moved between slots without changing the value. It is commutative. For centroid elements \(A,B\), move them among three slots in
\[
 \Phi(ABx,y,z,\ldots)=\Phi(Bx,Ay,z,\ldots)
 =\Phi(x,Ay,Bz,\ldots)=\Phi(Ax,y,Bz,\ldots)=\Phi(BAx,y,z,\ldots).
\]
The zero radical forces \(AB=BA\). The same identities show closure under products. A centroid idempotent splits the form into the two orthogonal summands from its image and kernel because any mixed evaluation vanishes by moving the idempotent between the two slots. Conversely any orthogonal direct-sum decomposition gives centroid idempotents. In a finite-dimensional commutative algebra a decomposition of the identity into orthogonal primitive idempotents is unique up to order. The resulting multiset of indecomposable form types is therefore an invariant. Cancelling the common types and restoring the radicals proves \(f\sim_{\mathrm{GL}}g\). The forward direction of the reduction extends an equivalence on \(x\) by fixing all three added variables. At fixed \(r,k\) the map is polynomial time in the dense encoding and the output coefficients are copied or constant. For \(r=1\) the zero/nonzero dichotomy already decides equivalence.

The scalar obstruction resolves the proposed map over arbitrary fields. The repaired construction proves the many-one reduction in the stated characteristics. The direct-sum cancellation argument uses classical center theory, which also appears in MathProofs’ determinant assembly. [Harrison-center treatment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12014), [MathProofs determinant assembly](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/5072239cac626156b25b8e37ab6dff94f907184d/SLDeterminantAssembly/PROOF.md)

## Additional result. Tyszka’s counting question on smooth integral quartics

Tyszka asked whether an algorithm can give an upper bound for the number of nonnegative integral solutions of an integer polynomial equation whenever the set of solutions is finite. His paper derives a negative answer from finite-fold Diophantine representations. The released single-fold theorem supplies that premise. We prove a stronger restriction result. **No such algorithm exists even on an effective family of smooth geometrically integral affine quartics in one fixed ambient dimension whose nonnegative integral point sets are always finite and nonempty.** Credit for the unrestricted implication goes to Tyszka and the authors of the single-fold theorem. [Tyszka’s paper](https://home.cyf-kr.edu.pl/~rttyszka/solutions_in_N.pdf), [OpenAI single-fold Lean documentation](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/lean/docs/242.md)

For a machine index \(e\) and a nonnegative integer \(k\), take the computably enumerable relation
\[
 k=0 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \text{machine }e\text{ halts at step }T\text{ and }1\le k\le T+1.
\]
Single-fold representability gives one fixed integer polynomial \(P(e,k,x)\) such that for every \((e,k)\) there is exactly one nonnegative integral witness \(x\) if the relation holds and zero otherwise. Fix \(e\). Split \(P\) into its two nonnegative-coefficient parts, and evaluate them with a fixed sequence of addition and multiplication gates, comparing the two final values. Give each gate its own variable and write its defining residual \(q_i\). These residuals have degree at most two, and the gate variables have one and only one extension for each nonnegative integral input satisfying the equation. Introduce a further variable \(v\) and include the residual \(v^2\), which forces \(v=0\) and ensures degree exactly four after squaring. Put \(G_e=\sum_i q_i^2+v^4\).

Choose effectively an integer \(a\ge2\) such that \(1/a\) is not a geometric critical value of \(G_e\). To see why this is possible, each irreducible component of the critical locus has constant \(G_e\). Its differential vanishes in the function field of the component, and characteristic zero gives constancy. There are finitely many components. Enumerate \(a\) and use the Gröbner ideal test for emptiness of the critical locus intersected with \(aG_e=1\). Now impose the quartic equation
\[
 z^2+aG_e=1 .
\]
On nonnegative integral inputs its solutions have \(z=1,G_e=0\), since \(a\ge2\) and \(G_e\) is a sum of integer squares. They therefore correspond bijectively to the admissible \(k\). The gradient can vanish only at \(z=0,G_e=1/a,\nabla G_e=0\), which is excluded. Over the algebraic closure \(1-aG_e\) is nonconstant and squarefree, since any repeated irreducible factor would give a critical point at that value. It cannot be a square in the rational function field, so \(z^2-(1-aG_e)\) is irreducible there and in the polynomial ring. This gives geometric integrality.

The ambient dimension is fixed because the single-fold representation and its gate list are fixed. If the machine never halts there is one point, from \(k=0\). If it halts at step \(T\) there are \(T+2\) points. Any algorithm returning an upper bound \(B\) on the count for every input in this family would decide halting by simulating \(B\) steps. Translation by 1 gives the same result for positive integral points. The proof combines the single-fold computation interface with the gate-equation and smoothness methods in MathProofs. [MathProofs rational-point compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/5072239cac626156b25b8e37ab6dff94f907184d/RationalPointDecision/PROOF.md)

## Appendix. An archival stable interpolation conjecture

Conjecture 5.5 in Lovett–Zhang’s 2022 ECCC version proposes that a bounded degree-\(d\) Boolean-cube polynomial uniformly small on a Hamming ball of dimension-independent radius has small mean square on the entire cube. The final ITCS version omits this conjecture. For the related claim about finding a uniformly short sensitive block from every input, one counterexample serves both purposes. Put
\[
 f(x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_N)=\frac{x_0}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N x_i .
\]
It has degree two, takes values in \([0,1]\), and is at most \(r/N\) on the radius-\(r\) ball around the origin, where \(f(0)=0\). Yet the independent fair bits give \(\mathbb E f^2=(N+1)/(8N)\) and \(\operatorname{Var} f=(N+2)/(16N)\). Fix \(\epsilon=1/32\) and choose \(N>r/\delta\) for any proposed finite \(r\) and positive \(\delta\). This refutes both the written mean-square inequality and a dimension-independent short-block guarantee from every input. For the mean-square inequality alone the degree-one average already suffices. [ECCC preprint, Section 5.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/107/download)
