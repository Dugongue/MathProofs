# Graph isomorphisms as determinant-one tensor isomorphisms

**Theorem.** Over every field, there is a polynomial construction of a concise ordered three-tensor from a finite simple undirected graph such that each graph isomorphism lifts to exactly one tensor isomorphism of determinant one in each mode. Every such tensor isomorphism is obtained this way, and the correspondence preserves composition. Over fields of characteristic different from two, a second construction gives the same bijection for graph isomorphisms of either prescribed vertex permutation sign. The sign uses the displayed vertex ordering in each input. The arrays have entries in $\{0,1,-1\}$. All group statements concern matrices over the given field.

For the first construction, a tensor given in arbitrary bases over a fixed finite field also has a polynomial reverse test on its image. Two such inputs are special-linearly equivalent exactly when their decoded graphs are isomorphic and one computable field element agrees. Arbitrary changes of basis can change that element.

## The graphs used in the construction

Use $h=6$ vertex layers for ordinary GI and $h=5$ for prescribed sign. Given a graph $G$ on $n$ vertices, take the least power of two $r\ge 14n+64$ and put $M=r-hn$. Make $h$ disjoint copies $V_0,\dots,V_{h-1}$ of its vertex set. For each $i>0$ put the identity matching between $V_0$ and $V_i$. In the six-layer case add the identity matching between $V_1$ and $V_5$. Thus there are six or four matching families, respectively. Put a copy of the ordered adjacency relation between $V_1$ and $V_2$ and a second copy between $V_3$ and $V_4$. Each copy contains the edge from $x$ in its first layer to $y$ in its second whenever $\{x,y\}\in E(G)$. The resulting edges are undirected.

Add $M$ anchor vertices. On them use the complement of one of the following graphs, choosing the one whose complement has odd edge count.

- A tree with three paths of lengths $1,2,M-4$ meeting at a common endpoint.
- A triangle with paths of lengths $1,2,M-6$ attached separately at its three vertices.

These graphs have $M-1$ and $M$ edges. Each is rigid because the three branches have different lengths, and their common root or the unique triangle is intrinsic. Fix an ordering of the anchors, for example from the root or triangle followed by the branch interiors in increasing branch-length order. Join every vertex in layer $i$ to the four anchors in positions $4i+1$ through $4i+4$. Call the resulting ordinary graph $J_h(G)$. For $n=0$ it consists of its anchors.

Every anchor has degree at least $M-4>r/2$. Every other vertex has degree at most $n+9<r/2$, and has at least four anchor neighbors. The anchor graph is connected because its minimum degree exceeds $M/2$. Its canonical identification between two outputs of the same size fixes the individual anchors. An isomorphism of outputs must identify the anchors by thresholding the degree at half the total vertex count, recover each layer by its four anchors and respect the matchings. All layer maps therefore come from one vertex permutation. The adjacency relation forces that permutation to be a graph isomorphism. Conversely, any graph isomorphism extends uniquely. This also proves recovery of $n$ if two different input sizes give the same $r$. The number $e$ of edges in $J_h(G)$ is odd. There is an even number of matching families, two equally sized adjacency families and $4hn$ attachment edges, in addition to the odd number of anchor edges.

## The centered tensor and its witnesses

Let $A,B$ have bases the vertices and edges of $J=J_h(G)$. Its incidence cells $z_{v,f}=a_v\otimes b_f$ for $v\in f$ span a coordinate space $U$ of dimension $2e$. Let $S$ be the kernel in $U$ of the functional summing the cell coefficients. Put $C=S^*$ and take as tensor the inclusion $S\hookrightarrow A\otimes B$, viewed in $A\otimes B\otimes C$. Its dimensions are $(r,e,c)$ with $c=2e-1$. In particular $r,e,c$ are pairwise coprime. The dense array has $O((n+1)^5)$ entries.

The following recovery argument explains why the construction controls *all* linear maps. For $x=a_v$ the space of $y$ with $x\otimes y\in S$ has dimension $\deg(v)-1\ge 2$. For $x$ on two or more supported rows, every nonzero entry of $y$ must index an edge common to those rows. Simplicity bounds that space by dimension one. Thus the row coordinate lines are intrinsic. Projecting $S$ onto a recovered row using the direct sum of the other row lines gives the full span of its incident edge vectors in the second mode. Each incident coefficient can be balanced by subtracting a cell on another row. The intersection for two distinct rows is the line of their common edge when present and zero otherwise. These descriptions prove concision in the first two modes, and the defining inclusion proves it in the third.

Consequently every tensor isomorphism induces a graph isomorphism on the recovered lines. After removing its permutation matrices, the first two maps are diagonal. Their entries $u_v,w_f$ satisfy $u_v w_f=\mathrm{constant}$ on all incidences, because the transformed sum functional has the same kernel. Connectedness gives constants $u_v=u$ and $w_f=w$. Conversely these scalars and a graph isomorphism always give a general-linear witness. If $L$ is the induced isomorphism on $S$, the third matrix is uniquely $(L^{-1})^\mathsf{T}$ in the dual bases. This is the centered incidence construction, here applied to graph families with controlled determinants.

## Bases, determinants and the exact correspondence

Order the vertices as anchors followed by layers in numerical order, each layer in the displayed input vertex order. Order the edges in these families, each family in a fixed sequence shared by both inputs. Use lexicographic order for anchor edges, original vertex order for each matching, lexicographic order of the original ordered adjacent pairs for each adjacency family, and one family per layer and attachment anchor ordered by original vertex. Orient each edge from the earlier endpoint in the displayed order of all vertices. Every graph witness preserves the orientation of every edge: moving edges join specified, distinct layers or go from an anchor to a layer, and anchor edges are fixed pointwise. Order the $2e$ incidence cells by edges, listing the initial endpoint before the terminal endpoint. With $N=2e$, take the basis $(z_1-z_N,\ldots,z_{N-1}-z_N)$ of $S$ and its dual basis of $C$. These conventions work for graph isomorphisms between different displayed labellings.

Let $s$ denote the sign of the underlying input vertex permutation. The vertex permutation on $J$ has determinant $s^h$. The edge permutation has determinant one. Its matching permutations occur an even number of times, its ordered-adjacency permutation occurs twice, and each layer's attachment permutation occurs four times. The incidence permutation acts on oriented endpoint pairs by a block permutation of pairs without internal swaps. Its determinant is one. The determinant on $S$ equals the determinant on all incidence cells: appending $z_N$ to the difference basis gives a determinant-one basis change, and a cell permutation acts as the identity on the one-dimensional quotient via the sum functional. This argument also works when the characteristic divides $2e$.

Write $D_\eta=\mathrm{diag}(\eta,1,\ldots,1)$ in the target first mode, for $\eta\in\{1,-1\}$. The determinant triple of the general-linear witness to the target twisted by $D_\eta$ is
$$
(\eta s^h u^r,\; w^e,\;(uw)^{-c}).
$$
If all three entries equal one, $w^e=(uw)^c=1$, so $u^{ec}=1$. Since $ec$ is odd, $u^r$ has odd order. In characteristic different from two this forces $\eta s^h=1$. In characteristic two this equality already holds as an equality of field elements. Then $u^r=1$, and $\gcd(r,ec)=1$ gives $u=1$. Finally $w^e=w^c=1$ gives $w=1$. Conversely $u=w=1$ gives a special-linear witness whenever $\eta s^h=1$ in the field.

With $h=6,\eta=1$ the resulting bijection includes all graph isomorphisms. With $h=5$ and odd or zero characteristic, the qualifying isomorphisms have sign $\eta$. In particular, the automorphism group in the latter untwisted construction is the even subgroup of the graph automorphism group. Over characteristic two the untwisted five-layer construction also includes every graph isomorphism. For a functorial formulation with orientations, give each graph an orientation label $\alpha\in\{\pm1\}$ and use the representative twisted by $D_\alpha$. The sign condition between labels $\alpha,\beta$ becomes $s=\beta/\alpha$. The unique lifts compose since the intermediate twists cancel. The untwisted six-layer functor preserves graph automorphism groups, counts and individual witnesses over every field.

## Recognition and transported volumes over fixed finite fields

For a hidden-basis input, recover the row lines by the following linear operations. From its third flattening image $S$, regroup $S\otimes S$ into $A^{\otimes2}\otimes B^{\otimes2}$. Intersect with the tensor product of the swap-fixed space on $A^{\otimes2}$ and the span on $B^{\otimes2}$ of $y\otimes z-z\otimes y$. Contract the latter two slots by all coordinate covectors to obtain a span $D\subseteq A^{\otimes2}$. A coefficient with unequal rows in the intersection must have unequal columns, as the alternating column subspace has zero diagonal even in characteristic two. The two swap conditions would force all four associated cells to be supported, contradicting simplicity. On a single row there are two independent allowable column vectors $y,z$ as seen above, so their nonzero alternating tensor gives its row square by contraction. Thus $D$ is exactly the span of the coordinate row squares. Solving $(X\otimes1)D\subseteq D$ yields the full diagonal algebra in those lines. Refine simultaneous eigenspaces for a computed basis using all field elements. This runs in polynomial time for fixed field size. Intersections of the row projections then give the edge lines.

Undo the graph construction by the degree threshold, anchor identification, layer matchings and adjacency channel. Anchor identification can be performed directly on its complement by walking the three branches from the tree root or triangle. Label the recovered input graph $G$ in any order. Obtain a general-linear map $F$ from the displayed constructed tensor of $G$ to the supplied tensor. In detail, choose generators of the recovered vertex and edge lines. In their incidence cell basis a defining functional for $S$ has coefficients $d_{vf}$. Rescale the generators by factors $p_v,t_f$ satisfying $d_{vf}p_v t_f=1$ on every incidence. Fix one row factor and propagate the others over a spanning tree of the connected incidence graph, checking all edges. The first two maps now take the model incidence hyperplane onto $S$. Comparing the actual third flattening isomorphisms onto their images uniquely recovers the third map. For recognition beyond the promise, require concision, direct-sum spanning line sets, the prescribed simple incidences and full rebuilt graph, the stated nonzero hyperplane coefficients and a successful rescaling. Finally verify that the three computed maps take the model array to the input. Passing provides an explicit general-linear membership certificate.

On triples of nonzero determinant values define the character
$$
\chi(d_1,d_2,d_3)=d_1^{ec}d_2^{rc}d_3^{re}.
$$
Its kernel over any field is *exactly* the image of the scalar lift map $(u,w)\mapsto(u^r,w^e,(uw)^{-c})$, with unique preimages. Indeed the cross product of the integer exponent columns $(r,0,-c)$ and $(0,e,-c)$ is $(ec,rc,re)$, whose entries have gcd one. Adjoin a Bézout column having dot product one with that vector. The resulting integer matrix has determinant one, so its monomial map has an inverse given by integer Laurent monomials. Applying $\chi$ to this parametrization returns its third parameter. This proves the assertion even over fields lacking nontrivial roots of particular powers.

Put $\mu(X)=\chi(\det F_1,\det F_2,\det F_3)$. For six layers it is independent of generators, normalization, chosen graph labelling and transporter. Any change is a graph transport with determinant character one and a scalar lift in the kernel. For two such inputs with matching dimensions, $X$ and $Y$ are special-linearly equivalent if and only if their decoded graphs are isomorphic and $\mu(X)=\mu(Y)$. In fact a graph witness induces the general-linear map $F_Y P F_X^{-1}$. Its determinant character equals $\mu(Y)/\mu(X)$. The kernel calculation makes all its determinants one by a unique scalar correction precisely when this ratio is one. Conversely a special-linear map decodes to a graph witness satisfying that equation. Thus one ordinary GI instance suffices after the volume comparison, with an immediate fixed no instance on mismatch. The construction of the graph and field element does not require a canonizer.

For five layers, fix an arbitrary displayed order on each decoded graph when computing $\mu$. A graph transport of sign $s$ now has character $s$ as a field element, since $ec$ is odd. The exact hidden-input condition is therefore $s=\mu(X)/\mu(Y)$; a ratio outside $\{\pm1\}$ gives an immediate no. An order change modifies the corresponding $\mu$ by precisely that order's sign, leaving the test well defined. In characteristic two the test reduces to equality of volumes and ordinary GI. In other characteristics it produces prescribed-parity GI. The polynomial algorithms in this section use a fixed finite field. The forward constructions and determinant identities hold over all fields.

## Relation to earlier work and verification

Graph-to-tensor decision reductions and combinatorial constructions of matrix spaces precede this result. Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang discuss the inclusion of GI in the class for special-linear tensor isomorphism in §1.5. For decision alone one can also strip unused dimensions from an ordinary tensor, then append an unused coordinate to each mode and extend each general-linear witness by its inverse determinant. The claim proved here gives concise targets, a bijection on witnesses, prescribed parity, and a reverse test on the six-layer image. The argument gives no reduction from arbitrary special-linear tensor isomorphism to general-linear tensor isomorphism. This is a mathematical proof in prose. Formal verification and priority remain unestablished. [Grochow and Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309), [Li, Qiao, A. Wigderson, Y. Wigderson and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04815), [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, §1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135#S1.SS5).
