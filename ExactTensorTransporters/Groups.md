# Recovering graphs and counting all isomorphisms of graphical groups

Fix a prime $p$. Let $q=p^e$ with $e\ge 1$, and write $R=\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$. The modulus is given in binary. Group inputs are valid centered power-commutator coordinates for finite groups of class at most two. These give cyclic bases for the center and its quotient, lifts of the quotient generators, their central powers to their quotient orders, and their pairwise commutators. Bases may be arbitrary. Witnesses specify generator images in these coordinates.

**Theorem.** There is a polynomial-time recognizable family of such groups $P_q(G)$, indexed by all finite simple graphs $G$. From any centered presentation in the family, one can recover a graph isomorphic to $G$ and explicit isomorphisms to and from its standard group presentation in deterministic polynomial time at fixed $p$. Consequently, graph and group isomorphism decision, search, and canonization are respectively equivalent on this family. Counting is equivalent by one oracle call and explicit multiplication or exact division. For graphs with equal constructed vertex and edge counts $N,M$, the exact formula is

$$
|\operatorname{Iso}(P_q(G),P_q(G'))|
=q^{NM}\,\varphi(q)^N\,|\operatorname{Iso}(G,G')|.
$$

Unequal constructed dimensions give zero. Every group isomorphism decodes to a graph isomorphism, and the formula counts the fiber over each particular graph isomorphism. It includes $p=2$ and variable $e$. Complexity is measured in the length of the succinct presentations. An empty graph on zero vertices gives the trivial group.

Grochow and Qiao gave moderately exponential search- and counting-to-decision reductions in the class of finite class-two exponent-$p$ groups. He and Qiao proved that the classical graphical construction preserves and reflects graph isomorphism types. Graphical groups over commutative rings and the automorphisms of graphical Lie algebras have also been studied. Here the graph modification ensures that linear equations recover individual hidden vertices, making it possible to decode arbitrary coordinate changes and count every lift. The theorem has an informal proof below. [Grochow and Qiao](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.16) · [He and Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07200) · [Rossmann](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12665) · [Dani and Mainkar](https://people.se.cmich.edu/maink1m/trans.pdf)

Mekler previously interpreted graphs satisfying a vertex-separation condition and excluding triangles and four-cycles in groups of class two and odd prime exponent. In Mekler's convention, edges impose commutation. Here each edge indexes an independent commutator. [Mekler, *Stability of nilpotent groups of class 2 and prime exponent*](https://doi.org/10.2307/2273227) · [Chernikov and Hempel, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03724)

## A graph modification with unique extensions

Subdivide every edge of $G$ once. At each original vertex $u$, attach two further graphs, each consisting of three internally disjoint paths from $u$ to a fresh terminal. Use lengths $2, 3, 4$ for the first and $5, 6, 7$ for the second. All new vertices are private to the specified subdivision, terminal, or path. Call the result $H$, with $N$ vertices and $M$ edges. It has size $O(|V(G)|+|E(G)|)$. Each vertex has degree at least two, and every cycle has length at least five. Indeed an attachment meets the rest only at its root, its cycles consist of two paths, and cycles in the subdivided original graph have length at least six.

The original vertices are exactly those of degree at least six. Two distinct original vertices were adjacent precisely when joined by a length-two path in $H$. Each such path is unique. Once the original vertex map is fixed, the subdivision vertices are forced. At each original vertex the two attachments are distinguishable by their path lengths, the degree-three terminals are forced, and each path with its endpoints and length specified admits a unique map. Thus restriction and extension biject the graph isomorphism sets. To recognize this construction on any presented simple graph, identify the high-degree vertices and their common neighbors, then check that the rest consists of exactly the stipulated paths with the stated lengths, incidences and disjointness. Check that the identified vertices and edges exhaust the input. This takes polynomial time and produces the inverse identification.

## Commutators and the projected derivation algebra

Take one generator $x_i$ per vertex of $H$ and a central generator $z_{ij}$ per edge $i<j$. Set all generator $q$-th powers equal to one. Put $[x_i,x_j]=z_{ij}$ for an edge $i<j$ and set the other commutators of distinct vertex generators equal to one, with the reverse orientation given by inversion. Use $[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. A concrete model has coordinates $V\times W=R^N\times R^M$ and multiplication
$$
(a,c)(a',c')=(a+a',c+c'+B(a,a')), \qquad
B(a,a')_{ij}=a_i a'_j \quad (i<j, ij\text{ an edge}).
$$
Bilinearity gives associativity. The indicated generators satisfy all relations and generate the model. Collecting any word in the presentation puts it into one of at most $q^{N+M}$ ordered forms, so the model realizes the presentation exactly. Its commutator $b=B-B^{\mathrm{op}}$ spans $W$. If $a_i\ne 0$, there is a neighbor $j$ of $i$, and the $ij$-coordinate of $b(a,e_j)$ is $\pm a_i$. Thus $W$ is both the center and derived subgroup, and the central quotient is $V$. This construction works for even $q$, when the whole group can have exponent $2q$.

For any such pairing form the following projected solution space over $R$.
$$
L_b=\{D\in\operatorname{End}_R(V)\mid
\exists E\in\operatorname{End}_R(W), b(Dx,y)+b(x,Dy)=E b(x,y)\text{ for all }x,y\}.
$$
The equations need only be imposed on pairs of basis vectors. Any group isomorphism induces a pair of $R$-linear maps $T,S$ on the quotient and center, respectively, with $b'(Tx,Ty)=Sb(x,y)$. Conjugating witnesses $D,E$ shows that $T L_b T^{-1}=L_{b'}$.

In graph coordinates $L_b$ consists of **all diagonal matrices**. Fix two distinct vertices $u,w$. There is a neighbor $v$ of $w$, distinct from $u$ and nonadjacent to $u$. If $u,w$ are adjacent, use any other neighbor of $w$ and the absence of triangles. Otherwise $u,w$ have at most one common neighbor by absence of four-cycles, and $w$ has at least two neighbors. Apply the equations to $e_u,e_v$. Their bracket is zero. The $wv$-coordinate on the left is $\pm D_{wu}$, as every contribution from $b(e_u,De_v)$ is indexed by an edge incident with $u$. Hence $D_{wu}=0$. Conversely a diagonal $D$ with entries $d_i$ extends by multiplying the $ij$-edge coordinate by $d_i+d_j$. This computation holds over any commutative coefficient ring.

Over the local ring $R$, the only scalar idempotents are zero and one. The nonzero diagonal idempotents admitting no further decomposition into orthogonal nonzero idempotents are exactly the $N$ coordinate projectors. Conjugation therefore permutes those projectors and $T$ permutes their cyclic images. Its matrix is monomial with a unit coefficient on each vertex. Whether two such cyclic lines commute detects their adjacency, so the underlying permutation is a graph isomorphism of $H$.

## Exact group witnesses

Given a graph isomorphism $\pi:H\to H'$, choose an arbitrary unit $s_i$ and central element $c_i\in W'$ independently for each $i$. Send $x_i$ to $(x'_{\pi(i)})^{s_i}c_i$, and send every $z_{ij}$ to the commutator of the corresponding images. These images preserve powers and commuting relations. The map on $V$ is monomial, and the map on $W$ permutes the edge generators up to unit multiples and orientation signs. Both are invertible, so the resulting map is an isomorphism. Conversely the projected derivation calculation forces the permutation and all units from any group isomorphism. The $c_i$ then follow uniquely from the generator images, and edge images are forced. This proves the fiber formula. It also gives actual maps in both directions between graph witnesses and group witnesses, and an independent parametrization of all lifts of any graph witness.

## Recognition and recovery in hidden bases

Check first that the supplied center and central quotient are free $R$-modules in the given cyclic decomposition. Form the commutator from the input. Reduce it modulo $p$ and compute a basis of $L_{\bar b}$ by linear algebra over $\mathbb F_p$. For each basis matrix successively refine the current subspaces into intersections with its kernels $\ker(D-aI)$ for $a\in\mathbb F_p$. Check that these intersections give a direct sum at each split, and end with $N$ lines. On a group in the family, all basis matrices are diagonal in the hidden frame. Their joint eigenvalues separate every pair of vertex lines, because their span contains every diagonal projector. Thus this procedure finds the entire frame over the residue field, including when $p=2$.

For each resulting line compute the projector $\bar P_i$ onto it along the other lines. Solve the linear derivation equations over $R$, adding $D\bmod p=\bar P_i$. These are linear congruences solvable by Smith normal form, including the residue constraint written as $p^{e-1}D=p^{e-1}\widetilde P_i$ for any entrywise lift $\widetilde P_i$. On the family, the hidden integral coordinate projector is a solution. Every solution $D$ is diagonal in the hidden integral frame, with entries reducing to the bits of that projector. Apply $f(X)=3X^2-2X^3$ repeatedly, $\lceil\log_2 e\rceil$ times. The identities $f(t)=t^2(3-2t)$ and $1-f(t)=(1-t)^2(1+2t)$ show that the precision of each entry around its specified bit doubles at every step. This yields the exact integral projector $P_i$. Choose a column of $P_i$ nonzero modulo $p$ to generate its cyclic image. Reject if any required solve, decomposition or primitive-column test fails.

Use the resulting vectors as columns of a matrix and verify that it is invertible. Lift the columns to words $y_i$ in the original group by the input coordinates. On the family, each is a unit power of its hidden vertex generator times a central element. Check $y_i^q=1$ using binary powering. Form the simple graph with edge $ij$ precisely when $[y_i,y_j]\ne 1$, and verify that its oriented edge commutators give an $R$-basis of the supplied center. Check the graph modification described above and extract its original $G$. Each accepted input now has an explicit isomorphism from the standard $P_q(G)$, given by the $y_i$ and their commutators. The defining relations give a homomorphism and the two basis checks make it bijective. This proves soundness even when an input outside the family happens to pass the intermediate eigenvalue tests. Completeness follows from the hidden-coordinate computation. To invert the isomorphism on a group element, solve for its projected coordinate word in the new quotient basis, divide off that word, and express the remaining central element in the new edge basis.

At fixed $p$ all the operations have polynomial bit complexity in the succinct input, including $e$. Only the common-eigenspace step enumerates the $p$ residues. The recognition assertion concerns valid centered presentations with specified $q$.

For decision and search, recover the two graphs. Any graph transporter lifts by the generator formulas and the computed coordinate isomorphisms. In the opposite direction read the permutation from the quotient matrix of a group transporter and restrict to original vertices. The counting reductions use the displayed fiber size, computable from the input dimensions, with its binary digit length polynomial in the input length. A canonical labeled graph gives a canonical group presentation by constructing its scaffold and ordering all vertices, paths and edges deterministically. Conversely, from a canonical centered presentation of a group, run the deterministic decoder with fixed pivot and ordering conventions. This gives a canonical labeled graph. These are reductions between their respective canonization tasks, with no assumption that a decision oracle supplies a canonical labeling.

## Prescribing the quotient and central determinants

**Corollary.** Assume that $G$ has at least one vertex. Fix ordered bases of each source and target central quotient and center. For every graph isomorphism $G\to G'$ and each prescribed pair $(\delta_V,\delta_W)\in(R^\times)^2$, exactly
$$
q^{NM}\,\varphi(q)^{N-2}
$$
group isomorphisms above that graph isomorphism have these determinants on $V$ and $W$. In particular, requiring both induced matrices to have determinant one preserves every graph witness. For the empty graph the unique group isomorphism has both determinants equal to one.

Start from the lift using unit coefficients one and central corrections zero. Composing with a source automorphism that scales vertex $i$ by $s_i$ multiplies the quotient determinant by $\prod_i s_i$. On an $ij$-edge it scales the commutator by $s_i s_j$, so the central determinant is multiplied by $\prod_i s_i^{\deg(i)}$. The factors are independent of the displayed bases because they are determinants of endomorphisms. Each attachment has an internal degree-two vertex and a degree-three terminal. Choose one vertex of each degree and assign arbitrary units at the other $N-2$ vertices. Divide the two desired determinants by the contributions of the initial lift and these chosen units, obtaining $A,B\in R^\times$. The two remaining scalars $s,t$ must satisfy $st=A$ and $s^2t^3=B$. They are uniquely determined by $t=BA^{-2}$ and $s=A^3B^{-1}$. All $q^{NM}$ choices of central generator corrections still give distinct group lifts with the same two determinants. This proves the count and constructs a lift for every prescribed pair.
