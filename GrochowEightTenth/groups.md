# Two finite-group algorithms

## 1. Minimum faithful permutation actions for abelian Hall extensions

**Source question.** Kantor and Luks ask for a polynomial-time algorithm for the minimum faithful permutation degree in the permutation model. Das and Thakkar study the constructive Cayley-table version and give a general quasipolynomial-time bound. [Kantor–Luks, §13](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~kantor/PAPERS/quotient.pdf) · [Das–Thakkar](https://doi.org/10.1137/24M1685353)

**Theorem.** Fix an integer \(r\). Given the Cayley table of a finite group \(G\) with an abelian normal Hall subgroup \(N\) such that every subgroup of \(G/N\) is at most \(r\)-generated, one can compute the minimum degree and an attaining faithful action in deterministic polynomial time. The input need not specify \(N\). In particular the theorem applies to every abelian-by-cyclic extension with coprime kernel and quotient orders, including kernels of arbitrary exponent and quotients of unbounded order.

**Stabilizers and faithfulness.** Put \(n=|G|\). Enumerate subsets of the primes dividing \(n\), of which there are at most \(n\). For each subset, a normal Hall subgroup for those primes must coincide with the set of elements whose orders have support in the subset. Compute that set and test normality, Hall order and commutativity. For each resulting candidate quotient \(C\), list subgroups generated by at most \(r\) elements and test closure of the list under adjoining one element. This guarantees the list is complete. All these tests are polynomial in \(n\) for fixed \(r\).

Write \(N\) additively. A set section of \(G\to C\) supplies a factor cocycle \(u(c,d)\) in \(N\). If \(v(c)=|C|^{-1}\sum_d u(c,d)\), summing the cocycle identity over its last argument gives \(v(c)+cv(d)-v(cd)=u(c,d)\). The inverse of \(|C|\) exists on \(N\). Replacing the section by its translate by \(-v(c)\) gives \(G=N\rtimes C\) explicitly. If an orbit stabilizer \(L\) has image \(B\le C\) and \(W=L\cap N\), then \(W\) is \(B\)-invariant and \(L/W\) in \((N/W)\rtimes B\) is the graph of a 1-cocycle. Averaging the 1-cocycle over \(B\) makes it principal. Conjugating \(L\) by a translation consequently gives \(W\rtimes B\).

Let \(M=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)\) with the contragredient action, and use the annihilator \(D=W^\perp\le M\). Orbit size is \(|D|[C:B]\). For a family of orbits, the action kernel meets \(N\) trivially exactly when the \(C\)-span of the annihilators is \(M\). This follows by taking annihilators of the intersection of the conjugates of the \(W\)'s. Set \(K_0=\ker(C\to\operatorname{Aut}(N))\). Any normal kernel disjoint from \(N\) centralizes \(N\) and so belongs to \(N\times K_0\). Taking suitable coprime powers of any element in that direct product shows that this kernel lies in \(K_0\). The trace on \(K_0\) of the core of \(W\rtimes B\) is \(K_0\cap\operatorname{core}_C(B)\). Thus the additional faithfulness condition is trivial intersection of these traces.

**Filtered lifting.** For a finite abelian \(p\)-group \(X\), filter \(\bar X=X/pX\) by \(F_{\le e}\bar X=(X[p^e]+pX)/pX\). For two such groups with an action of a group \(B\) of order prime to \(p\), the reductions of equivariant homomorphisms are precisely the filtered \(B\)-linear maps. To lift one, choose cyclic generators of the source. The exponent condition supplies a lift of each generator image of order dividing the order of the generator. Define the additive map and average its conjugates over \(B\) to make it equivariant. The averaged reduction is the original map.

Every such \(X\) splits as a direct sum of invariant homocyclic groups whose reductions are simple \(\mathbb F_p[B]\)-modules. Indeed, Maschke's theorem gives a simple decomposition of \(\bar X\) compatible with the filtration. Lift a projection onto one simple summand by the preceding procedure. The ideal of endomorphisms reducing to zero sends \(X\) into \(pX\), hence is nilpotent. The iteration \(f\mapsto f^2(3-2f)\) keeps the reduction and makes the idempotent error divisible by the square of the preceding error, because these are polynomials in one element. This yields an actual idempotent. Its image and kernel split \(X\), and their mod-\(p\) filtrations are the direct summands of the original filtration. A summand whose reduction is simple and enters at exactly one level \(e\) is homocyclic of exponent \(p^e\) by the cyclic decomposition. Repeat on the kernel. Conversely, a simple \(\tau\) of \(\mathbb F_p[B]\) has a homocyclic lift of any exponent \(p^e\). Split a surjection from the regular \(\mathbb F_p[B]\)-module to \(\tau\), lift the resulting idempotent to the regular \((\mathbb Z/p^e)[B]\)-module by filtered lifting and nilpotent correction, and take its image. These computations use matrices on the additive cyclic generators. The lift from the regular module is compact and needs no table with \(p^{e|B|}\) entries.

Split each nonzero \(D\) into its primary components and then into the homocyclic \(B\)-summands just constructed. Replacing an orbit by one orbit for each summand preserves both faithfulness tests and cannot increase cost, since the product of two or more integers each at least two is at least their sum. A summand whose map to the corresponding \(M_p/pM_p\) is zero may be replaced by the zero annihilator with the same \(B\). Spanning each \(U_p=M_p/pM_p\) by the images and their \(C\)-translates is equivalent to spanning \(M\), by the finite abelian \(p\)-group version of Nakayama's lemma.

**Finite optimization.** Choose a simple \(C\)-decomposition of \(U_p\) compatible with the filtration. Let \(n_{p,\sigma,h}\) count copies of simple type \(\sigma\) entering at level \(h\). For each listed \(B\), each prime \(p\mid|N|\), each simple type \(\tau\) occurring in \(U_p\) restricted to \(B\), and each level \(e\) up to the exponent of \(M_p\) written as \(p^e\), offer an orbit of nominal cost \(p^{e\dim \tau}[C:B]\). A reduced map \(\tau\to F_{\le e}U_p\) induces a \(C\)-linear map from \(I=\operatorname{Ind}_B^C\tau\) with that same bound on its image. For each \(\sigma\), the \(\sigma\)-capacity is

\[
 m_{B,\tau,\sigma}=\frac{\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\operatorname{Hom}_C(I,\sigma)}{\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\operatorname{End}_C(\sigma)}.
\]

An option may allocate any nonnegative counts to levels \(h\le e\) for each \(\sigma\), up to its capacity summed over those levels. Semisimplicity gives a surjection onto any chosen set of target simple copies realizing these counts. Compute it by finding a nonzero map to one such copy, splitting it by averaging, and recursing on the kernel. Simple decompositions of \(U_p\) and its restrictions to \(B\) are computable by enumerating vectors, taking a nonzero orbit span of smallest dimension and splitting it by averaging, also respecting the filtration when required. There are at most \(|N|\) vectors in \(U_p\). Simple type tests and capacity calculations are linear systems on representation matrices. Include an option for the zero annihilator at each \(B\), with zero allocation and cost \([C:B]\).

The shortest-path states retain the covered counts in every \((p,\sigma,h)\) and the current subgroup of \(K_0\). The state starts with all counts zero and subgroup \(K_0\). A transition adds an allowed allocation without exceeding any target count, and intersects the subgroup with \(K_0\cap\operatorname{core}_C(B)\). The final state requires all counts and the trivial subgroup. The number of count states is

\[
 \prod_{p,\sigma,h}(n_{p,\sigma,h}+1)\le 2^{\sum_{p,\sigma,h}n_{p,\sigma,h}}\le|N|.
\]

The bound also controls the number of allocations per option. The subgroup component comes from the already enumerated subgroup list of \(C\). This gives a polynomial-size graph, with polynomial bit lengths for costs.

**Optimality and recovery.** From a faithful action, first split its orbit annihilators as above. For each simple type \(\sigma\), the combined induced maps from its orbits are onto the target isotype. Their matrices on multiplicity spaces are over the finite field \(\operatorname{End}_C(\sigma)\). Choose a nonsingular maximal row minor and a nonzero term of its determinant. Each target copy is thereby matched to a distinct source copy in some orbit option, at a level that respects that option's filtration bound. The matches give feasible allocations, and the kernel state reaches the identity. The shortest path therefore costs at most the true optimum.

Conversely, take a shortest path and assign fresh target simple copies for each allocated count. Construct the induced maps onto those copies and restrict via Frobenius reciprocity to the corresponding filtered \(B\)-maps. Lift each to a map from the homocyclic lift of \(\tau\) into \(M_p\), using filtered lifting. Use the image \(D\) as annihilator. Its order is at most the nominal order, and its reduction in \(U_p\) is the designated image. Recover the stabilizer \(D^\perp\rtimes B\) and its coset action. All such orbits together are faithful by the two tests, so their degree lies between the optimum and the shortest-path cost. This proves equality and gives explicit permutations in polynomial time. For the trivial group the degree is handled directly according to whether an empty action is included. The cyclic-generator calculations throughout can be performed by polynomial-time integer normal forms. [Kannan–Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

## 2. Conjugator search across mixed-prime scalar-affine constituents

**Source question.** Allender, Grochow, van Melkebeek, Moore and Morgan ask whether search reduces to decision for permutation-group conjugacy. The preceding mixed-prime theorem for abelian permutation groups and common-prime theorem for prime-degree affine constituents give two special cases. [Allender et al., Open Problems](https://arxiv.org/html/1710.09806#S1.SS4) · [Orbit-marking proof](orbit-marking-lemma.md#encoding-distinguished-point-orbits)

**Theorem.** Permutation-group conjugator search has a deterministic polynomial-time Turing reduction to ordinary permutation-group conjugacy decision under the following promise. Each nontrivial orbit has prime-power size \(p^d\), its local transitive constituent has an abelian normal Sylow \(p\)-subgroup acting regularly, and conjugation by every local permutation acts on that Sylow subgroup by an integer scalar. Different orbits may involve different primes, exponents and additive types. The global extension may be nonsplit.

**Matching orbits and extracting coordinates.** Apply the prime-orbit marking reduction to find an orbit matching compatible with a conjugator, or report nonconjugacy. Denote the paired regular Sylow groups by \(V_i,V'_i\). A permutation centralizing an abelian regular Sylow group and fixing its chosen origin fixes the whole orbit. The point stabilizer therefore embeds into the scalar units. The local group has order at most \(|V_i|\varphi(\exp V_i)\le |V_i|^2\), so it can be enumerated. Its normal Sylow subgroup is the set of elements of \(p\)-power order. Choosing origins gives coordinates in \(V_i,V'_i\). Each local generator acts by \(x\mapsto bx+a\), with \(a\) obtained by evaluating it at the origin and \(b\) by testing its conjugation action on translations against residues modulo \(\exp V_i\). A conjugator respecting the matching acts by \(x\mapsto U_i x+t_i\) on each pair, where \(U_i\) is additive, since it conjugates the characteristic regular Sylow subgroups. It preserves each multiplier residue because additive isomorphisms commute with every integer scalar. Paired additive types and exponents agree. Matched fixed points can be filled in arbitrarily.

**Projection and kernels.** Write \(V=\prod_i V_i\), \(V'=\prod_i V'_i\), and let \(M\) be the product of the matched scalar unit groups. The input groups are subgroups \(G\le V\rtimes M\) and \(H\le V'\rtimes M\), with common image \(B\) in \(M\). Represent \(M\) faithfully on separate copies of \(\mathbb Z/\exp(V_i)\) by multiplication. The total auxiliary degree is at most the original degree. Use constructive permutation-group membership on the auxiliary points to compute the projections and for each original generator \((a_j,b_j)\) of \(G\) find a target lift \((a'_j,b_j)\). Evaluate the membership straight-line program in the original generators of \(H\). Adjoin the auxiliary points to the original action and take their pointwise stabilizer to find generators of \(A=G\cap V\) and \(A'=H\cap V'\). Store the resulting additive groups and subgroups in compact cyclic presentations. [Polynomial-time permutation-group algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539794229417) · [Kannan–Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

**A finite-module problem for each prime.** Fix \(p\) and now use \(V,V',A,A'\) for their \(p\)-primary parts. Use the restriction of each \(b_j\) to these coordinates. Let \(R_0=\mathbb Z/p^e\), where \(p^e\) is the largest orbit-group exponent at this prime. Set \(Q=(V/A)\oplus R_0\) and \(Q'=(V'/A')\oplus R_0\). The source diagram has vertices \(V_i\) for the selected prime, \(Q\), and a separate terminal \(R_0\). The arrows are the coordinate inclusions followed by quotient into the first summand of \(Q\), and the last-coordinate map \(Q\to R_0\). Give \(Q\) a distinguished endomorphism for each original generator,

\[
 T_j(x,s)=(b_j x+s\bar a_j,s).
\]

This is well defined by \(B\)-invariance of \(A\). Construct the corresponding target diagram using \(Q',\bar a'_j\). On the direct sum of all vertex groups of both diagrams, take the common unital endomorphism subring generated by pairs of corresponding vertex projections, arrows and distinguished endomorphisms. Regard each diagram's direct sum as a left module under these operators.

Start with the additive subgroup generated by the operators and identity. Close it under products of pairs of additive generators and recompute a cyclic presentation by integer normal forms. Each strict enlargement at least doubles the subgroup's size, and the logarithm of the ambient additive endomorphism-group order is polynomial in the compact input length. A stable round gives the ring with structure constants and the two module actions on their additive generators. Constructive finite-ring module isomorphism on these presentations returns an isomorphism in deterministic polynomial time. [Ciocănea–Teodorescu, §2 and Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08365)

**Witness recovery.** The vertex projections force a module isomorphism to map corresponding vertices. On the terminal \(R_0\) it multiplies by a unit \(\eta\). Multiply all its vertex maps by an integer representative of \(\eta^{-1}\) to normalize the terminal map to identity. This commutes with every additive operator and is invertible on every vertex group, all of which have exponent dividing \(p^e\). The local maps give an additive isomorphism \(U=\prod_i U_i\). Commuting with the arrows from the \(V_i\)'s forces \(U(A)=A'\), since these are the kernels of the summed quotient maps. This also determines the induced map on the first summand of \(Q\). The terminal arrow forces the \(Q\)-map to have the form

\[
 (x,s)\longmapsto(\bar U x+s\bar t,s)
\]

for some \(\bar t\in V'/A'\). Intertwining each \(T_j\) gives

\[
 U a_j+(1-b_j)t-a'_j\in A'
\]

for any lift \(t\in V'\) of \(\bar t\). Conversely these equalities and \(U(A)=A'\) define the diagram isomorphism for any compatible affine conjugator. Existence of a compatible orbit matching thus guarantees the required module isomorphisms.

Combine the recovered affine maps over all primes. The equations show that every source generator conjugates into the target group. The conjugated kernel is the full target kernel and the projection is the common group \(B\), so conjugation takes the source group onto the target. Write the resulting affine bijections in the original point coordinates. This procedure allows the global scalar projection and the global translation subgroup to have primes in common.

## Verification status

Both proofs are mathematical derivations for their stated input classes. General Cayley-table minimum faithful degree and unrestricted permutation-group conjugator search remain open in this treatment. These proofs have no accompanying Lean kernel certificate.
