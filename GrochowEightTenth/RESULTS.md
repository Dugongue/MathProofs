# Nine results on odd-order orbit recovery, finite-group algorithms, and spectral separation

1. **A single generic real Gram graph recovers every finite odd-order group.** For every faithful real orthogonal action it gives the exact multiplication table and regular action by a deterministic polynomial-time arithmetic algorithm. The proof allows Gram colors that merge different inverse pairs. [Proof](invariants.md#one-real-gram-graph-recovers-every-finite-odd-order-group)
2. **Explicit stability for odd-order group recovery.** The same multiplication table and action are recovered from entrywise noisy Gram data under an explicit radius in terms of the color and distance gaps. The perturbed entries can be given as an arbitrary real matrix. [Proof](invariants.md#stable-odd-order-group-recovery-from-a-noisy-gram-matrix)
3. **No dimension-free abstract recovery bound for linear orbits.** For every \(k\), \(k\) generic basis-orbits in dimension \(4k\) can come from either \(C_4\) or \(C_2^2\). The obstruction holds for Borel estimators in absolute real coordinates even when each conjugate group may choose a separate dense open generic set. [Proof](invariants.md#no-dimension-free-bound-for-abstract-recovery-from-linear-orbits)
4. **Cubic elementary-symmetric recognition is fixed-parameter tractable in the summand bound.** The deterministic algorithm works over every fixed finite characteristic-two field of size at least four. It includes an exact treatment of one summand over \(\mathbb F_4\) and a polynomial-time one-summand result over \(\mathbb F_2\). [Proof](complexity.md)
5. **Exact and border elementary-symmetric projections separate in characteristic three.** A ten-variable quartic lies in the border of the one-projection model and has no exact one-projection representation. For characteristic-two cubics over an algebraically closed field, exact and border classes coincide at every fixed summand bound. [Proof](complexity.md#2-exact-and-border-projection-classes-coincide-for-characteristic-two-cubics-and-separate-for-characteristic-three-quartics)
6. **Constructive minimum faithful actions for abelian Hall extensions.** In polynomial Cayley-table time, find the minimum degree and an attaining action when the group has an abelian normal Hall subgroup and every subgroup of the quotient is at most a fixed number of generators. This includes cyclic quotients of unbounded order and abelian kernels of arbitrary exponent. [Proof](groups.md#1-minimum-faithful-permutation-actions-for-abelian-hall-extensions)
7. **Search-to-decision for mixed-prime scalar-affine conjugacy.** A deterministic reduction returns a conjugating permutation when the nontrivial local constituents have regular abelian Sylow translation groups and scalar multipliers. It allows mixed prime-power orbit sizes and nonsplit global translation extensions. [Proof](groups.md#2-conjugator-search-across-mixed-prime-scalar-affine-constituents)
8. **Deterministic diagonal separation with a gap linear in the budget.** Every \(n\times n\) Hermitian matrix admits a real diagonal perturbation of norm at most \(\delta\) with gap greater than \(\delta/(4n^3)\), constructible in polynomial bit time for rational data. The algorithm uses \(O(n^6)\) weighted spectral evaluations. [Proof](coordinate-spectral-gap.md#linear-budget-diagonal-shattering-of-every-hermitian-matrix)
9. **Bidiagonal upper-band singular-value separation.** Perturbations in the bidiagonal pattern give a deterministic gap greater than \(\delta\tau^2/(136n^3)\) for adjacent singular values above \(\tau\) on inputs of norm at most one. Explicit bidiagonal matrices show that an all-spectrum inverse-polynomial guarantee fails in the same perturbation model. [Proof](coordinate-spectral-gap.md#bidiagonal-singular-value-gaps-above-a-threshold)
