# Counting epimorphisms onto abelian Hall extensions by character-height codes

**Theorem.** Let $G_1,G_2$ be finite groups given by Cayley tables. Suppose $G_2$ has an abelian normal Hall subgroup $A_2$ with abelian complement $B_2$, and every Sylow subgroup of $B_2$ is homocyclic. There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm to count all epimorphisms $G_1\twoheadrightarrow G_2$ and return one whenever the count is positive. The source $G_1$ is arbitrary. There is also a split version for two groups $G_i=A_i\rtimes B_i$ with abelian normal Hall subgroups for a common prime set and arbitrary abelian complements $B_i$. In this version the algorithm counts and constructs epimorphisms whose induced complement map is split.

Here a homocyclic $q$-group is a direct power of a single cyclic $q$-group. The trivial group is allowed. A complement map is split if it has a group-homomorphism right inverse. For the second assertion the Hall subgroups specify the quotient maps on which splitness is imposed. The proof is informal.

Grochow, Srivastava, and Thakkar prove polynomial decision for abelian Hall kernels with cyclic complements and for elementary Sylow Hall kernels with elementary abelian complements. The result here allows higher exponents, several complement primes, and arbitrary source groups. It answers the counting question for this class and covers part of the abelian Sylow tower question. [Algorithms for Finite Group Epimorphism Testing, Theorems 1 and 4 and Open Questions 2 and 3(4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)

## 1. Reduction to a single complement map

Return zero if $|G_2|$ fails to divide $|G_1|$. For the homocyclic-target assertion, try the sets of prime divisors of $|G_2|$. For a set $\pi$, the elements of $G_2$ with orders supported on $\pi$ must form the specified normal Hall subgroup, if one exists. Indeed any such element has trivial image in the complementary quotient. Test closure and the abelian Hall condition and determine the quotient's abelian cyclic invariants. The number of prime sets is at most $|G_2|$. On the promised domain choose any valid $\pi$. For the split-on-complement assertion the prime set or the Hall subgroups are part of its specification.

For an arbitrary source $G_1$, let $K$ be the normal subgroup generated by all elements of $G_1$ of $\pi$-supported order. Its index has order prime to all primes in $\pi$, since it contains every source Sylow subgroup at a prime in $\pi$. Under every epimorphism $f:G_1\to G_2$, $K$ maps into $A_2$. It maps onto $A_2$ because an epimorphism sends a Sylow subgroup to a Sylow subgroup at the same prime and the target Hall Sylows generate $A_2$. Every such $f$ kills $[K,K]$ and the preimage of the $\pi'$-primary part of $K/[K,K]$. That preimage is normal in $G_1$, as it is characteristic in $K$. Quotient the source by this preimage. Every epimorphism factors uniquely through the quotient, and the image of $K$ is now an abelian normal Hall subgroup $A_1$ with prime set supported on $\pi$. All these operations use subgroup closure and quotient tables in polynomial time.

We can construct a split section in polynomial time. Choose any normalized section $s_i:B_i\to G_i$. Its action on $A_i$ is independent of the section since $A_i$ is abelian. The resulting additive cocycle $c_i(x,y)=s_i(x)s_i(y)s_i(xy)^{-1}$ satisfies
$$
c_i(x,y)=b_i(x)+x\cdot b_i(y)-b_i(xy),\qquad
b_i(x)=|B_i|^{-1}\sum_z c_i(x,z).
$$
Sum the cocycle identity over $z$ to obtain this equality. Multiplication by $|B_i|$ is invertible on $A_i$ by coprimality. Adjusting the section by $-b_i(x)$ gives the splitting.

After splitting, an epimorphism has the form
$$
(a,b)\longmapsto (u(a)+v(b),\phi(b)),
$$
where $\phi:B_1\twoheadrightarrow B_2$ is a surjection, $u:A_1\twoheadrightarrow A_2$ is surjective and equivariant for the target action pulled back through $\phi$, and $v$ is a $1$-cocycle for this pulled-back action. Order considerations force the map on the Hall subgroup to have image in $A_2$. If a source element mapping into $A_2$ has complement coordinate $b$, then the image of $(0,b)$ lies in $A_2$ and has order prime to $|A_2|$, so it is the identity. This gives surjectivity of $u$. The converse follows from the semidirect product law. Every $1$-cocycle is $v(b)=w-b\cdot w$, by averaging its cocycle identity, so its contributions to the count are always $|A_2|/|A_2^{B_2}|$.

If $B_1$ is nonabelian, put $C=[B_1,B_1]$. Since $B_2$ is abelian, $\phi$ factors through $B_1/C$. Equivariance then forces $u$ to kill the subgroup of $A_1$ generated by $c\cdot a-a$ for $c\in C$ and $a\in A_1$. This subgroup is $B_1$-invariant. The quotient of $A_1$ by it carries an action of $B_1/C$. Conversely maps on these two quotients lift uniquely to the original pair $(u,\phi)$ and preserve surjectivity. The factor $|A_2|/|A_2^{B_2}|$ for the cocycles is unchanged. This reduces the rest of the algorithm to abelian $B_1$ in polynomial time. The split variant already assumes abelian $B_1$ and uses no such preprocessing.

## 2. The kernel test is a filtered character matching

Fix a Hall prime $p$ and denote the $p$-part of $A_i$ by $M_i$. On $M_i/pM_i$, use the $B_i$-invariant increasing flag
$$
F_j(M_i)=(M_i[p^j]+pM_i)/pM_i.
$$
Its successive quotients have dimensions equal to the numbers of cyclic summands of order $p^j$. A homomorphism $M_1\to M_2$ induces a filtered linear map on these spaces. Conversely, every filtered linear map lifts to a group homomorphism. In cyclic coordinates, the prescribed image in the head of a generator of order $p^j$ only has coordinates in cyclic target summands of order at most $p^j$, so it has a lift of order dividing $p^j$. If the map on the head is equivariant for a fixed $\phi$, averaging an arbitrary lift by
$$
|B_1|^{-1}\sum_{b\in B_1} b^{-1}\cdot u(b\cdot -)
$$
gives an equivariant lift of the same map. A map of finite abelian $p$-groups is surjective if its head map is surjective. In fact $M_2/\operatorname{im}(u)$ would equal $p(M_2/\operatorname{im}(u))$, hence vanish by iteration.

Each filtered head admits an equivariant splitting by Maschke's theorem. The simple factors of the head are described by the Frobenius orbits of characters in $D_i=\operatorname{Hom}(B_i,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$. A character of order $d$ has orbit size $f=\operatorname{ord}_d(p)$, with $f=1$ by convention when $d=1$. Its simple module and endomorphism field have dimension and order $f$ and $p^f$, respectively. To verify the description, over a splitting field the commuting actions diagonalize simultaneously, with one-dimensional eigentypes identified by characters. Frobenius permutes the eigentypes, and the orbit descends to the simple module given by scalar multiplication on $\mathbb F_{p^f}$. Equivalently each orbit gives one field factor of the finite commutative semisimple group algebra.

A complement surjection corresponds by finite abelian duality to an injection $\theta:D_2\hookrightarrow D_1$. It pulls every target orbit to exactly one source orbit, preserving its order and degree and commuting with Frobenius. For a fixed $\theta$, a surjective equivariant filtered head map exists precisely when every target simple copy of height $t$ can be matched to a distinct source copy of the corresponding orbit at height $j\ge t$. To prove this, within each isotypic component take the matrix over its endomorphism field. Each target row of height $t$ can use only source coordinates of heights at least $t$. Such a matrix has full row rank exactly when one can assign a distinct allowed column to each row. Necessity follows from a nonsingular minor, and a matching gives a suitable submatrix of the identity. The arguments apply independently at every Hall prime $p$.

All the simple multiplicities can be computed in polynomial time. Construct abelian cyclic coordinates for the complements by Smith normal form, for instance from their multiplication-table presentations, and enumerate their duals. For each order $d$, let $f=\operatorname{ord}_d(p)$ with the preceding convention. If $f$ exceeds the layer dimension, all the multiplicities at that order are zero. Otherwise construct $\mathbb F_{p^f}$ by enumerating and testing monic polynomials, and choose a primitive $d$-th root by direct enumeration. Its size is at most $|M_i|$. Use the same field and root for a given pair $(p,d)$ on both sides, identifying a character value $k/d\bmod\mathbb Z$ with the $k$-th power of that root. For each character, the rank of its eigenspace projector $|B_i|^{-1}\sum_b\chi(b)^{-1}R(b)$ gives the multiplicity, where $R(b)$ is the layer action. Use one representative of each orbit. All matrix dimensions, table sizes, and field sizes in this calculation are polynomial in the original input.

## 3. A logarithmic-size vertex set for all character constraints

Fix one representative of each target orbit, repeated for each occurrence and tagged by $(p,t)$, the Hall prime and the head height. Enumerate prospective images of this tuple as colored multisets. For each source copy of an orbit of degree $f$ and height $j$, either discard it or lower its height to $1\le t\le j$ and choose one of its $f$ representatives. Give it the tag $(p,t)$, and deduplicate the resulting multisets. This enumerates the candidate image multiset for every compatible $\theta$. It costs polynomial time, because each copy contributes at most $1+jf\le 2^{jf}$ choices and the sum of $jf$ over all copies is at most $\log_2 |A_1|$. Each image multiset is kept once, even if several ways of lowering yield it.

Consider one candidate tuple $\chi_{1,k}$ and the fixed target tuple $\chi_{2,k}$, where a position has color $(p,t,d)$ with $d$ its character order. If the color multiplicities differ, reject this candidate. Let $D_{1,q},D_{2,q}$ be the primary components at complement prime $q$, and let $Y_q\le D_{1,q}$ be a chosen ambient subgroup containing the required image of $D_{2,q}$. We specify these groups in Section 4. Discard tuples whose source characters do not lie in $Y=\prod_qY_q$. For an exponent tuple $a=(a_k)\in\prod_k \mathbb Z/d_k\$, compute in the corresponding ambient group the height vector of
$$
\sum_k a_k\chi_{i,k}.
$$
The $q$-height of a nonzero $q$-component is the largest $h\ge0$ for which it lies in $q^h$ times its ambient group. The zero component has height $\infty$. Thus the height vector includes whether the expression is zero. Compute table membership in the subgroups $q^h Y_q$ and $q^hD_{2,q}$ directly.

A color-preserving position permutation matches these two height tables precisely when the correspondence of marked characters induces a height-preserving isomorphism from their target span to their source span. Equality of zero relations gives a well-defined bijective homomorphism, and equality of every height then supplies the rest of the condition. The number of exponent tuples satisfies
$$
\prod_k d_k\le\prod_k p_k^{f_k}\le |A_2|,
$$
since $d_k\mid p_k^{f_k}-1$ when $d_k>1$ and the total degree of the occurrences at each prime is the dimension of that prime's head. Encode each exponent tuple as a hyperedge on the binary place vertices of its individual coordinates. Give each position one marker vertex, even if its order is $1$, and tie it to its place vertices by a block edge. Colors on block edges and on marker and place vertices enforce only the desired position permutations, with place numbers fixed. The exponent-tuple edges carry their height vector as an edge color. Consequently color-preserving hypergraph isomorphisms are exactly the allowed position permutations. The number of vertices is
$$
\sum_k(1+\lceil\log_2 d_k\rceil)=O(\log |G_2|).
$$
Luks's singly exponential colored hypergraph isomorphism algorithm therefore applies in polynomial table time. It returns an isomorphism and the transporter coset, or these can be recovered from its decision version by successive individualizations and stabilizer-orbit queries. Its coset size counts the position matchings. Dividing by the product of factorials of repeated identical tagged target representatives counts the distinct height-preserving span maps, since for each span map only those copy permutations remain free. [Luks, Hypergraph Isomorphism and Structural Equivalence of Boolean Functions](https://doi.org/10.1145/301250.301427)

## 4. Extend the span map and count its extensions

For the split-on-complement assertion, put $Y_q=D_{1,q}$. An injection is split if and only if it embeds $D_{2,q}$ as a direct summand at each prime. Test by cyclic invariant factors whether $D_2$ is isomorphic to a direct summand of $Y$, and construct a fixed such embedding $J:D_2\hookrightarrow Y$. For the homocyclic-target assertion, write $D_{2,q}=(\mathbb Z/q^{e_q})^{r_q}$ when $r_q>0$, and use $Y_q=D_{1,q}[q^{e_q}]$, or $Y_q=0$ when $r_q=0$. Every injection from $D_{2,q}$ into $D_{1,q}$ has image in $Y_q$ and it splits there. In fact $Y_q=(\mathbb Z/q^{e_q})^{s_q}\oplus L_q$ where $L_q$ has exponent dividing $q^{e_q-1}$. Project the injection to the first summand and reduce its matrix modulo $q$. A nonzero vector in the kernel of this reduction, multiplied by $q^{e_q-1}$, would contradict injectivity. Full column rank modulo $q$ gives an invertible square minor and hence a left inverse over $\mathbb Z/q^{e_q}$. This also proves the necessity and sufficiency of $r_q\le s_q$. Choose $J$ as above in $Y$. The split-on-complement algorithm thus counts the injections on the homocyclic-target inputs by using this smaller ambient group at each prime.

An isomorphism between subgroups of a finite abelian $q$-group extends to an automorphism precisely when it preserves heights in that group. Apply this classical finite height-extension theorem to the computed span map, using $J$ to embed its target span in $Y$. Direct summand embeddings preserve heights. It follows that a span map passes the height-table test if and only if it extends to a split injection $D_2\hookrightarrow Y$. Any two split injections $D_2\hookrightarrow Y$ differ by an automorphism of $Y$, since their complementary factors have the same cyclic invariants. [Richman and Walker, Finite Direct Sums of Cyclic Valuated $p$-Groups](https://msp.org/pjm/1977/69-1/pjm-v69-n1-p09-p.pdf)

For a given height-preserving map on the target span $X\le D_2$, the number of extensions is therefore
$$
\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}(Y)_{(J(X))}|}{|\operatorname{Aut}(Y)_{(J(D_2))}|},
$$
with parentheses denoting pointwise stabilizers. These orders are computable in polynomial time without enumerating automorphisms. Fix a cyclic generating tuple of $Y$, and count the possible images of each next generator subject to fixing the designated subgroup pointwise and fixing the earlier generators. The orbit size is found by trying all table elements and checking that the resulting partial correspondence induces a height-preserving isomorphism of the generated subgroups. The check uses simultaneous closure of a tuple of pairs and compares the heights of all paired elements. The height-extension theorem makes this test exact. The product of the orbit sizes is the order by orbit-stabilizer. The same test extends an actual marked map to an automorphism in polynomial time by greedily choosing one feasible image at a time. Dualizing a resulting injection gives the complement map. It can also be reconstructed by checking pairings against a generating tuple of characters.

## 5. Multiply and sum the exact counts

For a candidate image multiset choose one compatible $\phi$. For each source isotypic character orbit at a Hall prime $p$, let $Q=p^f$ be its endomorphism-field size. The source dimension $a_t$ of allowed row support at height $t$ is the number of its copies at heights at least $t$. Process the target rows in decreasing height. When $b$ earlier rows have been chosen independently, their span is inside the current allowed support, so the number of choices for the next row of height $t$ is $Q^{a_t}-Q^b$. Reject if $a_t\le b$. The product computes the exact number of surjective equivariant filtered head maps. Multiply over all orbits and Hall primes.

Each equivariant filtered head map has the same number of equivariant lifts to $A_1\to A_2$, since head reduction is a surjective homomorphism onto the group of filtered intertwiners by Section 2. Compute the number of lifts by dividing the order of the full equivariant homomorphism group by the number of filtered head intertwiners. For the numerator, write a homomorphism in cyclic coordinates and impose the generator-order and equivariance equations as linear congruences. Smith normal form gives the solution count. The denominator is the product, over all allowed pairs of source and target simple copies of the same orbit, of the corresponding endomorphism-field orders. Any lift of a surjective head map is surjective.

For different complement maps giving the same candidate image multiset, the pulled-back target actions on $A_2$ have the same irreducible multiplicities at every head layer. They are isomorphic as actions on $A_2$. To see this, take an invertible filtered intertwiner of the heads by Maschke splitting and lift it by coprime averaging. The lift is surjective and is between equal-size groups, hence is an isomorphism. Thus each complement map from the same candidate has the same number of lifts and the same cocycle count. For each candidate multiply its number of span maps, its extension count, its number of surjective kernel maps and its $1$-cocycle count. The candidates partition the contributing complement maps because an injection fixes the exact image multiset of the canonical target representatives. This sum gives the exact answer. Use a compatible injection, a matching matrix and its averaged lift, and the zero $1$-cocycle to obtain a witness. The integer counts have polynomial bit length, bounded by the number of functions between the tables. All loops, linear algebra and transporter calls above are polynomial in the table lengths.

## Corollary for a homocyclic source Sylow

For a valid target Hall prime set $\pi$, the source preprocessing in Section 1 produces the abelian complement $B_1=(G_1/K)_{\mathrm{ab}}$, where $K$ is generated by the elements of $\pi$-supported order. At each complement prime $q$, it suffices that either $B_{1,q}$ or $B_{2,q}$ is homocyclic. Use the previous truncation when the target Sylow is homocyclic. When the source Sylow is homocyclic, use $Y_q=D_{1,q}$ and fix any injection $J_q:D_{2,q}\hookrightarrow Y_q$. Existence and construction of $J_q$ follow by comparing the target cyclic exponents and its number of summands with the common source exponent and its number of summands.

All injective images of an element of order $q^s$ have the same height in the homocyclic ambient group $Y_q$, since that height is the source exponent length minus $s$. In particular, every injection on the target Sylow, and every injection on the marked target span, agrees with $J_q$ on heights. In the height table, use the height of $J_q(\sum_k a_k\chi_{2,k})$ for this prime. The same height-extension theorem shows that every admissible span map extends and that $\operatorname{Aut}(Y_q)$ acts transitively on the injections. The coset counts and witness construction apply without further changes. This includes inputs with a nonhomocyclic target Sylow at some primes and a nonhomocyclic source Sylow at others.

## Scope and relationship to earlier work

The constructions combine the short-code approach to Hall extension algorithms with the height-extension criterion for finite abelian groups. The earlier elementary abelian complement counting argument works over a single prime field. The relation code here uses integer exponent tuples whose sizes are bounded by the actual simple constituents, preserves a common complement map across primes, and uses heights in a truncated dual group. The truncation handles nonsplit complement surjections onto an arbitrary product of homocyclic Sylows. [Elementary abelian complement counting](../GroupGraphTensorCrossovers/CoprimeEpimorphismCounts.md)

Matei and Suciu give recursive epimorphism-counting formulas for finite solvable targets using group cohomology. The algorithm here counts whole families of compatible complement surjections without listing them, which is needed for the polynomial Cayley-table bound. General complement pairs with neither homocyclicity condition, and general abelian Sylow towers of height greater than two, remain open here. No priority claim is made. [Matei and Suciu, Counting homomorphisms onto finite solvable groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0405122)
