# Polynomial Calculus for arbitrary matrix congruence in characteristic two

**Theorem.** Let $F$ have characteristic two, and let $A,B\in\operatorname{Mat}_n(F)$ be noncongruent over $\overline F$. The ordinary Polynomial Calculus system
$$
XAX^T=B,\qquad XY=YX=I
$$
has a refutation over $F$ of degree at most $\max(3,2n)$. If the transpose-pair quiver representations of $A,B$ over $\overline F$ have distinct indecomposable multiplicities $a,b$, the bound is $\max(3,2\cdot 2^{v_2(|a-b|)})$. If the multiplicities are all equal, degree three suffices. At bounded degree the expanded size is polynomial.

This treats the characteristic-two, single-matrix part of Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She Open Question 7.3 on geometric noncongruence. With the earlier odd-characteristic result and the characteristic-zero result, it supplies universal degree upper bounds over every coefficient field for both matrix problems in that question. The earlier explicit sparse-size lower bound specializes to characteristic two, so the resulting linear worst-case degree scale is sharp up to constants. Base-field noncongruence that disappears after extension concerns a different feasibility condition for plain PC.

## Linear intertwiners at degree three

Work initially over $K=\overline F$. Use the two-arrow quiver with both arrows from vertex 0 to vertex 1. Represent $A$ by $M_A=(A,A^T)$ and $B$ by $M_B=(B,B^T)$, with arrow order fixed. There is a contravariant linear duality $D$ on this representation category sending $(P,Q)$ to $(Q^T,P^T)$ and sending a morphism $(f_0,f_1)$ to $(f_1^T,f_0^T)$. Identify $D^2$ with the identity via evaluation. Both $M_A$ and $M_B$ thus carry identity self-dualities $s_A:M_A\to DM_A$, $s_B:M_B\to DM_B$.

Write $C=XAX^T-B$. The identity
$$
XA-BY^T=C Y^T-XA(YX-I)^T
$$
and its counterpart using $A^T,B^T,C^T$ derive the linear intertwiner constraints for $f=(Y^T,X):M_A\to M_B$ in degree three. The dual $D(f)=(X^T,Y):M_B\to M_A$ obeys the corresponding reverse constraints, which are transposes of the forward ones. The inverse equations of $f,D(f)$ are exactly original axioms or transposes of original axioms.

Fix a coefficient-linear projection $\pi$ from the space of all candidate forward node maps onto $\operatorname{Hom}(M_A,M_B)$. Its difference from the identity has coordinates in the span of the linear intertwiner equations. Hence we may derive all degree-one lines stating $f=\widetilde f:=\pi(f)$, and dually $D(f)=D(\widetilde f)$. Using these degree-one lines in the inverse equations derives actual quadratic identities
$$
D(\widetilde f)s_B\widetilde f=s_A,\qquad
\widetilde fD(\widetilde f)=I_{M_B}.
$$
In the second identity the dual objects are identified with the same underlying displayed representations. Every entry of both equations has a degree-three derivation, because the lines proving projection equality are already of degree one before multiplying. A constant linear function of any matrix entry constraint is a PC linear combination.

## Residual multiplicity forms

Work in the additive category generated by the finitely many indecomposable summands of $M_A,M_B$ and their duals. Its radical quotient can be described by picking one representative for each indecomposable class and deleting all blocks between distinct types and reducing same-type entries modulo the radical of that type's endomorphism ring. This is a category quotient: a composite $R\to S\to R$ between distinct indecomposable types cannot be a unit, as then $R$ would be a direct summand of $S$. Each indecomposable endomorphism ring is local by Fitting's lemma, and its residue division algebra is $K$ because $K$ is algebraically closed. Duality respects both the distinct-type blocks and the radicals, so it descends to the quotient.

A self-dual simple type $R$ has a residual isomorphism $\phi:R\to DR$. Its dual has the same source and target, so $D\phi=c\phi$ for some scalar with $c^2=1$, by applying $D$ twice. Thus $c=1$ in characteristic two. Consequently the $R$-block of a self-duality $s_M:M\to DM$ of a representation $M$ becomes an invertible symmetric matrix $H_M(R)$ on its multiplicity space. To see this in coordinates, identify each dual copy with $R$ via $\phi$, express the $R$-block of $s_M$ by its coefficient matrix, and use $D(s_M)=s_M$ for symmetry. Changing the chosen basis in the multiplicity space changes $H_M(R)$ by congruence and preserves the property of being alternating.

The block map $\widetilde f\mapsto U$ on the multiplicity space is linear. On the dual, $D(\widetilde f)$ is $U^T$ with the above identifications. Therefore applying the linear block quotient to $D(\widetilde f)s_B\widetilde f=s_A$ gives the available quadratic identities
$$
U^T H_B(R)U=H_A(R).
$$
The inverse morphism gives the reversed identities at the same degree. If one multiplicity form is alternating and the other is nonalternating, choose the direction with the alternating form on the left. Every diagonal entry of $U^T H_{\mathrm{alt}}U$ is the zero polynomial, including all off-diagonal cross terms by characteristic two. At least one diagonal entry of $H_{\mathrm{nonalt}}$ is a nonzero scalar. Its available coordinate equation gives a degree-three refutation.

## Why alternation completes the classification

Use Sergeichuk's normal form for one bilinear form over an algebraically closed field of characteristic two. It consists of singular Jordan blocks $J_\ell(0)$, nonsingular hyperbolic blocks
$$
H(J_t(\lambda))=\begin{pmatrix}0&J_t(\lambda)\\ I&0\end{pmatrix}
$$
with $\lambda\ne0$, and cosquare-root blocks $\Gamma_t$ with cosquare similar to $J_t(1)$ for odd $t$. The hyperbolic parameter is taken up to inversion of $\lambda$. For each odd $t$, the normal form contains either cosquare-root blocks $\Gamma_t$ or hyperbolic blocks $H(J_t(1))$, with the canonical rule excluding both at the same size. Their multiplicities together with the other blocks uniquely specify the congruence class.

The transpose-pair Kronecker data determine the singular blocks and the $\lambda\ne1$ hyperbolic blocks. For clarity, a singular $J_{2k}(0)$ splits by parity of its row and column indices into $(J_k(0),I)\oplus(I,J_k(0))$, up to equivalent arrow shifts. A singular $J_{2k+1}(0)$ splits into the opposite rectangular singular Kronecker pair of minimal index $k$. Thus each singular type is recognizable in the Kronecker list and contributes no regular component at a nonzero eigenvalue. A hyperbolic block at $\lambda\ne0$ contributes the Kronecker representation $(J_t(\lambda),I)$ and its dual, by ordering the two coordinate halves oppositely at the two vertices. The second block has reciprocal eigenvalue. A cosquare-root block $\Gamma_t$ contributes one copy of $R_t=(J_t(1),I)$, after normalizing the second invertible arrow to identity. These decompositions and uniqueness also follow from the Kronecker computation in Sergeichuk's proof.

The only remaining ambiguity given the Kronecker data is whether the self-dual residual type $R_t$, for odd $t$, comes from $\Gamma_t$ summands, or from $H(J_t(1))$ summands. For a cosquare-root summand, identity self-duality on its sole $R_t$ component gives a nonzero $1$-by-$1$ form, which is nonalternating. The residual form of a hyperbolic summand is alternating: the underlying quiver decomposes as $R_t\oplus D R_t$ with the standard identity self-duality exchanging the two opposite copies. In fact, in the original two coordinate halves, the first representation uses the second half at vertex 0 and first half at vertex 1, and the opposite representation uses the first half at vertex 0 and second half at vertex 1. Thus the identity from the original object to its dual has only off-diagonal type-copy blocks in this decomposition. Its residual symmetric matrix has zero diagonal. The orthogonal direct sum induces the direct sum on these residual forms. Consequently, with equal Kronecker data, a different congruence class forces a different alternation flag at some odd $t$, giving the degree-three refutation above.

## Differing multiplicities and descent

If the quiver representations have different multiplicities $a,b$ at some type, the same quotient applied to both inverse equations produces rectangular matrices $U,V$ of linear forms and available quadratic equations $UV=I_a,\ VU=I_b$. In characteristic two, put $r=2^{v_2(|a-b|)}$. The $r$-th elementary coefficient $D_r$, the sum of principal $r$-minors, satisfies $D_r(UV)=D_r(VU)$ identically by Cauchy–Binet. Substituting the two available inverse equations yields $\binom a r-\binom b r$ in degree at most $2r$, by telescoping each product of $r$ entries of degree two. The scalar is odd by the base-two binomial identity. Scale it to one. This is the previously established positive-characteristic multiplicity argument on the same quiver, now supplemented by the residual alternation test.

Finally fix the degree cutoff $D$. The span of ordinary PC lines of degree at most $D$ is obtained inside the finite-dimensional polynomial space of degree at most $D$. Start with the input axioms and repeatedly adjoin variable multiples of the intersection of the current span with the degree-$(D-1)$ subspace. The finite chain stabilizes. Spans and intersections commute with scalar extension, so membership of 1 over $K$ gives its membership over $F$ and a derivation with the same degree. At fixed $D$ the monomial space, the number of chain increases and its stored basis are polynomial in the coefficient input dimensions, giving polynomial expanded PC size.
