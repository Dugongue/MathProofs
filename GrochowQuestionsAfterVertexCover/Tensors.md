# A polynomial ordinary tensor for an entire signed étale extension spectrum

Fix a finite field of odd order q. Given two finite étale multiplication tables A and B in coherent bases over this field, one can produce a single pair of ordinary 3-tensors in deterministic polynomial time such that, for every positive m, the output pair is ordinarily isomorphic over the degree-m extension exactly when the input tables have a determinant-one transporter there. The same output pair works at every m. The target may leave the étale multiplication-table class. This is a restricted answer to Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang's Question 1.9. [Published question, §1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

## Recognizing the orientation case

Unequal dimensions reject. For an isotopy h(xy)=f(x)g(y), put u=f(1) and v=g(1). Both are units by surjectivity. Dividing h pointwise by uv gives an algebra isomorphism with determinant det(f)det(g)/det(h). Conversely, one algebra isomorphism can be used on all three wires. Thus ordinary and determinant-one transport are respectively algebra isomorphism and determinant-one algebra isomorphism.

Compute the two trace-pairing determinants in the input bases. If unequal, a determinant-one isomorphism is impossible over every extension. Emit, for instance, the pair consisting of a zero and a nonzero tensor of format (1,1,1). Otherwise any algebra isomorphism has determinant 1 or −1. An automorphism has determinant equal to its permutation sign on geometric primitive idempotents, since the determinant is unchanged on extending scalars to the algebraic closure.

Compute the Frobenius cycle lengths, the degrees of the component fields. For example, the dimensions of the fixed spaces of the j-th Frobenius for j from 1 to n are sums of gcd(j,d) over all cycle lengths d. Since gcd(j,d) is the sum of Euler's totient over common divisors, divisor inversion first recovers the counts of lengths divisible by each integer, then the exact multiplicities. All these computations take polynomially many field operations.

At an extension where ordinary isomorphism holds, either the powered cycle profile has an even cycle or two equal odd cycles, in which case an odd rotation or an odd block exchange allows either orientation, or all cycle lengths are distinct and odd. In the latter case no original cycle split, as a split produces equal lengths. Hence the two base cycle profiles already coincide, and m is coprime to the joint cycle order. A bijection intertwining the m-th powers intertwines the originals, by taking a multiplicative inverse of m modulo that order. In geometric idempotent coordinates the same bijections give the same determinant signs in the original frames, over either field.

Thus keep the original pair as ordinary tensors unless the base profiles coincide, their cycles are distinct and odd, and a base isomorphism has the wrong orientation. To implement that test one can construct a base isomorphism by decomposing into finite fields and matching the equal degrees. For fixed q, split recursively by nontrivial idempotents from the Frobenius-fixed subalgebra using all q possible values of a nonscalar element. In a field component construct an irreducible polynomial of its degree and find one root by deterministic fixed-characteristic finite-field factoring. The resulting power bases identify corresponding components and give an actual matrix whose determinant is evaluated. These operations are polynomial for fixed q. In the exceptional case the desired spectrum is exactly the union, over prime divisors p of the cycle lengths, of the multiples of p. An empty union rejects. The degrees are at most n, so trial division suffices. ## How to encode the disjunction

For each such prime p take the ordinary algebra pair consisting of the degree-p field and the split algebra of dimension p. Over the degree-m extension these are isomorphic exactly when p divides m. Use a balanced binary tree of tests, repeating one test to pad to the next power of two if necessary. The following operation implements OR on tests of object isomorphism. Parentheses are ordered pairs and braces are unordered multisets with multiplicity. For tests (A,B) and (C,D) the two output objects are

$$
\{(A,C),(B,D)\},\qquad \{(A,D),(B,C)\}.
$$

An isomorphism of their roots either fixes or swaps the two child positions. A position-preserving match requires C isomorphic to D. A swapping match requires A isomorphic to B. Either condition suffices, including when coincident branches provide both matches. Applying the operation to copied subtrees gives at most four times as many subtrees per level, hence O(k²) leaves for k divisibility tests. No tower of nested tensor reductions is needed.

Here is an explicit fixed-signature presentation for the whole tree. Use a sort W equal to the split coordinate algebra on its nodes with its multiplication. Give it three endomorphisms sending each child idempotent to its parent along, respectively, an unordered edge, a first-position edge, or a second-position edge, and to zero when the relevant edge is absent. Every W-isomorphism permutes primitive idempotents, so intertwining these maps is precisely rooted tree isomorphism with the required order data. Over every extension W remains split with exactly the same primitive idempotents.

Use a second sort V, the direct sum of the underlying algebras at the leaves. A bilinear action of W projects to these leaf summands. A bilinear multiplication on V is componentwise leaf multiplication. Intertwining the action forces each leaf summand onto the matched summand, and intertwining multiplication requires exactly an algebra isomorphism there. Conversely any matching with such leaf isomorphisms assembles by direct sum. All sorts have dimension polynomial in n. The two trees have the same framework and dimensions.

Apply the fixed-signature system-of-tensors reduction once. Its block construction inserts the original entries in prescribed subarrays and uses field-independent zero and identity blocks. The placement and sizes depend on dimensions and the fixed signature. Thus extending the constructed tensors produces the same arrays as running the construction after extending the input. The reduction theorem over the extension applies to that same pair. ## Consequence

The same construction gives a field-extension-compatible disjunction for k independent ordinary tensor tests of arbitrary common arity three, where each individual test has matched formats. Replace the leaf algebra sort by three W-modules and the leaf multiplications by their blockwise bimap between these modules. Using duals on the two input spaces identifies ordinary tensor equivalence with bimap isotopy. Copies of a leaf at different positions get independent transporters, as required for an OR of independent tests. Thus the disjunction construction itself works beyond étale algebras. Deciding *which disjunction* represents a determinant character was the step that required the étale hypothesis.

For distinct odd primes, reversing one basis vector in the product of the corresponding finite fields realizes the divisibility disjunction with signed tables of dimension the sum of the primes. If both ordinary outputs must themselves be étale multiplication tables, the earlier cycle-profile calculation gives exact minimum common dimension equal to their product times two to the power k−1. The tree above realizes that same spectrum with polynomial ordinary tensor size. This explains exactly what changing the target buys for this family. The argument relies on the Frobenius description of finite étale tables and supplies no test for arbitrary signed tensors. No novelty claim for the Boolean OR operation is needed.
