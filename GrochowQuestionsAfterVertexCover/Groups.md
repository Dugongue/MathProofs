# Exact epimorphisms of products of finite Heisenberg groups

For a concrete class of two-step groups, epimorphism testing and exact counting reduce to a weighted assignment of the source's field factors to the target's factors. The criterion works for abstract groups, including characteristic two, and has an exact counterpart for two-step Lie algebras. It yields polynomial time on Cayley tables and an NP-complete problem for explicitly given Lie bracket tables. At fixed symplectic half-rank one it also transfers the full Vertex Cover extension-degree spectrum to actual epimorphisms with strictly larger source.

This is a scoped result for Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar Open Question 3(3) and their counting Open Question 2. Question 3(3) asks separately about quotients of genus-one groups and inputs both of genus two, citing Lewis–Wilson and Brooksbank–Maglione–Wilson. Here the nonabelian direct factors have genus one, and the class spans arbitrary finite products of such factors with independently varying ranks and coefficient fields. Broader unpublished notes already give Cayley-table polynomial time at bounded coefficient-field genus. The exact assignment formula, the representation-dependent hardness and the scalar-extension result are the additions here. ## Exact assignment theorem

Fix a finite field $F$ of characteristic $p$. For a finite extension $K/F$ and integer $r\ge1$, let $V=K^r\oplus K^r$, $W=K$, and write $u=(x,y)$, $v=(x',y')$. Write

$$
\beta(u,v)=x\cdot y'-x'\cdot y.
$$

The corresponding Lie algebra is $V\oplus W$ with central bracket $\beta$. Its Heisenberg group has the same coordinate sets and product $(u,w)(v,z)=(u+v,w+z+x\cdot y')$. In either case the center equals the derived subobject and is $W$, and the quotient is $V$. The group center and quotient are elementary abelian. For $p=2$ the square map from the quotient to the center is $x\cdot y$.

Let the source have field and half-rank pairs $(K_i,r_i)_{i=1}^a$ and the target pairs $(L_j,s_j)_{j=1}^b$. Each side is a direct product of the Lie algebras or of the groups above. Put $d_i=[K_i:F]$ and $e_j=[L_j:F]$. There is a surjective Lie homomorphism over $F$ if and only if the following assignment exists. Assign each source index to a single target index or omit it. For every target $j$ require

$$
e_j\mid d_i\quad\text{for every }i\text{ assigned to }j,
\qquad
\sum_{i\text{ assigned to }j}r_i d_i/e_j=s_j.\tag{1}
$$

The same condition characterizes surjective abstract group homomorphisms. In this group assertion neither the homomorphism nor its maps on the two characteristic layers need be $F$-linear.

### Necessity

An epimorphism induces surjective linear maps $P:V_G\to V_H$ and $R:W_G\to W_H$ of the quotient and center, satisfying $\beta_H(Pu,Pv)=R\beta_G(u,v)$. For abstract groups use the prime field as the scalar field in this argument. Multiplying all extension degrees by $[F: \mathbb F_p]$ preserves the divisibility and ratios in (1).

The kernel of $P$ is the common radical of $R\beta_G$, because $P$ is onto and the target bimap has zero common radical. It is stable under the coordinatewise action of the source algebra $A=\prod_iK_i$ by the balancing identity $\beta_G(au,v)=\beta_G(u,av)$. This induces on the target quotient a self-adjoint action with respect to the target bimap. Every such self-adjoint map $T$ is linear over the target scalar algebra $B=\prod_j L_j$. Indeed, for a target scalar $c$,

$$
\beta_H(T(cu),v)=\beta_H(cu,Tv)=c\beta_H(u,Tv)=\beta_H(cT(u),v),
$$

so equality follows by nondegeneracy. Thus the source scalar idempotents give an orthogonal direct-sum decomposition of $V_H$ into $B$-modules equal to the images of the source quotient factors. Each such image itself decomposes into its target-field components, and the restriction of the target form on each nonzero component is nondegenerate, by orthogonality and nondegeneracy of the whole.

Write $R_{ji}$ for the restriction followed by projection $K_i\to L_j$, and $P_{ji}$ for the matching projection on quotient factors. Since the entire $j$-th quotient factor is covered, if $R_{ji}=0$ then $P_{ji}=0$. If $R_{ji}\ne0$, then $R_{ji}\beta_i$ has zero radical, since for each nonzero $u$ the set $\beta_i(u,V_i)$ is all of $K_i$. Consequently $P_{ji}$ is injective. A source factor cannot have nonzero images in two target factors: its image is a $B$-module, so it is the direct sum of its component projections, whereas injectivity on either component prevents such a decomposition with two nonzero summands. This proves the required factor assignment, with the unassigned factors killed on both characteristic layers.

For one assigned pair, abbreviate its fields to $K,L$, and write $R:K\to L$ for the nonzero output map. The map $P$ identifies the source quotient factor with its nondegenerate image in the target quotient factor. For each $t\in L$, transport scalar multiplication by $t$ on this image back to an operator $S_t$ on the source quotient. It satisfies

$$
R\beta_K(S_tu,v)=tR\beta_K(u,v)=R\beta_K(u,S_tv).
$$

It commutes with every scalar of $K$, since for any $a\in K$ balancing gives

$$
R\beta_K(S_t(au),v)=tR\beta_K(u,av)=R\beta_K(aS_tu,v),
$$

and the compressed form has zero common radical. Pick $u,v$ with $\beta_K(u,v)=1$ and apply the first identity to $au,v$. Then $\iota(t)=\beta_K(S_tu,v)$ satisfies $R(a\iota(t))=tR(a)$ for all $a\in K$. This scalar is unique, since $z\mapsto(a\mapsto R(az))$ is injective over a field whenever $R\ne0$. The same identity and uniqueness show that $\iota$ is a unital field embedding over the scalar field in use. They also show that $R$ is $L$-linear under that embedding. Finally $S_t$ is exactly multiplication by $\iota(t)$, by nondegeneracy of the compressed form. Thus the dimension of this image over $L$ is $2r[K:L]$, which gives (1) after summing. No diagonalizability or characteristic assumption is needed in this argument.

### Sufficiency and witnesses

For each assigned pair choose an embedding of $L_j$ into $K_i$, which exists by divisibility, and choose any nonzero $L_j$-linear functional $R_i:K_i\to L_j$. The pairing $(x,y)\mapsto R_i(xy)$ is nondegenerate. Choose a basis of $K_i$ over $L_j$ on the $x$-coordinates and its dual basis on the $y$-coordinates. In these bases the bilinear cocycle $R_i(x\cdot y')$ is a standard dot product over $L_j$. Concatenate all these coordinate blocks for each target and use (1) to fill its quotient exactly. Send the source centers to the sum of the chosen $R_i$'s in each target center. This directly preserves the group cocycle and therefore constructs an actual group epimorphism, including in characteristic two. It also preserves the associated alternating brackets, giving the claimed Lie epimorphism. Omitted source factors map to the identity. For Cayley-table output all coordinates can be materialized within polynomial table cost.

## Complexity and exact counting

When the parameters are supplied, (1) is decided by a subset dynamic program. For each target enumerate the subsets of sources satisfying its divisibility and exact capacity. Process the targets, recording the set of already used sources. The total number of disjoint used-set and next-set combinations per layer is at most $3^a$; leftover sources are omitted. The same dynamic program reconstructs assignments. In a Cayley group $a\le\log_2|G|$, so this is polynomial in the full table length.

For a promised raw Cayley table or raw Lie bracket table, the parameters follow by computing the centroid of the commutator bimap. The centroid consists of pairs $(T,S)$ with $\beta(Tu,v)=\beta(u,Tv)=S\beta(u,v)$. On a single symplectic factor it is exactly its coefficient field. Indeed every self-adjoint $T$ is field-linear by the argument above, and evaluating a pair with bracket one shows that $S$ acts by multiplication by a field scalar. Nondegeneracy then forces $T$ to have that same scalar. On a product, testing arguments supported in different factors makes every off-diagonal block vanish. Thus one extracts the field factors by finite étale linear algebra and recovers the half-ranks from their quotient dimensions. For group inputs the additive layers and commutator map are accessible by table lookups. For raw Lie brackets this gives recognition: require center equal to derived and a finite étale centroid, then check each factor's output rank one and its nondegenerate alternating input form. Such a form has a symplectic basis found by splitting off planes. This proof uses the published centroid background and no assumed polynomial algorithm for the general group epimorphism problem. [Lewis–Wilson](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.5466) · [Grochow–Qiao](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol200-ccc2021/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.16/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.16.pdf)

Raw Cayley tables can also be recognized in polynomial time. In addition to the commutator checks require elementary abelian characteristic layers and, in odd characteristic, group exponent dividing $p$. In characteristic two, require that on the quotient module of each centroid field the square map lands in its corresponding center line, is homogeneous of degree two over the field, and is split as a quadratic form. The first two requirements are checked by scans through the table elements and field scalars. Distinct factors are orthogonal, so the square map splits across them by the group square identity. To test the split condition, repeatedly find a nonzero isotropic vector and an isotropic partner pairing to one by scanning vectors, then pass to the orthogonal complement. A split form remains split after deleting a hyperbolic plane by Witt cancellation. A successful sequence supplies a hyperbolic basis, and failure rules out such a basis. Given a compatible map on the characteristic layers, choose an ordered prime-field basis of the source quotient and a lift in the group of each basis element. Each source element has a unique ordered product of lifted basis powers times a central element. Send the lifts to arbitrary lifts of their target quotient images and the central element through the chosen center map. Multiplication in these normal forms depends only on the central commutators and the $p$-th powers of the basis lifts. The prescribed maps respect those relations because they respect brackets and, when $p=2$, squares. This constructs the group map on raw labels and also constructs adapters to the coordinate presentations on recognized inputs.

There is an exact weighted version. For a pair of fields with $[L_j:F]=e_j\mid[K_i:F]$, there are $e_j$ embeddings of $L_j$ into $K_i$ over $F$, and $|K_i|-1$ nonzero $L_j$-linear output functionals for each embedding. Distinct embeddings cannot give the same functional, by the uniqueness argument above. Fix an admissible assignment and choices of these functionals. In target factor $j$, the maps on the quotient are precisely the symplectic isometries from the source trace-compressed direct sum to the standard target space. Their number is $|\operatorname{Sp}_{2s_j}(L_j)|$. An arbitrary linear correction from the entire source quotient to the target center is then allowed for a Lie homomorphism. It gives a factor of $|W_H|^{\dim_F V_G}$, and surjectivity follows automatically from that on the two layers. Thus one can replace each transition by the product of $e_j(|K_i|-1)$ over the selected subset and multiply the final sum by the symplectic and central factors. For finite fields, the symplectic group orders are computed by counting ordered symplectic bases, with recursion $|\operatorname{Sp}_{2s}(L)|=(|L|^{2s}-1)|L|^{2s-1}|\operatorname{Sp}_{2s-2}(L)|$. This is an exact counting result for the Lie algebras and, over the prime field with odd $p$, for the abstract groups as well. The latter follows also by replacing $w$ with $w-(x\cdot y)/2$ in the group coordinates, yielding the standard alternating-cocycle group law.

For $p=2$, a group epimorphism must preserve in addition the square map on the quotient. Every compressed square form is the split form in the dual bases above. The same counting formula now uses $|\operatorname O^+_{2s_j}(L_j)|$ instead of the symplectic order. To check that all these possible maps lift, let $c$ be the dot-product cocycle and let $c'(u,v)=c_H(Pu,Pv)-R c_G(u,v)$. The bracket and square tests make $c'$ a symmetric alternating bilinear map over the prime field. In a basis $b_i$ of the source additive quotient, the function $\lambda(\sum x_i b_i)=\sum_{i<j}x_i x_j c'(b_i,b_j)$ has polar form $c'$, so adding it in the center gives a lift. All other lifts differ by an additive map to the center. The split orthogonal groups have standard product-order formula, or can be counted by the recursion obtained by choosing an ordered hyperbolic pair. Over a field of size $Q$ there are $(Q^s-1)(Q^{s-1}+1)$ choices of first nonzero isotropic vector, by counting solutions of $x\cdot y=0$. After choosing the first vector there are $Q^{2s-2}$ second isotropic vectors that pair to one with it. Split off this hyperbolic plane and recurse on the split complement. For group counts use absolute, prime-field extension degrees in the weights and central factor. The weights and final counts have polynomial bit length in the Cayley-table input.

By contrast, given explicit **Lie bracket coordinates** with no group table expansion, epimorphism testing is NP-complete at each fixed base field. For a strongly NP-complete 3-Partition instance with positive integers $r_1,\ldots,r_{3b}$ summing to $bB$ and all strictly between $B/4$ and $B/2$, let all source and target fields be the base field. Take those source half-ranks and $b$ target half-ranks equal to $B$. Then no source can be omitted, and (1) is exactly 3-Partition. Unary sizes can be assumed, so the full bracket coordinate arrays are polynomial size. A linear surjection matrix and bracket checks give an NP verifier even on unrestricted bracket inputs. The same hardness and membership hold for abstract groups **when supplied as lists of the field degrees and symplectic ranks**, by (1). This separation uses two different input representations. [Garey–Johnson on strong NP-completeness](https://doi.org/10.1145/322077.322090)

## Strict extension-degree epimorphisms and relation to the published question

Assume every half-rank is one. Over a finite scalar extension, (1) forces each target factor to receive precisely one source factor of the same relative field degree. Thus an epimorphism holds exactly when the powered target cycle profile is a submultiset of the powered source profile. Frobenius powering replaces each original $d$-cycle by $\gcd(d,m)$ cycles of length $d/\gcd(d,m)$ in degree $m$. The comparison uses polynomial time in the input and the bit length of $m$, without constructing the extension. The argument applies directly to class-two Lie algebras and to the abstract groups over the powered factors, since absolute degrees have the same divisibility ratios.

For strict unequal dimensions with the same hardness, start with the equal-dimensional finite étale pair from the Vertex Cover spectrum and double every factor multiplicity on both sides. Add one degree-one field factor on the source only. At a successful degree the extra factor is simply unused. At an unsuccessful degree, the original source profile has a deficit in at least one length, because the profiles have equal total dimension and differ. Doubling makes that deficit at least two, so adding one length-one cycle cannot repair it. Therefore the new source surjects onto the target at exactly the Vertex Cover degrees. In particular the least degree is hard to approximate within any fixed factor, and the strict input pair is never isomorphic. The source and target families are given succinctly by the base factor lists or compact coordinate tables. This result concerns selecting an extension parameter, and its pointwise verification is uniform polynomial time.

The source paper's unrestricted genus-two clause for full Cayley tables remains outside the proved scope. The fixed-field complexity contrast above concerns compact presentations, while its exact assignment and counting algorithms apply to the stated product class in the original full-table model.
