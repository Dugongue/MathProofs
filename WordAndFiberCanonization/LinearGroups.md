# Intrinsic word normal forms in a fixed integral matrix group

## Statement

There is an effectively specified finitely generated subgroup H of the direct product of two rank-two free groups, hence of SL₄(ℤ), with the following properties. Fix the finite symmetric generating alphabet constructed below. A normal form outputs a word in this alphabet representing the input element and gives identical outputs on equal elements. The input is always an explicit word in H's generators.

1. Word equality has a deterministic logspace complete invariant.
2. H has a deterministic polynomial-time word normal form if and only if P = NP. It has a deterministic logspace word normal form if and only if L = NP.
3. For every fixed ε with 0 < ε < 1, approximating intrinsic word length within a factor of m^(1−ε) on an m-letter input is NP-hard. Here an estimate s must obey OPT ≤ s ≤ m^(1−ε) OPT, where OPT is the shortest representative length. The same hardness holds for algorithms outputting representative words within that factor.

Consequently, a finitely generated linear group with no logspace word normal form exists if and only if L differs from NP. This characterizes the existence question in Fortnow–Grochow §6.1. It leaves the actual separation of those complexity classes open. Seeing the two reduced ambient coordinates as a complete invariant is essential: the normal form must be expressible over H's own generators. No finite presentation is asserted for H. The proofs below are informal. [Fortnow–Grochow, §6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/0907.4775v2#S6.SS1)

## The source group

Fix 3SAT with a usual binary encoding and logspace many-one completeness. Take the Sapir–Birget–Rips construction for a fixed nondeterministic 3SAT machine with polynomial running time. Choose a polynomial clock with superadditive fourth power as required in their theorem, padding the computation to a fixed polynomial schedule. Their construction gives a finite presentation Q = ⟨X | R⟩ with polynomial Dehn function, and a map sending a formula to a word w that is trivial in Q exactly when the formula is satisfiable. The polynomial Dehn bound means that a null word of length n admits a planar proof diagram with polynomially many relator cells in n. [Sapir–Birget–Rips, Theorem 1.3](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/156-2/p01)

We use the actual word map for its space bound. Section 13 identifies the output as K(σ(c)), for the initial configuration c on the given formula. This configuration consists of the input and fixed state and tape markers. The displayed definition of σ copies the contents of a fixed number of tapes and inserts fixed markers and unary runs whose lengths are tape lengths or their sum. Formula (27) defines K by a fixed list of forward and inverse copies punctuated by fixed letters. Each output position can be produced by scanning the input with logarithmic counters, including reverse scans for inverse copies. Thus the reduction is in FL and has linear output length. This conclusion follows from the formulas; linear running time alone would be insufficient. [Definition of σ](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9811105#page=48) · [Formula (27)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9811105#page=57) · [Section 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9811105#page=104)

## An exact relative area identity

Adjoin letters z,t to X and use the finite presentation

    P = ⟨X,z,t | R, [x,z] for x in X, t z t⁻¹ z⁻²⟩.

Use [a,b] = aba⁻¹b⁻¹. For a word w in X and its inverses and an integer k ≥ 0, put N = 2^k, u = t^k z t^(−k), and W = [w,u]. This word is always null in P: u equals z^N and w commutes with z. Among all expressions for W in the **free group** as a product of conjugates of defining relators and their inverses, the least number of factors of type [x,z] is **exactly N times the word length of w in Q**. Conjugating words and factors of the other relator types are unrestricted.

For the upper bound, use Q-relators to replace w by a geodesic word for its Q-element in the two occurrences. Expand u to z^N by the doubling relator. Commuting each of N letters through the geodesic requires just one square per letter of that geodesic, using an inverse conjugate for a negatively signed letter. The resulting word freely cancels.

For the lower bound let B be the free product of Q and the infinite cyclic group generated by t. Use the left-translation action of B on the direct sum of integer lamps at its elements, with e_g the unit lamp at g. Map the free group on X,z,t into the semidirect product of the lamp group by B. Send X and t to their corresponding top-group elements and z to e₁. Each defining relator maps into the abelian lamp group. A conjugation of a lamp translates it by the top coordinate of its conjugator.

Let h(g) be the t-exponent sum of g in B. Let b(g) be the intrinsic length in Q of the initial Q syllable in the reduced free-product form of g, with value zero when g is the identity or starts with a t syllable. Define a rational-valued additive functional on finitely supported lamps by λ(e_g) = 2^h(g) b(g).

* Right multiplication by t preserves b(g), including cancellation of a terminal t syllable, and increases h by one. Therefore λ(e_gt) = 2λ(e_g). The doubling relator maps to e_t − 2e₁ and every translate of this difference has value zero. Every Q-relator maps to the zero lamp.
* For each x in X, the [x,z] relator maps to e_x − e₁. Right multiplication by x can change the initial Q syllable only at g in Q. At such g the height is zero and the Q word length changes by at most one. Hence |λ(e_gx − e_g)| ≤ 1 for every g, also bounding inverse relators.
* Writing q for the Q-element of w, the word W maps to e_(qt^k) − e_(t^k). Its value is N times the intrinsic length of q, including when q is trivial.

Evaluating any relator expression for W now proves the lower bound. The functional counts cells without any algorithm for computing word lengths in Q.

## From cells to intrinsic words

Let F be the free group on X,z,t. Inside F × F, generate H by the diagonal elements Δ(a) = (a,a) for each free generator a, and by ρ(r) = (r,1) for the finitely many defining relators r of P. These are the usual generators of the fiber product over P. In fact diagonal words conjugate ρ(r) by any desired free word; all pairs in the fiber product have the form (v,1)Δ(b) with v in the normal closure of the relators. This construction uses only a fixed finite set of ambient words.

In any H-word representing (W,1), move all diagonal letters to the right. The final diagonal word freely represents the identity, by the second coordinate. Its first coordinate therefore expresses W as a product of conjugates of relators with one factor per original relator letter. The area identity forces at least N relator letters whenever w is nontrivial in Q.

Conversely, if w is null in Q and n = |w|, then (w,1) has an H-word v of length polynomial in n. To see the witness-length bound, take a diagram with A = poly(n) cells. Its one-skeleton has O(n+A) edges after removing gratuitous spurs, since every relator has bounded perimeter. A spanning tree and the associated dual-tree face traversal express the boundary word as A conjugates of signed Q-relators, with base paths of length O(n+A) per face. Equivalently, cut the diagram open along a spanning tree and read the based face loops so interior edges cancel. Cyclic rotations require only bounded extra conjugators. Replacing each conjugating letter by its diagonal lift gives a word of length O(A(n+A+1)+n). Consequently

    v Δ(u) v⁻¹ Δ(u)⁻¹

is an H-word for (W,1) of length at most poly(n) + O(k). Only existence is needed on satisfiable inputs. The diagram-to-word bound also follows directly from the stronger linear traversal bound in equation (22) of [Myasnikov–Nikolaev–Ushakov, §7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.5671#page=27).

The two reduced coordinates in F form a logspace complete invariant of H-words. Substitute the fixed words for the generators, and compute freely reduced words in logspace. To obtain the asserted dimension, embed the fixed finite-rank F in a rank-two free group with free generators a,b by mapping distinct basis letters to distinct conjugates a^i b a^(−i). A rank-two free group embeds in SL₂(ℤ), for example via the standard two unipotent generators with off-diagonal entry 2. Putting the two representations on diagonal blocks embeds H in SL₄(ℤ). In particular H is torsion-free. [Logspace normal forms for free groups, Proposition 1](https://cs.hofstra.edu/~cscgzo/Research/logspaceNormalForms.pdf#page=3)

## An explicit short fallback on every input

The reduction needs a word over H's generators even when w is nontrivial in Q. We describe a logspace-enumerable word of length O((n+1)N + k² + n) for (W,1). Write A = u z^(−N) in F and r = t z t⁻¹ z⁻². For j = 0 through k−1 put h = 2^j and l = k−j−1. The difference between successive doubling stages, as a product of a stage word and the inverse of the next, is

    t^l ( ∏_{i=0}^{h−1} z^(2i) r z^(−2i) ) t^(−l).

The product is in increasing i order. This follows by writing t z^h t⁻¹ as (tzt⁻¹)^h and telescoping. The stages themselves telescope to A. Lift r to ρ(r) and the conjugators diagonally. Within one stage cancel adjacent powers in the conjugators: the inner product becomes ρ(r) followed by h−1 copies of Δ(z²)ρ(r), followed by Δ(z^(−2(h−1))). The result has length O(h+l+1). Over all stages this gives an H-word for (A,1) of length O(N+k²).

The identity [w,u] = (wAw⁻¹)[w,z^N]A⁻¹ leaves the middle factor. First produce ([w,z],1) by repeatedly applying [ab,z] = a[b,z]a⁻¹[a,z]. For w = x₁⋯x_n, this visits letters in decreasing order of position i and uses the prefix ending at i−1 as conjugator. As these prefixes shrink by one letter per iteration, their adjacent diagonal words telescope, giving length O(n). For a negative letter use [x⁻¹,z] = x⁻¹[x,z]⁻¹x. Next write [w,z^N] as the product of z^j[w,z]z^(−j) for increasing j from 0 through N−1. Telescoping successive diagonal powers gives length O((n+1)N). Combine the factors using the identity for [w,u].

For k = d ceil(log₂(n+2)) with d fixed, a counter bounded by N needs O(log(n+2)) bits. The lists above use a fixed number of counters. Prefix changes are individual input letters, the repeated powers are output by loops, and inverse subwords use the reversed loops and inverted letters. Thus the literal H-word can be generated in FL, with the claimed length, without an oracle or knowing the truth of the source instance.

## Consequences

Suppose a deterministic polynomial-time canonizer of H exists, with output length bounded by a fixed polynomial. Choose the constant d *after* this bound and the fixed polynomial bound for the short word v have been fixed. For large n, N exceeds the canonizer's output bound on the short word of length poly(n)+O(d log n). By consistency it must produce the same short output on the explicit fallback word when w is null. When w is nontrivial, every valid H output has length at least N. Comparing the resulting output length with N decides the NP-complete word problem in polynomial time. Finitely many small input lengths can be handled separately. If the canonizer is in FL, the whole decision, including generating virtual input positions by replay and counting output letters, is in L. The reduction to w above is in FL as well, giving L = NP.

For the converse, every polynomial-time decidable equivalence relation has a shortlex-first representative computable in polynomial time with an NP oracle. Test for shorter members and extend candidate prefixes with NP existence queries, using length at most the given input. Under P = NP this gives an FP normal form. Under L = NP every output bit and the output length predicates, all in P with an NP oracle, are in L. A logspace transducer can emit the representative by querying the bits separately. This gives the asserted equivalences.

Fix 0 < ε < 1 for the approximation assertion. For k as above, the fallback's length m is at most O((n+1)N+k²+n). The intrinsic length on a yes-input is bounded by a fixed polynomial in n plus O(k). For a sufficiently large constant d depending on ε and the polynomial degree, m^(1−ε) times this bound is strictly less than N. On a no-input even the optimum is at least N. The fallback and threshold give a polynomial-time gap reduction, in fact a logspace reduction for each fixed ε. A simple baseline returns the length of the supplied word, after testing for identity to handle OPT = 0. This gives an m-factor upper estimate on all inputs. Related prior work proves NP-completeness for bounded subgroup membership in a fixed subgroup of a product of free groups. That problem supplies an ambient word; here every input is supplied directly in the subgroup alphabet. [Myasnikov–Nikolaev–Ushakov, *Knapsack Problems in Groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.5671)

**Subgroups of products of free groups.** For a fixed finite product of finite-rank free groups, every finitely generated subgroup has a logspace normal form if and only if at most one factor is nonabelian or L = NP. With at least two nonabelian factors the product contains our H. With at most one it has the form F_s × ℤ^a. Any finitely generated subgroup projects onto a finitely generated free group, and its kernel in the abelian factor is central and finitely generated free abelian. Lifting a free basis gives a section. The subgroup is itself the direct product of a finite-rank free group and a finite-rank free abelian group, which has a logspace normal form. For a fixed subgroup, translations between fixed finite generating sets are fixed substitutions. The same dichotomy holds for polynomial-time normal forms with P = NP in place of L = NP.

**Two published questions.** Every fixed finitely generated linear group has word equality in L. Accordingly, L = NP suffices to give all of them FL normal forms, whereas our particular subgroup witnesses the failure if L differs from NP. Also, by Elder–Elston–Ostheimer's definition, H is logspace embeddable since its ambient product of free groups has a logspace normal form. All logspace-embeddable groups have logspace word equality. Their question whether the class of logspace-embeddable groups strictly contains the class with intrinsic logspace normal forms therefore has an affirmative answer exactly when L differs from NP. These are equivalences with an open separation. The construction and the near-linear-power gap are unconditional. Publication priority for the combined results has not been established. [Fortnow–Grochow §6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/0907.4775v2#S6.SS1) · [Elder–Elston–Ostheimer, introduction and §9](https://cs.hofstra.edu/~cscgzo/Research/logspaceNormalForms.pdf#page=18)
