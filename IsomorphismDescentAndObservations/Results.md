# Extension spectra, determinant observations, and comparison capacity

These are informal proofs about three resources used in constrained isomorphism reductions: extension fields, marked objects, and batches of comparisons. The tensor applications concern ordinary tensor isomorphism (TI) and special-linear tensor isomorphism (SL-TI). Over the base field F₃ a positive determinant sign is equivalent to determinant one. In the first theorem, SL-TI means literal determinant one in every mode over each extension field. The packet does not establish an unrestricted reduction from SL-TI to TI. [Previous extension, quotient, and packing theorems](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ConstrainedIsomorphismTheorems/Results.md)

## 1. Every admissible extension spectrum occurs on multiplication tensors

For a pair of tensors defined over F₃, its extension spectrum is the set of positive integers f at which the pair is isomorphic over F_(3^f), for the specified acting group. For ordinary TI and for exact SL-TI the possible spectra are exactly the finite unions of sets dℕ_{>0}, including the empty union. Moreover every such spectrum occurs simultaneously for ordinary TI and exact SL-TI on a single pair of concise multiplication tensors.

For the upper direction, take the geometric transporter as a reduced scheme. If nonempty, it is a torsor under the geometric stabilizer. Its finitely many connected components are permuted by Frobenius. A transporter over F_(3^f) fixes its component under Frob^f. Conversely, on an Frob^f-stable component choose a geometric transporter t and write Frob^f(t)=tu for u in the connected stabilizer. Lang's theorem for the connected stabilizer gives h with u Frob^f(h)=h, so th is an extension-field transporter. A permutation has a fixed point under its f-th power precisely when some cycle length divides f. The same argument applies with either GL or SL in each mode. [Lang, Algebraic Groups over Finite Fields](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Lang-Algebraic_Groups_Over_Finite_Fields.pdf)

For the converse, let U be a nonempty union generated by d₁,…,d_s and let M be their least common multiple. Put F(f)=0 on U and F(f)=M off U. This function depends only on gcd(f,M). For each m dividing M put

```text
c_m = (1/m) Σ_{e | m} μ(m/e) F(e).
```

These are integers because all F(e) are multiples of M. Divisor Möbius inversion gives F(f)=Σ_{m | gcd(f,M)} m c_m for every f. Form two étale algebras A₊ and A₋ over F₃ using respectively max(c_m,0) and max(−c_m,0) copies of F_(3^m). Their dimensions are equal because F(M)=0. A common identical field factor may be added to make both nonzero.

The field factor F_(3^m) supplies an m-cycle of Frobenius on the geometric idempotents. The signed difference of its fixed counts after powering, summed over the two algebras, is F(f). Over degree f the two algebras are isomorphic precisely when Frobenius raised to f has the same cycle type on both. Cycle type is recovered from the fixed counts of all powers by divisor Möbius inversion. Thus equivalence at f holds exactly when F(fk)=0 for all k≥1. This is equivalent to f∈U by upward closure under multiplication, taking k=1 for the reverse implication.

Use the multiplication tensors of M₂(A₊) and M₂(A₋). If invertible linear maps P,Q,R give an isotopy P(x)Q(y)=R(xy) over an extension, then a=P(1) and b=Q(1) are units. Indeed R(x)=P(x)b and R(y)=aQ(y) give bijective right and left multiplication by b and a. The map φ(x)=a⁻¹P(x)=Q(x)b⁻¹ satisfies φ(xy)=φ(x)φ(y) and sends 1 to 1. In particular any ordinary TI witness induces an algebra isomorphism and hence a center isomorphism.

Conversely any center isomorphism φ extends entrywise. In the fixed base-field bases let G₊ and G₋ be the Gram matrices of the nondegenerate trace pairings of the centers. Preservation gives (det φ)²=det G₊/det G₋∈F₃×. In ordered matrix-entry bases the extended map has determinant (det φ)⁴=1. Applying it in all three roles gives an exact SL witness, with determinant one also on the dual modes. Conciseness of multiplication follows from the identity element. For the empty spectrum use the multiplication tensors of M₂(F₃) and F₃⁴. These have the same dimensions and unequal center dimensions over every extension.

### Consequences for uniform descent and realization size

A fixed collection E of probe degrees ≥2 suffices to descend every ordinary or exact-SL tensor comparison to degree one, in all dimensions, exactly when for every finite set P of primes some f∈E is coprime to the product of P. Indeed a spectrum missing degree one has generators >1, and choosing one prime from each generator gives a finite prime set meeting every successful degree. Conversely if P meets every probe degree, the union of pℕ_{>0} over p∈P furnishes a counterexample. In particular every finite fixed list fails. The degrees 3 and 5 can both succeed on one raw tensor pair whose base comparison fails. This construction makes no polynomial-size claim in the bit length of the input divisibility pattern.

Even with ordinary TI promised over the base field, both degree 3 and degree 5 can give exact SL witnesses while the base exact SL comparison fails. Let T be the multiplication tensor of F_(3^15), and let T' be T reflected in the output mode by a matrix S of determinant −1. Over K=F_(3^f), the algebra splits as d=gcd(f,15) copies of the degree-15/d extension of K. The unit calculation above writes every self-isotopy as (L_a φ,L_b φ,L_(ab) φ), with units a,b, multiplication maps L, and an algebra automorphism φ. The product of field norms on this étale algebra is onto K×, so for each φ one can choose a,b making the first two determinants one. The third is then (det φ)⁻¹. A transporter to T' multiplies this third determinant by −1. Each automorphism of an individual field factor has odd order dividing 15/d and determinant one in a normal basis. If d=1 no automorphism has determinant −1. If d>1 swapping two factors has determinant −1 because the factor dimension 15/d is odd. Hence the exact SL spectrum is precisely 3ℕ_{>0}∪5ℕ_{>0}.

For the étale realization itself, the minimum common dimension has an exact finite integer description. Take M to be the least common multiple of the divisibility-minimal generators of a nonempty U other than all degrees. Among integers c_m indexed by m|M, minimize (1/2)Σ_{m|M} m|c_m| subject to Σ_{m|g} m c_m=0 at positive divisors g of M and Σ_{m|g} m c_m≠0 at maximal negative divisors in the divisor order. These conditions suffice because a negative divisor has a maximal negative multiple dividing M. The vanishing at M ensures equal dimensions.

To see optimality among all étale realizations, start with any witnessing pair of Frobenius permutations with joint order L. Each minimal successful degree divides L, since taking gcd with L leaves all the powered fixed counts unchanged, so M|L. Replace every c-cycle in both permutations by c/gcd(c,M) cycles of length gcd(c,M). This keeps both dimensions. For g|M the new fixed-count difference at g equals the old difference at g*, where g* is the largest divisor of L with gcd(g*,M)=g. Indeed c|g* exactly when gcd(c,M)|g. If g is positive, g* is positive. If g is maximal negative, g* is negative and there is a multiple n of g* at which the old fixed-count difference is nonzero. This n is negative, so gcd(n,M)=g by maximality. Consequently gcd(n,L)=g*, giving a nonzero new difference at g. Cancelling common cycles can only improve the dimensions, giving precisely the optimization above.

## 2. Prime-wise witnesses combine exactly when their minimum-degree transporters meet

Let u,v belong to a finite group M and let C≤M be the allowed conjugators. Consider the successful positive degrees f for which u^f and v^f are conjugate by an element of C. For each prime p dividing either order, write u_p,v_p for the commuting p-primary parts in their respective cyclic subgroups. Let T_p(e) be the subset of C conjugating u_p^(p^e) to v_p^(p^e). Put a_p=min{e≥0 | T_p(e)≠∅}.

The successful degrees are exactly the f with a common element in all T_p(v_p(f)). A conjugator of powered elements respects their primary decomposition. The prime-to-p part of f is invertible as a powering operation on each cyclic p-group, so the p-condition is exactly membership in T_p(v_p(f)). Conversely a common conjugator multiplies the primary equalities to give the desired powered equality.

The gcd of the successful degrees is d=∏_p p^a_p. Necessity holds separately at each prime. To attain valuation a_p in a successful degree, pick its local witness and choose the other valuations large enough to kill the other primary parts. The entire spectrum is dℕ_{>0} exactly when the T_p(a_p) have a common element. This is a sufficient descent theorem whenever the minimum-level conjugators meet. In particular it holds if gcd(ord u,ord v) has at most one prime divisor. All remaining prime conditions are then one-sided, and achieving the minimum at such a prime has already made both sides trivial there.

Apply this to an affine Frobenius action P(c)=aF(c) on a finite component group C, with F an automorphism. Put u=aF and v=F in the finite semidirect product of C by the cyclic group generated by F. A fixed point c of P^f is precisely a solution of c⁻¹u^f c=v^f. This recovers the principal-spectrum case from the availability of a joint witness. As an example beyond nilpotence, every inner affine action on S₃ has a principal spectrum by the order criterion.

Failure of the intersection can occur even when each local condition has a witness at exponent zero. In GL₂(F₇), take a=−1, b=2, u=diag(1,ab), v=diag(a,b). Their 2-primary parts are conjugate, and their 3-primary parts are conjugate. The original diagonal matrices have different eigenvalue multisets. The successful degrees are the multiples of 2 or 3. This shows what the inverse theorem's nonprincipal spectra require at the level of conjugator witnesses.

## 3. The type of marked object determines the exact cost of determinant recovery

Let χ be a character of F_q× of order r>1, and let n≥1. A tuple of marks detects χ if every element of GL_n(F_q) fixing all marks has trivial χ of its determinant. Equivalently any two maps between a fixed pair of matching marked configurations have the same determinant character. Allow ordered individual vectors, projective lines, or linear subspaces as the three choices of mark type. The optimal numbers are

| Mark type | Minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Vectors | n |
| Projective lines, r divides n | n+1 |
| Subspaces, r=2 and n even | 3 |
| Subspaces, r>2 and r divides n | 4 |
| Projective lines or subspaces, r does not divide n | Impossible |

For vectors, below n marks a complementary coordinate can be scaled. An ordered basis achieves n. For lines and subspaces the scalar matrices remain invisible, giving impossibility if r∤n. For at most n lines, if they span, they must be the independent lines of a basis and each can be scaled separately. If they do not span, scale a complementary coordinate. The n basis lines and the line of their sum force the common stabilizer to be scalar.

Two subspaces admit a common adapted basis: start with their intersection, extend separately in each, then extend to the ambient space. Scaling any one coordinate preserves both. For the three-subspace upper bound in even dimension, take complementary summands X,Y of equal dimension and the graph of an isomorphism X→Y. The stabilizer is conjugate to the duplicated group diag(A,A), whose determinant is a square.

For the lower bound for r>2, any three subspaces A,B,C admit a simultaneous direct-sum decomposition into invariant pieces of ambient dimensions one or two, where each marked subspace is the direct sum of its intersections with the pieces. Here is an elementary splitting proof. If a nonzero vector v belongs to two subspaces, project onto its line by a functional taking value one on v. If the third subspace does not contain v, choose the functional to vanish on it. This respects all marks. Iterate on the residual space until their intersections are pairwise zero. If 0≠d=b+c belongs to A∩(B+C), the projections of this intersection into B and C are injective. Choose functionals on their images with the same pullback taking d to one, and extend them to B and C. Their projections onto b and c together carry A∩(B+C) into the line of d. Extend by zero on a complement of A∩(B+C) in A and then on an ambient complement. This produces a simultaneous projection onto the two-dimensional span of b,c. If no such d exists, the three spaces form a direct sum and adapted basis lines suffice. Induction proves the asserted decomposition. Scaling one piece by a scalar whose χ value has order r violates the character constraint since the piece has dimension 1 or 2.

For the four-subspace upper bound take complementary summands X,Y and the graphs of two maps f,g from X to Y. In even dimension take f to be the identity in chosen bases, and g a single nilpotent Jordan block. The stabilizer is diag(A,A), where A commutes with this block, so A is a polynomial in it. This follows by evaluating on a cyclic vector. Thus A has constant diagonal a, and the total determinant is a^n. In odd dimension n=2m+1 take bases e₁,…,e_m and u₁,…,u_(m+1) and put f(e_i)=u_i, g(e_i)=u_(i+1). A pair (A,B) preserving the two graphs satisfies Bf=fA and Bg=gA. For columns 2 through m compare fA(e_j)=gA(e_(j−1)). This gives equality along diagonals of A, and zeros off the main diagonal in the first and last rows. Propagating gives scalar A, hence scalar B from the spanning images of f and g. This proves the table.

### Several determinant constraints at once

Write G=∏_i GL(V_i), D for its determinant map, and H=D⁻¹(L) for a subgroup L of the finite determinant group. Let G act on the disjoint union of the chosen mark spaces for each mode. A map preserves every H-invariant relation of arity ≤k on these marks exactly when it sends every tuple of length ≤k into its H-orbit. For a tuple ω with stabilizer K, that condition is membership in HK, equivalently determinant grade in L D(K). Indeed gω=hω exactly when h⁻¹g fixes ω, and H contains the kernel of D. Taking all tuples amounts to intersecting those determinant subgroups. In finite abelian duality, the annihilator of an intersection is generated by the annihilators of the intersectands. Each of these annihilators consists of the characters trivial on L and on D(K). Because each mark belongs to one mode, the stabilizer is a product of the separate mode stabilizers. Hence the retained determinant-character constraints are generated by the characters in L's annihilator whose total minimum cost in the table is at most k. A trivial character in a mode costs zero.

The three-subspace construction gives a concrete sufficient interface for sign-preserving TI on a domain with a polynomial list of candidate marked configurations. Fix a source triple of the displayed form and a target list covering the image of that triple under at least one sign-valid transporter whenever one exists. The determinant sign for each possible image is computed by taking bases of its two summands and using its third subspace to identify them. Any matching map has that sign because the common stabilizer has square determinant. For each candidate with the required sign, impose equality of the three marked subspace images on the actual tensor transporter. For each subspace, use the inclusion matrix as a separate order-two tensor in the ambient space tensored with the dual of a fresh domain space. Allow arbitrary invertible maps on that domain, acting with the same tensor mode map on the codomain. The inclusion intertwiner exists precisely when the prescribed image subspace is correct, and an invertible domain map then follows by restriction. The system has four components and therefore compiles to one polynomial-size ordinary TI instance per candidate. Taking an OR of the answers gives the signed decision on this domain. The theorem uses the candidate list as an explicit input or promise. When a domain supplies a single covariant target triple, one ordinary TI instance suffices. [Tensor system compiler, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

## 4. Fault-tolerant comparison capacity is a simultaneous coloring problem

Fix a simple graph J of requested comparisons on a list of outputs. At each position there are at most r fine choices. We observe only coarse labels of the choices. Each observed coarse label has at most b fine versions, with b≥1. Let S_j be the positions offering coarse label j. An edge is positive at the fine level when its endpoint lists share a fine label.

The coarse data can represent a batch with at most s missing fine edges exactly when at most s edges of J are uncovered by the S_j. Necessity follows by coarsening. For sufficiency use one fine version at every occurrence of a given coarse label, making every covered edge fine-positive.

The same data can represent a batch with at most t fine-positive errors exactly when one shared set F⊆E(J) of size at most t makes every (J−F)[S_j] b-colorable. Necessity follows by restricting to one fine version per observed label per position, yielding a proper coloring off the fine-positive edges. For sufficiency assign fine versions by the colorings. Each vertex participates in at most r sets, so both constructions obey the list bound. Thus if C_max is the maximum covered-edge count achievable under this low-positive condition, a threshold above C_max distinguishes the two cases whenever |E(J)|−s>C_max. The colorings characterize the exact ambiguity of this observation model.

### Exact thresholds for eight lists of length two and four fine versions

Take J=K₈, r=2, b=4. At t=0 every S_j has size at most four and the incidence budget is 16. Unless there are four size-four sets, the raw pair count is at most 21, attained in the size pattern 4,4,4,3,1. This bound follows by moving incidences to the largest sets until they are full, allowing at most three full ones. With four size-four sets, every position belongs to exactly two sets. Only six membership pairs are available. At least two position pairs share a membership pair and hence were counted twice. The upper bound is 24−2=22. It is attained by taking the six two-subsets of four labels as memberships and duplicating the two pairs of a perfect matching. The observation can therefore tolerate five missing positives, and ambiguity occurs at six.

At t=1 each S_j has size at most five. Every size-five set must contain both endpoints of the single allowed edge. Thus there are at most two such sets. With at most one, the raw pair-count bound is 24 except for sizes 5,4,4,3. That exceptional case gives 25 before deduplication and uses all 16 incidences in four sets, so at least two pairs were counted twice. With two size-five sets whose intersection has size u≥3, the pair of sets covers at most 17 edges and the remaining budget of six incidences adds at most seven edges via sizes 4,2. If u=2, the first two sets cover 19 edges. Only the cross edges between their two disjoint three-vertex remainders are uncovered. The available six incidences can add at most five cross edges. A group of four contributes at most four and leaves at most one further edge, while groups of size at most three contribute at most four total. Attain 24 by using two size-five sets meeting exactly at the error edge's endpoints, then covering a 2-by-2 rectangle of the missing cross edges and the remaining singleton cross pair. Therefore three missing positives are tolerable, and ambiguity occurs at four.

At t=2 all 28 edges are coverable. Give four positions coarse memberships {a,b}, two {a,c}, and two {b,c}. Use two disjoint error edges on the four shared {a,b} positions. Each of the two size-six groups becomes four-colorable by identifying the endpoint pairs, and the size-four group is already four-colorable. This saturates the possible observations.

These thresholds give an exact guarantee for the earlier clustered tensor comparison model with eight positions and four fine versions when its list and error hypotheses are met. They concern the observation of a batch, and do not assert that arbitrary tensor pairs can be encoded into a batch with these hypotheses.
