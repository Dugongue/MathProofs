# Group Weisfeiler–Leman refinement and rank-metric quotient graphs

**Theorem.** Let $p$ be prime, let $m>n\ge 2$, and let $K\subseteq M_{m,n}(\mathbb F_p)$ be a linear space whose nonzero members have rank at least three. Form the class-two group $G_K$ below. For any two marked central elements, the full rank distribution of the associated matrix coset is determined by four-variable counting logic on $G_K$ with word predicates. In particular, stable 3-dimensional group Weisfeiler–Leman refinement in the sense of Brachter and Schweitzer, Version II, distinguishes an explicit infinite family of same-format outputs of our tensor-to-group construction that agree on all the previously established invariants, including the full marked $h$-generated subgroup distributions for $h< n$. The distinguishing formulas have quantifier depth $O(\log n)$. On the associated center graphs this pair requires $\Theta(\log n)$ rounds of ordinary graph 2-WL.

**Graph recovery theorem.** When every nonzero element of $K$ and $K'$ has rank at least four, the two associated center graphs are isomorphic exactly when $K$ and $K'$ are left–right equivalent. Thus, on all outputs of our tensor-to-group construction, center-graph isomorphism is equivalent to group isomorphism and to isomorphism of the starting tensors within each fixed format. All same-format center graphs are cospectral. These implicit graphs can have exponentially many vertices in the tensor input length, with adjacency decidable in polynomial time on the compact labels.

Both proofs are informal. Publication priority for these exact statements is unestablished.

## 1. Interpret the quotient of the bilinear forms graph

Write $X=\mathbb F_p^m$, $Y=\mathbb F_p^n$, $V=X\otimes Y$, and $Z=V/K$, with quotient map $\pi$. Use the group on $X\times Y\times Z$ with multiplication
$$
(x,y,z)(x',y',z')=(x+x',y+y',z+z'+\pi(x\otimes y')).
$$
Its commutator has central coordinate $\pi(x\otimes y'-x'\otimes y)$, up to convention. The center is $Z$. In fact any nonzero $X$-coordinate or $Y$-coordinate can form a nonzero simple tensor with a pure element of the opposite type, and the minimum-rank hypothesis makes its image nonzero.

For any elements, commuting is equivalent to $x\otimes y'=x'\otimes y$, since the difference has rank at most two. A noncentral element with $y=0$ has centralizer of index $p^n$, one with $x=0$ has centralizer of index $p^m$, and one with $x,y\ne 0$ has centralizer of index $p^{m+n-1}$. Indeed, in the mixed case the equation for simple tensors makes $(x',y')$ a common scalar multiple of $(x,y)$. These indices are distinct. Call the first two types pure $X$ and pure $Y$, respectively.

Give $Z$ the graph $\Gamma_K$ in which $a,b$ are adjacent exactly when their difference is the value of a commutator between a pure $X$ element and a pure $Y$ element. This is precisely the graph of cosets of $K$ with edges for differences represented by rank-one matrices. Center membership is expressed by one universal quantifier. Each purity condition is expressed by counting the centralizer to the appropriate known size. For fixed $m,n,p,|G_K|$, the adjacency formula uses four variables: two endpoints and two witnesses to the commutator. In each purity subformula, an unused symbol can be rebound for the centralizer count. All atomic expressions are group word equations, as allowed in Version II. Using the unquantified endpoints as reusable symbols in those subformulas gives quantifier depth at most three. [Brachter and Schweitzer, On the Weisfeiler–Leman Dimension of Finite Groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13745)

Let $\Gamma$ be the graph on $V$ with rank-one differences as its edges. The map $\Gamma\to\Gamma_K$ is locally bijective. Every neighbor of a quotient vertex comes from a rank-one step, and two distinct such steps cannot differ by an element of $K$ because their difference has rank at most two. Therefore every walk in $\Gamma_K$ lifts uniquely from a specified lift of its initial vertex.

## 2. Recover the full distribution by walks

For $0\le j\le n$, let $N_j(c)$ count rank-$j$ matrices in the coset $c\in Z$. Let $W_\ell(a,b)$ count length-$\ell$ walks in $\Gamma_K$ from $a$ to $b$. Let $w_\ell(j)$ be the number of length-$\ell$ walks in $\Gamma$ from the zero matrix to a given rank-$j$ matrix. The latter is independent of the chosen matrix because the left–right action is transitive on each rank. By translating and lifting walks,
$$
W_\ell(a,b)=\sum_{j=0}^{n} w_\ell(j)N_j(b-a).
$$
Rank subadditivity gives $w_\ell(j)=0$ for $\ell<j$. Every rank-$j$ matrix is the sum of $j$ rank-one terms, so $w_j(j)>0$, including $w_0(0)=1$. Thus the first $n+1$ walk counts form an invertible triangular system determining the full rank distribution.

Here is a precise bound on the logic needed to read those walk counts. Compare groups of the same finite size $Q$. For each fixed $\ell,t$, the statement $W_\ell(a,b)=t$ has a four-variable counting formula with free variables $a,b$. Length zero uses equality, and length one uses adjacency. For $\ell\ge 2$ let $r=\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor$ and $s=\lceil\ell/2\rceil$. Then
$$
W_\ell(a,b)=\sum_{z\in Z} W_r(a,z)W_s(z,b).
$$
For each possible pair $(i,j)$ of smaller counts, express the exact number of central $z$ such that the two counts are $i,j$. A finite disjunction over those count arrays whose weighted sum $\sum_{i,j}ij\,\#(i,j)$ is $t$ expresses the required equality. There are only finitely many arrays to consider at each $Q,\ell$, since every walk count is bounded by $Q^\ell$. Each smaller-count subformula has only two free symbols and can rebind either symbol unused in that subformula. The midpoint needs one new outer quantifier but no additional variable symbol beyond the four already available. This gives depth at most $3+\lceil\log_2\ell\rceil$ for $\ell\ge 1$. The triangular system then converts any prescribed condition on the tuple of rank counts to a finite boolean combination of walk-count formulas with the same variable bound. This argument bounds depth and variable count. The displayed formulas can be large.

The usual Version II logic equivalence therefore makes the rank counts readable from the stable colors of central endpoint pairs, padded to triples for 3-WL. A difference in the existence of a marked central pair with a specified rank property yields a four-variable counting sentence distinguishing the groups. The commutator-length rank invariant of Grochow and Levet gives related information about shortest word decompositions. The calculation here recovers the complete histogram of ranks of representatives of each quotient coset. [Brachter and Schweitzer, Lemma 3.7 and Theorem 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13745) · [Grochow and Levet, On the Parallel Complexity of Group Isomorphism via Weisfeiler–Leman](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022000025000856) · [Delsarte, Bilinear Forms over a Finite Field, with Applications to Coding Theory](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(78)90015-5)

## 3. The original tensor-to-group outputs include a separating family

Suppose every nonzero matrix in $K$ has full column rank $n\ge 3$. A coset with a rank-one representative has a unique such representative by the minimum-rank property. Write it $uv^{\mathsf T}$ with $u,v\ne 0$.

If $A\in K\setminus\{0\}$ and $u\notin\operatorname{im}A$, the matrix $A+uv^{\mathsf T}$ is injective: reducing its kernel equation modulo $\operatorname{im}A$ first gives $v^{\mathsf T}x=0$, then $Ax=0$. If $u=Aw$ for a nonzero $w$, its rank is that of $I+w v^{\mathsf T}$, which is $n-1$ if $v^{\mathsf T}w=-1$ and $n$ otherwise. To see the first claim, every vector in the kernel is a multiple of $w$, and $w$ is in the kernel exactly under the given scalar condition.

A one-dimensional subspace $\mathbb F_p A$ of $K$ therefore contributes one rank-$(n-1)$ representative of this coset precisely when $u\in\operatorname{im}A$ and $v^{\mathsf T}w\ne 0$. The unique nonzero scalar giving a drop is $-v^{\mathsf T}w$, since $A$ scaled by $\lambda$ has inverse vector $\lambda^{-1}w$ on $u$. This proves the criterion
$$
\exists c\,\bigl(N_1(c)=1 \land N_{n-1}(c)\ge 2\bigr)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{two distinct one-dimensional subspaces of $K$ have intersecting
column spaces with a nonzero intersection.}
$$
The term obtained from $A=0$ has rank one, which is different from $n-1$. For the backward implication, choose nonzero $u$ in the intersection and choose a functional $v^{\mathsf T}$ nonzero on the two inverse vectors. If those vectors are dependent, any functional nonzero on the first suffices. If they are independent, prescribe value $1$ on both.

Apply this criterion to the tensor-to-group construction on scalar tensors $T=(0)$ and $T'=(1)$. In its first replication step, the respective quotient maps from $U_1\otimes V_1$ have a one-dimensional kernel with generator $\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\otimes I_2$ and $\operatorname{diag}(1,1)\otimes I_2$. Here $\dim U_1=\dim V_1=4$ and the quotient $W_1$ has dimension 15. Every nonzero $x\in U_1$ gives the injection $B(x,-):V_1\to W_1$, where $B(x,y)$ is the class of $x\otimes y$.

In the zero-tensor case the rank-two generator can be written $x\otimes y-x'\otimes y'$ with $x,x'$ linearly independent and both summands nonzero. Their common class is nonzero because the kernel contains no rank-one matrix. Hence two distinct projective choices of $x$ give overlapping images. In the unit-tensor case intersecting images for independent $x,x'$ would put a nonzero matrix of rank at most two in the rank-four kernel. Thus distinct projective choices give disjoint images. The last replication sends each injection to its tensor product with $I_s$, preserving both the zero and nonzero intersections because it takes the direct sum of $s$ identical copies of the two subspaces. These are the column spaces of the final codeword lines.

The groups have $m=15s$, $n=4s$, $\dim K=4$, and order $p^{60s^2+19s-4}$ for each $s\ge 3$. Their 3-WL separation follows from the formula for the criterion above, marking the identity and one further central element. By the original construction they have precisely the same commuting relation on the given coordinates, power relation in the common split coordinates for odd $p$, conjugacy class sizes, character-degree multiplicities, and marked $h$-generated subgroup distributions for every $h\le4s-1$. For the last assertion, the cross products of the spans of the $X$- and $Y$-coordinates of $h$ chosen elements all have rank at most $h$. Both quotient maps are injective on that space. Identifying their images and extending this to an isomorphism of the centers matches the generated subgroups for each fixed set of quotient coordinates. Brachter and Schweitzer previously separated other groups with coincident $\Theta(\sqrt{\log |G|})$-subgroup profiles using 3-WL. The present conclusion gives an explicit separating mechanism on the outputs of a tensor-isomorphism reduction and yields the rank enumerator for every center coset in the family. [Brachter and Schweitzer, prior subgroup-profile separation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13745)

The same marked statistic recovers the third-mode flattening nullity of **every** compiler input. First, for a general full-column-rank code $K$, let $d_{A,B}$ be the dimension of the intersection of the images of two distinct projective codeword lines $[A],[B]$. Count unordered pairs of rank-$(n-1)$ representatives over all rank-one cosets. The rank perturbation calculation gives
$$
\sum_{\substack{c\in Z\\N_1(c)=1}}\binom{N_{n-1}(c)}2
=(p-1)p^{n-2}\sum_{\{[A],[B]\}}\bigl(p^{d_{A,B}}-1\bigr).
$$
Indeed, each line contributes at most one representative. For any nonzero common image vector $u$, its inverse vectors under $A$ and $B$ are independent: if $B^{-1}u=\lambda A^{-1}u$, the nonzero codeword $A-\lambda B$ kills the first inverse vector. Thus $(p-1)^2p^{n-2}$ functionals are simultaneously nonzero on the two inverse vectors. Each rank-one matrix has $p-1$ presentations as $uv^{\mathsf T}$, so division by that factor gives the formula.

For a compiler input $(A_i)_{i=1}^c$, put $\nu=\dim\{\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^c\colon \sum_i\lambda_i A_i=0\}$. The first kernel $C^{[2]}$ meets the matrices with column space in any two-dimensional space $L\subseteq U_1$ nontrivially only when $L=Q$, where $Q$ is the two-dimensional replicated row space of the padding. In fact every nonzero padded block is $(\sum_i\lambda_i A_i)\oplus\lambda^{\mathsf T}$, so its twice-replicated rank is two exactly when $\sum_i\lambda_i A_i=0$. Every such rank-two matrix has column space precisely $Q$, and these matrices with zero included form a $\nu$-dimensional subspace. For independent $x,x'\in U_1$, the intersection of the images of $B(x,-),B(x',-)$ is isomorphic to the first kernel restricted to $\langle x,x'\rangle\otimes V_1$: split a kernel element on the two lines, and take the common quotient class with one sign reversed. This map is onto by the definition of the intersection and is injective because the kernel has no rank-one elements. After the final replication the intersection dimensions are therefore $s\nu$ for the $\binom{p+1}{2}$ pairs of distinct lines in $Q$, and zero for every other pair. The displayed marked statistic determines $\nu$ since its right-hand side is
$$
(p-1)p^{n-2}\binom{p+1}{2}(p^{s\nu}-1).
$$
The scalar example therefore distinguishes outputs with different third-mode flattening nullities. General 3-WL separation of inequivalent tensors remains open.

## 4. The center graphs need logarithmically many rounds of graph 2-WL

Use ordinary graph 2-WL with the joint update for an ordered pair $(a,b)$ that records the multiset of $(\mathrm{color}(a,z),\mathrm{color}(z,b))$ as $z$ ranges over the vertices. Round zero colors equality, adjacency and nonadjacency.

Fix the dimensions, the field, and $\dim K$. Let $\delta$ be a lower bound on the nonzero ranks in each code under comparison. For every $t$ with $2^{t+1}<\delta$, the round-$t$ color of any pair is a fixed function of its distance truncated at $2^t$: record the exact distance if it is at most $2^t$, and one common marker otherwise. Both the function and the histogram of the resulting colors are independent of $K$.

The proof is by induction. Round zero is immediate. For the induction step put $R=2^{t-1}$, so $4R<\delta$. For any pair $(a,b)$ with distance at most $2R$, let $A$ be the unique matrix of rank at most $2R$ representing $b-a$. Quotient distance is the minimum rank of a representative, by path lifting and rank-one decomposition. The lift is unique since two such matrices differ by a matrix of rank at most $4R$. For $i,j\le R$, a midpoint $z$ at distances $i,j$ from $a,b$ lifts to representatives $B,C$ of $z-a,b-z$ of ranks $i,j$. Their sum is $A$, by uniqueness. Conversely, any rank-$i$ matrix $B$ with $A-B$ of rank $j$ projects to such a midpoint, and different $B$'s give different midpoints since $2R<\delta$. The number of these midpoints therefore depends only on the rank of $A$ by left–right transitivity. If the endpoint distance exceeds $2R$, all such counts are zero. The counts involving a midpoint whose distance to one endpoint exceeds $R$ follow by subtraction from the corresponding single-endpoint counts. Those are ambient numbers of fixed-rank matrices because the quotient is injective on each radius-$R$ ball. The count with both distances beyond $R$ follows by subtraction from $|Z|$. Thus the new color depends only on distance truncated at $2R$, and its histogram is fixed because the quotient is injective on balls of radius $2R$.

Conversely a round-$t$ pair color determines all walk counts of lengths at most $2^t$. The base case records length zero and length one. The midpoint sum in Section 2 proves the doubling step directly from the multiset in the joint update. For the scalar compiler pair from Section 3, all nonzero codewords have rank $n=4s$. The preceding lower bound shows the graphs are indistinguishable after each round $t$ satisfying $2^{t+1}<n$. The upper bound and triangular inversion recover $N_{n-1}$ on adjacent pairs after $\lceil\log_2(n-1)\rceil$ rounds, so the rank-one coset witness distinguishes them by then. In particular the graph pair requires $\Theta(\log n)=\Theta(\log\log |Z|)$ rounds of 2-WL. Also, 1-WL leaves both graphs monochromatic because their common degree is the number of rank-one matrices. These round lower bounds refer to the center graphs as the input structure. The group Version II result above uses its richer word predicates.

## 5. Recover the code up to left–right equivalence from the graph

Assume now the nonzero ranks in $K,K'$ are at least four. We use paths up to insertion or deletion of backtracks and replacement of a path along a simple triangle by its complementary path with the same endpoints. In the graph on all matrices, every closed walk reduces to the trivial path by these moves.

To see this, write $g(u,v)$ for the step $uv^{\mathsf T}$, with the zero step interpreted as the empty path. Step words concatenate, and backtrack cancellation gives inverses. The triangle and backtrack moves give the two identities
$$
g(u+u',v)=g(u,v)g(u',v),
\qquad
g(u,v+v')=g(u,v)g(u,v').
$$
When both displayed steps and their sum are nonzero, the two successive steps and their sum are rank one and form a triangle. If either step vanishes the identity is immediate, and if their sum vanishes the two-step path is a backtrack. Expand $g(u+u',v+v')$ first in $u$, then in $v$, and also in the other order. Cancel the identical first and last steps in the two resulting words. The middle steps $g(u,v')$ and $g(u',v)$ can then be interchanged. As the four vectors were arbitrary, every two rank-one steps commute modulo triangle and backtrack moves. Next expand each rank-one step by splitting $u$ and $v$ into their coordinates using the same identities. Order the resulting steps by matrix unit and combine each block by the same identity. A closed walk has total coefficient zero on each matrix unit, so the path contracts. These moves operate at any starting matrix by translation.

In either quotient, a lifted walk of length at most three that projects to a closed walk is closed. Its total difference lies in the kernel and has rank at most three. A simple triangle in the full graph projects to a simple triangle, and the lift of a simple triangle is simple, by preservation of adjacency and distinctness of the three projected vertices.

Suppose now $f\colon \Gamma_K\to\Gamma_{K'}$ is a graph isomorphism, translated on the target so that it carries zero to zero. For a matrix $A$, project any path from $0$ to $A$, apply $f$, and lift starting at $0$ into the full matrix graph. Define $\widetilde f(A)$ as the terminal matrix. Two paths to $A$ are equivalent by the closed-walk assertion. Their projected path moves are allowed, their images under $f$ are allowed, and each such move preserves the terminal point of a lift. For a triangle this follows from the closed-lift property, and it follows from local bijectivity for a backtrack. Hence $\widetilde f$ is well-defined. The construction with $f^{-1}$ gives its inverse, and appending one edge to a path shows adjacency preservation. The construction also gives $\pi'\widetilde f=f\pi$. Hence $\widetilde f(0)=0$ and $\widetilde f(K)=K'$.

By Hua's fundamental theorem of geometry of rectangular matrices, a rank-one adjacency automorphism of the full graph fixing zero is of the form $A\mapsto P A^\sigma Q$ with invertible $P,Q$, or has a transpose term when the two matrix dimensions coincide. The dimensions here differ, and the field automorphism $\sigma$ is trivial over $\mathbb F_p$. This proves left–right equivalence of $K,K'$. A left–right equivalence acts on the quotient graphs directly and gives the converse. The same proof works over any finite field with *semilinear* left–right equivalence as the conclusion. [Huang, Huang, Li and Sze, Graphs Associated with Matrices over Finite Fields](https://cklixx.people.wm.edu/graph-matrices.pdf) · [Šemrl, Hua's Fundamental Theorem of Geometry of Rectangular Matrices over EAS Division Rings](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.4790)

For every output of the tensor construction, $m>n\ge 2$ and the nonzero ranks in $K$ are at least twelve. In that construction, the first quotient and tensor replications preserve isomorphism by the shared-kernel argument and cancellation of equal Kronecker summands. Conversely a group isomorphism preserves the pure $X$ and pure $Y$ subspaces in the quotient by the center, distinguished by their conjugacy class sizes. It intertwines their commutator $\pi:X\otimes Y\to Z$ and thus induces a left–right equivalence of kernels. This gives the claimed exact correspondence on same-format outputs. The center graph uses $p^{mn-\dim K}$ vertices. The correspondence uses implicit center graphs, described by the compact group presentation.

## 6. Every same-format center graph is cospectral

The adjacency eigenvectors of $\Gamma_K$ are the additive characters of $Z$, identified with matrices in $K^\perp$ by the standard pairing and a fixed nontrivial additive character of $\mathbb F_p$. If the character matrix $B$ has rank $j$, its eigenvalue is
$$
\lambda_j=\frac{p^m(p^{n-j}-1)-(p^n-1)}{p-1}.
$$
To see this, sum the character on all rank-one steps. Represent each step as $uv^{\mathsf T}$ with $u,v\ne0$ and divide by $p-1$, the multiplicity of this representation. For each nonzero $v$ with $Bv=0$, the sum over nonzero $u$ is $p^m-1$. For each of the remaining $v$, the inner sum is $-1$ by character orthogonality. There are $p^{n-j}-1$ terms of the first type. Distinct $j$'s give distinct eigenvalues.

The rank distribution of $K^\perp$ is determined by that of $K$. In the character-orthogonality expression for the number of dual matrices of rank $j$, average over $A\in K$ the sum of its characters on the ambient rank-$j$ matrices. The inner sum depends only on $\operatorname{rank}(A)$, by contragredient left–right changes of coordinates. The compiler makes the nonzero ranks of $K$ equal to $n$, and fixes $\dim K$ at each format and replication size. Thus the entire same-format family of implicit graphs is cospectral, while their graph isomorphism relation still reflects tensor isomorphism by Section 5. In particular the scalar compiler pair separated by 2-WL has identical adjacency spectra. [Delsarte on the rank-metric association scheme and its duality](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(78)90015-5)

## 7. Compact construction and a polynomial-time adjacency test

For completeness the underlying tensor compiler is as follows. Given the $c$ slices $A_i$ of an $a\times b\times c$ tensor, replace each $A_i$ by $A_i\oplus e_i^{\mathsf T}$, where the last summand is the $i$-th $1\times c$ coordinate row. The new slice span $C$ has dimension $c$. The appended coordinate rows absorb any invertible change of slice coordinates by a right coordinate change. Conversely, for the slice change of any putative equivalence of the padded tuples, the appended row tuple is the same up to a column change, so cancel it by Krull–Schmidt for representations of the $c$-arrow Kronecker quiver. This proves that the padded spaces retain exactly the input tensor-isomorphism relation. For any matrix space $D$, replacing its matrices by $A\otimes I_s$ likewise preserves and reflects left–right equivalence. For any proposed equivalence of replicated spaces, fix its induced slice change and compare the two resulting fixed-arrow tuples. Their $s$-fold direct sums agree, so cancellation of equal indecomposable multiplicities recovers the original equivalence. [Grochow and Qiao, tensor isomorphism and reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

Apply this replication first with $s=2$, and take the quotient $U_1\otimes V_1\to W_1$ by the resulting kernel $C^{[2]}$. Its bilinear map $B(x,y)$ gives the matrix space of contractions $M=\{B(x,-):x\in U_1\}$. Two surjective maps from tensor products are equivalent exactly when an invertible product map identifies their kernels, because the map on the quotient is induced. Also $x\mapsto B(x,-)$ is injective by absence of rank-one kernel matrices. Hence equivalence of the matrix spaces $M$ is exactly isomorphism of their bilinear maps, and therefore exactly isomorphism of the input tensors. Every nonzero contraction is injective by that same rank-one exclusion. Finally set $K=M^{[s]}$ for $s\ge3$, with block sizes $m_0=\dim W_1,n_0=\dim V_1$. All its nonzero ranks equal $n=sn_0$, while $\dim K=\dim U_1$. The inequalities $m_0>n_0\ge4$ follow from $\dim W_1=4(a+1)(b+c)-c$ and $\dim V_1=2(b+c)$. All steps compute bases or quotient maps by field linear algebra and use space and time polynomial in the dense input size and $s$. The reverse comparison reads the tensor of $\pi$ from the compact coordinates, or equivalently a basis for $K$ from its known construction.

Equality of the character-degree multiplicities mentioned in Section 3 also follows from the dual rank counts in Section 6. A central character $B\in K^\perp$ of rank $j$ defines a twisted group algebra on $X\oplus Y$. The associated alternating pairing has rank $2j$. The center of the algebra has basis indexed by the radical of that pairing, since conjugation by basis units multiplies each unit by the corresponding character value. The algebra is free of rank $p^{2j}$ over its center, by taking coset representatives of the radical. It is semisimple over $\mathbb C$, being a central-character summand of the group algebra. Thus it contributes $p^{m+n-2j}$ irreducible representations of degree $p^j$, a number depending only on $j$. Section 6 fixes the multiplicities of all such ranks.

In the center graphs of this compiler, adjacency is decidable in polynomial time from the compact coordinates. Use Gaussian elimination to pick a section of the quotient and represent the difference of the two vertex labels by a matrix $H$. Regard the large matrix as an $s\times s$ array with $m_0\times n_0$ blocks. A codeword adds the same matrix $D\in M$ to all diagonal blocks and zero elsewhere, after reordering the tensor product bases. Decide whether some $H+\operatorname{diag}(D,\ldots,D)$ has rank exactly one.

If every off-diagonal block is zero, a rank-one result can have at most one nonzero diagonal block. Thus some diagonal block vanishes, and a complete candidate list for $D$ is the set of negatives of the diagonal blocks of $H$. If an off-diagonal block $(i,j)$ has a nonzero entry $\alpha=H_{ij}[a,b]$, choose a third index $k$ distinct from both $i,j$. In a rank-one result with block factors $u_\ell,v_\ell$, its $(k,k)$ block must be
$$
u_k v_k^{\mathsf T}
=\alpha^{-1} H_{kj}[:,b]\,H_{ik}[a,:].
$$
Both factors on the right are off-diagonal data. This forces one candidate for $D$ by subtracting $H_{kk}$. In either case test each candidate for membership in $M$ and verify the entire updated matrix has rank one. The tests are exact, including for vertices with zero difference, and the candidate count is at most $s$. In particular these implicit graphs admit polynomial-size adjacency circuits even though the vertex sets are exponential.
