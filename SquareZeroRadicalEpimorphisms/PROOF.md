# Exact epimorphism counting for square-zero radical algebras

Fix \(w\). Given full addition and multiplication tables of finite unital associative \(\mathbb F_p\)-algebras \(A,B\), assume \(J(B)^2=0\). Decompose the semisimple quotient \(S_B=B/J(B)\) into simple factors, with central idempotents \(e_i\). Join distinct \(i,j\) when either \(e_iJ(B)e_j\) or \(e_jJ(B)e_i\) is nonzero, using the quotient action on \(J(B)\). If this graph has treewidth at most \(w\), the number of onto algebra homomorphisms \(A\to B\) and one witness can be computed in deterministic polynomial table time from raw input. In particular this applies to forests and to any bounded number of simple factors. The matrix sizes, radical dimension, field characteristic, and number of factors may all vary within these bounds. This combines the earlier matrix-factor enumeration with affine lifts and subset dynamic programming.

Compute radicals by the criterion \(x\in J(A)\) if and only if \(1-yx\) is a unit for every \(y\). An epimorphism carries \(J(A)\) into \(J(B)\) since its image is a nilpotent ideal, and hence kills \(J(A)^2\). Replace \(A\) by \(A/J(A)^2\). Both radicals now have square zero. Write each algebra as a semisimple subalgebra plus its radical, using a Wedderburn–Malcev section over the perfect field \(\mathbb F_p\). For effective construction choose an additive linear section and solve for a correction with values in the radical; multiplicativity is linear in the correction because radical products are zero. A solution exists by the splitting theorem. Write \(A=S_A\oplus V_A\) and \(B=S_B\oplus V_B\).

For an onto head map \(\psi:S_A\to S_B\), its kernel is a sum of simple factors. Any lift kills that sum on \(S_A\): its identity is idempotent and maps into the nilpotent radical, so its image is zero. Thus restriction to \(S_A\) is a section on \(S_B\) after \(\psi\). Conversely such a section and an onto \(\psi\)-bimodule map \(V_A\to V_B\) give an epimorphism. This characterizes all lifts since multiplication is determined by the sections and the bimodule structures. There are \(|V_B|/|V_B^{S_B}|\) possible sections, where \(V_B^{S_B}\) is the subspace commuting with \(S_B\). Indeed the units \(1+v\) conjugate all complements transitively by Wedderburn–Malcev, and they fix the chosen section exactly when \(v\) commutes with it. The bimodule actions on \(V_B\) are independent of the section.

The head map chooses an injection \(\sigma\) from target factors into source factors of the same matrix size and field, plus an isomorphism for each match. Every isomorphism is a Frobenius twist followed by an inner automorphism. The number of inner choices is the corresponding \(|\operatorname{PGL}|\). This factor is independent of the radical lift count because inner twists in the left and right actions are undone by multiplying the target bimodule by the corresponding units on those sides. The nontrivial radical weight for a fixed \(\sigma\) and twists is a product over ordered pairs, including loops. The weight of \((i,j)\) counts epimorphisms from the source radical corner at \((\sigma(i),\sigma(j))\) onto \(e_iV_Be_j\), with the indicated twists. A zero target corner has weight one.

Each corner is a module over the semisimple ring \(S_i\otimes_{\mathbb F_p} S_j^{\mathrm{op}}\). If \(S_i=M_{d_i}(\mathbb F_{p^{f_i}})\), this tensor product is a product of \(\gcd(f_i,f_j)\) full matrix rings of size \(d_i d_j\) over \(\mathbb F_{p^{\operatorname{lcm}(f_i,f_j)}}\). This follows by factoring the field minimal polynomial over the other center field and then tensoring matrix units. The multiplicities of the unique simple in each factor are found by projecting with its central idempotent and dividing dimensions. A component with \(u\) source copies and \(v\) target copies has \(\prod_{j=0}^{v-1}(q^u-q^j)\) epimorphisms, where \(q\) is the corresponding field order and the product is zero if \(v>u\). This is the count of independent rows over the endomorphism field. The structural computations are polynomial in the table model. For example central idempotents of the heads can be found by scanning elements. In a simple factor a smallest nonzero principal left ideal is a simple module, and the action on it identifies the matrix coordinates over its center. The bimodule corner computations use only the linear coordinate algebra of the tensor product, not a full table of all its elements.

For explicit relative idempotents, factor one center's minimal polynomial over the other center using the Frobenius-fixed algebra of the squarefree quotient. This algebra is the product of copies of the base center field. Splitting by gcds for each fixed-space basis element and each element of that center distinguishes every pair of factors. The center has size at most the input table size. Thus this procedure does not enumerate elements of the possibly larger compositum field. Relative Chinese remaindering gives the projectors, evaluated by the commuting center actions on each corner. For a witness, a simple direct summand of a corner module can be found by scanning that corner for a smallest nonzero cyclic submodule and solving the linear equations for a retraction. The corner itself has at most the original table cardinality, and semisimplicity supplies each retraction. Match simple summands between source and target and project onto enough copies.

Build a width-\(w\) decomposition by dynamic programming on deleted sets of target vertices. Once a set has been deleted with fill edges, the remaining filled graph has an edge precisely when the original graph has a path whose internal vertices all lie in the deleted set. It is thus independent of deletion order. Test whether another vertex has at most \(w\) neighbors in this filled graph, and reconstruct a qualifying order from the empty set to the full set. Such an order exists exactly at width at most \(w\). A width-\(w\) decomposition supplies the order by eliminating a vertex private to a leaf bag after pruning redundant bags, retaining that decomposition after filling. Conversely the bags of a qualifying order consist of each eliminated vertex with its later neighbors, attached to the bag of its earliest later neighbor, with separate components joined at an empty bag. The later-neighbor cliques give the running-intersection condition. There are at most \(2^{k_B}\le |B|\) deletion states, where \(k_B\) is the number of target factors.

Sum the product of local weights over injective matchings and twists by dynamic programming on the resulting decomposition. Store for each bag vertex its selected source factor and Frobenius twist, and store the set of source factors already used below and outside the bag. At a join the two outside sets must be disjoint. At introduction and removal enforce injection. Charge each vertex and edge weight once at a bag containing its arguments. Thus multiplication and summation over states computes the required weighted sum. Storing positive branches also produces the matching and full-rank module maps for a witness. If \(k_A\) is the number of source head factors, then \(k_A\le \log_2|A|\). Even pairwise operations on the subset states cost at most \(4^{k_A}\) times a polynomial in the table lengths to a power depending on \(w+1\). Finally multiply the sum by the section count. This proof is informal.

## The role of the full-table encoding

For a simple graph \(X\), form an algebra \(A_X\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) with head \(\mathbb F_2^{V(X)}\) and one one-dimensional radical bimodule for each oriented edge, taking both orientations. Give its radical zero products. The weight formula above now gives
\[
\#\operatorname{Epi}(A_X,A_Y)
=2^{2|E(Y)|}\cdot\#\{\text{injective edge-preserving maps }Y\to X\}.
\]
In fact every matched head factor has its unique field identification. Each target edge then has radical weight one exactly when its source edge exists. The radical commuting with the head is zero because every edge is off-diagonal, so the factor for complements is precisely its cardinality. The algebra has polynomial-size structure constants in the graph input. In this representation existence is NP-complete even for target interaction graph a path, by using a path with \(|V(X)|\) vertices to test for a Hamiltonian path in \(X\). Membership follows by verifying a full-rank linear map on the structure constants. For unrestricted interaction graphs the same formula gives hardness of exact counting under polynomial-time reductions with a final division by a known integer. Encode a Boolean formula in conjunctive normal form with at most three literals per clause as a graph with one part per clause, vertices the local satisfying assignments, and edges the compatible pairs in different parts. Cliques of size equal to the clause count correspond to satisfying assignments on the variables that occur. Labeled embeddings of the corresponding clique multiply this count by a known factorial. Full tables for these graph algebras can be exponential in the compact description, so the compact-input result is consistent with the full-table algorithm.
