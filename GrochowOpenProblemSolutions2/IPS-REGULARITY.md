# GP162 · Equal syzygy regularity and different IPS sizes for two 2-CNF families

For every field $k$ and $n\ge 5$, there are explicit unsatisfiable 2-CNFs on the same $n$ variables, each with $\Theta(n)$ clauses, whose homogeneous zero-certificate syzygy modules have the same Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity. The minimum fan-in-two IPS circuit size is $\Theta(n)$ for the first family and $O(1)$ for the second. The separation also holds for Hilbert-like IPS. It answers the explicit-family alternative of GP162 for the standard homogenized syzygy module. [Grochow–Pitassi, General Question 1.18 and Lemma 1.12](https://arxiv.org/html/1404.3820)

## Encoding and grading

For a clause use the product of the indicators of falsity of its literals. Thus $(x_i)$ gives $1-x_i$, $(\neg x_i)$ gives $x_i$, and $(\neg x_i\lor x_j)$ gives $x_i(1-x_j)$. Include each Boolean equation $x_i^2-x_i=0$. The IPS size is the number of arithmetic gates for a certificate $C(x,y)$ with $C(x,0)=0$ and $C(x,F(x))=1$, where each equation has its own placeholder. Gates have fan-in two. Counting input gates as well leaves the same asymptotics.

Use the same homogenization for every instance. In $S=k[h,x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ with all variables of degree one, homogenize each generator to its own degree using $h$. Write $I$ for the ideal generated by the homogeneous polynomials $f_j$. The graded zero-certificate module for Hilbert-like IPS is
$$
K=\ker\!left(\bigoplus_j S(-\deg f_j)\longrightarrow I,\quad (g_j)_j\longmapsto\sum_j g_j f_j\right).
$$
This includes the generator slots for the full input, including the Boolean equations.

## The implication path

The first formula has $(x_1)$, $(\neg x_n)$, and both implications $x_i\Rightarrow x_{i+1}$ and $x_{i+1}\Rightarrow x_i$ for each $1\le i<n$. Put $r=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$.

In the homogeneous quotient impose $x_1=h$ and $x_n=0$ using the unit clauses. For each $i$, the difference of the two homogeneous implication polynomials is $h(x_i-x_{i+1})$. Their successive sums, using $x_n=0$, give $hx_i=0$ for every $i$, including $h^2=hx_1$. Consequently the two homogeneous implications give the adjacent products $x_i x_{i+1}=0$, and the Boolean equations give the squares $x_i^2=0$. Conversely these monomials and the two linear substitutions make every original generator vanish. The quotient is exactly
$$
S/I \simeq k[h,x_2,\ldots,x_{n-1}]
 /\langle h^2,\ hx_i,\ x_i^2,\ x_i x_{i+1}\rangle,
$$
where the monomials in the ideal use the surviving indices. Its nonzero monomials are $h$ and the square-free monomials indexed by independent sets of the path on $n-2$ vertices, including the empty set. Alternating path vertices give an independent set of size $\lceil(n-2)/2\rceil=r$, and adjacent disjoint pairs with a possible final singleton show this is maximal. The largest nonzero degree is $r$.

In the original, dehomogenized encoding let $y_1,y_n$ be the two unit placeholders and $u_i,v_i$ the forward and backward implication placeholders. The certificate
$$
C=y_1+y_n+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(u_i-v_i)
$$
has size $O(n)$ because its substitution is $(1-x_1)+x_n+\sum_i(x_i-x_{i+1})=1$. It is linear in the placeholders. [Lean replay of the identity](ImplicationCertificate.lean)

Every IPS certificate for this formula must use the two unit placeholders and all $n-1$ forward implication placeholders. To see this, deleting the first unit leaves the all-zero assignment, and deleting the last unit leaves the all-one assignment. Deleting forward implication $i$ leaves the assignment with the first $i$ variables one and the rest zero. Each assignment satisfies all other clauses and all Boolean equations. If the omitted placeholder were absent from the certificate polynomial, the substitution at this assignment would give $C(x,0)=0$ in place of $1$. So at least $n+1$ distinct placeholder inputs must reach the output. A single-output fan-in-two DAG with $a$ such inputs and $b$ gate vertices has at least $a+b-1$ edges by connectedness of its ancestors and at most $2b$ incoming gate edges. Thus $b\ge a-1$, even when a gate can be reused. The lower bound is $n$ gates.

## The formula with a contradictory pair of units

On the same variables, the second formula has $(x_1)$, $(\neg x_1)$, the negative units $(\neg x_i)$ for $2\le i\le n-r$, and the implications $x_1\Rightarrow x_j$ for $n-r<j\le n$. Each variable occurs in a clause. The units eliminate $h,x_1,\ldots,x_{n-r}$. The implications then vanish and the remaining Boolean equations are the squares of the $r$ surviving variables. Hence
$$
S/I \simeq k[x_{n-r+1},\ldots,x_n]/\langle x_j^2\mid j>n-r\rangle,
$$
whose largest nonzero degree is again $r$. The sum of the two contradictory unit placeholders is an IPS certificate of one addition gate.

## Regularity comparison

Both quotients are finite-dimensional graded rings, so their regularity is their largest nonzero degree $r$. For a proper nonzero homogeneous ideal, truncating the minimal graded resolution of $S/I$ gives $\operatorname{reg} I=\operatorname{reg}(S/I)+1=r+1$. The free source module $F=\bigoplus_j S(-\deg f_j)$ of the defining map for $K$ has regularity $2$. The regularity inequalities for $0\to K\to F\to I\to0$ give
$$
\operatorname{reg} K\le\max(\operatorname{reg}F,\operatorname{reg}I+1),
\qquad
\operatorname{reg} I\le\max(\operatorname{reg}F,\operatorname{reg}K-1).
$$
Since $r\ge2$, the second inequality forces $\operatorname{reg}K\ge r+2$, and the first gives equality. Both families therefore have syzygy regularity exactly $\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor+2$ for every $n\ge5$.

The same regularity and variable count coexist with a linear factor between optimal certificate sizes. Thus these parameters alone cannot determine IPS size up to a constant factor even for width-two clause encodings and uniformly chosen homogenization. The algebraic regularity computation and size bounds are written proofs.
