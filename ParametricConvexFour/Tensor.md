# Sharp diagonal special-linear invariants from parametric transportation

Let $I,J,K$ be finite nonempty coordinate sets and let $S\subseteq I\times J\times K$ be the common support of two tensors over a field $F$. Suppose each middle slice has rectangular support

$$
S=\{(i,j,k): i\in I_j,\ k\in K_j\},\qquad I_j,K_j\ne\varnothing.
$$

Write $a=|I|, b=|J|, c=|K|$, $L=\operatorname{lcm}(a,b,c)$, and let $\mu$ denote the concatenated marginal vector with entries $L/a,L/b,L/c$ in the three modes. Assume there exists a real table positive at every entry in $S$ with one-way marginals $\mu$. All the conditions depend only on the support and the chosen coordinate bases.

**Theorem.** One can deterministically construct a polynomial-size list of invariant monomials of degrees $L$ and $2L$ whose exponents generate the full invariant Laurent lattice for the product of the three determinant-one diagonal tori, as an abelian group. The construction and promise test run in polynomial time. Equality of the list classifies orbits on the support over an algebraically closed field. Over any field $F$, equality of the list and at most two explicit power-membership tests classify orbits under the literal determinant-one diagonal matrices over $F$. The degree bound $2L$ is sharp even over algebraically closed fields in format $2\times2\times2$.

The constructive lattice statement holds with any positive integral marginal vector $\mu=(u,v,w)$ with common total $D$, provided every supported entry can occur in a nonnegative real table of that marginal. In this version the lattice is the set of integer tables with marginals in $\mathbb Z\mu$ and the degrees are $D,2D$. The fixed-marginal feasibility tests and exponent construction use polynomial bit time with binary-encoded margins.

## Feasible tables and transportation exchanges

For a signed integer table $h$ on $S$, define its two pair margins by

$$
p_{ij}=\sum_{k\in K_j}h_{ijk},\qquad q_{jk}=\sum_{i\in I_j}h_{ijk}.
$$

Let $E_P=\{(i,j):i\in I_j\}$ and $E_Q=\{(j,k):k\in K_j\}$. A nonnegative integral table of marginals $(u,v,w)$ gives bipartite transports $P$ on $E_P$ and $Q$ on $E_Q$ of marginals $(u,v)$ and $(v,w)$, respectively. Conversely, for each $j$ connect the row counts $P_{ij}$ to the column counts $Q_{jk}$ on the complete bipartite graph $I_j\times K_j$. Their common total is $v_j$. Greedily allocating the smaller of the remaining row and column counts produces an integral table. If a given triple must carry at least one unit and both corresponding pair entries carry at least one unit, first allocate that unit and then use the same construction on the residual counts.

Each bipartite transport feasibility test can include integral edge bounds. We use the integral max-flow theorem after subtracting lower bounds from the requested marginal sums. In particular, each edge of $E_P$ and $E_Q$ occurs in an integral transport at the requested margins whenever it occurs in a real transport. Maximizing the entry on that edge over the bounded transport polytope has an integral maximizer, since the flow polytope has integral capacities and balances. The promise is therefore equivalent to feasibility on both supports with every edge active in some integral transport at the requested margins. The promise can be tested by forcing one unit on each pair edge in turn. When it holds, every triple occurs in an integral table of degree $D$. Averaging those witnesses recovers positivity everywhere. The network facts go back to [Ford and Fulkerson, *Maximal Flow Through a Network*](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/lans/readings/routing/ford-max_flow-1956.pdf) and [*Solving the Transportation Problem*](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.3.1.24).

For either pair support let $\nu$ be its prescribed margins and let $B_e$ be a witness containing edge $e$ with margins $\nu$. Their sum $B$ has every entry at least one and margins $m\nu$, where $m$ is the number of edges of the pair support. An integer transport $C$ of margins $k\nu$ splits into $k$ nonnegative integral transports of margins $\nu$. To produce the first summand, impose edge upper bounds $C_e$ and the margins $\nu$. The real flow $C/k$ proves feasibility, so an integral flow exists. Subtract it and iterate. The number of subproblems is $k$, independent of the magnitudes of $\nu$.

The integer kernel of the pair margin map is generated by the signed fundamental cycles of a spanning forest. Indeed, orient from the first bipartite class to the second, with the zero-sum constraints written as zero divergence. Successively cancel all nonforest edges using their fundamental circulations. A remaining circulation on a forest vanishes by peeling leaves. For any one fundamental cycle $z$, all entries of $B+z$ are nonnegative because the cycle coefficients belong to $\{0,1,-1\}$. Decompose $B$ and $B+z$ into $m$ transports of margins $\nu$ each, giving $z$ as a sum of differences of transports at the fixed margin. This realizes every signed cycle generator through a polynomial number of exchanges at degree $D$.

## Lifting the exchanges to tensor invariants

Let $N$ be the lattice of integer tables with zero one-way margins. Its image under $h\mapsto(p,q)$ lies in the direct sum of the two zero-pair-marginal lattices. For the $P$ cycles, glue each of the constructed degree-$D$ transports with one fixed feasible $Q$. The resulting differences of tables project to the desired cycle in $P$ and to zero in $Q$. Repeat with the roles of $P$ and $Q$ exchanged.

The kernel of $h\mapsto(p,q)$ is the direct sum over $j$ of the zero-row-and-column lattice of tables on $I_j\times K_j$. Choose an anchor $i_0\in I_j,k_0\in K_j$ for each $j$. The unit generators are the signed rectangles having $i_0,k_0$ as one corner. To see this, use a multiple of its unique anchored rectangle to cancel every entry off the anchor row and column. Zero pair margins then force the remainder to vanish. For each such unit rectangle, choose a degree-$D$ table containing its first negative entry and another containing its second. The sum is a degree-$2D$ table that stays nonnegative when the signed rectangle is added. Record the original sum and the result. Subtracting their exponents gives the rectangle.

Take the list to consist of all tables recorded in the cycle and rectangle constructions and one baseline table $h_0$ of degree $D$. If $h$ has margins $n\mu$, then $h-nh_0$ lies in $N$. Subtract integer combinations of the lifted cycle expressions to cancel its pair margins, then subtract integer combinations of the anchored rectangle differences. Thus the recorded exponents generate the whole claimed lattice. The list has size at most $1+2|E_P|^2+2|E_Q|^2+2|S|$. This is an integer span statement. For instance, the even parity table in the sharpness example below fails to split into two degree-$L$ balanced tables.

In the uniform case, a signed exponent is a character of the determinant-one diagonal action, with its weight given by its one-way marginals modulo the all-ones vector in each mode. Hence its weight vanishes exactly when all margins are constant within modes. Its total is then an integer multiple of $L$, and its margins are that multiple of $\mu$. The recorded nonnegative exponents give ordinary invariant polynomials. A Smith normal form of the integer character map shows that over an algebraically closed field its orbit image on $(F^\times)^S$ is the common kernel of these invariant characters. In the diagonal Smith coordinates, zero rows impose exactly the invariant equalities and each nonzero row is a surjective power map. This argument applies also when the field characteristic divides a Smith exponent.

## Exact arithmetic for literal determinant one

Suppose the degree-$L,2L$ fingerprints of tensors $x,y$ agree. Let $r_{ijk}=y_{ijk}/x_{ijk}$. First find one ordinary diagonal transporter $(s_i,t_j,u_k)$ with $s_i t_j u_k=r_{ijk}$. For each $j$, fix $i_j\in I_j,k_j\in K_j$. Check the identities

$$
r_{ijk}r_{i_jjk_j}=r_{ijk_j}r_{i_jjk}.
$$

Propagate the prescribed ratios $s_i/s_{i_j}=r_{ijk_j}/r_{i_jjk_j}$ on the graph of the $I_j$ overlaps, and propagate $u_k/u_{k_j}=r_{i_jjk}/r_{i_jjk_j}$ on the corresponding graph for $K_j$. With one representative set to one per component, compute $t_j=r_{i_jjk_j}/(s_{i_j}u_{k_j})$. These steps solve the complete multiplicative equations. Fingerprint equality implies the equations have a solution over an algebraic closure by the character argument. The displayed identities and consistency around the propagation cycles are equations over $F$, so the algorithm already succeeds over $F$.

More generally, for each feasible strictly positive integral profile $\mu$, impose a single character constraint on the ordinary diagonal action. Write $g$ for the tuple of all three modes' multipliers and require $g^\mu=1$, with powers taken coordinatewise and then multiplied. The degree-$D,2D$ list for that profile classifies these constrained orbits over the input field $F$. Indeed, fingerprint equality implies equality of every Laurent monomial with zero one-way margins because their exponents belong to the generated lattice. Smith form for the ordinary diagonal action gives an ordinary transporter over an algebraic closure, and the propagation algorithm gives one over $F$. Its value $g^\mu$ is the ratio of the two evaluations of the baseline monomial $x^{h_0}$, so it equals one. Conversely $g^\mu=1$ preserves every monomial in the list. In the uniform case this single character is the product of the three determinants raised to $L/a,L/b,L/c$.

For the stabilizer of $x$, let $A$ run over the components of the bipartite graph on $E_P$, and $C$ over those on $E_Q$. The diagonal values on the $I$-part of $A$ are a common scalar $\alpha_A$, and on the $K$-part of $C$ they are a common scalar $\gamma_C$. This follows by comparing stabilizer equations with one coordinate changed at a time inside each rectangle. Then the middle value at $j$ must be $\alpha_{A(j)}^{-1}\gamma_{C(j)}^{-1}$. These parameters vary freely and give the entire ordinary diagonal stabilizer. Its three determinants have the integer exponent matrix with columns

$$
\bigl(|I\cap A|,-|J\cap A|,0\bigr)^\mathsf{T},\qquad
\bigl(0,-|J\cap C|,|K\cap C|\bigr)^\mathsf{T}.
$$

An integral transport of uniform margins balances each connected component. Therefore every first-type column is proportional over $\mathbb Q$ to $(a,-b,0)^\mathsf{T}$ and every second-type column to $(0,-b,c)^\mathsf{T}$. Both types occur, so the matrix has rank two. Take its integer Smith form with unimodular multipliers and apply the row multiplier multiplicatively to the inverse determinants of the computed transporter. Its two active coordinates must lie in the images of its two power maps. Its last coordinate must equal one, which already follows from equality of the degree-$L$ baseline monomial. Indeed the annihilator of the two column lines is generated over $\mathbb Z$ by the primitive vector $(L/a,L/b,L/c)$. An ordinary transporter can be corrected by a stabilizer element to determinant one exactly when its inverse determinant tuple lies in this image. Smith form is computable in polynomial bit time by [Kannan and Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040).

For a finite field of known order $q$, testing whether $z\in(F^\times)^d$ is the exponentiation test $z^{(q-1)/\gcd(d,q-1)}=1$. The literal diagonal orbit decision is therefore deterministic polynomial time over a standard explicit finite field, including the exponent construction and evaluation. A witness over a general field can be constructed from roots supplied for the two successful tests. If the inputs include a fixed split simple-spectrum endomorphism in each mode and the morphisms must commute with these endomorphisms, the same result gives the determinant-one transporter test for the resulting marked tensor system. Each commutant consists of diagonal maps in the chosen eigenbasis.

## Varying the middle marginal

Fix the outer marginals $u,w$ of total $D$ and vary the middle vector $v$. For $A\subseteq J$, define the weighted coverage functions

$$
f_P(A)=\sum_{i\in\bigcup_{j\in A}I_j}u_i,\qquad
f_Q(A)=\sum_{k\in\bigcup_{j\in A}K_j}w_k.
$$

Assume all outer vertices with positive supply or demand have a neighbor. Each coverage function is nondecreasing and submodular with value $D$ on $J$. A nonnegative vector $v$ of total $D$ is feasible precisely when $v(A)\le f_P(A)$ and $v(A)\le f_Q(A)$ for every $A\subseteq J$. The two conditions are the max-flow cut inequalities for the respective pair transports. They describe the intersection of the two coverage polymatroid base polytopes. Each has an M-convex integer point set in the sense of the exchange axiom. Single-unit exchange can fail in the intersection. Give four middle vertices the singleton rectangles indexed by the four edges of $K_{2,2}$ and put one unit at each outer vertex. The two feasible middle vectors are then $(1,0,0,1)$ and $(0,1,1,0)$, with no single exchange between them. The discrete-convex setting comes from [Murota, *Discrete Convex Analysis*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02680565) and [Murota and Shioura, *M-Convex Function on Generalized Polymatroid*](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.24.1.95).

Add integral lower and upper bounds on the middle counts, linear pair-transport costs and a separable discrete-convex cost $\sum_j g_j(v_j)$. Split every $j$ into an input, a relay and an output. Put the bounded common arc from input to relay and follow it by $D$ parallel arcs of unit capacity from relay to output with costs $g_j(h)-g_j(h-1)$, $1\le h\le D$. An integer flow using $v_j$ such arcs can pay for the $v_j$ cheapest increments. Standard integral min-cost flow gives an optimal table after gluing. The explicit incremental representation gives a polynomial algorithm in its size, in particular when $D=L$ for an explicitly given tensor format.

For a marked middle vertex $j_*$, impose a value $t$ on its common arc and write $V(t)$ for the optimal cost before a penalty on $t$. If there is any feasible flow, the real projection onto $t$ is a closed interval whose endpoints are integers by network integrality. For each integer $t$ in that interval, fixing the common arc to $t$ preserves integrality, so there is an integer optimum of the same value as the real relaxation. The real optimal value is convex in $t$ because convex combinations of flows are feasible at their combined counts and the expanded arc cost is linear. It follows that the feasible integer counts have no gaps and the adjacent differences of $V$ are nondecreasing. The exact solution path for $V(t)+\lambda t$ is determined by these adjacent differences. An interior count is optimal precisely when $V(t)-V(t+1)\le\lambda\le V(t-1)-V(t)$, with the missing endpoint bound omitted. Test any selected profile for strictly positive table feasibility to obtain its corresponding constrained-orbit fingerprint.

## Sharpness, controls and prior work

On full $2\times2\times2$ support the degree-two invariant monomials are exactly the four opposite-corner products. Start with every coefficient equal to one, multiply $x_{000}$ by $\eta\ne1$ and $x_{111}$ by $\eta^{-1}$. The four degree-two invariants agree, while the degree-four even parity monomial changes by $\eta$. Thus degree two fails to separate geometric orbits. For a literal-field example over $\mathbb F_3$, act on the all-ones tensor by diagonal maps $\operatorname{diag}(-1,1),\operatorname{diag}(-1,1),I_2$. The product of the determinants is one, so every invariant monomial agrees at the two tensors. Every stabilizer determinant is a square on this full support, so the resulting transporter cannot be corrected to determinant one over $\mathbb F_3$.

An exhaustive finite check examined all 81 rectangular supports in $2\times2\times2$ with two nonempty middle slices. Nine passed the strict balance test. Across those supports it compared 336 coefficient-ratio tensors over $\mathbb F_3$ by enumerating all degree-two and degree-four invariants and all 64 ordinary diagonal triples. In each case geometric fingerprint equality and the stabilizer determinant test together agreed with direct enumeration of determinant-one triples. There were 14 fingerprint matches that failed the literal-field test. This check supplements the proof above.

Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang formulate the special-linear comparison with ordinary TI in Open Question 1.9 of Part III. Here the factors are diagonal or forced diagonal by split ordered markings. The determinant equation is literal in each factor, and the certificate consists of sharp bounded-degree polynomial invariants and a two-power test. This gives a tractable regime of the determinant-constrained question for fixed coordinate marks. [Part III, §1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135#S1.SS5).

Bürgisser, Doğan, Makam, Walter and Wigderson already solve general complex torus orbit equality in polynomial time and construct bounded-size systems of Laurent generators. Dobra derives primitive data swaps for decomposable graphical models with prescribed clique marginals. The specific strengthening here is a list of ordinary nonnegative monomials at the two specified balanced degrees, uniformly constructed by combining the pair-flow decomposition with the fiber exchanges. The statement concerns generation of the Laurent character lattice by the exponents of these polynomial monomials. [Bürgisser et al., CCC 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.07727) · [Dobra, Bernoulli 2003](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/adobra/Files/Papers/dobra-bernoulli.pdf).
