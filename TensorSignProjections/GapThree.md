# One two-sign instance for a small mode or a small castling complement

Work over $F=\mathbb F_3$ with dense explicit tensor inputs and let $N$ denote their encoding length. A sign below means the determinant in $\{1,-1\}$. For an all-even concise tensor pair of format $(a,b,c)$, set

$$
 d=\min\{a,b,c,bc-a,ac-b,ab-c\}.
$$

There is an exact deterministic one-instance reduction from SL-TI to two-sign TI taking time and output size polynomial in $N$ and $2^d$. In particular it is polynomial when $d=O(\log N)$, and the condition can hold when all three side dimensions are large. On equal-format pairs that are nonconcise or have at least one odd dimension, an exact polynomial-time reduction already follows from support, scalar and sign-subgroup adjustments as detailed below. The bounded-defect theorem is informal.

## Protecting one orientation without introducing a new sign

First suppose the two tensors are concise and all sides even, with one side of dimension $k\ge2$ to protect. Permute the modes and use the bilinear convention $\mu:U\times V\to W$ with $\dim U=k$. Inverse dualizing a side does not alter its sign over $F$. For each source or target let $\omega$ be the top wedge of the ordered raw basis of $U$.

Let $C_U$ be the algebra formed from the exterior algebra $\Lambda U$ and $F[z]/(z^{2k+1})$ with the same unit and zero mixed products between their positive ideals, then quotient by their shared relation $z^{2k}=\omega$. The identifying difference generates a one-dimensional ideal, because both terms annihilate the radical. This local associative algebra has basis given by the exterior monomials of degree less than $k$, including the unit, and $z,z^2,\dots,z^{2k}$. It has odd dimension $2^k+2k-1$. Call its radical $J_U$.

Every algebra isomorphism $\theta:C_U\to C'_U$ induces an action $g_U$ on the subspace $U\subset J_U/J_U^2$. Indeed, the square in $J_U^2/J_U^3$ of a tangent $u+\lambda z$ is exactly $\lambda^2z^2$. This follows from the exterior relation $u^2=0$ and the vanishing mixed products. The class of $z^2$ is nonzero, so the square-zero tangents recover $U$. Write $\nu\ne0$ for the induced action on the quotient tangent line. Then

$$
             \det g_U=1,\qquad \det\theta=1.
$$

For the first assertion, the image of any $u\in U$ has square zero. Express this image as the sum of an exterior-positive part of degrees less than $k$ and a polynomial in $z$ with zero constant term. If the polynomial has lowest exponent $i<k$, its square has a nonzero $z^{2i}$ coefficient. The exterior part cannot contribute at that exponent because the two parts meet only at $z^{2k}$. Thus all polynomial terms in $\theta(u)$ have degree at least $k$. If $k=2$, the exterior part belongs to $U$ and its square is identically zero, so the polynomial must have degree strictly greater than $k$. Hence the product of the images of $k$ basis generators of $U$ has zero contribution from the $z$-polynomials. For $k\ge4$ this follows from $k^2>2k$, and it also holds for $k=2$ by the strict bound. Its exterior contribution is precisely $(\det g_U) z^{2k}$. On the other hand $\theta(z)^{2k}=\nu^{2k}z^{2k}=z^{2k}$, since the only term that can survive is the leading $z$-term. Applying $\theta$ to the identifying relation proves the assertion.

For the second assertion, use the radical filtration. For $1\le j<k$, its graded piece in degree $j$ has the exterior grade $\Lambda^j U$ and the $z^j$ line. The exterior grade is preserved by the leading action $\Lambda^j g_U$, by the tangent computation and multiplication of leading terms. The remaining diagonal factor is $\nu^j$. In degrees $k\le j\le2k$ only the line $Fz^j$ remains, since the exterior top was identified in degree $2k$. Its leading action is also $\nu^j$. Thus the exterior determinants are powers of $\det g_U=1$, the unit grade contributes one, and the tag determinant is $\nu^{k(2k+1)}=1$ since $k$ is even. This uses the longer tag string, not the homogeneous identification $z^k=\omega$. Conversely every $g_U$ with determinant one extends by its exterior actions and fixes $z$.

## Glue the original tensor to two marked quotients

Let $C_V=F1\oplus V\oplus\langle t,t^2,t^3\rangle$ be local, with zero products within $V$ and across it and the tag, and $t^4=0$. The same square test recovers $V$, and the tag contributes the sixth power of its leading scalar to every determinant. Thus every quotient isomorphism has sign $\det g_V$; conversely every $g_V$ extends fixing $t$. This quotient has even dimension.

Build one algebra $R_\mu=F1\oplus J_U\oplus J_V\oplus W$ with the two original algebra multiplications on their radicals. Its only nonzero further radical product is $uv=\mu(u,v)$, using the exterior degree-one projection onto $U$ and the old tangent projection onto $V$; $W$ annihilates the entire radical. This multiplication is associative. Products within a single radical branch have zero tangent projection, and a mixed product into $W$ is killed by further radical multiplication. The radical is nilpotent and the residue is $F$. The algebra dimension is $\dim C_U+\dim C_V-1+\dim W$, which is even.

Mark the two ideals $I_U=J_V\oplus W$ and $I_V=J_U\oplus W$. Their quotients are $C_U,C_V$, and their intersection is $W$. An isomorphism $\phi$ of the marked algebras induces maps $g_U,g_V,g_W$ on the three original slots. On $[u,v]=\mu(u,v)$, the image is the commutator of elements of $I_V',I_U'$, whose mixed term reads precisely the two recovered tangent actions. This proves that the three maps transport the original tensor, with $\det g_U=1$. Conversely any original transporter with first sign one extends to such a $\phi$, by the established extensions on the branches and the given map on $W$. Fix the bases of the algebra using the common unit followed by the ordered radical blocks. In these frames, determinants multiply through $W\subseteq R_\mu$ and its quotient. Since the quotient is the fiber product of $C_U,C_V$ over their unit residues, this gives

$$
             \det\phi = (\det g_W)(\det g_V).
$$

## Put both remaining signs in one three-tensor

Use the shared algebra label $a\in R_\mu$. Take the direct-sum pencil of two copies of the regular block $L_a$ of left multiplication, one rectangular block $K_V(a)$, and one transposed rectangular block $K_U(a)^{\mathsf T}$. Here, for $D=C_U$ or $C_V$,

$$
 K_D(a):F^2\to D,\qquad K_D(a)=[\,\bar a,\ \epsilon(a)e_D\,],
 \qquad e_{C_U}=z^2,\quad e_{C_V}=t^2.
$$

The scalar residue of $R_\mu$ is $\epsilon$. The basis orders are fixed identically on the source and target. Test the two physical modes of the labels and of the pencil rows; the inverse dual notation for a tensor label preserves its sign.

Here are the details that ensure a common transporter is read. For fixed labels the regular block is indecomposable, since its endomorphism algebra is the opposite of the local algebra $R_\mu$. For a rectangular block, write a column endomorphism as $P=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p&q\\r&s\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Evaluating the second column at residue-zero labels forces $q=0$. The first column forces the row endomorphism $pI+r e_D\epsilon$, and the second column at the unit forces $s=p$. The endomorphism ring is again local. The rectangular and transposed rectangular blocks have different dimension vectors, and their large sides are greater than two. Neither matches the square regular block. Thus Krull–Schmidt for the finite-dimensional fixed-label quiver representations implies that any feasible label map admits a block-diagonal transporter, and all others with that label differ by a fixed-label automorphism. On sums of such blocks, the semisimple quotient of the fixed-label endomorphism ring has one factor for each distinct block, matrix entries recording multiplicities. Its radical units act unipotently. The repeated regular block contributes determinant one to the row mode, since a determinant on the multiplicity space is raised to the even dimension of $R_\mu$. The ordinary $C_V$ block also contributes determinant one for fixed-label units, because $\dim C_V$ is even. The transposed $C_U$ block contributes determinant $p^2=1$ on its two-dimensional row mode. Therefore this freedom cannot change the tested row sign.

The regular block equation for a label action $f$ gives $f(a)=u\phi(a)$, with $u=f(1)$ a unit and $\phi$ an algebra isomorphism. This follows by substituting the unit into the equation and then conjugating the left-regular representation. Its sign on the label space is precisely $\det\phi$ because left multiplication by $u$ has determinant $\epsilon(u)^{\dim R_\mu}=1$ by the radical filtration. The kernels in the two rectangular label maps are precisely the two marked ideals, so $\phi$ preserves them, since left multiplication by a unit preserves each ideal.

For each rectangular witness let $f_D$ be the induced quotient label map and write the column map as $P=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p&q\\r&s\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The column equations yield $q=0$ and row map $H=p f_D+r\epsilon(u)e_D'\epsilon$. Evaluating the second column at the unit gives $H(e_D)=s\epsilon(u)e_D'$. On $J_D^2/J_D^3$, the leading action on $e_D$ is $\epsilon(u)$ times the square of a nonzero tag scalar, hence $\epsilon(u)$. Comparing coefficients forces $s=p$, so $\det P=1$. In the nontransposed $C_V$ block the row sign is $\det g_V$: the scalar $p$ and the left unit have neutral determinant in even dimension, and the added rank-one term has determinant correction one because $f_D^{-1}e_D'$ lies in the radical. The transposed block contributes its neutral column determinant to the row mode. Finally, a block-diagonal representative can use the same witness on both regular copies, so their product contribution to the row sign is one.

Consequently the two tested signs are exactly $(\det g_V\det g_W,\det g_V)$, for a single original transporter with $\det g_U=1$. Conversely every such original transporter lifts to the same marked local algebra, two regular blocks, and rectangular blocks, since the canonical extensions fix the tag squares. The two physical signs are one precisely when all three original signs are one. All dense outputs and all calculations are polynomial in $N$ and $2^k$.

## Make a large protected mode small by castling

For concise input let $U^*$ be represented by its flattening image $L_T\subset V\otimes W$. If $0<bc-a<a$, use instead the annihilator $L_T^\perp$, represented with the other two spaces dualized. Both tensors are treated with the calibrated bases from the prior castling calculation. In each ambient flattening space start the basis with the ordered old image basis and complete to determinant one. The quotient then has its induced basis, and the annihilator its dual basis. A source transporter acts on the quotient with determinant $(\det g_U)(\det g_V)^c(\det g_W)^b=\det g_U$, since $b,c$ are even. Double annihilation gives the converse transporter, and the other two signs are unchanged. The new tested dimension is $k=bc-a$. If the replacement has deficient support in another mode, handle that by the next paragraph. If $bc-a=0$, both flattenings are invertible. Their determinant ratio is the first sign of every transporter, because $b,c$ are even. If the ratio is negative reject, and if it is positive, identity on $V,W$ and the induced identification on $U^*$ give a positive transporter. Handle each possible choice of the replaced mode by the same calculation.

For unequal support dimensions reject. If any mode has an unused support complement, that mode has an independent sign correction by mapping the complementary quotients at will. Work in adapted determinant-one bases, compute a two-character (or one-character or ordinary) problem on the supports, and use the existing equivalence of the line-subgroup tests to output one physical two-sign instance. If all modes are concise but some dimension is odd, the scalar stabilizers $(\lambda_1 I,\lambda_2 I,\lambda_3 I)$ with $\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3=1$ supply a nonzero sign direction. For mixed parity put the compensating scalar in an even mode. For all-odd dimensions the scalar subgroup is the full even-total plane. Test sign membership modulo that subgroup by the same existing comparisons. Zero tensors are already decided directly. These transformations require only polynomial time. They also cover the promised-defect instances where castling creates nonconciseness.

The complement branch can apply when all dimensions grow. For example, with even $m$ choose $(a,b,c)=(m^2-k,m,m)$ with positive even $k=O(\log m)$, and use a flattening whose image is spanned by all but $k$ matrix units, removing units without emptying any row or column. The tensor is concise, and the defect is small while none of the original side dimensions is logarithmic. These are example formats, not an assertion that every format admits such a reduction with polynomial output.

One can also repeat strictly decreasing even castling moves before applying the lock. At each step replace a positive side by a smaller positive flattening defect, using the calibrated bases just described. Each move preserves the entire sign set and decreases the sum of dimensions and the array length. There are at most polynomially many moves, and the support and zero-defect branches can be checked as they appear. If the process reaches a side of size $k$, the same time and output bound polynomial in $N,2^k$ applies. This is stronger than the displayed one-step bound on some formats. For instance reverse two castling moves beginning with $(k,m,m)$, where $k,m$ are even, $k=O(\log m)$, and $m$ grows. The resulting format is $(m^2-k,m(m^2-k)-m,m)$. Every side and every first-step complementary dimension is at least $m$ for all sufficiently large $m$, but the two decreasing moves reach the first dimension $k$. Any loss of conciseness in a descending move already permits the independent support sign correction.
## Dependencies and scope

The local-algebra and rectangular-block methods reuse the prior two-sign and exterior-lock constructions. The longer tag equating $z^{2k}$ to the top wedge is the change that neutralizes the whole protected quotient, so the algebra label can carry the remaining third-mode sign in the same output. The calibrated castling step then enlarges the covered domain from small physical mode to small flattening defect. The corresponding [published ordinary tensor-system compiler](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4) by itself does not assert preservation of physical determinant signs. The calculation here uses one actual pencil and verifies both tested signs directly.

A federated DAG search located the exterior lock, sign-wrapper and calibrated castling ingredients. It did not supply an unrestricted polynomial bound on the protected dimension, which can be large in all six choices. No unrestricted three-to-two collapse, novelty claim or Lean certificate is asserted.

An exact finite check enumerated generator images for the case $k=2$. Over $\mathbb F_3$ it found 11,664 automorphisms of the seven-dimensional locked algebra, all with determinant one on both the two-dimensional tangent and the full algebra. The proof by filtration establishes the statement for all positive even $k$.
