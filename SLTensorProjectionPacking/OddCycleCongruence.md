# Odd cycles in the matrix-block sign selector

The raw-recognizable [matrix-cycle family](EvenCycleMatrixSelectors.md) has a deterministic polynomial-time sign-profile algorithm without the even-cycle restriction. The additional case is an odd cycle with arbitrary invertible local matrices. All three determinant bits still come from the same transporter.

Compose the local transfer equations along the odd cycle, keeping exactly the same thin-edge sign bits at their two incident support vertices. Since an odd number of inverse transposes leaves one inverse transpose in the closing equation, it has the form
\[
 G=(-1)^\sigma A G^{-\mathsf T}B,\qquad
 G B^{-1}G^{\mathsf T}=(-1)^\sigma A.
\]
As in the companion proof, \(A,B\) are known invertible matrices and \(\sigma\) is the binary sum of the incident thin-edge contributions along the cycle. For each of its two candidate values, the displayed equation is ordinary congruence of one nonsingular bilinear form. Ivanyos and Qiao, Corollary 9, give constructive congruence for tuples of arbitrary matrices by passing to the mixed symmetric and alternating parts. The constructive algorithm in Theorem 7 is deterministic in polynomial time when the characteristic is fixed, so it supplies one \(G_\sigma\) if any exists over \(\mathbb F_3\). [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 7 and Corollary 9](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

The determinant possibilities for a successful congruence are the determinant of \(G_\sigma\) times the determinant image of the isometry group of the nonsingular form \(A\). Gerasimova, Horn and Sergeichuk, Theorems 2 and 4, show that this group has an element of determinant minus one exactly when the cosquare \(A^{-\mathsf T}A\) has an odd-sized Jordan block for eigenvalue \(1\). The condition is computable via the ranks of powers of \(A^{-\mathsf T}A-I\). Scaling \(A\) by \(-1\) leaves the cosquare unchanged. Transposing the convention for congruence does not affect determinants. [Gerasimova–Horn–Sergeichuk, Theorems 2 and 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3603)

The two recorded bits still range over an affine set. Given one solution \((\sigma_0,G_0)\), put \(H=GG_0^{-1}\). The equation becomes \(H A H^{\mathsf T}=(-1)^{\sigma+\sigma_0}A\). The pairs \((u,H)\) with \(H A H^{\mathsf T}=(-1)^u A\) form a subgroup of \(\mathbb F_2\times\mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb F_3)\). Taking the image on \(u\) and determinant bit proves the claim. The remainder of the global affine system and component assembly in the companion proof applies unchanged. Thus the entire matrix-component family, with odd or even large cycles, permits a safe projection computable from the raw pair.

A growing raw example uses one face of the dodecahedral graph as its large cycle. The graph has girth five and is 3-edge-connected. It is properly three-edge-colorable by Tait's reformulation of the four-color theorem for bridgeless cubic planar graphs. Give each edge of a fixed pentagonal face the same dimension \(m\ge3\), all other edges dimension one, and use the identity matrix at every large support vertex and coefficient one at every all-thin vertex. Each mode has a large edge since the five-cycle uses all three colors. For odd \(m\), all three dimensions are even. [Dodecahedral graph as a convex polyhedral graph and Balinski's theorem](https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1961.11.431)

The same off-diagonal isolation argument applies because the graph has no triangles or four-cycles. Every projected Lie matrix is therefore block diagonal in the graph description. Every skew-symmetric \(m\)-by-\(m\) matrix \(S\) is attained simultaneously on the large cycle edges by a Lie element with zero thin coordinates: at each large vertex the local Leibniz equation is \(S+S^{\mathsf T}=0\). Consequently the center of the Lie algebra acts on each large edge by matrices commuting with all \(E_{ij}-E_{ji}\). Such a matrix is scalar. To see this, for any three distinct \(i,j,k\), apply commutation to \(e_k\) to rule out the \((i,k)\) and \((j,k)\) entries, then apply commutation on the \((i,j)\) plane to equate its diagonal entries.

Scalar actions in the Lie stabilizer satisfy the unsigned edge-incidence equations of the graph over \(\mathbb F_3\). These distinguish two distinct edges. Otherwise the difference of their coordinate functionals would come from a vertex potential whose endpoint sums vanish on every other edge. Deleting those two edges leaves a connected graph with an odd cycle, since the twelve pentagonal faces cannot all be hit by two edges. The potential alternates signs along the remaining graph, and the odd cycle forces it to vanish on the whole connected graph. Hence the center's joint primary spaces are exactly the displayed graph blocks, and the raw recognition test succeeds after arbitrary rebasings. These examples show the odd-cycle extension is nonempty with growing absolute dimensions.

The proof is informal. It still uses recoverable graph-supported matrix data and does not establish the unrestricted reverse reduction.
