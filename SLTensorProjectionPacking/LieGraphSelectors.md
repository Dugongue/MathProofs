# Safe sign projections from Lie stabilizers and colored cubic graphs

Over \(\mathbb F_3\), identify determinant signs with bits. For an explicit tensor \(T\) let \(H(T)\subseteq\mathbb F_2^3\) be the image of its ordinary stabilizer. For an ordinary-isomorphic pair \(T,S\), the sign profile of the transporter set is a coset \(d+H(T)\). A nonzero \(u\in H(T)\) gives a safe projection with kernel \(\langle u\rangle\), since \(u\in d+H(T)\) implies \(0\in d+H(T)\). The fixed-field sign projection construction compiles this choice to one two-sign instance. An ordinary-nonisomorphic pair is automatically negative. [Prior sign-projection construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/SignProjections.md)

## A raw stabilizer selector

Compute the linear space
\[
 \mathfrak l(T)=\{(A_1,A_2,A_3): A_1\cdot T+A_2\cdot T+A_3\cdot T=0\}
\]
by solving explicit linear equations, where \(A_i\cdot T\) denotes application to the \(i\)-th mode only. Suppose each projected matrix space admits a common split diagonalization. This can be tested by commuting and split semisimplicity tests on its basis elements. Refine their eigenspace decompositions to obtain the full intrinsic joint weight subspaces on each mode. Any ordinary transporter conjugates the projected spaces and therefore carries the entire joint weight decomposition to its counterpart.

Use one binary variable \(x_{i,B}\) for each joint weight block \(B\) in mode \(i\). For every nonzero weight-block component \(T_{B_1,B_2,B_3}\), impose \(x_{1,B_1}+x_{2,B_2}+x_{3,B_3}=0\). Each solution gives a genuine tensor automorphism acting by \((-1)^{x_{i,B}}\) on the corresponding block. Its sign in mode \(i\) is \(\sum_B \dim(B)x_{i,B}\pmod 2\). Take the image \(P\) of this linear system in the three sign bits. If it is nonzero, find any nonzero image vector by linear algebra and use it as the projection direction. This uses the selected automorphism on the original tensor, so no local sign certificate is transferred to a different witness. All operations cost polynomial time in the dense input length.

This sign extraction also works on any explicitly found decomposition, without the commuting hypothesis. For example, try the primary subspace decompositions of the mode matrices of each element of a computed basis of \(\mathfrak l(T)\). For each resulting block system solve the actual support parity equations. Even in this variant, accepting a projection requires an automorphism verified against the original tensor. Covariance of the decomposition is useful for the following zero-image case.

## A family with unconditional polynomial-time sign-profile recovery

Suppose the intrinsic joint weight subspaces are all lines. In bases of those lines, interpret each nonzero coefficient as a support vertex and each coordinate variable as an edge joining its two support vertices. Require each coordinate variable to occur in exactly two coefficients and that the resulting graph be simple, with any number of connected components. Each vertex lies on one edge from each mode, making this a cubic graph with three edge colors and a perfect matching \(M_i\) of edges in color \(i\). This is a raw polynomial-time test. Under an ordinary tensor transporter, each mode map is monomial in the extracted bases. Its permutations give exactly an isomorphism of the colored support graphs, and its coordinate factors are signs which must satisfy the incidence equations below. If one endpoint fails any of these intrinsic tests while the other passes, ordinary TI also fails.

The entire sign profile is computable in deterministic polynomial time on this raw family. First compare two connected components. A colored graph isomorphism is determined by the image of one specified support vertex, since each incident edge color identifies a unique neighbor. Enumerate the target support vertices and propagate the chosen image along colored edges, checking consistency and bijectivity. Each candidate graph isomorphism gives explicit permutations of the three edge-color sets. In input frames, its coefficient equations are linear equations over \(\mathbb F_2\) in the signs of the diagonal factors, one variable per edge. The right side at a vertex is the sum of the source and its matched target coefficient bits. Solving those equations and taking the affine image of the three mode parities gives the full sign profile over that graph isomorphism, including its three known edge permutation signs.

With multiple connected components, construct the bipartite compatibility graph on source and target components using this connected comparison, with no sign requirement for the initial matching. Absence of a perfect matching gives no ordinary transporter. Otherwise take one witness from the matching and denote its full raw sign vector by \(d\), including the frame and component-permutation signs. Compute the global automorphism sign image \(H\) using the local automorphism sign images on the source components and the following exchange signs. For two components that are ordinarily isomorphic, exchange them using a computed isomorphism in one direction and its inverse in the other. In mode \(i\) the exchange sign is the common mode dimension of that pair modulo two: the two isomorphism determinants cancel, and the interchange of the equal-sized summands gives that dimension parity. Every support automorphism permutes components within ordinary-isomorphism classes. Factor that permutation into these exchanges, after which the remaining automorphism preserves each individual component. The listed sign generators therefore give exactly \(H\). The full profile is \(d+H\). This requires only polynomially many component tests and computations in \(\mathbb F_2^3\).

In particular one can always choose a safe direction in polynomial time for this family. If the pair is ordinarily nonisomorphic or the profile contains zero, any direction works. Otherwise choose a nonzero vector outside the computed profile. The original safe projection construction produces a single two-sign TI instance using that direction. Alternatively the profile decides SL-TI outright.

The connected comparison admits a separate extension beyond cubic support. Suppose the raw joint weight spaces are lines, the support triples are pairwise linear (two indices of distinct modes occur together in at most one nonzero coefficient), and each connected component is generated by at most a fixed number \(k\) of its coordinate labels under the rule that two known labels of a support triple determine its third label. Enumerating all seed sets of at most \(k\) labels recognizes this promise. A candidate support isomorphism is then determined by the images of a seed set: propagate the images along that closure rule and verify that the entire support is carried bijectively to the target. There are only polynomially many possible images because \(k\) is fixed. With one bit for every coordinate factor, the coefficient and determinant equations are again linear. The same component matching and exchange proof computes the full sign profile. The cubic graph case uses the different propagation rule along uniquely colored neighbors and needs no seed-closure condition.

## A parity cancellation for the connected zero-image branch
There is also a direct sign calculation that does not construct the colored isomorphisms. Let the support graph be connected and suppose \(P=0\). The parity equations are the cycle-space equations on the connected graph over \(\mathbb F_2\). The color functional \(x\mapsto\sum_{e\in M_i}x_e\) vanishes on the cycle space if and only if \(M_i\) is a cut, since the annihilator of the kernel of the incidence matrix is its row space. Thus find a vertex labeling \(\ell:V(G)\to\mathbb F_2^3\) such that each color-\(i\) edge flips exactly bit \(i\). It is unique up to one additive constant by connectedness. All eight fibers occur because one may follow a sequence of colors to reach any label, and all fibers have equal cardinality \(m\) by the matching bijections. Do the same for the target. A colored isomorphism \(\varphi\) necessarily satisfies \(\ell'(\varphi(v))=\ell(v)+c\) for one constant shift \(c\).

Fix a candidate shift \(c\). Denote the source and target matching bijections between neighboring fibers by \(E_{i,a}:V_a\to V_{a+e_i}\) and \(E'_{i,a+c}:V'_{a+c}\to V'_{a+c+e_i}\). Order each fiber arbitrarily. Set \(\eta_a=\operatorname{sgn}(\varphi|_{V_a})\) in these frames. The commutation of \(\varphi\) with matching edges gives
\[
 \eta_{a+e_i}=\eta_a\,\operatorname{sgn}(E'_{i,a+c})/\operatorname{sgn}(E_{i,a}).
\]
Known paths from zero in the cube express every \(\eta_a\) as \(\eta_0\) times a computable number. If a graph isomorphism exists, the formula holds along any such path. To order the color-\(i\) edges group them by their endpoints in the four fibers \(a_i=0\), using the known fiber orders. The edge isomorphism permutes these four blocks. Its action within an incident block uses \(\varphi|_{V_a}\) followed, if \(c_i=1\), by the known target matching bijection to its zero-side endpoint. Its full permutation sign is therefore determined from the raw data and \(c\), because the one unknown sign \(\eta_0\) occurs four times. The block permutation signs are known as well, with each block exchange contributing \((-1)^{m^2}\).

Write the signs of the source and target tensor coefficients at the support vertices as \(a_v\) and \(a'_w\) in bit notation. The coordinate factors of a transporter must solve
\[
 \sum_{e\ni v} x_e=a_v+a'_{\varphi(v)}
\]
over \(\mathbb F_2\). Connectedness makes this solvable exactly when the two total coefficient parities agree. This can also be seen by eliminating leaves in a spanning tree. For a solution, sum the equation over the vertices with \(\ell_i(v)=1\). Since \(M_i\) is exactly that cut, the resulting coordinate-factor sign is
\[
 \sum_{e\in M_i} x_e=
   \sum_{\ell_i(v)=1}a_v+\sum_{\ell'_i(w)=1+c_i}a'_w .
\]
It depends only on \(c\). Adding the already computed edge permutation determinant and the determinant ratio of the ordered extracted bases to the raw input frames gives the exact raw sign in mode \(i\). Thus, for each of the at most eight shifts, all determinant signs can be computed conditional on existence of a color-preserving graph isomorphism with that shift. Keep only shifts giving all three positive signs. The remaining decision is the disjunction of colored graph isomorphism comparisons with explicit fiber colors encoding each shift.

Here this constant disjunction can be encoded in one colored graph isomorphism instance. For comparisons \(A\sim B\) and \(C\sim D\), compare the two multisets of connected tagged wrappers \(\{(A,C),(B,D)\}\) and \(\{(A,D),(B,C)\}\). The two possible matches give precisely the disjunction. One wrapper has a distinguished central vertex, a distinguishable attachment vertex for each of the two input positions, and an adjacency to every input vertex of that position. Each position's internal graph and labels are retained. The wrapper is connected even when its inputs are not. At most eight shifts means constant iterations. A colored graph comparison is a bounded-order tensor system: the split coordinate algebra on its vertices forces a permutation, the adjacency bilinear forms for each edge color encode edges, and the vertex color indicator maps encode the marks. The scalar role is fixed by its unit. Compile that system to one ordinary TI pair. Append one unused dimension to each of the two tested modes to reduce ordinary TI to two-sign TI. The signs can be corrected independently on the two new complements, and an isomorphism of padded tensors restricts to one on their minimal mode supports. [Tensor-system compiler](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

Thus the connected zero-image branch can alternatively be compiled by just comparing a constant number of shifted colored graphs. The root propagation above computes the whole profile, including in the nonzero-image and disconnected cases.

## Growing examples and recovery from the raw array

Start with any connected simple bipartite cubic graph of girth at least five and edge-connectivity at least three, with a given proper three-edge coloring. In each mode use the graph edges of the corresponding color as a tensor basis. For every vertex place a nonzero coefficient at its incident triple. This tensor is concise, since support fibers are nonempty and any two coordinate indices of different modes occur together in at most one coefficient.

In these bases \(\mathfrak l(T)\) consists entirely of diagonal matrices. To see this let \(e,e'\) be distinct edges of one color. They are disjoint. At most one other graph edge joins their endpoint sets, since two such edges together with \(e,e'\) would give a triangle or a four-cycle. Choose an endpoint of \(e'\) whose other incident edges \(f,g\) do not meet \(e\). At location \((e,f,g)\) in the Lie equation, the \(e'\)-to-\(e\) entry of the first matrix contributes its nonzero source coefficient. All other contributions vanish because \(f,g\) meet at a unique vertex and neither meets \(e\). This forces that off-diagonal entry to vanish. The same argument applies in all modes.

The diagonal Lie equations say that the three edge coordinates sum to zero at every graph vertex. Changing signs on one bipartition's rows converts this to the usual oriented incidence kernel over \(\mathbb F_3\). Distinct edge coordinate functionals cannot coincide on that kernel: their difference would be in the incidence row space, giving a nonzero potential difference with support contained in two edges. Deleting any two edges leaves a connected graph, so such a potential would be constant. Thus the raw split Lie test recovers all individual edge lines even after arbitrary rebasings of the tensor.

For nonzero \(P\), use the graph with vertices \(L_j,R_j\) for \(j\in\mathbb Z_n\) and color matchings of shifts \(0,1,3\), where \(n\ge8\), \(4\mid n\) and \(3\nmid n\). The six oriented nonzero differences of shift values are distinct, excluding four-cycles. The shifts 0 and 1 form a Hamiltonian cycle. A cut of at most two edges would have to be one contiguous Hamiltonian interval, with no shift-3 edge crossing it. Choose the interval side of length at most \(n\). In the cyclic order \(L_j,R_{j+1},L_{j+1},R_{j+2},\ldots\), a first vertex of type \(R\) has its shift-3 mate five positions backwards. If the first vertex is of type \(L\) and the interval contains the next vertex of type \(R\), that next vertex's mate is four positions before the first vertex. Both are outside the interval. A singleton clearly has a crossing edge. This proves edge-connectivity at least three. One shift-3 edge plus a Hamiltonian path between its endpoints gives a cycle with odd parity in the third matching. Meanwhile each paired adjoint is diagonal by taking one Lie mode zero and negating another. Its coordinates are constant on the corresponding two-color cycles. These cycles have \(n\) or \(n/2\) edges in each mode, both even, so every paired adjoint unit contributes zero determinant bits. The centroid is scalar because one two-color cycle is Hamiltonian, forcing agreement of its two coordinates and then of the third one at each incident vertex. This example has even, unbounded dimensions in all three modes.

For zero \(P\), take a cyclic cover of the three-cube. Its vertices are \((a,t)\in\mathbb F_2^3\times\mathbb Z_m\), with \(m\ge3\) odd. An edge in color \(i\), from \(a_i=0\) to \(a_i=1\), increases the sheet index \(t\) by \(\sum_{j<i}2^{j-1}a_j\). Reverse edges subtract this value. Every cube square with directions \(i<j\) has sheet displacement \(\pm2^{i-1}\), so no four-cycle lifts to a four-cycle in the cover. In particular each square in the directions 1 and 2 has displacement \(\pm1\), which generates the sheet group and makes the cover connected. For edge-connectivity, a cut of size at most two must select equal counts from all eight base fibers. Otherwise a threshold set of those counts gives a cut of the cube with at least three edges, each contributing at least one crossing matching edge in the cover. For equal counts the number of crossing edges over each base edge is even, so the entire cut would be on lifts of one base edge. The cube remains connected after deleting that edge and retains at least one of the two edge-disjoint squares in directions 1 and 2. Its punctured cover therefore remains connected, a contradiction. All color matchings are cuts by the cube labeling itself, making \(P=0\). The three mode dimensions are \(4m\).

The organizing ideas are to preserve the candidate state in each exact transition and to distinguish a maintained invariant from a change in orientation. Here the sign calculations use the actual edge bijections of a shared candidate isomorphism and cancel the undetermined base parity rather than replacing that candidate. The empirical studies are not mathematical premises.
