# Simultaneous computability

**Proposed field.** Simultaneous computability studies how a single mathematical problem, with one shared input, changes its solvability and witness structure when tested in different mathematical environments. Its central problem is to determine which families of decision languages and witness transports can coexist in one uniformly presented family under a specified structural constraint.

## Objects and equivalence

Take an effectively presented small category $P$ of probes. For example, its objects can be finite extensions of a base field, with embeddings as arrows. A witness system consists of sets $W_e(p)$ for each input $e\in\mathbb N$ and each probe $p$, with uniformly computably enumerable witness codes. It has a computable transport map $W_e(p)\to W_e(q)$ for every arrow $p\to q$, respecting identities and composition. A probe may carry automorphisms that act on its witnesses. The existence profile is the linked family
$$
 S_p=\{e:W_e(p)\ne\varnothing\}\qquad (p\in P).
$$
It is monotone under probe arrows and retains the same input $e$ at every probe. The geometry or algebra of a realization can further constrain its witnesses. Possible constraints include one fixed target for a family of extension problems, equations of bounded degree and dimension, or prescribed behavior at local probes.

A simultaneous reduction from $W$ to $V$ uses one total computable map $f$ on inputs and uniformly computable witness maps $W_e(p)\to V_{f(e)}(p)$, natural in $p$, with $W_e(p)\ne\varnothing$ exactly when $V_{f(e)}(p)\ne\varnothing$. Mutual reduction gives one notion of equivalence. A stronger notion requires computable natural bijections on witness sets. Products and disjoint unions of witness systems with a shared probe category give coordinatewise intersections and unions of existence profiles. These operations apply to abstract witness systems. Whether a class of geometric realizations supports the operations is a theorem about that class.

A joint decision invariant already differs from the collection of one-probe invariants. For two probes with no arrows between them, consider the profiles $(\{0\},\{0\})$ and $(\{0\},\{1\})$. The two component languages agree in many-one degree at each probe, but the first profile cannot reduce to the second simultaneously. Input 0 would need to map to a target input accepted at both probes. Thus the separate degree calculations lose information even in a finite example. The theorem below realizes both profiles inside the same smooth arithmetic format.

## Finite-poset realization theorem

Fix a number field $K$ and a nonempty finite poset $P$. There are finite extensions $E_p/K$ with a $K$-embedding $E_p\to E_q$ exactly when $p\le q$. Given any family of computably enumerable sets $S_p\subseteq\mathbb N$ satisfying $S_p\subseteq S_q$ for $p\le q$, there is a computable family of affine hypersurface equations with coefficients in $\mathcal O_K$, one equation $H_e$ per input $e$, such that
$$
 H_e(\mathcal O_{E_p})\ne\varnothing \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e\in S_p .
$$
The generic fibers are smooth and geometrically integral. At every finite place of $K$ the equation has a point in the valuation ring, and it has points at the infinite places as well. The ambient dimension and total degree are bounded independently of $e$. For fixed $K,P$, these bounds can also be independent of the selected enumeration algorithms for $S_p$.

**Choose the probes.** Select distinct rational primes $\ell_j$, one for each $j\in P$, unramified in $K$. Their square classes in $K^\times/K^{\times 2}$ are independent, since the valuation at a place over $\ell_j$ is odd on $\ell_j$ and zero on the other chosen primes. Set
$$
 E_p=K\big(\sqrt{\ell_j}:j\le p\big).
$$
The fields embed over $K$ precisely under containment of their downsets by the Galois correspondence in the multiquadratic compositum. The fields are normal over $K$, so a $K$-embedding into $E_q$ exists exactly when $E_p$ is contained in $E_q$ inside the chosen algebraic closure. Fix $D=\max_p[E_p:K]$.

**Use a single definition of the integers.** Choose a finite Galois extension $L/\mathbb Q$ containing all the chosen fields. Koymans–Pagano gives a Diophantine definition of $\mathbb Z$ in $\mathcal O_L$. Express it in a fixed integral $\mathbb Z$-basis of $\mathcal O_L$, expanding every product using the integer multiplication table, and replace its tested scalar $x$ by $1\otimes x$. The resulting integer-coefficient system over $R=\mathcal O_{E_q}$ tests whether the original defining equations have a witness in $\mathcal O_L\otimes_{\mathbb Z}R$. For $x\in\mathbb Z$ the original witnesses map there by $a\mapsto a\otimes1$. Conversely the multiplication map $\mathcal O_L\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal O_{E_q}\to\mathcal O_L$, using the chosen embedding $E_q\hookrightarrow L$, carries any witness back to a witness in $\mathcal O_L$. Hence the same formula defines $\mathbb Z$ in all chosen $\mathcal O_{E_q}$. Use this formula on the quantified integer variables of a Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich representation of each $S_p$. For every $q$, the resulting system $F_p(e,\mathbf x)=0$ has a solution over $\mathcal O_{E_q}$ exactly when $e\in S_p$. A representation of a universal computably enumerable relation lets the enumeration codes enter as scalar parameters rather than change the format. [Koymans–Pagano, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01768v3#S1.SS2) · [Jones, Universal Diophantine equation](https://doi.org/10.2307/2273588)

**Join the conditions.** Choose a primitive element $\alpha_p\in\mathcal O_{E_p}$ for each $E_p/K$, multiplying by a nonzero integer if needed for integrality. Its monic minimal polynomial has coefficients in $\mathcal O_K$. A root in $\mathcal O_{E_q}$ exists exactly when $p\le q$, since a field root gives an embedding and every image of $\alpha_p$ is integral. Take the disjoint union over $p$ of the affine systems requiring this root and the equations $F_p(e,\mathbf x)=0$. Concretely, use distinct integer labels $a_p$ and an extra coordinate $t$ satisfying $\prod_p(t-a_p)=0$. Multiply each equation of branch $p$ by $\prod_{j\ne p}(t-a_j)$. At a point over any of the probe rings, the domain property selects exactly one label and allows cancellation of its nonzero multiplier. Pad unused coordinates if needed. This gives a single affine system $X_e$ with an integral point over $\mathcal O_{E_q}$ exactly if some $p\le q$ satisfies $e\in S_p$. That condition is $e\in S_q$.

**Smooth the whole family.** Apply the integral arithmetic-trace realization theorem at fixed $K,D$ to $X_e$. Its closed immersion preserves the integral point sets over every extension of degree at most $D$, compatibly with embeddings. It also supplies the required local points and bounds on the output dimensions and degrees. Thus all the languages are realized simultaneously by the same family of hypersurfaces. This proves realization of the existence profiles, together with the exact point witnesses of these constructed affine systems. Realization of arbitrary prescribed witness sets and transport maps is a separate problem. [Integral arithmetic-trace theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/0d5a4dcb98b86d977e6d7363cf612744d675b5a8/FourResearchPrograms/ArithmeticTraces.md)

**Independent channels of the same degree.** Take all the probes to be distinct quadratic extensions of $K$. They have no embeddings into one another over $K$, so the c.e. languages at these probes can be prescribed independently. For two such fields $E_0,E_1$, set $S_0=\mathbb N$ and take $S_1$ to be any c.e. language. The first degree with a field point is then exactly two at every input. To see this, neither primitive-element gate has a $K$-root, and the always-successful branch can be arranged without the existential integer-test variables. Bounded-degree trace preservation applies on field points as well. Hence the first field-point degree is constant even when integral-point decision at $E_1$ is arbitrarily complicated. Similarly the first integral-point degree is always two.

## Finite realizability without a global realization

Take the probe category of all finite extensions of $\mathbb Q$ with $\mathbb Q$-embeddings, and prescribe one fixed nonrecursive computably enumerable language $U$ at every probe. This is a monotone collection of ordinary c.e. languages. Every restriction to finitely many actual probe fields is realizable by a computable bounded-format family of the smooth integral equations above. For that finite list choose one common finite $L$, apply the same coefficient-expansion argument and one MRDP representation of $U$, and smooth the resulting system for a degree cutoff exceeding the degrees of the listed fields. The construction may change when the finite list changes.

There is no single computable family of integer-coefficient polynomial systems $H_e$ giving the prescribed profile on all finite extensions. If one existed, then
$$
 \{e:H_e(\overline{\mathbb Z})\ne\varnothing\}
 =\bigcup_{[E : \mathbb Q]<\infty}\{e:H_e(\mathcal O_E)\ne\varnothing\}
 =U,
$$
where $\overline{\mathbb Z}$ is the ring of all algebraic integers. The first equality holds because any finite tuple of algebraic integers belongs to the ring of integers of the number field it generates. The left side is recursive by van den Dries's decidability theorem for the ring of all algebraic integers. Every integer coefficient in the effectively generated system can be written in the language of rings using $0,1,+,-$, so evaluating its existential sentence needs no additional parameters or oracle. This contradicts the choice of $U$. The obstruction holds even without any smoothness, degree, dimension or local-solubility constraints on the putative whole family. [Van den Dries, Elimination theory for the ring of algebraic integers](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1988.388.189)

## Scope of the proposed field

Computable structure theory studies degrees of presentations and effective interpretability. Arithmetic geometry studies point functors and decision reductions over individual fields. Here the object to classify is the family of languages linked by one input and an actual environment-change operation. The finite-poset theorem shows universality of that object under geometric smoothness, local solubility, and bounded equation format. The two-probe example shows why reducing the languages individually cannot recover the simultaneous object. [Degree spectra](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~jsmiller8/Papers/compcone.pdf) · [Computable functors and effective interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02737) · [Integral affine smoothing](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/automorphism.pdf) · [Rational-point decision transfer](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/159)

The same definitions apply when probes are admissible enlargements of groups or localizations of spaces, with witnesses given by solutions of group equations or maps extending a boundary map. The conjectural field would study which simultaneous profiles transfer across these settings, which infinite profiles can fit in one finite format, and which transport structures and witness multiplicities can be prescribed in addition to solvability. The terminology is provisional. The finite-poset theorem has a written proof based on the arithmetic-trace theorem and published Diophantine inputs.
