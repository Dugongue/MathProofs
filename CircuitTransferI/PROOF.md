# Low-degree factors from bounded-order local arithmetic data

Let \(f\in\mathbb Q[X_1,\dots,X_n]\) be nonzero and given by an arithmetic circuit of size \(s\). Let \(h\) be an irreducible rational factor of total degree \(d>0\) and multiplicity \(a\). Then \(h\) has a rational arithmetic circuit of size \(\operatorname{poly}(s,n,d,a)\), with no dependence on \(\deg f\). The same construction works with directional Taylor access at rational centers and at scalar centers in extensions of degree at most \(d\). It requires Taylor order \(O(d^2+a)\). The assertion is nonuniform and the bound is arithmetic circuit size. It combines cofactor separation and Hensel lifting with rational coordinate descent and a resultant bound on the information required to recover the factor.

Choose rational coordinates \(x,y\) such that the coefficient of \(y^d\) in \(h(x,y)\) is a nonzero constant and normalize \(h\) to be monic. Because its \(y\)-degree is positive, \(h\nmid\partial_y h\). Thus \(h\) occurs exactly once in \(F=\partial_y^{a-1}f\), as follows by successively differentiating \(h^a\) times its cofactor and checking the factor of \(h\) at each step. Over \(\mathbb Q(x)\), \(h\) is coprime both to \(\partial_y h\) and to \(F/h\). Choose a rational \(x_0\) where both resultants remain nonzero. Let \(\alpha\) be a root of \(h(x_0,y)\) and \(E=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), of degree at most \(d\). Then \(F(x_0,\alpha)=0\) and \(\partial_yF(x_0,\alpha)\ne0\).

Put \(M=2d^2+1\). Lift the root \(\zeta(t)\equiv\alpha\bmod t\) of \(F(x_0+tx,\zeta(t))\) modulo \(t^M\), in \(E[x][[t]]\). Each new coefficient is obtained by dividing the negative of the existing residual coefficient by the fixed nonzero scalar \(\partial_yF(x_0,\alpha)\). This is the unique possible coefficient since adding \(u t^j\) changes the residual at order \(j\) by \(u\partial_yF(x_0,\alpha)\). The root obtained is also the unique lift of the root of \(h\) by the same argument with \(\partial_yh\).

Only bounded-order Taylor coefficients of \(f\) at \((x_0,\alpha)\) are needed. For example, with \(J_k(x,v)=[t^k]f(x_0+tx,\alpha+tv)\), the directional coefficient of \(F\) at order \(j\) is \(\partial_v^{a-1}J_{j+a-1}(x,v)\), by the chain rule. Replace \(v\) by \((\zeta(t)-\alpha)/t\) in these polynomials and use truncated arithmetic. The derivative in \(v\) can also be computed by interpolation since each jet has degree at most its order in \(v\). An explicit circuit computes the same data by truncated gate arithmetic. All previously constructed coefficients are shared across stages, so only polynomially many gates and scalar operations in \(E\) are used.

Recover \(h(x_0+tx,y)\) as the unique monic degree-\(d\) polynomial in \(y\), with other coefficients in \(\mathbb Q(x)[t]\) of \(t\)-degree at most \(d\), that evaluates to zero at \(\zeta(t)\) modulo \(t^M\). Existence follows from the actual \(h\). The determination is linear because all powers of \(\zeta\) are known. To prove uniqueness, let \(c\ne0\) be the difference between two candidates. It has \(y\)-degree less than \(d\) and \(t\)-degree at most \(d\). The polynomial \(h(x_0+tx,y)\) is irreducible in \(\mathbb Q(x,t)[y]\), since the coordinate substitution is invertible over \(\mathbb Q(t)\) and \(h\) is monic and irreducible. Consequently its \(y\)-resultant with \(c\) is nonzero, and the Sylvester determinant bounds the \(t\)-degree of the resultant by \(2d^2\). The Sylvester Bézout identity expresses the resultant as a polynomial combination of the two input polynomials over \(\mathbb Q(x)[t]\). Evaluating at \(\zeta(t)\) forces divisibility by \(t^M\) in \(E(x)[[t]]\), a contradiction.

Expand the linear system in a rational basis for \(E\), using polynomial-size coordinate arithmetic. A full rank minor exists by uniqueness. Cramer's rule using a fixed such minor gives rational circuits with a common denominator over \(\mathbb Q[x]\) for the coefficients. Set \(t=1\), restoring \(h(x_0+x,y)\). The resulting rational expression is a polynomial of degree at most \(d\). Choose a rational shift \(\beta\) in the \(x\)-coordinates where the common denominator is nonzero, substitute \(x=\beta+u(x-\beta)\), and expand numerator and inverse denominator through \(u\)-degree \(d\). The latter has nonzero constant coefficient, so it requires division only by a nonzero scalar. Setting \(u=1\) recovers the exact polynomial. Reverse the earlier coordinate changes and normalization. These nonuniform choices do not require an algorithm for discovering a factor or an expanded height bound for the scalar constants. The proof is informal.
