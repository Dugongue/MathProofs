# Arithmetic saturation makes the matrix multiplication orbit decidable

**Theorem.** The fixed halting-complete group of allowed transformations in the preceding construction can be chosen inside the image of an arithmetic group of paired integral two-by-two matrices. It has infinite index in that arithmetic group and the same image in every finite quotient. Its arithmetic saturation gives a decidable orbit problem for arbitrary multiplication tensors. On the hard targets themselves, saturation gives an explicit affirmative answer. One may retain preservation of the determinant quadratic form throughout this relaxation.

The fixed dimensions are four for the module of matrices and four in each mode of its multiplication tensor. The halting conclusion uses established integer computability and has no rational Diophantine premise.

## A free group that is already arithmetic

The two integral matrices
$$
a=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
b=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{pmatrix}
$$
generate a free group of rank two and a finite-index subgroup of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. Freeness follows by ping-pong. Finite index follows from the standard generators of the level-two principal congruence subgroup, which is generated by $a,b,-I$. For a prescribed rank $k\ge2$ set $d=k-1$. The subgroup given by exponent sum of $b$ equal to zero modulo $d$ has index $d$ in this free group and free basis
$$
a,\;bab^{-1},\ldots,b^{d-1}ab^{-(d-1)},\;b^d.
$$
Thus any free group rank needed by the fixed finite presentation below can be embedded as a finite-index subgroup $F$ of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$.

Choose a fixed finitely presented group $Q$ with no nontrivial finite quotients and a halting-complete word problem. The preceding packet constructs one using an integer-indexed graph group, Higman's embedding theorem and Bridson's universal group. Fix an epimorphism $\pi:F\to Q$ and put $R=\ker\pi$. The fiber product
$$
H=\{(u,v)\in F\times F : \pi(u)=\pi(v)\}
$$
is finitely generated by diagonal generators and finitely many defining relator pairs. [Previous construction and proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorTransportAndHalting/Profinite.md) · [Bridson](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08072)

The quotient set $(F\times F)/H$ has size $|Q|$. For example the cosets of $(1,s)$ are equal exactly when their images in $Q$ are equal, with a choice of left or right coset conventions. Since $Q$ has an undecidable word problem it is infinite. Hence $H$ has infinite index.

## No finite quotient distinguishes the restriction

For every homomorphism $\phi:F\to B$ to a finite group one has $\phi(R)=\phi(F)$. Indeed $\phi(F)/\phi(R)$ is a finite quotient of $Q$. Now let $\psi:F\times F\to C$ have finite image. Its restriction to $1\times F$ has the same image on $1\times R$. The image of $H$ therefore contains both the image of the second factor and the image of diagonal $F$, so it is the whole image of $\psi$.

In particular $H$ is dense in the full profinite completion of $F\times F$. If
$$
H\le K\le F\times F,\qquad [F\times F:K]<\infty,
$$
then $K=F\times F$. To see this, act on the finite coset set of $K$. The image of $H$ equals the image of $F\times F$, while every element of $H$ fixes the distinguished coset $K$. The full group fixes that coset, which proves equality. This statement concerns index in the ambient group. Finite index of $H$ in an enlarged group is a different condition.

The Zariski closure of $R$ in $\mathrm{SL}_2$ is all of $\mathrm{SL}_2$. Its closure is normal because $R$ is normal in the Zariski-dense group $F$. A proper normal algebraic subgroup of $\mathrm{SL}_2$ in characteristic zero is finite central. The nontrivial torsion-free subgroup $R$ cannot be contained in it. It follows that the Zariski closure of $H$ contains $1\times\mathrm{SL}_2$ and diagonal $\mathrm{SL}_2$, hence is the whole product.

## What this means for the actual multiplication table

On $V=M_2(\mathbb Z)$ use the determinant-one representation
$$
L_{u,v}(X)=uXv^{-1}.
$$
Let $\mu_s(X,Y)=XsY$. Under simultaneous change of basis,
$$
L_{u,v}\cdot\mu_s=\mu_{vsu^{-1}}.
$$
The $H$-orbit of $\mu_I$ is exactly the set of $\mu_s$ with $s\in R$. With all paired changes from $F\times F$, every $\mu_s$ for $s\in F$ is reached by the explicit map $L_{1,s}$. The same map lies in the closure of the $H$ image for every congruence modulus.

Each paired map preserves the fixed nondegenerate quadratic form $q(X)=\det X$. Consequently allowing all integral basis changes that preserve $q$ makes every hard target reachable, while keeping a fixed quadratic constraint on allowed changes. The connected algebraic image of the paired group is the split special orthogonal group of $q$, of dimension six. The general orthogonal group is also eligible for the algorithm below.

The ordinary real-topological closure of the integral group image adds no maps, since an integral subgroup is discrete and closed in the real matrix topology. Congruence closure and Zariski closure give the enlargements just analyzed.

## The algorithmic boundary

The arithmetic orbit theorem of Grunewald and Segal decides the following problem. Supply a rationally defined linear algebraic group, a rational representation on a finite-dimensional space, an explicitly given arithmetic subgroup in their effective sense, and two rational vectors. It decides whether the vectors are in the same subgroup orbit and can produce a transporter. No polynomial running-time assertion is used here. [Grunewald and Segal](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1980/112-3/p04)

Multiplication tables form a rational representation, namely $\operatorname{Hom}(V\otimes V,V)$ with action
$$
(g\cdot\mu)(x,y)=g\,\mu(g^{-1}x,g^{-1}y).
$$
This is regular on $\mathrm{GL}(V)$ and preserves the integral lattice for integral unimodular $g$. Therefore all the following choices of allowed basis changes have decidable orbit problems when supplied with the required effective descriptions.

* All integral points of a fixed rational algebraic subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}(V)$.
* Explicit arithmetic subgroups of that group, including fixed congruence restrictions.
* Integral basis changes preserving a specified finite collection of rational vectors, forms or tensors. Their simultaneous stabilizer is a rational algebraic group.
* In particular all integral isometries of $q(X)=\det X$, or its special orthogonal subgroup.

The input condition must describe an arithmetic group effectively. An arbitrary finitely generated subgroup of an algebraic group is not automatically arithmetic. That distinction applies directly to the hard group $H$. The theorem covers arbitrary pairs of fully specified tables under these arithmetic groups, beyond the explicit affirmative family above. Grochow, Qiao, Stange and Sun discuss the same classical arithmetic-orbit foundation in their work on tensors over commutative rings. [Their primary paper](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/189641/2/3717823.3718286.pdf)

This is an informal deduction specifying an exact decidable relaxation of the preceding undecidable problem. It makes no new priority claim for the arithmetic-orbit theorem.
