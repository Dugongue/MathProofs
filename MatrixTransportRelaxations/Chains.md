# Arbitrarily long chains of enlarged groups retain halting-completeness

**Theorem.** For every integer d ≥ 1 there are fixed finitely generated groups of determinant-one integral basis changes

    Γ₀ ⊊ Γ₁ ⊊ ··· ⊊ Γ_d ⊊ Λ ≤ SL₄(ℤ),

acting on the ordinary 4 by 4 by 4 multiplication tensor of M₂(ℤ), with these properties. Each orbit test Γ_i · T_I is halting-complete under computable many-one reductions. Every successive index is infinite. All Γ_i have the same image as Λ in every finite quotient of Λ and the same rational Zariski closure. Each projects onto both factors of the paired action X ↦ uXv⁻¹ and includes every simultaneous conjugation X ↦ uXu⁻¹ for u in the chosen finite-index free subgroup F of SL₂(ℤ). The ambient paired group Λ is arithmetic. Every table T_s in the reduction is reached under Λ by an explicit basis change.

The chain and finite generating lists are fixed once d is chosen. This asserts arbitrarily long finite chains with the same four-dimensional underlying module. It does not assert a single infinite chain with one fixed generating list.

**Proof.** Let U be a fixed finitely presented group with no nontrivial finite quotients and with computably enumerable complete word problem. The earlier packet constructs U from an integer Diophantine representation of halting, a recursively presented shift graph group, Higman's embedding theorem and Bridson's universal group. Set Q=U⁽ᵈ⁺¹⁾. A finite product of groups with no finite quotients also has no finite quotients. Its word problem is computably enumerable complete by including one U factor.

Choose a finite presentation Q=F/R. We may arrange that F is a free group of rank at least two, embedded as a finite-index subgroup of SL₂(ℤ). To do so start with the free finite-index Sanov subgroup generated by the two elementary unipotents with off-diagonal entries 2. The kernel of the exponent of its second generator modulo k−1 has rank k by Schreier's formula, and thus supplies every desired rank k≥2. Extra presentation generators if needed do not change Q.

Let π:F→Q be the quotient. For i=0,…,d, let M_i be the product of the first i factors of Q, and let N_i=π⁻¹(M_i). Define

    H_i = { (u,v) ∈ F×F : their images agree in Q/M_i }.

These groups are finitely generated. Generate them by the diagonal free generators, the second-coordinate relators of Q, and second-coordinate lifts of the finite generating lists of the first i factors. Those words normally generate N_i in F. As M_i is normal, diagonal conjugation generates precisely the missing normal conjugates.

The pair (u,v) acts by L_{u,v}(X)=uXv⁻¹. This action is faithful on F×F since F excludes −I and the only kernel of the paired SL₂ action consists of simultaneous central scalars. Its determinant on the four-dimensional matrix lattice is one. Define T_s(X,Y)=XsY. Transporting the whole table by L_{u,v} replaces s by vsu⁻¹. Hence the orbit from T_I is exactly {T_s : s∈N_i}. Put Γ_i=L(H_i) and Λ=L(F×F).

The quotient F/N_i is U⁽ᵈ⁺¹⁻ⁱ⁾, whose word problem is computably enumerable complete. A halting instance gives a word in one surviving factor, lift that word to F, and form its integer matrix s and table T_s. This is a computable reduction. Conversely enumerate words in the fixed allowed generators and compare the resulting tables. This proves halting-completeness on ordinary explicit arrays for each group.

Strictness and the index assertion follow from [H_{i+1}:H_i]=[N_{i+1}:N_i]=|U|, since U is infinite. The final ambient index is |U| as well. The quotient U is infinite because a finite group has a decidable word problem.

For every finite quotient of F, the images of N_i and F coincide. Indeed their quotient image factors through Q/M_i, which has no finite quotients. For any finite homomorphism out of F×F this gives the full image of 1×F from 1×N_i. Diagonal F then gives the whole image of F×F. Thus every Γ_i has the full finite quotient images of Λ. For Zariski density, the diagonal copy is dense in diagonal SL₂. The Zariski closure of N_i is a normal algebraic subgroup of SL₂, since N_i is normal in the dense group F. It is infinite and not central, so it is SL₂. Thus diagonal F together with 1×N_i has closure the full paired algebraic group. The image Λ is arithmetic, being the image of a finite-index subgroup of the integral points under the rational paired representation with finite kernel.

**How much has been relaxed.** Every step adds an entire infinite factor of allowable discrepancies between the left and right changes of basis. The ordinary tensor size stays fixed. The word problem of the remaining quotient gives the exact orbit test, so one surviving hard quotient is sufficient. The finite quotient and polynomial equality data of all the groups agree from the start. The difference concerns exact membership in the permitted subgroup.

These are informal deductions from classical group constructions and the earlier tensor transport formula. Publication priority for these restrictions has not been established.
