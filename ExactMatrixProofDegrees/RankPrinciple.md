# Exact degree and sharp sparse size for the Rank Principle

Let $P$ have shape $s$ by $r$ and $Q$ shape $r$ by $s$, with independent entry variables, over any field. The literal Rank Principle of Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She has just the equations $PQ=I_s$, for $1\le r<s$. Its minimum ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) degree is exactly $r+2$. For $s=r+1$ its minimum expanded PC size is $r^{\Theta(r)}$, counting monomial occurrences across proof lines. Yet for every $s>r$ it has a polynomial-size circuit IPS refutation, also in the Hilbert-like model. The degree comparison in GGPS Theorem 1.5 gives the earlier lower bound $r/2+1$. All statements here concern ordinary PC over the original variables. [GGPS, Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S1)

## Degree and the circuit certificate

Cofactor descent using the single equation family $PQ=I_s$ gives degree $r+2$, as detailed in the [rectangular inverse proof](ConjugacyDegree.md). In characteristic $p>0$, choose $q=p^a$ so large that $S=r+q\ge s$ and $2q\ge r+2$. Introduce a larger formal $S$-by-$r$ matrix and $r$-by-$S$ matrix. The top-left $s$-square block of their forward product uses only the first $s$ rows of the first matrix and first $s$ columns of the second. Each original equation is literally an equation of this larger system, with no new summands. Thus every original refutation replays verbatim after variable renaming even when the larger system also includes the reverse product equal to $I_r$. This larger two-sided system has degree $r+2$ by the companion theorem. In characteristic zero a bounded-degree proof descends to the rationals by linear-algebraic descent, since the input has integer coefficients. All coefficients in that finite rational proof can be reduced modulo a prime avoiding the finitely many denominators. Addition and legal variable multiplication are preserved, degrees can only drop, and the final one remains one. A proof below $r+2$ would therefore contradict the positive-characteristic result. This proves exactness in all characteristics.

Let $U$ denote the placeholder matrix for $PQ-I_s$. Use the first $r+1$ rows of $P$ and the first $r+1$ columns of $Q$ to form $Z$, the top-left square block of $PQ$ of size $r+1$. The circuit $\det(Z-U')-\det Z$ uses the corresponding block $U'$ of placeholders. At zero placeholders it vanishes identically, and at the actual inputs it is $\det I_{r+1}-\det Z=1$, because $\det Z=0$ by rank factorization as a polynomial identity. A division-free determinant circuit has polynomial size. The standard circuit simulation by Hilbert-like IPS also applies. In detail, each gate's difference between evaluation at the actual placeholders and at zero is expressed as a linear combination of axioms with circuit coefficients; for a product apply $ab-a'b'=a(b-b')+b'(a-a')$. This gives a placeholder-linear certificate with polynomial overhead. [Forbes, Shpilka, Tzameret and Wigderson](https://toc.cs.uchicago.edu/articles/v017a010/v017a010.pdf)

## Sparse ordinary Polynomial Calculus

For $s=r+1$, take a bipartite boundary expander $G$ with $r+1$ left vertices, $r$ right vertices, and absolute constant left degree bound $\Delta$. Mikša and Nordström give graphs of this shape for which the graph functional pigeonhole principle requires degree $\gamma r$ over every field, for a fixed $\gamma>0$. Its CNF says every pigeon uses a hole, no pigeon uses two holes, and no hole receives two pigeons. In ordinary PC we use indicator variables $x_{ij}=1$ for selected edges, together with the Boolean and collision equations and row axioms $\prod_{j\in N(i)}(1-x_{ij})=0$. Complementing the source variable convention if necessary preserves degree by affine substitution. [Mikša and Nordström, Theorem 5.8 and the passage before Theorem 5.9](https://doi.org/10.1145/3675668)

Pick any permutation $\pi$ of the $r$ hole labels. Substitute both $P_{ij}$ and $Q_{ji}$ by the edge variable $x_{i,\pi(j)}$ if that edge exists in $G$, and by zero otherwise. Work in the source quotient by the Boolean equations and the collision monomials. The normal basis consists of squarefree matchings, as one sees by evaluating on all partial matchings. The image of an off-diagonal target axiom is zero. The diagonal axiom becomes $\sum_{j\in N(i)}x_{ij}-1$, which has a degree-at-most-$\Delta$ proof from the row clause and row collisions. Expand the clause; terms of order two or higher vanish by a row collision.

At each line of a target proof take its image in this quotient and its unique matching normal form. Addition gives a source linear combination. Multiplication by a target variable becomes zero or one edge multiplication. Normalizing the result uses only Boolean equations to delete a repeated edge and collision equations to delete incompatible edges, each multiplied by a monomial of the current line's degree minus the axiom degree. Hence if every normal form has degree at most $k$, the source has an ordinary refutation of degree at most $\max(\Delta,k+1,2)$. No bound on the unnormalized degree or on source proof size is needed.

Choose $k=\lfloor c r\rfloor$ for a fixed $0<c<\min(\gamma/2,1/2)$. For each $\pi$, at least one original monomial occurrence must therefore have a surviving matching support of at least $k$ edges, for all sufficiently large $r$. A given original monomial specifies a fixed set of positions $(i,j)$ occupied by a positive power of either $P_{ij}$ or $Q_{ji}$. A row or column collision among distinct such positions makes survival impossible for every permutation. Fewer than $k$ distinct positions cannot give the required degree. Otherwise select $k$ of these positions, with distinct rows and distinct columns, before drawing $\pi$. For all selected positions to be graph edges, their column images must lie respectively in the prescribed rows' neighborhoods. The column images are sampled without replacement, so the probability is at most
$$
 \frac{\Delta^k}{r(r-1)\cdots(r-k+1)}
 \le \left(\frac{2\Delta}{r}\right)^k .
$$
The union over all monomial occurrences must have probability one. This gives $r^{\Omega(r)}$ sparse size with an absolute lower constant.

For the upper bound, PC closure in degree $r+2$ over $O(r^2)$ variables has dimension at most $\binom{O(r^2)+r+2}{r+2}=r^{O(r)}$. Recording independent line insertions, each generated by a bounded-degree combination and one variable multiplication, gives a proof whose number of monomial occurrences is polynomial in that dimension and the input size. This proves the upper bound. The combination with the circuit certificate gives a separation on the very same one-sided matrix equations in every characteristic, with no coordinate field equations.

The graph-expander degree bound and the one-sided cofactor upper bound are prior inputs. The one-sided degree lower bound here uses the exact two-sided transfer; the sparse lower bound uses the randomized relabeling of holes in a single fixed source graph. The arguments are written mathematical proofs.
