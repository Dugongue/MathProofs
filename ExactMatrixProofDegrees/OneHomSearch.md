# One Hom matrix for bounded-mode tensor certificates and finite-field search

**Theorem.** Fix $k$. Over **every** field, geometric nonisomorphism of $a\times b\times k$ tensors has a Hilbert-like circuit IPS certificate of size and total-product degree $(a+b+1)^{O_k(1)}$ in the ordinary tensor equations with inverse matrices. Over every explicitly represented finite field $\mathbb F_q$, ordinary tensor isomorphism in this format has a randomized, one-sided-error decision and transporter-search algorithm taking $(a+b+\log q+1)^{O_k(1)}$ bit operations. A successful answer includes a verifiable transporter. In the arithmetic negative case the [small-mode finite-field IPS theorem](FiniteModeIPS.md) gives a circuit certificate of the same polynomial size with field equations only for the forward small-mode map.

The all-field circuit bound strengthens the geometric coefficient-field scope in [the bordered-rank proof](BoundedModeIPS.md). The construction below also avoids its indecomposable decomposition. In the decision algorithm $q$ can grow independently of the matrix dimensions. The elementary enumeration bound stated by Grochow and Qiao is $q^{O(k^2)}\operatorname{poly}(a,b,\log q)$. Both their bound and the present theorem allow arbitrary dimensions in the other two modes. [Grochow and Qiao, Tensor Isomorphism-Completeness](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

## The transporter projector

Use the [notation for quiver representations and ideal consequence circuits](BoundedModeIPS.md). The mixing matrix is $z$, with designated inverse $z'$. Take $w=(w_0,w_1)$ in $s=2k^2$ dummy variables subject to the equations for mutually inverse matrices. Let $T_i(w_0)=\sum_j (w_0)_{ij}A_j$. One linear matrix suffices. Write $H(w_0)$ for the linear system in a pair of node maps $c=(c_0,c_1)$ with entries $T_i(w_0)c_0-c_1B_i$, and $H_B$ for the same system with $B_i$ in place of $T_i$. The node spaces in source and target have equal dimensions. Set $r=\operatorname{rank} H_B$. When $T(w_0)\cong B$, $H(w_0)$ has rank $r$, since its kernel is then linearly isomorphic to $\operatorname{End}(B)$.

Let $h=(h_0,h_1)$ denote the actual candidate isomorphism $B\to T(z)$ from the large-mode maps, using the designated inverses when required by the displayed action convention. The tensor and inverse equations give $H(z)h=0$ with bounded-degree polynomial-size entrywise ideal representations. On arbitrary node pairs set $D(c)=(h_0c_0,h_1c_1)$; on all arrow-error arrays let $E$ multiply each entry matrix on the left by $h_1$. Then $H(z)D=EH_B$ modulo the original equations. The maps $D,E$ and their displayed inverse maps have bounded degree. Thus every $(r+1)$-minor of $H(z)$ vanishes with a polynomial-size bounded-polynomial-degree determinant-difference certificate, using $H(z)\equiv EH_BD^{-1}$. Take as upper ideal $I$ the inverse equations in $w$ and a basis of the coefficient span of the $(r+1)$-minors of $H(w_0)$, chosen from the minors.

Choose scalar row and column selectors $U_0,V_0$ so $b_*=\det(U_0 H_B V_0)\ne0$. Set $U=U_0 E^{-1}$, $V_*=D V_0$, and
$$
G(w)=U H(w_0)V_*,\qquad b(w)=\det G(w),\qquad
J(w)=b(w)I-V_*\,\operatorname{adj}(G(w))\,U H(w_0).
$$
For $r=0$ use the empty determinant $b=1$ and $J=I$. At a geometric point where $\operatorname{rank} H\le r$ and $b\ne0$, the restriction of $U$ to $\operatorname{im}H$ is injective, since $UHV_*$ is invertible of size $r$. It follows that $HJ=0$, and $J$ is scalar multiplication by $b$ on $\ker H$. In particular its image equals that kernel. At the actual variables $b(z,z')\equiv b_*$, and $J(z,z')h\equiv b_* h$, by the previously proved identities.

Write $J(w)h$ as a pair of square matrices $P_0(w),P_1(w)$, with the same node dimensions as $h_0,h_1$. Use the dummy-parameter witness
$$
C(w,\xi)=b_*^{-(1+a+b)}b(w)\det P_0(w)\det P_1(w)
                 \det(h_0^{-1})\det(h_1^{-1}),
$$
where $\xi$ comprises the actual node variables. The last inverses are supplied variables. This normalization gives $C(z,z',\xi)\equiv1$ in the tensor ideal. For any $w$ in $X=V(I)$, the witness vanishes identically in $\xi$ unless $T(w_0)\cong B$: a nonzero value requires $b(w)\ne0$ and provides the two invertible node maps $J(w)h$ in the Hom kernel. This implication also holds for the polynomial over the rational function field in $\xi$, or by evaluation over the infinite algebraic closure. Conversely, at an isomorphism point one can substitute an actual transporting pair and its inverses to get the normalized value one.

All circuits, parameter degrees and actual-variable degrees here are polynomial in $a+b+k$. Apply the small-variable coefficient-span compression of the [geometric proof](BoundedModeIPS.md), with this $C$ in place of its former product. It uses only coefficient-vector linear algebra over the input field and the effective Nullstellensatz in a fixed number of *dummy* variables. Consequently the circuit and total-product degree descend directly over any base field, including imperfect fields. For finite fields the same replacement in the [Frobenius component proof](FiniteModeIPS.md) supplies the arithmetic certificate. In particular, there is no need to split endomorphism algebras or to norm rank tests in this proof.

## A polynomial degree bound for the small-mode transporter

Write $\mathcal T$ for the geometric locus in the inverse-pair parameter model where $T(w_0)\cong B$. It is empty or a closed coset of an algebraic subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_k$ by projection from the tensor transporter. It is also open in $X$. In fact for $w\in X$ existence of a tuple $\xi$ with $C(w,\xi)\ne0$ is exactly membership in $\mathcal T$, as just shown. Nonvanishing of some coefficient of $C$ expanded in $\xi$ is an open condition. Therefore $\mathcal T$ is a union of components of $X$ and is equidimensional when nonempty, by its coset description. Put $D_*=\max(2,r+1)$. The sum of the degrees of the geometric components of $X$, and hence of $\mathcal T$, is at most $(D_*+1)^{s+1}$ by affine Bézout, allowing components of different dimensions. Write $\Delta=(D_*+1)^{s+1}$.

## Uniform randomized search over finite fields

All field operations below take polynomial bit time in the specified field representation and $\log q$. Use a large-field branch for $q$ exceeding a fixed polynomial in $N=a+b+1$ (at fixed $k$). The bounds in the next paragraphs specify sufficient polynomial thresholds. For smaller $q$ enumerate the $q^{k^2}$ matrices in the small mode and for each invertible candidate use the polynomial-time module isomorphism algorithm for the corresponding two quiver representations. Its output can be converted into the two node maps and checked in the original tensor equations. This branch has polynomial bit complexity at fixed $k$. [Module-isomorphism algorithms and the enumeration reduction](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

In the large-field branch compute the upper minors' coefficient span probabilistically without listing all minors. Let $m$ be the small-parameter monomial count through degree $r+1$ and $t=r+1$. Sample $m$ independent row and column matrix pairs of the appropriate sizes with entries uniform in $F$, and compute $\det(U H(w_0) V_*)$ densely in the dummy parameters for each pair, using compression to $t\times t$. Skip these tests if the matrix dimensions are smaller than $t$. All samples belong to the span by Cauchy–Binet. Whenever a current span is still proper, an $F$-linear functional on coefficient vectors vanishes on the sampled span but detects one true minor. Applied to a new sample it is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $2t$ in the random entries, since selector matrices realize that particular minor. The chance of failure to enlarge the span on any step while the span is proper is at most $2tm/q$ by Schwartz–Zippel and a union bound. If every step enlarges until completion, $m$ samples suffice. Take $q>100(2tm+1)$. Computing the dense coefficient vectors is polynomial at fixed $s$, for example by determinant circuits over the ring truncated at the required total degree.

Independently choose random constant matrices $U,V_*$ at size $r$ and a random vector $c$ in the unrestricted node-map space. Form the *numerical-chart* version of $J(w)$ with these $U,V_*$ and put $P(w)=b(w)\det((J(w)c)_0)\det((J(w)c)_1)$. Each point of $X$ with $P(w)\ne0$ gives an actual geometric transporter by the projector argument. For any fixed *rational* isomorphism point, the determinant of the random compression is nonzero except with probability at most $2r/q$. Conditional on this, $Jc$ is uniform on the Hom space up to its fixed nonzero scalar. The determinant product on this space is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $a+b$, since the chosen point has an isomorphism over $F$. Thus $P$ keeps the point with probability at least $1-(2r+a+b)/q$. Impose also $q>100(2r+a+b+1)$. The open $P\ne0$ is recorded by one new variable $u$ and equation $uP=1$.

Try each possible dimension $d$ from zero through $s$. Cut by $d$ independent uniformly random affine linear equations in $w$ (both the constant and linear coefficients are random). If the resulting system of upper equations, inverse equations, cuts and $uP-1$ is zero-dimensional, find a rational solution if one exists. From a solution, take the small-mode matrix there and verify the two remaining maps by a module-isomorphism computation. Equivalently the evaluated $Jc$ itself supplies these two maps and they can be verified directly, even if a preceding randomized upper-span step erred. If the system is positive-dimensional or empty, skip it. The algorithm only accepts after checking a transporter in the original input.

For the success estimate, let $d$ be the true dimension of $\mathcal T$ on a yes input. A rational transporter point lies on a Frobenius-stable geometric component $Z$ which is defined over $F$ and absolutely irreducible, since the components are disjoint. All components have dimension $d$. For $d=0$ this component is a rational point. For $d>0$ the effective Lang–Weil estimate gives $\#Z(F)\ge q^d/2$ once $q$ exceeds a polynomial in $s,\Delta$. For example the Cafure–Matera error is bounded by $(\Delta-1)(\Delta-2)q^{d-1/2}+5\Delta^{13/3}q^{d-1}$ when $q>2(d+1)\Delta^2$, with the degree of the component used in the formula. The case of a linear component is immediate. A larger cutoff such as $1000(s+1)(\Delta+1)^{10}$ suffices for this estimate and the generic cuts below. [Cafure and Matera, Theorem 7.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0405302)

For each point of $Z(F)$ the probability of surviving the $d$ random equations is $q^{-d}$, and the inclusion events for distinct points are pairwise independent because the affine forms include independent constant terms. If $Y$ is the number of survivors, then $\mu=\mathbb E Y\ge1/2$ and $\mathbb E Y^2\le\mu+\mu^2$. Hence $\Pr[Y>0]\ge\mu/(1+\mu)\ge1/3$. The geometric slice of the whole transporter has dimension zero or is empty except with probability at most $d\Delta/q$. At every cutting step a hyperplane contains a specified positive-dimensional component with probability at most $1/q$, by testing a fixed geometric point. There are at most $\Delta$ such components at each step by Bézout, and every proper cut decreases their dimensions. When a rational point survives, choose the first such survivor in a fixed order for the analysis; it is independent of the randomly sampled chart. Its survival of $P\ne0$ has the bound above. Thus with absolute positive probability the span is correct, the open slice is finite, and it contains at least one rational point. Repeat independent trials a constant number of times to obtain success probability at least $2/3$, or an arbitrary prescribed constant. A no input can never pass the transporter check.

All finite-system computations involve $s+1$ variables, polynomial degrees and polynomially many equations. In a fixed number of variables Gröbner bases and the required linear algebra take polynomially many field operations in these degree and input bounds, for example by homogenization and bounded-degree row reduction. For a finite affine quotient, adjoin each equation $x_i^q-x_i$ after reducing it in that finite quotient, using binary powering. The remaining system has a rational point precisely when the ideal is proper. Recover coordinates by factoring univariate eliminants and retaining a root that keeps the ideal proper at each step. Quotient dimensions and the number of coordinates are polynomially bounded here. Finite-field univariate factoring is randomized polynomial in degree and $\log q$. Standard verification of factors or a time cap gives a bounded-error search, and every returned tensor transporter is independently checked. These operations use no loop of length $q$ in the large-field branch. [Dubé, Gröbner basis degree bounds](https://doi.org/10.1137/0219053) · [Cantor and Zassenhaus, finite-field factorization](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1981-0606517-5)

This is a randomized search theorem and a circuit-size existence theorem. The two claims do not assert deterministic polynomial-time construction of the IPS certificate. Both arguments are written proofs.
