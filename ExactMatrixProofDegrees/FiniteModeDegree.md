# Finite field tensor certificates with field equations in one mode

Let two tensors have format $a\times b\times k$ over $F=\mathbb F_q$. Add $z^q-z$ for the $k^2$ entries of the forward map in the third mode to the ordinary three-mode action and inverse equations. This system has a solution over $\overline F$ exactly when the tensors are isomorphic over $F$. Every negative instance has a Hilbert-like IPS refutation over $F$ of circuit size $q^{k^2}\operatorname{poly}(a+b+k,\log q)$ and total-product degree $O(a+b+k^2q+1)$. This also bounds ordinary PC degree. The field equations on the two large modes and their inverses can all be omitted. The certificate is polynomial size for fixed $q$ whenever $k^2\log q=O(\log(a+b+2))$.

**Descent after fixing one mode.** Write the slices as $A_i,B_i$, matrices from $F^b$ to $F^a$. For fixed invertible $T\in M_k(F)$ the remaining action is equivalence of the two tuples $(\sum_j T_{ij}A_j)_i$ and $(B_i)_i$ by one pair of bases. These are representations of the $k$-arrow Kronecker quiver. Isomorphism after field extension reflects isomorphism over $F$. For a direct proof a geometric isomorphism uses coefficients in some finite extension $L/F$ of degree $s$. Restrict scalars in the isomorphism over $L$ to obtain an isomorphism of $s$ copies of the first representation with $s$ copies of the second. Krull–Schmidt gives equality of every indecomposable multiplicity before scaling by $s$. Conversely an $F$-transporter solves the system. The field equations constrain $T$ to $F$, and the inverse equations keep it invertible.

Even a projective condition suffices for this decision statement. Replacing those field equations by $z_i^q z_j-z_i z_j^q=0$ for all pairs of matrix entries forces the nonzero matrix of the small mode to be a scalar multiple of an $F$-matrix. Divide by a nonzero entry and use the equations on each ratio. Absorb the scalar in a large-mode map and apply the same descent. The size and degree estimates below use the entrywise equations on the small mode.

**Certificate at a fixed value.** Singular $T$ gives a degree-one contradiction from $Tz'-I$ by a constant left-kernel vector. For invertible $T$ in a negative instance the two quiver representations are nonisomorphic over $F$. Choose a type $S$ of unequal multiplicities $u,v$ in their Krull–Schmidt decompositions. The endomorphism algebra of the sum of the two representations, modulo its Jacobson radical, has a simple factor $M_{u+v}(K)$ for this type, with $K$ a finite division ring and thus a finite field extension of $F$. The quotient ignores cross-type maps and reduces each same-type block modulo its local radical. Its degree $[K:F]$ is bounded by the dimension of the endomorphism algebra over $F$, at most $4(a+b)^2$.

The forward and reverse node maps from the original variables satisfy linear intertwining equations in bounded degree. For instance from $P A_i^{(T)}Q^T=B_i$ multiply by the supplied inverse transpose of $Q$ and correct by $Q^{-1}Q-I$. Use both inverse pairs to derive the reverse linear constraints in the same way. Project each map onto its actual Hom space along an $F$-linear complement. The entrywise differences are scalar combinations of the linear constraints. Replacing the maps and inverses in their two products gives identities for the projected products modulo the original ideal, with explicit polynomial-size coefficient circuits and bounded total-product degree. These operations are multiplication of boundedly many linear matrices and linear algebra on polynomial-dimensional spaces. Applying the chosen quotient gives both rectangular inverse equations on linear matrices over $K$ of dimensions $u,v$. The determinant on the larger side vanishes identically through the smaller dimension, but is one on the identity there. Determinant telescoping gives a Hilbert-like certificate with degree $O(1+\max(u,v))=O(a+b+1)$ and polynomial circuit size. For the size bound use multilinearity in the columns and division-free cofactor circuits to express a difference of determinants through the entrywise ideal representations. Taking the coefficient of $1$ in an $F$-basis of $K$ containing $1$ descends the certificate with polynomial overhead. This constructs an algebraic certificate given the constant decomposition data; the size statement does not charge an algorithm for finding these data. **Reassembly in the original variables.** Let $z=(z_1,\dots,z_{k^2})$. For each $T\in F^{k^2}$ take the certificate $1=\sum_i H_{i,T} E_i(T)$ just constructed, where $E_i$ denote the action and inverse equations and the remaining variables are suppressed. The coefficients use no forward $z$. Put
$$
e_T(z)=\prod_j(1-(z_j-T_j)^{q-1}).
$$
Then $\sum_T e_T=1$ identically by univariate interpolation and multiplication. Telescope $E_i(T)-E_i(z)$ along the small-mode coordinates into multiples of $z_j-T_j$. These differences are linear in the individual coordinate changes, of bounded total degree, with polynomial-size coefficients. The identity
$$
e_T(z)(z_j-T_j)=-(z_j^q-z_j)\prod_{i\ne j}(1-(z_i-T_i)^{q-1})
$$
holds as a formal polynomial. Multiply the fixed-$T$ certificate by $e_T$, use these identities to replace its input equations by the original ones plus small-mode field equations, and sum over $T$. Total-product degrees increase by at most $k^2(q-1)+O(1)$. Repeated squaring computes each indicator factor with $O(\log q)$ operations. Sharing the fixed certificates gives the stated circuit bound. This is a finite-field complement to the unrestricted-geometric bounded-mode theorem. It is a written proof.
