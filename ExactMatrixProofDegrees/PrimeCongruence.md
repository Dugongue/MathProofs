# Prime signatures and proof degree for a single bilinear form pair

Let $A,B$ be rational square matrices of side $n$ that are congruent over an algebraic closure of the rationals. There is an effective integer $M$ and a list of rational squareclasses $d_l$, each with a positive integer multiplicity $m_l$, such that, for every prime $p$ outside $M$, the reductions are congruent over $\mathbb F_p$ exactly when **all** $(d_l/p)=+1$. Here the index $l$ is a positive block length and $\sum_l l m_l\le n$. The same assertions hold with singular or nonsymmetric inputs.

At a negative prime let $h$ be the least multiplicity $m_l$ at a failed sign. For ordinary Polynomial Calculus on $XAX^T=B$, $XY=YX=I$ and the coordinate field equations, the minimum degree is between $\max(p,c h)$ and $\max(4,ph)$ for an absolute $c>0$ and sufficiently large $h$ (decreasing $c$ covers smaller $h$). In particular it is $\Theta_p(h)$ for fixed $p$. Every such instance has a polynomial-size Hilbert-like circuit IPS certificate in the field-operation model. A family below attains large sparse lower bounds at each prescribed signature in its displayed coordinates.

## Reduction to rational quadratic characters

Use the Horn–Sergeichuk congruence classification in characteristic different from two. Its three types are singular nilpotent Jordan blocks, paired Frobenius blocks, and cosquare-root blocks indexed by an irreducible reciprocal polynomial $f$ and a length $l$. In the last case the multiplicity space carries a nonsingular Hermitian form over $K=\mathbb Q[x]/f$ for the involution $x\mapsto x^{-1}$. Equivalence for that type is equivalence of these Hermitian forms. Scalar extension to the algebraic closure fixes the geometric primary and singular block data and forgets the arithmetic form classes. In particular geometrically congruent rational matrices have equal geometric multiplicities. This also follows by reading the common roots, with lengths, in the two pencils. [Horn and Sergeichuk, Theorem 2](https://www.math.utah.edu/~ptrapa/math-library/horn/horn_sergeichuk.pdf) · [Classification over finite fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3142)

The center involution is the identity precisely when the root satisfies $x=x^{-1}$ in $K$, so $f=x-1$ or $f=x+1$. Of these two choices precisely $x-(-1)^{l+1}$ admits an individual cosquare-root block of size $l$. The opposite parity is a paired type with no Hermitian class parameter. For the individual block the involution is trivial, $K=\mathbb Q$, and the auxiliary form is an ordinary nonsingular symmetric form of dimension $m_l$. Define $d_l$ as the determinant ratio of the two multiplicity forms in squareclasses. Choosing a different reference cosquare root rescales both multiplicity forms equally. This leaves their ratio unchanged.

To obtain $M$, use rational decompositions from the classification on both inputs. Clear denominators and exclude 2, determinants of the changes of basis, discriminants of the distinct **separable irreducible** polynomials, their nonzero constant terms, and nonzero mutual resultants (also with the reciprocals when distinct). For every nonexceptional polynomial exclude its nonzero values at $1,-1$. Here nonexceptional means different from $x-1,x+1$; the other value for each exceptional polynomial is already a unit away from 2. Also invert the nonzero norms needed for the inverses of the multiplicity forms and for the units used in the cosquare-root decompositions, together with the numerators and denominators of the $d_l$. Equivalently take the product of these finitely many nonzero integers with the denominators of the rational classification identities. Rational factorizations, their change-of-basis witnesses, and nonzero norms can be computed, or found by enumeration using the classification. The claim needs no polynomial height bound for this choice of $M$.

Upon reduction each center is then finite étale. A reciprocal primary away from $\pm1$ either splits into exchanged factors or gives on a preserved residue field a nonidentity involution. Indeed identity would make the image of $x$ equal its inverse and give a root at $\pm1$. On exchanged factors every nonsingular pair is equivalent, and on a preserved field with nonidentity involution all nonsingular Hermitian forms of the same rank are equivalent by surjectivity of the finite-field norm. Paired and singular blocks carry no additional obstruction. This can also be seen by splitting the rational canonical blocks with the coprime local primary idempotents. The absence of repeated roots of the underlying irreducible $f$ ensures each resulting Jordan length is the old $l$. In the exceptional scalar types finite-field symmetric forms of the same rank are classified by their determinant squareclass. This proves the criterion and the side budget. An effective finite exception list is required even for a geometrically congruent pair. Agreement at every good prime need not imply rational congruence.

## Degree of a negative reduction

Fix an odd finite field of size $q$ and a geometrically congruent pair in this classification. The arguments below apply with its own scalar types and discriminants. The polynomial degree is invariant under independent constant changes of input bases, since this induces invertible linear substitutions of $X,Y$. Each transformed field axiom is also a linear combination of the original coordinate field axioms by the $q$-Frobenius identity.

For the upper bound select a failed scalar type with multiplicity $h$. In the transpose-pair quiver for $(A,A^T)$, the associated indecomposable has residue field the base field. The quotient block of its endomorphism algebra modulo the radical is $M_h(\mathbb F_q)$, and the self-dual multiplicity form on this block is the symmetric form in the congruence classification. The projection argument for congruence gives $Z^T H_1 Z=H_2$ in degree at most three, with $Z$ an $h$-by-$h$ matrix of linear forms. In detail the inverse axioms turn $XAX^T-B$ and its transpose into linear intertwiner equations for $(Y^T,X)$ in degree three. Project onto the actual linear space of intertwiners along a constant complement. Projection errors are linear combinations of the derived equations, so both quadratic isometry identities for the projection follow in degree three. The semisimple quotient of the morphisms on the selected indecomposable gives $Z$ and the two symmetric multiplicity forms. This is precisely the scalar-center case of the general argument. [Transpose-pair projection and determinant certificates](ALL-ODD.md)

Write $t=\det Z$ and $d=\det H_2/\det H_1$. Determinant telescoping gives $t^2=d$ in degree $2h$ after the preparation. Telescoping the coordinate field equations gives $t^q=t$ in degree $qh$. Since $d^{(q-1)/2}=-1$, multiplying the first equation by the geometric-sum polynomial gives $t^q=-t$, hence $t=0$ and then $1=0$. The bound is $\max(4,qh)$. Telescoping can use determinant difference circuits, and exponentiation and the geometric sum use repeated doubling. Replacing each placeholder difference at a gate by its linear combination of the actual axioms gives Hilbert-like coefficients with polynomial circuit overhead. [Linear IPS simulation](https://toc.cs.uchicago.edu/articles/v017a010/v017a010.pdf)

For the lower bound, put the pair into canonical coordinates over the finite field. Take identical blocks on all types that agree. On each failed type use $C_l\otimes I_{m_l}$ and $C_l\otimes\operatorname{diag}(\nu,1,\ldots,1)$ in one order or the other, for a common nonsquare $\nu$ and a fixed invertible cosquare root $C_l$. This uses exactly the finite-field classification just described. Reduce a purported proof to the symmetric pair $I_h,D_h$ with $D_h=\operatorname{diag}(\nu,1,\dots,1)$. Set its two maps to $Z,W$. In every failed type put a repeated copy of $Z\oplus I_{m_l-h}$ and its inverse-variable counterpart $W\oplus I_{m_l-h}$, tensoring with $I_l$. Exchange $Z,W$ on reversed types. Use identities on agreeing summands and zeros on cross terms. Both inverse systems then map to zero or original inverse entries. Each forward congruence maps to entries of $ZZ^T-D_h$ times fixed scalars, or to entries of $WD_hW^T-I_h$ times scalars. For the reverse direction, putting $H=WZ-I_h$ gives
$$
WD_hW^T-I_h=H+H^T+HH^T-W(ZZ^T-D_h)W^T .
$$
These entries have degree-four PC derivations of polynomial sparse size. Field axioms specialize to original field axioms or zero. Thus a degree-$D$ proof yields a proof for the hard symmetric pair in degree at most $\max(D,4)$. The expander lower bound on that pair gives $D\ge ch$ for large $h$. Every proof must use a field axiom because the input is geometrically congruent, which gives $D\ge q$ outright. In the displayed normal coordinates the substitution maps variables to single variables or constants. If a sparse proof has size $L$, its specialization therefore yields a proof of size at most $L\operatorname{poly}(h+1)$, including simulations of the reverse equations. This gives $h^{\Omega(h)}$ sparse size for large $h$ in these coordinates. Arbitrary dense basis changes preserve the degree result. The sparse transfer uses the specified presentation. [Diagonal sparse separation](PROOF.md) · [Underlying expander parity bound](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/lineargaps/journalfinal.pdf)

## Joint profiles and an exact side bound

Let $r$ be the binary rank of the $d_l$ in rational squareclasses, enlarge $M$ by any needed factors of 4 so all corresponding quadratic characters are characters modulo $M$, and let $r_{<e}$ and $r_{\le e}$ be the ranks on indices with multiplicity less than and at most $e$, respectively. The density of good primes at which the minimum failed multiplicity is exactly $e$ is
$$
2^{-r_{<e}}-2^{-r_{\le e}}.
$$
The density of congruence is $2^{-r}$. Indeed the vector of quadratic signs is uniform on the binary dual of the squareclass span by the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions. The event of passing a sublist is precisely annihilating its span. If the displayed difference is positive, impose positive signs on the earlier span and a negative sign on one additional independent class at level $e$. This is a coset of index $2^{r_{<e}+1}$. Dirichlet GRH supplies a good prime $O(4^{r_{<e}+1}\log^2(2M))$ with that minimum failed multiplicity. An individual full compatible signature has a prime $O(4^r\log^2(2M))$. The bounds use only Dirichlet characters of the rational field. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.3595) · [Verification of Dirichlet GRH](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

For $b,r\ge1$, the least matrix dimension supporting $r$ independent scalar discriminant obstructions **of multiplicity at least $b$** in a single geometrically congruent rational pair is exactly
$$
b\,r(r+1)/2.
$$
Each length supplies at most one scalar obstruction. Choosing $r$ independent ones uses at least the $r$ smallest distinct positive lengths and at least $b$ copies at each. Conversely take the direct sums over $1\le l\le r$ of $C_l\otimes I_b$ and $C_l\otimes\operatorname{diag}(d_l,1,\dots,1)$ with distinct positive prime $d_l$. These rational matrices have the required geometric congruence and exactly these independent obstructions. At every good prime their displayed coordinates can be put into the hard presentations by diagonal rescaling of individual multiplicity coordinates only. This preserves monomial counts up to constants. Every one of the $2^r$ profiles occurs, and every negative profile requires $b^{\Omega(b)}$ sparse size for large $b$ alongside a polynomial-size circuit IPS certificate. Identity padding allows larger sides if desired by using identical extra scalar blocks; the exact minimum needs no padding.

The theorem concerns reduction to finite fields. A rational isometry problem also sees the real place and arithmetic at exceptional primes. General proofs here are written mathematics.
