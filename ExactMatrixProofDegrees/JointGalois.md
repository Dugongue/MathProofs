# The exact joint-prime threshold for products of Galois fields

Let $F$ be a number field and $A$ a product of finite Galois extensions. For a finite set $S$ of good primes of $F$, define $J_A(S)$ as the sum of $[E:F]$ over the original field-factor occurrences $E$ in which **every** prime of $S$ splits completely. A good prime is unramified in the fields under comparison. The empty test gives the dimension.

**Exact threshold.** Fix a common field-factor degree $d\ge1$ and a testing order $t\ge0$. If there is no prime $p$ with $p^{t+1}\mid d$, tests with $|S|\le t$ determine every such algebra at every dimension. Otherwise let $\ell$ be the smallest prime with $\ell^{t+1}\mid d$. The first dimension at which different algebras agree on all these tests is
$$
d\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1+\ell^i).
$$
Collisions occur at every larger multiple of $d$. The assertion allows arbitrary Galois groups of the factors and different groups for different factors. For $t\ge1$ the extremizers can be equivalent over every completion of $F$. The same results hold for their ordinary multiplication tensors.

For example, at common degree $72=2^3 3^2$ the first collisions at orders one and two occur in dimensions $216$ and $1{,}080$, respectively, and three-prime tests always suffice. At degree $18=2\cdot3^2$ the first one-prime collision has dimension $72$. At every squarefree degree, one-prime tests suffice even for nonabelian Galois factors.

**Mixed degrees.** For a finite group $Q$, its elementary abelian $p$-rank is the largest $r$ for which $(C_p)^r$ embeds in $Q$. Let $t$ bound every such rank in every factor Galois group of two input algebras. Then equality of their tests through order $t$ implies isomorphism. Factor degrees may vary both within and between the inputs. In particular, products of Galois fields whose groups have cyclic or generalized quaternion Sylow subgroups are determined by one-prime tests.

## Normal subgroups and the Frattini quotient

Embed both products' fields in a common finite Galois extension $L/F$, and put $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)$. Each field occurrence gives a normal subgroup $H$ of $G$, its pointwise stabilizer. Global isomorphism classes of Galois subfields correspond to individual normal subgroups. For Frobenius representatives $g_1,\ldots,g_j$, complete splitting of all primes in the fixed field of $H$ means $\langle g_1,\ldots,g_j\rangle\le H$. Chebotarev realizes every tuple of conjugacy classes by distinct good primes. Membership in $H$ is constant on conjugacy classes.

Write $\delta_H$ for the difference of multiplicities of $L^H$. For equal degrees divide every test by $d$ and use coefficients $c_H=\delta_H$. For mixed degrees keep the weights and set $c_H=[G:H]\delta_H$. For any subgroup $U$ put
$$
a(U)=\sum_{H\ge U}c_H.
$$
Vanishing of the tests is equivalent to vanishing of $a(U)$ for every subgroup generated by at most $t$ elements. If the products differ, an inclusion-maximal $H$ with nonzero coefficient has $a(H)=c_H\ne0$. Choose a subgroup $K$ minimal under inclusion with $a(K)=b\ne0$. The empty test ensures $K\ne1$. The assumed equalities imply that $K$ needs more than $t$ generators.

For $U\le K$ set $b_U=\sum_{H\cap K=U}c_H$. Its upper sums $\sum_{U\ge V} b_U$ are $a(V)$ for $V\le K$. They vanish except at $V=K$. Möbius inversion gives $b_U=b\mu(U,K)$. In particular $b_U=-b$ at every maximal proper subgroup of $K$. Each such $U$ therefore occurs as $H\cap K$ for a normal subgroup $H$ of $G$. Consequently **every maximal subgroup of $K$ is normal in $K$**.

Let $\Phi$ be the intersection of the maximal subgroups of $K$. As $K/U$ has no proper nontrivial subgroup for each maximal $U$, it is cyclic of prime order. Thus $T=K/\Phi$ embeds diagonally in a product of cyclic groups of prime order. It has the form $\prod_p(C_p)^{r_p}$. Lifts of generators of $T$ generate $K$ since every proper subgroup lies in a maximal subgroup. Hence $\max_p r_p>t$.

On $\Phi\le U\le K$ the Möbius values are those of the subgroup lattice of $T$, a product of the subspace lattices of its primary parts. In the $p$-part the Möbius value in codimension $j$ is $(-1)^jp^{j(j-1)/2}$. Indeed the expansion of $\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(1+p^ix)$ has coefficient $\binom{r}{j}_p p^{j(j-1)/2}$, by the Gaussian binomial recurrence. Evaluating at $-1$ proves the Möbius recursion in every nontrivial interval and evaluating at $1$ sums the absolute values. Thus
$$
\mu(\Phi,K)\ne0,\qquad
\sum_{\Phi\le U\le K}|\mu(U,K)|
=\prod_{p:r_p>0}\prod_{i=0}^{r_p-1}(1+p^i).
$$
In particular some input factor has $H\cap K=\Phi$. Under $K\to G/H$ the image is $T$. A primary rank of this subgroup exceeds $t$. This proves the group-sensitive assertion by contradiction. In the equal-degree case $p^{t+1}\mid |T|\mid d$ for such a prime $p$. If each product has $m$ factors, then
$$
2m\ \ge\ \sum_H|c_H|\ \ge\ \sum_{\Phi\le U\le K}|b_U|
\ \ge\ 2\prod_{i=1}^t(1+\ell^i),
$$
because $|b|\ge1$ and $p\ge\ell$. Multiplying by $d$ proves the lower bound.
## Attainment over every number field

Put $r=t+1$. In $\mathbb F_\ell^r\oplus(\mathbb F_\ell^r)^*$ take the two families of maximal totally singular subspaces for the split quadratic form $q(x,f)=f(x)$. They have $\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(1+\ell^i)$ members each, and every subspace of dimension below $r$ lies in equal numbers of members from the two families. These are the established extremal subspace trades. Every totally singular hyperplane of a maximal singular space lies in exactly two maximal spaces, one from each family. Counting flags gives equality at lower dimensions. The standard count of the two families gives the product above. For $r=1$ use the two singular lines. [Krotov, Mogilnykh and Potapov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.3792) · [Krotov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.02592)

Use the space as character group of an elementary abelian Galois extension. A singular $r$-space $W$ indexes the degree-$\ell^r$ fixed field of $W^\perp$. Its contribution to the $j$-test is $\ell^r\prod_{a=1}^j1_{W^\perp}(g_a)$. Its Fourier coefficient on a character tuple is $\ell^{r(1-j)}$ if all tuple entries lie in $W$, and zero otherwise. Thus the two families give different algebras with identical tests through order $r-1$.

Over $\mathbb Q$ choose $2r$ rational primes $s_i$ successively, unramified in $F$. Require $s_i$ to split completely in the compositum of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2\ell})$, all earlier cyclic degree-$\ell$ fields, and the splitting fields of $X^\ell-s_j$ for $j<i$. Chebotarev for the identity class supplies such primes. Use the unique cyclic degree-$\ell$ field in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{s_i})$. Every earlier $s_j$ splits in the new field since $s_j$ is an $\ell$th power modulo $s_i$. The new $s_i$ splits in all earlier fields. They are totally real, independent by separate ramification, and their compositum $K_0$ has cyclic decomposition groups everywhere. Inertia at the $s_i$ generates its Galois group. Every nontrivial subfield of $K_0$ ramifies at a chosen prime, so $F\cap K_0=\mathbb Q$. The extension $K=FK_0$ has the required group over $F$, and its decomposition groups embed in those of $K_0/\mathbb Q$. For $t\ge1$ the equal singleton tests are equal permutation characters. On a cyclic subgroup their values at all powers of a generator determine cycle counts by divisor inversion. Thus the initial two products are locally isomorphic even at ramified places.

For $h=d/\ell^r>1$ take a fresh prime $s\equiv1\bmod h$ unramified in $FK_0$ and the cyclic degree-$h$ subfield of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_s)$. Every nontrivial subfield of the latter field ramifies at $s$. Consequently its compositum $C$ with $F$ is cyclic of degree $h$ over $F$ and linearly disjoint from $K$ over $F$. Tensor both products by $C$. Every resulting field factor is Galois of degree $d$. Each joint count at a good tuple becomes the original count times $h$ if all primes split in $C$, and zero otherwise. Distinct factor fields persist because $(EC)\cap K=E$ for $E\subseteq K$. Local isomorphisms persist by tensor product. For $h=1$ use $C=F$. Adjoin identical degree-$d$ factors to reach each larger allowable dimension.

For commutative unital algebras ordinary multiplication tensor equivalence gives $R(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$ for three invertible linear maps. Both $P(1)$ and $Q(1)$ are units because multiplication by each is bijective. The map $x\mapsto R(x)/(P(1)Q(1))$ is then an algebra isomorphism.

## The minimum distinguishing order without prime search

For input products with Galois factors $E_1,\ldots,E_s$, allow repetitions and attach signed weights $w_i$ equal to the factor degree on the first side and its negative on the second. Define
$$
M_j=\sum_{i,k=1}^s\frac{w_iw_k}{[E_iE_k:F]^j}.
$$
For $j$ independent uniform elements of the common Galois group, the expected square of the joint-count difference is $M_j$. Indeed the $(i,k)$ cross term survives exactly when every element lies in $H_i\cap H_k$, of relative size $1/[E_iE_k:F]$. Thus $M_j>0$ exactly when a distinguishing $j$-tuple exists. Tuples of conjugacy classes can be realized by distinct good primes.

Compute $M_j$ for $0\le j\le\lfloor\log_2(\max_i[E_i:F])\rfloor$, handling empty products directly. The mixed-degree theorem shows this covers every nonisomorphic pair. The first positive entry gives the exact least order. Vanishing throughout certifies isomorphism. For any fixed base number field $F$ and dense irreducible defining polynomials, this takes deterministic polynomial bit time. To compute $[E_iE_k:F]$, factor the defining polynomial of $E_k$ over $E_i$. Since the fields are Galois over $F$, each resulting irreducible factor has degree $[E_iE_k:E_i]$. Standard algorithms factor these polynomials in polynomial time. The compositum degrees are at most $[E_i:F][E_k:F]$, so all powers, common denominators and sums have polynomial bit length. No full compositum or Galois group has to be constructed. [Lenstra, polynomial factorization over number fields](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/2541) · [Landau](https://doi.org/10.1137/0214015)

## A shortest prime witness from Dirichlet GRH

Suppose $F=\mathbb Q$ and every input factor is abelian. Let $D$ be the product of the absolute discriminants of the integral defining orders. An explicit tuple achieving the minimum distinguishing order can be computed in deterministic polynomial bit time, with all primes good and at most $C\log^2(2D)$ for an absolute $C$.

Keep just factor occurrences splitting at all primes selected so far. When $a$ further primes are required, scan good primes $p$ in increasing order and tentatively filter at $p$. Accept the first for which $M_{a-1}>0$ on that filtered list. At $a=0$ the signed total weight is nonzero. At a good prime complete splitting is tested by divisibility of $X^p-X$ by the reduction of the monic defining polynomial, using modular powering. Integral primitive polynomials and defining orders of polynomial bit length may be obtained by rescaling primitive elements.

For the prime bound, freeze all but the next coordinate of a witnessing tuple in the group. The resulting nonzero function $h=\sum_\chi b_\chi\chi$ is an integral difference of regular quotient characters for selected fields. Let $M=\sum |b_\chi|^2$, $L=\sum|b_\chi|$, and $W=\sum|b_\chi|\log q_\chi$ for their primitive conductors. Then $L\le M$ by integrality and $W\le\log D$ by the conductor-discriminant formula. Every nontrivial primitive conductor is at least three, so Cauchy–Schwarz gives $L^2\le(1+W/\log3)M$.

For a fixed nonnegative smooth weight supported in $(1/2,1)$ with positive integral $I$, the smoothed Dirichlet prime sum, weighted by $\log p$, for a primitive character $\eta$ on GRH has main term $Ix$ for the trivial character and zero otherwise. The error is $O(\sqrt x\log(2q_\eta)+\log(2D))$ on deleting the primes dividing $D$. The zero terms have this bound by Mellin decay and the unit-interval zero count. Higher prime powers cost $O(\sqrt x)$ and the excluded primes cost $O(\log(2D))$. Expanding the weighted sum of $|h(p)|^2$ gives main term $IxM$ and error
$$
O\!\left(\sqrt x(L^2+LW)+L^2\log(2D)\right),
$$
since $q_{\chi\bar\psi}\le q_\chi q_\psi$. At $x=C\log^2(2D)$ the sum is positive for a sufficiently large absolute $C$. Hence some good prime in the scan preserves a witness and passes the exact residual test. An optimal tuple produced this way has no repeats since a repeated prime could be discarded. The stopping rule uses exact rational arithmetic and needs no numerical value of $C$. This proves the polynomial-time witness bound without enumerating tuples. The cutoff uses only Dirichlet GRH over the rationals. [Bach and Sorenson](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-96-00763-6) · [Dirichlet GRH verification](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

Here the threshold applies at every order and degree for Galois factors, the rank criterion covers mixed degrees, and the energy formula computes a shortest distinguishing order without constructing the full Galois group.
