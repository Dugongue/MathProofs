# Small prime panels for a large genus of nonabelian fields

For every $n=16m\ge16$ the rational lower-bound family of the [nonabelian genus theorem](NonabelianGenus.md) can be constructed with a common panel of **fewer than $n$ primes**, each $O(n\log(2n))$. Each product has $m$ Galois field factors with group $D_8\times C_2$. The family has at least $\exp((n/16)\log n-O(n))$ global classes, equivalent at every completion, and pairwise counts on this one panel recover each class. Under Dirichlet GRH the common defining data for all the classes and the probe primes can be constructed deterministically in time polynomial in $n$. All varying ramification is supported on $O(n)$ explicitly known primes of size $O(n^2\log^2(2n))$. The log absolute discriminant of each product is $O(n^2\log(2n))$. The polynomial time is for a common construction parametrized by the complete mappings, with each selected member specified by its own list of values. The family itself has superexponentially many members.

## A fixed dihedral field

Take $L=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt[4]{97})$, with group $D_8$ of order eight. The real quartic is Eisenstein at 97, adjoining $i$ doubles the degree, and the rotations and complex conjugation act as the dihedral group. Its center consists of the identity and the square $c$ of a rotation. It ramifies only at 2 and 97. At 2 the integer 97 is a fourth power by Hensel's criterion at 1, since $v_2(1-97)=5>2v_2(4)=4$. The only remaining local extension is quadratic from $i$. At 97 the fourth roots of unity are rational locally and the radical extension is cyclic of order four.

As in the genus theorem, choose $U=\mathbb F_4^a$ with $4^a$ the least power of four at least $m$. Put $R=2a+1$, $T=(C_2)^R$ and $C=\langle c\rangle\times T$. Then $s=|C|=4\cdot4^a<16m=n$. For each of the two central conjugacy classes of the fixed group $D_8$, choose the first $s/2$ good odd primes with that Frobenius in $L$. Assign to the primes with a given central Frobenius all the distinct labels of $T$, one per prime. This design uses each element of $C$ exactly once. Each of those two fixed conjugacy classes has prime density $1/8$ by Chebotarev for the fixed extension, so the entire list $q_1,\dots,q_s$ lies below $O(s\log(2s))$. This prime enumeration uses no GRH. Checking each prime uses only elementary modular powers: a good $q$ has central Frobenius exactly if $q\equiv1\bmod 4$ and 97 is a quadratic residue modulo $q$. Among these primes Frobenius is the identity exactly if 97 is a fourth power.

## Interpolating the labels at the probes

Scan primes $p\equiv-1\bmod(8\cdot97)$ distinct from every $q_i$. Associate the binary row with entries defined by $(-p/q_i)=(-1)^{u_i(p)}$. All these rows for $p=O(s^2\log^2(2s))$ span $\mathbb F_2^s$ under Dirichlet GRH. Indeed, for any nonempty subset $I$ of the probes, the product of the Legendre symbols at the $q_i$ for $i\in I$ is a nontrivial character on units modulo their product. Since each $q_i\equiv1\bmod4$, using $-p$ or $p$ has the same effect. Over modulus $M=8\cdot97\cdot\prod_i q_i$ impose the fixed progression at $8\cdot97$ and require product sign $-1$ on the subset. These requirements are compatible by CRT. They specify a coset of index $2\varphi(8\cdot97)$, a constant independent of $s$. The least-prime-in-a-coset bound on Dirichlet GRH supplies a prime $p=O(\log^2(2M))$ in that coset, automatically avoiding every excluded prime. As $\log(2M)=O(s\log(2s))$, no nonzero functional vanishes on all the listed rows. This proves the span assertion. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.3595) · [Dirichlet GRH verification](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

Collect $s$ independent rows by exact binary elimination. For each coordinate of the assigned labels of $T$ solve for its target length-$s$ signature as a sum of selected rows. Let $d_j$ be the integer product of the corresponding $-p$, and $E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{d_1},\ldots,\sqrt{d_R})$. The signatures show that the $d_j$ are independent squareclasses, since all labels of $T$ appear. A nonzero class in their span is a product of distinct selected $-p$ modulo squares and ramifies at a selected prime $p$. The field $L$ is unramified at every such $p$, hence $E\cap L=\mathbb Q$. Thus $\operatorname{Gal}(LE/\mathbb Q)=D_8\times T$. The computed Frobenius elements at the $q_i$ are exactly the designated central elements. Both the prime scans and the linear algebra take polynomial time. There is no call to an effective Chebotarev estimate for a varying nonabelian extension.

## Agreement at every completion

Put $G=D_8\times T$ and identify $C$ with its center. Form the plane collections of the nonabelian genus construction in $C^*$, with the commutator $c$ evaluated nontrivially in the first tag layer. The field-factor kernels $H=W^\perp$ are subgroups of $C$. The corresponding $G$-sets have the same permutation character across all collections, all factors are nonabelian of degree 16, and distinct collections yield distinct global classes.

It suffices at each place to check that its decomposition group $P$ has **cyclic intersection with $C$**. In fact the stabilizer in $P$ of every point in the constituent $G/H$ is the same subgroup $P\cap H$, which is enclosed in $P\cap C$. If the latter is cyclic, group elements generating each of its subgroups give the cumulative fixed-point counts for all possible stabilizers. Divisor inversion recovers the number of points with each stabilizer and division by the orbit lengths recovers the orbit multiplicities. Equality of the full characters therefore gives local isomorphism. This argument permits a noncyclic decomposition group in the global splitting field.

At primes unramified in $LE$ and at infinity, $P$ itself is cyclic. At 2 and 97, each $-p$ is a local square: it is $1\bmod8$ and $1\bmod97$, respectively. The local field from $L$ is cyclic at both places. At a selected prime $p$, the Frobenius of $L$ is a reflection, since $p$ is unramified and $p\equiv3\bmod4$ acts nontrivially on $i$. Every element of $D_8$ outside its rotation subgroup is a noncentral involution. Locally $L$ is thus the unramified quadratic extension, and all remaining local fields are quadratic. The composite is multiquadratic of rank at most two over $\mathbb Q_p$, since odd $p$ has a local squareclass group of order four. The image of $P$ in $D_8$ is generated by the noncentral reflection. Consequently $P\cap C$ is the kernel of this projection, of order at most two. This includes cases where several radicands share the ramified prime $p$.

Ramification at the selected odd $p$ is tame. For a degree-16 Galois factor the discriminant exponent at a tamely ramified $p$ is at most 16. The contributions at 2 and 97 are bounded by constants because the local fields come from the fixed $L$. Thus each factor has logarithmic discriminant $O(s\log(2s))$ and multiplication over $m$ factors gives the stated estimate.

## Recovering a class from its pair counts

The good probes realize every $g\in C$ once. Let $J(g,h)$ denote their joint count, using a singleton on the diagonal. The contribution of an occurrence of $W$ is $16\,1_{W^\perp}(g)1_{W^\perp}(h)$. For two independent characters $u,v$ of $C$, its normalized Fourier coefficient at $(u,v)$ is precisely $1$ if $u,v\in W$, and zero otherwise. Indeed $[C:W^\perp]=4$, and the product of the two normalized singleton coefficients has the factor $1/16$. Hence $\widehat J(u,v)$ gives the exact multiplicity of the field indexed by $\langle u,v\rangle$. The panel and the inversion are the same for the entire family. This is a concrete use of Dirichlet GRH in the polynomial-time construction of a probe panel for nonabelian Galois fields. The structural genus and measurement statements have written proofs.
