# Sharp sparse Polynomial Calculus size for matrix congruence over odd finite fields

Let $F=\mathbb F_q$ have odd characteristic. Consider the ordinary PC system $XAX^T-B=0$, $XY-I=YX-I=0$, and $z^q-z=0$ for every entry variable of $X,Y$, for arbitrary $n$-by-$n$ matrices over $F$.

**Theorem.** For every noncongruent pair, there is a PC refutation over $F$ of degree at most $\max(4,2qn)$, and a polynomial-size circuit IPS refutation over $F$. For fixed $q$ the expanded PC size is $n^{O_q(n)}$. The [diagonal lower-bound family](PROOF.md) gives matching worst-case degree and sparse size on an infinite sequence of dimensions. Thus for fixed odd $q$ the sparse PC worst case is $n^{\Theta_q(n)}$, though every negative instance has a polynomial-size circuit IPS certificate. This extends the previous symmetric upper bound to singular and nonsymmetric matrices. The result treats the finite-field equation version of the matrix congruence half of [Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She Open Question 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3). The exact geometric system in that question omits the field equations. The argument below is informal.

## Reduction in the original variables

Use representations of the two-arrow quiver with both arrows from vertex 0 to vertex 1. The representation $M_A$ has arrow maps $(A,A^T)$ and $M_B$ has $(B,B^T)$. Duality $D$ swaps the two vertex spaces, takes transposes, and exchanges the arrows, and both representations have the identity self-dualities $s_A,s_B$ under the displayed bases. A morphism $f:M_A\to M_B$ is an isometry when $D(f)s_B f=s_A$ and $f$ is invertible. For node maps $(Y^T,X)$ this is precisely the original congruence and inverse requirement. Conversely an isometry has node maps of this form because the identity self-dualities force its node maps to be inverse transposes.

Let $G=XAX^T-B$. The identity
$$
 XA-BY^T=GY^T-XA(YX-I)^T
$$
and its transpose-input counterpart derive both linear intertwiner constraints in degree three. Let $\widetilde f$ be the coefficient-linear projection of $f=(Y^T,X)$ onto the actual Hom space. The entries of $f-\widetilde f$ are linear combinations of the derived linear constraints. In both quadratic inverse equations for $f,D(f)$ replace $f$ by $\widetilde f$ and $D(f)$ by its dual. The differences are products of already available linear lines and linear forms. This gives actual quadratic lines
$$
 D(\widetilde f)\,s_B\,\widetilde f-s_A=0,
 \qquad
 \widetilde f D(\widetilde f)-I_{M_B}=0,
$$
using the displayed identifications in the second expression. The preparatory degree remains three, and the expanded size is polynomial.

**Case 1, different underlying quiver representations.** By Krull–Schmidt there is an indecomposable type with unequal multiplicities $a,b$ in $M_A,M_B$ over $F$. In the endomorphism algebra of their direct sum, the corresponding simple factor of the radical quotient is $M_{a+b}(K)$ for a finite extension $K/F$. This follows by taking each same-type block modulo the local endomorphism radical and deleting different-type blocks. A composition through a distinct indecomposable type cannot be a unit in the original local ring, and the residue division ring is finite and hence a field. Apply this linear quotient to the prepared inverse equations. The result is rectangular linear matrix polynomials $U,V$ with $UV=I_a$ and $VU=I_b$ as quadratic lines, under a choice of ordering. Take the equation on the larger space. Its product determinant vanishes identically since the inner dimension is smaller. Determinant telescoping gives a refutation of degree at most $\max(3,2\max(a,b))\le\max(3,4n)$ over $K$. The field extension has degree polynomial in $n$, since the residue field is a quotient of a subspace of the endomorphism algebra. The same determinant difference with placeholders gives a polynomial-size IPS circuit by upgrading the preparatory lines to circuits in the original placeholders.

**Case 2, equal underlying representations.** Choose a quiver isomorphism $\phi:M_A\to M_B$ over $F$. Put $E=\operatorname{End}(M_A)$, use the involution $g^*=s_A^{-1}D(g)s_A$, and set $P=s_A^{-1}D(\phi)s_B\phi$. Then $P$ is invertible and self-adjoint. For $g=\phi^{-1}\widetilde f$ the available prepared equality is $g^*Pg=1$. Let $J$ be the Jacobson radical, preserved by the involution. There is a solution by a unit in $E$ if and only if there is one in $E/J$. In the reverse direction lift a unit quotient solution arbitrarily to a unit $g_0$. Put $T=g_0^*Pg_0=1+j$ with $j\in J$. This is self-adjoint, and a polynomial $b$ in $T$ with $b^2T=1$ exists by solving the equation one power of $j$ at a time. At each step the derivative of the square equation at constant term $1$ is $2$, a unit, so coefficients through the nilpotence index suffice. Then $b=b^*$ and $g_0b$ is the desired unit. This argument is valid in small odd characteristic.

On two simple factors swapped by the involution, write it as $(a,b)^*=(\theta^{-1}(b),\theta(a))$ for an anti-isomorphism $\theta$. The corresponding entries of $P$ have the form $(u,\theta(u))$. The choice $(u^{-1},1)$ solves the equation. On a preserved factor $M_m(K)$, the involution is adjoint to a nonsingular form $H$ with center action $\sigma$ and matrix rule $g^*=H^{-1}g^{\sigma T}H$. This follows from Skolem–Noether after composing with the entrywise $\sigma$-transpose. The involution condition gives $H^{\sigma T}=\lambda H$ and $\lambda\sigma(\lambda)=1$. If $\sigma$ is nontrivial, Hilbert 90 rescales $H$ to be Hermitian. The forms $H$ and $H\bar P$ are then equivalent by Hermitian diagonalization and surjectivity of the finite field norm. If $\sigma$ is trivial and $\lambda=-1$, they are both nonsingular alternating forms and are equivalent by symplectic bases. Thus a failure in the quotient must occur on a preserved factor with $\sigma=\mathrm{id}$ and $\lambda=1$, where $H$ and $H\bar P$ are symmetric and have different determinant square classes.

Fix such a factor, write $d=[K:F]$, and let $Z$ be the block image of $g$, a matrix of linear forms over $K$ in the original variables. Applying the linear quotient to the prepared quadratic lines and left-multiplying by $H$ gives
$$
 Z^T(H\bar P)Z=H.
$$
Take determinants by telescoping to obtain $(\det Z)^2-\delta$ in degree $2m$, where $\delta=\det H/\det(H\bar P)$ is a nonsquare in $K$. Apply every coefficient Frobenius automorphism $\tau$ of $K/F$ to the derivations, fixing the original variables and axioms. Multiply and telescope the determinant equalities to obtain
$$
 t^2-N_{K/F}(\delta),\qquad
 t=\prod_{\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)}\det(Z^\tau),
$$
in degree $2dm$. The polynomial $t$ has coefficients in $F$ because the conjugates are permuted by Frobenius. The finite-field norm sends a generator to a generator, so $\delta_0=N_{K/F}(\delta)$ is a nonsquare. From $z^q-z$ for all original variables derive $t^q-t=t(\mathbf z^q)-t(\mathbf z)$ by telescoping, in degree $qdm$. For $r=(q-1)/2$, multiply the norm-square line by $t\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(t^2)^{r-1-i}\delta_0^i$. This gives $t^q+t$ since $\delta_0^r=-1$. Subtract $t^q-t$, multiply the resulting $2t$ by $t$, and combine with the norm-square line to obtain $1$. As in the symmetric proof, polynomial multipliers mean separate variable-monomial multiplications in ordinary PC.

The total dimension satisfies $dm\le 2n$. Indeed $E$ acts faithfully on the direct sum of the two vertex spaces. On the associated graded for its radical filtration, every simple factor of $E/J$ occurs. Otherwise a lift of the missing factor's identity would act as a map raising the filtration and hence be nilpotent by faithfulness, though its quotient image is a nonzero idempotent. The simple module for $M_m(K)$ has $F$-dimension $dm$. Thus the degree is at most $\max(4,2qn)$. A proof using extension coefficients descends in the same degree, since the bounded-degree PC span is constructed by linear spans, intersections with lower degree subspaces, and multiplication by the original variables. These operations commute with scalar extension. Recording basis updates gives $n^{O_q(n)}$ expanded size over $F$.

For the IPS circuit, every polynomial-size preparatory PC derivation yields a circuit in the original placeholders by replacing source axioms by the corresponding placeholders and retaining its arithmetic operations. For each conjugate block construct a determinant difference $g_\tau$ that takes values zero and $(\det Z^\tau)^2-\tau(\delta)$ at the two IPS substitutions. Put $u_\tau=\det Z^\tau$ and use the single polynomial $g_*=\prod_\tau u_\tau^2-\prod_\tau(u_\tau^2-g_\tau)$. This supplies the norm-square equation at the true substitution. Likewise the circuit $t(\mathbf z+\mathbf y_{\mathrm{field}})-t(\mathbf z)$ supplies $t^q-t$. Write $f_*=t(\mathbf z+\mathbf y_{\mathrm{field}})-t(\mathbf z)$ and $S=\sum_{i=0}^{(q-3)/2}(t^2)^{(q-3)/2-i}\delta_0^i$. The certificate is $t(tSg_*-f_*)/(2\delta_0)-g_*/\delta_0$. Geometric sum doubling computes $S$ in polynomial size in $\log q$. All determinants and products have polynomial circuit size. An arithmetic circuit over $K$ of polynomial size descends by replacing scalar operations by coordinate operations in an $F$-basis of $K$ starting with $1$ and taking its first coordinate. At the two IPS substitutions, the output in $K$ is $0$ or $1$, so that coordinate is the correct scalar. This costs polynomial overhead and gives the required circuit IPS over $F$.
