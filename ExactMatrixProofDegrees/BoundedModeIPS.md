# Polynomial circuit certificates for tensor nonisomorphism with one bounded mode

**Theorem.** Fix a positive integer $k$. For two three-tensors in format $a\times b\times k$ over a perfect field $F$, suppose that they are inequivalent over an algebraic closure. The ordinary cubic tensor isomorphism equations, with two-sided inverse variables in the three modes, have a Hilbert-like IPS refutation over $F$ of arithmetic circuit size $(a+b+1)^{O_k(1)}$. Its total-product Nullstellensatz degree is $(a+b+1)^{O_k(1)}$. The same degree bound holds over an arbitrary field. The number of nonzero slices, the ranks of the tensors and the matrix-pencil elementary divisors have no further restrictions. The equations include no field equations. A Hilbert-like certificate here is a polynomial identity $1=\sum_i H_i E_i$ in the original variables where the $E_i$ are the original input equations and the $H_i$ have a shared division-free circuit.

This gives a circuit upper bound on a subclass of the tensor systems in Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She Question 7.2. Their question concerns expanded Polynomial Calculus size on unrestricted tensors. A circuit certificate can have exponentially many expanded monomials. The theorem does give the same upper bound on ordinary PC degree, by multiplying out the certified products. No Dirichlet hypothesis is required. [Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She, Sections 2 and 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320)

## Multiplicity from three matrix ranks

Write $A_1,\ldots,A_k$ for linear maps from a $b$-dimensional space to an $a$-dimensional space, and likewise for $B$. View each tuple as a representation of the quiver consisting of $k$ arrows from vertex 0 to vertex 1. Put $N=a+b$ and work first over an algebraically closed field. For a fixed representation $S$ write $D_S(T)$ for the linear constraint matrix whose kernel, on node maps $f=(f_0,f_1)$, is $\operatorname{Hom}(S,T)$. Its arrow components are $T_i f_0-f_1 S_i$. Write $D^S(T)$ for the constraint matrix for $g:T\to S$, with components $S_i g_0-g_1 T_i$. For a tuple $T$ of the format of $B$ both matrices have $O(kN^2)$ rows and columns whenever $\dim S_0+\dim S_1\le N$.

Choose an indecomposable summand $S$ of $B$. Its endomorphism algebra is local with residue field the coefficient field. Extend the scalar residue map $\ell$ linearly to the ambient vector space of pairs of node endomorphisms of $S$. Write $P$ for the matrix of the scalar bilinear form $f^T P g=\ell(g f)$ on the two spaces of unrestricted node maps. Besides $D_S(T)$ and $D^S(T)$ use the bordered pencil
$$
L_S(T)=
\begin{pmatrix}
P & D_S(T)^T\\
D^S(T)& 0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its rank minus the ranks of the two constraint matrices is the rank of $P$ restricted to their two kernels. To see this, reduce each constraint matrix to an identity pivot block and zeros. The border pivots eliminate the entries outside the two kernels, leaving exactly the restricted pairing. On the Hom spaces the rank of the pairing is precisely the multiplicity of $S$ in $T$. Indeed, decompose $T$ into indecomposables. A composite through a different indecomposable cannot be a unit in $\operatorname{End}(S)$, since otherwise $S$ splits off that indecomposable. It therefore has zero residue. Each copy of $S$ contributes the pairing given by multiplying its two scalar residues, of rank one. Distinct summands are orthogonal for this pairing.

There are at most $N$ isomorphism classes of summands of $B$. Agreement of all three ranks for each such $S$ implies equality of their multiplicities. Their dimensions account for the entire dimension of $B$, so no other summands remain in $T$. Conversely isomorphic representations have all the same ranks. This yields a finite test family centered at $B$. The use of matricized Hom spaces for orbit separation has prior work by Derksen, Klep, Makam and Volčič. Here the three explicit tests for each target summand are used inside an ideal certificate in the tensor variables. [Matrix-pencil rank separation](https://jvolcic.neocities.org/ranks_orbits_21jan23.pdf)

## Polynomial identities for changing node bases

Let $z=(z_{ij})$ be the matrix in the third tensor mode and put $T_i(z)=\sum_j z_{ij}A_j$. The other mode variables and their designated inverses give formally invertible node maps $h=(h_0,h_1)$ satisfying $B_i h_0=h_1T_i(z)$ modulo the tensor ideal. For example, for equations $h_1T_i(z)Y^T=B_i$, take $h_0=(Y^{-1})^T$, using the supplied inverse variable. Multiply the tensor error on the right by $h_0$, and correct $Y^Th_0-I$ by the given inverse error. The reverse intertwining relations follow by multiplying by the two inverse node maps and correcting the inverse errors. These identities have bounded degree and polynomial circuit size.

For every pencil $L$ from the preceding test family there are square block matrices $R,Q$ in the large-mode node maps and their designated inverses with
$$
L_B= R L(z) Q,\qquad L(z)=R^{-1}L_B Q^{-1}
\quad\text{modulo the tensor equations}.
$$
Here $L_B=L(B)$ and $L(z)=L(T(z))$. The inverse symbols denote explicit polynomial matrices using the supplied variables. To verify the formulas, the forward Hom domain change is $f\mapsto h f$ and the reverse change is $g\mapsto g h^{-1}$. Write these as $U,V$. The arrow-error changes $U',V'$ act respectively by left multiplication by $h_1$ and right multiplication by $h_0^{-1}$ on each arrow. Therefore $D_S(B)U=U'D_S(T)$ and $D^S(B)V=V'D^S(T)$ modulo the intertwiner errors. Also $U^TPV=P$ modulo the inverse errors, by the definition of $\ell$ and composition of unrestricted node maps. Consequently
$$
\operatorname{diag}(U^T,(V')^{-1}) L_S(B)
\operatorname{diag}(V,(U')^{-T})=L_S(T).
$$
The inverse change gives the other identity. All these congruences mean explicit entrywise ideal consequences of polynomial circuit size and bounded degree, obtained by tensoring node maps with identity matrices, transposing, and multiplying out the displayed formulas.

We use the following circuit bookkeeping throughout. Sums and polynomial multiples of known ideal consequences preserve explicit Hilbert-like representations. A division-free circuit evaluated on two tuples whose entrywise differences have such representations gives a representation for the output difference. At a product use $uv-u'v'=u(v-v')+(u-u')v'$. Storing one coefficient per input equation at each gate costs polynomial overhead. For a determinant difference of order $t$ with input entries of degree at most $c$, one can use a polynomial-size bounded-formal-degree determinant circuit, or telescope along matrix columns and expand each replaced column using its cofactors. The latter gives coefficient degrees at most $c(t-1)$ plus the degrees in the entrywise representations. Products and positive powers with known polynomial exponents preserve polynomial degrees and circuit bounds.

## Eliminating the small mode

The only elimination variables are the $s=k^2$ entries of $z$. For a test pencil let $r=\operatorname{rank} L_B$. Every $(r+1)$-minor of $L(z)$ is an ideal consequence with a polynomial-size circuit and polynomial degree. Indeed use the second identity above and compare with the corresponding minor of $R^{-1}L_B Q^{-1}$, which vanishes identically by factorization through a dimension-$r$ space. Let $I$ be the ideal in the $s$ parameters generated by these upper minors over all tests. Use a basis of their coefficient span chosen from the minors as a generating list $h_1,\ldots,h_t$. They have degree polynomial in $N$ for fixed $k$, hence the span has polynomial dimension and the list needs only polynomially many entries.

For each pencil select a nonzero $r$-minor of $L_B$ with row and column sets $J,H$ and determinant $b$. For rank zero use the empty determinant. Introduce dummy parameters $w$ of the same format as $z$ and set
$$
 C_L(w)= b^{-1}\det((R L(w) Q)_{J,H}).
$$
All coefficients are polynomials in the actual node variables. Substitution $w=z$ gives $C_L(z)\equiv1$ modulo the given tensor equations, with a determinant-difference proof. Put
$$
 C(w)=\det(w)\det(z')\prod_L C_L(w),
$$
where $z'$ is the designated inverse of $z$. The identities give $C(z)\equiv1$, including $\det(z)\det(z')-1$ by determinant multiplicativity and a determinant difference for $zz'-I$. There are polynomially many factors of polynomial degree, so the circuit size and both the parameter degree and actual-variable degree are polynomial in $N$ at fixed $k$.

For every $w_0$ in the geometric zero set of $I$, the polynomial $C(w_0)$ in the actual node variables and $z'$ is identically zero. If $\det w_0=0$, this follows from the first factor. Otherwise all upper rank conditions hold there. If all lower ranks also held, the three-rank multiplicity test would give $T(w_0)\cong B$, a tensor isomorphism over the algebraic closure. Thus some test has rank strictly below $r$, and every $r$-minor of $R L(w_0) Q$ vanishes identically. This proves the claim pointwise in the small parameters, without bounding the large node dimensions by $k$.

Here is the certificate compression step. Write $\xi$ for the actual variables occurring in $C(w)$. Let $W$ be the coefficient span in the dummy variables $w$ of $C(w,\xi)$ expanded in $\xi$. The dimension $l$ of $W$ is polynomial because $\deg_w C$ is polynomial and $s$ is fixed. Take a basis $f_1,\ldots,f_l$ of $W$. Every $f_i$ vanishes on the zero set of $I$. The effective Nullstellensatz in $s+1$ variables applied to $h_j(w)$ and $1-yf_i(w)$, followed by substituting $y=1/f_i$ and clearing denominators, gives
$$
 f_i^e=\sum_j a_{ij}(w)h_j(w)
$$
with a common positive $e$ and all degrees polynomial in $N$ for fixed $k$. One may enlarge all exponents to their maximum. The coefficients of these identities descend from any scalar extension by solving the bounded linear coefficient equations. The fixed-variable degree bound follows, for instance, from Kollár's weak effective Nullstellensatz with the input degree taken at least three. Dense polynomials of these degrees in $s$ variables have polynomial circuit size. [Kollár, Sharp Effective Nullstellensatz](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Kollar/kollarnullstellen.pdf)

If $l=0$, then $C(z)=0$ identically and its congruence to one already gives the proof. Otherwise set $E=(e-1)l+1$. The coefficients in $\xi$ of $C(w,\xi)^E$ lie in the span $W_E$ of length-$E$ products of the $f_i$. Choose a basis of $W_E$ from those products. Its dimension is polynomial because it is again a space of polynomials of bounded polynomial degree in $s$ variables. Every chosen product contains $f_i^e$ for some $i$, and hence its representation in terms of the $h_j$ has polynomial-size coefficients and polynomial degree. To obtain the coefficient of each basis product in the polynomial-valued $C(w,\xi)^E$, apply a fixed linear functional to its $w$-coefficient vector. Compute that vector by running the original division-free circuit in the ring of $\xi$-polynomials with dummy variables $w$ truncated above total degree $E\deg_w C$. The truncated ring has polynomial rank over the coefficient ring. The construction therefore compresses the potentially exponentially long expansion in the large node variables without any restriction on characteristic or field size. The resulting coefficient circuits have polynomial semantic degree in $\xi$, since $C^E$ does. Substitute $w=z$ to obtain a polynomial-size Hilbert-like proof of $C(z)^E\equiv0$. Combining with $C(z)\equiv1$ yields the desired identity for one. The circuit-size and total-product degree bounds follow from the indicated polynomial bounds throughout.

## Coefficient fields

Over a perfect base field $F$ the same circuits can be built over $F$, including when several geometric summands require different fields of definition. Put $D=\operatorname{End}_F(B)$, of dimension at most $N^2$. Write its semisimple quotient as a product of matrix algebras over division algebras. Each division algebra has finite separable center $K$ over $F$ and has a separable maximal subfield $L/K$ splitting it, with $[L:F]\le N^2$. The quotient is a separable algebra, so after extension its radical is exactly the extension of the radical. In the factor for $K\hookrightarrow L$ of the scalar extension, choose a rank-one idempotent of the split matrix block and zero on the other blocks. Lift it over the nilpotent radical. Acting on $B_L$ it cuts out a summand $S$ with local endomorphism residue $L$, hence one that stays indecomposable after passing to the closure. Every absolute indecomposable class of $B$ is reached by the embeddings of these individual fields into the closure. Indeed over the closure the matrix factors of the endomorphism quotient are indexed by the embeddings of their centers, and a rank-one idempotent in each gives its unique indecomposable class. Apply the three-rank construction over each such $L$, choosing one $S$ for each division-algebra type before conjugation. The residue form defining $P$ is available over $L$ itself.

Replace the upper minors for each chosen $S$ by their $F$-basis coordinates in $L$. Their common vanishing over the closure is exactly the vanishing of the minors for every embedding of $L$, since the matrix of embeddings on a basis is invertible by separability. A Hilbert-like identity for a minor over $L$ splits coordinatewise into identities over $F$ since the tensor equations are defined over $F$. Computing circuits in an $F$-basis of $L$ costs polynomial overhead. Take a basis from the resulting coordinate polynomials to define $I$ over $F$.

For each lower test replace $C_L(w)$ by its field norm, the determinant of the multiplication matrix of that polynomial on the chosen extension. The matrices $R,Q$ in each conjugate include the same actual node variables with conjugated constants. The norm is the product over all embeddings and identically vanishes when one such conjugate rank drops. At $w=z$, the componentwise identities $C_L(z)\equiv1$ give the same conclusion for the norm by a determinant difference. No compositum of the individual fields is needed. The number of tests and the sum of the extension degrees are polynomial in $N$. All of $I,C,W,W_E$ can now be chosen over $F$, so the preceding compression gives the certificate over $F$. In particular the claim holds over the rationals and over finite fields of every characteristic for geometric nonisomorphism.

For an arbitrary field, the algebraic-closure proof yields the same degree over the base field by descent of the linear coefficient equations for a bounded-degree Nullstellensatz identity. This gives no circuit-size descent assertion in the imperfect case. The theorem concerns existence and algebraic size of certificates. It does not supply a uniform deterministic bit-time algorithm for producing them. The argument is a written proof and its publication priority has not been established.
