# Exact proof degree for matrix conjugacy

**Proposed placement in the Grochow briefing: joint third, alongside the sparse Polynomial Calculus lower bounds.** The reason is the quantifier. For every pair of nonconjugate square matrices over every field, the main proof gives the minimum degree of an ordinary Polynomial Calculus refutation in the variables and equations specified in Open Question 7.3. The result covers arbitrary matrices in their input coordinates. The argument uses algebra and matching designs. The full question also asks about size and general matrix congruence. The general theorems here are written proofs awaiting independent review and a priority determination. [Published question and equations](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3) · [Exact formula and proof](ConjugacyDegree.md)

### What the formula detects

In characteristic zero every nonconjugate pair has degree two or three. Degree two suffices exactly when the minimal polynomials differ or an eigenvalue has differing algebraic multiplicities in the coefficient field, computed after passage to a splitting field. The same degree-two test holds in positive characteristic, but when it fails the answer can grow. For example, take the two diagonal matrices with four ones and eight zeros, and eight ones and four zeros. Their refutation degree is six in characteristic two and two in every other characteristic.

The proof extracts a pair of rectangular inverse maps from each mismatched Jordan block type. The exact degree for inner and outer dimensions $0<r<s$ is $\min(r+2,2p^{v_p(s-r)})$ in characteristic $p$. The lower bound assigns an independent Laurent variable to each edge of a partial matching. This tracks how much ordinary polynomial degree remains after the forward and reverse variables on each edge cancel. The proof extends Riis's design argument by one endpoint and separately checks all degree-two consequences in the original matrix equations. The companion congruence proof gives exact degrees for symmetric matrices in characteristic different from two. [Matching designs, Section 14](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07280v1#S14) · [Symmetric congruence](SymmetricCongruenceDegree.md)

### What IPS adds

If $P$ has $s$ rows and $r<s$ columns, the one-sided Rank Principle $PQ=I_s$ has exact ordinary PC degree $r+2$ for $r\ge1$. At $s=r+1$ its sparse PC size is $r^{\Theta(r)}$, whereas a division-free determinant gives a polynomial-size Hilbert-like Ideal Proof System (IPS) certificate on the same equations. For a different application, fix one mode $k$ of a three-tensor. Over $\mathbb F_q$, every negative tensor-isomorphism instance has a Hilbert-like IPS certificate of size $(a+b+\log q+1)^{O_k(1)}$ using field axioms on just the $k^2$ forward entries of that mode. Both other modes and the field size can grow. A complementary interpolation proof bounds degree by $O(a+b+k^2q+1)$. [Rank Principle proof](RankPrinciple.md) · [Geometric tensor certificates](BoundedModeIPS.md) · [Finite-field tensor certificates](FiniteModeIPS.md) · [Degree bound](FiniteModeDegree.md)

Over arbitrary coefficient fields, a single matrix of linear equations for maps between the two tuples of slices gives a polynomial-size geometric IPS certificate when one tensor mode is fixed. Over finite fields its kernel also gives a randomized algorithm that returns a verifiable transporter in time polynomial in $a+b+\log q$ for fixed $k$. Closely related prior lower bounds include exponential PCR size over the binary field for weak rank encodings, and exact real sum-of-squares degree for the weak Rank Principle. [One-matrix certificate and search algorithm](OneHomSearch.md) · [Garlík, Gryaznov, Ren and Tzameret](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08760) · [Kunisky and Yu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05250)

### Where Dirichlet GRH enters

For two nonisomorphic products of abelian number fields over the rationals, a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm constructs a *shortest* tuple of good primes distinguishing their joint complete-splitting counts. Every prime in it is at most $C\log^2(2D)$, where $D$ is the product of the absolute defining-order discriminants. Dirichlet GRH controls the search radius at every step. For general Galois factors, pairwise compositum degrees already determine the shortest length, and a separate sharp theorem gives the first indistinguishable dimension at every testing order. In a family with nonabelian factors, Dirichlet GRH also constructs a shared panel of fewer than $n$ primes whose pair counts distinguish an entire genus in dimension $n$. A worked degree-64 family has eight members with identical singleton counts, all distinguished by pairs among 16 primes at most 389. These results concern products of fields and their multiplication tensors. Grochow–Qiao's general question about changing fields for Tensor Isomorphism remains open. [Joint-prime theorem](JointGalois.md) · [Nonabelian genus](NonabelianGenus.md) · [Explicit prime panel](NonabelianPanel.md) · [MIT verification record](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

There is also a direct example linking prime choice to IPS. For odd good primes compare the pencils $\operatorname{diag}(u+v,u-v)$ and $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&dv\\v&u\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, with nonzero rational $d$. If $d$ is a square modulo $p$, diagonalize the coefficient of $v$ and rescale $v$ to obtain equivalence. Otherwise their binary determinants have different numbers of rational projective roots, an invariant of ordinary tensor isomorphism. For a list of independent quadratic characters the signs therefore prescribe simultaneously which tensor pairs have a rational transporter and which admit the finite-field IPS refutations above. Dirichlet GRH supplies small primes for any chosen sign vector. An extension to arbitrary rational bilinear forms gives an exact good-prime criterion with weighted character counts controlling PC degree. [Bilinear form theorem and prime bound](PrimeCongruence.md)

### A common prime for tensor certificates

More generally, take any list of rational tensor pairs with one fixed mode and total dense input bit length $L$. In any prescribed coset of an index-$h$ subgroup modulo $m$, Dirichlet GRH gives a single prime $p\ll h^2(\log(2m)+L^{O_k(1)})^2$ preserving every geometric yes/no answer. Every negative reduction has a polynomial-size IPS refutation over the prime field. Every positive reduction has a transporter over a residue-field extension of polynomial degree. This is an existence bound for a common prime with the specified properties. [Common-prime theorem and proof](CommonReductionPrime.md)
