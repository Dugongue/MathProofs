# Short finite-field IPS proofs with field equations on one tensor mode

**Theorem.** Let $F=\mathbb F_q$ be any finite field and fix $k$. For any two $a\times b\times k$ tensors that are nonisomorphic over $F$, the ordinary cubic tensor and two-sided inverse equations have a Hilbert-like IPS certificate over $F$ of arithmetic circuit size $(a+b+\log q+1)^{O_k(1)}$ when $z_{ij}^q-z_{ij}$ is added **only for the $k^2$ forward entries in the third mode**. All entries in the two larger modes, and all designated inverse variables, may range over the algebraic closure. This includes geometric nonisomorphisms and arithmetic forms that become isomorphic over the closure.

The proof uses [the bounded-mode geometric certificate](BoundedModeIPS.md). All polynomial size bounds below permit dependence on fixed $k$. The group-theoretic existence argument uses Lang's theorem over finite fields. The main size assertion concerns division-free arithmetic circuits sharing subcalculations. Raising to the $q$th power has cost $O(\log q)$ but need not have small PC degree. No GRH enters the certificate theorem.

## The algebraic transporter in the small variables

Put $K=\overline F$. Use parameter pairs $w=(z,z')$ with $zz'=z'z=I_k$, giving a closed affine model of $\mathrm{GL}_k$ in $s=2k^2$ variables. Write $V$ for the points of this model where the mixed source tuple $T(z)$ and the target tuple $B$ are isomorphic as Kronecker quiver representations. If nonempty, $V$ is a coset of the image in $\mathrm{GL}_k$ of the geometric stabilizer of one input tensor. This is the image of an algebraic group under a group morphism and is closed in $\mathrm{GL}_k$. Indeed its image is constructible and is a subgroup. It is dense in its closure and contains a nonempty relatively open piece. Translating this piece and its translates shows that the subgroup is open and closed in its closure. Thus $V$ is closed in the chosen affine parameter model. Use reduced varieties throughout. The input tensors are defined over $F$, so Frobenius permutes $V$ and its geometric components.

The components of this coset are pairwise disjoint translates of the identity component of the reduced projected stabilizer. A Frobenius-stable component is a torsor over the finite field under this connected smooth linear algebraic group and has an $F$-point by Lang's theorem. Conversely an $F$-point lies on a Frobenius-stable component. Given $z\in V(F)$, the remaining isomorphisms of quiver representations form a torsor under the unit group of the endomorphism algebra of one representation. That group is connected and smooth, since it is the determinant-nonzero open subset of an affine vector space. Lang again gives an isomorphism over $F$. Thus $F$-nonisomorphism of the tensors implies that every geometric component of nonempty $V$ belongs to a Frobenius orbit of length at least two. [Lang, Algebraic groups over finite fields](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Lang-Algebraic_Groups_Over_Finite_Fields.pdf)

Use the upper rank minors of the bounded-mode proof over $F$, including its separate coefficient descent for each indecomposable test, and add the inverse equations in $w$. Let $I$ be this ideal of small-variable polynomials and let $X$ be its geometric zero set. The lower rank conditions for the finitely many conjugate test pencils say that at least one maximal minor at each required rank is nonzero. Their conjunction defines an open subset of $X$, which by the rank criterion is exactly $V$. All defining equations of $X$ have degree polynomial in $N=a+b+1$, and the ambient dimension $s$ is fixed. By the affine Bézout inequality the sum of the degrees of all irreducible components of $X$, across the different dimensions, is polynomial in $N$. One may use successive generic combinations of the equations cutting every component not yet contained in $X$, for at most $s+1$ steps. As an open and closed subset, $V$ is a union of entire components of $X$. In particular its number of components and their sum of degrees are polynomial in $N$.

## Certified equations for functions vanishing on the transporter

Let $P(w)$ be any polynomial of polynomial degree over a finite extension $L/F$ of polynomial degree. Suppose that $P$ vanishes on $V$. There is a polynomial-size Hilbert-like certificate over $L$ that $P(w)=0$ follows from the original equations and the small-mode field axioms, at the actual small-mode variables. The exponent of the polynomial-size bound is independent of $q$.

First work without the field equations. Use the product $C(w,\xi)$ of all the lower witness determinants of the preceding paper, using the norms of individual tests to keep the coefficients over $F$. Here $\xi$ are the large node variables and inverses. In this parameter model omit the factor for invertibility of $w$, since its inverse equations are part of $I$. At the actual variables $C\equiv1$ has a polynomial-size certificate. For every $w_0\in X$, the polynomial $(P C)(w_0,\xi)$ is identically zero. This follows from $P=0$ on $V$ and from a rank drop in at least one conjugate test on $X\setminus V$. Apply the coefficient-span compression in the bounded-mode proof to the polynomial $P C$ in place of $C$. Its bounded dummy-variable degree gives some $E\ge1$, polynomial in $N,\deg P$ at fixed $s$, and a polynomial-size representation of $(P C)^E$ in the upper generators. The test certificates and $C\equiv1$ give a Hilbert-like certificate of $P^E$ in the actual tensor ideal. If $P C$ vanishes identically the same conclusion holds already with $E=1$.

The field equations for the inverse small-mode entries follow by circuits of size polynomial in $k,\log q$. Denote entrywise $q$th powers by brackets $[q]$. From $zz'=I$ obtain $z^{[q]}(z')^{[q]}=I$ by $q$th powers, replace $z^{[q]}$ by $z$, then multiply on the left by $z'$ and use $z'z=I$. Additive Frobenius holds as a polynomial identity in characteristic $p$. Choose $Q=q^{[L:F]t}\ge E$ with the smallest positive $t$. Then $\log Q\le [L:F]\log q+\log E$. The consequences $w^{[Q]}=w$ have polynomial-size arithmetic circuits by iterating Frobenius or telescoping powers. Since the coefficients of $P$ belong to $L$,
$$
P(w)^Q-P(w)=P(w^{[Q]})-P(w)
$$
has a polynomial-size ideal certificate by the gate difference identity. Multiply the certificate for $P^E$ by $P^{Q-E}$, computed by binary powering, and subtract the last difference. This proves $P=0$ with the asserted circuit cost. This argument uses $O(\log Q)$ gates per powering and makes no polynomial-in-$\log q$ degree assertion.

## Component idempotents and field descent

Assume $V$ is nonempty, as the geometric theorem already covers the empty case. Let $D$ be a geometric component and $d$ its Frobenius orbit length. Then $D$ and the union $D'$ of all other components are defined over $L=\mathbb F_{q^d}$ and $d\le\deg V=\operatorname{poly}_k(N)$ when degree counts all components. There exists a polynomial $e_D(w)$ over $L$, of degree polynomial in $N$, equal to one on $D$ and zero on $D'$. For details, both closed sets have polynomial degree in a fixed affine space. By Heintz's set-theoretic defining-equation bound each is a common zero set of polynomials of degrees bounded by its degree. Use a basis of the space of all vanishing polynomials of at most that degree if necessary to bound the number of equations. The union of the two lists has no common root. The weak effective Nullstellensatz in the fixed number of variables expresses one as a sum from the two ideals with polynomial total-product degrees. The contribution from the equations vanishing on $D'$ gives the desired indicator. This proves the degree bound over $K$. The affine linear conditions on coefficients of that bounded-degree polynomial, namely vanishing on $D'$ and being one on $D$, descend to $L$ since both varieties are defined there. Equivalently their vanishing ideals commute with extending $L$ to $K$, and membership here takes place in finite polynomial-degree spaces. The linear system is therefore solvable over $L$. [Heintz's defining-equation bound](https://mate.dm.uba.ar/~tera2005/ConfJeronimo.pdf) · [Kollár's effective Nullstellensatz](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Kollar/kollarnullstellen.pdf)

Choose one such $e=e_D$ for each Frobenius orbit, and take the coefficient conjugates for its other components. Write $\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha^q$. The polynomials $e^2-e$ and $e\,e^\sigma$ vanish on $V$, since the indicators take values zero and one and $D^\sigma\ne D$. The preceding step proves these two equations with polynomial-size circuits over $L$. Also
$$
e(w)^q=e^\sigma(w^{[q]})\equiv e^\sigma(w)
$$
by the small-mode field equations. The identity $e^q\equiv e$ follows from $e^2-e$ by the geometric sum or repeated squaring. Consequently $e=e^\sigma$ modulo the equations, and multiplying by $e$ shows $e\equiv0$. The same proof holds for each conjugate.

For one orbit its sum of indicators has $F$ coefficients. Add the $L$-certificates and apply the $F$-coordinate projection in a basis of $L$ starting with one. This produces an $F$-certificate of the orbit sum equal to zero, since every original equation is over $F$. Computing a circuit in such a basis costs polynomially in $d$. Sum the resulting orbit certificates over $F$. On $V$ the sum of all indicators equals one. Apply the certified-vanishing step over $F$ once more to this sum minus one. The two conclusions give one in the ideal. Throughout, each individual coefficient field has degree at most the polynomial bound on the component count. Extensions from different orbits are never combined.

The classical classification inputs, rank tests, effective Nullstellensatz and Lang's theorem have independent sources. The proposed proof-complexity contribution is the finite-field circuit certificate from their combination, with field axioms restricted to one mode and circuit size polynomial in the bit length of the field order. This is an existence theorem with an informal mathematical proof. Deterministic polynomial bit-time construction and literature priority are separate questions.
