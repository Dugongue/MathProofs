# A common small reduction prime for rational bounded-mode tensor isomorphism

**Theorem.** Fix $k$. Let a finite list of pairs of rational $a_i\times b_i\times k$ tensors have total dense input bit length $L\ge2$. There is a nonzero integer $D$ with $\log(2|D|)\le L^{O_k(1)}$ such that at every prime $p\nmid D$ all input coefficients reduce and **every geometric isomorphism answer in the list is preserved** from $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ to $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$. Let $H\le(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times$ have index $h$. For any prescribed coset $cH$, Dirichlet GRH supplies one common good prime in that coset with
$$
p\ll h^2\big(\log(2m)+L^{O_k(1)}\big)^2 .
$$
The arithmetic input for this cutoff concerns Dirichlet characters over $\mathbb Q$. At such a prime a geometrically positive pair already has a transporter over some $\mathbb F_{p^j}$ with $j\le (a_i+b_i+1)^{O_k(1)}$. A geometrically negative pair has a polynomial-size raw Hilbert-like IPS refutation over $\mathbb F_p$. Thus the cutoff can simultaneously preserve different yes/no instances, prescribe Dirichlet-character information, and give an actual finite-field certificate for each negative.

## Integer equations in a bounded number of parameters

Clear the denominators of the two tensors in a pair by a common nonzero positive integer and exclude its prime divisors. The coefficients after this step have polynomial bit length. In the [one-Hom construction](OneHomSearch.md) put $n=a+b+1$ and $s=2k^2$. Work with integer coefficients and the dummy parameter pair $w=(z,z')$. The upper tests are the two inverse-matrix equations for $w$ and the $(r+1)$-minors of the linear Hom matrix $H(z)$. Here $r=\operatorname{rank} H_B$ over $\mathbb Q$. Exclude the prime divisors of one nonzero $r$-minor of $H_B$, with value one for rank zero. Since the larger minors of the integer constant matrix $H_B$ are zero, the same target rank now applies to every remaining characteristic.

There is also a characteristic-independent integer test for the open lower condition. Use formal compressing matrices $U,V$ and a formal vector $c$ with algebraically independent coordinates $\xi$. Define
$$
 b=\det(U H(z)V),\qquad J=bI-V\operatorname{adj}(UH(z)V)UH(z),\qquad
 P(w,\xi)=b\det((Jc)_0)\det((Jc)_1).
$$
Use the empty determinant and $J=I$ when $r=0$. On the upper zero set $X$ in any algebraic closure, the rank is at most $r$. A point there gives an isomorphic tuple if and only if $P(w,\xi)$ is not identically zero in $\xi$. Indeed, a nonzero value gives nonzero $b$ and two invertible node maps in the kernel by the projector identity. Conversely an isomorphic tuple has rank $r$ and its kernel contains an invertible pair of node maps. Choose a sensing compression with nonzero $b$ and choose $c$ from that pair, on which $J$ acts as $bI$. This proves both directions, including over closures of small finite fields. Equivalently at least one of the $w$-polynomials in the coefficients of $P$, expanded in $\xi$, must have nonzero value there.

The upper minors and all these lower coefficient polynomials have degrees and integer coefficient bit lengths polynomial in $L$ at fixed $k$. Here is a direct height estimate that avoids any assumption on coefficient heights produced by an IPS existence argument. For an integer polynomial use the sum of the absolute values of its coefficients as its norm. It is submultiplicative, subadditive, and bounds the norm of a coefficient extracted with respect to any subset of variables. A determinant of order $t$ with entry norms at most $B$ has norm at most $t! B^t$. The entries of the original Hom pencils have coefficient bit length polynomial in $L$, and the unrestricted node spaces and arrow spaces have polynomial dimension. Each matrix multiplication in the displayed construction multiplies entry norms and includes at most polynomially many summands. Along any chain the formula uses a constant number of nested determinants or adjugates, all of polynomial order. Thus the logarithms of the norms, and the parameter degrees by the same fixed-depth calculation, are polynomial in $L$. This bound applies even though the expansion in all entries of $\xi$ could have many monomials.

Choose upper minor polynomials $h_j$ spanning their rational coefficient space, and lower polynomials $f_i$ spanning theirs, each chosen from the corresponding integer list. There are at most $\binom{s+d}{s}$ of each if $d$ is the degree bound. Both integer families can therefore be compressed to polynomial cardinality. For each nonempty basis exclude the divisors of one nonsingular square minor of its coefficient matrix on the $w$-monomials. By Cramer's rule the determinant of that minor is a common denominator for expressing *every* original vector in the basis. The same subset remains a spanning basis in each good reduction. The two pivot determinants have polynomial bit length by the determinant bound above. Empty lists require no pivot. Testing the nonvanishing of some $f_i$ is therefore equivalent to testing the entire integer coefficient list simultaneously in characteristic zero and all remaining characteristics.

## Preserving consistency in both directions

For each $f_i$ form the polynomial system consisting of the upper basis, the mutually inverse parameter equations, and
$$
 1-y f_i(w)=0 .
$$
The geometric tensor answer is positive exactly when at least one of these systems has a solution. If the lower list is empty the answer is always negative in the characteristics being considered. Each system has $s+1$ variables, polynomially many generators and degrees at most a polynomial in $L$. Use a uniform characteristic-independent weak effective Nullstellensatz bound $T=L^{O_k(1)}$ on multiplier degrees for certifying one in these ideals, increasing the common input degree bound to at least three. This bound depends only on the degree and variable counts and applies also if some input terms disappear after reduction. [Kollár, Sharp Effective Nullstellensatz](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Kollar/kollarnullstellen.pdf)

Build the integer coefficient matrix $M_i$ of the linear map from multipliers of degrees at most $T$ to their sum times the input generators. The rows index monomials up to $T$ plus the largest generator degree. Over any coefficient field, equality of the ranks of $M_i$ and $(M_i\mid e_0)$ is equivalent to geometric inconsistency of the polynomial system, where $e_0$ is the constant-one vector. This follows from the stated degree bound and extension invariance of solvability of linear equations. The two matrix dimensions and all entry bit lengths are polynomial in $L$ for fixed $k$. For each matrix exclude all prime divisors of one maximal-rank nonzero minor over $\mathbb Q$. Use determinant one for rank zero. Exact vanishing of larger integer minors persists automatically, so both ranks are preserved at all remaining primes. By Hadamard's determinant bound, both excluded integers have polynomial bit length.

Multiply all the excluded integers for the polynomially many lower tests and all input pairs, together with the cleared denominators. Their product is a legitimate $D$ with polynomial bit length. This is a bound on the product of bad primes and an existence proof. Computing a basis from an exponentially long formal list is unnecessary for this statement; no claim of deterministic polynomial-time construction of $D$ is used.

## Choosing the prime and descending a positive solution

Lift the prescribed coset by inverse image along the surjection on units from modulus $m|D|$ to modulus $m$. Its subgroup index remains $h$ and every prime representing that coset is good for all tensors. For $h>1$ the Dirichlet-GRH least-prime-in-a-coset bound gives $p\ll h^2\log^2(2m|D|)$, absorbing finitely many small moduli into the absolute constant. For $h=1$ the least prime outside the divisors of $mD$ is $O(\log(2m|D|))$ by the primorial bound. Substitution of the height estimate proves the asserted common cutoff. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.3595) · [Verification scope for Dirichlet GRH](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

Finally, at a good prime the geometric transporter projected to the small mode is the closed coset from the [finite-field proof](FiniteModeIPS.md), cut out inside bounded-dimensional parameter space using the upper rank equations and open conditions. Its component count is at most its degree bound $n^{O_k(1)}$. For any one geometric component choose its Frobenius orbit length $j$. It is stable over $\mathbb F_{p^j}$ and Lang's theorem gives a rational small-mode point over that field. The large node maps descend over the same field by Lang applied to the connected group of units of the quiver endomorphism algebra. This proves the bounded extension claim. For a negative reduction the [raw one-Hom certificate](OneHomSearch.md) works over the prime field itself.

All size and height bounds concern this bounded-mode tensor format and geometric equivalence. Ordinary equivalence over $\mathbb Q$ introduces separate rational-point questions. The bounded-height spread, closed-coset application and simultaneous prime consequence are written mathematical arguments.
