# Tensor factorization of modular group algebras with central Frattini subgroup

**Theorem.** Let $G$ be a finite $p$-group with central Frattini subgroup and let $k$ be any field of characteristic $p$. If the unital $k$-algebra $kG$ is isomorphic to $A\otimes_k B$, there are direct factors $G_A,G_B$ with $G=G_A\times G_B$, $A\cong kG_A$, and $B\cong kG_B$.

The hypothesis is equivalent to requiring nilpotency class at most two and an elementary abelian derived subgroup. It allows the Frattini subgroup to have arbitrarily large exponent. This proves the central-Frattini case of the question raised by Carlson and Kovács in 1995 and asked again as Question 1 by García-Lucas, del Río, and Sakurai. The question for all finite $p$-groups remains open here. [Carlson–Kovács, §5](https://archives.maths.anu.edu.au/people/Kovacs/K089.pdf) · [García-Lucas–del Río–Sakurai, Question 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09036v1)

## 1. The first commutator of the algebra

Write $R=kG$, $W=G'$, $E=G/W$, $V=E/pE=G/\Phi(G)$, and use additive notation for the last three groups. Centrality of $\Phi(G)$ implies that $G$ has class at most two and $[g,h]^p=[g^p,h]=1$. Thus $W$ is elementary abelian, and commutation defines a bilinear alternating map $\beta:V\times V\to W$ whose image spans $W$. The converse implication for the group hypotheses follows from the same power identity and $\Phi(G)=G^pG'$. Write $\mathcal R=\operatorname{rad}(\beta)$, $\bar V=V/\mathcal R$, and $\pi:E\to\bar V$ for the projection. A subscript $k$ denotes scalar extension from $\mathbb F_p$.

Let $J$ be the augmentation ideal of $R$, and $I$ the two-sided ideal generated by additive commutators of $R$. Thus $I$ is generated by the central elements $w-1$ for $w\in W$, and $\Lambda=R/I=k[E]$. In particular $I\subseteq J^2$ and $IJ=JI$. Write $I_A,I_B$ for the corresponding commutator ideals of the two factors, and put $A_0=A/I_A$, $B_0=B/I_B$. Abelianizing the tensor product gives
$$
 I=I_A\otimes B + A\otimes I_B,\qquad \Lambda=A_0\otimes B_0.
$$
The tensor identification is used to regard both factors as subalgebras. The augmentation of $R$ restricts to their augmentations, whose kernels are nilpotent, since they lie in $J$.

There is a natural $\Lambda$-module isomorphism $I/I^2\cong\Lambda\otimes_k W_k$. In fact a transversal for $G/W$ expresses $I$ as a direct sum of copies of the augmentation ideal of $kW$. Centrality gives the same description of $I^2$ using the square of that augmentation ideal. In the first quotient of the augmentation ideal, the class of $w-1$ identifies with $w\in W_k$. A change of representative by a member of $W$ acts trivially on this first quotient. Multiplication by a lift of $e\in E$ therefore acts on the coset index by translation by $e$, proving the module description. Also $[R,I]\subseteq I^2$, since $I$ has central generators and $[R,R]\subseteq I$. The commutator of lifts of elements of $\Lambda$ is consequently well defined in $I/I^2$.

Let $t_e$ denote the group basis of $\Lambda$. In an $\mathbb F_p$-basis for $V$, define derivations $D_j(t_e)=v_j(e)t_e$, where $v_j(e)$ is coordinate $j$ of $e\bmod pE$. Additivity of $v_j$ gives the derivation rule, and $D_j^p=D_j$. Put $Df=(D_j f)_j$. The first commutator of $f,h\in\Lambda$ equals $\beta_\Lambda(Df,Dh)$ in $\Lambda\otimes_k W_k$. On the group basis this is $t_{e+e'}\otimes\beta(e\bmod pE,e'\bmod pE)$, by the group commutator identity. Bilinearity proves the formula.

## 2. Rational summands of the commutator map

Choose generators $f_a$ and $h_b$ in the augmentation ideals of $A_0$ and $B_0$ lifting bases of their respective tangent spaces, the augmentation ideals modulo their squares. Such lifts generate the algebras by nilpotence of the augmentation ideals. The combined tangent classes form a basis of the tangent of $\Lambda=A_0\otimes B_0$. This tangent is $V_k$, with $t_e-1$ representing $e\bmod pE$, as is seen from the truncated polynomial presentation of $k[E]$. The augmentation of the gradient of any $f$ is its tangent class if $f$ has augmentation zero. Therefore the gradients $Df_a,Dh_b$ form a basis of $\Lambda\otimes_k V_k$, because their determinant is a unit in the local algebra $\Lambda$. Write $P_A,P_B$ for the spans of the two respective groups of gradients. The first commutator formula and the commuting $A,B$ lifts give $\beta_\Lambda(P_A,P_B)=0$.

There is also a direct-sum decomposition of $\Lambda$-modules
$$
 I/I^2\cong ((I_A/I_A^2)\otimes B_0)\ \oplus\ (A_0\otimes(I_B/I_B^2)).
$$
It follows by quotienting $I_A\otimes B+A\otimes I_B$ by $I_A^2\otimes B+A\otimes I_B^2+I_A\otimes I_B$. The two internal bracket spans of $P_A,P_B$ belong to the respective displayed modules. Together these spans are the entire module $\Lambda\otimes W_k$, because $\beta$ spans $W$ and the cross bracket vanishes. Thus each bracket span is its entire respective module. On specializing at the augmentation of $\Lambda$, these modules give a direct sum of $I/IJ\cong W_k$ with summands denoted temporarily by $W_{A,k},W_{B,k}$.

The projections along $P_A\oplus P_B$ preserve the radical $\Lambda\otimes_k\mathcal R_k$ of the bracket. For if $a+b$ is in the radical with $a\in P_A,b\in P_B$, then $a$ has zero bracket against $P_A$ because $b$ does, and it has zero bracket against $P_B$ by cross-orthogonality. The same argument applies to $b$. Consequently the projected input summands in $\Lambda\otimes_k\bar V_k$ are complementary. Let $e$ be the projection onto the first projected input summand and $c$ the projection onto the first bracket-output summand in $\Lambda\otimes_k W_k$. For the induced bracket $\bar\beta$ on $\bar V$, one has
$$
 \bar\beta_\Lambda(e x,y)=\bar\beta_\Lambda(x,e y)=c\bar\beta_\Lambda(x,y).
$$

Pairs of endomorphisms satisfying these equations form the centroid algebra of $\bar\beta_\Lambda$, with componentwise composition. It is commutative. For two pairs $(e,c),(e',c')$, evaluate $\bar\beta_\Lambda(e x,e'y)$ in both orders to get $cc'=c'c$ by spanning of the output. Apply this to $\bar\beta_\Lambda(ee'x,y)$ and use radical-freeness to get $ee'=e'e$. The centroid is the extension to $\Lambda$ of the centroid over $k$, since its defining equations are linear over $k$. Any idempotent in it equals the constant lift of its specialization at augmentation. Indeed, the two idempotents differ by an element of a nilpotent ideal, and in a commutative ring idempotents $a,b$ whose difference is nilpotent agree, since $a(1-b)$ and $b(1-a)$ are nilpotent idempotents. This applies to the pair $(e,c)$. In particular the projected gradient spans are extensions to $\Lambda$ of complementary subspaces $\bar U_{A,k},\bar U_{B,k}$ of $\bar V_k$.

For a functional $\lambda$ on $\bar V_k$, view it as a functional on $V_k$ and put $D_\lambda=\sum_j \lambda_j D_j$. The derivation $D_\lambda$ annihilates $A_0$ exactly when it annihilates the $f_a$. The constant projected gradient span identifies this condition with $\lambda$ annihilating $\bar U_{A,k}$. On the group basis, $D_\lambda^p=D_{\lambda^{(p)}}$, with $\lambda^{(p)}$ the coefficientwise $p$-th power in an $\mathbb F_p$-basis. Thus the annihilator is stable under coordinatewise Frobenius. In the reduced row echelon basis of such a stable subspace, Frobenius preserves the pivots and row independence. Uniqueness of reduced row echelon form fixes every coefficient, proving that the subspace descends to $\mathbb F_p$, including for imperfect $k$. This applies also to $B_0$. We obtain rational complementary summands $\bar U_A,\bar U_B\subseteq\bar V$. Their internal bracket spans give complementary rational summands $W_A,W_B\subseteq W$ lifting the summands of $I/IJ$.

We also obtain a support statement. Every $f\in A_0$ is a linear combination of $t_e$ with $\pi(e)\in\bar U_A$. If a term with coefficient $a_e\ne0$ occurs, every $D_\lambda$ for $\lambda$ in the annihilator of $\bar U_A$ vanishes on $f$, and the coefficient of $t_e$ in $D_\lambda f$ is $a_e\lambda(\pi(e))$. Thus $\pi(e)\in\bar U_A$. The same statement holds with $B$ throughout.

## 3. Replacing algebra generators by elements of prescribed order

By Carlson–Kovács's abelian theorem, $A_0$ and $B_0$ are group algebras of abelian $p$-group factors of $E$. It follows that each is a truncated polynomial algebra with one generator for each cyclic factor. Choose those generators $f_j$ for both algebras, with each assigned its side $i=A$ or $B$. If the corresponding cyclic factor has order $m_j=p^{s_j}$, then $f_j^{m_j}=0$, the combined tangent classes of the $f_j$ form a basis of $V_k$, and $\prod_j m_j=|E|$. Every $m_j$ is at least $p$. [Carlson–Kovács, Theorem 2.2](https://archives.maths.anu.edu.au/people/Kovacs/K089.pdf)

Fix a side $i$ and an exponent $m=p^s$ with $s\ge 1$. Define
$$
 D_{i,s}=E[m]\cap\pi^{-1}(\bar U_i),\qquad
 q_{i,s}:D_{i,s}\longrightarrow W/W_i,\qquad
 q_{i,s}(d)=\tilde d^{\,m}\bmod W_i ,
$$
where $\tilde d$ is any lift to $G$. This value is defined because $d\in E[m]$, and independent of lift because $W$ is central of exponent dividing $p$. It is a group homomorphism. Indeed, the group lifts of elements of $\pi^{-1}(\bar U_i)$ commute modulo $W_i$, as their bracket is valued in $W_i$. In that quotient the $m$-th power on those elements is multiplicative. The value $q_{i,s}$ factors through $D_{i,s}/pD_{i,s}$, and the tangent map from $D_{i,s}$ to $E/pE=V$ factors through the same $\mathbb F_p$-space.

Let $f=f_j$ on this side with $m=m_j$, and write $f=\sum_e a_e t_e$. Each $e$ in the support satisfies $\pi(e)\in\bar U_i$. In the commutative $\Lambda$, the equality $f^m=0$ implies that for each fiber of $e\mapsto me$ the sum of the $a_e^m$ is zero. Frobenius injectivity implies that the sum of the $a_e$ in each fiber is also zero. Choose a representative $b(e)$ in the support of each nonempty fiber and put $d_e=e-b(e)\in D_{i,s}$. An augmentation-zero lift of $f$ to $R$ is
$$
 r=\sum_e a_e\big(\tilde e-\widetilde{b(e)}\big).
$$
The tangent of $f$ is $\sum_e a_e(d_e\bmod pE)$.

In $R/IJ$, the ideal $I/IJ$ annihilates the augmentation ideal on both sides. In particular its $k$-subspace $W_{i,k}$ is an ideal. Quotient $R/IJ$ by $W_{i,k}$ and call the result $S_i$. All the group lifts with $\pi(e)\in\bar U_i$ commute in $S_i$. Indeed their group commutator lies in $W_i$, and hence its difference from $1$ is zero there. The $m$-th power of $r$ in $S_i$ equals that of any augmentation-zero lift of $f$, because the difference of two lifts is in $I$ and all mixed products with this difference vanish in $S_i$. In particular we may use a lift in the algebra factor on side $i$. Its $m$-th power lies in $I_i$, whose image in $I/IJ$ lies in $W_{i,k}$. Therefore $r^m=0$ in $S_i$.

Frobenius on the commuting group lifts expresses this equality as
$$
 0=\sum_e a_e^{\,m}\big(\tilde e^{\,m}-\widetilde{b(e)}^{\,m}\big)
   =\sum_e a_e^{\,m} q_{i,s}(d_e)\quad\text{in }(W/W_i)_k .
$$
For the second equality, the two group lifts commute in $S_i$ and $\tilde e\widetilde{b(e)}^{-1}$ is a lift of $d_e$. Its $m$-th power is in $W$, and $\widetilde{b(e)}^m$ acts as the identity on $I/IJ$. This identifies their difference with the desired class. Since $q_{i,s}$ takes its coordinates in $\mathbb F_p$, Frobenius injectivity now gives $\sum_e a_e q_{i,s}(d_e)=0$. It follows that the tangent of $f$ lies in the $k$-linear span of the tangent images of the rational subgroup $\ker q_{i,s}$. More explicitly, $\sum_e a_e[d_e]$ lies in the kernel of the scalar extension of the linear map $D_{i,s}/pD_{i,s}\to W/W_i$. This kernel is the scalar extension of the rational kernel, and all its rational elements are represented by members of $\ker q_{i,s}$.

Apply this to each $f_j$. Choose an actual element $d_j\in\ker q_{i,s_j}$ for each $j$, with the tangent images of all chosen elements independent over $\mathbb F_p$. To see such choices exist, expand each tangent of $f_j$ in rational candidates just obtained. Their combined determinant is nonzero. The multilinear expansion contains a nonzero term, which selects one candidate per generator and certifies independence. Their images generate $V=E/pE$, so the $d_j$ generate $E$, by successively lifting modulo the powers of $pE$. The natural homomorphism $\prod_j C_{m_j}\to E$ sending the cyclic generators to $d_j$ is thus surjective, and the two orders are equal. Hence the $d_j$ give a direct product of the indicated cyclic groups in $E$.

## 4. Recovering the group direct product and the algebra factors

For each $j$ choose a lift $x_j$ of $d_j$ to $G$. Let $H_A,H_B$ be the subgroups generated by the lifts assigned to each side. The cross commutators vanish because the $\bar U_i$ are orthogonal for $\bar\beta$. Within side $i$, commutators belong to $W_i$, and $x_j^{m_j}\in W_i$ by construction. A word in the lifts on side $i$ can be collected using central commutators, then reducing the exponent on each $x_j$ modulo $m_j$. If it lies in $W$, all reduced exponents are zero because the corresponding $d_j$ form independent cyclic direct factors in $E$. Thus $H_i\cap W\subseteq W_i$. The images of $H_A,H_B$ in $E$ intersect trivially and $W_A\cap W_B=0$, so $H_A\cap H_B=1$. The lifted generators together generate $G$ because their images form a basis of $G/\Phi(G)$. Since the two subgroups commute, $G=H_A\times H_B$. A nontrivial algebra factor has a nonzero tangent because its augmentation ideal is nilpotent. Its commutator ideal lies in the square of its augmentation ideal, so its quotient on that side has a generator and $H_i\ne1$. Consequently any directly indecomposable $G$ in the theorem has tensor-irreducible $kG$.

To match the prescribed factors up to isomorphism for arbitrary $G$, take a decomposition of $G$ into directly indecomposable groups. Each has central Frattini subgroup, so the preceding proof makes its group algebra tensor-irreducible. Refine each of $A$ and $B$ into tensor-irreducible factors. Sakurai's uniqueness theorem for tensor factorizations of split local algebras matches the two multisets because $kG$ has residue field $k$. Grouping the matched subgroups according to the lists for $A$ and $B$ gives $G_A,G_B$ as claimed. [Sakurai, Theorem A](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38323v1#S1)

## Scope

For example, take $E=(\mathbb Z/p^m)^r$, with $m\ge3$ and $r\ge4$, and $W=\bigwedge^2\mathbb F_p^r$. Give $E\times W$ the central cocycle $\sum_{a<b}\bar e_a\bar e'_b(v_a\wedge v_b)$, where $v_a$ are the standard basis vectors and bars denote reduction modulo $p$. The group has derived subgroup $W$, central Frattini subgroup of exponent $p^{m-1}$, and at least four generators. Its wedge commutator makes it directly indecomposable, since nonzero vectors in distinct complementary tangent slots would have nonzero commutator. This illustrates independent higher power and commutator coordinates in the theorem. Earlier results include the abelian case, two-generator and cyclic-center cases, and prime-field decompositions with a commutative algebra factor. The proof above places no restriction on commutativity of the two factors or on the coefficient field beyond its characteristic.
