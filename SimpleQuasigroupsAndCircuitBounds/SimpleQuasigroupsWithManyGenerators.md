# Simple loops of order $8^m$ requiring $2m$ generators

**Theorem.** For every integer $m\geq 2$ there is an explicit finite simple loop $Q_m$ of order $8^m$ whose minimum generating-set size is exactly $2m$. In particular no absolute constant bounds the number of generators needed for finite simple quasigroups. Grochow, Kardeş and Levet explicitly leave this question open in their discussion of the group-versus-quasigroup gap. [*Group Isomorphism and the Polylogarithmic-Time Hierarchy*, §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26257v1#S7).

Here simple means there are only two congruences, equality and the universal relation. The construction preserves the identity, so the same generating-set bound holds whether the identity is taken as a named constant or only multiplication and the two divisions are allowed.

## The loop table

Take the additive vector space $V=(\mathbb F_2)^{3m}$, partitioned as the direct sum of three-dimensional spaces $B_1,\ldots,B_m$. In each block $B_j$ use a basis $e_1,e_2,e_3$ with cyclic subscripts, where the basis notation in the following construction is local to that block. Start with the addition table of $V$.

For each $i\in\{1,2,3\}$ in each block, use the two row and column sets
$$
A_i=\{e_{i+1},e_{i+1}+e_i\},\qquad
D_i=\{e_{i+2},e_{i+2}+e_i\}.
$$
On the four cells $A_i\times D_i$, replace the original entry $x+y$ by $x+y+e_i$. This switches an intercalate, the $2\times 2$ Latin subsquare on those rows and columns. The two entries of each affected row are interchanged, as are the two entries of each affected column within those rows. This is the standard intercalate trade in an elementary abelian $2$-group table. [Cavenagh and Falcón, *Latin bitrades derived from quasigroup autoparatopisms*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14987).

The row pairs inside each block are disjoint. Their weight-one vectors are the three distinct basis elements and their weight-two vectors are the three distinct pair sums. Row pairs from different blocks are disjoint because they contain only nonzero vectors from their own block. Therefore all trades preserve the Latin property jointly: their row swaps are disjoint and, within any column, their swaps exchange disjoint pairs of row positions. Neither row $0$ nor column $0$ is affected. The resulting quasigroup operation $*$ has two-sided identity $0$, so it gives a loop.

## Simplicity

Let $\equiv$ be any congruence of the loop. For any unmodified row $r$, the permutation $x\mapsto r*x=r+x$ preserves $\equiv$. There are $2^{3m}-6m>2^{3m-1}$ unmodified rows for $m\geq 2$. Their labels span $V$, because a proper subspace has at most $2^{3m-1}$ elements. Composing their translations gives every additive translation of $V$. Hence $\equiv$ is translation invariant, so its classes are the cosets of the subspace
$$
K=\{v\in V:v\equiv 0\}.
$$
Indeed, translation by $y$ shows that $x\equiv y$ exactly when $x+y\in K$, and translation invariance and transitivity imply closure of $K$ under addition.

Suppose $K\ne 0$. In a particular block, fix any trade direction $e_i$. If $K\subseteq\langle e_i\rangle$, nontriviality gives $e_i\in K$. Otherwise choose $k\in K\setminus\langle e_i\rangle$ and take $x=e_{i+1}$, $y=e_{i+2}$. The columns $y$ and $y+k$ are equivalent, and in row $x$ exactly the first of these two cells is switched. The row belongs to only this trade, with affected column set $\{y,y+e_i\}$. The two outputs are $x+y+e_i$ and $x+y+k$. Congruence compatibility and the coset description give $e_i+k\in K$, so $e_i\in K$. This argument applies to every basis direction in every block. Thus $K=V$. The only congruences are equality and the universal relation.

## Lower bound on the number of generators

Let $S\subseteq V$ and start with the vector subspace $W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_2}(S)$. As long as some trade direction $e_i\notin W$ has both its row set and its column set meeting $W$, enlarge $W$ to $W+\langle e_i\rangle$.

Such an enlargement can occur at most once per block. In fact, the selected row and column vectors have the forms $e_{i+1}+\alpha e_i$ and $e_{i+2}+\beta e_i$ for $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb F_2$. These two vectors and $e_i$ form a basis of their common block. Once the enlargement takes place, the entire block is contained in $W$ permanently. Each enlargement increases the dimension by one. The procedure therefore stops with $\dim W\leq |S|+m$.

The final $W$ is closed under $*$. For $x,y\in W$, an unmodified cell has output $x+y\in W$. At a modified cell its direction lies in $W$, since otherwise the cell would permit another enlargement, so the modified output also lies in $W$. A nonempty finite subset of a quasigroup that is closed under multiplication is closed under both divisions, since translations by its elements restrict to injective self-maps and hence to permutations. Here $W$ also contains $0$. Thus $W$ contains the subloop generated by $S$. If $S$ generates $Q_m$, then $W=V$ and
$$
3m\leq |S|+m.
$$

## A generating set of size $2m$

Take $e_2,e_3$ in each block. Within that block, $e_2*e_3=e_1+e_2+e_3$ and $e_3*e_2=e_2+e_3$. Then $(e_2+e_3)*(e_1+e_2+e_3)=e_1$. That cell is unmodified because the triple sum is in no trade column set. Thus all three basis vectors and the triple sum are generated. For every pair of distinct basis elements, at most one of the two input orders is modified, so the unmodified order gives their sum. The identity is already present or is obtained as $e_2*e_2$. These are all the elements of the block.

Two vectors supported on disjoint sets of blocks multiply by ordinary addition. Indeed every modified cell has both nonzero operands in the same block. Combining the generated pure-block elements therefore gives every vector of $V$. This proves the upper bound $2m$, and the previous section proves equality.

The family changes $12m$ cells of the elementary abelian group table. It has generator rank $(2/3)\log_2|Q_m|$, despite simplicity. This is an informal proof of the generator bound.
