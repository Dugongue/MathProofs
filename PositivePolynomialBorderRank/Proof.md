# An Unbounded Positive-Polynomial Border-Rank Gap from the W-State

**Date:** 11 November 2026

**Research status:** A self-contained informal proof of a concrete affirmative instance of a published, open-ended question. Algebraic claims below are proved; independent peer review and a comprehensive novelty/priority check have **not** been completed. The statements do not assert an answer to the separate three-party PSD-tensor questions.

## 1. Published question and context

A. Klingler, T. Netzer and G. De las Cuevas, *Border Ranks of Positive and Invariant Tensor Decompositions: Applications to Correlations*, **Quantum 9**, 1649 (2025), ask in their Conclusions and Outlook whether **positive decompositions of polynomials exhibit gaps between border ranks and ranks**. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2025-02-26-1649>.

We use the *sum-of-squares (SOS) (Omega,G)-rank* from G. De las Cuevas, A. Klingler and T. Netzer, *Polynomial Decompositions with Invariance and Positivity Inspired by Tensors*, **Linear Algebra and its Applications 698** (2024), 537–588, Definition 31. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2024.05.025>.

This is **different** from the same paper's Conjecture 46 concerning rank separations in two variables with unbounded degree. It is also different from its Question 38, for which a formally checked doubled-triangle counterexample is already publicly available: <https://www.trureturing.org/research/delascuevas-klingler-netzer-2024-nonfactorizable-doubled-triangle/>. We do not claim that counterexample as new.

## 2. Exact definition needed

Let `Omega` be the single full facet on `n` vertices (the `n`-party simplex), with no group action initially. Its SOS rank of a real polynomial `p(x_1,...,x_n)` is the least `r` such that

\[
  p=\sum_{\mathbf k\in S_1\times\cdots\times S_n} q_{\mathbf k}^2,
  \quad
  q_{\mathbf k}(x)=\sum_{a=1}^{r}\prod_{i=1}^{n} f^{(i)}_{k_i,a}(x_i),
\]

for finite local index sets `S_i`. Thus every individual square root `q_k` has ordinary tensor rank at most `r` in the tensor product of univariate polynomial spaces. Allowing multiple square roots is essential, and the lower bound below addresses **every** such family.

Its **border SOS rank**, in the coefficient topology of a fixed finite-dimensional space of bounded local degree, is the least `r` such that `p` is a limit of polynomials of SOS rank at most `r`.

## 3. Main theorem

**Theorem.** For each integer `n >= 3`, put

\[
 L_n=x_1+\dots+x_n, \qquad p_n=L_n^2.
\]

Then `p_n` is a nonnegative, symmetric, SOS real polynomial of degree two and

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{sosrank}_{\Sigma_{n-1}}(p_n)=n,
 \qquad \underline{\operatorname{sosrank}}_{\Sigma_{n-1}}(p_n)=2.}
\]

The border-rank gap is therefore `n-2`, which grows without bound. The approximating sequence stays symmetric and nonnegative, and lies in the **fixed space of local degree at most two** (independent of `n`).

For `n=3`, the same statement holds **with full permutation invariance required of the decompositions**:

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{sosrank}_{(\Sigma_2,S_3)}((x+y+z)^2)=3,
 \quad \underline{\operatorname{sosrank}}_{(\Sigma_2,S_3)}((x+y+z)^2)=2.}
\]

Here `S_3` permutes the three variable blocks and the single facet is fixed; a one-square-root invariant decomposition is precisely a symmetric product decomposition.

### Lemma 1 — Unique SOS square roots of a squared linear form

If `L` is a nonzero real linear polynomial and `L^2 = sum_{j=1}^m q_j^2` as an identity in `R[x_1,...,x_n]`, then **every** `q_j` equals `c_j L` for some real constant `c_j`, and `sum c_j^2=1`.

*Proof.* At every real point on the hyperplane `L=0`, the sum of nonnegative real squares is zero, so each `q_j` vanishes there. Since the ideal of this hyperplane is generated by `L`, every `q_j=L h_j` for a polynomial `h_j`. Cancelling `L^2` in the integral domain gives `sum h_j^2=1`. If any `h_j` had positive degree `d`, the degree-`2d` homogeneous part of that identity would be a sum of squares of real nonzero homogeneous forms of degree `d`, which cannot be identically zero (evaluate at a real point where one is nonzero). Thus all `h_j` are constants. QED.

### Lemma 2 — Tensor rank of the W-linear form

For every `n>=1`, the ordinary separation rank of `L_n` across its `n` one-variable factors is exactly `n`:

\[
\operatorname{rank}(L_n)=n.
\]

*Proof.* The displayed sum of `n` monomials gives the upper bound. For the lower bound, apply to every local factor the linear coefficient projection `R[x_i] -> span{1,x_i}` extracting constant and linear coefficients. Any product decomposition of length `r` remains a product decomposition of length at most `r` after projection. Hence it suffices to prove the order-`n` tensor

\[
W_n=\sum_{i=1}^n e_0^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes e_1\otimes e_0^{\otimes(n-i)}
\]

has tensor rank `n` over `R`.

For `n=1` its rank is one. For the induction, write `W_n=e_0\otimes W_{n-1}+e_1\otimes E_{n-1}`, where `E_{n-1}=e_0^{\otimes(n-1)}`. Suppose `W_n` has an `r`-term product decomposition

\[
W_n=\sum_{j=1}^r (a_j e_0+b_j e_1)\otimes t_j,
\]

with each `t_j` a decomposable `(n-1)`-tensor. At least one `b_j` is nonzero since the `e_1` slice is `E_{n-1}`; relabel so `b_r != 0`. Subtract `(a_r/b_r)` times the `e_1` slice from the `e_0` slice:

\[
W_{n-1}-\frac{a_r}{b_r}E_{n-1}
 =\sum_{j<r}\Bigl(a_j-\frac{a_r}{b_r}b_j\Bigr)t_j.
\]

So the left tensor has rank at most `r-1`. But changing basis in **one** of the remaining factors by `e_0 -> e_0`, `e_1 -> e_1+c e_0` sends `W_{n-1}` to `W_{n-1}+c E_{n-1}`. This change is invertible, so `rank(W_{n-1}+cE_{n-1})=rank(W_{n-1})=n-1` by induction. Thus `r-1>=n-1`, or `r>=n`. QED.

### Lemma 3 — Exact SOS rank

For `p_n=L_n^2`, the one-element SOS family containing `L_n` together with its `n`-term product decomposition proves `sosrank(p_n)<=n`.

Conversely, consider any SOS decomposition allowed by Definition 31, for any bond dimension `r`, any finite sets `S_i`, and any local factors. Lemma 1 implies every `q_k=c_kL_n`. At least one `c_k` is nonzero because `p_n != 0`. Thus some nonzero `q_k` has tensor rank `n` by Lemma 2. Every `q_k` in a bond-`r` decomposition has rank at most `r`, so `r>=n`. Therefore `sosrank(p_n)=n`. QED.

### Lemma 4 — A rank-two nonnegative approximating sequence

For real nonzero `t`, set

\[
q_{n,t}(x)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^n(1+t x_i)-1}{t},\qquad
p_{n,t}=q_{n,t}^2.
\]

This is a **two-term tensor decomposition** of the single square root:

\[
q_{n,t}=t^{-1}\prod_i(1+t x_i)-t^{-1}\prod_i1,
\]

and hence `sosrank(p_{n,t})<=2`. Each `p_{n,t}` is globally nonnegative and invariant under all permutations of its `n` variables. The finite identity

\[
q_{n,t}=\sum_i x_i+t\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j+\cdots+t^{n-1}x_1\cdots x_n
\]

shows `q_{n,t}->L_n`, and thus `p_{n,t}->p_n`, **coefficientwise** as `t->0`. Although the total degrees vary with `n`, at every fixed `n` the entire approximation lies in `\bigotimes_i R[x_i]_{<=2}`, so this is a genuine finite-dimensional border-rank statement. Consequently `border-sosrank(p_n)<=2`.

### Lemma 5 — Border rank cannot equal one

At bond dimension one, the single-facet SOS formula factors:

\[
p=\sum_{\mathbf k}\prod_i f_{k_i}^{(i)}(x_i)^2
 =\prod_i\left(\sum_{k_i\in S_i}f_{k_i}^{(i)}(x_i)^2\right).
\]

Thus **every** bond-one SOS polynomial is a pure product of univariate polynomials, and in particular its coefficient flattening across the partition `x_1 | (x_2,...,x_n)` has matrix rank at most one. Rank-one matrices form a closed set (all `2x2` minors vanish). But

\[
p_n=(x_1+L_{n-1})^2=x_1^2+2x_1L_{n-1}+L_{n-1}^2,
\]

whose coefficient flattening has rank **three** for `n>=2`: the coefficient polynomials `1, 2L_{n-1}, L_{n-1}^2` are linearly independent. Hence `p_n` cannot be a coefficientwise limit of bond-one SOS polynomials. Therefore `border-sosrank(p_n)>=2`. Together with Lemma 4, this proves exact border SOS rank two. QED.

## 4. Full permutation-invariance in three variables

Take `n=3` and `G=S_3` acting on the single full facet. We have the **explicit real symmetric** three-term decomposition

\[
 x+y+z
 =\tfrac12(1+x)(1+y)(1+z)
 -\tfrac12(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)-xyz.
\]

Every signed scalar coefficient can be absorbed as the **real cube root** into each of the three identical univariate factors, so this is a valid `(Sigma_2,S_3)`-decomposition of bond dimension three. Lemmas 1–3 force at least three even if arbitrary SOS families are allowed.

Likewise, for real `t>0`,

\[
 q_{3,t}=t^{-1}(1+t x)(1+t y)(1+t z)-t^{-1}\cdot 1\cdot1\cdot1
\]

is a **real symmetric** two-term tensor decomposition: absorb the coefficients `+1/t` and `-1/t` into the three identical local factors by real cube roots. Thus `p_{3,t}=q_{3,t}^2` has invariant SOS rank at most two and tends to `(x+y+z)^2`. Rank one cannot approximate its coefficient flattening. This gives the stated `3 versus 2` invariant border-rank gap. QED.

## 5. How the earlier interpolation proof transfers

The previous two-variable rank-separation construction used finite evaluation maps to **extract a matrix-factorization lower bound** from an arbitrary polynomial decomposition. Here we use the analogous **coefficient projection** onto `{1,x_i}` to extract the `W_n` tensor from an arbitrary product decomposition. Instead of a squared Lagrange basis, the **real SOS identity** makes the extraction rigid: every possible sum-of-squares square root of `L_n^2` is forced to be proportional to `L_n`.

The transferred ingredient is therefore a *faithful positivity-preserving encoding plus a rank-recovery map*. The new feature is that a **degeneration of tensor-product factors** has lower bond dimension even while every polynomial remains nonnegative and symmetric.

## 6. Claims and limitations

* **Proved here (informally, self-contained):** For all `n>=3`, exact SOS rank `n` and border SOS rank `2` for `p_n=(sum x_i)^2`, with local degree bounded by two. For `n=3` this also holds with `S_3`-invariant decomposition factors.
* **Published question addressed:** The existence of positive polynomial decomposition rank/border-rank gaps, as raised explicitly in the 2025 *Quantum* article. This gives a positive instance for the 2024 paper's SOS-decomposition model, not a universal result for every positivity cone or every tensor network geometry.
* **Not claimed:** A three-party **PSD tensor** border-rank gap, a gap for separable polynomial rank (which may be closed), a gap for every `n` with fully invariant rank exactly `n`, or a resolution of any of the 2025 paper's other specific open questions.
* **Priority:** Searches of the cited sources and targeted queries found no matching explicit `(sum x_i)^2` SOS-bond-rank family, and the current `Dugongue/MathProofs` README does not identify it; these are **bounded searches**, not proof of historical novelty. An external literature and independent-proof audit is still needed before representing this as an original publication-ready resolution.
