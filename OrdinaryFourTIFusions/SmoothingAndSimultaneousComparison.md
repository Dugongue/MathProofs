# Support-optimal smoothing and simultaneous ordinary four-tensor isomorphism

This informal proof combines the inverse-cut covariants and finite étale cell algorithm for [ordinary four-tensor isomorphism](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FourTIAlmostAll/FullCoverage.md), the [squarefree covariant construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GenericOrdinaryFourTI/PROOF.md), and the one-parameter perturbation idea for [classical tensor actions](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/Problem18FurtherExtensions/SparseSmoothing.md). The acting group here is the ordinary product of four general linear groups.

## 1. Results

Let \(n\ge 37\), let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) be explicitly represented, and put \(D=n^2+16n^3(n-1)\). Let \(U\) be the open condition that the prescribed \(12\mid34\) flattening is invertible and the four inverse-cut covariants of the squarefree construction have squarefree characteristic polynomials.

1. If \(q>D\), there is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time construction of a single direction \(\Delta\) of CP rank exactly \(n^2\), supported on exactly \(n^2\) coordinates, such that for **every** source \(A\), all but at most \(D\) scalars \(t\in k\) put \(A+t\Delta\) in \(U\). Both the rank and support bounds are optimal for a direction required to put the zero source in \(U\). On a passing perturbation, an exact polynomial-bit-time comparison with every target is available. It is deterministic in fixed characteristic and has zero error and expected polynomial bit time in variable characteristic. A passing scalar is found deterministically by testing at most \(D+1\) distinct field elements. A single direction works for any list of adversarial sources. For \(r\) sources and \(q>rD\), one common scalar is found by at most \(rD+1\) trials.
2. Over **every** finite field, take two explicitly given ordered lists \((A_j)_{j=1}^r,(B_j)_{j=1}^r\) of such tensors. If the source span over the algebraic closure meets \(U\), a zero-error expected-polynomial-bit-time algorithm decides whether one tuple in \(\operatorname{GL}_n(k)^4\) sends every \(A_j\) to its assigned \(B_j\). With a bounded number of trials it can abstain, with probability at most \(2^{-s}\) after \(s\) independent combination trials, apart from any separately imposed arithmetic timeout. The target list is arbitrary. The theorem applies even when no combination over the base field passes. Once a passing combination is supplied over an explicitly presented polynomial-degree extension, the decision phase is deterministic at fixed characteristic. The test puts no separate source condition on the other list entries.

These are exact orbit tests for perturbed tensors or for lists with one common transporter. Restricting the list test to a supplied auxiliary marker enforces the marker comparison as one of the constraints.

## 2. Constructing the sparse direction by symbolic specialization

Reuse the weighted permutation support from Section 2 of the [squarefree four-tensor construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GenericOrdinaryFourTI/PROOF.md#2-a-combinatorial-certificate-in-every-side-dimension). Its permutation \(P\) on \((\mathbb Z/n)^2\) preserves the sum of the two coordinates. The supported tensor \(T(w)\) has entry \(w_{ab}\) at \((a,b,P(a,b))\) and zero elsewhere. The prescribed cut is a weight-diagonal matrix times a permutation. On this support each of the four inverse-cut covariants is diagonal. For every chosen mode \(i\) and distinct labels \(r,s\), the difference of these diagonal entries is a nonzero Laurent polynomial over every field. The published construction proves nonvanishing by a matching monomial with coefficient 1 in the entry for one label and absent in the other. Its eight-row certificate and its translates apply for all \(n\ge37\).

There are \(4\binom n2\) such Laurent polynomials. Each is computable explicitly as a sparse expression in polynomial time. The contraction diagram has four copies of the weighted tensor and four inverse copies, so an expansion can enumerate the eight sets of support row labels, with at most \((n^2)^8\) possibilities. Each term has four weight factors and four inverse factors. Thus the exponent of each weight in a term is between \(-4\) and \(4\), and the number of monomials is polynomial. Compute each difference by exact addition and combine equal monomials.

Specialize the weights one by one, maintaining symbolic nonvanishing of every remaining polynomial. In the current variable multiply any one difference by the fourth power of that variable, obtaining a polynomial of degree at most 8 over the fraction field of the remaining variables. At most eight nonzero substitutions can turn that difference into the zero polynomial. Consequently at most \(32\binom n2=16n(n-1)\) nonzero values are forbidden across the whole list. Test \(16n(n-1)+1\) distinct nonzero field elements using exact sparse evaluation and retain a successful one. No specialization increases the number of monomials or magnitudes of exponents of the other variables. There are \(n^2\) steps, and all field arithmetic and the enumeration of polynomially many distinct coefficient strings take polynomial bit time. The hypothesis \(q>D\) supplies enough field values. The resulting tensor \(\Delta\) lies in \(U\), and its support has exactly \(n^2\) entries. Each coordinate contributes one pure tensor and its invertible cut has matrix rank \(n^2\), proving CP rank exactly \(n^2\).

For the zero source, any successful nonzero scalar perturbation \(t\Delta'\) has an invertible \(n^2\times n^2\) cut. Thus \(\Delta'\) has at least \(n^2\) supported coordinates and at least \(n^2\) pure summands. This gives both lower bounds in the stated smoothing model.

## 3. From the direction to a uniform exceptional-set bound

Write \(M(T)\) for the cut matrix and \(W_i(T)\) for its four rational inverse-cut covariants. Clearing denominators makes \(P_i(T)=(\det M(T))^4 W_i(T)\) polynomial, homogeneous of degree \(4n^2\) in its entries as functions of the tensor coordinates. The degree counts four copies of the tensor and four adjugates. The discriminant of an \(n\times n\) characteristic polynomial is homogeneous of degree \(n(n-1)\) in the entries of its matrix. Thus

\[
F(T)=\det M(T)\prod_{i=1}^4 \operatorname{disc}(\chi_{P_i(T)})
\]

is homogeneous of degree \(D\), and \(U\) is exactly its nonzero locus. In particular \(F(\Delta)\ne0\). For every \(A\), the coefficient of \(t^D\) in \(F(A+t\Delta)\) is \(F(\Delta)\). This gives the uniform root bound, including inputs chosen adversarially. Use a union bound on the forbidden scalars for several sources. A scalar trial evaluates \(U\) by ordinary matrix and polynomial operations. The orbit comparison on \(U\) is the large-field method in the [squarefree four-tensor proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GenericOrdinaryFourTI/PROOF.md#4-solve-the-entire-residual-orbit-including-zero-entries-and-nonsplit-spectra). Its finite-étale decomposition and cyclic-quotient check allow arbitrary vanishing cells and do not compute discrete logarithms.

## 4. Descending a testing combination while keeping the original transporter

Choose an explicit finite extension \(K/k\) of smallest degree with \(|K|\ge2D\), using \(k\) itself when it is large enough. The degree is \(O(1+\log_q n)\), and an irreducible defining polynomial can be found in expected polynomial bit time by random trial and deterministic irreducibility checking. Write \(A(s)=\sum_j s_j A_j\), \(B(s)=\sum_j s_j B_j\) for \(s\in K^r\). The promise that the algebraic-closure span meets \(U\) is precisely that \(F(A(s))\) is a nonzero polynomial. It has degree at most \(D\). Uniform \(s\) passes with probability at least one half. Keep a passing \(s\) and reject if \(B(s)\) fails the same test. Any common transporter over \(k\) must carry the selected combination to the selected target combination. No choice of \(s\) is used as a basis-change parameter.

For each mode let \(W_i,W_i'\in M_n(K)\) be the source and target covariants of the selected combinations. The necessary conditions on a transporter are the linear equations \(W_i'G_i=G_i W_i\), with \(G_i\) over **\(k\)**. Expand in the fixed \(k\)-basis of \(K\) to solve them over \(k\), obtaining a space \(H_i\). Also compute the \(k\)-algebra

\[
E_i=\{X\in M_n(k): XW_i=W_i X\}.
\]

Over \(K\) the source centralizer is commutative and reduced, since its characteristic polynomial is squarefree and the operator is cyclic. Hence \(E_i\) is commutative and reduced, so it is a product of finite fields. Its structure constants and the actions on \(k^n\) are obtained by linear algebra. Split it by the same finite étale routine as in the [four-tensor decision proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FourTIAlmostAll/FullCoverage.md#2-exact-decision-for-every-accepted-first-input).

An invertible matrix in \(H_i\) can now be found by a direct test. For each primitive idempotent \(e\) of \(E_i\), form the subspace \(H_i e\). Reject if any of these is zero. Choose one nonzero matrix in each and add them, yielding \(G_i^0\). If this sum is singular, reject. To justify rejection, if some invertible \(G\in H_i\) exists, then \(H_i=GE_i\). Every nonzero element of \(H_i e\) is \(G\) times a nonzero element of the field factor \(E_i e\). The sum of the chosen multipliers is a unit of \(E_i\), so the constructed \(G_i^0\) is invertible. In the success case the whole set of invertible intertwiners is exactly \(G_i^0 E_i^\times\).

Apply the inverses of \(G_i^0\) in the four modes of every target list member. The problem is now to find one tuple of units \(x_i\in E_i^\times\) carrying all source members to their transformed targets. For each mode decompose \(k^n\) into its primitive-idempotent summands. A summand is a vector space over its acting field factor. Choose a field basis for it by adjoining a vector whenever its field span increases, testing by \(k\)-linear algebra. Across four factors and one list member the corresponding space is a free module over the tensor product of the four chosen fields. Split that tensor product into finite fields. Each output cell is thus a vector space over one displayed finite field. Each tuple of units acts on it simply by the product of the four embedded units. The sum of the \(k\)-dimensions, with all multiplicities and list indices included, is \(rn^4\).

On each cell compare the source and target vectors. A zero vector must correspond to zero, which imposes no constraint. Otherwise compute a nonzero scalar ratio from one nonzero coordinate and check the full vector against it. The residual problem is now exactly the cyclic-group image membership problem in the four-tensor decision proof. There is one unknown for each original field factor of each \(E_i\), and one output equation for each nonzero cell of each list member. Repeated factors across cells or list members use the same unknown. The embedding exponents and output orders are computed in the compatible field models of that proof and the integer Smith quotient test runs unchanged. It is therefore a decision about a **single** base-field transporter. Passing all the tests is sufficient since the solved unit conditions give actual invertible \(k\)-linear maps in all four modes.

The dimensions of the working algebras are bounded by the explicit input length and the extension degree. Field splitting is deterministic in fixed characteristic and can use zero-error polynomial-expected-time polynomial factoring in arbitrary characteristic. Integer Smith operations and powering have polynomial bit cost. This proves the claimed bit-time and probability guarantees.

## 5. Scope and attribution

The large-field four-tensor result bounds the rejected fraction of uniformly random raw inputs. The first theorem here constructs a deterministic single direction that works around each adversarial base tensor, with optimal rank and coordinate support for the invertible-cut test. The second theorem uses the same covariants to compare ordered tensor families with a common base-field transporter even when the reference combination exists only after extending scalars. Neither theorem uses a larger mode group to test the base-field comparison. The scope is ordinary four-tensor decision, with exact finite-field arithmetic. Related published average-case and smoothed results have different distributions or actions.
