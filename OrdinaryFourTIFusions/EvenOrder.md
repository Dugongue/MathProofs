# Almost-all ordinary tensor isomorphism in every even order

Fix a finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\). Let \(d\ge4\) be even, let all \(d\) dimensions be \(n\ge2\), and use dense inputs. The group is \(\operatorname{GL}_n(k)^d\), with one independent change of basis in each physical mode. The order \(d\) can be part of the input.

**Theorem.** There is an invariant deterministic polynomial-time recognizable source class of density \(1-o(1)\) as \(n\to\infty\), uniformly over the even orders. For every recognized source and every target, deterministic polynomial bit time suffices for exact orbit decision, a transporter on yes-instances, and the exact number of transporters. The exact count is also computable with any prescribed determinants and, more generally, any separately specified polynomial-time filter on each mode matrix. For \(d\ge6\) the recognition failure is \(O_q(1/\log n)\), uniformly in \(d\). The exponent of the polynomial runtime is independent of \(d\).

The proof uses the invertible-cut mixing and the singular-cut projection arguments of the [almost-all ordinary four-tensor result](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/FourTIAlmostAll). An even tensor can be grouped across a square cut while retaining a *single physical mode* on each side. For invertible cuts this allows reciprocal contractions with one small factor on each side. For singular cuts exact kernel forms project the remaining grouped factors down to duals of those small factors. Changing the two retained modes recovers anchors for the entire physical action. The proof does not require the grouped transporters to admit Kronecker factorizations.

## 1. One square cut and two physical modes

Use one prescribed cut \(L\mid R\) with \(d/2\) modes on each side. Its pair flattening is a square matrix \(F\) of side \(N=n^{d/2}\). For any \(i\in L,j\in R\), regroup the format as \((m,n,n,m)\), retaining modes \(i,j\) as the two dimensions \(n\), and bundling the other modes on each side into dimension \(m=n^{d/2-1}\). Both grouped sides of the cut are still of dimension \(mn=N\). Fix any bijection between \(L\) and \(R\), so a collection of \(d/2\) such pairings visits every physical mode once.

For an invertible cut use \(F^{-T}\) as a tensor in the four dual factors. It transforms contragrediently. Contract the original and dual tensor on the two bundled factors, obtaining an endomorphism \(C_{ij}\) on the tensor product of the retained physical modes. Then

\[
E_i=\operatorname{Tr}_j(C_{ij}^2),\qquad E_j=\operatorname{Tr}_i(C_{ij}^2)
\]

are conjugated by the physical changes of basis in modes \(i,j\), respectively. This is the reciprocal crossed-contraction construction of the four-tensor theorem with the two outer modes enlarged. The following argument shows that enlargement preserves the mixing estimate needed on each retained mode.

## 2. The rectangular reciprocal-anchor estimate

Conditional on invertibility, \(F\) is uniform in \(\operatorname{GL}_{mn}(k)\). Uniformly for \(m\ge n\), each anchor above is within \(q^{-\Omega(n)}\) in total variation of a uniform \(n\)-by-\(n\) matrix in odd characteristic, or of a uniform matrix with trace \(mn\cdot1_k\) in characteristic two. Constants can depend on the fixed field. Here are the modifications that give this estimate from the [square reciprocal-anchor proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FourTIAlmostAll/SingleAnchor.md).

Split the first grouped mode of size \(m\) into two halves and shear the flattening by \(I+X\), with \(X\) arbitrary between the halves and zero elsewhere. The tensor of dual rows shears by \((I-X)^T\). Each selected primal row of the uniform baseline now reshapes to an \(n\)-by-\(m\) matrix \(U\), and each selected inverse-transpose row reshapes after transpose to an \(m\)-by-\(n\) matrix \(V\). The pairing between the two row spaces is \(\operatorname{tr}(UV)\), and the changes of primal and dual rows still lie on disjoint first-mode indices. Consequently the crossed contraction is affine in the shear entries. Every matrix product contributing to a block of the linear shear term is still \(UV\) of size \(n\)-by-\(n\). The polar form for a character with label \(P\in M_n(k)\) still pairs blocks with \(\omega_P(U_0,V_0)=\operatorname{tr}(P(U_0V_0+V_0U_0))\) for square \(n\)-by-\(n\) blocks \(U_0,V_0\). All labels, anticommutator ranks, restrictions to traceless blocks, and scalar exceptions therefore use the same (n) as in the square proof.

For clarity, the following are the places where the baseline row dimensions enter the two estimates.

1. In the low-rank-label restriction, take \(a=b=\lfloor n/5\rfloor\) first-mode indices from the two halves, and use the same target-index count and fixed source index in the second mode of size \(n\). For a nonscalar dual label \(Z\in M_n(k)\), choose a column \(u\) and row \(v\) with \(vu=0\) and \(vZu=1\). In the annihilator calculation the rows \(vU_j\) are now vectors of length \(m\). Exposing the other directions leaves independent uniform length-\(m\) vectors \(vZU_j\). Projection modulo the span of the \(vU_j\) gives the deficit bound \(4q^{-\delta(m-2b+\delta)}\), at least as strong as the old bound with \(n\) in place of \(m\). Every ensuing surjectivity and label-union bound is identical. For the numerical parameters of that proof, failure is \(q^{-\Omega(n^2)}\) and the shear polar rank is at least \(4n^2\).
2. In the high-rank-label restriction take \(a=b=\lfloor10^{-4}n\rfloor\) first-mode indices from the two halves as before. The proof's independent inverse-row model consists now of \(m\)-by-\(n\) matrices. For \(h=\lceil2\sqrt n\rceil\), a nonzero prescribed combination has rank below \(n-h\) with probability at most \(4q^{-h(m-n+h)}\). Union over the \(q^a-1\) combinations still has failure \(q^{-\Omega(n)}\). When \(V\) and \(M\) have rank at least \(n-h\), the map \(U\mapsto UV\) from \(M_{n,m}\) has image of codimension at most \(nh\) in \(M_n\), and \(Z\mapsto MZ\) from \(M_n\) has kernel of dimension at most \(nh\). Thus \(U\mapsto M(P(UV)+(UV)P)\) loses at most \(2nh\) from the square anticommutator rank, exactly the loss used in the old batch-span estimate. The annihilator of the allowable primal rows has dimension at most \(a\), and the duality in the row-pool rank proof is now the perfect rectangular trace pairing above. The batch-span and row-pool union bounds therefore still have failure \(q^{-\Omega(n^3)}\), apart from the inverse-row rank event. The shear polar rank is at least \(4n^2\) for every nontrivial label.
3. In both restrictions, the prescribed primal and dual baseline rows have disjoint labels. Their total number is at most \(n^2/2\) once \(n\) is large, since the low-rank restriction uses at most \(a(n-1)+b\) rows and the high-rank one at most \(b(n-1)+a\). The independent row models condition on independence of the selected rows in the indicated ambient space and its annihilator. Ambient dimension is \(mn\ge n^2\), so the probability losses are no worse than the square bounds.

Thus outside an event of probability \(q^{-\Omega(n)}\) over the baseline, each relevant phase has polar rank at least \(4n^2\) on the full shear space. Bias squared is at most \(q^{-4n^2}\), by additive-character differencing. The trace restriction in characteristic two follows from \(\operatorname{tr}(C_{ij})=mn\cdot1_k\) and \(\operatorname{tr}(C_{ij}^2)=\operatorname{tr}(C_{ij})^2\). Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz bound the conditional total variation by \(\tfrac12 q^{-3n^2/2}\) since there are at most \(q^{n^2}\) characters. Averaging over the baseline proves the estimate. Swapping the two sides gives the same estimate for the other retained mode. No joint mixing claim is needed.

## 3. The singular cut: project each half using an exact kernel form

Suppose \(F\) has positive corank \(c\) and \(q^c\le n\). Write \(K_L,K_R\) for its left and right kernels as covectors on the two grouped spaces. Choose one exact nonzero \(\ell\in K_L\) and \(r\in K_R\). The algorithm enumerates the at most \(n^2\) pairs of choices. For each retained mode \(i\in L\), \(\ell\) is a pairing between the physical \(V_i=k^n\) and the remaining bundle of dimension \(m\). If it has rank \(n\), contraction on its bundle gives a surjection to \(V_i^*\). Extend it by the identity on \(V_i\) to a map

\[
\pi_i:L_{\mathrm{space}}\longrightarrow V_i\otimes V_i^*=\operatorname{End}(V_i).
\]

For \(j\in R\) define \(\pi_j\) from \(r\) in the same way. We retain only form pairs of rank \(n\) for all the selected pairings. Apply \(\pi_i\otimes\pi_j\) to \(T\), obtaining \(Z_{ij}\in\operatorname{End}(V_i)\otimes\operatorname{End}(V_j)\). Take the two partial traces of \(Z_{ij}^2\). If the tensor and the two exact kernel forms are transported together, the contractions send each bundle change to the contragredient change on the physical dual. Therefore these two anchors conjugate in the physical modes. No basis of a large radical, no extension of its automorphisms, and no covariant choice of a representative of a kernel form is necessary. The algorithm matches form pairs by enumeration at both endpoints.

We prove coverage for \(d\ge6\), and use the existing search theorem for \(d=4\). Conditional on the two entire kernel spaces of dimension \(c\), the tensor is uniform among the invertible coefficient matrices on their annihilator spaces of dimension \(N-c\). For fixed form choices and retained modes, the images \(S_i,S_j\) of the respective annihilator spaces are subspaces of \(M_n(k)\) of codimension at most \(c\), since each \(\pi\) was surjective before restriction. Both consist of trace-zero matrices because the two chosen forms vanish on the corresponding annihilators. In bases putting the two projections in coordinate form, \(Z_{ij}\) is a rectangular block of the uniform invertible matrix of side \(N-c\). The block has at most \(n^2\) rows and at most \(n^2\) columns. Its law is within \(q^{n^2-(N-c)}/(q-1)\) of uniform on \(S_i\otimes S_j\). Indeed the first \(b\le n^2\) columns of the uniform invertible matrix are uniform independent columns conditioned on linear independence, which fails for unconditioned columns with probability at most \(q^{b-(N-c)}/(q-1)\). Projecting to the retained rows does not increase total variation. For \(d\ge6\) we have \(N\ge n^3\), so the bound is exponentially small uniformly in \(d\).

The [singular four-tensor partial-trace argument, Section 3](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FixedFieldOrdinaryFourTI/PROOF.md#3-the-live-partial-traces-have-almost-uniform-joint-law) applies directly on \(S_i\otimes S_j\). Its hypotheses are the two trace-zero subspaces, codimension at most \(c\), and uniform input in their tensor product. To record its quantitative scope, remove the radicals of the two restricted trace pairings, leaving spaces of dimension at least \(n^2-2c\) with nondegenerate pairings. On their tensor product the polar operator has form \(A_P\otimes I+I\otimes A_Q\), where \(A_P\) is the projected anticommutator. For nonscalar \(P\), every scalar shift of the full anticommutator has rank at least \(n\), by restricting to matrices \(u\otimes v\) with \(Pu\notin ku\) and projecting modulo the line of \(u\). The trace-space operation loses at most \(4c\) ranks. Triangularizing the other anticommutator over an algebraic closure now gives a polar rank of at least \((n-4c)(n^2-2c)\). The argument is symmetric in the labels. For scalar labels in odd characteristic, the polar operator is a nonzero scalar on the whole restricted tensor product unless the character is trivial on the equal-trace target space. In characteristic two the scalar labels are trivial, since the anchor traces are zero. The Fourier estimate is therefore \(q^{-\Omega(n^3)}\) when \(c\le\log_q n\), uniformly in the subspaces. Each anchor marginal is exponentially close to a uniform matrix, with trace zero in characteristic two. This proof controls the genuine tensor law by adding the preceding projection error.

Finally, conditional on any fixed positive corank, its left and right kernel spaces are uniform by the independent general linear actions on the two square-cut sides. A uniform nonzero choice in a uniform kernel is uniform among the nonzero covectors of that side. In the \(n\)-by-\(m\) reshape for a fixed physical mode, failure of rank \(n\) has probability at most \(2q^{n-m}/(q-1)\), by the count of full-row-rank matrices and conditioning on nonzero. Union over the \(d\) modes gives \(O_q(dq^{n-m})=o(1/\log n)\) uniformly in \(d\ge6\), since \(m=n^{d/2-1}\). This random choice is used only to bound the probability that there exists a good form pair. The actual algorithm tries all pairs. The probability that the cut corank exceeds \(\lfloor\log_q n\rfloor\) is at most \(4q^{-(\lfloor\log_q n\rfloor+1)^2}\), from the elementary square-matrix rank count in the reciprocal-anchor proof.

## 4. Uniform coverage and exact comparison

For an anchor \(E\), put \(D(E)=\sum_{m_f\ge2}(\deg f)m_f^2\), where \(m_f\) is the multiplicity of the irreducible \(f\) in its characteristic polynomial. The [four-tensor primary count](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FourTIAlmostAll/FullCoverage.md#1-the-operators-and-their-laws) gives \(\mathbb E D(E)=O_q(1)\) for a uniform matrix or one with a prescribed trace. For any matrix, \(D(E)\le n^2\), because \(\sum_f(\deg f)m_f=n\). Thus the exponentially small marginal errors in Sections 2 and 3 preserve the bounded expectations, uniformly in the order. For every attempted source form pair, accept its anchor shape if

\[
q^{\sum_{i=1}^d D(E_i)}\le n^d.
\]

On the singular branch the conditional expectation is bounded for every fixed pair of full-rank form choices and entire kernel spaces. The cut-corank and form-rank estimates then allow Markov's inequality for the sum, with threshold \(d\log_q n\). It bounds the chance of failing to find a good pair by \(O_q(1/\log n)\). On the invertible branch the same bound follows from the sum of the unconditional marginal expectations in that sector. This use of the sum avoids any deterioration of the density with \(d\). For \(d=4\) use the existing \(1-o(1)\) recognition bound.

For a recognized source choose one good form pair on the singular branch, and enumerate all exact target form pairs of full rank when the corank agrees. Every genuine transporter appears for one of these pairs. For each comparison align the physical anchors by rational canonical form. A mismatch rejects that branch. The remaining candidate mode maps are in the products of centralizers. Enumerate the repeated-primary units, at most \(n^d\) by the shape test. On the other blocks the only variables are multiplicative field units. Split their tensor products into finite-field cells, with total dimension \(n^d\). The equations are exactly the original tensor action, with labelled repeated coordinates and the remaining units acting by explicit field embeddings. Compare zero supports and test the vector of nonzero coefficient ratios for membership in the image of the field-unit map. The [cyclic-quotient procedure of the four-tensor proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FourTIAlmostAll/FullCoverage.md#2-exact-decision-for-every-accepted-first-input) is valid with any number of modes. Its field order entries are obtained from the finitely many displayed cell degrees, and its lattice has one row per nonzero cell. It uses integer Smith normal form, modular powering and transport through common subfields, without discrete logarithms in extension fields or construction of a large common compositum. All those degrees, the sum of dimensions of the cells, and the number of variables are bounded by a polynomial in \(n^d\). An accepted branch gives a genuine transporter of the original tensor, whether or not it happens to carry the selected kernel forms.

## 5. Certified stabilizers, witnesses, and determinant profiles

A nonzero tensor has all central stabilizers \((u_i I_n)_i\) with \(\prod_i u_i=1\). A specified tuple with a nonscalar mode fixes at most a \(q^{-n^{d-1}}\) fraction of tensors. Indeed take a vector \(v\) in that mode whose image is outside its line. Restrict the difference of the tensor-product action and the identity to \(v\otimes\bigotimes_{j\ne i}V_j\), and project the output modulo the line of \(v\) in mode \(i\). The projected map is injective. There are fewer than \(q^{dn^2}\) invertible tuples, so the chance of any nonscalar stabilizer is bounded by \(q^{dn^2-n^{d-1}}\), which tends to zero uniformly over \(d\ge4\) as \(n\to\infty\). The zero tensor has negligible probability and can be rejected by the recognizer.

Certify this condition deterministically against the raw source by running the comparison against itself. In each successful branch the finite-field cell solutions form a coset of the kernel of the field-unit map. Its exponent is computable from the same integer congruence presentation. When it does not divide \(q-1\), the branch witnesses a nonscalar stabilizer and the recognizer abstains. Otherwise the [bounded-exponent field-unit solver](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FourTIAlmostAll/SearchAndLifting.md#2-inverting-a-successful-field-unit-test) constructs a witness and kernel generators in polynomial time. In outline, each variable's \((q-1)\)-st power is an explicit product of powers of the visible ratios, since the corresponding coordinate character is zero on the kernel. Extract roots by factoring a polynomial of fixed degree \(q-1\), and correct the choices using logs only in the base field's fixed unit group. The homogeneous congruences in this base group give the kernel generators. Test that the resulting stabilizer witness and all its kernel generators act by scalars on the physical modes. Run the test for every successful branch. Every genuine stabilizer is included in some branch. All maps in each branch satisfy the original tensor equation, so there are no false nonscalar witnesses even when a branch includes maps beyond its chosen kernel-form correspondence. The test succeeds exactly when the stabilizer of a shape-accepted nonzero tensor is the stated scalar group. For order four the previous search proof already certifies this property on its recognition domain.

After successful certification, any successful source-to-target branch again has unit kernel exponent dividing \(q-1\), since that kernel acts by actual stabilizers of the source with its chosen repeated parts fixed. Use the bounded-exponent solver to get one explicit transporter \((g_i)_i\). The complete set of transporters is then

\[
\{(u_i g_i)_i : u_i\in k^\times,\ \prod_i u_i=1\}.
\]

Count these directly as \((q-1)^{d-1}\). Given independent predicates on the individual mode matrices, determine on each mode its allowed scalar list by trying the \(q-1\) choices. For a prescribed determinant \(\delta_i\), the list equality is \(u_i^n\det(g_i)=\delta_i\). Other allowed predicates include preservation of specified bilinear or sesquilinear forms, fixing specified vectors, or intersecting any of these with determinant restrictions. A product-state dynamic program on \(k^\times\) counts the allowed tuples with product one and can reconstruct one from a nonempty count. The overhead is at most \(O(dq^2)\) additions of integers of \(O(d\log q)\) bits after the filter evaluations. This finishes decision, construction and counting with the same density and with the target arbitrary.

The recognition law and the proof concern balanced physical dimensions, even order, and fixed finite field. The new proof is informal. [Li–Li–Qiao–Tao–Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00591v2) pose the four-factor average-case question. [Grochow–Qiao's TI-completeness theory](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110) gives worst-case reductions between tensor orders. The reciprocal and singular projections above preserve the actual random-source model and recover separate physical basis changes.
