# Exact rotor-routing compression on finite ribbon covers

A regular ribbon cover admits a canonical deck-invariant affine compression from all rotor forests rooted at a sink fiber to the spanning trees downstairs precisely when the covering degree is coprime to the base sandpile order. The compression forgets the sheet, fixes every lifted spanning tree after identification, and intertwines every rotor addition. It exists for covers of arbitrarily large ribbon genus with planar base. In that case Chan, Church, and Grochow's canonical planar action governs the compressed dynamics, while the ordinary upstairs action with a single sink depends on the sink somewhere.

## Setup

Let $\pi:\widetilde G\to G$ be a finite connected regular cover of connected loopless ribbon multigraphs. A ribbon structure is a cyclic order on the outgoing darts at each vertex. The covering map gives bijections on stars and preserves their cyclic orders. The deck group $\Gamma$ acts transitively on each fiber, and each fiber has $d$ vertices. For a base vertex $s$, take the whole fiber $S=\pi^{-1}(s)$ as the upstairs sink set. Rotor routing stops a chip at its first visit to $S$. Equivalently, identify all vertices of $S$ to a single sink while retaining the edges and orders at nonsinks.

Let $\mathcal T(G)$ be the spanning trees of $G$, oriented toward $s$ when they represent downstairs rotor states. Let $\widetilde{\mathcal F}_S$ be the upstairs rooted forests, with one outgoing dart at every nonsink and every directed path terminating at a sink. They are spanning rotor states, and their undirected components each meet $S$ in one vertex. Write $K_s$ and $\widetilde K_S$ for the integral cokernels of the respective reduced Laplacians. The rotor-router torsor theorem gives free transitive actions of these groups on the corresponding sets. A deck transformation carries rotor additions to the additions at the transformed vertices. [Holroyd et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.3306), [Chan, Church, and Grochow](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2677).

For a chip vector, define $j$ by copying its value at each base nonsink to every lift, and define $p$ by summing over each nonsink fiber. The reduced Laplacian relations respect these maps. Write $\bar j$ and $\bar p$ for their maps on sandpile groups. Write $T^\uparrow$ for the full lift of the tree $T$, oriented toward $S$.

**Theorem.** For every sink fiber the following hold.

1. The map $T\mapsto T^\uparrow$ is a bijection $\mathcal T(G)\to\widetilde{\mathcal F}_S^\Gamma$ that intertwines $K_s$ with its lifted action under $\bar j$. The map $\bar j$ identifies $K_s$ with $\widetilde K_S^\Gamma$, and $\bar p$ identifies the coinvariants $(\widetilde K_S)_\Gamma$ with $K_s$.
2. A deck-invariant affine map $\Phi_s\colon\widetilde{\mathcal F}_S\to\mathcal T(G)$ with $\Phi_s(T^\uparrow)=T$ exists exactly when $\gcd(d,|K_s|)=1$. Here affine means that one group homomorphism $\rho:\widetilde K_S\to K_s$ satisfies $\Phi_s(x\cdot F)=\rho(x)\cdot_s\Phi_s(F)$ for all states and additions. When it exists, the map is unique, with $\rho=d^{-1}\bar p$.
3. Under the natural identification of every $K_s$ with $\operatorname{Pic}^0(G)$, the action compressed from a common upstairs degree-zero chip divisor has the same class at every sink fiber. For a planar ribbon base, these classes act by the very same permutations of the unrooted base trees.

## Descent on the integral lattices

Let $L$ and $\widetilde L$ denote the two reduced Laplacians, acting on column vectors. They are nonsingular over $\mathbb Q$. Indeed, extending a vector by zero on the sink set, its Laplacian quadratic form is a sum of squared differences over edges. Vanishing forces the vector to be constant on the connected graph and zero at the sinks. The star bijections give $\widetilde L j=jL$. Since $p=j^{\mathsf T}$ and both Laplacians are symmetric, also $p\widetilde L=Lp$. Thus the group maps exist, and $\bar p\bar j=d\operatorname{id}$.

View $\widetilde K_S$ as the subgroup $\widetilde L^{-1}\mathbb Z^{\widetilde V\setminus S}/\mathbb Z^{\widetilde V\setminus S}$ of the rational coordinate torus. This identification sends the class of $z$ to that of $\widetilde L^{-1}z$ and respects the deck permutation of coordinates. If a class is fixed by $\Gamma$, its representative with each coordinate in $[0,1)$ is constant on each fiber. It is $j y$ for a rational vector $y$ downstairs. Now $\widetilde L j y=jLy$ is integral, so $Ly$ is integral. This proves that every fixed class comes from downstairs. In the other direction, $j y$ is integral only when $y$ is integral, proving injectivity. The argument works integrally on the sandpile classes, including primes dividing $d$.

Coinvariants of a group action on an abelian group mean the quotient by all differences $\gamma x-x$. On the free upstairs nonsink lattice, imposing these differences identifies the basis elements in each fiber. The quotient map is exactly $p$. Imposing the reduced Laplacian relations as well gives the lattice quotient by $p\widetilde L\mathbb Z^{\widetilde V\setminus S}$. Since $p$ is surjective and intertwines the Laplacians, this image is $L\mathbb Z^{V\setminus\{s\}}$. Thus $\bar p$ is the coinvariant quotient. This is the augmentation of a permutation module, applied fiber by fiber before taking the cokernel. The corresponding regular-representation augmentation and fiber torsor formulations appear in the cited Lean library sources. The lattice calculation here is independent of those formalizations. [Mathlib on cyclic coinvariants](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Homological/FiniteCyclic.html), [Mathlib on quotient covering fibers](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Covering/Quotient.html).

## Descent of rotor states and additions

A deck-fixed upstairs rotor choice gives one common outgoing dart downstairs at each nonsink, because the stars map bijectively to the base star. A directed cycle among those choices would lift to a directed cycle after finitely many traversals, since its transport permutes a finite fiber. Thus the descended choices form a tree directed toward $s$. Conversely, a path in a base tree reaches $s$, and every lift reaches a vertex of $S$. This proves the bijection on fixed states. The lifted unoriented forest consists of $d$ copies of the tree, each containing one lift of every base vertex.

A downstairs routing run lifts one step at a time by firing each lift of the chosen vertex once. Each step moves one chip per sheet along the corresponding dart. The receiving map on the destination fiber is a bijection, unless the chips are being absorbed at the sink fiber. Each bundle of steps thus returns to a symmetric chip configuration and implements exactly the base rotor update. With the lifted input chip vector, this legal sequence yields precisely the lift of the base output. By order independence and passage to the relative sandpile group, this intertwines the actions on all fixed states.

If $\Phi_s$ is deck-invariant and affine, its homomorphism $\rho$ is deck-invariant. Indeed applying a deck element to both a state and one of its translations gives translated images with the same base state, and the base action is free. By the coinvariant calculation $\rho=f\bar p$ for a unique group endomorphism $f$ of $K_s$. The identity on lifted trees implies $\rho\bar j=\operatorname{id}$. Consequently $f(d x)=x$ for every $x\in K_s$. The finite abelian group has such a left inverse to multiplication by $d$ exactly when $d$ is coprime to its order. Then $f$ must be multiplication by $d^{-1}$ on that group.

Conversely, under this coprimality condition choose any base tree $T_0$. For every upstairs forest $F$ there is one $x\in\widetilde K_S$ with $F=x\cdot T_0^\uparrow$. Define
$$
 \Phi_s(F)=(d^{-1}\bar p(x))\cdot_s T_0.
$$
Replacing $T_0$ by $y\cdot_s T_0$ replaces $x$ by $x-\bar j(y)$ and leaves this expression unchanged. The map is deck-invariant, affine, and fixes lifted trees after identification. Its group map and its value at one lifted tree determine the entire map. In particular, any prescribed sequence of rotor additions projects exactly to the sequence of their normalized pushforwards. Independent random additions yield exact Markov lumping for any distribution on additions.

## Changing the sink fiber

The identification $\operatorname{Pic}^0(G)\cong K_s$ deletes the $s$ coordinate in a degree-zero divisor. The column of the full Laplacian at $s$ is the negative of the sum of its other columns, so the firing relations after deletion are precisely the reduced Laplacian relations. Let $D$ be any degree-zero divisor on $\widetilde G$ and feed its restriction to upstairs nonsinks into the action for the sink set $S$. Forgetting the coordinates on $S$ and then applying $p$ gives the restriction of $\pi_*D$ to base nonsinks, where $\pi_*$ adds divisor coefficients over full fibers. Thus the compressed addition is $d^{-1}[\pi_*D]\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(G)$ for every choice of $s$. This comparison uses a common divisor upstairs. Individual firings at discarded upstairs sinks need not descend through the relative quotient.

On the fixed sector, the comparisons are especially direct. The lifts of an unoriented base tree have the same edges for every sink fiber and can be reoriented toward the new sinks. Each choice of sink gives the corresponding downstairs action under the same tree identification. Chan, Church, and Grochow's Theorem 2 says that these downstairs actions are independent of $s$ for planar ribbon $G$. Therefore on a planar base, any two upstairs states with the same projected tree continue to have the same projected tree under additions restricted from the same degree-zero divisors, even when the two wired forest spaces have different sizes. [Chan, Church, and Grochow, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2677).

## Unequal orbit sizes

There is also an integral test when a finite group of ribbon automorphisms has vertex stabilizers. Take a nonempty invariant sink set. For its nonsink orbits $O_1,\ldots,O_b$, put $D_0=\operatorname{diag}(|O_i|)$. With $M$ now the upstairs Dirichlet Laplacian and $v_i\in O_i$, define $(M_+)_{ij}=\sum_{w\in O_j}M_{v_iw}$. This is the restriction to orbit-constant vectors. On orbit sums the corresponding matrix is $M_-=M_+^{\mathsf T}$ by symmetry. The fixed and coinvariant groups are $\operatorname{coker}M_+$ and $\operatorname{coker}M_-$, by the same fractional-part and basis-identification arguments as above. Their natural map is induced by $D_0$, and $D_0 M_+=M_-D_0$. Both groups have the same finite order since the matrices are nonsingular transposes. An invariant group retraction onto the fixed subgroup exists exactly when the induced $D_0$ map is an isomorphism. Equivalently the columns of $[M_-\mid D_0]$ generate $\mathbb Z^b$, a Smith normal form test.

When each nonsink stabilizer fixes its outgoing darts, a fixed rooted forest exists. For each orbit choose one dart toward a vertex with strictly smaller distance from the sink set, and translate that choice throughout the orbit. Distances decrease along every selected path. With a fixed forest as reference, the group criterion is also the exact criterion for an invariant affine retraction onto fixed forests, by the same torsor proof. This allows singular orbits at sinks and does not require a common degree on all nonsink orbits.

## Simple covers of arbitrary ribbon genus

For $k=2g+1$ take a planar bouquet of $k$ triangles sharing just a vertex $s$, with consecutive triangle darts in the rotation at $s$. Assign one edge of each triangle the nonzero voltage in $\mathbb Z/2$ and all others zero. The connected double cover is simple, as is the base. Lift the rotation system. Every inner face has nonzero voltage, and the outer face has voltage $k\bmod 2=1$. Each face therefore has one face upstairs, with twice the boundary length. Upstairs there are $4k+2$ vertices, $6k$ edges and $k+1$ faces. Its ribbon genus is $g$. Topologically, each lifted face boundary of degree two can be filled by a disk with one branch point over the base face. The result makes the surface genus calculation visible in the branched covering picture.

Each triangle contributes three independent spanning-tree choices downstairs, so $|K_s|=3^k$. Thus the degree two is coprime to the sandpile order. For $g>0$ the upstairs ribbon graph is nonplanar, and Theorem 2 of Chan, Church, and Grochow makes its ordinary one-vertex-sink action basepoint-dependent. Its wired fiber dynamics nevertheless have the canonical planar compression just proved. For an opposite control, the connected double cover of the square has base order $4$, upstairs wired group $(\mathbb Z/4)^2$ with sheet exchange, and a fixed diagonal subgroup. Its affine deck-invariant compression fails the stated coprimality test.

For $g=1$, direct dart enumeration gives $14$ vertices, $18$ edges and $4$ lifted faces. Wiring the central fiber gives $729$ forests, of which $27$ are fixed, and wiring the fiber of a degree-two vertex instead gives $2{,}025$ forests. On the $729$ central-fiber forests, exhaustive literal routing checked all $8{,}748$ one-chip additions at nonsinks against the compressed action. Each triangle supplies two factors of order three, interchanged by the deck action. The projected coordinate is twice their sum modulo three, so one upstairs chip projects to two downstairs copies of the corresponding chip. The executable control uses the Python standard library. [TopologyBridge.py](TopologyBridge.py).

## Relation to prior work

Reiner and Tseng construct pushforward and pullback and prove prime-to-degree splitting for ordinary critical groups of covering graphs. The group in this note is the relative group obtained by wiring a full fiber. Huss and Sava study rotor groups of finite truncations of infinite periodic tree covers. The result here gives an exact invariant/coinvariant description for the wired finite cover, a unique affine retraction criterion, and a sink-natural projection onto the action from Chan, Church, and Grochow. The Markov consequence is an exact state-space reduction of this particular dynamics, in the spirit of Wolpert, Grochow, Libby, and DeDeo's broader framework. The combined statement has a mathematical proof and finite checks. Priority is unverified, and there is no Lean formalization. [Reiner and Tseng](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.2977), [Huss and Sava](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.4267), [Wolpert et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.7403).
