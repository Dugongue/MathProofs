# Determinant transport for square-zero multiplication tensors

Let $F$ be a field and $A$ a nonzero finite-dimensional unital associative $F$-algebra with radical $J$ satisfying $J^2=0$ and with finite étale quotient $B=A/J$. The raw tensor may be any isotope of multiplication in $A$, with independent changes of basis in all three modes.

The theorem computes the exact determinant transporter coset from the étale quotient and the radical bimodule. Over a fixed finite field it gives a deterministic polynomial-time, single-instance reduction from coordinatewise special-linear tensor isomorphism on this locus to ordinary tensor isomorphism, starting with raw arrays. The left and right determinant subgroups can be prescribed independently. The proof applies to nonsplit quotient fields, repeated factors, loops, and arrow multiplicities divisible by the characteristic.

## 1. The raw algebra and its intrinsic radical

Write a bilinear tensor as $T:U\times V\to W$. A transporter satisfies $T'(Px,Qy)=RT(x,y)$. The covariant formulation replaces the first two matrices by their inverse transposes and has the same special-linear orbit relation.

Recover the two spaces of projected operators from the linear equations $T(Xx,y)=T(x,Yy)$ for all $x,y$. For true multiplication these are right and left regular multiplication: setting each argument in turn to the identity gives $X(x)=xa$ and $Y(y)=ay$ for the same $a$, and associativity suffices. The projections are unital endomorphism algebras; the product on pairs is $(X,Y)(X',Y')=(XX',Y'Y)$. In an isotope the projected action on each mode is conjugate to the regular action of that projection algebra on itself. Constructive module isomorphism on the finitely many basis operators therefore recovers regular cyclic vectors $\alpha\in U,\beta\in V$ as the images of the identity. In true multiplication coordinates they are units. Indeed a cyclic vector in either regular module admits a one-sided inverse, and in finite dimension a one-sided inverse implies a two-sided inverse. [Brooksbank and Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

With $S_1=T(-,\beta)$ and $S_2=T(\alpha,-)$ invertible, define $x\star y=T(S_1^{-1}x,S_2^{-1}y)$ on $W$. In multiplication coordinates with $\alpha=a,\beta=b$, the isomorphism from the original algebra to this table is $z\mapsto zab$. Thus $(S_1,S_2,I)$ is a raw-to-normalized transporter. Equal side lengths, successful module comparisons, invertible slices, and the identities for unital associativity can all be checked. Any accepted table witnesses that the input is an isotope of a unital associative algebra. The zero-dimensional case has one orbit.

The released trace Gram lemma supplies a direct radical test in characteristic zero. For $B=\prod_i E_i$, where each $E_i/F$ is a finite separable field, let $J_i=e_iJ$ for the distinguished left idempotents in $B$. Left action on $J$ is well-defined since $J^2=0$. On $B\oplus J$ viewed as the associated graded of $A$, the trace of left multiplication by $ab$ is
$$
 \operatorname{Tr}_F L_{ab}
 =\sum_i \bigl(1+\dim_{E_i}J_i\bigr)
    \operatorname{Tr}_{E_i/F}(\bar a_i\bar b_i).
$$
The block over $E_i$ is the restriction of the one-dimensional form with Gram scalar $1+\dim_{E_i}J_i$. It is nonsingular by the release's **traceGram_det_ne_zero**, using the identity involution. Every Gram scalar is nonzero in characteristic zero, and the same proof works in positive characteristic when all scalars are nonzero. Consequently $J$ is exactly the kernel of the Gram form $(a,b)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_F L_{ab}$ in these cases. With the right regular action the form gives the right-hand weights. Recovery of $J$ is necessary to recover the incidence data and the determinant exponents from raw tables. For a general characteristic-zero candidate, the kernel test can be followed by verification that the output is a square-zero ideal with étale quotient. [Alpöge's release](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Trace Gram source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/main/src/CompanionTheory/SpectralGeometry/LocalGlobalProofs/AdelicAnalysis/TraceGramDetNeZero.lean)

Here is a separate recovery when a multiplicity vanishes in $F=\mathbb F_q$. Compute the two-sided ideal $C$ generated by all $[a,b]$ by linear closure, and work in the commutative quotient $A/C$. The map $x\mapsto x^q$ there is $F$-linear. Under the promised hypotheses, $C\subseteq J$, $J/C$ is killed by the $q$-power map, and that map is an automorphism on the product of finite fields $A/J$. The preimage of its kernel is therefore exactly $J$. To recognize the locus, verify this candidate is a square-zero ideal and the quotient is reduced, by injectivity of Frobenius. A nilpotent ideal is in the radical, and a finite product of fields has zero radical, so the two checks also exclude false positives. Each Frobenius calculation uses $O(\log q)$ algebra multiplications on a basis.

## 2. Split the extension and recover the arrow bimodule

All choices below can be made over $F$. Write $B=\prod_{i=1}^s E_i$ and lift its orthogonal primitive idempotents. If a lift $e$ lies in the current corner with unit $1$ and $t=e^2-e\in J$, then $t^2=0$, $et=te$, and
$$
 e'=e+(1-2e)t,\qquad (e')^2-e'=0.
$$
The last equation follows from $(2e-1)^2=1+4t$. Continue in the complementary corner $(1-e')A(1-e')$. The last lift is the complement of the sum. In each corner choose a primitive element of $E_i/F$ with separable minimal polynomial $f_i$. For a lift $a$, the element $f'_i(a)$ is a unit since its residue is invertible. An inverse can be obtained from an arbitrary lifted inverse by the square-zero correction. It commutes with $a$. Therefore $a-f'_i(a)^{-1} f_i(a)$ still lifts the primitive element and satisfies $f_i$ by first-order polynomial expansion. This gives a unital section $B\to A$ in every characteristic. The two corner computations give a constructive proof of splitting in this case.

The multiplication on $A=B\oplus J$ is now $(b,u)(c,v)=(bc,bv+uc)$. The two commuting actions make $J$ a module over $B\otimes_F B$. In particular
$$
 E_i\otimes_F E_j=\prod_{c\in I_{ij}} C_{ijc},
 \qquad
 e_iJe_j=\bigoplus_{c\in I_{ij}} C_{ijc}^{\,m_{ijc}} .
$$
Each $C_{ijc}$ is a finite separable field with specified left and right inclusions of $E_i,E_j$, respectively. The ranks $m_{ijc}$ include zeros. The tensor product algebra is étale, so its modules have exactly these decompositions. The left and right action and every rank are intrinsic because changing a lift of an element of $B$ by an element of $J$ has zero effect on $J$.

An algebra isomorphism $A\to A'$ induces a quotient isomorphism $\sigma:B\to B'$ and an intertwiner $\tau:J\to J'$ for the transported $B\otimes_F B$ action. Such an intertwiner exists precisely when the permutation of the arrow-field factors induced by $\sigma\otimes\sigma$ preserves the ranks. Conversely, whenever the ranks match, choose coefficientwise semilinear maps in field bases, one on each arrow summand. Their direct sum $\tau$ gives the algebra isomorphism $(b,u)\mapsto(\sigma b,\tau u)$ in the chosen splittings. In particular ordinary tensor isomorphism of the raw multiplication isotopes is exactly the comparison of these quotient and arrow data. The next section proves this last statement directly for isotopes.

## 3. Exact determinant images over an arbitrary field

For a unital multiplication transporter $(P,Q,R):A\to A'$, take $a=P(1), b=Q(1)$. Setting one input to $1$ shows $R(y)=aQ(y)$ and $R(x)=P(x)b$. Invertibility of $P,Q,R$ implies that $a,b$ are units. Now $\varphi(x)=a^{-1}R(x)b^{-1}$ is a unital algebra isomorphism by substitution. Conversely every pair of units $a,b\in (A')^\times$ and algebra isomorphism $\varphi:A\to A'$ yields
$$
 (P,Q,R)=(L_a\varphi,R_b\varphi,L_aR_b\varphi),
$$
where $R_b(x)=xb$. The three choices are independent.

The determinant of $L_a$ on $A'$ is the product of its determinants on the quotient and on the radical, since both are diagonal blocks for a basis adapted to the radical. On the $i$th part it is the determinant of $\bar a_i$ on $1+\dim_{E_i}(e_iJ)$ copies of $E_i$ as an $F$-space. The corresponding calculation on the right uses $Je_i$. Suppressing primes in the data for $A'$, put
$$
 \ell_i=1+\dim_{E_i}(e_iJ),\qquad
 r_i=1+\dim_{E_i}(Je_i),\qquad
 H_L=\prod_i N_{E_i/F}(E_i^\times)^{\ell_i},\quad
 H_R=\prod_i N_{E_i/F}(E_i^\times)^{r_i}.
$$
These are exactly the left and right determinant images, because every unit of the quotient lifts to a unit. For example the summand $C_{ijc}^{m_{ijc}}$ contributes $[C_{ijc}:E_i]m_{ijc}$ to $\ell_i-1$ and $[C_{ijc}:E_j]m_{ijc}$ to $r_j-1$. The equality $\sum_i [E_i:F]\ell_i=\dim_F A=\sum_i [E_i:F]r_i$ is a consistency check on both sets of weights.

Let $D$ be the set of determinants of the algebra isomorphisms $A\to A'$ in the specified bases, empty if there are none. An exact formula for $D$ is available even over infinite fields. For each admissible $\sigma$ in Section 2 choose one such isomorphism $\varphi_\sigma$ from the sections and field bases. All isomorphisms inducing $\sigma$ have determinant in the single coset
$$
 \det_F(\varphi_\sigma)\, H_J,\qquad
 H_J=\prod_{i,j,c\,:\,m_{ijc}>0} N_{C_{ijc}/F}(C_{ijc}^\times).
$$
To see this, the block on the quotient is fixed. On the radical the admissible intertwiners are a coset of the product of all $\mathrm{GL}_{m_{ijc}}(C_{ijc})$. Any two maps with the same quotient and radical blocks have identical determinant, regardless of their off-diagonal block. Restriction of scalars takes the determinant of an invertible $C$-matrix to the field norm of its determinant, as follows by base-changing to a separable closure and multiplying the determinants of its conjugate matrices. Each $\mathrm{GL}_m(C)$ for $m>0$ maps onto $C^\times$ by its determinant. The coefficientwise maps and every such module automorphism lift through the chosen splitting, which proves equality. The set $D$ is the union of these nonempty cosets over the finite set of admissible quotient maps. This gives a formula using the finite quotient and only explicitly identified field norm groups.

The exact determinant triples in normalized bases are
$$
 (\lambda d,\mu d,\lambda\mu d)\qquad
       (\lambda\in H_L,\ \mu\in H_R,\ d\in D).
$$
In particular for prescribed determinants $(x,y,z)$ the three independent tests are
$$
 z/y\in H_L,\qquad z/x\in H_R,\qquad xy/z\in D .
$$
For raw inputs let the determinant triples of the two normalization transporters be $\nu,\nu'$, respectively. The normalized target to test is $\nu'\nu^{-1}(x,y,z)$, with multiplication componentwise. This follows by taking the determinants of the conjugated transporter from one normalized table to the other; it includes the determinant-one request by putting $x=y=z=1$.

## 4. A single ordinary TI query over a fixed finite field

If $J\ne0$ and the normalized algebras are isomorphic, $H_J=F^\times$ by surjectivity of the norm for every finite field extension. Thus $D=F^\times$ whenever ordinary TI holds. Also $H_L=(F^\times)^{\gcd(q-1,\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_s)}$, and similarly for $H_R$. Membership of $u$ in $(F^\times)^g$ for $g\mid q-1$ is exactly the test $u^{(q-1)/g}=1$.

The deterministic reduction first carries out the recognition and normalization in Section 1. Use the quotient field decomposition to get the weights by applying the lifted idempotent operators on $J$ and taking dimensions of their images. Over a fixed field all computations are polynomial in the array size. One way to split the finite étale quotient is to compute the kernel of $b\mapsto b^q-b$, a product of copies of $F$. A basis of this kernel separates its factors. Refine the current components by eigenprojections for the multiplication operators of those basis elements, testing at most the fixed number $q$ of eigenvalues per operator. Each surviving component is one finite field. Its dimension gives the denominator needed to convert the corresponding $F$-rank on $J$ to the field dimension. This avoids constructing any semilinear arrow isomorphism during the reduction.

If only one radical vanishes, the answer is negative. If both are nonzero, return a fixed ordinary NO instance if either of the two unit tests fails, and otherwise return the original ordinary TI instance. All normalized algebra isomorphisms are equivalent to ordinary transporters, and the radical automorphisms supply every missing orientation. Tests may use the weights of just the target since ordinary isomorphism enforces agreement. This is one deterministic many-one reduction.

If both radicals vanish, the algebras are products of finite fields. The unit determinant images are both all of $F^\times$. Decide algebra isomorphism by matching field degrees. At a fixed finite field, find a comparison map by representing each factor as $F[t]/(f)$ and finding a root of $f$ in the matched field. A primitive element of a factor of degree $e$ can be recovered from its multiplication table. For each prime power $\ell^v\mid e$ with maximal $v$, take the fixed space of the $\ell^v$th power of base Frobenius and pick a basis vector outside the fixed space of the $\ell^{v-1}$th power. Sum those vectors. For every prime $\ell$ dividing $e$, the $(e/\ell)$th power of Frobenius fixes all other summands and moves the $\ell$ summand. The sum therefore has degree $e$. All fixed spaces use just linear algebra. Deterministic finite-field factorization in fixed characteristic makes this polynomial. For example one can split square-free factors using the prime-field trace and a basis of the Frobenius-fixed subspace, testing the fixed number of prime-field values. Over degree $e$ the Frobenius automorphism has determinant $(-1)^{e-1}$ in a normal basis, and swapping two isomorphic degree-$e$ factors has determinant $(-1)^e$. These generate the automorphism determinant image because the automorphism group of a product of finite fields consists of field Frobenii and factor permutations. Thus $D$ is the determinant of the constructed comparison map multiplied by that sign subgroup. The reduction returns a fixed ordinary YES or NO instance according to the prescribed determinant tests. [Lenstra, Finding isomorphisms between finite fields](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

## 5. Independent control of the left and right obstructions

For any positive integers $a,b$ dividing $q-1$, take $B=F\times F$. Choose a common multiple $M$ of $a,b$ with $M>\max(a,b)+1$. Prescribe left weights $(a,M-a)$ and right weights $(b,M-b)$. After subtracting one from each weight the totals on both sides are $M-2$. A nonnegative integer $2\times2$ matrix with these row and column sums exists: choose its upper-left entry in the integer interval
$$
 [\max(0,(a-1)+(b-1)-(M-2)),\,\min(a-1,b-1)]
$$
and fill the other entries from the margins. The four entries are the dimensions of $e_iJe_j$ for an algebra with square-zero radical, whose multiplication is defined by these two scalar actions. Its determinant subgroups are exactly the $a$th and $b$th power subgroups. A radical automorphism gives arbitrary $d\in F^\times$ because $J$ has positive dimension. This realizes separate failures of the left and right tests whenever the relevant power subgroup is proper, even in ordinary-isomorphic raw pairs. Enlarging $M$ preserves the prescribed groups. Taking $q=2^h$ and appropriate weights also shows why Gram-kernel recognition alone would miss examples in small characteristic.

The proof extends the finite-field semisimple multiplication-isotope calculation to square-zero radicals with a separable commutative quotient. The radical ranks and their left and right asymmetry supply the additional determinant data. The release lemma is used for the intrinsic Gram extraction in the separable nonvanishing-weight case. The finite-field proof adds Frobenius recovery to cover the vanishing weights. The reduction applies on the stated recognizable locus.

## Finite controls

Over $\mathbb F_3$ the split two-vertex species with arrows $(1,0),(1,1)$ has 36 units and 12 algebra automorphisms by exhaustive enumeration, with left determinant image $\{1,2\}$, right image $\{1\}$, and automorphism image $\{1,2\}$. Reversing the nonloop arrow interchanges the unit images and leaves both counts unchanged. The determinant-triple test agrees with the image of all pairs of units and all automorphisms in each example. The left regular Gram weight at vertex 1 is 3 and vanishes in the ground field, although the intrinsic radical still has dimension two.
