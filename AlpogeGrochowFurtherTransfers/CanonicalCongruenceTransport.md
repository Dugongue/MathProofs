# Canonical tensor transport in every marked congruence class

Let $R$ be a finite commutative local ring with radical $\mathfrak m$, residue characteristic $p$, and $\mathfrak m^a=0$. Let $G=\prod_{\ell=1}^q \mathrm{GL}_{n_\ell}(R)$ and $K_k=\ker(G\to\prod_\ell\mathrm{GL}_{n_\ell}(R/\mathfrak m^k))$, where $1\le k\le a$. A mark specifies the residue of a transporter modulo $\mathfrak m^k$. Fix any lift $g_0\in G$, which exists because a determinant that is a unit modulo $\mathfrak m^k$ is a unit in $R$. The marked class is $g_0K_k$.

Assume an explicit ring presentation providing polynomial bit cost for ring operations, reduction, and coordinates and lifts in a supplied ordered $\mathbb F_p$-basis of every $\mathfrak m^j/\mathfrak m^{j+1}$. One concrete presentation uses nested residue digits and multiplication on those digits. This presentation includes $\mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z$, arbitrary finite chain rings presented with their residue coordinates, and nonprincipal rings such as $\mathbb F_p[x,y]/(x,y)^3$. The residue prime and the basis digits are part of the input; no factorization or unmarked residue-field isomorphism is assumed available.

**Theorem.** On arbitrary dense tensor data, there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm that decides transport in $g_0K_k$, produces a witness and the exact number of witnesses. It gives a compact description of *every* witness as a normal form with independent base-$p$ digits. It also computes a canonical representative for the $K_k$-orbit and a map to that representative. It works at $p=2,\ k=1$, for every Smith profile, and for prescribed determinant tuples or simultaneous tensor records. Repeated natural and dual occurrences of each mode use the same transporter. The same proof applies to any efficiently evaluated $R$-linear representation on a free module for which $\rho(K_j)$ acts trivially modulo $\mathfrak m^j$ for every $j\ge1$. The bit cost, including the exact counts, is polynomial in the explicit input, the dimensions of the data and matrix spaces, and $\log|R|$.

This is a local algorithm for a supplied residue class. At $k=1$ the mark can be any invertible residue-field transporter. The theorem lifts **every choice** independently and never enumerates any of the large fibers of the reduction map.

## The description of a bounded move from the RH release

In the theta-lattice part of Alpöge's release, **PositivityTheory.Sph.below_iff_rows** identifies the bounded relation on matrices $Y,Y_0$ with membership of each row of $Y$ in the lattice generated by rows of $Y_0$ and the error vectors $t e_i$. The definition of the bounded relation gives integral coefficients and error witnesses. Its consequence **PositivityTheory.Sph.below_iff** gives one common change of coordinates on the right in which the whole lattice is recognized by integrality. Within the same file, **PositivityTheory.Sph.latSt** uses strict lower-stratum subtraction to recover exact strata from bounded membership data. [Release source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/main/src/Development/WeilBound/ThetaLifts/SphericalLattices/BelowIffRows.lean#L50)

Here every correction vector will lie in an explicit prime-field layer $V=\mathbb F_p^b$. Given rows $v_1,\ldots,v_s$ in that layer, lift them to integral rows over $\mathbb Z_p$ and add zero rows if necessary to make at least one row. Write $Y_0$ for this lift and $Y$ for a matrix of the same size whose first row is a lift of $r\in V$ and whose other rows vanish. Apply the released theorem with $t=p$. It identifies the admissible residuals with reduction of the single lattice
$$
\Lambda=\mathbb Z_p\widetilde v_1+\cdots+\mathbb Z_p\widetilde v_s+p\mathbb Z_p^b.
$$
Equivalently, the bounded move exists exactly when $r\in\sum_i\mathbb F_pv_i$. Integral coefficient witnesses reduce to coefficients of a correction. The empty-row case means $r=0$, and a zero-dimensional layer needs no test. Gaussian elimination realizes this specialization with finite prime-field arithmetic. Different choices of integral lifts give the same lattice because it includes $p\mathbb Z_p^b$.

The nonlinearity is handled by choosing the rows to be actual movements of **stabilizer generators**, recomputed at each precision. Thus the lattice result applies to the entire set of achievable corrections inside the current fiber. The same theorem applied to suffixes of the ordered row list tells exactly which generators can be corrected into the next stabilizer. No tangent approximation is used in this part.

## 1. An ordered group filtration with a single digit at a time

Put $d=\sum_\ell n_\ell^2$. For $k\le j<a$, entrywise subtraction of the identity gives a surjective homomorphism
$$
K_j\longrightarrow (\mathfrak m^j/\mathfrak m^{j+1})^d
$$
with kernel $K_{j+1}$. Terms quadratic in entries disappear since $2j\ge j+1$. Surjectivity follows because a tuple of matrices $I+X$ with $X$ in $\mathfrak m^j$ is always invertible. The target is an $\mathbb F_p$-space because $p\in\mathfrak m$. The commutator of matrices in $K_k,K_j$ is in $K_{k+j}$, as one sees by reducing products modulo $\mathfrak m^{k+j}$ where they commute. In particular each quotient $K_j/K_{j+1}$ is central in $K_k/K_{j+1}$.

Refine each such quotient by the suffixes of its fixed, ordered prime-field coordinates. This gives a descending chain
$$
K_k=N_1\supset N_2\supset\cdots\supset N_{L+1}=1
$$
with additive maps $\lambda_i:N_i\to\mathbb F_p$ onto, and kernel $N_{i+1}$. Every $N_i$ is normal in $K_k$ by centrality just shown. The initial generators for $K_k$ have one prescribed leading coordinate equal to 1. Construct them by lifting each prime-field coordinate vector to its chosen matrix entry, leaving all other coordinates zero in that layer. The total length $L=d\log_p|\mathfrak m^k|$ is polynomial in the inputs.

For any subgroup $H$ of $K_k$, call $i$ a pivot if $\lambda_i(H\cap N_i)\ne0$. In that case the image is all of $\mathbb F_p$. Choose $h_i\in H\cap N_i$ with $\lambda_i(h_i)=1$. For each $h\in H$, take $\lambda_i(h)$ at the first available pivot and multiply on the left by $h_i^{-\lambda_i(h)}$. The remainder is one layer deeper. Iterating gives a factorization of $h$, and peeling off the unique leading coordinate proves uniqueness:
$$
h=\prod_{i\ {\rm increasing\ pivot}} h_i^{c_i},\qquad 0\le c_i<p.
$$
This also shows that the factors at indices $i$ and later represent precisely $H\cap N_i$. Therefore $H$ has $p^{\#\text{pivots}}$ elements. None of the $h_i$ is required to have order $p$ in $H$.

The kernel calculation uses the same ordered list. Given an additive homomorphism $\chi:H\to V$ to a prime-field vector group, compute $v_i=\chi(h_i)$. A pivot $i$ belongs to the kernel exactly when $v_i$ is in the span of the later $v_j$. To see necessity, a kernel element whose first digit $c_i$ is nonzero has $0=c_i v_i+\sum_{j>i}c_jv_j$; divide by $c_i$ in the prime field. To see sufficiency, when $v_i+\sum_{j>i}c_jv_j=0$, take
$$
h_i' = h_i\prod_{j>i}h_j^{c_j}.
$$
Its leading digit is still 1 and it belongs to the kernel. Using the normal form again with the $h_i'$ proves that these are precisely a complete set of echelon generators of the kernel. All right-hand factors are from the old list. Suffix spans and correction coefficients can be computed in one backwards elimination sweep, retaining coefficients in the original rows. For any acceptable residual the same linear equation produces a group element with that residual. In particular no loop over the prime field is needed.

## 2. Exact orbit lifting and exact order

Use the action on the direct sum $M$ of all tensor spaces. Any dual occurrence acts by the inverse transpose, which commutes with reduction, so every $h\in K_k$ gives $\rho(h)\equiv I\bmod\mathfrak m$. To require determinants append one line for each determinant relation. The $\ell$-th determinant line multiplies by $\det(g_\ell)$. More generally a line may transform by a product of fixed integer powers of the mode determinants. Begin each line with 1 and append its desired value to the target. A zero or nonunit desired value cannot be reached. Normalize the target $u$, including any lines, by $\rho(g_0)^{-1}$; write $s$ for the resulting source tuple.

For $k\le m\le a$, define
$$
H_m=\{h\in K_k : \rho(h)s\equiv s\bmod\mathfrak m^m M\}.
$$
Thus $H_k=K_k$. On $H_m$ the displacement descends to
$$
\chi_m(h)=\rho(h)s-s\bmod\mathfrak m^{m+1}M
$$
in $\mathfrak m^m M/\mathfrak m^{m+1}M$. It is an additive homomorphism: the product displacement is
$$
\rho(hh')s-s=(\rho(h)s-s)+\rho(h)(\rho(h')s-s),
$$
and $\rho(h)-I$ sends $\mathfrak m^m M$ into $\mathfrak m^{m+1}M$. The next stabilizer is exactly $H_{m+1}=\ker\chi_m$.

First compare $s$ and $u\bmod\mathfrak m^k$. If they agree, set $g_k=1$. Given $g_m\in K_k$ such that $g_m s\equiv u\bmod\mathfrak m^m M$, let $r_m=g_m^{-1}u-s$ in the next layer. A candidate lift equals $g_m h$ for some $h\in H_m$, because every solution at precision $m$ is in this right-translated stabilizer coset. The lift works exactly when $\chi_m(h)=r_m$. By the echelon normal form and the homomorphism property, this is precisely the bounded row test with rows $\chi_m(h_i)$ from the RH source. Its coefficients give an actual product $h$. Set $g_{m+1}=g_m h$ and update the stabilizer using the suffix calculation. Every step matches a prescribed precision and maintains all alternative witnesses implicitly. Consequently a failed test proves that the whole marked class is impossible. A completed run gives $g_0g_a$ and every witness is uniquely
$$
g_0g_a\prod_{i\ {\rm pivot\ of}\ H_a} h_i^{c_i},\qquad 0\le c_i<p .
$$

This also proves an exact counting formula in terms of the actual displacement ranks $r_m'=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_p}(\chi_m(h_i))$:
$$
|H_a|=p^{L-\sum_{m=k}^{a-1}r_m'},\qquad |K_k s|=p^{\sum_{m=k}^{a-1}r_m'}.
$$
The equalities follow because the kernel index at stage $m$ is $p^{r_m'}$; a successful target is a stabilizer coset. This is an exact calculation for arbitrary tensors even when derivative ranks change, the radical is nonprincipal, or the residue characteristic is 2. With a separately supplied list of residue classes modulo $\mathfrak m^k$, exact total counts are the sum over the distinct classes, whose witness sets are disjoint.

With determinant lines omitted, the full set of determinant tuples of transporters in a nonempty marked fiber is a translate of the subgroup of $(R^\times)^q$ generated by the determinant tuples of the final $h_i$. This follows by applying the determinant homomorphism to the displayed normal form. Keeping the lines instead tests an exact desired tuple and counts that fiber without enumerating the determinant subgroup. This directly solves determinant correction inside the marked class.

## 3. A constructive canonical representative

Fix once an order of the input coordinates and a residue-digit convention compatible with the filtration. Use the usual order of representatives $0,\ldots,p-1$ in each prime-field digit. Compare arrays by increasing depth and then lexicographically in the current digit vector. The initial depth-$k$ digits of a $K_k$-orbit are constant. At stage $m$, suppose the precise minimum prefix modulo $\mathfrak m^m$ has already been attained by $g_ms$. All representatives attaining that prefix are precisely $g_mH_ms$. Since $g_m$ acts trivially on the next vector layer, their next-digit vectors form exactly one affine coset $c+\operatorname{im}\chi_m$. Here $c$ is the digit vector of $g_ms$, obtained after subtracting a fixed lift of its already chosen prefix.

Take the row-echelon span basis with normalized leading entries and eliminate these leads from $c$. The result is the unique point of this affine space whose basis-pivot digits are zero. It is also the lexicographic minimum: every nonzero vector of the span first changes a pivot digit, and at the first changed digit the selected value is 0. Elimination supplies coefficients in the old rows, hence a group word lifting the minimum. The recursion yields the lexicographically smallest full array in the orbit, together with its normalizing mode maps. Two arrays have the same canonical representative exactly when their $K_k$-orbits agree. In a fixed marked coset $g_0K_k$ compare the canonical forms of $s$ and $g_0^{-1}u$. Composing the two normalizing witnesses reconstructs a transporter.

This greedy procedure does not require testing every lift. Each successive bounded lattice controls one layer, and the kernel pruning removes exactly the directions no longer available. It supplies an algorithmic refinement from nested bounded tests to a complete normal form, parallel to the strict-stratum organization in **PositivityTheory.Sph.latSt**. The elimination proof above establishes the counting and canonical-form claims in the tensor setting.

There are $a-k$ stages and at most $L$ group elements at each one. Evaluating the action, computing $L$ suffix spans and correction words naively, and obtaining inverses in the congruence group all take polynomial time. For exponentiation use repeated squaring with digit exponents of at most $\log p$ bits. Inverses of identity plus a radical matrix are finite geometric series, and general $g_0$ can be inverted by unit-pivot elimination over a local ring. The exponent in the count is at most $L$, so the binary count has at most $1+L\log_2 p$ bits. The same argument over known local factors of a finite product ring combines componentwise by CRT, with product of cardinalities. The required decomposition and component presentations are part of that extension.

## 4. Closed Smith formula and multi-digit lifts in the exponential range

For $R=\mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z$, the group algorithm above is unconditional at every $k\ge1$. For $p$ odd with $k\ge1$, or $p=2$ with $k\ge2$, an additional theorem for the usual tensor, dual, and determinant-power representations gives an exact closed form and fewer steps. Set $e=k$ in the odd case and $e=k-1$ in the dyadic case. Let $D_s$ be the differential at the identity of the action on the source point. A natural slot contributes $X$, each dual slot contributes $-X^{\mathsf T}$, and a determinant-power line contributes the corresponding linear combination of traces. With repeated slots, sum the contributions of every occurrence. Write $\sigma_i$ for the Smith valuations of $D_s$ at precision $a$, taking zero pivots to have valuation $a$.

**Claim.** For $k\le m\le a$, the stabilizer of $s\bmod p^m$ has exactly
$$
p^{(a-k)d-\sum_i \max(0,m-k-\sigma_i)}
$$
elements inside $K_k$ at precision $a$. Transport decision, reconstruction and counting can be done using one Smith form of $D_s$ and at most $\lceil(a-k)/e\rceil$ corrections. In particular no bound involving the largest visible invariant factor is needed.

Work first over $\mathbb Z_p$ using lifts of the entries of $s$. If $X$ is a matrix tuple of integral entries then $\exp(p^k X)$ and $\log(I+p^kX)$ are convergent and inverse. The valuations $v_p(n!)\le (n-1)/(p-1)$ and $v_p(n)\le n-1$ ensure their terms lie at precision $k$ and tend to zero in the stated range. The usual inverse identities follow from the formal one-variable series. Differences under changing $p^kX\bmod p^a$ are divisible by $p^a$, by telescoping products in each power, so the bijection descends to precision $a$. Thus exponentiation uniquely parametrizes the $K_k$ group by $X\bmod p^{a-k}$. Even for repeated dual slots the identity
$$
\rho(\exp(p^kX))=\exp(p^k d\rho(X))
$$
holds: distinct slots commute as operators, the dual of the exponential is the exponential of the negative transpose, and the determinant of the exponential is the exponential of the trace. The last equality follows from the derivative of the determinant and its initial value at zero.

Write $T=p^k d\rho(X)$. The displacement factorization is exact:
$$
(\rho(\exp(p^kX))-I)s
 =F(T)p^kD_sX,\qquad F(T)=I+\sum_{n\ge1} \frac{T^n}{(n+1)!}.
$$
In the odd case $v_p((n+1)!)\le n/2$. For $n=1$ the denominator is a unit, and for $n\ge2$ the terms all have valuation at least $k$. In the dyadic case $v_2((n+1)!)\le n$, so every nonconstant term has valuation at least $k-1$. Thus $F(T)\equiv I\bmod p^e$ and is invertible. This gives the stabilizer equivalence
$$
\exp(p^kX)\in H_m\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p^kD_sX\equiv0\bmod p^m.
$$
Counting this kernel by Smith form immediately proves the claimed order. The exact order statement applies to the augmented representation as well, yielding the appropriate count on every nonempty determinant-constrained fiber.

Given $g_m$ at precision $m$, set $\ell=\min(m+e,a)$ and take the displacement of its normalized target modulo $p^\ell$. For any group element preserving the source modulo $p^m$, the factorization shows that its displacement at precision $\ell$ equals $p^kD_sX$; the error $ (F(T)-I)p^kD_sX$ is divisible by $p^{m+e}$. Conversely any solution to the single linear equation at precision $\ell$ already stabilizes modulo $p^m$, so its exponential effects the target correction. This is again the bounded row-lattice criterion with threshold $t=p^\ell$, using the columns of $p^kD_s$ as rows of the generating matrix. Update $g_\ell=g_m\exp(p^kX)$. A failed correction excludes all possible transporters, by the same coset argument as before. The Smith form and evaluation of the series have polynomial bit cost; only $O(a)$ terms and $O(a)$ extra digits for separating the $p$-part of factorial denominators suffice. Indeed $nk-v_p(n!)$ increases at least linearly with $n$ in the sense of the uniform lower bound $n(k-1)+1$ when $k\ge2$, and of $n/2$ in the odd case. The method exploits a relative error that is small compared with each actual displacement, so a small Smith pivot causes no loss of precision.

The dyadic depth-one edge is a genuinely different chart. For example $g=-1$ belongs to $1+2\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z$, but $g$ is outside the image of the exponential on $4\mathbb Z_2$ even modulo 8. The group filtration algorithm works there without any analytic chart. On the nonprincipal example above, one-dimensional records and multiple natural or inverse slots work by the same prime-field layers, even though no single uniformizer generates the radical.

The release ingredients used here are the bounded theta row-lattice theorem and its exact-stratum organization. No analytic RH or GRH hypothesis is used. The result addresses Grochow and collaborators' tensor-isomorphism framework over commutative rings, including lifting with special-linear constraints inside each supplied class. [Tensor isomorphism over commutative rings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00735)

## Finite controls

Independent exhaustive controls applied the generator correction algorithm and compared it to literal enumeration of each marked group. The dyadic test used paired natural and mixed dual records on mode dimensions $2,1,1$ over $\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z$ and all three determinant coordinates. All 4,096 mode maps gave 1,024 distinct augmented targets with 4 witnesses each. The successive stabilizer sizes were 4,096, 128, and 4. Over $\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z$, 729 mixed-record maps gave 9 targets with 81 witnesses each. Over the nonprincipal ring $\mathbb F_2[x,y]/(x,y)^3$, 1,024 two-mode maps gave 1,024 augmented targets, with intermediate stabilizer size 64. In each case the algorithm reconstructed a witness for every listed target, recovered the brute-force canonical minimum, and agreed with exhaustive membership on single-digit perturbations across 39 orbit representatives.
