# The ordinary symbolic subgraph question over every field

Fix any field \(K\). The ordinary nonuniform version of Grochow–Urisman Question 1.11 is equivalent to fixed-parameter-size circuits for the vertex- and edge-weighted clique polynomials over \(K\). It is enough to ask for fixed-parameter-size circuits for unoriented simple paths. The reduction from \(k\)-cliques uses paths with \(O(k^2)\) edges, hosts of order \(f(k)n^2\), and coefficient extraction on one auxiliary variable of degree \(O(k^2)\). The weighted clique family is the complete family for the Bläser–Engels formulation of \(\mathsf{VW}[1]\). This is an equivalence of circuit-size questions. [Question 1.11](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26244v2#S1.SS3) · [Bläser–Engels, Theorem 7.22](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.12287) · [Earlier characteristic-zero project proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowQuestionEquivalences/Subgraphs.md)

## A signed path reduction for Euler trails

Let \(F\) have an open Euler trail of \(m\) edges, with endpoints of degree one and all other degrees two or four. Parallel edges are allowed and their weights are separately prescribed. Each vertex of \(F\) has a separate color and a set of candidate labels. An edge assigns a weight, potentially zero, to each pair of candidates at its endpoints. The target polynomial is the sum over one candidate per color of the product of the \(m\) edge weights.

Use a sequence of \(m+1\) visits along the Euler trail. Each visit \(i\) has vertices \(L_i(a),R_i(a)\) for each candidate \(a\) of that color and a directed edge \(L_i(a)\to R_i(a)\) of weight one. From \(R_i(a)\) to \(L_{i+1}(b)\) put the weight of the corresponding pattern edge. Add a source \(s\) pointing to all vertices of the first left layer and a sink \(t\) reached from all vertices of the last right layer, with unit weights. Each internal color occurs once or twice and each endpoint occurs once. At two visits \(i,j\) of the same color, for every candidate \(a\) put bidirectional left edges of weight \(+1\) between \(L_i(a),L_j(a)\) and bidirectional right edges of weight \(-1\) between \(R_i(a),R_j(a)\). These four-vertex sets are disjoint. The construction adapts the modular path cancellation of Curticapean–Dell–Husfeldt by giving the partners opposite weights. [Curticapean–Dell–Husfeldt, §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00629)

Any simple \(s,t\)-path enters each such set only at left vertices and exits only at right vertices. Its local fragments therefore have the following form.

- If there are two fragments, both are direct links. Each fragment uses two vertices, and any fragment with a jump would use at least three.
- A single crossed fragment between different visit indices has two routes, one taking the left jump and the other the right jump. They both have length two and their weights are opposites.
- A single uncrossed fragment is either a direct link or a three-edge detour through the other visit. Their weights are \(+1\) and \(-1\).

Cancel paths with a crossed fragment by toggling the first gadget with such a fragment in a fixed order of gadgets. This preserves length and all exterior edge choices, and it is an involution on simple paths because the chosen gadget has just one fragment. The remaining paths traverse all the visits in order. Their lengths lie in \([2m+3,4m+5]\), since they use the \(m\) graph edges, the two terminal edges and between one and three edges per visit. Within this range, toggle the first gadget containing a single uncrossed fragment, preserving simplicity, exterior weights and this length interval. In either involution the paired weights sum to zero, also in characteristic two. The survivors use the same candidate at both visits of each repeated color. They are in weight-preserving bijection with the intended assignments. Hence the sum of the directed \(s,t\)-path polynomials over lengths in the displayed interval is the target polynomial.

## Reading the directed paths from ordinary unoriented paths

For each vertex of this digraph take an input copy and an output copy connected by a link of weight \(y\). Replace each arc by one undirected edge from the source output copy to the target input copy, carrying the arc weight. Add a new leaf at the input copy of \(s\) and another at the output copy of \(t\). For a directed length \(\ell\), evaluate the unoriented path polynomial of length \(2\ell+3\). Use the four-term inclusion–exclusion over the two leaf edges to require both. Take the coefficient of \(y^{\ell+1}\). Removing the two endpoint leaves gives a path of \(2\ell+1\) edges with exactly \(\ell+1\) links. Links form a matching and cannot be consecutive. Consequently the path alternates between links and arc edges, starting and ending with links. Orienting it from the first leaf forces precisely a directed \(s,t\)-path of length \(\ell\) in the old digraph. Conversely any such directed path lifts uniquely. These steps use no scalar normalization.

For a circuit-size implication, substitute \(y\) in the quotient by \(y^{\ell+2}\), represent each gate by its coefficient vector and implement multiplication by truncated convolution. An unbounded fan-in gate may first be replaced by a binary tree in the ordinary model. This increases size by only a parameter factor when size counts wires, or by a polynomial factor for an explicit circuit encoding. This procedure works over the original field, including a field with too few points for interpolation. The same identity, before taking coefficients, holds over \(K(y)\) and will be useful for the symmetric lower bound.

## From clique to paths and back

To encode the weighted \(k\)-clique polynomial, put a \(k\)-by-\(k\) grid of colors into a toroidal \((k+2)\)-by-\((k+2)\) grid for \(k\ge2\). Use candidate pairs \((a,b)\in[n]^2\) at the active grid cells. Horizontal grid edges require equality of the first coordinate and vertical edges equality of the second. These constraints choose a value \(a_i\) per row and \(b_j\) per column. At cell \((i,i)\) require \(a_i=b_i\) and supply the vertex weight at \(a_i\). At cell \((i,j)\) with \(i<j\) require \(a_i<b_j\) and supply the edge weight for that pair. Each cell factor can be multiplied into the weight on one incident edge. The remaining cells have singleton domains, with all unused weights one. Delete an edge outside the active grid and attach a new leaf to each of its endpoints. The graph remains connected since the toroidal grid is bridgeless. It has a trail of the required form. The resulting polynomial has exactly one summand per increasing \(k\)-tuple, with all the intended weights. The number of Euler visits and path lengths is \(O(k^2)\). The cases \(k=0,1\) are direct.

In the other direction, for each simple pattern \(H\) of order \(h\), choose one total ordering of its vertices in each orbit of \(\operatorname{Aut}(H)\) acting on orderings. For a chosen ordering use \(h\) colors in a host for the size-\(h\) clique polynomial. Each color represents one pattern vertex and has one copy of the host labels. Edges within colors are forbidden. Across colors allow only host pairs in the prescribed order, using the original edge weight if the color pair is an edge of \(H\), and one otherwise. The sum over the chosen orderings picks exactly one injection per automorphism orbit, since each orbit of injections induces one free orbit of order types. For no-isolate patterns the resulting monomials are exactly the copies counted by the subgraph polynomial. This spare use of ordering in the substitutions avoids division by \(|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|\), even when the characteristic divides that order. Under the original edge-support convention isolated pattern vertices can first be deleted. A fixed-parameter bound for clique now gives size \(f(H)n^{O(1)}\) for every pattern. As a path has treewidth one, this proves the claimed equivalence.

## The ordered-pair variable convention

The same equivalence holds with ordered arc variables, as in the literal orbit-sum convention of Grochow–Urisman. The path family can then be taken to have all arcs consistently directed. Apply the signed path construction directly, append two directed pendant edges and use the four masks to require the endpoints. There is no orientation reversal or normalization. In the reverse clique substitution, if a pattern color pair carries two opposite arcs, its pair weight is the product of the two corresponding host arc variables. If it carries one arc, use that single variable in the prescribed direction. All order-type and automorphism arguments are unchanged. [Grochow–Urisman, §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26244v2#S3.SS3)
