# The field-characteristic split for ordinary matching circuits

Over any field of characteristic different from two, fixed-parameter-size circuits for symbolic \(k\)-matchings are equivalent to fixed-parameter-size circuits for vertex- and edge-weighted \(k\)-cliques. This remains true if the matching oracle is restricted to bipartite hosts. In characteristic two every symbolic matching coefficient has uniform polynomial-size ordinary division-free circuits. Thus the matching witness for the ordinary version of Question 1.11 has different behavior from the path witness in characteristic two. [Question 1.11](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26244v2#S1.SS3) · [Numeric modular antecedent, Curticapean–Dell–Husfeldt, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00629)

## Consistency and weighted edge gadgets when 2 is invertible

Start with any vertex-colored constraint graph of maximum degree at most three, with disjoint candidate sets per color and weighted edges on candidate pairs. For every candidate \(u\) make three ports, one per incidence and unused ports if necessary. Add three private vertices to each candidate. Between its ports and private vertices put the weights of the matrix
\[
C=\begin{pmatrix} 1/2&0&0\\1&-1&0\\1&1&-2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Give every such edge at port \(i\) of color \(a\) the edge color \((a,i)\). The weighted sums of partial matchings occupying exactly port \(2\), exactly port \(3\), or exactly the two ports \(2,3\) are all zero. The last equality is the disjoint-dot-product identity using the two zero row sums and zero cross dot product. The full permanent is one, and the empty occupation also has weight one.

For an edge constraint on \(u,v\) at their two assigned ports, with desired weight \(w\), add a private edge of weight \(w\), a fresh private leaf at each port of weight \(-w\), and the edge between the two ports of weight \(w\). Put all four choices in a separate edge color for that constraint type. If the two ports are available with indicators \(a,b\), the total weight of selecting one edge of this gadget is \(w(1-a-b+ab)=w(1-a)(1-b)\). Different constraint types cannot conflict on a port, because a port belongs to at most one incidence type. The internal private vertices are disjoint. For a bipartite constraint graph all gadgets are bipartite in a single host: put each candidate's three ports on its color side, its consistency auxiliaries and its leaves on the opposite side, and put private edge endpoints on opposite sides.

Consider a matching that uses each edge color exactly once. First sum over the consistency selections. If the selected three ports of a color belong to multiple candidates, at least one candidate occupies a nonempty subset of ports \(2,3\) that excludes port \(1\), and the grouped weight is zero. This cancellation is valid conditioned on the chosen ports, since consistency auxiliary vertices occur nowhere else. If all three ports belong to one candidate, their weight sums to one. The constraint colors independently contribute precisely the prescribed edge product for this choice of candidates, by the formula \(w(1-a)(1-b)\). Thus the colorful matching sum is exactly the vertex-colored weighted constraint polynomial. Use inclusion–exclusion over deleting edge colors on the uncolored size-\(r\) matching polynomial, where \(r\) is the number of colors, to force every color. A matching of size \(r\) using every color uses each once. The reduction has parameter-only many calls and polynomial host blowup.

To encode clique by such a pattern, replace color \(i\) by a path of \(k-1\) slots, one for each pair containing \(i\). Each slot has candidates \([n]\). Along paths enforce equality. Join the two slots for each color pair by a constraint requiring \(a_i<a_j\) for \(i<j\) and supplying the clique edge weight. Multiply the vertex weight of each color into one incident constraint. Slot degree is at most three. Subdivide every constraint edge once, giving the original slots one bipartition side and the new vertices the other. A subdivision vertex copies the first endpoint's candidate, imposed by an equality constraint, and its other edge carries the original constraint. Thus bipartiteness preserves the count exactly. The number of colors is \(O(k^2)\). The reverse size implication follows from the order-type construction for arbitrary patterns. [Clique-to-subgraphs construction](ordinary-paths.md)

## Characteristic two

The Hafnian of an alternating matrix in characteristic two equals its Pfaffian, since all matching signs agree. A division-free polynomial-size Pfaffian circuit works over every commutative ring. For a host on \(n\) vertices give each vertex a private twin. Use the original variables between old vertices, unit weights from each old vertex to its twin, and a weight \(z\) on every distinct twin pair. A perfect matching in this \(2n\)-vertex graph restricts to a matching with, say, \(j\) original edges. All other old vertices must take their own twin. The remaining \(2j\) twins can be matched in \((2j-1)!!\) ways, which is one in characteristic two, including \(j=0\). The coefficient of \(z^j\) is therefore the matching polynomial with one copy of each monomial. Evaluate the Pfaffian circuit with truncated coefficient arithmetic to obtain all coefficients in polynomial size. The construction is symbolic over the input field. [Division-free Pfaffian circuit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12081)

For a pattern with one consistently specified arc on each disjoint edge, the ordered-variable orbit sum equals the undirected matching polynomial after replacing \(x_{uv}\) by \(x_{uv}+x_{vu}\), because each selected edge has two independent directional choices. For a pattern with both arcs on each edge, use \(x_{uv}x_{vu}\) instead. The odd-characteristic lower reduction restricts to bipartite hosts, where one can either keep only left-to-right variables or set one direction to one in the bidirected case. The same characteristic split therefore applies to these orbit sums.
