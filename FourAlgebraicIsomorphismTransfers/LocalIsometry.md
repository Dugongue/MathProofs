# Characteristic-two matrix isometry with commutative adjoint residue

## Theorem

Let $A$ be an explicitly presented finite-dimensional associative algebra over a finite field of characteristic two. Let $A$ have a linear involution $ * $ and Jacobson radical $J$, and assume $S=A/J$ is commutative. Given a self-adjoint unit $c$, one can decide and solve $g^*g=c$ in Las Vegas polynomial bit time. The bound allows arbitrary radical length, any field factors of $S$, their permutation by the involution, and arbitrary twists between the left and right residue actions on radical layers. No involution-invariant section is required. Consequently ordered matrix-tuple isometry over a fixed finite field of characteristic two has the same bound whenever the semisimple quotient of the transpose-closed source adjoint algebra is commutative.

This is an informal result on the characteristic-two matrix-tuple isometry problem raised by Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun. It uses additive congruence lifting and the Peirce analysis of the earlier MathProofs square-zero result. The key step here is the exact residue-group update for arbitrarily many layers. [Published problem](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1), [finite-algebra algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(08)80017-X), [adjoint reduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03495).

## Filtration and the exact unipotent subgroup

Restrict scalars to $\mathbb F_2$. Compute $J$ and its powers, writing $J^s=0$, and decompose $S$ into its field factors using the primitive idempotents of the commutative algebra. For $j\ge1$ put
$$
 H_j=\{g\in A^\times:g^*g=1\bmod J^j\},
 \qquad K_j=H_j\cap(1+J).
$$
The groups $1+J^i$ give a central filtration of $1+J$ with additive $\mathbb F_2$-vector factors. Refine these by arbitrary complete flags to cyclic factors of order two. Elements and leading coordinates in the resulting filtration are available through the algebra operations and linear algebra. In particular inverses of $1+x$ are calculated by a finite geometric sum.

We use the following explicit group arithmetic. In any subgroup given by generators, retain at most one pivot at each index of this filtration. Sift each generator by multiplying by inverse pivots to cancel successive leading coordinates. Insert a pivot at the first required vacant index. Saturate under squares and pairwise commutators of pivots, with each added pivot triggering a fresh pass. There are no more insertions than filtration indices. When the process stops, the collected words with each pivot used to exponent zero or one form a group. In descending order, the tail generated by the later pivots is normalized by the next pivot, since its commutators with them lie farther down the filtration and sift to identity, and the square of that pivot belongs to the tail. The tail grows by at most two cosets. Induction proves the group assertion and the correctness of membership sifting. Pivot elements always remain in the original subgroup, which proves equality with it.

If $\phi$ is a computed homomorphism from such a subgroup into an additive binary space $W$, sift the graph generated by the pairs $(g,\phi(g))$ inside $(1+J)\times W$. Put the $W$ coordinates first in its central filtration. The later pivots project to generators of $\ker\phi$. The first pivots decide any prescribed output and give a preimage by retaining the group components that cancel its leading coordinates. All these algorithms use polynomially many algebra and vector-space operations.

For the passage from $j$ to $j+1$ take $X=J^j/J^{j+1}$. The map
$$
 \phi_j\colon K_j\longrightarrow X,\qquad k\longmapsto [k^*k-1]
$$
is additive, since multiplication on either side by $1+J$ acts trivially on $X$. Its kernel is $K_{j+1}$. Compute $D_j=\operatorname{im}\phi_j$, its preimage routine, and the next generators by the graph procedure. Initially $K_1=1+J$. In particular these steps do not enumerate the elements of $1+J$.

## Reduce the layer to scalar field actions

The initial residue norm equation has an explicit solution. In a self-dual field, $\bar c$ is in the fixed field, and its square root there has the required norm. On a pair of exchanged factors identify the fields via the involution. The two coordinates of $\bar c$ agree, so choose that value in one coordinate and $1$ in the other. Lift the solution to a reference $r\in A^\times$.

Write $Q_j$ for the image of $H_j$ in $S^\times$. We will show inductively that it is a product over some of the involution orbits of the full norm-one groups of those orbits, with the other coordinates equal to $1$. At $j=1$ this is automatic. A self-dual field $E$ has either trivial norm-one group or the group of order $1+\sqrt{|E|}$ for its nontrivial involution. A pair of exchanged isomorphic fields has norm-one group $E^\times$, with coordinates $(\lambda,\lambda^{-1})$. Every nontrivial factor is odd, and as a subset of its field it generates the field over $\mathbb F_2$. For a self-dual orbit of the second kind this follows from its order, which exceeds the number of units in any proper subfield. Call the nontrivial factors still in $Q_j$ active.

Lifts for this group are computable without a homomorphic section. Given $\lambda\in Q_j$, lift it arbitrarily to a unit of $H_1$. Correct it successively at layers $i<j$ using the stored preimage routines for $\phi_i$. If the current lift is in $H_i$, it differs from some actual $H_j$-lift by right multiplication by $K_i$. Its norm defect at layer $i$ therefore belongs to $D_i$, so the correction exists and gives an $H_{i+1}$-lift. At arbitrary stages we need only polynomially many such residues lifted.

At layer $j$, $X=J^j/J^{j+1}$ is an $S$-bimodule, and $D_j$ lies in $X^*=\{z\in X:z^*=z\}$. Since $1+z\in K_j$ for any $z\in J^j$, $D_j$ contains the image of $1+*$. On $X^*/D_j$, $Q_j$ acts affinely by congruence on the fiber $1+X^*$ modulo $J^{j+1}$. Its linear part at $\lambda$ is $z\mapsto\lambda^{-1}z\lambda$. Its shift is the class of $g^*g-1$ for any $H_j$-lift $g$ of $\lambda$. This is independent of the lift, since $K_j$ acts by translation. In particular $D_j$ is invariant under the linear parts.

We first describe the intermediate space $\mathcal T=X^*/\operatorname{im}(1+*)$. If $e_i$ is the central idempotent of a factor $i$ of $S$, the involution sends the Peirce space $e_i X e_h$ to $e_{h^*}X e_{i^*}$. Two distinct exchanged spaces make zero contribution to $\mathcal T$ because every fixed vector in their sum is a trace. The only surviving spaces are $e_i X e_{i^*}$. Identify the two fields of this bimodule as a single field $E$, using the involution on a swapped pair. Write $\sigma$ for the involution on a self-dual field, or the identity under this identification on a pair. Split the bimodule into twist sectors on which $z a=\theta(a)z$ for $\theta\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb F_2)$; this is the standard split decomposition of $E\otimes_{\mathbb F_2}E$ using the embeddings of $E$. It is computed by linear equations in the left and right actions of one generator of $E$. The involution sends the sector of $\theta$ to that of $\theta^{-1}$ and is $\theta^{-1}\sigma$-semilinear on left scalars. Distinct exchanged sectors again contribute zero. On a fixed sector where $\theta^{-1}\sigma\ne1$, choose $b\in E$ of trace one to the fixed field for this scalar involution. For any fixed vector $x$, the element $bx$ has trace $x$. Thus only the sector $\theta=\sigma$ survives. There the induced involution is $E$-linear, so its fixed space modulo its trace image is an $E$-space.

This describes $\mathcal T$ as a sum of field vector spaces with particularly simple scalars. On the surviving space of a self-dual active factor, the scalar is $\lambda^{-1}\sigma(\lambda)=\lambda^{-2}$. On the two surviving spaces of an active swapped pair the two scalars are $\lambda^{-2}$ and $\lambda^{2}$, respectively. Elements of the other factors act trivially there. On all inactive spaces the full $Q_j$ acts trivially.

## The quotient still separates the active factors

The image $\overline D_j$ of $D_j$ in $\mathcal T$ is invariant under $Q_j$. For each active factor choose any nonidentity scalar $\alpha$ in it. Its linear action minus the identity is invertible on that factor's part of $\mathcal T$ and is zero on every other part. Therefore projection onto that part is a polynomial in the action of $\alpha$, by the coprimality of the corresponding minimal polynomial and $x-1$. It follows that $\overline D_j$ splits by active factors and the trivial part. The projections are computed in the already known direct-sum coordinates.

Within a self-dual active factor, $\overline D_j$ is an $E$-subspace, since it is binary linear and invariant under the scalars $\lambda^{-2}$ which generate $E$. Within an active swapped pair with $|E|>4$, the two scalar representations $\lambda^{-2}$ and $\lambda^{2}$ are inequivalent over $\mathbb F_2$. For an abstract generator of $E^\times$, their eigenvalues would be conjugate only if $2^b=-1\bmod (|E|-1)$ for some $0\le b<[E\mathbin{\colon}\mathbb F_2]$, which is impossible for $|E|>4$. Its two minimal polynomials are coprime. Their projectors show that $\overline D_j$ splits between the two original spaces, and their projected binary spaces are $E$-linear. The argument needs no algorithm to find that generator. For $|E|=4$, apply entrywise squaring after choosing field coordinates in the second space. This changes its character $\lambda^2$ to $\lambda^4=\lambda^{-2}$ and allows the two spaces to be treated together as a single $E$-space, with $\overline D_j$ an $E$-subspace. For $|E|=2$ the pair is inactive. Thus $X^*/D_j$ has computable coordinates with the same scalar actions.

The odd group $Q_j$ has a common affine fixed point, by averaging over it. This point can be obtained without performing that average. On the part for each active factor, use the stored lift routine for the chosen $\alpha$ to compute its affine shift there. Since its linear action minus the identity is invertible on that part, the single fixed-point equation finds the component of a common fixed point. On the trivial part the affine action is a homomorphism to translations of exponent two, so it is trivial. Choose the origin there. A nonidentity $\alpha$ for a swapped pair is any unit distinct from $1$. For a self-dual active field choose $a$ outside the fixed field and use $a/\sigma(a)$, which is nontrivial. All these computations are polynomial field and binary linear algebra.

## Exact transport, stabilizer update and running time

Center the affine action at the computed fixed point. The source point is the centered class of $0$, and the target point is the centered class of $(r^*)^{-1} c r^{-1}-1$, where $r^*r=c\bmod J^j$ is the current reference. On the trivial part the two coordinates must agree. On every active field space, vanishing source requires vanishing target. A nonzero source requires that the target be a nonzero scalar multiple of it. The multiple is determined by an entry, checked against all other entries, and gives an equation $\lambda^{2}=a$ or $\lambda^{-2}=a$. These equations determine a unique $\lambda$ by inverse Frobenius and inversion. Check the norm-one condition and consistency across both spaces of a swapped pair. With no such equation on a factor, its entire norm group is allowed, so choose the identity there.

This decides the layer exactly on the quotient. For a successful residue, lift it to $g\in H_j$ and correct by an element of $K_j$ using the preimage routine for $D_j$. If $h$ is the resulting correcting unit, replace $r$ by $hr$. This is a witness modulo $J^{j+1}$. A failed test excludes every lift, since any potential witness differs from $r$ on the left by some element of $H_j$.

For the stabilizer computation compare the centered source with itself. Each active factor on which it has any nonzero entry is forced to the identity. Every other factor stays active, with no restriction. This is exactly $Q_{j+1}$, because acting trivially on the quotient point is equivalent to its actual displacement belonging to $D_j$, which is then correctable. This proves the product invariant used in the induction, even for a nontrivial involution with no invariant section. No constraints coupling different factors are lost.

There are at most $\dim_{\mathbb F_2} A+1$ layers, and only polynomially many lifts of selected residues per layer. The storage for the stored congruence subgroup routines, all field spaces, and all linear systems is polynomial. The last witness at $J^s=0$ has exact norm $c$. The sole needed algebra decomposition and field arithmetic subroutines can be taken Las Vegas polynomial time. This yields the theorem including cases where $J=0$ or every residue involution factor is trivial. In a successful instance the number of solutions is $|K_s||Q_s|$, since the solutions form a coset of $H_s$. The first factor is $2$ to the number of kernel pivots and the second is the product of the retained residue norm-group orders. This gives exact counting in the same time bound, also for matrix-tuple isometry by the adjoint correspondence.

## Applying the theorem to matrix tuples

Close both ordered tuples under transpose. Test for an invertible left–right equivalence $X^{\mathsf T}A_iY=B_i$ by module isomorphism. If there is none, no isometry exists. Compute the adjoint algebra
$$
 \mathcal A=\{(L,R): L^{\mathsf T}A_i=A_iR\text{ for every }i\},
$$
with multiplication $(L,R)(L',R')=(LL',R'R)$ and involution $(L,R)^*=(R,L)$. The reference equivalence and its transpose make $c=(YX^{-1},YX^{-1})$ a self-adjoint unit. For an isometry $P$ the pair $(PX^{-1},YP^{-1})$ belongs to $\mathcal A^\times$ and has norm $c$. Conversely if $(L,R)$ has norm $c$, then $RL=YX^{-1}$ and $P=LX$ gives $P^{\mathsf T}A_iP=X^{\mathsf T}A_iRLX=B_i$. The source-side commutative-residue hypothesis is checkable on $\mathcal A/\operatorname{rad}\mathcal A$. This gives decision and search on that promise. [Constructive adjoint and module isomorphism methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03495).
