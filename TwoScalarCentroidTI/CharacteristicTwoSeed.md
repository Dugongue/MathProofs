# Exponential Laurent complexity for sparse cubics in characteristic two

Let \(k\) have characteristic two and let \(K=k((t))\), \(R=k[[t]]\). Consider ordinary linear substitutions of all variables of one cubic. An image arc is polynomial when all its coefficient functions are in \(k[t]\), and its image degree is the maximum of their degrees after fixing its nonzero special value. The symmetric pole depth of a substitution is the maximum of zero and the entry pole orders of its matrix and inverse.

**Theorem.** For every \(m\ge 1\) there are explicit sparse cubics \(F_m^+,F_m^-\) of dimension \(M=O(m)\), with \(O(M)\) monomials and a fixed direction \(E_m\) of bounded support, with the following properties.

- For any field extension and any nonzero \(c\), \(F_m^-+cE_m\) is equivalent to \(F_m^+\) by a linear substitution over that field exactly when \(c\) is a \(2^{m-1}\)-st power. In particular the degree-one geometric orbit arc \(F_m^-+tE_m\) lifts over \(k((t^{1/e}))\) exactly when \(2^{m-1}\mid e\).
- Every polynomial image arc with a Laurent substitution over \(K\) from \(F_m^+\) to \(F_m^-\) has degree at least \(2^{m-1}/O(M^8)\). A monomial substitution gives an image arc of degree \(2^{m-1}\).
- The least symmetric pole depth of a Laurent substitution is \(\Theta(2^m)\).

The lower bounds allow all linear substitutions. The forms can be defined over \(\mathbb F_2\). Together with the earlier odd-characteristic construction, the exponential cubic separation holds over every coefficient field. [Earlier cubic construction](CubicSeed.md)

## 1. The sparse algebra and its trivial extension

Start with the terminal-cap associative algebra \(A_c\) of the Möbius telescope, with coefficient \(c\) on the one cap flag and every other specified product equal to one. Its basis consists of vertex idempotents and strict corners. The flag products are \(p_{ij}p_{j\ell}=a_{ij\ell}p_{i\ell}\). The target has \(c=0\). For the selected cap flag the two input edges each belong to another retained flag, and its output edge is also that of another retained flag. In the punctured surface the last cap boundary is \(\pm 2^{m-1}\) times the primitive initial circle. [Contact-lattice construction](ContactSeed.md)

As in the odd-characteristic construction, append a private root \(o\) and path \(c_1,\ldots,c_v\) for the \(v\) old vertices. The root arrows to \(c_1\), the remaining \(c_i\), and the old vertices have dimensions \(4,2,3\), respectively. Add one-dimensional arrows \(c_i\to c_{i+1}\) and \(c_i\to i\). All new strict products vanish, and all diagonal corners of \(A_c\) remain one-dimensional. Set \(C_c=A_c\ltimes A_c^*\), with multiplication \((x,f)(y,g)=(xy,xg+fy)\), where \((xgy)(z)=g(yzx)\). The functional \(\tau(x,f)=f(1)\) has perfect symmetric product pairing. The same written table has only \(O(m)\) nonzero entries, since every original product contributes two transposed module-action entries in addition to the original entry. All vertices have an incident strict corner.

The radical \(J_C=J_A\oplus A_c^*\) is the two-sided ideal generated by commutators over every field, including characteristic two. Indeed a commutator with a vertex idempotent gives any off-diagonal basis vector of \(C_c\) up to sign. For an original arrow basis vector \(p:i\to j\) and its dual \(p^*\), one has \(pp^*=e_i^*\) and \(p^*p=e_j^*\), so the dual diagonal lines are also in the ideal. These identities follow by evaluating on the written basis: no product of two positive-degree vectors can equal the input arrow when the other input is that same arrow, by the positive grading. Conversely \(C_c/J_C\) is commutative. This allows rational recovery of the radical without the use of its regular trace pairing.

The based dimensions of radical powers agree at \(c=0\) and \(c\ne0\). In degree two the original part is the span of live flag outputs, and the dual part is spanned by duals of incident vertices and duals of input edges of live flag products. In degree three the original part is zero and the dual part consists of the duals of vertices appearing in live flags. Higher powers vanish. These statements follow directly from
\[
 J_C^h=J_A^h\oplus\sum_{i+j=h-1}J_A^i A_c^*J_A^j.
\]
The two input edges of the removed cap flag are retained inputs of other flags, its output is retained, and each of its vertices occurs in a retained flag.

The off-diagonal dimensions of \(C_c\) identify all old vertex labels: the root is the unique vertex incident to dimensions \(2,3,4\), and the path among the dimension-two and dimension-four neighbors identifies its vertices from the marked endpoint. Its matching edges identify the dimension-three neighbors. The same graph occurs in the opposite algebra. The two orientations are distinguishable in the labeled graded algebra by a retained old flag chord: its forward line is in degree two, and its reverse dual line is in degree one. Thus a generic isomorphism between products \(C_1^{\mathrm{op}}\times C_1\) and a graded comparison with \(C_0^{\mathrm{op}}\times C_0\) cannot exchange the two orientation components.

## 2. A cubic with a uniquely determined isotropic space

Set \(B=M_d(C_c)\), with \(d=3\). The variable space is \(\mathcal X\oplus\mathcal Y\), where \(\mathcal X=B^2\) and \(\mathcal Y=(B^{d^2})^*\). Write its elements as \((X,Y,Z)\) with \(Z=(Z_{ij})\). Define
\[
 F_c(X,Y,Z)=\sum_{i,j} Z_{ij}(XE_{ij}Y).
\]
The \(E_{ij}\) are constant matrix units. The variable dimension is \(M=(2d^2+d^4)\dim C_c\). Sparsity follows from the sparse product table and fixed \(d\), and the only dependence on \(c\) comes from the marked coefficient and its two dual module entries. Put \(F_m^+=F_1,F_m^-=F_0\).

Use full third polarization \(P_F\), defined by inclusion-exclusion of the values of \(F\) on sums of three inputs. It is equivariant under linear substitution and in this characteristic is alternating. In the written decomposition \(P_F\) vanishes whenever two inputs are in \(\mathcal Y\). Moreover \(\mathcal Y\) is the unique subspace of dimension at least \(\dim\mathcal Y\) having that property.

For the uniqueness, let \(S\) be any subspace with \(P_F(S,S,-)=0\). Let \(L\) be its projection to \(\mathcal X\), and set \(J_0=S\cap\mathcal Y\). Write \(L_X,L_Y\) for the two projections of \(L\) to \(B\). For \((X,Y)\in L\), \(J_0\) annihilates every value of
\[
 C_{X,Y}(u,v)=(u E_{ij}Y+X E_{ij}v)_{ij}.
\]
Indeed evaluate the polarization on a lift of \((X,Y)\), an element of \(J_0\), and \((u,v,0)\). Identify the tuple space \(B^{d^2}\) with \(\operatorname{Hom}_k(M_d(k),B)\). Under the left-right multiplication identification of \(\operatorname{End}_k(M_d(k))\) with \(M_d(k)\otimes M_d(k)^{\mathrm{op}}\), testing all \((0,T\otimes1)\) for scalar matrices \(T\) shows that the span of these values contains a space of dimension \(d^2\dim L_X\): it contains all maps \(E\mapsto XET\) with \(X\in L_X\). Testing the other inputs gives dimension at least \(d^2\dim L_Y\). Since \(\dim L\le\dim L_X+\dim L_Y\), \(L\ne0\) would imply \(\operatorname{codim}_{\mathcal Y}J_0>\dim L\), so \(\dim S=\dim J_0+\dim L<\dim\mathcal Y\). This proves uniqueness over every field.

Thus for an arbitrary integral orbit arc the transported generic \(\mathcal Y_K\) has a free saturated lattice obtained by intersecting it with the ambient free \(R\)-module. Its special reduction has dimension \(\dim\mathcal Y\) and satisfies the same polarization identity. It is therefore the written special \(\mathcal Y\) by uniqueness. In particular this saturation argument preserves the intended space even before constructing rational bases.

## 3. Computing the space by six Hessian slices

The Hessian slice is the polarization in which one input is fixed. At \((X,Y,Z)\) its matrix has blocks \(\begin{psmallmatrix}A_Z&C_{X,Y}^{\mathsf T}\\C_{X,Y}&0\end{psmallmatrix}\) in the displayed decomposition. Write \(s=\dim C_c\) and \(\delta=\sum_i\dim(e_iC_ce_i)\). The value \(\delta\) is common to the two endpoints. The largest rank of \(C_{X,Y}\) is \(q=\dim\mathcal X-s\). For units \(X,Y\) its kernel consists of \((Xa,-aY)\) where \(a\in I_d\otimes C_c\), as follows by summing the diagonal selector equations and then using all matrix units. That locus is dense after extension to the algebraic closure. Therefore the upper rank bound holds identically, even in small fields.

The restriction of \(A_Z\) to that kernel is the bilinear form \((a,b)\mapsto \ell(ab+ba)\) for some \(\ell\in C_c^*\), by expansion of the \(X E_{ij}Y\) terms and the commutation of \(a,b\) with \(E_{ij}\). Every such \(\ell\) occurs for fixed units \(X,Y\), using the \(11\) output slot and evaluating \((X^{-1}bY^{-1})_{11}\). In characteristic two \(ab+ba=[a,b]\). Write \(\ell(a)=\tau(ha)\) using perfectness. Its alternating pairing has radical the centralizer of \(h\) in \(C_c\), by cyclicity of \(\tau\).

The minimum dimension of such a centralizer equals \(\delta\). To see this over an infinite field, take the dense open set of \(h\) with pairwise distinct residues \(\lambda_i\) at the split vertices. The product of \(h-\lambda_i\) lies in the radical, so is nilpotent. The Chinese remainder theorem in \(k[h]\) gives idempotents reducing to the vertex idempotents which commute with \(h\). They are simultaneously conjugate to the written idempotents by a unit congruent to one modulo the radical: for two systems \(f_i,e_i\) with the same residues use \(\sum e_i f_i\). Thus one can assume \(h\) block diagonal. On an off-diagonal corner the commutator map is \(\lambda_i-\lambda_j\) times the identity plus a nilpotent operator, since the left and right nilpotent parts commute. It is invertible on that corner. On the diagonal corners the commutator vanishes, because they are commutative dual-number algebras. The centralizer dimension on that open set is therefore \(\delta\). This proves the global rank bound \(s-\delta\) for the restricted pairing by density. The same bound holds over any field by coefficient extension. A Hessian slice has rank \(2\operatorname{rank} C+\operatorname{rank}(A_Z|_{\ker C})\), by elimination on the mixed blocks. On the dense unit locus this is at most \(2q+s-\delta\), so that is also the global bound.

For the following fixed evaluation points the coefficient field may be enlarged by a purely transcendental extension, so that pairwise distinct \(\lambda_i\) are available. Such an extension preserves the degree and valuations of any witness over the original coefficient field. Choose \(h=\sum_i\lambda_i e_i\), and six linearly independent invertible matrices \(T_1,\ldots,T_6\in M_3(k)\). For example use \(I\) and \(I+E_{ij}\) for five different off-diagonal positions. At evaluation \(i\) set \(X=Y=T_i\otimes1\) and choose \(Z\) realizing \(\ell(a)=\tau(ha)\), as described above. Each slice attains maximal rank \(2q+s-\delta\). Its kernel projects to \(\mathcal X\) as the space
\[
 \{(T_i\otimes a,T_i\otimes a):a\in \textstyle\bigoplus_j e_j C_c e_j\},
\]
since this projection identifies with the radical of \(A_Z|_{\ker C}\). Its intersection with \(\mathcal Y\) is the annihilator of \(\operatorname{im} C_{X,Y}\).

In the left-right matrix coordinates of Section 2,
\[
 \operatorname{im} C_{X,Y}
   =(M_d(k)\otimes kT_i+kT_i\otimes M_d(k))\otimes C_c.
\]
For any three independent \(T_i\), these three spaces have zero intersection: extend them to a basis, and an entry in a two-factor tensor cannot have one coordinate equal to each of three distinct indices. Therefore the vertical intersections of each triple of Hessian kernels span \(\mathcal Y\). Also the projections of the six kernels to \(\mathcal X\) have independent sums because the \(T_i\) are independent. Thus if \(S_1\) is the sum of the first three kernels and \(S_2\) is the sum of the other three, then
\[
 \dim S_1=\dim S_2=\dim\mathcal Y+3\delta,\qquad S_1\cap S_2=\mathcal Y .
\]

Apply the same six evaluations to an arbitrary Laurent witness, using the fixed points for its special law. Their Hessian ranks remain maximal, by the special maximal minors and the global generic bound. The ranks of their mixed maps \(C\) also remain \(q\), since the saturated space from Section 2 specializes correctly, and the \(q\)-minors in adapted integral frames survive. Thus the six vertical kernels and projections have their endpoint ranks. In each triple the vertical kernels span the saturated space, since they do in the special fiber. The sum of the whole kernels has generic rank at most \(\dim\mathcal Y+3\delta\) and achieves this rank specially, so it is split and has that rank in both fibers. The intersection of these sums contains the saturated space and has dimension at most its special dimension. It therefore gives the saturated space exactly. This construction requires only evaluating the slices, taking kernels, sums, and an intersection by unit-pivot elimination.

## 4. From the recovered polarization to contact and degree bounds

Quotient by the recovered \(\mathcal Y\) and restrict the remaining input of the polarized form to \(\mathcal Y\). This gives the symmetric bimap \((x,y),(u,v)\mapsto(x E_{ij}v+u E_{ij}y)_{ij}\). Its adjoint algebra of pairs \((P,Q)\) with \(b(Px,y)=b(x,Qy)\), using composition \((PP',Q'Q)\), is \(C_c^{\mathrm{op}}\times C_c\). To verify this, sum the diagonal output components. For the resulting bimap \(xv+uy\), comparing the four pairs of input slots and evaluating at the unit makes the two diagonal blocks of \(P\) right multiplication by \(a\) and left multiplication by \(b\), with the adjoint blocks exchanged. Off-diagonal blocks would require left or right annihilators of all commutators, and vanish because \(E_{12}E_{21}-E_{21}E_{12}\), restricted to its two matrix positions, is invertible there. More explicitly, use \(E_{12},E_{21}\) and \(E_{13},E_{31}\); the two diagonal commutators have kernels on disjoint block positions, hence their simultaneous left and right annihilators are zero. Restoring every selector equation forces \(a,b\) to commute with every \(E_{ij}\). Their commutant is \(I_d\otimes C_c\). This is the characteristic-two variant of the adjoint calculation in [the earlier cubic proof](CubicSeed.md).

The adjoint algebra is a kernel of linear equations, with the same rank in generic and special fibers. Its radical is obtained as the image of the span of \(u[x,y]v\), because the commutator ideal of the written endpoints has the prescribed radical dimension by Section 1. The successive powers of this image are also free and specialize exactly by their equal endpoint ranks and unit-minor elimination. The split quotient idempotents lift over \(R\), and act on the associated graded pieces. Labels and orientation are recovered as in Section 1. The generic isomorphism acts on each old original-order corner line by a scalar \(\eta_{ij}\). Its old flag coefficients are therefore \(\eta_{i\ell}/(\eta_{ij}\eta_{j\ell})\). On unmarked flags their valuations are zero, and the marked valuation is positive. The integer cocycle of negative valuations has marked contact divisible by \(2^{m-1}\).

For a static field equivalence the unique \(\mathcal Y\) is recovered before any coefficient extension, and the adjoint argument uses the original field. The same cochain argument with multiplicative coefficients forces \(c\) to be a \(2^{m-1}\)-st power there. Conversely a cocycle giving this power yields a diagonal map of \(A_c\) and extends to the trivial extension, matrix amplification and cubic. In particular \(c=t^{2^{m-1}}\) gives the upper image degree. For symmetric pole depth, a cubic substitution of depth \(b\) acts on the quotient by the saturated \(\mathcal Y\) with depth at most \(b\). Its conjugation action on the adjoint operators costs at most \(2b\) and bounds the induced maps on the graded corners in integral frames. Thus \(|v_t(\eta_{ij})|\le2b\). The six-edge terminal cycle has circulation at least \(2^{m-1}\) in absolute value, forcing \(12b\ge2^{m-1}\). The previous integral cocycle with terminal contact \(2^{m-1}\) has weights \(O(2^m)\), and its extension on dual and matrix spaces retains this bound. [Sharp terminal witnesses](TerminalSeed.md)

For the degree lower bound use \(Q=D/J\), \(W=J/J^2\), and \(U=J^2/J^3\) for the recovered adjoint algebra \(D\). With \(\theta\) the inverse of the regular trace pairing of the split algebra \(Q\), form the map
\[
 W\otimes W\longrightarrow U\otimes Q,\qquad
 x\otimes y\longmapsto\sum_{i,j}\theta_{ij}((xq_i)y)\otimes q_j .
\]
The trace pairing on \(Q\) is unimodular since it is the identity in the split idempotent basis. The map tags multiplication by the middle vertex, and in graded corner frames splits into blocks by triples of vertices. The original marked flag is a one-by-one block with valuation at least \(2^{m-1}\). Every maximal-rank minor is thus divisible by this power.

If the input cubic arc has polynomial degree \(D_0\), say an array has height \(h\) when it has one denominator which is a unit at zero, with that denominator and every numerator of degree at most \(h\). Unit-pivot kernel solving multiplies height by at most the number of pivot columns, by clearing denominators and using the adjugate. Products add heights. Thus the six Hessian kernels and the two sums have height \(O(MD_0)\). Their intersection has height \(O(M^2D_0)\), since one more kernel system has at most \(2M\) unknowns. Complementing by standard vectors and contracting the polarization gives the bimap with the same height order. The adjoint system has at most \(2M^2\) unknowns and yields multiplication of height \(O(M^4D_0)\), reading products in free kernel coordinates. The commutator ideal and its relevant powers use a bounded number of multiplication factors per column, so their adapted basis has the same height order. Inverting that basis costs a factor at most \(M\). The adapted multiplication then has height \(O(M^5D_0)\). The inverse trace pairing and tagged map have height \(O(M^6D_0)\). A maximal-rank minor has size at most \(M^2\), so its numerator degree is \(O(M^8 D_0)\), with denominator a unit at zero. Divisibility gives the claimed lower bound.

The minor and kernel operations used here concern an arbitrary orbit arc in one fixed form space. The characteristic-two recovery differs from the odd-characteristic recovery because the Hessian is alternating and each individual Hessian kernel may have a nonzero projection outside \(\mathcal Y\). The two sums and their intersection recover \(\mathcal Y\) without assuming that an arbitrary substitution respects the written variables. These are written proofs. [Grochow–Qiao on cubic-form equivalence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

## A corollary for alternating forms and determinant-one substitutions

The same theorem holds for the alternating trilinear forms \(P_{F_m^\pm}\) under one change of basis in their argument space, with the same arc \(P_{F_m^-}+cP_{E_m}\). Every recovery step uses the trilinear coefficients alone. They vary linearly on the displayed family, and \(P_{E_m}\ne0\) because the changing monomials use three distinct variable groups. The diagonal maps attaining the line and the upper bounds can also be chosen in the special linear group, for both the cubics and the alternating forms. On the trivial extension an algebra map \(g\) is paired with its inverse dual on complementary spaces, hence has determinant one. Matrix amplification, the two argument copies and the output dual copies all preserve this determinant-one property.
