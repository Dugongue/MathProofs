# Rational specialization preserving two quantifier blocks

Let a formula over \(\mathbb Q(t_1,\ldots,t_\ell)\) have at most two blocks of field quantifiers, and a quantifier-free Boolean combination of polynomial equations and inequations. Input scalar rational functions and polynomials are given by arithmetic circuits, with the remaining variables distinguished from the parameters \(t\). There is a deterministic polynomial-time construction of a rational circuit description of a specialization of the \(t\)'s at which the formula has the same truth value over an algebraic closure. The specialized circuits have polynomial description size. The same point works for an input list of such formulas. This extends the earlier generic-rank specialization to geometric solvability and its two-block alternation.

Clear scalar denominators to obtain integer polynomial circuits, keeping those denominators as extra nonvanishing requirements on the specialization. From the circuit size there are bounds \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(s)}\) on degrees and on logarithms of coefficient absolute sums. We use the effective weak Nullstellensatz over characteristic zero in its usual form: for polynomials in \(n\) variables of degrees at most \(D\ge2\), if 1 is in the generated ideal, it has a certificate whose individual products have degree at most \(D^{O(n+1)}\). This bound is independent of the number of input polynomials. It is enough here to use any standard singly exponential bound in \(n\log(D+1)\). [Kollár, *Sharp effective Nullstellensatz*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-1988-0944576-7)

For a conjunction of equations and inequations, replace the inequations by one equation saying that their product has an inverse. Emptiness over an algebraic closure is now membership of the constant column in the span of the degree-bounded Macaulay coefficient columns. Ranks of the Macaulay matrix and its augmentation by this column test membership. The number of rows is bounded by the number of monomials up to the degree cutoff. All these statements also apply when some polynomials specialize to smaller degree, using columns for a degree cutoff at least the certificate bound plus their original maximum degree.

## One quantifier block

Each branch of the disjunction of conjunctions of the input atoms has singly exponentially bounded degrees and logarithmic coefficient sums. The Macaulay matrix for a branch has at most \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(s)}\) rows. Its entries are coefficients of the branch polynomials and constants. Every determinant of size \(k\) made from entries of degree at most \(D_0\) and absolute coefficient sum at most \(B_0\) has degree at most \(k D_0\) and absolute coefficient sum at most \(k! B_0^k\). Thus every minor in every branch has degree and log of coefficient absolute sum at most \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(s)}\), independently of the number of branches or columns. A universal block is the negation of an existential block with the predicate negated and obeys the same bounds.

## Two blocks

For an inner block, treat outer variables \(x\) as unassigned and regard all the inner minors as polynomials in \(t,x\). At each assignment of \(x\), the truth value of the inner block is determined by the rank fingerprints of the two inner matrices in each branch. Encode a given exact rank of a matrix by equations requiring all minors of the next size to vanish, and by one chosen nonzero minor of the required rank. A rank-zero witness is the constant 1, and an impossible-size next minor condition is empty. Taking the possibilities for the ranks and the witness minor positions expresses either truth value of the inner block as a union of conjunctions of minor equations and inequations. A conjunction uses no more than two nonzero witnesses per original branch, hence \(2^{O(s)}\) inequations. It may contain more equations than that. Neither list is computed.

Now if the outer quantifier is existential, use the conjunctions for the inner predicate to be true. If it is universal, use the conjunctions for the predicate to be false and negate the resulting existential answer. Within each outer conjunction, replace the inequations by one inverse equation as before. The product involves at most singly exponentially many factors of singly exponentially bounded degree and logarithmic coefficient absolute sum. Consequently the outer systems still have degree and log coefficient bounds \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(s)}\) and polynomially many variables. Their Macaulay row counts are again at most \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(s)}\), regardless of the number of equations. Every minor of every outer matrix therefore has the same singly exponential form of bound. These bounds use the same integer polynomials before and after specialization, and the rank conditions express the inner truth value at all outer assignments on both sides.

## The simultaneous point

Choose a base \(D\) above all individual parameter exponent bounds and a positive integer \(H\) larger than all absolute coefficient sums. Both choices have polynomial-size constant circuits from the established bounds. Set \(t_i=H^{D^{i-1}}\). For every nonzero parameter polynomial under consideration, distinct monomials map to distinct powers of \(H\), and the leading term dominates the total lower contribution. So the zeros and nonzeros of all relevant scalar minors agree at the generic parameters and at this rational point. The same point preserves the explicit scalar denominators. It follows that the outer rank tests agree, and hence so does the complete formula. The smaller one-block case follows directly. This procedure constructs the rational specialization only, without expanding formulas, coefficient matrices or minors. Its conclusion concerns truth over algebraic closures, including when the original formula came from an orbit or incidence condition defined over the coefficient field. This proof is informal.
