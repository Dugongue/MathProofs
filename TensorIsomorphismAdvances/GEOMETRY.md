# Tensor isomorphism in a smooth rational Fano family of Picard rank two

**Theorem.** Over every field $k$, ordered three-tensor isomorphism has a deterministic polynomial-size reduction to abstract $k$-isomorphism of smooth geometrically integral $k$-rational Fano varieties of geometric Picard rank exactly two. In the standard explicit field encodings the construction runs in polynomial time. The varieties are hypersurfaces of bidegree $(2,1)$ in a product of two projective spaces, with one projection a projective bundle. They also have polynomial-size single-projective presentations by quadratic equations. For each fixed tensor format the outputs form a smooth Fano family with the same integral Chow ring, anticanonical class, Tate Chow motive, and, over finite fields, point counts over every finite extension.

On the natural presentation of all basepoint-free hypersurfaces of this bidegree with independent coefficient quadrics and the dimension conditions stated below, the converse reduction to three-tensor isomorphism also holds. The construction further gives a natural bijection on all separably split forms of each tensor and its Fano variety. In particular, every finite abelian group occurs as the entire everywhere-local genus of an explicit rational Fano variety of geometric Picard rank two, with the group law furnished by the arithmetic construction. These are informal proofs.

## 1. A basepoint-free quadratic system that retains the transporter

Write the tensor as a tuple of matrices $A(z):V\to U$, linear in a slice parameter $z\in Z$. Let $F(z):Z^*\to k$ be the universal row $w\mapsto w(z)$. Use three copies of the matrix pair of spaces $U,V$ and three copies of the row pair $k,Z^*$, giving six left and six right blocks. Pad all twelve individual spaces by zeros to distinct consecutive dimensions $N+1,\ldots,N+12$, with $N\ge\max(\dim U,\dim V,\dim Z,2)$, using fixed coordinate inclusions. For a row or matrix interpreted as an element of target tensored with the dual of source, the left block is the padded target and the right block is the dual of the padded source. Denote the resulting twelve spaces of linear forms by $E_i$, and put $E^\vee=\bigoplus_i E_i$.

In the mixed summands of $\operatorname{Sym}^2 E^\vee$, put the graph of the linear map
$$
 z\longmapsto B(z)=A(z)\oplus A(z)\oplus A(z)\oplus F(z)\oplus F(z)\oplus F(z).
$$
Here each of the six maps occupies its own left-right pair. The diagonal forms and these mixed forms give the system
$$
 S_A=\left(\bigoplus_i\operatorname{Sym}^2 E_i\right)\oplus B(Z).
$$
The row copies make the last map injective. The system contains the square of every variable and is therefore basepoint-free over an algebraic closure. Its dimension $r=\sum_i\binom{\dim E_i+1}2+\dim Z$ depends only on the tensor format. Write $m=\dim E^\vee$, so $m\ge4$ and $r>m$.

The twelve blocks are intrinsic to the system, in every characteristic. For a nonzero linear form $\ell$, consider $C(\ell)=\{\ell':\ell\ell'\in S_A\}$. If $\ell=v+w$ in the sum of the left and right blocks, a product with $\ell'=v'+w'$ has left-right component $v\otimes w'+v'\otimes w$, of rank at most two. A nonzero $B(z)$ has rank at least three between those same two sums, from the three copies of $F(z)$. Hence a product in $S_A$ has zero left-right part and all of its off-diagonal parts between individual blocks vanish.

For $\ell$ wholly in $E_i$, the last condition gives $C(\ell)=E_i$. If $\ell$ has at least two nonzero block components, the equation in each pair of occupied blocks forces $\ell'_i$ proportional to $\ell_i$, with the proportionality scalars opposite on each pair. All components outside the occupied blocks must vanish. Thus $\dim C(\ell)\le1$, including in characteristic two. It follows that in projective linear-form space the locus $\dim C(\ell)\ge2$ is exactly the union of the twelve linear subspaces $\mathbf P(E_i)$. The components are defined over $k$ and their dimensions distinguish them. Any linear congruence between two such systems preserves the labeled blocks.

The remaining congruence condition says that each matrix-pair change of bases carries its slice $A(z)$, or its row $F(z)$, to the target slice with one common invertible substitution $z\mapsto bz$. The first matrix pair therefore recovers an ordinary $3$-tensor isomorphism. Indeed the intersection of the slice kernels and the sum of the slice images are intrinsic. Restrict the padded pair maps to the images and the corresponding quotients by the kernels, then extend to the unpadded spaces, whose complement dimensions agree. Conversely repeat any given tensor transporter on the three matrix pairs. Its common substitution $b:Z\to Z$ acts on each row pair by leaving the target scalar space fixed and taking the contragredient on $Z^*$. This realizes the same change of slice parameter. Extending by identities on the padding gives a congruence of the quadratic systems. All steps are polynomial in the dense tensor input, and the coefficient formulas commute with field extension.

## 2. Intrinsic Fano geometry

For any basepoint-free $r$-dimensional system $S\subset\operatorname{Sym}^2 E^\vee$ with $m=\dim E\ge4$ and $r>m$, let
$$
 X_S=\{(x,[q])\in\mathbf P(E)\times\mathbf P(S):q(x)=0\}.
$$
The first projection presents it as $\mathbf P(K_S)$ for the kernel of the surjective evaluation $S\otimes\mathcal O\to\mathcal O(2)$ on $\mathbf P(E)$, in the convention parametrizing lines. Thus $X_S$ is smooth, geometrically integral and $k$-rational. Adjunction gives $-K_{X_S}=(m-2)H_x+(r-1)H_y$, where $H_x,H_y$ are the restrictions of the two hyperplane classes. Both coefficients are positive, so the anticanonical line bundle is ample. The projective-bundle Picard formula gives the basis $H_x,H_y$, including after every field extension.

The nef cone is exactly their nonnegative span. The classes are globally generated. A line in a fiber of the first projection tests the coefficient of $H_y$. Over an algebraic closure, any fiber of the second projection is a quadric in $\mathbf P^{m-1}$, contains an integral curve, and tests the coefficient of $H_x$. This proves both converse inequalities. Each ray is intrinsic and has its own primitive integral generator. The restriction sequences from the ambient product show that the complete linear systems have dimensions of sections $m,r$, respectively. For the first sequence the kernel term is $\mathcal O(-1,-1)$, and for the second it is $\mathcal O(-2,0)$. Their groups in degrees zero and one vanish by projective-space cohomology and Künneth. The unequal numbers $m,r$ distinguish the rays. Consequently an abstract isomorphism identifies these line bundles and their complete linear systems over $k$.

The product of the two maps is the given inclusion in $\mathbf P(E)\times\mathbf P(S)$. Every abstract isomorphism therefore comes uniquely from a pair of ambient projectivities. Since the equation is a Cartier divisor of fixed bidegree, the projectivities preserve its equation up to a scalar. Reading the coefficients of the independent $y$-variables now says exactly that the first projectivity transports $S$ to the target quadratic system. Its projectivity in the second factor is determined by its action on $S$. Conversely a congruence of the quadratic systems gives such a pair. Both projective transformations over $k$ lift to linear maps over $k$.

The systems for every fixed tensor format give a family over affine tensor space. The squares of the fixed coordinate variables make the evaluation map surjective relatively over that affine base, so the incidence hypersurfaces form one smooth projective family. The Chow ring over any field follows from the projective-bundle formula, with $c(K_S)=(1+2H_x)^{-1}$. Hence the same integral Chow ring and the same anticanonical class in it occur for every tensor of the format. The Tate motive and the point counts follow from the same projective-bundle formula. In particular, over a finite field of size $q$, the number of points over its degree-$j$ extension is $(1+q^j+\cdots+q^{j(m-1)})(1+q^j+\cdots+q^{j(r-2)})$. The basic construction of a projective-bundle hypersurface is related to the Cayley trick used in Fano visitor results. The abstract isomorphism problem here recovers the full tensor relation. [Kiem–Kim–Lee–Lee on Fano visitors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00125) · [Stacks Project, projective-bundle formula](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02TX)

For a single-projective presentation use the Segre embedding. Multiply $q(x)=\sum_j y_jq_j(x)$ by each $y_i$, and express the resulting forms of bidegree $(2,2)$ as quadratics in the coordinates $x_a y_b$. Together with the Segre minors these cut out $X_S$ scheme-theoretically. On the product, some $y_i$ is invertible in a local trivialization of $\mathcal O(0,1)$, so the products generate exactly the ideal of the original equation. This presentation has polynomially many quadratic terms and equations at polynomial coefficient bit cost.

For the converse reduction on the promised product presentation, associate to $S$ the commutative unital algebra
$$
 k\oplus E^\vee\oplus(\operatorname{Sym}^2 E^\vee/S),
$$
with the displayed graded multiplication and all products above degree two zero. Its radical and radical square recover the degree-one and degree-two spaces and their multiplication. The kernel of the resulting symmetric-square multiplication map is precisely $S$. Algebra isomorphism is therefore exactly quadratic-system congruence, and any congruence extends to an algebra isomorphism. The multiplication tensor is a polynomial-time encoding of isomorphism of unital associative algebras, because any isotopy between them normalizes at the units to an algebra isomorphism. This proves the converse in the natural presentation with the basepoint-free promise. [Grochow–Qiao on TI-completeness](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

## 3. All separable forms and the whole local genus

Work over a field $K$, with $K_s$ its separable closure, and use continuous Galois cohomology on separable-closure points. The arguments over fields above show that the automorphism group of the constructed $X_{S_A}$ on $K_s$-points is $H/\mathbb G_m$, where $H$ consists of the twelve block linear transformations preserving $S_A$ and $\mathbb G_m$ is the common scalar on the twelve spaces of linear forms. All equations and this identification are Galois-equivariant. Indeed each field-point automorphism extends uniquely to ambient projectivities, and the first projectivity determines the second. The consecutive dimensions of the two first blocks are relatively prime, so a product of integer powers of their determinant characters restricts to the identity on the common scalar. This splits the central scalar subgroup. Hilbert 90 gives $H^1(K,H)\simeq H^1(K,H/\mathbb G_m)$.

For clarity, replace the source of $A$ by its quotient by the common kernel, and the target by the sum of all images, leaving the slice space $Z$ unchanged. Write $G$ for the ordinary stabilizer of this two-mode concise core, including its action on $Z$. The original tensor stabilizer $G_A$ projects split onto $G$ by choosing complements. Its kernel has quotient a product of complement general linear groups and kernel a vector group of off-diagonal shears. A section of $G$ acts trivially on the complement quotient and linearly on the shears. Its Galois twists therefore have trivial $H^1$, so $H^1(K,G_A)\simeq H^1(K,G)$. This also covers zero source or target cores.

For $H$, make the same core operation on each of the six row or matrix pairs individually. The resulting projection to a group $H_{\rm core}$ has a section that acts by identity on all chosen complements. Its kernel is again built from free complement general linear groups and vector groups, with the same twisting argument. Projection $H_{\rm core}\to G$ reads the first copy and the common slice map, and splits by copying the core maps and lifting the slice map to the universal rows. Its kernel is a product of strict stabilizers of separate row or matrix tuples with the slice parameter fixed. Each strict stabilizer is the unit group of the finite-dimensional associative endomorphism algebra
$$
 \{(P,Q):P A(z)=A(z)Q\ \text{for all }z\}
$$
for the corresponding tuple. Composition is componentwise. A unit is exactly a pair with both maps invertible. Conjugation by the chosen section acts on this algebra by algebra automorphisms, since the section preserves the tuple space up to its common slice change. Therefore a twist of each unit group is again the unit group of a finite-dimensional $K$-algebra.

For any finite-dimensional $K$-algebra $D$, $H^1(K,D^\times)=1$. One proof interprets a cocycle as a form of the free rank-one right $D$-module by semilinear descent. Over an infinite $K$, the elements generating the descended module constitute a nonempty Zariski-open set, because the determinant of the map $D\to M$ defined by an element of $M$ is nonzero after base change to $K_s$. The open set has a $K$-point by affine-space density. For finite $K$ use Lang's theorem, since $D^\times$ is the invertibility open set of an affine space and hence geometrically connected. Consequently the split projection $H_{\rm core}\to G$ induces a bijection on $H^1$. Each of the three split comparisons just used follows from the same cocycle argument: above a fixed quotient cocycle, compare with its section and twist the kernel by that cocycle. The kernel's vanishing $H^1$ gives a single cohomology class above it. [Lang's theorem, finite-field case](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Lang-Algebraic_Groups_Over_Finite_Fields.pdf)

The map $G_A\to H$ given by repeating the original maps on the three relevant pairs and acting trivially on the additional coordinates is compatible with the two projections to $G$. It therefore induces a bijection on $H^1(K,-)$. Combining this with the split scalar gives a natural bijection between $H^1(K,G_A)$ and $H^1(K,\operatorname{Aut}(X_{S_A}))$, commuting with extension of $K$. Hilbert 90 for the three general linear groups says every class on the tensor side is represented by a tensor over $K$ of the original format, geometrically conjugate over $K_s$. For the varieties, two separably split forms give the same cocycle class exactly when they are isomorphic over $K$. Every class is realized because the construction applied to the corresponding rational tensor gives the form. Thus each such form of the Fano variety is rational and again has the stated projective-bundle presentation. Over a perfect field this accounts for all geometric forms.

For a number field, the bijections on forms commute with localization at every completion. An isomorphism over a completion places the pair in one geometric isomorphism class. For the varieties this follows by finite-type parametrization of the graphs with the Hilbert polynomial of the given isomorphism. Thus the entire everywhere-local genus transfers. The explicit prior construction realizes any finite abelian group as the based local genus of a rational tensor through Tate–Shafarevich groups of tori. Applying the Fano construction realizes the same genus by smooth rational Fano varieties of geometric Picard rank exactly two. Likewise every modular isomorphism profile previously prescribed for a rational tensor pair transfers to a smooth rational Fano pair, simultaneously over all finite extensions of the good residue fields and with identical point counts within the chosen format. ## Evidence and priority

The earlier smooth blowup construction supplied recovery of two projective-coordinate maps from intrinsic geometry and the split-stabilizer approach to forms. The basepoint-free quadratic system, projective-bundle construction, and unit-group cohomology give a new route into the Fano locus with Picard rank two. Exact finite checks confirmed the dimensions of $C(\ell)$ for pure-block and mixed representatives in characteristics two, three and five. In the test all twelve block dimensions were distinct, from 3 to 14. The mixed cases included matched matrix blocks, matched row blocks, unmatched pairs and a representative supported on all twelve blocks. Federated DAG probes for projectivization, projective space, symmetric powers, canonical divisors, Chow rings, Hilbert 90 and Segre maps each queried 103 registered source catalogs. These probes supplied discovery evidence for this informal proof. A decidability result in that model is compatible with the complexity reduction here. The proposed contribution is the explicit all-field tensor equivalence in the Picard-rank-two Fano family and its arithmetic forms comparison. Publication priority remains unestablished.
