# Tensor isomorphism in groups with identical commuting relations

**Theorem.** Fix a prime $p$ and an integer $h\ge 1$. Arbitrary ordered $3$-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb F_p$ is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to group isomorphism on the compact presentations constructed here. Each output is a special nilpotent group of class two, of exponent $p$ for odd $p$ and exponent four for $p=2$. At a fixed input format, every group in the family has exactly the same:

- underlying labeled commuting relation, including on noncentral elements,
- multiset of conjugacy class sizes and multiset of complex irreducible character degrees,
- distribution of marked subgroups generated by ordered $h$-tuples.

In particular, the numbers of homomorphisms and embeddings from **every** finite group generated by at most $h$ elements agree throughout the family. The construction is polynomial in the dense tensor input size and $h$ in unary. For cubic side $n$ one may take $h=n$, with a faithful matrix representation of polynomial dimension. The compact input consists of explicit bilinear structure constants, or the corresponding displayed unipotent matrix generators. The group order can be exponential. The theorem is stated with compact presentations.

## 1. Make tensor isomorphism survive a constant-rank promise

View a $3$-tensor of format $a\times b\times c$ as a $c$-tuple $(A_i)$ of $a$-by-$b$ matrices. Dimensions are positive. Append the same Kronecker representation to every input: replace $A_i$ by $A_i\oplus e_i^{\mathsf T}$, where $e_i^{\mathsf T}$ is a $1$-by-$c$ coordinate row. The new tuple is linearly independent, and the common row-tuple summand transports under any invertible slice change by a column change. Reflection follows by fixing the slice changer of a proposed isomorphism and cancelling the shared row summand by Krull–Schmidt for finite-dimensional representations of the $c$-arrow Kronecker quiver. Thus the resulting subspace $C\subseteq U\otimes V$, with $\dim U=a+1,\dim V=b+c,\dim C=c$, retains exactly the original equivalence.

More generally, replicate a matrix space $D$ by replacing each of its elements $A$ by $A\otimes I_s$ with the **same coefficient choice in all copies**. The resulting subspace is denoted $D^{[s]}$. Its left–right equivalence is exactly that of $D$. Choose the induced invertible slice change from any equivalence of the replicated spaces. For that fixed slice change the original tuples are fixed-arrow Kronecker representations. Their $s$-fold direct sums are isomorphic, so their indecomposable multiplicities multiplied by $s$ agree. Cancel the multiplicity $s$. This also explains why the cancellation preserves the single shared slice change. Ranks of nonzero matrices are multiplied by $s$. All assertions hold over every extension field. [General tensor reductions and Kronecker representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

Put $U_1=U\otimes k^2,V_1=V\otimes k^2$, where $k=\mathbb F_p$, and form

$$
B_1:U_1\times V_1\longrightarrow W_1=(U_1\otimes V_1)/C^{[2]},\qquad
B_1(x,y)=x\otimes y\bmod C^{[2]}.
$$

This tensor carries the same isomorphism relation as $C^{[2]}$, since isomorphisms of a surjective linear map from a tensor product are exactly left–right maps preserving its kernel, with the quotient map induced. No nonzero rank-one tensor lies in that kernel. Therefore every contraction $B_1(x,-)$ with $x\ne0$ is injective, and so is every $B_1(-,y)$ with $y\ne0$. This works over the algebraic closure. The matrix space $M=\{B_1(x,-):x\in U_1\}\subseteq W_1\otimes V_1^*$ is isomorphic in the left–right sense exactly when the original tensors are isomorphic. Its nonzero matrices all have rank $\dim V_1$, and its dimension is $\dim U_1$.

Consequently arbitrary TI over any field already reduces to matrix-space equivalence on spaces with geometrically constant full column rank. Replacing each $B_1(x,-)$ by the square-zero operator $(w,y)\mapsto(B_1(x,y),0)$ on $W_1\oplus V_1$ gives the same conclusion for matrix-space conjugacy of spaces with every nonzero matrix of the same Jordan type and all pairwise matrix products zero. The common kernel is $W_1$. A conjugator therefore induces the two left–right maps, and any valid left–right maps lift block diagonally. [Constant-rank matrix spaces and their vector bundles](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/am/pii/S0001870823005510)

## 2. Build the groups and recover the tensor

Choose $s\ge3$ with $s\dim V_1>h$. Put $X=W_1\otimes k^s$, $Y=V_1^*\otimes k^s$, $K=M^{[s]}\subseteq X\otimes Y$, and $Z=(X\otimes Y)/K$. Write $\pi:X\otimes Y\to Z$ for the quotient. Thus every nonzero matrix in $K$ has rank $s\dim V_1>h$ and at least three. The dimensions $m=\dim X,n=\dim Y$ are distinct and at least two. Indeed $\dim W_1=4(a+1)(b+c)-c>2(b+c)=\dim V_1$. All dimensions and the nonzero rank distribution of $K$ depend only on the original format and $h$. For cubic side $a=b=c=N$, the constant choice $s=3$ allows all $h\le12N-1$, with faithful matrix dimension $O(N^3)$.

On $X\times Y\times Z$ define the product

$$
 (x,y,z)(x',y',z')=(x+x',y+y',z+z'+\pi(x\otimes y')).
$$

Bilinearity gives associativity. The commutator is central with value $\pi(x\otimes y'-x'\otimes y)$, up to the choice of commutator convention. The derived group is all of $Z$, since simple tensors span $X\otimes Y$. The center is exactly $Z$, by the absence of rank-one tensors in $K$. The $r$-th power has coordinates $(rx,ry,rz+\binom r2\pi(x\otimes y))$. For odd $p$ this gives exponent $p$, while for $p=2$ nonzero $x,y$ give elements of order four. The Frattini subgroup also equals $Z$, since the derived group is $Z$ and all $p$-th powers are in $Z$. Thus the groups are special and of class two. A faithful matrix realization acts by left multiplication on $(x',y',z',1)$, homogenized on $X\oplus Y\oplus Z\oplus k$. Matrices for the basis elements of $X,Y,Z$ generate. Alternatively the multiplication rule is an explicit compact presentation. [Grochow–Qiao on the matrix-group input model](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

Two group elements commute exactly when $x\otimes y'=x'\otimes y$ before applying $\pi$. The difference has rank at most two. This gives literally the same commuting relation on the coordinate labels throughout a fixed-format family. If $x\ne0,y=0$, its centralizer modulo $Z$ is $X$, and its class size is $p^n$. If $x=0,y\ne0$, its class size is $p^m$. If both are nonzero, the centralizer modulo $Z$ is the line through $(x,y)$, since equality of two nonzero simple tensors forces common scalar multiples here. The class size is $p^{m+n-1}$. These three numbers are distinct. Thus the subspaces $X,Y$ of the intrinsic quotient by the center are recovered by class sizes. An abstract group isomorphism induces linear maps on $X,Y,Z$ and must intertwine the commutator. Restricting one argument to $X$ and the other to $Y$ shows it intertwines $\pi$. Conversely any such three linear maps preserve the displayed multiplication. Hence group isomorphism is exactly left–right equivalence of $K$, and replication cancellation and Section 1 recover the original TI.

For the reverse computational direction, the displayed compact data give the tensor of $\pi$, so comparison reduces to 3TI without enumerating the group. When the data are presented as matrices, the displayed block coordinates and ordered generators are supplied. The reverse statement gives no recovery algorithm for arbitrary independently scrambled matrix generators. Pairs of distinct original formats can be rejected before constructing a fixed no pair in a many-one reduction. Thus preservation of the listed invariants is a same-format assertion, and the reduction can map every input pair within one such family.

The alternating map $(x,y),(x',y')\mapsto\pi(x\otimes y'-x'\otimes y)$ also works over any field without forming a finite group. Its contraction ranks recover the two subspaces in the same way and give the three fixed nonzero ranks $n,m,m+n-1$. Thus the corresponding rank data are fixed even within a tensor-isomorphism-complete class of alternating maps. For odd prime $p$, the coordinate change $z\mapsto z-\tfrac12\pi(x\otimes y)$ also makes powers simply multiply all three coordinates by the exponent, so in these coordinates the directed power relation and the commuting relation are simultaneously fixed.

## 3. Equal counts for all $h$-generated probes

Fix the quotient coordinates $(x_i,y_i)$ of an ordered $h$-tuple. Set $D=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_h\rangle\otimes\langle y_1,\ldots,y_h\rangle$. Every element of $D$ has rank at most $h$. Both quotient maps in a compared pair are injective on $D$. The map $\pi(d)\mapsto\pi'(d)$ therefore extends to an isomorphism $L:Z\to Z'$, since the ambient center dimensions agree. The coordinate map $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y,Lz)$, restricted to $\langle x_i\rangle\times\langle y_i\rangle\times Z$, is a group isomorphism onto its counterpart, by the product formula. It induces a bijection on central coordinates of the ordered tuples with the fixed quotient coordinates. Hence the two lists of ordered tuples have identical marked generated subgroup types. Summing over all quotient coordinates proves equality of the distributions.

For a finite group with at most $h$ generators, choose an ordered generating tuple and pad it with identities. Whether a target tuple defines a homomorphism, or defines an injection, depends only on the marked isomorphism type of its generated subgroup. This proves equality of both counts for each such source group separately. The proof does not require computing any of these counts to encode the tensor.

## 4. Equal character-degree distributions

Use a fixed nontrivial complex additive character of $\mathbb F_p$ to identify central characters with linear functionals in $Z^*$. Under the quotient, $Z^*$ is the matrix space $K^\perp$. If its corresponding matrix has rank $j$, the commutator form on $X\oplus Y$ after applying the functional is the block alternating pairing with that matrix as the off-diagonal block. Its radical has dimension $m+n-2j$.

The complex group algebra summand for the chosen central character is a twisted group algebra on the additive quotient $X\oplus Y$. Its center consists precisely of the span of the twisted basis elements indexed by the radical of the commutator pairing. Indeed conjugation by each basis element scales each other basis element by the applicable commutator phase. A basis element is fixed by all conjugations exactly when its index is in the radical. The algebra is semisimple and free over this center with a basis indexed by the quotient by the radical. Each simple matrix block consequently has dimension $p^{2j}$. There are $p^{m+n-2j}$ such blocks, so this central character contributes exactly that many irreducibles of degree $p^j$.

The rank distribution of $K^\perp$ is determined by the rank distribution of $K$. For clarity, the number of rank-$j$ matrices in $K^\perp$ is

$$
 \frac{1}{|K|}\sum_{A\in K}\sum_{\operatorname{rank} B=j}\psi(\langle A,B\rangle),
$$

by additive-character orthogonality for the trace pairing. The inner sum depends only on the rank of $A$, by applying the contragredient row and column changes to the set of rank-$j$ matrices. All nonzero elements of $K$ have the same format-dependent rank. The formula fixes the full dual rank distribution throughout the family and therefore fixes every character-degree multiplicity. [Delsarte on the bilinear-forms association scheme](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(78)90015-5)

## Scope and provenance

This result combines the project's support-preserving padding and tensor quotient constructions with replication cancellation, the alternating-map description of class-two groups and the rank-metric Fourier identity. Classical reductions already show that ordinary TI is complete for appropriate algebra and group isomorphism problems. Nonisomorphic groups with the same commuting and power graphs were also previously constructed. The contribution here is a polynomial-size TI-complete family with all the stated invariants simultaneously fixed, including bounded-generator marked subgroup distributions and character-degree multiplicities. The complexity statement uses the displayed compact group presentations. Publication priority for these additional restrictions is unestablished. The proof is informal.
