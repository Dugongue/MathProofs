# Number-field norms occur in determinant-one isomorphism of regular binary pencils

**Theorem.** Given a number field $K/\mathbb Q$ of degree $d\ge2$ by an irreducible polynomial and a rational unit $c$, a deterministic polynomial-bit-time construction produces two $3d\times3d\times2$ rational tensors with the following properties.

- Their pencils are $A(x,y)=xI-yT$ and $B(x,y)=D A(x,y)D^{-1}$, with $\det D=c^{-1}$.
- Their common determinant is squarefree and has trivial projective stabilizer over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$.
- They are isomorphic under the three special linear groups over any extension $F/\mathbb Q$ exactly when $c$ lies in the norm image of $(K\otimes_{\mathbb Q} F)^\times$ in $F^\times$.

Thus even a supplied ordinary isomorphism, regular semisimplicity, and the absence of any projective symmetries leave the full rational norm membership problem. In particular there are $12\times12\times2$ rational pencils of this kind that are determinant-one isomorphic over every completion of $\mathbb Q$ and inequivalent over $\mathbb Q$. The exact reduction transfers the decision problem for norm membership and its local–global failures to this restricted tensor family.

There is also a converse algorithmic description. On all rational regular binary pencils with at least three distinct geometric determinant roots, ordinary TI is decidable in deterministic polynomial bit time. SL-TI reduces in deterministic polynomial time to a polynomial disjunction of weighted number-field norm-image tests. For fixed slice dimension and a finite projective determinant stabilizer, ordinary TI is in deterministic polynomial bit time for any rational regular square pencil. SL-TI is also in deterministic polynomial bit time on the subclass with geometrically reduced determinant whose absolute components are all defined over $\mathbb Q$. This includes reducible ternary determinants with rational components in general position.

## 1. Fixing the slice map

Use the pencil transporter convention $P A(Rx)=B(x)Q$, with one invertible matrix in each mode. Inverses and transposes in switching to coefficient tensors preserve determinant one. For a projectivity $[R]$ necessary to any transporter, the determinant matching condition is $f_A(Rx)=z f_B(x)$ for a nonzero scalar $z$.

When the projective determinant stabilizer is finite, enumerate the rational candidates $[R]$ in fixed dimension using the affine charts $R_{ij}=1$, the coefficient equations for determinant matching, and an auxiliary inverse variable for $\det R$. These systems have a fixed number of variables, degree and coefficient height polynomial in the input length, and finite geometric solution sets. Obtain a univariate elimination polynomial for each coordinate, factor over $\mathbb Q$, and check the Cartesian product of the rational roots. The fixed-variable Gröbner degree and ideal membership bounds give polynomial bit time by exact linear algebra in the bounded monomial space. They also give a deterministic polynomial-bit-time test for the existence of a nonidentity projective automorphism over the algebraic closure in this setting, by adding one inverse constraint for a nonzero off-diagonal entry or diagonal difference. A binary divisor with at least three distinct geometric points has finite projective stabilizer, since a projectivity is determined by an ordered triple of points. [Fixed-variable degree bounds](https://doi.org/10.1137/0219053) · [Ideal membership bounds](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~maschenbrenner/pdf/ideal2.pdf) · [Rational factorization](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01457454)

For a candidate $R$, select a rational linear combination $x_0$ where $A_0=A(Rx_0)$ and $B_0=B(x_0)$ are invertible. For fixed slice dimension a grid of size polynomial in the degree finds one. Write $A_i$ for the slices of $A(Rx)$, and $B_i$ for those of $B(x)$. The strict equation is equivalent to simultaneous similarity

$$
P A_i A_0^{-1} = B_i B_0^{-1} P,\qquad Q=B_0^{-1}P A_0.
$$

Over $\mathbb Q$, the finite-algebra module isomorphism algorithm decides this and returns a similarity in polynomial bit time. For two slices one may instead use the ordinary constructive rational canonical form algorithm, since the tuple is generated by identity and the matrix of one independent slice. If $P_0,Q_0$ solve the strict equations, every strict solution has $P=P_0U$, $Q=Q_0A_0^{-1}U A_0$, where $U$ is an invertible element of the source simultaneous centralizer. Thus both determinants are multiplied by the same number. In the SL test, first normalize $R$ by a scalar to determinant one, using exact rational roots, and try all possibilities. For each resulting strict solution the remaining conditions are $\det P_0=\det Q_0$ and membership of $1/\det P_0$ in the determinant image of the centralizer units. [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski on module isomorphism](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf)

## 2. Exact norm images for binary pencils

For a binary pencil a strict centralizer is the automorphism group of a finite torsion $\mathbb Q[t]$-module. Let $m_{f,\ell}$ be its multiplicities of summands $\mathbb Q[t]/f^\ell$, for monic irreducible $f$, and put $K_f=\mathbb Q[t]/f$. Its determinant image is exactly

$$
\prod_{(f,\ell):\,m_{f,\ell}>0} N_{K_f/\mathbb Q}(K_f^\times)^\ell .
$$

Indeed the primary decomposition separates the $f$. On one primary module, use its $f$-adic filtration. At level $j$, the pieces from summands of length $\ell>j$ are $K_f^{m_{f,\ell}}$. A homomorphism from a shorter block to a longer block is divisible by the difference of their powers of $f$. In the quotient at level $j$ it is therefore zero between those summands in that direction. In increasing length order the layer matrix is block triangular, with a repeated diagonal block $U_{f,\ell}$ over $K_f$, once in each of the $\ell$ layers for that length. Its rational determinant is $N_{K_f/\mathbb Q}(\det_{K_f} U_{f,\ell})$ on each layer. Conversely one may prescribe these determinants independently by multiplying a single summand of each length by a lift of an arbitrary nonzero scalar of $K_f$, leaving the others unchanged.

Polynomial rational factorization and rational canonical form give these fields and weights explicitly. Together with Section 1 this proves the weighted norm upper reduction. In the fully split case $K_f=\mathbb Q$ the norm subgroup is just the rational powers of exponent $\gcd\{\ell:m_{f,\ell}>0\}$, and exact root extraction suffices. The ordinary-matrix norm calculation is classical. The next section arranges that all allowed projective changes of the binary slice variables leave exactly one arbitrary input field norm. [Regular semisimple conjugacy and its norm invariant](https://math.mit.edu/~rud/2025/papers/PRIMES2025_Orbital_Integrals.pdf)

## 3. A squarefree determinant with no geometric projective symmetries

Represent $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$ with $\alpha$ integral, which can be achieved by scaling the supplied generator. Pick a prime $p$ not dividing its polynomial discriminant. The first polynomially many small primes suffice, because there are at most the bit length of the discriminant many distinct prime divisors. At any place of $K$ above $p$, unramifiedness gives valuation one to $p$. Therefore $p$ is not a square in $K$, and $E=K(\sqrt p)$ has degree $2d$. Search for a primitive $\beta=\alpha+a\sqrt p$ with integer $a$. Two of the $2d$ conjugate expressions can agree for at most one $a$, unless the two expressions are identically equal, which distinct embeddings exclude. A polynomial-size search therefore suffices. Test primitivity by squarefreeness of the degree-$2d$ characteristic polynomial of multiplication by $\beta$. All multiplication matrices and tested polynomial coefficients have polynomial bit length. Let $S$ be the $2d$ roots of this polynomial and let $U$ be the $d$ conjugates of $\alpha$.

We need an integer $t$ of polynomial size such that $S\cup(U+t)$ has no nonidentity projective stabilizer over the algebraic closure. First regard $t$ as an indeterminate. For four distinct points their cross ratio is

$$
[a,b;c,e]=\frac{(a-c)(b-e)}{(a-e)(b-c)}.
$$

Its limit at $t=\infty$ is in $\{0,1,\infty\}$ exactly when two points came from each block. Indeed, if all four are from one block the cross ratio is constant and nondegenerate. If three are from a block, the point in the other block tends to infinity in the coordinates of the first block, which still gives a nondegenerate cross ratio. For two in each block, one obtains the limit $1$ exactly when the first pair belongs to one block and the second pair to the other. The other pairings yield $0$ or $\infty$. Cross-ratio preservation therefore preserves the unordered two-block partition. In detail, any pair from one block extends to an ordered quadruple with limit $1$ by selecting a pair from the other. The two image pairs must be in opposite blocks. The block sizes $2d$ and $d$ differ, so neither block can be interchanged.

Any stabilizing projectivity over $\overline{\mathbb Q}(t)$ is now constant because it takes three distinct points of $S$ to points of $S$. If it maps $\alpha_0+t$ to $\alpha_1+t$ for some $\alpha_i\in U$, this symbolic identity forces it to be the translation by $\alpha_1-\alpha_0$. A nonzero translation cannot preserve a finite set in characteristic zero. The identity is thus the only generic symmetry.

This argument gives a deterministic polynomial-bit-time specialization. Fix a triple in $S$. There are at most $n(n-1)(n-2)$ ordered image triples among the $n=3d$ point functions. Each triple defines one candidate Möbius matrix with entries of degree at most two in $t$, since the source triple is constant and the target triple is affine in $t$. For each candidate other than the identity triple, at least one source point has no symbolic image among the $n$ target functions. Otherwise the candidate would preserve the entire union generically. Equating its image with one target gives a nonzero polynomial of degree at most four by cross multiplication. So at most $4n$ integer values are bad for each candidate. For rational $t$ the two blocks are disjoint because their irreducible polynomials have different degrees. Also the points within each block remain distinct. Test $4n^4+1$ successive integers by the geometric automorphism test in Section 1. The bound guarantees a successful integer. This procedure uses the defining polynomials throughout, with no need to represent a splitting field.

## 4. Realizing exactly the input norm problem

Take $T$ to be the companion matrix of the product of the two distinct irreducible polynomials with root sets $S$ and $U+t$. Its determinant pencil is squarefree and has trivial projective automorphism group by construction. Its centralizer is $\mathbb Q[T]\cong K\times E$, because the companion is cyclic. This description commutes with arbitrary extension of scalars. The determinant image of the centralizer units over $F$ is

$$
N_{K\otimes F/F}((K\otimes F)^\times)\,
N_{E\otimes F/F}((E\otimes F)^\times)
=N_{K\otimes F/F}((K\otimes F)^\times).
$$

The second factor lies in the first by transitivity of the norm for finite étale algebras. A determinant-one transporter between $A(x,y)=xI-yT$ and $B=DAD^{-1}$ must have $R=\lambda I$ by projective rigidity and $\lambda^2=1$. The coefficient of $x$ gives $Q=\lambda P$. The coefficient of $y$ then gives $P=DU$ for an invertible centralizer element $U$. Thus determinant one on $P$ implies $\det U=c$. Conversely choose such a $U$ whenever $c$ lies in the norm image, and take $P=Q=DU$ and $R=I$. This proves the equivalence over every extension, with input and output bit lengths polynomially related.

In fact one obtains the complete splitting into determinant-one orbits of the ordinary orbit restricted to this exact determinant polynomial. Over $F$ the orbit set is canonically based by $A$ and identified with

$$
F^\times/N_{K\otimes F/F}((K\otimes F)^\times).
$$

Every pencil $B$ in the stated ordinary orbit has, after absorbing the scalar slice map, a strict transporter $P A=B Q$. Equality of determinant polynomials gives $\det P=\det Q$, so $B$ and $P A P^{-1}$ differ by an allowed determinant-one right multiplication. Representatives therefore have form $DAD^{-1}$ for arbitrary $\det D\in F^\times$. Two such representatives coincide under SL-TI precisely when the ratio of their determinants is a norm, by the same calculation.

For a concrete Hasse failure, use $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt{13})$ and $c=3$. Its norm equation has a solution over every completion and none over $\mathbb Q$. One can use $E=K(\sqrt5)$, take the roots of $\alpha=\sqrt{-3}+\sqrt{13}$ shifted by $t=1$, and let $\beta=\alpha+\sqrt5$. The two monic polynomials are

$$
(X-1)^4-20(X-1)^2+256,\qquad
X^8-60X^6+1262X^4+1620X^2+32761 .
$$

The squarefree degree-twelve product has trivial geometric projective stabilizer. An exact cross-ratio control enumerated all $12\cdot11\cdot10$ images of three selected roots in the eight-dimensional multiquadratic field. The identity was the only triple admitting images of all the remaining roots. The general reduction already proves existence of a successful shift independently of this control. The explicit pair takes $D=\operatorname{diag}(1/3,1,\ldots,1)$. A short direct check for rational projectivities is also available: Galois equivariance forces a putative map to preserve separately the four shifted and the eight unshifted roots. On the shifted quartet its permutation commutes with the regular Klein-four Galois action, so its only possible nonidentity maps are $x\mapsto 2-x$ and $x\mapsto 1\pm16/(x-1)$. The sum of the eight unshifted roots is zero, while those three maps change it to $16$ and $8\mp16f_E'(1)/f_E(1)$, all nonzero. Thus the arithmetic failure follows already from an exact hand-checked projective obstruction. [Kedlaya, Hasse norm counterexample](https://kskedlaya.org/cft/sec_ideles-cohom2.html)

## 5. A polynomial-bit-time extension to reducible ternary determinants

For any fixed slice dimension, suppose that the projective stabilizer of the determinant is finite. Section 1 already gives a polynomial-bit-time ordinary TI algorithm. Suppose also that the determinant is a product of pairwise distinct, absolutely irreducible polynomials $f_i\in\mathbb Q[x]$. The simultaneous centralizer algebra $C$ of $A(x)A_0^{-1}$ is the degree-zero endomorphism algebra of its graded cokernel module. This follows by lifting the degree-zero generators and then the degree-one relations in its injective linear presentation. The two matrices of any endomorphism pair agree because the normalized matrix at $x_0$ is identity.

The adjugate injects the cokernel into a shift of a finite free module over $S/(\prod_i f_i)$. The map to the product of its generic fibers is therefore injective. At the generic point of each $f_i$, the determinant has valuation one and the cokernel has rank one over the corresponding function field. Consequently $C$ embeds in the product of those function fields and is reduced and commutative. Its projection in each factor is a finite-dimensional unital domain over $\mathbb Q$, hence a field finite over $\mathbb Q$. Geometric integrality forces that projected field to be $\mathbb Q$. For example, a nontrivial extension inside the function field would split into several copies after extension to $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, contradicting geometric integrality. Thus $C$ is a split reduced algebra $\mathbb Q^s$.

Compute $C$ by linear equations. A separating element is found by testing the integer parameters of a moment-curve combination of a basis of $C$. Each of the at most $\binom{s}{2}$ differences of characters on that combination is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $s-1$, so $s^3+1$ trials suffice. A successful operator has a squarefree, split minimal polynomial of degree $s=\dim C$, giving a direct recognition criterion even without factoring the determinant. Let $d_j$ be its eigenspace dimensions, computed by linear elimination. Every unit in $C$ is scalar independently on these spaces. The exact determinant image is $(\mathbb Q^\times)^{\gcd_j d_j}$, since the individual images are the $d_j$-th powers and Bézout combines the exponents. Use the matching algorithm of Section 1 with rational root extraction.

In particular generic line arrangements, including simple normal-crossing arrangements of at least four rational lines in the projective plane, give a reducible determinant class in deterministic polynomial bit time. The action on the set of lines is finite and the pointwise stabilizer of four lines in dual projective frame position is trivial. The reduced-component argument also applies to unions having nonlinear rational components. This extends the previous integral ternary result by changing the algebra of a strict transporter from a single scalar to a product of independent rational scalars. ## Scope and prior work

The componentwise norm formula uses ordinary determinant characters and rational canonical modules. The contribution is preserving exactly an arbitrary field norm while eliminating every geometric projective slice symmetry, together with the explicit three-mode special-linear Hasse failure. The closest sources found concern matrix conjugacy norms, tensor isomorphism over commutative rings, and geometric presentations of determinant curves. The Hasse norm counterexample itself is classical. Publication priority for the exact binary tensor reduction remains unestablished, and the argument is informal. The reduction concerns rational SL-TI in a regular two-slice format. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun on rings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00735) · [Classical group actions and Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

Federated probes for reduced norms, rational canonical forms, determinant cosets, norm-one groups, module isomorphism and Smith normal form each reached 103 registered source catalogs. These located the prior endomorphism-norm material and canonical-form and Smith interfaces. The geometric rigidity and arithmetic composition are proved in the text.
