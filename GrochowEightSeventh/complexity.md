# Two characteristic boundaries for symmetric algebraic complexity

Both results use the independent row and column action \(\Gamma_n=S_n\times S_n\) on the variables of a square matrix. The source is Dawar, Dwivedi, Pago and Seppelt, *Depth Hierarchies for Symmetric Algebraic Circuits*, Definition 7.1 and Theorems 7.3 and 7.7. These are informal proofs. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.23541)

## 1. The permanent loses symmetric projection completeness in every positive characteristic

The source proves completeness of the permanent for symmetric Boolean-cube sums in characteristic zero. Under its Definition 7.6, a symmetric affine projection of the permanent consists of a matrix \(M(X)\) of affine forms such that for every \(\gamma\in\Gamma_n\) there are row and column permutations of \(M\) taking each entry to its \(\gamma\)-translate. The dimension of \(M\) is unrestricted, and the choices of row and column permutations need not be coherent. [Definition 7.6 and Theorem 7.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.23541)

**Theorem.** Let \(K\) have characteristic \(p>0\) and \(n>p\). The invariant \(F_n(X)=1+\prod_{i,j=1}^n x_{ij}\) has a depth-two symmetric circuit with \(O(n^2)\) gates and wires. It is not a symmetric affine projection of the permanent in any dimension. Hence the characteristic-zero completeness theorem fails over every field of positive characteristic.

**Proof.** Choose \(P=C_p\leq \Gamma_n\) generated by a \(p\)-cycle on the rows and the identity on the columns. There are variable orbits of size \(1\) and \(p\). For each variable orbit \(O\), set \(z_O=\prod_{x\in O}x\). A \(P\)-fixed monomial is exactly a monomial in the \(z_O\).

For a \(P\)-invariant polynomial \(u\), let \(B(u)\) retain only its \(P\)-fixed monomials. This gives a ring map \(K[X]^P\to K[z_O]\). To see multiplicativity, fix a \(P\)-fixed monomial \(m\) and expand the coefficient of \(m\) in \(uv\) over pairs of monomials having product \(m\). On each \(P\)-orbit of pairs the coefficient product is constant. Nontrivial orbits have size divisible by \(p\), so only pairs of fixed monomials contribute.

Suppose \(M=M(X)\) is such a symmetric affine projection, with row and column sets \(R,C\). Let \(L\leq P\times S_R\times S_C\) be the group of triples \((\gamma,\alpha,\beta)\) satisfying \(M_{\alpha(i),\beta(j)}=\gamma M_{ij}\). It surjects onto \(P\). For a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup \(Q\) of \(L\), the image of \(Q\) in \(P\) has index dividing \([L:Q]\), so it is all of \(P\). The group \(Q\) acts on row-to-column bijections \(\sigma\) by \(\sigma\mapsto\beta\sigma\alpha^{-1}\). If \(m\) is a \(P\)-fixed monomial, its coefficient in \(\prod_iM_{i,\sigma(i)}\) is constant on each \(Q\)-orbit of bijections. Again, summands from nontrivial orbits cancel. The permanent is \(P\)-invariant, so its image under \(B\) is the image of the sum over \(Q\)-equivariant bijections.

For such a bijection, rows in an orbit of cardinality \(p^a\) must go to a column orbit of the same size. Split rows and columns into blocks by orbit cardinality. If any two corresponding block sizes differ, there are no equivariant bijections. Otherwise the sum over equivariant bijections factors as \(\prod_a G_a\), where \(G_a\) sums the products of affine entries over equivariant bijections on the two blocks with orbit size \(p^a\), and an empty block contributes \(1\). If one block has no such bijections its factor is zero. For each block bijection, the product over each individual row orbit \(I\) is \(P\)-invariant, since the projection \(Q\to P\) is surjective and permuting the factors does not change their product.

Expand the product over \(I\) by selecting a constant term or a variable term from each affine entry. Regard zero coefficient terms as available with weight zero. The terms giving a \(P\)-fixed monomial are permuted by \(Q\), with constant coefficient product on orbits. Only \(Q\)-fixed choices survive. A fixed choice is a \(Q\)-equivariant map from the transitive set \(I\) to the singleton constant symbol or to one variable orbit \(O\). In the latter case it is onto and all fibers have the same size \(p^{a-b}\), where \(|O|=p^b\). Thus the fixed monomial is \(z_O^{p^{a-b}}\), and \(a\ge b\).

Work now in \(D=K[z_O]/(z_O^p:O)\). By multiplicativity of \(B\), every factor \(B(G_a)\) in \(D\) depends only on the \(z_O\) with \(|O|=p^a\). Therefore
\[
B(\operatorname{per}M)=A((z_O)_{|O|=1})\,C((z_O)_{|O|=p})
\qquad\text{in }D ,
\]
absorbing other factors, which are constants, into \(A\). The same product form holds in the case with no equivariant bijections, when the image is zero. On the other hand \(B(F_n)=1+\prod_Oz_O\). Setting both blocks of variables to zero would force \(A(0)C(0)=1\). Setting just one block to zero then shows that each factor is constant in its own truncated polynomial ring. This contradicts the mixed monomial \(\prod_O z_O\), which is nonzero in \(D\) and uses both blocks. The circuit for \(F_n\) consists of one multiplication over the matrix variables followed by one addition of \(1\), with the evident row and column action. \(\square\)


## 2. Symmetric Boolean-cube sums omit matrix invariants in every positive characteristic

Theorem 7.3 identifies symmetric Boolean-cube sums with all matrix-symmetric polynomial families in characteristic zero. In Definition 7.1 the auxiliary variables may carry any permutation action of \(\Gamma_n\), and the complexity measure is the largest gate orbit of a joint-symmetric circuit. [Definition 7.1 and Theorem 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.23541)

**Theorem.** Over every field of characteristic \(p>0\) there is a matrix-invariant family of degree at most \(n^2\) outside \(\mathsf{symVNP}\). At infinitely many orders \(n\) every joint-symmetric Boolean-cube witness for this family requires a gate orbit of size \(n^{\Omega(\log n)}\), with an arbitrary finite number of auxiliary bits and an arbitrary action on them. The hidden constant can depend on \(p\).

**Matrices and circulation action.** Use the following parity gadget of Cai–Fürer–Immerman type on the oriented complete graph \(K_m\), with one orientation fixed for each edge. Let \(\partial:\mathbb F_p^{E(K_m)}\to\mathbb F_p^{[m]}\) be its oriented incidence map. Its image is the zero-sum subspace, by sending any required difference of two unit vectors along a path.

For a charge vector \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^m\), form a zero-one bipartite matrix with main rows \((v,a)\), where \(a\in\mathbb F_p^{\delta(v)}\) satisfies \(\sum_{e\ni v}\varepsilon_{v,e}a_e=\lambda_v\), with the signs from \(\partial\). Each main row block has \(D=p^{m-2}\) rows. For every edge \(e\) and \(t\in\mathbb F_p\) put in two column copies of \((e,t)\). A main row \((v,a)\) has a \(1\) in either copy of \((e,t)\) exactly when \(e\ni v\) and \(a_e=t\). Add one marker column per \(v\), adjacent to all main rows of its block and to \(v\) additional private leaf rows of degree one. Pad the shorter side with isolated labels to make a square matrix. For large \(m\), the common order is \(n=mD+m(m+1)/2\). Take matrices \(A\) with \(\lambda=0\) and \(A'\) with \(\lambda'_1=1\) and all remaining charges zero.

Each ordinary edge column has degree \(2D/p\le D\). Marker degrees \(D+v\) are distinct, and main row degrees are \(2(m-1)+1\), so any row-column isomorphism recognizes the marked row blocks and the edge columns between each pair. Consider the property that one can select one main row in each block such that the chosen rows from each pair of blocks have a common ordinary edge column. This says exactly that the chosen local vectors agree on each shared edge and are the restrictions of a single edge labeling with the given divergence. The property holds for \(A\) and fails for \(A'\). Thus the matrices are in different \(\Gamma_n\)-orbits.

The additive \(p\)-group \(P=\ker\partial\) acts on either matrix by translating edge values and translating each local vector by its incident edge values. It fixes marker and padding labels. It acts by even permutations on each side. For odd \(p\), each element has cycles of length \(1\) or \(p\). For \(p=2\), each nonzero translation within a main row block acts by \(D/2\) transpositions, an even number for \(m\ge4\), and any edge-state column swap occurs in both copies. Write \(H=A_n\times A_n\), so \(P\) embeds into \(H\) on either side of the comparison. We use the same name for its two representations. They can also be identified by choosing translations of the individual row spaces between the two charges, which conjugate the two actions.

**Transferring short tuples.** Put \(k=\lfloor m/4\rfloor\) and \(\Delta=\lambda'-\lambda\), with total charge \(1\). Any tuple of at most \(k\) row and column labels touches a set \(S\) of base vertices through its main rows and a set \(T\) of base edges through its ordinary columns. Protect all edges incident to \(S\) and all edges in \(T\). The unprotected edges on the complement of \(S\) form a connected graph. Indeed, deleting at most \(k\) edges from the complete graph on at least \(m-k\) vertices cannot disconnect it, because every nontrivial cut has at least \(m-k-1>k\) edges for large \(m\).

Choose a defect vertex \(d\notin S\) and an edge vector \(\xi\) satisfying \(\partial\xi=\Delta-\mathbf e_d\). Translate each column of the tuple by \(\xi_e\), each main row in block \(v\) by the restriction of \(\xi\) to \(\delta(v)\), and leave fixed labels unchanged. This preserves side, equality, and all matrix entries between labels in the tuple. With any other admissible choice \((d',\xi')\), the difference of the vectors has divergence supported on \(d,d'\) and of total zero. Subtract a vector with that divergence supported on an unprotected path between \(d\) and \(d'\), or subtract zero when \(d=d'\). The corrected difference belongs to \(P\) and acts as the original difference on the tuple. Translations also commute with the \(P\)-actions. Hence the construction provides a well-defined map on \(P\)-orbits of tuples. Interchanging the charges gives its inverse, as witnessed by negating a chosen \(\xi\). It commutes with restriction and permutation of tuple coordinates, since an admissible \(\xi\) for a tuple remains admissible for any subtuple.

For any fixed pair of tuples in corresponding orbits of length less than \(k\), there is a side-preserving bijection of the full row and column label sets that pairs all one-point extensions in corresponding orbits. By adding a circulation, take \(\xi\) realizing the specified tuple match and defect \(d\). Choose a second defect \(d'\) outside \(S\cup\{d\}\). Change \(\xi\) along an unprotected path from \(d\) to \(d'\) to a vector \(\xi'\) with divergence \(\Delta-\mathbf e_{d'}\). Use \(\xi\) to map all columns and all main row blocks other than \(d\), and use \(\xi'\) on block \(d\); fix the remaining labels. This is a bijection on each side. Any new row at \(d\) admits the second vector, and all other extensions admit the first vector. Both agree on the old tuple. Iterating gives bijections on ordered extensions of a tuple, including tuples required to have a specified equality pattern.

**Supports for gates and auxiliary bits.** Rigidify the joint-symmetric circuit by merging equal typed multisets of children from the bottom up, keeping wire multiplicities and input labels. This preserves the computed polynomial and does not increase gate orbit size. Every variable permutation now has a unique extending automorphism for that permutation, giving a \(\Gamma_n\)-action. This is the syntactic rigidification of Lemmas 2.3–2.4 of the source and applies with the auxiliary labels and over every field. [Lemmas 2.3–2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.23541)

A set of row and column labels is an alternating support of an object if its pointwise stabilizer in \(H\) fixes the object. Put \(r=\lfloor m/16\rfloor\), take \(m\) large, and suppose the maximal gate orbit is less than \(\binom nr\). Every gate and every used auxiliary bit has an alternating support of size at most \(s=2(r-1)\). Indeed, the index of the object's stabilizer intersected with either symmetric coordinate subgroup is at most the index of its stabilizer in \(\Gamma_n\). The small-index theorem gives a set of fewer than \(r\) labels on that side whose alternating pointwise stabilizer fixes the object. The theorem applies because \(\binom nr\le \frac12\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\) for large \(m\). Take the union of the two sets. A used bit belongs to a unique input gate in the reduced circuit, with the same stabilizer under the joint action. An unused bit contributes a scalar \(2\) to the Boolean sum, so it suffices to compare sums over the used bits. The input \(x_{ij}\) can be assigned its support \(\{i,j\}\), and the output has empty support. In this range \(2s\le k\). [Theorem A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.23541)


**Transferring fixed auxiliary assignments.** Each \(P\)-fixed assignment of the used bits is a choice of one Boolean value per \(P\)-orbit of bits. To transport such an orbit, take a bit \(y\) and a supporting tuple for it. Apply an admissible translation to the tuple, complete its map to \(h\in H\), and send \(P y\) to \(P(hy)\) in the second action. A partial side-preserving injection on at most \(k\) labels extends to \(H\), because at least two unused labels on each side allow correction of either permutation's parity. Two completions of the same tuple map differ by a pointwise stabilizer on the source support and hence give the same bit. Two admissible images of the tuple differ by a target \(P\)-translation and give bits in the same target orbit. Two choices of supports for a bit, or two tuple descriptions representing the same bit, can be compared by using one admissible translation and one completion on their union, of size at most \(2s\le k\). Changing the bit to a \(P\)-translate has no effect on the transported orbit, since the tuple translations commute with the two actions. The reverse construction for interchanged charges is inverse. These facts give a bijection of \(P\)-orbits of used bits and thus a bijection \(w\mapsto w'\) of the \(P\)-fixed Boolean assignments.

**Gate evaluation.** Consider the two full inputs \((A,w)\) and \((A',w')\). For a gate \(g\) with alternating support \(S_g\) of size at most \(s\), choose any image \(hS_g\) matched to \(S_g\) by the ordered tuple correspondence, with \(h\in H\) realizing the matching. The value of \(g\) on the first input equals the value of \(hg\) on the second input. We prove this by induction on gate height, allowing any such support, with the usual two labels at a matrix-variable input. For a constant the assertion is immediate. For a matrix variable it follows from preservation of entries, and for a bit it follows from the orbit bijection defining \(w'\).

For an internal gate, partition its distinct children into orbits under the pointwise stabilizer \(H_{(S_g)}\). Within each orbit all wires have the same multiplicity. For a representative child \(t\) choose a support \(S_t\), of size at most \(s\) and with the usual two labels if \(t\) is a matrix variable. Parameterize that orbit by embeddings of \(S_g\cup S_t\) fixing the already chosen labels of \(S_g\). Each embedding extends to an element of \(H_{(S_g)}\) and sends \(t\) to a well-defined child, because two extensions agreeing on \(S_t\) send \(t\) to the same gate. This is onto the child orbit and every child has the same positive integer number of preimages, by the transitive action on that orbit. For the corresponding orbit at \(hg\), parameterize by embeddings extending the specified image on \(S_g\) and use the same representative \(t\). Composing with \(h^{-1}\) on the images shows its common preimage count is exactly the same. The one-point extension construction pairs the parameterizing embeddings bijectively and matches their images on \(S_t\), so the induction hypothesis pairs equal values. Equality of the integer counts of each value among the embeddings yields equality of the integer counts among the children, since the preimage count is the same positive integer on both sides. This comparison occurs before field arithmetic. The matching of wire multiplicities and the addition or multiplication operation now gives equality of the parent values. The output is fixed by \(H\) and has empty support, proving equality of the two witness values for every paired fixed assignment.

Finally, for either concrete matrix input, the witness value is constant on every \(P\)-orbit of bit assignments by joint symmetry and invariance of that matrix. In characteristic \(p\), its Boolean-cube sum equals the sum over \(P\)-fixed assignments, since all non-singleton orbits have cardinality divisible by \(p\). The bijection above then proves that the two cube sums agree whenever the maximal gate orbit is less than \(\binom nr\).

For each constructed \(n\), define the polynomial
\[
f_n(X)=\sum_{B\in\Gamma_n\cdot A}\ \prod_{B_{ij}=1}x_{ij}\prod_{B_{ij}=0}(1-x_{ij}),
\]
and use zero at other orders. The sum is over distinct matrices in the orbit, so \(f_n(A)=1\), \(f_n(A')=0\), and it is a matrix-invariant polynomial of degree at most \(n^2\). The comparison forces a gate orbit at least \(\binom nr\) for every witness. Since \(m=\Theta(\log n)\) for fixed \(p\), the bound is \(n^{\Omega(\log n)}\). The reverse inclusion of \(\mathsf{symVNP}\) in the invariant families is the reindexing argument of Observation 7.2 and is valid over every field. \(\square\)
