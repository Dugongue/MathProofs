# Exact epimorphism counts for abelian Hall kernels and parallel coset intersection

## 1. Counting epimorphisms onto a group with an abelian normal Hall subgroup

Fix constants \(b,c,d\). Given Cayley tables for arbitrary \(G\) and for \(H\) with an abelian normal Hall subgroup \(N\), suppose \(Q=H/N\) either has order at most \(b\), or is nilpotent of class at most \(c\) with at most \(d\) generators. A deterministic polynomial-time algorithm counts all epimorphisms \(G\to H\) and produces one when the count is positive. The algorithm accepts arbitrary prime-power exponents in \(N\). This gives a scoped answer to Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar Open Question 2, and includes arbitrary source groups even when the target complement is cyclic. The cited cyclic-complement decision theorem treats source and target groups of the specified coprime form. [Results and open questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7) [Cyclic-complement algorithm](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S4)

If \(N\) is unmarked, enumerate prime subsets of \(|H|\). There are at most \(|H|\) of them, and the elements whose orders use only primes in the subset give the unique candidate for a normal Hall subgroup with that prime support. Verify the subgroup, normality, abelian property, Hall order and quotient conditions by table operations. Testing the generator bound uses enumeration of \(d\)-tuples. Use any successful \(N\) to compute the count. For bounded \(|Q|\), enumerate homomorphisms \(G\to Q\) by assigning images of a generating tuple of length at most \(\log_2|G|\). If \(Q\) is nilpotent of fixed class and generator rank, enumerate homomorphisms along its upper central series using finite abelian linear equations for each central lift. Every subgroup of every quotient of \(Q\) has bounded generator rank, by intersecting with the lower central series whose factors are generated by at most \(d^i\) weight-\(i\) commutators. Each central lift, when it exists, is a torsor for homomorphisms from \(G/[G,G]\) to a bounded-rank abelian group. There are at most \(|G|^{O(1)}\) such homomorphisms per lift, by cyclic decomposition and the fact that there are at most \(|A|\) homomorphisms from a finite abelian group \(A\) to a cyclic group. There are at most \(c\) layers. For each central extension in the enumeration, choose any section of the quotient and use one unknown central correction per element of the source. For each source pair, impose that the sum of its two corrections minus the correction of its product equals minus the factor set evaluated on the quotient images. This is an explicit finite abelian linear system. Retain the surjections \(\alpha:G\to Q\).

### Lifts of a quotient map

Fix a surjection \(\alpha:G\to Q\), let \(K=\ker\alpha\), and write \(M=K/[K,K]\). Conjugation gives a well-defined action of \(Q\) on \(M\), since conjugation by \(K\) acts trivially on its abelianization. Conjugation in \(H\) similarly gives a \(Q\)-action on \(N\). An epimorphism inducing \(\alpha\) must restrict to a \(Q\)-equivariant surjection \(\psi:M\to N\). Indeed, its restriction to \(K\) must cover the whole kernel of the target quotient, and equivariance follows from conjugation.

Every equivariant \(\psi\) lifts to a homomorphism inducing \(\alpha\), and the number of lifts is \(|N|/|N^Q|\). For this, write the kernels additively and choose sections \(s:Q\to G\), \(t:Q\to H\), both taking the identity to the identity. The factor sets of \(s,t\) are given by \(s(q)s(r)s(qr)^{-1}\) and \(t(q)t(r)t(qr)^{-1}\). The difference \(u(q,r)\in N\) of the first factor set after applying \(\psi\) and the second factor set is a 2-cocycle, by associativity and equivariance. To define the lift by \(ks(q)\mapsto(\psi(k)+v(q))t(q)\), the equation on the correction is
\[
 v(q)+qv(r)-v(qr)=u(q,r).
\]
Set \(v(q)=|Q|^{-1}\sum_{r\in Q}u(q,r)\). Multiplication by \(|Q|\) is invertible on \(N\), and summing the cocycle identity verifies the displayed equation. The difference between two solutions is a 1-cocycle \(a\). Summing \(a(qr)=a(q)+qa(r)\) over \(r\) shows that \(a(q)=(1-q)z\) with \(z=|Q|^{-1}\sum_r a(r)\). These corrections are parametrized by \(N/N^Q\). Lifts are surjective exactly when \(\psi\) is, since the quotient map is already surjective. Thus each chosen \(\alpha\) contributes \(|N|/|N^Q|\) times the number of equivariant epimorphisms \(M\to N\).

### Counting equivariant epimorphisms

Separate the primary parts of \(M,N\). For one prime \(p\) dividing \(|N|\), put \(U=M_p/pM_p\), \(V=N_p/pN_p\). Give each the ascending exponent filtration \(U_s=(M_p[p^s]+pM_p)/pM_p\) and likewise \(V_s\). Both filtrations are invariant under \(Q\). A homomorphism \(M_p\to N_p\) induces a linear map carrying each \(U_s\) into \(V_s\). Conversely any filtered linear map lifts to an ordinary homomorphism, since in cyclic coordinates its coefficient from a source generator of order \(p^s\) to a target generator of order \(p^e\) is arbitrary modulo \(p\) when \(s\geq e\) and is zero when \(s<e\). For a \(Q\)-equivariant filtered map, average any ordinary lift under the action on homomorphisms, using the invertibility of \(|Q|\) on \(N_p\). Its reduction modulo \(p\) is unchanged. Thus the reduction image of \(\operatorname{Hom}_Q(M_p,N_p)\) consists exactly of the \(Q\)-equivariant filtered linear maps.

Since \(p\nmid |Q|\), split the two filtrations into sums of simple \(\mathbb F_p[Q]\)-modules by averaging linear projections. This can be carried out in polynomial table time. To find a simple submodule, enumerate nonzero vectors and compute the \(\mathbb F_p\)-span of each \(Q\)-orbit. A nonzero span of minimum dimension is simple, and there are only \(p^{\dim U}\leq|M_p|\) or \(p^{\dim V}\leq|N_p|\) vectors to try. Repeatedly split off complements within each filtration level. Mark each simple summand by the first filtration level in which it occurs. Group them into isomorphism types by solving the linear intertwining equations. For one type \(S\), its endomorphisms form a finite division ring, hence a finite field of cardinality \(q_S\), computable as \(p^{\dim\operatorname{End}_Q(S)}\). Matrices between the multiplicity spaces over this field describe exactly the maps between the summands of type \(S\).

In such a matrix, order target rows by decreasing filtration level. For row \(j\), let \(a_j\) count source copies of \(S\) whose filtration levels are at least the level of that row. The allowed row vectors comprise a coordinate space of dimension \(a_j\) over the endomorphism field. These spaces are nested in increasing order. If the previous rows are independent, their span is a subspace of the current coordinate space of dimension \(j-1\). The number of ways to extend independence is therefore \(q_S^{a_j}-q_S^{j-1}\). If a level fails \(a_j\geq j\), there are no surjections of this type. Otherwise the product of the row counts is the number of surjective matrices of this type. Multiply over types for the total filtered surjection count. The entire filtered linear map space has cardinality equal to the product of \(q_S^{\sum_j a_j}\) over all types, with the empty-source and empty-target spaces interpreted in the usual way.

The order of \(\operatorname{Hom}_Q(M_p,N_p)\) is computable by a finite abelian linear system. Use one unknown image in \(N_p\) for each element of \(M_p\), impose addition, and impose equivariance for each \(Q\)-action value. The solution group order can be computed using integer normal forms for the finite abelian domain and relation lattice. Every map in the reduction image has the same number of lifts, given by this order divided by the cardinality of the filtered map space. Surjectivity to \(N_p\) is equivalent to surjectivity modulo \(p\), by the Frattini property of finite abelian \(p\)-groups. This proves the exact count. To construct a witness, choose independent standard rows in the nested spaces, lift in ordinary cyclic coordinates and average as above. Multiply the counts over primary parts and sum over the distinct \(\alpha\). The constructions from a positive branch give an epimorphism. Each operation and branch count is polynomial in table length under the stated quotient bounds. [Polynomial-time integer normal forms](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

## 2. Uniform NC coset intersection with a bounded-orbit permutation group

Fix a constant \(b\). Let \(G,P\leq S_n\) be given by generating permutations, with every point orbit of \(P\) of size at most \(b\). There is a deterministic logspace-uniform NC algorithm which, given \(x,y\in S_n\), computes \(Gx\cap Py\) as a coset by a representative and generators, or reports emptiness. This supplies a bounded-orbit case of Levet–Srivastava–Thakkar Problem 12.3, including the full coset output, and works for any \(P\) under the orbit promise. The index of \(G\cap P\) in \(P\) can be exponential. For example \(P\) can be generated by independent transpositions of disjoint pairs and \(G\) can be trivial. [Problem 12.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16039#S12)

Take composition rightmost first. Compute the \(P\)-orbits \(O_i\) in parallel from the generator edges. For each \(i\), let \(\mathcal I_i\) be the set of injections \(O_i\hookrightarrow[n]\). On their disjoint union \(\mathcal I\), let \(S=G\times P\) act by
\[
 (g,p)\cdot f=g\circ f\circ(p|_{O_i})^{-1}\quad(f\in\mathcal I_i).
\]
Each \(O_i\) is \(P\)-invariant, so this is an action. The domain has size at most \(n^{b+1}\). Enumerate it and construct the generating permutations in uniform NC. Also include a faithful action of \(S\) on two disjoint copies of \([n]\), so that either coordinate of a computed element can be read back directly.

Let \(j_i\) be the inclusion of \(O_i\). The stabilizer of the ordered list \((j_i)_i\) consists of the diagonal pairs \((g,g)\) with \(g\in G\cap P\). Compute this stabilizer by the uniform NC pointwise stabilizer algorithm of Babai–Luks–Seress and project the generators. For the coset witness, let \(f_i=(xy^{-1})|_{O_i}\). Transporting \((f_i)_i\) to \((j_i)_i\) under \(S\) requires \(gxy^{-1}=p\), exactly the equality \(gx=py\).

For completeness, an ordered-list transporter for a permutation group \(S\) of polynomial degree can be obtained in uniform NC from its pointwise stabilizer subroutine. On ordered pairs of points let \((S\times S)\rtimes\langle\tau\rangle\) act componentwise with \(\tau\) swapping the two positions. Include two tagged copies of the point domain, exchanged by \(\tau\), to read the factors and the swap bit from any output permutation. Compute the pointwise stabilizer of all pairs \((f_i,j_i)\). An element of this stabilizer in the swap coset has the form \((v,w)\tau\), with \(w(f_i)=j_i\) and \(v(j_i)=f_i\) for every \(i\). Conversely, a transporter \(w\) gives the stabilizer element \((w^{-1},w)\tau\). The stabilizer meets the swap coset exactly when one of its returned generators has the swap bit set, since that bit is a homomorphism to \(C_2\). Read off \(w=(g,p)\) from such a generator. The resulting representative is \(z=gx=py\), and the intersection is \((G\cap P)z\). The pointwise-transporter trick is a published ingredient. [Pointwise stabilizers in NC](https://doi.org/10.1145/28395.28439) [Luks's partial-permutation reduction](https://www.cs.uoregon.edu/Reports/TR-1990-021.pdf)

Both main results are paper proofs.
