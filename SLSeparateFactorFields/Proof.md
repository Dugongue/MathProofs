# An orientation test with factors in separate finite fields

Fix an odd finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$ and a positive constant $d$. Consider two tensors written as matrix pencils $M_A(x),M_B(x)$, with label dimension $\ell$ and square matrix dimension $n$. Suppose $\det M_A(x)\ne0$, all its distinct geometric factors have degree at most $d$, and two of these are quadratic forms with symmetric matrices $Q_1,Q_2$ over the algebraic closure. Assume $Q_2$ is invertible and $Q_2^{-1}Q_1$ has squarefree characteristic polynomial. The factor multiplicities are unrestricted. The bounded-degree factors may use different fields, with no bound imposed on their common splitting degree.

There is a deterministic polynomial-time same-field many-one reduction of the total determinant character test to ordinary 3-TI on this class. In the convention
$$
 M_B(fx)=h M_A(x)g^{-1},
$$
the total character on the original covariant tensor is $(\det f)^{-1}(\det h)/(\det g)$. In particular the construction decides SL-TI if $\ell,n$ have the same residue modulo $q-1$ and that residue is a unit. To see this, the center triples with product one contribute all triples of determinant scalars of product one when these dimensions have a common unit residue. A single field of definition for the chosen pair of quadratic factors is used within a branch. No common splitting field is built for the full determinant polynomial.

The proof extends the two-quadratic pencil method for determinant recovery. Its new steps are to recover the factors in separate small fields, take products of leading coefficients by norms, and enumerate feasible quadratic multipliers using degree-bounded equations over the branch field. The first input alone needs the promise. Any ordinarily isomorphic second input has the same factor properties.

## 1. Finite field computations

At fixed characteristic $p$, univariate factoring over explicit finite extensions is deterministic polynomial time in the extension degree and polynomial degree. For completeness, given squarefree $h$ over a field $K$ of characteristic $p$, the algebra $C=K[t]/(h)$ is a product of finite fields. Compute $\ker(x\mapsto x^p-x)$ by $\mathbb F_p$-linear algebra. Its dimension is the number of factors, and an element therein outside the scalar $\mathbb F_p$ line has at least two distinct values on the factors. Compute the polynomial gcds with that element minus $a$ for each $a\in\mathbb F_p$ to split $h$, and repeat. The derivative and gcd identify the factors whose multiplicities are not divisible by $p$. Strip all their occurrences by division, then take coefficientwise $p$-th roots of the remaining $p$-th power and recurse. This also handles inseparable binomials. No enumeration of the field of coefficients is used. Use [Shoup's deterministic irreducible-polynomial construction for fixed characteristic](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf) to construct finite fields.

Use compatible field models over $\mathbb F_p$ for all represented degrees up to an explicit polynomial bound. For each prime power dividing a requested degree, build the largest needed prime-power extension. Its smaller members are the kernels of the corresponding Frobenius maps minus the identity. The field of a composite degree is the tensor product of its prime-power parts, which are of coprime degrees. Using a fixed ordering gives compatible embeddings for divisibility. One can prepare all prime-power fields below a polynomial numerical bound, since there are only polynomially many and factoring these small integer degrees is polynomial. Deterministic irreducible construction and the fixed-characteristic factor procedure above suffice here. For two represented degrees, the lcm gives a field containing both, and intersections are accessed by Frobenius kernels.

## 2. Recover geometric factors in local fields

Here the determinant can be replaced by any nonzero homogeneous polynomial $P$ of degree $n$ accessible by polynomial-time evaluation over the represented fields, with the same bound $d$ on all distinct geometric factor degrees.

Choose an extension $E_0/F$ of polynomial cardinality, hence logarithmic degree for fixed $F$. Use deterministic line restrictions from the earlier two-quadratic proof. Explicitly, on $k$ parameters use the substitution $x_i=t^{w_i}$, with weights $w_i=\sum_{j=0}^{D} i^j b^j$, $b>Dk^D$, for a suitable constant $D=O(d^2)$. Monomials of total degree at most $D$ have distinct weights because equality gives the same no-carry power sums for multisets of size at most $D$. The curve hits the common nonvanishing locus of polynomially many nonzero polynomials of degree at most $D$: the product of their nonzero curve substitutions has polynomial degree, and $|E_0|$ is chosen greater than that total. Coefficients may lie in an extension of $E_0$. The parameter points in $E_0$ still obey the root bound.

Apply the curve to the $2\ell$ coordinates of an affine line $v+tu$. A good line has nonzero leading terms for all geometric factors and gives simple, disjoint roots for the distinct reduced factors. Each requirement is the nonvanishing of a coefficient, a discriminant, or a resultant of degree bounded in terms of $d$, regardless of the fields of definition. To see the discriminants are nonzero, take a direction with nonzero leading coefficient and nonzero directional derivative. Absolute irreducibility implies that the factor has a nonzero derivative and is coprime to such a derivative. Viewing that direction as the univariate axis proves nonvanishing of the generic-line discriminant. Distinct factors are coprime and the same argument applies to their resultant along a direction with nonzero leading coefficients. Thus a good line occurs on the curve. Interpolate each restriction, discard degree-dropping lines, and maximize the degree of the squarefree radical of the restriction, computed by univariate factoring. This degree is at most the sum of the distinct geometric factor degrees, and a good line attains it. Any maximizing full-degree line is good.

Fix the good line and complete $u$ to a basis with the columns of $C$. The polynomial $P(v+tu+Cz)/P(u)$ is monic in $t$, since $P$ is homogeneous. Let the irreducible residue polynomials of its squarefree part at $z=0$ have degrees $a_i\le n$ over $E_0$. Enumerate sets of at most $d$ such residue polynomials. For each set work over the field of degree $\operatorname{lcm}(a_i)\le n^d$ over $E_0$. Every chosen residue polynomial splits there. Enumerate subsets of size at most $d$ from their listed roots. There are polynomially many choices at fixed $d$, and every actual geometric reduced factor has one of these root subsets in some enumerated field.

In a local field, lift each selected root $a$ along directional substitutions $z=s\beta$. The residue root has multiplicity $e$, so lift the coprime factor $(t-a)^e$ against the other residue factor, by ordinary coefficientwise monic Hensel lifting. Its true formal lift is $(t-a_\beta(s))^e$, where $a_\beta(s)$ is the simple-root lift of the geometric reduced factor. Writing $e=p^j e'$ with $p\nmid e'$, the coefficient of $t^{e-p^j}$ is $-e'a_\beta(s)^{p^j}$. Lifting to precision $s^{dp^j+1}$ and taking inverse Frobenius of those coefficients recovers the simple-root lift through degree $d$. One lift has precision and polynomial degrees $O_d(n)$ and uses computations only in the selected local field.

Multiply the resulting simple-root factors for a subset of at most $d$ roots. The coefficient of $s^i$, truncated at $i=d$, is the evaluation at $\beta$ of a homogeneous polynomial of degree $i$ in $z$. This follows by restricting the unique multivariate simple-root lifts to $z=s\beta$. Interpolate these coefficients from the same bounded-degree curve substitution and obtain the candidate in $(t,z)$. Discard total degree above $d$. In the correct subset, all roots associated with a single reduced factor are included, so that factor's monic expression is recovered exactly. The truncated calculation takes polynomial time because $d$ is constant.

To certify the candidate, restore raw coordinates and test divisibility of $P$ on every line of another such curve family. A candidate coprime to all true factors fails on a full-degree line where its leading coefficient and all its resultants with those factors are nonzero. The curve hits this simultaneous nonvanishing condition by bounded factor degrees. A passing candidate must therefore share a true factor. That factor's good-line root subset is contained in the candidate's subset, using monicity on that line. Both are present in the same local field and within the same selected residue-polynomial set. Thus the inclusion-minimal passing subsets recover exactly the geometric reduced factors present there. On equality of subsets, both monic polynomials have the same degree in $t$ and the true factor divides the candidate, so they coincide.

Normalize in raw coordinates by dividing by the leading coefficient for a fixed monomial order. Read the factor multiplicities from its good-line roots. Use the compatible embeddings to compare normalizations occurring in different local fields. For each factor, the minimal field containing its raw normalized coefficients over $F$ can be computed by iterating $q$-Frobenius until all coefficients return. Its degree is at most $n$, since Frobenius permutes at most $n$ geometric factors. Recover its coordinates in that subfield by a linear solve. This provides all factors in fields of degree at most $n$ over $F$, without realizing their common compositum.

## 3. Leading coefficients as products of local norms

Let $K/F$ be any represented field of polynomial degree, and let $L$ be an explicit $K$-linear coordinate change of the label space. We can compute the leading monomial and coefficient of $P(Lx)$ without expanding $P$ or constructing the compositum of all factor fields.

For each normalized factor, extend its own field to the lcm with $K$, of degree polynomial over $F$, substitute $Lx$, and expand this one factor, which has bounded degree. The leading exponent vector is constant on its orbit under Frobenius of $K$, because this Frobenius fixes $L$ and preserves nonzero coefficients. The product of the leading coefficients in this orbit is the norm of one leading coefficient down to $K$, computed by multiplying its Frobenius conjugates in that one local field. Use the multiplicity of the factor as an exponent and repeat across orbits. This gives the leading exponent of the product by summing the orbit sizes times the individual exponents, with multiplicities, and gives its coefficient by multiplying these local norms. The leading product identity holds for every monomial order by its multiplicative total ordering. To find the overall scalar $c\in F^\times$ in $P=c\prod_i \phi_i^{e_i}$, evaluate at the good-line direction $u$ where $P(u)\ne0$, and multiply the factor evaluations by the same norm rule over $E_0$. Each norm orbit length is no larger than its represented field degree.

Only two-way composita are used in this multiplication. In particular their degrees are polynomial even when the lcm of all factor degrees is superpolynomial.

## 4. The determinant test

Enumerate matching choices of the two distinguished quadratic factors from the two lists. It suffices to choose any suitable source pair and enumerate target matches. The absolute factors are explicitly represented, with compatible embeddings. The field $K$ for a candidate is the compositum of at most four factor fields, each of degree at most $n$ over $F$, so its degree is at most $n^4$. On both sides form $T_X=Q_{X,2}^{-1}Q_{X,1}$ and check invertibility of the second form and squarefreeness of the characteristic polynomial of the operator. A genuine $F$-transporter on such a branch extends to $f_K$ and satisfies
$$
 T_B f_K=\rho f_K T_A,\qquad
 f_K^{\mathsf T}Q_{B,2}f_K=\lambda Q_{A,2}
$$
for nonzero $\rho,\lambda\in K$. These two scalars do not require searching $K$. If $c_{A,i}\ne0$ is a non-leading characteristic polynomial coefficient in degree $\ell-i$, necessarily
$$
 \rho^i=c_{B,i}/c_{A,i}.
$$
Such an $i$ exists for $\ell>1$ by squarefreeness; dimensions bounded by one can be handled directly. Enumerate the roots of this binomial in $K^\times$ by fixed-characteristic factoring and check all remaining coefficients. Similarly
$$
 \lambda^\ell=(\det f)^2\,\frac{\det Q_{B,2}}{\det Q_{A,2}}.
$$
Try the $q-1$ possible determinants of a base-field map, and factor the corresponding binomials over $K$. These give at most polynomially many candidates, even when $|K|$ is exponential. Impose the two displayed operator and form equations in the branch on the actual extended label map.

Put $E=K[t]/(\operatorname{charpol}(T_A))$ and identify the source and target extended label spaces with its regular module using the operators $T_A,\rho^{-1}T_B$. These cyclic coordinates are found by factoring the characteristic polynomial and choosing one nonzero vector in each simple subspace. The forms $Q_{X,2}$ are symmetric nondegenerate and make their operators self-adjoint. In cyclic coordinates, the identity $B_X(ax,y)=B_X(x,ay)$ for all $a\in E$ implies $B_X(x,y)=B_X(xy,1)$. Write this as $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}(a_Xxy)$ using perfectness of the étale trace pairing. Here $a_X$ is a unit by nondegeneracy. Solve $b^2=\lambda a_A/a_B$ in the field factors by factoring degree-two polynomials, or discard the branch. A chosen solution determines an explicit reference map $f_0$. Every allowed map then differs from $f_0$ by independent signs $s_j$ on the field factors $E_j$, since the quotient of two solutions is a square root of one in each odd-characteristic field. This reference map can be defined only over $K$.

Use source coordinates grouped by the $E_j$. Compare leading monomials of $P_A(x)=\det M_A(x)$ and $P_B(f_0x)$ by Section 3. If they disagree, reject the branch, since sign flips preserve support. Otherwise let $p_A,p_B$ be their respective leading coefficients, and $\alpha_j$ the total exponent of the common monomial in block $E_j$. By the original tensor equation,
$$
 \frac{\det h}{\det g}
  = \frac{p_B}{p_A}\prod_j s_j^{\alpha_j}.
$$
Also $\det f=(\det_K f_0)\prod_j s_j^{\dim_K E_j}$ holds over $K$ for every base-field transporter, because the determinant of its scalar extension is unchanged. Thus the total character is exactly
$$
 (\det_K f_0)^{-1}\frac{p_B}{p_A}
      \prod_j s_j^{\alpha_j-\dim_K E_j}.
$$
Record each sign using the induced action on one chosen $K$-line in its block, with target line its $f_0$-image. Multiply the sign lines with the displayed exponents and impose total value one by a scalar pin. This is a constant-degree calculation on the attached sorts.

Retain the original tensor in every branch. The extended mode maps are tied to the actual base-field maps via $K\times V\to K\otimes_F V$. Keep $K$ pointwise fixed with an ordered basis, and impose the needed $K$-linear actions on the auxiliary sorts by its multiplication maps. This is all encoded over $F$. The lines, intertwining conditions, forms and scalar products use linear and bilinear maps. Every branch isomorphism therefore gives a genuine original transporter with total determinant one. Every original such transporter chooses valid factor matches and multipliers, and lifts to all attached maps in that branch. If the unpromised second input is ordinarily nonisomorphic, it cannot pass any branch retaining the original tensor. If the arithmetic preprocessing fails to find the promised factorizations, one can reject, with failure rules whose work stays polynomial on all inputs.

Finally use fixed-signature packing and the existing polynomial positive-OR construction. The [ordinary tensor-system compiler](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4) applies. The output is one ordinary TI instance over $F$, and all preprocessing is deterministic polynomial time at fixed $q,d$.

This covers arbitrary numbers of bounded-degree factors in separate finite fields. It removes the common-field restriction from the earlier compiler. It still needs a squarefree quadratic pair, or an independently proved replacement that gives an explicit bounded character calculation. Arbitrary scalar-adjoint tensors need not have such a pair, so universal closure of Step 17 remains open. All arguments are informal.

### Keeping the tensor-system signature fixed

Each branch can have polynomially many auxiliary spaces and equations. Collect its spaces in a direct sum and use the bilinear action of a label algebra $F^I$ to project to the individual spaces. Pin the label idempotents by an ordered basis map, whose coordinate space is itself pinned by a shift and a cyclic vector. For each type of bilinear operation, take one copy of its three spaces for each operation record. Tie these copies to the original spaces by the linear maps from the first direct sum to each argument or result sum, using the coordinate projection on each record. A second pinned label algebra on the records forces each copy to follow its original space. The maps now combine into one blockwise bilinear map per type. Linear maps and named vectors use the same construction, with fixed one-dimensional arguments for named vectors. Evaluation pairings handle duals. Thus the signature stays fixed, and the compiler costs polynomial time in the total explicit dimension. For determinant lines carrying only a sign in a field extension, the scalar on an attached subline over the base of the extension is the same sign. The degree parity gives its norm directly, without an exterior algebra of the full extension degree.

### Combining the branch tests

For two tests of equality of isomorphism types, the multiset identity
$$
\{(a,c),(b,d)\}=\{(a,d),(b,c)\}
$$
holds exactly when $a=b$ or $c=d$. Use ordered pairs and unordered pairs as different node types in a rooted expression tree. A balanced OR over polynomially many tests has logarithmic depth, so this encoding has polynomial size when unfolded. Use the label algebra $F^P$ of tree nodes and separate label algebras for relation records. Their primitive idempotents encode the node types and the colored edge incidences by linear maps. Give each tensor leaf the action of its label idempotent on the three sums of all the leaf mode spaces. An isomorphism preserves the labeled summands and is precisely a tree isomorphism with the indicated leaf tensor isomorphisms. The signature has a fixed number of maps, so the tensor-system compiler is applied once to the full tree. The ordered and unordered pairing construction follows [Jenner, Köbler, McKenzie and Torán](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.190/Mitarbeiter/toran/completeiso.pdf).
