# Expander cuts give linear refutation degree for spectrally simple orthogonal tensors

**Theorem.** There are arbitrarily large $n$ and two integer $n\times n\times n$ tensors with exactly $2n$ nonzero entries each and at most two nonzero entries per slice, both with the same positive diagonal Gram in each mode and all Gram eigenvalues simple. They are inequivalent under $O(n)^3$. The standard orthogonal action equations require degree $\Theta(n)$ in both Polynomial Calculus and real Sum of Squares, and admit polynomial sparse-size refutations. Orthogonal nonisomorphism on the constructed class is decidable by binary linear algebra. The exact minimum degree of a real orthogonal polynomial invariant separating the pair is $2n$.

The construction maps a three-edge-colored simple bipartite cubic graph to a tensor support. The cycle and cut spaces of that graph govern distinct features of the instance. Signs of transported entries form a Tseitin system. The orthogonal invariant that realizes the optimal degree is the same graph with its edges interpreted as index contractions.

## 1. The graph is the support

Let $H$ be a simple, connected, cubic bipartite graph on $2n$ vertices. Its edges split into three perfect matchings by Hall's theorem and successive matching removal. Number the edges of each color separately. At every graph vertex $v$, write $(i_v,j_v,k_v)$ for the labels of the three incident colored edges. Give $v$ a positive integer weight $w_v$. Put $T^+_{i_v j_v k_v}=w_v$ and $T^b_{i_v j_v k_v}=b_v w_v$, where $b_v\in\{1,-1\}$, and set all other entries to zero. There is just one entry per vertex since $H$ is simple. No two entries share two coordinates, since that would mean two parallel graph edges.

For a real array $X$ let $G_r(X)$ denote its $r$-th one-mode flattening multiplied by its transpose. Each $G_r$ is diagonal on both tensors because no two positions of the support share two coordinates. The label of a color-$r$ edge $uv$ has Gram eigenvalue $w_u^2+w_v^2$, independent of the signs. To make all these values distinct, number the vertices $1,\dots,2n$ and choose $w_v=B+v$, with $B=(2n)^2+1$. An equality of two sums of squares first forces equality of the sums of the vertex labels: the square remainder has magnitude less than $2B$. Equality of the remaining squares then forces equality of the two unordered pairs. Thus all entries have polynomial magnitude, and each mode has simple positive spectrum.

Any real orthogonal transporter must preserve the common Gram in each mode, by the Gram transformation under orthogonal changes of bases. As the eigenvalues are simple, all three transporter matrices are diagonal signs. A sign $s_e$ belongs to every colored edge, and the required action is precisely
$$
s_e^2=1,\qquad q_v:=\prod_{e\ni v}s_e=b_v.
\tag{1}
$$
Conversely every solution gives the diagonal orthogonal transporter. Binary linear algebra decides (1) by taking the signs as exponents modulo two. In this connected graph it is solvable exactly when $\prod_v b_v=1$, since the binary incidence matrix connects all vertices and has image the even-parity space. We use odd charge, for example a single negative $b_v$.

## 2. Sparse certificates and exact degree transfer

Use matrix variables $P_1,P_2,P_3$, the coefficient equations of $(P_1\otimes P_2\otimes P_3)T^+=T^b$, and the quadratic equations $P_r^{\mathsf T}P_r=I$. Adding the other orthogonality equations $P_r P_r^{\mathsf T}=I$ or adding inverse variables with $P_r^{-1}=P_r^{\mathsf T}$ leaves the statements below valid. We prove the claims in the basic encoding; the diagonal substitution also satisfies those optional extra axioms.

Substitute $\operatorname{diag}(s_e)$ in the three matrix slots. The orthogonality equations become zero or Boolean-square equations. The action equations become zero or nonzero scalar multiples of the vertex equations, exactly. This substitutes single variables or zero, so any degree-$D$ derivation of $1=0$ in Polynomial Calculus becomes one of degree at most $\max(D,3)$ from (1). For equalities-only Sum of Squares, substitute into the certificate $-1=\sum_i h_i f_i+\sum_j p_j^2$. The squares remain squares, and each projected equality is zero or a scalar multiple of (1), preserving degree.

Conversely, the Gram equations and every equation of (1) are derivable in degree bounded by an absolute constant from the tensor system, with polynomially many monomials. Write $S=(P_1\otimes P_2\otimes P_3)T^+$, a polynomial tensor. In $G_r(S)-G_r(T^b)$ telescope every product as $(S_i-T^b_i)S_j+T^b_i(S_j-T^b_j)$ before summing. This gives a polynomial combination of action equations with total degree at most six. Expanding $G_r(S)$ and applying $P_t^{\mathsf T}P_t=I$ for $t\ne r$ gives $P_rG_r(T^+)P_r^{\mathsf T}$ by polynomial combinations of total degree at most six. For the latter step the two contracted factors telescope in the same way, replacing their two quadratic pairings in succession. Consequently $P_rG_r(T^+)P_r^{\mathsf T}=G_r(T^b)$ has such a derivation. Right multiplication by $P_r$, and one more use of $P_r^{\mathsf T}P_r=I$, give $P_rG_r(T^+)=G_r(T^b)P_r$. The unequal diagonal eigenvalues force each off-diagonal entry of $P_r$ to vanish by division by a nonzero fixed scalar. Orthogonality and the action equations now give (1) on the diagonal entries. Every displayed calculation uses polynomially many summands of bounded degree and can be expanded by variable multiplications and linear combinations in Polynomial Calculus.

The odd-charge contradiction has a sparse proof. Multiply the vertex binomials successively, reducing each square factor by (1) as it appears. An explicit Polynomial Calculus step from $m-a=0$ and $q-b=0$ derives $mq-ab=0$ by multiplying the former by the at-most-cubic monomial $q$ and adding $a(q-b)$. Each square removal subtracts a monomial multiple of $s_e^2-1$. At the end the left monomial is $1$, as each edge occurs twice, and the right sign is $-1$. This is a polynomial-sized derivation with degree $O(n)$. For Sum of Squares one can use a certificate containing only the ideal part. Telescope the difference $\prod_v q_v-\prod_v b_v$ with the vertex binomials, then telescope $\prod_e s_e^2-1$ with the square binomials. Each multiplier here is a signed monomial, and the degree is $O(n)$. Substitute the bounded-degree polynomial combinations of tensor equations just given. This derives a certificate of linear degree and polynomial sparse size.

## 3. Why every low-degree proof fails

Choose the graphs to have uniform edge expansion $h>0$, meaning $|\delta(U)|\ge h |U|$ for $|U|\le n$, where $\delta(U)$ is the set of graph edges leaving $U$. Such simple bipartite cubic graphs exist at arbitrarily large sizes by iterated Ramanujan two-lifts of $K_{3,3}$, with the spectral gap providing a uniform $h$. The tensor construction is polynomial time given the graph and the charges. [Marcus–Spielman–Srivastava, Interlacing Families I](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.4132)

Here is the short cut-space degree argument, including the arbitrary odd-charge case. For a monomial let $A\subseteq E(H)$ consist of the edges occurring to an odd power. Let $d=\lfloor hn/4\rfloor$, increasing $n$ if necessary so $d\ge3$ and $d/h+1<n$. If a cut $\delta(U)$ has size at most $2d$, there is a unique shore of size less than $n$. This follows from expansion and the fact that a cut in a connected graph determines its shores up to complement. Call $b(U)=\prod_{u\in U}b_u$ its sign. If two such cuts each arise as differences of sets of size at most $d$, each small shore has size at most $2d/h$. More precisely $4d/h\le n$, and one may decrease $d$ by one to make the inequality strict. Then the symmetric difference of the small shores is the small shore for the sum of the cuts when the latter is also such a difference, and its sign is the product of the two signs.

For Polynomial Calculus, group the monomials of degree at most $d$ by their odd edge sets modulo the binary cut space. For each nonempty class of such edge sets choose a representative $A_0$. Map each monomial with odd set $A$ to the basis vector of its class times the sign of the small shore for the cut $A\triangle A_0$. The multiplicativity just proved implies that the relative sign of any two terms in a class depends only on their difference. Every axiom in (1) maps to zero, using the singleton shores for vertex cuts. If a proof line maps to zero, within each class its signed coefficients sum to zero. The line is therefore a sum of cancelling binomials using representatives among its own monomials. An allowed multiplication by one edge variable leaves the difference of each such pair unchanged and keeps the two terms within degree $d$. Thus the kernel is stable under all degree-$d$ proof steps. Since $1$ has nonzero image, there is no such refutation. This is the signed cut relation of the Tseitin degree argument. [Buss–Grigoriev–Impagliazzo–Pitassi, Section 4](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/lineargaps/journalfinal.pdf)

For Sum of Squares define a linear functional on monomials of degree at most $d$. It takes value zero if the odd edge set is outside the cut space, and otherwise the sign of its small shore. It takes $1$ to $1$ and annihilates every monomial multiple of (1) of degree at most $d$. For the square equations the two odd sets coincide. For a vertex equation the odd sets differ by its singleton cut, and toggling that vertex in a small shore still leaves a small shore, so the value changes by the required charge. For a square of a degree-at-most-$\lfloor d/2\rfloor$ polynomial, group its monomials by the same cut-space cosets as above. The moment matrix is zero between different groups. Within each group it is the outer product of the signs relative to a representative in that group, by the same small-shore addition identity. Hence the functional is nonnegative on squares and contradicts a degree-$d$ refutation. [Grigoriev, Linear Lower Bound on Degrees of Positivstellensatz Calculus Proofs for the Parity](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(00)00157-2)

## 4. Exact invariant degree for an arbitrary real sign pattern

The following stronger invariant statement applies to any fixed support $S$ of a real three-tensor, with positive magnitudes $w_e$ and signs $b_e$ for $e\in S$. Define $d_*$ to be the minimum size of a subset $J\subseteq S$ with two properties: each coordinate label in each mode occurs an even number of times in $J$, and $\prod_{e\in J}b_e=-1$. Use $d_*=\infty$ when there is no such subset. The minimum degree of a real orthogonal polynomial invariant distinguishing the all-positive and signed tensors is exactly $d_*$. In particular, these tensors are orthogonally isomorphic if and only if there is a diagonal sign transporter, with no spectral hypothesis. Given the two real arrays with equal magnitudes and a nonnegative first array, binary linear algebra tests the sign condition and constructs a transporter when it exists.

For the degree lower bound, restrict an invariant to the coordinate support $S$; this restriction is invariant under all individual diagonal sign switches in the three modes. Each monomial must therefore have even incidence at every coordinate label. Its odd-multiplicity support $J$ satisfies the first property in the definition of $d_*$. A monomial that distinguishes the two inputs has the second property, and its degree is at least $|J|$. For the upper bound, take a tensor copy for every element of a minimum $J$. Pair the incidences labeled by any given coordinate in a mode arbitrarily and contract each pair using the inner product. This is a globally invariant polynomial of degree $|J|$. Its term from the original labels has sign ratio $-1$, and no other term increases from the all-positive to the signed tensor. Thus the values differ. If no such $J$ exists, the sign right-hand side belongs to the image of the binary coordinate-incidence system, by orthogonality to the kernel of its transpose. A diagonal sign choice then gives an actual transporter.

### The graph family attains exactly $2n$

Restrict an $O(n)^3$-invariant real polynomial to tensors supported on the positions indexed by $V(H)$. Write the coordinate there as $z_v$. The restricted polynomial is invariant under flipping the diagonal sign for any colored edge $uv$, which simultaneously changes the signs of $z_u,z_v$. Therefore every surviving monomial has exponents of equal parity at $u$ and $v$. Connectedness implies that either all exponents are even or all are odd. Below degree $2n$, only the even case occurs. Such a polynomial takes equal values on $T^+$ and $T^b$.

For the matching upper bound, place one copy of an input tensor at every vertex of $H$. Contract the corresponding two slots along every edge using the ordinary inner product of its color. This is an $O(n)^3$-invariant polynomial of degree $2n$. Evaluating on $T^+$ gives a sum of nonnegative terms, each indexed by an assignment of an index to each contraction edge. The assignment that gives each edge its original label contributes $\prod_v w_v>0$. Evaluating on $T^b$ keeps every absolute term value the same. The original labeling contributes the negative of its term, and no term increases. Hence the invariant separates the pair, with exactly the minimum possible degree.

## 5. A uniform bound throughout the simple-spectrum real case

For any two real $n_1\times n_2\times n_3$ tensors with simple one-mode Gram spectra that are orthogonally inequivalent, some orthogonal polynomial invariant of degree at most $(n_1+n_2+n_3+1)^2$ separates them. If a Gram spectrum differs, the traces of its first $n_r$ powers suffice by Newton identities. Otherwise align the spectral labels. For each shared eigenvalue $\lambda$ in a mode, the polynomial
$$
E_\lambda(X)=\prod_{\mu\ne\lambda}\frac{G_r(X)-\mu I}{\lambda-\mu}
$$
is a conjugation-covariant matrix polynomial, and at both tensors it is the rank-one projector to that label. These projectors can be inserted into contractions. Two tensor copies with one projector per mode give each squared coordinate in the aligned orthonormal frames. If magnitudes agree, the remaining question is whether slot signs can realize the entry signs. An inconsistent parity system has a subset of at most $n_1+n_2+n_3+1$ equations whose coefficient vectors sum to zero and whose signs multiply to $-1$. Indeed choose a minimal such subset over the binary field. Any proper nonempty dependence would itself be sign-inconsistent or could be removed while retaining a smaller sign-inconsistent subset. This proves the bound by the size of a minimal linear dependence.

Take a tensor copy for every entry in this subset, and pair all incidences of each spectral label in each mode, using its projector in each contraction. This evaluates on the pair to the two products of the selected eigenframe coordinates. The products have opposite signs, independent of how the eigenvectors were signed, since each incidence count is even. For $k$ copies the degree is $k(1+\sum_r(n_r-1))$, which is within the stated bound. The construction gives a polynomial invariant with real coefficients for the pair. For rational inputs, expansion of the projector expression into contractions of Gram powers also shows that at least one rational-coefficient contraction of that degree separates them.

## 6. The least real separating degree is NP-complete to compute

There is a difference between deciding equivalence and finding a least-degree certificate of inequivalence. For the common-support real sign pairs of Section 4, deciding whether $d_*\le K$ is NP-complete, even for promised inequivalent pairs with simple identical Grams and at most five nonzeros per slice. Approximation within some fixed factor greater than one is NP-hard. Here $K$ is a binary input.

Membership in NP is the parity-incidence witness $J$ from Section 4. For hardness, use perfect three-dimensional matching on three sets of size $k$. Over the binary field take a selector $z_S$ for each candidate triple, a slack $y_i$ and its duplicate $y_i'$ for every element, and one common selector $t$. Impose
$$
\sum_{S\ni i} z_S+y_i+t=0,\qquad y_i'+y_i=0.
$$
Turn the system into a tripartite support by the occurrence construction of [the companion integer-lattice proof](Spectral.md). For clarity, make four copies of each equation, each a vertex in the first mode. Each variable's occurrences are cyclically ordered, with the two alternating edge matchings supplying its vertices in the other two modes. Each occurrence is a tensor entry. Its two matching constraints enforce equal values across the whole occurrence cycle. Hence a binary incidence dependence is exactly a source solution repeated in every occurrence. The variables occur at most once per equation and each equation has at least two variables. The cyclic padding by different variable blocks from the companion proof supplies a common cubical shape, giving a fresh degree-one vertex for each padding entry so that every padding selector is zero. The support has no repeated pair of labels. Choose positive magnitudes to make the Gram spectra simple by the companion greedy distinct-sums construction. Mark one occurrence of $t$ as the entry whose sign changes.

An odd relation requires $t=1$. Its weight is $12(\sum_S z_S+\sum_i y_i+k)$, counting the occurrences, with the binary values here counted as integers. For each element $i$, let $p_i$ be its number of selected incident triples in the integers. Then $p_i+y_i$ is odd and at least one. Summing gives $3\sum_S z_S+\sum_i y_i\ge3k$, so the weight is at least $24k$. Weight $24k$ forces all slacks to vanish and every $p_i=1$, giving a perfect matching. Conversely any perfect matching attains that weight. Taking all slacks equal to one and no triples selected gives a feasible odd relation for every source instance, so the pair is always inequivalent. This proves NP-completeness. [Karp, Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems](https://cgi.di.uoa.gr/~sgk/teaching/grad/handouts/karp.pdf)

For the gap, take the bounded-occurrence version in Petrank's Theorem 4.4 with every element in at most three triples. There is an absolute $\delta>0$ such that it is NP-hard to distinguish a perfect matching from maximum matching size at most $(1-\delta)k$. Let $Z=\sum z_S$, $Y=\sum y_i$, $a=Z+Y-k$, and let $b_0$ count elements with $p_i=0$. Then $b_0\le Y$. Total excess incidences above one among nonempty elements are $3Z-3k+b_0$. By deleting a triple from an overloaded element until no overload remains, one obtains a matching of size at least $Z-(3Z-3k+b_0)=k-2a+2Y-b_0\ge k-2a$. Thus on a gap NO instance $a\ge\delta k/2$ and $d_*\ge24k(1+\delta/4)$. The YES degree is $24k$. Every row in the tripartite construction has at most five entries, and the other modes have at most three after padding. [Petrank, The Hardness of Approximation, Theorem 4.4](https://csaws.cs.technion.ac.il/~erez/Papers/gap.pdf)

## Scope and relation to earlier work

The Gram extraction is the underlying spectral step in earlier orthogonal tensor algorithms. Its finite-field counterpart appears in the checked Problem 1.8 development. The degree lower bounds here concern the ordinary action-and-orthogonality equations on the real spectrally simple family, including its small sparse proofs. The invariant-degree classification uses contractions on the original tensor coefficients. The graph input is supplied explicitly, and the statement about unbounded size uses existence of expanding graphs. This is an informal proof.
