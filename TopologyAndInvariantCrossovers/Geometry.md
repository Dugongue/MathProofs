# Tensor isomorphism in rational homotopy with fixed cup products

**Theorem.** Rational homotopy equivalence of closed simply connected smooth seven-manifolds, presented by their rational cup product and Bianchi–Massey tensor tables, is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb Q$. Hardness holds on pairs of spin manifolds with the same torsion-free integral cohomology ring, zero first Pontryagin class, and trivial ordinary rational Massey products. Here trivial means that every defined product of positive-degree classes contains zero, in every length at least three. For each input tensor side bound $n$, one cohomology ring serves every encoded pair of side at most $n$, with second Betti number $6n+106$.

The input model uses explicit finite rational invariant tables. The realization theorem guarantees smooth manifolds for the tables constructed here. The reduction does not require producing a triangulation or extracting these invariants from a triangulation.

## 1. Cohomology from a space of minors

Put $k=\mathbb Q$, $K=k^2$, and take eight labeled vector spaces $U_1,\ldots,U_4,V_1,\ldots,V_4$. Give the $U_i$ dimensions $n+10,n+11,n+12,2$, respectively, and the $V_i$ dimensions $3,4,5,6$. Write $P$ for the sum of these eight spaces and $X=P\otimes K$. With basis $e_1,e_2$ of $K$, define

$$
[a,b]=(a\otimes e_1)(b\otimes e_2)-(a\otimes e_2)(b\otimes e_1)\ \in\operatorname{Sym}^2 X.
$$

Let $E$ be spanned by these minors for $a,b$ in the same block or in two distinct blocks other than the four omitted pairs $U_i,V_i$. Set $D=\operatorname{Sym}^2X/E$ and write $q$ for the quotient. Use the graded algebra with

$$
H^0=k,\quad H^2=X,\quad H^3=D^*,\quad H^4=D,\quad H^5=X^*,\quad H^7=k o.
$$

All other pieces vanish. The cup product on $H^2$ is $q$. Multiplying $x\in X$ and $\alpha\in D^*$ gives the element of $X^*$ with value $\alpha(q(xx'))$ at $x'$. The two dual pairings $X$ with $X^*$ and $D^*$ with $D$ give their respective scalar multiples of $o$. Extend by graded commutativity, the unit, and zero for other products. These rules give an associative Poincaré duality algebra. The only possibly nonzero associativity check on three positive degrees has degrees $2,2,3$, and both products evaluate to $\alpha(q(xx'))o$.

The automorphisms of $H$ are exactly the pairs $(g,c)$ where $g\in\operatorname{GL}(X)$ preserves $E$ and $c\in k^\times$ scales $o$. In fact the surjective cup product induces the map $d=\overline{\operatorname{Sym}^2g}$ on $D$. Poincaré duality then prescribes $c d^{-\mathsf T}$ on $D^*$ and $c g^{-\mathsf T}$ on $X^*$. Conversely those formulas preserve the full product table.

## 2. Intrinsically recovering the eight factors

View $E$ as quadratic functions on $X^*$. In block coordinates, an element of $X^*$ gives a list of two-component columns for each of the eight blocks. Over an algebraic closure of $k$, the common zero set of $E$ is the union of the global rank-at-most-one matrix cone and four further cones. The $i$-th further cone is supported on $U_i^*\oplus V_i^*$ in the first factor, with each of its two column blocks separately of rank at most one. Indeed, each nonzero block has all its nonzero columns on a single line. Any two nonzero blocks with an allowed pair must have the same line. The complete graph on the eight labels with four disjoint edges removed has connected induced graph on any set of at least three labels. Thus distinct lines can occur only if the nonzero support is a single omitted pair.

These five cones are exactly the irreducible components. A rank-one matrix cone is irreducible by its parametrization as outer products, and a product of two of the cones has the same property by parametrizing the two factors independently. The global cone has points with full support. Each further cone has a point with two independent lines, and the four pair supports are different. The global cone has dimension $\dim P+1$, strictly exceeding the dimension $\dim U_i+\dim V_i+2$ of each other component. Hence every $g$ preserving $E$ preserves the global rank-one cone on the dual.

A linear automorphism preserving the rank-one cone of $P^*\otimes K^*$ is a tensor product of factor automorphisms in these dimensions. Here is a proof over $k$. The maximal linear spaces of rank-one tensors are $P^*\otimes\ell$ for lines $\ell\subset K^*$ and $p\otimes K^*$ for lines $p\subset P^*$. Indeed a sum of two nonzero simple tensors with independent factors on both sides has matrix rank two. Comparing two independent elements with a common factor then forces every element of their linear space to share that factor. The two types of maximal spaces have different dimensions. Choose a basis of $K^*$ and examine the maps $A_1,A_2$ on its two $P^*$-fibers. The image of each $p$ tensored with the sum of the two basis vectors is simple, so $A_1p$ and $A_2p$ are proportional for every $p$. An invertible linear operator preserving every line is scalar, as follows by considering a basis and its pairwise sums. Thus $A_1,A_2$ differ by a scalar, giving the required tensor product over $k$.

Inside each of the four smaller components, its intrinsic singular locus is the union of the cones where either of the two blocks is zero. To see this, a rank-one $r\times 2$ cone is smooth off zero by solving the minors on the chart of a nonzero entry. At zero its tangent dimension is $2r$, greater than its dimension $r+1$ because $r\ge2$. Tangent dimensions add in the product of the cones. The spans of the two singular components are therefore precisely the separate block spaces tensored with $K^*$. All eight block dimensions are distinct. Consequently these eight spaces, and thus the corresponding eight blocks in $P$, are preserved individually. Conversely any product of an independently block-diagonal map on $P$ and a common map on $K$ preserves $E$, since a change of basis on $K$ scales the bracket by its determinant.

The equations studied here are a binomial edge ideal, using the graph on individual columns whose cliques and missing pairs are specified above. Its five components have the direct description just proved. The rank-one preserver argument is the unequal-dimension case of the classical tensor product preserver theorem. [Herzog–Hibi–Hreinsdóttir–Kahle–Rauh, Binomial edge ideals](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.4717) · [Marcus–Moyls, Transformations on tensor product spaces](https://msp.org/pjm/1959/9-4/pjm-v9-n4-p17-s.pdf)

## 3. The Plücker component of the higher operation

In degree eight the Bianchi–Massey source is

$$
\mathcal B^8(H)=\ker\left(\operatorname{Sym}^2 E\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}^4 X\right),
\tag{1}
$$

where the displayed map multiplies quadratics. One can check (1) also with the unital definition of Crowley–Nordström. The kernel of the cup product on graded symmetric squares has no part below degree four, and in degree four is the graph of $-q$ in $\operatorname{Sym}^2X\oplus (1\cdot D)$. Split $\operatorname{Sym}^2X=E\oplus J$, with $q:J\to D$ invertible. In the degree-eight full symmetrization, products of two graph elements from $J$ are detected injectively in the component $1^2\operatorname{Sym}^2D$. After those terms vanish, the mixed graph terms from $E$ and $J$ are detected injectively in the component $1\cdot(\operatorname{Sym}^2X)D$. Only the terms of $\operatorname{Sym}^2E$ remain, proving (1).

The eight blocks grade (1) by multidegree, which every cohomology transporter preserves by Section 2. In the multidegree that uses each $U_i$ once and no $V_i$, the source of the multiplication map is the sum of three copies of $U_1\otimes U_2\otimes U_3\otimes U_4$, with a common factor $(\bigwedge^2 K)^{\otimes 2}$. Its three summands come from the three perfect matchings of the four data blocks. For fixed basis coordinates $u_i\in U_i$, their relation in the polynomial ring is precisely

$$
[u_1,u_2][u_3,u_4]-[u_1,u_3][u_2,u_4]+[u_1,u_4][u_2,u_3]=0 .
\tag{2}
$$

This is the Plücker relation for two rows. It is the only relation for those four basis coordinates. Evaluate the three products on four columns $(e_1,e_2,e_1,e_2)$ and $(e_1,e_1,e_2,e_2)$ to obtain two linearly independent evaluation rows. Terms from different choices of the one basis coordinate in each block lie in distinct polynomial multidegrees by basis coordinate. Therefore this Bianchi–Massey component is exactly one copy of $U_1\otimes U_2\otimes U_3\otimes U_4$, with the common determinant-square action of $\operatorname{GL}(K)$. Its identification is given by (2).

Pad the three input tensor factors to $U_1,U_2,U_3$ by zero in added coordinates, viewing the input as a trilinear form $T$. This preserves and reflects isomorphism for inputs of a common format. Any padded transporter restricts to a transporter on the essential supports, which are the images of contractions from the other two slots in the coordinate duals. Equal original side dimensions let such maps extend back to an original transporter. Fix a nonzero basis covector $u_4^*$ on $U_4$. Define $\mathcal F_T\colon\mathcal B^8(H)\to k o$ to be $T\otimes u_4^*$ times $o$ on the component (2) and zero on other multidegrees, taking $e_1\wedge e_2$ as generator of the determinant line.

If $T,T'$ are isomorphic, use a transporter on the first three data blocks and identity maps on the remaining blocks and the two-dimensional factor. The resulting automorphism of $H$ relates $\mathcal F_T,\mathcal F_{T'}$. Conversely an automorphism of $H$ relating $\mathcal F_T,\mathcal F_{T'}$ must preserve the component (2). Its group on this component is blockwise general linear with the one extra determinant-square multiplier, and the target $k o$ allows a further scalar. A nonzero pure covector in the fourth factor can only yield a proportional one if two nonzero decomposable tensors in the first-three versus fourth grouping agree. The other factor is then proportional to the transported $T'$, and a scalar on one data block absorbs that proportion. If one input tensor is zero, the comparison holds exactly when the other is zero. This proves exact reflection.

The forward construction fixes $o$, so the hardness result also applies when the input fixes the rational orientation class. The reverse reduction then records $o$ as one additional marked vector.

## 4. Manifold realization and trivial Massey products

Crowley–Nordström’s rational realization theorem applies in dimension seven and with connectivity one. Given $H,\mathcal F_T$, take $p_1=0$. Its rational-manifold classification theorem then says that rational homotopy equivalence is exactly equivalence of the pairs $(H,\mathcal F_T)$. [Crowley–Nordström, Theorems 1.2 and 1.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04184)

The stronger integral cohomology and spin promises follow from their integral construction. Use the indicated integral bases for $X$ and $E$. The minors spanning $E$ have disjoint monomial pairs up to sign, so the lattice quotient $D$ is free. Clear all denominators of each $T$ first by a nonzero scalar, which preserves its rational orbit. The functional supported on (2) is then integral. Concretely it extends to an integral linear functional on $\operatorname{Sym}^2 E$ by putting its coefficients on the first matching of (2) only. Thus one can use a torsion-free linking model with zero torsion form. It is compatible because the functional is integer-valued. Set the spin characteristic class equal to zero. Proposition 5.6 realizes this model by a simply connected spin seven-manifold, and permits torsion-free $H^3$. Poincaré duality and the universal coefficient theorem give the remaining free integral groups and their products from the truncated cup product and the dual pairings described in Section 1. The integral first Pontryagin class vanishes as twice the chosen spin characteristic class. [Crowley–Nordström, Theorem 4.4 and Proposition 5.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04184)

Every nonzero quadratic in $E$ has rank at least four. Grouping the first and second column coordinates into two lists, its matrix has off-diagonal blocks given by a nonzero skew-symmetric matrix and its transpose, up to a common nonzero scalar. Its rank is twice that of the skew-symmetric matrix. A product of two linear forms has rank at most two. Therefore the cup product of any two nonzero degree-two classes is nonzero.

Consider a defined ordinary Massey product of length $r\ge3$ of positive-degree homogeneous classes. If an input class is zero, choose zero for its representative and set all entries of a defining system spanning its position to zero, keeping the existing entries on intervals avoiding the position. This is still a defining system and gives zero. For nonzero inputs the result has degree $\sum_i d_i-r+2$, where $d_i\ge2$. All $r\ge6$ have output degree above seven. For $r=5$ the only nonzero target degree would be seven with all inputs degree two, and for $r=4$ it would be seven with three degree-two inputs and one degree-three input. Both lists have consecutive nonzero degree-two inputs, whose nonzero cup product prevents definition. For $r=3$ the degree-five case also requires consecutive degree-two inputs, and degree six has zero cohomology. In degree seven the ordinary triple-product indeterminacy contains the product of the nonzero first class with its complementary cohomology degree. That product is all of $H^7$ by duality, so the triple product contains zero. Thus every manifold in the family has trivial ordinary rational Massey products. For $T\ne0$ its Bianchi–Massey tensor is nonetheless nonzero, so it is nonformal. [Crowley–Nordström, Theorem 1.3 and Section 5.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04184)

## 5. Complexity and scope

Constructing the cohomology table and (1) requires rational linear algebra in spaces of polynomial dimensions. The coordinates of $\mathcal F_T$ are computed by reading the first matching of (2), and clearing denominators costs polynomial bit time. The second Betti number is $\dim X=6n+106$. These computations give the hardness reduction on invariant tables.

For the other direction, start with arbitrary explicit rational cup and Bianchi–Massey tables of closed simply connected seven-manifolds. Use the spaces $H^i$ together with the graded cup maps in a fixed-signature tensor system. Compute $E$ from the degree-two cup product and $\mathcal B^8$ by (1). Record $E\hookrightarrow X\otimes X$ and $\mathcal B^8\hookrightarrow E\otimes E$ via the canonical symmetric-square inclusions, and record the map $\mathcal F\colon\mathcal B^8\to H^7$. The degree-zero unit is fixed. Every cup algebra isomorphism induces unique changes on $E,\mathcal B^8$ compatible with the inclusions. The last map imposes exactly the remaining classification condition. Thus all constraints are on tensors of order at most three in a fixed typed system. The Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk compiler gives a many-one reduction to ordinary rational three-tensor isomorphism. [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk, Wildness for tensors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219)

The crossover combines the tensor-system compiler and intrinsic rank-based recovery with the Plücker relations and higher cohomology operations. The same mixed-to-ordinary strategy has a checked instance in the project’s balanced tensor reduction. Crowley–Nordström already give nonformal manifolds with trivial ordinary Massey products. The added result is exact rational TI-completeness inside a fixed-ring family of them. Grochow and collaborators relate integral tensor orbits to Calabi–Yau classification through ordinary cup products. The present reduction uses the rational homotopy problem and places all input dependence in a secondary operation. This proof is informal and publication priority is unestablished.
