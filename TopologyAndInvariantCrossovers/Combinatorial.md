# Schreier-tree lifting for semigroup minimum generation

**Theorem.** Let $G$ be given by its finite Cayley table, and let $H_1,\ldots,H_c$ be explicitly listed subgroups. There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm to decide and, on positive inputs, construct
$$
G=\langle g_1H_1g_1^{-1},\ldots,g_cH_cg_c^{-1},x\rangle
\tag{1}
$$
for the following class of groups. At each nonabelian chief layer, allow the previously treated simple, bounded-order or cyclic-outer-action cases. Also allow arbitrary-order composite chief factors $N=T^\Omega$ for which the monolithic action group is a split permutational wreath product:
$$
G_0/C_{G_0}(N)\ \cong\ T^\Omega\rtimes P.
$$
Here $G_0$ is the relevant chief quotient, $T$ is nonabelian simple, and $P$ acts faithfully and primitively by pure permutations on $\Omega$. The identification is required to respect $N$ and its simple factors. In particular, the previous Cayley-table reduction gives a deterministic polynomial-time minimum generating set algorithm for finite semigroups with maximal subgroups in this larger class. There is no order bound on $T$ or $P$. For example, $T^k\rtimes S_k$ is allowed.

The new lifting step applies when $P$ is noncyclic. Cyclic $P$ at this layer is covered by cyclic outer action.

## 1. Spanning trees and weights on the factors

Write $L=T^\Omega\rtimes P$ and $A=T^\Omega$, with $|\Omega|=k$. Use the following equivalent description. For $\ell\in L$, its projection $p_\ell\in P$ sends a sheet $v$ to $p_\ell v$. The corresponding transport of the copy of $T$ is conjugation by an edge weight $t_{\ell,v}\in T$. For fixed $p_\ell$, all $k$ edge weights can be chosen independently. The weight along a path is the product in order of composition. Inverting an edge inverts its weight.

Suppose the projections of the marked subgroups $K_i\le L$ together with $p\in P$ generate $P$. We can conjugate any chosen $K_i$ by an element of $A$ while its projection stays fixed, and can choose $y$ arbitrarily above $p$. Use a graph on $\Omega$ with the edges of $p$ and those of elements of the $K_i$. This graph is connected since the projected group is transitive. Simultaneous conjugation by $(b_v)_{v\in\Omega}\in A$ changes each edge weight $t$ from $u$ to $v$ to $b_v t b_u^{-1}$. On a spanning tree the $b_v$ can be chosen recursively so every weight becomes $1$, fixing one root gauge if desired. Such a change of coordinates conjugates all the fixed $K_i$ at once and preserves existence of completions. It is the spanning-tree normalization of a group-valued edge labeling.

Write $J$ for a candidate generated subgroup. A closed path at a chosen root is a word in the marked subgroup elements and $y$ fixing that factor. Its weight lies in the image of the factor stabilizer in $J$. If the root has two closed paths with weights generating $T$, and some element of $J\cap A$ has nontrivial root coordinate, then $J\cap A$ projects onto the root copy of $T$. Indeed that projection is normal in the root stabilizer's image $T$. Transitivity gives surjectivity onto every copy.

We use the elementary subdirect-product structure of powers of a nonabelian simple group. Every subgroup that projects onto each copy is a product of full diagonal groups on a partition of the copies, with automorphism twists in the individual diagonal links. For completeness, project onto the first $k-1$ copies and apply induction. Goursat's argument for the resulting subdirect group in $T^b\times T$ either adds an independent last copy or gives the graph of an epimorphism $T^b\to T$. Such an epimorphism factors through one copy as an automorphism because its factor images are commuting normal subgroups of $T$. This proves the assertion. The partition is intrinsic: two different factors belong to the same block precisely when their joint image is a graph of a group isomorphism. Thus if the subdirect subgroup is $J\cap A$, the partition is $P$-invariant. By primitivity, it is either the singleton partition or the one-block partition. In the singleton case $J\cap A=A$, hence $J=L$. In the one-block case write $J\cap A=D$, a full diagonal, so $J$ normalizes $D$.

In this one-block case all diagonal links are inner automorphisms after normalizing the link at the root to the identity. In fact write $D=\{(\beta_v(t))_v:t\in T\}$. If $\ell\in J$ induces $\gamma_\ell\in \operatorname{Aut}(T)$ on this parameter, the link relation is
$$
c_{t_{\ell,v}}\beta_v=\beta_{p_\ell v}\gamma_\ell
\qquad (v\in\Omega),
\tag{2}
$$
where $c_t$ means conjugation by $t$. In $\operatorname{Out}(T)$, equality of the classes of two $\beta_v$'s is preserved under simultaneous sheet motion because both classes are multiplied on the right by the same element $[\gamma_\ell]^{-1}$. Again primitivity gives either all classes equal or all distinct. In the latter case an element of $P$ fixing a point would fix every sheet by (2), so the action would be regular. In a primitive regular group, cosets of any subgroup form an invariant partition, hence the group is cyclic of prime order. This contradicts the noncyclic hypothesis. Thus all links are inner, and (2) also forces every $\gamma_\ell$ to be inner.

## 2. The lifting lemma

**Lemma.** In this split primitive situation, if $p$ has at least two cycles, the marked subgroups can all be fixed arbitrarily and one can choose $y$ above $p$ with $J=L$. If $p$ is a single cycle, fix all marked subgroups except any one whose projection acts nontrivially. A conjugation of that reserved subgroup by an element of $A$, and a choice of $y$ above $p$, suffice to generate $L$.

*Several cycles.* Form a tree by first including a spanning path of each $p$-cycle and then connecting these paths by selected marked-subgroup edges. This is possible by connectivity. In particular each cycle retains one unused $p$-edge. Choose arbitrary lifts for the included $p$-edges and normalize all the tree weights to $1$ by a gauge change. The unused $p$-edge on each cycle still has independently adjustable weight.

Take a generating pair $a,b$ of $T$ with $a$ an involution and $b$ of prime order $q$. The order $q$ is odd because two involutions generate a dihedral group, which cannot be nonabelian simple. Such a pair exists by King's theorem. [King, *Generation of finite simple groups by an involution and an element of prime order*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.04717)

Let $m$ be the order of $p$. Choose a cycle $C$ of length $l$ with $v_2(l)=v_2(m)$. Put $a$ on its unused edge, $b$ on the unused edge of any other cycle, and $1$ on all other unused edges. After following tree paths to the cycles, the two closed walks at a root in $C$ have weights $a,b$. The element $y^m\in J\cap A$ has value $a$ on $C$, because $m/l$ is odd. It has values in $\langle b\rangle$ on the second cycle and value $1$ elsewhere. Therefore $y^{mq}$ has support exactly $C$, which is proper and nonempty. The root stabilizer image is $T$, so $J\cap A$ is subdirect by Section 1. A full diagonal cannot contain a nonidentity element of proper support. This gives $J=L$.

*One cycle.* At least one marked subgroup has nontrivial projection, since $P$ is noncyclic. Reserve such a subgroup $K_j$, pick $h\in K_j$ moving a sheet $u$ to a distinct sheet $v$, and choose $y$-weights equal to $1$ except on the one cycle edge omitted from the spanning tree. Call this edge's weight $a$, and take the root to be the target of that edge. The word $y^k$ is in $J\cap A$ and has weight $a$ at the root. Conjugate $K_j$ by an element of $A$ that varies only at $v$. The weight $b$ of its resulting $h$-edge from $u$ to $v$ now ranges bijectively over $T$. The tree paths to and from that edge have identity weight, so it gives a root loop of weight $b$. We will always choose $a\ne1$ and $\langle a,b\rangle=T$. This is possible for any nonidentity $a$ by $3/2$-generation of the nonabelian finite simple groups. Moreover there are at least two choices of generating partner, since $b$ and $ab$ are distinct and both generate together with $a$. [Guralnick–Kantor, *Probabilistic generation of finite simple groups*](https://doi.org/10.1006/jabr.2000.8357)

To exclude the full diagonal, apply (2) to $y$ in any putative normalized diagonal. Number the sheets from the root along the tree in cyclic order. Each tree edge has weight $1$, so the links are $\beta_i=\gamma_y^{-i}$ with $\beta_0=1$. The remaining edge gives $\gamma_y^k=c_a$. By Section 1, $\gamma_y=c_d$ for some $d\in T$. Since $T$ is centerless this means $d^k=a$.

If a prime $r$ divides both $k$ and $|T|$, pick $a$ of $r$-power order maximal among the $r$-parts of element orders in $T$. Such an element exists by taking a power of an element attaining that maximum, and its order exceeds $1$ by Cauchy's theorem. The $k$-th power of every element has smaller $r$-part, so $d^k=a$ is impossible. Choose any generating partner $b$.

If $\gcd(k,|T|)=1$, take any $a\ne1$. The map $d\mapsto d^k$ is bijective on $T$, with inverse given by the power inverse to $k$ modulo $|T|$. Thus there is at most one normalized full diagonal that $J$ could normalize, since $d$ determines $\gamma_y$ and then every link $\beta_i$. Fix this candidate. While varying the marked conjugator only at $v$, the $h$-edge weights can change only at edges that start or end at $v$. There are at most two such edges, and $k\ge3$ because no permutation group of degree at most two is noncyclic. An unchanged edge determines $\gamma_h$ uniquely from (2) for this fixed diagonal. Its $u$-to-$v$ equation then permits at most one value of $b$, since $t\mapsto c_t$ is injective in $T$. Choose one of the at least two generating partners avoiding that value. This makes normalization impossible. In either case $y^k$ is nontrivial in the root coordinate, so $J\cap A$ is subdirect, and the diagonal exclusion gives $J=L$. This completes the lemma.

## 3. Applying the lemma to raw Cayley tables

At a nonabelian chief layer of the algorithm for (1), let $N$ be the minimal normal subgroup of the current group $G_0$ and let $L=G_0/C_{G_0}(N)$. The fact that any successful quotient tuple can be used is the mixed subgroup and element lifting lemma in the previous result. In particular the algorithm can fix one successful tuple for $G_0/N$ before computing lifts. From that tuple, all choices of the lift of each conjugator form one coset of $N$, and likewise for the free element. In $L$ these give the marked subgroups $K_i$ and the free coset, with the full freedom allowed in Section 2 since $N$ maps isomorphically to $A$.

As explained in the earlier crown algorithm, there is a polynomial bound on the number of tagged, locally generating tuples in $L$ that fail when lifted to generate $G_0$. The bound $|N|^3\lfloor\log_2 |G_0|\rfloor$ suffices. Indeed the preceding components of the relevant crown quotient are fixed. A last coordinate with full image in $L$ can fail only when its tagged tuple is an automorphic image of one of those components under an automorphism inducing the identity modulo the socle. The exact count of lifts into each proper group is not needed. Any representative of a tagged tuple can be tested directly by table generation in $G_0$. [Prior crown lifting argument, Sections 1–3](../TensorIsomorphismAndAlgebraicCrossovers/Groups.md) · [Lucchini–Thakkar, *The minimum generating set problem*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07633)

List and deduplicate the at most $|N|$ tagged options for each marked slot, retaining representative lifts, and list the free-element options. If $p$ has multiple cycles, put the free slot last. If it has one cycle, put one marked slot with nontrivial projection next-to-last and the free slot last. Section 2 proves that every prefix before the reserved one or two slots has a locally generating completion, even after arbitrarily fixing any other marked slots. For prefixes reaching the reserved slots, exact feasibility can also be checked by scanning the at most $|N|^2$ choices for those slots. The input is explicit, so this is polynomial. Traverse the prefix tree, pruning infeasible prefixes. The exact feasibility check gives polynomial delay between locally generating tagged tuples, and the crown failure bound means that polynomially many tuples suffice, or the enumeration exhausts them. Apply the earlier algorithms at simple, bounded-chief-order, cyclic-outer, and abelian layers. Recursing on the chief quotient takes logarithmically many layers. The resulting polynomial-time group algorithm plugs into the earlier semigroup construction of an optimal generating set. The claim is in the full Cayley-table model.

The wreath condition itself is testable in polynomial time at the layer. Find the simple factors of $N$ as its minimal nontrivial normal subgroups by normal closure within $N$. Primitivity of the induced transitive permutation action can be tested by enumerating subsets of the $k$ factors and checking that no proper nonsingleton subset is disjoint from or equal to every translate. There are $2^k\le |N|$ subsets. The pure-permutation condition holds exactly when the setwise stabilizer of a single simple factor acts on that factor through inner automorphisms. The forward implication is immediate. For the converse identify each factor with the root factor by an element transporting the root to it. In these coordinates any transporter from one factor to another differs from the chosen identifications by a root-factor stabilizer element, and hence acts by an inner map on the coordinate. Thus $L$ embeds in $T^k\rtimes P$ and contains the full base $T^k$. Its projection is onto $P$, so its image is the whole semidirect product. Testing inner automorphisms can be done by scanning the root copy of $T$ for a conjugating element. The recognition algorithm needs no supplied splitting or generators.
