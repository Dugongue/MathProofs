# A one-variable integral ring realizes independent determinant differences

Let
\[
 R=\mathbb Z[t],\qquad R'=R[1/t].
\]
The input consists of **two independent** framed three-tensor pairs \(x,y\) over explicitly presented finite fields \(K_x,K_y\), which need not be the same field or have the same characteristic. Each pair has a matching positive format internally. Let \(\alpha\in(K_x^\times)^3\) and \(\beta\in(K_y^\times)^3\) be arbitrary prescribed determinant triples. Write \(A_\alpha(x)\), \(A_\beta(y)\) for exact determinant feasibility, and \(B(x)\) for ordinary equivalence over \(K_x\). The target uses ordinary tensor isomorphism on free modules, with no named parts or operators.

**Theorem.** There is a uniform deterministic polynomial-time map to one pair of ordinary free three-tensors \(\mathcal T(x,\alpha;y,\beta)\) over \(R\), used with identical coefficients upon each base change, such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal T \text{ isomorphic over }R
   &\Longleftrightarrow A_\alpha(x)\land A_\beta(y),\\
 \mathcal T \text{ isomorphic over }R'
   &\Longleftrightarrow A_\alpha(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
The localization defect is thus exactly \(A_\alpha(x)\land\neg A_\beta(y)\). Every height-one localization and the fraction field give an ordinary isomorphism on **every** output. Ordinary isomorphism at all prime localizations, all finite quotients, or the profinite completion of \(R\) is in each case equivalent just to \(B(x)\), independently of the second pair. The written coefficient degree in \(t\) is at most the larger of one and the two field presentation degrees. Integer coefficient bit lengths are \(O(\log p_x+\log p_y+\log L)\), where \(p_x,p_y\) are the two field characteristics and \(L\) is the input length.

This shrinks the prior universal characteristic-zero ring from five polynomial variables to one, replaces three inverted generators by a single \(t\), and allows independent comparisons instead of two determinant tests on one pair. It needs no primitive-element or discrete-logarithm algorithm. The proof uses the earlier free congruence/linked-system packing from *DirectionalSignFusion/transfer.md*, a permanent determinant screen in \(\mathbb Z[t]\), and a separate ordinary disjunction which is stable under domain base change. These are paper proofs.

## 1. A permanent determinant screen with one horizontal pin

For \(K=\mathbb F_p[z]/(f(z))\) with given monic irreducible \(f\) of degree \(n\ge2\), let \(I=(p,\widetilde f(t))\) in \(R\), with canonical integer lifts of its coefficients, and identify \(E=R/I\) with the input \(K\), sending \(t\) to \(\theta=z\bmod f\). For \(n=1\) use \(I=(p,t-1)\), identifying \(K\) with its prime field. Put
\[
 J=(t-2).
\]
In all cases \(\theta\ne0\) and \(\theta-2\ne0\) in \(K\), with \(2\) reduced in the given characteristic. For \(n\ge2\) this follows because \(\theta\) is not in the prime field. Thus \(I,J\) are comaximal and \(t\) is a unit modulo \(I\). For example, the polynomial lift of \((\theta-2)^{-1}\) multiplied by \(t-2\) is a CRT mask equal to one at \(I\) and zero at \(J\).

Consider a tensor transporter modulo \(I\) whose three linear maps are also required to be the identity modulo \(J\). In \(R\), a unit congruent to \(1\) modulo \(J\) is exactly \(1\), since \(R^\times=\{\pm1\}\). In \(R'\), units are exactly \(\pm t^k\), \(k\in\mathbb Z\), by unique factorization in \(\mathbb Z[t]\). The quotient \(R'/JR'\) is \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\). Hence the pin evaluates such a unit to \(\pm2^k\), which is one only for the unit \(1\). Thus the same determinant constraint remains on each role before and after inverting \(t\). The pin does not need to distinguish two residue values of a finite-field generator. It uses the infinite order of \(2\) in its horizontal quotient.

The previous free congruence diagram applies with these two ideals. Briefly, comparing residue products \(T,U:X/IX\times Y/IY\to Z/IZ\), keep free roles \(X,Y,Z\) and the tensor product role \(P=X\otimes Y\). Force the induced map on \(P\) by the bilinear tensor-product map. Record the inverse image of the product graph inside \(P\oplus Z\) by the image of the free linear arrow
\[
 (v,z_1,z_2)\ \longmapsto\
 (v,\widetilde T(v)+p z_1+\widetilde f(t) z_2)
\]
and its counterpart with \(U\). In the degree-one presentation use \(t-1\) instead of \(\widetilde f\). Require an invertible map on this free cover as part of the diagram. Ordinary maps respecting the linked graph images are exactly residue transporters. Conversely whenever the graph is preserved, write the difference of the two product maps as \(pD_1+\widetilde fD_2\). A triangular map on the free cover has the required correction columns \(D_1,D_2\), so reflection and lift hold even though the graph preimage need not itself be free. For each tested role impose its identity reduction modulo \(J\) by the same free-cover graph of the identity, with \(J\)'s single generator and with a copy of that role fixed by point marks. The marks can be expressed by maps from a fixed scalar line.

The dimension of a role can be one, in which case its determinant-one group is trivial. In larger dimension every special-linear residue matrix is a product of elementary matrices by row elimination. Lift each elementary entry with the CRT mask above so that it is zero modulo \(J\), and multiply the lifts. The resulting integral map has determinant one and is identity at the pin. This proves that the screen imposes **exactly** the three residue determinant-one constraints over both \(R\) and \(R'\). For a prescribed tuple, apply the corresponding inverse determinant frame change to the residue target before lifting; this makes determinant one on the modified pair equivalent to the desired determinant tuple on the original pair. This is only a residue operation and needs no integral unit representing a requested determinant.

Denote the resulting ordinary pair by \(Q(x,\alpha)\). It is ordinarily isomorphic over \(R\) or \(R'\) exactly when \(A_\alpha(x)\) holds. At a prime of \(R\) outside \(I\) the graph condition disappears, and the identity on the original roles satisfies the remaining pin. At the unique maximal ideal \(I\) the pin disappears; arbitrary residue invertible matrices lift over the local ring, so \(Q(x,\alpha)\) there tests \(B(x)\), regardless of \(\alpha\). The graph and pin claims here are exact over every characteristic-zero domain base change with the stated image ideals.

The plain free tensor packing is the one proved in the previous transfer, using a two-idempotent algebra with distinct integer character labels on each slot, master-to-private occurrence maps, and the product and graph maps in the private slots. It reflects diagram isomorphism over any characteristic-zero domain: the nilpotent ideal is intrinsic, the two idempotent lifts are unique because the relevant operators act injectively, and all slot labels remain distinct. Its conversion to a free tensor uses fixed partitions and then the raw identity-slice partition erasure theorem. The construction copies written coefficients and adjoins integer, zero and identity blocks. Thus the same output coefficients give the reflection and lift over \(R,R'\), their prime localizations, their fraction fields, and the complete characteristic-zero domains used below. It does not require reflection of that packing after an arbitrary nonreduced finite quotient. ## 2. An ordinary scalar switch and dimension-preserving Boolean assembly

The scalar bimap pair \(S_t=(1,t)\) of shape \(1\times1\times1\) is an ordinary YES over a ring exactly when \(t\) is a unit. Use the following positive decoder on ordinary comparisons:
\[
  Q(x,\alpha)\ \wedge\ \bigl(Q(y,\beta)\ \vee\ S_t\bigr).
  \tag{*}
\]
This decoder is compiled by a single uniform polynomial-size ordinary tensor pair, with the same coefficients over every domain in this proof. For completeness, here is the algebraic assembly.

Over a domain \(D\), a bimap \(T:U\times V\to W\) has the standard free triangular algebra on three diagonal copies of \(D\), with \(U,V,W\) in positions \(01,12,02\), and sole nonzero corner product \(T\). The strictly upper ideal is the largest nilpotent ideal, since the quotient \(D^3\) has no nonzero nilpotent ideal. An isomorphism takes the ordered diagonal idempotents to the same directed corner positions after alignment. Two sets \((e_i),(f_i)\) agreeing modulo the largest nilpotent ideal align by the unit \(\sum_i f_i e_i\), which is one modulo the ideal. The nonzero directed corners then force the total order, and their product equation is exactly TI. Conversely a tensor transporter gives such an algebra map. A unit in this proof exists even when \(D\) is not a field, as a nilpotent perturbation of \(1\) is inverted by a finite series. To distinguish fixed tags replace the three diagonal rings by \(D[u]/(u^j)\) for different fixed \(j\), acting by evaluation at zero on each corner. The same nilpotent ideal argument uses the diagonal \(u\)-ideals as well. The diagonal corner ranks distinguish the tags. Each such algebra has only trivial central idempotents, as a central idempotent is diagonal and all nonzero corners force its diagonal values to agree.

A product of separately tagged algebras thus gives ordinary AND. To give OR of \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\), wrap each ordered tagged pair into a centrally indecomposable triangular algebra \(C(-,-)\), by taking its multiplication tensor as the tensor for the outer triangular construction. Compare
\[
 C(a,c)\times C(b,d)\quad\text{with}\quad C(a,d)\times C(b,c).
\]
An isomorphism must match the two central factors, and the straight and crossed matches are precisely the two alternatives. The tensor wrapper reflects ordinary isomorphism of each unital algebra: evaluate an arbitrary multiplication isotopy at its two units, see that the resulting multipliers are units, and cancel them to obtain a unital algebra isomorphism. Bijective left multiplication supplies a right inverse of the unit candidate; its injectivity then also gives the left inverse. This elementary normalization is valid over arbitrary commutative rings. The number of wrappers in (*) is fixed. Their free structure constants copy the supplied entries, with added zero and unit coefficients. It follows that the Boolean assembly works on the same displayed coefficients before and after localization. The original triangular tensor encoding over fields is due to Grochow and Qiao. [Tensor–algebra isomorphism encoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

Over \(R\), \(S_t\) is false, and over \(R'\) it is true, while both permanent determinant screens retain their exact conditions. This proves the two central formulas. At any prime supporting \(Q(y,\beta)\)'s data ideal, \(t\) is a unit, so its switch alternative is true. At any other prime the second data condition vanishes, and its permanent pin is met by identity maps. Thus the second clause is always local YES, even if \(y\) is ordinary NO and even if the two data ideals coincide. The first clause has the unique possible local failure at \(I_x\), where it is exactly the ordinary answer \(B(x)\). This proves the claimed prime behavior, including at the horizontal prime \((t-2)\), since the corresponding data ideal vanishes there.

## 3. Exact behavior at finite quotients and completion

If \(B(x)\) holds, the preceding paragraph supplies ordinary isomorphism at every prime localization. For any ideal \(N\) with \(R/N\) finite, its Artinian ring decomposes as a finite product of localizations at the maximal ideals over \(N\). Reduce the local witnesses and assemble the resulting matrices componentwise. Thus the output is isomorphic in every finite quotient. Their finite sets of invertible transporter triples form an inverse system indexed by reverse inclusion of ideals of finite index; it is directed since intersections still have finite index. The finite intersection property supplies a coherent family of triples even when the bonding maps are not individually surjective. This gives an isomorphism over the profinite completion, since the compatible inverses are also determined and compatible. Conversely an isomorphism in that completion reduces to every finite quotient.

To see that passing all finite quotient tests really requires \(B(x)\), let \(p\) be the characteristic of \(K_x\) and \(\theta\) its chosen residue parameter. Let \(\mathcal O\) be \(\mathbb Z_p[z]/\widetilde f_x(z)\) for degree at least two, or \(\mathbb Z_p\) with \(\theta_0=1\) for degree one. In the former case \(\widetilde f_x\) is the same monic lift, irreducible over \(\mathbb Q_p\) by irreducibility of its reduction and Gauss' lemma; \(\mathcal O\) is a finite free \(\mathbb Z_p\)-domain with residue field \(K_x\) modulo \(p\). Set \(D=\mathcal O[[u]]\) and map \(t\mapsto\theta_0+u\), where \(\theta_0\) is the lift of \(\theta\). The finite rings \(D/(p,u)^k\) all receive coefficients from \(R\) through quotients of finite index. Hence ordinary isomorphism at all those \(R\)-quotients gives ordinary isomorphism of the images at every \(D/(p,u)^k\), and by the same finite intersection argument gives an actual isomorphism over the complete domain \(D\). Over this domain the linked-system reflection of Section 1 and the Boolean reflection of Section 2 apply. At its residue map the first data equation is still the original tensor comparison, and the first pin maps to a unit because \(\theta-2\ne0\). A \(D\)-isomorphism therefore implies an ordinary transporter for \(x\) over \(K_x\). This proves all the claimed converses without assuming the packing reflects isomorphism over nonreduced finite quotients.

## 4. Size, an exact ordinary-difference consequence, and the dimension boundary

All graph coefficients are lifted in degree less than their respective field degree, together with the generators \(p,\widetilde f,t-2\). To construct a witness from a supplied residue transporter in the permanent screen, lift each elementary coefficient by multiplying its degree-\(<n\) representative by the CRT mask \((t-2)\widetilde{(\theta-2)^{-1}}\). This mask has degree at most \(n\) and polynomial coefficient bit length. A polynomial-size elimination list gives polynomial-size integral entries after multiplication. Cover correction coefficients can be computed by division by monic \(\widetilde f\), followed by coefficient reduction modulo \(p\); for the horizontal ideal use evaluation at \(2\) and exact division by \(t-2\). In the localization the switch uses just the unit \(t\). The output tensors themselves need none of those computed witness products. The graph covers, marked-to-plain packing, and Boolean assembly only copy those initial equation coefficients and introduce bounded integer or index tags, so the stated degree and coefficient-bit bounds apply. The rectangle sizes stay polynomial because the input modulus and all label counts are explicit and the Boolean expression has fixed size.

Padding all three modes by unused lines converts arbitrary ordinary tensor isomorphism over each finite field to the corresponding exact determinant-one test: extend any ordinary transporter on the new line of each mode to correct its determinant, and restrict a padded transporter to the original intrinsic supports for the converse. Thus (*) also gives an unconditional realization of the Boolean difference of **two independent ordinary TI inputs**, possibly over different characteristics, as one localization defect. If the first ordinary input is YES then every prime and every finite quotient test passes, even when the second input is NO and the global isomorphism fails. No collapse of signed TI is assumed. This relates the independent directional switch of the previous contribution to one smaller universal arithmetic target.

Finally, dimension two is minimal **for fixed finite-type characteristic-zero domain schemes that realize every input finite field as an actual residue quotient of the target**. In fact a fixed finite-type characteristic-zero domain \(S\) of dimension at most one has bounded residue degrees at all finite primes. If \(\operatorname{Frac}(S)\) had positive transcendence degree over \(\mathbb Q\), Noether normalization over \(\mathbb Q\) and clearing finitely many denominators would give, after inverting one integer, an integral finite extension of \(\mathbb Z[1/m][x_1,\ldots,x_d]\), \(d\ge1\). Its dimension would be at least two. Thus the fraction field is a number field. After inverting one integer, \(S\) is a finite module over \(\mathbb Z[1/m]\), by making each of its finitely many algebraic generators integral. At every prime not dividing \(m\), the residue degree is bounded by the fixed number of module generators. At the finitely many excluded primes, the fiber has dimension zero because the prime integer is nonzero in a one-dimensional domain. Each such fiber is a finite-type zero-dimensional algebra over a finite field and is therefore finite. This gives a uniform bound there as well. A fixed \(\mathbb Z[t]\) can instead realize all finite residue fields. The argument does not rule out using linearized coefficients or another non-quotient encoding into a one-dimensional ring.
