# Cube-zero multiplication over an arbitrary finite residue field

Work over $\mathbb F_3$. Consider raw tensors isotopic to multiplication in finite-dimensional unital local associative algebras $R$ with $J^3=0$. Set $K=R/J$, $e=[K : \mathbb F_3]$ and $f=\dim_K({}_KJ^2)$. Write $f_i$ for the left $K$-dimension of the part of $J^2$ on which the right action is the $3^i$-Frobenius twist of the left action. The residue field need not be the base field, and its left and right actions may differ.

**Theorem.** The exact set of attainable determinants in any one specified mode of a relative transporter between such tensors is computable in deterministic $e3^{e\sum_i f_i^2}\operatorname{poly}(L)$ time, where $L$ is the written input length. This gives a polynomial-time decision algorithm for the relative one-sign and odd-automorphism tests on the family $e\sum_i f_i^2=O(\log(2+L))$ with a fixed implicit constant. There is also a polynomial-time algorithm with no bound on $e$ when $f=1$ and all nonzero radical products come from one left-input Frobenius type of odd order. This includes the case of central $K$ and $f=1$. If either conjunct in the independent odd-automorphism gate belongs to this family, their conjunction reduces to one one-sign TI instance. This is an informal proof.

## The raw algebra and radical grades

Recover each unital multiplication table and its three basis changes by the fixed-label regular-module test. Compute the Jacobson radical, check $J^3=0$, and use the finite-field algebra algorithms to test that the quotient is a field and compute its coordinates. Different residue degrees or radical-grade dimensions reject. These steps are polynomial in the explicit table size. [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

For each table choose a copy of the residue field $K$ in $R$. This can be computed without enumerating $K$. A generating element for a residue field given by a multiplication table can be found by linear algebra on Frobenius. For every prime-power divisor $d=p^a$ occurring in the prime-power factorization of $e$, take an element in the subfield of degree $d$ outside its degree-$d/p$ subfield. Each subfield is the kernel of a power of Frobenius minus the identity. The sum of these elements has Frobenius period $e$: if a Frobenius power fixes the sum, the differences in each prime-power subfield must individually lie in the prime field by coprimality of the degrees. For $p\ne3$, summing the difference around its orbit, of $p$-power length, then gives zero difference. The possible $p=3$ difference must also vanish since the sum of differences is zero. For $e=1$ take 1. Lift the generating element to $a\in R$ and compute its separable minimal polynomial $p\in\mathbb F_3[X]$ by linear dependence of powers. In the commutative subalgebra $\mathbb F_3[a]$, the element $p'(a)$ is invertible. Its residue is nonzero, and the inverse of a unit in a finite commutative subalgebra is a power of that unit. Compute the inverse by linear equations. Replacing $a$ by $a-p(a)p'(a)^{-1}$ squares the ideal containing the error $p(a)$. Two steps send the error from $J$ to $J^2$ and then to $J^4=0$. The resulting root defines the desired section.

Write $W=J^2$ and $V=J/W$. The inclusion $W\subset J$ has a $K$-bimodule complement since $K\otimes_{\mathbb F_3}K$ is a product of fields. Find that complement by solving linear equations for a projection onto $W$ that commutes with both field actions. In these coordinates the multiplication is given by the field operations, the two field actions on each grade and the bilinear map $b:V\times V\to W$. All other radical products vanish.

An algebra isomorphism can be assumed to take the chosen copy of the source residue field to the chosen target copy. The conjugacy part of Wedderburn–Malcev aligns the target copies by conjugation by a unit of $1+J$, which is the identity on all radical grades and has determinant one. An isomorphism inducing a field map $\sigma:K_{\rm src}\to K_{\rm tgt}$ is consequently determined for sign purposes by its induced grade actions $g:V_{\rm src}\to V_{\rm tgt}$ and $h:W_{\rm src}\to W_{\rm tgt}$. Both intertwine left and right field multiplication by $s$ with the corresponding multiplication by $\sigma(s)$. They satisfy
$$
 b_{\rm tgt}(gx,gy)=h\,b_{\rm src}(x,y).
$$
Conversely, every such triple $(\sigma,g,h)$ defines a blockwise algebra isomorphism in the chosen coordinates. Every isomorphism with these grade maps has determinant $\det_{\mathbb F_3}(\sigma)\det_{\mathbb F_3}(g)\det_{\mathbb F_3}(h)$ in the filtration bases. [Lam, *A First Course in Noncommutative Rings*, Wedderburn–Malcev theorem](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4419-8616-0)

Use the deterministic algorithm of Lenstra to compute one field isomorphism between the two residue presentations and enumerate its $e$ Frobenius translates. For each $\sigma$, decompose $W$ into its Frobenius bimodule types on both sides. For a field generator $\alpha$, type $i$ is the kernel of the difference between right multiplication by $\alpha$ and left multiplication by $\alpha^{3^i}$. These kernels give a direct sum because $K\otimes_{\mathbb F_3}K$ is isomorphic to a product of $e$ copies of $K$, one for each Frobenius twist. A map $h$ intertwining both actions for $\sigma$ must preserve the types, since field isomorphisms commute with Frobenius. Reject a mismatch of their dimensions. On each matched type enumerate invertible matrices of size $f_i\times f_i$ for the left-$\sigma$-semilinear map $h$. This costs at most $3^{e\sum_i f_i^2}\operatorname{poly}(L)$ for each $\sigma$. There is no comparable enumeration on $V$. When $f=0$ this loop uses just the unique map between the zero spaces. [Lenstra, Finding isomorphisms between finite fields](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

## Solve the congruence on the tangent quotient with the field actions enforced

Fix $\sigma,h$. Use a coordinate basis of $W_{\rm tgt}$, and let $B_i,C_i$ be the associated square matrices for $b_{\rm tgt},h b_{\rm src}$. Include the transpose of each form in its tuple. On each of $V_{\rm src},V_{\rm tgt}$ make a paired list $\Lambda_{\rm src},\Lambda_{\rm tgt}$ of both left and right multiplication operators, with $s$ and $\sigma(s)$ paired on a basis of $K_{\rm src}$.

Use constructive module isomorphism on $V^*\oplus V$ to find a common $P,Q:V_{\rm src}\to V_{\rm tgt}$ with
$$
 P^{\mathsf T}B_iQ=C_i
$$
for all original and transpose forms, and with both maps intertwining the lists $\Lambda_{\rm src},\Lambda_{\rm tgt}$. Encode this as a module isomorphism of the two operator tuples that give idempotent markers for the two summands, the forms as arrows $V\to V^*$, the listed operators as loops on $V$, and their transposes as loops on $V^*$. A block map $\operatorname{diag}(S,Q)$ intertwines an arrow exactly when $B_iQ=SC_i$. Writing $P=S^{-\mathsf T}$ gives the displayed equations. It intertwines the dual loops precisely when $P\Lambda_{\rm src}=\Lambda_{\rm tgt}P$, while the loops on $V$ impose the same condition on $Q$. A desired congruence provides this module isomorphism with $P=Q=g$. Failure of the module test therefore rejects this branch. [Brooksbank and Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

Put $D=QP^{-1}$ on $V_{\rm tgt}$. It commutes with the operators in $\Lambda_{\rm tgt}$. Transposing the equation for $B_i^{\mathsf T}$ gives $Q^{\mathsf T}B_iP=C_i$. Comparing with the original equation gives $D^{\mathsf T}B_i=B_iD$. Define the paired adjoint algebra
$$
 E=\{(z,a): a^{\mathsf T}B_i=B_i z\ \text{for all forms},\
       z\Lambda=\Lambda z,\ a\Lambda=\Lambda a\
       \text{for all }\Lambda\in\Lambda_{\rm tgt}\}.
$$
Its multiplication is $(z,a)(w,b)=(zw,ba)$, and its involution is $(z,a)^*=(a,z)$. Closure under involution follows by transposing the relation for the transposed form. Both projections can be retained even when the common radical is nonzero. The first projection is a unital representation on the target tangent space, and $(z,a)\mapsto \operatorname{diag}(z,a^{\mathsf T})$ gives a faithful matrix representation for the algebra algorithm. The pair $\Delta=(D,D)$ is an invertible self-adjoint element.

The desired congruences are exactly the maps $g=zP$ for which some $(z,a)\in E^\times$ satisfies $(z,a)^*(z,a)=\Delta$. In the forward direction put $z=gP^{-1}$ and $a=Dz^{-1}$. The congruence implies $z^{\mathsf T}B_i z=B_iD$, hence
$$
 a^{\mathsf T}B_i=z^{-\mathsf T}D^{\mathsf T}B_i
 =z^{-\mathsf T}B_iD=B_i z.
$$
Both entries commute with the target operators. Their norm pair is $(az,az)=(D,D)$. Conversely the adjoint equations and $az=D$ give $z^{\mathsf T}B_i z=D^{\mathsf T}B_i=B_iD$, so $g=zP$ has the required form and field properties.

Compute $N=\operatorname{rad}(E)$ and solve the norm equation in $E/N$ factor by factor as in the base-residue proof. The classical form algorithms are deterministic in fixed characteristic. For completeness, a quotient solution lifts across any nilpotent radical by the following explicit correction. Choose an arbitrary lift $u$, which is invertible, and put $v=(u^*)^{-1}\Delta u^{-1}$. It is self-adjoint and belongs to $1+N$. Choose $3^k$ at least the nilpotence index of $N$, so $v^{3^k}=1$. Pick an integer $j$ satisfying $2j=1\bmod 3^k$. Then $y=v^j$ is a self-adjoint square root of $v$, and $(yu)^*(yu)=\Delta$. In particular the same correction with $\Delta=1$ lifts every quotient unitary element, including in a square-zero extension. If $V=0$ the tangent map is unique and has determinant one, so no adjoint algebra test is needed. [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18 and Proposition 23](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

If a congruence exists, compute the sign of one witness $zP$. All other signs are obtained by multiplying by the determinant character of the constrained isometry group on $V_{\rm tgt}$, which is the group of first projections of the unitary elements of $E$. A tuple isometry respecting the operator lists has a unitary pair with partner its inverse, even when the common radical is nonzero. Determinants in this representation factor through $E/N$, since $1+N$ acts by unipotent maps. On the radical filtration of $V_{\rm tgt}$, let $m_i$ be the multiplicity of the natural simple module of the factor $M_{r_i}(K_i)$, summed over the semisimple grades. The determinant contribution of a quotient unit in that factor is $\operatorname{Norm}_{K_i/\mathbb F_3}(\det_{K_i})^{m_i}$. Thus an exchanged pair contributes an odd determinant exactly when the sum of the multiplicities is odd. A fixed orthogonal factor contributes an odd determinant exactly when $m_i[K_i : \mathbb F_3]$ is odd. A fixed symplectic or unitary factor contributes only a positive determinant. The factors and multiplicities are computable by the finite-field algebra algorithm and linear algebra. This determines the exact sign set in the branch.

## One-dimensional square without a residue degree bound

Suppose $f=1$, and suppose there is one left-input Frobenius type $\tau(s)=s^{3^i}$ such that $b$ vanishes whenever the first argument is in any other type. This is testable by computing the Frobenius decomposition on $V$ and restricting $b$ to each summand. Assume the order of $\tau$ is odd. It is enough to have these properties at the target endpoint, because a transporter must preserve the Frobenius types. Central $K$ has just the identity type.

For fixed $\sigma$, an admissible $h$ is specified by one scalar in $K_{\rm tgt}^\times$. If $(g,h)$ works, then $(t g,t\tau(t)h)$ also works for every $t\in K_{\rm tgt}^\times$. Indeed, balancing across the two arguments gives $b(tx,ty)=t\tau(t)b(x,y)$ on the one active type and both sides vanish on the others. Scalar multiplications here are the left actions. They commute with both field actions. In the cyclic group $K_{\rm tgt}^\times$ the image of $t\mapsto t\tau(t)$ is the subgroup of squares. To see this, put $M=3^e-1$ and choose the odd order $r$ of $\tau$. If $d$ divides $M$ and $1+3^i$, then $3^i\equiv-1\bmod d$ and $3^{ir}\equiv1\bmod d$, so $d$ divides $2$. The greatest common divisor is $2$.

Thus use just the two square-class representatives for $h$ in its one-dimensional chart, with the usual check of right intertwining. A nonsquare can be found deterministically in polynomial time in fixed characteristic. A scalar correction by $t$ changes $\det_{\mathbb F_3}(g)$ by $\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb F_3}(t)^{\dim_K V}$ and the determinant of $h$ stays unchanged. If $\dim_K V$ is even, every represented square class therefore has exactly the signs already found at its representative. If $\dim_K V$ is odd, then $\dim_K R=\dim_K V+2$ is odd and the unit action in the next section supplies both signs on any feasible relative branch. Hence enumerating the two classes still gives the exact one-mode answer, in polynomial time independent of $3^e$. [Ivanyos and Qiao, finite-field forms algorithms](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

## Restore the original tensor signs

For unital associative multiplication every isotopy is a product of an algebra isomorphism with independent left and right unit actions. In bilinear-map coordinates these are $L_u\phi$, $R_v\phi$ and $L_uR_v\phi$. A unit with residue $s$ has left and right determinants both equal to $\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb F_3}(s)^{\dim_{\mathbb F_3}(R)/e}$: on each grade use the left or right action over $K$, and multiply the filtration determinants. If $\dim_{\mathbb F_3}(R)/e$ is odd, a unit with negative norm gives an independent sign correction in any prescribed single mode. If it is even, both unit actions have positive sign. Include the known determinants of the original physical frame changes at both endpoints on the chosen mode. Taking a dual or inverse in the tensor convention does not change a sign over $\mathbb F_3$.

Take the union of these sign sets over all feasible $\sigma,h$. This is the exact relative answer, and the odd-automorphism answer is the existence of a negative sign for the self-pair. The enumeration and every subroutine have the stated bound. When the promised conjunct fails, output a fixed negative one-sign comparison. On success, return the remaining comparison, representing an odd-automorphism test by a self-pair with a reflection on its target.
