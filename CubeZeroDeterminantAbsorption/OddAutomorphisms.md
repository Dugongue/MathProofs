# Odd tensor automorphisms of square-zero extensions

Work over the field F of three elements. Let F_neg be the problem of whether a raw order-three tensor has a stabilizing triple whose determinant is negative in a designated physical mode. Let A be ordinary three-tensor isomorphism with determinant positive in one mode. All complexity statements concern deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions.

**Theorem.** Suppose the tensor is equivalent under independent basis changes to the multiplication tensor of a nonzero finite-dimensional unital associative F-algebra R. Suppose its Jacobson radical J satisfies J²=0. Write R/J≅∏ᵢ M_(nᵢ)(Kᵢ) with Kᵢ finite extensions of F. Then F_neg has answer yes in any designated mode exactly when at least one nᵢ is odd. The family is recognizable and the criterion is computable in deterministic polynomial time from the raw tensor. Thus the conjunction of an arbitrary A comparison with an F_neg comparison from this family reduces to one A instance, with no dimension or number-of-factors bound.

## Recover the algebra from the raw tensor

If the input format has three equal positive dimensions d, regard its slices as M(u):V→W for u in U. Compute the algebra E of pairs (P,Q) satisfying Q M(u)=M(u) P by linear equations. Require the first flattening to be injective, require dim(E)=d, and test whether V and W are isomorphic to the left regular E-module. This is a constructive finite-dimensional module isomorphism problem with the E-action as the fixed operator tuple. If both tests succeed, choose the resulting two module identifications with E. Every M(u) is then right multiplication by its value at 1. Injectivity of the first flattening makes u↦M(u)(1) an isomorphism onto E. Hence these identifications give multiplication in the opposite algebra R=E^op. Conversely a unital multiplication tensor passes these tests because the identity slice identifies the two endomorphism components, and its fixed-label endomorphisms are the right multiplications. Independent rebasings do not change these conclusions. Compute J and the simple matrix factors over finite extensions using finite-field algebra algorithms. Check J²=0 on a basis. [Brooksbank and Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf) · [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

## Isotopies and determinants

An autotopism p(x)q(y)=r(xy) of unital multiplication has p=L_u φ, q=R_v φ and r=L_u R_v φ for an algebra automorphism φ and arbitrary units u,v. Indeed, setting one variable at a time to 1 gives r(x)=p(x)v=u q(x), with u=p(1), v=q(1). Both multiplications are invertible because p,q,r are invertible. The map u⁻¹p(x)=q(x)v⁻¹ is then a unital multiplicative bijection. Conversely every triple of the indicated form works. Passing between tensor and bilinear-map conventions may dualize a mode, without changing its sign over F. Conjugating the mode maps back to the input frames also leaves their self-comparison signs unchanged.

Put S=R/J. Each radical grade J^r/J^(r+1) is an S-bimodule, with left and right actions given by arbitrary quotient lifts. The simple types supported on the ordered pair i,j are the natural modules of the factors of

    M_(nᵢ)(Kᵢ) ⊗ M_(nⱼ)(Kⱼ)^op ≅ M_(nᵢ nⱼ)(Kᵢ⊗Kⱼ).

The field product Kᵢ⊗Kⱼ is a finite product of fields of degree lcm([Kᵢ:F],[Kⱼ:F]) over F. This decomposition follows by factoring the separable defining polynomial of one extension over the other. On a simple natural module over one such field L, the determinant of an i-unit gᵢ acting on the left is the norm to F of its determinant over L raised to nⱼ. The right-action calculation interchanges i and j. On S itself, left and right determinants on factor i are both Norm_(Kᵢ/F)(det gᵢ)^(nᵢ) by acting on nᵢ columns or rows respectively.

## All matrix sizes even

Every left or right unit action has determinant positive, by multiplying the signs on S and on the radical grades. For an algebra automorphism φ, its quotient map on S permutes isomorphic simple factors, then acts by inner conjugation and field automorphisms. Inner conjugation has determinant one, field automorphisms are repeated nᵢ² times, and each permuted block has even F-dimension. Thus φ on S is positive.

On each radical grade the induced map is bimodule-semilinear along the same quotient automorphism. It permutes simple isotypic components in blocks of equal and even F-dimension. Within a matched component, choose field and matrix coordinates so the quotient automorphism is a field isomorphism plus the inner conjugations on the two matrix factors. For the field part, the action on coordinates repeats nᵢ nⱼ times per multiplicity, hence is positive. For the inner part use gᵢ⊗gⱼ^(−T) on the natural module; each determinant has the opposite even width as a power. The remaining module-linear freedom is a GL map on the multiplicity space over L. It acts on nᵢ nⱼ copies and is positive. To justify matching the stated charts, choose one model for each isomorphic quotient field and each matrix size. Permutation of factors then reorders these charts. The natural module of the matched simple tensor-product factor is determined by its matrix-unit basis, and the field isomorphism induced on that factor accounts for mismatched embeddings of Kᵢ,Kⱼ. All remaining changes are exactly the module-linear multiplicity freedom. Hence every grade determinant is positive. The filtration formula for det(φ) now gives positive determinant in all three modes for every autotopism. This no criterion holds for arbitrary radical length.

## Some matrix size odd and J²=0

The finite-field quotient S is separable. The Wedderburn–Malcev theorem gives a subalgebra lifting S, so R is isomorphic to the split square-zero extension S⊕J with product

    (s,x)(t,y)=(st,sy+xt).

If J contains a simple bimodule block supported on i,j with both nᵢ,nⱼ odd, act as the identity on S and on all but one simple copy in J. On that copy act by a scalar of field norm negative. Finite-field norms are surjective. Its F-determinant is Norm_(L/F)(scalar)^(nᵢ nⱼ), which is negative. This S-bimodule automorphism extends to an algebra automorphism of the displayed square-zero extension. Applying it in all three modes supplies the required determinant.

Otherwise let nᵢ be odd and choose a unit in quotient factor i with determinant of field norm negative, using the identity on all other factors. Lift it to a unit of R, which is possible since the kernel of R→S is nilpotent. Its left determinant on S is negative. Every J constituent on which the left action can be nontrivial has right index j of even size, so each radical contribution is positive. This gives a negative left multiplication. A negative right multiplication follows by the same argument: odd i as the right index forces an even left index. In each case choose the other unit and the algebra automorphism to be identities in the autotopism formula. The output mode is negative when the left multiplication is negative and the right unit is 1. This covers every designated mode. The square-zero splitting used in the first case is a consequence of the standard splitting theorem for finite-dimensional algebras over perfect fields. [T. Y. Lam, *A First Course in Noncommutative Rings*, Wedderburn–Malcev theorem](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4419-8616-0)

There is a second immediate extension with arbitrary radical length. If some nᵢ is odd and no radical grade has a simple block with both sizes odd, the same left or right unit gives the witness using the product over all grades. More generally the exact left-unit sign character is the product of the displayed norm powers across the quotient and the grades, and the same holds for the right-unit character. These characters are computable by linear algebra on the represented Wedderburn factors. In particular an odd unit sign immediately solves the corresponding designated mode and the output mode.

## One-instance consequence

Given the independent arbitrary A comparison and the F_neg tensor in the square-zero multiplication family, compute the latter verdict. Return the given A comparison when the answer is yes, and return a fixed negative one-sign instance otherwise. This proves the promised one-instance reduction. The same proof applies to the arbitrary-radical branches just proved decisive. This is an informal theorem on the specified recognizable family.


## Radical cube zero carries the full odd-automorphism degree

There is a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction from the unrestricted odd-automorphism question to multiplication tensors of local associative algebras with J³=0. Here is a direct construction using calibrated castling. [Castling and orbit correspondence](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/maximum-likelihood-estimation-for-tensor-normal-models-via-castling-transforms/FF03C608CF421F399C16127F8476538F)

View the tested role U as the first tensor mode. If it has dimension zero the answer is no. If its support is deficient there is an odd reflection on an unused direction. If its dimension is odd the tested map −I, compensated by −I in a second mode, gives an odd stabilizer. Handle those cases with fixed outputs in the claimed local family. A positive output is the field F itself. For a fixed negative output apply the construction below to the multiplication tensor of M₂(F). Its unit actions have positive determinant from two matrix copies, and its algebra automorphisms are inner and have positive determinant. Thus its tested dimension is four and its tested support is full.

For the remaining case u=dim U is positive even and the first flattening is injective. Pad the other two modes with unused coordinates to odd sizes v,w>u. This preserves the complete attainable first-sign set, since unchanged support maps extend over the unused directions. Write T as its u-dimensional image L in V⊗W by its first flattening. Replace it by the annihilator L° in V*⊗W*, treated as the new flattening. Choose a basis of the quotient by L after fixing its input basis and complete the ambient basis to determinant one. Use the dual complement basis for L°. For every transporter the determinant on the quotient equals the old first determinant times the second determinant to the wth power times the third determinant to the vth power, as follows from the block-triangular determinant in the adapted ambient frames. Inverse or dual leaves the sign unchanged. Annihilator transporters correspond in both directions by double annihilation. The total determinant sign after castling is therefore precisely the old first sign because both v,w are odd. The new format (vw−u,v,w) is all odd and concise: its flattening is injective by construction, and a deficiency in either remaining mode would force codimension at least min(v,w)>u in the full tensor product.

Interpret this concise tensor as a bilinear map m:X×Y→Z. Form the unital local algebra R=F1⊕X⊕Y⊕Z with xy=m(x,y) its only nonzero radical product. Then J²=Z and J³=0. In J/J² the left and right annihilators of the induced multiplication recover Y and X, respectively, by concision. Hence the automorphisms induce precisely the stabilizers of m on the three radical roles. Every such stabilizer extends by direct sum. The determinant of the algebra automorphism is the total sign of these three maps by the radical filtration. The dimension of R is even, since its three radical roles have odd dimensions. Every left or right unit multiplication has positive determinant: on every radical grade it is multiplication by the unit's scalar residue, so its determinant is that residue to the even power dim R. The autotopism formula above now identifies the attainable sign in any physical tensor mode of R with the attainable original first sign. This proves the reduction, including the fixed negative example. The local cube-zero family is recognizable by the earlier regular-module test and a radical-power check. This identifies the complexity of the entire odd-automorphism problem with its cube-zero local case.


## One signed comparison reduces to ordinary TI on the square-zero family

The all-even calculation also calibrates relative transporters between different input tensors. At each endpoint use the regular-module identifications above to obtain the displayed algebra frame from each physical mode. In the algebra choose a basis by lifting bases of S and of the radical grades, ordered in that filtration order. In S use matrix units with fixed F-bases of the coordinate fields. In each grade choose the isotypic decomposition into simple S-bimodules, and use the natural matrix-unit coordinates and any multiplicity basis over the component field. These data can be computed by the split finite-field algebra algorithm and linear algebra: the central idempotents give each isotypic part, one diagonal matrix unit gives its multiplicity space, and the other matrix units recover all the natural copies. Record the three resulting physical basis-change determinants. No common isomorphism or search among permutations of factors is needed.

If all nᵢ at both endpoints are even, the preceding calculation gives determinant one for every algebra isomorphism in these frames. The sign of any matched block permutation is positive because every block has even dimension, and the isomorphisms of the coordinate fields also have even repetitions. Unit actions are positive for arbitrary radical length on this branch, so all relative tensor transporters have exactly the raw sign prescribed by the basis-change determinants of the two endpoints. If the prescribed sign in the tested mode is negative, return a fixed ordinary no comparison. Otherwise use the original ordinary comparison. If the two semisimple quotient types differ, the original ordinary comparison also fails, since an isotopy of unital algebras yields an algebra isomorphism by the same unit calculation.

If a source in the square-zero family has at least one odd matrix degree, it has both stabilizer signs by the theorem, so any nonempty relative transporter set admits a positive witness. In this branch use the original ordinary comparison. This gives a deterministic single-instance reduction A→ordinary TI when both raw tensors belong to the square-zero multiplication family. Consequently A∧F_neg reduces to one A even when the F_neg input is unrestricted, provided the relative A comparison belongs to this family: express F_neg as one signed self-comparison with a reflected target, then attach the ordinary comparison by the existing determinant-neutral conjunction construction. The earlier section treated the complementary promise, where the relative comparison is unrestricted and the automorphism input belongs to the family.
