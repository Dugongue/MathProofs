# Singular cycles and recovery of the symmetry group

Companion to [the four main proofs](FourTheorems.md).

## Two extensions obtained by combining the routes

**Singular cycles with no choice of vector normalization.** The cyclic trace test works even if every edge is singular. Label the edges in order. Compute prefix products T_i from vertex zero to i and suffix products S_i from i back to zero, so H_i=T_i S_i. This takes O(n d³) field operations without inverses. If the trace word is aperiodic and every image line of P_i is H_i-cyclic, form the local Krylov candidates B_i as above for the resulting shift. Verify local conjugacy. Every possible isomorphism has local maps a_i B_i. On edge i the two matrices L'_i B_i and B_{i+1} L_i must both vanish or be nonzero proportional. If their ratio is q_i (first equals q_i times second), the remaining equation is a_{i+1}=q_i a_i. Propagate ratios on the graph of nonzero edges and check consistency around each closed walk. This gives all lifts, one free nonzero scalar per connected component, including isolated vertices. Krylov iteration and elimination at each vertex keep the total at O(n d³) field operations. The cyclic word can be matched in linear comparisons. For an all-singular example over Q use L_i=diag(0,1) everywhere and P_i with both rows equal to (1−x_i,x_i), where x=(0,0,1). These are conjugate rank-one idempotents, all with cyclic image generated by (1,1). The mixed word is (0,0,1). Both separate reducts admit every rotation.

**Counting with unbounded group generator rank.** Keep the section-spanning promise of Theorem 1 and assume the following ordinary graph of identifiable moves is connected. In the recovered local bases color each edge by its threshold and complete tuple of transport matrices. For a color c, retain the move v to w if w is the unique distinct head of an outgoing c-edge at v and v is the unique distinct tail of an incoming c-edge at w. Repeated identical edges between those endpoints count as one neighbor. Use these moves in both directions for the connectivity test. Finally assume that the kernel of the target group action on its vertices has order at most a fixed b. Then the entire group, graph, edge and fiber isomorphism problem, including exact counting, is solvable in polynomial time even if the group needs unboundedly many generators.

Indeed for each proposed image of one root, propagate along these uniquely identified moves to determine the entire vertex map. Check consistency and bijectivity. The vertex-mode maps are forced by the canonical frames. Compute a generating list for the source group by adding a generator only when it lies outside the subgroup already generated. Every addition at least doubles the subgroup, so the list length is at most log₂|G|. For a source generator g, its image must act as the target permutation obtained by conjugating the known permutation of g by the candidate vertex map. Its choices are empty or a coset of the target vertex kernel. Hence there are at most |G| raised to log₂ b candidate generator-image tuples per root choice. Check group products, costs, local intertwiners and edge lifts as in Theorem 4. Distinct choices yield distinct full matches, apart from the parallel-edge automorphisms already counted exactly. All promises are testable in the explicit model. With a faithful vertex action there is at most one group map per vertex candidate.

For an infinite example, use vertices V=F₂^r for r≥1 and group G=V×F₂. At each vertex use a two-dimensional mode and named vector e1. For every i put one threshold-i edge from x to x+e_i (here e_i in the vertex index denotes the i-th binary coordinate); its linear transport sends the first local basis vector to the second and the second to zero. At threshold zero put two zero-map loops labeled a in F₂ at each vertex. The group element (h,t) translates vertices by h, uses identity linear maps, and sends the loop (x,a) to (x+h,a+t). Give group elements zero cost. One incoming sum at threshold 1 recovers the second local basis vector. The moves indexed by i connect the entire vertex set. The vertex-action kernel has order two.

Every vertex automorphism preserving those moves is a translation x↦x+s. The group isomorphism over this translation must have the form (h,t)↦(h,t+u(h)) for a linear functional u on V. Equivariance forces the loop permutation to be (x,a)↦(x+s,a+b+u(x)) with one constant b in F₂. Conversely all choices work, while the local linear maps and the other edge images are forced. The exact automorphism count is therefore 2^(2r+1). All ordinary edge maps are singular. The group generator rank and the fiber dimension play different roles here.
