# Recovering coordinates and counting isomorphisms in tensor networks with transport

Four informal results for [the seven object types](SevenTypes.md). The algorithmic statements below use explicit finite inputs, a common fixed finite field and a fixed tensor signature unless another field is specified. Field elements have a fixed encoding. Vertices and parallel edges have separate identities. A homomorphism of symmetries in an isomorphism is an actual group isomorphism, even when some group elements act trivially. Nothing below assumes the conjectured equivalence of ordinary and special-linear TI.

## 1. Recovering local coordinates before matching vertices

A section of mode i assigns one vector in that mode to every vertex. Start with all named vector constants as sections. Use two rules to generate further sections. First apply any named multilinear operation with a single vector output pointwise to sections of its input modes. Second, for any edge threshold and mode, form at each vertex the sum of all vectors transported to it from tails of incoming edges of that threshold. Empty sums are zero. Additional multi-output tensors need only be retained for the final comparison. These rules respect every isomorphism, including graph relabelings and independent changes of basis at the vertices. They need not respect arbitrary noninjective graph homomorphisms.

**Theorem.** There is a deterministic polynomial-time test for whether these sections span every mode *on evaluation at each vertex*. On the class passing this test, Transportensor isomorphism reduces to ordinary Graph Isomorphism by one polynomial-time many-one reduction. Every isomorphism of the colored incidence data constructed in the reduction has a unique lift through the recovered linear coordinates. This last assertion concerns the colored incidence model before any generic encoding gadgets are introduced. Edge maps may be singular.

**Proof.** Work in the direct sum of the vertex spaces separately for each mode. Keep an ordered linearly independent list of sections. Start from the constants, rejecting dependencies. In each round, in fixed order, apply the rules to all well-typed tuples of entries of the current lists, and append independent results. Freeze the lists of arguments at the start of each round. Order modes and operation names by the signature and thresholds by value. At most D independent vectors are retained in total, where D is the sum of all vertex-mode dimensions. At stability, linearity of the incoming sums and multilinearity of the operations imply closure on the spans. This computes the entire generated section space. For fixed arity and an explicit input the number of rule evaluations and field operations is polynomial. No basis of an input space is used to resolve a tie. Independence is invariant under linear isomorphism. Thus isomorphic inputs process the same formal expressions in the same order, including all rejected expressions.

At each vertex and mode, read the retained list in order and take its first independent evaluations until it forms a basis. Reject the spanning promise if these do not fill the space. A generating expression has a well-defined value on each vertex, so every isomorphism carries these ordered local bases to their counterparts. In these bases record the dimensions and coefficients of *all* named tensors at vertices and the thresholds and coordinates of *all* edge maps at edges. An isomorphism of the original objects preserves these colors. Conversely any color- and incidence-preserving isomorphism lifts by mapping the computed bases to their counterparts. The recorded coefficients give exactly the local operation and edge intertwining tests. The bases span, so this lift is unique. Directed incidence with distinguishable tail and head relations, edge identities, and polynomial-length color strings is a bounded-arity relational structure reducible to GI (use vertex/edge sorts, position nodes and anchored labeled color gadgets, or standard colored GI with shared color numbering on the two inputs).

The class is GI-hard even with nonzero spaces: put a one-dimensional space and a named nonzero vector at each graph vertex and use identity transports. Graph maps between isomorphic graphs then lift uniquely. Together the two directions identify the worst-case decision complexity on this recognizable class. If all resulting vertex colors within each object are distinct, match by color and verify the multiset of edge records for each ordered vertex pair. This gives an ordinary polynomial-time decision and constructs a match without a GI oracle.

The spanning promise can hold even when the local operations alone generate proper subspaces at *every* vertex. On a directed cycle give each vertex the vector e1 in a two-dimensional space and use the singular map sending e1 to e2 and e2 to zero on every edge. One incoming sum creates the missing basis vector everywhere. Independent changes of coordinates at every vertex do not affect the test. A graph with regular incidence may still have many vertex symmetries after the coordinates have been recovered.

For explicitly listed group actions on a graph one may use exactly the same section algorithm. It commutes with each group element because the action preserves all thresholds, tensors and edge transports. Record the multiplication relation of the group, its costs, its vertex and edge actions and the coordinates of its vertex-mode action maps. These data again have bounded relational arity after coefficient strings are assigned colors. Thus even the isomorphism of these mixed objects with an *unknown group correspondence* reduces to GI on the spanning class in the group-table model. The input size qualification matters for succinct groups.

The span computation uses familiar ideas from reachable spaces of weighted automata and invariant aggregation on graphs. [Kiefer, Notes on Equivalence and Minimization of Weighted Automata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01217) and [Dell, Grohe and Rattan, Lovász Meets Weisfeiler and Leman](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08876) give related background. The section criterion above is for vector-valued data with independent local changes of coordinates.

## 2. A mixed trace recovers the phase on directed cycles

Work over any effective field, on an oriented cycle of length n at least three, with one invertible d-dimensional edge map at each step and a unary local operator P_i at each vertex. Let H_i be the monodromy, obtained by composing the edge maps once around the cycle starting at i. Compute the cyclic word with letters tr(H_i P_i). An isomorphism simultaneously conjugates H_i and P_i onto the corresponding pair, so it must preserve this word. If its least period is n there is at most one possible cyclic shift. Comparing the words finds it or rejects. Distinct letters at every vertex are unnecessary.

**Theorem.** Suppose additionally that at some vertex r the image of P_r is a line and a nonzero vector u on it is cyclic for H_r. The two objects can then be tested for isomorphism and an isomorphism constructed in polynomial time without a given shift or basis map. For the unary signature every successful unique shift has precisely one scalar multiple family of lifts.

**Proof.** At the matching target vertex require the same rank and cyclicity. Pick any generators u and u' of the lines. Write K and K' for the invertible matrices of their first d Krylov vectors. A root intertwiner must take u to a nonzero multiple of u', so its matrix must be that same multiple of K' K inverse. Directly check this one candidate against the monodromy and propagate it around the cycle, checking each unary tensor and the final edge. All equations are homogeneous in a common scalar, proving sufficiency and the asserted count of q−1 over a finite field. The promise on r can itself be tested by scanning vertices. This takes polynomial field work even with a direct implementation. A generator of a line and the Krylov basis can be found by elimination.

For a mixed example over the rationals take three edges I,I,diag(2,3), so the three monodromies are identical. At vertices 0 and 1 put P=diag(1,0). At vertex 2 put the matrix with rows (0,1),(0,1). All three local operators are conjugate rank-one idempotents. Yet the mixed word is (2,2,3), and the last operator has image generated by (1,1), cyclic for the monodromy. Ignoring the edge maps leaves all rotations possible with independent changes of basis. Ignoring the local tensors also leaves all rotations possible. Imposing both fixes the phase, including which of the two equal-trace vertices is which.

## 3. Root reconstruction across singular maps

Here a graph identification *is supplied*. Use one root per component, with one mode map there for each mode. In a mode, allow directed paths plus formal reversals of invertible edge maps. Require that images of all such path maps from the root jointly span every vertex space. No individual spanning-tree edge need be invertible.

**Theorem.** Under this recognizable promise, a root tuple has at most one lift. A lift can be tested and reconstructed in polynomial field work. For a finite field, if the linear root intertwiners for selected named unary operators in each positive-dimensional root mode form a line generated by an invertible matrix, existence and the number of invertible lifts have deterministic polynomial-time algorithms.

**Proof.** For each mode, keep a list of root-to-vertex path matrices whose images successively enlarge the accumulated span at their endpoint. Start with the identity at the root. Extend each retained matrix once along every outgoing arc. A discarded matrix already has image in the span of retained images, and this containment persists under postcomposition. Thus at termination the attained spaces are precisely the reachable spans. The number of retained paths is at most the sum of the space dimensions (zero-dimensional modes may be skipped). Record paths compactly by predecessors.

Concatenate the retained maps at vertex v horizontally into Q_v. Evaluate the *same paths* on the target to get Q'_v. Reject an invertible lift if dimensions or required invertibility for reversed arcs disagree or the target paths fail to span. For an invertible root map A, a lift requires A_v Q_v = Q'_v diag(A,...,A). The right side must have the same kernel as Q_v, and that condition is sufficient for the unique invertible A_v. Compute it via any right inverse for Q_v. Directly check every original edge and tensor, as well as the root condition. The construction is polynomial and gives the converse.

Under the extra line hypothesis, solve the unary linear systems to obtain generators B_i. Every possible root tuple has components a_i B_i with nonzero scalars a_i. Test the path kernels and edge equations once at B_i, since the same scalar propagates throughout its mode. For any named tensor operation, evaluate the two sides of its naturality equation at the B lifts. Two zero sides impose nothing. Exactly one zero or two nonproportional sides reject. For two proportional nonzero sides the remaining equation is a monomial equation in the a_i, with exponents equal to output multiplicities minus input multiplicities (take the ratio in the corresponding direction). Apply integer Smith reduction to this exponent matrix, applying the same row operations multiplicatively to the right sides and the corresponding change of variables in the domain. A zero row needs right side 1. A diagonal d_j>0 needs its right side raised to (q−1)/gcd(d_j,q−1) to equal 1. For s scalar parameters and Smith rank r, the exact positive count is (q−1)^(s−r) times the product of gcd(d_j,q−1). The fixed signature bounds the number of possible exponent row types and variables. Repeated rows first check equality of their right sides. Over a fixed finite field one can construct witnesses by solving the diagonal equations by direct field search. Empty root modes force zero reachable spaces and have no free scalar.

For example, take two parallel rank-one projections diag(1,0) and diag(0,1) from a two-dimensional root to a two-dimensional sink over F5. Put E11 and E12 at the root as two named unary maps and zero at the sink. Their joint centralizer is scalar. Mark the sink with the two-output tensor λ e1⊗e2 and put zero at the root. The directions fix the vertices, and the two different kernels preclude an edge swap by a scalar root map. All lifts are scalar also at the sink. The objects with nonzero parameters λ and μ are isomorphic exactly when μ/λ is a square, and then there are two isomorphisms. This example even determines its graph identification from the input.

## 4. Blind counting for group actions with parallel edges

Use a one-object groupoid (a group), supplied by its finite multiplication table, with a cost on its elements. The input includes its actions on the vertices, edges and mode spaces. Allow arbitrary parallel edges and singular transports. Suppose the group can be generated by at most a fixed r elements. Join vertices by ordinary edges, disregarding orientation, and by moves under the group. Suppose each resulting connected component has at most a fixed k vertex orbits. Allow arbitrarily many components. Assume either that the section-spanning test of Theorem 1 passes, or that mode dimensions are bounded over the fixed finite field.

**Theorem.** There is a polynomial-time algorithm for isomorphism decision, construction and exact counting under these promises. The graph correspondence, base group correspondence, edge permutations and linear maps are all unknown.

**Proof.** Find a generating tuple by testing tuples of length at most r. Enumerate its possible target images. Each tuple determines at most one group isomorphism F. Extend by Cayley traversal, checking all products, costs and bijectivity. Fix one surviving F at a time. In a proposed pair of components enumerate target vertices for one source representative of each vertex orbit. Extend by F-equivariance, check well-definedness and bijectivity. These choices count each possible vertex bijection exactly once. With section frames the mode maps are forced by the computed bases (use the global, object-wide basis lists for a full isomorphism, or recompute on each proposed component pair for the component count). Without frames, try all invertible mode maps at the orbit representatives. This costs a constant to the power k for fixed dimensions, signature and field. Extend by symmetry, check all action squares and all tensors.

For each proposed vertex and fiber match assign to every edge a record consisting of its tail, head, threshold, and all transport matrices. Put source records in target coordinates by conjugating each rectangular matrix with its appropriate head and tail maps. An edge lift must be a group-equivariant bijection preserving these records. For each orbit of records, compare the fibers over one record d as sets acted on by its stabilizer H. This is sufficient since an H-equivariant bijection at d extends uniquely by translating with group elements. Each transitive H-set is H/K up to conjugacy of K, and its number of equivariant automorphisms is |N_H(K)|/|K|, by choosing the image of the identity coset. If multiplicities differ for any conjugacy class the contribution is zero. Otherwise m copies of type H/K contribute m! (|N_H(K)|/|K|)^m. Multiply over types and record orbits. Orbit traversal, stabilizer comparisons and normalizers here all take polynomial time on the given table. Store orbit bijections for witness construction.

Sum the edge counts over distinct vertex and fiber matches to obtain each component-pair weight for this fixed F. Positive entries form disjoint complete bipartite blocks when isolated unmatched rows or columns are detected separately. Indeed, composing a match, an inverse match and a match again gives a match over F. All positive weights in a block agree by composition with component isomorphisms over the identity on the group. Reject unbalanced blocks. A balanced block with m components on each side and weight w contributes m! w^m. Multiply across blocks and sum across group isomorphisms. Every complete isomorphism contributes exactly once. All count bit lengths are polynomial because all discrete maps and all finite-field matrix entries of one witness have polynomial-length encodings. This proves the claim and gives witnesses by choosing positive matches in each block.

Why the edge stabilizers matter: let the four-group H act on six identical parallel loops at one vertex. The regular orbit plus two fixed points and the union of its three two-point coset orbits have the same numbers of fixed loops for each individual group element, but different orbit-size profiles. No edge lift exists under any group isomorphism. The algorithm detects this even if the action on all vertices and local linear coordinates is trivial.

## Scope and status

The four arguments are informal. The first and fourth handle unknown combinatorial identifications under different recognizable hypotheses. The third uses a supplied identification in general. The cyclic theorem recovers that identification itself. No general polynomial algorithm for unrestricted tensor isomorphism or graph isomorphism follows. These proofs develop the proposed framework. Publication priority and independent external review are outstanding.

[Two further theorems and an exact infinite family](Combined.md) extend the cycle result to singular maps and the counting result to groups of unbounded generator rank. [Finite check](check_frames.py) compares the recovered-frame witnesses with exhaustive search on small binary inputs.
