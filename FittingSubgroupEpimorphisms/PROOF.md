# Counting epimorphisms through nonabelian Fitting subgroups

Fix \(d\) and \(c\). Let \(G,H\) be given by Cayley tables, with no restriction on \(G\), and suppose \(H\) can be generated by at most \(d\) elements. Let \(F=F(H)\) be its Fitting subgroup and \(Q=H/F\). There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm to count the epimorphisms \(G\twoheadrightarrow H\) and produce one when the count is positive, under any of the following conditions.

- The derived group \(F'\) has order at most \(c\).
- The derived group \(F'\) is abelian and has generator rank at most \(c\), with no bound on its exponent.
- The group \(F\) has class at most two. For each primary component \(B=(F')_p\), consider its conjugation \(Q\)-module structure. Each simple module \(T\) in its socle occurs with multiplicity at most \(c\,\dim_{\operatorname{End}_Q(T)}T\).

Here the socle is the sum of the simple submodules. In the third condition \(F'\) may have unbounded order and generator rank. The proof combines the earlier chief-factor kernel search, the marked commutator recovery used in class-two problems, and the generator-preimage counting argument for metabelian targets. It proves new target cases of Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar Questions 2 and 3(2). [The published questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)

## 1. Enumerating quotient maps

The Fitting subgroup of a finite group is the intersection of the centralizers of all sections of any one chief series. Here is the argument. The intersection \(C\) is normal and the intersections of \(C\) with the chief series form a central series of \(C\). Conversely, a normal nilpotent subgroup maps to a normal nilpotent subgroup \(N\) in the ambient quotient of any chief section \(A\). If \(N\cap A=1\), the two normal subgroups commute. Otherwise \(A\le N\) by minimal normality. A nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite nilpotent group meets its center nontrivially, as follows by taking the first upper central term that meets the subgroup. The intersection \(A\cap Z(N)\) is also ambient normal, so \(A\le Z(N)\). Thus every normal nilpotent subgroup centralizes every chief section, proving the assertion.

Compute a chief series by choosing a smallest nontrivial normal closure in each successive quotient. An epimorphism \(f:G\twoheadrightarrow H\) induces a chief series in \(H\) after deleting repetitions. A surviving chief section is equivariantly isomorphic to the corresponding source section by minimal normality. Its conjugation centralizer pulls back to the corresponding centralizer in \(G\). Therefore \(f^{-1}(F)\) is an intersection of some of the centralizers from the source chief series. Enumerate and deduplicate these intersections. A chief series has length at most \(\log_2|G|\), so there are at most \(|G|\) candidates.

For each candidate \(L\), form \(G/L\). Enumerate all isomorphisms \(Q\to G/L\) by guessing images of a generating tuple of \(Q\) of length \(d\), propagating products, and checking the table equations and bijectivity. Invert the maps to enumerate all quotient epimorphisms \(\psi:G\to Q\) that can arise from an epimorphism onto \(H\). An extra quotient map with no lift contributes zero. All costs so far are polynomial for fixed \(d\).

## 2. Recovery from the commutator map

Choose an ordered generating tuple \(h_1,\ldots,h_d\) of \(H\), padding with identities. For each \(\psi\) enumerate tuples \(x_1,\ldots,x_d\) of possible preimages of the \(h_i\) in the proper cosets of \(L=\ker\psi\). Choose generators \(n_j\) of \(F\), write them as words in the \(h_i\), and use the same words in the \(x_i\) to obtain \(u_j\in L\). Short words can be found by breadth-first search in the Cayley graph on the chosen target generators.

For fixed anchors, every true lift supplies a homomorphism \(b:[L,L]\to F'\). For \(l\in L\), scan all \(a\in F\) and impose

\[
[a,n_j]=b([l,u_j])\qquad\text{for each }j,
\]

where \([a,n]=ana^{-1}n^{-1}\). If there is a solution, all solutions form a single coset of \(Z(F)\), since they act by the same conjugation on every generator of \(F\). Otherwise discard the candidate. This recovers the image of \(l\) in \(H/Z(F)\). For a general \(g\in G\), pick a word \(w\) in the \(x_i\) mapping to \(\psi(g)\) in \(Q\), express \(g=(gw^{-1})w\), and multiply the recovered coset for \(gw^{-1}\in L\) by the coset of the word \(w\) evaluated in the \(h_i\). This specifies one candidate map to \(H/Z(F)\). Check all homomorphism equations and the quotient and anchor conditions. Every actual epimorphism belongs to its specified candidate.

Finally solve for lifts through the finite abelian normal subgroup \(Z(F)\). A section of \(H\to H/Z(F)\) turns the homomorphism equations into affine congruences, with multiplication represented by the section's cocycle and the linear action on \(Z(F)\). Add equations requiring \(f(x_i)=h_i\) and \(f|_{[L,L]}=b\), by translating each required value in its respective coset. Smith normal form decides consistency, gives a solution if one exists, and computes the size of the affine fiber. All solutions are epimorphisms because they contain the chosen generating tuple. No two distinct pairs \((\psi,b)\) describe the same epimorphism. If \(|H|\) divides \(|G|\), each epimorphism is counted once per preimage tuple, namely \((|G|/|H|)^d\) times. Divide the sum by that number. If divisibility fails the count is zero.

The same proof permits any specified values of \(f\) at additional elements of \(G\), since these add affine assignments and preserve the multiplicity of each admissible epimorphism. Fix a generating tuple of \(G\) and search over image prefixes, skipping any prefix with zero completions. The tree has depth at most \(\log_2|G|\) and branching factor at most \(|H|\). Between outputs one goes up and down at most that depth, with only polynomially many checks of skipped children. This enumerates all epimorphisms with polynomial delay. Choosing each child with probability proportional to its exact completion count instead gives uniform sampling, with expected polynomial running time using unbiased random bits.

## 3. Three polynomial bounds on the commutator enumeration

For the first condition, choose a generating tuple of \([L,L]\) of length at most \(\log_2|G|\). Guess its images in the bounded group \(F'\), propagate over the table and verify. For the second condition all maps factor through the abelianization of \([L,L]\). For a finite abelian group \(M\) and a cyclic group \(C_k\), there are at most \(|M|\) homomorphisms \(M\to C_k\), by decomposing \(M\) into cyclic factors. Decomposing \(F'\) into at most \(c\) cyclic factors gives at most \(|G|^c\) lists and lets us enumerate them. Abelian decompositions and the lifting equations have polynomial bit complexity via integer Smith normal form.

For the third condition, \([F,F]\) is central in \(F\), so the action of \(H\) on it descends to \(Q\). A genuine \(b\) factors through the abelian group \(M=[L,L]/[L,[L,L]]\) and gives a \(Q\)-equivariant map on \(M\), using the \(G/L\) action. Work separately on the primary components. If \(B=(F')_p\) has exponent \(p^a\), its source component can be reduced modulo \(p^a\). Write \(M_0\) for this reduced source, \(S=\mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z\), and \(D=S[Q]\). The coinduced module \(J=\operatorname{Hom}_S(D,S)\) has the property

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_D(M_0,J)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_S(M_0,S).
\]

The isomorphism is evaluation at the identity of \(D\). Its inverse sends \(\ell\) to the map whose value at \(m\) is \(d\mapsto \ell(dm)\), using the left module action on \(J\) given by right multiplication in the argument. The character extension property of finite abelian groups killed by \(p^a\) shows that \(J\) is injective among finite \(D\)-modules. Indeed homomorphisms into \(S\) can be viewed as characters into \(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\), and an extension still lands in the \(p^a\)-torsion because the group has exponent dividing \(p^a\).

For each simple \(T\), the same identity shows that its multiplicity in the socle of \(J\) is \(\dim_{\operatorname{End}_Q(T)}T\). In detail, \(T\) is killed by \(p\) and its additive dual has cardinality \(|T|\). The dimension of \(\operatorname{Hom}_D(T,J)\) over the endomorphism division ring is consequently the dimension of \(T\) over that division ring. Thus the multiplicity bound gives an embedding \(\operatorname{Soc}B\hookrightarrow J^c\). Extend to \(B\) by injectivity. The extension is injective since any nonzero kernel has a simple submodule. Applying \(\operatorname{Hom}_D(M_0,-)\) now bounds the number of component maps by \(|\operatorname{Hom}_S(M_0,S)|^c=|M_0|^c\). The product of these bounds over primes is at most \(|M|^c\). Enumerate the module maps from the solutions of the linear congruences imposing \(Q\)-equivariance. Smith form represents the finite solution group by explicit cyclic parameters, permitting polynomial work per element. The stated algorithm can cap enumeration at \(|G|^c\) if run on arbitrary input outside the third promise.

The proof has no hypothesis about a split extension or coprime quotient action. For example class-two Fitting subgroups with cyclic derived subgroup of unbounded order satisfy the second condition. For the third condition, a semisimple \(p\)-primary derived module generated by at most \(c\) elements over \(S[Q]\) satisfies the multiplicity condition. To see the inequality, reduce to \(\mathbb F_p[Q]\). A simple \(T\) occurs in the semisimple head of one regular module with multiplicity \(\dim_{\operatorname{End}_Q(T)}T\), by evaluation of module maps at 1. The multiplicities of a semisimple quotient of \(c\) regular modules are therefore bounded as required.

For an example with unbounded rank and nonsemisimple action, let \(E_p\) be the Heisenberg group of upper unitriangular \(3\times3\) matrices over \(\mathbb F_p\). Take \(H=E_p^Q\rtimes Q\), where \(Q\) acts regularly on its copies and \(Q\) is a bounded-generator Fitting-free finite group, for example alternating groups \(A_n\) on odd \(n\ge5\). The Fitting subgroup is \(E_p^Q\), because it is normal nilpotent and \(Q\) has no nontrivial normal nilpotent subgroup. The two generators of one copy of \(E_p\) together with generators of \(Q\) generate \(H\). The derived group of its Fitting subgroup is the regular \(\mathbb F_p[Q]\)-module. It is isomorphic to the coinduced module \(J\) by pairing each element with the coefficient of 1 in its product with the argument. Thus \(c=1\) suffices even for unbounded \(|Q|\). If \(p\mid |Q|\), this module is nonsemisimple because the augmentation quotient to the trivial module has no section. The fixed vectors are multiples of the sum of the group basis and have augmentation zero. This proof is informal.
