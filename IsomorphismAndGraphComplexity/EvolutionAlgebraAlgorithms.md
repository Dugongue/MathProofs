# Evolution-algebra recognition and graph-isomorphism reductions

Informal proof addressing the algorithmic clauses of Question 50 of Kaygorodov, Martín González and Viruel. From arbitrary multiplication tables, recognize evolution algebras in deterministic polynomial bit time over $\mathbb Q$ and over a finite field given in an explicit polynomial-basis representation, even with the field varying. Over $\mathbb Q$ the algorithm also constructs a natural basis and describes every natural basis. Over finite fields, finding one natural basis is polynomial-time equivalent to finding all roots of a given fully split squarefree polynomial. Isomorphism of perfect evolution algebras is polynomial-time equivalent to graph isomorphism over every fixed finite field and over $\mathbb Q$. Both reductions allow raw multiplication tables. The separate question about computer certification of low-dimensional classifications is outside the proofs here. [Published Question 50](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22248v1#S2.SS2.SSS9)

## 1. Construct the input blocks by linear algebra

An evolution algebra admits a natural basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ with $e_i e_j=0$ for unequal indices. It is commutative. Given a multiplication table, test commutativity and compute the annihilator $K$ by linear equations. On $W=V/K$ in the input positions, retain $V$ as the output and use the resulting symmetric bimap $U:W\times W\to V$. It has zero input annihilator. The zero-square vectors in any natural basis span $K$, by multiplying a linear combination against each basis vector. The remaining vectors project to a natural basis of $W$ for $U$. Conversely a natural basis for $U$ lifts to one of $V$ after including any basis of $K$. If $W=0$, all bases work.

Compute
$$
\{(A,B):U(Ax,y)=U(x,By)\}\subset
 \operatorname{End}(W)\times\operatorname{End}(W)^{\mathrm{op}}.
$$
The first projection is injective because $U$ has zero input annihilator. Its image $R\subset\operatorname{End}(W)$ is a unital associative algebra with involution $A^*=B$, because $U$ is symmetric. Compute its center $Z$ by linear equations. If a natural basis of $W$ exists, write $u_i=U(e_i,e_i)\ne0$. The pair-adjoint equation reads
$$
A_{ji}u_j=B_{ij}u_i.
$$
Thus $R$ has a full matrix block on each group of indices with proportional nonzero $u_i$, with no maps between different groups. The center $Z$ consists of independent scalars on these blocks and every element of $Z$ is self-adjoint.

Over $\mathbb Q$, compute the minimal polynomial of each member of a linear basis of $Z$ and require it to split into distinct linear factors over $\mathbb Q$. Deterministic rational polynomial factoring and rational linear algebra take polynomial bit time. The commuting split matrices give their common eigenspaces $W_i$ by successive intersections of kernels. Retain the original ambient coordinates when solving stacked kernel equations to keep all bit sizes bounded by polynomial-size minors. Test that cross-products vanish and the image spanned by $U(W_i,W_i)$ has dimension one on every block. These conditions are necessary by the preceding coordinate calculation. They suffice because on each block $U$ is then one nonzero output vector times a nondegenerate symmetric form $\beta_i$, nondegenerate by the zero input annihilator. Over $\mathbb Q$ every such form has an orthogonal basis.

More explicitly, all natural bases are obtained by choosing arbitrary orthogonal bases of the $\beta_i$, arbitrarily lifting their vectors through $V\to W$, adjoining any basis of $K$, and permuting the result. Conversely the intrinsic center calculation forces every natural basis to arise this way. For a bit-bounded choice of one orthogonal basis, in fixed block coordinates maintain a matrix $C$ whose columns already chosen have nondegenerate Gram matrix and each are raw coordinate vectors or sums of two. Project each raw coordinate vector by
$$
v\longmapsto v-C(C^{\mathsf T}\beta_i C)^{-1}C^{\mathsf T}\beta_i v .
$$
The projections span the orthogonal complement. Choose a projected vector with nonzero square, or a sum of two projected vectors with nonzero pair value if all the squares vanish. Append its raw vector or raw sum to $C$ and output the corresponding projected vector. The added direction is orthogonal to the existing span. The raw columns are bounded, and the inverse and projection use polynomial-bit minors of Gram matrices formed from the original form. This constructs one basis in the asserted bit complexity. [Lenstra–Lenstra–Lovász, rational polynomial factorization](https://eudml.org/doc/182419)

## 2. Recognize over a varying finite field without factoring

Use $R,Z,U$ as above, now over $\mathbb F_q$. Require every basis element of $Z$ to be self-adjoint, and test $z^q=z$ for each basis element as a matrix identity by binary powering. Since the center is commutative, the $q$-power map is additive and $\mathbb F_q$-linear there, so every element of $Z$ then satisfies the same equation. Each center basis element is split and diagonalizable since its minimal polynomial divides $X^q-X$. The simultaneous eigenspace decomposition gives a faithful action of a split algebra $Z\simeq\mathbb F_q^h$. One can see that its spectral idempotents belong to $Z$ by taking products of the ordinary eigenprojector polynomials for the center basis operators. This argument establishes existence of the decomposition without computing its eigenvalues.

The primitive idempotents $f_i$ give its nonzero spaces $W_i=f_iW$. By self-adjointness of $Z$, $U(W_i,W_j)=0$ for $i\ne j$. The trace pairing $(a,b)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(L_{ab}|_Z)$ is nondegenerate because it is the ordinary dot product in the primitive idempotent basis. Compute any trace-dual bases $z_b,z_b^\vee$ by linear equations and form
$$
 \widetilde U(x,y)=\sum_b z_b\otimes U(z_b^\vee x,y)
                    \in Z\otimes_{\mathbb F_q}V .
$$
In the not-computed primitive idempotent basis the same map is $\sum_i f_i\otimes U(f_i x,y)$, by the basis independence of the duality tensor. The image span of $\widetilde U$ is the direct sum over $i$ of the spaces $f_i\otimes \operatorname{span} U(W_i,W_i)$. Indeed each may be isolated using $x,y\in W_i$, and the cross terms vanish. Each has dimension at least one by the zero input annihilator. Thus testing $\dim\operatorname{span}\widetilde U(W,W)=\dim Z$ tests exactly that the output span of every block has dimension one, without splitting any polynomial.

In odd characteristic this suffices. In characteristic two, additionally compute the span of $\widetilde U(w,w)$ over an arbitrary basis $w$ of $W$, and require this span also to have dimension $h$. Since $\widetilde U$ is symmetric, the span using a basis equals the span using every vector, by expansion of the square. In the intrinsic decomposition the latter span is the direct sum of the square spans on the individual blocks, as seen by choosing each vector inside one block. After the first dimension check, the second test says exactly that every scalar form is nonalternating. A nondegenerate symmetric form in characteristic two admits an orthogonal basis precisely when it is nonalternating. To prove sufficiency, first split off an anisotropic line. Split further anisotropic lines if possible, then split the remaining alternating complement into hyperbolic planes. A line $e$ of value $a\ne0$ and a hyperbolic plane with basis $u,v$ and pairing $b\ne0$ can be replaced by the three orthogonal anisotropic vectors $e+u,\ e+(a/b)v,\ e+u+(a/b)v$, each of square value $a$. Reuse one such line for every remaining plane. Necessity follows from the diagonal Gram of a nondegenerate orthogonal basis.

All computations in these tests are matrix operations over the explicitly given finite field and $O(\log q)$-bit exponents. This proves the uniform finite-field recognition claim. To construct an actual basis, roots of the split squarefree minimal polynomials of the center basis are sufficient. One may ask for the roots of their lcm, itself split squarefree and of polynomial degree. Form the simultaneous eigenspaces, recover the block scalar forms and use orthogonalization. Conversely, for a given squarefree fully split $f\in\mathbb F_q[X]$, hand the raw multiplication table of $\mathbb F_q[X]/(f)$ to a natural-basis search algorithm. It is a perfect evolution algebra by the Chinese remainder theorem. The natural basis vectors are scalar multiples of the coordinate idempotents. In the split product, their pairwise vanishing products give disjoint nonempty supports, and there are as many basis vectors as coordinates. Multiplication by the class of $X$ on these lines gives all roots of $f$. These are deterministic polynomial-time search reductions. In fixed characteristic $p$, split factoring itself is deterministic polynomial bit time. In the quotient by a split squarefree polynomial use the $\mathbb F_p$-linear fixed space of $z\mapsto z^p$, which is a product of copies of $\mathbb F_p$, and split by gcds against the $p$ scalar values of a nonconstant fixed vector.

## 3. Isomorphism of perfect algebras over a fixed finite field

Perfectness means that the natural squares form a basis. In the preceding calculation all blocks are then one-dimensional, so any natural basis is a rescaling and permutation of their lines, and the raw basis is found as above over fixed $F=\mathbb F_q$. Write $M$ for the square matrix, with column $j$ the coordinates of $e_j^2$. An isomorphism must be monomial in natural bases. The map $e_i\mapsto\lambda_i e'_{\pi(i)}$ is multiplicative exactly when
$$
M'_{\pi(i),\pi(j)}=\lambda_i\lambda_j^{-2}M_{ij}.
$$
Create a directed colored graph with one root node per basis index and a separate directed cyclic fiber at each root labeled by $F^\times$. The successor is multiplication by one fixed generator. The only maps of a fiber preserving successor are rotations, precisely $z\mapsto\lambda_i z$. Add one relation type
$$
(j,z)\longrightarrow(i,M_{ij}z^2)
\quad\text{for }M_{ij}\ne0,\ z\in F^\times .
$$
The rotations and root permutation preserve these relations exactly when the transporter equation holds. The empty relation at zero coefficients preserves the support. Directed colored arcs become undirected colored incidence gadgets with separately colored tail and head ports. A vertex color can be encoded into degree by attaching distinct intervals of private leaf counts, separated by more than the pre-leaf graph size. Thus the output is a pair of ordinary simple graphs with polynomial size for fixed $F$. This gives the GI upper reduction, including in characteristic two.

## 4. Remove all absolute-value gauges over $\mathbb Q$

In a raw perfect rational algebra recover a natural basis by Section 1. Its matrix $M$ is invertible. On the arc set $E=\{(j,i):M_{ij}\ne0\}$ put the integral matrix $B$ with row $e_i^{\mathsf T}-2e_j^{\mathsf T}$, where $e_i$ is now the standard coordinate vector of the vertex space. The determinant expansion of $M$ gives a directed cycle cover of this support. Hence $Bx=0$ over $\mathbb Q$ forces $x_i=2x_j$ along each arc, and following its cycle to return multiplies the coordinate by $2^\ell\ne1$. Thus $B$ has full column rank. Every support isomorphism permutes the rows and columns of $B$ accordingly.

For an integer valuation vector $y\in\mathbb Z^E$, compute
$$
u=(B^{\mathsf T}B)^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}y,\qquad r=y-Bu .
$$
These are equivariant under simultaneous arc and vertex relabeling. If the absolute weights are changed by positive rational $\lambda_i$ as in the transporter equation, then $y$ on a given prime changes by $Bz$ for some $z\in\mathbb Z^V$. The residual $r$ and the vertex values $u_i\bmod\mathbb Z$ are invariant. Conversely equality of these labels under a support isomorphism makes $y'-y=B(u'-u)$ and $u'-u$ integral. Over all prime valuations this gives exactly the existence of positive rational multipliers.

The labels can be computed without integer factoring. Compute a joint gcd-free basis of the positive numerators and denominators of the absolute weights of both inputs. To do so iteratively split distinct bases $b,c$ by their nontrivial gcd $g$, using $g,b/g,c/g$ and updating their powers in each original integer. Merge duplicates and discard ones. The product of all stored bases decreases by at least a factor two at every step, so the number of steps is bounded by the original total bit length. All factors and nonnegative exponents remain of polynomial size. The resulting bases $b_k$ are pairwise coprime. Replace each by its maximal perfect-power-free root, updating the exponents. An exact maximal exponent is found by testing every integer exponent up to $\log_2 b_k$ using integer root comparison. This makes the gcd of the prime valuations of each $b_k$ equal to one.

Now for the vector $y_k$ of exponents of $b_k$ in the absolute rational weights, use its $r$ and $u\bmod\mathbb Z$ as edge and vertex labels. For each unknown prime $p\mid b_k$, the prime labels are scaled by $v_p(b_k)$. Equality of scaled residuals is equivalent to equality of the unscaled residual by nonzero scaling. Equality of all scaled fractional labels is equivalent to equality of the unscaled fractional labels, because the scaling integers have gcd one. This proves equivalence using only known data. In the forward construction of an actual isomorphism from equal labels one can directly take positive magnitudes $\lambda_i=\prod_k b_k^{u'_{k,i}-u_{k,i}}$, since the indicated differences are integers. For signs, the denominator $\lambda_j^2$ is positive. The only freedom is one independent sign flip at each head $i$. Record the product of signs on every pair of arcs with that head. Equality of these labels is exactly equality up to the required common flip in each row.

The positive multipliers also have polynomial bit-size. For an integral solution $x$ of $Bx=y'_k-y_k$, follow any directed cycle of length $\ell$ through vertex $i$ and substitute the equations successively. This expresses $(1-2^\ell)x_i$ as a weighted sum of $\ell$ coordinate differences with weights $1,2,\ldots,2^{\ell-1}$. Their sum is $2^\ell-1$, so $|x_i|\le\|y'_k-y_k\|_\infty$. Every vertex lies on such a cycle. Thus the reconstruction of the positive rational multipliers takes polynomial bit time given a matching graph isomorphism.

All label calculations use bounded input exponents, exact rational linear algebra and gcd and integer-root computations with polynomial bit size. Arc, vertex, and pair-of-arcs labels are encoded as colored relations, then as ordinary simple graphs as before. For ordinary colored graph isomorphism the possible rational-vector colors can simply be assigned integers from the list of different labels in the two instances together. The gcd-free basis itself may also be constructed jointly from the two inputs. This is a deterministic pair-level many-one reduction to graph isomorphism.

## 5. A bit-size reduction over varying finite fields with small exceptional part

Suppose natural bases are supplied. For a common support type set $d=\det(B^{\mathsf T} B)>0$ as an integer and $N=q-1$. Its value is invariant under support isomorphisms. Decompose $N=g b$, where $g$ has precisely the prime-power factors of $N$ with primes not dividing $d$, by repeatedly removing $\gcd(N_{\rm current},d)$ until it equals one. Thus $(g,b)=1$. Raise each edge weight to $b$ and to $g$ to place it in the two complementary cyclic subgroups, giving an injective product description and independent allowed multipliers. No discrete logarithms are needed.

On the subgroup of order $g$, $C=(B^{\mathsf T}B)^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}\bmod g$ exists and $CB=I$. Compute $\gamma_i=\prod_e w_e^{C_{ie}}$ for the projected weights $w$. It transforms by exactly the corresponding projected multiplier $\lambda_i$. The labels $w_e/\prod_i\gamma_i^{B_{ei}}$ are invariant and complete there, since equality gives the required ratio of the computed $\gamma$'s. This is an equivariant computation under support relabeling. If $g=1$, that component is void. On the subgroup of order $b$, use the $b$-point fiber construction. Therefore the complete GI upper reduction is polynomial in binary input size whenever $b$ is polynomially bounded and the roots of $X^b-1$ are available, which holds for example in fixed characteristic by Section 2. For raw tables the same natural-basis search algorithm applies in fixed characteristic. In the coprime case $b=1$ the entire reduction with supplied bases needs only field arithmetic and integer operations polynomial in the binary input size. The general explicit-fiber construction of Section 3 already covers every fixed field.

## 6. The GI lower reduction works even for absolutely simple algebras

Turn an arbitrary simple graph $G$ on $n\ge1$ vertices into a connected one by adding a universal root and $n+1$ new leaves attached only to this root. The root is the unique vertex of maximum degree. Deleting it, then deleting $n+1$ isolated vertices, recovers $G$ up to isomorphism. Regard this connected graph as a loopless symmetric digraph, with adjacency matrix $A$. Use the square matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}I+A&I\\ I&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
It is invertible over every field, as one sees from its two block equations. Its support has loops exactly on the first copy, ordinary arcs of $G$ on that copy, and the two-way matching arcs between the copies. A monomial isomorphism therefore preserves both copies and the matching. The diagonal ones force every first-copy multiplier to be one, and the matching equation then forces the same for every second-copy multiplier. Thus its isomorphisms correspond exactly to graph isomorphisms of the connected graphs. Its support is strongly connected, so the resulting evolution algebra is simple. Indeed from a nonzero vector in an ideal, multiplying by a basis vector of nonzero coefficient gives a nonzero square of that basis vector. Multiplication by further basis vectors then yields the squares at all downstream vertices, hence all squares. They span the algebra. The argument is unchanged after any extension of scalars. This is the graph-to-algebra construction in the survey's Theorem 10, here used at the level of transporter sets. [Source Theorem 10](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22248v1#Thmp10)

## 7. Literature scope

The survey attributes evolution recognition to the congruence framework of Bustamante, Mellon and Velasco. The earlier natural-family decomposition of Boudi, Cabrera Casado and Siles Molina gives the proportional-square blocks once a natural basis is available. The center method computes their intrinsic data from an arbitrary multiplication table. It does not require solving a generic tensor Waring decomposition. The finite-field recognition result is uniform in the field's bit representation, while the general GI reduction over finite fields is per fixed field. Section 5 gives an additional uniform case. [Congruence recognition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04493), [Natural families](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14460)

Wei and Zou established the monomial orbit description. The weighted-fiber relations in Section 3 are related to the multiplicative set maps used to describe lifts in *Solenoids in automorphism groups of evolution algebras*. The earlier total-colored graph construction for isotopisms uses the whole algebra's element set. The graph reductions here impose scalar multiplication on small basis fibers, or use exact rational valuation labels over $\mathbb Q$. The bounded literature screen found these antecedents and no exact statement of the combined raw-input complexity results. This does not assert exhaustive priority. [Wei–Zou](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12368), [Solenoids](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14584), [Total-colored graphs](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/7/11/1068)
