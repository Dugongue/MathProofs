# Determinant signs on balanced ternary tensors

The input consists of two dense ordered arrays over the ternary field in a common positive format. A sign bit of one denotes determinant minus one. The output is one ordinary tensor comparison over the same field. On the families below the compiler runs in deterministic polynomial time and computes whether (1,0,0) is attained and (0,0,0) is absent. Both answers have explicit scalar output pairs. The methods apply to ordinarily isomorphic inputs even when the caller supplies no transporter. The unrestricted polynomial-time case remains open.

## Matching two row spaces

Take a concise tensor in format (m,n,k), with the largest mode as output, and write the k slices as rows of a matrix of width mn. Each row is itself an m by n matrix. Act on the left of every source slice by P, and on the right of every target slice by Q. Equal row spaces mean precisely that the two transformed lists differ by an invertible output map. Moving one input action to the other side takes an inverse, which preserves determinant bits. Transposes between the slice and tensor conventions preserve these bits too.

Store the reduced row echelon matrix, the bit of P and the determinant bit of the source pivot minor. For each target row-space match, use the bit of Q and the sum of the two pivot-minor bits. Indeed, the two full-rank pivot submatrices differ by the same output map as the complete row lists. Keep every pair of bits associated with a stored row space. This recovers exactly the transporter profile by enumerating the two input groups separately. For positive dense input length L measured in ternary entries per tensor, the implementation permits at most B = 2 × 3⁹ + L² candidate matrices across the two sides. Every 3 by 3 by 3 pair is therefore covered, including pairs with smaller supports, which compress before this step. [Group-action identity](MeetWeight.lean)

## Intrinsic direct summands

For a concise three-tensor T solve the linear equations defining its centroid. A triple of endomorphisms belongs to the centroid when their single-mode actions on T all agree. Each projection is faithful by conciseness. The endomorphisms from two centroid elements commute in each mode: move the two actions onto the other two distinct modes, commute them there, and move them back in opposite order. This also shows closure under multiplication. Thus the centroid is a commutative finite-dimensional algebra C.

Compute the kernel of the linear map z ↦ z³ − z on C using any one faithful projection. This subalgebra is a copy of the ternary field to the power s, where s is the number of primitive idempotents. Indeed each element there is diagonalizable with eigenvalues 0, 1, 2, and every idempotent belongs to it. Simultaneous left eigenspaces of a basis of the subalgebra recover its s joint characters in each mode. Changes to these eigenframes are explicit. Acting on the tensor with the resulting three row frames kills every entry whose three labels disagree, since some centroid element then acts by different eigenvalues on the same entry. The matching diagonal blocks are the primitive direct summands. Any transporter permutes these summands. These are the usual centroid and direct-sum constructions. [Huang, Lu, Ye and Zhang on centers of multilinear forms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07915) · [Wilson on direct product decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.0548)

Compare blocks of equal format. A greedy ordinary matching suffices because compatibility is equivalence of isomorphism type. For a chosen matching, add the two endpoint frame-sign triples, all local transporter cosets and the block permutation signs independently in each mode. Crossing blocks of dimensions a and b adds ab modulo two in that mode. The other matchings differ by transpositions within repeated isomorphism types. A swap via a factor isomorphism and its inverse adds exactly the three block dimensions modulo two. These swaps generate all possible changes of matching. Each addition operates on at most eight triples.

The algorithm needs at most s² local comparisons and uses only one factor-matching level. Its polynomial domain includes arbitrary repetitions of blocks on which all required local comparisons succeed within the leaf bounds. The frame signs and crossing factors are essential even on tensors given in unrelated dense bases.

## One indecomposable balanced family in every dimension

Let d ≥ 3. Suppose that in format (d,d,d) the centroid is isomorphic to R = F₃[t]/(tᵈ) and the tensor is concise. The compiler recognizes this condition and computes the full profile in polynomial time from the two input arrays. Every independently reframed copy of the following tensor satisfies the condition:

Δ = Σ eᵢ ⊗ eⱼ ⊗ eₖ, summed over 0 ≤ i,j,k < d with i+j+k = 2(d−1).

Indeed Δ is equivalent, by reversing two indexing bases, to the multiplication table of R. The centroid of the latter consists exactly of multiplications by a common element of R, as follows by setting either input equal to the unit. Locality implies indecomposability.

For recognition, first require centroid dimension d. Subtract each of the three possible scalars from each basis element and check for a single nilpotent Jordan block. In the model algebra the regular nilpotents are precisely the elements with zero constant coefficient and nonzero linear coefficient. Some basis element must have nonzero linear coefficient. The faithful d-dimensional projections of such a regular nilpotent have minimal polynomial tᵈ and thus a single block. Conversely a single such operator generates a d-dimensional truncated-polynomial subalgebra, hence the entire centroid. Powers of that operator applied to a cyclic standard vector give a basis on each mode.

In these bases the standard shift N acts identically on all three legs. Its coefficient equations identify all entries with the same sum of indices: unit transfers between coordinates connect each bounded level set. At the boundary, a coordinate zero and another coordinate less than d−1 force the entry to vanish by the shift equations. Such an entry exists at every total degree below 2(d−1). Thus the normalized tensor is uniquely u(N)Δ, with u read from the entries at (r,d−1,d−1). Conciseness gives u(0) ≠ 0, since positive powers of N on one mode have combined image inside the image of N. Remove this unit on the first mode, recording its determinant u(0)ᵈ and the three computed basis determinants.

Every autotopism of Δ induces the same algebra automorphism σ of R by conjugation on the three centroid actions. Consequently each leg is a unit multiplication followed by σ: removing σ gives an R-linear map on the rank-one regular module. Write σ(t) = at + higher terms with a ≠ 0. Its determinant is a raised to d(d−1)/2, by the triangular power basis. The tensor σ⊗³Δ equals v(N)Δ with v(0) = a raised to 2(d−1), hence v(0)=1. This follows from the lowest nonzero total degree, and normalization of the centroid by σ ensures the same shift equations. Multiplication on any leg by a unit has the same effect on Δ. Thus the three unit constants have product one. Every choice with that product can be realized by correcting one unit by v⁻¹; this correction has determinant one.

The stabilizer profiles in residues d = 0, 1, 2, 3 modulo four are respectively the zero subgroup, the four even-total triples, the two diagonal triples and all eight triples. The transporter profile between independently framed copies is the appropriate translate. In particular a first-mode reflection of Δ gives a directional YES precisely for d congruent to 0, 1 or 2. No enumeration of GL(d,3) occurs in this construction.

## Intertwining equations for the remaining slice families

Choose a smallest mode m and express its slices as n by k matrices Sᵢ and Tᵢ. Enumerate coefficient changes g on the m-mode within the budget B. For each such change form the mixed target slices Uᵢ and solve the linear system X Sᵢ = Uᵢ Y for square X and Y. Invertible solutions give exactly the attainable triples with this g; converting Y or g to an inverse or transpose leaves its determinant sign unchanged. Slice-rank discrepancies reject a g immediately. When the solution space has h dimensions, enumerate its 3ʰ elements only if that number is at most B. A skipped g makes the result unresolved unless every sign has already been witnessed. This uses at most B² linear-algebra trials. As a sufficient condition it covers arbitrary n and k when all surviving g have small intertwiner spaces and the chosen mode meets the budget.

One infinite example in format (3,n,n) has slices I, a cyclic permutation matrix and the first coordinate projection. Those slices generate the full matrix algebra via the conjugates of the projection and the cyclic shift. Their commuting algebra consists of scalars. Every invertible mixing still has I in its span. In the displayed intertwining system, I on the source side implies ker Y ⊆ ker X, and I in the target span implies im Y ⊆ im X. The ranks are therefore equal and the common kernel is invariant under all source slices. Irreducibility makes every nonzero solution invertible, and dividing by any invertible solution identifies the intertwiner space with the one-dimensional commuting algebra. The same conclusions hold in arbitrary input bases since independent left and right equivalence transports the Hom spaces, and coefficient changes merely reindex the g trials.

## Verification and remaining scope

Run `python check.py` and `python check_balanced.py`. The first checks the earlier descent and the unsupported-core exit. The second compares both single-mode orbit tables and the intertwiner solver with a separate two-mode exhaustive comparator. It also checks dense independent basis changes, repeated blocks, unequal block crossings, the ternary volume tensor and truncated-polynomial tensors in every residue class. The Lean kernel verified the auxiliary orbit-collision identity. The centroid, chain and assembly arguments above are written proofs. The Python implementation was checked by execution.

A single large local centroid can survive the split and the chain test. In particular the earlier signed-nonisomorphism construction realizes ordinary nonisomorphism inside a witnessed family of local multiplication tensors. A local centroid may have multiple independent radical generators with a nontrivial product. The single-generator chain classification does not apply to such arbitrary algebras. No polynomial worst-case bound is established for the exhaustive fallback on unresolved cores.
