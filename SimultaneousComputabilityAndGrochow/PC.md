# Sharp finite-field Polynomial Calculus bounds for matrix congruence

Let $F=\mathbb F_q$ have odd characteristic. Use the entries of the three original matrix equations
$$
 XAX^{\mathsf T}=B,\qquad XY=I,\qquad YX=I
$$
and the equations $z^q-z=0$ for every original variable $z$. Ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) uses binary scalar linear combinations and multiplication by a single variable. Sparse size counts the expanded monomial occurrences across the proof. These are the congruence equations of Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She, Question 7.3, with the finite-field equations made explicit. [Question 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

**Theorem.** Every size-$n$ negative instance over $F$, including arbitrary singular and nonsymmetric inputs, has an ordinary PC refutation of degree at most $qn$ and sparse size $n^{O_q(n)}$ for $n\ge 2$. For each such field there are arbitrarily large $n$ and nonsingular diagonal symmetric negative instances with minimum degree $\Omega(n)$ and minimum sparse size $n^{\Omega(n)}$. Their equations without finite-field axioms have solutions over the quadratic extension of $F$. More generally their transporter existence profile over $\mathbb F_{q^s}$ is exactly the even $s$. At every fixed odd $s$, the same pairs have degree $\Theta_{q^s}(n)$ and sparse size $n^{\Theta_{q^s}(n)}$ in the system using the $\mathbb F_{q^s}$ field equations.

## 1. Refutation using a nonsquare value of a polynomial

Consider the field axioms on the original variables and an already derived line $Z^2-\delta=0$, where $Z$ is a polynomial of degree at most $m$ with coefficients in $F$ and $\delta\in F^\times$ is a nonsquare. Frobenius and telescoping over the variable factors of each monomial give $Z^q-Z=0$ in degree at most $qm$. Indeed the difference of the two products in a monomial is a sum of multiples of $z^q-z$, each of total degree at most $qm$. The coefficients of $Z$ are fixed by the $q$-power map. Put $k=(q-1)/2$. Then
$$
 Z^q-Z
 = Z(Z^2-\delta)\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} Z^{2(k-1-j)}\delta^j
   +(\delta^k-1) Z.
$$
Since $\delta^k=-1$, this derives $Z=0$ in degree at most $qm$. Multiply the latter line by $Z$ and subtract $Z^2-\delta=0$ to derive a nonzero constant. Every displayed multiplication by a polynomial can be performed by expanding that polynomial, deriving the individual monomial multiples using the ordinary rules, and summing. In particular the bound uses neither extension variables nor circuit representations of proof lines.

If $P$ is an $m$-square matrix of linear forms in the original variables, and $P^{\mathsf T}H_BP-H_A=0$ is already derived for nonsingular constant matrices, determinant telescoping gives $Z^2-\delta=0$ with $Z=\det P$ and $\delta=\det(H_A)/\det(H_B)$ in degree at most $2m$. In detail, for each permutation term replace the factors of the first determinant by the corresponding factors of the second one at a time. Every summand is one known entry equation multiplied by at most $m-1$ factors of degree at most two. The same calculation applies to a constant minor of the target whenever the corresponding symbolic minor is identically zero.

## 2. Extracting the obstruction for general inputs

Use the two-arrow representations $R_A=(A,A^{\mathsf T})$ and $R_B=(B,B^{\mathsf T})$, each with both vertex dimensions $n$. The duality transposes the arrows, exchanges them, and exchanges the vertices. The representations are self-dual in the given bases. A morphism from $R_A$ to $R_B$ whose two components are $(Y^{\mathsf T},X)$ is the forward part of an isometry precisely when its adjoint is its inverse. The isometry conditions then give $Y=X^{-1}$ and $XAX^{\mathsf T}=B$.

The two linear morphism constraints on $(Y^{\mathsf T},X)$ are derivable in degree at most three. One follows from
$$
 XA-BY^{\mathsf T}
 =(XAX^{\mathsf T}-B)Y^{\mathsf T}-XA(YX-I)^{\mathsf T},
$$
and the second uses the transpose of the congruence equation. Project the pair of formal matrices linearly onto the actual morphism space and call the result $h$. The errors of this projection are scalar combinations of the two linear constraints. Replacing the original pair and its adjoint by the projected pair and its adjoint in the inverse equations therefore derives
$$
 h^*h=e,\qquad hh^*=f
$$
in degree at most three. Here $e,f$ are the two orthogonal projectors onto $R_A,R_B$ in $E=\operatorname{End}(R_A\oplus R_B)$ and $h\in fEe$. All coordinates of $h$ are linear forms over $F$. For example, a difference of two products is a sum of products each containing one linear projection error. These preparations also show that constant linear projections of the two equations to any quotient factor are available in the same degree. [Earlier preparation for geometric congruence](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/5e2ab397cd7bcaaec1a54642f5e62c0558a8e77d/GeneralCongruenceSparseConjugacy)

Let $J$ be the Jacobson radical of $E$. It is invariant under the adjoint. A solution to the corner isometry equations in $E/J$ lifts to one in $E$. To see this, lift its forward map to an element $u\in fEe$. Then $u^*u$ and $uu^*$ are units in their respective corners, since each differs from the corresponding identity by a nilpotent radical element. Thus $u$ has a left and a right corner inverse, and they agree. Put $t=u^*u-e$. Over $\mathbb Z[1/2]$, the formal equation $(1+T)s(T)^2=1$ with constant coefficient $s(0)=1$ determines each successive coefficient by dividing a known expression by $2$. Truncate after a nilpotence bound for $t$. Then $s(t)$ is self-adjoint and a unit, and $u s(t)$ is the desired corner isometry. In particular the lifting uses only odd characteristic.

By Krull–Schmidt, the semisimple quotient $E/J$ is a product of matrix algebras $M_\ell(K)$ on the multiplicity spaces of the indecomposable types, with $K$ the corresponding residue division algebra. Finite division algebras are fields, so $K/F$ is a finite extension. This description of the quotient follows by taking the scalar residues of same-type blocks. Off-type blocks and the within-type radical blocks constitute the radical. The images of $e,f$ are complementary projections of sizes $a,b$ with $a+b=\ell$, namely the two multiplicities on that factor. If $e_0=[K:F]$, then
$$
 e_0a\le n,\qquad e_0b\le n .
$$
Indeed, choose a nonzero vertex space $U_v$ of one indecomposable copy $U$. Its quotient by the action of $\operatorname{rad}\operatorname{End}(U)$ is nonzero by nilpotence and is a vector space over $K$. Thus $\dim_F U_v\ge e_0$, and each copy contributes this much at that vertex. [Sergeichuk, classification of systems of forms and maps](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.0823)

If $a\ne b$, the corner equations give two rectangular inverse identities in that factor. Apply restriction of scalars to the corner matrices to obtain them in sizes $e_0a,e_0b$ over $F$, still on linear forms in the original variables. In the product of larger side, a principal minor of order $\min(e_0a,e_0b)+1$ is identically zero by Cauchy–Binet and equals one on the required identity. The telescoping calculation in Section 1 refutes it in degree at most $2n$, or three if needed for preparation.

Suppose all the matching corner ranks agree. Factors exchanged by the adjoint impose no further obstruction. On one factor choose an arbitrary invertible map from its $e$-space to its $f$-space and on its paired factor choose the adjoint of the first map's inverse. An adjoint-fixed factor is the adjoint of a nonsingular bilinear or Hermitian form on $K^\ell$. Indeed, the adjoint on its center is an automorphism $\sigma$ of order at most two, and composition with $\sigma$-transpose yields an inner automorphism of $M_\ell(K)$. The formula for the adjoint is $M\mapsto H^{-1}M^{\sigma,\mathsf T}H$ with $H^{\sigma,\mathsf T}=\lambda H$ and $\lambda\sigma(\lambda)=1$. For trivial $\sigma$ this gives symmetric or alternating $H$. For nontrivial $\sigma$, rescale $H$ by finite-field Hilbert 90 to make it Hermitian. Since the two corner idempotents are self-adjoint, $H$ splits orthogonally into its nonsingular restrictions $H_A,H_B$. In the alternating case dimension determines its isometry class, and in the Hermitian case diagonalization and surjectivity of the finite-field norm do the same. For a symmetric form, dimension and determinant squareclass determine the isometry class. One direct proof is to diagonalize and rescale each entry to $1$ or a fixed nonsquare $\nu$. A pair $\operatorname{diag}(\nu,\nu)$ is congruent to $I_2$, by taking $a,b$ with $a^2+b^2=\nu^{-1}$ and the matrix with rows $(a,b)$ and $(-b,a)$. Such $a,b$ exist because the set of squares including zero and the translate $\nu^{-1}$ minus this set each have cardinality $(|K|+1)/2$. [Finite-field form classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3142)

A negative instance with equal ranks must therefore have a symmetric residual factor of some size $a=b>0$, with $H_A,H_B$ of different determinant squareclasses. Its projected equation is
$$
 P^{\mathsf T}H_B P-H_A=0,
$$
where $P\in M_a(K)$ consists of linear forms in the original variables. Take trace forms $T_A(v,w)=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(v^{\mathsf T}H_Aw)$ and $T_B$ defined likewise. The trace forms are nonsingular, and applying the trace after the original equation derives
$$
 P_F^{\mathsf T} T_B P_F-T_A=0 ,
$$
where $P_F$ is the matrix of the restriction of scalars, an $(e_0a)$-square matrix of $F$-linear forms. To compute the ratio of the constant determinants, express the trace forms as the fixed $K/F$ trace pairing on $K^a$ composed with $H_A,H_B$. The fixed factor cancels to give
$$
 \frac{\det_F T_A}{\det_F T_B}
 =\operatorname{Nm}_{K/F}\!\left(\frac{\det_K H_A}{\det_K H_B}\right).
$$
This norm is a nonsquare in $F$: for a nonsquare $c\in K^\times$, $\operatorname{Nm}(c)^{(q-1)/2}=c^{(q^{e_0}-1)/2}=-1$. Sections 1 and 2 now give a proof of degree at most $q e_0a\le qn$, including the original degree-three preparation.

For the stated sparse upper bound, any degree-$D$ derivation on $O(n^2)$ variables is reproducible using a basis of its degree-$D$ consequence space. As each new line is examined, retain it only if it is outside the span of earlier retained lines. Before a needed multiplication or addition, recover its original parent lines by scalar combinations of retained lines, keeping degree at most $D$. There are at most $M=\binom{2n^2+D}{D}$ retained lines, and at most $O(M)$ recovered parents, each costing $O(M^2)$ monomial occurrences. If the final $1$ was already in the span, recover it in the same way. This also works when a parent has lower degree than the separate lines used to recover it. For fixed $q$ and $D\le qn$, $M^{O(1)}=n^{O_q(n)}$.

## 3. Sharp lower bound from local finite-field transporters

Take a fixed even regular degree $d\ge4$ and a family of simple $d$-regular expanders on an odd number $N$ of vertices, with a uniform positive expansion constant. The existence follows, for example, from the spectral gap of uniform simple regular graphs and the spectral expansion inequality. [Friedman, random regular graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0405020)

Write $n=dN=2|E(G)|$. On an edge $e$, let $t_e$ have values $1,-1$, with the latter value meaning a swap. Use the Tseitin equations
$$
 t_e^2-1=0,\qquad 1+\prod_{e\ni v}t_e=0 \quad(v\in V(G)).
$$
Buss, Grigoriev, Impagliazzo and Pitassi prove that these equations require PC degree $\Omega(N)$ over every field of odd characteristic. The constant is uniform when the expansion is fixed. [BGIP, Theorem 10](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/lineargaps/journalfinal.pdf)

Use the $n$ half-edges as both coordinate sets. The source diagonal matrix $A$ carries one $1$ and one $\nu$ on the two half-edges of each edge, for a fixed nonsquare $\nu\in F^\times$. Let $a_v$ be the parity of the number of $\nu$ entries originally incident to $v$. The target block $B_v$ on the half-edges of $v$ has one $\nu$ if $a_v+1$ is odd and two $\nu$'s if $a_v+1$ is even, with all other entries $1$. Put $B=\bigoplus_v B_v$. The determinant classes of $A,B$ differ, because the prescribed parity differences at the vertices sum to odd $N$. Both matrices are nonsingular. Each of the two value classes of $A$, and each of the two value classes of $B$, has size $\Omega(n)$.

For each choice of the signs, let $S(t)$ be the permutation matrix swapping the two half-edges of exactly the edges with $t_e=-1$. The block of $SAS^{\mathsf T}$ on $v$ is a nonsingular diagonal form. If its vertex equation holds, its determinant parity is $a_v+1$, so choose a matrix $L_v$ carrying it to $B_v$ by congruence. If the vertex equation fails, choose $L_v=I_d$. The choice of both $L_v$ and $L_v^{-1}$ is a finite table depending only on the $d$ incident edge signs. Define, on each sign assignment,
$$
 X=(\bigoplus_v L_v)S,\qquad Y=S^{\mathsf T}(\bigoplus_v L_v^{-1}).
$$
Take for each displayed entry its multilinear polynomial representative on its at most $d$ signs. This interpolation is over $F$, since $1$ and $-1$ are distinct. The two inverse equations hold on every assignment. The off-diagonal vertex blocks of the congruence equation vanish on every assignment and its diagonal block on $v$ vanishes whenever the vertex equation at $v$ holds. The field equations hold on every assignment. This construction applies unchanged over any extension field when its own field equations are used.

The support graph $H$ is bipartite between the source and target half-edge coordinate sets. Allow a pair precisely when the source half-edge is incident to the target's vertex, or is the twin of such a half-edge. Every entry of $X$ or transposed entry of $Y$ outside these pairs is zero under the substitution, and $H$ has maximum degree at most $2d$ on both sides. For every permitted pair, the two entry images depend only on signs incident to its target vertex.

We give the proof transfer in the quotient by $t_e^2-1$. Each polynomial has a unique multilinear representative, determined by its values on the sign cube. An axiom with image vanishing everywhere has representative zero. A congruence axiom within $B_v$ has image zero on all locally satisfying signs. Extend the quotient of its value by the vertex polynomial $C_v=1+\prod_{e\ni v}t_e$ to the entire local cube by taking zero on satisfying signs. On the other signs $C_v=2\ne0$. Interpolate this quotient as a multilinear polynomial of degree at most $d$. Multiplying it into $C_v$, then multilinearizing, derives the required image in degree at most $2d$. Multilinearizing any line of degree $D$ costs degree at most $D$, by subtracting monomial multiples of $t_e^2-1$. This proves constant-degree preparation for all axiom images, including the field equations whose representatives are zero.

More precisely, suppose every monomial in a matrix proof uses at most $k$ distinct variables with nonzero substitution images, counting only monomials with no zero-image variable. Its multilinear image has degree at most $dk$, since each such monomial depends on the union of at most $k$ sets of at most $d$ signs. This estimate is independent of the raw exponents. Replay a scalar combination by linearity. For a variable multiplication, multiply the earlier multilinear representative by the degree-$d$ image of the variable, using monomial multiples and then reducing by $t_e^2-1$, all in degree at most $d(k+1)$. The reproduced target line is the multilinear representative of the original substitution, since the reduction is in a quotient ring. Thus a matrix refutation of the stated width would give a Tseitin refutation in degree at most $\max(2d,d(k+1))$. It follows that every matrix refutation has a surviving monomial with at least $c n$ distinct variables for an absolute $c>0$ from the fixed expanders. This supplies the degree lower bound as well. If the coefficient field is varied, the hard examples also have degree at least $q$, since a proof must use a field axiom and the raw system has an algebraic-closure solution.

For the size lower bound, independently permute the source coordinates inside each equal-value class of $A$ and the target coordinates inside each equal-value class of $B$. Permute the substitution accordingly. The transporter identities are preserved, and the permuted support is the correspondingly relabeled $H$. Therefore for **every** such relabeling, a fixed proposed refutation must have an occurrence with at least $cn$ distinct variables all supported on the relabeled graph.

Fix a monomial occurrence with at least $cn$ distinct variables. Its bipartite set of pairs has at least $cn/2$ edges, since each pair labels at most one $X$- and one $Y$-variable. If the edge set has maximum degree exceeding $2d$, it cannot survive. Otherwise a greedy matching has $\Omega(n)$ edges. Fix a submatching of $\lfloor c'n\rfloor$ edges where $c'>0$ is small enough that each source value class retains $\Omega(n)$ unexposed positions after revealing all those edges. Condition on the target permutation and expose the source preimages along the chosen matching. Each is uniform among the unused coordinates of its value class and has at most $2d$ permitted neighbors in the fixed support, so each conditional success probability is $O(1/n)$. The probability that the fixed monomial survives is at most $n^{-\Omega(n)}$. Taking a union bound over the original proof occurrences forces $n^{\Omega(n)}$ occurrences. No degree bound on the original proof was required for this counting argument.

Finally, $\nu^{(q^s-1)/2}=(-1)^{1+q+\cdots+q^{s-1}}=(-1)^s$ in $\mathbb F_{q^s}$. The determinant classification therefore gives exactly the claimed profile. In the even probes actual witnesses exist and may be transported along extension embeddings. At odd probes every refutation has the same lower bounds, since the local tables are already over the base field. This exhibits one extension-existence profile carrying an unbounded resource requirement on the negative probe certificates. The one-dimensional pair $([1],[\nu])$ has the same existence profile and admits degree at most $q^s$ on each fixed odd probe.

## 4. Exact extension profiles and every threshold for labelled forms

**Theorem.** Consider any finite labelled tuple of bilinear forms on one $F$-space with one common congruence variable. For two such tuples, the existence of a common transporter over $\mathbb F_{q^s}$, as $s\ge1$ varies, is either identically false or exactly $2^k\mid s$ for some integer $k\ge0$. Every $k$ is attained. For every fixed $k\ge1$ there is a family of pairs of two nonsingular symmetric forms realizing $2^k\mid s$ whose negative probes have sharp sparse PC size $n^{\Theta_{q^s,k}(n)}$ and degree $\Theta_{q^s,k}(n)$ for each fixed $s$ with $2^k\nmid s$. The universal degree bound $qn$ from Section 2 also applies to all negative tuple comparisons over the base field.

*Proof.* Give the two-arrow quiver in Section 2 one pair of adjoint arrows for each labelled form. The same degree-three preparation derives the two projected inverse equations on the common morphism space, since the preparation is applied individually to each arrow equation. Everything else in the upper bound is on $E/J$ and is unchanged. In particular the dimension bound $e_0a,e_0b\le n$ still holds at the two vertices. The sparse upper bound remains valid since the compression depends on the number of proof variables and degree, and selects only the used input equations.

Under finite base extension $L/F$, the Hom spaces, and hence $E$, extend by scalars since their defining equations are linear. The radical becomes $J\otimes_F L$, because that ideal is nilpotent and its quotient remains semisimple under scalar extension of the finite center fields. A residue corner rank mismatch therefore persists under every extension. If all ranks match, let the obstructing factors over $F$ be the adjoint-fixed symmetric factors with nonsquare determinant ratios. Neither an alternating factor nor a unitary factor can acquire a symmetric obstruction after extension. An alternating form remains alternating. A unitary factor either is exchanged with another factor or retains a nontrivial involution on its center, because on a fixed component the copy of its original center embeds injectively and retains its nontrivial involution. An originally exchanged pair still has its two factors exchanged in pairs.

For each obstructing center $K=\mathbb F_{q^e}$ and $L=\mathbb F_{q^s}$, $K\otimes_F L$ is the product of $\gcd(e,s)$ copies of $\mathbb F_{q^{\operatorname{lcm}(e,s)}}$. In each component, a nonsquare of $K$ becomes a square precisely if the relative extension degree $s/\gcd(e,s)$ is even, equivalently $v_2(s)>v_2(e)$. Thus the condition is $2^k\mid s$ with $k=1+\max v_2(e)$ over the obstructing factors. If this list is empty, there is no obstruction and $k=0$. Correctness in each extension follows from the same lifting through the nilpotent radical.

Here is an explicit realization for a fixed $k\ge1$. Set $e=2^{k-1}$, choose a primitive field generator $\theta\ne0$ of $K=\mathbb F_{q^e}/F$ and a nonsquare $\nu\in K^\times$. On the $F$-space $K$, use source forms with matrices inverse to those of $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(\nu v w)$ and $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(\nu\theta v w)$. Use corresponding target forms with $\nu$ replaced by $1$. Taking inverses in the congruence equations is equivalent to transporting the Gram matrices by $X^{\mathsf T}H_BX=H_A$. The ratio between the two Gram matrices on either side is the operator of multiplication by $\theta$. Thus simultaneous isometries commute with that operator, and over $L$ are multiplication by a unit $u$ of $K\otimes_F L$. By nondegeneracy of the trace forms the first equality is equivalent to $u^2=\nu$, which implies the second. This gives exactly $2^k\mid s$. Equal tuples attain $k=0$. Unequal ranks attain the identically false profile.

To get hard pairs at this same threshold, run the construction of Section 3 over $K$, making the source and target diagonal matrices $A_K,B_K$ on $K^m$ for $m=dN$. Write $D_A=A_K^{-1},D_B=B_K^{-1}$. Use the pair of inverse Gram matrices over $F$ of the trace forms defined by $D_A,\theta D_A$ on the source, and $D_B,\theta D_B$ on the target. The common isometry is again $K\otimes_F L$-linear, by the ratio operator. Taking the trace against all scalar multiples of a test vector shows that a $K\otimes_F L$-linear trace isometry is exactly a $K\otimes_F L$-linear isometry of the corresponding $K$-forms. The determinant classification on all simple components gives the exact threshold $2^k\mid s$.

For the proof lower bounds, interpret every local $K$-matrix entry of the transporter as an $e$-square block over $F$ by the multiplication representation $K\to M_e(F)$. Each scalar entry of that block has a local multilinear table on the same at most $d$ signs. Both inverse identities hold on all assignments. Each congruence equation for either labelled form has the same local validity as before, since the restriction of scalars preserves isometries of the trace form, and simultaneously preserves its twist by $\theta$. The field equations again vanish on the sign cube. Thus the multilinear proof transfer gives a required surviving occurrence of $\Omega(m)$ distinct scalar matrix variables. For the relabeling argument, permute the $K$-coordinate blocks inside each of the two equal diagonal value classes on either side. These permutations preserve both trace forms. Each bipartite pair of blocks labels at most $2e^2$ scalar matrix variables. A surviving occurrence must therefore use $\Omega_e(m)$ pairs in a block support of maximum degree $2d$, and has probability $m^{-\Omega_e(m)}$ under the same matching and permutation argument. Since $n=em$ with fixed $e$, this gives sparse size $n^{\Omega_k(n)}$ on every negative probe. The degree bounds follow because each distinct variable contributes at least one to the raw degree, and the upper bound is $q^s n$ over the fixed probe. $\square$

These examples distinguish feasibility along field extensions from the resources required to certify absence. For each prescribed dyadic threshold, the small trace pair and the hard trace-pair family give the same decisions on all probes with sharply different negative-certificate complexity.

The results are written proofs with the cited degree and form results as inputs. They settle the sharp worst-case degree order and sparse-size exponent for matrix congruence with field equations over fixed odd finite fields. For varying odd $q$, the stated upper degree is $qn$ and the hard family gives $\Omega(\max(n,q))$. All bounds are unconditional.
