# Integral tensor isomorphism and exact entry degrees

The [simultaneous-computability calibration theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/5e2ab397cd7bcaaec1a54642f5e62c0558a8e77d/SimultaneousComputability/InfiniteProbes.md) prescribes the least accepting field degree for integral systems. Here the input is an ordinary pair of tensors on free modules of side two. The same entry profile occurs for matrix conjugacy, matrix congruence, and integral rank-two decomposability.

This results from passing from field witnesses to witnesses over rings of integers. Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She Open Question 7.3 uses matrix conjugacy and congruence over fields. The arithmetic profile below concerns the same matrix equations over integer rings. It is directly within the free-module tensor setting of Grochow, Qiao and Stange, Part V. Their small-tensor principality result treats integer cubes of nonsquare fundamental discriminant. The split cubes here classify an integral genus over a general Dedekind domain and track base change and entry degrees. [Open Question 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3) · [Part V, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735#S4).

## 1. Split diagonal tensors over Dedekind domains

Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain with fraction field $K$. Write $D_r(x,y,z)=\sum_{j=1}^r x_jy_jz_j$ for the split diagonal trilinear form of side $r$. Two trilinear forms are isomorphic if they agree after independent invertible changes of basis in their three arguments. This agrees with ordinary tensor isomorphism by passage to the dual bases. Consider forms $T$ on three free rank-$r$ $R$-modules that are isomorphic to $D_r$ over $K$ and over $R_{\mathfrak m}$ for every nonzero maximal ideal.

**Theorem 1.** The isomorphism classes of these forms are in bijection with $3\times r$ tables in the ideal class group $\mathrm{Cl}(R)$ whose row and column sums are zero, up to one common permutation of the columns. The bijection commutes with scalar extension to the ring of integers of any finite number field extension when $R$ is a ring of integers. With explicit ideals, an isomorphism at any such probe is precisely a column permutation and ideal rescalings whose product is one in each column.

*Proof.* In any field, contraction of $D_r$ in one argument identifies that argument with the diagonal subspace of $r\times r$ matrices. Its rank-one lines are exactly the coordinate matrix lines. Thus an isomorphism of split forms must permute the coordinate lines in each argument. Evaluating on triples supported at a common index shows that the permutations are the same and the three scalings at each index have product one. This argument uses matrix rank over the fraction field.

Fix an isomorphism over $K$ and write $I_{ij}$ for the intersection of the $i$-th module lattice with its $j$-th coordinate line. Each intersection is a nonzero fractional ideal of $R$. At each localization, a local isomorphism with $D_r$ differs from the chosen fraction-field isomorphism by the monomial maps just computed. Consequently the localized intersections form a direct decomposition of the module, and the products of the three ideals in each column are the unit ideal there. A map of finitely generated modules is an isomorphism if this holds at all maximal localizations. Hence each lattice is $\bigoplus_j I_{ij}$ and $\prod_i I_{ij}=R$. The row class sum is zero by taking the top exterior power of the free lattice.

Conversely realize a class table by fractional ideals. In each column the product ideal is principal, and scaling one factor in that column makes the product exactly $R$. The row-lattice is free by the Steinitz classification of finitely generated projective modules over a Dedekind domain. On these three lattices use $D_r$. Locally each ideal has a generator and the product can be made one in each column, so the form is locally split.

Any integral isomorphism is a monomial one over $K$, and therefore restricts to ideal rescalings. If the ideal classes agree after a column permutation, choose such rescalings. Their product in each column is a unit by the product-ideal identities. Rescale one of them by the inverse unit so the product is one. This gives an actual isomorphism, and specifies the witnesses without choosing arbitrary new isomorphisms at each extension. Under extension of integer rings the decomposed lattice is the sum of the extended ideals and the displayed formula still defines the tensor. This proves base-change compatibility. $\square$

For $R=\mathcal O_K$, write $T_I,T_J$ for two such forms. For each column permutation $\pi$, let $C_\pi$ be generated by the differences $[I_{ij}]-[J_{i,\pi(j)}]$. At a probe $E/K$, the complete isomorphism test is whether some $C_\pi$ maps to zero in $\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_E)$. Define the entry degree as the minimum $[E:K]$ at which this happens. Then
$$
 \min_\pi\exp(C_\pi)\ \le\ \text{entry degree}\ \le\ \min_\pi |C_\pi|.
$$
Indeed extension followed by norm multiplies an ideal class by $[E:K]$, giving divisibility by the exponent of at least one $C_\pi$. For the upper bound choose a cyclic decomposition of $C_\pi$. For each generator represented by $A$ of order $d$, write $A^d=(a)$. In an extension adjoining a $d$-th root of $a$ the extended ideal is generated by that root, since equality of $d$-th powers of fractional ideals gives equality by valuations. The compositum of those root extensions has degree at most the product of the orders, namely $|C_\pi|$. If every $C_\pi$ is cyclic, the bounds coincide. These are formulas for the joint profile across probes, as opposed to tests over one ring.

## 2. One ideal gives four simultaneous exact profiles

**Theorem 2.** Let $I$ have class order $d$ in the integer ring $R=\mathcal O_K$. There are an ordinary pair of $2\times2\times2$ tensors, a pair of $2\times2$ idempotent matrices, and a pair of $2\times2$ unimodular symmetric matrices over $R$ with the following common profile. For every finite extension $E/K$, each pair is isomorphic in its respective sense over $\mathcal O_E$ exactly when $I\mathcal O_E$ is principal. The source tensor has integral tensor rank exactly two at precisely those same probes. All the pairs are isomorphic over every finite completion's integer ring and over every extension field of $K$. For number-field integral probes, the least accepting degree is exactly $d$, all accepted degrees are multiples of $d$, and every multiple occurs.

*Proof.* Take $M=I\oplus I^{-1}$ and $N=I^{-1}\oplus I$, both free of rank two. On $M,N,R^2$ put
$$
 T_I(x,y,z)=x_+y_+z_+ + x_-y_-z_- .
$$
Compare with $D_2$. Theorem 1 gives the exact isomorphism condition. In an extension where $I$ is principal, the displayed sum gives a rank-two decomposition in free bases. Conversely, a length-two decomposition over $\mathcal O_E$ is also a length-two decomposition over $E$. In each mode the flattening has rank two, so the two factor vectors are independent in that mode. The rank-one lines of the contracted diagonal matrix space identify these two terms with the two coordinate terms, up to a common permutation. Thus their factor covectors belong to the duals of the corresponding line lattices. Their product in each column equals one. For invertible ideals $A_1A_2A_3=R$, membership $a_i\in A_i$ and $\prod a_i=1$ imply $(a_i)=A_i$, by comparing local valuations and using the equality of their sum. The dual line lattices are therefore principal. The tensor rank is always finite, since the lattices themselves are free and the tensor has a coordinate expansion.

For conjugacy, compare the projection $P_I$ onto $I$ along $I^{-1}$ with $\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$. Conjugacy preserves the image and kernel, and generators of the two lines construct the conjugator. For congruence use the form $B_I(x,y)=x_+y_-+x_-y_+$ on $M$ and compare it with the usual hyperbolic binary form. It is unimodular by ideal duality. Over the fraction field of any probe the two isotropic lines are exactly the two coordinate lines. Any isometry permutes them and hence identifies their integral line lattices. Conversely a generator and its inverse give an isometry when $I$ is principal.

Every fractional invertible ideal is principal over a completed discrete valuation ring and over a field, which supplies all the claimed local tests and field tests. For the exact entry degree, norm gives $d\mid[E:K]$ at every integral positive, and a $d$-th root of a generator of $I^d$ realizes a positive in degree at most $d$. It therefore realizes exactly $d$. Taking extensions of that field of arbitrary prescribed degree, for example by Eisenstein polynomials, realizes every multiple. $\square$

Ankeny–Chowla give imaginary quadratic fields with a class of every prescribed order, so allowing the base integer ring to vary yields each entry degree even at side two. Side one cannot have a nontrivial threshold. A scalar $a$ and a scalar $b$ become isomorphic over an extension ring of integers if both are zero, or if $a/b$ is a unit there. For $a,b\in R$ nonzero, the latter holds exactly when $a/b$ and its inverse already lie in $R$, by integrality in the base fraction field. [Ankeny–Chowla](https://msp.org/pjm/1955/5-2/pjm-v5-n2-p02-p.pdf) · [Byeon–Lee, introduction](https://www.math.snu.ac.kr/~dhbyeon/51_ideal_2_r.pdf).

## 3. An explicit degree-three instance

Let $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-23})/2$, $R=\mathbb Z[\omega]$ and $I=(2,\omega)$. The congruence $\omega^2-\omega+6=0$ makes $2$ split. The element $2-\omega$ has norm $8$, lies in $I$, and is a unit at the other prime above $2$. Thus $I^3=(2-\omega)$. The norm form $a^2+ab+6b^2=(a+b/2)^2+23b^2/4$ never equals $2$ at integers, so the class order is exactly $3$. One first positive probe is $K(\sqrt[3]{2-\omega})$.

Take $\beta=(1-\omega)/2$, so that $I^{-1}=(1,\beta)$ and $\omega\beta=3$. The ordered pairs $(2,-\beta),(\omega,-1)$ give a basis of $I\oplus I^{-1}$, and use the swapped coordinate pairs for the second mode. The basis determinant is $-2+\omega\beta=1$. In these bases the two tensor slices are $A,A^{\mathsf T}$, and the projector and symmetric form are $P,B$, where
$$
 A=\begin{pmatrix}\omega-1&-2\\-3&-\omega\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 P=\begin{pmatrix}-2&-\omega\\1-\omega&3\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}2\omega-2&-5\\-5&-2\omega\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The determinant of the slice pencil is $\det(XA+YA^{\mathsf T})=-XY$. Thus the example has split discriminant $1$ and is already a geometric rank-two tensor. All global negatives here refer to integral witnesses.

The arithmetic and module arguments above are written proofs. The companion Lean file checks the exact determinant identity for the explicit pencil. It does not formalize Steinitz classification or ideal norms.
