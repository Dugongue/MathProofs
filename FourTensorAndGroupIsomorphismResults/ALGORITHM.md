# Subquadratic-exponent tensor isomorphism over the integers modulo four

**Theorem.** Ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $R=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ for explicit free arrays of maximum side length $n$ can be decided in time $2^{O(n^{7/4}\log(n+2))}$. The proof uses the alternating matrix-tuple algorithm of Sun, Appendix A, Theorem A.1. It covers all inputs, including degenerate tensors. This is an informal proof. The problem concerning a near-linear reduction to one binary tensor instance remains open in this work. [Sun, *Faster Isomorphism for Groups of Class 2*](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920#A)

All linear algebra in the proof is over $R$ unless a field is specified. For a matrix $A$, let $\nu(A)$ be the minimum number of generators of its image, equivalently the number of nonzero Smith pivots. It is invariant under invertible left and right multiplication and is additive on direct sums. It cannot increase upon taking a submatrix in any bases: a Smith factorization with $\nu(A)$ intermediate coordinates restricts to a factorization of the submatrix, and submodules of a finite abelian $2$-group can be generated by at most as many elements as the group. Smith reduction over $R$ and the associated kernel, image, membership, and linear equation computations run in polynomial time. A smallest generating list of a module maps to a basis of its quotient by twice the module.

## 1. Two alternating markers

View an ordinary tensor as a rectangular matrix tuple $(T_1,\ldots,T_c)$ of shape $a\times b$. Ordinary TI permits invertible mixing of slices and independent invertible left and right multiplication. Introduce $E_1=X\oplus X'\oplus H_1$ and $E_2=Y\oplus Y'\oplus H_2$, where $X,X'$ have rank $a$, $Y,Y'$ have rank $b$, and $H_i$ have even ranks $h_i$. On $E_1\oplus E_2$, keep the alternating data slices with $T_j$ on the $X,Y$ block, $-T_j^{\mathsf T}$ on the reverse block, and zeros elsewhere. Append a marker $C_1$ that pairs $X$ with $X'$ by the identity, is standard symplectic on $H_1$, and vanishes elsewhere. Append the analogous $C_2$ for $Y,Y',H_2$. Regard the resulting tuple as an alternating bilinear map to $R^{c+2}$ with a variable invertible output map. Its common radical is zero since the marker radicals intersect trivially. The ranks $h_i$ can be chosen linear in $a+b$ by taking $h_1>2(a+b)$ and $h_2>h_1+2(a+b)$.

The marker supports cannot mix. For any linear combination of the slices with respective marker coefficients $s_1,s_2$, the generator count of its image lies in

$$
[h_1\mathbf 1_{s_1\ne0}+h_2\mathbf 1_{s_2\ne0},\quad h_1\mathbf 1_{s_1\ne0}+h_2\mathbf 1_{s_2\ne0}+2(a+b)].
$$

The four intervals are disjoint. This uses the private symplectic block, whose image needs $h_i$ generators for every nonzero scalar, including $2$. In an isometry of tuples with slice mixing, each data output slice therefore uses only data, and each marker output slice uses only its own marker plus data. The two marker coefficients are units and the data coefficient block is invertible, because the full slice-mixing matrix is invertible and block triangular.

Use the action $A\mapsto P A P^{\mathsf T}$. After mixing, simultaneously remove the added $X,Y$ blocks of the two markers by a shear adding multiples of $X'$ to the $Y$ coordinates and of $Y'$ to the $X$ coordinates. Explicitly, for marker scalars $s_1,s_2$ and added $X,Y$ blocks $D_1,D_2$, the shear has blocks $(Y,X')=-s_1^{-1}D_1^{\mathsf T}$ and $(X,Y')=s_2^{-1}D_2$, with identity on the diagonal. Each block clears one marker and has zero effect on the other. The shear fixes every data slice. The remaining congruence carries each $E_i$ to itself, since $E_i$ is the image of the corresponding cleaned marker. The data then give left-right equivalence of the same rectangular tuples padded with zeros in extra rows and columns. Cancel the padding by Krull–Schmidt for finite-length modules: tuples of maps between two modules are representations of the quiver with two vertices and $c$ parallel arrows, and padding is direct sum with fixed zero-arrow representations on the extra modules at the two vertices. These representations are finite modules over the path algebra over $R$. Conversely an original transporter extends to the new summands by the inverse transpose on $X',Y'$ and identity on $H_1,H_2$, and trivially on marker output coordinates. Thus the reduction is exact and has linear side lengths. The field analogue of alternating marker reductions is due to Grochow–Qiao. [Grochow–Qiao IV, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S2)

## 2. A short probe for a space of alternating matrices

Let $V=R^d$, $W=R^m$, and $b:V\times V\to W$ be alternating. Write $A_z$ for the matrix of $z^{\mathsf T}b$ for $z\in W^*$, identifying linear functionals with their coefficient vectors. Pick $t=\lceil 8\sqrt{m+1}\rceil$. There is a list $P$ of $2t$ vectors in $V$ such that every $A_z$ vanishing on $P\times P$ has $\nu(A_z)<8t$.

To prove existence take $P$ as the rows of two independent uniform $t\times d$ matrices $L,M$. For a fixed $A_z$ with at least $8t$ nonzero Smith pivots, the distribution of $L A_z M^{\mathsf T}$ is unchanged upon replacing $A_z$ by its Smith form, because invertible linear transformations of the random rows are uniform. There are at least $4t$ pivots of one of the two types, unit or twice a unit. In the unit case reduce the product modulo two. In the even case condition on the contributions of all other pivots and, after dividing the even contribution by two, test the remaining product modulo two. In either case one obtains a necessary equation $B C=D$ for independent uniform binary matrices of sizes $t\times s$ and $s\times t$, with $s\ge4t$ and $D$ fixed under this conditioning. When the fixed contribution in the even case is not even the conditional probability is zero. Let $k=\lceil t/2\rceil$. If $\operatorname{rank} B< t/2$, its left kernel contains a $k$-dimensional subspace. There are at most $2^{t k}$ such subspaces and each lies in the left kernel with probability $2^{-s k}$. Otherwise each of the $t$ columns of $C$ satisfies its required equation with probability at most $2^{-t/2}$ independently. Hence the total failure probability is at most $2^{-(s-t)k}+2^{-t^2/2}$. A union bound over $4^m$ possible $z$ gives a probability strictly below one. If no slice combination has $8t$ pivots the condition holds for every probe.

## 3. Small complement to a common isotropic summand

First, for a binary vector space of alternating $d\times d$ matrices with maximum binary rank $r$, there is a common isotropic subspace of codimension at most $r s^2$, where $s=\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil$ for $d\ge1$. Over the extension field with $2^s>d$ elements, a generic maximum-rank minor of the linear matrix can be made nonzero by evaluation since its degree is at most $d$. Write $A$ for an extension-field matrix attaining this generic rank $\rho$. Expanding its coefficients in a binary field basis expresses it as a sum of $s$ scalar multiples of binary-space matrices, so $\rho\le sr$. For every matrix $B$ in the original space, the coefficient of the linear term in the minors of size $\rho+1$ of $A+uB$ shows that $B$ vanishes as a pairing between the left and right kernels of $A$. Indeed, use independent bases to put $A$ in rank normal form and take each minor using all its nonzero diagonal pivots and one additional row and column. Alternation makes the left and right kernel the same subspace under transpose. Thus the binary vectors satisfying $Av=0$ form a common isotropic subspace. The $\rho$ independent extension-field equations give at most $s\rho$ binary equations. The zero-dimensional case is immediate.

Over $R$, suppose all matrices of an alternating matrix submodule have $\nu(A)\le r$. Apply the preceding argument to the space of their reductions modulo two. Lift a binary basis of the common isotropic space to a free direct summand in $V$ by lifting and extending a basis. Every compressed form on that summand now has even entries. Divide those entries by two, obtaining a binary space of alternating matrices. These have binary rank at most $r$ by Smith reduction of the even matrices and monotonicity of $\nu$ under compression. Apply the binary argument again and lift a basis within the summand. This gives a free common isotropic direct summand $U_0$ with codimension $h=O(r\log^2(d+2))$. The first lift ensures divisibility by two and the second ensures vanishing modulo four. This argument is purely existential, since the search below only needs lists certifying the resulting property.

## 4. Short lists and a canonical submodule from them

Use a good $P$ from Section 2 and put $\mathcal A=\{A_z:A_z(P,P)=0\}$. Section 3 gives $U_0$ of codimension $h=O(t\log^2(d+2))$ isotropic for $\mathcal A$. The module $\sum_{A\in\mathcal A} A U_0$ of covectors lies in the annihilator of $U_0$, hence has cardinality at most $4^h$. Choose at most $2h$ individual vectors $Au$ from its generating set, with $u\in U_0$, to span it. This can be done by adding a vector outside the span until it is full, since each increase at least doubles the span. Take the list $T$ of the corresponding $u$'s. The following modules are computable from $b,P,T$ by Smith operations:

$$
S=\sum_{A\in\mathcal A} A\operatorname{span}(T),\qquad
K=\{v\in V: s^{\mathsf T}v=0\text{ for all }s\in S,\quad Av\in S\text{ for all }A\in\mathcal A\}.
$$

For the chosen lists, $K$ contains $U_0$. For arbitrary lists, $K$ is common isotropic for $\mathcal A$ by definition. There is a list $J$ of at most $h$ extra vectors with $V=K+\operatorname{span}(P,T,J)$, since a basis of a free complement of $U_0$ suffices. Write $D=(P,T,J)$ for the combined list and $H=\operatorname{span}\{b(u,v):u,v\in P\}\subseteq W$. The annihilator of $H$ in $W^*$ is precisely the set of $z$ defining $\mathcal A$. Therefore $b(K,K)\subseteq H$ by double annihilator over $R$. For example, in Smith coordinates the annihilator of $2R$ on one coordinate is $2R$ and its double annihilator is $2R$, with $0$ and $R$ interchanged under one annihilator.

On the source enumerate lists of these lengths until one satisfies the span test $K+\operatorname{span}(D)=V$, which is a polynomial-time test. An explicit choice of caps is $r=8t$, $h=\min(d,2r\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil^2)$, and list lengths $2t,2h,h$, with zero padding. The bounds above guarantee success. On the target enumerate all lists of the same lengths, discarding failures of the span test. If $(g,q)$ is a true bimap isometry, the list $gD$ occurs on the target. In fact $S$ transforms contragrediently and $K$ transforms by $g$, since the contractions $A_z$ transform by simultaneous inverse congruence with the coefficient vector changed by $q$. The number of choices on each side is bounded by $4^{O(d t\log^2(d+2))}$. All computations of $\mathcal A,S,K,H$ can use generators over $R$ obtained from linear equations. There is no need to enumerate matrix spaces inside a branch.

## 5. Verify each surviving branch by alternating matrix-tuple isometry

Use primes to denote the target data. Check whether the indicated correspondence $b(u_i,u_j)\mapsto b'(u_i',u_j')$ for probe vectors respects the relations in both directions. This is an equality test for two kernels of generator maps. If successful it defines an isomorphism $\phi:H\to H'$. Seek simultaneous invertible actions $g:V\to V'$ and $q:W\to W'$ that carry all list vectors to their mates and intertwine the linear maps $b(u,-):V\to W$ for $u\in D$. These equations imply $q|_H=\phi$.

Choose minimal generating covers $E:R^k\to K\subseteq V$ and $E':R^{k'}\to K'\subseteq V'$, given by the images of basis vectors. Reject if $k\ne k'$. Add an invertible action $c:R^k\to R^k$ and enforce $gE=E'c$. A genuine $g:K\to K'$ admits such a lift: choose arbitrary preimages of the desired generator images, and the resulting matrix is invertible modulo two because minimal generators give bases modulo twice their modules. For each coordinate functional $\lambda:W\to R$, choose an extension $\lambda':W'\to R$ of $\lambda\phi^{-1}:H'\to R$. Such extensions are computable using a Smith basis of $H'$ inside $W'$: the image of $2e_i$ under any functional on $H'$ belongs to $2R$ and therefore extends to a value on $e_i$. Require the ordered alternating forms $\lambda b(E-,E-)$ and $\lambda' b'(E'-,E'-)$ to intertwine by $c$. These forms and the cross maps together impose all bimap equations. On $K\times K$ the two outputs lie in $H,H'$, and testing every coordinate through $\phi^{-1}$ gives equality after $q$. If one argument is in $D$ the cross maps give equality. The span test and bilinearity now give equality on $V\times V$. Conversely every genuine isometry carrying the lists satisfies the conditions, with a suitable cover lift.

Here is a linear-size compiler for this ordered data. For each of the three free sorts use two primal copies and two dual copies. Mark the four alternating matrices pairing every primal copy with every dual copy by the identity. Their image submodules distinguish the four copies by intersection. Under matrix congruence $F\mapsto MFM^{\mathsf T}$ the markers then force one common matrix $G$ on the primal copies and $G^{-\mathsf T}$ on the dual copies. A linear map is recorded as the alternating cross block between its target primal and source dual. A covariant alternating form on two inputs is recorded on their dual sort, so its matrix becomes $G^{-\mathsf T} F G^{-1}$. For vectors, use three auxiliary line coordinates with pairwise alternating pair markers, which recover the lines by image intersections. Their actions are units $a,b,c$ with $ab=bc=ca=1$, hence all equal to a unit $\eta$ with $\eta^2=1$. Mark each listed vector through the cross block with the first line. Replacing the entire implementing matrix by $\eta$ times itself preserves all congruences and makes the line actions the identity, yielding the desired pointwise transporter. Conversely genuine transporters extend with identity on the auxiliary lines. Each matrix thus has dimension $4(d+m+k)+3=O(d+m)$ since $k\le d$, and the ordered tuple has polynomial length. This is a single ordered alternating tuple check per surviving list.

## 6. Runtime and scope

The initial marker construction gives $d,m=O(n)$. List generation and enumeration cost $2^{O(n^{3/2}\log^2(n+2))}$ times a polynomial. Per list, use Sun's alternating matrix-tuple isometry algorithm over $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$. For matrix dimension $D'$ and tuple length $M'$, the running time from Theorem A.1 is $2^{O((D')^{7/4}\log(D'+2))}\operatorname{poly}(M')$. Here $D'=O(n)$ and $M'$ is polynomial, so the total product has exponent $O(n^{7/4}\log(n+2))$. Different ordered formats and empty arrays can be handled before constructing the matrices. The algorithm uses no genericity, full-rank flattening, or supplied decomposition promise. The input uses the ordinary dense format.

The quantitatively relevant steps are a linear-size alternating reduction over the ring, a short-list search that exploits the lower and upper bits separately when solving the common-isotropy problem, and a linear-size ordered tuple check. The overall architecture is related to the individualization method for alternating ring TI of Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Section 5.2. Sun also uses the three-line scalar marker in his group-restriction construction. The reduction and search above give explicit linear dimensions and keep the final invocation to the alternating tuple algorithm in characteristic two. A targeted literature comparison found no earlier statement of the unrestricted modulus-four bound, and publication priority remains unestablished. This proof does not assert a practical benchmark or independently verify the cited alternating tuple algorithm. It was prepared with AI assistance and audited informally. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun V, Section 5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S5.SS2), [Sun, Section 5 and Theorem A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920)

## 7. Other fixed integer moduli

The preprocessing extends to $R=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$ for every fixed $p,e$, as follows.

- In Section 1, a nonzero scalar multiple of a unimodular block still requires one generator per coordinate in the image. The same marker intervals, shears and finite-length cancellation apply.
- In Section 2, a matrix with at least $4et$ nonzero Smith pivots has $s\ge4t$ at a single valuation $v$. Condition on every other pivot contribution and reduce the chosen contribution, after dividing by $p^v$, modulo $p$. The necessary equation is again a prescribed product $BC=D$, now over $\mathbb F_p$. If the conditioned right-hand side fails the divisibility condition, the original equation already fails. The same failure bound has base $p$. Union bound over $p^{em}$ coefficient vectors, using $t=O_e(\sqrt{m+1})$ large enough.
- In Section 3, use an extension of $\mathbb F_p$ of degree $s=\lceil\log_p(d+1)\rceil$. The same degree-of-minor, rank subadditivity, and derivative arguments apply. After lifting an isotropic basis, divide the compressed blocks by $p$ and view them over $\mathbb Z/p^{e-1}\mathbb Z$. The number of nonzero Smith pivots is preserved by this division, as can be seen by diagonalizing over the shorter ring and lifting the two invertible Smith transformations. Repeating through $e$ residue layers gives a common free isotropic summand with codimension $O(e r\log^2(d+2))$.
- In Sections 4–5, the minimum generating covers use reduction modulo $p$. A module contained in a rank-$h$ free summand has size at most $p^{eh}$, so a strict-growth list of at most $eh$ elements suffices. The annihilator of $p^iR$ is $p^{e-i}R$, and a functional on $p^i R$ extends to $R$, since its value on $p^i$ must lie in $p^iR$. Thus the double-annihilator and extension steps still hold. The marked tuple compiler uses only units and identity pairings and has the same dimension.

The exponent of list enumeration remains $O(n^{3/2}\log^2(n+2))$ for each fixed $p,e$. For $p=2$ use Sun's Theorem A.1 as before. For odd $p$, Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun's Theorem 5.22 gives deterministic polynomial-time ordered tuple isometry for alternating forms on free modules over this fixed ring. This yields the bound $2^{O(n^{3/2}\log^2(n+2))}$ for each fixed odd prime power. For any fixed modulus $M>1$, apply the component algorithms under Chinese remaindering, which preserves the side lengths and splits invertible matrix actions componentwise. The odd-modulus bound applies when $M$ is odd, and the $7/4$ bound applies when $M$ is even. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Theorem 5.22](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S5.SS3)
