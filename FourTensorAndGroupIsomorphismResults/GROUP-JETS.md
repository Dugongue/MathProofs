# A tensor reduction for presented p-groups of bounded class and exponent

**Informal theorem.** Fix $p,c,e$, with $p$ prime. Given word-relator presentations promised to define finite $p$-groups of class at most $c$ and exponent dividing $p^e$, their isomorphism problem reduces by a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction to ordinary 3-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb F_p$. Relators can also be specified by straight-line programs with product and inverse gates.

## Recovering the group from truncated data

Put $k=\mathbb F_p$ and $D=cp^{e-1}$. For such a group $G$, let $I$ be the augmentation ideal of $k[G]$, and let $J_G=k[G]/I^{D+1}$ with induced filtration $F_r$ given by the image of $I^r$. Let $K_G$ be the quotient of $J_G\otimes_k J_G$ by $\sum_{r+s>D}F_r\otimes F_s$. Write $q:J_G\times J_G\to K_G$ for the bilinear projection, $\epsilon$ for the augmentation, and $\Delta$ for the algebra map induced by $g\mapsto g\otimes g$. It descends because $\Delta(g-1)$ starts in filtration degree one. Write $j(g)$ for the image of a group element in $J_G$.

The Zassenhaus–Jennings dimension subgroup $G_r=G\cap(1+I^r)$ has formula
$$
G_r=\prod_{a p^b\ge r}\gamma_a(G)^{p^b},
$$
where $\gamma_a(G)$ is the lower central series. At $r=D+1$ every factor is trivial. If $a\le c$ and $b<e$, then $a p^b\le D$, and outside this range the class or exponent kills the factor. So $j$ is injective.

The associated-graded theorem of Jennings and Quillen identifies $\operatorname{gr} k[G]$ with the restricted enveloping algebra $u(L)$ of $L=\bigoplus_{r\ge 1}G_r/G_{r+1}$. Its canonical map sends the class of $g\in G_r$ to $g-1\bmod I^{r+1}$. These classes are primitive under the associated-graded coproduct since the extra term $(g-1)\otimes(g-1)$ has degree $2r>r$. The identification preserves the coproduct because $L$ generates $u(L)$ and both coproducts preserve multiplication. See the original papers by [Jennings](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1941-0004626-6) and [Quillen](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(68)90069-0), and the constructive proof in [Usefi, Theorem 3.2](https://www.math.mun.ca/~usefi/papers/JPAA.pdf).

The primitives of $u(L)$ are exactly $L$. Filter by Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt length. The associated graded is a symmetric algebra on a basis of $L$ with every variable's $p$-th power zero, and with each variable primitive. For a basis monomial of degree at least two, choose one of its variables. The coproduct coefficient of that variable on the first side and the residual monomial on the other is the chosen variable's exponent, strictly between $0$ and $p$. Only this original monomial can contribute to the specified pair, so no homogeneous elements of degree at least two are primitive. The top-length part of any primitive in $u(L)$ is primitive in this associated graded, hence its length is at most one. Its constant part vanishes by the defining equation for a primitive.

If $u\in J_G$ satisfies $\epsilon(u)=1$ and $\Delta(u)=q(u,u)$, let $v$ be the first nonzero homogeneous part of $u-1$ in degree $r$, when $u\ne1$. The equation in total degree $r\le D$ says exactly that $v$ is primitive in $\operatorname{gr} k[G]$. It belongs to $G_r/G_{r+1}$ by the preceding paragraph. Choose $g$ from that coset and replace $u$ by $u j(g)^{-1}$, raising its first nonzero degree. This preserves the condition by multiplicativity of $\Delta$ and of $a\mapsto q(a,a)$. Iteration ends at one, proving that every such $u$ is a product of genuine $j(g)$. Conversely every $j(g)$ satisfies the equation. The recognized set spans $J_G$, since group elements span the full group algebra, and multiplication recovers the group law.

Consequently $G$ isomorphism is equivalent to isomorphism of the linear sorts $J_G,K_G$ and a rigid scalar line, with the marked operations consisting of multiplication, augmentation, $\Delta$, and $q$. A compatible isomorphism restricts to the recognized group. A group isomorphism induces compatible maps by preservation of the augmentation filtration.

## Computing the data from relators

Suppose the presentation has generators $g_1,\ldots,g_m$. Form the noncommutative algebra $B$ on $X_1,\ldots,X_m$ truncated after degree $D$. Substitute $g_i\mapsto 1+X_i$ in each relator, with inverse given by $\sum_{r=0}^D(-X_i)^r$, and let $W$ be the two-sided ideal generated by the relator values minus one. Then $B/W\cong J_G$. Indeed the forward map sends $X_i$ to $g_i-1$. In the other direction the units $1+X_i\bmod W$ satisfy the presentation and induce an algebra homomorphism from $k[G]$. All group elements map to elements of scalar coefficient one, so $I$ maps into the augmentation-zero ideal of $B/W$ and its $(D+1)$-st power maps to zero. The two maps are inverse on generators. A straight-line relator is evaluated gate by gate, with inverses computed as geometric series in the augmentation-zero part.

The monomial dimension of $B$ is $\sum_{r=0}^D m^r$, polynomial for fixed $D$. The subspace $W$ is the span of the relator values minus one multiplied on each side by monomials. Elimination obtains a basis, quotient coordinates, and multiplication for $J_G$. Filtered pieces are spans of words of length at least the specified degree. Compute $K_G$ by another quotient, and its $\Delta$ by the images
$$
\Delta(X_i)=q(X_i,1)+q(1,X_i)+q(X_i,X_i).
$$
This constructs all basis-explicit tables using polynomially many field operations. Compare the labeled sort dimensions, use a fixed no-instance if they differ, and otherwise take the direct sum and mark its coordinate projections. Apply the fixed-signature compiler of [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219), in the form of Theorem 10.3 of [Grochow–Qiao](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309).

The proof allows $p\le c$. The standard Lazard-based result in [Grochow–Qiao, CCC 2021](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.16) has different input and parameter assumptions, including matrix generators, exponent $p$, and $c<p$. This local note is an informal proof and has not established publication priority.
