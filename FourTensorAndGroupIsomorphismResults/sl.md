# Searching and counting determinant-one tensor isomorphisms over fixed finite rings

**Theorem.** Fix a nonzero finite commutative ring $R$ with identity. Given two ordinary three-tensors in matching free $R$-module formats of maximum side length $n$, one can decide whether they are isomorphic by matrices of determinant one, find a transporter of any attainable determinant triple, compute generators of the full ordinary automorphism group, and count the transporters of every determinant triple. The time bound for all these outputs is $2^{O_R(n^{7/4}\log(n+2))}$. If $R$ has odd characteristic, it improves to $2^{O_R(n^{3/2}\log^2(n+2))}$. For ordinary bilinear map isomorphism between explicitly presented finite $R$-modules, the same bounds apply to finding a transporter, generating its stabilizer, and counting all transporters, with $n$ the maximum number of module generators and polynomial overhead in the presentation length.

The proof is informal. It combines rectangular individualization with Sun's algorithms for ordered alternating matrix tuples. The group output of the ordered problem is essential. It allows the determinant test and exact counting after each individualization, directly on the original modes.

## 1. Reduction from a fixed coefficient ring to free module sorts with linear marks

By the Chinese remainder theorem, a fixed finite commutative ring splits into its prime-characteristic-power factors. The same idempotents split its modules, bilinear maps, and tensor isomorphisms componentwise. Determinant-one means determinant-one on every component. The groups of transporters are products when the transporter sets are nonempty. It suffices to treat characteristic $p^e$ with $p,e$ fixed.

Put $S=\mathbb Z/p^e$. For an $R$-module $M$, use Smith normal form over $S$ to write the underlying additive module as a direct sum of nonzero cyclic modules $S/(p^{a_i})$, with $1\leq a_i\leq e$. There are $O_R(n)$ of these for an $n$-generated module. In particular each free $R$-coordinate contributes a fixed number of cyclic summands. For a presentation over fixed $R$, translate to one over $S$ by writing the additive structure of each $R$-coordinate and multiplying each defining relation by the fixed elements of $R$. The resulting relation subgroup is exactly the original $R$-span of the relations.

With $r$ cyclic summands, let $\pi:S^r\twoheadrightarrow M$ be the minimal free cover in these coordinates and $\iota:M\hookrightarrow S^r$ the coordinatewise injection given by multiplication by $p^{e-a_i}$. Use both free spaces as labeled sorts. Record $D=\iota\pi$ and all $D_s=\iota\,s\,\pi$, where $s$ acts by multiplication in $M$ and runs through the fixed ring $R$. An invertible pair $G,H$ on these spaces satisfying $H D=D'G$ and $HD_s=D_s' G$ induces an isomorphism on $M$, as $H$ identifies the images of $D,D'$ and the latter equations force commutation with all scalars. Before forming sorts, reject pairs of corresponding modules with different additive types.

Conversely, every $R$-linear $g:M\to M'$ lifts to such a pair. To obtain $G$, choose preimages of the desired images of the standard generators along $\pi'$. They give an invertible matrix modulo $p$ since both covers are minimal. To obtain $H$, first use the dual free cover $(S^r)^*\to M^*$ given by restriction along $\iota$. This cover is surjective and minimal. In coordinate $i$ the image of the coordinate functional generates $\operatorname{Hom}_S(p^{e-a_i}S,S)$, of order $p^{a_i}$. Lift $g^*:(M')^*\to M^*$ through these covers and dualize the lift to get $H$. The restriction equation implies $H\iota=\iota' g$, and the equations for $D_s$ follow. Coordinates on a free module give faithful duality for this equality.

Encode a bilinear $b:M_X\times M_Y\to M_W$ by the free $S$-bimap $\iota_W b(\pi_X-,\pi_Y-)$, with the indicated three pairs of module marks. A free-sort isometry preserving the marks induces exactly an original bimap transporter, since both domain covers are surjective and the output injection is injective. Every original transporter lifts by the preceding paragraph. Later, take the image of the generators on the original modules. This avoids counting the possible auxiliary lifts. For free $R$-tensors, contraction in the first two modes identifies ordinary tensor transporters with bimap transporters by inverse transpose in those modes and the same map in the third. This bijection preserves determinant-one. For determinant triples in the original convention, invert the first two recorded determinants.

## 2. A rectangular individualization

We now work over $S$ with a free bimap $b:X\times Y\to W$ and a fixed number of additional free sorts and linear marks. Every free rank is $O_R(n)$. A matrix's rank in this section is the number of nonzero Smith pivots, equivalently the minimum number of generators of its image. It is invariant under invertible row and column changes and cannot increase under restriction to rows or columns.

Let $A_z$ be the matrix of $z\circ b$ for $z\in W^*$. There are lists $P_X$ in $X$ and $P_Y$ in $Y$, each of length $t=O_R(\sqrt{\dim W+1})$, such that every $A_z$ vanishing on all probe pairs has fewer than $4et$ nonzero Smith pivots. To prove this, take independent uniform lists on the two sides. For a fixed matrix of rank at least $4et$, diagonalize it. At least $4t$ pivots have one common valuation $v$. Condition on the contributions of every other pivot, divide by $p^v$ where possible, and reduce modulo $p$. The zero restriction requires $BC=D$ for independent uniform field matrices of shapes $t\times s$ and $s\times t$, with $s\geq 4t$ and $D$ fixed. The probability that $B$ has rank below $t/2$ is at most $p^{-(s-t)\lceil t/2\rceil}$, by taking a union bound over $\lceil t/2\rceil$-dimensional left kernels. Otherwise the probability of the required product is at most $p^{-t^2/2}$, column by column. A union bound over $p^{e\dim W}$ choices of $z$ gives the claimed lists.

Write $H=\langle b(P_X,P_Y)\rangle\leq W$ and $\mathcal L=\{A_z:z\in H^\perp\}$. We need large free summands $U_X,U_Y$ with all matrices in $\mathcal L$ zero on $U_X\times U_Y$. Here is the rank argument. For a field subspace of rectangular matrices over $\mathbb F_p$ of maximum rank $r$, choose an extension of degree $s=O(\log(n+2))$ with size above the side lengths. A generic maximum-rank minor can be made nonzero over this extension by the polynomial degree bound. The resulting combination $B$ has rank $\rho\leq sr$, since its coefficients expand in the $s$ field basis elements. In independent row and column bases with $B$ in rank normal form, the linear coefficient of the $(\rho+1)$-minors of $B+uA$ shows that every $A$ in the original space vanishes between the left and right kernels of $B$. Restrict the two kernels to $\mathbb F_p$. Each has codimension at most $s\rho$, so both dimensions are large. Over $S$, apply this argument to the residues and lift bases of the two summands. The restrictions of the forms to these summands are multiples of $p$. Divide them by $p$ over $\mathbb Z/p^{e-1}$ and repeat. The number of nonzero pivots on the smaller ring is the same as before division, by lifting the invertible Smith changes. It follows that both codimensions can be bounded by $h=O_R(t\log^2(n+2))$.

The module $\sum_{A\in\mathcal L}A U_Y\subseteq X^*$ annihilates $U_X$ and is contained in a free summand of rank at most $h$. It is generated by at most $eh$ individual values $Au$ from the indicated set, by greedily picking an element outside the prior span, which increases its order by a factor at least $p$. Choose a list $T_Y$ of those input vectors $u$, and choose $T_X$ in the transposed construction. For any lists, define

$$
 S_X=\sum_{A\in\mathcal L}A\langle T_Y\rangle,\qquad
 S_Y=\sum_{A\in\mathcal L}A^{\mathsf T}\langle T_X\rangle,
$$
$$
 K_X=\{x:S_X(x)=0,\ A^{\mathsf T}x\in S_Y\ \forall A\in\mathcal L\},\qquad
 K_Y=\{y:S_Y(y)=0,\ Ay\in S_X\ \forall A\in\mathcal L\}.
$$

For the chosen lists these contain $U_X,U_Y$, respectively. For any lists, $b(K_X,K_Y)\subseteq H$: every $A_z$ with $z\in H^\perp$ vanishes on $K_X\times K_Y$, and $H^{\perp\perp}=H$ by Smith form over $S$. In a cyclic coordinate this is the identity $\operatorname{Ann}(p^iS)=p^{e-i}S$.

Choose additional lists $J_X,J_Y$ of lengths at most $h$ so that the complete lists $L_X=(P_X,T_X,J_X)$, $L_Y=(P_Y,T_Y,J_Y)$ span their modes together with $K_X,K_Y$. Existence follows from the free complements to $U_X,U_Y$. Find one pair of lists on the source by deterministic enumeration up to these bounds, accepting the first pair for which the two span tests hold. On the target enumerate all pairs of lists of the same lengths and skip failures of these tests. The two spaces $K_X,K_Y$ are intrinsic to the bimap and marked lists. A genuine transporter carries the lists and spaces to a target branch. This enumeration costs $2^{O_R(n^{3/2}\log^2(n+2))}$, since the lengths of all lists are $O_R(\sqrt n\log^2(n+2))$. The spaces, annihilators and span tests within a branch use polynomial-time Smith operations.

## 3. An ordered tuple for each branch

Match the named images $b(P_X,P_Y)$ with their target values. Reject unless the two generator lists satisfy the same linear relations over $S$, which is a comparison of their relation kernels. This gives an isomorphism $\phi:H\to H'$. Mark each vector in the two complete lists and mark the linear maps $b(x,-):Y\to W$ for listed $x$, and $b(-,y):X\to W$ for listed $y$. These marks already force the output map to restrict to $\phi$ on $H$.

Mark minimal free generator maps $E_X:S^{k_X}\to K_X\subseteq X$ and $E_Y:S^{k_Y}\to K_Y\subseteq Y$. A genuine isomorphism on either $K$ lifts to an invertible map on its free generator space by minimality modulo $p$. Reject when corresponding $k_X,k_Y$ differ. For each coordinate functional $\lambda$ on $W$, mark the rectangular form

$$
 (u,v)\longmapsto \lambda\, b(E_Xu,E_Yv).
$$

On the target use $\lambda\phi^{-1}$ evaluated on $H'$, which contains every output of that restricted bimap. Matching these forms forces matching on $K_X\times K_Y$, since the source coordinate functionals separate points and the output map induces $\phi$. The marked cross maps and the two span tests then force matching on all of $X\times Y$ by bilinearity. Conversely every genuine transporter that preserves the lists lifts to the covers and satisfies the marks. Include the original linear marks of Section 1 as well.

To compile the marks at linear matrix size, for each free sort use two labeled primal copies and two dual copies. Mark four alternating matrices giving the identity pairings between each primal and each dual. Consecutive intersections of their image submodules distinguish the four copies individually. Their congruence equations force a common transformation $G$ on the primal copies and $G^{-\mathsf T}$ on their duals. A specified linear map uses the alternating cross block between its primal target and dual source. A rectangular form uses the two dual input sorts, so its congruence equation is exactly that of the specified form. To enforce fixed vectors, add three lines, mark all three alternating line pairs, and place each vector in a block with the first line. The line pair matrices distinguish their lines by intersecting image submodules. Their scalings are all equal to one $\eta$ with $\eta^2=1$. Multiplying the entire ambient transporter by $\eta$ preserves all alternating matrix congruences and makes its action on the lines the identity. The desired action on each primal sort is therefore $\eta G$. The three-line scalar marker also occurs in Sun's group-restriction construction. [Sun, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920)

The result is an ordered alternating matrix tuple of matrix dimension $O_R(n)$ and polynomial tuple length. Its common radical is zero because every sort and each line occurs in a unimodular pair marker on the corresponding two summands. The normalization is multiplicative on automorphisms: the line scaling $\eta$ is a character and commutes with the recovered block matrices. Composing with restriction to the original modules is again multiplicative. On any successful branch the tuple transporter thus decodes to an original transporter, and every original transporter matching that branch lifts to an ambient one.

## 4. Witnesses, all determinant values, and exact counts

Sun's Theorem A.1 finds an ordered tuple transporter or reports that none exists. Theorem A.2 computes a generating set for the ordered tuple automorphism group in the same asymptotic time. For fixed $p,e$, dimension $O_R(n)$, and polynomial tuple length the bound is $2^{O_R(n^{7/4}\log(n+2))}$. Sun uses the congruence convention $U^{\mathsf T}AU$, which agrees with the compiler after taking the transpose of a returned transporter. Transposing a generator set still generates the corresponding automorphism group because transpose reverses the product. [Sun, Theorems A.1 and A.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920#A)

For odd $p$, use the deterministic polynomial-time ordered tuple decision algorithm of Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun. To prescribe any set of vector correspondences for an ambient congruence, keep the original tuple on a distinguished primal copy of its underlying space. Add its four-copy pairing markers, then use a new scalar triangle and pair each prescribed vector with its first line. The normalization of Section 3 gives exactly the prescribed images, so the decision algorithm works with these extra constraints at only a constant dimension factor. There are $2^{O_R(n)}$ possibilities for each basis vector's image, and checking feasible images one column at a time produces a transporter within that time. For the ordered tuple automorphism group, take the pointwise stabilizer chain of an ordered ambient basis. With preceding basis vectors fixed, find one automorphism for every feasible image of the next vector by the same witness algorithm. This supplies a transversal at every level and their union generates the group because the final pointwise stabilizer is trivial. The overhead again stays in $2^{O_R(n)}$, dominated by the branch enumeration. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Theorem 5.22](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S5.SS3)

Project the generators of the *source* marked tuple to the original modules. Their images generate the image of that group. Enumerate the source-to-source branches and take one decoded transporter for every successful pair of target lists. The projected tuple generators together with these decoded transporters generate the entire original automorphism group. In detail, let $a$ be any original automorphism and lift it to the free sorts by Section 1. Its images of the fixed source lists give one of the tested source-to-source branches. A decoded witness from that branch, inverted and then composed with the chosen lift, fixes the source lists before projecting to the original modes. The resulting free marked bimap automorphism lifts to an automorphism of the source ordered tuple. Thus $a$ is the product of that branch representative and an element generated by the projected tuple generators. The same argument covers the free $R$-tensor case under the tensor-bimap correspondence.

One successful source-to-target branch supplies a transporter $a_0$. If no branch succeeds, all counts are zero. Otherwise all transporters are the right coset $a_0G$, where $G$ is the computed ordinary source stabilizer. The determinant map $\chi:G\to(R^\times)^3$ is a group homomorphism. Compute the subgroup generated by the determinant triples of the generators. Its size is bounded by the constant $|R^\times|^3$. Each attainable determinant triple belongs to $\chi(a_0)\chi(G)$ and has exactly $|G|/|\chi(G)|$ transporters. A short traversal of this constant-size image records representative words in the generators, giving a witness for each attainable triple, including $(1,1,1)$ if it is attainable.

Compute $|G|$ by the faithful action on the disjoint union of the three underlying finite modules. Its permutation degree is $2^{O_R(n)}$, since each module is generated by at most $n$ elements over the fixed ring. Schreier–Sims computes the order in time polynomial in this degree and the written generator list. Membership in the same action permits retaining each generator only when it enlarges the generated subgroup. There are $O_R(n^2)$ retained generators, since each strict addition at least doubles the subgroup order and the ambient matrix group has order at most $2^{O_R(n^2)}$. Permutations can be computed from the extracted maps in time polynomial in their degree, even for mixed-exponent modules. For those modules only ordinary search, generation and counting are asserted. [Sims, *Computation with permutation groups*](https://doi.org/10.1145/800204.806264)

Degenerate inputs require no restriction on any flattening. In particular, if a free tensor has an empty mode, the other formats can be read from the dimension headers. The stated enumeration bound is in $n$ and still applies for such formats. If the pair has unequal original ordered formats, return no transporter before constructing the marks. Including all outputs, the total time is $2^{O_R(n^{7/4}\log(n+2))}$. For odd characteristic the branch enumeration dominates, giving $2^{O_R(n^{3/2}\log^2(n+2))}$.

## Relation to previous results

The individualization approach follows the Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun program for alternating ring tensors. Here the two input modes are individualized separately and survive as distinguished free sorts through the ordered tuple compiler. The new output is the full transporter determinant distribution, witness generation, and counting for fixed finite coefficient rings. The proof uses Sun's ordered alternating tuple algorithms as published subroutines. It makes no claim of solving the polynomial-time equivalence of ordinary and special-linear tensor isomorphism. A targeted search located the ordered-tuple algorithms and existing field tensor reductions. It did not establish publication priority for the stated determinant-resolved bound over all fixed finite rings. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Section 5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S5.SS2), [Grochow–Qiao, search and counting reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16317), [Sun, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920#A)
