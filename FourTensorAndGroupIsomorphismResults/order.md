# Balanced tensor-order reduction over commutative rings

**Theorem.** Let $R$ be any nonzero commutative ring with identity. There is a uniform objectwise reduction from ordinary isomorphism of dense free order-$d$ tensors, $d\geq3$, to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over the same $R$. On a common input format $n\times\cdots\times n$, every output side is at most $O(d^6 n^{\lceil d/3\rceil})$. The constructor uses only copying and the constants zero and one. Over explicitly encoded finite rings, including $\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$ with the modulus part of the input, this is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time reduction. The binary length of the modulus is included without factoring it. The proof is informal.

The stronger underlying compiler removes arbitrary specified partitions on all three modes of a free three-tensor, in a polynomial in the number of parts times the sum of the mode lengths. This extends the compact tags in the existing balanced field reduction. Two markers for each part let us recover the actual free summand as an intersection, including on degenerate inputs and over rings with zero divisors. [Grochow–Qiao IV, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S5), [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun V, Section 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S3.SS3)

## 1. Erasing one partition

Write the three free module sorts as $U,V,W$ and view the tensor as a matrix slice in $U\otimes V$ at each basis coordinate of $W$. Suppose the row module has a fixed decomposition $U=\bigoplus_{i=1}^k U_i$. A partitioned isomorphism acts separately on the labeled summands. Leave any given partitions of the column and slice modes in force. For each $U_i$ add two markers with different labels $\lambda=(i,1),(i,2)$, and index all labels by $1,\ldots,L$ with $L=2k$.

For each marker add disjoint auxiliary row and column modules as follows. Add rows $Z_\lambda\cong R^b$ with $b=\mathrm{rank}(V)$ and rows $D_\lambda\cong R^{e_\lambda}$. Add columns $C_\lambda\cong U_i$ and $D'_\lambda\cong R^{e_\lambda}$. Use fixed coordinate copies. The marker matrix $M_\lambda$ has exactly three identity blocks, on $(Z_\lambda,V)$, $(U_i,C_\lambda)$, and $(D_\lambda,D'_\lambda)$. Pad each old slice by zero. Give each marker its own new slice direction, append all these directions to the last old slice part, and append all new columns to the last old column part. Use no partition in the enlarged row module.

Let $B=\max_i\mathrm{rank}(U_i)$, let $K=(L+1)(B+1)$, and set $e_\lambda=K+\lambda(B+1)$. To analyze an isomorphism of enlarged arrays, localize at any maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ of $R$. Over the local ring $R_\mathfrak m$, write $\mu$ for the least number of generators of a finitely generated module. This is its dimension modulo the maximal ideal by Nakayama's lemma. It is additive on direct sums and nonincreasing on passage to a quotient. For any nonzero scalar $s$ there, the nonzero cyclic module $sR_\mathfrak m$ needs one generator, so a nonzero scalar multiple of an identity block of size $u$ has image needing $u$ generators. Matrix image modules are preserved up to isomorphism by invertible left and right actions.

An old slice combination has image needing at most $b$ generators. If exactly marker $\lambda=(i,j)$ has nonzero coefficient, the generator count of the image lies in

$$
\bigl[b+e_\lambda,\ b+e_\lambda+B\bigr].
$$

Indeed projection to the private rows $Z_\lambda,D_\lambda$ gives a quotient needing $b+e_\lambda$ generators, and only the $V,C_\lambda,D'_\lambda$ columns may be nonzero. If at least two markers occur, projecting to all their active tag rows and to one active copy $Z_\lambda$ gives a quotient needing at least $b+2K$ generators. These bounds allow arbitrary contamination by old slices and arbitrary nonzero nonunit marker coefficients. The singleton intervals are disjoint and all fall below $b+2K$.

Now take the slice change-of-basis matrix of a purported enlarged isomorphism. Every row producing an old target slice has zero coefficients on the markers, as seen in each localization by the generator bound. Every row producing target marker $\lambda$ has zero coefficients on all other markers, by its singleton interval. An element zero in every maximal localization is zero in $R$: for a nonzero element, a maximal ideal containing its annihilator gives a localization where it remains nonzero. Thus the two coefficient conclusions hold globally. The slice change is block triangular, with an invertible old-to-old block respecting the old slice partition, and with diagonal units $s_\lambda$ on the marker quotient. The units and the invertibility of the old block follow from the determinant of the full invertible change.

After applying that slice change, marker $\lambda$ becomes $s_\lambda M_\lambda+A_\lambda$, where $A_\lambda$ is an old-slice combination. A single row shear removes all $A_\lambda$ simultaneously: for each $\lambda$ use the off-diagonal row map $Z_\lambda\to U$ with matrix $-s_\lambda^{-1}A_\lambda$, with the copy $Z_\lambda\cong V$ understood. These off-diagonal maps have pairwise zero composites, and the shear fixes every old padded slice. In coordinate $\lambda$, only the shear on $Z_\lambda$ acts on a nonzero row copy of that marker, so precisely the unwanted block is removed. Adjust the enlarged row transporter by the inverse shear. The new transporter carries each cleaned marker to the same target marker, with its scalar unit allowed.

For a clean marker, its column image is $U_i\oplus Z_\lambda\oplus D_\lambda$ and its transposed image is $V\oplus C_\lambda\oplus D'_\lambda$. The adjusted row transporter carries each column image to the corresponding image and therefore carries the intersection for the two markers of $i$ to itself. That intersection is exactly $U_i$. The column transporter carries each transposed image to itself and therefore carries their joint intersection, exactly $V$, to itself. These are equalities of submodules, so the restrictions are invertible. Applying those restrictions and the old slice-change block to the old padded tensor yields the required original partitioned isomorphism. The column restriction also respects any old column partition, since the enlarged column parts are obtained by extending the last old part only.

Conversely, extend a partitioned isomorphism by the inverse transpose of its action on $V$ in each row copy $Z_\lambda$, and the inverse transpose of its action on $U_i$ in each column copy $C_\lambda$. Use identity on the tags and marker directions. This transports the padded data and fixes each marker. Hence the erasure preserves and reflects orbit equivalence on all inputs.

There are $L$ tags of lengths $O(L(B+1))$, and $L$ auxiliary copies of $V$. The sum of output sides grows by a factor at most $O((k+1)^2)$ applied to the old sum plus one. Apply the erasure a further two times after cyclic relabeling, preserving the remaining labels at each step. If the three starting partitions have at most $k$ parts each, this gives an ordinary three-tensor with side sum $O((k+1)^6(s+1))$ from a partitioned tensor of side sum $s$. A one-part decomposition can also simply be skipped.

## 2. Marked tensor networks with a bounded number of occurrences per sort

For each named free module $E$, introduce a primal copy $P_i(E)$ and a dual copy $D_i(E)$ in each axis $i\in\{1,2,3\}$ of a partitioned tensor. Give each axis one additional named free line $L_i$. In the block $L_1,L_2,L_3$ put the tensor $1\otimes1\otimes1$. For each $E$ and each pair $i\ne j$, put the standard identity tensor in the block $P_i(E),D_j(E),L_\ell$, where $\ell$ is the third axis. Put the data in triples of named slots of the appropriate variance. In particular, a bilinear map $E\times F\to G$ is a tensor in $D_1(E),D_2(F),P_3(G)$. A payload in three named modules uses their primal copies. All unspecified blocks are zero. This arrangement applies when the data occupy distinct blocks, as they do for the binary trees below.

In a partitioned transporter the three free line actions are units $u_1,u_2,u_3$ with product one. Multiply the action on the entire $i$th axis by $u_i^{-1}$, preserving all tensor equations and making the line actions trivial. Each identity block then forces the actions on its two non-line factors to be inverse transposes. The six edges with $i\ne j$ form a connected bipartite graph on the six copies of every module, so all primal copies have one common action and all dual copies have its inverse transpose. Thus the transporter is exactly a family of shared changes of basis preserving the marked data, up to the harmless normalization just described. In the other direction any such family gives a partitioned transporter with trivial line actions. Ordinary TI now follows from the partition-erasing construction. The sum of the initial sides is six times the sum of the named module ranks plus three, and the number of parts of each axis is linear in the number of named modules. No scalar roots or division by a nonunit occur.

## 3. Balanced order reduction

Partition the ordered modes into three consecutive nonempty groups of sizes differing by at most one. Use a binary comb tree for each group. Every leaf has its original free module, and each internal node has the free tensor product of its two child modules, with its standard product basis. Record the canonical bilinear map from those two child modules to their parent as one marked tensor. Include the source coefficient array, reindexed as one payload in the three root modules. These data occupy distinct blocks because the parent label distinguishes the tree maps, and a tree map uses dual inputs whereas the payload uses three primal slots.

Any source transporter induces actions at internal nodes by tensor products and preserves all tree data and the payload. Conversely the bilinear equations force the action at each internal node to be the tensor product of the child actions, since pure tensors generate the parent. Induction up the trees and the payload equation give precisely an original modewise transporter. Apply Sections 1–2 to remove the markings. For equal mode size $n\geq2$, the module ranks in a comb on at most $s_0=\lceil d/3\rceil$ leaves sum to at most $s_0 n+\sum_{j=2}^{s_0}n^j=O(n^{s_0})$. The number of labels is $O(d)$, so the final side bound is $O(d^6 n^{s_0})$. For $n=1$ all tree ranks are one and the same bound follows by handling the scalar case directly, or a looser $O(d^7)$ also follows from the construction. More generally one can use any three binary trees partitioning the modes, with size at most $O(d^6)$ times the sum of their node ranks plus one. Equal-format pairs are the usual orbit input. Unequal formats can be rejected before construction, and free zero-size modes can be handled directly.

All the output data are obtained by indexing or reshaping the original entries and by recording identities and zeros. This includes the case of unknown prime-power factors of the modulus, since localization is used only in the correctness argument. The construction writes polynomially many entries in the dense input size. Each node rank is at most the number of original entries for positive dimensions, and the order and format are included in the input. This proves the uniform size and computational assertions.

## 4. Consequences and prior scope

For $d$ divisible by three and fixed, the side exponent over each nontrivial finite ring is $d/3$. This is the information-theoretic exponent for objectwise reductions: a ring of size $q$ supplies $q^{n^d}$ source tensors, with each orbit having size at most $q^{dn^2}$. Outputs of common maximum side $m$ give at most $q^{m^3}$ distinct fixed-format arrays, so $m^3\ge n^d-dn^2$ for such reductions. The construction does not attain the fractional exponent for fixed orders outside multiples of three.

For each fixed integer modulus $M>1$, applying the ring three-tensor algorithms to the output yields time $2^{\widetilde O(n^{(7/4)\lceil d/3\rceil})}$ for even $M$ and $2^{\widetilde O(n^{(3/2)\lceil d/3\rceil})}$ for odd $M$, with fixed $d$. These are inherited upper bounds and need not beat direct enumeration at every order. At order six, for example, the bounds via this reduction exceed the naive quadratic exponent. The structural result is the size-controlled same-ring reduction itself.

Grochow–Qiao Part I proves polynomial-time $d$-TI to three-TI over fields and asks for a nearly counting-optimal size bound in Question 7.8 of the published paper (Question 10.8 of the longer version). The balanced field exponent in the divisible cases is already in the project's balanced reduction. Part IV gives a linear-in-dimension partition erasure for a fixed number of parts over fields. Part V gives a same-ring single-axis partition compiler under its ring assumptions with dimension bound $2^{O(L)}(n\ell+m)$, and discusses difficulties with degeneration in direct adaptations of the field gadgets. The present result replaces that combination of dependencies with a polynomial in the number of parts times the side sum over arbitrary commutative rings. The general published size question remains unresolved when the exponent $d/3$ is fractional. Priority for this specific strengthening remains unestablished.
