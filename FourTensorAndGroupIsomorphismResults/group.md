# Fast isomorphism for class-two groups with bounded commutator exponent

**Theorem.** Fix a positive integer $e$. Let $p$ be any odd prime, and set $N=p^n$. Isomorphism of two groups of order $N$ and nilpotency class at most two, whose commutator subgroups have exponent dividing $p^e$, can be decided in deterministic time

$$
p^{O_e(n^{3/2}\log^2(n+2))}=N^{O_e(\sqrt{\log_p N}\log^2(\log_p N+2))}.
$$

This bound applies to Cayley tables and to the centered power-commutator data specified below. It also permits finding an isomorphism or a generating set of automorphisms within the same time bound. The same conclusions hold for $p=2$ with elementary abelian commutator subgroup, with bound $2^{O(n^{3/2}\log^2(n+2))}$. For every fixed prime $p$ and $e$, including powers of two of any fixed exponent, isomorphism in the centered power-commutator representation is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $\mathbb F_p$.

The hypothesis bounds the exponent of the commutator subgroup. The group and its center can have arbitrarily large exponents. The proof gives the bound of order $N^{\widetilde O(\sqrt{\log_p N})}$ on this family. Prior priority for the exact bound is unestablished. The proof is informal.

## 1. Baer coordinates and input

Use Baer's class-two correspondence for odd order. On the same underlying set as $G$, define addition by $x+y=xy[x,y]^{-1/2}$ and bracket by $[x,y]$ with commutator convention $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$. The class-two identities show that this is an abelian $p$-group $A$ with a biadditive alternating bracket $b:A\times A\to A$ of class at most two. Multiplication is recovered by $x\cdot y=x+y+\tfrac12 b(x,y)$. The additive order of $A$ is $p^n$ and $p^e b(A,A)=0$. Consequently $b$ factors canonically as an alternating map

$$
b_e:(A/p^e A)\times(A/p^e A)\longrightarrow A[p^e].
$$

For Cayley tables, this conversion takes polynomial time in $N$. The abelian additive group can be put in explicit cyclic coordinates in polynomial time in $N$: greedily choose generators outside the subgroup already generated, enumerate that subgroup along with words while recording the relative order relation of each generator, then apply Smith operations over $\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z$. The collected relations present the additive group since their triangular normal forms give the product of the relative orders, namely $N$. Bracket values on the cyclic generators follow by bilinearity from the change of generators.

The succinct input variant consists of cyclic bases of $Z(G)$ and $G/Z(G)$, chosen lifts $x_i$ of the quotient basis, their central powers $x_i^{p^{a_i}}$ where the quotient orders are $p^{a_i}$, and the central commutators of the $x_i$. Supply the coordinates explicitly and write the cyclic orders themselves in binary, so their lengths count toward the input. The additive group in Baer coordinates has the same generators and the central order relations, and $p^{a_i}x_i$ is the recorded central power of $x_i$. There are at most $N$ collected words for this abelian presentation, and it surjects onto $A$ of order $N$, so the presentation is exact. The bracket on generators is the supplied commutator data, with every central argument giving zero. Smith operations and the induced bracket transformations are polynomial in the description length and $n\log p$.

## 2. A free module encoding tied action on two truncations

Put $R=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$. For every cyclic additive summand $A_l=\mathbb Z/p^l\mathbb Z$ of $A$, assign the free rank-$l$ $R$-module $B_l=R[t]/(t^l)$ with the endomorphism given by multiplication by $t$. Let $B$ be the sum of all $B_l$ and $T$ its marked multiplication-by-$t$ endomorphism. Then $\operatorname{rank}_R B=\sum_l l\,d_l=n$, where $d_l$ is the number of summands of exponent $p^l$.

On one summand, evaluation at $p$ gives $\operatorname{coker}(T-p)\cong R/p^lR\cong A_l/p^eA_l$. For the kernel, write

$$
K_l(\alpha)=\alpha\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}p^{l-1-j}t^j,
\qquad \alpha\in R[p^l].
$$

These are exactly the kernel elements, because the equations for the coefficients of $(t-p)v$ determine all the lower coefficients by the highest one and impose $p^l\alpha=0$. The map $K_l(\alpha)\mapsto p^{l-e}\widehat\alpha\bmod p^l$ identifies the kernel with $A_l[p^e]$. Here $\widehat\alpha$ is an integer representative and, when $l<e$, the written expression divides by $p^{e-l}$, which divides every representative. Changing representatives changes the answer by a multiple of $p^l$. Both identifications, and their inverses on the relevant images, are explicit.

We must track the two actions simultaneously. A homomorphism of $R[t]$-modules $B_l\to B_r$ is determined by the image of $1$, which can be written uniquely as $t^{(r-l)_+}u(t)$ with $\deg u<\min(l,r)$. Its action on the cokernel multiplies by $p^{(r-l)_+}u(p)$. On the kernel it sends $K_l(\alpha)$ to $K_r(p^{(l-r)_+}u(p)\alpha)$. For this last identity, compare the coefficient of $t^{r-1}$ after multiplying $K_l(\alpha)$ by $t^{(r-l)_+}u(t)$. Every term of $u$ survives in that coefficient, with the additional power $p^{(l-r)_+}$. Under the identification with $A_l[p^e]$ this is exactly the action induced by the abelian summand map $A_l\to A_r$ sending $1$ to $p^{(r-l)_+}u(p)\bmod p^r$. Any integer evaluation of the coefficients gives the same induced maps on both truncations. For example, changing an integer representative by a multiple of $p^e$ acts trivially on the $p^e$-torsion.

These statements apply to all blocks between all cyclic summands. Every endomorphism of $B$ commuting with $T$ therefore gives an endomorphism of $A$ with the same induced pair of actions. Conversely every endomorphism of $A$ arises in this sense by taking constant polynomials for the individual blocks. For both classes of maps, invertibility is equivalent to invertibility of the diagonal blocks linking summands of each equal exponent, reduced modulo $p$. On $A/pA$ the block matrix is triangular in increasing exponent order, since a lower-order source maps into the $p$-multiple part of a higher-order target. Surjectivity modulo $pA$ implies surjectivity on $A$ by repeated lifting modulo powers of $p$. On $B/(p,t)B$ the same triangular matrix appears. The ideal $(p,t)$ acts nilpotently, so the same lifting argument gives invertibility on $B$. This proves equality of the linked actions of the two automorphism groups on the two truncations. An independent identification of the two actions would allow false matches.

## 3. One free bimap, with a commuting endomorphism

Let $\pi:B\to A/p^e A$ be the explicit cokernel map from Section 2, and let $\iota:A[p^e]\to B$ be the kernel inclusion under its explicit identification. The $R$-bilinear map

$$
\widetilde b(v,w)=\iota\bigl(b_e(\pi(v),\pi(w))\bigr)
$$

is alternating and is computed from the cyclic coordinates in polynomial time. First compare the additive types of the two Lie rings. When the types agree, their isomorphism is equivalent to simultaneous preservation of $\widetilde b$ and $T$ by an invertible $R$-linear map $g:B\to B'$. If a Lie ring isomorphism exists, use a block lift to $B$ as in Section 2. It preserves the map by functoriality of the two induced actions. Conversely any commuting $g$ preserving $\widetilde b$ gives an additive isomorphism of $A$ with the same actions on the two truncations. Surjectivity of $\pi$ and injectivity of $\iota$ force the latter isomorphism to preserve $b_e$, hence the full bracket. The transition back by Baer gives a genuine group isomorphism.

## 4. Runtime, search, and generators

Use Sections 2–5 and 7 of the [short-probe alternating bimap algorithm](ALGORITHM.md). Its input consists of a free domain $V$ and output $W$ of ranks $O(n)$. For each surviving selection of $O_e(\sqrt n\log^2(n+2))$ vectors, it encodes the exact remaining transporter conditions into ordered alternating matrix-tuple isometry on matrices of dimension $O(n)$. The encoder can include polynomially many additional marked linear maps or marked vectors between its fixed set of free sorts. Such maps and vectors simply add entries to the ordered tuple after its dimension has been allocated.

Here take both $V$ and $W$ to be copies of $B$. Add the identity link between them to identify the two unknown matrices and the marked endomorphism $T$ to enforce $gT=T'g$. The short-list selection and verification stay complete because the genuine linked transporter maps the lists and intertwines every additional fixed mark. The converse follows from exact branch verification. The number of lists is $p^{O_e(n^{3/2}\log^2(n+2))}$, since a vector of $B$ has $p^{en}$ possible values. The probe existence proof groups the Smith pivots into the $e$ possible valuations and counts over $\mathbb F_p$, and the residue-field width proof uses an extension of degree at most $\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$. These estimates allow varying odd $p$ with constants depending only on $e$. The terminal ordered-tuple problem has the deterministic bound $\operatorname{poly}(n,m,e,p)$ of Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun's Theorem 5.22 for odd $p$, where $m$ is tuple length. This cost, and polynomial-in-$N$ Cayley table preprocessing when necessary, are absorbed by the bound. [Theorem 5.22](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S5.SS3)

The algorithm accepts additional point marks on $B$. It therefore finds an actual compressed isomorphism one column at a time, by trying each of the at most $p^{en}$ images and keeping one with a positive decision after adding the column point mark. Evaluating its blocks as in Section 2 gives a group isomorphism. For automorphisms, take the chain of pointwise stabilizers of the successive initial segments of the ordered $R$-basis of $B$. At each stage obtain one representative for each feasible image of the next basis vector, with the earlier basis vectors marked fixed. These are a transversal modulo the next subgroup. The union generates the entire compressed automorphism group by induction down the chain, since the final pointwise stabilizer is trivial. Their shared action on the two truncations then generates the corresponding action of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. Section 5 lifts those generators and accounts for the kernel. The overhead for evaluating all next-vector candidates and finding representatives stays within $p^{O(en)}\operatorname{poly}(n)$ invocations of the decision algorithm.

## 5. Automorphisms and the ambiguity of lifting

Block evaluation on $B$ is canonical as an action on the two truncations. Its action as a map on all of $A$ can depend on the representatives of coefficients, since the modules in $A$ may have exponent larger than $p^e$. The following explicit kernel step gives full automorphism generators. Let $\rho_A\colon\operatorname{Aut}(A)\to\operatorname{Aut}(A/p^eA)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A[p^e])$ be the natural action. Every member of its kernel is of the form $1+D$, where $D$ takes $A$ into $p^e A$ and vanishes on $A[p^e]$. Conversely all such maps $1+D$ are invertible, since $D$ maps into $pA$. All such $D$ form a computable additive group of block homomorphisms. For a block of type $l\to r$ in the natural coefficient $u$ of Section 2, the two conditions are precisely divisibility by suitable powers of $p$. Thus the kernel fixes all possible brackets of exponent dividing $p^e$.

Filter this endomorphism group by $D(A)\subseteq p^j A$, for $j=e,e+1,\ldots,\max l+1$. For each $j$ choose additive generators of the allowed $D$ at that filtration index, and put $1+D$ in the generating list. There are polynomially many such block generators. To see that they generate the multiplicative kernel, the product of two terms in filtration degree $j$ is in degree $2j\ge j+1$, by linearity. Thus multiplication of the corresponding maps $1+D$ acts additively modulo the next filtration level. Eliminate any kernel element at degree $j$ by a product of the chosen representatives, then proceed until the filtration is zero. The quotients and generators are computable by the explicit block divisibility rules. Each representative compressed automorphism from Section 4 may now be lifted by any block evaluation to an actual automorphism of $A$. The induced pair of actions agrees between the two lifts, so the lifts of the compressed generators together with this kernel list generate all bracket-preserving automorphisms, and hence all group automorphisms. This avoids relying on multiplicativity of an arbitrary choice of lift.

## 6. Prime-field TI completeness for odd primes

For fixed $p,e$, apply the [augmentation-quotient encoding](JETS.md) to the free $R$-bimap $\widetilde b$ and to the extra unary map $T$. Its proved transport property concerns arbitrary recorded biadditive and additive maps, so it applies with one common transporter on the input and output of $\widetilde b$. The resulting basis-explicit linear signature has constant arity and polynomial size, and the published tensor compiler gives one ordinary $\mathbb F_p$ three-tensor instance. This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction on the stated centered power-commutator data since its explicit cyclic orders and generators bound the additive rank sum $n$ by the bit length of that data.

For the reverse direction use the linear field reduction of ordinary TI to a radical-free alternating bimap. Its class-two Lie ring is $V\oplus W$ with bracket $b$ in the central $W$ summand. Since the bimap has zero radical, the center is exactly $W$ and the intrinsic bracket on its central quotient is $b$. Thus Lie ring isomorphism here is exactly bimap isometry. The Baer group has exponent dividing $p$, so it satisfies the fixed-$p^e$ commutator promise and admits centered power-commutator data with all chosen powers zero. The reduction writes these commutator coefficients directly in polynomial time. [Grochow–Qiao's alternating marker reductions](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S2)

## 7. Prime-field TI completeness for every fixed prime

For any prime $p$, let $Z=Z(G)$, $V=G/Z$, $C=Z/p^eZ$ and $D=Z[p^e]$. Set $H=G/(p^e Z)$, using multiplicative group powers for the subgroup denoted by $p^eZ$. Since $[x^{p^e},y]=[x,y]^{p^e}=1$, the exponent of $V$ divides $p^e$. The exponent of $H$ divides $p^{2e}$ because $H/C\cong V$ and $C$ has exponent dividing $p^e$. The original commutator gives a well-defined alternating bimap $b:V\times V\to D$. From Cayley tables, compute $Z$, form $V$, and use cyclic decompositions of these two abelian groups to obtain the centered data in polynomial time in $N$.

Record $H$, the maps $C\to H$ and $H\to V$, and the original bimap $b$. Link the actions on $C,D$ by applying the marked shift construction of Section 2 to the abelian group $Z$ in place of $A$. Record the quotient map from the free module $B_Z$ to $C$ and the inclusion of $D$ into $B_Z$, together with $T$. These records have an isomorphism exactly when the original group does. In one direction any group isomorphism gives such a record isomorphism by functoriality and a lift through the marked shift. In the other direction let $q$ be an actual center isomorphism inducing the two obtained actions on $C,D$, as guaranteed by Section 2. Let $g$ be the recovered map on $V$. For each original chosen lift $x_i$ of a cyclic generator of order $p^{a_i}$ in $V$, choose any lift $y_i$ in the target of the image of $x_i$ in $H$. Its commutators agree with those required by $b$ and $q$. Its $p^{a_i}$-th power agrees with $q(x_i^{p^{a_i}})$ modulo $p^e Z'$ by the isomorphism on $H$. Their difference lies in $p^eZ'\subseteq p^{a_i}Z'$, since $a_i\le e$. Adjust $y_i$ by a central element so its power agrees exactly. The resulting map on lifts and center preserves the defining relations. Indeed the center orders, commutators, and quotient generator powers give a presentation by collecting words, with the number of normal words bounded by $|Z||V|=|G|$. The obtained map is a group isomorphism because it is an isomorphism on both the center and the quotient.

From centered power-commutator data, write a word-relator presentation of $H$ by replacing the central orders $p^l$ with $p^{\min(l,e)}$ and reducing central coordinates mod $p^{\min(l,e)}$. The quotient generator orders, power relations, and commutators are encoded using repeated squaring chains for binary powers. This presents $H$ by the same collecting argument. Apply the [nonabelian augmentation encoding](GROUP-JETS.md) to $H$ with the common cutoff $2p^{2e-1}$ on all group-algebra sorts. Each bounded-exponent abelian object admits the same encoding, and the recorded group homomorphisms induce linear maps by functoriality. The bimap is recorded by [separate extension in each input variable](JETS.md). This gives a fixed number of vector spaces and recorded maps of arity at most two over $\mathbb F_p$, of polynomial size for fixed $p,e$. The recognized group elements recover every original isomorphism constraint and the ordinary tensor compiler yields one $\mathbb F_p$ TI instance.

For the reverse direction when $p=2$, form the radical-free alternating bimap $b:V\times V\to W$ over $\mathbb F_2$ from the initial tensor. Make the class-two group with central $Z=(\mathbb Z/4)^m$, $m=\dim W$, and quotient $V$. Put the commutators into $2Z\cong W$ and give each chosen quotient basis lift trivial square. The triangular cocycle of the [central enlargement proof](CENTRAL.md) yields a group with these relations and center exactly $Z$. Any bimap isometry lifts: lift its action on $W$ to $Z$, and choose quotient basis lifts in the target. Their squares lie in $2Z$, so a central adjustment can cancel each square independently. The converse follows by taking the characteristic center, its subgroup $2Z$, and the commutator map. This group has elementary abelian derived subgroup and its centered data is polynomial-size. Hence it gives the reverse reduction for every fixed $e\ge1$.

## 8. The fast bound for two-groups with elementary abelian derived subgroup

Assume $p=2,e=1$. Then $V=G/Z$, $C=Z/2Z$ and $D=Z[2]$ are vector spaces. Write $\kappa:D\to C$ for inclusion in $Z$ followed by quotient. The square map $Q:V\to C$ sends the class of any $x$ to $x^2\bmod 2Z$. This is well-defined because a change of lift contributes a square from $Z$. It is quadratic with polar form $\kappa b$, by the class-two formula for $(xy)^2$. Retain $b,Q$ and the linked shift actions on $C,D$. These data classify the original group: they give all the squares and commutators needed to identify the two bounded-exponent extensions $H$ of Section 7, with squares compared modulo $2Z$. The resulting isomorphism on $H$ follows by mapping basis lifts and checking the presentation, and the lift to the original group follows from the same central adjustment as in Section 7.

Apply the [short-probe algorithm](ALGORITHM.md) to $b:V\times V\to D$. For each branch retain its list $D_0$ of probe, witness, and completion vectors, and its canonical subspace $K$, with $K+\operatorname{span}(D_0)=V$. The proof gives $b(K,K)\subseteq H_D$, where $H_D\subseteq D$ is generated by values on probe pairs. Set $H_C=\kappa(H_D)$. The prescribed probe matches give an isomorphism of $H_D$ and, or else the branch is rejected, an induced isomorphism of $H_C$. The induced map on $Q(K)$ modulo $H_C$ is linear because its polar form there vanishes. Form the subspace

$$
E=\{(v,w)\in K\times C:Q(v)+H_C=w+H_C\}.
$$

It is computed by evaluating $Q$ on a basis of $K$ modulo $H_C$ and taking a kernel. Add its projection maps to $V,C$ as linear marks, add the vectors $Q(u)$ in $C$ for every $u\in D_0$ as point marks, and add the ordered scalar coordinate forms of the quadratic map $(v,w)\mapsto Q(v)+w\in H_C$ on $E$. In these ordered forms use the fixed correspondence on $H_C$ from probe pairs. Preservation of the projection marks means that the implementing map on $E$ is the restriction of the two underlying mode actions. For $v\in K$ use $(v,Q(v))\in E$: its form value is zero, which forces exact preservation of $Q(v)$. The point marks then enforce $Q$ on the remaining spanning vectors and preservation of the polar form extends this to all sums. Conversely a genuine compatible group isomorphism gives all the marked maps, including the induced action on $E$.

Use the linked shift maps and all prior ordered linear and bilinear branch marks in the final tuple check. Over $\mathbb F_2$, each alternating matrix mark for a cross block, a pairing of copies, or a marked point is implemented by its corresponding squarefree quadratic expression. For the prior branch's scalar alternating forms on one auxiliary sort, put the form on a cross block between its two dual copies. The expression is $x^{\mathsf T}Ay$, with both copies tied to the same implementing matrix by the linear markers, so preservation gives exactly the requisite alternating-form congruence. The new ordered quadratic forms on $E$ are included with their actual square coefficients. Writing a linear term as a square gives the homogeneous quadratic polynomial with the same values. Homogeneous quadratic polynomials over $\mathbb F_2$ agree if their functions agree, since coefficients are determined by values on individual basis vectors and their pairwise sums. All pairing and copy arguments apply directly to the polar forms used as markers.

There are $O(n)$ ambient coordinates. The short list has length $O(n)$ since the common-isotropy codimension is capped by the domain dimension and the probe length is $O(\sqrt n)$. There is one ordered form per cross map and per marked point, a constant number of shift, projection, and sort maps, and at most $O(n)$ coordinate forms for each of the two designated small-image subspaces. Thus the ordered tuple length is also $O(n)$. Grochow–Ivanyos–Qiao–Sun's Theorem 1.3, which depends on both ambient dimension and tuple length, costs $2^{O(n^{3/2})}$ per branch. The short-probe enumeration costs $2^{O(n^{3/2}\log^2(n+2))}$. [Grochow–Ivanyos–Qiao–Sun, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00874)

The column-by-column search and stabilizer-chain construction from Section 4 now mark bases of $V$ and the center's free shift module. This adds at most $O(n)$ ordered forms and $2^{O(n)}$ decision calls. Lifting the recovered compressed generators gives representatives for the action of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ on $V,C,D$. For the kernel, first generate the automorphisms of $Z$ acting identically on $C,D$ by the filtration of Section 5 and lift them with the identity on $V$ by central adjustments. The remaining kernel, fixing both $V$ and $Z$, changes each selected quotient generator by an arbitrary central element of order at most two. These independent twists are generated by changing one basis lift at a time by one basis element of $D$. Together these generators give all automorphisms within the same bound.

## Scope and source comparison

For example, take a class-two group with central cyclic generator $z$ of order $p^l$, lifts $x,y$ of order $p$, and $[x,y]=z^{p^{l-1}}$ for $l\ge3$. The derived subgroup has exponent $p$ and the group exponent is $p^l$. This construction exists as the class-two Baer group on an elementary two-dimensional summand with a cyclic center and the stated bracket. The center is cyclic, so the group has no nontrivial abelian direct factor. The family lies outside Frattini class two and has unbounded exponent even when the derived exponent is $p$.

The closest group algorithms are Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun for odd groups of class two, with bound $N^{O((\log N)^{8/9})}$, and Ivanyos–Mendoza–Qiao–Sun–Zhang for odd Frattini class-two groups, with bound $N^{\widetilde O((\log N)^{1/2})}$. The theorem gives the latter exponent on the distinct family defined by bounded commutator exponent, including the display above. The construction uses Baer's correspondence and the short-probe marked tensor algorithm. Its additional ingredient is the simultaneous compression of both truncation actions with a single marked nilpotent shift. A targeted primary literature search did not find this exact construction or result. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Theorem 1.8 and Section 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1), [Ivanyos–Mendoza–Qiao–Sun–Zhang, FOCS 2024](https://chuanqizhang.com/assets/pdf/pdf/focs24.pdf), [Sun's general class-two algorithm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05920)
