# Arithmetic circuit worlds seen from the tensor gap

Let \(A,B,H,H_R,H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) denote the languages and reductions in the oracle chapter. The ordinary and signed finite-field languages considered here have binary encodings. The arithmetic field used to reason about their Boolean verifiers may be unrelated to the field of the tensors.

## An explicit integer witness-count family

Fix a deterministic polynomial-time witness verifier \(V(x,w)\), with a fixed polynomial number of witness bits at each input length \(n\). In particular, take \(V\) to check the canonical binary encoding of the three determinant-one matrices over \(\mathbb F_3\), then multiply and compare the tensors. Make the verifier false on malformed inputs. A canonical encoding gives one valid witness for each transporter.

Build a uniform Boolean circuit for the verifier. Introduce a Boolean variable for the output of every gate. A gate condition on its at most two inputs and one output has a constant-degree arithmetic indicator computed from its truth table: sum the minterms of the satisfying assignments, with bit 0 represented by \(1-X\) and bit 1 by \(X\). Multiply these gate indicators and the indicator that the circuit output is 1. Call the result \(g_n(x,w,z)\). Its formal degree, arithmetic circuit size, and variable count are polynomial in \(n\), and its integer constants may be chosen from \(\{0,\pm1\}\). For each Boolean \(x,w\), the gate values \(z\) are uniquely determined, and the sum over \(z\) is exactly the acceptance bit. Thus
\[
 f_n(x)=\sum_{w,z\in\{0,1\}^{\operatorname{poly}(n)}}g_n(x,w,z)
\]
is a constant-free VNP family. At Boolean \(x\), it is the number of verified witnesses, is nonnegative, and is bounded above by \(2^{|w|}\). For the signed verifier, testing \(f_n(x)>0\) decides \(A\).

## Characteristic zero: the named family already suffices

If \(f_n\) for the signed verifier has polynomial-size division-free arithmetic circuits over \(\mathbb Q\) with rational constants of polynomial bit length, then \(A\in\mathrm{P/poly}\) and \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) holds. If such circuits are produced in deterministic polynomial time from \(1^n\), then \(A\in\mathrm P\) and \(H\) holds. Constant-free arithmetic circuits are a special case, with no denominator restriction to check.

To see this, evaluate the given circuit at the Boolean input modulo many small primes. For a rational constant skip primes dividing its written denominator. The bad primes divide the product of all written denominators, whose bit length is polynomial. Using deterministic trial division and the prime number theorem estimate for polynomially many small primes, select enough remaining primes with product bigger than \(2^{|w|}\). Equivalently evaluate at a polynomial initial range of primes whose total log product exceeds the bit length of this denominator product plus \(|w|\). All arithmetic now takes polynomial bit time even if internal rational circuit values would be much larger. Because each valid residue equals the integer count modulo the chosen prime, all residues vanish exactly when the count is zero. For the nonuniform version, take the small circuit description as advice.

Consequently \(\neg H\) implies that this particular constant-free VNP witness-count family lacks polynomial-time uniform polynomial-size constant-free arithmetic circuits. Failure of \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) strengthens this to a nonuniform circuit lower bound for the same family, and therefore \(\mathrm{VP}^0\ne\mathrm{VNP}^0\) in characteristic zero. Unrestricted rational constants of arbitrary bit size fall outside the modular evaluation argument. [Grochow and Pitassi, arithmetic versus Boolean complexity](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~toni/Papers/jacm-gp.pdf).

## Finite characteristic: first isolate, then count

For every fixed finite arithmetic field \(k\), if \(\mathrm{VP}_k=\mathrm{VNP}_k\), then \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{P/poly}\), so \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) holds. If the collapse is polynomial-time uniform, in the sense that each uniform verifier-count family above has polynomial-time constructible polynomial-size circuits over \(k\), then \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\). In particular \(H_R\) holds and \(P=RP\) would then force \(H\).

The reason for isolating is that a nonempty witness set may have zero count in the characteristic of \(k\). Given an NP verifier, add a random affine binary hash constraint on its witness \(w\). Valiant–Vazirani isolation gives a uniformly generated restriction with inverse-polynomial probability of exactly one witness on every YES input, and with no witnesses on every NO input. The hash description is part of a new verifier input. The gate-indicator construction gives a VNP polynomial whose Boolean evaluations count its accepting witnesses in \(k\). With a small arithmetic circuit, each field gate on this fixed finite field is a constant-size Boolean computation. Testing for a nonzero residue is sound on NO inputs and succeeds on all singleton outcomes. Polynomial repetition and an OR implement one-sided randomized decision. For nonuniform circuits, a polynomial random seed list can be fixed simultaneously for all inputs of length \(n\) by amplification and the union bound. For uniform circuits, the procedure is an RP algorithm. Fixed small ordinary YES and NO queries turn the latter into a bounded-error randomized many-one map to ordinary TI. [Valiant and Vazirani, *NP is as easy as detecting unique solutions*](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/ValVaz.pdf), [Grochow and Pitassi, finite-field Boolean simulations](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~toni/Papers/jacm-gp.pdf).

Thus failure of \(H_{\mathrm{adv}}\) implies \(\mathrm{VP}_k\ne\mathrm{VNP}_k\) for every fixed finite \(k\), including \(\mathbb F_2\), even though ordinary and signed tensor isomorphism over \(\mathbb F_2\) are identical. Failure of \(H_R\) forces failure of the stated uniform finite-field collapse. Neither implication turns a purely uniform tensor degree separation into an unconditional nonuniform arithmetic lower bound.
