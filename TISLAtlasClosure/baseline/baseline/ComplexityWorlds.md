# Consequences of a strict determinant-one tensor-isomorphism degree over F₃

## Scope and results

Let \(A\) be dense three-tensor isomorphism under the product of the three \(\mathrm{SL}\) groups over fixed \(\mathbf F_3\). Let \(B\) be ordinary three-tensor isomorphism under the corresponding \(\mathrm{GL}\) groups. Let \(H\) be the assertion \(A\le_m^{P}B\). Null-line padding in each mode gives the opposite reduction. This chapter studies the strict deterministic many-one branch \(\neg H\). The proofs are informal. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5).

- A single ordinary transporter query, followed by unblinding, samples a perfectly uniform transporter. Repeating it gives unconditional one-sided randomized reductions for signed decision and signed witness search from ordinary witness search. Under \(\neg H\), either \(\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{RP}\) or ordinary transporter search has no worst-case PPT algorithm.
- If a randomized many-one reduction \(A\to B\) exists despite \(\neg H\), every language in \(\mathrm E\) has subexponentially small \(B\)-oracle circuits on infinitely many lengths, in the precise sense below.
- Both problems belong to the intersection of the exact-zero and nonzero GapP classes. This extends to adaptive oracle machines in a precise form, and sharpens the counting-class separation implied by \(\neg H\).
- When \(A\notin\mathrm{BPP}\), the explicit orbit-rejection inverter problem has one recursive sequence of hard auxiliary indices against every uniformly randomized polynomial-time adversary after direct-product amplification. A separate all-length average-case assumption yields a standard one-way function.

## Exact uniformization from one ordinary witness

Let \(G\) act on a finite set and write \(G_x\) for the stabilizer of \(x\). Suppose that \(x,y\) are in the same orbit. A solver is given only a pair of points and may return any transporter, with its own arbitrary choice rule. Draw \(u\) uniformly in \(G\). Send the pair \((ux,y)\) and set the output to \(wu\), where \(w(ux)=y\) is any answer. For a fixed first query point \(z\), the conditional law of \(u\) is uniform on \(u_0G_x\), where \(u_0x=z\). Conditional on \(z\) and on the solver's answer \(w\), it remains uniform there, because the solver's information about the fresh random \(u\) comes only from \(z\). Multiplication by \(w\) is a bijection from this coset onto the full transporter \(\{g:gx=y\}=wu_0G_x\). The conclusion holds conditional on any earlier execution history and on the solver succeeding in the current trial. If a solver sometimes fails, verify reported witnesses and keep only genuine answers. A fresh blind still gives the same conditional uniform law on every successful trial.

Apply this to \(G=\prod_i\mathrm{GL}_{d_i}(\mathbf F_3)\) and the homomorphism \(\chi:G\to\{\pm1\}^3\) given by the three determinants. In a nonempty ordinary transporter the possible signs are one coset of \(\chi(G_x)\). Every attainable sign occurs in exactly the same number of transporters. Thus, whenever the all-positive sign is feasible, a uniform ordinary transporter has that sign with probability at least \(1/8\). Rejecting all other signs gives an exactly uniform signed transporter in at most eight ordinary witness calls in expectation, on an input promised to have a signed witness. For decision, cap the number of trials and reject upon exhausting them. If the ordinary solver succeeds with probability at least \(s\) per blinded ordinary yes query, the failure probability on a signed yes input after \(r\) trials with fresh private randomness is at most \((1-s/8)^r\). Signed no inputs never pass the witness checks. An arbitrary ordinary witness solver can be exact or have verifiable success bounded below by an inverse polynomial. The reverse witness-search reduction is deterministic, using null padding and restriction to supports followed by extension to the original spaces.

For exact group sampling, index the independent-row choices in mixed radix, as in the preceding complexity chapter, and use rejection from a random binary integer. The expected number of attempts is below two. For a strict polynomial-time RP decision algorithm, cap the uniform group draw at even one trial from a power-of-two interval and count a rejected index as a failed blind. Its acceptance probability is at least \(1/2\) and the conditional law is still exact. These procedures do not require computing any stabilizer generators or testing completeness of an enumerated stabilizer subgroup.

A further corollary is a Monte Carlo ordinary stabilizer generator list from single-witness search. Apply the exact blinding sampler to \((x,x)\). Given any proper subgroup of \(G_x\) already generated, each independent uniform stabilizer element is outside it with probability at least \(1/2\) and then at least doubles its size. At most \(\log_2|G_x|\) doublings suffice. A polynomial number \(O(\log|G|+\log(1/\epsilon))\) of independent uniform samples therefore generates all of \(G_x\) with probability \(1-\epsilon\), by domination of the still-needed doubling events by fair independent coins until completion. The sampled subgroup is always a subgroup of \(G_x\). This does not certify that the group has been generated on any particular execution.

If worst-case PPT ordinary witness search exists, the verified algorithm above gives \(A\in\mathrm{RP}\). If additionally \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{RP}\), then \(A\in\mathrm P\) and constant-output instances give \(H\). Consequently \(\neg H\) implies the stated disjunction. In the stronger nonuniform separation branch, even nonuniform randomized polynomial-size ordinary witness search with uniform success probability on every yes input is impossible. Composing and amplifying would put \(A\) in \(\mathrm{BPP/poly}\subseteq\mathrm{P/poly}\) by seed fixing, and fixed ordinary outputs would yield a nonuniform many-one reduction. Another sufficient route for \(H\) is an ordinary witness algorithm in PPT together with the exponential-circuit-hardness hypothesis of Impagliazzo and Wigderson, which implies \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{BPP}\). [Impagliazzo and Wigderson](https://doi.org/10.1145/258533.258590).

## A hardness-versus-randomness consequence without assuming ordinary TI circuits

Fix an oracle \(O\) for which \(B\) has polynomial-size \(O\)-oracle circuits. This holds for \(O=B\) with a single oracle gate and for \(O=\mathrm{SAT}\) by the NP verifier for \(B\). Suppose there is a language \(J\in\mathrm E=\mathrm{DTIME}(2^{O(n)})\) and \(\eta>0\) such that for all sufficiently large \(m\), every \(O\)-oracle circuit computing \(J\) on inputs of length \(m\) has size at least \(2^{\eta m}\). Under this assumption,
\[
 A\le_m^{\mathrm{BPP}}B\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H .
\]
Here randomized many-one means a polynomial-time map with one output, whose membership in \(B\) agrees with membership of the input in \(A\) with probability at least \(2/3\).

Use the Klivans–van Melkebeek relativized hardness-versus-randomness theorem in its truth-table form. For each polynomial circuit-size and output-length bound, the truth table of \(J\) on a suitable \(m=\Theta(\log L)\) gives a deterministically constructible seed-\(O(\log L)\) generator that fools \(O\)-oracle circuits of that size by error \(<1/6\). Its construction uses the truth table as data and does not need access to \(O\). The table is computable in polynomial time in \(L\) because \(J\in\mathrm E\). For fixed input \(x\), compose the randomized reduction \(f(x,r)\) with a circuit for \(B\) to get a polynomial-size \(O\)-oracle circuit on the random tape \(r\). Different output lengths cause no issue: supply the finitely many circuit sizes up to the polynomial bound and select by actual length. Enumerate all generator seeds, compute the corresponding tensor instances, and compile their strict majority into one ordinary TI instance by the logarithmic-depth monotone Boolean construction in the prior chapter. Only the hypothetical analysis uses \(O\)-oracle circuits. The resulting mapping algorithm is deterministic and has no oracle, and hardcoding finitely many short inputs handles any initial lengths.

Taking the contrapositive with \(O=B\), if \(\neg H\) and \(A\le_m^{\mathrm{BPP}}B\) both hold, then for every \(J\in\mathrm E\) and every \(\eta>0\) there are infinitely many lengths \(m\) at which \(J\) has a \(B\)-oracle circuit of size less than \(2^{\eta m}\). This is an infinitely-often assertion with lengths allowed to depend on both \(J\) and \(\eta\). The same follows for SAT-oracle circuits. The quantifiers do not give one circuit family with an all-length bound. For arbitrary randomized Turing reductions the same generator yields a deterministic Turing reduction, since every seed simulation still requires its TI oracle answers. The additional many-one step above uses the single-output form. [Klivans and van Melkebeek, relativized hardness versus randomness](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539700389652), [Impagliazzo, Kabanets and Wigderson, truth-table formulation of the relativized generator](https://www2.cs.sfu.ca/~kabanets/papers/exp_journal.pdf).

## Exact counting gap and adaptive oracle closure

For either group choice \(G\), the counts \(t(x,y)=|\{g:gx=y\}|\) and \(a(x)=|G_x|\) are \(\#\mathrm P\) functions. Guess a canonical bit encoding of the three matrices, verify the group tests and perform the action. A fixed legal element has exactly one accepting encoding. On well-formed same-format input pairs the identity witness makes \(a(x)\ge1\), and the coset identity gives
\[
 t(x,y)=
 \begin{cases}
   a(x),&x\cong_G y,\\
   0,&x\not\cong_G y .
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus \(t\) is nonzero exactly on yes inputs, whereas \(a-t\) is nonzero exactly on no inputs. For malformed or incompatible pairs set \(t=0,a=1\), which preserves the GapP guarantees with a deterministic validity test. Write \(\mathcal C=\mathrm C_{=}\mathrm P\cap\mathrm{coC}_{=}\mathrm P\), where \(\mathrm C_{=}\mathrm P\) is the class of zero tests of GapP functions. Both tensor languages are in \(\mathcal C\). In the quantum nonzero-acceptance convention, equivalently both are in \(\mathrm{NQP}\cap\mathrm{coNQP}\). There is no bounded quantum acceptance gap in this statement. [Hemaspaandra, Hemaspaandra, Spakowski and Watanabe, Theorem 2.9](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol117-mfcs2018/LIPIcs.MFCS.2018.51/LIPIcs.MFCS.2018.51.pdf), [Fenner, Green, Homer and Pruim, Corollary 3.5](https://aleph0.clarku.edu/~fgreen/papers/nqp.pdf).

More generally, for every fixed oracle \(D\in \mathcal C\),
\[
 \mathrm P^D\subseteq\mathcal C,\qquad
 \mathrm{NP}^D\subseteq\mathrm{coC}_{=}\mathrm P,\qquad
 \mathrm{coNP}^D\subseteq\mathrm C_{=}\mathrm P .
\]
Choose GapP functions \(g_1,g_0\) nonzero exactly on the yes and no instances of \(D\), respectively. For each nondeterministic tape of the oracle machine, sum over all prospective adaptive answer transcripts. Simulate with the guessed answers and multiply \(g_b(q)^2\) over its query positions \((q,b)\), taking factor 1 after the last query and retaining only accepting transcripts. Incorrect transcripts have a zero factor at the first inaccurate answer, and a truthful accepting computation gives a positive product. Uniform polynomially long products and sums over polynomial-bit strings preserve GapP. Their use can also be seen by expanding the GapP signed paths into a nondeterministic product machine and then summing path gaps. Squaring removes cancellation between transcripts, so the resulting GapP function is nonzero precisely when there is an accepting path. The deterministic case can use the same argument for the complementary machine, establishing both class containments. [Fenner, Fortnow and Kurtz, GapP and its closure properties](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(94)90020-5).

The previous chapter's perfect zero-knowledge protocols therefore strengthen the consequence of \(\neg H\) to
\[
 \mathrm P\subsetneq
 \mathrm{NP}\cap\mathrm{PZK}\cap\mathrm{coHVPZK}\cap\mathrm C_{=}\mathrm P.
\]
A conditional hardness consequence is that if either tensor problem were \(\mathrm C_{=}\mathrm P\)-hard under deterministic polynomial-time Turing reductions, then \(\mathrm C_{=}\mathrm P=\mathrm{coC}_{=}\mathrm P\) and \(\mathrm{PH}\subseteq\mathrm C_{=}\mathrm P\). Indeed, the first closure inclusion places all of \(\mathrm C_{=}\mathrm P\) in the intersection in that case, and the remaining two inclusions allow induction over the polynomial hierarchy. This establishes no hardness of a tensor problem for a counting class.

## Strong inversion hardness on one sequence of auxiliary inputs

Use the preceding chapter's functions \(F_x(b,r)\), indexed by signed TI pairs \(x=(T_0,T_1)\). They output \(gT_b\) for a uniform signed transform \(g\) on an accepted bounded-bit draw or a tagged \(\star\) on rejection of probability at most \(1/16\). Their output law is independent of \(b\) on signed yes inputs. On signed no inputs the bit \(b\) is determined by every non-\(\star\) output. Whenever \(A\notin\mathrm{BPP}\), the earlier verified decision test shows that every uniform PPT inverter has failure at least \(1/4\) on infinitely many no instances. This applies under \(\neg H\) and \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{BPP}\), or under failure of even nonuniform many-one reducibility.

Here is a direct amplification lemma for these fixed finite laws. Let \(Y=F_x(U)\) with \(U\) uniform. Suppose a randomized algorithm inverts all \(k\) coordinates on \(k\) independent samples with probability at least \(\delta\), for this particular \(x\). For a candidate \(y\) let \(p(y)\) be the inversion probability when \(y\) is placed in a uniformly chosen coordinate and the other coordinates are freshly sampled. Put \(S=\{y:p(y)<\delta/(2k)\}\). On a fresh product sample, success and membership of any coordinate in \(S\) together have probability at most
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{k}\Pr[\text{success and }Y_j\in S]
    = k\,\mathbb E_{y\sim Y}\!\left[\mathbf 1_S(y)p(y)\right]\le\delta/2 .
\]
Therefore \((1-\Pr[Y\in S])^k\ge \delta/2\) and \(\Pr[Y\in S]\le k^{-1}\ln(2/\delta)\). For any \(y\notin S\), independent embeddings and checks invert \(y\) with failure at most \(\epsilon\) after \(O((k/\delta)\log(1/\epsilon))\) trials. If checking all coordinates, a valid answer at the embedded coordinate follows on any verified vector success. Thus the overall failure probability for the single-sample inverter is at most \(k^{-1}\ln(2/\delta)+\epsilon\). An exactly uniform choice of insertion slot can use the next power of two and avoid rejection by taking \(k\) a power of two. This optional choice increases the selected \(k\) by at most two.

Take \(k\) to be the next power of two at least \(|x|\). For any fixed polynomial-time product inverter \(I\) and integer \(c\ge1\), if \(I\) succeeds with probability at least \(|x|^{-c}\) on all but finitely many no inputs, the amplification lemma supplies a uniform polynomial-time single inverter with failure below \(1/4\) on all but finitely many no inputs. This contradicts the previous paragraph. Hence every such \(I,c\) has infinitely many signed no inputs on which its product inversion probability is below \(|x|^{-c}\).

A single sequence can be chosen simultaneously for all uniform algorithms. Enumerate clocked PPT inverters and at stage \(j\) combine the first \(j\) machines, accepting any verified product preimage. For this fixed \(j\) the combined algorithm is polynomial time, with exponent allowed to depend on \(j\) in the existence argument. There are arbitrarily long no instances on which it has success below \(|x|^{-j}\), so choose one \(x_j\) of strictly larger length than the preceding choices. Since signed TI is decidable by exhaustive search, each stage can enumerate no instances and compute exact rational success probabilities by enumerating each finite random tape until it finds such an \(x_j\). This makes the sequence recursive. Each fixed PPT inverter then has negligible success as a function of \(|x_j|\) along this sequence. Recursiveness alone gives no efficient index sampler and does not prove a standard one-way function. If \(A\notin\mathrm{BPP}^B\), this same conclusion holds with every \(x_j\) ordinary-isomorphic. In the underlying decision contradiction, first reject ordinary-no pairs by the \(B\) oracle, then apply the inverter test to the remaining promise. In particular this also applies under \(A\notin\mathrm{BPP}\) and \(B\in\mathrm{BPP}\).

## Exactly what average-case assumption yields a standard one-way function

Let \(D_n\) be a uniformly polynomial-time samplable distribution on signed instances of length at most \(\operatorname{poly}(n)\), equipped with an explicit parameter \(1^n\). Suppose that for some fixed \(c\), every uniform PPT decision algorithm given \((1^n,x)\) errs on at least an \(n^{-c}\) fraction of \(D_n\), counting its coins, for all sufficiently large \(n\). Then uniform standard one-way functions exist. If this assumption is against nonuniform polynomial-size randomized families, the conclusion is secure against those families. The statement requires the eventual lower bound, beyond what comes from \(\neg H\).

Let \(x=D_n(s)\) be the deterministic sampler on its uniform tape \(s\). Define the keyless efficiently computable function family
\[
 G_n(s,b,r)=(x,F_x(b,r)).
\]
Pad inputs and outputs to fixed polynomial lengths and ignore unused random bits. Include the parameter as a tag or give \(1^n\) to the adversary. If an inverter for \(G_n\) has average failure \(\rho_n\), let \(e(x)\) be its conditional failure on challenge \(x\), so \(\mathbb E_{x\sim D_n} e(x)=\rho_n\). Given any \(x\), sample fresh \((b,r)\), run the inverter and test the returned preimage. On yes inputs the probability of a valid non-\(\star\) preimage with the correct \(b\) is at most \(1/2\). On no inputs with \(e(x)<1/4\), the probability exceeds \(11/16\) by the disjoint-orbit argument. Threshold the observed rate at \(5/8\) over \(O(\log n)\) repetitions. Except on a set of distribution mass at most \(4\rho_n\) by Markov's inequality, the error is arbitrarily inverse-polynomial by increasing the constant in the repetition count. If \(\rho_n<n^{-c}/8\) for infinitely many \(n\), this produces a decision algorithm contradicting the assumed eventual lower bound. Hence \(G_n\) is a weak one-way function with inverse-polynomial failure. The usual product amplification gives strong one-way functions. Alternatively the preceding amplification lemma applies to the entire distribution of a single \(G_n\) output: take \(k\) a power of two at least \(n^{c+2}\), so \(\ln(2/\delta)/k\) is smaller than \(n^{-c}/16\) for every inverse-polynomial \(\delta\) and all large \(n\). These are the usual polynomially indexed function-family conventions. This explicit tensor derivation parallels the general statistical zero-knowledge average-case connection. [Sahai and Vadhan, Theorems 5.12–5.13](https://web.cs.ucla.edu/~sahai/work/web/2003%20Publications/J.ACM2003.pdf), [Ostrovsky, hard-on-average problems and one-way functions](https://web.cs.ucla.edu/~rafail/PUBLIC/06.pdf), [Yao, weak-to-strong hardness amplification](https://doi.org/10.1109/SFCS.1982.45).

## Polynomial compilation of arbitrarily many typed tensor constraints

This independent compression fills the growth requirement for the parity and span-program constructions. It applies to two systems with the same indexed tensor signatures, each consisting of \(c\) tensors of order at most three, supported on named spaces \(V_1,\ldots,V_m\) and their duals. The coupled action uses one map \(g_i\) on each \(V_i\), and \(g_i^{-T}\) on its dual. Allow repeated occurrences of the same type in a tensor. Compare any scalar constraints directly, so assume all remaining constraints have positive order.

For every \(i\) take a marker space \(W_i\) of dimension \(k_i=c+i+1\), with the nilpotent Jordan shift \(J_i\) in its standard basis. Put \(V=\bigoplus_i V_i\otimes W_i\), and add the two fixed operators
\[
 J=\bigoplus_i (I_{V_i}\otimes J_i),\qquad
 K=\bigoplus_i (I_{V_i}\otimes J_i^T)
\]
as tensors of type \(V\otimes V^*\). A map of \(V\) preserves these operator tensors exactly when it commutes with both matrices. As matrices on \(W_i\), \(J_i\) and \(J_i^T\) generate the entire matrix algebra. Choose the shift convention \(J_i e_j=e_{j+1}\) for \(j<k_i\) and \(J_i e_{k_i}=0\). Then \(I-J_iJ_i^T=E_{11}\), and \(J_i^{a-1}E_{11}(J_i^T)^{b-1}=E_{ab}\). It follows that the \(W_i\) are simple modules for the free algebra on two generators, with endomorphism algebra \(\mathbf F_3\). Their distinct dimensions make them pairwise nonisomorphic. Hence the invertible common centralizer on \(V\) is exactly the set of \(\bigoplus_i(g_i\otimes I_{W_i})\) with \(g_i\in\mathrm{GL}(V_i)\). Zero-dimensional type spaces can be removed without changing the action.

For constraint \(j\), embed each occurrence of \(V_i\) into \(V_i\otimes e_j\) in \(V\), and each dual occurrence into \(V_i^*\otimes e_j^*\) in \(V^*\). For each ordered variance pattern of order one, two, or three, take the sum of the resulting tensors of that pattern. There are at most \(2+4+8=14\) such sums. Tiles for distinct constraints are in different marker positions and cannot mix under the common centralizer. Thus equality of the packed sums under an allowed change of basis is equivalent to equality of every original tensor constraint under the intended \(g_i\)'s. In both directions, use the same \(g_i\) on every occurrence of its named space. The two operator tensors and these sums form a system of at most 16 tensors supported on the one named space \(V\). The dimension of \(V\) is at most \((c+m+1)\sum_i\dim V_i\), so all resulting dense tensors have polynomial encoding length in the input. The ordinary tensor-system-to-three-tensor result of Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk now applies with a constant number of tensors. Its growth in the number of tensors would be insufficient if invoked directly on the original system. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4).

For the proposed span compilation, take the product of the two centrally indecomposable triangular algebras associated with one ordinary TI test. The swap of the two primitive central idempotents is possible exactly when that test is affirmative. Mark their two-dimensional linear span by an inclusion tensor, and add its alternating determinant form with one-dimensional output. Equivariance forces the action on that output line to be the sign of the swap. This realizes an optional bit, available exactly for a true query. To impose any collection of linear equations in these bits over \(\mathbf F_2\), use chains of one-dimensional bilinear multiplication maps over \(\mathbf F_3\) to multiply their signs. In each equation fix the initial line map to \(+1\), and demand \((-1)^b\) on the final line for a right-hand-side bit \(b\). A line with its own multiplication forces its map to fix \(1\), since \(\lambda=\lambda^2\) and \(\lambda\ne0\). Linear maps from this fixed line impose the desired endpoints by changing the target map's scalar. This uses polynomially many typed tensors, so the preceding compression suffices.

The resulting ordinary TI closure applies to monotone span programs on ordinary TI answers. A row labelled by a negative literal would require a separate ordinary TI realization of that complemented predicate. Positive monotone span programs already give aggregations that no polynomial-size monotone Boolean circuit computes on freely varying input bits. For instance, label each possible edge \(uv\) in a bipartite graph by an ordinary TI question and select only those whose answer is yes. Take the available incidence columns \(e_u+e_v\) and ask whether they span the all-ones vector. This holds exactly when the available graph contains a spanning subgraph with every vertex of odd degree. Babai, Gál and Wigderson prove that this function on \(n\) edge variables has an \(n\)-size monotone span program over \(\mathbf F_2\), monotone circuit size \(n^{\Omega(\log n)}\), and monotone formula size \(\exp(\Omega(\sqrt n))\). Using target \(e_s+e_t\) instead realizes undirected \(s\)-\(t\) connectivity. In both cases the claims concern the monotone postprocessing on abstract query answers and do not establish a lower bound for ordinary TI itself. [Babai, Gál and Wigderson, Theorems 2.1, 2.3 and 2.5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004930050058), [Karchmer and Wigderson, Theorem 5](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/MYPAPERS/KW93/proc.pdf).

For affine sign aggregation under \(H\), form the eight determinant-feasibility tests by explicit sign twists for each independent tensor pair, then apply \(H\) to each test. For pair \(i\), use a swap bit \(s_{i,v}\) for each feasible sign vector \(v\in\mathbf F_2^3\). Demand \(\sum_v s_{i,v}=1\) and use \(\sum_v s_{i,v}v\) in the global parity constraints. A nonempty determinant profile is an affine subspace of \(\mathbf F_2^3\), which is closed under sums of an odd number of its elements. Each desired element is realized by one nonzero bit. An empty profile cannot meet the oddness equation. Thus the coupled test reduces to one ordinary TI instance. Conversely the special case of a single pair with its full determinant vector constrained to zero gives \(H\).

## Verification of the earlier ComplexityWorlds chapter

Audited the 110-line chapter with source SHA-256 408c42a22f56fc522af685eb2aa902ff0600b4e61cbe79fb8765a3a9939b0978. Its stated results survive review in their recorded models.

- Lines 1–21. Null padding gives reflection because contraction supports stay inside the original spaces. The triangular algebra is associative, the bridge forces a unique vertex order, and its connected corner graph forces a trivial central-idempotent decomposition. Conjunction, disjunction and majority depths are correct. A useful exact detail is \(f(a)=P(1)^{-1}P(a)=Q(a)Q(1)^{-1}\) for the normalized multiplication isotopy. That identity proves \(f(ab)=f(a)f(b)\). The logarithmic-query transcript argument uses complement closure exactly at the negative literals.
- Lines 23–25. Exponential amplification and a union bound give polynomial advice. A majority-correct seed list is sufficient for compiling the given random map. The phrase “exact criterion” should be read in that sense, without a converse from existence of some deterministic map to existence of such a list for that specific randomized map. The previous circuit-based generator works under its stated \(B\in\mathrm{P/poly}\) assumption. The oracle-circuit version above avoids that extra assumption under a stronger hardness hypothesis.
- Lines 27–43. The fixed-language random external-oracle result is valid. For the positive-measure direction, approximate the transducer's correctness set by a finite union of cylinders in symmetric difference. It then has density \(>2/3\) on at least one elementary cylinder of a common finite-coordinate partition. The 1 direction uses a summable bound and may hardcode the resulting finitely many errors separately for each oracle.
- Lines 45–58. The recursive-oracle separation works with both injection completions and a unique marker. The two sets of already-touched outputs are disjoint and their total is \(<2^n\), so an equal-image completion and a disjoint-image completion both exist. The marker prefix selected at the end has \(2^{n-1}\) possible words and fewer queries than that. Keep the relativized statistical zero-knowledge closure here separate from the later malicious-verifier perfect protocol.
- Lines 60–72. The yes simulator uses exact expected-time group sampling and the fixed-tape rewind retention probability \(1/2\), including an invalid challenge. The no prover has perfect completeness, and the honest verifier rejects all wrong sampled branches with soundness at most \(5/8\). The stated convention governs the perfect simulator and does not require a strictly bounded-bit exact uniform group draw.
- Lines 74–97. The swap-product distributions are flat even when the orbits have different sizes. Their entropy gap is one precisely in the disjoint case; two KT estimates and a polynomial threshold table establish randomized truth-table reducibility. The generic exact \(\mathrm{ZPP}^{\mathrm{MKTP}}\) and orbit-specific \(\mathrm{RP}^{\mathrm{MCSP}}\) statements use distinct models and are correctly scoped.
- Line 98. For clarity the positive logarithmic-depth postprocessing hypothesis must apply to \(\mathrm{KTdiff}\) as a whole, rather than just to MKTP. Setting \(d=\lfloor t/2\rfloor\), the natural threshold circuit is
  \[
   \bigvee_{s=0}^{U}
     \left[\mathrm{MKTP}(v,s+d)\wedge\neg\,\mathrm{MKTP}(u,s-1)\right],
  \]
  where \(U\) is a polynomial upper bound for \(\mathrm{KT}(u)\) and a negative threshold is always false. For a valid comparison take \(s=\mathrm{KT}(u)\). The converse follows because each true term has \(s\le\mathrm{KT}(u)\). This circuit is monotone in the \(v\)-threshold bits and antitone in the \(u\)-threshold bits. A simultaneous deterministic many-one reduction of \(\mathrm{MKTP}\) and its complement to ordinary TI, or one of MKTP together with \(\overline B\le_m^P B\), thus suffices to compile \(\mathrm{KTdiff}\) to one ordinary TI question and get a randomized signed-to-ordinary map. A separate derandomization premise is still needed to infer uniform \(H\).
- Lines 100–110. The input-indexed weak inversion and exact bit-prediction results have the stated quantifiers. The \(5/8\) decision cutoff lies strictly between \(1/2\) and \(11/16\). Constant-sized finite exceptions can be hardcoded in a uniform algorithm. The direct-product and average-case deductions above require their respective extra hypotheses.
