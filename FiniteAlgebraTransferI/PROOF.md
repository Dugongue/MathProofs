# Uniform exact surjection counting onto full matrix monoids

Fix \(t\). Given explicit finite operation tables for algebras \(S,B\), with a distinguished multiplication operation and at most \(t\) full matrix monoids in the product decomposition of the target multiplication, one can recognize the target condition, count all surjective homomorphisms \(S\to B\), and construct one if it exists in deterministic polynomial time in the table lengths. Matrix dimensions and field orders are allowed to vary. The source multiplication is arbitrary. Any additional basic operations are allowed. The proof combines the one-seed semilattice enumeration, cancellative quotients, and recovery of coordinates through intrinsic matrix probes.

## Recovering target coordinates and short lists of pins

Check associativity, identity and absorbing zero of the target multiplication. Find the minimal nonzero central idempotents under the order \(e\le f\) if \(ef=e\). For a product \(\prod_j M_{d_j}(\mathbb F_{q_j})\), these are exactly the identities of the individual factors, embedded with zeros elsewhere. Compute the corners \(e_jB\) and explicitly verify that \(x\mapsto(e_jx)_j\) is a bijection onto their product. A singleton factor can be discarded, with the singleton target treated separately. To recognize a factor, try each \(q,d\) with its cardinality \(q^{d^2}\), construct the field and its matrix multiplication table, and test the model against the raw corner.

Each \(M_d(\mathbb F_q)\) has at most four multiplicative generators, apart from optionally including the identity. For \(d\ge2\), take the cyclic coordinate permutation, \(\operatorname{diag}(\xi,1,\ldots,1)\) for a primitive element \(\xi\), \(I+E_{12}\), and \(I-E_{dd}\). Conjugation by the first two yields transvections for all directed cyclic edges and all coefficients. For any other ordered pair, commutators along a simple directed path give the desired transvection. Inverses needed in these words are positive powers, as the conjugating elements are units in a finite group. The transvections and diagonal generators generate all invertible matrices by elementary elimination. Products of conjugates of \(I-E_{dd}\) give diagonal projections of every rank. Multiplying on the left and right by invertible matrices gives all matrices of those ranks. For \(d=1\) take a primitive scalar and zero. To test a raw corner against the standard model, enumerate images of the bounded generating tuple, propagate products, and check all products and bijectivity. Finite fields and primitive elements may be found by exhaustive searches taking time polynomial in \(q\), which is at most the explicit target table size.

For the product choose at most \(5t\) named elements by embedding the per-factor generators with identities in other factors, and include the global identity if needed. These generate the product. During propagation record straight-line multiplication words for each target element. Enumerate candidate source preimages of this bounded list of elements, then interpret all recorded words on the source, with their fixed parenthesizations. Any compatible homomorphism sends the interpreted constants to their target values.

## Recovering a homomorphism with those pins

For each target factor \(j\), the expression \(E_{1i}^{(j)}xE_{k1}^{(j)}\) has value \(x^{(j)}_{ik}E_{11}^{(j)}\), with all other blocks zero. Interpret these terms on the source and close the set of their values under multiplication, giving \(D_j\). Every compatible map sends \(D_j\) into the scalar corner \(C_j=\{aE_{11}^{(j)}:a\in\mathbb F_{q_j}\}\), whose multiplication is the cyclic unit group with an absorbing zero. All the matrix-entry probes together distinguish every target element.

There are at most polynomially many multiplication-preserving maps \(D_j\to C_j\), and one can list them. First the indicator of nonzero is a meet homomorphism to \(\{0,1\}\), so its true fiber \(Y\) is the empty set or the closure of one seed. A nonempty fiber contains a fixed parenthesized product of all its elements, and the backward implication from a product to both factors recovers every element from that seed. List these fibers by implication closure and deduplicate.

On a nonempty \(Y\), force associativity and commutativity by merging the two values of each defect, with congruence closure. Then repeatedly merge all pairs required by left and right cancellation, taking congruence closure again. Every merge is forced for a map into the cyclic unit group. At termination the quotient is a finite commutative cancellative semigroup, hence a group. Indeed an idempotent exists by taking a power. Cancellation makes it an identity, and the translations give inverses. Decomposing the finite abelian quotient by Smith normal form lists its homomorphisms into the cyclic group. There are at most \(|Y|\) of them, because for a cyclic input factor of order \(k\) the number into a cyclic group of order \(u\) is \(\gcd(k,u)\). Extend each map by zero and verify its multiplication equations. This gives at most \(O((|S|+1)^2)\) maps per \(j\).

Choose one candidate map on every \(D_j\). Their values on the probes determine every proposed image in the original target coordinates. Verify consistency with the partial maps and pins, all basic operation equations, and surjectivity. Count an accepted map only if the pinned preimages are the least in a fixed order of \(S\) for the named generators. Every surjection has this unique collection of pins, and its restrictions to all \(D_j\) are then unique. Pin choices and scalar-corner lists have polynomial total size for fixed \(t\). In particular the proof gives uniform polynomial exact counting for a single full matrix monoid at arbitrary dimension and field size. This proof is informal.
