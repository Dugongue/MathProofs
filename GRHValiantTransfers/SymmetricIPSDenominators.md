# Exact denominators in symmetric pigeonhole certificates

Let $N\ge 2$ and $d\ge 1$. Use $N$ pigeons and $N-1$ holes, with Boolean variables $X=(x_{ij})$. The main equations $F(X)$ are the row equations $\sum_j x_{ij}-1$ and the collision equations $x_{ij}x_{i'j}$ for unordered pairs of distinct rows. Write $q_{ij}=x_{ij}^2-x_{ij}$ for the Boolean equations. The group $S_N\times S_{N-1}$ acts simultaneously on the variables $X$, the main placeholders $Y$, and the Boolean placeholders $Z$.

**Theorem.** An invariant integral polynomial $C(X,Y,Z)$ with $Y$-degree at most $d$, $C(X,0,0)=0$, and $C(X,F(X),q(X))=b$ exists precisely when

$$
 D_N(d)\mid b,\qquad
 D_N(d)=\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,N)}
 {\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\min(d,N))}.
$$

Over $\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$, the possible values of $b$ form the ideal generated by $D_N(d)\bmod M$. Over $\mathbb Q$, $D_N(d)$ is the least common denominator attainable by a symmetric IPS certificate of $Y$-degree at most $d$. The theorem measures degree in $Y$ alone and permits unrestricted $X$- and $Z$-degree.

## Integer interpolation and evaluation ideals

Consider any finite group permuting Boolean variables and indexed integer equations equivariantly, and assume every Boolean point violates some main equation. Put $H_a=G_a$ for a Boolean assignment $a$. Let $I_a(d)$ be the ideal in $\mathbb Z$ generated by the evaluations at $F(a)$ of the distinct-monomial orbit sums under $H_a$, using monomials of positive $Y$-degree at most $d$. The admissible multipliers form $\bigcap_a I_a(d)$.

For necessity, set $X=a,Z=0$. The specialized polynomial is invariant under $H_a$ and has zero constant term. Such polynomials have an integral basis of orbit sums. For sufficiency, for each assignment orbit representative choose an integral combination $U_a(Y)$ of orbit sums giving $b$. Transport it to all points of that orbit, which is well defined by $H_a$-invariance. With $E_a(X)=\prod_{i:a_i=1}x_i\prod_{i:a_i=0}(1-x_i)$, set $Q(X,Y)=\sum_a E_a(X)U_a(Y)$. This is invariant, $Q(X,0)=0$, and $R(X)=Q(X,F(X))-b$ vanishes on the Boolean cube.

The integral basis $X^\beta\prod_i (x_i^2-x_i)^{\alpha_i}$, for $\beta_i\in\{0,1\}$, follows by triangularity from its distinct monic leading monomials $X^{\beta+2\alpha}$. Expand $R$ in this basis. The $\alpha=0$ part agrees with $R$ on the cube and is zero by multilinear interpolation. Replace each basis term's Boolean factors by $Z^\alpha$, giving an invariant polynomial $L(X,Z)$ with $L(X,0)=0$ and $L(X,q(X))=R(X)$. Thus $C=Q-L$ works. Interpolation and the monic basis work over $\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$ as well, since multilinear evaluation over the Boolean cube has an inverse with integer entries. Invariants still have a basis of distinct-monomial orbit sums. The reduced local ideals have generators $\gcd(M,g_a)$, where $g_a$ generates $I_a(d)$. Since $\operatorname{lcm}_a\gcd(M,g_a)=\gcd(M,\operatorname{lcm}_a g_a)$, reduction gives the claimed multiplier ideal. Clearing a common denominator and dividing an integral construction prove the rational statement.

## Exact p-adic evaluation

Fix $a$, a prime $p$, and a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ of $H_a$. For a label $j$ with $F_j(a)\ne0$, put $u_j=\log_p|Pj|$ and $v_j=v_p(F_j(a))$. Write $l=\lfloor\log_p d\rfloor$. Then

$$
 v_p(g_a)=\min_{j:F_j(a)\ne0}
 \begin{cases}
   \max(u_j-l,0),&v_j=0,\\
   u_j+v_j,&v_j>0 .
 \end{cases}
 \tag{1}
$$

All monomials in an $H_a$-orbit have the same value at $F(a)$. For a nonzero evaluation, conjugate the monomial so that its stabilizer meets $P$ in a Sylow subgroup of the stabilizer. Let $J$ denote that intersection. Its orbit sum has valuation $\log_p[P:J]+v_p(m(F(a)))$. Pick $j$ in the support. Its $J$-orbit has size $h=p^t\le d$ and $h\le |Pj|$. The support contains this orbit, with equal positive exponents, and all its equation evaluations are equal. It follows that $v_p(m(F(a)))\ge h v_j$. Moreover $|Pj|\le [P:J]|Jj|$ because a union of $[P:J]$ translates of $Jj$ covers $Pj$. The valuation is therefore at least $u_j-t+p^t v_j$. When $v_j=0$, use $t\le\min(l,u_j)$. When $v_j\ge 1$, use $p^t-1\ge t$.

For attainment when $v_j=0$, take an overgroup $J$ of $P_j$ in $P$ with $[J:P_j]=p^{\min(u_j,l)}$. Such overgroups exist at all intermediate powers because a proper subgroup of a finite $p$-group is properly contained in its normalizer. Iterating the normalizer argument gives index-$p$ steps. The squarefree monomial on $Jj$ has degree at most $d$, unit value in $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, and its stabilizer in $P$ contains $J$. The $p$-part of its $H_a$-orbit length therefore divides $[P:J]$. When $v_j>0$, use the degree-one monomial $Y_j$, whose orbit sum has valuation at most $u_j+v_j$. These bounds give (1).

## Applying the formula to pigeonhole

Set $s=p^{\lfloor\log_p N\rfloor}$. There is a Boolean assignment with $s$ empty rows and all other rows assigned injectively to distinct holes. Exactly the empty-row equations are active, all with value $-1$. Their stabilizer contains the full symmetric group on the empty rows as a direct factor. A Sylow subgroup projects onto a Sylow subgroup of that factor, which acts transitively because $s$ is a power of $p$ and $S_s$ contains an $s$-cycle. Thus the local valuation is $\max(\lfloor\log_p N\rfloor-l,0)$.

For the upper bound at every assignment, we show some active equation of valuation zero has $P$-orbit size at most $s$. Any row equation of valuation zero suffices. Otherwise the row sums are all $1\bmod p$. For each row $i$, the stabilizer $P_i$ acts on its occupied holes. Some hole is fixed by $P_i$, since the number of occupied holes is $1\bmod p$ and each nontrivial orbit has cardinality divisible by $p$. Choose a fixed hole on each row orbit representative and extend equivariantly. This gives a $P$-equivariant map $\phi$ from rows to holes using only occupied entries.

If $\phi$ is injective on each row orbit, then two distinct row orbits map onto the same hole orbit, since $\phi$ cannot be injective globally. Take two preimages of a hole, one in each orbit. The hole stabilizer fixes both by injectivity on the respective row orbits. Thus their collision label has orbit size at most that of one row orbit, hence at most $s$.

Otherwise, take a row orbit $\mathcal O$ on which $\phi$ is noninjective and a hole $j$ in its image. The stabilizer $T=P_j$ of the hole acts transitively on $B=\phi^{-1}(j)\cap\mathcal O$. If $i\in B$, its stabilizer $U$ in $T$ has an overgroup $V\le T$ with $[V:U]=p$. The group $V$ acts on $Vi$ through a $p$-subgroup of $S_p$, of order at most $p$. An unordered pair of distinct rows in $Vi$ therefore has $V$-orbit size at most $p$, and $T$-orbit size at most $[T:V]p=|B|$. The corresponding collision label over $j$ has $P$-orbit size at most $|Pj||B|=|\mathcal O|\le s$. The collision equation evaluates to $1$. This proves the upper bound.

Taking the maximum of the local valuations proves $v_p(D_N(d))=\max(\lfloor\log_p N\rfloor-\lfloor\log_p d\rfloor,0)$. These are exactly the valuations of the stated lcm quotient. At $N=8$, the least denominators for $d=1,2,\ldots,8$ are $840,420,140,70,14,14,2,1$.

## GRH connection and scope

The construction uses the exact congruence approach from Alpöge's released lattice arguments in a different algebraic setting. Evaluation on the integer orbit-sum basis gives a one-row lattice image test at each assignment. The exact local valuations survive the lift to polynomial identities. The denominator formula is unconditional.

The Dirichlet endpoint in Alpöge's announcement would give quantitative control over designated denominator primes. Assume Dirichlet GRH and set $x=(N+d)/2$ and $h=(N-d)/2$ for $1\le d<N$. For any reduced residue class $a\bmod q$ with $q\ge3$, if $x\ge(23\varphi(q)\log q)^2$ and $h\ge\varphi(q)(\log x+8)\sqrt{x}$, every degree-$d$ certificate has a denominator divisible by a prime from that class in $(d,N)$. If $23$ is replaced by $46$ and $\log x+8$ by $2\log x+8$, every denominator has at least $\sqrt{x}$ such prime divisors. These statements follow from the short-interval theorem and the fact that every prime in $(d,N]$ divides $D_N(d)$. For $d=N-H$, the exact formula gives $\log D_N(d)=\psi(N)-\psi(N-H)$, with $\psi$ the Chebyshev prime-power function. Under RH this equals $H+O(\sqrt N\log^2(2N))$. [Dudek, Grenié and Molteni, Theorem 1.1 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.08616)

This extends the characteristic-$p$ symmetric placeholder-degree calculation to exact integral and prime-power multiplier ideals. The theorem concerns the polynomial certificates, with no bound on representing circuit size.

The Lean companion verifies the divisibility of integral linear combinations of orbit values and checks the two-pigeon sharp case at main-placeholder degree one through explicit polynomial identities and the parity obstruction at the all-zero assignment. The general theorem has a written proof.
