# One-query counting and automorphism transfer for finite rings and bilinear maps

**Theorem.** Finite rings given by their addition and multiplication tables, with arbitrary distributive multiplication, and biadditive maps \(U\times V\to W\) of finite abelian groups admit polynomial-size transformations to unmarked finite class-two groups. The transformations have all of the following properties.

- Isomorphism of rings and isotopism of bimaps, with independent invertible maps on \(U,V,W\), are each equivalent to one ordinary group isomorphism decision instance.
- Every isomorphism of the output groups gives a source isomorphism in polynomial time. On automorphisms the extraction is a surjective group homomorphism.
- The exact size of every nonempty extraction fiber is a positive integer computable from the input additive types and fixed marker graph. Thus one exact class-two isomorphism count, divided by this integer, yields the exact source count.

The output order is \(N^{O(1)}\), where \(N\) is the ring order or \(|U||V||W|\). There is no associativity or unit requirement on the source multiplication. Source additive groups can have arbitrary mixed prime-power orders, including 2-power orders. Applying the all-prime black-box class-two theorem gives deterministic polynomial-time Turing reductions from search, exact counting and automorphism generation to **ordinary class-two decision**. The one-query assertion for counting refers to an oracle for the count, which the black-box class-two theorem implements via decision.

The key step is to compute the complete fibers of the unmarked Heisenberg encoding used in the prior Lazard reduction. The marked unitriangular reduction for associative rings goes back to Mal'cev and Belegradek. Zhang–Wang–Zhuang also use marked triangular rings for bilinear isotopism and apply Qiao–Zhang's November 2026 general group isomorphism manuscript. The construction below provides an unmarked class-two decision reduction, arbitrary distributive source products, and exact count factors independently of the general group algorithm. ## 1. Finite triangular records

First work prime by prime. Put \(R=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z\), where \(p^e\) is the least common multiple of the additive exponents of the nontrivial inputs at this prime. Every additive map is \(R\)-linear. We can reject unequal additive types before comparing two inputs. Trivial factors are handled directly.

We use a finite associative ring \(A\) built from a directed graph of named corners. Write \(A_{ij}=e_i A e_j\) for the corner at an arrow \(i\to j\). The designated data vertices have diagonal ring \(R\). Add a hub \(h\), also with diagonal ring \(R\), and an arrow with corner \(R\) from it to each data vertex. Number the \(k\) data vertices from 1 through \(k\), with \(k=3\) or \(7\) below. Give data vertex \(j\) exactly \(j\) outgoing pendant leaves and give the hub 20 outgoing pendant leaves. The diagonals and incident arrows at leaves have module \(\mathbb F_p\); the action at the other endpoint is reduction to \(\mathbb F_p\). All other scalar corners specified below have module \(R\). All products between off-diagonal corners are zero except those listed. Their outputs are annihilated by off-diagonal corners on both sides, so no nonzero product of three such corners can occur. Balance of the scalar actions gives associativity with diagonal elements. The unit is the sum of the vertex idempotents.

For a bimap \(m:U\times V\to W\), take three data vertices \(0,1,2\), with corners \(U,V,W\) on \(01,12,02\), respectively. The only data product is \(01,12\to02\), prescribed by \(m\). A trivial module gives a zero corner. This is the triangular ring of a bimap, with enough additional corners to make its data vertex labels intrinsic.

For a distributive product \(m:L\times L\to L\), use seven data vertices \(s_0,s_1,m_0,m_1,m_2,t_0,t_1\). Use the following data corners and products.

| Corner module | Corners |
|---|---|
| \(L\) | \(s_0m_0,\ s_0m_1,\ m_0t_0,\ m_2t_0,\ s_0t_0,\ s_0t_1,\ s_1t_0\) |
| \(R\) | \(s_0m_2,\ s_1m_0,\ s_1m_2,\ m_0t_1,\ m_1t_0,\ m_1t_1\) |

| Composable vertex triple | Product |
|---|---|
| \(s_0,m_0,t_0\) | \((x,y)\mapsto m(x,y)\) |
| \(s_0,m_1,t_0\), \(s_0,m_0,t_1\), \(s_0,m_1,t_1\) | \((x,a)\mapsto ax\) |
| \(s_0,m_2,t_0\), \(s_1,m_0,t_0\), \(s_1,m_2,t_0\) | \((a,y)\mapsto ay\) |

Let \(I\) be the ideal of off-diagonal corners. This ideal is intrinsically the additive subgroup generated by all associative commutators in \(A\). Every commutator has zero diagonal, and for an off-diagonal \(x\in e_iAe_j\) one has \(e_i x-xe_i=x\). The product ring \(A/I\) has one cyclic local ring at each vertex. Its primitive central idempotents identify the vertices. Two full systems \((f_i)\), \((e_i)\) lifting the same labeled residue idempotents are conjugate by the unit \(\sum_i f_i e_i\) of \(1+I\). This conjugation action is free since a unit commuting with all the \(e_i\) is diagonal, and \(1+I\) has trivial intersection with diagonal units. Consequently an isomorphism gives the isomorphism of directed graphs of nonzero Peirce corners, even if its chosen idempotent lifts vary.

The hub has out-degree \(k+20\), greater than every other out-degree and every in-degree. After recognizing it, data vertex \(j\) is the unique vertex with \(j\) outgoing neighbors of out-degree zero. All target data vertices have pendant arrows, even when a data corner vanishes. The only permutations left are arbitrary permutations of the leaves having a common parent. These permutations are realized by ring automorphisms because pendant corners carry no products between off-diagonal elements. There is no anti-isomorphism between any two rings of the same construction, since that would reverse their directed graphs. A central element commutes with all vertex idempotents and is diagonal. The hub arrows force every data diagonal scalar to equal the hub scalar, and pendant arrows force every leaf scalar to be its reduction. Conversely these scalars commute with every element. This identifies \(Z(A)=R\), including when \(m=0\).

## 2. A surjection with an explicit kernel size for the triangular records

For a given ring isomorphism, align the chosen idempotents and then restrict to the data corners. On the bimap record the three restrictions give an isotopism before scalar normalization. In the tied product case call the restrictions on \(s_0m_0,m_0t_0,s_0t_0\) respectively \(X,Y,Z\). The identity triangles give \(Z=aX=bY\) for units \(a,b\). If the units on the scalar corners \(m_1t_0,m_0t_1,m_1t_1\) are \(u,v,w\), then \(a=uv/w\). If those on \(s_0m_2,s_1m_0,s_1m_2\) are \(u',v',w'\), then \(b=u'v'/w'\). The data triangle gives \(Z(m(x,y))=m'(Xx,Yy)\). Hence \((ab)^{-1}Z\) is a product-preserving bijection.

In the bimap case every isotopism extends by identity on all scalar corners. In the tied case a product-preserving bijection extends by the same map on all seven \(L\)-corners and identity on all scalar corners. More precisely fix a product-preserving bijection \(f\) and choose the six units on the scalar corners freely. They give \(a,b\), after which \(Z=ab f\), \(X=a^{-1}Z\), and \(Y=b^{-1}Z\). Each of the four remaining \(L\)-corner maps is uniquely forced by one scalar-triangle equation. The other equations are exactly \(Z=aX=bY\), so no further restrictions occur. All hub and pendant arrow maps likewise have independently chosen units in their scalar rings.

The extraction on automorphisms is multiplicative. First use the unique alignment in \(1+I\), after aligning the leaf labels, and then use restrictions and the displayed normalization. Equivalently, on the subgroup fixing the residue vertices the action on the choices of idempotent lifts is accounted for by the normal subgroup \(1+I\) acting by inner automorphisms. Multiplication of maps fixing the chosen idempotents multiplies the corresponding scalar corner parameters. Leaf permutations act trivially on data. The projection also kills all inner automorphisms of \(A\). After aligning lifts, a diagonal conjugation multiplies a data corner \(ij\) by \(d_i/d_j\) for diagonal units \(d_i,d_j\in R^\times\). On the tied product record the factors on \(X,Y\) multiply to the factor on \(Z\), so they cancel in \((ab)^{-1}Z\). For the bimap record write \(h_i\in R^\times\) for the scalar map on the full-order arrow from the hub to data vertex \(i\). For a raw map \(P_{ij}\) on corner \(ij\), extract \((h_i/h_j)P_{ij}\). The multipliers satisfy \((h_i/h_j)(h_j/h_k)=h_i/h_k\), so the resulting maps still transport the bimap. Diagonal conjugation multiplies \(P_{ij}\) by \(d_i/d_j\) and \(h_i/h_j\) by \(d_j/d_i\). Its effect cancels, and every source isotopism lifts with all hub multipliers equal to 1. The multiplication law of the arrow parameters shows that this normalized extraction is a homomorphism on automorphisms. The normalized restrictions also compose for isomorphisms between different records.

For precise counts in both formats, define \(\kappa_A\) as
\[
 |I|\left(20!\prod_{j=1}^{k}j!\right)
 \prod_{\text{scalar arrows }i\to j}|R_{ij}^{\times}|.
\]
Use \(k=7\) for the tied product and \(k=3\) for the three-vertex format, with \(R_{ij}=R\) or \(\mathbb F_p\) according to the arrow. Alignment of idempotents accounts for the \(|I|\) factor, the marker permutations account for the factorials, and the scalar choices account for the last product. The analysis above proves \(|\operatorname{Aut}(A)|=\kappa_A\) times the ring automorphism count or bimap autotopism count, respectively. This identity follows by choosing the marker parameters freely and solving for the underlying raw maps from the normalized source maps.

## 3. Exact automorphism fibers of the Heisenberg group

Put \(B=M_2(A)\) and form \(H(B)\) on \(B^3\) with product
\[
(x,y,z)(x',y',z')=(x+x',\,y+y',\,z+z'+xy').
\]
Its center and derived subgroup are both the last copy of \(B\). This follows from the commutator \(xy'-x'y\) and substitution of 1. The first two copies of \(B\) are intrinsically determined as the two image planes of the central primitive idempotents of the adjoint ring of this alternating bimap. In more detail an adjoint is a pair of additive maps \((S,T)\) with \(\beta(Sa,b)=\beta(a,Tb)\), multiplied by composing \(S\) in the forward order and \(T\) in the opposite order. Expanding in the two summands and taking one variable at a time equal to 1 shows its diagonal blocks are \((R_a,L_b)\) and \((R_b,L_a)\), where \(R_a,L_a\) are right and left multiplication by \(a\). The off-diagonal blocks are left or right multiplication by elements annihilating all associative commutators on the corresponding side. The off-diagonal matrix units \(E_{12},E_{21}\) are commutators in \(B\) and have zero joint annihilator on either side. Thus the adjoint ring is \(B^{\mathrm{op}}\times B\). Its center has two primitive central idempotents because \(Z(B)\cong R\) is local.

A ring isomorphism \(M_2(A)\to M_2(A')\) can be normalized by a target inner automorphism to an entrywise isomorphism. The semisimple quotient is a product of \(M_2(\mathbb F_p)\)'s. The image of \(E_{11}\) has rank one in every residual factor, so conjugate its image to \(E_{11}\) modulo the radical and lift the conjugating unit. Two idempotents with the same residue are conjugate by a unit congruent to 1, using their complementary idempotents in the lifting formula of §1. With \(E_{11}\) now fixed, the unit
\[
u=\sum_i \phi(E_{i1})E_{1i}, \qquad
u^{-1}=\sum_i E_{i1}\phi(E_{1i})
\]
aligns the full systems of matrix units. An isomorphism fixing the matrix units acts entrywise. Applying the same argument after transposition in the opposite matrix ring deals with anti-isomorphisms. Thus the absence of an anti-isomorphism for \(A,A'\) means a group isomorphism cannot swap the two adjoint idempotents. The normalization and restriction to \(E_{11}BE_{11}\) can all be computed by finite arithmetic. For example, one can enumerate the elements of \(B'\) to find units achieving the proven conjugations. Two normalization choices differ on the recovered \(A'\) only by an inner automorphism. The intersection of entrywise automorphisms with \(\operatorname{Inn}(B)\) is the entrywise image of \(\operatorname{Inn}(A)\), since a unit centralizing all matrix units is \(\operatorname{diag}(d,d)\) for \(d\in A^\times\). Therefore
\[
 |\operatorname{Aut}(B)|=|\operatorname{Aut}(A)|
 \frac{|B^\times|}{|A^\times|}.
\]

The resulting isomorphism on \(H(B)\) modulo its center has independent additive bijections \(\alpha,\delta\) on the two planes, and the center map \(\gamma\) satisfies \(\gamma(xy)=\alpha(x)\delta(y)\). Setting \(u=\alpha(1)\), \(v=\delta(1)\) gives \(u,v\) units, because the corresponding left and right multiplication maps are bijective. The normalized map \(\theta(x)=u^{-1}\alpha(x)=\delta(x)v^{-1}\) is a unital ring isomorphism \(B\to B'\), with
\[
 (\alpha(x),\delta(y),\gamma(z))=(u\theta(x),\,\theta(y)v,\,u\theta(z)v).
\]
These formulas preserve the full group law, including its powers at \(p=2\). The remaining freedom is adding an arbitrary additive map \(B^2\to B'\) of the two quotient coordinates to the center coordinate. Indeed a group automorphism acting trivially on the center and quotient has exactly this form. The normalized map to \(\operatorname{Aut}(B)\) is a group homomorphism, since under composition the next left-unit factor is \(u_2\theta_2(u_1)\), leaving normalized part \(\theta_2\theta_1\). We obtain
\[
 |\operatorname{Aut}(H(B))|
 =|\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(B^2,B)|\,|B^\times|^2
   \frac{|B^\times|}{|A^\times|}\,|\operatorname{Aut}(A)|.
\]
For both records, composing with the triangular normalization yields a surjective homomorphism onto the source automorphisms or autotopisms. Both normalizations are independent of the choices of conjugating units because the final triangular normalization kills all inner automorphisms. The extraction also composes for isomorphisms between different records, and every source isomorphism lifts. Exact fibers then follow by the action of the automorphism kernel on a nonempty isomorphism torsor.

## 4. Computing the count and the complete coset

An isomorphism of the output groups gives one source witness by reading the characteristic quotient and the center, applying the normalization of §3, and restricting to the named corners of §2. A list of generators of the output automorphism group maps to generators of the respective source group under the surjective homomorphism. This computes a compact description of the entire source isomorphism set. The construction uses the complete ordinary group automorphism group with no pointwise stabilizer query. A negative decision instance has source count zero.

All omitted count factors are computable directly. The ring \(A/I\) is the product of the cyclic diagonal rings \(R_i\), so
\[
 |A^\times|=|I|\prod_i |R_i^\times|,\qquad
 |B^\times|=|I|^4\prod_i |\operatorname{GL}_2(R_i)|.
\]
Here \(|\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z)|=p^{4(a-1)}(p^2-1)(p^2-p)\) by lifting invertible matrices modulo \(p\). If the additive cyclic orders of \(B\) are \(p^{e_i}\), the central-shift factor is \(p^{2\sum_{i,j}\min(e_i,e_j)}\). The bit lengths of these counts are polynomial in the table input. The source count is obtained by dividing by
\[
 \kappa_A\,|\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(B^2,B)|
 \frac{|B^\times|^3}{|A^\times|}.
\]
The torus and central-shift factors also show why simply equating group and source counts would give incorrect answers.

The cardinality of \(A\) is polynomial in the source size because it contains constantly many copies of the inputs, \(R\), and \(\mathbb F_p\). The group order is \(|A|^{12}\). An additive prime-power component can be found by factoring the table order via trial division and testing element orders. Products involving inputs from distinct primes vanish because their outputs are killed by two coprime integers. Both ring isomorphisms and bimaps therefore split over the additive primary factors. Taking the direct product of the class-two \(p\)-group instances still gives a single class-two instance of polynomial order, and all of the extraction and count factors multiply over the primes. The trivial input is handled separately.

In particular Sun's all-prime class-two decision bound transfers to all these table problems by the preceding black-box theorem. For odd order one can use the \(N^{O((\log N)^{8/9})}\) decision bound of Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun instead. These are bounds from independently stated class-two decision results. If Qiao–Zhang's proposed general algorithm is used, its stated running time would also yield polynomial-time algorithms, while the reductions here use neither that algorithm's proof nor its internal states.
