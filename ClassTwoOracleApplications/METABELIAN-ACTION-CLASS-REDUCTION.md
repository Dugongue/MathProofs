# Class-two decision for metabelian groups with bounded-rank action on their derived subgroup

Let $p$ be prime. Suppose a polynomial-time, isomorphism-invariant rule finds a subgroup $K\triangleleft G$ in each input $p$-group, such that $K$ has nilpotency class less than $p$ and $G/K$ is abelian with at most $b$ cyclic factors. With Cayley tables of order at most $N$, decision, isomorphism search, exact isomorphism counting, and automorphism generation all reduce to ordinary class-two $p$-group decision. The number of queries and the orders of all query groups are $N^{O(1+b)}$, with absolute constants. This also works for the marked-input problem where the comparison must take a supplied normal subgroup to a supplied normal subgroup.

In particular, take $K=C_G(G')$. This is characteristic, and $K'\subseteq G'$ implies $K'\subseteq Z(K)$ because every element of $K$ centralizes $G'$. For metabelian $G$ we have $G'\subseteq K$, so $G/K$ is abelian. At odd $p$, the result therefore applies to every metabelian $p$-group with $d(G/C_G(G'))\le b$, with no bound on its class or exponent. At $p=2$ the same conclusion holds for this choice of $K$ when $K$ is abelian. For fixed $b$, the theorem transfers the odd-order $N^{O((\log N)^{8/9})}$ decision bound to all four problems on the odd-prime family. More generally this yields the odd-prime time bound $N^{O((1+b)^{17/9}(\log N)^{8/9})}$, which is $N^{o(\log N)}$ when $b=o((\log N)^{1/17})$. The proof applies the [black-box class-two search and generation theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowResearchSynthesis/CLASS-TWO-BLACK-BOX.md) to auxiliary unmarked groups and maps their automorphism generators onto the desired group. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Part V, Theorem 1.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1)

## 1. The extension records

Write the quotient $Q=G/K$ as a product of cyclic groups of orders $d_1,\dots,d_s$, where $s\le b$, and select lifts $x_1,\ldots,x_s$ of these generators. Store the maps and elements on $K$ given by
$$
\alpha_i(k)=x_i k x_i^{-1},\qquad v_i=x_i^{d_i},\qquad w_{ji}=[x_j,x_i]\quad(j>i),
$$
using $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$. The ordinary multiplication table of $K$ and these relations present $G$. The image of $K$ in this presentation is normal by the conjugation relations. The quotient by that image is an abelian group generated by elements of orders dividing $d_i$, so the presentation has order at most $|K|\prod_i d_i$. It surjects onto $G$, whose order equals that bound.

For a second input $H$, let $K_H$ be the corresponding subgroup. Reject unmatched quotient types and enumerate at most $N^s$ ordered tuples $y_i\in H$ whose images in $H/K_H$ form a cyclic basis of the same orders. The isomorphisms sending every $x_i$ to $y_i$ are in bijection with isomorphisms $f:K\to K_H$ commuting with the indicated conjugation maps and carrying all source powers and commutators to the corresponding target elements. The presentation proves the reverse direction since the specified images generate $H$. This also gives an efficient extension formula. On the unique normal form $k x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_s^{a_s}$ use $f(k)y_1^{a_1}\cdots y_s^{a_s}$.

The Lazard correspondence applies to $K$, with its given Cayley table. Convert it to the finite Lie ring $L=\log K$ on the same set. Every group automorphism of $K$ is a Lie-ring automorphism in these coordinates. Thus the $\alpha_i$ are additive linear operators, and the $v_i,w_{ji}$ are individual points in this module. This works for $p=2$ when $K$ is abelian, using its ordinary addition and zero bracket. For the uniform polynomial-time bound, let $c<p$ be the class of $K$ and work in the free noncommutative polynomials on $X,Y$ truncated above degree $c$ over $\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$. Construct words whose logarithms equal $X+Y$ and $[X,Y]$ by correcting one homogeneous degree at a time. For a degree-$j$ Lie error $\sum_u d_u u$, the Dynkin–Specht–Wever formula gives the corresponding sum of left-nested Lie commutators with coefficients $d_u/j$. Append the matching group commutators raised to those residues. Their leading logarithms are exactly the Lie commutators, and the prior lower degrees remain fixed. The needed denominators are units since $j\le c<p$. The word programs and the truncated expansions cost $2^{O(c)}\operatorname{poly}(\log N)$, which is polynomial in $N$ because $c\le\log_p N$. Evaluate the programs on every pair of elements of $K$. This is the truncated-word construction from §1 of the prior [Lazard-range class reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowResearchSynthesis/LAZARD-CLASS-REDUCTION.md). The characteristic subgroups, quotient groups and cyclic bases used here can be computed directly from the tables, using Smith normal form for the abelian quotient. [Lazard correspondence, as stated in Grochow–Qiao Part IV, Theorem 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S7.SS2)

## 2. Recording all extension relations with a linear number of cyclic tags

Reject different additive exponents for the two kernels and write $R=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$ for the common exponent ring. When the kernels are trivial the input groups are abelian and their ordered basis images can be enumerated directly within the claimed bound. Let $T_j$ be the conjugation operators. Use the module
$$
M=L\oplus R e_0\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^s R E_j\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^s R F_i.
$$
Put $r_{ii}=v_i$, $r_{ji}=w_{ji}$ if $j>i$, and $r_{ji}=0$ if $j<i$. If $a,b$ denote the scalar vectors on the displayed tags, define the bilinear operation
$$
(x,a)\diamond(y,b)=
\left([x,y]+\sum_j a_{E_j}T_j(y)+\sum_{j,i}a_{E_j}b_{F_i}r_{ji},\
      (a_t b_t)_t\right).
$$
Mark each tag's cyclic line, in order. All $2s+1$ tags are idempotent because the $E$ and $F$ copies are distinct. Any marked isomorphism sends each tag to a unit multiple of its counterpart. The idempotent equation forces that unit to be one, so each tag is recovered as an exact element. The common space of elements with zero right product by every tag is $L$. In fact the scalar component of right multiplication by the tag $t$ detects exactly the scalar coordinate $a_t$, and every element of $L$ gives zero right products. On $L$ one recovers the bracket by restriction and conjugation operator $T_j$ by left multiplication by $E_j$. The prescribed elements are recovered exactly as $E_j\diamond F_i=r_{ji}$. Conversely every decorated Lie-ring isomorphism gives the unique marked isomorphism of $M$ acting identically on the tag coordinates.

The $s(s+1)/2$ powers and commutators therefore use cross products of the same $2s$ named tags. They do not contribute separate cyclic marks or extra summands. The tensor of structure constants contains at most quadratically many such products, while both the module and its marks have logarithmic order $O((s+1)\log_p N)$.

## 3. Recovering the exact automorphism group as an image

The following algebra uses the Peirce-corner and matrix Heisenberg construction from the [Lazard-range class reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowResearchSynthesis/LAZARD-CLASS-REDUCTION.md). Its data corner can use any bilinear operation. Use orthogonal idempotents for the seven core vertices $s_0,s_1,m_0,m_1,m_2,t_0,t_1$, with diagonal corners $R$. The corner $ij$ denotes $e_iA(M)e_j$.

| Corner module | Core corners |
|---|---|
| $M$ | $s_0m_0,\ s_0m_1,\ m_0t_0,\ m_2t_0,\ s_0t_0,\ s_0t_1,\ s_1t_0$ |
| $R$ | $s_0m_2,\ s_1m_0,\ s_1m_2,\ m_0t_1,\ m_1t_0,\ m_1t_1$ |

| Composable triple | Product of off-diagonal corners |
|---|---|
| $s_0,m_0,t_0$ | $(x,y)\mapsto x\diamond y$ |
| $s_0,m_1,t_0$, $s_0,m_0,t_1$, $s_0,m_1,t_1$ | $(x,a)\mapsto ax$ |
| $s_0,m_2,t_0$, $s_1,m_0,t_0$, $s_1,m_2,t_0$ | $(a,y)\mapsto ay$ |

For the $i$-th cyclic mark of order $p^{a_i}$ and representative $z_i$, add a vertex $w_i$ with diagonal ring $\mathbb Z/p^{a_i}\mathbb Z$. Give the corners $s_0w_i,w_it_0$ that same module and define their product by $(r,t)\mapsto rt z_i$ in $s_0t_0$. Put a directed path on the marked vertices, with corner $w_iw_{i+1}$ of order $p^{\min(a_i,a_{i+1})}$. Add a hub vertex $h$ with diagonal $R$ and an arrow to every core and marked vertex with module equal to the target diagonal ring. Add $10j$ private pendant leaves out of the $j$-th core vertex in the displayed order, and $2k+100$ out of the hub if there are $k$ marks. Each leaf and its incident corner have module $\mathbb F_p$. Every unlisted corner and unlisted product of two off-diagonal corners is zero. The diagonal actions are scalar reductions. The displayed products are balanced for these scalar actions. Each listed off-diagonal product lands in an output corner that annihilates every off-diagonal corner on both sides. Thus the algebra is associative and its unit is the sum of all vertex idempotents.

The off-diagonal corners together with the $p$-multiples in all diagonals form a nilpotent ideal with quotient a product of copies of $\mathbb F_p$, so this ideal is the Jacobson radical. Once the vertex idempotents are aligned, the Peirce graph identifies the hub by its maximum out-degree and the core vertices by their distinct numbers of outgoing leaves. The remaining nonleaf vertices form the marked path. The hub has out-degree $3k+107$, greater than every in-degree, which is at most $k+9$. This excludes an anti-isomorphism to a matched instance. The center is exactly the diagonal image of $R$. A central element is diagonal by commuting with every vertex idempotent. Each hub corner forces the target scalar to be the reduction of the hub scalar, and each leaf corner imposes the same rule for its endpoint.

Set $B=M_2(A(M))$ and construct the group $U(M)=B^3$ with group law
$$
(x,y,z)(x',y',z')=(x+x',y+y',z+z'+xy').
$$
The center equals the derived group and is the last copy of $B$. Indeed the alternating commutator on $B^2$ is $xy'-x'y$, which has zero radical and takes every value by using the unit of $B$. Its adjoint ring, formed by the pairs $(S,T)$ of additive endomorphisms satisfying $\beta(Su,v)=\beta(u,Tv)$ with composition reversed in the second coordinate, is $B^{\mathrm{op}}\times B$. The calculation substitutes the associative unit into each of the four block equations. The diagonal blocks are right and left multiplications. The off-diagonal blocks give annihilators of the set of commutators of $B$. Those annihilators vanish because the two off-diagonal $2$ by $2$ matrix units are commutators and annihilating both on the same side implies zero. These arguments are valid also when $p=2$.

The earlier construction established the equivalence of existence of isomorphisms. For counting and generation we now give a well-defined and multiplicative map on the actual transporters. This distinction matters because many different auxiliary-group automorphisms can give the same operation-preserving map of $M$.

1. Any group isomorphism induces an additive map on the central quotient and conjugates the adjoint factors by that map. Both factors have local center $R$, so they are identified up to a potential swap. A swap would give $B\cong (B')^{\mathrm{op}}$ and hence $A(M)\cong A(M')^{\mathrm{op}}$ by the corner argument below. The Peirce degree pattern excludes this. Thus restriction to the $B$ factor is canonical and multiplicative. In coordinates it can be computed by taking the adjoint whose relevant block is left multiplication by $b\in B$, conjugating by the given map on $B^2$, and evaluating the resulting left multiplication at $1$.
2. Align the image of $E_{11}$ with $E_{11}'$ by a unit in $B'$, then restrict to the corner. Such a unit exists. In the semisimple quotient, both idempotents have rank one in every $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ factor, so one first aligns them by a unit lifting a unit of this quotient. Idempotents that agree modulo the radical can be aligned by the explicit simultaneously conjugating unit from the two-idempotent families $(E_{11},1-E_{11})$. This argument applies also to an anti-isomorphism. For any two choices of aligning unit, their ratio centralizes $E_{11}'$, and its action on that corner is conjugation by a unit of $A(M')$. Hence the corner map $A(M)\to A(M')$ is well defined modulo target inner conjugation. To see that this choice is compatible with composition, take the second aligning unit multiplied by the image, under the second map, of the first aligning unit. This aligns the image of the original $E_{11}$ under the composite. All these choices can instead be computed by enumerating the elements of $B'$ and testing the aligning equation and invertibility in its table.
3. Conjugate vertex idempotents into the chosen family of $A(M')$. The labeled core vertices and ordered marked vertices are fixed by the Peirce graph. The local idempotent conjugation can be computed explicitly, since two complete families $f_i,e_i$ agreeing modulo the radical are aligned by the unit $\sum_i e_i f_i$. The iso then gives maps $X,Y,Z:M\to M'$ on the first, second and output data corners. The scalar triangles yield $Z=aX=bY$ with units $a,b\in R^\times$. If the scalar corner units on $m_1t_0,m_0t_1,m_1t_1$ are $u_1,v_1,w_1$, then comparison through $s_0m_1$ and $s_0t_1$ gives $a=u_1v_1/w_1$. If those on $s_0m_2,s_1m_0,s_1m_2$ are $u_2,v_2,w_2$, then comparison through $m_2t_0$ and $s_1t_0$ gives $b=u_2v_2/w_2$. The data product implies $Z(x\diamond y)=(ab)^{-1}(Zx\diamond' Zy)$, so the normalized map $F=(ab)^{-1}Z$ respects the operation and all the cyclic marks. Composing two aligned isomorphisms multiplies their two scalar ratios and composes their $Z$ maps, so it composes these normalized maps.
4. The normalized map is independent of target inner conjugations and of the chosen idempotent alignments. Inner conjugations act trivially on the product of residue fields of $A(M')$, so after aligning the same families the residual conjugation centralizes every vertex idempotent. It is given by diagonal corner units. Let their values at $s_0,m_0,t_0$ be $u,v,w$. This multiplies $X,Y,Z$ by $u/v,v/w,u/w$ respectively. Consequently the product $ab$ is multiplied by $u/w$, which cancels the change of $Z$. Other vertex scalars do not occur in the normalized map. The scalar ratios are unambiguous since $M$ has an $R$ summand, and thus no nonzero scalar annihilates it.

Together these steps give a computable composition-preserving map from auxiliary-group isomorphisms to marked isomorphisms of $M$. It is surjective. Given a marked isomorphism of $M$, act by that map on all seven $M$ slots and by the identity on the core scalar slots. The mark can scale by a unit on its representative, so adjust one of its incident scalar arrows by that unit. All other marked-arrow products vanish, so this extends to $A(M)$. Apply it entrywise to $B$ and then to the Heisenberg group. Its three core maps are equal and the normalized map is exactly the original map. Finally, §2 identifies the resulting marked transporters of $M$ with the intended exact transporters of $L$. In particular, the image of any generating set of $\operatorname{Aut}(U(M))$ generates the full automorphism group of the decorated kernel. A usual permutation-group order algorithm on the recovered permutations of $L$ counts precisely this last group. The order of the auxiliary group and its own automorphism count need not agree with those of the decorated kernel. [Furst–Hopcroft–Luks polynomial-time permutation-group algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1109/SFCS.1980.34)

## 4. Completing the reductions and class bounds

The $2s+1$ cyclic tags have total logarithmic order at most $O((1+s)\log_p N)$, and the module $M$ has order $p^{O((1+s)\log_p N)}$. In $A(M)$ there are a constant number of slots of module $M$, and a constant number of scalar slots of order $p^e$. For a mark of length $a$ its diagonal and its two neighboring corners contribute $3a$ to the logarithmic order, its hub edge contributes $a$, and its path edge costs at most $a$ when charged to the preceding mark. The $O(1+s)$ pendant slots contribute the same number of bounded-length terms. The group $U(M)$ has order $|A(M)|^{12}$, hence $N^{O(1+b)}$, and its Cayley table is constructible within this order bound raised to an absolute power.

For each candidate $y$ tuple, use the earlier unmarked class-two black-box theorem to decide feasibility and extract one auxiliary-group isomorphism. Apply §3 and the normal-form formula to recover a corresponding original isomorphism. For counting, the feasible set for each tuple is a torsor under the fixed decorated source automorphism group. Obtain its order once by applying the class-two generator theorem to the auxiliary group of the source with its original tuple. Sum that order over all feasible target tuples for an exact count. For automorphism generation, apply this to $H=G$. The decorated source automorphisms are exactly the stabilizer of the source lift tuple, and one transporter to each attainable tuple supplies coset representatives. Their union with the lifted stabilizer generators generates $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$.

The stated uniform query and time bounds follow from the $N^s$ tuples and polynomial overhead in the order of the auxiliary groups. For instance the unbounded-class odd family contains $(\mathbb Z/p^m\mathbb Z)^d\rtimes C_{p^{m-1}}$, with the cyclic generator acting by $1+p$ and $m\ge2$. The lower central terms from the second onward are $pA,p^2A,\ldots$, where $A=(\mathbb Z/p^m\mathbb Z)^d$. Its class is $m$, while $G/C_G(G')$ is cyclic. Arbitrarily large $d$ makes the group's generator rank unbounded even though the conjugation quotient has rank one. For $p=2$, the generalized dihedral groups with abelian kernel of exponent at least eight have a characteristic abelian $C_G(G')$ and cyclic quotient, and also realize unbounded class.

The theorem is a reduction to an unmarked class-two decision oracle with explicit size bounds. The November 2026 manuscript by Qiao and Zhang claims a polynomial-time algorithm for general Cayley-table group isomorphism and its full isomorphism coset. The argument above establishes the class-two reduction independently of that manuscript. [Qiao–Zhang manuscript](https://github.com/group-iso/group-iso.github.io)

This is a written proof. The companion finite controls test exact tag products, the normalization under conjugating units, and the extension calculation on small groups, including two $2$-groups with equal conjugation action and different lift powers.
