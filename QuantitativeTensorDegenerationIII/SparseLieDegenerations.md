# Sparse Lie brackets with exponential ramification and polynomial image degree

Fix any coefficient field \(k\), and write \(R=k[[t]]\) and \(K=k((t))\). The action on a Lie bracket is \(P\cdot\beta=P\beta(P^{-1}-,P^{-1}-)\). A Laurent witness is a bracket arc over \(R\) in this generic orbit over \(K\) with the specified special value. Polynomial image degree means the maximum degree of the bracket's structure coefficients in the fixed original coordinates.

**Theorem.** There are explicit split solvable Lie laws \(\beta_m^+,\beta_m^-\) of dimension \(M_m=O(m)\), with \(O(M_m)\) nonzero structure constants in \(\{1,-1\}\), and a fixed bracket coefficient direction \(E_m\) with two signed entries, such that:

- The line \(\beta_m^-+cE_m\) lies in the geometric orbit of \(\beta_m^+\) for \(c\ne0\). Over a field, the bracket on the line is isomorphic to \(\beta_m^+\) exactly if \(c\) is a \(2^{m-1}\)-st power in that field.
- Thus \(\beta_m^-+tE_m\) admits a lift over \(k((t^{1/e}))\) precisely when \(2^{m-1}\) divides \(e\).
- Any polynomial Lie-law arc from \(\beta_m^+\) to \(\beta_m^-\) admitting an unramified \(K\)-witness has degree at least \(2^{m-1}/O(M_m^3)\). There is an arc of degree \(2^{m-1}\) with a Laurent witness. Every change-of-basis witness also has symmetric pole depth \(\Omega(2^m)\), meaning that one of its matrix or inverse entries has a pole of that order. A monomial witness has symmetric pole depth \(O(2^m)\).

The derived ideal of each law is two-step nilpotent. More generally, the construction transfers every integer contact lattice from the earlier sparse tensor realization to such Lie laws, with linear overhead in the number of vertices and edges. The same coordinate-line equations over the original field hold for multiple marked coefficients.

## 1. Lie laws and intrinsic labels in all characteristics

The input to the construction is a subdivided, capped simplicial complex as in the sparse multiplication-table construction, with a total order on its vertices refining barycentric rank. Its old edge set contains any added successor edges, whose products with other edges vanish. Write \(\{1,\ldots,v\}\) for the old vertices in their chosen order. The law has vertex idempotents \(e_i\) and scalar corners \(p_{ij}\) for edges. It is the rank-three Peirce multiplication table: products \(p_{ij}p_{j\ell}=a_{ij\ell}p_{i\ell}\) arise exactly on flag faces, with coefficient \(1\) in the source and coefficient \(0\) on the marked faces in the target. For given first and last vertices of a flag, there are two possible middle vertices. The allowed marked faces leave at least one of these two products nonzero. This is true for the single puncture and for the separate punctured caps in the contact-lattice construction. [Sparse seed](SparseSeed.md) · [Contact lattices](ContactSeed.md)

Adjoin a new root vertex \(o\) and auxiliary vertices \(c_1,\ldots,c_v\), all placed before the old vertices in the total order, with \(o\) first and the \(c_i\)'s increasing. Add edges from \(o\) to \(c_1\) of corner dimension \(4\), from \(o\) to each other \(c_i\) of dimension \(2\), and from \(o\) to each old vertex \(i\) of dimension \(3\). Add a single arrow \(c_i\to c_{i+1}\) for each \(1\le i<v\) and \(c_i\to i\) for each \(i\). Every product of positive-degree edges involving a new arrow vanishes. All arrow corners have the corresponding idempotent left and right action. This is again associative, because existing nonzero products of arrows have ranks \(0,1,2\) in succession, and any product of three arrows is zero. Dimensions and numbers of nonzero entries grow by \(O(v)\).

Take the commutator bracket of this algebra. Its derived ideal \(J=[L,L]\) is exactly the arrow span, including every parallel arrow. Indeed \([e_i,p_{ij}]=p_{ij}\), and no bracket leaves the arrow span. Also \([J,J]=J^2\) equals the span of the face endpoints, because reverse products of composable arrows are zero. The dimension and based subspace of this second ideal are the same at the two endpoints by the condition on the marked faces. We use \(W=J/[J,J]\) and \(Z=[J,J]\). The bracket \(\gamma:W\otimes W\to Z\) is well-defined because \([J,Z]=0\).

Put \(H=L/J\). Its adjoint actions on \(W,Z\) are well-defined since lifts differing by \(J\) induce the same actions, and \(H\) is abelian. In the written basis the nonzero weights on \(W\oplus Z\) are the covectors
\[
\lambda_{ij}=e_i^*-e_j^* .
\]
Different edge corners have distinct weights, including in characteristic two, because at most one oriented corner is used for each unordered pair. The weights \(\eta_x=e_o^*-e_x^*\) on the root edges are recognizable by their multiplicities \(2,3,4\), distinguishing the auxiliary vertices, the old vertices, and \(c_1\). For two distinct root weights, a non-root edge weight is equal up to sign to their difference exactly when it has those two endpoints. Thus the multiplicity-two and multiplicity-four weights see an unoriented path starting at the distinguished \(c_1\). That identifies each \(c_i\). The relations with the multiplicity-three weights see the unique matching \(c_i\to i\), fixing all old vertices. Once their labels are fixed, the endpoint difference identifies each old edge weight. This argument works in characteristic two, where only the unordered endpoint relation is used. The extra arrow spaces have no bearing on the face brackets.

## 2. Reading contact from every arbitrary change of basis

For two laws with the written structure, a Lie isomorphism induces a bijection on the weight spaces in \(W\oplus Z\), respecting all the displayed weight relations. Hence it fixes all their old edge labels. On those one-dimensional spaces its maps are scalars \(\eta_{ij}\). In particular a marked product coefficient on a coordinate-line law is \(\eta_{i\ell}/(\eta_{ij}\eta_{j\ell})\). On retained faces this ratio is \(1\). Consequently the inverse edge scalars define a multiplicative cocycle on the punctured complex, whose values on the marked boundaries are the marked coefficients. Conversely any such cocycle gives the exact coordinate-line law by the diagonal Lie basis change using those inverse cochain values on edges and the identity on vertices and added arrow spaces. This is the same spanning-tree description of cocycles as for the sparse multiplication tensors.

Now consider an arbitrary Laurent witness, with the written special Lie law. In its integral free module let \(J_R\) be the span of brackets and \(Z_R\) the span of its brackets with itself. Both spaces are free direct summands and specialize as required, since the ranks at the two endpoints agree. To see this, choose columns for each image with a unit minor. Eliminating their block leaves no residual matrix block by generic rank. The quotient \(H_R\) and \(W_R\) are free. The iterated identity \([J_R,Z_R]=0\) holds since it holds over \(K\). The adjoint actions of \(H_R\) on \(W_R\oplus Z_R\) are therefore defined and commute.

Extend the coefficient field, if needed, to make it infinite. This preserves the valuations, pole bounds, and polynomial degrees of every witness over the original field. Choose a special element of \(H\) at which its finitely many distinct written weights have pairwise distinct evaluations, and lift it to \(H_R\). Let \(T\) be its action on \(W_R\oplus Z_R\). All its generic eigenvalues are in \(K\), since the generic bracket is isomorphic over \(K\) to the written source. They lie in \(R\) by the monic characteristic polynomial. There are at most as many distinct generic eigenvalues as written weights, and at least that many because their reductions include all the distinct special evaluations. The eigenvalues therefore match bijectively to the special weights with differences that are units. The corresponding spectral projectors, obtained by Lagrange interpolation on \(T\), are integral. They split \(W_R,Z_R\) into free direct summands of the expected ranks. The other \(H_R\) actions are scalar on those summands because this is true over \(K\) and the chosen bases are integral.

The generic-to-special matching on weight labels preserves each additive relation from the source, by reduction of the scalar covectors. In particular the non-root edges between root labels match to edges of the corresponding endpoints. The numbers of edges in the two finite diagrams are equal, so these relations give a bijection of the labeled incidence diagrams. The path and matching reconstruction from Section 1 now pins every old edge label. This uses the fixed dimensions of both \(W\) and \(Z\) and the distinct generic eigenvalues established above. Choose integral bases for the weight spaces reducing to the chosen special bases. In these frames an arbitrary generic isomorphism acts on each old edge line by some \(\eta_{ij}\in K^\times\). Its bracket transport equation gives the face coefficient ratio used above and specializes to the correct coefficient on every face. The old edge weights
\[
w_{ij}=-v_t(\eta_{ij})
\]
are an integral cocycle on the punctured complex. The marked coefficients have positive valuations, given by the evaluation on their oriented boundaries. Changing any integral frame multiplies such a coefficient by a unit. Conversely every integral cocycle whose evaluations are positive is realized by a diagonal law arc using monomial edge scalings.

For the terminal-cap telescope, the first homology of the punctured complex is cyclic generated by \(C_1\), and the punctured boundary is \(\pm 2^{m-1}[C_1]\). Thus the marked contact is a positive multiple of \(2^{m-1}\). The scalar powers and the exact ramification assertion follow from the multiplicative cocycle computation, including over finite coefficient fields. The diagonal witness obtained from a primitive integral cocycle gives a degree-\(2^{m-1}\) arc. For the symmetric pole assertion let \(b\) bound both the change of basis and its inverse. The integral frames of \(J_R,Z_R\) and their quotients bound the entries and inverse entries of each induced line map by \(b\), because the induced maps are blocks of maps in integral adapted frames. In particular \(|w_{ij}|\le b\). Along the six edges of the subdivided terminal cycle \(C_m\), the circulation has absolute value at least \(2^{m-1}\). Therefore \(6b\ge2^{m-1}\). For the matching upper bound, use the block cocycles from the sparse seed. Extend a primitive initial circulation through the same handle blocks, where weights grow by doubling. Use the fixed punctured-cap pattern at the terminal circle, multiplied by \(2^{m-1}\). These cochains agree on the common circle after a coboundary adjustment as in the seed construction and have all entries \(O(2^m)\).

For the general contact-lattice construction, evaluating the cocycle on the marked boundaries gives exactly the positive part of the specified integer lattice. Realizability of every integer cochain and every multiplicative cochain on those homology generators follows by normalizing on a tree. This establishes the general transfer.

## 3. Bounding contact using only the polynomial Lie-law coordinates

The weight generators found above may be power series of large complexity. The following computation detects the required contact without using polynomial expressions for their individual entries.

For this part let \(Q\) be the number of distinct weights of \(W\oplus Z\). Use the single action \(T\) from Section 2 and define a linear map
\[
\mathcal B:W_R\otimes W_R\longrightarrow (Z_R)^Q,\qquad
x\otimes y\longmapsto \big(\gamma(T^j x,y)\big)_{0\le j<Q}.
\]
In integral weight bases, any ordered flag bracket has a single output edge \((i,\ell)\) and a tag vector \((1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{Q-1})\), where \(\xi\) is the \(T\)-eigenvalue of its first edge. The \(Q\) tag vectors are an integral basis, by the Vandermonde determinant and pairwise distinct residual eigenvalues. For a given output and first edge, there is at most one nonzero second-edge input. The two orders of each flag bracket give different first-edge tags. All unmarked columns have unit coefficients, and the two columns for the omitted flag have coefficients of valuation at least \(2^{m-1}\). Thus the ideal of maximal-rank minors of \(\mathcal B\) has valuation at least \(2\cdot2^{m-1}\). The rank is twice the number of flags, which is at most \(4M_m\), since each face endpoint has at most two flag inputs. This calculation uses two distinct ordered tensor-product columns even in characteristic two.

Suppose the original Lie-law arc has degree \(D\), and set \(M=M_m\). Say a rational array has height \(h\) if a common polynomial denominator, a unit modulo \(t\), and every numerator have degree at most \(h\). Choose an adapted integral basis by first choosing columns for \(Z_R\) from brackets of bracket columns, then adding columns for \(J_R\), then standard vectors. The original bracket columns have degree at most \(D\), and brackets of two such columns have degree at most \(3D\). The adapted change-of-basis matrix has degree at most \(3D\) and is invertible at zero. The adjugate formula gives its inverse height at most \(3MD\). The bracket in this adapted basis has height at most \(h=(3M+7)D\). The action \(T\) and the bracket \(\gamma\) are subarrays or constant linear combinations of this same array, and share its denominator. Therefore the array of \(\mathcal B\) has common denominator of degree at most \(Qh\) and each numerator of degree at most \(Qh\): its component with \(T^j\) uses \(j+1\) factors of the same denominator. In particular any nonzero maximal-rank minor has numerator of degree at most \(4M Q (3M+7)D\), and its denominator is a unit modulo \(t\). The valuation of that numerator is at most its degree. Combining this with the intrinsic ideal of maximal minors gives
\[
D\ \ge\ \frac{2\cdot 2^{m-1}}{4M^2(3M+7)}.
\]
The argument also lower-bounds the image degree of any polynomial matrix presentation after normalization to degree zero. The result concerns Laurent lifts in a specified orbit, with the same distinction between geometric membership and a witness over the original Laurent field as in the sparse tensor construction.

## Verification and context

These are written proofs. The construction reuses the earlier sparse telescope with one deleted face, the contact lattice of that puncture, and valuation recovery from integral filtrations. The new pieces are the linear-size and characteristic-independent Lie weight labels and the Vandermonde-tagged bracket calculation. The Lie orbit action is conjugation on a bracket law by one change of basis. It is distinct from either change of basis in several independent tensor modes or degeneration from a diagonal rank source. For the general relationship between isomorphism questions on tensor and algebra laws, see [Grochow–Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309). The usual DVR formulation for Lie-law degenerations allows a function field or a finite extension. The quantitative distinction here fixes \(k((t))\) and measures the required ramification if the displayed line is used. [Burde, §1.3](https://am-brno.math.muni.cz/07-5/burde.pdf)
