# Weisfeiler–Leman for unique direct product decompositions

Fix \(d\geq 1\). Let \(G\) be a finite group of order \(n\) whose fully refined direct product decomposition is unique as a set of subgroups and whose indecomposable factors are at most \(d\)-generated. Against an arbitrary finite group of order \(n\), counting group Weisfeiler–Leman Version II identifies \(G\) in constant dimension and a constant number of rounds. Count-free Version II identifies \(G\) in constant dimension and \(O(\log\log n)\) rounds. All constants depend only on \(d\). This proves both extensions asked for in Johnson–Levet–Vojtěchovský–Widholm Remark 1.4. A further consequence in Section 4 is deterministic logspace recognition and canonization of this class from Cayley tables. [Open question and original bounds](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06478#S1)

## 1. A direct factor test with a constant number of variables

Fix a tuple \(\mathbf{s}\) of \(d\) elements, put \(S=\langle\mathbf{s}\rangle\), and put \(C=C_X(S)\) for an ambient finite group \(X\). Suppose \(X=SC\), so \(Z(S)=S\cap C\). Then \(S\) is a direct factor of \(X\) if and only if \(Z(S)\) injects into \(B=C/[C,C]\) and its image \(A\) is a pure subgroup of \(B\). Here purity of a subgroup of a finite abelian group means \(A\cap B^k=A^k\) for all positive integers \(k\).

For the forward implication a direct complement \(R\) gives \(C=Z(S)\times R\), and hence \(B=Z(S)\times R/[R,R]\). For the converse, write \(B/A\) as a direct product of cyclic groups. A lift \(y\) of a generator of a cyclic quotient factor of order \(k\) satisfies \(y^k\in A\cap B^k=A^k\). Multiplying \(y\) by the inverse of a suitable element of \(A\) gives a lift whose order divides \(k\). The cyclic lifts give a section \(B/A\to B\) because \(B\) is abelian. Thus \(A\) is a direct summand and there is a retraction \(C\to Z(S)\) induced by the projection \(B\to A\). Its kernel \(R\) satisfies \(C=Z(S)\times R\) and \(X=S\times R\).

By Nikolov–Segal, choose \(w=w(d)\geq 1\) such that every element of the derived subgroup of any finite \(d\)-generated group is a product of at most \(w\) commutators. The same bound holds for an arbitrary finite direct product of such groups, since expressions in separate coordinates can be padded by identity commutators and evaluated coordinatewise. It still holds after adjoining an abelian direct factor. Consequently, if \(S\) is one of the indecomposable factors of the promised group \(G\), the centralizer \(C_G(S)\) has commutator width at most \(w\). [Nikolov–Segal, Theorem 1.7](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2007/165-1/p05)

For a candidate tuple in any ambient group \(X\) of order \(n\), let \(W\) be the set of products of \(w\) commutators of elements of \(C\). Each of the following tests uses only a constant number of variables and constant quantifier rank, with constants depending on \(d,w\).

- The group \(S\) is nontrivial and directly indecomposable. Its marked isomorphism type is available in the initial color of Version II.
- \(X=SC\). Express this by \(\forall x\,\exists a\in S\,\exists b\in C\) with \(x=ab\). Membership in \(S\) is determined by the initial color of the tuple augmented by the candidate, and membership in \(C\) is the conjunction of commutation with the marked generators.
- The set \(W\) is closed under multiplication. Each membership test for \(W\) uses \(2w\) existentially quantified elements centralizing \(S\) and one group-word equation. The set contains the identity and all commutators and is closed under inverses, so this test gives \(W=[C,C]\).
- For all \(z\in S\cap C\), if \(z\in W\) then \(z=1\). This ensures injectivity of \(Z(S)\to B\).
- For each \(1\leq k\leq n\), for all \(z\in S\cap C\) and \(y\in C\), if \(z^{-1}y^k\in W\), then there exists \(a\in S\cap C\) with \(z=a^k\). The conjunction over \(k\) preserves quantifier rank. It suffices because the exponent of \(B\) divides \(n\), and these conditions assert exactly the needed purity when \(W=[C,C]\).

Call a subgroup **certified** if some \(d\)-tuple generating it passes these tests. Every certified subgroup of any \(X\) is a nontrivial indecomposable direct factor. Every indecomposable direct factor of the promised \(G\) is certified, by the width bound and the direct factor test. Uniqueness means that these are exactly the factors of its single decomposition. Indeed, every indecomposable direct factor can be extended by a fully refined decomposition of a complement.

The use of marked types here has the following direct game interpretation. On any pebbled tuple, corresponding marked types agree only if the coordinate map extends to an isomorphism of the generated subgroups. All word equations, membership in the subgroup generated by a subtuple, and properties of the isomorphism type of that subtuple are consequently determined before any further move. Starting from these tests, a formula of bounded variable count and quantifier rank is preserved if Duplicator survives the corresponding number of moves with the requisite pebbles. This follows by induction on formulas. Boolean operations cost no moves, an ordinary quantifier costs one move, and a counting quantifier costs one bijective move. The usual translation between these games and the Version II refinements changes the dimension by at most a constant. [Version II and pebble games](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06478#S2)

## 2. How the factors fit together

For distinct certified subgroups \(S,T\) of \(G\), the groups commute elementwise and \(\operatorname{Hom}(S,Z(T))=0\). For the second claim, a nonzero homomorphism \(f\) would let us replace \(S\) by its graph \(\{s f(s):s\in S\}\) in the product with \(T\). The graph is isomorphic to \(S\), commutes with \(T\), has trivial intersection with \(T\), and together with \(T\) generates \(ST\). This would give a different fully refined decomposition. These properties for an ordered pair of certified subgroups are visible with a constant number of variables and rounds. Apply the certificate to two tuples, then inspect the marked subgroup generated by their concatenation. That marked group determines whether the two subgroups agree, whether they commute, and the isomorphism types needed to evaluate the homomorphism condition.

Consider now an arbitrary comparison group \(H\) of the same order. If distinct certified subgroups violate either pairwise condition, the preceding constant-rank test distinguishes it from \(G\). Otherwise its certified subgroups \(T_1,\ldots,T_u\) form an internal direct product. To see this, for each \(i\) let \(\rho_i:H\to T_i\) be a projection obtained from some direct complement of \(T_i\). If \(j\ne i\), commutation shows that \(\rho_i(T_j)\subseteq Z(T_i)\), and the homomorphism condition forces \(\rho_i(T_j)=1\). Thus the product map from all the \(T_i\) into \(H\) is injective, as applying \(\rho_i\) recovers each coordinate separately. It is a homomorphism because the factors commute.

## 3. Counting and count-free identification

For each isomorphism type \(L\) of a certified subgroup, membership of \(x\) in some certified subgroup of type \(L\) is a constant-variable, constant-rank test. Existentially mark a generating tuple, test membership of \(x\) in its span, test its type, and apply the certificate. In either group, nonidentity members of different certified factors are disjoint. If there are \(r_L\) factors of type \(L\), the number of nonidentity elements passing this membership test is exactly \(r_L(|L|-1)\). Counting compares these numbers in constant rounds. Equality for every type implies that the certified product in \(H\) has exactly the order of \(G\) and the same multiset of factor types. This exhausts \(H\) and implies \(G\cong H\).

For count-free identification, let \(P_1^L(x)\) mean that \(x=1\) or that \(x\) lies in a certified subgroup of type \(L\). Put \(P_0^L(x)\) equal to \(x=1\). For \(j\geq 2\), define \(P_j^L(x)\) by
\[
 \exists a,b\ \bigl(x=ab\ \wedge\ P_{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}^L(a)\ \wedge\ P_{\lceil j/2\rceil}^L(b)\bigr).
\]
This expresses membership in the set of products of at most \(j\) elements from the type-\(L\) factors. Variable names in the two recursive subformulas are reused, since each subformula has only one free variable. Three names suffice for the recursion itself, along with the fixed supply needed for \(P_1^L\). Rank increases by at most \(2\lceil\log_2 j\rceil\) above the constant base. In the certified internal direct product, \(P_j^L(x)\) holds exactly when \(x\) has nonidentity coordinates in at most \(j\) type-\(L\) factors and is trivial elsewhere. In particular, \(\exists x\,(P_j^L(x)\land\neg P_{j-1}^L(x))\) holds exactly when \(r_L\geq j\). Each certified subgroup has order at least two, so there are at most \(\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor\) of them. Comparing type multiplicities therefore requires only \(O(\log\log n)\) rounds, and once again equality forces isomorphism. The trivial group is identified by its order.

## 4. Deterministic logspace recognition and canonization

The factor certificate also gives a deterministic logspace algorithm that recognizes the stated class, outputs its unique fully refined direct product decomposition on positive instances, and computes a canonical labeling. The input is a finite Cayley table.

Membership in the subgroup generated by any tuple of constant length reduces to undirected reachability in a graph on the group elements with edges for right multiplication by a tuple element or its inverse. Reingold's logspace reachability algorithm therefore implements this membership test. Direct indecomposability of a tuple-generated subgroup with at most \(d\) generators is also decidable in logspace. If it decomposes as a nontrivial direct product, each factor is an image of the subgroup under projection and so is at most \(d\)-generated. Enumerate in logspace pairs of generating tuples contained in the subgroup and test that their subgroups are nontrivial, commute, have trivial intersection, and multiply to cover the subgroup. Every variable in each certificate test can likewise be enumerated with a fixed number of element counters. Thus the set of certified subgroups can be enumerated without repeats by taking the first generating tuple of each subgroup in the input order. Equality of generated subgroups is tested by mutual membership of generators. [Reingold, undirected reachability](https://doi.org/10.1145/1391289.1391291)

Check commutation and the vanishing of cross homomorphisms for all ordered pairs of distinct certified subgroups. The latter condition is equivalent to coprimality of \(|S/[S,S]|\) and \(|Z(T)|\). Necessity follows from the factorization of homomorphisms through abelianization and Lagrange's theorem. If a prime divides both orders, a quotient of order that prime in the first group and a cyclic subgroup of that order in the second give a nontrivial map. Both orders are found by counting with fixed-space counters. Use the width \(w\) for membership in \([S,S]\), since \(S\) is \(d\)-generated. If the pair tests succeed, the certified subgroups form a direct product by Section 2. Check that the product of their orders equals \(n\). If all checks pass, this is a fully refined decomposition. The Remak–Krull–Schmidt theorem makes every factor in any other fully refined decomposition at most \(d\)-generated, and the corresponding complement is again a product of such factors. Section 1 then certifies every such factor. So all alternative factors occur in our already enumerated direct product, which forces the same decomposition. Conversely, the class promise ensures every check succeeds. [Remak–Krull–Schmidt](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06478#S3)

For canonization, first compute a canonical labeling of each individual factor \(S\). For a fixed generating \(d\)-tuple, a universal traversal word depends only on the order of \(S\) and the formal generator slots. Follow it in the Cayley graph starting at the identity and label each element by its first-visit rank. The word can be obtained from Reingold's Theorem 5.4 on the Cayley graph with inverse slots, or from its connected simple cubic expansion with a cycle of slots and an inverse matching between two layers. First occurrences and ranks can be computed by rescanning the polynomial-length word: to rank an element, count the elements whose first occurrence comes earlier. This uses only a constant number of element, position, and count registers. The resulting labeled table depends only on the marked subgroup isomorphism type. Among all generating \(d\)-tuples, choose one with lexicographically least labeled table by comparing entries on demand. That table is a complete canonical form for \(S\). [Reingold, Theorem 5.4](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/sl.pdf)

Order the factors by their canonical tables, breaking identical-table ties by their first input tuple. For an input element \(x\), find its coordinate \(s\) in a factor \(S\) by testing whether \(s^{-1}x\) lies in the product of all other factors. Use undirected reachability from the identity in the Cayley graph whose allowed multipliers are the union of certified factors other than \(S\). Its adjacency is computed in logspace by enumerating certified factors and checking membership. The number of nested calls to logspace subroutines is fixed. Output the mixed-radix label of \(x\) in the ordered list of factor labelings. A label fits in \(O(\log n)\) bits and the factor order can be navigated by rescanning and comparing on demand. The labeled table is the componentwise product of the sorted canonical factor tables. Ties occur only for identical labeled tables, so the resulting labeled table is independent of those tie choices. This decreases the bound for this class from the uniform \(\mathsf{AC}^3\) canonization of Theorem 1.8 to deterministic logspace. [Prior canonization bound](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06478#S1)

The results above are paper proofs.
