# The matrix-vector query threshold for Gaussian linear systems

The paragraph following [Simons Workshop Problem 2.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05394v3#S2) asks whether the matrix-vector query complexity of a random linear system is Ω(n). This note gives the sharp constant-fraction threshold for relative residual with independent Gaussian entries and an exact-query alphabet distinction. The model allows adaptive exact products with A, with no entry or A-transpose oracle. The arithmetic-time question in Problem 2.8 remains separate. The recent [smoothed-analysis paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18545) already identifies a linear obstruction for Gaussian forward accuracy and studies related worst-case residual bounds.

For each n let A have independent standard normal entries and let b be fixed and nonzero. Fix 0<ε<1. If the query budget q satisfies q/n≤1−ε²−γ for a fixed γ>0, the probability of returning x with ‖Ax−b‖≤ε‖b‖ is exp(−Ω_(ε,γ)(n)). If q/n≥1−ε²+γ, least squares using the first q columns succeeds with probability 1−exp(−Ω_(ε,γ)(n)). Probabilities include private randomness. Consequently the critical query fraction is exactly 1−ε² away from an o(n) transition window.

## Deferred Gaussian columns

Condition on private randomness, absorbed into the transcript. Orthogonalize each new query against the previous queries. If the orthogonal component is nonzero its response can be recovered by subtracting known products and dividing by its length. If the component vanishes no new measurement occurs. Equivalently simulate with q orthonormal inputs, padding with arbitrary determined orthogonal vectors if necessary and allowing the output to see the padded responses.

After revealing A on the input span V, its restriction to V-perpendicular is an independent standard Gaussian matrix in any transcript-selected orthonormal basis. This follows by induction. Conditional on the past, an adaptive normalized next direction is deterministic, and rotating the unexposed Gaussian block to this direction separates its first column from the remaining independent Gaussian columns. In particular the q exposed responses to the orthonormal inputs are independent standard Gaussian n-vectors. Their span L is a uniform q-dimensional subspace of the output space.

The squared distance d² from b to L, after division by ‖b‖², has the distribution X/(X+Y) for independent chi-square X and Y with n−q and q degrees of freedom, respectively. This follows by normalizing a standard Gaussian vector to obtain the uniform direction on a sphere and projecting onto fixed complementary subspaces. The cases q=0,n are deterministic. For every fixed γ>0 the ratio differs from 1−q/n by more than γ/2 with probability exp(−Ω_γ(n)), by Chernoff bounds for the two sums of squares. The constants are uniform in q because these bounds can be applied to additive deviations of order n.

## Lower and upper bounds

After the transcript, any chosen output decomposes as x=x_V+x_perp. Its residual is m+σg, where m=Ax_V−b has norm at least d, σ=‖x_perp‖, and g has conditional law N(0,I_n). If σ=0 and d>ε‖b‖ success is impossible. Otherwise success implies that the Gaussian direction g/‖g‖ is in the spherical cap around −m whose angular sine is at most ε‖b‖/‖m‖. This is because the closest point on the ray in that direction must be within ε‖b‖ of −m. On d²≥(ε²+γ/2)‖b‖² the cap has angular radius bounded strictly below π/2. A uniform sphere direction falls in such a cap with probability exponentially small in n. For example represent it as a normalized Gaussian and use the one-dimensional Gaussian tail together with concentration of its squared norm. This proves the lower bound. Conversely, query q fixed orthonormal directions and choose x in their span with Ax the orthogonal projection of b onto L. It attains residual d exactly, yielding the upper bound.

## Bernoulli and Rademacher entries

With unrestricted exact real arithmetic, one product suffices to recover the entire matrix when all entries lie in {0,1}. Query x=(1,2,…,2^(n−1)). The binary expansion of the i-th output entry is exactly row i, recovered by comparisons and subtractions of successive powers of two. For entries in {−1,1}, add Σ x_j to each output and divide by two before decoding. On invertible inputs the recovered matrix can then be solved exactly. The query uses entries with up to n bits and does not establish a faster arithmetic solver.
