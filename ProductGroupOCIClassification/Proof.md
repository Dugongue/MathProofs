# Tensor orbit-closure intersection for products of general linear groups

Informal proof. The input is a pair of rational tensor tuples of the same shape, and orbit closures are over C. Fix any finite signature of ordered mixed-tensor slots for a product of general linear groups. The dimensions of the defining spaces are arbitrary input parameters. A port has a color (the defining space) and a sign (primal or dual). Write a_{ij}, b_{ij} for the numbers of primal and dual ports of color j in slot i.

## Classification

Delete slots i for which no nonnegative integer multiplicity vector c has c_i>0 and sum_i c_i(a_{ij}-b_{ij})=0 for every color j. Call remaining slots active. Test this linear feasibility system:

    0 <= p_j <= 1,
    sum_j [p_j a_{ij} + (1-p_j) b_{ij}] <= 1   for every active slot i.    (1)

If (1) is infeasible, the orbit-closure intersection problem is TOCI-complete by a rational affine reduction valid on all complex inputs.

If (1) is feasible, form the following finite grammar. Its states are signed colors, representing one free port. For every active slot and choice of one free port of type s, the grammar has a production of type s whose children match the complements of the other port types. A leaf production comes from a one-port slot. Delete unproductive states and productions using them. The remaining dependency digraph points from a production's output state to its child states.

- If some production has at least two children in the strongly connected component of its output, the problem is equivalent under deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions to EqSLP, the zero test for division-free integer straight-line programs.
- Otherwise the problem is in deterministic polynomial bit time.

Thus every fixed signature is either TOCI-complete or in coRP. In the deterministic interpretation of [Lysikov–Walter, Question I.12](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04639v2#S1.SS4), their proposed dichotomy holds if and only if EqSLP is in P or is itself TOCI-complete. This last equivalence makes no separation assumption.

## Contractions and the linear program

A contraction diagram is a multigraph with vertex labels given by slots. Each occurrence has precisely the ordered ports of its slot. Edges match two ports of the same color and opposite signs. All ports are matched, allowing loops and parallel edges. The contraction invariants span the invariant ring as a vector space, and equality on all of them characterizes orbit-closure intersection. See [Lysikov–Walter, Theorems II.7 and IV.23](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04639v2). Inactive slots cannot occur in a contraction. Assigning zero to them or omitting them leaves the problem unchanged. Zero-port slots can be compared directly.

If there is a solution of (1), charge p_j to a primal endpoint of color j and 1-p_j to a dual endpoint of that color. Each edge costs one in total, and each vertex costs at most one. Hence every connected component has at most as many edges as vertices and is a tree or has exactly one cycle (allowing a loop as a cycle of length one).

Conversely, assume the system infeasible and there are active slots. Let f_i(p) be the left side of the i-th slot constraint. The minimum of max_i f_i(p) on the cube is greater than 1. Finite linear programming duality, or minimax on the simplex of slot mixtures, gives nonnegative c_i summing to 1 such that min_p sum_i c_i f_i(p)>1. The minimum separates by color and equals

    sum_j min(sum_i c_i a_{ij}, sum_i c_i b_{ij}).

One can take the c_i rational by strictness and continuity. Scale to integers with strict inequality against sum_i c_i. Make c_i occurrences of each slot and join as many opposite ports of each color as possible. This partial diagram has more edges than vertices. For each active slot choose one balancing multiplicity vector witnessing activity and sum all these vectors. The sum is balanced and strictly positive on active slots. A large integer multiple dominates the current occurrence counts. Add the missing vertices and complete the remaining pairings, which is possible because counts balance and each existing edge consumes one of each sign. Some connected component of the partial diagram had more edges than vertices. Its completion still does, since adding the other vertices of the completed component requires at least one additional edge for each such vertex. This gives a connected full diagram with at least two independent cycles. Consequently (1) is equivalent to the assertion that all full diagrams have only tree and unicycle components.

## TOCI-completeness from a multicyclic diagram

### A diagram with unique role assignment

Fix a connected full diagram D with more edges than vertices. A port-preserving cover is a graph map preserving slot labels and the two ordered port names at each edge. Local bijectivity means each port at a vertex maps to the corresponding port at its image. Think of a port name as recording both the slot and its position. A loop has two separate port names at its single vertex.

Starting with the partition of vertices by slot label, refine it by recording for each port the mate's port name and the current class of the vertex of that mate. Iterate until stable. In the quotient D_0, a port of a class has a well-defined mate, and following that mate back gives the original port. There are no fixed ports under this involution, since signs are opposite. Quotient vertices still have exactly the ports of their slot. Hence D -> D_0 is a port-preserving cover. For each edge the fibers of its endpoints are in bijection by the port matching, including when the edge is a loop. Connectedness then gives a constant cover degree, so the positive difference |E|-|V| remains positive in D_0.

Distinct vertices of D_0 are distinguishable by their finite port observations: after at most the number of refinement rounds, two distinct classes have different observations. These observations consist of the initial slot and the successively observed mate port names and slot labels along finite port walks. Each cover preserves every such observation. It follows that a diagram admits at most one port-preserving cover to D_0, component by component: each of its vertices has at most one possible image. This concerns the ordered port structure, so graph symmetries that permute ports do not create additional maps.

### Encode a tensor with two independent pairs of ports

Take a spanning tree of D_0 and cut two distinct non-tree edges e_1,e_2. They provide four free ports, each with the sign and name it had before cutting. Give e_i the lane U_i for i=1,2. Give each other non-tree edge the one-dimensional trivial lane. The source tensor A lies in End(U_1) tensor End(U_2), with its four factors ordered to match the four free ports. The source acts by H=GL(U_1) times GL(U_2).

Root the spanning tree at any vertex. At a nonroot vertex v, every port other than its parent port already has an assigned representation once child edges have been processed. Let R_v be the ordered tensor product of those port representations. Choose the parent edge lane so that its port at v carries R_v^* and its port at the parent carries R_v. This simply means using R_v or R_v^* as the lane according to the physical signs. Put the canonical coevaluation in R_v tensor R_v^* at v, permuted into its port positions. At the root put A in the four surviving factors with trivial factors filled by 1, reshaped according to the same tree routing. Every nonroot tensor is fixed by H. Contracting the tree gives A, with one-dimensional factors on uncut non-tree ports. This follows by induction using the identity obtained from evaluating a coevaluation across its dual lane.

For a target color, take the direct sum of the lanes of all template edges of that color (add a trivial line if there are none). At each slot output the sum of the tensors of all template vertices with that slot label, placed in the blocks prescribed by their incident lanes. Call this affine map F. Its coefficients are rational in the chosen bases, with only polynomially many output entries: the diagram and all port counts are fixed, and all lane dimensions are products of a bounded number of the input dimensions. The blockwise action of H on the lanes makes F equivariant for a homomorphism into the target product of general linear groups. Equivariance and continuity preserve orbit-closure intersection.

For reflection take any full contraction of a number of copies of A. For every copy start with a copy of D_0 cut along e_1,e_2. Join its four free ports to the other copies by exactly that source contraction. For both pairs, the opposite physical signs are paired with each other. The resulting target diagram E covers D_0 by forgetting the copy indices, even if e_i is a loop or E is disconnected. Expand its evaluation on F(A) into choices of one template role for each vertex. Evaluation between different lanes is zero, since dual blocks annihilate the other direct summands. A term can therefore survive only if across every edge the chosen roles are the two specified endpoint roles of the same lane with their correct port names. Those choices constitute a port-preserving cover E -> D_0. The preceding uniqueness gives exactly the intended term. Routing the spanning trees and contracting trivial edges now gives exactly the chosen source contraction of A. Hence every source contraction is recovered by a target invariant, which proves reflection by the invariant criterion.

### Completeness of the source with one four-port tensor

Lysikov–Walter Theorem V.11 gives TOCI-completeness for three labeled copies of End(U) tensor End(V) under GL(U) times GL(V). Here is a direct reduction to one copy, so the proof does not require a prior single-copy completeness claim.

Double both spaces. On the doubled U let D_U be diag(I,-I), and let S_U swap its summands. Define D_V,S_V similarly. In the single output tensor place the fixed terms

    D_U tensor I + I tensor D_V + S_U tensor D_V + D_U tensor S_V.

For each of the three input tensors use a different block direction in which both color indices move between their two summands. There are four such directions. Identify the corresponding blocks with End(U) tensor End(V), and add the three data blocks. The construction is equivariant for the diagonal copies of the original group.

From the output Y, the normalized partial trace over each doubled space recovers the D on the other space. Every data block is off-diagonal in both colors and has zero partial trace on either. The normalized partial trace over doubled V of (I tensor D_V)Y, minus I, gives S_U. The same calculation exchanging colors gives S_V. The trace of either D or S is zero and the trace of their product is zero; each D squares to I. These are equivariant polynomial readouts with rational dimension factors. Using the projections (I ± D)/2 and multiplication by S, one extracts each of the three designated off-diagonal blocks of Y and moves its source and target to the first summand of each color. The recovered operators are exactly the inputs embedded with zero outside those first summands. Every invariant contraction of the original triple therefore lifts to an invariant polynomial in Y by using these readouts, and its recovered value is unchanged by zero extension. This proves reflection. Equivariance proves preservation. [Source theorem and reduction model](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04639v2).

## The pseudoforest cases

Let E_s be the defining space or its dual for the signed color s, so E_s^*=E_{s*}. Put D=sum_s dim(E_s). The grammar in the statement describes exactly all trees with one free port, with the free port at the root. A production fills the non-free ports of its occurrence with rooted trees of complementary types. Every grammar tree evaluates to a vector in its free port space. We count productions with their named ports, so this description retains the contraction ordering. A grammar state is productive precisely if it has some finite tree. A complete tree contraction has an arbitrary root occurrence at which all ports are filled with branches. A unicycle contraction consists of one cycle, with a branch on every non-cycle port of its vertices.

If zero dimensions are allowed, discard any slot with a zero-dimensional factor as identically zero and omit absent colors before running the algorithms. Discarded slots cannot contribute to invariant differences. A color that remains in a positive-dimensional slot has its dimension bounded by its explicit table size. Deleting slots cannot create either an infeasible system or a recursive branching production in a feasible signature that previously lacked one. Treat zero-port values separately.

### Bounds on the trees and cycles needed for separation

For two inputs X,Y, consider simultaneously the pair of evaluations of each tree of type s, in E_s direct sum E_s. The direct sum over all states of the spans of paired evaluations at height at most h has dimension at most 2D. From height h to h+1 one adds leaves and closes one step under every production applied componentwise to pairs. Multilinearity allows the inputs of each production to range over any spanning bases already computed. If no span grows at a step, the system has stabilized. Therefore H=2D+1 is a sufficient height bound for the paired spans of all trees.

For a complete tree, the root contraction is multilinear in its branch arguments. Hence branches of height at most H suffice for every tree test. For a vertex on a cycle, pick ordered incoming and outgoing ports of types i,o. Once every side branch is contracted, the two free ports form a matrix E_i^* -> E_o. Regard it as a single block in End(E), where E=direct sum_s E_s. A cycle value is the trace of the word of its blocks, and an incompatible word has zero trace. Loops and parallel edges are included. Multilinearity in side branches again lets us choose height at most H. Take corresponding block pairs for X,Y and span products by starting with the identity pair and multiplying by each paired block. This algebra has dimension at most 2D^2. Thus testing lengths at most 2D^2 suffices to test trace equality; the empty word already agrees. Together these bounds show that the resulting finite tree and cycle family separates all distinct orbit closures.

### One straight-line program for all the tests

The bounded family can be exponentially large, so use one rational arithmetic circuit for the sum of the squared differences of its values. For an ordered input pair (R,T), used below as (X,X), (Y,Y), or (X,Y), define

    C_s(h) = sum_{B of type s and height at most h} ev_R(B) tensor ev_T(B).

The initial vector C_s(0) is the sum over leaf occurrences. The next vector equals the same leaf sum plus, for each non-leaf production of type s, the corresponding doubled transition contracted against the tensor product of its child C_t(h) vectors. The doubled transition is the tensor product of the transitions for R and T, with the child factors interleaved. This recursion follows by expanding the independent choices of the children and counts each ordered branch once. This is the sum-product recursion for weighted tree automata. [Kiefer, Marusic and Worrell, Minimisation of Multiplicity Tree Automata](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5352)

For complete trees, use C_s(H) to fill all ports of each doubled root occurrence and sum. For cycles, for each doubled occurrence with a designated ordered pair of cycle ports, fill its remaining ports with C_s(H). This gives one doubled block in the endomorphism space of the direct sum of all E_s tensor E_s. Form the matrix M by summing these doubled blocks over all choices. Then trace(M^k) sums the pairwise products of evaluations of all ordered cycles of length k with allowed branches: block multiplication enforces the port type match, and trace(P tensor Q)=trace(P)trace(Q). Distinguishing starting vertices can repeat cycles, with the same positive multiplicity in all three aggregates. Add these traces for 1<=k<=2D^2 to the tree sum, giving S_RT. The arrays and operation count are polynomial in the dimensions, because all degrees and the number of types are fixed.

The value S_XX+S_YY-2S_XY is a sum of squares of rational differences, and vanishes exactly when all of them vanish. Include the scalar-slot squared differences. Replace every rational circuit gate by paired numerator and denominator integer gates using cross multiplication for addition and subtraction. Denominators are nonzero, so testing the final numerator gives one EqSLP instance of polynomial size. This proves membership in coRP by the standard modular straight-line zero test. The algorithm does not evaluate or store potentially exponentially long rationals.

### Deterministic bit-polynomial cases

Suppose no productive production has two children in the same strongly connected component as its output. Inside any component a rooted tree can then continue in that component along at most one descending chain. A height-H tree uses at most H+1 vertices on the chain, with a bounded number of children in lower components at each step. By induction on the number of remaining components, every tree of height at most H has O((H+1)^q) vertices for some constant q depending only on the signature. This bound also covers signatures with no productive states, for which all side branches requiring a filled state are impossible.

Compute the paired tree spanning bases using the bounded-height recursion described above. Keep actual evaluations of trees as basis elements: choose independent members of the finite list of productions applied to previously accepted bases and retain those members rather than their row-reduced forms. Each round has only polynomially many candidates because each fixed production has bounded arity. Pairings in root trees and side branches now cost polynomial bit time. For a direct bit bound, all evaluations of polynomial-size diagrams have polynomial numerator and denominator bit lengths. Clear the input entries using a single common denominator whose bit length is at most their total bit length, and bound each resulting integer contraction by the number of coordinate assignments times a product of the largest absolute entry. A word of polynomial length in side-filled cycle blocks has the same bit bound. Test all root combinations on paired bases and all cycle traces on a paired word basis, again retaining actual word products. Exact rational rank tests and the stated size bounds give deterministic polynomial bit time.

### EqSLP hardness from recursive branching

Assume (1) is feasible and a productive production of type s has two children in the strongly connected component of s. Use the production as a root occurrence. Fill all but the two distinguished branches with fixed finite productive trees. For each distinguished child of type t, choose a dependency path t -> ... -> s. Follow its productions, filling every off-path child with a fixed finite productive tree; this leaves one hole requiring an s-branch. The resulting fixed open diagram B is a tree with output type s and exactly two holes requiring an s-branch. It may consist of a single three-port vertex. Productivity also gives a fixed finite seed tree Z of type s.

There is a fixed readout context C with one hole requiring an s-branch. To see this, include Z in a multiset of occurrences having balanced port counts (a large multiple of the positive balanced multiplicity vector suffices). Complete all port pairings without touching the internal edges of Z. Its free port is the only unmatched port of Z and must meet a port outside Z. Use that connected component and remove Z and its attachment edge. The remaining context C is connected and has just one free port, complementary to s. It can have a cycle; the construction below permits that. In particular, it need not contain a slot of indegree zero.

We first describe the input to be encoded. Given a division-free integer SLP, append a squaring of its output and arrange its gates in layers with every operation reading the preceding layer. Polynomially many copy gates suffice. Let the rational space S have one coordinate for each gate state in each layer, including a distinguished 1 coordinate in every layer. Let u be the vector of layer-zero constants and its 1, with all other coordinates zero. There is a bilinear map M:S times S -> S encoding all gate operations:

- A multiplication gate reads one operand from each input vector.
- Addition and subtraction read the stated sum or difference from the first input and multiply by the preceding layer's 1 coordinate of the second.
- A copy uses its preceding value and the preceding 1. The next layer's 1 multiplies two preceding 1 coordinates.

Each output coordinate in layer h+1 reads only from layer h in both arguments. Nothing produces layer zero. Let ell:S -> Q be the coordinate functional for the final squared output. A full binary M-tree with every leaf u evaluates either to the value vector of some layer (if all child depths consistently match) or to zero. In particular ell of such a tree is either zero or the final SLP value squared. Conversely the complete binary tree of the required last depth evaluates to the last layer vector. If one input of M is fixed and the other varies, the resulting linear map on S strictly raises the layer and has trace zero; so does a nonempty product of such maps.

We realize these three objects inside the fixed target signature. Use one shared lane for the logical S state in the defining space of color s, taking its dual if needed so that the raw port of type s carries S. A hole then carries S*. Use private lanes for every internal edge of each of the three templates B, Z and C, all distinct even when they have the same color. On B use the tree coevaluation routing described above to place M as the effective tensor with three free ports, one S output and two S* inputs. On Z use the same routing to place u at its sole free port. On C every private lane is one-dimensional. Use ell at the vertex with the sole free port S*, filling all other factors of its term with 1, and use 1 at all other vertices. Thus C can read its input even when it contains a cycle. The target defining space for each color is the direct sum of all of its lanes (use one trivial line for absent colors). The target tensor at any slot is the sum over all template roles with that slot label. Make the second input by setting just the term carrying ell to zero. Every local role has fixed order and every private lane is of polynomial dimension by the same tree routing bounds as before. Constructing the two full dense tuples takes polynomial bit time. It does not evaluate any SLP gate.

To verify correctness, expand an arbitrary full contraction into assignments of template roles to its vertices. Only matching lane labels along each edge can contribute. Every private edge lane has exactly two designated endpoints, so within each of B, Z and C, the expansion's private edges form coverings of that template, with its free ports omitted. A covering of a tree is a union of exact copies because every vertex is reached along a unique path from the root. Therefore B and Z private components reduce to copies of M and u. A private component covering C has a positive number of sheets, and each sheet contributes exactly one hole, all others being paired with one-dimensional factors. It evaluates to the product of ell on the states at those holes. This holds for cyclic coverings as well because each internal C lane has dimension 1.

All physical edges using S must join an output of a B or Z copy to a hole of B or C: their raw types are complementary, and all uses of the shared lane have exactly those roles. Direct such edges from outputs to holes. Each B or Z copy has exactly one output. C copies consume but never produce S. A connected network of B and Z edges feeding a C hole is a finite rooted binary tree with Z leaves. Indeed every vertex has one successor leading towards the given sink, so no cycle is possible, and each B has precisely two predecessors. A component with no sink instead has a directed cycle, with side trees on its incoming ports. Its evaluation is a trace of a nonempty product of the strictly layer-raising maps above, hence zero. This analysis applies separately to every role assignment, even when different roles happen to share a slot label.

If the SLP output is zero, every assignment involving the changed ell term vanishes by the tree analysis; all other terms are identical for the two inputs. Thus all invariants agree. If the output is nonzero, choose the particular closed diagram obtained by wiring a complete binary tree of B copies of the required depth, with Z leaves, into C. Its intended assignment gives the positive squared output. Every other contribution involving ell is nonnegative: sink evaluations are each zero or the same square, while components without sinks have value zero. Removing the ell term therefore strictly changes this contraction. This proves reflection also for arbitrary signed SLP constants and prevents cancellation across different role assignments.

## Consequences and attribution

All cases follow. In particular, EqSLP-complete signatures occur already for one group: use V tensor (V*)^{tensor 2} together with a V slot. System (1) is feasible with p=1. The primal branch state is productive and recurses through two children of its own type. Therefore the proposed deterministic dichotomy would put EqSLP in P or make it TOCI-complete, by applying it to this particular signature. Conversely, if either statement about EqSLP holds, the classification immediately puts every signature in P or makes it TOCI-complete. The three specific formats in Question I.12 all contain one balanced slot having at least two primal-dual pairs; one vertex with its pairs wired into loops already has multicyclic excess. Our proof therefore implies all three concrete completeness assertions as well.

The inherited results are the invariant description and the three-copy four-port completeness theorem of Lysikov and Walter, and the bounded-height and paired-aggregation method from weighted tree automata. The constructions here use the feasibility criterion to characterize exactly when colored contraction diagrams can have more than one cycle, use unique port-cover decoding to simulate arbitrary four-port contractions in every such signature, and embed a binary SLP computation through a productive typed tree context in the remaining recursively branching signatures. The classification is an informal proof. It asserts no separation of P, coRP, or TOCI.
