# Complexity of Transportensor Isomorphism

Fix a finite field $k$ with a common arithmetic encoding and a finite named signature $\Sigma$. The problem $\mathrm{TrTI}_{k,\Sigma}$ takes two finite [Transportensors](Transportensors.md) and asks whether there exists an isomorphism. The inputs list vertices, edges, endpoints, binary rational thresholds, bases (so total fiber dimensions are bounded by written length), all local operations in dense coordinates, and all edge matrices. A fixed signature bounds the total arity of each operation. Theorems below do not give the same size estimates for succinct graphs, binary dimensions without bases, circuit tensors or input-dependent unbounded arity.

**Classification.** For every such $\Sigma$ there is a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction
$$\mathrm{TrTI}_{k,\Sigma}\le_m^p \mathrm{3TI}_k.$$
For the fixed signature consisting of three independent modes and a single named tensor with those three outputs and no inputs, the reverse reduction holds as well. The same is true for any extension of that signature permitting the other data to be set to zero. Thus the expressive decision problem is TI-complete over the same field. Here TI-complete means complete for the problems polynomial-time many-one reducible to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $k$. A signature that forgets linear data can instead give precisely GI. This quantification distinguishes the upper bound for every signature from hardness for particular signatures.

### Building the upper reduction

Use two split algebras $A=k^{V_X}$ and $B=k^{E_X}$ with named coordinatewise multiplication. Their primitive idempotents are exactly the point indicators, so algebra isomorphisms are exactly set bijections, including in characteristic two. Give two unary maps from $B$ to $A$ sending the indicator of an edge to the respective indicator of its tail and head. They force the two bijections to preserve incidence, including loops and parallel edges.

Precompare the sorted lists of *distinct* numerical threshold values of the two inputs. On disagreement return a fixed no-instance. On agreement encode the ordering and equalities on the edges by one named bilinear operation on $B$: on two point indicators $e,f$ return $e$ if $\lambda(e)\le\lambda(f)$, and zero otherwise. A permutation preserving this table preserves the total preorder. Its action on the finite ordered set of threshold classes must match the first to the first, the second to the second, and so on. The numerical precomparison therefore guarantees preservation of the exact thresholds with a constant number of operation names even when the number of distinct weights grows.

For each mode introduce $W_i=\bigoplus_v W^X_{v,i}$ and the bilinear $A$-module action by block projections. Compatibility with $A$ forces the map on $W_i$ to send exactly the $v$-fiber onto the fiber at $p(v)$. Conversely any such collection of fiber maps is allowed. Encode every transport in mode $i$ by one bilinear map $B\times W_i\to W_i$. On an edge indicator it projects to its tail fiber, applies its edge matrix, and includes the result in its head fiber. The equation for this bilinear map is exactly the edge intertwining equation once incidence is matched.

For local tensors introduce named dual spaces $W_i^\vee$ and perfect evaluation maps into a named copy of $k$. Give that scalar copy its field multiplication; its only $k$-linear algebra automorphism is the identity. Evaluation forces each dual action to be the inverse transpose of the primal map. For each operation $\alpha$, form the ordered tensor product $P_\alpha$ of its global output factors and its dual input factors. Use a unary map $A\to P_\alpha$ sending the vertex indicator to that vertex's operation tensor, embedded by the block inclusions (and zero extension of local functionals on the other fibers). This handles zero tensors, empty words and multiple distinct scalar observations. Because fiber maps are already forced, its preservation says exactly that all vertex equations hold.

To stay within a published reduction with an arity bound, build a named space for each needed prefix of each product $P_\alpha$. Give each extension of a prefix by one factor its ordinary bilinear tensor-product map. Its values on pure tensors span the new space, forcing its automorphism to be precisely the tensor product of the already specified maps. Use the scalar copy for an empty product. There are only constantly many such sorts and maps for fixed $\Sigma$. All dimensions, and even all the zero-padded dense binary multiplication tables in this construction, are polynomial in input length. Sum the named spaces into one space. Mark each summand by a unary subspace relation and extend the maps as zero off their prescribed argument summands. This leaves only unary subspace relations and unary or binary functions. Empty summands may be retained as zero spaces. Dimension incompatibilities can be rejected directly.

Every Transportensor isomorphism extends to this linear structure. Conversely its action on primitive idempotents, mode spaces and the recorded tables recovers the isomorphism of Transportensors. Now apply Grochow–Qiao, Theorem 7.3 (attributed there to Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk), on basis-explicit linear structures. Its hypotheses concern relation arity at most three and function arity at most two. Our use of prefix spaces is what places every original signature under those bounds. The final published step supplies a decision reduction, without requiring it to preserve numbers of witnesses. [Grochow–Qiao, Definitions 7.1 and Theorem 7.3](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full) · [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09219).

### Lower bounds and smaller regimes

A single isolated vertex carrying the original three-tensor, with the three modes independent, proves the reverse reduction. No guessing of a graph identification is involved, hence even the version with a prescribed graph identification is TI-hard for this signature. A single sort with a binary algebra multiplication also suffices for hardness by the published algebra-isomorphism completeness result. The NP certificate lists the two permutations and the local invertible matrices. Ranks and both sets of equations can be checked in polynomial time. The finite-field coAM upper bound follows by reduction to 3TI and its known membership, giving NP ∩ coAM. [Grochow–Qiao, overview and finite-field bounds](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.00309).

For contrast, over a fixed finite field the subproblem with at most polynomially many vectors per vertex-mode is GI-equivalent when it permits ordinary graph inputs. Enumerate all vectors and covectors at every occurrence, with addition and scalar multiplication (or complete pairing data); encode each tensor by its scalar evaluations on all ordered input vectors and output covectors. Fixed arity makes the list polynomial. Record edge incidences, thresholds and the linear evaluations for each edge map. Isomorphisms of this finite relational structure are exactly the required maps, by linearity and separation by dual vectors. Standard bounded-arity colored incidence encoding gives GI. This includes uniformly bounded dimensions and dimensions at most $c\log_q N$ for fixed $c$. Zero spaces on graph inputs give the lower bound. Unbounded dimensions can also lie in GI for extra reasons, for example when polynomially many isomorphism-invariant unordered vector and covector labels span both spaces.

### Computational tasks involving automorphisms

Decision returns one bit. Search asks for one actual witness, counting asks for the number of witnesses, generator computation asks for a generating list of $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$ in vertex, edge and matrix form, and canonization asks for an invariant representative. An arbitrary many-one decision gadget does not automatically solve those output problems. For the finite-field explicit model the two counting functions #Iso and #Aut belong to #P: use a single canonical encoding of each proposed tuple of maps and the polynomial verifier above.

In fact **#Iso and #Aut are polynomial-time Turing equivalent** in this model. One direction uses #Aut$(X)=$#Iso$(X,X)$. For the converse, connectify each input by adding a new apex with all modes zero, one outgoing zero-transport edge to every old vertex and one loop at the apex. Use two fresh thresholds larger than every input threshold, common to the pair, one for the spokes and one for the loop. Give the apex zero operations using the fixed signature. Its special loop forces its identification, and the spokes are then forced. This construction preserves all isomorphism and automorphism counts, including when an input graph is empty. Call the resulting connected objects $C,D$. Compute $a=$#Aut$(C)$, $b=$#Aut$(D)$ and $h=$#Aut$(C\sqcup D)$. If $C,D$ are nonisomorphic then $h=ab$. If they are isomorphic then the component-swap coset gives $h=2ab$. In the latter case return $a$ for the original #Iso; in the former return zero. Count bit lengths are polynomial because witnesses have polynomial-length descriptions. The proof uses the full thresholded problem and its disjoint-union operation. It gives no parsimonious reduction through the final ordinary TI gadget.

As a baseline exhaustive decision algorithm, after checking sizes enumerate the two permutations and every allowable invertible local matrix, then test the equations. For n vertices, m edges and dimensions $d_{v,i}$, an upper bound is $n!m!q^{\sum_{v,i}d_{v,i}^2}$ times polynomial verification cost. This is a baseline, not a best-known runtime claim. No uniform bit-complexity statement for arbitrary infinite fields is being inferred from the fixed finite-field classification.
