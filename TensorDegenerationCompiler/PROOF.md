# A degeneration-preserving compiler for tensors

Fix the tensor order \(d\ge2\). There is a polynomial-size map from tensors of any prescribed format to unital associative algebras, given by structure constants, that preserves and reflects orbit-closure inclusion over every algebraically closed field. Composing it with the previous hyperbolic encoding gives the same equivalence with orbit-closure inclusion under ordinary, split symplectic, or determinant-one split quadratic conjugacy of matrix subalgebras. This combines the tagged path-algebra construction used for Grochow–Qiao's functorial reduction question with the idempotent-lifting method for algebra degenerations.

## The algebra and its rank markers

For \(T\in\bigotimes_{i=1}^d V_i\), take vertices \(v_0,\ldots,v_d,z\). The chain edges \(v_{i-1}\to v_i\) have arrow spaces \(V_i\). The arm \(v_0\stackrel{a}{\longrightarrow}z\stackrel{b}{\longrightarrow}v_d\) has scalar arrows. Impose the relation \(ab=T\), where a main-chain path is identified with its tensor product. Paths are multiplied in traversal order. There are no arrows into \(v_0\) or out of \(v_d\).

For every core vertex \(u\), add a private source \(m_u\) and a space of arrows from \(m_u\) to \(u\) of rank \(N_u\). The \(N_u\) are distinct positive integers larger than the dimension of every original corner, and depend only on the format. Set every product of one of these marker arrows with any positive-length path to zero. The resulting algebra has the basis consisting of all vertices, all retained original paths other than \(ab\), and the marker arrows. The tagged arm gives a single eliminated basis element with coefficient 1. The marker relations discard every path continuing out of a marker arrow. Thus the multiplication table is polynomial in the tensor coefficients, and basis size is polynomial for fixed \(d\). In particular the whole corner \(e_{v_0}Ae_{v_d}\) is freely identified with the full main path space, even for \(T=0\).

Invertible tensor-mode changes give algebra isomorphisms by acting on the chain arrows and fixing the labels and tag arrows. This algebra construction works on any free tensor family over a DVR. It immediately preserves tensor degenerations in the variety of unital algebra structures. For a converse consider a complete DVR family \(\mathcal A\) whose generic algebra is the encoding of \(T\) and whose special algebra is the encoding of \(S\). Such a family exists for any orbit-closure inclusion, by taking a curve through the special point with generic point in the orbit, normalizing and completing, and if needed allowing a finite extension of the fraction field to realize the generic isomorphism. The residue field remains the given algebraically closed field.

## Reflecting the degeneration

Lift every special vertex idempotent to a complete orthogonal family in \(\mathcal A\). Idempotents lift in a complete DVR algebra by the Newton correction for \(x^2-x\) with derivative \(2x-1\), which is a unit near an idempotent. Orthogonal lifting follows by applying the procedure to complementary corners. Each corner defined by the lifted family is a finite free direct summand, with rank given by its special dimension.

The diagonal corners of the lifted family have rank one, so the generic vertices are primitive. In the generic encoded algebra the positive path space including the markers is a nilpotent ideal with quotient a product of copies of the field. It is the radical. The complete primitive family is therefore aligned with the original vertices after a permutation and inner conjugation. For the alignment, the images modulo the radical are the coordinate idempotents, and for two ordered families \(f_i,e_i\) with these same residues the unit \(\sum_i e_i f_i\) intertwines them. The permutation is determined at the core vertices by the full corner dimensions: each ordered corner of dimension \(N_u\) is unique and forces both ends of that marker. This argument works with full corner dimensions, which are constant in the family. It does not need the generic and special radical squares to form one flat family.

On \(\mathcal A\), let \(E_i=e_{v_{i-1}}\mathcal A e_{v_i}\). The main-path product
\[
\bigotimes_i E_i\longrightarrow e_{v_0}\mathcal A e_{v_d}
\]
is an isomorphism because it is an isomorphism in the special fiber between free modules of equal rank. Choose generators of the two scalar tag corners lifting the special tag arrows. Take their product and pull it back through the main-path isomorphism to give a tensor in \(\bigotimes_i E_i\). This tensor specializes to \(S\). In the generic fiber the core-aligned isomorphism multiplies each tag generator by a nonzero scalar and acts by an invertible map on each chain corner. The tensor is therefore in the ordinary orbit of \(T\), since the two tag scalars can be absorbed into one mode. Choose free bases of \(E_i\) lifting the special bases. This gives the required tensor degeneration.

## Linear dimension for three tensors

For a three-tensor use the corresponding bilinear map \(\mu:U\times V\to W\), identifying the first two modes with dual spaces. Use just three core vertices with corners \(U\) from 0 to 1, \(V\) from 1 to 2, and \(W\) from 0 to 2. Set the product of the first two corners equal to \(\mu\), and all other off-diagonal products to zero. This is associative because products of three off-diagonal elements vanish. Add one private rank marker per core vertex as above. At every point the commutator ideal is the sum of off-diagonal corners, since \([e_i,x]=x\) for \(x\) in an off-diagonal corner starting at \(i\). This ideal has cube zero. The algebra dimension is \(O(1+\dim U+\dim V+\dim W)\). The same idempotent and rank alignment in a degeneration family gives free corners lifting \(U,V,W\), and their product itself is the family of bilinear maps. There is no tag scalar or main-path inversion in this case. Consequently even this class of split algebras with cube-zero radical realizes all three-tensor degenerations.

The previous hyperbolic construction preserves and reflects all unital algebra degenerations between encoded points. Applying it after this compiler proves the three classical comparisons. The result changes the scope of the earlier isomorphism reduction by allowing limits of generic orbit representatives. This is an informal proof.
