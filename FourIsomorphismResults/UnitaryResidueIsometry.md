# Matrix tuple isometry over fields of characteristic two with unitary residue factors

Fix a finite field $F$ of characteristic two with $|F|\ge4$. Let $A$ be a finite-dimensional associative unital $F$-algebra, $ * $ an $F$-linear involution, and $J$ the Jacobson radical. Suppose each simple factor of $A/J$ fixed by the involution either has nontrivial involution on its center or is a field. Given a self-adjoint unit $c$, there is a Las Vegas polynomial bit-time algorithm to decide and solve $g^*g=c$ and count its solutions. It also gives a compact representation of the norm-one group with constructive lifts. Arbitrarily many simple factors, arbitrary matrix ranks in the exchanged and second-kind factors, and arbitrary radical length are allowed.

Consequently ordered matrix tuple isometry over $F$ has the same bound for decision, witness and counting when the transpose-closed source adjoint algebra meets the hypothesis. The condition is recognized from the input. This is an informal proof and a case of the characteristic-two problem of [Grochow, Qiao, Stange and Sun](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1). The proof combines the radical sifting and trace-layer analysis in our earlier commutative-residue and square-zero results with affine orbit calculations on individual invariant flag grades.

## 1. Residue computation and the maintained group representation

Compute $J$, its powers and a matrix-factor presentation of $S=A/J$ by finite algebra algorithms. Their running time is Las Vegas polynomial at fixed base field. [Rónyai, finite algebra algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(08)80017-X)

An exchanged pair of matrix factors, in coordinates where the involution swaps transposes, has residue norm-one group $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ acting by pairs $(g,g^{-T})$. Obtain these coordinates by choosing the first matrix-factor presentation and using the involution to identify the second with its transpose copy. A second-kind fixed factor has $x^*=B^{-1}\sigma(x)^TB$, with $\sigma$ the order-two field automorphism of its center $E$ and $B$ nonsingular Hermitian. Find $B$ by linear intertwiner equations, then apply Hilbert 90 to the scalar in $\sigma(B)^T=\lambda B$ to make it Hermitian. Hilbert 90 here is a linear equation over the fixed field. The factor contributes the unitary group $\mathrm U(B)$. A fixed first-kind field has trivial norm-one group.

For $j\ge1$, put $H_j=\{h\in A^\times:h^*h=1\bmod J^j\}$. Maintain the following for $H_j$, and for each successive subgroup $G\le H_j$ within that layer.

1. In the original natural space of each general linear or unitary residue factor, maintain an explicit invariant flag. The actual residue image of $G$ acts trivially on every grade except at most one active grade per factor. The joint action on the active grades surjects onto a product $L$ of general linear and unitary groups. Each active unitary grade has a specified nonsingular Hermitian form.
2. $P$ is the kernel of the joint graded action. It is a normal $2$-subgroup: its image in the residue group lies in a product of unitriangular flag kernels, and the congruence kernel lies in $1+J$. Store generators of $P$.
3. Given any prescribed element of $L$, compute an actual lift to $G$ with that graded action. The lift may have off-diagonal residue blocks. No splitting of the extension or homomorphic lifting is required.

Initially $H_1$ is the full inverse image of the residue norm-one group. Use one grade per factor, $P=1+J$, and lift by any linear representative. A binary basis of $J$ adapted to radical powers supplies generators $1+x$ for $1+J$, by successive additive quotients.

First solve the residue norm equation for $\bar c$. On an exchanged pair assign the two coordinates as $(1,C^T)$ for target $(C,C^T)$. On a fixed field take the square root. On a unitary factor the equation is congruence of the two nonsingular Hermitian forms $B$ and $B\bar c$, of the same dimension. Diagonalize them and normalize coefficients: if $a$ is in the fixed field, its square root there has norm $a$. Lift the residue solution to a unit $r$ with $r^*r=c\bmod J$. At a later stage $r$ will be a solution modulo $J^j$.

## 2. Normal $2$-subgroup arithmetic

Represent $P$ by left regular matrices on the binary space underlying $A$. Every action of a finite $2$-group on a nonzero binary vector space has a nonzero fixed vector: nontrivial orbits have even size and the zero vector is fixed. Successively apply this to quotients and find fixed vectors by linear equations on the supplied generators. The resulting basis makes the matrices unitriangular. Superdiagonal order, refined by individual entries, gives a central filtration with order-two factors.

To compute a short pivot list, sift generators in this filtration, keeping one pivot at each occupied position. Saturate by sifting squares and pairwise commutators of pivots, retrying after each insertion. Each insertion adds a previously unoccupied position. At termination the collected words with exponents zero or one form a group by induction from the last pivot: the next pivot normalizes the preceding tail, since each commutator sifts using only later positions, and its square belongs to that tail. This gives membership and group order, and uses polynomially many operations. For an additive homomorphism out of $P$, use the graph subgroup with the additive coordinates first. Sifting yields a preimage for each point in the image, and the pivots after the additive coordinates generate the kernel.

One can also find a transporter for an affine action of $P$ on a binary space and give generators of the point stabilizer. Use a flag for the linear action as above. On the highest one-dimensional quotient $P$ acts by translations, so apply its additive homomorphism to match that coordinate. Move the target back by the chosen lift and restrict to the kernel. The source and adjusted target lie in a common invariant affine fiber. Repeat on its difference space. This algorithm takes polynomial time.

## 3. The representation that acts on a radical layer

Let $X=J^j/J^{j+1}$ and $X^+=\{x\in X:x^*=x\}$. The left affine action of $H_j$ is
$$
 h\cdot x=(h^{-1})^*(1+x)h^{-1}-1\bmod J^{j+1}.
$$
Its linear part comes from residue congruence. The subgroup $1+J^j$ acts by exactly the translations in $\operatorname{tr}(X)=\{z+z^*:z\in X\}$. This follows by expanding the inverse of $1+z$ on the layer and using $J^{2j}\subseteq J^{j+1}$. Write $\mathcal T=X^+/\operatorname{tr}(X)$, with its resulting affine action.

Here are explicit coordinates for its **linear** action. If $e_i$ are central primitive idempotents of $S$, the Peirce space $e_iXe_k$ is sent to $e_{k^*}Xe_{i^*}$. Exchanged pairs of spaces contribute nothing to fixed space modulo traces. For $k=i^*$, identify the two center fields with $E$, using the residue involution in an exchanged pair. Split this bimodule according to its right-center twists $xa=\theta(a)x$ for $a\in E$. This follows from $E\otimes_F E\cong\prod_{\theta}E$ and is computed by linear equations in the action of one field generator. A field generator can be sampled and certified by its full Frobenius orbit. For a cyclic group of order $m$, the primitive-element proportion is $\varphi(m)/m\ge (1+\log_2 m)^{-1}$: order the distinct prime divisors $p_i$ increasingly and use $p_i\ge i+1$ in the product formula. The sampling success probability is at least this lower bound, while certification does not use integer factoring. The involution takes $\theta$ to $\theta^{-1}$ and, on a fixed twist, is $\theta^{-1}\sigma$-semilinear on left scalars. Here $\sigma$ is the given center involution for a fixed factor, and identity under the exchanged-factor identification. To see the semilinearity, use $(ax)^*=x^*\sigma(a)$ and the right action rule on the inverse-twist space. Exchanged twist sectors contribute no quotient. If the scalar involution on a fixed sector is nontrivial, pick a scalar of trace one to its fixed field. Multiplying any fixed vector by that scalar gives a preimage under $1+*$. Thus only $\theta=\sigma$ remains.

Matrix units identify this last sector with copies of the matrix bimodule, with the right matrix action twisted by $\sigma$. For the unitary case, change matrix coordinates by $Y=BX$ in each copy. Ordinary transpose on these coordinates exchanges the two actions in the same way as the involution, using $\sigma(B)^T=B$. The remaining operation commutes with both matrix actions and therefore acts only on the multiplicity space. It is an $E$-linear involution. For an exchanged pair the same argument works with $B=I$ on its two surviving corners. The multiplicity involution decomposes into size-one and size-two Jordan blocks; size-two blocks have fixed space equal to trace image. On a size-one block ordinary transpose gives symmetric matrices modulo alternating matrices, with diagonal coordinates. Taking square roots of the diagonal converts its congruence action to the ordinary vector or covector action. In a fixed unitary factor take $\sigma$ on the coordinates to undo the field twist, and use the Hermitian form to identify dual and natural representations over $\mathbb F_2$. For an exchanged pair the two corners give natural and dual copies respectively. The fixed first-kind fields contribute only trivial linear actions. All coordinate changes are linear over $\mathbb F_2$ and computable by matrix-unit and Gaussian elimination routines.

For a current $G$, give each of these natural copies the current invariant flag on the original factor space. Give each dual copy its reversed annihilator flag. Concatenate the flags across the copies. This gives an explicit $G$-invariant flag of $\mathcal T$ whose individual grades are trivial or are single natural or dual modules of the current quotient factors. The action of $P$ is trivial on each grade. It may be nontrivial on the entire layer.

## 4. An affine orbit step without any residue splitting

Let $G$ currently act on an invariant affine fiber in $\mathcal T$, with original source $0$ and difference space $V$. Initially $V=\mathcal T$. It contains the adjusted target. The group contains $1+J^j$, which acts trivially on $\mathcal T$.

If $V\ne0$, choose the least flag index $a$ with $V\subseteq\mathcal T_a$, using the current flag from Section 3. Set $W=V\cap\mathcal T_{a-1}$, a proper invariant subspace. Linear algebra realizes $V/W$ inside the single grade $\mathcal T_a/\mathcal T_{a-1}$. The affine action of $P$ on $V/W$ is translation. Let $D$ be its image and use Section 2 to compute both its kernel and preimages. Normality makes $D$ invariant under every linear action of $G$, by conjugating translations. The group $G/P\cong L$ therefore acts affinely on $(V/W)/D$. Evaluation of its action uses any lift in $G$, with independence of lift on this quotient.

The nontrivial natural and dual grades are simple over $\mathbb F_2$. For $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$, scalar matrices span $E$, and with elementary matrices they generate the full $E$-matrix algebra. For $\mathrm U_n(r)$ with center field $E=\mathbb F_{r^2}$, the scalar norm-one group spans $E$. In fact a generator of order $r+1$ has binary degree $2\log_2 r$: $2^m\equiv1\bmod(r+1)$ is excluded for smaller positive $m$, using size when $m\le\log_2 r$ and $r\equiv-1\bmod(r+1)$ otherwise. In an orthonormal basis a diagonal matrix with one nontrivial norm-one entry, minus identity, gives a diagonal matrix unit up to a nonzero scalar. Permutations give all other matrix units. The dual versions follow by inverse transpose. Consequently a subquotient of a nontrivial single grade is zero or its full module. In the latter case the map to the grade coordinates already gives an isomorphism. On trivial grades all subquotients remain trivial.

On a nonzero natural or dual quotient, use any nonidentity scalar in its active factor. For a general linear factor take any nonzero scalar distinct from $1$. For a unitary factor find a scalar $b$ outside the fixed field and use $b/\sigma(b)$. The scalar is central in $L$ and its linear action has no fixed vector. Its affine action has a unique fixed point, computed by linear equations. Centrality makes it fixed by all of $L$. After translating this point to the origin, the orbit test is ordinary linear vector or covector transport in the active factor.

On a trivial quotient the affine action of $L$ is a homomorphism to the additive binary space, hence trivial. For general linear groups the determinant quotient has odd order and every elementary transvection is a commutator, by conjugating it by a diagonal scaling unequal to one. For unitary groups over fixed field of order $r\ge4$, the group is generated by embedded $\mathrm U_2(r)$ and diagonal scalars in an orthonormal basis. Indeed a unit vector can be made the first basis vector by two-coordinate unitary operations: if two nonzero coordinates have distinct norms, merge them using the nonsingular-vector isometry in their plane. If all nonzero norms coincide and at least two coordinates remain, there must be at least three since their total norm is $1$. Use the isometry between nonzero isotropic vectors in a plane to change the equal nonzero norms of two coordinates to another common nonzero value, retaining a third of the prior norm, then merge an unequal pair. Iterating followed by induction on the orthogonal complement proves generation. The two-coordinate extensions used here have the elementary constructive Witt proof in Section 5. For $\mathrm U_2(r)$ use a hyperbolic basis, with Gram matrix $C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. Its determinant-one subgroup is $\mathrm{SL}_2(r)$: comparing $g^TCg=C$ with $\sigma(g)^TCg=C$ forces $g$ over the fixed field, and the converse is immediate. The determinant quotient is odd and $\mathrm{SL}_2(r)$ is perfect by elementary generation and diagonal conjugation scaling upper and lower transvections by a nonidentity square. This excludes additive binary images of all the unitary generators, including the odd-order diagonal scalars.

Choose a successful quotient transporter in $L$, or reject if none. Lift it and correct its displacement by a $P$-translation to match the target exactly in $V/W$. Move the target back by the inverse of this transporter and restrict $G$ to the exact stabilizer of the source in $V/W$. Its image in the old quotient $L$ is precisely the stabilizer of the centered source marking, since any residual discrepancy in $D$ is corrected by $P$. The new difference space is $W$. Section 5 describes the refined flags, their new quotient groups, and the generators of their flag kernels inside the **old graded quotient**. Lift each new kernel generator through the old lift routine and correct by a $P$-translation to fix the source. Along with the translation kernel these generate the new $P$, by exactness of the old projection. To lift an element of the new graded quotient, lift it into the old marked stabilizer using the explicit block-diagonal section there, then apply the old lift routine and correct by a $P$-translation. These steps use only the **graded** action of each lift. After at most $\dim\mathcal T$ substeps, they produce a transporter in $\mathcal T$ and the full stabilizer of zero with the maintained representation.

## 5. Marked stabilizers and flag refinements

For a general linear factor, vector transport tests whether the specified correspondence defines an isomorphism of the marked spans and extends it by completing bases. For covectors use the dual space. In the vector case a marked span $U$ gives the flag $0\subseteq U\subseteq V_0$ on the old active space. The mark stabilizer is trivial on $U$, surjects onto $\mathrm{GL}(V_0/U)$ and has a block-diagonal section from a complement. Its flag kernel is the additive group of off-diagonal blocks. In the covector case keep the common kernel $K$ of the covectors and use $0\subseteq K\subseteq V_0$, with only $K$ active; the same argument constructs the section and kernel. A binary basis of the off-diagonal blocks generates each kernel. The procedure uses a single grade in Section 4, so no joint vector and covector test is required at one substep; later substeps retain prior constraints through the exact stabilizer.

For a unitary factor use its Hermitian form to interpret covectors as vectors. The marking determines an isometry of the marked spans if and only if linear relations and all pairings agree. Extend that isometry constructively by first splitting off a nonsingular complement $U_0$ to the radical inside the marked span. In $U_0^\perp$ the remaining marked space $R$ is totally isotropic. Complete its chosen basis to hyperbolic pairs with dual isotropic vectors: satisfy the cross pairing by a linear solve and adjust their internal Gram matrix by adding vectors in $R$. The adjustment is an equation $Z+Z^\dagger=B_0$ for Hermitian $B_0$, where $\dagger$ is conjugate transpose. Solve it by assigning an off-diagonal triangle and taking traces on the diagonal. Match the remaining nondegenerate Hermitian complements with orthonormal bases. This handles degenerate marked spans and provides every needed transport. [Witt extension in formed spaces](https://web.math.ku.dk/~wahl/Forms.pdf)

For the marked stabilizer use coordinates $(R,M,R')$ on $U_0^\perp$, where $R'$ is the isotropic dual of $R$ and $M$ is their nondegenerate orthogonal complement. The point stabilizer surjects onto $\mathrm U(B_M)$, with block-diagonal section. Its kernel consists of the matrices
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
 I&-Y^\dagger B_M&Z\\0&I&Y\\0&0&I
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad Z+Z^\dagger=-Y^\dagger B_MY.
$$
Multiplying against the Gram matrix gives the equation. The map to $Y$ is additive and onto by the trace solve. Lift a binary basis of $Y$ and add generators from a basis of the kernel $Y=0$. The refined flag is
$$
0\subseteq R\subseteq R+U_0\subseteq R+U_0+M\subseteq V_0 .
$$
Only the grade represented by $M$ is active, with the induced Hermitian form. Pull the flags in this section back through the old active-grade quotient. Every other old grade stays fixed pointwise. This proves the invariant used in Section 4 and does not assert that the image in the original residue group splits.

## 6. Correct the remaining trace and count

At stage $j$ take the target $x=(r^*)^{-1}cr^{-1}-1$ in $X^+$. Success in $\mathcal T$ means an actual transporter sends $0$ to $x$ up to a trace. Correct it on the left by $1+z$, with $z\in J^j$ obtained by solving $z+z^*=$ the residual error on the layer. This is an exact affine translation. If $h$ is the corrected transporter, replace $r$ by $h^{-1}r$; its norm equals $c\bmod J^{j+1}$.

The stabilizer $G$ of zero in $\mathcal T$ acts affinely on the trace fiber over zero. Every trace translation is available in $1+J^j\subseteq P$, so passing to the exact point stabilizer $H_{j+1}$ does not change the image in the active graded groups. Compute its new $P$ by taking the zero stabilizer of $P$ on the trace fiber using the $2$-group algorithm of Section 2. For each prescribed quotient element, lift to $G$ and correct by $1+z$ as above. The flags remain valid. Iterate through $J^s=0$.

There are at most $\dim_{\mathbb F_2}J$ flag substeps across all layers, since each decreases the current fiber dimension and the dimensions of all the layers sum to that bound. A lift through one substep invokes the preceding lift exactly once and then uses stored additive preimage routines, so the compounded lifting time stays polynomial. Group generators are sifted at each step. All bases, flags and matrices are of polynomial size. A successful solution set is a coset of $H_s$, and $|H_s|=|P||L|$. Use the pivot count for the first order and the usual classical group formulas for the second. The output group representation consists of the $P$ generators, quotient factor coordinates, and an explicit lift procedure. It does not require computing multiplicative generators of large extension fields.

## 7. Application to ordered matrix tuples

Close the source and target tuples under transpose. Constructive module isomorphism for the corresponding Kronecker representations gives invertible $X,Y$ with $X^TA_iY=B_i$, or finds none. The source adjoint algebra is
$$
 \mathcal A=\{(L,R):L^TA_i=A_iR\text{ for every }i\},\qquad
 (L,R)(L',R')=(LL',R'R),\qquad (L,R)^*=(R,L).
$$
The reference equivalence and its transpose make $c=(YX^{-1},YX^{-1})$ a self-adjoint unit. Every isometry $Q$ gives the norm solution $(QX^{-1},YQ^{-1})$. Conversely a norm solution $(L,R)$ gives $RL=YX^{-1}$ and the isometry $Q=LX$. These operations are inverse, so the counting statement also transfers. [Ivanyos and Qiao, constructive adjoint and module algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03495)

Fixed first-kind matrix factors of rank greater than one remain outside the theorem. The literature question includes them and includes smaller fields. Novelty for the invariant-flag algorithm is unverified.
