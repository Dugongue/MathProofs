# Determinant-one transport of division multiplication tensors over higher local fields

The two results concern literal $\mathrm{SL}(n,K)^3$ equivalence, with the three determinant constraints taken in the actual input frames. The tensor inputs are dense arrays. The field computation is measured in field arithmetic and, where stated, queries to the displayed valuation.

## 1. A same-field reduction from raw arrays by a canonical commutator volume

**Theorem 1.** Let $K$ be an infinite field and $d\ge1$ an integer invertible in $K$. Consider bilinear tensor arrays in the ordinary isomorphism classes of multiplication tensors of central division $K$-algebras of degree $d$, expressed in independently chosen frames in their three legs. From two arrays $T,T'$ one can compute three elements $c_i\in K^\times$ using a deterministic polynomial number of field operations, without an ordinary transporter. The arrays are special-linear isomorphic if and only if they are ordinarily isomorphic and
$$
c_3=c_1c_2,\qquad
c_1,c_2\in \big(\operatorname{Nrd}_{A/K}(A^\times)\big)^d,
\tag{1}
$$
where $A$ is the central division algebra normalized from either array when they are ordinarily isomorphic.

In particular let $K=k((t_1))\cdots((t_{2r}))$, with $k$ algebraically closed, $\ell$ a prime different from the characteristic and $d=\ell^r$. Use the full composed valuation with $\Gamma=v(K^\times)\simeq\mathbb Z^{2r}$. On the promised family of algebras given up to isomorphism by tensor products of $r$ degree-$\ell$ symbols whose $2r$ parameter values form a basis modulo $\ell$, there is a deterministic same-field many-one reduction from the restricted special-linear problem to ordinary tensor isomorphism. It uses polynomially many field operations and valuation queries, with no division-algebra presentation or basis supplied. The arithmetic test in (1) is just $v(c_i)\in \ell^{2r-1}\Gamma$ for $i=1,2$. A failed arithmetic test maps to a fixed ordinary no-instance. A passed test keeps the original pair.

### Extract a unital product and its volume

For a raw tensor $T$, let $x_0,y_0$ be the first basis vectors in its first two legs. The slice maps $J=T(-,y_0)$ and $L=T(x_0,-)$ are invertible because every nonzero element of the division algebra is a unit. On the third leg define
$$
x\star y=T(J^{-1}x,L^{-1}y).
$$
The unit is $T(x_0,y_0)$. In any coordinates from the original division algebra the product is $x b_0^{-1}a_0^{-1}y$, with $a_0,b_0$ the elements corresponding to $x_0,y_0$. Right multiplication by $(a_0b_0)^{-1}$ identifies it with the original algebra. Thus $T$ is multiplication in the recovered unital algebra $A_T$ with known frame maps $\rho_{T,1}=J,\rho_{T,2}=L,\rho_{T,3}=I$.

For any central simple algebra $A$ of degree $d$ and a regular element $a$ whose reduced characteristic polynomial is separable of degree $d$, let $C=K[a]$. On $A/C$ the alternating form
$$
b_a(x,y)=\operatorname{Trd}\big(a[x,y]\big)
$$
is nondegenerate. Indeed $b_a(x,y)=\operatorname{Trd}([a,x]y)$; the reduced trace pairing is perfect by splitting, and the centralizer of $a$ is $C$. Choose a complement $w_1,\ldots,w_{d^2-d}$. Define a top form $\Omega_A$ by setting
$$
\Omega_A(1,a,\ldots,a^{d-1},w_1,\ldots,w_{d^2-d})
   =\operatorname{Pf}\big(b_a(w_i,w_j)\big).
\tag{2}
$$
Addition of centralizer vectors to the $w_i$ has no effect. Changing their basis scales the Pfaffian by exactly the basis determinant.

The value prescribed by (2) is also independent of $a$. Over a splitting field diagonalize $a=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d)$. Use the matrix-unit order consisting of the diagonal units followed by pairs $(E_{ij},E_{ji})$, $i<j$, in fixed lexicographic order. The determinant from the diagonal units to the powers of $a$ is $\Delta=\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)$. The Pfaffian on the off-diagonal pairs is $\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)$. Their ratio is the same sign for every regular $a$. Inner changes of split coordinates have determinant one on the matrix space, since left and right multiplications by $g,g^{-1}$ have reciprocal determinants. Thus all choices give the same form over the splitting field and hence in $A$. Formula (2) is invariant under algebra isomorphisms and works also in characteristic two.

On a division algebra as in the theorem a regular element is computable without factoring. Start with the central field $K$ and repeatedly compute the centralizer of the commutative field already obtained, by linear equations. If it is larger, adjoin a basis element outside that field. It commutes with the old field, and the commutative subalgebra generated by it is a field by finite-dimensionality and absence of zero divisors. Its degree over $K$ divides $d^2$ by viewing the division algebra as a module, so it is separable under the characteristic hypothesis. The double-centralizer theorem stops this procedure at field degree $d$. To maintain a primitive element for an enlarged field, try $a+\lambda b$, where $a$ generates the old field and $b$ is the new element. Among $\binom d2+1$ distinct scalars some choice separates all embeddings, because each pair forbids at most one scalar. The degrees are checked by spans of powers. An infinite-field arithmetic model uses a supplied sequence of this many distinct scalars, for instance successive powers of $t_1$ in the Laurent field. All the calculations are polynomial in the dense input dimensions. Use $\operatorname{Trd}(x)=\operatorname{tr}(L_x)/d$. The Pfaffian in (2) can be computed by alternating elimination, also in characteristic two. No splitting field enters the algorithm. [Gille–Szamuely, *Central Simple Algebras and Galois Cohomology*](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~benoist/refs/Gille-Szamuely.pdf).

### Read the three determinant coordinates

Compute the three scalars $w_{T,i}$ given by $\Omega_{A_T}$ on the frames $\rho_{T,i}$. Do likewise for $T'$ and put $c_i=w_{T,i}/w_{T',i}$. If an algebra isomorphism $\phi:A_T\to A_{T'}$ exists, its determinant in these leg frames is exactly $c_i$, by invariance of the volume.

Every isotopy of unital associative algebras is a triple $x\mapsto a\phi(x)$, $y\mapsto\phi(y)b$, $z\mapsto a\phi(z)b$, with $a,b$ units and $\phi$ a unital isomorphism. Evaluating at the two units identifies $a,b$, proves that their multiplications are bijective, and yields $\phi$ by dividing the third map on the left and right. Conversely substitution verifies the formula. Over a splitting field, regular left or right multiplication by $a$ has determinant $\operatorname{Nrd}(a)^d$, by the $d$ columns or $d$ rows of a split matrix algebra. Therefore, if ordinary isomorphism holds, the complete determinant list in the given frames is
$$
(c_1\alpha,\ c_2\beta,\ c_3\alpha\beta),
\qquad
\alpha,\beta\in \big(\operatorname{Nrd}(A^\times)\big)^d .
$$
Requiring the three entries to equal one gives exactly (1). The same determinant calculation applies when a scalar extension makes the division algebra into a matrix algebra. It only requires the existing algebra isomorphism and the canonical volume ratios, which survive extension. [Albert, *Non-associative algebras I*](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968960).

## 2. Exact extension criterion and two sharp degrees

**Theorem 2.** Let $K,k,\ell,r$ be as in Theorem 1 and let
$$
D=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}(t_{2i-1},t_{2i})_\ell.
$$
An arbitrary product of $r$ symbols with independent parameter values modulo $\ell$ obeys the same theorem with its parameters in place of these $t_i$. For a finite extension $L/K$, write $\Lambda=v_L(L^\times)\supseteq\Gamma$. Map the bivector
$$
\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{r} v(t_{2i-1})\wedge v(t_{2i})
      \ \in\bigwedge\nolimits^2(\Gamma/\ell\Gamma)
$$
to $\omega_L\in\bigwedge^2(\Lambda/\ell\Lambda)$. Let $2s$ be its rank and $U\subseteq\Lambda/\ell\Lambda$ its contraction image. Write $\widetilde U\subseteq\Lambda$ for the full inverse image. The regular determinant image has valuation subgroup
$$
\mathcal D_L=
\begin{cases}
  \ell^r\Lambda,&s=0,\\
  \ell^{r+s}\Lambda+\ell^{r+s-1}\widetilde U,&s>0 .
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
$$
It contains all value-zero units. For any pair already ordinarily isomorphic over $K$ through algebras isomorphic to $D$, there is an $\mathrm{SL}^3(L)$ transporter exactly when $c_3=c_1c_2$ in $K$ and $v(c_1),v(c_2)\in\mathcal D_L$. For two explicit corrections this is an exact lattice test once the value lattice is supplied.

The least extension degree that accommodates **every** pair of corrections satisfying the volume relation is $\ell^{2r^2}$. For a single correction with $\ell$-primitive value $\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\ell\Gamma$, the least degree is $\ell^{2r-1}$. The second statement also applies when the other correction has value in $\mathbb Z\gamma$. Both minima are attained by separable extensions, and the lower bounds apply to all finite extensions, including ones with defect.

### Division and reduced norms from value lattices

The composed valuation is Henselian, with final residue $k$. A finite field extension remains Henselian with the same final residue because its residue extension is finite and $k$ is algebraically closed. Let $F$ denote either $K$ or such an extension and $\Lambda_F$ its value lattice. For an integer $\ell^a$, all units for the composed valuation are $\ell^a$th powers, by lifting a residual root with nonzero derivative. Thus
$$
F^\times/(F^\times)^\ell \simeq \Lambda_F/\ell\Lambda_F .
$$
Kummer symbol classes are bilinear, and alternating here because $-1$ is an $\ell$th power. Consequently an alternating normal form for $\omega_F$ expresses the scalar-extended Brauer class as a tensor product of $s$ symbols whose $2s$ parameter values are linearly independent modulo $\ell$. Their generated value classes are exactly the contraction image $U$. This uses the symbol identities, without requiring surjectivity of the symbol map for unrelated Brauer classes. [Gille–Szamuely, Kummer cup products and cyclic algebras](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~benoist/refs/Gille-Szamuely.pdf).

For this product assign each standard monomial in the symbol generators the value given by the corresponding parameter values divided by $\ell$, with exponents $0,\ldots,\ell-1$. The monomials form an $F$-basis and have distinct value classes modulo $\Lambda_F$. Define the value of a sum to be the least of the coefficient-adjusted values. The leading monomial of a product is the product of the two leading monomials, since all other pairs have strictly larger value. Thus the product algebra has no zero divisors and is a division algebra of degree $d'=\ell^s$, with value group $\Lambda_F+\ell^{-1}\widetilde U$ for $s>0$.

For a nonzero element $z$ in this division algebra, the determinant of left multiplication in the monomial basis has a unique leading permutation. In every input column the least adjusted term is multiplication by the leading monomial of $z$, and these terms occupy distinct output rows since monomial multiplication translates the exponent group. In the determinant sum the row and column basis weights cancel. Hence $v(\det L_z)=d'^2 v_D(z)$. Since $\det L_z=\operatorname{Nrd}(z)^{d'}$, the reduced norm has valuation $d'v_D(z)$. All value-zero units of $F$ occur as central reduced norms by the preceding Hensel argument. Thus its norm group is determined by the value lattice $\ell^s\Lambda_F+\ell^{s-1}\widetilde U$. The split case has unrestricted reduced norms. Passing from the division algebra to a matrix algebra over it preserves the reduced norm image: elementary off-diagonal row operations have reduced norm one, diagonal entries contribute products of division norms, and diagonal matrices show the reverse inclusion. Row swaps are products of elementary matrices and diagonal scalars. This uses Gaussian elimination over the division ring.

The degree of $D\otimes_KF$ remains $\ell^r$, so regular determinants are the $\ell^r$th powers of these reduced norms, giving (3). For $F=K$ the entire residue value space is the support, $s=r$; hence $\mathcal D_K=\ell^{2r-1}\Gamma$. Over $K$, this proves the promised same-field reduction of Theorem 1. [Tignol–Wadsworth, *Totally ramified valuations on finite-dimensional division algebras*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1987-0887507-6).

### Minimal extension degrees

If every base correction is to become available, (3) forces $\Gamma\subseteq\ell^r\Lambda$. This kills the map $\Gamma/\ell\Gamma\to\Lambda/\ell\Lambda$, so $s=0$ and this containment is also sufficient. Independent changes of the three raw frames realize any desired corrections with $c_3=c_1c_2$, so there is no smaller value requirement. The containment forces $[\Lambda:\Gamma]\ge\ell^{2r^2}$. The field degree is at least the value-group index by the fundamental inequality. Adjoining the $\ell^r$th roots of all $2r$ uniformizers supplies the necessary lattice at degree at most $\ell^{2r^2}$, proving equality.

For one primitive correction $\gamma$, use Smith bases for $\Gamma\subseteq\Lambda$. Let $e_i$ be the $\ell$-valuations of its elementary divisors, and $j$ the number of positive $e_i$. The kernel of the reduction map has dimension $j$. Restricting a nondegenerate alternating form on the dual to a codimension-$j$ space lowers its rank by at most $2j$, so $s\ge r-j$. If $s=0$, (3) requires $\gamma\in\ell^r\Lambda$. Primitivity forces $\max e_i\ge r$, whence $\sum e_i\ge r+j-1\ge2r-1$.

If $s>0$, put $b=r+s-1\ge1$. Condition (3) requires $\gamma\in\ell^b\Lambda$ and the residue of $\gamma/\ell^b$ to be in $U$, hence in the image of $\Gamma$. Primitivity gives $\max e_i\ge b$, so $\sum e_i\ge b+j-1\ge2r-2$. Equality would force $\max e_i=b$. Express $\gamma$ in the Smith source basis, where at least one coefficient is prime to $\ell$. That coordinate must have $e_i=b$, by divisibility in $\Lambda$. It then supplies a nonzero coordinate of $\gamma/\ell^b$ modulo $\ell$ outside the image of $\Gamma$, a contradiction. Thus $\sum e_i\ge2r-1$ in both cases. The field degree is at least $[\Lambda:\Gamma]\ge\ell^{2r-1}$.

Finally extend the nonzero residue class of $\gamma$ to a Lagrangian for the symplectic form inverse to the original bivector. Choose monomials realizing lifts of the other $r-1$ basis classes and adjoin their $\ell$th roots. Adjoin also the $\ell^r$th root of an element of value $\gamma$. The killed Lagrangian makes the projected bivector zero, and the value $\gamma$ is now divisible by $\ell^r$. This is sufficient by (3). The degree is at most $\ell^{2r-1}$, completing sharpness.

## Scope and precedents

The split-matrix and quaternion precedents use a degree-two intrinsic volume and reduced-norm labels. Here (2) computes an intrinsic volume in every degree invertible in the field from a raw division multiplication array. The exact value-lattice formula and the two extension minima concern determinant-one transport over higher-rank Henselian valuations. Tignol and Wadsworth give the underlying valuation and Brauer structure. The same-field reduction proved here has the stated division-multiplication promise and arithmetic model. The proof is informal and publication priority for the tensor statements remains unverified. [Earlier split-matrix and quaternion volume labels](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/AlpogeGrochow/SplitMatrixAlgebraSLPrimeCertificates.md) · [Tignol–Amitsur, *Totally ramified splitting fields of central simple algebras over Henselian fields*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(86)90017-7) · [Grochow–Qiao IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317).

Finite controls checked 32 Pfaffian and Vandermonde instances with complement shears. Exhaustive diagonal value-lattice controls for ranks two, four and six recovered the stated primitive and universal exponents.
