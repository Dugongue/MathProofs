# Elliptic theta groups and determinant-one tensor transport

Use the multiplication bimap convention
$$
M_{E,L}(x,y)=xy,\qquad
H^0(E,L^a)\times H^0(E,L^b)\longrightarrow H^0(E,L^{a+b}).
$$
A transporter is a triple $(P,Q,C)$ satisfying $C M(x,y)=M'(Px,Qy)$. Inverting the first two coordinate actions switches to the usual covariant tensor convention and preserves the determinant-one question.

Throughout, $E$ is a smooth elliptic curve with origin $O$, $L=\mathcal O_E(dO)$, $d\ge3$, and the field has characteristic zero or characteristic greater than three prime to $d$. Choose consecutive positive integers $a=e,b=e+1$ for which the [mixed rank-one reconstruction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FirstRankDropFour/Deformations.md) applies. All sufficiently large $e$ work. In the plane cubic case $d=3$ one can use every $e\ge1$. In that case the ambient projective ideal is generated by a cubic, its degree $2e+1$ sections generate the ideal sheaf, and ambient sections in the needed degrees map onto the curve's sections by the cohomology of line bundles on the projective plane. The dimensions of the three legs are $da,db,d(a+b)$ by Riemann–Roch.

The mixed rank-one section is the paired image of $E$ by the two complete systems. An ordinary transporter induces an isomorphism of these curves preserving both powers of $L$, hence preserving $L$ by taking their ratio. Conversely every polarized isomorphism induces a transporter after choosing lifts on the two powers. Fixing one coherent lift on $L$, all lifts on the two factors differ from those obtained by taking powers by independent units $u,v\in k^\times$. Indeed global functions on either projective geometrically integral elliptic curve are constants. The multiplication map is surjective by ambient multiplication and restriction, so the output map is forced. This also follows by recovering the entire transporter from the paired rank-one section and its linear span.

## Theorem 1. Exact splitting of an ordinary orbit over an algebraic closure

Let $k$ be algebraically closed under the hypotheses above. For a pair within the ordinary orbit of $M_{E,L}$, take any one ordinary transporter with determinants $(x,y,z)$. Put
$$
\chi(x,y,z)=x^{-b(a+b)}y^{-a(a+b)}z^{ab},\qquad
\nu=a^2+ab+b^2-\frac{d\,ab(a+b)}2.
$$
Let $N=|\operatorname{Aut}(E,O)|$, which is $2$, $4$ or $6$ in the specified characteristic. The pair is determinant-one equivalent if and only if $\chi(x,y,z)\in\{u^\nu:u\in\mu_N\}$. In particular this ordinary orbit splits into determinant-one orbits in bijection with $k^\times/\{u^\nu:u\in\mu_N\}$. For a plane cubic with $j$ different from $0$ and $1728$, and $e\equiv1,2\pmod4$, this is $k^\times$ itself.

**Proof.** Scalar gauges on the three legs contribute
$$
(u^{da}, v^{db}, (uv)^{d(a+b)}).
\tag{1}
$$
The integer exponent matrix of (1), divided by $d$, has columns $(a,0,a+b)$ and $(0,b,a+b)$. Its entries have greatest common divisor one. Its maximal minors, up to sign, are $ab,a(a+b),b(a+b)$ and also have greatest common divisor one. This follows from $\gcd(a,b)=1$. Smith normal form therefore makes (1), before dividing by $d$, a pair of $d$th powers in two coordinates. The annihilating character has primitive coefficient vector $(-b(a+b),-a(a+b),ab)$. Algebraic closure implies all $d$th powers are available, so the image of (1) is exactly $\ker\chi$.

Every polarized automorphism of $(E,dO)$ is a product of an origin-fixing automorphism and a translation by $p\in E[d]$. This follows from the identification of $\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)$ with $E$ by $p\mapsto\mathcal O_E(p-O)$ and the decomposition of a curve automorphism using its value at the origin. We compute the image under $\chi$ of both factors.

For a translation of order $n$, its theta-group lift to $L$ has $n$th power a scalar. Over $k$ rescale the lift so that its $n$th power is the identity. The cyclic action is free on the curve, and this lift linearizes $L$ for the degree-$n$ étale quotient isogeny to $E/\langle p\rangle$. The descended bundle has degree $d/n$. On the $r$th power, decompose the quotient's direct-image algebra into its $n$ character line bundles. They are degree-zero because their $n$th powers are trivial. Riemann–Roch gives each character eigenspace dimension $rd/n$. The determinant is accordingly
$$
(-1)^{(n-1)rd/n}.
$$
The exponent of this sign in $\chi$ is $-(n-1)(d/n)ab(a+b)$, which is even. This uses only that $ab(a+b)$ is even. Thus translations disappear under $\chi$ even though their individual determinants can be nontrivial. The normalization of the theta lift does not affect $\chi$ because changing it gives a scalar gauge (1). [Oprea, theta groups and section representations](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~doprea/verlindeams.pdf)

For an origin-fixing automorphism in a short Weierstrass model, write $(x,y)\mapsto(u^2x,u^3y)$ with $u\in\mu_N$. On sections of $\mathcal O_E(sO)$ its matrix in the pole-order basis is diagonal with determinant $u^{w(s)}$, where $w(s)=s(s+1)/2-1$. Indeed the basis pole orders are $0,2,3,\ldots,s$ and each basis monomial has that same scaling weight. Taking the three weights at $s=da,db,d(a+b)$ in $\chi$, the quadratic terms cancel and the remaining exponent is exactly $\nu$. Thus the character image of the ordinary stabilizer is $\mu_N^\nu$.

Since all transporters are obtained from any one by a stabilizer, its determinant triple can be corrected precisely when its character lies in that subgroup. The group determinant map of the three general linear groups is onto $(k^\times)^3$. Its quotient by the stabilizer determinant image is consequently the claimed set of determinant-one orbits. For $d=3$ and $N=2$, one has $\nu$ even when $e\equiv1,2\pmod4$: $a^2+ab+b^2$ is odd and $ab(a+b)/2$ is odd in those two residue classes. This gives the stated family with no identifications in the remaining parameter.

## Theorem 2. Constructive signed isomorphism over finite fields without discrete logarithms

For short Weierstrass models over a finite field $k$ of characteristic greater than three with $p\nmid d$, the determinant-one isomorphism problem for the corresponding section multiplication tensors can be decided and a transporter constructed in expected polynomial bit time, with the curve presentations and the section basis changes supplied. The algorithm is deterministic polynomial time for each fixed field. It handles arbitrary basis changes on all three tensor legs and does not need a generator of $k^\times$ or a discrete logarithm.

**Proof.** The first paragraph recovers exactly all ordinarily allowed polarized isomorphisms, so it is enough to test the finite list and correct the two scalar gauges. A polarized curve isomorphism factors as an origin-preserving map followed or preceded by a rational $d$-torsion translation on the appropriate curve. Its value at the origin determines the translation. Origin-preserving maps between short Weierstrass models have the form $(x,y)\mapsto(u^2x,u^3y)$ with the appropriate fourth- and sixth-power conditions on the model coefficients. At least one coefficient is nonzero. Factoring a polynomial of degree at most six lists all such maps. The division polynomials, of degree $O(d^2)$ in $x$, list the possible $x$ coordinates of rational nonzero $d$-torsion points. For even $d$ include the points of order two from $y=0$. Take the points on the equation over $k$ and verify their orders divide $d$ by multiplication. The identity is added separately. Computing the division polynomials by their multiplication recursions and extracting the linear factors are expected polynomial time, using finite-field polynomial factorization. Thus the list has at most $6d^2$ elements and is constructed without enumerating the field. [Schoof, division polynomials over finite fields](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1985-0777280-6) · [Cantor–Zassenhaus, finite-field polynomial factoring](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1981-0606517-5)

For each map, use pullback from $E$ to $E'$ and let $p_0$ be the inverse image of $O$. Work in the pole-order bases of the two models. A correcting function $g$ on $E'$ has divisor $d[p_0]-d[O']$. If $p_0=O'$, use $g=1$. Otherwise solve for a nonzero section in $H^0(E',\mathcal O(dO'))$ whose first $d$ local Taylor coefficients at $p_0$ vanish. The line has dimension one because the two degree-$d$ divisors are linearly equivalent. This also constructs the function over $k$. A local parameter is $x-x(p_0)$ when $y(p_0)\ne0$, and $y-y(p_0)$ otherwise. The implicit curve equation gives all $d$ coefficients by elementary truncated series because the derivative in the other variable is a unit. For $r=a,b,a+b$ the map on sections is $s\mapsto g^r f^*s$. Its coefficients in the pole-order basis can be found by rational-function arithmetic and linear elimination, using the relation $y^2=x^3+Ax+B$. This is polynomial because each section has pole order at most $dr$ and the explicit translation formulas have bounded rational-function degrees before substitution. Basis changes transport these matrices to the given tensor coordinates.

Let the determinants for that coherent choice be $D_a,D_b,D_{a+b}$. For a signed transporter, the right-hand side of (1) must be $(D_a^{-1},D_b^{-1},D_{a+b}^{-1})$. Smith reduction of its three-by-two integer exponent matrix has nonzero entries $d,d$, by the same minors calculation as above. Apply the associated unimodular transformations as monomial maps of $(k^\times)^3$ and $(k^\times)^2$. The test reduces to one equality to $1$ and two tests for $d$th powers. An element $\eta$ of $k^\times$ is a $d$th power exactly when $\eta^{(q-1)/\gcd(d,q-1)}=1$. To output a witness, extract roots with a degree-$d$ polynomial and invert the unimodular coordinate changes. These calculations are polynomial in the tensor and field encodings. The output matrices satisfy the original bimap equation and each determinant is one. Reject when every polarized isomorphism fails the scalar test. Conversely every signed transporter occurs by the raw transporter characterization, so the decision is exact.

For a fixed field the necessary finite-field factorizations or root lists can be computed deterministically by directly testing its elements. In the uniform statement the randomization is only the usual always-correct factorization with expected polynomial running time. As an immediate same-field many-one consequence, these presented inputs reduce to ordinary tensor isomorphism in expected polynomial time, and deterministically for fixed fields, by outputting fixed ordinary YES and NO pairs after the test.

The two results combine elliptic theta-group representations with the mixed rank-one tensor recovery. The determinant character is computed on the entire ordinary stabilizer in the first result; the second retains the two arithmetic power tests that the algebraically closed argument absorbs. The proofs are in prose. Comparison with [Fisher, theta groups and elliptic normal model invariants](https://www.dpmms.cam.ac.uk/~taf1000/papers/invenqI.pdf) and [Grochow–Qiao, tensor-isomorphism reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16317) leaves publication priority for these exact statements unconfirmed.

Exact finite controls checked 120 combinations of small finite fields, consecutive section powers, and polarization degrees. They verified the two Smith factors, gauge image cardinality, origin exponent, and cancellation of the cyclic translation sign.
