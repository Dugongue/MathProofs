# Computing the determinant profile from two finite projective lists

**Theorem.** Work over any fixed finite field F. Consider a tensor in bilinear form T:U×V→W whose values span W. Suppose each input comes with lists of projective covector lines spanning U* and V*. For each mode, assume every ordinary transporter between the input objects maps the list to the corresponding list by its induced dual map. Assume also that the stabilizer of each input preserves its own lists. If the lists have polynomial size, the exact determinant profile of the tensor pair can be computed in deterministic polynomial time with ordinary 3-tensor-isomorphism decision queries over F. In particular SL-TI reduces to TI under these hypotheses. An intrinsic polynomial-time procedure to produce the lists suffices to apply the theorem to raw tensors. The lists may have arbitrary order and arbitrary representatives. The algorithm needs no such list in W*.

The proof combines the tensor-to-algebra bridge with point individualization and stabilizer chains from permutation-group algorithms. It uses the small determinant quotient directly. Lifts of permutations need only supply their determinant vectors, and the entire pointwise kernel is solved by linear congruences. No group orders are needed.

## A line-marking query using an ordinary TI oracle

Use an associative algebra with semisimple quotient one copy of F per vertex. Its radical consists of arrow spaces and auxiliary loops described below. Use three main vertices 0,1,2 and the arrow spaces U from 0 to 1, V from 1 to 2, and W from 0 to 2. Here composition is written left to right, so the corresponding Peirce spaces are e_0 J e_1, e_1 J e_2 and e_0 J e_2. Multiply U by V by the supplied tensor.

To mark a covector α on U, add a new sink s, with one-dimensional spaces from 1 to s and from 0 to s. The product of u with the first arrow is α(u) times the second arrow. To mark a covector β on V, add a new source t with one-dimensional spaces from t to 1 and from t to 2. The product of the first arrow with v is β(v) times the second. Use fresh vertices for each mark. All other products between radical elements are zero. Sources reach middle vertices and then sinks, so all triple products from the radical vanish and the multiplication is associative. For every vertex add a private space of radical loops with distinct dimensions 1,2,…, and keep all products involving such loops zero. Orthogonal vertex idempotents act by the indicated Peirce rules, and their sum is the unit.

The described radical J is the Jacobson radical because it is nilpotent and the quotient is a product of fields. Every algebra isomorphism matches the vertices by the loop dimensions. Indeed the dimensions of the Peirce spaces are invariant under the choice of idempotent lifts. To see this, two systems f_i and e_i lifting the same ordered idempotents have f_i a = a e_i for a = Σ f_j e_j. The element a equals 1 modulo J and is invertible, so simultaneous conjugation aligns the lifts. After aligning, an isomorphism is given on the three main arrow spaces by an actual transporter, and a pair of one-dimensional arrow maps in each mark shows that the corresponding covectors are matched projectively. Conversely, any transporter taking the marked covector lines to their matching target lines extends. Use the two nonzero arrow scalars at each mark to match the induced covector multiplier, and extend arbitrarily on the tag loops. Thus the marked query is exactly algebra isomorphism. Its dimension and structure constants have polynomial size. The reduction of algebra isomorphism to ordinary 3-TI gives the claimed oracle query. [Grochow and Qiao, Theorem A and the algebra-isomorphism reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

## Fixing projective images on two spanning bases leaves linear congruences

Choose covector bases from the respective source lists. If their images are prescribed by marking, the two linear maps are diagonal in the vector bases dual to these chosen and prescribed covector bases. Write those maps e_i↦a_i e'_i and f_j↦b_j f'_j. Their unknown scalars a_i,b_j are nonzero. Put w_ij=T(e_i,f_j), w'_ij=S(e'_i,f'_j). Each zero w_ij must match a zero w'_ij.

Choose a source W-basis indexed by r pairs (i,j) among the w_ij, where r=dim W. The same indexed target w'_ij must be a basis. In these bases the output map sends each indexed vector to a scalar c_k times its target, and the condition on every pair is

    c_k [w_ij]_k = a_i b_j [w'_ij]_k  for all k.

The support mismatches can be rejected directly. On each matched nonzero coefficient, take discrete logarithms in the fixed field's multiplicative group. These are linear equations over ℤ/(|F|−1). Each requested determinant value in each mode is one further linear equation, using the products of its diagonal scalars and the determinant ratios of the fixed coordinate frames. Smith normal form, or integer linear congruence elimination, decides the system in polynomial time and supplies a transporter when one exists. This also works with no determinant constraints. One can enumerate the constant number of possible determinant tuples and compute the entire profile. These arguments use the list marks only on the two covector bases. No projective output choices are needed.

## Obtain reference and stabilizer lifts with ordinary decision queries

A greedy pass fixes the images of the source covector basis lines, one line at a time, using the line-marked oracle with each candidate from the corresponding target list. If ordinary TI accepts, at every step the current partial matching extends because the preceding query certified it. Every extension maps the next line into the target list by hypothesis. After a full pass the congruence calculation supplies a reference transporter g in the original coordinates.

Next order the source basis lines across both modes. For each prefix, let G_i be the stabilizer of its first i lines inside Aut(T). For each candidate image of the next line, mark that image and the fixed prefix. A positive answer permits completion to images for both full bases by the same greedy process. Compute one actual lift and retain its determinant tuple. The candidates enumerate precisely the orbit of the next line under G_i, so the chosen lifts are representatives for the cosets of G_(i+1) in G_i. Every element factors as a product of the chosen lifts and a member of the group fixing both bases projectively. The latter group's full determinant image is the linear congruence calculation with identical source and target. Taking the subgroup generated by the recorded tuples and this image therefore computes det Aut(T), including all correlations. Finally multiply it by det g to obtain the exact transporter profile for T,S. A rejected unmarked query gives the empty profile.

If each list has at most M lines and the sum of the two mode dimensions is b, these procedures use O(b² M²) ordinary queries. Each query and final congruence calculation has polynomial bit size. The result uses a decision oracle through polynomially many queries. Interchanging tensor modes allows the two projective lists in any pair of modes, with the third viewed as output and the other covector conventions adjusted by duality. If W has a proper unused complement, first restrict the output to the actual image and restore all determinant options from that complement. Coordinates and determinant calibrations can be tracked by extending the chosen support bases.

## Scope

This result bypasses the need to enumerate every vector or complete flag in an input mode. The sufficient data are finite invariant spanning lists of lines in two dual spaces, which may overlap and may include many more points than the dimension. For example it applies whenever intrinsic split endomorphisms with distinct eigenvalues in two dual spaces can be computed from the tensors. Use their eigenlines as the lists. It also applies if a previously available structural routine returns any invariant spanning line sets of polynomial size. The proof supplies an oracle algorithm once those lists are available, including determinant extraction even when matching the points has a large permutation group. It does not assert such lists for arbitrary input tensors. This is an informal deduction, with priority for the combination unverified.

## Combine the algorithm with a safe extension without computing discrete logarithms

The same marked-lift computation works over an explicit extension E of F when the list size is polynomial in the original input length and the desired determinant outputs are their norms to the fixed base field. The field E may have exponentially many elements. Replace discrete logarithms and finding a lift by a polynomial-time test of multiplicative equations. In particular, for each completed match of the two bases enumerate the constant number of candidate norm tuples and retain the feasible ones.

A system of monomial equations is described by an integer exponent matrix and a vector of constants in E×. Apply unimodular row and column operations bringing the exponent matrix to Smith normal form. Row operations act on the constants by the same monomials, and column operations give a bijective monomial substitution on the unknowns. Use a polynomial bit complexity algorithm that returns the transformation matrices. The norm constraints add exponents (|E|−1)/(|F|−1) to the equations for the determinants, with bit length O(log |E|). A Smith diagonal entry s gives one equation y^s=c, solvable exactly when c^((|E|−1)/gcd(s,|E|−1))=1 by cyclicity. A zero row requires its constant to be 1. These are polynomial-time field arithmetic tests and require no extraction of roots. For each stabilizer coset representative and the reference transporter, any feasible norm tuple suffices. The coset multiplication argument uses only its value and the fact that an actual lift exists. The pointwise kernel is handled by its full norm image as before.

Thus it suffices to compute invariant spanning lists of polynomial size in two modes over a safe degree extension. Apply the norm profile theorem in [Extensions](Extensions.md) to recover the exact base profile. Queries over the extension become ordinary base-field TI by restricting scalars and marking multiplication by the extension generator on each mode in the tensor-system compiler. This gives a sufficient raw input algorithm if the lists are recovered uniformly there in polynomial bit time. The extension removes the need to recover those lists over the original field. [Ordinary tensor-system compiler](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)
