# Four reductions for the determinant search in tensor isomorphism

The main setting is Open Question 1.9 of Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang. Over a fixed finite field of odd order, ordinary tensor transporters, when they exist, are a coset of the source stabilizer. Their determinant tuples form a coset of the stabilizer's determinant image. Over the field of three elements that image is a subgroup of a three-dimensional binary space. The earlier integral orbit results suggest tracking the exact image of the allowed group. Here the quotient has constant size, and the remaining work is to compute its image and one relative coset from raw arrays. [Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS4)

| Reduction | What it gives |
|---|---|
| Projective covector lists | A polynomial-time reduction to ordinary TI decision for tensors admitting polynomially enumerable invariant spanning projective lists in two input modes. Ordered frames and a third list are unnecessary. The proof uses a quiver algebra to mark individual lines, plus linear congruences to compute the signs in the pointwise kernel. [Proof](ProjectiveLists.md) |
| Semisimple stabilizer witnesses | Over the field of three elements every achievable correction sign has a witness of order at most four times the largest mode dimension. Enumerating rational conjugacy profiles costs exp(O(log² n)) profiles. Field multiplication realizes the order bound. A supplied reference transporter reduces the remaining work to intersections of ordinary tensor orbits with explicit linear fixed spaces. [Proof](Semisimple.md) |
| One safe extension | Choose any prime avoiding the ambient matrix group order. A prime-power extension then preserves the full determinant profile after norm and rescaling. This gives exact transfer from a single chosen extension for all input tensors and requires no stabilizer data. [Proof](Extensions.md) |
| Geometric components | For a particular tensor, extension degrees coprime to its geometric stabilizer component group preserve the test. An explicit polynomial batch works without knowing the components, and it also permits square-determinant tests over large odd-degree fields. Field multiplication exhibits failure at degree three when descent is unchecked. [Proof](Components.md) |

The first item ends in ordinary TI decision on an explicit input class. The second reduces stabilizer corrections to quasipolynomially many class tests. A class test asks whether the orbit meets a linear subspace, and no polynomial reduction for those tests to point-to-point TI is supplied here. The last two permit reductions that work over extension fields to be used over the base field, with effective degree control. They do not themselves remove the determinant constraint.

These are informal proofs. The work does not settle unrestricted containment or equality in Open Question 1.9. In particular a bound on the number of semisimple profiles is not a bound on the number of tensor isomorphisms, a polynomial-time reduction, or a percentage of the open problem solved.
