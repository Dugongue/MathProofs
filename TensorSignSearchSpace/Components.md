# Polynomial finite-field extension descent for determinant constraints

This is an informal reduction on explicit 3-tensors. Over any fixed finite field, ordinary TI and special-linear TI can each be tested by the conjunction of polynomially many tests of the same kind over prime-degree extensions, with all extension degrees above any polynomial threshold in the raw input length. Over \(\mathbb F_3\), the special-linear test can also be recovered by testing only quadratic characters of the determinants over the extension fields. No stabilizer description is needed to construct the batch.

In particular, to reduce \(\mathrm{SL\!-\!TI}_{\mathbb F_3}\) to ordinary \(\mathrm{TI}_{\mathbb F_3}\), it suffices to give one uniform polynomial ordinary-TI reduction for testing square determinants in all three modes on raw tensors over odd prime-degree extensions beyond a chosen polynomial field-degree threshold. The bound and reduction refer to extension degree and explicit coefficient length. They do not require enumerating the extension field.

## Descent through the geometric stabilizer

Write \(k=\mathbb F_q\), let \(\overline k\) be its algebraic closure, and write \(\sigma(x)=x^q\). Suppose an algebraic group defined over \(k\) acts on the tensors. This could be the product of general or special linear groups, or a cover described below. For a source \(T\) let \(H\) be its stabilizer over \(\overline k\). Use the reduced algebraic subgroup on geometric points and let \(C=H/H^\circ\) be its finite group of connected components. For an extension degree \(e\) coprime to \(|C|\), a transporter over \(\mathbb F_{q^e}\) exists precisely when one exists over \(k\).

Only descent needs proof. Pick a geometric transporter \(g\) from \(T\) to the target \(S\), guaranteed by the extension witness. Set \(c=g^{-1}\sigma(g)\in H\). The condition for \(gh\) to descend to \(k\) is \(h^{-1}c\sigma(h)=1\). If this equation is solvable in \(C\), lift a solution to \(H\) and replace \(g\) by its product with the lift. The resulting cocycle value now lies in \(H^\circ\). This is a connected algebraic group defined over \(k\), so Lang's theorem gives the remaining correction. Conversely a rational transporter supplies a solution in \(C\). This holds equally with \(\sigma^e\).

Use \(t\) as the generator of an abstract infinite cyclic group acting on \(C\) by the automorphism induced by \(\sigma\). In \(C\rtimes\langle t\rangle\), the extension condition just established says \((\bar c t)^e\) is conjugate to \(t^e\) by \(C\). Adjust the representative \(x=\bar c t\) by that conjugation so that \(x^e=t^e\). Both \(x\) and \(t\) now centralize \(t^e\). This centralizer is \(C^{t^e}\rtimes\langle t\rangle\), where \(C^{t^e}\) denotes the fixed subgroup. Quotienting the centralizer by its central subgroup \(\langle t^e\rangle\) gives \(C^{t^e}\rtimes(\mathbb Z/e)\). The images of \(x\) and \(t\) generate two complements of \(C^{t^e}\), since each has order \(e\) and projects to the same generator. The order of \(C^{t^e}\) divides \(|C|\), which is coprime to \(e\). Schur–Zassenhaus gives conjugacy of the complements by \(C^{t^e}\), with solvability supplied by the cyclic quotient. Conjugation takes the generators with projection \(1\) to each other. Lift to the centralizer and compare integer exponents of \(t\). Both representatives have exponent \(1\), so equality modulo \(\langle t^e\rangle\) gives equality. Thus \(\bar c t\) is conjugate to \(t\) by \(C\), proving descent. [Lang's theorem](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Lang-Algebraic_Groups_Over_Finite_Fields.pdf) · [Schur–Zassenhaus and the solvable quotient form](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/solvableSylow.pdf)

## Select the extensions without seeing the component group

Write the stabilizer equations in the entries of the three matrices and their inverses. Include \(g_i h_i=I\) to certify invertibility and the cubic equations expressing \(gT=T\). For an SL test also impose \(\det(g_i)=1\). For each chosen sign character \(\chi(g)=\prod_{i\in I}\det(g_i)\), an alternative is to use the algebraic group with an extra coordinate \(z\) and the relation \(z^2=\chi(g)\), with multiplicative composition on \(z\) and the action on tensors induced by projection to \(g\). One can impose multiple such relations. Over \(\mathbb F_3\), the square condition on a nonzero value is exactly that the value equals one. Over an odd extension field, the projected group imposes a square condition without requiring the determinant itself to be one.

Let \(m\) bound the number of the equations and \(d\ge 2\) their degrees. Both are polynomial in the explicit input length. The graph of the unique inverse variables does not enlarge the geometric component count. The total degree of the components of the solution variety is at most \(d^m\). Indeed, intersect with the equations one at a time. A component lying in the next hypersurface is retained, and the degrees of the components cut from any other component sum to at most \(d\) times its degree by affine Bézout. Removing redundant components in the resulting union only reduces the sum. Thus \(|C|\le d^m\), and \(|C|\) has at most \(B=m\lceil\log_2 d\rceil\) distinct prime divisors.

Choose \(B+1\) distinct odd prime extension degrees above the desired polynomial threshold. Polynomial bounds for the \(n\)-th prime and a sieve give such a list in polynomial time. For a fixed base field the extension polynomials can be constructed in deterministic polynomial time. If the base answer is yes then all extension answers are yes. If all answer yes, at least one chosen degree is coprime to \(|C|\), and descent proves base yes. Therefore conjunction recovers the exact base verdict. [Shoup on constructing irreducible polynomials](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

If the extended square-determinant tests reduce uniformly to ordinary TI, the latter comparisons can be taken back to \(\mathbb F_3\). Write a tensor as a bilinear map and restrict its scalars from \(E=\mathbb F_{3^e}\) to the base. Include multiplication by the chosen primitive field-generator element as a distinguished endomorphism of each mode. Intertwining it is exactly \(E\)-linearity because it generates \(E\) over the base. The resulting tensor systems contain the actual extended multiplication law and exactly enforce the \(E\)-linear transporter conditions. The published ordinary tensor-system compiler and ordinary conjunction packing finish the conditional one-instance assembly. The square-determinant test remains the required extension-field input to that assembly. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

## Why odd degree alone fails even for a known ordinary orbit

For any odd prime \(\ell\) and any fixed odd \(q\), take the multiplication tensor \(T\) of \(A=\mathbb F_{q^\ell}\) regarded as an algebra over \(\mathbb F_q\). Let \(S\) be obtained by a determinant-minus-one rebase on the output alone. There is an explicit ordinary transporter.

An autotopism of a commutative unital algebra has the form

    (L_a α, L_b α, L_ab α),

where \(a,b\) are units, \(L\) denotes multiplication and \(\alpha\) is an algebra automorphism. To see this, if \(F(x)G(y)=H(xy)\), take \(a=F(1), b=G(1)\). Setting one argument to \(1\) gives \(H=L_bF=L_aG\), hence \(a,b\) are units. Then \(\alpha=L_{(ab)^{-1}}H\) is unital and multiplicative. The determinant tuple is \((ud,vd,uvd)\), writing \(u=\det L_a\), \(v=\det L_b\) and \(d=\det\alpha\). For this tuple to correct the output reflection to determinant one in all modes, the tuple must be \((1,1,-1)\). The product of the first two entries divided by the third equals \(d\), so the correction requires \(d=-1\).

Over \(\mathbb F_q\) every algebra automorphism has odd order dividing \(\ell\), so its determinant cannot be \(-1\). When the extension degree is divisible by \(\ell\), the algebra splits as a product of \(\ell\) copies of the extension field. A transposition now has determinant \(-1\) and multiplication by a unit of determinant \(-1\) exists. Choose both \(a,b\) this way to get \((1,1,-1)\). Thus ordinary TI already holds over the base while SL-TI holds first after a field change that splits these components. Over an odd-degree extension of \(\mathbb F_3\), \(-1\) is nonsquare. The same counterexample applies to the square-character comparison, since the first two entries divided by the third must have the negative character before correction. This obstruction occurs already at dimension three and extension degree three.

The result is an exact field-transfer theorem rather than an algorithm computing geometric stabilizers. It is informal, uses classical algebraic group and finite group results, and establishes no publication priority for the quantitative tensor formulation.
