# An exact support criterion for tensor null cones and closed orbits

Let a real or complex tensor \(T\ne0\) be given in orthogonal decompositions of its modes into nonzero named blocks \(V_{j,i}\). Suppose any two nonzero tensor blocks differ in at least two block indices, and every nonzero tensor block has scalar one-mode Gram matrices on its own spaces. Single-coordinate blocks give the usual free-support condition on a raw coordinate tensor. For a nonzero block \(e\), put \(c_e=\|T_e\|^2\) and take its incidence column \(a_e\) with a 1 for the used block in each mode. Let \(A\) be the incidence matrix and let \(u_{j,i}=\dim V_{j,i}/\dim V_j\). The following hold.

- The product special-linear norm infimum equals the infimum using only positive scalar operators on the blocks, with determinant one in each mode.
- The infimum is zero exactly when \(Ap=u,\ p\ge0\) is infeasible.
- The infimum is attained exactly when the same equations have a strictly positive solution. Over \(\mathbb C\), this is the criterion for the special-linear orbit to be closed.
- For every feasible instance the logarithm of the squared norm infimum is
  \[
  \max_{\substack{Ap=u\\p\ge0}}\sum_e p_e\log(c_e/p_e).
  \]

Thus two linear feasibility tests decide the null-cone and closed-orbit alternatives on this entire class, with no bound on the dimension of the relation space. This combines the support approach used for compact isomorphism with norm minimization on the full group.

## Why scalar blocks suffice

Work on the symmetric space of the product special-linear group modulo the compact groups on the left. The function \(F([g])=\|gT\|^2\) is geodesically convex. Indeed along a geodesic represented by \(\exp(sH)g\) with traceless self-adjoint generators in the modes, it is a sum of nonnegative multiples of real exponentials. The block-scalar operators give a totally geodesic flat. At every point of the flat, the marginal Grams are block scalar. There are no off-block contributions because two occupied blocks cannot differ in a single index, and each surviving contribution to a diagonal block is scalar by the individual Gram hypothesis. The differential in a Hermitian normal direction is thus zero, since the differential pairs the generators with those Grams.

For \(\lambda>0\) add \(\lambda\) times squared distance to the identity. This is still geodesically convex on the nonpositively curved symmetric space. Its restriction to the flat attains a minimum by coercivity. At that minimum its normal derivative vanishes as well, since the distance gradient to a point of the flat lies in the flat. The point is therefore a global minimum of the regularized function. Sending \(\lambda\) to zero shows that the flat infimum equals the full group infimum by comparison with each fixed group point. If the full infimum is attained at \(p\), the comparison also shows that each regularized flat minimizer is within distance \(\operatorname{dist}(p,1)\) of the identity. Taking a convergent subsequence produces an actual flat minimizer. Conversely any flat minimizer attains the common infimum.

## Evaluating the flat problem

A block-scalar direction has logarithms \(h_{j,i}\) with \(\sum_i h_{j,i}\dim V_{j,i}=0\) for each \(j\). If \(u\) is outside the convex hull of the incidence columns, strict linear separation and centering by the dimension-weighted mode means give such a direction with negative exponent on every occupied block. The norm tends to zero. If there is feasible \(p\), weighted arithmetic-geometric mean bounds every squared flat norm below by \(\prod_{p_e>0}(c_e/p_e)^{p_e}\), since the centered exponential contributions cancel. An actual flat minimizer has scalar marginals proportional to the identity in every mode by differentiating along the flat; its normalized block norms give a strictly positive feasible \(p\).

For the converse and the value statement, let \(I\) be the support of the minimal face of the column hull containing \(u\). Feasible weights are zero outside \(I\), and there are strictly positive feasible weights on \(I\). Maximize the displayed entropy expression, which is strictly concave and has its maximum positive on all of \(I\) by its inward derivative at a missing coordinate. Its derivative equations give \(\log(p_e/c_e)=2a_e^{\mathsf T}\ell\) on \(I\), for some \(\ell\), absorbing a constant into the row span. After centering \(\ell\) by the mode means, the resulting scalar-block action on the face tensor has squared norm \(\exp(\sum_e p_e\log(c_e/p_e))\). Choose a centered exposing direction of the minimal face, with exponent zero on \(I\) and negative outside. Its limit deletes exactly the outside blocks, giving the same value as an upper bound on the infimum of the original tensor. The lower bound was already proved by arithmetic-geometric mean. If \(I\) contains the entire support, the scaled face tensor is in the actual orbit and the infimum is attained. The closed-orbit interpretation uses the complex Kempf–Ness minimum norm criterion.

The supplied block conditions can be tested by support and Gram scans. In rational coordinate input the feasibility tests are polynomial bit-time linear programs even when the exact norm or scaling is more expensive to represent. For the coordinate case no decomposition is supplied. This is an informal proof.

There is also a subgroup conclusion when the individual blocks are not balanced. Retain only the condition that two occupied blocks cannot differ in one index. Then all marginal Grams remain block diagonal under invertible block-diagonal actions. The determinant-one block-diagonal subgroup determines a totally geodesic subspace containing the identity, and the same regularization has zero derivative normal to that subspace. The argument therefore proves that restriction to the whole block-diagonal subgroup preserves the norm infimum and its attainment. Scalar-block restriction and the support formulas above apply under the individual Gram hypothesis.
