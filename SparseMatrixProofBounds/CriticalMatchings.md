# Sharp sparse size, including the logarithmic factor, for diagonal conjugacy with a visible multiplicity gap

Work in ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) on the entries of $XA-BX,XY-I,YX-I$. Size counts the monomial occurrences on all used lines, including axioms, and linear combination has two premises. The field is arbitrary. Suppose the two displayed diagonal matrices have a fixed, nonempty list of mismatched eigenvalues with fixed nonzero integer multiplicity gaps. For each such eigenvalue, assume both multiplicities are at least $cn$ for a fixed $c>0$, where $n$ is the matrix side. If some gap is nonzero in the coefficient field, then the minimum sparse refutation size is
$$
\Theta(n^2\log(2n)).
$$
The lower bound allows proofs of arbitrary degree. There is no restriction on equal-count blocks. The hypotheses include characteristic zero. The rectangular inverse system $PQ=I_s,QP=I_r$ for $s=r+g$ and fixed $g>0$ has the same sharp order when $g$ is nonzero in the field. The lower bound below holds also when every gap vanishes in the field. [Published matrix equations](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

## A binary fan-in observation

Evaluate a purported PC proof at $t$ points, with all input polynomials supported at at most $b$ points apiece. A line's support here means the probe indices where its polynomial has nonzero value. Linear combinations have support contained in the union of the two parent supports. Multiplication by any variable can only shrink support. The last line is nonzero at every index. For $t$ sufficiently large compared with $b$, take the first line whose support has size at least $t/4$. All lines in the prefix ending there have supports of size at most $t/2$.

The sum of their support sizes is $\Omega(t\log(t/b))$ when $t/b$ is large. To prove this, trace each nonzero index of the selected line back to an axiom through a parent nonzero at that index. Choose one such path per index. At most $2^{d+1}$ different nodes in the dependency graph are reachable within $d$ edges backwards. Since each input supports at most $b$ indices, at most $b2^{d+1}$ indices can terminate at an input in this distance. Take $d$ a constant less than $\log_2(t/b)$ in the additive sense, for example $d=\lfloor\log_2(t/b)\rfloor-5$. A positive fraction of the $t$ indices have paths of length at least $d$. Each path visits any particular line at most once, and the distinct indices using that line lie in its support. This proves the bound even for a proof that shares earlier lines.

Distinguish one base probe, numbered zero. Let $V(f)$ be the number of indices where $f$ differs from its value at zero. For every line in this prefix, $V(f)$ is at least the support size: if the base value is zero this is immediate, and otherwise every zero entry differs from the base value and there are at least $t/2$ such entries. If $f$ is expanded as a sum of monomials, $V(f)$ is at most the sum of their variations. Thus the whole fixed proof has total monomial variation at least $\Omega(t\log(t/b))$ for each such family of probes.

## Random matchings with moving unmatched columns

In an $r$-by-$s$ board, $s=r+g>r$, choose a uniform injection $\pi$ of the rows into the columns and let $H$ be its $g$ unmatched columns. Pick $t-1\le\lfloor r/g\rfloor$ disjoint groups $T_j$ of $g$ rows by a uniform permutation of the rows, independently of $\pi$. At probe $j>0$, replace the partners of the rows in $T_j$ by a uniform bijection onto $H$, keeping the other edges. Write $\pi_0=\pi$. The unmatched column sets are $H$ at probe zero and $\pi(T_j)$ at probe $j$; they are mutually disjoint.

Set the two matrix maps on this board to its matching and its transpose. All rows are matched at each probe. Inverse errors are diagonal indicators of unmatched columns with a minus sign. In particular every individual input error on the board is supported at at most one probe.

For every fixed monomial in edge entries, uniformly in its degree, the expected variation is at most $4/s$. A conflicting support vanishes, powers of a Boolean edge agree with that edge, and constants have zero variation. Otherwise let the support be a $k$-edge matching, $1\le k\le r$. The base injection contains it with probability $1/(s)_k$, with $(s)_k=s(s-1)\cdots(s-k+1)$. On this event at most $k$ different probes can lose it, by disjointness of the row groups. Conversely, a matching newly present at $j$ must occur in $\pi_j$ and contain a row in $T_j$. Conditional on $T_j$, column permutation symmetry makes $\pi_j$ uniform over all injections. The chance of the required row intersection is at most $kg/r$. Summing the gains over $j$ costs at most $k/(s)_k$ as well. Thus the expected variation is at most $2k/(s)_k\le 4/s$.

## Assembly in the input coordinates

Use an independent board per discrepant eigenvalue, orienting each injection from the smaller eigenspace to the larger. Use the same $t$ probe indices on every board, with $t=1+\min_i\lfloor r_i/g_i\rfloor$ and unsigned gaps $g_i$. Set equal-count blocks to fixed inverse permutations, use zero on cross-label blocks, and put each board's matching in the two appropriate transposed positions. All intertwining equations vanish and every used inverse entry is supported at at most one probe. The fixed-gap and multiplicity hypotheses give $t=\Theta(n)$. For any original monomial, variation of its product across boards is bounded by the sum of the variations of its factors, where one factor is taken to be the *entire* local monomial on a given board. The number of discrepant types is bounded and $s_i\ge cn$. Thus the expected variation is $O(1/n)$ per monomial occurrence. Comparing expectation with the binary fan-in observation gives size $\Omega(n^2\log(2n))$. The very same one-board argument applies to the two-sided rectangular inverses.

For the upper bound select a label whose gap is nonzero in the field. In diagonal coordinates all forward-map entries crossing distinct labels have one-term linear derivations. In a diagonal inverse entry of the selected block, remove products containing these forward entries using single multiplications and balanced sums. This costs $O(n\log(2n))$ monomials per needed diagonal inverse entry. The two traces of the resulting rectangular products have identical quadratic parts and distinct constants; balance the two trace sums and subtract. In the original rectangular system the diagonal entries are already available. All these proofs use degree at most two. [Earlier sparse exponent and trace proof](SparseConjugacy.md)

## Bounded-support coordinates

The same sharp size holds after independent changes of the two input bases if every row and column of the basis matrices and their inverses has at most a fixed number $w$ of nonzero entries, and every occurring label, including the matched labels, has both multiplicities at least $cn$. Use independent uniformly random full permutations for the matched blocks. In the literal variables each forward equation vanishes and each inverse entry is a linear combination of at most $w^2$ old diagonal inverse errors. Thus its support is at most $w^2$. Each literal variable is a linear form on at most $W=w^2$ candidate edges, and each edge occurs in at most $2W$ forms by the row and column bounds. The forms have no constant parts before the random permutations are chosen.

When a monomial involves a bounded number of distinct variable names, all possible variations arise from changes in their bounded set of candidate edges. Each edge on a mismatched board has expected variation $O(1/n)$, and those in the matched boards are fixed across probes. Powers need no special treatment. Otherwise take two of its factors with disjoint candidate sets: each nonempty set meets at most $2W^2$ others, so more than $2W^2+1$ distinct names suffice; an empty set kills the monomial. For the product to be nonzero at any fixed probe, there must be a surviving edge in each of these two sets. For two distinct prescribed edges, incompatible edges have probability zero and compatible ones have probability $O(1/n^2)$ by the uniform injections and independence of different boards. The union over at most $W^2$ pairs still has that bound. Counting one copy of the base event per other probe, and all the individual probe events, gives expected variation $O(t/n^2)=O(1/n)$. This proves the same lower bound.

For the upper bound, substitute the basis changes in the degree-two diagonal proof. Each of its monomials expands by at most a constant factor; multiplication by a substituted variable is simulated using at most $W$ single multiplications and a sum. Each used intertwining input is a combination of boundedly many original intertwining entries of bounded support. At most $O(n^2)$ such entries are needed. Only $O(n)$ diagonal inverse inputs are used and each costs $O_w(n)$ to supply. Thus the same asymptotic size upper bound holds. The general result is a written proof. The estimates do not use Dirichlet GRH.
