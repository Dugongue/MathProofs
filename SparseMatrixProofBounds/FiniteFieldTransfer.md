# A same-field reduction from arbitrary conjugacy to nonsingular nonsymmetric congruence

Over every finite field $F$, two matrices $M,N$ of side $n$ give two invertible nonsymmetric forms of side $10n$ over **the same field**, congruent over an extension exactly when $M,N$ are conjugate over that extension. If the forms have an ordinary sparse PC refutation of size $L$, the original conjugacy equations have a refutation of size at most $L+O(n^5\log(2n))$. The constants are independent of the field. The reduction permits arbitrary input matrices, ranks, and original bases. Together with the common-shift construction over infinite fields, this makes lower bounds above the additive overhead on unrestricted conjugacy into lower bounds for the nonsingular nonsymmetric congruence problem over any field. No coordinate field equations or algebraic extension constants are introduced in the PC proof. [Original systems](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

**Choosing tags.** Write $F=\mathbb F_q$. For each irreducible polynomial of degree $d$ in the two characteristic polynomials choose a different Frobenius orbit of nonzero elements of degree $5d$. Require that no two selected elements, including two in the same orbit, be reciprocals. This is always possible. There are at most $q^d/d$ input polynomials of degree $d$. Put $l=5d$. The number of elements of degree smaller than $l$ in $\mathbb F_{q^l}$ is at most $\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor l/2\rfloor}q^j\le2q^{l/2}$. If inversion preserves a full-degree orbit it must act by a shift of order two on that orbit: the identity would imply $x^2=1$. This can happen only for even $l$, in which case $x^{q^{l/2}+1}=1$. After these exclusions at least $q^l-3q^{l/2}-1$ elements remain. Divide by $2l$ for full orbits paired under inversion. For $Q=q^d\ge3$, $Q^5-3Q^{5/2}-1\ge10Q$, so this supplies enough pairs. In the remaining case $q=2,d=1$ there are 30 full-degree elements in the degree-five extension, giving three pairs for the two possible linear inputs. Orbits of different degrees cannot meet under inversion.

For each chosen representative $\lambda$ of a source or target eigenvalue orbit, identify $F(\lambda)$ with the subfield of size $q^d$ in the closure. Choose a representative $\alpha$ of the assigned tag orbit. Its minimal polynomial over this subfield has degree five. Assign this monic polynomial to $\lambda$ and assign its Frobenius conjugates to the conjugates of $\lambda$. Their root sets are disjoint, since $\alpha$ has degree $5d$. The five coefficients below its leading coefficient therefore come from five scalar polynomials on the semisimple spectrum, over $F$, by polynomial remainder interpolation at the distinct irreducible factors. Use the same choice for both input matrices.

**Preserving all Jordan data.** Write $M=M_s+D_M$ with $M_s$ semisimple and $D_M$ nilpotent commuting. Over the finite field $M_s$ is a polynomial in $M$, and the *same* polynomial works for $N$. Indeed on each length-$e$ primary factor of degree $d$ take $t^{q^j}$ with $j$ a multiple of $d$ and $q^j\ge e$, and combine the choices modulo the corresponding primary powers for both inputs by the Chinese remainder theorem. Evaluate the five coefficient polynomials at $M_s$. Form their five-by-five block companion on five copies of the original space and add $\operatorname{diag}(D_M,\ldots,D_M)$; call the resulting matrix $T_M$. Its entries in each block are scalar polynomials in $M$ and use the same polynomial for $N$. Over the algebraic closure, on each $\lambda$-primary part the scalar companion diagonalizes with the five distinct tagged eigenvalues. The commuting nilpotent part gives precisely the original Jordan lengths at *each* of those five tags. Different original eigenvalues have disjoint tags. Thus similarity of $T_M,T_N$ is equivalent to similarity of $M,N$. Similarity descends to any intermediate base field by invariant factors. The tags also ensure invertibility and separation of the two spectra under inversion.

**Passing to the literal proof systems.** Use the forms $K(T)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&I\\T&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. They are invertible and nonsymmetric since none of the tags equals one. In a congruence $XK(T_M)X^T=K(T_N)$ with $Y=X^{-1}$, both identities $XK(T_M)=K(T_N)Y^T$ and $XK(T_M)^T=K(T_N)^TY^T$ hold. Its two off-diagonal blocks $C,D$ satisfy $C=T_N^T C T_M$, $D=T_N D T_M^T$. Spectral separation forces both zero. The lower-right block of $X$ is invertible and intertwines $T_M,T_N$. Conversely a conjugating matrix $Z$ gives the congruence $\operatorname{diag}(Z^{-T},Z)$. This proves the witness equivalence over every extension. [Earlier hyperbolic calculation](SparseTransfer.md)

For the size statement start with the original variable matrices $Z,W$ and set $Z'=I_5\otimes Z$, $W'=I_5\otimes W$. Substitute $X=\operatorname{diag}((W')^T,Z')$, $Y=\operatorname{diag}((Z')^T,W')$. Each variable becomes one variable or zero, irrespective of the input basis. The block entries of $Z'T_M-T_N Z'$ are consequences of $H=ZM-NZ$ of degree one. For a scalar polynomial $f$, telescope $Z f(M)-f(N)Z$ through $H$; it is a field-linear combination entrywise of the original $n^2$ forward entries. Any scalar polynomial used may be reduced modulo the common multiple of the minimal polynomials. To generate all needed lines, each such combination uses at most $n^2$ original lines of $O(n)$ monomials. Balanced binary summation costs $O(n^3\log(2n))$ per combination, and there are $O(n^2)$ output entries. The specialized Gram block $Z'T_M W'-T_N$ follows by right multiplication of the prepared equations by $W'$ and using $Z'W'-I$. An entry of the prepared matrix before this multiplication has $O(n)$ monomials. The other Gram and inverse blocks are inverse equations or zero. These preprocessing steps are covered by the displayed additive bound and degree two. Replaying the given proof costs at most $L$ including cancellations. Thus even finite fields with no scalar shift separating a given pair admit a linear-side same-field sparse hardness transfer.
