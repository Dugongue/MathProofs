# When every forward input is indispensable for conjugacy

Let $A,B$ be geometrically nonconjugate matrices of side $n>0$ over a field $F$. For the ordinary PC system $H=XA-BX=0$, $XY=YX=I$, every refutation uses **all $n^2$ individual entries of $H$** if and only if the characteristic polynomials of $A,B$ are coprime, every standard column $e_i$ is cyclic for $B$, and every standard row $e_j^T$ is cyclic as a row for $A$. Cyclic means that the successive powers up to $n-1$ span the whole vector or covector space. The criterion uses the displayed coordinates over the original field. [Published system](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

An entry is indispensable exactly when deleting it leaves a geometrically consistent system. Ordinary PC is sound, and inconsistency gives a Nullstellensatz identity which descends to the base field by coefficient linear algebra. If deleting the $(i,j)$ entry is consistent, the resulting invertible $X$ has $XA-BX$ a nonzero scalar multiple of $E_{ij}$, since the original pair is nonconjugate. Thus if every input is indispensable, the image of the Sylvester linear map $T:X\mapsto XA-BX$ contains all the matrix units over the closure. Surjectivity of $T$ is equivalent to disjoint spectra: after triangularizing the constant matrices its eigenvalues are all the source-target eigenvalue differences. This also establishes invertibility over $F$.

For disjoint spectra let $Z$ solve $ZA-BZ=u v^T$ for nonzero $u,v$. Then $Z$ is invertible exactly when $u$ and $v^T$ are cyclic for $B$ and the row action of $A$ respectively. For sufficiency suppose first that $u\in\operatorname{im} Z$. The image is then $B$-stable by the displayed equation, so it contains the cyclic span of $u$ and is full. Otherwise for $w\in\ker Z$ we have $ZAw=u v^Tw\in\operatorname{im}Z$. Thus $v^Tw=0$ and $Aw\in\ker Z$. The kernel is killed by the entire cyclic row span of $v^T$ and is zero. For necessity, quotient the target by the $B$-cyclic span of $u$. The induced map from the source represented by $Z$ satisfies a homogeneous intertwining equation, and must vanish by disjoint spectra. If $Z$ was onto, that quotient was zero. Apply the same argument to the transpose with source and target exchanged. This is the rank-one case of the classical controllability and observability test for a nonsingular Sylvester solution. [Sylvester-observer criterion, Theorem 3.5](https://doi.org/10.1155/2011/184314)

Apply this test to $u=e_i$, $v=e_j$. In the coprime case there is an invertible omission witness if and only if these two specific vectors are cyclic. In particular the exact count of individually indispensable entries is the product of the two counts of cyclic standard vectors. This proves the theorem, including necessity of spectral separation for simultaneous indispensability. A pair of distinct irreducible characteristic polynomials furnishes an application: every nonzero base-field vector is cyclic for the corresponding constant matrix. Consequently all $n^2$ displayed forward equations are indispensable in **every** choice of bases over $F$.

## An integral equal-trace family in every characteristic

Let $n=4s+1$, $s\ge1$. Write $J$ for the upper-shift regular nilpotent Jordan matrix, $u=(0,1,\ldots,1)^T$ and $v=(1,0,-1,0,1,0,-1,\ldots,1)^T$. Both $v^T u$ and $v^T J u$ are zero as integers. With $S=I+uv^T$ put $A=SJS^{-1}=J+uv^TJ-(Ju)v^T$. Take $B$ to be the cyclic permutation matrix on $n$ basis vectors. Its characteristic polynomial is $t^n-1$ and that of $A$ is $t^n$. Every standard vector is cyclic for $B$. The kernel of $A$ is generated by $Se_1$, the all-ones vector. A row is cyclic for a single nilpotent Jordan block precisely when it does not vanish on its kernel, so all standard rows are cyclic for $A$. All $n^2$ entries are individually indispensable in all characteristics.

In the middle rows $2,\ldots,n-1$ of $A$, the entries away from the upper-shift position in column $j$ are $v_{j-1}-v_j=\pm1$, with $v_0=0$. Thus every displayed forward input contains $\Omega(n)$ monomials: terms from column $j$ of $A$ with variable column different from $j$ cannot cancel against any term of $(BX)_{ij}$. This gives size $\Omega(n^3)$. All traces of positive powers below $n$ agree for the two inputs.

For the matching size upper bound let $w=Se_1$. Derive $Hw=-BXw$ row by row as a balanced sum of raw linear inputs. The support in a partial row sum over $m$ columns has at most $n+m$ monomials since $B$ is a permutation. Summation over all levels costs $O(n^2)$ for each of $n$ rows. Permuting the derived lines gives every entry of $Xw=0$. Multiply by the entries of a single row of $Y$, sum, and use the same row of $(YX-I)w=0$. The final cost is $O(n^2\log(2n))$ and the constant obtained is nonzero. Hence these equal-trace systems have **$\Theta(n^3)$ sparse size** and degree two, with no logarithmic gap. This upper construction more generally works for $Au=\lambda u$ over $F$ when $B-\lambda I$ and its inverse both have uniformly bounded row support. These assertions are written proofs.
