# Relabeling orbits and sparse Polynomial Calculus size

For integers $1\le r<s$, consider the two-sided rectangular inverse equations $PQ=I_r$, $QP=I_s$, with $P$ of shape $r\times s$. Write $G=S_r\times S_s$ for independent coordinate relabelings. The footprint of an ordinary Polynomial Calculus refutation is the set of all monomials occurring in its lines. Let $I$ be the number of distinct translates of its footprint under $G$, and let $S$ count all monomial occurrences in the proof. Define $b=p^{v_p(s-r)}$ in characteristic $p>0$ and $b=1$ in characteristic zero.

**Theorem.** For every positive $k\le b$ with $2k\le r$,
$$ I S\ \ge\ \binom{r}{k}(s)_k. $$
Here $(s)_k=s(s-1)\cdots(s-k+1)$. If only the larger side is relabeled, its footprint orbit count $I_s$ satisfies $I_s S\ge(s)_k$. In particular, suppose $r,s,b$ are proportional to a parameter $n$. Among proofs with $I\le\exp(Cn)$, for any fixed $C\ge0$, the minimum sparse size is $n^{\Theta(n)}$. The same conclusion holds if the orbit condition refers only to the larger side.

**Proof.** Set $P_{ij}=Q_{ji}=x_{ij}$. Reduce all lines after each step using $x_e^2=x_e$ and $x_ex_f=0$ for distinct incident edges. The resulting matching quotient has a basis of matchings. A multiplication by a variable becomes multiplication by its edge and immediate normalization. Inputs become row or column totalities and zero. Set $j=k-1$. The normalized design argument in the linked proof supplies a nonzero matching of size $m\ge k$ occurring in some normalized line. In detail, the existence and extension of order-$j$ designs hold on the original board and on the board with both endpoints of one edge deleted, since $j<b$ and $2j\le r-1$. The matching-degree obstruction for PC follows by lifting a conditioned order-$(j-1)$ design at a multiplied edge and using an order-$j$ extension. For $j=0$, a calculation confined to constants cannot use any nonzero totality. [Matching-normalization lemma and its proof](SparseConjugacy.md) · [Riis, Sections 14.1–14.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07280v1#S14)

Each original monomial maps to zero or a single matching monomial. Let $W$ be the set of all nonzero monomial images of the footprint, allowing also individual images that cancel within a line. It contains the matching just found. The map is equivariant. Hence the union of all translates of $W$ contains the full orbit of that matching and has at most $I S$ members. The full orbit has $f(m)=\binom r m(s)_m$ members. Relabeling only columns gives an orbit of size $(s)_m\ge (s)_k$.

To minimize $f(m)$ for $k\le m\le r$, its successive ratio $(r-m)(s-m)/(m+1)$ decreases, so a minimum is attained at an endpoint. With $u=r-k\ge k$,
$$ \frac{f(r)}{f(k)}=\frac{(s-k)_u}{\binom r k}\ \ge\ \frac{(u+1)!}{\binom{2u}u}\ \ge\ 1. $$
The last inequality follows by induction from $\binom21=2!$ since the ratio of successive middle binomial coefficients is $2(2u+1)/(u+1)\le u+2$ for $u\ge1$. Thus $f(m)\ge f(k)$, as asserted.

For proportional $r,s,b$ take $k=\min(b,\lfloor r/2\rfloor)=\Theta(n)$. Then $(s)_k\ge(s/2)^k$, and division by $\exp(Cn)$ preserves $n^{\Omega(n)}$. An ordinary refutation of size $n^{O(n)}$ follows from the cofactor descent on the first $r+1$ columns of $P$: multiplication by the corresponding $r+1$ rows of $Q$ gives $I_{r+1}$. Alternatively the principal-minor proof uses degree $2b=O(n)$ and size $n^{O(n)}$. To obtain invariant footprint, append copies of the proof under all coordinate permutations in the group, retaining each copy's inputs and stopping at a final conclusion $1$. If a convention stops at the first $1$, run all translated derivations through the premises of their final constant lines, then conclude with one copy's final step; earlier nonzero constant lines can first be pruned by truncating the chosen source derivation at its first nonzero constant. For a convention that charges only ancestors of the last line, start from the ancestor subproof of a first nonzero constant and omit its final inference in every copy. From each translated final premise derive zero by subtracting the premise from itself. Sum these zeros and add the sum to a final premise of the original copy before its last inference. All translated ancestors now feed the conclusion, and the extra lines introduce no new monomial names. Translating constants adds only the common constant monomial. The group order is at most $(r!)(s!)=n^{O(n)}$, so the upper bound is preserved. [Rectangular inverse equations and matrix proof complexity](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

## Diagonal conjugacy with several unequal blocks

Consider $XA=BX,\ XY=YX=I$ for displayed diagonal $A,B$ of common side $n$. For every discrepant label $a$, let $r_a<s_a$ be its unordered pair of multiplicities. Assume $r_a\ge2$ and choose positive $k_a\le b_a$ with $2k_a\le r_a$, using the characteristic rule above for $s_a-r_a$. Let $G$ independently permute coordinates of each of these two multiplicity sets for each discrepant label, fixing all other coordinates. A coordinate permutation for the $A$-side acts on the columns of $X$ and rows of $Y$, and a $B$-side permutation acts on the rows of $X$ and columns of $Y$. Then every refutation satisfies
$$ I S\ \ge\ \min_a\binom{r_a}{k_a}(s_a)_{k_a}. $$

Indeed, set $X$ to an independent rectangular matching board on each discrepant block (transposing to put its smaller side first as necessary). Set its reverse $Y$ block to the transpose. Put fixed inverse bijections on corresponding equal-multiplicity blocks and set entries between unequal labels to zero. These assignments commute with $G$. The product-design matching-normalization lemma guarantees a matching tuple surviving on some line with $m_a\ge k_a$ on at least one board. Its orbit has at least $\binom{r_a}{m_a}(s_a)_{m_a}$ members by acting on that board alone. The same footprint count and endpoint estimate apply. Under relabeling just the larger side of each discrepant board, the lower bound becomes $\min_a(s_a)_{k_a}$. For a nonempty list of discrepant blocks with $r_a,b_a$ uniformly proportional to $n$, this gives the sharp $n^{\Theta(n)}$ size bound under either orbit condition, using a single discrepant block for the usual principal-minor upper bound and symmetrizing as above. The permutation group is a subgroup of $S_n\times S_n$. [Diagonal product-design proof and upper bound](SparseConjugacy.md)

The orbit factor is part of the hypothesis of the sharp-size conclusion. An arbitrary short proof can have many distinct relabelings. These bounds supply no factorial sparse-size lower bound for ordinary proofs without a footprint orbit constraint. The argument uses no prime-distribution hypothesis.
