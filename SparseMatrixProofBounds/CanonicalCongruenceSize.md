# Exact sparse exponents for canonical matrix congruence families

Consider two bilinear forms displayed in the same list of congruence canonical block representatives over the coefficient field, each block of side at most a fixed $d$. Use one representative per type on both sides and the normalized alternating flags in characteristic two, with no conflicting flags. Suppose the signed counts of all discrepant types have a fixed nonempty list of nonzero gaps $g_i$, and each smaller discrepant count is at least $cn$ for fixed $c>0$, where $n$ is the common matrix side. Equal-count blocks are unrestricted subject to the side bound on each block. The ordinary Polynomial Calculus sparse size $S$ is measured on the original entries of $XAX^T=B$, $XY=YX=I$. [Published question](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

**Theorem.** In positive characteristic put $q=\min_i p^{v_p(|g_i|)}$. Whenever $q\ge2$,
$$
 \Omega(n^{2q})\le S\le O(n^{2q}\log(2n)).
$$
The lower bound allows proofs of unrestricted degree. The theorem includes matrices with singular and nonsymmetric blocks, as well as characteristic two. For two diagonal projections with ranks and nullities proportional to $n$ and rank gap four, this gives sparse exponent eight in characteristic two. The constants in these estimates may depend on the collection of fixed gaps, $d$, and $c$.

**Lower bound.** For a discrepant type with block $C$ on both sides, specialize the corresponding copy coordinates of $X$ to $P\otimes I_{\dim C}$, and $Y$ to $P^T\otimes I_{\dim C}$. Other cross blocks vanish and matched components use identity matrices. Every original variable becomes zero, one, or a single entry. Taking $P$ to be the matrix of partial matching variables on the copy board, modulo Boolean and collision relations, sends both inverse products to ordinary row and column totalities. The Gram input becomes zero or a scalar multiple of one such totality, since its entries are the coefficients of $C$ times entries of $PP^T-I$. The copy-index construction also works for a doubled symplectic block since the unit passed through the specialization is its whole original block.

Apply the live-board restriction proof for diagonal conjugacy to these disjoint boards. Explicitly for side counts $r,r+|g_i|$, let $b_i=p^{v_p(|g_i|)}$, retain independent uniform sets of $2b_i$ rows and $2b_i+|g_i|$ columns and pin their complements by a uniform bijection. Riis's normalized matching designs through degree $b_i-1$ and extension from order $b_i-2$ on the one-edge complement show that every proof, after immediate normalization, must contain a matching term with $b_i$ live edges on some board. Products of the designs handle simultaneous boards. A fixed original occurrence has probability $O(n^{-2b_i})$ of doing so on board $i$: if it uses fewer than $2b_i$ distinct matching positions, then $b_i$ of them must have both endpoints live. If it uses at least $2b_i$ distinct compatible edges, all of $2b_i$ designated edges must be live or pinned, at cost $O(n^{-2b_i})$ by independent live-row choices and the complementary random bijection. Colliding positions have zero image. Summing over the fixed list of boards and all original monomial occurrences gives the claim. The cited diagonal theorem gives the multiplication and immediate-normalization argument in full. [Diagonal matching proof](SparseConjugacy.md) · [Riis's design construction, Section 14](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07280v1#S14)

**Canonical-coordinate preparation.** The entry matrices of $A,B$ have at most $d$ nonzero entries per row and column, so each Gram entry has $O_d(n)$ terms. The identities
$$
 XA-BY^T=(XAX^T-B)Y^T-XA(YX-I)^T
$$
and their constant-form transposes yield all the true-intertwiner equations for the transpose-pair quiver, at sparse cost $O_d(n^4\log(2n))$. Each of the $O(n^2)$ entries on the left uses $O_d(n)$ variable multiples of raw Gram or inverse entries on the right, with at most $O_d(n)$ monomials per term. Binary balanced sums include all intermediate expanded lines.

Project to the space of genuine intertwiners between the two-arrow representations. This projection is done separately on each pair of original input blocks: the entry equations for the pair involve $O_d(1)$ variables, and projecting to their kernel changes each entry by a combination of $O_d(1)$ already prepared lines. Use the adjoint of the projected forward map for the reverse map. For each entry of the two projected inverse products minus identity, telescope the difference from the original products. This needs $O_d(n)$ bounded-size terms, each obtained from a projected error and single-variable multiplications. The entry itself has $O_d(n)$ monomials. Passing to the residue type on a selected pair of bounded-size original blocks is a fixed linear map on their $O_d(1)$ coordinates. Thus obtaining every needed entry of the residual inverse products costs at most $O_d(n^3\log(2n))$ after preparation. One way to see localization of this last step is to apply a residue coordinate just to an individual pair of copies of the same block; products factor through every intermediate block, while components through other quiver types vanish in the radical quotient. A doubled block has just two equal underlying quiver types, still bounded independently of $n$. All residual map entries are linear forms of sparsity $O_d(1)$. This is the usual multiplicity quotient by the radical of the endomorphism algebra, applied blockwise. The exact-degree proofs describe both quadratic relations and the symplectic construction. **Refutation in the selected multiplicity space.** Choose $i$ with $b_i=q$. For a nonselfdual pair or a symmetric multiplicity form use both rectangular inverse products. The sums of their size-$q$ principal minors agree by Cauchy–Binet. At identity the two constants differ: the coefficient of $z^q$ in $(1+z)^{r+|g_i|}-(1+z)^r$ is nonzero by the definition of $q$. The multiplicity forms on the normalized identical copies can be taken as identities in the symmetric case, up to a common scalar. For a symplectic type in any characteristic use the two symplectic Gram products of the $2r$- and $2(r+|g_i|)$-dimensional multiplicity spaces. In a standard hyperbolic basis the sums of Pfaffians of the $2q$-row principal submatrices indexed by $q$ full planes agree by the Pfaffian minor-summation identity. Indeed expansion of each product selects a $q$-plane subset on the opposite side, with the common double sum of ordinary $2q$-minors of the map. Use compatible basis order and signs on both sides. The constants at the two standard forms are the same two binomial coefficients. This comparison remains valid in characteristic two. [Integral Pfaffian identity and canonical application](BinaryCongruence.md)

In either comparison, only $O_q(n^q)$ determinant or Pfaffian terms occur, since $q$ is fixed. Each term is a product of $q$ quadratic entries of sparsity $O_d(n)$. To replace these products by their constants, expand one factor at a time and multiply its quadratic monomials by the two individual variables with a binary summation tree. At stage $j$ the expanded product has $O_{q,d}(n^j)$ occurrences. The standard telescoping recurrence for the difference of products accumulates all signed terms in $O_{q,d}(n^{2q}\log(2n))$ total sparse size. All preparation fits this bound because $q\ge2$.

**A cubic lower estimate at smaller gaps for diagonal rank inputs.** Suppose $A$ is a diagonal projection of rank $r$, $B$ a diagonal projection of rank $s$, $r<s$, and $r,s$ are proportional to $n$ with fixed gap $g=s-r>0$. Form the graph on the $s$ active target coordinates whose edges are pairs with neither ordered Gram constraint used by a particular proof. A matching of size $g$ in this graph would give a completion by setting both entries in each pair to one. Its $g$ two-by-two all-ones blocks each lose one unit of rank and are congruent to a rank-one diagonal block, over every field. The result is congruent to $A$, contradicting soundness. The endpoints of a maximal matching of size below $g$ cover the graph, which therefore has at most $2(g-1)s$ edges. Hence $\binom{s}{2}-O_g(s)$ distinct off-diagonal Gram polynomials must be used. Each has $r$ monomials, giving $\Omega(n^3)$. If $r>s$ and the ranks and nullities are all proportional to $n$, use the analogous free bipartite graph between the $s$ active and the $n-s$ inactive target coordinates. Adding ones on a disjoint active-inactive pair changes the principal block from $\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ to $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, increasing rank by one. Over the closure the resulting form is symmetric and nonalternating, including in characteristic two, and so is congruent to the diagonal projection of its new rank. The same matching argument forces $s(n-s)-O_g(n)$ used pairs and again gives $\Omega(n^3)$. For $q=1$ these two orientations have upper bound $O(n^4\log(2n))$ by the preceding preparation and the residual trace or first Pfaffian comparison. A lower-cost construction for this particular regime is not part of the present theorem.
