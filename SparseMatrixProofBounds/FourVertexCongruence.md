# A four-vertex obstruction and the sharp canonical congruence exponent

Consider displayed split congruence canonical block families as in [the canonical upper bound](CanonicalCongruenceSize.md). Matrices of side $n$ use bounded-side block representatives on the original coordinates, share each discrepant type on both sides with positive multiplicities at least $cn$, and have a fixed finite nonempty list of signed count differences $g_i$. The same normalized flags and representative are used on both sides. Each original variable is a literal matrix entry, and every monomial occurrence in every used ordinary PC line counts toward size. Equal-multiplicity types can be arbitrary subject to the same bound on block sides. Set $q=1$ in characteristic zero and $q=\min_i p^{v_p(|g_i|)}$ in characteristic $p$. Then the minimum sparse size has sharp exponent
$$
 \boxed{\max(4,2q)}.
$$
More precisely the lower bound is $\Omega(n^{\max(4,2q)})$ and the upper bound is $O(n^{\max(4,2q)}\log(2n))$. This includes geometric noncongruence for symmetric, alternating and nonsymmetric canonical blocks in every characteristic. Unlike the trace proof for the diagonal *conjugacy* system, even a single rank gap of one in diagonal projections with both ranks and nullities proportional to $n$ has congruence exponent **four**. All lower bounds cover unrestricted PC degree. [Question and original variables](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

The previous matching-design lower bound gives exponent $2q$. The same canonical-coordinate preparation costs $O(n^4\log(2n))$ before the trace or Pfaffian coefficient argument and applies also for $q=1$. This proves the required upper bound in every case. We prove a quartic lower bound without matching designs by allowing all cross-type entries in a small residual system.

## A local invariant of the literal variables

View each individual source block copy and each individual target block copy as a vertex, with the two sets disjoint. All entries of the forward block from a source vertex $a$ to a target vertex $j$, and the corresponding inverse block, have endpoints $a,j$. A monomial touches a vertex if any of its factors touches it; exponents and internal coordinates do not count as extra vertices.

Partition the two sets of vertices into classes of identical block copies, of sizes $c_a$ on the source and $r_j$ on the target. Hereafter $a,j$ index *classes* in formulas for constants, and individual vertices in the parity argument are distinguished even within a class. Suppose all classes have size at least two. Assume there are arrays $M_{ja},N_{aj}$ over a field extension, of the respective forward and reverse matrix shapes, with
$$
 \sum_a c_a M_{ja} C_a M_{ja}^T = B_j,\qquad
 \sum_a c_a M_{ja}N_{aj}=I,\qquad
 \sum_j r_j N_{aj}M_{ja}=I
$$
for every appropriate class. Here $C_a$ and $B_j$ are the source and target blocks of the classes. These formulas concern only diagonal vertex blocks. The constants need not satisfy any of the off-diagonal vertex-block equations. We show that **every PC refutation contains a monomial touching at least four vertices**.

Give each variable parity one at both endpoints, extending by addition modulo two on products. This is a formal grading of the polynomial ring, valid also in characteristic two. A polynomial all of whose individual monomials touch at most three vertices decomposes into four kinds of parity components: no odd vertices, one odd source and one odd target, two distinct odd targets, or two distinct odd sources. In the first two kinds impose the condition that the component evaluates to zero under the literal class-constant assignment $X\mapsto M, Y\mapsto N$. For a component of the third kind, with odd targets $i,j$, every monomial involves exactly one source vertex. Record a vector indexed by source vertices by evaluating separately the monomials at each such vertex. Require that this vector be constant within every source class. Use the transposed condition for a pair of odd sources. All these conditions are linear.

The three identities ensure that every input axiom of parity zero meets its evaluation condition. For an off-diagonal vertex-block axiom, its evaluation vector on the opposite side is constant within each class by construction. There are no other input parities. The final scalar one fails the condition.

Consider a legal multiplication by a variable $z$ between two lines on which all monomials touch at most three vertices. Multiplication by $z$ is injective on monomials, and adds a fixed parity vector to each component. Thus there can be no cancellation between input monomials in checking this support bound. If old and new grades both require just zero total evaluation, multiplication preserves the condition. If a component with zero total evaluation acquires two odd targets, the old grade was an odd edge $(i,k)$, and $z$ joins a second target $j$ to that same source $k$. Every surviving monomial in the product touches the two targets, and can therefore use only the single source $k$. Thus the evaluation vector is supported at $k$ and that entry is zero by multiplicativity of evaluation. The argument for two odd sources is identical. If an old component has two odd targets, any multiplication meeting the width bound must touch one of those targets, and all monomials of the old component must already use the particular source of $z$. Its evaluation vector is supported at that one source, so is zero because the class of that source has at least two members. The new component then has zero total evaluation. A multiplication not touching either target would produce at least four vertices and cannot occur for a nonzero component. This exhausts the grades. Consequently the stated conditions are preserved even when linear combinations produce degree falls, regardless of monomial powers.

## Building the diagonal-block assignment

Retain only discrepant types and use identities on matched types. Write $s_i$ for each retained block side and choose one of maximum side $D$. We shall leave boundedly many live copies of each type, with target count minus source count equal to the original signed difference. This is possible with equal numbers of pinned copies in each type. The class sizes below may depend on the fixed gaps and the characteristic. They are independent of $n$.

Take one dedicated source class per type, with a large size at least two that is nonzero in the field. For the maximum-side type also take six extra source classes in three equal-size pairs, each class of size at least two nonzero in the field. Choose the dedicated sizes large enough that the desired target counts are all large positive integers. Split them into target classes of size at least two, nonzero in the field, with at least two target classes of the chosen maximum-side type. This is possible for all sufficiently large totals: choose two coprime positive sizes at least two avoiding the characteristic, such as $p+1,2p+1$ in characteristic $p$, and use the elementary coin bound. All chosen counts fit in the original arrays for large $n$. The signed dimension sum is still zero because the original side lengths agree and the matching types cancel. Work over the algebraic closure for constructing the arrays.

For each target class put $M_{ja}=c_a^{-1/2}I$ on its same-type dedicated source class, and zero on the other dedicated classes. This already gives the prescribed Gram block. On each pair of extra source classes put maps $T$ and $\lambda T$, where $\lambda^2=-1$. Their Gram contributions cancel in every characteristic, including two. The following choices of $T$ ensure the only systems of square matrices $P_j,Q_a$ with $P_jM_{ja}=M_{ja}Q_a$ for all edges are a common scalar times identity.

On a first maximum-side target class use $T=I_D$ for all three extra pairs, identifying all six endomorphisms with its $P$. On a second maximum-side target use identity for the first pair, a diagonal matrix with distinct diagonal entries for the second, and a cyclic coordinate permutation for the third. Thus $P$ commutes with both the diagonal and the cycle, which forces a scalar. This includes $D=1$. Every remaining target class attaches to the first pair by choosing a full-row-rank $T$, possible since its dimension is at most $D$. The other free maps may be zero. Thus all target endomorphisms equal the same scalar; the nonzero scaled identities to dedicated classes enforce it for every remaining source endomorphism.

To obtain $N$, first solve the linear system in $Z_{aj}$ with right sides $r_j I$ and $c_a I$,
$$
 \sum_a M_{ja} Z_{aj}=r_j I,\qquad \sum_j Z_{aj} M_{ja}=c_a I .
$$
Under the nondegenerate trace pairings on matrix spaces, the annihilating linear functionals, with a minus sign on the second component, are exactly $P_j,Q_a$ satisfying the preceding intertwiner relations. The right sides satisfy the single scalar compatibility since the total dimensions agree. Therefore the system is solvable in every characteristic. Put $N_{aj}=Z_{aj}/(r_jc_a)$. This constructs every constant required by the local invariant; there is no assumed auxiliary gadget.

## Restricting an unrestricted-degree sparse proof

In each discrepant type choose live source and target sets uniformly and independently of their respective prescribed sizes. Match the complements by an independent uniform bijection within that type, assigning identity matrices on its pinned blocks in both directions. Across two live endpoints retain **all** original variables, including those between different types. Assign zero to every other block. On types with equal counts specialize matched identities and zero cross terms from the outset. Each raw input becomes zero or a literal input of the live system, because the two input forms are block diagonal. Every variable becomes zero, one, or a single variable. A proof therefore specializes line by line without increasing any support. By the local invariant, on every restriction some original monomial occurrence must survive with at least four distinct live copy vertices in its support graph.

For a fixed occurrence the probability of this event is $O(n^{-4})$. Collapse all factors on the same pair of copy vertices to one edge, including inverse-direction occurrences. A component with more than one edge must be entirely live for the monomial to be nonzero, since any pinned edge is isolated from every other nonzero incident edge. If a component has at least four vertices, specify four of them in advance. Their joint live probability is $O(n^{-4})$ because all involved types have linearly many original copies and bounded live size, with independent sets on the two sides. Otherwise every component has at most three vertices. If there are at most three components, there are at most nine vertices; sum over their boundedly many four-vertex subsets. If there are at least four components, choose one edge from each of four different components. These edges form a matching. For the whole monomial to survive they must each be live or pinned. Specify which are live. The needed live source vertices alone cost $O(n^{-1})$ each; conditional on the selected vertex sets, the pinned edges require specified complementary bijection values, at cost $O(n^{-1})$ each, or are impossible across distinct types. Sum over the bounded list of choices. This proves the estimate also for high powers and arbitrarily large original support. The union bound over occurrences gives $\Omega(n^4)$.

This proof is a direct argument for the original PC equations. Its class-constant evaluation invariant and quartic lower bound do not use a field equation or a degree bound. The general exponent statement and its assembly are written mathematics.
