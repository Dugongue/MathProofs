# Exact sparse cost at minimum degree for a rectangular trace discrepancy

Let $1\le r<s$, and suppose the field element $s-r$ is nonzero. The two-sided rectangular equations on independent entries of $P$ of shape $s$ by $r$ and $Q$ of shape $r$ by $s$ are $PQ=I_s$ and $QP=I_r$. Their minimum degree in ordinary Polynomial Calculus is two. The minimum sparse size **among degree-two refutations** is
$$
\Theta\big(rs\log(2s)\big).
$$
The constants are absolute. This includes large gaps and unbounded aspect ratio. Proof size counts expanded monomials with multiplicity across proof lines and uses binary linear combination.

**Upper bound.** Each of the $s+r$ diagonal input equations contributes at most its number of product summands plus one. The two traces have the same quadratic part. Sum and subtract the diagonal lines with a balanced binary tree. Each level costs $O(rs)$ and the nonzero resulting scalar gives a contradiction. Each input is genuinely quadratic, so degree zero or one cannot suffice.

**Lower bound.** Stop at the first derived nonzero constant. All previous nonzero lines have degree two: inputs have only quadratic and constant parts, and until a constant appears scalar linear combinations cannot produce a linear term. Multiplication of a nonzero such line by a variable would have degree three. Thus only scalar linear combinations need be tracked. Discard the quadratic monomials other than $P_{ji}Q_{ij}$ for $i\in[r]$ and $j\in[s]$, retaining the constant. The off-diagonal inputs now have zero image. Write $t_{ij}=P_{ji}Q_{ij}$. Any remaining image has coefficients and constant of the form
$$
 \sum_{i,j}(a_i+b_j)t_{ij}-\sum_i a_i-\sum_j b_j .
$$
Assign unit coefficient vectors to their corresponding diagonal input lines, zero to the off-diagonal inputs, and propagate the vectors $(a,b)$ by exactly the linear-combination rules used in the proof. For a nonzero constant image all $b_j$ must equal a single nonzero scalar and all $a_i$ its negative. It suffices that the true endpoint is a nonzero constant.

Let $h$ be the number of nonzero $b_j$ for a line. Whenever $h\le s/2$, every row indexed by $i$ contributes at least $h$ distinct monomials: if $a_i=0$ exactly those $h$ columns survive; if $a_i\ne0$ all the $s-h$ zero-coefficient columns survive. Thus the original sparse size of that line is at least $rh$. At the first line with $h\ge s/4$, all lines in the prefix have $h\le s/2$ for $s$ sufficiently large, since a binary combination can at most add the support sizes. For each nonzero $b_j$ at the chosen line trace back through parents with that coefficient nonzero. Initially only one column coefficient is nonzero per input. The binary ancestry argument gives $\Omega(s\log s)$ visits to these lines, counting distinct column indices at each visited line. Indeed at most $2^{d+1}$ indices can reach an input in at most $d$ steps. The total of all $h$ in the prefix bounds the visits from above, and multiplying by $r$ proves the lower estimate. Adjust absolute constants for bounded $s$. [Binary ancestry and unrestricted fixed-gap bounds](CriticalMatchings.md)

**Diagonal conjugacy.** For displayed diagonal $A,B$, specialize entries joining different labels to zero and use identity permutations at matched labels. The surviving inverse systems on the other labels are in disjoint variables. A degree-two proof remains a degree-at-most-two proof after this substitution, and vanishing lines can be discarded. If the specialized derivation reaches a nonzero constant early, take that prefix. Track one pair of vectors per rectangular system as above. At a nonzero constant, each nonempty board must have constant $b_j=-a_i$ within that board, and all constants sum to a nonzero value. Provided every discrepant label is present on both sides, at least one board of dimensions $r_i\le s_i$ must have a gap nonzero in the field and a nonzero coefficient. The support argument on that board gives $\Omega(r_i s_i\log(2s_i))$ sparse size, independently of the other coefficients. In particular if all discrepant multiplicities on both sides are $\Omega(n)$ and at least one gap is nonzero in the field, the exact minimum sparse size at degree two is $\Theta(n^2\log(2n))$ even for growing gaps. The upper bound extracts the chosen block's diagonal inverse entries and takes traces. [Published conjugacy equations](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3) · [Block extraction](SparseConjugacy.md)

**A size-degree tradeoff.** Over a field with visible gap, keeping $r$ fixed and increasing $s$ forces the degree-two size to tend to infinity at rate $s\log(2s)$ up to constants depending on $r$. But the first $r+1$ rows of $P$ and columns of $Q$ already satisfy the one-sided contradiction $P'Q'=I_{r+1}$. Cofactor descent for this fixed smaller system gives a proof in degree $r+2$ and size depending only on $r$, over every field. Thus a degree-optimal sparse proof can be arbitrarily larger than an unrestricted one for two-sided rectangular inverses. When $g=s-r$ stays fixed as both sides grow, the moving-hole lower bound gives the same order as the degree-two bound even without restricting proof degree. [Cofactor descent](ConjugacyDegree.md)
