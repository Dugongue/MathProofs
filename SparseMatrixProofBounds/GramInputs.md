# Exact numbers of Gram equations for rank-increasing congruence

Use the entries of $XAX^T-B$ and both ordinary inverse equations in Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She's Question 7.3. Count the minimum number of distinct Gram entries used by any PC refutation, with the inverse equations free. For symmetric or alternating forms the transposed duplicate can be used in either orientation. Counting both never improves the minimum. This count lower-bounds sparse size if one also charges the monomials in every used input. All ranks and congruence classes in the proof can be read over an algebraic closure. [Published equations](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

| Source $A$ over the closure | Displayed target $B$ | Exact minimum |
|---|---|---:|
| $I_r\oplus0$ up to congruence | $I_s\oplus0$ | $(r+1)(r+2)/2$ |
| $J^{\oplus r}\oplus0$ up to congruence | $J^{\oplus s}\oplus0$ | $(r+1)^2$ |

Here $r<s$, $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and the source and target have the same side. The statements hold in every characteristic, including two. A source basis change is unrestricted since it preserves the set of possible Gram matrices. When fewer entries admit a completion of the correct congruence type, soundness rules out a refutation. Conversely geometric inconsistency on just those entries yields a PC refutation by the Nullstellensatz, with scalar descent by linear algebra on a fixed identity. If $r=0$ one displayed nonzero target entry already suffices.

**Symmetric target.** Consider its $s$ active rows. If at most $r$ of their diagonal ones are required, choose a diagonal completion with precisely $r$ ones, covering those requirements. If fewer than $r(r+1)/2$ distinct zero off-diagonals inside the active block are required, regard these pairs as edges of a simple graph. It is $r$-colorable: a vertex-minimal obstruction would have minimum degree at least $r$ and at least $r+1$ vertices. Assign the same coordinate unit vector to rows of one color, and zero outside the active block. If necessary split color classes to use exactly $r$ colors. The resulting row matrix has rank $r$ and realizes every selected pairing, including every required diagonal. Extend its $r$ columns to an invertible matrix. The two separate lower bounds therefore add. For the matching upper bound enforce all entries of an $(r+1)$-square principal identity block.

**Alternating target.** Index its active pairs by $i$ and their rows by $e_i,f_i$. Unless at least $r+1$ of the pairings $\langle e_i,f_i\rangle=1$ are required, a completion supported on just $r$ of the target planes meets every requirement. Independently, suppose fewer than $r(r+1)$ distinct cross-plane zero entries are required. We assign each plane one of $r$ coordinate hyperbolic planes and one of two orientations on it. In the first orientation use $e,f$, in the other use $f,-e$. This always realizes its own pairing one, including in characteristic two.

Between each two planes record which relative orientations would violate a required zero if their colors agreed. There are at most two forbidden relative orientations, and each recorded prohibition requires at least one of the selected zero entries. Draw a multigraph edge for each distinct prohibition. Given the choice at one endpoint, each edge excludes just one of the $2r$ oriented colors at the other. A vertex-minimal uncolorable multigraph would have minimum degree at least $2r$, since otherwise the last vertex could be colored greedily. Its multiplicity per pair is at most two, so it would also have at least $r+1$ vertices and at least $r(r+1)$ edges. Consequently an assignment exists under the supposed budget. Split color classes if necessary to occupy all $r$ coordinate planes; this keeps every required zero and provides the full-rank completion. Rows in the radical of the target can be zero. Hence both lower bounds apply to each refutation.

For attainment choose $r+1$ target planes. Require the $r+1$ diagonal cross-pair ones, zeros on one triangular half of the other $\langle e_i,f_j\rangle$, and all $\langle e_i,e_j\rangle=0$ for $i<j$. The triangular matrix has full rank, making the $r+1$ vectors represented by the $e_i$ independent in the source quotient by its radical. They span a totally isotropic subspace in a $2r$-dimensional symplectic space, an impossibility. This uses exactly $(r+1)+2\binom{r+1}{2}$ entries. **Generic target coordinates.** Over any algebraically closed field, fix the source type in either row of the table and take the target general in its rank-$s$ symmetric orbit or rank-$2s$ alternating orbit. The exact counts in these coordinates are respectively
$$
 \binom{r+2}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\binom{2r+2}{2}.
$$
General means in a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the stated orbit. The alternating case therefore exhibits a change in input complexity under a target basis change. This is an application of algebraic independence in low-rank matrix completion; we give the elimination argument to include small characteristic. [Completion matroids](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.3777) · [Symmetric completion ranks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06593)

Parametrize the source Gram matrix by $n$ independent generic rows in dimension $d=r$ for symmetric forms, or $d=2r$ for alternating forms. Full column rank is dense. In a minimal algebraically dependent selection of entries, every participating row must have more than $d$ incident entries, with a diagonal counted once. Indeed pairings of one new row with at most $d$ distinct earlier generic vectors are independent linear forms over the field of all other row coordinates. For a symmetric diagonal accompanied by $t<d$ off-diagonal entries, the quadratic norm is nonconstant on the generic fiber of those $t$ independent linear forms. For generic old vectors their span has an orthogonal complement containing a vector of nonzero norm: this property is open on the full-rank locus and holds for $t$ distinct standard basis vectors. On a line in that direction the quadratic coefficient is nonzero, also in characteristic two. Thus this group of incident entries is algebraically independent over the field of the other rows. Removing it would preserve any dependence elsewhere, contradicting minimality.

For an alternating pattern the graph is simple, so minimum degree $d+1$ implies at least $d+2$ vertices and at least $(d+1)(d+2)/2$ entries. For a symmetric pattern there can also be loops. With at most $d$ vertices no row has more than $d$ entries. With $d+1$ vertices the degree condition forces the whole principal triangle. With at least $d+2$ vertices the same handshaking bound applies, because a loop contributes just one incidence. Consequently every smaller selection is algebraically independent. Its coordinate projection of the source orbit is dominant and constructible, hence contains a dense open set. The larger-rank target orbit has the smaller-rank orbit in its closure by scaling the extra coordinates toward zero. Its projection is therefore also dominant, so a general target on that orbit is completable on each smaller selection. There are finitely many selections. Conversely a general target has a nonsingular leading principal submatrix of size $r+1$ for symmetric forms, or $2r+2$ for alternating forms, as witnessed by the standard representatives. Enforcing its principal triangle (strict triangle for alternating forms) excludes a source of the prescribed rank.

For positive rank proportional to the side, these counts force quadratically many input lines. In dense source coordinates off-diagonal output entries each have one monomial per nonzero source entry, so a quadratically dense source gives a quartic sparse-size lower bound. At most $n$ selected entries can lie on the diagonal. The exact counts concern the Gram inputs; the operations required after reading them are charged separately in total sparse PC size.
