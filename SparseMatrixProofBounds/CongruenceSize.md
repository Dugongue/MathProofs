# Quartic sparse size for matrix congruence

Use ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) on the entries of $G=XAX^T-B$, $XY-I$, and $YX-I$, with maximum-degree unrestricted, binary scalar linear combinations, and multiplication by one variable. Size counts all nonzero monomial occurrences in all used lines, including the used input lines. The matrices have side $n$ and the field is arbitrary. [Galesi, Grochow, Pitassi and She, Question 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

**Theorem.** Suppose a pencil $C=aA+bA^T$ has a nonzero right kernel vector with support at most a fixed $w$ and the same pencil $D=aB+bB^T$ is invertible. The worst-case minimum sparse PC size on this class is between $\Omega(n^4)$ and $O_w(n^4\log(2n))$. Its exponent is exactly four over every field, including when both original matrices must be nonsingular and nonsymmetric. The upper bound holds if the pencil witness is defined over an extension of bounded degree, with constants depending on that bound. The hard examples have minimum degree three.

**Universal characteristic-zero bound.** For all geometrically noncongruent pairs over any field of characteristic zero the minimum sparse size is $O(n^5\log(2n))$. Thus the general characteristic-zero worst case is bounded below quartically and above quintically up to a logarithm. The quartic lower examples are over the prime field and survive extensions. These statements enforce no finite-field equations and do not use Dirichlet GRH.

## The sparse pencil certificate

Put $H=XC-DY^T$. The two pencil combinations give
$$
 H=(aG+bG^T)Y^T-XC(YX-I)^T .
$$
Every term on the right can be assembled from the raw axioms by a variable multiplication and a scalar combination. For a fixed entry of $H$ the first product uses $O(n)$ Gram inputs, each of size at most $n^2+1$. The second uses $O(n^2)$ variable multiples of inverse entries, each of size at most $n+1$. Adding by a balanced tree costs $O(n^3\log(2n))$ occurrences, since at each level total support is at most the sum of the leaf supports. The result $H_{ij}$ itself has only $O(n)$ terms. Cancellations in the cubic computation are valid steps of ordinary PC.

For a witness $u$ of support $w$ one needs only the $nw$ entries corresponding to its nonzero coordinates. Take the indicated column combination and use $Cu=0$, then constant elimination with $D^{-1}$ gives $Y^Tu=0$. Choose $j$ with $u_j\ne0$. The $j$th component of $u^TYX=0$ follows by multiplying these available linear entries by individual entries of $X$. The inverse equations also give $u^T(YX-I)e_j=0$. The result is a nonzero scalar. The total size is $O(w n^4\log(2n))$, and the maximum degree is three. For a degree-$e$ extension, translate every line to its $e$ coordinates in a base-field basis containing one. Addition with coefficients becomes $e$ base-field linear combinations of at most $2e$ coordinates, and variable multiplication acts coordinatewise. A balanced summation implements the combinations with overhead polynomial in $e$, preserving the claimed exponent at bounded $e$.

## A quartic lower bound from symmetric matrices

Take $B=I_n$ and $A=\operatorname{diag}(D,0)$ where $D$ has side $n-1$ and equals the all-ones matrix plus $\operatorname{diag}(0,1,\ldots,1)$. Its determinant is one by subtracting the first row from every other row. Its first diagonal entry is one, so $D$ is nonalternating even in characteristic two. In all characteristics $D$ is congruent to $I_{n-1}$: subtract its first basis vector from each of the others. Thus $A$ has side $n$, rank $n-1$, and a one-coordinate kernel.

Every off-diagonal unordered pair of Gram constraints must be represented in a proof. If both entries $(i,j),(j,i)$ are omitted, change both entries of $B$ there to one. The modified two-by-two principal block is the all-ones matrix, congruent to $\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ by subtraction. This completion is congruent to $A$ and therefore satisfies all remaining equations with an actual inverse. Each necessary off-diagonal Gram polynomial contains at least $(n-1)(n-2)$ distinct monomials from the off-diagonal entries of $D$, since its two rows of variables are independent. The $\binom n2$ separate requirements yield $\Omega(n^4)$ for proofs of any degree. Omitting a diagonal constraint also permits a completion by changing that entry to zero. The pencil upper bound applies with $a=1,b=0$.

## Nonsingular and nonsymmetric examples

First work in odd characteristic or characteristic zero. Let $m=2h$ grow, choose the first $h$ coordinates as a set $P$, its complement as $Q$, and let $v$ be its indicator. Pick $c=1,d=-1$ and a scalar $t\ne0$ different from $cdh$. This can be done in the prime field, even at three. Set
$$
 B=\begin{pmatrix}I_m&cv\\dv^T&t\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_0=\begin{pmatrix}I_m-E_{ij}-E_{ji}&cv\\dv^T&t\end{pmatrix}
$$
for a fixed $i\in P,j\in Q$. The upper-left block of $A_0$ has rank $m-1$ and radical spanned by $u=e_i+e_j$. As $v^T u=1$, $A_0$ is invertible: if $A_0(x,s)^T=0$, multiplication of the top equations by $u^T$ gives $s=0$, then $x$ is a multiple of $u$, then the bottom equation gives $x=0$. The scalar Schur complement $t-cdh\ne0$ proves $B$ invertible. Both matrices are nonsymmetric.

Use the pencil $C=A_0-(c/d)A_0^T$. Its upper-right block vanishes and its top-left is a nonzero scalar times $I_m-E_{ij}-E_{ji}$. The bottom-right equals $(1-c/d)t\ne0$. A nonzero right kernel vector has top part $u$, and its last coordinate is determined by the bottom row. Its support is at most three. The target pencil is invertible by the same block triangular test.

Leave $i,j$ and the border coordinate fixed, and change basis on the other $m-2$ coordinates of $A_0$. Use the integer unimodular shear $R$ that adds one designated basis row to each other basis row in that complement. In the new source $A$ the corresponding principal block is $RR^T$, with every off-diagonal entry nonzero in every characteristic. The small-support pencil kernel continues to use only the three fixed coordinates. For every $k\in P,l\in Q$ let $B^{kl}$ change just the symmetric pair $(k,l),(l,k)$ of $B$ to $-1$. Permutations within the two halves show that $B^{kl}$ is congruent to $A_0$, hence to $A$. Each of the $h^2$ disjoint pairs of indexed Gram constraints must be used at least once, since omitting a pair permits this completion with both inverse equations satisfied. Each required input has $\Omega(m^2)$ distinct raw quadratic monomials from the dense principal block. This gives the asserted quartic lower bound for this pencil class.

In characteristic two run the bordered construction over $K=\mathbb F_4$, with $h$ even, $c=1$, $d=\omega$ a primitive cube root, and $t=1$. All preceding arguments over $K$ hold, since $c\ne d$ and $h=0$ in the field. To obtain literal prime-field inputs, replace each bilinear form $J$ by
$$
 (x,y)\longmapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_2}(x^TJy)
$$
on the restriction of scalars, using the same fixed basis of $K$ in every coordinate. A nonzero entry of $J$ gives a nonsingular two-by-two coefficient block by nondegeneracy of the trace pairing. This preserves nonsingularity and nonsymmetry, and the congruences for the completions descend immediately from maps over $K$. Each edited pair now occupies two disjoint two-by-two blocks. Any proof must use at least one of their indexed Gram entries. Every such entry is off-diagonal in the full matrix and has $\Omega(m^2)$ monomials from distinct pairs of row variables. This proves the lower bound also after further arbitrary extensions of characteristic two.

For the upper bound on this last family, extend scalars to a field containing $K$, of degree at most two over the input field. The trace form splits, up to independent basis changes inside each coordinate pair, as the original $K$ form plus its conjugate. Indeed the two embeddings identify $K\otimes_{\mathbb F_2}K$ with $K\times K$, the trace with sum, and multiplication with componentwise multiplication. In the original summand the source pencil at $\lambda=c/d$ has its stated kernel. The target pencil at this same $\lambda$ is invertible in **both** summands: for either conjugate bordered form the top-left block is $(1-\lambda)I_m$, and the Schur correction from the cross-blocks is a scalar times $v^Tv=h=0$. The remaining diagonal entry $(1-\lambda)t$ is nonzero. Undoing the per-coordinate changes doubles the support bound at most. Equal scalar identity padding works at all larger dimensions here, because $1-\lambda\ne0$. The odd-characteristic construction has the same padding property. Since the displayed dimensions recur with bounded gaps, the lower bounds hold at all sufficiently large sides. No degree-two refutation exists for these examples: these nonsymmetric, nonalternating sources have their Gram matrices spanning all matrices and can produce no constant by scalar combinations of quadratics. This also follows from the exact prior degree formula. [Question and equations](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19320#S7.SS3)

## Sharp sparse size in degree two

There is a separate sharp classification for the stratum whose minimum PC degree is **two**: its worst-case minimum sparse size is $\Theta(n^2)$ over every field. To prove the upper bound, recall the elementary degree-two test. Exclude literal nonzero constant axioms. In degree two the proof space is just the span of the input quadratics until a constant is obtained; there are no linear terms. The inverse equations have different bidegree from the Gram equations in their homogeneous terms and contribute zero constant whenever their quadratic part vanishes, by evaluation at $X=Y=I$. A refutation thus requires $B$ outside the span of all homogeneous Gram matrices over the closure. For nonzero symmetric nonalternating $A$ that span is the symmetric matrices, by rank-one substitutions and polarization of $vv^T$. For alternating $A$ it is the alternating matrices, by isolating pairs of rows. Otherwise it is every matrix: the nonsymmetric and nonalternating case gives all symmetric matrices by rank-one substitutions, and a substitution on two rows with $u^TAv\ne v^TAu$ provides the additional off-diagonal direction modulo symmetric matrices on those rows. Consequently a degree-two instance is witnessed by a transposed pair of Gram entries, using their difference for a symmetric source and their sum for an alternating source. A nonzero diagonal in the latter target would already give degree zero. This uses $O(n^2)$ terms in total.

For the matching lower bound use $A$ equal to the dense nonsingular matrix $D$ above, now on $n$ coordinates, congruent to $I_n$ over the prime field. Let $B=I_n+E_{12}$. A refutation must use the $(1,2)$ Gram entry since all other axioms have a common solution for the target $I_n$. That one polynomial has $\Omega(n^2)$ terms, whereas the two transposed Gram entries directly refute the displayed input. This proves the sharp size lower bound even among proofs with unlimited degree.

## All congruence instances in characteristic zero

Prepare the linear equations $L$ consisting of the entries of $XA-BY^T$ and $XA^T-B^T Y^T$. There are at most $2n^2$ and each costs $O(n^3\log(2n))$ as above. The highest total degree is three. For a negative input in characteristic zero, there is a scalar combination $Q(z)-1$ of the original inverse equations such that the homogeneous quadratic $Q$ vanishes identically on $\ker L$, with zero polynomial understood in the case of identities. Here $z$ lists all variables in $X,Y$. To see this over the closure, use the multiplicity-space argument for the transpose-pair quiver: in the space of true intertwiners, both forward and adjoint map have two inverse relations in the semisimple quotient. A mismatched multiplicity gives a discrepancy of traces of the two identities, a nonzero integer in characteristic zero. At an absent multiplicity one obtains a constant identity directly. The trace of the two rectangular products agrees identically, including for doubled types if one uses the full multiplicities. Each of these projected inverse relations is a scalar combination of the original inverse equations on the space $\ker L$, since on that subspace the projection to true intertwiners fixes the maps, and quotient maps are linear on endomorphism entries. Thus their scalar combination gives the assertion, rescaled to constant $-1$. In particular this uses the full characteristic-zero canonical classification. [Canonical forms and self-dual decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.2408) · [Classification theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.0565)

A homogeneous quadratic vanishing on a linear space over the closure belongs to its linear ideal. In coordinates obtained by extending a basis of $L$ to the dual space, every surviving quadratic term has at least one such coordinate factor. Consequently
$$
 Q=\sum_i L_i\ell_i
$$
for linear forms $\ell_i$. The simultaneous conditions on the unknown scalar inverse coefficients and the coefficients of all $\ell_i$ are linear over the original field. A solution therefore descends by Gaussian elimination. Each $L_i$ has $O(n)$ monomials and $\ell_i$ has at most $2n^2$. Produce $\ell_i L_i$ term by term using the prepared $L_i$, single-variable multiplication, and binary addition. The total sparse cost, including adding the signed inverse combination and all products, is $O(n^5\log(2n))$. This proof charges all intermediate monomials even when an intermediate polynomial is later cancelled.

The same quintic bound applies in positive characteristic whenever a mismatch of full quiver multiplicities has nonzero image in the field. The cubic and quartic estimates above for pencil witnesses need no multiplicity or characteristic assumption. All general results in this note are written proofs.
