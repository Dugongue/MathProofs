# Optimal max-rank hardness and extension spectra for tensor isomorphism

These are two follow-up informal proofs.

**[TI-completeness at the square-root max-rank threshold](SquareRootMaxRank.md).** Over any fixed finite field, 3-TI remains complete on a deterministically recognizable class of balanced, concise tensors that are indecomposable over the algebraic closure and have every matrix contraction of rank $O(\sqrt n)$ in all three modes. This is the optimal exponent by the max-rank bound of Briët, Christandl, Leigh, Shpilka and Zuiddam. The reduction repeats a hard alternating matrix space in three summands. Bounded-degree minors recover the small spaces in raw coordinates. Arbitrary core terms are allowed in the recognizable class and can be canonized modulo linear shears after enumerating the small frames, giving a deterministic $q^{O(n)}\operatorname{poly}(n)$ canonization algorithm on the class. [Max-rank lower bound and examples](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.01718v4#S1.SS3)

**[Complete extension-degree spectra and nilpotent descent](ExtensionSpectra.md).** Every nonempty extension-degree spectrum is a finite union of divisibility progressions, and every such union occurs already for balanced, absolutely indecomposable tensors with scalar fixed-label matrix-mode adjoints. Nilpotent geometric stabilizer component groups force a single progression, and hence descent to the gcd of any list of successful degrees. The note also computes the sharp minimum side length within the étale template for prescribed prime degrees to allow isomorphism while the base field does not. The forward component description uses Lang descent and was used previously in our modular profile argument. [Lang](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372673)

The hardness reduction preserves ordinary isomorphism over every extension. Thus all the spectra also occur inside the optimal-max-rank class, and deciding if one degree at most a binary bound works remains NP-hard there even on geometrically isomorphic pairs. NP membership for the earlier étale family uses its particular raw recognizer and is not asserted for this broader class.

Exact finite controls checked 4,382 pair-and-divisor spectrum cases, the minimum for the $2,3$ example by exhaustive integer partitions, the $2,3,5$ construction, 64 affine dihedral actions, and 336 pure-cube minor identities in three characteristics. These controls test components of the proofs. Human review, Lean formalization and publication priority are pending.
