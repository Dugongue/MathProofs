# Tensor Isomorphism completeness at the square-root max-rank threshold

Fix a finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$. Tensor Isomorphism is complete under deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions on a deterministically recognizable class of raw, concise $n\times n\times n$ tensors with two simultaneous properties:

1. No tensor is a nontrivial direct sum, even after extension to $\overline F$.
2. For each of the three modes, every matrix in the corresponding slice span over $\overline F$ has rank $O(\sqrt n)$.

The exponent is optimal for a statement that bounds the max-ranks of *all three* modes: Briët, Christandl, Leigh, Shpilka and Zuiddam proved that the product of any two geometric max-ranks of a concise balanced tensor is at least $n$. They also exhibited concise tensors at the square-root scale. The claim here is that full TI hardness persists at that scale, on a recognizable class of absolutely indecomposable inputs. [Discreteness of Asymptotic Tensor Ranks, Theorem 1.13 and Example 2.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.01718v4#S1.SS3)

## 1. Matrix spaces used in the construction

From a nonzero alternating matrix space $\mathcal D$ on $B$, with $b_0=\dim B\ge 3$, form the alternating space on $A\oplus B$
$$
 S(\mathcal D)=\left\{
 \begin{pmatrix}0&C\\-C^{\mathsf T}&D\end{pmatrix}
 : C\in F^{3b_0\times b_0},\ D\in\mathcal D
 \right\},\qquad \dim A=3b_0 .
$$
Alternating means the form vanishes on $(x,x)$ in every characteristic. Write $b=4b_0$, $m=\dim S=3b_0^2+\dim\mathcal D$. In particular $m=\Theta(b^2)$.

The matrix-space equivalence relation used below is left-right equivalence of subspaces of $F^b\otimes F^b$. For these $S$'s it equals congruence and reflects isometry of the original spaces $\mathcal D$. Here are the needed checks. Any pair $g,h$ satisfying $hM=Mg$ for all $M\in S$ has $h$ diagonal because it preserves every image plane of an elementary cross form, and the intersections of these planes are the coordinate lines. The transposed equations make $g$ diagonal as well. The cross forms impose two common scalars, one on each bipartite part of $h$ and on the opposite part of $g$. A nonzero entry of a bottom-block form equates the two scalars. This argument holds over the algebraic closure. If $H S K^{\mathsf T}=S'$ is a left-right equivalence between alternating spaces, alternation gives $(K^{-1}H)M=M(H^{\mathsf T}K^{-\mathsf T})$. Thus $H=\lambda K$ and the equivalence is congruence of subspaces.

The large block $A$ is the unique largest common totally isotropic subspace. For two vectors $(x,y),(x',y')$ all cross forms vanish precisely when $x\otimes y'=x'\otimes y$. If a common isotropic space contains a vector with both coordinates nonzero, it has dimension at most one. Otherwise it lies entirely in $A$ or entirely in $B$, by linearity. Since $\dim A=3b_0>b_0$, this recovers $A$. The forms annihilating $A$ are exactly the space $\mathcal D$ on the quotient. Every isometry of $\mathcal D$ extends to one of $S$ using the identity on $A$. Alternating matrix-space isometry is TI-complete by the published 3-TI reduction. The finitely bounded side dimension $b_0<3$ and zero-space cases can be handled by exhaustive constant-size computation or immediate dimension tests and mapped to fixed answers. [Grochow and Qiao, Part IV, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317v2#S2)

We also need the earlier deterministic raw recognizer for the left-right closure of $S$. Its principal steps are as follows. Solve for an invertible output change making the raw space alternating. On a true input the unknowns lie in a one-dimensional solution space by the scalar pair-adjoint argument. For an alternating space, start with a zero form and increase its rank until its radical is common totally isotropic. If $M$ has radical on which some $N$ is nonzero, restrict $M+tN$ to a nondegenerate complement for $M$ and two radical vectors paired nontrivially by $N$. Its determinant has nonzero coefficient of $t^2$, the determinant on the complement times the determinant of the alternating two-dimensional restriction of $N$. Thus over an auxiliary finite extension of polynomial cardinality greater than the side dimension, scanning $t$ finds a rank increase. On a true input all matrices have rank at most $2b_0$, so a terminal radical has dimension at least $2b_0$ and lies in $A$. Its common perpendicular with respect to the entire space equals $A$, because the cross forms separate any vector outside $A$ from any nonzero vector inside it. This subspace descends to $F$ in echelon coordinates. Check the required dimensions, isotropy and full cross projection. In an arbitrary complement of $A$ the bottom blocks may have extra terms. Changing the complement by a shear adds a linear expression in that shear to each bottom block. Solve to place all bottom blocks in the kernel space of the cross projection, taken inside the alternating forms. If this succeeds and that kernel space is nonzero, the full verified space is $S$ in the recovered coordinates; dimensions certify equality. A true template always admits such a complement. This is a polynomial-time recognizer on arbitrary raw input, with all positive outputs witnessed by an exact coordinate change. The auxiliary field has logarithmic degree and can be constructed by enumerating monic polynomials of that degree.

## 2. Put a matrix space into each of three tensor summands

Take three small spaces $U_0,V_0,W_0$, each of dimension $b$. Give each a complement $U_1,V_1,W_1$ of dimension $m$. A tensor in the class has the form
$$
 T=T_U+T_V+T_W+R
$$
with
$$
\begin{aligned}
 T_U&\in U_1\otimes V_0\otimes W_0, &
 T_V&\in U_0\otimes V_1\otimes W_0,\\
 T_W&\in U_0\otimes V_0\otimes W_1, &
 R&\in U_0\otimes V_0\otimes W_0 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The three maps obtained by contracting their respective large coordinate spaces are injective with images in the left-right closure of $S(\mathcal D_U),S(\mathcal D_V),S(\mathcal D_W)$, respectively. The three underlying alternating spaces may vary independently subject to the common dimensions. The core $R$ may be arbitrary. Take the whole ordinary isomorphism closure of these templates.

All three sides equal $n=b+m=\Theta(b^2)$. In a contraction along $U$, the lower-right block on $V_1\otimes W_1$ vanishes. Hence rank is at most $2b$, and the two cyclic bounds are identical. These support arguments are independent of the field of coefficients. The tensor is concise. For example a nonzero component of a contraction label on $U_0$ produces a nonzero off-diagonal block, since the elementary cross forms of the corresponding $S$ have zero common kernel. If the label vanishes on $U_0$, only $T_U$ contributes, and its map on $U_1^*$ is injective. The other two modes follow cyclically.

## 3. Extract the small spaces in polynomial time

A raw algorithm must find the small spaces without enumerating vectors or testing large minors. Write $p=\operatorname{char}F$, and let $s$ be the least power of $p$ at least three. In the $U$-label variables, form the span of degree-$s$ monomial multiples of all $3\times3$ minors of the contraction matrix. Compute the subspace of linear forms whose $s$-th powers lie in this span. The $s$-power map is additive and semilinear on coefficients, so linear algebra on the pure power monomials and inverse scalar Frobenius solves this. Let $H_U\subseteq U^*$ be the common kernel of the recovered forms. This procedure is covariant and its kernels contain all geometric rank-at-most-two labels after extending scalars. Covariance follows by Cauchy–Binet for the minors and linear substitution for the homogeneous multiplier space. The fixed degree $s$ gives polynomial time.

We show that on a template $H_U\subseteq\operatorname{ann}(U_0)$. In coordinates adapted to the $T_V$ arm, take a small $U_0$-label coordinate $x_j$. The cross forms of $S$ on $U_0,W_0$ include $\pm(e_j e_{k}^{\mathsf T}-e_k e_j^{\mathsf T})$ for every $k$ in the opposite part. Choose three distinct $k$'s, possible because both parts have size at least three. In the off-diagonal block on $V_1,W_0$, use the three columns or rows corresponding to these forms and the three opposite coordinates $k$ from $W_0$. This submatrix is diagonal, with entries $\pm x_j$, so its minor is $\pm x_j^3$. Neither the other two arms nor the core contributes to that block. Multiplying by $x_j^{s-3}$ gives $x_j^s$. This argument can use any convenient basis of the $T_V$ arm, since changing that basis or the small coordinates leaves the computed span covariant. Therefore the claimed linear forms span the full coordinate space of $U_0$.

The images of contractions in $H_U$ now lie in $V_0\otimes W_0$. They contain every elementary cross form of the $T_U$ arm, since each has rank two and its pure $U_1^*$ label is rational in a chart over $F$. These forms span all of $V_0$ and $W_0$ in the two factor supports. Consequently recover $V_0$ and $W_0$ as the two factor supports of the span of those contractions, by linear algebra. Repeat cyclically to recover $U_0$. The rank-two step need never find every rank-two slice, or recognize a determinantal locus.

For exact recognition, split complements to the recovered spaces, check the four indicated support types and dimensions, and run the raw matrix-space recognizer on each extracted arm. These checks certify membership, and all of them succeed on every true template in arbitrary coordinates. Indeed the three arms are intrinsic after projection to the corresponding quotient in their large mode, and any choice of complements can affect only the allowed core. The algorithm requires the three annihilator procedures to agree on the common small spaces when they recover a given space twice. Equality and support checks are elementary.

## 4. The reduction and absolute indecomposability

For hardness use the same input space $\mathcal D$ in all three arms, with $R=0$. If two original matrix spaces are isometric, the same congruence between their $S$'s, applied in each of $U_0,V_0,W_0$, matches the three arm spaces. The uniquely induced changes of coordinates on the large label quotients extend block-diagonally to an isomorphism of the two zero-core tensors. Conversely any tensor isomorphism preserves the recovered small spaces, so on the $U$ arm it gives a left-right equivalence between the $S$'s. Section 1 recovers an isometry of $\mathcal D$. Thus the equivalence is exact. Positive and negative fixed instances exist, for example by using one-dimensional spaces spanned by alternating forms of different ranks on the same $B$ of dimension at least four. The polynomial-size construction and recognizer prove TI-completeness on the stated raw class.

To rule out geometric direct-sum decompositions, work over $\overline F$. The centroid consists of triples $(c_U,c_V,c_W)$ whose three actions on $T$, each in just its own mode, are equal. Any such $c$ preserves the three small spaces. Indeed for a scalar $t\ne0$ avoiding the finitely many singularities, acting by $I+t c_U$ in $U$ and its corresponding inverse $(I+t c_V)^{-1}$ in $V$ preserves $T$. The construction of the small spaces is equivariant over $\overline F$: the pure-power calculation commutes with field extension, or can be repeated there using the same homogeneous coefficient spaces. Hence $I+t c_U$ and $(I+t c_V)^{-1}$ preserve $U_0,V_0$ and so $c_U,c_V$ do; use the analogous pair for $c_W$. On the intrinsic $U$ arm, the equality of the $V$ and $W$ actions gives a fixed-label pair adjoint of $S$, with the transpose in one slot. These small-mode actions are one common scalar by Section 1. The cyclic arms make the same scalar act on all three small spaces. Comparing with the action on each large quotient and using injectivity of the arm map then makes the three quotient actions equal to that scalar. Thus every centroid member, after subtracting a common scalar, consists of maps from the large quotients into the small spaces and has square zero. In particular the only centroid idempotents are zero and identity. A direct-sum decomposition would supply a nontrivial centroid idempotent via projection onto one summand. This proves absolute indecomposability.


## 5. Canonize the entire class, including arbitrary cores

The raw extraction gives a $q^{O(n)}\operatorname{poly}(n)$-time deterministic canonization and transporter algorithm on this class without a TI oracle. Enumerate ordered bases only in $U_0,V_0,W_0$, taking at most $q^{3b^2}$ choices. In any such coordinates, represent the three arm image spaces by their unique row-reduced ordered bases. The corresponding bases on the large quotient duals are forced by injectivity of each contraction arm. Choose arbitrary lifts of the resulting bases on the primal quotients to the full tensor modes. In the resulting three bases the arm arrays are fixed within the frame, and only the core can depend on the lifts.

For changes with identity actions on the small spaces and quotients, each mode changes by a shear from its quotient into its small space. Acting on an arm inserts the corresponding shear in its sole large slot, so the total change in the core is a sum of the three linear images of the shear spaces. There are no mixed products because no term of the input has two large slots. Gaussian elimination gives a unique representative of the core modulo this sum, for example by eliminating the pivot coordinates in a row-reduced basis for the sum. It returns a shear that attains the representative by solving the same linear system.

The serial encoding of the three normalized arms and the reduced core is independent of the chosen lifts. Any raw tensor isomorphism transports small frames to small frames and matches this data exactly. Conversely matching data gives an actual isomorphism through the two recorded choices of full bases and the linear shear solutions. Take the minimum branch encoding and record its maps. This gives canonical representatives and explicit transporters, with $q^{3b^2}$ branches and polynomial work per branch. The relation $b^2=O(n)$ gives the stated time bound.

## 6. The hardness and spectrum results hold on the same class

The reduction above commutes with finite field extension as a test of isomorphism. In the alternating-space reduction of Grochow and Qiao the extra blocks use just identities, zeros and signs, and its rank comparisons and transporter equations apply over the larger field to the very same blocks. The construction of $S(\mathcal D)$ and the three-arm reduction use field-independent formulas and recover their intrinsic spaces over every extension. Apply this composition to the balanced étale template of [the extension-spectrum proof](ExtensionSpectra.md) and to the two tensors in the earlier bounded-extension-degree reduction. This proves that every nonempty extension-degree spectrum occurs even within the present balanced, absolutely indecomposable class with optimal max-rank exponent. The binary-bound decision remains NP-hard on pairs in this class that are geometrically isomorphic. The conclusion is NP-hardness for this broader class. The earlier NP membership uses the additional ability to recognize and decode the étale template. [Grochow and Qiao, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317v2#S2)
The fixed-label scalar-adjoint condition is used on the recovered matrix spaces. The resulting tensors themselves are claimed to be absolutely indecomposable. The low max-rank condition concerns the largest rank of an individual matrix contraction in each slice span. Neither tensor rank nor the invariant commonly called slice rank is being bounded by this theorem. The hardness construction and raw recovery are informal, with publication priority for their combination unverified.
