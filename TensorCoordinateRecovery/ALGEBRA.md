# Recovering tensor coordinates from ordered-slice module decompositions

We give two raw-input algorithms over a fixed finite field $F=\mathbb F_q$. The first extracts the maximum common repetition of any two modes and preserves ordinary tensor isomorphism. On every concise input it extracts the maximal rectangular matrix-multiplication tensor factor, with a core unique up to tensor isomorphism. The second recovers an intrinsic labeled family of subspaces in the slice mode, then searches only its effective algebraic ambiguity. In particular it finds tensor transporters in deterministic polynomial time when that ambiguity is logarithmic, including transporters with all three prescribed determinants. Neither algorithm requires supplied factors, flags or a TI oracle. The proofs are informal.

## 1. Ordered slices as a module

Write a tensor as $A\colon W\to\operatorname{Hom}_F(V,U)$. Fix a basis of $W$ for computation. The two vertices $U,V$ and the maps $A(w_j)\colon V\to U$ form a representation $M_A$ of the quiver with one arrow for each basis element. Its path algebra has only the two vertex idempotents and the arrows as basis elements, since no two arrows compose. The endomorphism algebra is computed by solving $P A(w_j)=A(w_j)Q$ for $(P,Q)$ and has componentwise multiplication. An invertible module map is exactly an invertible pair recovering the other modes for a fixed slice map.

A change of basis in $W$ gives an autoequivalence of this representation category. It leaves the vertex spaces, morphism equations and direct-sum structure intact, replacing the ordered arrows by invertible linear combinations. In particular it preserves the multiplicities of pairwise nonisomorphic indecomposable summands up to relabeling. Fixed-field module algorithms give a full decomposition, inclusions, projections and strict isomorphism witnesses in deterministic polynomial time. Specifically, compute primitive orthogonal idempotents of the endomorphism algebra and take their images in the two vertex spaces. Group the resulting indecomposables by constructive module isomorphism. The algorithms used here have polynomial cost in the explicit tensor size for fixed $F$. [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, §§2 and 4, especially Proposition 11](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf)

## 2. Extracting all hidden repetitions

A repetition of factor $r$ in modes $U,V$ means that the tensor is isomorphic to one given by slices $A_0(w_j)\otimes I_r$. No such factor is supplied. If $M_A=\bigoplus_i N_i^{m_i}$ is the computed decomposition, with the $N_i$ pairwise nonisomorphic, set $r=\gcd_i m_i$. An identically zero tensor can be handled separately in both ordinary and determinant-constrained comparison, since all three mode changes then act trivially on the tensor. More generally in positive mode dimensions this recipe even works for the zero representation, which has only vertex simple summands.

Krull–Schmidt implies that the possible repetition factors are exactly the positive divisors of $r$. For a divisor $s$, choose $m_i/s$ copies of every indecomposable. This gives an explicit smaller tuple $A_s$. The constructive strict isomorphisms between copies give actual bases exhibiting $A$ as $A_s\otimes I_s$. Conversely any repetition multiplies each indecomposable multiplicity by $s$, including after arbitrary mixing of slices. Thus $r$ is intrinsic. If $A,B$ have the same original format, they are isomorphic exactly when their $r$'s agree and their $r$-roots are isomorphic. One direction uses cancellation by Krull–Schmidt after the same slice change on both sides. The other lifts a root transporter blockwise in the explicit bases. This proof imposes no semisimplicity on the tensor or its endomorphism algebra.

For an arbitrary concise tensor, let $r_{ij}$ denote the three maxima computed before any peeling. A single sweep over the three pairs removes all three values $r_{ij}$. In fact removal in any one pair leaves both other maxima unchanged.

Here is the identity that proves this. Relative to the pair under consideration, amplifications on the other two pairs, of sizes $e,f$, enlarge the arrows to $A(w_j)\otimes E_{ab}$ for all the basis indices $j$ and matrix units $E_{ab}$ of $M_{e,f}(F)$. The same algebraic identity is valid over any field. The new endomorphism algebra is precisely
$$
 \{(P\otimes I_e,Q\otimes I_f)\mid PA(w)=A(w)Q\ \text{for all } w\}.
$$
Indeed, impose the morphism equations separately on $A(w)\otimes E_{ab}$. In the $e\times e$ and $f\times f$ block arrays of the unknown maps, every off-diagonal block kills every image of $A(w)$ or has image in their common kernel, hence is zero. The diagonal block equations compare every pair of diagonal blocks and force the same $P$ and $Q$, since the images span $U$ and the common kernel is zero. The same calculation applies to morphisms between any two concise ordered tuples on the enlarged arrow space. Every nonzero summand of a concise tuple is concise on the two endpoint modes, because a common kernel or missing image in that summand would persist in the whole tuple. Thus enlarging along the other two pairs retains each indecomposable, preserves all isotype distinctions, and leaves the multiplicities unchanged. Conciseness in the slice mode is preserved by root extraction because a linear combination vanishing in the root vanishes in every repeated copy. Applying the enlargement identity with one of $e,f$ equal to one shows why stripping any pair leaves both other maxima unchanged. For the current pair, removal divides all its multiplicities by their gcd, so its new maximum is one and stays one.

The product of three identity factors on the paired modes is a rectangular matrix-multiplication tensor. Indeed, writing the three edge indices as $a,b,c$, its entries are the tensor
$$
 \sum_{a=1}^{r_{12}}\sum_{b=1}^{r_{13}}\sum_{c=1}^{r_{23}}
       e_{a,b}\otimes e_{a,c}\otimes e_{b,c},
$$
which becomes the standard matrix-multiplication tensor after reordering the axes within a mode. Therefore the procedure recovers, from the raw coefficients, the maximal simultaneous matrix-multiplication factor of every concise tensor. A matrix-multiplication factor of shape $(s_{12},s_{13},s_{23})$ occurs exactly when each $s_{ij}$ divides $r_{ij}$. Necessity is the single-pair multiplicity theorem for each pair. For sufficiency, remove the whole maximal factor and restore the complementary factors of sizes $r_{ij}/s_{ij}$ into the core. Any two maximal extractions have isomorphic cores: cancel the same three factors one by one using Krull–Schmidt after any slice change imposed by the hypothesized isomorphism. Thus the core is independent up to TI of both the peeling order and the choice of summands. For an arbitrary concise $k_1\times k_2\times k_3$ core amplified along all pairs with hidden factors of sizes $s_{12},s_{13},s_{23}$, its input dimensions are $(k_1s_{12}s_{13}, k_2s_{12}s_{23}, k_3s_{13}s_{23})$. All planted factors are removed and the dimensions of the recovered core are at most the original $k_i$.

For a nonzero nonconcise tensor, first restrict to its intrinsic active supports, the images of the three flattenings. Remember the original three dimensions. The resulting concise tensor controls ordinary isomorphism, since each mode map on an active support can be extended across a chosen complement. This preprocessing is ordinary linear algebra. An identity-factor shape $(s_{12},s_{13},s_{23})$ occurs in the original input exactly when it occurs in the concise tensor and each $s_{ij}s_{ik}$ divides the original dimension of mode $i$. Necessity follows because the active supports of a tensor product are the tensor products of the active supports, by the rank-of-Kronecker-product formula for flattenings. Conversely extend the extracted concise core by zeros to the original dimensions divided by those products. The resulting amplified tensor has the right ambient dimensions and the same concise part. Unlike the concise case, the three ambient divisibility conditions can couple the choices of factors.

The end-to-end time is $L^{O(1)}$ plus the time of the chosen TI solver on the recovered core, including witness reconstruction. For an explicit unconditional bound, brute-force only the smallest mode map on the reduced tensor and perform a strict module test for each. A given core of maximum dimension $k$ costs at most $q^{k^2}L^{O(1)}$, even when the three independent amplifications balance the input at dimension $N\gg k$. In a balanced example with $N=k s^2$ and $k=\Theta(N^{1/2})$, this is $q^{O(N)}L^{O(1)}$, using only explicit constructive linear and module algorithms. Any general bound for the recovered dimensions can replace this exhaustive bound. On fixed odd prime fields one can plug in the published $q^{\widetilde O(k^{3/2})}$ decision bound. The latter substitution yields the stated decision bound without assuming that decision returns a transporter. [Grochow–Qiao, published linear-length reductions and their applications](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/189643)

### Any number of modes

The independence of pairwise repetition factors holds for every concise tensor of order $d\ge3$. For a pair $i,j$, use its two mode spaces as the quiver vertices, and index the ordered arrows by a product basis of all other modes. A change in any other mode induces an invertible mixing of these arrows. Thus the greatest possible pair repetition is recovered as the same gcd of indecomposable multiplicities.

Consider inserting a repetition factor on a different pair. If it meets $i,j$ in one mode, the expanded arrow spaces are the rectangular amplification just proved, with one of the two rectangle dimensions set to one. If it is disjoint, the new arrow list consists of repeats and zero copies of each old arrow. Its endomorphism equations and strict Hom equations are therefore identical to the old equations. In both cases the indecomposables and their coincidences are preserved after applying the enlargement to each summand, so their multiplicities are unchanged. This applies in reverse when an extracted factor is removed, because the smaller tensor remains concise in each mode. Removing a factor on $i,j$ divides its own gcd and leaves every other pair gcd unchanged.

Consequently all pairwise maxima can be extracted in any order and simultaneously displayed after composing the recorded mode bases. The residual is unique up to tensor isomorphism by cancellation of each identity factor, and every extractable array of repetition factors divides the maximum entrywise. The extraction is polynomial in the explicit input size over a fixed finite field. Each of the $\binom d2$ operations uses a two-vertex quiver whose arrow count is at most the number of explicit tensor entries. If $d=3$, the tensor product of the three pair identities is the rectangular matrix multiplication tensor after grouping its corresponding endpoint indices.
## 3. Intrinsic labeled subspaces from raw slices

For each indecomposable isotype $N_i$, let $A_i(w)$ be its slice tuple in the computed bases. Define its support by
$$
 L_i=\{w\in W\mid A_i(w)=0\}^{\perp}\subseteq W^* .
$$
The support is independent of the chosen copies and bases by strict isomorphism. Give each isotype the tuple of intrinsic integers consisting of its multiplicity, its two dimensions, its endomorphism algebra dimension and its support dimension. Any tensor isomorphism permutes isotypes of the same tag and sends their supports by the single induced slice map. A tag realized exactly once supplies an individually labeled subspace. For tags with multiple occurrences, the sum and intersection of their supports are also individually labeled and can be included. An unequal tag multiset is an immediate certificate of nonisomorphism. A tag supplies no claim that the corresponding blocks match as modules under any particular slice map.

We may assume that $A$ is concise in $W$. If needed, pass to the quotient by the common slice kernel with bases computed by elimination and remember this kernel's dimension. Any isomorphism on the quotient lifts by choosing any isomorphism on the kernel and extending across chosen complements. All supports now span $W^*$. Zero supports have no effect on the following equations.

Compute an invertible reference map $G_0$ for the two labeled subspace arrangements, or certify that none exists. This is a strict module isomorphism computation. Represent the arrangement by one central vertex $W^*$, one vertex for each labeled subspace, and its inclusion as the arrow into the central vertex. The path algebra has size polynomial in the arrangement. Given an invertible central map respecting each subspace, its restrictions uniquely specify the other vertex maps. Conversely an isomorphism of the modules gives the required central map. This calls only the ordinary polynomial-time strict module algorithm. Define
$$
 E=\{X\in\operatorname{End}_F(W^*)\mid X L\subseteq L \text{ for every extracted labeled subspace }L\}.
$$
This is a unital algebra found by linear equations. The full set of invertible arrangement transporters is $G_0 E^\times$. We use the convention that a tensor transporter satisfies $P A(w)=B(Rw)Q$. Its corresponding support map is $G=R^{-T}$.

## 4. Removing scalings that never require search

Let $C$ be the subalgebra of maps that act as a scalar on each individual nonzero $L_i$, including the supports whose tags were ambiguous. This condition is linear in the map and one scalar per support. Thus $C$ is directly computable. It lies inside $E$, since such maps preserve every selected support, and every selected sum and intersection.

On each $L_i$ there is a character $\chi_i\colon C\to F$ recording that scalar. Group identical characters. For each resulting character $\xi$, form the sum $U_\xi$ of its support spaces. These spaces form a direct decomposition of $W^*$. In fact, for each other character $\zeta$, pick $X\in C$ with $\xi(X)\ne\zeta(X)$, subtract $\zeta(X)I$, and normalize to make the value at $\xi$ one. The product of these maps over all $\zeta\ne\xi$ is the identity on $U_\xi$ and zero on all other pieces. The pieces span $W^*$, so this product is the projection $e_\xi$ onto $U_\xi$. It follows that $C\cong F^c$, where $c$ is the number of characters. Both the projections and the decomposition can alternatively be computed by bases of the indicated sums.

Every $H\in C^\times$ is the support-mode map of an actual tensor automorphism. If its scalar on $L_i$ is $h_i$, then $A_i(H^{-T}w)=h_i^{-1} A_i(w)$. In the decomposition of the source use $P_H=h_i^{-1} I$ on every copy of the corresponding $U$-vertex, $Q_H=I$, and $R_H=H^{-T}$. A zero-support summand can use the identity. Thus a successful $G$ stays successful under right multiplication by $C^\times$.

The classes of $E^\times$ under right multiplication by $C^\times$ can be enumerated efficiently. There is a direct sum $E=\bigoplus_\xi Ee_\xi$, since the $e_\xi$ are pairwise orthogonal and sum to one. Each $Ee_\xi$ has dimension $b_\xi\ge 1$. For an invertible $X$, every $Xe_\xi$ is nonzero, and right multiplication by $H\in C^\times$ independently rescales the nonzero components. Enumerate one representative of each projective line in each $Ee_\xi$, add the selected representatives, and discard singular sums by elimination. Two units yield the same tuple of projective lines precisely when they are in the same class. The number of candidates before discarding singular sums is at most
$$
 \prod_\xi\frac{q^{b_\xi}-1}{q-1}
 \ \le\ (q+1)^{\dim E-\dim C},
$$
where the inequality is $\sum_{j=0}^{b_\xi-1}q^j\le(q+1)^{b_\xi-1}$ on each factor. For each surviving $X$, use $R=(G_0X)^{-T}$ and run the ordered-slice module-isomorphism test on the full original tensors. That test decides the actual tensor relation and produces $P,Q$. Together with the demonstrated completeness of the coset search, this gives an end-to-end deterministic $L^{O(1)}(q+1)^{\dim E-\dim C}$ bound to decision and search. In particular only one full-tensor check is needed for $E=C$, even when $E$ has large dimension. The dimension difference is available before enumeration.

## 5. Prescribed determinant triples

For a fixed candidate passing the strict test, all witnesses with that slice map are determined up to precomposition by a source strict automorphism, the unit group of $\operatorname{End}(M_A)$. Across the full tested class one additionally precomposes by the explicitly constructed $C^\times$ lifts. Consequently the set of actual determinant triples for that class is the triple of the reference witness times the subgroup generated by the two kinds of determinant triples. Compute the $C^\times$ images using one generator of $F^\times$ at each $e_\xi$. These are explicit matrices on the original input after changing back from the decomposed bases.

For the strict group, the Jacobson radical acts nilpotently on both vertex spaces. Its reduction kernel contributes only determinant one on both. On each semisimple quotient factor $M_m(K)$, the determinant on a vertex space is a power of $N_{K/F}(\det_{K} X)$, with exponent obtained by its composition multiplicities on the radical filtration. This follows from the constituent representations being column modules over $M_m(K)$ and block upper-triangularity of the filtration. To generate the strict determinant image it suffices to lift one diagonal unit per factor whose entry has norm generating $F^\times$. Such lifts exist because the radical is nilpotent. Finite-algebra decomposition computes the quotient, the matrix presentations, and preimages of its displayed units in polynomial time over fixed $F$.

For completeness, a field norm generator is computable here without factoring $|K|-1$. For each prime $\ell$ dividing the fixed $q-1$, start with an element of order $\ell$ in $F^\times$. In $K$ extract $\ell$-th roots repeatedly until none exists. At each successful step the element's order is multiplied by $\ell$, so the last element generates the $\ell$-primary subgroup of $K^\times$. Its norm generates the corresponding subgroup in $F^\times$ by surjectivity of the finite-field norm. Use the product over the distinct primes. Root tests use polynomial factoring over $K$ in fixed characteristic at degrees bounded in terms of $q$, and there are only $O(\log |K|)$ steps. This argument also covers $q=2$ with an empty generator list. [Ivanyos–Qiao, finite-algebra algorithms in Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495v3)

The determinant group has at most $(q-1)^3$ elements. Enumerating products of the computed generator triples gives both membership and a witness word. Apply the word to the explicit source automorphism matrices, compose with the reference witness, and verify the original tensor equation and all requested determinants. If the slice kernel was removed, any required nonzero determinant in that mode can be achieved independently using the nonzero kernel. The retained endpoint spaces already include their full strict automorphism groups. This proves the same runtime bound for SL-TI and for any prescribed determinant triple.

## 6. A family with no line markers

Let $W^*=X\oplus X$ with $k=\dim X\ge2$, $J$ the nilpotent shift sending $e_i$ to $e_{i-1}$ and $J e_1=0$, and $D=e_1e_1^T$. Consider the five subspaces
$$
 X\oplus0,\quad 0\oplus X,\quad \Gamma(I),\quad \Gamma(J),\quad\Gamma(D),
$$
where $\Gamma(B)=\{(x,Bx)\mid x\in X\}$. A linear map preserving the first two is block diagonal, and preserving the diagonal $\Gamma(I)$ forces its two blocks to agree. Preservation of the last two requires the common block to commute with $J,D$. The commutant of $J$ consists of polynomials in $J$, because one image of a cyclic vector determines the whole commuting map. A polynomial in $J$ commuting with $D$ preserves $\ker D$. Evaluating its first row on $e_2,\ldots,e_k$ kills each positive coefficient. Thus the common endomorphism algebra of the five subspaces is scalar.

Each of these $k$-dimensional subspaces $L$ comes from an indecomposable summand of endpoint dimensions $(k,k)$. With a basis $v_0,\ldots,v_{k-1}$ for $L$, take $A_L(w)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} v_j(w)J^j$. The surjection of coefficients means that the endomorphism equations include $P=Q$ and $P J=J Q$, hence give the local algebra $F[J]\cong F[t]/(t^k)$. Its only idempotents are zero and one, so the tuple is indecomposable, with support $L$.

Take these five types with distinct multiplicities and append any further core, choosing the multiplicities sufficiently separated above all multiplicities in the core. This ensures each remains uniquely tagged even if some type already occurred in the core. For example if all core multiplicities are at most $M$, add $(i+1)(M+1)$ copies of the $i$th chosen type. Their final multiplicities are in disjoint intervals and exceed $M$. The algorithm receives the raw tensor after arbitrary endpoint and slice coordinate changes and recovers the needed labels itself. The five recovered labels force $E=C=F I$ regardless of the residual core, making the entire isomorphism and prescribed-determinant decision polynomial. The five summands are witnesses through $k$-dimensional supports. One can also choose the core to have no one-dimensional support summands.

## Comparison and scope

The established module decomposition and isomorphism algorithms are essential ingredients. Earlier project work on geometric rank loci used arrangement transporters and semisimple determinant images. The new combination here uses ordered-slice indecomposables as the raw source of labels, with a checkable relative dimension and an explicit quotient by the harmless block scales. The common repetition theorem upgrades classical direct-sum cancellation to maximal raw extraction and proves that removal is compatible with amplifications on both overlapping mode pairs. Linear-algebraic coloring gadgets and average-case coordinate-recovery algorithms are related precedents. The conclusions apply to the stated structural classes and make no claim of generic small residual dimension or of priority or Lean certification.
