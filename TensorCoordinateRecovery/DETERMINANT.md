# Recovering slice coordinates from split determinants

## Theorem

Let $A(x)=\sum_{j=1}^k x_j A_j$ be a rational or fixed-finite-field $n$-by-$n$ matrix pencil in $k$ variables. Suppose its nonzero determinant is a product of powers of ground-field linear forms. Each projective linear factor whose multiplicity occurs exactly once receives that multiplicity as an intrinsic label. Suppose these labeled forms span the $k$-dimensional dual space and their pointwise projective stabilizer is trivial. Then ordinary tensor isomorphism between two such pencils is decidable in deterministic polynomial bit time, and on acceptance the algorithm returns all three change-of-basis matrices. There is no fixed bound on $k$ in this theorem. A basis among the labeled forms and one other labeled form with all coordinates nonzero suffice for the stabilizer hypothesis. Over $\mathbb F_q$ the same algorithm works if the pointwise stabilizer has $c$ independent diagonal blocks, with a factor of at most $(q-1)^{c-1}$ in the runtime. In particular over $\mathbb F_2$ any basis of labeled factors suffices.

All factor lines and multiplicities are extracted from the matrices themselves without expanding a polynomial in $k$ variables. The promise includes repeated factors and pencils for which every base-field evaluation is singular. Over the rationals, a subclass with simultaneously triangularizable normalized slices also has a deterministic raw-input recognition procedure. All results below are informal.

## 1. Separating directions from raw matrices

Write $f(x)=\det A(x)=C\prod_{i=1}^r \ell_i(x)^{m_i}$, with the $\ell_i$ pairwise nonproportional. Let $F$ be the input field. Use $F$ itself over the rationals. Over a fixed finite field use an extension $K/F$ of size greater than $1+(k-1)(n+\binom n2)+n$. For a fixed field the extension can be constructed deterministically by testing monic polynomials of the least degree meeting this size bound until one is irreducible. Its cardinality is polynomial in $n,k$ with a field-dependent constant. The extra space for evaluation and arithmetic is polynomial. In the case $k=1$ the sole possible factor is the coordinate form and can be handled directly.

For $k\ge2$, put $u(t)=(1,t,\ldots,t^{k-1})^\mathsf{T}$. Each nonzero $\ell_i(u(t))$ is a nonzero univariate polynomial of degree at most $k-1$. Test the first $n(k-1)+1$ distinct parameters to find a vector $p=u(t)$ with $A(p)$ invertible. Normalize each form by $\ell_i(p)$ so that $\ell_i(p)=1$, and set $M(x)=A(p)^{-1}A(x)$. Then
$$
 \det(yI-M(x))=\prod_i (y-\ell_i(x))^{m_i}.
$$
For $v=u(t)$, the distinct normalized forms $\ell_i,\ell_j$ can agree on $v$ for at most $k-1$ parameters. Among $1+(k-1)\binom n2$ distinct parameters some $v$ separates every label. To find one, maximize the number of distinct roots of the univariate characteristic polynomial of $M(v)$ over this list. The multiplicities at a maximizing $v$ are precisely the $m_i$. Over $\mathbb Q$ use deterministic rational factorization. Over the constructed finite field, enumerate its elements and divide out each linear factor repeatedly, including inseparable powers. The normalization and this choice of $v$ can use extension-field parameters while all extracted projective lines are defined over $F$. [Lenstra–Lenstra–Lovász rational factorization](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01457454)

## 2. Exact coordinates even in small characteristic

At the separating direction let $\lambda_i=\ell_i(v)$. For each coordinate vector $e_j$, compute the bivariate polynomial
$$
 H_{ij}(z,s)=\det((\lambda_i+z)I-M(v)-sM(e_j)).
$$
Its total degree is at most $n$. Evaluations on an $(n+1)$-by-$(n+1)$ grid followed by bivariate interpolation suffice. The lowest nonzero homogeneous part has degree $m_i$ and equals
$$
 C_i(z-s\ell_i(e_j))^{m_i},\qquad C_i=\prod_{h\ne i}(\lambda_i-\lambda_h)^{m_h}\ne0.
$$
Take the coefficient of $z^{m_i}$ to read $C_i$. If the characteristic is zero, put $b_i=1$. In characteristic $p_0>0$, put $b_i=p_0^{v_{p_0}(m_i)}$. The coefficient of $z^{m_i-b_i}s^{b_i}$ in this part is
$$
 C_i\binom{m_i}{b_i}(-\ell_i(e_j))^{b_i}.
$$
The binomial multiplier is nonzero: in characteristic $p_0$, writing $m_i=b_i h_i$ gives $(1+X)^{m_i}=(1+X^{b_i})^{h_i}$ and $h_i\not\equiv0\pmod{p_0}$. Divide by the multiplier and invert the Frobenius power over the finite field, or use the ordinary first coefficient in characteristic zero. This gives all values of each linear form. Normalize each recovered coefficient row by its first nonzero entry. The normalized row lies in $F$, even if the probes used $K$.

The reconstruction only computes univariate characteristic polynomials and determinants with two formal variables. It applies to defective normalized slices as well as diagonalisable ones. It uses neither eigenvectors nor division by the multiplicity in positive characteristic. One can reuse $\det(yI-M(v)-sM(e_j))$ for all roots and shift $y$ individually. For context, earlier work recovered finite roots from rational binary pencils. The step here recovers the full arrangement in an unbounded number of variables with multiplicities, including characteristic dividing a multiplicity. [Earlier binary-pencil proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FourIsomorphismResearchBets/PENCILS.md)

## 3. Align the slice coordinates

If two pencils $A,B$ are tensor-isomorphic with slice substitution $R$, then
$$
 P A(Rx)=B(x)Q
$$
for invertible $P,Q$. Taking determinants shows that their weighted arrangements of factor lines correspond under $R$. Any factor with a unique multiplicity must go to the factor with the same label, so reject if the multiplicity multisets differ. Choose a basis of labeled rows $F_A$ in the first arrangement. The corresponding rows $F_B$ must be independent. Any surviving $R$ satisfies
$$
 F_A R=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_k) F_B.
$$
For each remaining labeled pair, express the rows in their respective bases as $\alpha,\beta$. Their supports must be equal. On that support the condition is that $\alpha_i t_i/\beta_i$ is constant. Thus it supplies ratios of the $t_i$ within that support. Make a graph on the $k$ basis indices by connecting the indices of each support, for instance by a spanning star in that support. Propagate ratios along the graph and reject any inconsistent cycle. With $c$ connected components all solutions have one free nonzero multiplier per component. In particular, the labeled configuration has trivial pointwise projective stabilizer exactly when $c=1$ over the algebraic closure. Over a particular finite field the projective stabilizer has exactly $(q-1)^{c-1}$ elements. Connectivity and all ratio calculations are polynomial-time operations. A projective frame among the uniquely tagged points implies connectivity for any chosen basis of the tagged points, because its pointwise projective stabilizer is trivial.

A common scalar multiplier on $R$ is absorbed in $Q$. Over $\mathbb Q$ in the connected case use the one projective candidate. In the finite-field case try the indicated number of projective candidates. For each candidate check strict equivalence using constructive module isomorphism for a two-vertex $k$-arrow Kronecker representation. Concretely put the two vertex projections and the $k$ coefficient maps in off-diagonal blocks acting on the sum of the two endpoint spaces. Their module intertwiners have exactly the equation above. Test for an invertible intertwiner and find it with the constructive strict module-isomorphism algorithm. This step uses the actual original coefficient matrices, so two isomorphic arrangements alone cannot create a false yes. Conversely a true transporter induces one of the tested projective maps and then a strict transporter. [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski on constructive module isomorphism](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf)

All probe counts are polynomial in $n,k$. Over the rationals, moment curve parameters are integers of bit length $O(\log(nk))$, so the coordinate entries have polynomial bit length. Matrix inverses, determinants, characteristic polynomials, rational interpolation, and the output factor rows have polynomial bit complexity by denominator clearing and determinant height bounds. Reconstructing one slice map uses rational products along paths of length at most $k-1$, followed by linear algebra, so its bit length is polynomial too. The final strict test operates on inputs of polynomial bit size and returns a polynomial-bit witness. Over a fixed finite field, extension size, all search lists, and the strict tests are likewise polynomial aside from the stated component factor.

For an explicit infinite growing-width family, take $k+1$ forms $x_1,\ldots,x_k,x_1+\cdots+x_k$ with respective multiplicities $1,2,\ldots,k+1$. Then $n=(k+1)(k+2)/2$. Put them on the diagonal with their multiplicities and allow arbitrary entries above the diagonal that are linear in the $k$ variables. All three input bases may be changed arbitrarily. The proof covers all their pair comparisons with $k=\Theta(\sqrt n)$. This replaces an explicit $\mathrm{PGL}_k(\mathbb F_q)$ scan, of size $q^{k^2-1}\prod_{i=2}^k (1-q^{-i})$, by a single strict comparison on these examples. This comparison concerns explicit slice-map enumeration. It does not compare performance with a state-of-the-art TI solver.

## 4. A recognizable rational subclass

On the split pencil promise the determinant is used as a black box given by the pencil. The preceding samples do not establish general deterministic polynomial identity testing for arbitrary pencils. A raw recognizable subclass is available with simultaneous triangularity. Run the bounded search for $p$. If it succeeds, test whether the rational tuple $A(p)^{-1}A_j$ has a common rational complete invariant flag. If it does, the determinant really splits into the linear diagonal profiles. This condition is invariant on the class. A different invertible slice $p'$ replaces the matrices by $M(p')^{-1}M(e_j)$, which preserves the same flag. Mixing the slices and making independent endpoint basis changes likewise preserves existence of the flag.

The flag test is polynomial. Find a common rational eigenvector by intersecting eigenspaces successively over the generators. At each step keep the nonzero simultaneous eigenspaces, classified by their eigenvalue tuples so far. Their sum is direct, so there are at most $n$ subspaces to refine. A common eigenvector yields an invariant line, which can be passed to the quotient. If a complete invariant flag exists, the images of its subspaces in that quotient yield a complete one there, irrespective of the selected line. Failure to find a rational line at any step therefore excludes simultaneous triangularizability.

Alternatively the algorithm can construct the flag in the original space without successive coordinate inverses, maintaining a subspace $U$ known invariant and searching for $v$ with $(M(e_j)-a_j I)v\in U$ for all $j$. Eigenvalue options for each stage are among the rational roots of the original characteristic polynomials. A polynomial bit-size choice of each lift follows from bounded heights as follows. Let $\mathcal A$ be the tuple's generated algebra, computable by keeping a basis of words of length at most $n^2$ in the original matrices. In a simultaneously triangular tuple algebra the ideal generated by all commutators is strictly upper triangular. Conversely, if that ideal annihilates a subspace, the tuple commutes there. The filtration $V_i=\{v:J^i v=0\}$ is invariant, where $J$ is this two-sided ideal. Compute the needed ideals and powers as subspaces of the original matrix algebra. Check $J^n=0$, then on each successive quotient of the filtration the tuple commutes. Require each generator's original characteristic polynomial to split. It follows that each quotient has a rational triangular flag by joint eigenline induction for commuting split matrices. These conditions are necessary for triangularizability and sufficient by lifting the quotient flags. For bit bounds all $J^i$ have bases from products of at most $n$ generators of $J$, which can be selected as commutator products of bounded-length words in the given tuple. Thus all ideal tests can keep actual selected products of polynomial bit size and use polynomial-size rational rank tests. No extraction of an explicit flag is needed for recognition. When the bounded search finds no $p$, the required nonzero split determinant cannot hold.

## 5. A complementary generic finite-width theorem over the rationals

Fix $k\ge 3$ and let $n\ge k+1$. There is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time algorithm for ordinary TI whenever the nonzero determinant form of the source has finite projective stabilizer over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. The form may be irreducible or singular. When the ordered-slice strict endomorphism algebra of the source is scalar, the algorithm also decides determinant-one TI and returns a witness if one exists. Both properties are simultaneously generic and can be tested from the raw entries. Thus this branch covers a Zariski-open dense class without split factors, with $k$ fixed.

Compute $f_A=\det A(x)$ and $f_B=\det B(x)$ by dense interpolation. The variable count is constant here. The Lie algebra test on $f_A$ forms the linear equations
$$
 \mathrm d f_A(x)[Hx]=c f_A(x)
$$
in the entries of $H$ and the scalar $c$. The stabilizer of the line of $f_A$ in $\mathrm{GL}_k$ is an algebraic group; in characteristic zero its dimension equals the dimension of its tangent Lie algebra. Its scalar matrices have dimension one. Therefore its projective stabilizer is finite exactly when the solution dimension is one. Inspect this dimension by linear algebra.

In the projective normalization chart for each candidate nonzero entry of $R$, put that entry equal to one and solve
$$
       f_A(Rx)=u f_B(x),\qquad v\det R=1,\qquad zu=1
$$
by coefficient comparison. If $f_B=0$ there is no match. Otherwise the projective maps form an empty set or a torsor for the finite stabilizer, and the displayed auxiliary coordinates are determined. Thus the solution set in every chart is zero dimensional or empty. There are only $k^2+3$ variables. For a deterministic rational solver, compute a nonzero univariate eliminant for each coordinate by bounded-degree exact Macaulay elimination. Dubé’s bound, applied with the chosen coordinate last in lexicographic order, guarantees an eliminant of polynomial degree in a fixed number of variables. The effective ideal-membership degree bound then guarantees a polynomial-degree representation in the original generators. Intersect the corresponding span of monomial multiples with the univariate coefficient space by exact rational elimination. All linear algebra is polynomial size with rational determinant height bounds. Factor these univariate polynomials over $\mathbb Q$, take the Cartesian product of their rational root lists, and verify every solution. This Cartesian product has polynomial size because the number of lists is fixed. An inconsistent system is recognized by the unit ideal. Each surviving rational projectivity is passed to the same constructive strict test as above. For the size estimates one can also use fixed-variable certified zero-dimensional solving. [Dubé on Gröbner degree bounds](https://doi.org/10.1137/0219053) · [Effective ideal membership, Theorem A](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~matthias/pdf/ideal2.pdf) · [Kobel and Sagraloff on certified zero-dimensional solving](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.08944)

For the determinant-one branch, check that $\{(P,Q):PA_j=A_jQ\text{ for all }j\}$ has dimension one. This ensures that for any strict witness $(P_0,R_0,Q_0)$, every strict witness with projective slice map $[R_0]$ has the form $(aP_0,bR_0,ab Q_0)$ for $a,b\ne0$. The conditions are precisely
$$
    b^k\det R_0=1,\qquad a^n\det P_0=1,\qquad b^n\det Q_0=\det P_0 .
$$
Enumerate the rational roots for each of the first two equations and check the last. Root extraction is deterministic polynomial bit time by taking exact integer roots of the numerator and denominator, checking signs separately.

To verify genericity, for the first rank test take a diagonal pencil whose determinant has distinct ground-field factors and includes $x_1,\ldots,x_k,x_1+\cdots+x_k$. A projectivity preserving this finite arrangement lies over a permutation of its factors. The kernel acts trivially on a projective frame, so the stabilizer is finite. Hence some coefficient-matrix minor of order $k^2$ in the Lie algebra test is a nonzero polynomial in the raw entries. Its degree is at most $n k^2$. For the strict test use among the slices the identity, a diagonal matrix with distinct entries, and a matrix whose off-diagonal entries are all one. The first forces $P=Q$, the second forces it diagonal, and the third forces all diagonal entries equal. Hence some minor of order $2n^2-1$ in this test is also a nonzero polynomial. Its degree is at most $2n^2-1$. Their product is nonzero in the coefficient ring, so both tests hold generically, even though the two example pencils were used separately. If all entries are independent uniform integers from a set of $H$ distinct integers, the probability of either test failing is at most $(nk^2+2n^2-1)/H$. Thus the coverage statement also holds in a concrete bounded-bit sampling model. The time bound in this section is polynomial for each fixed $k$. It does not replace the growing-width algorithm of Sections 1–4.
## Evidence and scope

The method combines determinant profiles with recovery from a small family of observations and a final constructive strict test. Deterministic moment-curve probing and characteristic-zero black-box factorization of products of linear forms have precedents. The explicit positive-characteristic coefficient rule above is used in the tensor transporter algorithm, where the frame controls the actual slice substitution. [Koiran–Ressayre, §§5–6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03663) · [Koiran–Skomra, §§6–7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02021) The direct coefficient calculation extends to fixed positive characteristic without assuming simple roots. This is a polynomial transporter algorithm on a structural class of general-linear tensors with growing slice width. Its coverage proof does not establish density in the set of unrestricted tensors. Publication priority for the particular algorithm and class is unestablished.

The exact control [check_determinant.py](check_determinant.py) scrambles all three modes of a triangular tensor over $\mathbb F_2$, gives the recovery procedure only the resulting slices, and checks the five recovered multiplicity-labeled lines. The pencil has no invertible base-field evaluation, and two factors have even multiplicities, 2 and 4.
