# Recovering hidden coordinates directly from tensors

Four informal proofs give constructive algorithms on identifiable classes of raw tensors. They use distinct input structures and do not share a universal coverage hypothesis. The proofs and exact or numerical controls are linked below. Publication priority for the new formulations is unestablished.

| # | Proof | Result and scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Crossed invertible flattenings](FINITE.md) | Constructive polynomial-time ordinary and determinant-one isomorphism on an explicit nonempty Zariski-open class of matched even-order tensors, over the rationals and every fixed finite field. Explicit coverage over large fields, infinite accepted families over small fields, and canonical representatives on a stronger class. |
| 2 | [Ordered-slice modules](ALGEBRA.md) | Polynomially extract all maximal pairwise identity repetitions from a concise tensor over a fixed finite field, recovering an isomorphism-equivalent core. For a three-way tensor the repetitions combine into a rectangular matrix multiplication factor. A separate algorithm recovers labeled slice subspaces and searches only the effective ambiguity for ordinary or prescribed-determinant transporters. |
| 3 | [Stable recovery under noise](NOISE.md) | Deterministic polynomial-bit-time recovery of paired central block projectors in unknown nonorthogonal bases. It covers repeated profiles and rectangular full-column factors. The spectral computation is polynomial in the logarithms of inverse gaps and accuracy. |
| 4 | [Determinant-labeled coordinates](DETERMINANT.md) | Recover split determinant factors without dense multivariate expansion. Intrinsic multiplicity labels give polynomial-time transporters on growing-width pencils, including characteristic two and matrices singular at every base-field slice. A complementary generic branch treats fixed-width rational pencils with nonsplit determinant and can also enforce determinant one. |

The crossed-flattening theorem gives the broadest generic coverage. In its scalar-commutant class, two partial traces of powers of one crossed inverse produce matrices related by conjugation in each mode. Intertwiner linear systems recover half the bases up to scalars, and a final Kronecker-product check recovers the other half and verifies the original tensor equation. For four equal modes of size $n$, this takes $O(n^6)$ field operations on the stated class. The exact binary control recovered 30 planted transporters. It accepted 101 of 108 raw inputs with both required cuts invertible, drawn from 1,200 uniform inputs across six formats. A uniform positive fixed-field coverage guarantee for random binary tensors remains open here.

The module theorem provides an end-to-end compression bound. Over a fixed odd prime field, an $N$-by-$N$-by-$N$ input with recovered core side $O(\sqrt N)$ can be decided in polynomial preprocessing time plus $q^{\widetilde O(N^{3/4})}$ by applying a published general decision algorithm to the recovered core. Its proof and comparator are specific to inputs with a sufficiently small recovered core. The noisy computation improves the earlier explicit spectral-filter construction. The split-determinant algorithm replaces enumerating all slice changes by at most one strict test in the connected labeled case. No wall-clock improvement over a competing unrestricted solver has been measured. [Published linear-length reductions and runtime](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/189643)

The class conditions are part of the statements. The results give no worst-case improvement for unrestricted TI, and the unrestricted ordinary-versus-determinant-one reduction remains open. The proofs are informal.
