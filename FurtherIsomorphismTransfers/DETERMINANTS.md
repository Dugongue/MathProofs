# Polynomial-time rational tensor isomorphism for integral ternary determinants

**Theorem.** For rational $n\times n\times3$ tensors with $n\ge3$ whose pencil determinant is geometrically irreducible, ordinary and determinant-one tensor isomorphism are decidable in deterministic polynomial bit time. The class is recognizable in polynomial time. The determinant can be singular and have an infinite projective automorphism group. For any fixed slice dimension $k$, the same algorithms apply under the promise of a geometrically irreducible determinant with finite projective stabilizer.

Write $A(x),B(x)$ for the matrices of linear forms. A transporter consists of invertible rational constant matrices $P,Q,R$ with $PA(Rx)=B(x)Q$. Inversion and transposition on individual modes depend on tensor conventions and preserve the determinant-one test. Put $f_A=\det A,f_B=\det B$. First find projectivities carrying $f_A$ to a scalar multiple of $f_B$.

## Strict transporters

Fix any such slice map over an extension $K/\mathbb Q$. Write $S=K[x_1,\ldots,x_k]$ and $f$ for the shared irreducible determinant factor. The graded module $M_A=\operatorname{coker}(A:S(-1)^n\to S^n)$ has rank one and is torsion-free over $S/(f)$. The adjugate induces an injection into a shift of $(S/(f))^n$. If $\operatorname{adj}(A)v=(\det A)w$, multiply by $A$ and cancel to get $v=Aw$. At the discrete valuation ring $S_{(f)}$ the determinant has valuation one, so exactly one elementary divisor contributes to the generic rank. The same holds for $B$.

Every nonzero degree-zero map between the cokernels is injective, by torsion-freeness and rank one. Identical Hilbert functions from the presentations give surjectivity in every degree. A degree-zero map lifts uniquely to a constant pair $(P,Q)$ with $PA=BQ$. Lift the degree-zero generators, then lift the degree-one relations through the injective presentation. An inverse map lifts as well, making both matrices invertible. Over $\overline K$, the degree-zero endomorphisms form a finite-dimensional domain in the function field of the cone, hence are scalars. The linear equations commute with scalar extension. Thus the strict system has dimension zero or one and all its nonzero pairs are invertible. This extends the line-bundle argument to singular integral determinants. [Beauville](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9910030)

## Finite projective stabilizers

Solve the Lie equation $\sum_i(Xx)_i\partial_i f=\lambda f$ and quotient by scalars. In characteristic zero this quotient has dimension zero exactly when the projective stabilizer is finite. In each chart $R_{ij}=1$, impose the coefficient equations $f_A(Rx)=z f_B(x)$ and $w\det R=1$. The solution set is finite or empty, since the projective transporter is a torsor under the projective stabilizer and $z,w$ are determined by $R$.

The number of variables depends only on $k$. Fixed-variable Gröbner degree and ideal-membership bounds give polynomial bit time by rational linear algebra in the bounded-degree monomial space. Compute a nonzero univariate elimination polynomial for each coordinate, factor over $\mathbb Q$ to get its rational roots and test their Cartesian product. That product is polynomial in size because the number of variables is fixed. [Dubé](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/abs/10.1137/0219053) · [Aschenbrenner, Theorem A on ideal membership](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~maschenbrenner/pdf/ideal2.pdf) · [Lenstra–Lenstra–Lovász](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01457454)

For each rational $R$, solve the strict linear system. Given a nonzero pair $(P,Q)$, all lifts of the same projectivity have the form $(aP,acQ,cR)$, with $a,c\in\mathbb Q^\times$. Test $a^n=(\det P)^{-1}$, $(ac)^n=(\det Q)^{-1}$, and $c^k=(\det R)^{-1}$ by exact roots of numerators and denominators, checking the possible signs.

## Infinite projective stabilizers in three variables

Take a rational nonscalar $X$ in the linear Lie solution space. Over the algebraic closure its commuting semisimple and nilpotent parts induce commuting semisimple and nilpotent operators on the space of forms of degree $n$. An eigenvector of their sum is killed by the nilpotent part. In Jordan type $2+1$, the nilpotent derivation is $y\partial_x$, whose invariants are binary forms. In Jordan type $3$ the derivation is $y\partial_x+z\partial_y$. Inverting $z$ gives the translation coordinate $y/z$ and the invariant $\Delta=y^2-2xz$. Its polynomial invariant ring is $K[z,\Delta]$ over the algebraic closure $K$: the localized kernel is $K[z,z^{-1},\Delta]$, and a nonzero polynomial of $\Delta$ stays nonzero after setting $z=0$. Every homogeneous invariant factors into factors of degree at most two by factoring the weighted homogeneous polynomial with weights one and two. Thus geometric irreducibility in degree at least three forces the nilpotent part to vanish.

The semisimple matrix $X$ must have three distinct eigenvalues. With two eigenvalues, all monomials of $f$ have a fixed exponent on the one-dimensional eigenspace and the remaining binary form factors. With three distinct eigenvalues, the exponent vectors lie on an affine line, imposing both total degree and fixed dot product with the eigenvalues. The integral differences are multiples of a primitive integer vector. Remove any common monomial factor and factor the resulting polynomial in one Laurent monomial over the algebraic closure. Irreducibility leaves one primitive binomial with disjoint supports. In suitable coordinates it is $y^n-\gamma x^u z^v$, with $u,v>0$, $u+v=n$ and $\gcd(u,v)=1$. Both product variables are needed since a binary form would factor.

The Galois action permutes the three eigencoordinates and preserves the unordered support of the binomial. The singleton term distinguishes the $y$ direction and the exponents distinguish the other two. Equality $u=v$ would give a conic by primitivity. Hence the eigenspaces are rational and one obtains their basis by factoring a cubic and solving linear systems. Moreover the entire Lie algebra in these coordinates is diagonal. The off-diagonal terms from differentiating $y^n$ have $y$-degree $n-1$, whereas those from $x^u z^v$ have $y$-degree at most one. All these off-diagonal monomials are distinct and outside the original support. Thus the projective Lie algebra has dimension one, and any projective transporter matches its intrinsic sets of eigenlines. [Cano–Loeza–Dávila Figueroa, projective stabilizers of curves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02620)

If just one projective stabilizer is infinite, reject. Otherwise try the six eigenline permutations, keeping the ones that match the supports and exponents. Normalize the $y$-coordinate multiplier to one. The determinant-curve equation becomes $r_x^u r_z^v=\eta$ for a known rational unit $\eta$. Bézout gives a particular solution $(r_{x,0},r_{z,0})$ and shows that all rational solutions are $(r_{x,0}t^v,r_{z,0}t^{-u})$ for $t\in\mathbb Q^\times$. Write $R(t)$ for this matrix in the original input bases. This enumerates the entire rational projective transporter by at most six rational one-parameter families.

## Solving strict equivalence and determinant one on the torus

The strict system $PA(R(t)x)=B(x)Q$ is a matrix in the $2n^2$ unknown entries over the Laurent polynomial ring. Compute its generic rank over $\mathbb Q(t)$. If the generic kernel is zero, choose a nonzero full-column-rank minor, clear negative powers of $t$, and enumerate its nonzero rational roots. Every parameter admitting strict equivalence is on that list. Use the fixed-projectivity tests on those roots.

If the generic kernel has dimension one, it has dimension one at every nonzero parameter over the algebraic closure. Rank can only drop under specialization, and the cokernel-module argument bounds each kernel by dimension one. Form a polynomial generator of the generic kernel by clearing denominators and dividing out the gcd of all components. The components have no common zero, so the generator specializes to an invertible strict pair $(P(t),Q(t))$ everywhere on the torus. Each determinant is a polynomial with no nonzero zero, hence a monomial. Write them as $A_0t^\alpha,B_0t^\beta$, and write $\det R(t)=D_0t^{v-u}$. Ordinary TI is decided by taking $t=1$. The exact determinant-one test is

$$
 a^n t^\alpha=A_0^{-1},\qquad
 a^n c^n t^\beta=B_0^{-1},\qquad
 c^3 t^{v-u}=D_0^{-1}.
$$

These equations retain the determinants in the original input bases. Apply integer Smith normal form to the three-by-three exponent matrix. Its column and row operations are invertible substitutions and combinations of equations over the multiplicative group of rational units. A zero row requires its modified constant to equal one. A positive Smith entry $d$ requires its modified constant to be a rational $d$-th power.

This test takes polynomial bit time even for compactly represented powers. Compute a gcd-free basis of pairwise coprime positive integers $b_i>1$ for the original numerators and denominators, storing their signs separately. Carry the Smith row operations on the binary exponents of this basis. An absolute value $\prod_i b_i^{e_i}$ is a rational $d$-th power precisely when each $b_i$ is an exact integer power with exponent $d/\gcd(d,e_i)$, by prime valuations and pairwise coprimality. Use exact integer roots and reject any such root exponent larger than the base bit length. The sign uses parity. Identity tests require zero exponents and positive sign. [Kannan–Bachem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040) · [Bernstein, gcd-free bases](https://cr.yp.to/lineartime/dcba-20040404.pdf)

## Recognition, bit complexity, and control

For fixed $k$ compute the determinant polynomial by interpolation on a polynomial-size rational grid. At each point exact determinant computation and interpolation have polynomial bit complexity. For recognition, reject a zero determinant or one divisible by the dehomogenizing variable. Dehomogenize and apply Gao's bivariate absolute-irreducibility rank test in characteristic zero. For the derivative-coprimality condition, first reject a nonsquarefree polynomial using its two partial derivatives and gcd. Try degree plus one rational directions and choose one whose directional derivative is coprime to the polynomial. Each absolute factor rules out at most one trial direction, since any nonzero derivative of the factor has smaller degree. An invertible rational change of coordinates makes that direction a coordinate direction. For larger fixed $k$ the theorem is stated with an irreducibility promise. [Gao, absolute factorization via differential equations](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-02-01428-X)

Polynomial coefficients in the constant-variable systems and Laurent matrices have polynomial degree and polynomial bit size. In the finite branch this follows by expansion in a fixed number of variables. In the torus branch the Bézout exponents have absolute values at most $n$, and the two variable-dependent exponents are $v,-u$. Polynomial-matrix ranks, null vectors and minors can be computed fraction-free. Determinant coefficient bounds or fraction-free elimination give polynomial bit size for these polynomials and the gcd computations. The finite branch uses fixed-dimensional ideal-membership bounds so that the elimination has polynomially many monomials at polynomial coefficient height. Rational polynomial factoring and the integer operations above give the claimed deterministic bit-time bound.

As a determinant-sensitive control, take even $n\ge4$ and $A=yI$ minus a weighted cyclic shift with one edge labeled $x$ and $n-1$ labeled $z$. Its determinant is the primitive binomial $y^n-xz^{n-1}$, hence geometrically irreducible. Left-multiply the pencil by a matrix of determinant $-1$ to obtain $B$. They are ordinarily isomorphic. Every projective determinant match preserves the unique singleton axis. The coefficient of $y^n$ in $\det A(Rx)$ is therefore an even power of a nonzero rational number, and cannot equal $-1$. This certifies failure of determinant-one isomorphism, already on singular determinants with infinite stabilizer.

The new combination is the strict module algorithm at singular integral determinants and the exact rational determinant decision across the full positive-dimensional ternary branch. The closest sources found treat determinantal presentations, projective curve stabilizers, and general TI separately. The proof is informal. Publication priority remains unestablished. [Grochow–Qiao on general TI](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)
