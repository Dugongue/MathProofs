# Graph isomorphism for isospectral entanglement-breaking quantum channels

**Theorem.** There is a deterministic polynomial-time construction assigning every simple graph on $n\ge1$ vertices a channel on $d=3n^2$ levels, with rational Choi entries. For each $n$ all channels in the family have the same Choi spectrum and the same linear-superoperator spectrum. Each channel is trace-preserving, unital, self-adjoint for the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product, invertible as a linear operator, and entanglement-breaking. Its Choi state is positive definite, and its diamond distance from the completely depolarizing channel is less than $2^{-n}$. Two graphs are isomorphic exactly when the channels are isomorphic by independent unitary changes of input and output bases. The full Choi transporter has finite quotient exactly the graph transporter, with explicit phase fibers. The local-unitary orbit saturation of this family is recognizable in deterministic polynomial time on explicitly given Gaussian-rational Choi inputs, and is GI-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions.

The self-adjointness and fixed superoperator spectrum assertions concern the displayed templates in their common input and output basis. Independently changing those bases preserves the Choi spectrum and the unitary-isomorphism question. The result concerns exact isomorphism. Quantitative proximity to the depolarizing channel does not impose an approximate decision promise. The only invertibility assertion is as a linear map, with no claim that the inverse is a quantum channel.

## 1. A weighted Markov encoding

For every graph vertex $i$, create three anchor coordinates $a_i^+,a_i^-,a_i^0$. For every unordered pair $I=\{i,j\}$, create six coordinates $p_{I,1},\dots,p_{I,6}$. There are $d=3n+6\binom n2=3n^2$ coordinates. We construct a graph-independent weighted Laplacian $L$. Its diagonal is the total incident weight and each off-diagonal entry is minus the edge weight. At each triple of anchors use weights $1000n,2000n,4000n$, respectively for $+,-$, $+,0$, and $-,0$. Put $w=(-5,-3,-1,1,3,5)$. The port $p_{I,r}$ connects to the $+$ anchor of each endpoint of $I$ with weight $20+w_r$, and to the $-$ anchor of each endpoint with weight $20-w_r$. There are no other marker edges.

Put $b_0=(1,-1,-1,1,0,0)^T$ and $b_1=(1,-1,0,0,-1,1)^T$. On the six coordinates of every unordered pair add $b_t b_t^T$ to $L$, where $t=1$ means an edge and $t=0$ means a nonedge. Denote the resulting matrix by $K_G$. Both $b_t$ are orthogonal to ${\bf1}$ and $w$, have squared norm 4, and are permutations of each other. In particular $K_G{\bf1}=0$. The matrix $K_G$ is positive semidefinite, since the Laplacian and every added rank-one matrix are positive semidefinite.

The port diagonals of $L$ equal 80. The anchor diagonals in order $+,-,0$ are $3000n+120(n-1)$, $5000n+120(n-1)$, and $6000n$. These are distinct and exceed 81. In $K_G$ all port diagonals are 80 or 81. Every off-diagonal entry is at most 1. The three anchor types are recognizable from their distinct diagonals, and their triples are exactly the connected components of the nonzero off-diagonal relations restricted to anchors. Each remaining coordinate connects to exactly the $+,-$ anchors of two such triples, recovering its unordered pair. Its link weight to either $+$ anchor recovers its port index $r$. Finally the third port's diagonal, 81 for $b_0$ and 80 for $b_1$, recovers the edge bit. Therefore any simultaneous index permutation between two such matrices is exactly the coordinate map of a unique graph isomorphism. Every graph isomorphism induces such a map.

There are three graph-independent multisets. First, the multiset of diagonal entries of $K_G$ is fixed, because both added matrices have four diagonal ones and two diagonal zeros. Second, its off-diagonal entry multiset is fixed, because the only varying blocks are the pair blocks and the two rank-one choices are permutation conjugates. Third, the eigenvalue multiset is fixed. In each pair block, a Householder reflection along $b_0-b_1$ carries $b_0$ to $b_1$ and fixes ${\bf1},w$. Its coefficients are rational. The internal marker block is $80I$, and its couplings to other coordinates use vectors in the span of ${\bf1},w$. Thus each such reflection fixes the full marker by conjugation. The reflections on distinct blocks commute. They give an explicit orthogonal similarity between any two $K_G$ of the same order, fixing the global all-ones vector.

## 2. Channel normalization and both spectra

Let $J$ be the all-ones $d\times d$ matrix. Set $M=10^4n$, $\tau=2^{-n}$, $\epsilon=\tau/(4dM)$, $s=\tau/(8d)$, and $A_G=J/d+\epsilon K_G$. The bound $|(K_G)_{ij}|\le M$ follows immediately from the marker degrees and added rank-one blocks. Thus $A_G$ is symmetric and doubly stochastic, with every entry in $[(1-\tau/4)/d,(1+\tau/4)/d]$. It is also positive semidefinite.

Define a classical measure-and-prepare channel by
$$
\Lambda_G(X)=\sum_{i,j}(A_G)_{ij}\langle j|X|j\rangle\,|i\rangle\langle i|.
$$
Use $\Phi_G=(1-s)\Lambda_G+s\,{\rm id}$ as the final channel. Its normalized Choi state, using the output-first convention and $|\Omega\rangle=d^{-1/2}\sum_i|ii\rangle$, is
$$
\rho_G=(1-s)\sum_{i,j}(A_G)_{ij}|ij\rangle\langle ij|/d+s|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|.
$$
This is Gaussian-rational, in fact real rational. It is positive definite and both of its reduced states equal $I/d$. Rational bit sizes and the matrix dimensions are polynomial in the graph size.

On diagonal matrices the channel acts by $(1-s)A_G+sI$. On off-diagonal matrices it acts by $sI$. It follows that the channel is Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint and invertible. The orthogonal similarities from Step 1 fix $J$ and give the same spectrum on the diagonal part for all $G$ on $n$ vertices. Thus the full channel spectra match. To compare Choi spectra, split the Choi space into $D=\operatorname{span}\{|ii\rangle:i=1,\dots,d\}$ and its orthogonal complement. On the complement, the Choi eigenvalues are a fixed multiple of the off-diagonal entries of $A_G$, with the same multiset. On $D$ the restriction is a diagonal whose multiset is fixed, plus the same all-ones rank-one update. Permuting the diagonal to match another graph leaves that update unchanged. The Choi spectra therefore also match.

The channel remains entanglement-breaking even after the identity mixture. Indeed $(1-s)\operatorname{diag}(A_G/d)$ dominates $sd\,I/d^2$, because $(1-s)(1-\tau/4)>sd$. The sum $sd\,I/d^2+s|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|$ is proportional to a separable state. To see this, the Haar average of $|u\rangle\langle u|\otimes|\bar u\rangle\langle\bar u|$ over unit vectors equals $(I+d|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|)/(d(d+1))$, by taking the partial transpose of the normalized projector on the symmetric square. The remaining diagonal part is positive and separable in the standard product basis. Thus the normalized Choi state is separable, which characterizes entanglement-breaking channels. [Horodecki–Shor–Ruskai](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0302031)

Let $\Delta(X)=\operatorname{tr}(X)I/d$. The difference $\Lambda_G-\Delta$ first pinches to input diagonal blocks and then applies the signed classical matrix $A_G-J/d$. For any ancilla, the triangle inequality bounds its trace norm on block-diagonal inputs by the maximum absolute column sum times the input trace norm. Pinching has trace norm at most one even with an ancilla, since it is an average of unitary conjugations. Therefore $\|\Lambda_G-\Delta\|_\diamond\le\tau/4$. Using $\|{\rm id}-\Delta\|_\diamond\le2$ gives $\|\Phi_G-\Delta\|_\diamond\le\tau/4+2s<\tau$.

## 3. The actual unitary transporter

For output-first Choi states, independent unitary actions on the two factors express independent unitary changes of output and input bases on the channel. The input action uses the usual transpose in the Choi convention. Write their state action as $U\otimes V$.

The largest $d$ eigenvalues of every $\rho_G$ are separated from the rest, and their spectral space is exactly $D$. The matched diagonal entries of $A_G$ are strictly larger than every unmatched entry. Adding the positive update on $D$ can only increase its smallest eigenvalue. Thus any state transporter $U\otimes V$ sends $D$ to $D$. A nonzero decomposable vector $x\otimes y$ lies in $D$ only if both $x,y$ are supported on the same single coordinate. Apply this to $(U\otimes V)|ii\rangle$. The two unitaries have the form $Ue_i=\alpha_i e_{\pi(i)}$, $Ve_i=\beta_i e_{\pi(i)}$, with the same permutation and unit phases. Comparing all diagonal Choi weights then shows that $\pi$ simultaneously relabels $K_G$ to $K_H$. This is exactly a graph isomorphism by Step 1. Finally the Choi entries between $|ii\rangle$ and $|jj\rangle$ equal $s/d$ for $i\ne j$, so $\alpha_i\beta_i$ must be independent of $i$. Conversely these permutations and phases satisfy the full Choi equation. In particular the automorphism group on the two Choi factors is $\mathbb T^{d+1}\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. The proof describes the transporter without replacing either unitary by a general linear map. On the explicit real templates, an isomorphism can even be implemented by special orthogonal matrices. Use its graph permutation in both factors and the same diagonal sign change in both factors to correct its parity while keeping every phase product equal to one.

There is also a useful general diagonal statement. If $\operatorname{diag}(W_{ij})$ and $\operatorname{diag}(W'_{ij})$ are locally unitarily equivalent, then the entrywise intertwining equation implies $W_{ij}=W'_{kl}$ whenever $U_{ki}V_{lj}\ne0$. Expanding each unitary determinant exhibits a perfect matching of nonzero entries in its support, giving a row permutation and a column permutation with $W_{ij}=W'_{\pi(i),\sigma(j)}$. The converse is immediate. The same proof works for any finite number of diagonal tensor factors. For rational weights the permutations are exactly the isomorphisms of the corresponding edge-colored complete multipartite relations. This diagonal equivalence was conjectured in 2025 by Chu–Liu–Zhang. The subsequent 2026 result of Liu et al. covers a larger class of globally permutation-equivalent density matrices, so the diagonal statement is prior art. [2025 conjecture](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0219749925500145) · [2026 theorem](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physleta.2025.131223)

## 4. Raw rational Choi recognition after arbitrary basis scrambling

The recognizing algorithm accepts exactly Gaussian-rational Hermitian matrices locally unitarily equivalent to some constructed $\rho_G$. On this recognizable class, it extracts $G$ up to isomorphism and gives the GI upper bound. All operations on potentially scrambled inputs use the given matrix entries and known rational constants. Unitaries are not supplied.

Require a square local format $d=3n^2$ with $n\ge1$. The set of low Choi eigenvalues of a constructed state is contained in an explicit rational list $E$, computed from the possible off-diagonal entries $0,\pm1$, the three anchor weights with negative sign, and the port coupling weights with negative sign. This list is independent of $G$, may contain unused values, and every value in it is strictly below the smallest high eigenvalue. Form $S=\operatorname{im}p(\rho)$, where $p(z)=\prod_{\lambda\in E}(z-\lambda)$. Require $S$ to have dimension $d$, and compute its orthogonal projector $P_S$ rationally.

Flatten each $x\otimes y$ as $xy^T$. From a Gaussian-rational basis $M_a$ of $S$, compute
$$
\mathcal A=\operatorname{span}\{M_aM_b^*\},\qquad
\mathcal B=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{M_a^*M_b\}}.
$$
If the input was scrambled, these spaces are the full diagonal algebras in the two unknown orthonormal bases. Indeed $S$ then has an orthonormal basis $x_i\otimes y_i$ with both endpoint sets orthonormal, and the displayed products span the corresponding rank-one projections. Require each computed space to have dimension $d$ and its chosen basis matrices to commute pairwise. The spaces are closed under adjoint by definition. A commuting adjoint-closed family is simultaneously diagonalizable by a unitary, and dimension $d$ forces the space to equal the whole diagonal algebra there. Thus the checks give full orthonormal coordinate algebras and a product grid in which every diagonal cell is rank one.

Let $\sigma$ be the orthogonal Hilbert–Schmidt projection of $\rho$ onto $\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B$. This is the diagonal part in that product grid and is computed by Gaussian-rational linear algebra. There is an explicit palette $F$ of its possible eigenvalues, obtained by the normalized entries of $(1-s)A_G$ with the extra $s/d$ on matched indices only. It splits into matched and unmatched ranges separated by a gap. Require $\prod_{\lambda\in F}(\sigma-\lambda I)=0$. This makes the decoded weights exact: $\sigma$ is Hermitian, so every product-cell diagonal entry lies in $F$. Using the interpolation polynomial which is 1 on the matched palette and 0 on the unmatched palette, require its value on $\sigma$ to be $P_S$.

Here is a polynomial implementation of the decoding step. In each of $\mathcal A,\mathcal B$, produce Hermitian generators by taking self-adjoint parts and multiplying skew-adjoint parts by $i$. Choose a simple-spectrum Hermitian element of the form $\sum_j t^j H_j$ by trying polynomially many successive integers $t$. For any two distinct diagonal characters at least one generator distinguishes them, so their bad parameters are roots of a nonzero polynomial of degree at most the number of generators. Testing a squarefree characteristic polynomial suffices to select $t$. Its simple eigenvalues specify the rank-one projections by $\operatorname{adj}(\lambda I-H)/\chi_H'(\lambda)$. Each diagonal weight is $\operatorname{tr}(\rho P_i\otimes Q_j)$ in these two families of projections. Rational real-root isolation and interval refinement evaluate these expressions to less than a quarter of the minimum distinct palette gap. Their bit costs are polynomial: the characteristic polynomials have polynomial degree and height, their simple roots have separation at least $2^{-\operatorname{poly}}$, the denominators are the products of the corresponding root differences, and the palette itself has polynomial bit height. A weight is then matched to its unique exact palette value, using the previous polynomial identity. [Certified real root refinement](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.1362)

Check that the matched cells define a bijection of the two coordinate sets. Identify their labels by that bijection and decode $K$ from the recovered weights, subtracting $s/d$ on matched cells. Check the marker pattern and the rank-one block rule in Step 1. This amounts to grouping anchor triples, grouping their pair ports, recovering edge bits, and comparing the entire resulting $K$ to the template. The check requires polynomial time. Finally form the Gaussian-rational matrix $R=\rho-\sigma+(s/d)P_S$. Require it to have rank one, be Hermitian with trace $s$, be supported in $S$, and have both partial traces equal to $sI/d$. These tests ensure that $R$ is positive and is a rank-one state of mass $s$ supported on the matched product lines with equal-magnitude amplitudes. Its phases can be removed by independent local diagonal unitaries. In combination with the decoded diagonal part this proves that the original matrix is locally unitarily equivalent to the template. Each test is necessary on the orbit saturation. The procedure recognizes that saturation and gives a polynomial many-one reduction to GI by extracting the graph in each input. If a promise-free language is wanted, a failed recognition test gives a fixed no-instance.

## Scope, sources, and DAG reuse

The closest complexity precedent located is the general reduction to unitary tensor isomorphism by Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang. The construction above concerns actual trace-preserving quantum channels with both spectra fixed, linear invertibility and explicit entanglement-breaking normalization, and describes the exact phase fibers. The diagonal state reduction and standard Choi and separability tools are established. Publication priority for the tightly constrained channel family and raw recognizer remains unestablished. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03135)

The project survey supplied the vectorization and Choi transport interface. The full federated lookup queried 103 registered source catalogs for each of Choi, entanglement breaking, doubly stochastic matrices, quantum channels, permutation matrices, Perron–Frobenius and adjacency, with zero catalog errors. Direct hits included Choi inversion and extensionality, the named entanglement-breaking interfaces, transpose closure of doubly stochastic matrices and permutation-matrix operations. The rank-one directions are recovered using the same paired factor-space principle as the earlier matrix-code work, specialized here to orthogonal endpoint directions and avoiding an arbitrary tensor factorization. The proof is informal. Exact rational arithmetic checked all 75 labeled graphs of orders one through four for the zero row sums, fixed diagonal and off-diagonal entry multisets, and port recovery of every edge bit. In each order the same check verified every Householder move on the marker and its associated single-edge change.
