# Orbit geometry of the final-digit tensor anchor

Let $k$ be a finite field of characteristic $p$ and work geometrically over an algebraic closure $K$. The ring-valued affine tensor space in this note has either of two meanings. In the equal-characteristic case, take a finite local $k$-algebra $R$ with residue $k$, nonzero maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, and $0\neq s\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(\mathfrak m)$. For a test $k$-algebra $D$ use $\mathcal R(D)=R\otimes_k D$. In the unramified mixed-characteristic case use $\mathcal R(D)=W_e(D)$, the length-$e$ Witt vectors, with $e\geq2$. In particular $W_e(\mathbb F_p)=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z$. Entries of tensors and matrices are affine coordinates of these ring functors, so the base field for closures is $K$ in both cases.

For a bimap $t:K^a\times K^b\to K^c$ take the fixed $K$-space $B=U\oplus V\oplus W\oplus P$ of dimensions $a,b,c,d$, with $d\in\{0,1\}$ chosen so that $N=1+\dim B$ is prime to $p$. Denote its padded law on $B$ by $q_t(x,y)=t(x_U,y_V)\in W$. Put this law in the last ideal of $\mathcal R$ and unitize on $\mathcal R^N$, obtaining the multiplication tensor $\mu_t$. In the Witt case the geometric parameter is the last Witt coordinate. The convention using $p^{e-1}$ times lifted coefficients replaces coefficients by their $p^{e-1}$-th powers. This is identical on $\mathbb F_p$. More generally entrywise Frobenius is a homeomorphism on the underlying algebraic closures and commutes with the tensor action after applying it to the basis changes, so the degeneration equivalence is the same in that convention.

**Theorem.** Let $H=\operatorname{GL}_a(K)\times\operatorname{GL}_b(K)\times\operatorname{GL}_c(K)$. For any two tensors of the same format,
$$
 t'\in\overline{H t}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mu_{t'}\in \overline{\operatorname{GL}_N(\mathcal R)^3\cdot\mu_t}.
$$
The right side is the full ambient tensor orbit closure, and the equivalence permits rank drops on all input and output roles. With reduced structures, if $t'$ uses both input support quotients and its output space fully, the orbit-closure germ at $\mu_{t'}$ is a product of the germ of $\overline{Ht}$ at $t'$ and a smooth factor. In the Witt case this statement about the reduced closures coexists with a Frobenius twist in the action on the last digit.

There is a projective incidence map recovering $\overline{\operatorname{GL}(B)q_t}$ from $\overline{Ht}$. Its nonempty scheme fibers are smooth towers of Grassmannians, as described below. For source tensors with full support ranks it is birational and an isomorphism on the full-support part.

## The final ideal and the tensor chart

Write $i:D\to\mathcal R(D)$ for the final additive coordinate, $i(u)=su$ in the equal-characteristic case and $i(u)=V^{e-1}(u)$ in the Witt case. Its image is a square-zero ideal. Let $x_0$ denote the residue of $x\in\mathcal R(D)$. The multiplication rule is
$$
 x\,i(u)=i(x_0^{p^j}u),\qquad
 j=0\text{ in equal characteristic},\quad j=e-1\text{ for Witt vectors}.
$$
In equal characteristic this follows from $s\mathfrak m=0$ and the coefficient field. For Witt vectors it follows by iterating the projection formula for Verschiebung and using that Frobenius on Witt coordinates over a characteristic-$p$ ring raises them to the $p$-th power. The coordinate $V^{e-1}(u)$ has zeros in all earlier positions, so the operation is additive and is defined on nonreduced $D$ as well. The same formula shows that elements with residue zero annihilate the last ideal. [Hazewinkel, Witt vectors, Part 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.3888) · [Bertapelle and González-Avilés, The Greenberg functor revisited](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.0051)

In either case $\mathcal R$ has affine coordinates and an element is invertible exactly when its residue is invertible. In fact the kernel of the residue map is nilpotent, so a lift of the residue inverse can be corrected by a finite geometric sum. For Witt vectors a residue lift is the Teichmüller vector. Thus ring operations, inverses on the unit open, and inverses of matrices via the adjugate are regular in these coordinates.

Let $E=\mathcal R^N$ and fix a basis $e,b_1,\ldots,b_{N-1}$ with $e$ the unit in every $\mu_t$. There is an open chart $\Omega$ in the whole tensor space where both maps $A_T(x)=T(x,e)$ and $B_T(y)=T(e,y)$ are invertible and the first coordinate of $u=T(e,e)$ is a unit. Normalize
$$
 \nu_T(x,y)=T(A_T^{-1}x,B_T^{-1}y).
$$
It has unit $u$, because both $A_T(e)$ and $B_T(e)$ are $u$. Set $\epsilon_T(x)=\operatorname{tr} L^{\nu_T}_x/N$. In this expression the trace is the ordinary matrix trace over $\mathcal R(D)$, and $N$ is a unit. We have $\epsilon_T(u)=1$ as $L^{\nu_T}_u$ is the identity. Define a regular frame $F_T$ by sending $e$ to $u$ and $b_i$ to $b_i-\epsilon_T(b_i)u$. It is invertible since it is the basis $(u,b_i)$ followed by a shear.

In this frame impose the closed condition that the product of pairs from the $b_i$ block belongs to the final ideal times that block. On the chart, this gives exactly the normalized laws formed by unitizing $i(q)$, for $q\in\operatorname{Hom}_K(B\otimes B,B)$ with $\operatorname{tr} q(x,-)=0$ for all $x$. Indeed the normalized trace on every $b_i$ is zero by construction of the frame, which imposes precisely those linear equations on $q$. Conversely those equations ensure the prescribed augmentation agrees with normalized trace. For the latter assertion it suffices to compute the trace for each basis vector, where final coordinates add ordinarily. These laws are associative since every triple product in their augmentation ideal has two last-ideal coefficients and vanishes. In particular the normalized chart condition does not need extra associativity equations.

This closed subvariety of $\Omega$ is a product $S\times\mathcal L_0$, where $\mathcal L_0$ is the trace-zero space of residue laws and $S$ is smooth. Its coordinates are $u$ with first coordinate a unit, the arbitrary values $\epsilon_T(b_i)$, and the two invertible matrices $F_T^{-1}A_T,F_T^{-1}B_T$ fixing $e$. These latter matrices are block matrices $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&r\\0&D_0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $D_0$ invertible. Both the construction and its inverse are regular on the indicated opens. For the inverse, compute $F$ from the specified $u$ and trace values, and let $J_1,J_2$ be the prescribed matrices fixing $e$. Set $T(x,y)=F\mu_q(J_1x,J_2y)$, where $\mu_q$ is the marked unitization. The recovered input contractions are $FJ_1,FJ_2$, and normalization recovers the law transported by $F$. These formulas hold on arbitrary test rings. If $\ell$ is the number of affine ring coordinates, $S$ has dimension $\ell(2N^2-1)$.

## Recovering the full ambient orbit closure

A unital isotope of an associative unital algebra is isomorphic to the original algebra, over any commutative base ring. Here a direct proof also allows the target to be initially just a unital bilinear law. For a transporter $h(xy)=f(x)g(y)$, let $a=f^{-1}(1_{\rm target})$ and $b=g^{-1}(1_{\rm target})$. Then $h(ay)=g(y)$ and $h(xb)=f(x)$, so left multiplication by $a$ and right multiplication by $b$ are bijective in the associative source. Each gives a one-sided inverse, and injectivity makes it two-sided. Then pulling the target multiplication back through $h$ gives $x\star y=x b^{-1}a^{-1}y$, which is isomorphic to the original algebra by multiplication by the intervening unit.

Apply this to the normalized law of each point in the tensor orbit inside $\Omega$. Its trace augmentation and last-ideal product property are transported from $\mu_t$, so it lies in the closed chart subvariety. Between two marked last-digit laws any algebra isomorphism preserves the augmentation by trace, fixes the unit, and acts by some invertible matrix $M$ on the $b_i$-space. The residue-law relation is exactly change of basis by the matrix $M_0^{[p^j]}$, with the entrywise indicated power. Conversely every such $M$ inducing a residue-law transporter lifts to the algebra isomorphism, by the last-ideal multiplication rule. At geometric points $M_0$ can be any invertible matrix, and the entrywise Frobenius has surjective image over $K$. Hence the tensor orbit in the chart is precisely $S\times\operatorname{GL}(B)q_t$. The free $S$-coordinates are all realized by the explicit frame and the two separate input maps.

Taking the reduced closure in the chart gives
$$
 \Omega\cap\overline{\operatorname{GL}_N(\mathcal R)^3\mu_t}
    \ \cong\ S\times \overline{\operatorname{GL}(B)q_t}.
$$
Here the chart subvariety is closed in $\Omega$, and the intersection of an orbit closure with an open chart is the closure of the orbit in that chart. Thus the equality is for full ambient degenerations, including paths that do not have fixed lower coordinates. In mixed characteristic taking reduced closures matters: Frobenius is surjective on geometric points but is not a separable action map.

## Proper incidence for the residue law

Set $n=\dim B$ and $C=\overline{Ht}$ in the original tensor space. Let $\mathcal F$ parametrize triples $(L,R,I)$ with $\operatorname{codim} L=a$, $\operatorname{codim}R=b$, $\dim I=c$, and $I\subseteq L\cap R$. It is smooth, irreducible and projective: choose $I$ in a Grassmannian, then choose $L/I,R/I$ independently in Grassmannian bundles over it. The law bundle over $\mathcal F$ has fiber $\operatorname{Hom}((B/L)\otimes(B/R),I)$. It is a closed vector subbundle of the trivial law bundle, consisting of laws factoring through the indicated subspaces and quotients. Replace the fiber by $C$ after choosing local quotient and image frames. The replacements glue because $C$ is $H$-invariant. The resulting variety $Y$ is closed in $\mathcal F\times\operatorname{Hom}(B\otimes B,B)$, and projection is projective.

The flags with $L+R=B$ form a dense open set, since $n\geq a+b+c$. On it the group $\operatorname{GL}(B)$ is transitive on the flags and the flag stabilizer induces all changes of basis on $B/L,B/R,I$. In fact the decomposition $B/L\oplus B/R$ identifies with $B/(L\cap R)$, and independent maps on the two quotients and on $I$ extend using complementary subspaces. Thus the dense subset of $Y$ formed by transverse flags and points of $Ht$ in the fibers projects exactly to $\operatorname{GL}(B)q_t$. The proper image of $Y$ is exactly that orbit closure.

For a law $q$ in the image, let $L_q,R_q$ be its two annihilators and $W_q$ its image span. The underlying flag equations of its fiber are
$$
 L\subseteq L_q,\qquad R\subseteq R_q,\qquad W_q\subseteq I\subseteq L\cap R .
$$
These give the full scheme fiber. To prove this, fix the law $q$ over $K$, so its annihilators and image commute with arbitrary extension of scalars. On the flag equations over a test ring, $B/L$ surjects onto $B/L_q$, $B/R$ surjects onto $B/R_q$, and $I$ contains $W_q$ as a subbundle. These exact sequences split locally since their terms are locally free. In bases using these splittings the induced bimap is the fixed supported bimap of $q$ with unused rows, columns and output coordinates added. Its equivalence class over $K$ is therefore independent of the chosen valid flag. At least one such flag represents a point of $Y$, so the padded bimap lies in $C$. The local frames then show that all the flag equations suffice for membership in $Y$ over the test ring, since they make that bimap isomorphic to this constant point of the stable closed subscheme $C$. This proves the scheme claim.

Explicitly the nonempty fiber first chooses $I/W_q$ in the Grassmannian of $(c-\dim W_q)$-planes in $(L_q\cap R_q)/W_q$. It then chooses $L/I,R/I$ independently in the appropriate Grassmannians in $L_q/I,R_q/I$. It is smooth, projective and geometrically irreducible. The formulas use the given fixed ranks for $L,R,I$, even at rank drops of $q$.

Taking $q=q_{t'}$ proves that membership in the image is equivalent to $t'\in C$. In one direction use its original block flag when $t'\in C$. In the other direction any valid flag gives the same supported bimap as $t'$, with precisely the original format once complements are added, so membership in $C$ is unchanged. If all three support ranks of $q$ are $a,b,c$, its flag is necessarily $(L_q,R_q,W_q)$. It is recovered regularly by constant-rank kernels and image. The incidence map is therefore an isomorphism on this open part, and is birational for a source of full support. The local product of the incidence bundle with $C$, together with the tensor chart, proves the assertion about singularities at concise targets. The additional smooth factor from $\mathcal F$ has dimension $c(n-c)+a(n-a-c)+b(n-b-c)$.

## Examples and comparison

For $a=b=2,\ c=1,\ d=0$ and $p=5$, a rank-two bilinear form has closure all $2$-by-$2$ matrices. At a rank-one target the image already fixes $I$. Each of $L/I$ and $R/I$ is then a two-plane in a three-space, so the full incidence fiber is $\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^2$. The incidence variety in this case is a smooth vector bundle over $\mathcal F$. The allowance for nontransverse flags matters even with no rank drop. If the two nominal inputs have dimension one, deform $q=\ell(x)\tilde\ell(y)w$ by replacing $\tilde\ell$ with $\ell+\tau\tilde\ell$, where both covectors vanish on $w$ and are independent. At $\tau=0$ the two annihilators coincide although the separate support ranks stay one.

Bläser and Lysikov prove over fields that tensor degeneration and algebra degeneration agree at unital specializations of associative algebras. Their binding-tensor normalization is the precedent for the slice at $e$. The additional results here are the trace-and-last-ideal product chart over ring schemes, the all-rank reflection to the original separate-mode tensor orbit, and the exact incidence fibers. The projective incidence uses the bundle-collapsing method, allowing a nontransverse flag boundary and an orbit closure as fiber. [Bläser and Lysikov, On Degeneration of Tensors and Algebras, Theorem 3.2 and Lemma 3.5](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.19) · [Lőrincz, On the collapsing of homogeneous bundles in arbitrary characteristic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08270)

The final-digit embedding originates in the socle-and-trace congruence construction for exact finite-ring tensor isomorphism. Its geometric form here concerns affine ring-scheme models in equal characteristic and unramified Witt characteristic. Grochow, Qiao, Stange and Sun develop the broader isomorphism complexity theory over commutative rings. Geometry.py verifies 24 normalized isotopies each over $\mathbb F_3[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ and $\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z$, checks 24 last-coordinate products where Witt squaring differs from the untwisted product, and enumerates the 961 rational flags in the rank-one example over $\mathbb F_5$. The proof is in prose, with arbitrary ramified ring-scheme models, publication priority and Lean verification outside its scope. [Grochow, Qiao, Stange and Sun, On the Complexity of Isomorphism Problems for Tensors, Groups, and Polynomials V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00735)
