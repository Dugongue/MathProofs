# A trichotomy for arithmetic constraints on quadratic Postnikov lifts

A fixed family of two-stage Postnikov spaces and a fixed multisorted arithmetic language together determine a decision problem. The input specifies partial maps, cycles, and arithmetic constraints on their periods. The complexity is governed by the integral and rational polar maps of the Postnikov classes and by the finite modular pp images of the arithmetic language. The cup products may link coordinates of distinct arithmetic sorts.

## The problem

Fix a finite relational signature and a many-sorted structure $M$ with scalar integer sorts and nonempty finite sorts. Each integer sort has its own addition graph and pin for $1$. In each basic relation, after fixing the finite arguments, the integer fibre is given by a Boolean combination of affine integer equations and congruences in the externally written coordinates. This includes relations mixing different integer sorts. An instance uses existential conjunctions of the basic atoms and sorted equalities. Explicit input integers and coefficients are written in binary.

The arithmetic core $M_0$ is a retract whose finite sorts have smallest total cardinality among images of endomorphisms of $M$. All endomorphisms fix the integer sorts, since they preserve addition and $1$. Taking a power of a finite-sort map attaining the minimum gives an idempotent with the same image. Any endomorphism of the retract that is noninjective on a finite sort would give a smaller image of $M$. For an arithmetic core, every map on finite sorts is therefore bijective if it comes from an endomorphism. Retraction preserves satisfiability of arithmetic atoms, even when integer variables are shared with a topological witness. This core is effective. There are finitely many candidate tuples of functions on the finite sorts, and preservation is a finite list of validity questions about the equality–congruence formulas over the integers.

Use the condition $Q(M_0)$ from the equality–congruence CSP classification. For each positive integer $N$, reduce the numerical arguments of every pp-definable relation modulo $N$ and retain the exact image, leaving finite arguments alone. Let $D_N^{\rm pp}(M_0)$ be the structure on these finite carriers with those images as relations. Condition $Q(M_0)$ says that this structure has an idempotent Taylor polymorphism for every $N$. We use the earlier theorem that $Q$ is decidable for these fixed signatures, that $Q$ gives a polynomial-time constructive CSP algorithm, and that failure of $Q$ gives NP-hardness for a core. When applying the constructive theorem, adjoin all finite singleton pins if needed. This preserves $Q$: in a pp formula replace each pinned constant by an extra free variable, take the exact modular image with these extra variables present, then slice the finite variables to their pinned values and project. Each idempotent Taylor family preserves that slice. The reverse implication follows by taking the reduct. [Equality–congruence CSP classification and hardness proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/EqualityCongruenceCSP) · [Barto–Kozik cyclic-term theorem](https://lmcs.episciences.org/673)

In addition to $M$, fix a finite family of the following topological targets, indexed by $j$. Fix an even degree $d_j\ge 2$, a free abelian group $L_j=\mathbb Z^{r_j}$, a finitely generated abelian group $G_j$, and a coefficient $c_{ik}\in G_j$ for each $1\le i\le k\le r_j$. Each coordinate of $L_j$ is assigned an integer sort of $M$, with repetition allowed. Within an individual target write $d,L,G$ for its data. Let $u_i\in H^d(K(L,d);\mathbb Z)$ be the universal coordinate class. Put

$$
\kappa=\sum_{i\le k} c_{ik}\,(u_i\smile u_k)\in H^{2d}(K(L,d);G),
\qquad
W=\operatorname{hofib}\bigl(\kappa:K(L,d)\longrightarrow K(G,2d)\bigr).
$$

The coefficient notation takes the image under the homomorphism $\mathbb Z\to G$ sending $1$ to the indicated coefficient. Define the symmetric bilinear map $B:L\times L\to G$ by

$$
B(v,w)=\sum_i 2c_{ii}v_iw_i+\sum_{i<k}c_{ik}(v_iw_k+v_kw_i).
$$

The image subgroup of $B$ means the subgroup generated by all its values.

An input contains finitely many ordered simplicial pairs $(X,A)$, each assigned one of the fixed targets and satisfying $\dim X\le 2d$ for that target. It specifies a boundary map into $W$ in the cochain encoding below. For each coordinate $i$ the input may supply explicit integral $d$-cycles in $X$ and a list of affine equalities linking evaluations on those cycles to integer variables of its assigned sort. Every equality uses evaluations and integer variables of one sort only. The same variable can be shared between observations on different complexes and between the topological and arithmetic parts. Finally, the input contains an arithmetic instance over $M$ on these variables, with auxiliary numerical and finite variables allowed. The question is whether the boundary maps extend with all the prescribed observations and arithmetic atoms satisfied.

The cochain encoding uses normalized integral simplicial cochains. A boundary map is a pair $(a_A,h_A)$ with $a_A\in Z^d(A;L)$ and $h_A\in C^{2d-1}(A;G)$, satisfying $\delta h_A=\kappa(a_A)$. On cochains $\kappa(a)=\sum_{i\le k}c_{ik}(a_i\smile a_k)$ uses Alexander–Whitney cup products. A witness is a tuple of arithmetic values and, on each pair, $(a,h)$ with

$$
\delta a=0,\qquad a|_A=a_A,\qquad h|_A=h_A,\qquad \delta h=\kappa(a).
$$

The evaluation of a coordinate $a_i$ on a $d$-cycle is an integer, independent of changing $a_i$ by a coboundary. Invalid encodings may be rejected in polynomial time.

This is a map-extension problem in a fixed simplicial model. The standard cocycle model has $K(H,n)_q=Z^n(\Delta^q;H)$. The simplicial abelian group $C^{n-1}(\Delta^\bullet;H)$ maps to it by the coboundary, surjectively in each degree since simplices have zero cohomology in positive degrees. This is a Kan fibration. Its source is contractible: maps to it are cochains, and every cochain on a simplicial subset extends as a cochain to the whole set. The pullback along the natural cup-class map is a Kan model for the homotopy fibre. Maps to this model are exactly the displayed cochain witnesses. The geometric and simplicial existence problems agree for this boundary encoding. The weak equivalence from the Kan target to the singular complex of its realization induces weak equivalences on mapping spaces from $X$ and $A$. Restriction is a Kan fibration, so it gives the same existence of points in the fibre over the specified boundary map. The comparison preserves cohomology evaluations on the specified cycles. [Hatcher, §§3.2 and 4.3](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf)

## Classification

**Theorem.** For every fixed choice of the arithmetic language and the finite family of cup classes, the following hold.

| Condition | Decision complexity |
| --- | --- |
| At least one polar image subgroup is infinite | $\Sigma^0_1$-complete under computable many-one reductions |
| All polar images are finite and some polar map is nonzero | NP-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions |
| All polar maps vanish and $Q(M_0)$ fails | NP-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions |
| All polar maps vanish and $Q(M_0)$ holds | Deterministic polynomial time, with polynomial-bit constructive witnesses |

The case is decidable from the stated finite descriptions, using the arithmetic classification to test $Q(M_0)$. The input dimensions and target and language parameters are fixed for the complexity assertions. The structural cases are disjoint. The polynomial-time and NP-complete complexity classes would overlap if P equalled NP.

### Finite congruence couplings preserve the arithmetic criterion

Let $M'$ extend $M_0$ by finitely many relations among numerical sorts, each of the form $\sum_i \ell_i x_i=0$ in a fixed finite abelian group, where the $\ell_i$ are fixed group elements. Such a relation can be expressed by a finite conjunction of linear congruences. Then $Q(M')$ holds if and only if $Q(M_0)$ holds.

For the nontrivial direction, choose a common multiple $m$ of all the relevant exponents. Fix $N$ and first work modulo $T=\operatorname{lcm}(N,m)$. The finite exact-image structure for $M_0$ has a Taylor polymorphism. The cyclic-term theorem gives a cyclic polymorphism of prime arity $p$ larger than $T$ and the product of the finite carrier sizes. The theorem applies to the finite algebra generated by a Taylor operation on the product of the sorted carriers. Coordinate equality makes the resulting term sorted, and even if the relation signature of the exact-image structure is infinite, term operations preserve all its relations. On a numerical carrier the operation is an additive homomorphism from $p$ copies of $\mathbb Z/T$, by preservation of addition. Cyclicity makes its coordinate coefficients equal. Idempotence makes the common coefficient $p^{-1}$ modulo $T$. Thus its operation on every numerical carrier is the same modular average, preserving the added relations on residues.

To check preservation of an exact image of a pp relation over $M'$, keep the existential variables temporarily as additional free variables and remove all added atoms. The full-variable $M_0$ relation then has an exact image modulo $T$. Reinstating the extra atoms intersects this image with conditions on residues, since $m\mid T$. The modular average preserves this intersection. Projecting the auxiliary variables now gives the desired preservation. The operations descend to modulus $N$ because the numerical average commutes with the divisor quotient and the finite-sort operations stay the same. Every exact image modulo $N$ is the image of its corresponding exact image modulo $T$. The reverse implication follows by taking the reduct.

### Polynomial-time branch

Suppose all $B$ vanish. Then $c_{ik}=0$ for $i<k$ and $2c_{ii}=0$. Fix a pair and write $c_i=c_{ii}$. Integer normal forms find an integral particular cocycle $a^0$ extending $a_A$, or decide that none exists. Choose an arbitrary cochain extension $h^0$ of $h_A$. Write every candidate $a_i=a_i^0+\eta_i$, with $\eta_i\in Z^d(X,A;\mathbb Z)$ given by integer coefficients in a computed lattice basis. Cochain and probe constraints are polynomial-size integer linear equations within their assigned sorts. [Kannan–Bachem integer normal forms](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

In relative cohomology we have

$$
[\kappa(a)-\delta h^0]=[\kappa(a^0)-\delta h^0]+\sum_i c_i[\eta_i\smile\eta_i].
$$

The two cross terms between $a_i^0$ and $\eta_i$ agree in $H^{2d}(X,A;\mathbb Z)$ by graded commutativity for the product of absolute and relative classes. The cup commutativity homotopy is relative here, since one argument vanishes on $A$ and its restriction to every simplex of $A$ is zero. Their sum is killed by $c_i$. The same calculation on two relative classes makes each coefficient-weighted square additive. In particular, if $\eta_i=\sum_b z_{ib}e_b$ in the integral cocycle basis, the term equals $\sum_b z_{ib}c_i[e_b^2]$. Each square can be evaluated on explicit cochains in polynomial time, including when the basis entries have polynomial bit length rather than constant magnitude. The equation is in cohomology with coefficients in $G$, where coboundaries are taken with those same coefficients.

Since there are no simplices of degree greater than $2d$, take Smith normal form of the integer matrix of $\delta:C^{2d-1}(X,A;\mathbb Z)\to C^{2d}(X,A;\mathbb Z)$. Its unimodular row change also acts on $G$-valued cochains. In each resulting row, the desired congruence has the form $\sum_i c_i x_i-w\in tG$, where $t\ge0$ is a diagonal entry (including zero rows), $w$ is computed from the fixed boundary, and each $x_i$ is a linear combination of the integer coordinates $z_{ib}$ in its own sort. Enumerate the $2^r$ assignments in $\{0,1\}^r$ to find one pattern $\epsilon$ satisfying this congruence. If there is none, reject. Otherwise impose $\sum_i c_i(x_i-\epsilon_i)\in tG$. If $E$ is the exponent of the torsion subgroup $T_G$ of $G$ (with $E=1$ for the zero subgroup), this last condition on a torsion-valued sum is equivalent to membership in $\gcd(t,E)G$, with $\gcd(0,E)=E$. Indeed $tG\cap T_G=tT_G$, and multiplication by $t$ has the same image on $T_G$ as multiplication by $\gcd(t,E)$.

There are only finitely many possible extra residue relations for the fixed target family. For every divisor $v$ of $E$, the homogeneous condition $\sum_i c_i y_i\in vG$ is a finite-group linear equation, using the image of $T_G$ in $G/vG$. Thus the reduction uses only an expansion $M'$ of the preceding kind. The linear equations from probes, the lattice coefficients, and the shifts by $\epsilon_i$ use addition and the pin $1$ in their own sorts. Arbitrary binary coefficients and constants are expressed by repeated doubling and addition with polynomial overhead. After copying all original arithmetic constraints, this is a polynomial-size reduction to CSP over $M'$. When $Q(M_0)$ holds, the arithmetic theorem and congruence invariance give a polynomial-time algorithm. Its polynomial-bit witnesses give the $a_i$. Recover $h$ by solving $\delta(h-h^0)=\kappa(a)-\delta h^0$ in the specified relative cochain group, using integer normal forms on the fixed free and torsion coordinates of $G$. This also has a polynomial-bit solution.

If $Q(M_0)$ fails, its arithmetic CSP reduces to the combined problem with contractible carriers and no observations. The retraction shows the same hardness for $M$. The next upper bound covers this case.

### Upper bounds beyond the polynomial-time branch

Suppose all $B$ have finite image. Every off-diagonal coefficient is a value of $B$, and $2c_{ii}=B(e_i,e_i)$, where $e_i$ denotes a coordinate basis vector. Hence every $c_{ik}$ is torsion. Fix a common exponent $m$ for all the coefficients. Guess the residues modulo $m$ of the entries of each $a$, and the values of the finite variables in $M_0$. The cup obstruction now has a determined cochain value, computable from these residues. Having fixed $h^0$ extending the boundary cochain, check whether $\kappa(a)-\delta h^0$ is a relative coboundary over $G$, by integer normal forms. Reject any residue assignment that disagrees with the prescribed boundary residues.

The remaining system has integer linear equations and congruences for cocycles, prescribed boundary values, observations and residues. Each fixed arithmetic atom, with finite values chosen, is a Boolean combination of affine equations and congruences. Guess Boolean outcomes consistent with each formula. For a failed congruence choose its actual nonzero residue. The remaining conditions are linear equations, congruences and disequalities. Normal forms reduce the equalities and congruences either to inconsistency or to a nonempty affine integer lattice, given as $z_0+Vt$ with integer columns of full rank. A disequality violated identically on the lattice makes this branch impossible. Otherwise each excluded nontrivial equality cuts a proper affine hyperplane in the free parameters. Finitely many such hyperplanes cannot cover the lattice: if the dimension is $r>0$, restriction to $(T,T^2,\ldots,T^r)$ gives nonzero polynomials with at most $r$ roots each, so testing $0\le T\le r$ times the number of hyperplanes finds an admissible point. Dimension zero is checked directly. These calculations use polynomial bit size. This is an NP algorithm and also proves existence of polynomial-bit witnesses, including the relative cochains $h$ recovered by normal forms.

For unrestricted $B$, enumerate integer cochains and arithmetic values, including the free coordinates of the $h$, and all possible finite labels and torsion coordinates. All constraints are effectively checkable. This proves computable enumerability.

### Multiplication on a fixed carrier

Suppose a target has a nonzero polar value. Pick indices $i,k$ with $g=B(e_i,e_k)\ne0$, where $i=k$ is allowed. Each occurrence of the gadget uses the fixed connected sum

$$
C=(S^d\times S^d)\mathbin{\#}(S^d\times S^d).
$$

There are four integral $d$-cycles, the two product axes in each summand, with a dual cohomology basis. Their pairwise cup products are zero except for the two ordered axis pairings within a summand and their reverses, each giving the positive top generator. This follows using the pinch map to the wedge of the two oriented products. The pinch map gives an isomorphism on middle cohomology and takes the fundamental class to the two oriented fundamental classes. The sphere-product cup formula follows from the external product, with positive reversal sign since $d$ is even. These spaces and cycles can be fixed as finite triangulated data. The top cohomology $H^{2d}(C;G)$ identifies with $G$ by evaluating on the fundamental class, since $H_{2d-1}(C;\mathbb Z)=0$ and $H_{2d}(C;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$.

Let $u$ be an integer multiple of $e_i$ and $v=e_k$, and put $b=B(u,v)$. Prescribe the four period vectors of the variable cocycle $a$ to be $u x$, $v y$, $-u z$, $v$. Impose these as linear observations, coordinate by coordinate. The variables $x,z$ have the sort of $i$, and $y$ has the sort of $k$. If some coordinates of $u$ and $v$ are zero, simply pin the corresponding periods to zero. Such pins are available even if $i=k$, since the four cycles are distinct. Any four period vectors are realized by a degree-$d$ class. The cup calculation gives

$$
\langle[\kappa(a)],[C]\rangle=b(xy-z).
$$

Thus these observations are realizable by a lift exactly when $b(xy-z)=0$. If the sorts differ, the same carrier with periods $u x,v,-u,v y$ gives $b(x-y)=0$ and acts as an exact or modular copy between those sorts. This is a topological constraint, expressed solely by same-sort observations. No cross-sort equality predicate is required.

Take $A$ empty or a single basepoint with its zero boundary map. Any number of gadget copies can be used independently, sharing the indicated arithmetic variables. If an input with just one carrier is desired, wedge the copies at their basepoints. The middle and top reduced cohomology are direct sums and cup products between different summands vanish. Each occurrence adds only a fixed number of simplices and observations. All hardness inputs can use simply connected carriers.

### Hardness from the polar image

If some image is infinite, a basis value $B(e_i,e_k)$ has infinite order because $G$ is finitely generated and there are finitely many matrix values. Use $u=e_i,v=e_k$. The multiplication and copy gadgets impose exact integer multiplication and equality. Use source circuit variables in the sort of $i$ and copy right inputs into the sort of $k$ when needed. Addition, pinning $1$, and the multiplication gadgets realize every finite system of integer arithmetic equations, by introducing a variable per arithmetic gate. The Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich theorem gives computably enumerable hardness for Diophantine equations over the integers. If using its formulation over the natural numbers, replace each nonnegative variable by a sum of four integer squares. Membership was established above. [Matiyasevich, enumerable sets are Diophantine](https://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/~yumat/papers/21_paper/21_eng.pdf)

If a polar image is nonzero and finite, choose a basis value $g$ of order $n>1$, a prime $p\mid n$, and $u=(n/p)e_i,v=e_k$. Then $b$ has order $p$, and the same gadgets impose arithmetic modulo $p$. Encode Boolean inputs by $x^2=x\pmod p$. Use $xy$ for AND and $1-x$ for NOT, with output pinned to $1$. For addition and constants choose exact integer gates. Any satisfying lift projects to a Boolean satisfying assignment in $\mathbb F_p$. Conversely use integer Boolean values for inputs, evaluate the circuit with exact integer operations, and choose identical integer representatives for copy constraints. The witnesses then satisfy every modular multiplication constraint. This is a polynomial-time reduction from Boolean circuit satisfiability. Together with the upper bound it proves NP-completeness.

## Relation to the structural criterion

For one fixed target, $W$ is an $H$-space exactly when $B=0$. For sufficiency, let $+$ be the abelian multiplication on the cocycle models of the Eilenberg–MacLane spaces. On the product of two copies of $K(L,d)$, the class of $\kappa(x+y)-\kappa(x)-\kappa(y)$ is the cross term $\sum_{i,k}B(e_i,e_k)u_i\otimes u_k$. The difference map is zero on the wedge of the factors. Its absolute class vanishes if $B=0$, and then its relative class vanishes too, because restriction to the wedge in degree $2d-1$ is surjective, by the two projections. A relative nullhomotopy of the difference gives the correction to addition of the two lifts to the homotopy fibre and is zero on the axes. The resulting product has a homotopy unit. For necessity, use any multiplication on $W$ with homotopy unit to combine two sphere maps with arbitrary $\pi_d$ values $v,w$ into a map $S^d\times S^d\to W$. These values are available because $W\to K(L,d)$ induces an isomorphism on $\pi_d$. The obstruction of its projection evaluates to $B(v,w)$, which must vanish.

The analogous criterion rationally is $B\otimes\mathbb Q=0$. The fibre of the rational cup map gives the rationalization of $W$ by the induced maps on its two homotopy groups. Thus the topological conditions in the theorem can also be stated as the existence of integral and rational $H$-space structures, with the additional arithmetic Taylor test in the case where the integral structures exist.

For an example with genuine interaction in the polynomial-time branch, use $d=2$, two scalar integer sorts with addition and $1$, $G=\mathbb Z/2$, and $\kappa=(u_1^2+u_2^2)\bmod 2$. Add a finite bit sort with both singleton pins and the graph assigning the parity of the second numerical sort to this bit. The arithmetic core satisfies $Q$ by the induced Mal'cev operations on every exact modular image. On $\mathbb{CP}^2$, observing both coordinates on a projective line imposes equality of their parities. Pinning the first period to $1$ and the bit to $0$ makes the mixed instance infeasible. The arithmetic data alone are feasible, and the unobserved topological lifting problem has a constant solution. Changing the bit to $1$ gives a lift. Keeping the original arithmetic data and replacing the carrier by $S^2\vee S^4$ gives a lift as well. The two carriers have the same integral cohomology groups, and their cup products determine the different answers.

The earlier undecidability constructions for continuous extension and Manin's connection to bilinear arithmetic provide the topological context. The new theorem concerns a fixed-template problem with arithmetic and finite-label constraints linked to the periods of maps, including cross-sorted cup classes, and gives the upper bounds and hardness reductions for that same problem.
