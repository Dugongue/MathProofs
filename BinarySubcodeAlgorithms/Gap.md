# Subspace enumeration and repeated columns in binary subcode containment

Let $C,D$ be binary linear codes given by generators at a common length $r$. We seek the coordinate permutations $\pi$ such that $\pi C\subseteq D$. Write $k=\dim C$, $l=\dim D$, $u=r-l$, and $g=l-k$. If $k>l$ the answer is zero. Otherwise the algorithms below decide existence, recover a permutation, and compute the exact number of such permutations. A polynomial factor in $r$ is suppressed in the bounds. Finite-field operations here have constant bit cost, and the count has $O(r\log(r+1))$ bits.

## 1. The dimension gap

The fixed-gap conclusion is a standard consequence of published algorithms. Santini, Baldi and Chiaraluce search over subcodes and test permutation equivalence in §4.2, Algorithm 1. Proposition 3 counts trials by a Gaussian coefficient, and Remark 2 gives the exponent $kg$ in the present notation. Exhaustive echelon enumeration with the deterministic equivalence algorithm of Babai, Codenotti, Grochow and Qiao yields the worst-case bound below, including negative instances. Sections 2 and 3 instead exploit short dependencies and repeated column types, respectively. [Santini, Baldi and Chiaraluce, §4.2](https://iris.univpm.it/bitstream/11566/325875/5/2022-1749.pdf) · [Babai, Codenotti, Grochow and Qiao](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611973082.107)

**Theorem.** The running time is at most
$$
2^{r+o(r)+g\min(k,u)}.
$$
In particular it is singly exponential in the length for every bounded gap, as well as for variable gaps satisfying $g\min(k,u)=O(r)$.

Enumerate the $k$-subspaces $E$ of $D$, by their unique reduced echelon bases in coordinates on $D$. For each $E$, compute the set of coordinate equivalences $C\to E$. Every permutation carrying $C$ into $D$ occurs in exactly one such set, since its image is uniquely determined. One nonempty set supplies a transporter. A nonempty set is a coset of the permutation automorphism group of $C$, hence its cardinality follows from a stabilizer chain. The deterministic algorithm of Babai, Codenotti, Grochow and Qiao computes the equivalences of two generator-matrix codes in time $2^{r+o(r)}$. [Corollary 7.1 of Code Equivalence and Group Isomorphism](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/papers/soda11.pdf)

The Gaussian coefficient satisfies
$$
{k+g\brack k}_2=2^{kg}\prod_{i=1}^k\frac{1-2^{-(g+i)}}{1-2^{-i}}
\le \frac{2^{kg}}{\prod_{i=1}^\infty(1-2^{-i})}.
$$
The infinite product is positive. Each pivot pattern gives a family of echelon matrices by freely filling its permitted entries. This enumerates with polynomial overhead per output, including the empty basis. Alternatively $\pi C\subseteq D$ is equivalent to $\pi^{-1}D^\perp\subseteq C^\perp$. The smaller dimension in this dual search is $u$ and the gap is still $g$. Invert each dual witness when returning to the original orientation. Choosing the cheaper search proves the bound.

## 2. Short relations can determine a projection kernel

Let $A$ and $B$ be full-row-rank generator matrices for $C$ and $D$ with columns $a_i$ and $b_j$, respectively. Fix an integer $1\le w\le r$. Write $L_w$ for the span of the words of $C^\perp$ of weight at most $w$, and put
$$
h=\dim C^\perp-\dim L_w,\qquad
T=\left\{\sum_{j\in S} b_j : 1\le |S|\le w\right\}\setminus\{0\},
\qquad t=\min(g,h),\quad e=g-t .
$$
These data are computable by enumerating subsets of size at most $w$ and by elimination. This initial work takes at most $2^r\operatorname{poly}(r)$ bit operations.

**Theorem.** In addition to the initial enumeration, time
$$
2^{r+o(r)+kt}\binom{|T|}{e}
$$
suffices. If $|T|<e$ one can reject immediately. The dual instance gives a second bound by the same construction.

If $\pi C\subseteq D$, there is a surjective linear map $F$ on column coordinates such that $Fb_{\pi(i)}=a_i$ for every $i$. This follows by writing a basis of $\pi C$ as combinations of rows of $B$. Put $K=\ker F$, of dimension $g$. The column-sum map $x\mapsto\sum_j x_jb_j$ sends $\pi(C^\perp)$ onto $K$. Indeed $F$ applied to that sum is zero for every permuted dependency, and every vector in $K$ is a column sum since $B$ has rank $l$. Every preimage of a kernel vector is then a permuted dependency. The image of $\pi L_w$ has codimension at most $h$ in $K$, and it is spanned by elements of $T$ lying in $K$. Thus $K$ contains an independent $e$-subset of $T$.

For each such subset with span $W$, enumerate $g$-subspaces $K\supseteq W$ by echelon bases of $(g-e)$-subspaces in $\mathbb F_2^l/W$. The number of these extensions is at most a constant times $2^{kt}$ by the Gaussian bound in §1. A basis of $K^\perp$ supplies a $k\times l$ matrix $M_K$ with kernel $K$. Compare the code generated by $M_KB$ with $C$ by ordinary code equivalence. The map $v\mapsto vB$ is injective on row vectors, so distinct kernels specify distinct image subcodes. To count without storing or recounting kernels, retain a candidate only when its enumerated subset is the lexicographically first independent $e$-subset of $T\cap K$ in a fixed order. It is obtained greedily by rank tests. The cost of scanning $T$ per candidate is at most $2^r\operatorname{poly}(r)$, absorbed by the displayed bound. Summing the disjoint coset sizes gives the exact count.

For fixed $w$, $|T|=O(r^w)$. If the dual of $C$ is generated by words of weight at most $w$ and $g=O(r/\log(r+1))$, the algorithm is singly exponential, even when both $k$ and $u$ grow linearly. The elementary dimension-gap bound by itself permits only bounded $g$ in that range of dimensions.

## 3. A subset algorithm for a small number of relations among column types

Let $a_1,\ldots,a_s$ now denote the **distinct nonzero** columns of $A$. Let $m_i$ denote their multiplicities and $m_0$ the number of zero columns. Put $d=s-k$. These numbers depend only on the code, since a row-basis change preserves equality of columns. There is an exact deterministic algorithm in time
$$
2^{r+du}\operatorname{poly}(r).
$$
It also applies with source and target replaced by $D^\perp$ and $C^\perp$, respectively. In particular any fixed $d$ allows arbitrary dimension gap.

Take a parity-check matrix $H$ of $D$ with $u$ independent rows and columns $p_j$. Under a permutation, let $S_i$ be the set of target positions receiving source columns of type $a_i$, and let $y_i=\sum_{j\in S_i}p_j$. Orthogonality to the rows of $H$ is exactly
$$
\sum_{i=1}^s a_i y_i^{\mathsf T}=0 .
$$
The $k\times s$ matrix of distinct columns has rank $k$. For each of the $u$ syndrome bits, the vector of its values on all $s$ types lies in a fixed kernel of dimension $d$. Enumerate the resulting $2^{du}$ syndrome lists by a basis of this kernel. Each actual permutation induces a unique list.

For a fixed list define $F_i(X)$ to count the $m_i$-subsets of $X$ with syndrome $y_i$. Give index zero the analogous count without a syndrome requirement. First tabulate each subset's syndrome and cardinality. Then tabulate each $F_i$ at every $X$ by the subset zeta transform of its indicator function. This costs $O(r2^r)$ additions per type. Inclusion–exclusion counts the set assignments by
$$
\sum_{X\subseteq[r]}(-1)^{r-|X|}\prod_{i=0}^s F_i(X).
$$
Indeed, summing over supersets of the union cancels each tuple of subsets that misses any target position. A tuple covering all $r$ positions is disjoint since the prescribed cardinalities sum to $r$. Multiply by $m_0!\prod_i m_i!$ to assign the individually labeled source positions within each type. The intermediate products are bounded by $r^r$ when $r\ge1$, using $\binom{r}{m_i}\le r^{m_i}$, so the bit overhead is polynomial. To recover a witness, fix each target position's source type in turn, retaining the first choice for which the count remains positive. With types fixed on some positions, restrict each type's indicator to subsets containing its forced positions and avoiding those of other types. The same inclusion–exclusion evaluates the restricted count. Within a type, any bijection gives the required permutation.

One can sometimes save $u$ bits in the exponent. Form a matrix $A_*$ of all distinct column types of positive multiplicity, including zero when present. Append an all-ones row to get $\widetilde A$. Beside $A_*Y=0$, impose that the sum of all type syndromes is $\sum_jp_j$, since the assigned subsets partition the target. Each syndrome bit now solves an affine linear system with the same coefficient matrix $\widetilde A$. If consistent, its nullity is $d_*=\#\mathrm{columns}(A_*)-\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde A)$. Enumerate these $2^{d_*u}$ syndrome lists and use the preceding covering test with every type syndrome prescribed. If zero occurs then $d_*=d$. If it does not and the all-ones row is independent of the previous rows, then $d_*=d-1$. An inconsistent system immediately rejects.

For example, start with $s=3m$ disjoint repetition blocks of size three and impose one binary parity constraint on all their $s$ block values, with $m\ge2$. The restricted code has dimension $3m-1$ and has $3m$ nonzero column types. Against an arbitrary target of dimension $6m$ at length $9m$, both outer dimensions and the dimension gap grow linearly. Nevertheless $d=1$ and the subset algorithm is singly exponential.

There is a sharper bound when some free syndrome variables correspond to small multiplicities. Compute the actual sets
$$
Q_m=\left\{\sum_{j\in S}p_j: |S|=m\right\}
$$
from the subset table in $2^r\operatorname{poly}(r)$ time. Choose a column basis $I$ of $\widetilde A$. Once the $y_i$ for $i\notin I$ are given, linear algebra determines at most one list of pivot syndromes satisfying the affine equations. Enumerate each free $y_i$ only from $Q_{m_i}$. An admissible list occurs exactly once in this enumeration. Thus the cost, including exact counting and witness recovery, is bounded by
$$
2^r\operatorname{poly}(r)\min_{I\text{ a column basis of }\widetilde A}
\prod_{i\notin I}|Q_{m_i}|.
$$
The minimizing basis is obtained by scanning types in decreasing order of $|Q_{m_i}|$ and retaining independent columns until full rank. This is the greedy maximum-weight basis rule with weights $\log|Q_{m_i}|$. Since $|Q_m|\le\min(2^u,\binom rm)$, the result includes $d_*=O(r/\log(r+1))$ when the multiplicities of all nonbasis types in some affine column basis are bounded. The dimension gap may grow linearly.

The subgroup family associated to these code pairs has order exponential in $r$, so every singly exponential class above gives polynomial Cayley-table time for the corresponding epimorphism problem via the code correspondence. The earlier result gives the exact multiplicative factor for the number of epimorphisms. [Binary subcode correspondence, §5](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SylowTowerActionRanks/PROOF.md)
