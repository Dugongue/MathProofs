# Recovering a subcode from weights in a larger code

For a binary code $A$ and a set of positive weights $W$, let $A[W]$ be the span of its words of weights in $W$. Let $C,D$ be codes of length $r$. The following reductions preserve exactly the set of admissible coordinate permutations, including on negative inputs.

1. If $C[W]=C$, replace $D$ by $D[W]$.
2. If $D^\perp[W]=D^\perp$, replace $C$ by $(C^\perp[W])^\perp$. Here the brackets apply to the dual code before the last orthogonal complement.

Indeed, for every coordinate permutation $p$, the condition $pC\subseteq D$ takes each chosen generator to a word of the same weight in $D$. Their span lies in $D[W]$. The reverse follows by inclusion. Applying the first rule to $p^{-1}D^\perp\subseteq C^\perp$ proves the second. The first implication works for any preserved predicate on a generating set and is formalized in Lean. [Formal pruning theorem](Filter.lean)

Enumerate the codewords once per round and compute a basis for each weight class. Replace the host by the **intersection** of $D[W]$ over all masks with $C[W]=C$. Every admissible image lies in this intersection. Intersecting the spaces takes polynomial time per mask by summing their orthogonal complements. It suffices to consider the at most $2^r$ subsets of weights occurring in $C$. Extra weights only enlarge $D[W]$. Apply the same construction on the dual pair and repeat. A strict change decreases the dimension gap. Stop at a dimension contradiction or after a round with no change. This costs $2^{O(r)}$ bit operations in total. A negative gap proves noncontainment. At gap zero call exact binary code equivalence once. More generally let $k',l'$ be the final dimensions and $g'=l'-k'\ge0$. Enumerating subspaces of the smaller of the primal and dual searches yields deterministic time $2^{O(r)+g'\min(k',r-l')}$ for counting and recovering a witness. The count remains the count for the original input. [Enumeration and code-equivalence bound](Gap.md) · [Weight filter implementation](subcode.py)

One mask can be strictly weaker than the intersection. On eight coordinates let $C$ have generator rows encoded as binary integers $141,74,37,27$, with the least significant bit first. Its nonzero weights are $3,4,5,6$. The weight-three words $74,37,168,81$ form one basis, and the weight-five words $199,220,62,179$ form another, as binary elimination verifies. Extend $C$ by zero to length 16. Adjoin to the host a repetition word $a$ on three fresh positions and $b$ on five other fresh positions. The weight-three host span is $C+\langle a\rangle$ and the weight-five span is $C+\langle b\rangle$, since $C$ has no word of weight two. Their intersection recovers $C$. Every generating mask contains weight three or five, since even-weight vectors cannot span $C$. Thus each individual host span is strictly larger than the hidden code. [Example check](check.py)

## A random-supercode theorem with a deterministic conclusion

Fix a $k$-dimensional code $C$ generated by words of weights in $W$. Choose a uniformly random $(k+g)$-dimensional supercode $D$ of $C$, with $0\le g\le N=r-k$. In the quotient $V=\mathbb F_2^r/C$ form the set $F$ of **distinct nonzero cosets** meeting the weight classes $W$. Let $M=|F|$, $U=D/C$ and $t=\dim\langle F\cap U\rangle$. Then

$$
\dim D[W]=k+t,\qquad
\Pr(t\ge j)\le {M\choose j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{2^g-2^i}{2^N-2^i}
\quad(1\le j\le g).
$$

To prove the identity, project the generators of $D[W]$ to $V$. A coset in $U$ lies entirely in $D$, so the nonzero images are exactly $F\cap U$. The kernel on $D[W]$ is $C$ because the selected words of $C$ span it. For the bound, dimension at least $j$ entails the presence of $j$ independent cosets of $F$ in $U$. Each independent ordered tuple in $V$ has the same probability of lying in $U$. Counting ordered independent tuples in a $g$-space and an $N$-space gives the displayed probability for one fixed tuple. The union bound over independent subsets gives the result. For $j>g$ the probability is zero.

In particular for $N>0$ the deterministic span recovers the hidden code with probability at least $1-M(2^g-1)/(2^N-1)$. On recovery, every equivalent subcode of dimension $k$ in the host equals $D[W]$, so one code-equivalence call returns all admissible permutations as a coset. The same recovery holds after independent unknown scrambles of source and host. For instance, if $W\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}$ with fixed $d$, a host of codimension at least $(d+\epsilon)\log_2 r$ succeeds with probability $1-O(r^{-\epsilon})$, for any fixed $\epsilon>0$. This includes growing source dimensions and linear dimension gaps. The algorithm is exact on arbitrary inputs. The probability concerns only collapse of the gap to zero under this planting distribution.

There is also an exact finite formula. In the expression below the Gaussian coefficient is zero for infeasible dimensions. With $a(S)=\dim\langle S\rangle$,

$$
\Pr(t=0)=\frac{1}{{N\brack g}_2}
\sum_{S\subseteq F}(-1)^{|S|}{N-a(S)\brack g-a(S)}_2 .
$$

Indeed a $g$-space containing $S$ is a $(g-a(S))$-space in the quotient by its span. Inclusion-exclusion now counts the spaces avoiding every forbidden coset.

## A sharp example with logarithmic host redundancy

Partition the coordinates into $m$ nonempty blocks. Take $C$ to consist of words of even weight inside each individual block. It is generated by within-block pairs. Modulo $C$ the coordinates are the $m$ block parities. With $W=\{2\}$ the forbidden cosets are exactly $e_i+e_j$ for distinct blocks. Write $u=m-g$ for the codimension of the random host in the full coordinate space. The quotient $D/C$ is the kernel of a uniformly random full-row-rank binary $u\times m$ matrix. The canonical recovery $D[\{2\}]=C$ occurs exactly when the matrix has **pairwise distinct columns**, since this excludes every $e_i+e_j$ from its kernel.

For $m\to\infty$ and $u=2\log_2 m+O(1)$, its success probability is
$$
\exp\!\left(-\frac{m(m-1)}{2^{u+1}}\right)+o(1).
$$
Before conditioning on full row rank the columns are independent uniform points in a set of size $2^u$, hence the probability is $\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}(1-i/2^u)$. The probability of rank deficiency is at most $(2^u-1)2^{-m}$ by a union bound over nonzero row relations. Conditioning therefore changes the probability by $o(1)$. Expanding the logarithm of the product gives total error $O(m^3/2^{2u})=o(1)$. Taking blocks of size two gives length $2m$, source dimension $m$, and gap $m-u$. Thus the success calculation describes linear gaps all the way to hosts with logarithmic redundancy.

The use of low-weight words for subcode equivalence has prior literature. The deterministic bidirectional rule, the quotient-coset tail estimate, and the preceding specialization specify the claims made here. Priority is unverified. [Santini, Baldi and Chiaraluce](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2023.3323068)
