# Joint prime distributions for ordinary tensor isomorphism

Grochow and Qiao's across-fields question compares the decision problems over different fields. This note treats simultaneous reductions of the same integral tensor data modulo many different primes. It gives a complete classification of the joint density distributions and an exact specialization formula that also covers all powers of each residue field. [Grochow–Qiao, journal Question 7.6](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10494304) · [First SC briefing](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/SimultaneousComputabilityAndGrochow/README.md).

## 1. Specialization of the whole transporter profile

**Theorem 1.** Fix a finite list of pairs of same-format ordinary tensors over a number field $K$. There are a finite Galois extension $L/K$, finite $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$-sets $Y_i$, and a finite exceptional set of places such that at every other finite place $v$ and for every $e\ge1$, the $i$-th pair is isomorphic over the residue-field extension of degree $e$ if and only if $\operatorname{Frob}_v^e$ fixes a point of $Y_i$. Here $Y_i$ is the geometric connected-component set of the characteristic-zero transporter. This is a simultaneous statement for one fixed list of tensor pairs and one common exceptional set.

*Proof.* Invert the input denominators. Each transporter is an affine scheme of finite presentation over the resulting ring of integers, represented by the invertible matrices carrying one tensor to the other. If a characteristic-zero transporter is empty over the algebraic closure, its defining ideal in a polynomial presentation contains one over $K$. Clearing denominators in such an identity makes every remaining special fiber empty. Use $Y_i=\varnothing$ for that pair.

For a nonempty transporter choose $L$ large enough to define all geometric components and one geometric transporter. A chosen point identifies the geometric transporter with the stabilizer. In characteristic zero the stabilizer is smooth and its geometric components are translates of the smooth connected identity component. They are therefore geometrically irreducible and disjoint. The finite open-and-closed decomposition descends to $L$, then extends to the model after localization, since the finitely many coordinate-ring idempotents and their relations spread. Each piece remains geometrically irreducible in every fiber after another localization, by generic geometric irreducibility. They remain nonempty by spreading a point in each piece after enlarging $L$. The Galois action on the idempotents also spreads. Delete the finitely many places below these localizations and the ramified places of $L/K$. It follows that over the algebraic closure of a good residue field, the geometric transporter component set is exactly $Y_i$ and its Frobenius action is the indicated Galois action. [Stacks Project, geometric irreducibility of fibers](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0559) · [Aizenbud–Avni, Theorem 3.4.3 and Proposition 3.4.4, equivariant specialization](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~aizenr/4Publications/Global.pdf).

Over a finite field the reduced geometric stabilizer is a smooth group. On a component stable under the relevant Frobenius power, choose a geometric transporter $x$. Its Frobenius image is $xg$ for some element $g$ in the connected reduced identity group. Lang's theorem gives $h$ with $h\operatorname{Frob}^e(h)^{-1}=g$, so $xh$ is a transporter over the degree-$e$ residue-field extension. The other implication holds because a rational point fixes its component. [Lang, *Algebraic groups over finite fields*](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Lang-Algebraic_Groups_Over_Finite_Fields.pdf) · [Earlier finite-field component argument](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/IsomorphismCrossoversRoundThree/tensors.md). $\square$

In particular, the entire joint distribution of the prime tests, including any finite list of residue-extension exponents, is computed by finite group fractions once the component actions are given. For any prescribed pattern, use the proportion of $g\in G$ whose indicated powers satisfy the fixed-point tests. Chebotarev gives the natural density. This describes reductions at all good primes, and uses no GRH.

## 2. Exactly which joint distributions occur

At base residue-field probes, a pattern is a subset of the pair indices, the indices at which an isomorphism exists. A distribution on these patterns has a **greatest supported pattern** if the union of the patterns with positive mass itself has positive mass.

**Theorem 2.** A probability distribution on finitely many binary tensor-isomorphism tests is the joint natural prime density distribution of some fixed finite list of ordinary integral three-tensor pairs if and only if it is rational and has a greatest supported pattern. The pairs can all be nonisomorphic over $\mathbb Q$. Each coordinate which is positive with nonzero probability can be realized by a pair of finite étale multiplication tensors, isomorphic over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. All pairs can have the same tensor side.

*Necessity.* In Theorem 1 the identity of $G$ fixes every point. It accepts exactly all geometrically isomorphic pairs, and no other group element can accept any index outside that set. Completely split primes have positive density, so that pattern is greatest and supported. Rationality also follows from Chebotarev.

*Sufficiency.* Discard identically negative coordinates until the end, so that the greatest pattern is the all-positive pattern $\top$. Choose a common denominator $b\ge2$ of the probabilities $a_P/b$. Take $G=C_b\times V$ where $V=(C_2)^r$. The aim is to label $G$ by patterns $P$ in the prescribed proportions, with the property that taking any positive power can only add indices to the label.

On the fiber $v=0$, use label $\top$ everywhere. On each of the other fibers over $V$ choose one of the following labelings. The entire fiber can have a single pattern $P$. Alternatively use $P$ at its $b-1$ positions with nonidentity $C_b$ coordinate, and $\top$ at its identity position. Even powers send the $V$ coordinate to zero. Odd powers stay within the fiber and can only preserve a label or replace it by $\top$. Thus the power condition holds.

For each $P\ne\top$ of positive mass, the desired count is $N_P=a_P2^r$. Take $r$ so large that each such $N_P\ge b^2$. Express $N_P=b u_P+(b-1)w_P$ with $u_P,w_P\ge0$, $w_P<b$: choose $w_P\equiv -N_P\bmod b$, then take the quotient for $u_P$. Use $u_P$ full fibers and $w_P$ partial fibers. Their total count is at most
$$
 \sum_{P\ne\top}\frac{N_P}{b}+\#\{P\ne\top:a_P>0\}
 = (1-a_\top/b)\,2^r+\#\{P\ne\top:a_P>0\}.
$$
For large enough $r\ge2$ this fits inside the $2^r-1$ nonzero fibers. All unused labels are $\top$. The chosen labels thus have the exact desired counts.

Realize $G$ over $\mathbb Q$ by a cyclic degree-$b$ prime-conductor cyclotomic subfield and independent quadratic fields with distinct prime discriminants away from that conductor. Dirichlet gives a conductor congruent to $1\bmod b$ and the quadratic discriminant primes. The inertia groups at the separate conductors show the required disjointness.

For coordinate $i$, take the downward family $\mathcal C_i$ of subgroups generated by the cyclic subgroups $\langle g\rangle$ whose label contains $i$. A generator and any other generator of its cyclic subgroup have the same pattern by the power condition in both directions. The subgroup $\langle g\rangle$ belongs to $\mathcal C_i$ exactly when the label of $g$ contains $i$. The family contains the trivial subgroup and excludes $G$, because $r\ge2$. The finite étale Burnside-mark construction in the first SC briefing gives a multiplication tensor pair with exactly that subgroup profile. It is geometrically isomorphic and is nonisomorphic over $\mathbb Q$. Use bases of the integer rings of the two étale algebras to get integral multiplication tensors. At an unramified prime, the local étale algebra is the permutation algebra of the Frobenius action on the embedding set. Its multiplication tensor has the same test by unital normalization. Consequently the joint test at a good prime is precisely the label at its Frobenius. For discarded coordinates use a zero and a nonzero tensor. Pad étale pairs to a common positive side by taking the product with the same split factor on both sides. The new identical fixed points cancel in every subgroup comparison. [Earlier Burnside-mark realization](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/SimultaneousComputabilityAndGrochow/ArithmeticTI.md). $\square$

## 3. A prescribed finite set of primes can be held fixed

**Corollary 3.** In the realization direction of Theorem 2, prescribe at any finite set of rational primes any patterns of positive mass. The tensors can meet those prescriptions, with the prescribed primes unramified in the common splitting field and with the same decisions over the corresponding local fields.

*Proof.* Give one distinct nonzero basis vector of $V$ to each prescribed prime. Reserve its full fiber for the required pattern, including the $\top$ case. Subtract $b$ from each non-top desired count per full fiber reserved for that pattern. For large enough $r$ the adjusted positive counts still exceed $b^2$ and the fiber argument still fits: the reserved finite number of fibers and all remainder costs are bounded while the top mass times $2^r$ grows. Give any other unused positions $\top$.

Choose the cyclic conductor $\ell$ outside the prescribed primes, splitting completely in the splitting field of all $X^b-p$ for prescribed $p$ together with $\mathbb Q(\zeta_b)$. Chebotarev supplies such a prime. All prescribed $p$ are $b$-th powers modulo $\ell$, so they have trivial Frobenius in the cyclic degree-$b$ subfield of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)$. For each coordinate of $V$, choose a distinct auxiliary prime discriminant congruent to $1\bmod4$ with the required quadratic residue symbol at every prescribed odd prime. If $2$ is prescribed, use residue $1$ or $5\bmod8$ for the prescribed positive or negative symbol there. The Chinese remainder theorem and Dirichlet give the choices. The fields have separate prime discriminants and are unramified at the prescribed primes. They realize the chosen vector of Frobenius signs at each such prime. Its Frobenius in $G$ has the reserved label. Unramified étale local algebras have the same test as their residue-field algebras, so the local-field prescription holds as well. $\square$

Even if a coordinate has prime density one, its rational answer can be negative. The theorem makes all rational answers negative even when every prescribed finite list of primes gives all-positive answers. This is compatible with the previously established specific local–global failures at side six and does not assert a size bound of six or preserve any fixed tensor shape. The exact joint-density classification and finite calibration use the earlier subgroup realization and standard arithmetic theorems. They concern the behavior of fixed inputs across fields, leaving polynomial-time reductions across distinct characteristics as a separate problem.
