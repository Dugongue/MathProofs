# Alternating matrix tuple isometry with bounded lifting obstructions

This proves a decision, search, and counting result for the ordered alternating portion of the characteristic-two Matrix Tuple Isometry question in Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Part V, §1.4. The coefficient ring may be any compactly presented $R=\mathbb Z/2^\tau\mathbb Z$, including $\mathbb F_2$. The result is an informal proof. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Part V, §1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S1.SS4)

## Theorem

Let $A=(A_1,\ldots,A_m)$ and $B=(B_1,\ldots,B_m)$ be ordered $n\times n$ alternating matrix tuples over $R$. Define the adjoint algebra $E$ of $A$ as in Section 1 and let $J=\operatorname{rad} E$. Suppose the semisimple algebra $E/J$ is split over $\mathbb F_2$, so each simple factor is a full matrix algebra over $\mathbb F_2$. For each $k\ge 1$ form the explicitly computable spaces
$$
I_k=J^k/J^{k+1},\qquad
H_k=\operatorname{image}(J^k\cap E_{\rm alt}\longrightarrow I_k),\qquad
W_k=H_k/\operatorname{image}(a\mapsto a+a^*).
$$
Here $E_{\rm alt}$ consists of the self-adjoint pairs $(Z,Z)$ for which every $A_i Z$ is alternating. Write $D=\sum_k\dim_{\mathbb F_2}W_k$. There is a deterministic algorithm using $2^D\operatorname{poly}(n,m,\tau)$ bit operations. It decides whether $B_i=C^t A_i C$ for one $C\in\operatorname{GL}_n(R)$ and all $i$, produces $C$ when it exists, and returns the exact number of such $C$. It also produces generators of the isometry group. In particular the algorithm runs in polynomial time whenever $D=O(\log(n+m+\tau))$. The condition allows nonzero Jacobson radicals, singular slices, and failure of surjectivity of reduction on isometry groups. General ordered alternating tuple isometry remains open outside the stated regime.

## 1. Reduction to a norm equation

Obtain $P,Q\in\operatorname{GL}_n(R)$ with $B_i=P^t A_i Q$ from the polynomial-time left-right algorithm. Failure proves nonisometry. Replace $A_i$ by $P^t A_i P$ and write $B_i=A_i T$, where $T=P^{-1}Q$. This change replaces the original adjoint algebra by an isomorphic one and carries the spaces defining $D$ to their counterparts. [Sun, Theorem A.26](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920v1#A1.SS4)
Here is a deterministic construction for this preprocessing step. Regard each tuple as a representation with two free modules and one map between them per slice. Compute the endomorphism algebra of the direct sum of the two representations by linear equations. Compute its semisimple quotient by entrywise reduction and the finite-algebra algorithms below. Let $e_A,e_B$ be the two summand idempotents. If their images in a simple matrix factor have unequal rank, there can be no isomorphism. If the ranks agree in every factor, choose inverse maps between their image spaces and sandwich lifts of those maps by the original idempotents. The two composites are identity modulo the nilpotent radical in the respective corners, so both are units there by a finite geometric series. Correcting by either composite gives inverse actual module maps and yields the two left-right matrices. This works for residue matrix factors over extension fields as well.

Use the paired algebra
$$
E=\{(X,Y)\in M_n(R)\times M_n(R)^{\mathrm{op}} : X^t A_i=A_iY\ \,\forall i\}.
$$
Its unit is $(I,I)$ and its involution is $(X,Y)^*=(Y,X)$, since each $A_i^t=-A_i$. The entries are unrestricted subject to these linear equations. In particular, no projection to either component needs to be injective. A pair in $E$ is a unit exactly when both matrices are invertible, as inverting the defining relation shows. Since $A_i T$ is alternating, $T^t A_i=A_i T$ and $h=(T,T)$ is a self-adjoint unit in $E_{\rm alt}$.

If $C^t A_i C=B_i$ for each $i$, then $(C,T C^{-1})\in E^\times$ and its norm $x^*x$ is $h$. Conversely any $x=(C,Y)$ with $x^*x=h$ has $Y C=T$ and gives $C^t A_i C=A_i T$. Solve $g^* h g=1$ and use the first matrix of $g^{-1}$ for $C$. For automorphisms of the source, $T=I$, so the first projection of the unitary group is an isomorphism onto the source isometry group. For the original pair, multiply $C$ on the left by the original $P$. The norm reformulation is standard in adjoint algebra methods. [Ivanyos–Qiao, *Algorithms based on \*-algebras*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03495)

## 2. The residue problem

Polynomial-time modular linear algebra constructs $E$, its entrywise reduction $\overline E\subset M_n(\mathbb F_2)\times M_n(\mathbb F_2)^{\mathrm{op}}$, and the preimage $J$ of $\operatorname{rad}\overline E$. The kernel of reduction is nilpotent because its $\tau$-fold products are divisible by $2^\tau$, so taking the preimage gives exactly $\operatorname{rad}E$. The residue algebra is $\overline E/\operatorname{rad}\overline E$. Use finite-algebra structure algorithms over the fixed field $\mathbb F_2$ to compute the radical and explicit Wedderburn coordinates. This also tests the split condition. The radical calculation is deterministic, and finding explicit matrix coordinates reduces deterministically to polynomial factoring. Even if a factoring call uses an extension $K$ of $\mathbb F_2$, factoring is deterministic in the absolute input size. For a squarefree polynomial $f\in K[x]$, the squaring-fixed binary subspace of $K[x]/(f)$ has one binary coordinate for each simple factor. Solve $u^2=u$ by binary linear algebra and use a basis of fixed elements to split $f$ by gcds with $u$ and $u-1$. Each pair of distinct factors is separated by a basis element. Standard squarefree decomposition, using square roots in $K$ where needed, handles repetitions. [Rónyai, Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.2](https://www.cs.uoregon.edu/Reports/TR-1985-012.pdf) Computing $J^k$ uses products of module generators. There are at most $(2n^2+1)\tau$ nonzero layers, since a product of $2n^2+1$ elements of $J$ is in the kernel of entrywise reduction. Each layer is an $\mathbb F_2$-space because $2E\subset J$. Smith normal form over $R$ and binary elimination compute all layer descriptions, lift representatives, and test membership. [Rónyai, *Computing the structure of finite algebras*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(08)80017-X)

For $E_1=E/J$, compute one $g_1\in E_1^\times$ solving $g_1^*\bar h g_1=1$, if it exists, and generators for $U_1=\{g:g^*g=1\}$. The involution permutes the simple factors, so work on one or two factors at a time.

* On exchanged factors write the involution as swap-transpose by choosing the coordinates of the second factor via the involution on the first. The self-adjoint input is $(H,H^t)$, the solution is $(H^{-1},I)$, and the unitary group consists of $(L,L^{-t})$. Elementary row transvections generate $\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb F_2)$.
* On a fixed factor $M_d(\mathbb F_2)$, the involution is $X^*=F^{-1}X^tF$ for an invertible symmetric $F$, since composing with transpose gives an automorphism of the full matrix algebra. Solve $X^tF=FX^*$ on elementary matrices to find $F$. The solution space is a line, because after one invertible solution any other differs by a matrix commuting with the full matrix algebra. Involutivity makes $F^t$ a scalar multiple of $F$, so it equals $F$ over $\mathbb F_2$. The form $F\bar h$ is also symmetric and invertible, and the equation is congruence to $F$. Two nondegenerate alternating forms of the same dimension are congruent by symplectic elimination. All nonalternating symmetric nondegenerate forms of the same dimension are congruent to the identity, as follows by splitting off a unit norm vector and replacing a unit line plus one hyperbolic plane by three orthonormal lines. Explicitly, for the line vector $v$ and hyperbolic pair $a,b$, use $v+a,v+b,v+a+b$. The alternating and nonalternating types are distinguished by the diagonal.

For completeness the classical groups in this base step have explicit polynomial generating sets. In symplectic coordinates $e_i,f_i$, use the transvections along these basis vectors and along $e_j+f_i$ for $i\ne j$. Each pair can first be changed independently to a multiple of $e_i$. The product of the transvections along $e_j+f_i,e_j,f_i$ implements the elementary addition $e_i\mapsto e_i+e_j$ on the $e$-space and its inverse transpose on the $f$-space. Hence the generated group is transitive on nonzero vectors. Conjugation supplies all transvections. These generate the full symplectic group by induction on symplectic pairs. After matching the first $e$, match $f'$ to $f$ by the transvection along $f'+f$ when $(f',f)=1$. If $(f',f)=0$, first send $f'$ to $f'+e$ with the transvection along $e$. Both operations fix $e$. Continue on the orthogonal complement of this pair.

For a nonalternating symmetric form $F$, the characteristic vector $w$ is defined by $F(v,v)=F(w,v)$. Every isometry fixes $w$. If $F(w,w)=1$ the group is the symplectic group on $w^\perp$. Otherwise choose $f$ with $F(w,f)=1$, put $V_0=\langle w,f\rangle^\perp$, and lift all symplectic maps on $V_0$ by fixing $w,f$. The kernel of the full isometry group's induced action on $w^\perp/\langle w\rangle$ consists exactly of the maps
$$
w\mapsto w,\quad f\mapsto f+b+t w,\quad v\mapsto v+F(v,b)w\ (v\in V_0),\qquad b\in V_0,\ t\in\mathbb F_2.
$$
The formula follows by preserving the pairings with $w$ and $f$, and substitution verifies every displayed map. Generators use the basis vectors of $V_0$ for $b$ with $t=0$, together with $b=0,t=1$. Multiplication adds the $b$ coordinates and changes the $t$ coordinate by their pairing. This gives the required group generators and orders. The orders of $\operatorname{GL}$ and $\operatorname{Sp}$ are obtained by counting ordinary and symplectic bases.

## 3. The affine obstruction at a square-zero layer

Set $E_k=E/J^k$ and $U_k=\{u\in E_k^\times:u^*u=1\}$. In $E_{k+1}$, the ideal $I_k$ has square zero, since $2k\ge k+1$. Fix $z\in H_k$ and lift $u\in U_k$ to a unit $\widetilde u\in E_{k+1}^\times$. Such lifts exist through nilpotent ideals and can be obtained through module lifting. Congruence of $1+z$ becomes the affine map
$$
(1+z)\cdot\widetilde u=\widetilde u^*(1+z)\widetilde u
=1+\left[\widetilde u^*z\widetilde u+(\widetilde u^*\widetilde u-1)\right].
\tag{1}
$$
The bracket lies in $H_k$. Indeed, congruence on pairs gives congruence of physical alternating matrices, and both the input and the identity are represented by such matrices. For a representative $g=(X,Y)$ and $z=(Z,Z)$, the physical congruence law is $A_i(YZX)=X^t(A_iZ)X$. For $a=(L,R)\in J^k$, the physical matrix for $a+a^*$ is $A_i L+L^t A_i$, which is alternating. Thus the trace image is a subspace of $H_k$.

The linear part of (1) preserves the trace image, since $g^*(a+a^*)g=g^*ag+(g^*ag)^*$. Changing a lift by right multiplication by $1+a$, $a\in I_k$, translates the bracket by $a+a^*$. In fact all products of two of $z,a,a^*$ vanish. The maps therefore induce a right action of $U_k$ on the actual vector space $W_k$. A class comes from an exact norm defect removable by $1+a$ precisely when it is zero. Finding $a$ is then a binary linear solve.

## 4. Lifting a transporter and the entire stabilizer

Suppose $g_k^*h g_k=1$ in $E_k$. Fix any lift $g\in E_{k+1}^\times$, so $g^*hg=1+z$ for $z\in H_k$. Every other solution modulo $J^k$ is $g_k u$ for some $u\in U_k$. Therefore a next-level solution exists exactly when $[z]$ belongs to the $U_k$-orbit of zero in $W_k$. Enumerate this orbit by applying the generators and retain a matrix representative of each orbit point. If $[z]$ is present, take the inverse representative which takes $[z]$ to zero, lift it, and perform the binary trace correction. This produces $g_{k+1}$. If the point is absent the answer is no. The stabilizer computation below can continue independently to obtain the source isometry group.

The same orbit gives generators for the subgroup of $U_k$ that lifts to $U_{k+1}$. Its elements are exactly those fixing zero. Take Schreier generators $r_v s r_{v\cdot s}^{-1}$, where $r_v$ takes zero to the orbit point $v$ and $s$ runs over the original generator list. Traversing positive generators suffices, since each has finite order. Lift each Schreier generator and trace-correct it to a unitary. The kernel of $U_{k+1}\to U_k$ consists of $1+a$ with $a+a^*=0$. A binary basis of this kernel supplies the final generators needed at this stage.

If there are $b_k$ generators for $U_k$, the next list has size at most $|0\cdot U_k| b_k+\dim I_k$, and all orbit points require at most $2^{\dim W_k} b_k$ edges to process. The sum of the kernel dimensions is bounded by $2\tau n^2$. Group representatives are stored as matrices, so long words do not need to be expanded. This establishes the bound $2^D\operatorname{poly}(n,m,\tau)$. The exact order recurs as
$$
|U_{k+1}|=|U_k|\,2^{\dim\ker(a\mapsto a+a^*)}/|0\cdot U_k|,
$$
where the kernel is on $I_k$. At the final level $J^k=0$. A nonempty transporter is a coset of the isometry group, so this order is also the desired number of physical congruences.

The bound can use the product of the actual orbit sizes in place of $2^D$. The affine analysis addresses which semisimple unitary elements lift. Sun's unipotent affine transporter instead handles the principal-unit part and then enumerates residue representatives in the general procedure. [Sun, Lemmas A.21–A.24 and the proof of Theorem A.19](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920v1#A1.SS4)

## 5. An unbounded family with a genuine lifting obstruction

Let $S=\mathbb F_2[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$, and let $\lambda:S\to\mathbb F_2$ read the $\epsilon$ coefficient. Let $d\ge4$ be even. On $S^d$, use the alternating forms $\beta_Q(x,y)=\lambda(x^tQy)$, where $Q$ ranges over a standard invertible alternating pairing matrix $Q_0$, the elementary alternating matrices, and $\epsilon Q_0$. The pairing $Q_0$ is nondegenerate over $\mathbb F_2$ because $\lambda(uv)$ is a nondegenerate pairing on $S$. The matrices of the relative operators are $Q_0^{-1}Q$, and they generate $M_d(S)$. To see this, the elementary alternating matrix within a hyperbolic pair yields the projector onto that pair. Composing the corresponding projector with a relative elementary alternating matrix between distinct pairs yields a cross-pair matrix unit. Products of cross-pair matrix units give the within-pair matrix units. The operator $\epsilon I$ generates the coefficient extension.

Take two copies of this tuple, multiplying every form on the second copy by $1+\epsilon$ before applying $\lambda$. Their relative operators agree, so the paired adjoint algebra is identified by its first projection with $M_2(S)$, the common centralizer of the $M_d(S)$ action. The involution is $X^*=H^{-1}X^tH$ with $H=\operatorname{diag}(1,1+\epsilon)$. Its radical $J=\epsilon M_2(S)$ has square zero, and $E/J=M_2(\mathbb F_2)$. For these tuples $E_{\rm alt}$ is the full self-adjoint space, because an alternating form over $S$ tensored with a symmetric multiplicity form is alternating, also after applying $\lambda$. The space $H_1$ is therefore the space of symmetric two-by-two matrices times $\epsilon$, and the trace image comprises the alternating ones. Thus $D=2$ in unbounded dimension and the two quotient coordinates are the diagonal entries.

The swap of the two copies is unitary in the residue. Its lifted norm is $(1+\epsilon)I$, with defect coordinates $(1,1)$. This is outside the trace image, so the swap has no unitary lift. The residue unitary group has exactly two elements. A target twist by $1+\epsilon I$ reaches the swap's defect and succeeds. A twist by $1+\epsilon\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ is outside the orbit of zero and fails. Thus the method handles a class with nonliftable residue unitaries and distinguishes actual positive and negative instances there.
