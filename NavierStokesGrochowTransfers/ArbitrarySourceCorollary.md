# The paired-cut algorithm with arbitrary source groups

Let $H$ have central elementary abelian radical and suppose $Q=H/Z(H)$ is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups. The source $G$ is arbitrary. Write $H=H'\times C_H$ as in the paired-cut theorem, where $H'$ is perfect and $C_H$ elementary abelian. Take $p$ to be the prime of $Z(H')$ when that group is nontrivial, and take $p=2$ otherwise.

**Corollary.** The algorithm below constructs from the tables a family of at most $|G|$ quotients $\overline G_L$ that all satisfy the input promise of the paired-cut theorem. With $b_*$ the largest paired-cut exponent for the resulting pairs $(\overline G_L,H')$ after elementary rank rejection, decision, construction and exact counting for $G\twoheadrightarrow H$ take deterministic $p^{2b_*}\operatorname{poly}(|G|+|H|)$ time. An empty list gives count zero. This is polynomial whenever the displayed, computable family has $b_*=O(\log_p(|G|+|H|))$. [Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar, Open Question 3(5)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)

First list the possible kernels $L$ of an epimorphism $G\to Q$. Construct a chief series $1=N_0\lhd\cdots\lhd N_s=G$ by taking at each step a smallest nonidentity normal closure of an element of the relevant quotient. For each nonabelian chief section form the kernel $C_i$ of the conjugation action of $G$ on $N_i/N_{i-1}$. Enumerate all intersections of the $C_i$, including the empty intersection, and deduplicate. There are at most $2^s\le |G|$ of them.

To check completeness, let $\psi:G\twoheadrightarrow Q$. The image series in $Q$ is a chief series after deleting its trivial steps. Each nontrivial section is isomorphic to its source chief section, since the kernel of the induced map on that section is invariant under conjugation by $G$ and the source section is minimal normal in the relevant quotient. Every surviving section is a single simple direct factor of $Q$, as the normal subgroups of a product of centerless simple groups are products of factors. It follows that $\ker\psi$ is the intersection of the $C_i$ over surviving sections. It lies in each such kernel by the equivariant isomorphisms on sections. Conversely an element in their intersection has trivial conjugation action on every simple direct factor of $Q$ and maps to the identity, since the factors are centerless. This kernel listing is the earlier chief-section method. [Earlier chief-section algorithm](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/6b091c10bce8f1f3fca282e739f4bbb1790615b3/FourAlgebraicIsomorphismTransfers/Epimorphisms.md)

Discard any candidate for which $G/L\not\cong Q$. This is a direct product recognition and multiset comparison of the simple types. Find minimal normal subgroups as above, test each for simplicity by its own normal closures, and check that the simple groups commute and their product has order equal to the quotient order and to the product of their orders. Two-generated simple groups are matched constructively by trying images of a generating pair, as in the main theorem. All this takes polynomial table time.

For each retained $L$ put

$$K_L=[L,G]L^p,\qquad \overline G_L=G/K_L,$$

where $L^p$ is the subgroup generated by the $p$-th powers of elements of $L$. The image $L/K_L$ is central elementary abelian, and the quotient by it is $Q$. Since $Q$ has trivial solvable radical and center, the radical and center of $\overline G_L$ both equal $L/K_L$. Thus $\overline G_L$ satisfies the paired-cut promise. Any epimorphism $G\to H'$ whose composite with $H'\to Q$ has kernel $L$ sends $L$ into $Z(H')$. It annihilates $K_L$, so it factors through $\overline G_L$. Conversely any epimorphism $\overline G_L\to H'$ sends its center into $Z(H')$, and the induced onto map of their centerless quotients is invertible because both are isomorphic to $Q$. The composite from $G$ has kernel exactly $L$ after projection to $Q$. Consequently the counts from different $L$ are disjoint and sum to $\#\operatorname{Epi}(G,H')$.

Finally, epimorphisms onto $H'\times C_H$ are exactly the products of independent epimorphisms onto $H'$ and onto $C_H$. If $u:G\twoheadrightarrow H'$ and $v:G\twoheadrightarrow C_H$, then $u([G,G])=H'$ and $v([G,G])=1$. After choosing any preimage of a desired second coordinate, one corrects its first coordinate inside $[G,G]$. This proves the claimed product rule. The map onto $C_H$ factors through the largest elementary abelian $q$-quotient of $G$: compute $G/([G,G]G^q)$ for the prime $q$ of $C_H$ and count its onto linear maps, with one map when $C_H=1$. All constructions are table polynomial outside the paired-cut recursions.
