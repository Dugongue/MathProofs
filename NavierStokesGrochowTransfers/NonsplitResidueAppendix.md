# Removing the split residue hypothesis for decision, search, and counting

Keep the alternating matrix tuples, compact ring, adjoint algebra, and obstruction dimensions of the main theorem. Allow arbitrary residue field extensions in the semisimple factors of $E/J$. Decision, search for one congruence, and exact congruence counting take deterministic $2^{O(D)}\operatorname{poly}(n,m,\tau)$ bit operations. The full automorphism generating-set claim stays with the split theorem.

## 1. A subgroup that has every relevant orbit

Let $M=\max_k 2^{\dim W_k}$, with $M=1$ if the filtration has no positive layers. The residue unitary group $U_1$ is a product of groups on the involution orbits of the simple matrix factors. On an exchanged pair it is $\operatorname{GL}_d(K)$ for a finite field $K$. On a fixed factor with a first-kind involution it is a symplectic group or the isometry group of a nonalternating symmetric bilinear form. On a fixed factor with a second-kind involution it is a unitary group $\operatorname{U}_d(\mathbb F_{q^2}/\mathbb F_q)$, for $q$ even.

In each general linear or second-kind unitary factor, use the determinant map onto its scalar group. These scalar groups are $K^\times$ and the norm-one group of $\mathbb F_{q^2}^\times$, respectively. Keep the inverse image of the product of the Sylow subgroups for primes at most $M$. Keep the other factors whole. This gives a normal subgroup $U_1'\triangleleft U_1$ whose index has only prime divisors exceeding $M$. At every later level set $U_k'=U_k\cap \rho_k^{-1}(U_1')$, where $\rho_k:U_k\to U_1$ is reduction modulo $J$. The index $[U_k:U_k']$ divides $[U_1:U_1']$ because $U_1'$ is normal.

For a group $G$ acting on $W_k$, a point $v$ has orbit size $[G:G_v]\le M$. If $H\triangleleft G$ has index with only prime divisors greater than $M$, then $HG_v$ is a subgroup and $[G:HG_v]$ divides both $[G:H]$ and $[G:G_v]$. Thus $HG_v=G$ and $H$ has the same orbit on $v$. Apply this with $G=U_k$ and $H=U_k'$. The main theorem’s orbit enumeration with generators of $U_k'$ therefore decides every lifting step, with the same trace corrections. Schreier generation and the trace-zero kernel produce exactly $U_{k+1}'$ for the next step. Full group orders follow by starting the main theorem’s order recurrence with the order of $U_1$. The orbit sizes appearing in that recurrence agree for the two groups.

## 2. Computing the scalar generators

Enumerate primes $\ell\le M$, and compute $a=v_\ell(b)$ for each scalar group order $b$ by repeated integer division. If $a>0$, obtain a primitive $\ell$-th root from a nonidentity root of $X^\ell-1$. Repeatedly take an $\ell$-th root $a-1$ times. At each iteration the required roots exist and every root has order exactly one additional power of $\ell$, since its $\ell$-th power has the preceding order. The final element generates the $\ell$-Sylow subgroup. The factoring method from the main theorem is deterministic in the absolute field degree and the polynomial degree. In the norm-one case the full multiplicative group has order $(q-1)(q+1)$ and the norm-one group has order $q+1$. The two factors $q-1,q+1$ are coprime because $q$ is even, so every root in the construction has order dividing $q+1$ and is automatically norm-one. Diagonal matrices with the resulting scalar in one position give determinant lifts in both standard forms.

For the general linear factors, the determinant kernel is generated by elementary matrices with entries from an additive $\mathbb F_2$-basis of the field, because products give arbitrary entries and elementary elimination gives the full special linear group. The symplectic construction in the main theorem works over every finite field of characteristic two with transvection parameters in an additive basis. Their powers and products supply arbitrary parameters on the same line. Use arbitrary coefficient elementary additions to eliminate the live $e$-coordinates and use each local symplectic plane to scale its nonzero coordinate. For the nonalternating symmetric cases use the characteristic vector of $v\mapsto \sqrt{F(v,v)}$. This function is linear over the field in characteristic two. The same decomposition and kernel formulas apply, with generator parameters from additive bases. The orthogonalization of nonalternating symmetric forms uses square roots in the coefficient field.

## 3. Computing the special unitary generators

On a fixed second-kind factor write the involution as $X^*=F^{-1}\bar X^t F$, by composing with the known conjugate transpose and solving the resulting intertwining equations on matrix units. Here the bar is the involution on the coefficient field $K=\mathbb F_{q^2}$, extracted from its action on the center. Involutivity gives $\bar F^t=cF$ with $c\bar c=1$. Rescale $F$ by $1$ if $c=1$, and by $1+c$ otherwise, to make it Hermitian. Hermitian elimination finds bases with Gram matrix the identity for both $F$ and $F\bar h$, where in this expression $\bar h$ denotes the residue of the given self-adjoint term. In detail, a nonzero diagonal entry gives a nonisotropic vector. If the diagonal is zero and an off-diagonal entry $z$ is nonzero, find $t$ with $zt+\overline{zt}\ne0$ by checking a binary basis. A nonzero norm is rescaled to one using a square root from $\mathbb F_q$. Repeat on its orthogonal complement. These bases give a residue solution and the coordinates for generating the group.

The determinant kernel in these coordinates is $\operatorname{SU}_d(K/\mathbb F_q)$. First, in dimension two, choose the hyperbolic basis $v=(1,1)$ and $w=(a,1+a)$, with $a+\bar a=1$. Such an $a$ is found by binary linear algebra. The Gram matrix in this basis is $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. A determinant-one matrix satisfies $g^{-1}=Jg^tJ$. The isometry identity says $g^{-1}=J\bar g^tJ$. Thus the determinant-one isometries in these coordinates are precisely $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb F_q)$. Upper and lower unit transvections with parameters from a binary basis of $\mathbb F_q$ generate these groups.

If $q>2$, embed these two-coordinate groups on all coordinate pairs. In a nonzero-norm plane vector, its span and its orthogonal complement are nondegenerate. Hermitian elimination extends any matching of such vectors to a plane isometry, and a norm-one scalar on the complementary line corrects its determinant. Thus the embedded $\operatorname{SU}_2$ can combine any two coordinates with unequal nonzero norms into one coordinate. If every occupied coordinate has the same norm and there is more than one, there must be at least three because the vector being reduced has total norm one. An occupied pair then has norm zero. Extend that isotropic pair vector to a hyperbolic basis of its plane by choosing a partner of pairing one and adjusting the partner to norm zero via the coefficient trace. In this basis the matrix $\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1})$ for $t\in\mathbb F_q^\times$ has determinant one, preserves the plane form, and rescales the original vector by $t$. Choose $t\ne1$. Both modified coordinate norms differ from the third, so a subsequent pair operation reduces the support. Eventually the vector is moved to the first unit coordinate. To generate an arbitrary special unitary matrix, send its first column to that coordinate, then induct on the orthogonal complement.

If $q=2$, each nonzero coordinate has norm one. A norm-one vector has odd support. For three occupied coordinates, any unitary map on those three coordinates carrying the restricted vector to the first of them can have its determinant corrected on the complementary two dimensions. Use the embedded $\operatorname{SU}_3(\mathbb F_4/\mathbb F_2)$ on every coordinate triple. This fixed group can be enumerated by testing the $4^9$ matrices of size three for the two defining identities. Each use reduces the support by two until one coordinate survives. Embedded $\operatorname{SU}_2$ maps move that coordinate to the start and normalize it. Induct on the remaining dimension, using the two-coordinate generators in dimension two. Dimension one has trivial determinant kernel. This gives deterministic polynomial-size residue generator lists without enumerating a varying field.

The initial order for a second-kind unitary factor is $q^{d(d-1)/2}\prod_{i=1}^d(q^i-(-1)^i)$. One derivation counts orthonormal bases. The number of norm-one vectors in dimension $d$ is $q^{d-1}(q^d-(-1)^d)$, by separating the last coordinate and using one element of norm zero and $q+1$ elements of each nonzero norm in $K$. The remaining choices are an orthonormal basis of the complement. The other residue orders are computed as in the main theorem over their own fields.

The scalar constructions require time polynomial in $M$ and in the input length, and the layer calculations require at most the product of the explicit orbit sizes times a polynomial. Since $M\le 2^D$, the resulting bound is $2^{O(D)}\operatorname{poly}(n,m,\tau)$. General matrix tuple isometry remains open when the obstruction dimensions are unrestricted.

## Ordered tuples with arbitrary bilinear forms

The same algorithm applies to an ordered tuple of unrestricted matrices after adjoining its transposes. Use the obstruction dimensions of the augmented tuple and replace the alternating subspace by the full self-adjoint subspace of its paired adjoint algebra. This gives deterministic $2^{O(D)}\operatorname{poly}(n,m,\tau)$ decision, search, and exact counting under that obstruction measure.

Indeed, common congruence preserves the transposed equations because $(C^tA_iC)^t=C^t A_i^t C$. The augmented paired adjoint algebra has the swap involution. Transpose the defining relation for the transposed entry to check that swapping the pair preserves each original relation. After reducing a left-right equivalence for the augmented tuples to $B_i=A_iT$, the equation on the transposed entry simultaneously gives $B_i^t=A_i^tT$. Transposing and comparing yields $T^t A_i=A_i T$ for every entry, so $(T,T)$ is self-adjoint as required. Conversely the norm equation gives the common congruence for the augmented tuples and therefore for the original tuples. The involution preserves the radical filtration, and both the affine defect and the trace term belong to the self-adjoint part of each grade. All previous quotient, orbit, and counting arguments then apply verbatim.
