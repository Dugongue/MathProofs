# Comparison of the extension-field norm problems

This proof uses the numbering in the preceding twenty-problem list and deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions over the common base field $F=\mathbb F_3$. Let $P_H$ denote membership of an ordinary transporter sign triple in $H\subseteq \mathbb F_3^{\times 3}$. The norm problems allow one common Frobenius exponent on a block. We write label and matrix actions in any of the equivalent dual conventions, since changing the sign convention by inversion preserves a sign in $\{1,-1\}$.

## A cubic lock with faithful determinant signs

We construct a local associative algebra over $E=\mathbb F_{27}$ from a concise base-field tensor pair, and compute the exact determinant characters of its maps. First replace the input by a concise balanced pair with even sides and the same determinant set, using [the padding proof](ScalarCentroidGlue.md). A common larger even square directed-path pencil with a non-Frobenius-invariant edge color has determinant-trivial stabilizer. Its side length can be chosen in any prescribed even residue class modulo 16, and its format distinguishes it from the earlier components. Adding this pad makes the common side length a positive multiple of 16 without changing the determinant set. Constant cases can be handled directly. Write the resulting tensor as a bilinear map $\mu:U\times V\to W$; it is concise, so neither input has a common kernel and the outputs span $W$.

For $d=\dim U$, use the truncated commutative $E$-algebra
$$
 B_U= E[t,u_1,\ldots,u_d]/
           (u_i^3-t^2u_i,\ \text{all monomials of degree greater than }3).
$$
Similarly define $B_V$ with variables $s,v_i$ and cutoff degree 8. Both have a homogeneous basis of monomials $t^a u_1^{b_1}\cdots u_d^{b_d}$ with each $0\le b_i\le 2$ and total degree at most the cutoff, and analogously for $B_V$. Indeed before truncation the monic relations give a free $E[t]$-module on the stated restricted powers, by adjoining the variables in order and dividing by their monic cubics. The relations are homogeneous, so truncation retains exactly the indicated degrees. This also gives an explicit polynomial-size multiplication table, since the cutoffs are constant.

Let $J_U,J_V$ be their positive-degree ideals. Define $A=E\oplus J$ with $J=J_U\oplus J_V\oplus W_E$ and unit the displayed scalar 1. The two original branch products are kept. The only cross product on $J$ takes the $U_E$-component of the first tangent, the $V_E$-component of the second tangent, and returns $\mu$ in $W_E$, where tangent means the quotient by the square of the corresponding branch ideal, and the components discard the tags $t,s$. All products with $W_E$ in $J$ are zero. This algebra is associative. In any triple product a cross product already formed is killed by the third radical element, and a within-branch product has zero tangent, so every mixed triple product vanishes in either parenthesization. The unit extends this to $A$. Its radical is $J$, its residue is $E$, and
$$
 J^2=J_U^2\oplus J_V^2\oplus W_E,\qquad
 J^i=J_U^i\oplus J_V^i\quad (i\ge 3).
$$
The span of the commutators in $J$ is exactly $W_E$, by conciseness of $\mu$ and commutativity within the branches.

For an algebra transporter its associated graded map preserves the following intrinsic structures. In $J/J^2$, the tangent of the low branch is the set of $x$ such that $x^3y=0$ in degree four of the graded algebra for all tangents $y$. Mixed products have disappeared by degree three and the low branch ends in degree three. For a nonzero high component of $x$, multiply its cube by the high tag $s$. The result is a nonzero linear combination of $s^4$ and $s^3v_i$, by characteristic three, the cubic relations and the monomial basis. The low branch is therefore recovered as the unital graded subalgebra generated by this tangent. The high branch is the graded quotient by the ideal generated by this tangent, which removes $W_E$ too because the mixed multiplication spans it. In either branch, its tag square line $Et^2$ is intrinsically the space of degree-two elements whose product with every degree-one element lies in the $E$-linear cube span in degree three. To check this, multiply any degree-two expression by $t$. The terms on $u_i u_j$, including squares, give distinct monomials $t u_i u_j$ outside the cube span. Only terms on $t^2$ and $t u_i$ remain. Multiplying by a variable $u_j$ eliminates all $t u_i$ terms by the same independence. Conversely $Et^2$ gives precisely the cube span under these multiplications. As degree two is a symmetric square with no relations, preservation of $Et^2$ forces the degree-one tag line $Et$ itself to be preserved.

Consider an $E$-linear isomorphism of the two algebras. Let $\lambda_U$ and $\lambda_V$ be its two tag scales. In branch degree one the map is respectively $\lambda_U H_U$ and $\lambda_V H_V$, where each $H$ is a base-field matrix fixing its tag. Indeed, apply the isomorphism to $u_i^3=t^2u_i$, including the identical tautological relation for $t$. In the degree-three basis $t^2(t,u_1,\ldots,u_d)$ this says that each tangent coefficient $z$ satisfies $z^3=\lambda_U^2 z$, hence $z/\lambda_U\in F$. The same applies to the high branch. The bracket of low tangents and the quotient by them is the original $\mu$ on discarding the tags, with values in the intrinsic $W_E$; any changes of representatives vanish in the bracket. Thus the induced quotient maps $h_U,h_V$ of the two $H$'s satisfy the input tensor equation with the map $(\lambda_U\lambda_V)^{-1}g_W$ on $W_E$, where $g_W$ is the algebra map there. The latter map has coefficients in $F$ because the outputs of base-field basis inputs span $W$, and the equation sends all of them to vectors in $W$. This gives an actual base-field transporter $(h_U,h_V,h_W)$. Conversely any such transporter extends using $H_U,H_V$ that fix the tags with no off-diagonal terms and $\lambda_U=\lambda_V=1$; the cubic relations hold after the base-field substitutions, and the bracket equation is precisely the original tensor equation.

For the cubic extension the norm of a base-field sign is that same sign. We compute the full norm character of $A$. In each branch the map fixing the tag and acting over $F$ has sign determined by its determinant character on the quotient by the tag: the shear subgroup and the elementary transvections there have order a power of 3 and so give sign one. A coordinate reflection $u_1\mapsto -u_1$ contributes the parity of the number of monomials having $b_1=1$. Because $16\mid d$ and the degree cutoff is at most 8, the graded dimension generating series
$$
 \frac{(1+z+z^2)^d}{1-z}
$$
has every coefficient through degree 8 odd. The reflection character in degree $j$ is the coefficient of $z^{j-1}$ in
$$
 \frac{(1+z+z^2)^{d-1}}{1-z}
       =\frac{1}{1+z^3}\pmod{(2,z^9)}.
$$
The coefficient is one precisely for $j=1,4,7$ in the relevant range. Therefore the full lower branch and full upper branch each read the original reflection character once. A uniform $\lambda$ on the tangent acts as $\lambda^j$ on degree $j$. The sum of the odd degrees' dimensions is even, since it is two odd terms at cutoff 3 and four at cutoff 8. So the norm of that uniform contribution is one on each branch. Also $\dim W$ is even, so the factor $\lambda_U\lambda_V$ contributes sign one there. The determinant on the full algebra is the product on the radical grades and then their invariant subspaces and quotients. We obtain
$$
 N_{E/F}(\det_E\phi)=\det_F h_U\det_F h_V\det_F h_W.
$$
The low full branch dimension is even, the high full branch dimension odd. Adding the two positive ideals, the unit and the even-dimensional $W_E$ makes $\dim_E A$ even.

## Multiplication tensors and exact one- and two-character transfers

The regular multiplication tensor of a unital associative algebra has the following property. For an isotope written $h(xy)=f(x)g(y)$, set $a=f(1)$, $b=g(1)$. Both are units since $h(x)=f(x)b=a g(x)$ and all three maps are bijective. Set $\phi(x)=a^{-1}f(x)=g(x)b^{-1}$; the equality for $xy$ makes $\phi$ an algebra isomorphism, and $h(x)=a\phi(x)b$. On a local algebra of residue $E$, a unit acts by its scalar residue on every radical grade by either left or right multiplication. If the full dimension is even, both unit multiplications have norm sign one. Thus in our regular multiplication tensor all three mode signs equal the displayed total sign of the base-field transporter. Its centroid is the center of the algebra, which is local with residue $E$. In fact the centroid equations evaluated on the unit make the three operators multiplication by the same central element, and the center intersects the radical in a nilpotent ideal with scalar quotient. This is a valid concise input to both norm candidates 15 and 18. All coefficients in the construction lie in $F$, so allowing common cubic Frobenius cannot introduce other transporters: remove its coordinate action, which is a symmetry of both tensors and has base-field determinant $+1$ on every mode. Thus base total orientation 3 reduces to both 15 and 18. Trace gives $15\le_m^p 1$, $18\le_m^p 3$. Together with the one-character equivalences in [the castling proof](CastlingAndConjunction.md), this establishes
$$
                   1\equiv_m^p 3\equiv_m^p 15\equiv_m^p 18 .
$$

The same algebra also reads two characters using restricted right multiplication. Define a subspace $Y\subseteq A$ spanned by the unit, all positive degrees of the low branch, high degrees $1,3,4$, and the high tag-square line $Es^2$ of degree 2. In the graded algebra this contains both tangent spaces, so the corresponding displayed homogeneous subspace generates $A$ as an algebra, using the mixed product to span $W_E$. Take the pencil $R_y:x\mapsto xy$ on $A$ with labels $y\in Y$. It is concise because $1\in Y$ gives an invertible slice and $R_y(1)=y$ makes the label map injective.

For any ordinary transporter $(\ell,f,h)$ of two such pencils, let $b=\ell(1)$. Since $R'_b f=h$, $b$ is a unit. Then
$$
 fR_y f^{-1}=R'_{\ell(y)b^{-1}}.
$$
The right multiplication operator algebra generated by $Y$ is the regular opposite algebra. The conjugation inclusion into the target regular opposite algebra is equality by dimensions. It defines an algebra isomorphism $\phi$ with $f(x)=c\phi(x)$, where $c=f(1)$ must be a unit, and $h(x)=c\phi(x)b$, $\ell(y)=\phi(y)b$. The two regular matrix modes thus read the total sign just computed. In the label filtration by radical powers the action of right multiplication by $b$ is scalar residue on every grade. Its determinant has norm sign one since $\dim_E Y$ is even: the unit plus the low ideal contribute even dimension, and the three high grades and the tag-square line are four odd contributions. On high grades $1,3,4$ the respective reflection exponents are $1,0,1$; the tag-square contributes none. The uniform tag scaling also cancels, with the only odd-degree contributions in grades 1 and 3. Hence the label norm is exactly $\det_F h_U$, with no high or output character. Here the induced map of each high label grade has that determinant because it is the prescribed high branch component in the associated graded; any low-branch mixing in grades shared with the low label part gives a block triangular action, and the tag-square line in the high quotient is preserved. Positive base-field transporters extend to these pencils with $b=c=1$, preserving $Y$ in the chosen homogeneous coordinates.

Apply [the scalar-centroid marker embedding](ScalarCentroidGlue.md) to make the last raw pencil satisfy the norm input condition. Use even-length Jordan marker blocks whose dimensions exceed the whole old output dimension. The three distinct hyperplanes, anchor, and Jordan block entries all lie in $F$, and the same proof works over $E$, whose even norm character is all that is used: the marker's label dimension and matrix block lengths are even and its determinants are squares. Every map on the evaluation glue contributes square norm as well. As the coefficients stay in $F$, common cubic Frobenius again has determinant $+1$ and can be removed. Orient the label and one matrix mode. This imposes exactly $\det_F h_U=1$ and total sign one, which is a permutation of candidate 5. Trace gives the converse upper reduction $16\le_m^p 4$, so with the two-line equivalence this proves
$$
                        4\equiv_m^p 5\equiv_m^p 16 .
$$

## A direct comparison of the cubic norm profiles

There is also a direct reduction $16\le_m^p 17$ without first returning to the base field. Treat the first constrained mode as output $U$, the other constrained mode as input $V$, and the free mode as label $W$. If an $E$-dimension of a constrained mode is odd, its sign can be individually adjusted by the central scalar group independently of the other constrained mode. Reduce to the one-mode problem or to ordinary semilinear TI in these cases; ordinary semilinear TI here reduces to ordinary base-field TI by trace, and the previous lower reduction from base-field TI to 17 applies. For the remaining case $\dim_E U$ is even. Apply the same marker glue but with label dimension three, and choose the four determinant factors $x,y,z,x+y+z$ with distinct even Jordan lengths. Use an anchor $(1,1,-1)$ and nilpotent term $x-y$. At any nonzero marker label at least one coordinate factor is nonzero, so taking all Jordan lengths larger than $\dim_E U$ recognizes the old label space by the same rank cut. The marker label action is scalar and its norm sign $c$ is arbitrary since its dimension is odd; its two matrix-mode norm signs are one. The evaluation module contributes $d_U^3 c^{\dim_E U}$ in norm to the input mode. In the glued problem the signs in output, input and label order are thus $(d_U,d_Vd_U,d_Wc)$. A witness with the first two old signs one extends with $c=d_W$; a determinant-one witness of the glued pair projects to the two required signs. The scalar-centroid proof is unchanged. The chosen marker is defined over $F$, so semilinear lifts are still exact.

Finally $17\le_m^p20$ and $19\le_m^p20$ follow by adding a common scalar block of the other extension degree with even dimensions and trivial norm-sign subgroup. For the cubic-to-mixed or quadratic-to-mixed comparison one can use the even-label, even-length Jordan marker itself. Its centroid has scalar residue over its coefficient field (the scalar-label proof excludes every geometric centroid idempotent), so this is a valid block. Both degrees are intrinsic and cannot be exchanged, and all signs of the added block are one. Its dimensions are even so that the quadratic Frobenius also has sign one. This preserves the original three sign constraints exactly.
