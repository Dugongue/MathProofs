# Tight degree-parameter orbit bounds for matching coefficients

For \(1\le k\le n\), let \(P_{n,k}(X)\) be the sum of all principal \(k\)-by-\(k\) permanents. Equivalently it is the coefficient of \(t^{n-k}\) in \(\operatorname{per}(tI+X)\). Let \(M_{n,k}(X)\) be the sum of all \(k\)-by-\(k\) permanents with independent row and column subsets, the weighted \(k\)-matching polynomial of the bipartite graph. Over any field of characteristic different from two, the optimal maximum gate orbit in square-symmetric arithmetic circuits is
\[
2^{\Theta\left(k\log_2(1+n/k)\right)}
\]
for each family, uniformly for \(1\le k\le n\) as \(n\) grows. This lower bound holds even for circuits that need only agree on zero-one inputs. The upper circuits also have this size up to constants in the exponent.

For the upper bound sum over the subsets defining the polynomial. Use Ryser's inclusion-exclusion expression on each selected square matrix. Its subsets and row sums relabel naturally, so it makes a square-symmetric circuit. The principal case uses at most \(\binom nk 2^k\operatorname{poly}(n)\) gates and wires, and the all-minors case at most \(\binom nk^2 2^k\operatorname{poly}(n)\). The binomial upper bound and \(k\log(1+n/k)\ge\log(1+n)\) give the displayed order. The formulas are integral.

For the lower bound, put the two zero-one permanent witnesses from #33 on the same \(k\) active labels and make all other entries zero. Identity padding within the active block allows every sufficiently large \(k\). A contributing \(k\)-minor must use exactly those active labels on each side. Thus either target polynomial restricts to the ordinary permanent of the active block. The two inputs are indistinguishable with \(\Omega(k)\) pebbles: treat active and padding labels as separate classes and play the #33 strategy on the active part. [Directed witnesses from #33](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightFourth/complexity.md#square-symmetric-permanent-lower-bounds-in-all-odd-characteristics)

Choose \(r=\lfloor c k\rfloor\) for a sufficiently small absolute positive \(c\), so that \(2r\) is within that winning pebble bound and \(r\le n/4\). If the orbit were below \(\binom nr\), the small-index support theorem would give supports of size less than \(r\) in the rigid circuit. Arithmetic circuits on these finite Boolean inputs can be converted to symmetric Boolean threshold circuits without enlarging orbit size, as in Dawar and Wilsenach. Take acceptance to be one of the two distinct output values. The threshold support theorem and its counting argument prevent the circuit from distinguishing the padded pair. Hence the orbit is at least \(\binom nr\), which is \(2^{\Omega(k\log(1+n/k))}\). [Dawar–Wilsenach, Theorems 5.1 and 6.2–6.4](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v021a014/v021a014.pdf)

For bounded \(k\) the lower bound is already forced by essential variable orbits. In the principal case \(P_{n,1}\) depends on each diagonal input. For \(k\ge2\), take a permutation matrix on a \(k\)-subset containing the two endpoints of an off-diagonal entry, with a transposition on those endpoints and identity on the other chosen labels. Switching off that entry changes the value from one to zero. The same test applies to \(M_{n,k}\), and \(M_{n,1}\) depends on every entry. The orbit of an essential diagonal variable has size at least \(n\), and that of an off-diagonal variable at least \(n(n-1)\). This covers the small \(k\) range and completes the uniform estimate.
