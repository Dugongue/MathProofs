# Exponential monomial-equivariant determinantal complexity

On the Boolean cube in characteristic different from two, a determinantal representation of the permanent still requires order \(2^{\Omega(B)}\) if it is only equivariant under independent alternating symmetries on \(B\) prescribed blocks of size five. There are two allowed forms of equivariance.

- For each \(g\in A_5^B\) there exists a permutation matrix \(P_g\) with \(L(gX)=P_g L(X)P_g^{-1}\), where \(L\) is an affine matrix pencil. The lifts can be chosen individually.
- The same identity holds with an actual monomial linear representation \(\rho\) of \(A_5^B\) in place of \(P_g\). This allows an arbitrary nonzero rescaling on each basis line.

The bounds hold over arbitrary fields of the stated characteristics. They also apply to border representations in either class, where a coefficient of the determinant of an equivariant Laurent-series pencil gives the permanent. The proofs show equality of the entire determinant at two witnesses for every pencil below the bound, so extracting a specified coefficient cannot separate them.

The first case extends Dawar and Wilsenach's permutation-equivariant formulation in §7.4 to odd characteristic and to the smaller group. Landsberg and Ressayre study other symmetry conditions, including torus actions. [Dawar–Wilsenach, §7.4](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v021a014/v021a014.pdf) · [Landsberg–Ressayre](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.05788)

## Permutation lifts

Replace each distinct scalar coefficient of the affine pencil by a separate indeterminate, using a characteristic-zero rational function field. For each allowed permutation lift, the old equivariance is equality of individual coefficients, so it still holds for the lifted pencil. The determinant of this pencil, evaluated at a zero-one matrix, is a polynomial with integer coefficients in the indeterminates. Equality of its lifted values implies equality of the original values by specializing those indeterminates.

Compose the lifted pencil with the characteristic-zero square-symmetric determinant circuit of Dawar and Wilsenach. Its size is polynomial in the output order \(D\). Build the affine inputs to the determinant circuit in unordered sums, with one slot per input entry. For each \(g\), any chosen permutation lift extends the action to the composed circuit. On the \(O(D^2)\) affine forms, occurrences are labelled by their output coordinate pair and their input coordinate pair, if any. Thus for a fixed occurrence there are at most \(25D^2\) possible images under all the chosen lifts and group actions, since each input coordinate pair has at most \(5^2\) images even when the total input dimension greatly exceeds \(B\). If repeated occurrences or identical affine inputs are present, use the structurally collapsed circuit with the induced action as in the block proof. Its maximum \(G\)-orbit is polynomial in \(D\). By the gate-evaluation theorem in the block proof, if \(D=2^{o(B)}\) this circuit gives equal lifted determinants at the two witnesses. Quantitatively the same argument gives \(D\ge2^{c' B}\) for some absolute \(c'>0\) whenever the determinants differ. [The block witness and gate theorem](block-alternating.md)

## Monomial lifts

Let \(D\) be the number of basis lines, and set \(s=\lfloor\log_2 D\rfloor\). For every orbit of lines choose one representative with a nonzero vector on it. Its stabilizer contains the product of coordinate groups outside a set of at most \(s\) blocks by the group-index lemma. This product acts trivially on the representative vector because it is perfect and its action on the line is a scalar character. Hence a choice of group elements on just those blocks, extended arbitrarily by identity elsewhere, determines a transported vector. Call such a choice a tag. The tags for a line orbit cover its lines with a constant fiber size \(h\), since transporting by any group element permutes the tag assignments and acts transitively on the line orbit.

For a fixed matrix input \(X\) and two tags \(a,b\), let \(\lambda_X(a,b)\) be the coefficient from the line of \(b\) to the line of \(a\) in \(L(X)\), in the bases given by their transported vectors. This coefficient is well-defined even when the tags are on the same line. When a winning position matches all blocks supporting \(a,b\), it sends them to tags \(a',b'\) and
\[
\lambda_X(a,b)=\lambda_Y(a',b').
\]
To prove this, use the equivariance to put both tag pairs in the same coordinates by extending the winning partial matching. The affine functional for this coefficient is invariant under the group fixing the two supports. Its weights on input entries are therefore constant on the orbits of that subgroup. Each such orbit uses a fixed row block and a fixed column block, or fixed individual labels outside the blocks. Extend the winning position to those at most two additional blocks. This supplies a bijection of entries in that orbit with matching Boolean values. The weighted contributions agree in the field. Constants are unchanged. This argument requires at most \(2s+2\) held blocks.

Lift each nonzero field value \(u\) to its basis element \([u]\) in the commutative group ring \(\mathbb Z[K^\times]\), and lift zero to zero. This lift is multiplicative and the group ring is torsion-free as an additive group. Fix a length \(r\ge1\) and an ordered itinerary of line orbits. Sum products of lifted coefficients around closed walks of \(r\) tags of those types. The sums for \(X,Y\) agree. Indeed, choose a winning position matching the first tag, keep it and the current tag, and extend to the next tag, using at most \(3s+2\) blocks including the two for checking a coefficient. The extension gives a bijection of all assignments for that next tag by composition on its support blocks. One may forget the previous tag once the next one is chosen, always retaining the first so that the last coefficient agrees. The resulting bijection of walks preserves every coefficient and thus every lifted product.

Rescaling a separate vector at each occurrence of a line does not affect the lifted weight of a closed walk because the scalar ratios telescope. Given the fixed orbit itinerary, every underlying walk of lines is counted exactly the product of its orbit fiber sizes \(h\) times, even if the walk visits a line repeatedly. Cancel this positive integer in the torsion-free group ring. Summing over itineraries now gives equality of all power traces for the two matrices whose entries are the multiplicative lifts of the entries in the original basis. Newton's identities give equality of their characteristic coefficients by induction, since cancellation by positive integers is valid in that ring. Applying the ring map \([u]\mapsto u\) to the determinant equality proves \(\det L(X)=\det L(Y)\).

When \(3s+2\le k=\Omega(B)\), the block strategy supplies all the required winning positions. The integer permanent values of the witnesses differ by a power of two. The same proof is valid over a Laurent-series coefficient field and forces every determinant coefficient to agree. This gives the stated lower bounds.
