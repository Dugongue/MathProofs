# Exponential permanent lower bound under local alternating symmetries

Let \(B\) disjoint labelled blocks of size five be chosen in the index set of a square matrix. Let \(G=A_5^B\) act by independent even permutations inside those blocks, simultaneously on row and column labels, and fix all other labels. Every \(G\)-symmetric arithmetic circuit agreeing with the permanent on all zero-one inputs in characteristic different from two has maximum gate orbit \(2^{\Omega(B)}\). The hidden constant is absolute. In particular a linear number of five-point blocks suffices, with no permutation between blocks. The result also applies to circuits whose gates are arbitrary multiset functions of their child values, provided all required values are defined on the witnesses.

## A support bound for products of simple groups

For \(H\le S^B\) with \(S=A_5\), let \(t\) be the number of individual coordinate factors not contained in \(H\). Then \([S^B:H]\ge2^t\). Split the coordinates into \(I\), where the projection of \(H\) is all of \(S\), and \(J\), where it is proper. The projection to \(I\) is subdirect. Every subdirect subgroup of \(S^I\) is a product of diagonal copies of \(S\), one per block of a partition of \(I\), with coordinate automorphisms allowed. To see this, apply Goursat's lemma after adding one coordinate to the product. The new copy is free or matched to a quotient onto \(S\). Any surjection from a product of copies of \(S\) onto \(S\) uses exactly one factor, since the images are commuting normal subgroups of the nonabelian simple group \(S\).

The projection to \(J\) has no \(S\) composition factor. Indeed, successive kernels of coordinate projections have factors that embed in proper subgroups of \(S\). Goursat's lemma therefore splits \(H\) across \(I\) and \(J\), since every nontrivial quotient of the subdirect product on \(I\) has an \(S\) composition factor. Each \(J\)-coordinate costs an index factor of at least two. Each diagonal block of length \(\ell\ge2\) costs \(60^{\ell-1}\ge2^\ell\) and accounts for exactly \(\ell\) additional missing coordinate factors. Multiplying proves the claim. This gives support on at most \(\log_2 O\) blocks for an object with orbit size \(O\): permutations on all other blocks fix that object.

## The witnesses with five-point blocks

Use the two directed matrices \(A_0,A_1\) from #33. Their common base is a connected cubic bipartite expander on \(v=6\cdot2^j\) vertices, with \(3v/2\) edges. At each vertex there are four inner parity labels and a balance vertex. At each edge there are two bit labels. The two directions use, respectively, all incidences and a deletion of entire incidence fibers selected from a fixed oriented 2-factor. The integer permanent gap is \(\pm2^{4v+1}\). The matchings and gap are proved in #33. [Earlier directed construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowEightFourth/complexity.md#square-symmetric-permanent-lower-bounds-in-all-odd-characteristics)

Place the four inner labels and the balance label of a base vertex in one block. Place the two bit labels of a base edge in another block, with three new isolated labels carrying only diagonal ones. Use isolated identity padding on all remaining labels. For large \(B\), take the largest \(v\) with \(5v/2\le B\). Then \(v=\Theta(B)\) and the gap is unchanged.

A partial matching now fixes whole blocks. An occupied vertex block blocks one base vertex, while an occupied edge block blocks its two endpoints. With at most \(k=\lfloor c v\rfloor\) occupied blocks for a sufficiently small absolute \(c>0\), the unblocked base has a component of size greater than \(v/2\). For if it did not, some union of its components would have size between \(v/4\) and \(v/2\), and expansion would force more edges into the blocked vertices than their total degree. The large components are nested when blockers increase, since any two such components intersect.

Maintain the edge-bit translation from #33 whose single parity defect lies in that large component. Once a new block is requested, choose a path in the old large component from the old defect to a vertex of the new large component. Flipping the bits on its edges moves the defect without changing the previously matched blocks. The resulting map of the requested block is an even permutation of its five labels. For vertex blocks, first identify the even and odd parity fibers by a fixed translation at each vertex. After this identification the permitted translations on the four inner labels are the identity and double transpositions, and the balance label is fixed. For edge blocks, compensate a swap of the two bit labels by a swap of two of the three dummy labels. The correction preserves the identity block and is unchanged as long as that block remains occupied. The partial map preserves all observed matrix entries, including the asymmetric incidence deletions, which depend only on the fixed base labels. One may forget any occupied blocks and extend again by the same argument. We call every position reached by this strategy a winning position.

## Gate evaluation from whole-block supports

The symmetry definition only requires each input permutation to extend to a circuit automorphism. To get an actual action, identify gates with the same recursive syntactic description consisting of their gate label and the multiset of their children's descriptions, starting from the labelled inputs. The quotient retains multiplicity on wires. Its gate action is unique by induction, and its maximum gate orbit under \(G\) is at most the original maximum orbit. The output is fixed.

Suppose every quotient gate is fixed by permutations outside some set of at most \(s\) blocks and \(2s\le k\). Induct on the gate height. If a winning position matches the supporting blocks of a gate \(g\), evaluate \(g\) on the first matrix and its translate on the second. The translate is well-defined because any extension of that partial permutation differs by a permutation fixing \(g\). For an input variable this assertion follows by adding its row and column blocks when needed. More precisely, use the actual coordinate-block support of input gates, of size at most two, and replace \(s\) by \(\max(s,2)\). Constants are immediate.

For an internal gate, split its child multiset into orbits under the subgroup fixing the parent support. For one child representative \(h\), extend the winning position to the union of the two supports. The new block maps give a bijection from all assignments of even permutations on the extra child-support blocks to their corresponding assignments on the other side, by composition in each block. These assignments sample the child orbit with constant fiber size because the subgroup action is transitive and because the child is fixed outside its support. Wire multiplicities are constant along each orbit. The winning position on the union, restricted to each translated child, therefore pairs equal child values with their multiplicities. Induction and multiset evaluation give equal values at the parent. At the output the support can be empty.

A circuit with maximum orbit below \(2^{k/2}\) would have supports of size below \(k/2\) by the group bound, and its two outputs would coincide. Their prescribed values differ in every characteristic other than two. This proves the exponential bound. The group-support argument is adapted to independent constant-size factors, and the witness labeling and game use only this smaller group. [Context for support games](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.10986#S4)

## Sharpness for the specified group action

Ryser's formula gives a matching upper bound on the maximum orbit under the specified group action. A subset of the index set has at most \(\binom5j\le10\) possible images inside each block, with \(j\) the subset size there. Thus its orbit has size at most \(10^B\). The ordinary Ryser gates are indexed by a subset and at most two additional labels, giving orbit size at most \(25\cdot10^B\). This bounds the group orbit independently of the number of fixed labels, though the total Ryser circuit can be larger.
