# Affine equivalence in ordinary multiplication tensors

The cubic construction uses one intrinsic variable to retain a scalar. Adding one generator with a prescribed annihilator also retains the affine chart. The resulting unmarked algebra handles affine substitutions over arbitrary commutative rings, and the same construction preserves directional degeneration of formal polynomials.

## Theorem

Fix $n\ge1$ and $D\ge4$. Let $R$ be a commutative unital ring, $V$ free with basis $x_1,\ldots,x_n,t$, and set
\[
U=V\oplus Rz,\qquad L=R(t-z),\qquad
W_D=\operatorname{Sym}^D V\oplus Rz^D.
\]
For any submodule $K\subset W_D$ with free quotient, there is a finite free unital commutative associative $R$-algebra $B_K$. The following are equivalent for two inputs:

1. Their unmarked $R$-algebras are isomorphic.
2. Their ordinary multiplication tensors are isomorphic.
3. Some $h\in\operatorname{GL}(V)$ with $h(t)=t$ takes $K$ to $K'$ by $\operatorname{Sym}^D h\oplus1$.

Every algebra or tensor transporter gives an explicit $h$. These constructions commute with scalar extension whenever the displayed free quotient hypothesis holds at the starting ring, so they apply to nilpotent base changes as well. On dense fixed-degree input with quotient bases given, the multiplication tables have polynomial size.

For a formal polynomial $f$ of degree at most $D$, let $F$ be its degree-$D$ homogenization and use $K=R(F-z^D)$. The equivalence above becomes **exact affine equivalence of $f$**, with no scalar ambiguity or root extraction. The theorem also treats bounded-degree equation spaces and polynomial maps under independent affine transformations on inputs and outputs. Over an algebraically closed field it preserves and reflects directional affine degeneration of single polynomials under ordinary three-tensor degeneration.

## Construction

Start with
\[
A_K=\operatorname{Sym}(U)/(J^{D+1},\,zV,\,K),
\]
where $J$ is the positive ideal and the last relations are in degree $D$. The pieces in degrees $1\le i<D$ are $\operatorname{Sym}^i V\oplus Rz^i$. The last is $W_D/K$. Attach a degree-one element $w$ and the degree-two module
\[
M=(U/L)\oplus R e.
\]
Define $w u=u\bmod L$ in $M$ for $u\in U$, set $w^2=e$, and set $w$ times each base element of degree at least two to zero. All of $M$ kills all positive-degree elements. The unit acts on the attachment by the identity. These products are associative: a product of three positive-degree elements that includes $w$ or $M$ vanishes under both bracketings. Products entirely in the base are those of $A_K$.

The positive-degree ideal $I$ is nilpotent, is generated by $U,w$, and has powers equal to the sums of the corresponding and higher degrees. The module $M$ is free on the classes of the $V$ basis and on $e$, since $t$ and $z$ are identified in $U/L$. By the hypothesis on the top quotient the full module is free. In the single-polynomial case, eliminate $z^D$ using its unit coefficient. Entries of the table are then zero, one, or coefficients of the homogenized polynomial. In particular this case gives a polynomial bit-time construction over $\mathbb Z$ for fixed $D$.

## Recovering the chart over a field

Over a field the positive ideal is the unique maximal ideal, so every unmarked isomorphism induces a graded one. In the graded algebra the degree-two elements killing degree one are exactly $M$. A nonzero degree-two symmetric polynomial persists when multiplied by a fixed basis variable, and the $z^2$ direction persists when multiplied by $z$. Their products are in independent directions in degree three, which is unaffected by the top relations since $D\ge4$.

Reduce the bilinear product from degree one to degree two modulo $M$. The kernel of multiplication against all degree-one elements is exactly $Rw$. The product of $w$ with degree one in the original degree-two space has kernel exactly $L$, since $wu$ tests the class of $u$ modulo $L$ and $w^2=e$ is independent of that quotient. Modulo $w$ in degree one, the reduced multiplication is the direct sum of ordinary symmetric multiplication of $V$ and the product on $Rz$. For a nonzero vector $v+bz$, its contraction rank is $\dim V$ if $v\ne0$, plus one if $b\ne0$. Since $\dim V\ge2$, the rank-one line is exactly $Rz$, and its annihilator for this reduced multiplication is $V$.

## Nilpotent bases and exact transporter extraction

Let $\phi:B_K\to B_{K'}$ be an unmarked algebra isomorphism over $R$. Write $\phi(v)=c_v+A_v+\cdots$ for the constant, degree-one, and higher terms of a degree-one generator. Reduction at every maximal ideal and the field argument imply that the matrix of all $A_v$ is invertible over $R$: its determinant lies in no maximal ideal. The degree-one part of $\phi(zx)=0$ for each basis element $x\in V$ is
\[
c_z A_x+c_x A_z=0.
\]
Independence of these columns gives $c_x=c_z=0$. The relation $w(t-z)=0$ gives $c_w(A_t-A_z)=0$ and hence $c_w=0$, since the difference is part of some free basis. The inverse isomorphism obeys the same calculation. Thus the positive ideal and all its powers are preserved over $R$ itself.

The product tests recovering $M,Rw,L$ use just monomial independence and unit coefficients, so those three recoveries hold in the graded algebra over $R$ too. In the quotient by $Rw$, write the degree-one maps as
\[
x\longmapsto Sx+\ell(x)z,\qquad z\longmapsto v+az.
\]
The field tests modulo maximal ideals make $S$ invertible and $a$ a unit. In reduced degree-two multiplication the mixed relation gives $(Sx)v=0$ in $\operatorname{Sym}^2 V$ and $a\ell(x)=0$. Multiplication by a basis element of a free symmetric algebra is injective even over $R$. Since the $Sx$ for the basis elements form a basis, $v=0$ and $\ell=0$.

Preserving the line $L$ now gives $St=at$. There is no residual degree-one shear from $U$ into $Rw$. Indeed, write the actual graded images as $x\mapsto Sx+b_xw$, $z\mapsto az+b_zw$. In the $U/L$ component of the mixed equation, identifying the class of $z$ with $t$ gives
\[
b_z Sx+a b_x t=0.
\]
Take any basis variable $x_i$. The vectors $Sx_i$ and $t$ belong to a basis, because $St=at$ and $a$ is a unit. This gives $b_z=0$ and then $b_x=0$ for every basis element, including $t$. The map on $w$ is multiplication by a separate unit.

The kernel of the degree-$D$ multiplication from $W_D$ is precisely $K$. Therefore $(\operatorname{Sym}^D S,a^D)$ takes $K$ to $K'$. Since a common unit factor preserves a submodule, this is precisely the assertion for $h=a^{-1}S$ fixing $t$. Conversely such an $h$ extends by fixing $z,w$ and using the induced map on $M$. More generally all graded isomorphisms are obtained from $h$ and two independent units, the common scalar $a$ on $V,z$ after applying $h$, and the scalar on $w$. The associated-graded map on algebra automorphism groups thus splits onto the affine stabilizer times two copies of the unit group, with the kernel consisting of maps inducing the identity on the grading. The split maps and extraction are given by the same formulas after any base extension.

For multiplication tensors, an isotopy has $H(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$. Evaluating at the two units makes $P(1)$ and $Q(1)$ units, because multiplication by either is bijective. Dividing the two input maps by those units and the output map by their product yields a common unital algebra map. Conversely any algebra isomorphism gives an isotopy. This proves the ordinary tensor assertion over the same base ring.

## Polynomial maps and equations

The linear map on homogenized variables with $h(t)=t$ sends each $x_i$ to a linear combination of the $x_j$ plus a multiple of $t$. Its invertibility is exactly invertibility of the linear part of the affine substitution. Homogenization $x^\alpha\mapsto t^{D-|\alpha|}x^\alpha$ is equivariant for this action and is inverted by $t=1$. For a single polynomial, preservation of the relation submodule $R(F-z^D)$ says exactly $hF=F'$, by comparing the coefficient of $z^D$. The field of coefficients and the ring of solutions for the affine map are the original base.

For instance, exchanging $x$ and $t$ exchanges the ordinary degree-four forms $x^3t$ and $xt^3$. The encoded multiplication tensors of $x^3$ and $x$ are inequivalent over every field, since invertible affine substitution preserves polynomial degree.

For a tuple $(f_1,\ldots,f_m)$ homogenized to $(F_1,\ldots,F_m)$, use the top submodule
\[
K=\operatorname{span}(t^D,F_1,\ldots,F_m)\subset\operatorname{Sym}^D V\subset W_D.
\]
Equality of these submodules after $h$ is equivalent over a field to affine left–right equivalence of the polynomial maps, with source and target dimensions fixed. In fact the images of the two lists in $\operatorname{Sym}^D V/Ft^D$ have the same span exactly when their two surjections from $F^m$ onto that span differ by an invertible map of $F^m$. Choose complements to the two kernels and match the corresponding lifts of a common basis. The remaining discrepancies are multiples of $t^D$ and give the target translation. Conversely any invertible target affine change preserves this submodule. This includes dependent coordinate lists and constant maps. Over a general ring the same conclusion holds for tuples for which the displayed list with $t^D$ is a basis of a direct summand with free quotient. There the map between the quotient lists gives the invertible target matrix directly.

Over every effectively represented field the affine left–right problem for formal polynomial maps of bounded degree at least two is TI-complete. For hardness it already suffices to use homogeneous quadratic coordinate lists. Given a three-tensor, take independent frontal slices after eliminating any dependence, and use the two-block symmetric matrix-space construction from Grochow–Qiao IV, Section 2. Its data are the off-diagonal blocks $D_i,D_i^{\mathsf T}$ and its anchor uses identity pairs between distinct blocks. Replace each such symmetric slice matrix by the quadratic polynomial with the entry of each unordered off-diagonal pair used once. The forward congruence for that construction is block diagonal. Its matrix equation is exactly coefficient equality of those polynomials, because their terms occur only between distinct blocks. In the reverse direction the bilinear polarization $q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)$ recovers the slice matrices in every characteristic, so quadratic equivalence implies the original tensor equivalence through the published matrix-space reduction. For homogeneous quadratic lists, the leading terms of any affine left–right comparison give a linear left–right comparison, and linear maps also give affine equivalences with zero translations. This proves hardness, including in characteristic two. The unmarked construction above gives membership. [Grochow–Qiao IV, Theorem 2.1 and Section 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317)

For equation subspaces use their homogenized subspace itself as $K$, with no need to adjoin $t^D$. In particular suppose the input for an affine scheme is a basis of the entire degree-at-most-$D$ part of its defining ideal, and that this part generates the ideal. Preservation of this subspace is equivalent to ambient affine equivalence of the ideals, since affine changes preserve the degree filtration. This conclusion uses the degree-truncated ideal as input.

## Exact preservation of affine specialization

Let the base be an algebraically closed field $k$ and fix $n,D$ for all inputs. Then $g$ lies in the Zariski closure of the affine orbit of $f$ if and only if the multiplication tensor of $B_{R(G-z^D)}$ lies in the closure of the full ordinary three-tensor orbit of the multiplication tensor of $B_{R(F-z^D)}$ (with $R=k$). Here closure of polynomials is taken in the finite-dimensional space of polynomials of degree at most $D$.

In the displayed graph bases the law depends polynomially on the input coefficients. The lift of the group fixing $t$ fixes $w,z$ and acts on the quotient $U/L$, so it gives an equivariant ordinary change of algebra basis. This proves preservation of degeneration.

For reflection first reduce an ordinary multiplication-tensor degeneration to a law degeneration near the endpoint. At $\mu_g$ take $u_0,v_0$ to be its two input units. The maps $D_\beta=\beta(-,v_0)$ and $E_\beta=\beta(u_0,-)$ are invertible on an open neighborhood of $\mu_g$. Replace a tensor in this neighborhood by the law
\[
m_\beta(x,y)=\beta(D_\beta^{-1}x,E_\beta^{-1}y).
\]
At $\mu_g$ this is the original product. On any ordinary tensor translate of $\mu_f$ in the neighborhood, the two invertible slices correspond to multiplication by units of $B_f$. Dividing by those units makes $m_\beta$ algebra-isomorphic to $B_f$. Since this replacement is regular on the neighborhood, the endpoint law lies in the algebra-law orbit closure.

Now choose a DVR degeneration of laws with generic algebra isomorphic to $B_f$ and special algebra $B_g$. One obtains such a family by taking the graph closure of the change-of-basis map in a projective compactification of the matrix coordinate space, cutting a curve through the required boundary point, and normalizing. The generic unit extends because the special linear equation for the unit has a full-column-rank minor. The generic augmentation extends because its value on any element is an eigenvalue of that element's integral multiplication matrix, and a DVR is integrally closed. At the special point it must be the unique augmentation. The augmentation ideal and its powers have the same ranks in the two fibers, so the unit minors (or Smith form) give free direct summands and formation of the associated graded commutes with both fibers.

Perform the multiplication kernel tests above on this graded family over the DVR. The degree-two annihilator $M$, the line $Rw$ obtained from the reduced product, and its annihilator $L$ all have the exact same dimensions in the two fibers. Each therefore lifts as a free direct summand and specializes correctly by the same unit-minor argument. In the quotient by the line $Rw$, take a primitive generator $z_R$ of the saturated generic rank-one line. Its rank-two minors vanish identically, and its special image is nonzero, so it specializes to the required rank-one line. Its contraction kernel gives the free complement $V_R$ using the unit minor for rank one. The projection of $L$ to the $z_R$ line is a unit at the special point. Normalizing its generator therefore writes the line as $R(t_R-z_R)$ with $t_R$ primitive in $V_R$. Choose a basis of $V_R$ including $t_R$.

Degree-$D$ products of the lifts from degree one modulo $Rw$ are well-defined. Every triple product involving $w$ vanishes on the generic fiber and therefore over the DVR, since the graded pieces are free. The degree-$D$ multiplication map from $\operatorname{Sym}^D V_R$ is an isomorphism because its special fiber is an isomorphism between free modules of the same rank. Pull $z_R^D$ back to a form in that symmetric power and dehomogenize with $t_R=1$. This gives a family of formal polynomials over the DVR. On each fiber the constructions recover that fiber's original polynomial up to the affine action. Indeed a rescaling of $z$ and $t$ by the same unit is absorbed by the same degree-$D$ common scalar, as in the transporter proof. The generic polynomial lies in the orbit closure of $f$, hence the special polynomial does too. The closure is stable under the affine group, so it contains $g$.

## Literature and scope

Affine polynomial equivalence appears in Kayal's work and Grochow's Lie-algebra approach to special affine equivalence problems. Agrawal–Saxena use a truncated algebra for homogeneous forms, and Grochow–Qiao study ordinary TI and quadratic lists. The new ingredient here is the intrinsic annihilator of $w$, which binds the homogenizing coordinate to the scalar-retaining direction. It gives a finite free unmarked algebra realization, a split chart-preserving transporter description after arbitrary base change, and an exact directional degeneration statement. The proof concerns formal polynomial identities even over finite fields. Publication priority remains unverified. It checks every basis-product equation for the translation $x\mapsto x+2$ over $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$.
