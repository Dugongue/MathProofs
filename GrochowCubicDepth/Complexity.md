# Exact algorithms for formal cubics in characteristic three

**Theorem.** Let \(q\) be a power of \(3\). Given two formal cubics in \(n\) variables over \(\mathbb F_q\), one can decide linear equivalence, produce a transporter when it exists, and count all transporters in time
\[
       q^{\widetilde O(c^{3/2})}\operatorname{poly}(n,\log q),
\]
where \(c\) is the larger contraction rank of their polarizations. The same bound holds for explicit automorphism generators when \(c>0\). When \(c=0\), one can return generators with an additional \(q\operatorname{poly}(n,\log q)\) bound, while decision, one witness, and counting take polynomial time in \(n,\log q\). Equivalently, these tasks reduce to \(q^{O(c)}\operatorname{poly}(n,\log q)\) ordinary tensor-isomorphism decision calls on tensors of side lengths \(O(c)\), plus \(q^{O(c)}\operatorname{poly}(n,\log q)\) work. If Graph Isomorphism is in \(\mathsf P\), the time bound becomes \(q^{O(c)}\operatorname{poly}(n,\log q)\).

The result extends the fast finite-field trilinear algorithms to all formal cubics in characteristic three. The prior compact contraction algebra directly handles the case where the cubic is nonzero on its polarization radical. The algorithm below also handles a zero radical restriction, including the case of a concise polarization with no radical at all.

## 1. Search and counting for trilinear isometries

Use column vectors and call \(S\) a transporter from \(T\) to \(T'\) if \(T'(Sx,Sy,Sz)=T(x,y,z)\). Equivalently, under the covariant pushforward \(S\cdot T=T\circ(S^{-1},S^{-1},S^{-1})\), the condition is \(S\cdot T=T'\). First we construct a line-constrained decision oracle. Its inputs also specify two ordered lists of independent projective lines, and it asks for a transporter carrying each source line to the corresponding target line.

Separate basis changes in the two inputs reduce both line lists to the first \(k\) coordinate lines. Regard a trilinear form as a 3-tensor on three copies of its covector space. Its transporter uses the same contragredient matrix on all three copies. In those coordinates, carrying an argument coordinate line to itself means that the covector transporter preserves the corresponding coordinate hyperplane. Consequently, one may instead append an auxiliary copy of the argument space, carrying the inverse transpose of the covector transporter, and pin its coordinate lines. An identity tensor link enforces this duality, as in linked-partition tensor isomorphism. The diagonal links between the original three covector factors still enforce trilinear isometry. We give the full block layout below.

### An explicit tensor that fixes an ordered list of lines

Let \(H\) be a simple rigid graph with minimum degree at least two and exactly \(k\) edges. Its edge–vertex incidence space is spanned by the matrices \(E_{a,v}\), one for each incidence \(v\in a\). The corresponding tensor \(\Gamma\) has first direction indexed by edges of \(H\), second direction indexed by vertices of \(H\), and third direction indexed by incidences.

The projection of \(\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)\) on its first direction is the full diagonal group. Here is the matrix-space argument. If \(L\mathcal M R^{\mathsf T}=\mathcal M\) for the incidence space \(\mathcal M\), choose support permutations \(\sigma,\tau\) in \(L,R\) using their nonzero determinant expansions. For every incidence \((a,v)\), the entry \((\sigma(a),\tau(v))\) of \(L E_{a,v}R^{\mathsf T}\) is nonzero. Thus \((\sigma,\tau)\) preserves incidence and its two sides. Such an automorphism is induced by an automorphism of \(H\), so both permutations are identities. The diagonal entries of \(L,R\) are therefore all nonzero. A nonzero off-diagonal entry of \(L\) would force the endpoint set of one edge to be contained in that of another. These are distinct two-element sets. A nonzero off-diagonal entry of \(R\) would require one vertex's incident-edge set to be contained in another's. Two different vertices have at most one common incident edge, and every vertex has degree at least two. Thus both matrices are diagonal. Conversely any diagonal \(L\) extends by setting \(R=I\) and scaling each incidence direction by the inverse of its edge scalar. The argument uses the support-permutation method of Grochow and Qiao's diagonal gadget, with incomparable neighborhoods in place of regularity. [Grochow and Qiao IV, Section 4.5 and Lemma 4.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S4.SS5)

For every \(k\ge18\), take the Frucht graph and subdivide one fixed edge into a path of \(k-17\) edges. Its original vertices still have degree three. Contracting the maximal degree-two paths sends each automorphism to an automorphism of the original rigid graph, so every original vertex is fixed. The fixed endpoints then fix every vertex on the subdivided path. This produces the required rigid graph deterministically in linear time. [Frucht, *Graphs of Degree Three with a Given Abstract Group*](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1949-033-6)

Embed the first direction of \(\Gamma\) in the span of the first \(k\) coordinate vectors of the full argument space. Its first contraction image is precisely this span, so every extendible first-leg transformation preserves that span. The preceding argument makes its restriction diagonal there. Conversely a transformation preserving each pinned line extends through all incidence directions, whatever it does outside their span.

### The linked-partition query

Let \(V\) denote the argument space and \(V^*\) its covector space. Take first-leg blocks \(V^*,V\), second-leg blocks \(V^*,B\), and third-leg blocks \(V^*,J\). Keep the input form in the block with three \(V^*\)'s, and put the padded incidence tensor in \(V\otimes B\otimes J\). All other blocks are zero. Link the three \(V^*\) transformations by equality and link the \(V\) transformation to their inverse transpose. Each auxiliary transformation is otherwise free. The resulting linked-partition tensor question is exactly trilinear isometry with the chosen line constraints. There are a constant number of blocks and each direction has length \(O(\dim V)\).

For \(k<18\), the incidence graph is unnecessary. For each pinned line separately put \(e_i\otimes b_i\otimes j_i\) in the argument block and two individually partitioned one-dimensional auxiliary blocks. Preservation of that block forces preservation of the line and permits any nonzero scalar on it. There are still a constant number of blocks. With no lines, use the linked form alone. Theorems 5.3 and 5.1 of Grochow and Qiao IV convert each such linked-partition instance to ordinary 3-tensor isomorphism with linear side lengths. No extraction of witnesses from that reduction is required. [Grochow and Qiao IV, linked-partition and partitioned tensor reductions](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S5)

### From constrained decisions to a coset

Suppose a constrained decision is positive and the current source pins form a prefix of an ordered basis. Enumerate candidate lines for the image of the next basis line, extending the pin list only when the decision remains positive. A nonzero vector normalized at its first nonzero coordinate represents each projective line exactly once. Discard candidates dependent on the earlier images. When all images are pinned, at most \((q-1)^c\) diagonal scalar choices remain. Check these directly in the original trilinear equations. This produces one witness using at most \(c q^c\) decision calls and \(q^{O(c)}\) additional operations. It also works from any already prescribed prefix.

For a trilinear form \(T\), let \(G_i\) be the subgroup of \(\operatorname{Aut}(T)\) preserving the first \(i\) coordinate lines individually. For each \(i<c\), enumerate the orbit of the next line under \(G_i\) using the constrained oracle. For each orbit point, run the witness procedure while keeping the existing pins and assigning the next line to that point. The resulting representatives form a transversal of \(G_{i+1}\) in \(G_i\), with orientation determined by their action on the next line. Enumerate all elements of \(G_c\) by the diagonal choices. These elements and the transversals generate \(\operatorname{Aut}(T)\), and multiplying the orbit lengths by \(|G_c|\) gives its exact order. With \(c=\dim V\), the total number of decision calls is \(q^{O(c)}\), and each query has side lengths \(O(c)\). This is the projective stabilizer chain of Grochow and Qiao's counting method, with explicit exact witnesses for the trilinear action. [Grochow and Qiao IV, Section 7.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S7.SS4)

## 2. Reconstructing the cubic orbit

For \(f\in\operatorname{Sym}^3 V^*\) define its unnormalized polarization by
\[
 P_f(x,y,z)=[t_1t_2t_3]\,f(t_1x+t_2y+t_3z).
\]
This map is equivariant for pushforward. In characteristic three its kernel consists exactly of pure-cube sums. In fact the coefficient of a monomial is read by the corresponding basis triple, multiplied by \(1\), \(2\), or \(6\) according to its exponent multiplicities. Only the pure cubes disappear. No condition on the radical is needed for this kernel identity.

Let \(R\) be the contraction radical of \(P_f\), and choose a complement \(C\). Since any tensor entry with an argument in \(R\) vanishes, the same coefficient test eliminates every non-pure monomial that involves a radical variable. Finite-field Frobenius is bijective, so
\[
             f(x+r)=f_C(x)+\ell_f(r)^3,\qquad x\in C,\ r\in R,
\]
for a unique linear form \(\ell_f\) on \(R\). On \(\mathbb F_q\) the inverse of cubing is \(a\mapsto a^{q/3}\). The first term has concise polarization on \(C\), and \(c=\dim C\). Any transporter respects the radicals, so different ranks or different zero/nonzero types of the radical restrictions give an immediate rejection.

**Zero radical restriction.** If \(\ell_f=\ell_g=0\), work on the two \(c\)-dimensional quotients. Use the trilinear procedure to find \(A_0\) with \(A_0\cdot P_{f_C}=P_{g_C}\), and obtain generators for \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(P_{g_C})\). Under \(G\), enumerate the orbit of \(A_0\cdot f_C\) by breadth-first search. Every element lies in the one affine fiber \(P^{-1}(P_{g_C})\), which has \(q^c\) elements. Store a cubic by its \(c\) pure-cube coefficients in this fiber. For each generator the induced affine map is obtained by one substitution on a fixed representative and the Frobenius-twisted linear action on pure cubes. Thus \(g_C\) is reached if and only if a cubic transporter exists. Recording products of group generators with visited states gives the witness.

For the exact number of quotient transporters, compute the orbit of \(g_C\) under its polarization group. Its size \(m\) gives \(|\operatorname{Aut}(g_C)|=|G|/m\) by orbit–stabilizer. Using a representative at each visited point, the usual Schreier edge products generate this stabilizer: a word returning to the start factors into edge products by inserting the intermediate representatives. These procedures also use \(q^{O(c)}\) work. In the full space, every quotient isometry extends by an arbitrary radical-to-radical invertible map and an arbitrary linear shear \(C\to R\). For equal radical dimension \(r=n-c\), the number of full transporters therefore equals the number on the quotients times
\[
                        q^{rc}\,|\mathrm{GL}(r,q)|.
\]

**Nonzero radical restriction.** If both linear forms on the radicals are nonzero, any quotient polarization isometry \(S\) lifts. Choose a radical isomorphism \(U\) with \(\ell_g U=\ell_f\). The discrepancy \(f_C(x)-g_C(Sx)\) has zero polarization and therefore equals \(b(x)^3\) for a unique linear \(b\) on \(C\). Choose a linear shear \(W:C\to R_g\) with \(\ell_g W=b\). The map \(x+r\mapsto Sx+Wx+Ur\) is an exact cubic transporter. There are \(q^{(r-1)c}\) choices for \(W\), and the number of \(U\)'s fixing the specified radical functional is \(q^{r-1}|\mathrm{GL}(r-1,q)|\). Thus the full count is the number of quotient polarization isometries times
\[
                     q^{(r-1)(c+1)}|\mathrm{GL}(r-1,q)|.
\]
The same argument describes generators in both cases. Lift each generator of the quotient image, and adjoin generators of the kernel. The latter consist of elementary shears into the allowed radical space and the ordinary linear group on the radical, or the stabilizer of its designated functional. Those generators are obtained from elementary matrices and the block description fixing one covector. For the determinant scalings, it suffices to use all \(q-1\) nonzero field elements, avoiding a primitive-element search. For \(c>0\), this enumeration is within \(q^{O(c)}\). The case \(c=0\) uses the trivial quotient group and the same formulas.

## 3. Running time and literature scope

Grochow and Qiao IV, Theorem 2.1, converts the decision queries to alternating matrix space congruence with linear total length. Ivanyos, Mendoza, Qiao, Sun, and Zhang give time \(q^{\widetilde O(\lambda^{3/2})}\) for alternating matrix spaces of length \(\lambda\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) in Theorem I.4 of their FOCS paper. Here \(\lambda=O(c)\). The bounded sampling in Section III-E can also be replaced by deterministically enumerating the two individualizing matrices. Their small side has length of order the square root of the padded matrix dimension, and the rank condition can be checked on each pair as in that section, so this fits the same time bound. The \(q^{O(c)}\) query count and the affine-fiber computations fit within the same bound. Field arithmetic, Gaussian elimination on the original coefficients, and group-order arithmetic are polynomial in the input parameters outside those factors. [Grochow and Qiao IV, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S2) · [Ivanyos et al., Theorem I.4](https://chuanqizhang.com/assets/pdf/pdf/focs24.pdf) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1137/24M1719839)

For the conditional bound, any of the ordinary tensor queries has a color-preserving relational encoding on \(q^{O(c)}\) elements. List the vectors of its three argument spaces, the three addition tables and scalar multiplication relations with each field scalar distinguished, and the ternary value table. Isomorphisms of this structure are precisely linear tensor transporters. The conventional colored incidence graph encoding of a fixed-arity relational structure has polynomial overhead. A polynomial Graph Isomorphism algorithm would therefore make each query \(q^{O(c)}\), including over extension fields.

The result concerns formal polynomials, worst-case inputs, and exact transporters. In comparison, the cubic algorithm of Grochow, Qiao, and Tang has an average-case guarantee, and the direct fast cubic corollary in the full arXiv version of Grochow and Qiao IV assumes characteristic greater than three. The STOC summary names commutative cubic equivalence in its general runtime corollary. Its explicit conditional cubic corollary and polarization argument use good characteristic. This note gives the characteristic-three reduction and its precise search and counting guarantees. Sun's recent class-two group algorithm also uses an orbit computation to recover information beyond an alternating tensor, in that setting recovering central power data. The orbit here is the full affine fiber of the formal polarization map, with a separate radical calculation and a deterministic line gadget. [Grochow–Qiao–Tang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.01085) · [Grochow–Qiao IV, arXiv Corollary 1.11](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.16317#S1.SS4) · [STOC version, Corollaries 1.6–1.7](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/189643) · [Sun, *Faster Isomorphism for Groups of Class 2*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05920)

The proof is mathematical prose. Complexity.py checks all 6,561 ordered binary cubic pairs over \(\mathbb F_3\), 81 projective stabilizer chains, all 11,232 invertible ternary substitutions for three radical controls, seven edge subdivisions of the rigid graph, and 81 Frobenius checks over \(\mathbb F_9\). In particular it verifies the non-equivalent pair \(x^2y\) and \(x^2y+y^3\) with identical polarization. No Lean kernel receipt or literature-priority claim is attached.
