Log-free short-probe bounds for tensor isomorphism over odd finite rings

1. Statement

Fix a nonzero finite commutative ring R of odd characteristic. There is a deterministic algorithm for ordinary bimap isomorphism of explicitly presented finite R-modules, in time 2^{O_R(n^{3/2})} poly(L), where n bounds the numbers of presented generators per module and L is the input length. It decides isomorphism, finds a witness when one exists, generates the source stabilizer and counts the transporters. The same bound applies to ordinary three-tensors on three free R-modules. For those tensors, it also finds a witness and counts the transporters for each determinant triple, so it decides special-linear three-tensor isomorphism.

More uniformly, for S = Z/p^e with odd prime p and fixed positive e, the bound for S is p^{O_e(n^{3/2})} poly(L). The prime p is allowed to vary. For class-two p-groups of order N and exponent dividing p^e, supplied by Cayley tables with odd p, this gives a deterministic isomorphism test in time N^{O_e(sqrt(log_p N))} poly(N). Here e is fixed and the group need not have elementary abelian quotient or centre.

The main algorithmic improvement is to obtain the short-probe rectangles with dimension loss linear in the maximum residue rank, by applying Pang's rank-specialization bound to the isomorphism problem. It removes the logarithmic slack in the bound highlighted by Ivanyos, Mendoza, Qiao, Sun and Zhang in FOCS 2024, Lemma III.6 and Remark IV.6, as well as both logarithms in the previous fixed-ring short-probe bound. The coefficient extraction is inspired by Davis and Astra's use of first vanishing orders in their chromatic coefficient proof. They compute a first Hasse coefficient after a specialisation. Here the same vanishing-order viewpoint suggested the use of Pang's determinant specialization for the isomorphism bottleneck. The packing and bipartite examples from the earlier proof-transfer collection are separate results.

2. Rectangles over finite fields

Let B be a space of linear maps V -> W over F_q. Let r be its maximum rank at base-field points and let rho be the maximum rank after scalar extension to the algebraic closure. Then there are subspaces U <= V and Y <= W* such that every form (y,x) -> y(Ax), for A in B, vanishes on Y × U, and

  codim U + codim Y <= 2 rho <= 2 qr/(q-1).

Pang's Lemma 3.1 gives this specialization estimate in stronger mean-rank form over prime fields. The same proof works over F_q. For a basis A_1,...,A_d of B, the generic matrix Σ t_i A_i has a nonzero determinant minor f of degree rho. The zero space case is immediate. Fix a base-field tuple a. In the translated minor at a, use a constant invertible row change to make the rows below the rank of its constant block have zero constant term. Every product in the determinant must pick one factor from each of these rows, so its lowest possible degree is at least rho-rank(Σ a_i A_i). Multiplicity Schwartz–Zippel bounds the average multiplicity of f by rho/q. Averaging shows that the mean sampled rank is at least (1−1/q)rho, and hence so is r. The factor q/(q-1) cannot be replaced by a smaller uniform constant. For each one-dimensional line of linear forms on F_q^k take one representative and use these forms as the diagonal entries of a matrix pencil. The generic rank is (q^k-1)/(q-1). At each nonzero base-field point exactly q^{k-1} diagonals are nonzero. Their ratio tends to q/(q-1) with k. For q=2 and k=2 this is diag(x,y,x+y), with generic rank three and base-field maximum two.

Over the algebraic closure choose B_0 of rank rho. Use independent bases so B_0 = diag(I_rho,0) as a rectangular matrix. Each entry of the complementary rectangular block of A in the extended space is the coefficient of s in a (rho+1)-minor of B_0+sA adjoining its row and column to the pivot block. All such minors vanish identically by maximality of rho. Thus the block is zero and B(bar U) lies in the rho-dimensional image of B_0 for bar U=ker B_0. The same conclusion holds when rho is the minimum side dimension, since the complementary block is empty.

For any subspace Z over the algebraic closure write BZ for the span of all images AZ. The function d(Z)=dim Z-dim BZ is supermodular: B(Z+T)=BZ+BT and B(Z∩T) lies in BZ∩BT, so dimension additivity gives d(Z+T)+d(Z∩T)>=d(Z)+d(T). Consequently the sum of two maximisers is a maximiser. There is a unique largest maximiser U_bar, by choosing one of largest dimension and adding any other. Frobenius preserves the family B, hence fixes U_bar. The reduced-row-echelon descent of Pang's Lemma 3.2 then applies with q-th powers: the unique echelon basis is fixed entry by entry and therefore lies over F_q. Let U be its base-field row span. Formation of BU and its dimension commute with extending scalars. The original kernel witness shows d(U)>= dim V-2rho. With Y=(BU)^perp the claimed vanishing and codimension bound follow.

3. Rectangles over prime-power rings

Set S=Z/p^e and let B be an S-submodule of rectangular matrices with at most r nonzero Smith pivots per matrix. Write X,Y for its two free mode sorts. There are free direct summands U_X,U_Y, with sums of complementary free ranks at most 4er, such that every matrix in B vanishes on U_X × U_Y.

Apply the field theorem to the matrices reduced modulo p. Their ranks are at most r and 2p/(p-1)<=4. Lift bases of the two subspaces along with complements to free direct summands over S. The restricted forms have entries in pS. Divide by p using pS≅Z/p^{e-1} as modules. This gives a space of matrices over the shorter ring still having at most r nonzero pivots. In fact the image of a restriction is a quotient of a subgroup of the original image, and taking subgroups or quotients of a finite abelian p-group cannot increase its minimum generator number. For subgroups use the dimension of elements killed by p. The division by p identifies the restricted images with the corresponding images over the shorter ring. Iterate, lifting each new chosen subspace to a direct summand of the previous one. After e stages the actual restriction is zero, and the costs in complementary ranks add. The entire argument proves existence. Exhaustive selection of short lists in the algorithm below is what uses this bound.

4. Short probe lists and canonical rectangles

For a free S-bimap b:X×Y -> W, set d = rank_S W. Take t=ceil(sqrt(4(e d+1)))+1. For z in W* the scalar form is A_z:X×Y -> S. If its image as a linear map Y -> X* has s>=4t nonzero Smith pivots, choose t independent uniform samples in Y. Their images are independent uniform samples in L=im A_z, and their reductions mod pL are independent uniform vectors in F_p^s. The probability their rank is below t/2 is at most p^{-(s-t)ceil(t/2)}. To see this, the kernel of the map F_p^t -> F_p^s would contain a space of dimension h=ceil(t/2). The count of such spaces is at most p^{th}, by counting all ordered generating lists. For a fixed one, the annihilation probability is p^{-sh}.

Otherwise the generated subgroup of L has order at least p^{t/2}. The perfect duality X × X* -> S means that the proportion of X annihilating a subgroup K of X* is 1/|K|. One can check this directly by putting K in Smith coordinates. Hence t additional independent uniform X probes annihilate all chosen Y images with conditional probability at most p^{-t²/2}. For a fixed high-rank z the total probability of a zero restriction is at most 2p^{-t²/2}. A union bound over p^{ed} choices of z is strictly below one. In particular there exist probe lists P_X,P_Y, each of size t, on which no scalar form with at least 4t pivots vanishes. This argument does not pigeonhole Smith valuations and applies unchanged when they are all different.

For any proposed lists P_X,P_Y form H=span{b(x,y): x∈P_X,y∈P_Y}, and let B={A_z:z∈W*, z|_H=0}. The good probes make every B member have fewer than 4t pivots. Section 3 gives U_X,U_Y of total codimension O(e t) with all B members zero on U_X × U_Y.

We next describe data testable without deciding whether B has bounded rank. For arbitrary lists T_X,T_Y define

  D_X = span{A y : A∈B, y∈T_Y} inside X*,
  D_Y = span{A^t x : A∈B, x∈T_X} inside Y*,

and then

  K_X = {x : D_X(x)=0 and A^t x∈D_Y for every A∈B},
  K_Y = {y : D_Y(y)=0 and A y∈D_X for every A∈B}.

For every choice of lists, b(K_X,K_Y)≤H. Indeed A_z vanishes on the two K's for every z annihilating H, and perfect S-duality gives H^{perp perp}=H. The K's are intrinsic to the bimap with the marks. All these tests are polynomial-time module linear algebra over S, and it is enough to range over module generators of B in the displayed definitions.

For a good branch take T_Y in U_Y and T_X in U_X. Since B(U_Y) annihilates U_X, its span is in a free module of rank at most 4er with r=4t, so its group order is at most p^{4e²r}. It is generated by at most 4e²r of the actual vectors Ay, using elements outside the span so far. Including each corresponding y in T_Y suffices. Apply the transpose argument to T_X. Thus K_X contains U_X and K_Y contains U_Y. Add lists J_X,J_Y of at most 4er complement generators, with L_X=(P_X,T_X,J_X) and L_Y=(P_Y,T_Y,J_Y). The algorithm only demands the two tests span(L_X)+K_X=X and span(L_Y)+K_Y=Y. A passing branch exists by the argument. There is no need to compute the generic rank, the descended maximiser, or the U's in the algorithm.

Choose one passing set of lists in the source by exhaustive search. Enumerate the target lists of corresponding lengths, skipping failures of the span tests. The total count of enumerated lists is p^{O_e(n sqrt(d+1))} for maximum free rank n and fixed e, with polynomial work before further processing each branch. Genuine witnesses take source lists to target lists accepted by these intrinsic tests.

5. Compiling a branch to ordered alternating tuple isometry

Write H' for the target span of the named probe values. Reject unless the two lists of probe values have the same relations, so that matching them defines a unique isomorphism phi:H -> H'. For each K_X,K_Y choose minimal free covers S^{k_X}, S^{k_Y} with maps E_X,E_Y to their ambient modes. These ranks are at most the respective ambient free ranks. Reject if matching cover sizes differ. Keep the following marks:

  • The named vectors of L_X and L_Y.
  • The linear maps y↦b(x,y) for named x, and x↦b(x,y) for named y.
  • The linear maps E_X and E_Y, tying their source cover sorts to X and Y.
  • The rectangular forms (u,v)↦λ b(E_X u,E_Y v) for each coordinate λ on W.

On the target in the last mark use λ phi^{-1} on H', since the K rectangle maps there. These maps are well-defined linear or bilinear maps even when H is not a free summand. The named probe and cross-map equations imply that the W transporter restricts to phi on H. The last marks therefore force the bimap equation on K_X × K_Y, and bilinearity and the span tests force it everywhere. Conversely any genuine transporter carrying the lists lifts to the cover marks: an isomorphism between finite S-modules lifts to an invertible map between minimal free covers by choosing preimages of generators and testing invertibility modulo p. Its lifts respect all last marks because the maps into K commute.

For completeness, these simultaneous linear and bilinear marks have an ordered alternating tuple implementation of total matrix dimension O(n). Work with the bivector convention P M P^t. For each sort take two primal and two dual copies and mark the four identity pairing cross-blocks of the complete bipartite graph between them, each in a separate alternating matrix. A pairing marker has image exactly the corresponding two-copy sum, and intersections recover each copy individually. Their four equations force a single primal map G and a single dual map G^{-t}. Separate three rank-one lines are made individually recoverable by the analogous triangle of line pairs. Preservation of the three pair equations forces all three scalings to equal eta with eta²=1. Use the first line paired with a marked vector in its primal sort, and decode the sort maps as eta G. Thus the line scaling multiplies the vector by exactly the factor needed. A linear map is its block between a primal target and dual source, and a rectangular form is its block between the two dual input sorts. The signs in the opposite block of each alternating matrix are fixed by alternation. Their equations are unchanged on replacing all sort transformations by their common eta multiple, by eta²=1. A genuine marks-preserving transporter extends with eta=1. The decoder respects multiplication on the marked tuple stabilizer, since eta is a central character. The tuple is ordered, has a polynomial number of marks, and all relevant matrix sizes remain linear in n.

Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Theorem 5.22, solves this ordered isometry decision in time polynomial in n, the number of marks, e and p. Their congruence convention is equivalent by inverse transpose. We use that theorem as a published subroutine. The tuple here need not arise from an alternating bimap input. It arises from the explicit marks described above.

6. Witnesses and counting

For an ordered alternating tuple of ambient rank O(n), the decision solver can be made to preserve arbitrary prescribed vector images. Use separate primal and dual marker copies of its whole ambient module, put the original tuple on one primal copy, and use the three-line construction of Section 5 for the named vectors. The decoding eta G preserves the original tuple equations because eta²=1. Thus a passing partial assignment extends precisely when this augmented ordered instance passes. Its dimension increases by a fixed factor.

Successively prescribe the images of an ambient basis, trying its p^{O(e n)} possible vectors at each step. This constructs one witness in p^{O_e(n)} poly(L) time. A stabilizer chain by pointwise fixing successive basis vectors similarly yields generators. At each level recover one element for every feasible image of the next basis vector while fixing all previous images. Such elements are transversals. Their union generates since fixing the full basis gives the trivial group. All of this uses p^{O_e(n)} times polynomial input work per branch.

Return a decoded witness from any successful source-to-target branch. To generate the ordinary source automorphism group, generate the single marked source tuple stabilizer, then enumerate source-to-source branches and retain at least one witness per successful image of the source lists. Every original automorphism has a lift taking those lists to a tested tuple of lists. Composing that lift with the inverse of the retained lifted witness for that tuple fixes the lists. The resulting map therefore comes from the marked source stabilizer, and projecting to the original modes proves generation. It is important here that the branch witnesses are kept on the modeled free sorts before taking their images on the original modules.

If a witness g_0 exists, all transporters form its right coset g_0 G for the computed source automorphism group G. To compute |G|, let G act faithfully on the disjoint union of the underlying original modules, and use deterministic permutation group order and membership algorithms. The permutation degree over S is at most p^{O(e n)}. Binomial or exponential numeric sizes of output counts do not change their bit lengths. To keep the written generating list short, discard any candidate generator already in the subgroup generated by those kept. Each accepted generator at least doubles its order, and the size of the ambient group of mode automorphisms is at most p^{O(e n²)}. These steps and decoding of actual module images have total cost bounded by the branch count times p^{O_e(n)} and polynomial input work.

For free R-tensors, the determinant character chi:G -> (R^×)^3 has image generated by the determinants of the generators. Enumerate it and keep representative words, using a finite traversal of the multiplication table of the image. The attainable triples are exactly chi(g_0)chi(G), each with |G|/|chi(G)| transporters. A representative word gives one witness in that class. The image size is bounded by |R^×|³. It is constant for fixed R and at most p^{3e} for S. There is no assumption that one can adjust a witness to determinant one by scalar multiplication.

7. General inputs and the class-two corollary

First let R be fixed of characteristic p^e. Regard each presented R-module M as an S-module by expanding the finite additive presentation of each coefficient coordinate. Expand generating relations by multiplication by every element of R, so the resulting relations generate their R-span. Smith reduction gives M as a sum of S/(p^a), with O_R(n) cyclic factors. Modules of differing additive types cannot be isomorphic. For a chosen additive decomposition with k factors, let pi:S^k -> M be the coordinate quotient and iota:M -> S^k the coordinate embedding (multiply the factor S/(p^a) by p^{e-a}). Keep both free sorts and the linear marks iota pi and iota r pi for each r∈R. Preserving these marks is equivalent to inducing a common R-linear isomorphism on M. Indeed iota pi has image iota M and kernel ker pi, and the extra marks imply commutation with every r. Conversely an R-linear isomorphism lifts to both free sorts. For the quotient lift use preimages of generators and invertibility modulo p, since the cover is minimal. For the embedding lift, dualise the minimal quotient lift of the dual isomorphism. In the cyclic coordinates the restriction from the dual of the free embedding space to Hom_S(M,S) is surjective and minimal. The restriction equation then dualises to the required equation on iota. Evaluation on all coordinate functionals separates points, so equality on the dual implies the desired original equality.

For a bimap b insert the free bimap iota_W b(pi_X -,pi_Y -) on the free input covers and output embedding. The surrounding linear marks already tie their actions to the genuine module actions. Surjectivity of pi_X and pi_Y and injectivity of iota_W give reflection of isomorphism, not merely preservation. Keep all those marks in the tuple compiler. When counting witnesses apply the group algorithm to the projected original module maps. Counting auxiliary lifts would give a different answer. The input modules and the free bimap over S have all ranks O_R(n). For S itself there is no need for the extra multiplication marks, since S-linearity already enforces scalar action. The dimensions are O(n) and the overhead is polynomial in the presentation length and in p,e.

A fixed finite R splits by the central idempotents for its prime power characteristic components. Tensor and module maps split componentwise. Combine witnesses, take the product groups and multiply the relevant counts. An ordinary three-tensor on free modules corresponds to its bilinear contraction on the first two dual modules, with original transporter inverses transposed in those modes. The third mode map is unchanged. This identifies determinant-one witnesses and converts all determinant labels explicitly by inverting the first two. Thus the branch bound p^{O_e(n sqrt(d+1))} and d<=O_R(n), together with the costs in Sections 5 and 6, yield the stated bound without logarithms. Small or zero mode dimensions pose no problem: when there is a matching zero-format the tensor is zero, and the same bimap tests work with zero free ranks and padded probe lists.

For a class-two p-group with odd p, the class-two Lazard correspondence gives a Lie ring on the same set, with abelian addition and bracket given by the central commutator. Explicitly, addition is the group product with half its central commutator subtracted, and the inverse formula recovers the group product from addition and half the bracket. The half is unique by odd order. Cayley-table computations give this addition and bracket in polynomial time in N, and an additive generating list of size at most log_p N. One way to obtain a presentation is to greedily enlarge the span by adding elements until the full table is spanned. In that generator list all relations in (Z/p^e)^k can be enumerated in p^{e k} <= N^e operations. This is polynomial for fixed e. An isomorphism of groups is exactly an isomorphism of the bracket bimap with a common linear map on both inputs and output. In Section 7 give these three copies separate names for use in the rectangular algorithm, and add identity-map marks on their corresponding free covers and embeddings to tie the lifts. Identical module presentations and additive decompositions allow identical lifts for the three roles, so these marks preserve and reflect actual Lie ring isomorphism. The algorithm then gives N^{O_e(sqrt(log_p N))} poly(N). For example the upper unitriangular 3×3 group over Z/p² has class two and exponent p² for odd p, by the usual binomial power formula. Its commutator subgroup contains the central copy of Z/p². The Frattini subgroup therefore has a nontrivial p-th power, so the group need not have Frattini class two.

An aspect-ratio bound follows from the same counts. For a free tensor over S, permute matching modes in both instances so the smallest mode, of size k, is the output. The bound becomes p^{O_e(n sqrt(k+1))} poly(L), where n is the largest size. Relabel the determinant coordinates on output. For a general finite field F_q of odd characteristic, work directly in F_q: the argument is the field step of the rectangle theorem, the probe image is a vector space, and every enumeration uses q rather than p. Theorem 5.22 applies to the field as a finite local principal ideal ring. With a standard explicit finite-field representation this gives q^{O(n sqrt(k+1))} poly(L), uniformly in q. These are decision, search, generator and counting bounds under the same stated inputs.

8. The published logarithmic gap

For a square matrix space, dim V minus the maximal deficiency d(U) is the noncommutative rank, by the usual shrunk-subspace characterization. Thus Section 2 proves ncrk(B) <= 2q/(q-1) maxrank_Fq(B). The field FOCS 2024 argument used ncrk(B) <= O(r log r) in Lemma III.6, obtained via an extension-degree factor in Lemma IV.5. Its Remark IV.6 notes the logarithmic slack between that bound and the elementary lower bound ncrk(B) >= r. The estimate here has the same linear order in r as the lower bound. Their runtime analysis in Section III-E costs q^{O((s+h)n)} with s=O(sqrt(n)), h=ncrk(B) for the probe kernel, and maximum probe-kernel rank O(sqrt(n)). Applying this bound gives q^{O(n^{3/2})} for that decision procedure. The separate algorithm of Sections 3–7 gives the exact search and determinant-resolved outputs and extends the argument to local residue layers. The bounded-exponent group bound includes the odd Frattini-class-two setting, since its central elementary abelian Frattini subgroup contains all p-th powers and commutators.

9. Relation to existing work

The odd-characteristic ordered tuple algorithm is Theorem 5.22 of Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun. Their general odd-order class-two isomorphism bound and Sun's subsequent all-class-two bound concern unrestricted exponent. The fixed exponent restriction above is essential here. Ivanyos–Mendoza–Qiao–Sun–Zhang obtain the square-root exponent scale up to logarithmic factors for Frattini class two, which includes the exponent-p class-two regime. Pang's paper uses rank specialization and Frobenius descent for proof complexity. The contributions here are their application to the isomorphism bottleneck, the valuation-free probes, and the log-free end-to-end bounds across the stated fixed-ring and bounded-exponent families, including exact determinant-resolved counts and no promise on either tensor. It proceeds by combining the prior rectangular short-probe framework with the coefficient multiplicity bound and canonical base-field descent. No priority claim is made beyond the scoped comparison.
