# Exact signed local genera at the first ordinary failures

**Theorem.** Over a number field \(F\), take either of the everywhere locally isomorphic pairs of geometrically diagonal tensors from the side-six classification. Choose multiplication bases so that the two trace-pairing discriminants agree as elements of \(F^\times\). The two tensors then have a common signed local genus in the geometric diagonal orbit. This genus contains exactly five global special-linear classes for the Klein four pair and exactly two for the order-eight dihedral pair. In the first case the two ordinary orbits contribute four and one classes respectively. In the second case they contribute one each. [Degree-six diagonal classification](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/DickeAndIsomorphismMethods/GEOMETRY.md).

**Passage from ordinary to signed local equivalence.** Under an algebra isomorphism, trace pairings are isometric. The discriminants of the two algebras have the same squareclass because their ratio is a square over every completion. Indeed a nonsquare in \(F^\times\) gives a quadratic extension with a nonsplit place by Chebotarev. Rescaling one basis vector makes the actual discriminants equal. A local algebra isomorphism in the new bases now has determinant \(+1\) or \(-1\). Each algebra in either pair has a global odd algebra automorphism, coming from conjugation on a quadratic factor or swapping two scalar factors. This automorphism remains odd locally. Composing with it when needed gives a local algebra isomorphism with determinant \(+1\), hence a special-linear isotopy in all three slots.

There are exactly two ordinary orbits in the common local genus. In the side-six classification a faithful permutation action determines its common splitting field, and the Klein four partner of either pattern is unique in this field. In the dihedral case the companion quartic is the field of the other reflection class, and the quadratic factor is prescribed by the character comparison. This again gives exactly one other ordinary orbit. In each orbit the sign factor is trivial by the global odd automorphism, so the signed contribution of that orbit is \(|\mathfrak K(E/F)|^2\) by the [determinant calculation](determinants.md).

**Two-field calculation for the dihedral pair.** For every finite separable field \(A/F\) and every quadratic field \(B/F\), the product algebra \(A\times B\) satisfies the multinorm principle. This is the quadratic case of Hürlimann's result on a cyclic factor. Here is a reciprocity proof of the precise case used.

For a quadratic extension \(C/K\) of number fields, let \(h_w\) be its local Hilbert norm symbol in \(\mathbf F_2\), zero at split places. An assignment of finitely many symbols is realized by an element of \(K^\times\) exactly when it is zero at split places and has even sum. To see sufficiency, use the Brauer–Hasse–Noether exact sequence with the prescribed local invariants \(0\) or \(1/2\). The resulting Brauer class splits over \(C\), by local degree and the injectivity of localization over \(C\), and therefore is the cyclic algebra of a global element. The projection formula gives
\[
h_v(N_{A/F}b)=\sum_{w\mid v}h_w(b)
\]
with \(B/F\) on the left and \(AB/A\) on the right. It applies to each local factor. [Hürlimann, Proposition 3.3](https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=com-001%3A1984%3A59%3A%3A34), [Macedo, introduction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.11941).

Suppose \(a\in F^\times\) is an everywhere local product of the two norms. If \(B\subseteq A\), the norm from \(A\) is already a norm from \(B\), so the cyclic norm theorem applies. Otherwise \(AB/A\) is quadratic. At each \(v\) with \(h_v(a)=1\), the local product condition and projection formula require a nonsplit place \(w\) of \(AB/A\) over \(v\). Mark one such \(w\), putting zero everywhere else. Reciprocity over \(F\) gives an even number of marks. The assignment is therefore realized by some \(b\in A^\times\). Then \(a/N_{A/F}b\) is a local norm from \(B\) everywhere, hence a global norm from \(B\). Both dihedral algebras are a quartic field times a quadratic field, so each contributes one signed class.

**Three-field calculation for the Klein four pair.** Let \(B_1,B_2,B_3\) be the three quadratic subfields of the biquadratic closure \(L\). At every place at least one field splits, so \(B_1\times B_2\times B_3\) has full local norm group. Its global multinorm group has index two, as follows.

For each \(i\), let \(V_i\) be the places where exactly \(B_i\) splits. Else all three fields split. On \(V_i\) the two nonsplit local quadratic characters are identical. For \(a\in F^\times\) let \(p_i(a)\in\mathbf F_2\) be the sum of the common nonsplit Hilbert symbol at \(V_i\). Hilbert reciprocity for the three characters says \(p_1=p_2=p_3\). The common parity kills each norm group, since the symbols of an element in the \(B_i\) norm group are supported only on \(V_i\) and have even sum.

Every even finite signature supported on \(V_i\) can be supplied by an element of the \(B_i\) norm group. For \(j\ne i\), apply the quadratic Hilbert-symbol assignment above to \(B_iB_j/B_i\), marking one of the two \(B_i\)-places over each requested \(v\in V_i\). The projection formula gives the requested \(B_j\) symbols for the global norm, and the \(B_i\) symbols vanish. Consequently an \(a\) with common parity zero can be adjusted by the three norm groups to have no symbols anywhere, so is itself in their product.

Each \(V_i\) has a prime by Chebotarev. Select one prime in each and prescribe symbol \(1\) there for the two nonsplit characters. First realize the prescribed \(B_1\) signature by reciprocity, since it contains two marks. Its \(B_2\) signature can differ from the prescribed one only on \(V_1\), because on \(V_2\) both are zero and on \(V_3\) they both equal the \(B_1\) signature. The discrepancy has even sum by reciprocity and can be supplied by a \(B_1\) norm as above. This realizes common parity one. Hence the norm quotient has order two, yielding four signed classes. This arithmetic obstruction is classical. [Colliot-Thélène, Proposition 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.3644).

The other Klein four algebra \(L\times F\times F\) has full global norm group from its scalar factors. It contributes one signed class. The counts now follow.



These counts hold for every signed local genus above the specified ordinary local genus. For any representative in one of the two ordinary orbits, apply its ordinary change of bases to both trace-normalized tensors. Normality of the special-linear subgroup transports their entire signed local genus bijectively.
