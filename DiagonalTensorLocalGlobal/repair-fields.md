# Exponentially large minimum global isomorphism fields

**Theorem.** For every number field \(F\) and every \(k\ge1\) there are geometrically diagonal tensors \(X,Y\) of side \(6k\) that are ordinarily isomorphic over every completion and have the following exact extension criterion. There are \(k\) independent biquadratic fields \(L_i/F\), and \(X,Y\) become ordinarily isomorphic over an extension \(M/F\) if and only if \(M\) contains a quadratic subfield of each \(L_i\). In particular their minimum finite isomorphism degree is \(2^k\), and there are exactly \(3^k\) inclusion-minimal fields of isomorphism in a fixed algebraic closure of \(F\).

**Proof.** Take a Galois extension \(L/F\) with group \(G=V_4^k\) whose decomposition subgroups are cyclic. Such an extension exists by the locally cyclic realization theorem for finite solvable groups. The \(L_i\) are the fixed fields of the kernels of the \(k\) coordinate projections. Thus the \(L_i\) are linearly disjoint as a family and their compositum is \(L\). [Frei–Loughran–Newton, Proposition 5.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.02518).

For block \(i\) let \(A_i\) be the product of the three quadratic subfields of \(L_i\), and let \(B_i=L_i\times F\times F\). Let \(X,Y\) be the multiplication tensors of \(A=\prod_i A_i\) and \(B=\prod_i B_i\). The corresponding permutation sets in a block consist of the three two-point quotient sets, and the regular four-point set with two fixed points, respectively. Their restrictions to any cyclic subgroup of \(V_4\) agree. For the trivial group this follows from the cardinalities. For an order-two subgroup both restrictions have two fixed points and two pairs. All local images in \(G\) are cyclic, so each block gives a local isomorphism. [The side-six Klein four pair](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/DickeAndIsomorphismMethods/GEOMETRY.md).

Embed \(L\) and \(M\) in a common overfield and put \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(LM/M)\subseteq G\) through restriction. If the image \(H_i\) of \(H\) in a block is cyclic, the block restrictions are isomorphic. If \(H_i=V_4\), the first restricted set contributes zero \(H\)-fixed points and the second contributes two. These differences have the same sign in every block. Thus the full restricted sets agree exactly when all \(H_i\) are proper. This argument allows isomorphisms that mix points from different blocks.

By Galois correspondence, \(H_i\) is proper exactly when its fixed field in \(L_i\) contains a quadratic subfield, which is equivalent to \(M\) containing such a field. The fixed-field statement also holds for arbitrary extensions \(M/F\), because \(LM/M\) is Galois and the elements of \(L\) fixed by that group are exactly \(L\cap M\). There are three choices in each block. The resulting composita have degree \(2^k\), are distinct by projection on the factors of \(G\), and each is already a field of isomorphism. Every field of isomorphism contains one. This proves both counts.

Every positive multiple of \(2^k\) occurs as a finite isomorphism degree. Enlarge any minimal field by the requested factor using an Eisenstein polynomial at one of its finite primes. Over \(\mathbb Q\), the multiplication tables on the maximal orders also give integral representatives isomorphic modulo every positive integer. Every local field-algebra isomorphism preserves the maximal integral orders, so it reduces to an integral-algebra isomorphism modulo each prime power, and the Chinese remainder theorem combines the maps.



**Special-linear strengthening.** The same exact field criterion can be required for special-linear equivalence. Choose the bases of \(A,B\) so that their trace discriminants agree. Their ratio is a square in \(F\) by their ordinary local equivalence and Chebotarev for the associated quadratic extension. Over any field where the two algebras become isomorphic the determinant of an isomorphism is then \(+1\) or \(-1\). Swapping the two scalar factors in any \(B_i\) gives a global determinant \(-1\) automorphism. Correcting the determinant gives a special-linear isomorphism over the same field. This also gives special-linear equivalence over every completion.

For a concrete side-twelve example over \(\mathbb Q\), use \(L_1=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{17},\sqrt{89})\) and \(L_2=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{257},\sqrt{769})\). The four distinct primes are \(1\bmod 8\) and are pairwise quadratic residues. Therefore, at a prime in this set only its own square-root factor can contribute to the decomposition group. The dyadic and real places split, and the remaining places are unramified. This gives nine minimal fields, each of degree four.
