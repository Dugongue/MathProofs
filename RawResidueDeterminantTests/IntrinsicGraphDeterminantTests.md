# Determinant constraints from intrinsic graphs on tensor blocks

Informal proof. Fix a finite field \(F=\mathbb F_q\). The main construction is a deterministic polynomial-size reduction to a single ordinary tensor-isomorphism instance when a mode has a covariant decomposition into low-dimensional blocks indexed by a connected graph of logarithmic maximum degree. In odd characteristic, require that the degree of a vertex has the same parity as its block dimension. The graph may have exponentially many automorphisms and need not have a covariant spanning tree or an order on the blocks. The construction uses the same maps that transport the original tensor. Section 5 gives a raw pencil class with one ordinary TI instance for SL-TI, in which unboundedly many factor labels have the same intrinsic degree and multiplicity tags.

## 1. Sign from the flags of a graph

A flag is a pair \((v,e)\) consisting of an edge and one endpoint. Work first with two simple undirected graphs whose vertices have associated block dimensions \(d_v\), possibly zero for auxiliary vertices. A graph isomorphism \(\pi\) preserves these dimensions. On each input order the vertices and edges arbitrarily. At each vertex order its incident flags arbitrarily. At each edge order its two flags arbitrarily. These orders serve as coordinates and are not imposed as graph markings.

Let \(c_{M,\Gamma}\) be the sign of the basis change between the flag order grouped by vertices and that grouped by edges on input \(M\). For a local map on the flags of vertex \(v\), write \(\epsilon_v\) for its permutation determinant in the chosen coordinates. For edge \(e\) write \(\epsilon_e\) for the sign on its two endpoint flags. Denote by \(\operatorname{sgn}_{\mathrm{odd}}(\pi)\) the permutation sign on the subsequence of odd-degree vertices, in the respective input orders. Grouping flags by vertices gives a block-permutation matrix. Swapping two blocks of dimensions \(r,s\) contributes \((-1)^{rs}\), so the full flag determinant in this order is
\[
 \operatorname{sgn}_{\mathrm{odd}}(\pi)\prod_v\epsilon_v.
\]
Grouping by edges makes all blocks two-dimensional, so their permutations contribute sign one. The full determinant in that order is \(\prod_e\epsilon_e\). The two values differ by the factor \(c_{A,\Gamma}/c_{B,\Gamma}\) when converting edge order to vertex order. Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{sgn}_{\mathrm{odd}}(\pi)
  =\frac{c_{A,\Gamma}}{c_{B,\Gamma}}
     \prod_e\epsilon_e \prod_v\epsilon_v^{-1}.
\]

For a block decomposition of a mode, choose one basis of each block and use their concatenation in vertex order. Let \(c_{M,V}\) denote its determinant in the raw reference coordinates. The transporter map on block \(v\) has local determinant \(a_v\) in its source and target block bases. If \(\deg(v)\equiv d_v\bmod 2\), the odd-degree subsequence is exactly the odd-dimensional subsequence. Thus the literal mode determinant is
\[
 \det f
  =\frac{c_{B,V}}{c_{A,V}}\frac{c_{A,\Gamma}}{c_{B,\Gamma}}
     \prod_v(a_v\epsilon_v^{-1})\prod_e\epsilon_e.
\]
In characteristic two the signs may all be set to one, and the degree parity assumption can be dropped. In either characteristic one may use auxiliary zero-dimensional vertices. The proof also permits parity exceptions at individually marked vertices. To see this, put the exceptions last in corresponding marked order in both inputs. Their restricted permutation has sign one on both the degree and the dimension lists.

## 2. Read the local quantities with the actual transporter

Encode the graph and its flag set by the coordinate algebras of their finite label sets. Primitive idempotents encode the vertices, edges and flags. Attach the two incidence maps from flags. Attach the vertex projection action to the sum of blocks in the physical tensor mode. An isomorphism of this system must send each block to the block of the same vertex image. Keep the original tensor in the system.

For each vertex, add the exterior algebra of its block. The inclusion of degree one and its multiplication force the induced action on the top line to have scalar \(a_v\). Likewise add the exterior algebra of the vector space on the incident flags at each vertex and the exterior algebra on the two flags at each edge. Force their actions to agree with the global flag permutation by the incidence projections and degree-one inclusions. Their top lines carry \(\epsilon_v\) and \(\epsilon_e\). Use exterior degree projections or the graded incidence sorts to distinguish the top lines, including the scalars at empty rank. All dimensions are polynomial in the input when the block dimensions and degrees are logarithmic.

A block determinant can instead be supplied by any already proved local tensor construction that reads its determinant as an attached line under the actual block map. Such a construction must allow every genuine transporter to lift and must force the correct scalar on every lift. For example, the local semilinear Frobenius-frame construction applies when its hypotheses hold. This substitution does not require low total block dimension.

## 3. Aggregate without ordering the graph

Subdivide each graph edge once, so there are vertex nodes and edge nodes. The new links have a canonical direction from the vertex nodes to the edge nodes. On each link attach an auxiliary one-dimensional \(F\)-space, with arbitrary invertible action. Its scalar in the chosen link bases is \(t_\ell\in F^\times\). At an ordinary vertex node \(v\) impose that the product of the \(t_\ell\) on incident links equals \(a_v\epsilon_v^{-1}\). At an edge node \(e\) use the product of their inverses and set it to \(\epsilon_e\). Choose one source root. On the target branch pin a candidate for its image. At the root use the same equation with an additional variable factor \(\rho^{-1}\) on the right, where \(\rho\) is the action on a new output line.

These are equations on the actions on one-dimensional spaces. A multiplication table \((x,y)\mapsto xy\) forces the multiplier on its output line to be the product of the two input multipliers. A dual pairing forces inverse multipliers. To multiply incident lines while allowing their permutation, use one auxiliary line for each subset of the incident links, identify singletons with the given lines and fix the empty-subset line by a unit mark. Include the insertion multiplications for each subset and new link. Their equations are precisely unsigned multiplication, so every permutation of the incident links preserves the indexing and can lift. This costs \(O(d2^d)\) relations at degree \(d\).

Multiplying all local equations cancels each link twice, with opposite exponents. Thus \(\rho=\prod_v(a_v\epsilon_v^{-1})\prod_e\epsilon_e\). Conversely, for this value of \(\rho\) the equations on link scalars are solvable. Given any spanning tree of the connected subdivided graph, set the non-tree link scalars to one and solve from the leaves toward the root. The last equality holds by the product identity. The spanning tree is used for the existence proof only. It need not be preserved by any transporter. Independent flows can aggregate other products of known local line multipliers.

Rescale the output line on each input by its computed basis factors from Section 1 to read \(\det f\) in raw coordinates. For several mode determinants, attach independent readouts to the same original tensor. Prescribed characters of the resulting lines are computed with line multiplication and duality, and their requested values are imposed by comparing marked vectors on the two inputs. Root-image guesses are polynomial for a fixed number of modes and flows. Other auxiliary covariant data are free to permute in the same instance.

## 4. One-instance assembly and hypotheses

The label algebras, all incidence maps and all block or flag exterior algebras above are explicit. For each of the finitely many roles, use the direct sum of its component spaces with coordinate-algebra projection actions. Encode the incidence of each family of maps by its own finite label sort and the maps from those labels to the argument and output labels. With occurrence copies and equality maps, this packs the componentwise linear and bilinear maps into a fixed tensor-system signature. Each individual graph branch is therefore one ordinary TI instance by the published fixed-arity tensor-system construction. [Chen, Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

Pack the polynomially many root-image branches by a balanced positive OR. For two equality-of-isomorphism-type tests \(a=b\) and \(c=d\), compare \(\{(a,c),(b,d)\}\) with \(\{(a,d),(b,c)\}\) as unordered pairs of ordered pairs. Their two matchings give exactly the desired OR. A balanced tree expands to polynomially many nodes. Encode its ordered and unordered incidence with finite label algebras and place the leaf tensors in the corresponding direct sums. One application of the tensor-system construction suffices for the whole tree.

Every isomorphism of the output pairs therefore uses one original tensor transporter and reads its actual determinant through the local formula. Every original transporter preserving the graph data and passing the desired character constraints extends to the corresponding root-image branch. If the graph data were recovered covariantly from raw tensors, all genuine transporters preserve them before the optional root marking. If the data were supplied, the theorem applies to the graph-preserving transporter problem.

The cost is polynomial in the dense input size, \(2^a\), and \(2^b\) for maximum block dimension \(a\) and maximum graph degree \(b\), provided the number of mode graphs and global determinant equations is fixed. Local determinant gadgets, when substituted for full block exterior algebras, contribute their explicit sizes instead of \(2^a\). Branching and leaf determinant handling extend the earlier bounded-branching determinant construction. The flag identity in Section 1 is the step that permits cycles and unordered graph incidences.

## 5. Raw square pencils with same-tag factor orbits

Write \(M_A:U_A\to\operatorname{Hom}(X_A,Y_A)\) with \(\dim X_A=\dim Y_A=n\), and likewise \(M_B\). A transporter satisfies \(M_B(fx)=h M_A(x)g^{-1}\). Assume the nonzero formal source determinant has geometric linear factors and a tagged set of rational factors that form a basis of \(U_A^*\). The tag selects the whole set and need not distinguish its members. The other Frobenius orbits of geometric factors are paired by distinct intrinsic tags, for example their (degree of definition, multiplicity) pairs. The target need only satisfy the matching properties when an ordinary transporter exists. Recover the factors of the determinants in separate finite fields by the [deterministic bounded-degree factor algorithm](GeometricPencilFactorRecovery.md), with degree bound one.

Write the rational basis in its normalized coordinates as \(\ell_i\), with dual basis \(e_i\). An isomorphism maps the block lines \(Fe_i\) by a permutation, with scalars \(a_i\) given by \(\ell_{B,\pi(i)}(fx)=a_i\ell_{A,i}(x)\). Use any graph on these lines computed from invariants of the pencil. One such graph joins a pair when the least rank of \(M(u+v)\) over nonzero vectors on the two lines is globally minimal among pairs. The least ranks and any corresponding singleton-rank colors have polynomial cost at fixed \(q\), and are covariant by the transport equation. Suppose the computed graph is connected with all degrees odd and bounded by \(O(\log L)\), where \(L\) is the raw input length. In characteristic two any degree parity suffices. Then Sections 1–4 read \(\det f\) in one instance.

To read \(\mu=\det h/\det g\), write each determinant as
\[
 P_M=C_M\prod_i\ell_{M,i}^{s_i}\prod_j
             N_{E_j/F}(\phi_{M,j})^{r_j}.
\]
The exponents are factor multiplicities. The field \(E_j\) models orbit \(j\), and \(\phi_{M,j}\) is a chosen representative as an \(E_j\)-valued linear form. \(C_M\) can be computed by evaluating the determinant and factor norms at the good-line direction of the factor recovery algorithm. In a target orbit with matched tag, any transporter satisfies \(\phi_{B,j}(fx)=b_j\sigma_j(\phi_{A,j}(x))\) for a scalar \(b_j\) and a field automorphism \(\sigma_j\). Include the corresponding field and regular-module data and force this equation on the actual \(f\). A binary powering chain on one-dimensional \(E_j\)-modules reads \(N_{E_j/F}(b_j)\), by using the exponent \((q^{[E_j:F]}-1)/(q-1)\) and restricting the last module to its base-field line. The field maps retain the same \(\sigma_j\) throughout. Taking determinants in the pencil equation gives
\[
 \mu=\frac{C_B}{C_A}\prod_i a_i^{s_i}\prod_jN_{E_j/F}(b_j)^{r_j}.
\]
Read the first product by a second flow on the graph, with vertex charges \(a_i^{s_i}\) and edge charges one. Combine it with the separately tagged norm lines, which have an intrinsic order. Thus one ordinary TI instance tests any prescribed pair \((\det f,\mu)\) or any one character of this pair.

When \(\gcd(n,q-1)=1\), testing \((1,1)\) decides SL-TI. The equality \(\mu=1\) makes the two matrix-mode determinants equal. Multiplying \(g,h\) by the same scalar leaves the pencil equation unchanged and corrects both determinants because the \(n\)-th power map on \(F^\times\) is surjective. This argument does not require the label dimension to be a unit. The covariant convention inverts the determinant on dualized modes, which preserves the identity conditions.

## 6. An explicit family with exponentially many matches

Let \(\Gamma\) be a connected simple cubic graph on \(\ell\ge6\) vertices. Put one scalar block \(x_i\) at every vertex. For each oriented edge \((i,j)\), include a block
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}x_i&x_j\\0&x_i\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Include the extra scalar block \(\sum_i x_i\). The square side is \(n=7\ell+1\) and the determinant is \((\sum_i x_i)\prod_i x_i^7\). The rational basis factors have one common tag and the extra factor has multiplicity one. The pencil is concise. For a nonempty support \(S\), the scalar vertex blocks and oriented edge blocks together have rank \(10|S|-2|E(S)|\), where \(E(S)\) is the induced edge set. A block with \(x_i\ne0\) contributes two, and a block with \(x_i=0\) and \(x_j\ne0\) contributes one. The extra scalar block contributes zero or one. For two distinct support coordinates its contribution can be set to zero by taking opposite nonzero values, also in characteristic two. Thus the minimum pair rank is 18 for edges and 20 for nonedges. The graph is recovered from the hidden rational basis lines in arbitrary input coordinates.

For example, take a cyclic chain of \(r\ge3\) diamonds. Each diamond is \(K_4\) with one edge removed. Call the endpoints of the removed edge its ports, and join one port to the opposite port of the next diamond cyclically. The resulting graph is cubic and connected. Its internal vertices in each diamond can be interchanged independently, giving at least \(2^r\) distinct vertex permutations. Each extends to a transporter of the pencil by simultaneously permuting the blocks in both matrix modes, with the same permutation of individual label coordinates. Taking \(r\) a multiple of \(q-1\) gives \(n\equiv1\bmod(q-1)\). Arbitrary input rebasing is allowed. This family verifies that the one-instance construction does not depend on a polynomial number of total orbit matchings.

The theorem removes the need for individually tagged rational basis factors in this structured setting. Arbitrary residue tensors can lack a recoverable graph or any of the determinant-factor marks. No universal reduction or publication-priority claim is made here.

## 7. Combine partial readouts with certified determinant corrections

The graph can read selected mode determinants or characters, while the other modes are treated by the ordered-form and radical-filtration methods. Here is an exact assembly criterion. Write \(D=(F^\times)^3\). On a fixed branch suppose all allowed transporters have determinant triples in an explicitly computed coset \(\delta_0 U\), with \(U\le D\). It is enough to derive this inclusion from independent form isometries or from reference maps on radical grades. Let \(H\le D\) be a computed subgroup realized by genuine source autotopisms, for example those fixing a mode as in the form-tuple or graded adjoint construction. For tests of an explicitly decorated transporter, require these source autotopisms to preserve the decorations.

Suppose the graph, Frobenius frames or other local line constructions read a family \(\Lambda\) of characters from the same transporter. Put \(K=\bigcap_{\chi\in\Lambda}\ker\chi\). If \(U\cap K\le H\), a prescribed triple \(\eta\) is testable in one ordinary TI pair. First check \(\eta\in\delta_0U\). Then pin only the readings \(\chi(\eta)\), retaining the original tensor and the branch equations. Any returned transporter of determinant \(d\) has \(d^{-1}\eta\in U\cap K\). Compose it with a genuine correction of that determinant to get the requested triple. A true witness passes all preliminary and pinned tests. This applies even if \(H\) is strictly smaller than \(U\). All subgroup calculations have constant cost in the field and polynomial cost in the input representation of the certificates. A polynomial covering list of such branches still packs into one instance.

The graph on block labels can also come from a bounded-degree rank-locus certificate. When its recovered projective points form a basis of one mode, one may compute the rank graph directly from those lines, as in the raw pair-rank computation above. This uses neither a formal determinant nor square matrix slices. For example, if each of the three modes supplies such a basis and a connected parity-compatible rank graph of logarithmic degree, the three graph readouts decide SL-TI in one instance. With fewer graph readouts, the inclusion \(U\cap K\le H\) accounts for the remaining determinants when the indicated certified corrections suffice.

## 8. Repeated nonsplit factor orbits

The graph also allows an unbounded number of same-tag geometric factor orbits of degree above one. For each orbit in a covariantly selected collection choose a representative \(\phi_i:U\to E_i\) as in Section 5. Suppose these forms have zero common kernel and that
\[
 \sum_i \dim_F\operatorname{im}(\phi_i)=\dim_F U.
\]
Separately tagged extra orbits may be retained as in Section 5. Then \(\phi=(\phi_i)_i\) identifies \(U\) with the direct sum of the subspaces \(W_i=\operatorname{im}(\phi_i)\subseteq E_i\). The actual subspaces \(U_i=\bigcap_{j\ne i}\ker\phi_j\) are recovered by linear solves. Every original transporter permutes these blocks and carries each \(W_i\) to its matched \(W_j\) by a map \(x\mapsto b_i\sigma_i(x)\). In particular no correspondence of individual orbits is needed to recover the decompositions.

We describe how to attach a compatible small family of local flags to blocks of any dimension. Group all \(W_i\) of the same orbit degree and multiplicity by equivalence under \(\Gamma L_1(E_i)\), using one finite-field model at each degree. To test a candidate equivalence, enumerate the field-degree-many Frobenius powers, and for each solve the linear equations on \(b\) expressing \(b\sigma(W_i)\subseteq W_j\). If the dimensions agree, any nonzero solution gives equality. Thus these equivalence classes and explicit reference maps to every block in each class are obtained in polynomial time across both inputs. Use one reference \(F\)-subspace \(W_0\subset E\) for the whole class. Its scalar stabilizer is \(D^\times\), with \(D=\{b\in E:bW_0\subseteq W_0\}\) a subfield. Choose one ordered \(D\)-line frame of \(W_0\). Under the full \(\Gamma L_1(E)\)-stabilizer of \(W_0\), that frame has at most \([E:F]\) distinct translates, since each \(D^\times\) element fixes each line. Compute the family of all such translates using the same linear solves, one representative per achievable Frobenius map. On each original block use the image of this family under its reference map. Every possible block transporter permutes the marked frames from one block to the next because its conjugate by the two reference maps stabilizes \(W_0\).

In each matched frame, a line \(D\) is transported by \(a\sigma|_D\) in its specified generator coordinates. Its \(F\)-determinant is the product of \(N_{D/F}(a)\) and the determinant of \(\sigma|_D\). The norm is attached by the powering chain. A normal basis shows the latter determinant is one when \([D:F]\) is odd, and is the determinant on the quadratic subfield when \([D:F]\) is even. Thus this sign is read by a two-dimensional exterior algebra. Multiply these lines in the order of the frame slots. Glue every frame's resulting line to one common block line via the known determinant of that frame's basis in the block reference coordinates. The induced scalar is then exactly the block determinant for every frame. Every legitimate semilinear block map extends to the frame family. The same field map for the block is retained in its original \(\phi_i\) equation, its frame equations and its multiplier norm equations. The number of frame slots, relative field degrees and powering gates are polynomial in the input. This is the local Frobenius-frame argument of the split-pencil proof, applied per semilinear equivalence class instead of to separately matched orbits.

Now require a covariantly computed connected graph on the \(U_i\), of logarithmic maximum degree and the block-parity condition in odd characteristic. This gives a single ordinary TI test for \((\det f,\mu)\). Use Sections 1–4 with the common block lines as the local \(a_v\) inputs. For \(\mu\), aggregate the powers of the multiplier norm lines by another flow on the same graph. Its exponents are vertex colors and hence preserved. Multiply in the separate norm lines for tagged extra orbits as in Section 5. The ratio \(C_B/C_A\) is calibrated as in Section 5. This handles repeated orbit degrees and multiplicities, with no logarithmic bound on the individual block dimensions.

For an explicit family fix an odd integer \(e\ge3\), or let such \(e\) grow, and replace the scalar coordinates of the cubic-graph pencil by \(x_i\in E=\mathbb F_{q^e}\). Replace each entry \(x_i\) by multiplication by \(x_i\) on \(E\), and replace the final scalar block by \(\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F}(\sum_i x_i)\). The determinant is \(\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F}(\sum_i x_i)\prod_iN_{E/F}(x_i)^7\). Each vertex orbit has length \(e\), with its own \(E\)-valued form whose image is \(E\); all these orbits have the same tag. The trace factor has a unique tag and can be included as an extra marked rational factor without entering the block decomposition. To recover the graph, let \(I_i=\sum_{x\in U_i}\operatorname{im} M(x)\) in the row mode. This sum requires only the slices of a basis of \(U_i\). For distinct \(i,j\), the intersection \(I_i\cap I_j\) contains exactly the common one-dimensional trace-block contribution and, when \(i,j\) form an edge, one \(e\)-space from each of the two oriented edge blocks. This follows by inspection of the disjoint row blocks and the two images of a regular multiplication edge block. Thus the larger intersection dimension \(2e+1\) detects the edges, versus dimension one for a nonedge. The graph is cubic, each block dimension is odd and \(n=7e\ell+1\) is a unit modulo \(q-1\) when \(\ell\) is a multiple of \(q-1\). The same diamond-chain swaps give exponentially many total permutations over repeated nonsplit factors. The trace term does not alter their feasibility.
