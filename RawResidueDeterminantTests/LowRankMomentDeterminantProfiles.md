# Raw determinant profiles from unordered low-rank points

Informal mathematics. The finite rank-locus calculation below works over every fixed finite field. Its unordered moment extension gives a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm over \(\mathbb F_3\) for a raw class of tensor pairs, with independent input bases and no TI queries. The class contains an explicit growing family with an absolutely irreducible determinant pencil, scalar full stabilizer, and scalar adjoints in all three pairs of modes. Matrix-mode dimensions in that family agree but differ from the label dimension.

## 1. Recover a finite low-rank locus by bounded-degree closure

Let \(M:F^\ell\to\operatorname{Mat}_{n_y,n_x}(F)\) be a raw matrix pencil, with \(F=\mathbb F_q\) fixed. Fix nonnegative \(r\) and degree bound \(D\) independent of the input length. In \(P=F[x_1,\ldots,x_\ell]\), let \(I\) be the ideal generated by the order-\(r+1\) minors and the field equations \(x_i^q-x_i\). If \(r+1\) exceeds a matrix dimension the minor list is empty. Every polynomial is represented in a monomial basis of degree at most \(D\).

Start with the span of all monomial multiples of the defining equations of total degree at most \(D\). Repeatedly intersect the current span with the polynomials of degree at most \(D-1\), multiply a basis of this intersection by each \(x_i\), and adjoin the products. Stop when the span \(I_D\) stabilizes. All added elements are in \(I\), and the number of strict increases is at most \(\binom{\ell+D}{D}\). Both the original bounded span and this closure are covariant under invertible \(F\)-linear substitutions in the labels and independent changes of the matrix-mode bases. Cauchy–Binet gives the minor-span covariance. The F-field equations are preserved by the substitution because \(q\)-Frobenius fixes the coefficients, and the closure uses all linear coordinates, so it is preserved as well.

Set \(V=P_{\le D}/I_D\). Require that classes of degree at most \(D-1\) span \(V\). Define \(m_i:V\to V\) by multiplication by \(x_i\) on such representatives, followed by projection. It is well-defined by the stabilized closure. Check that these operators commute, satisfy \(m_i^q=m_i\), and satisfy all the defining minor equations as matrix polynomials. These are explicit polynomial-time checks at fixed \(q,r,D\).

If the checks pass, \(V=P/I\). To see this, the inclusion-induced map \(\pi:V\to P/I\) is well-defined since \(I_D\subset I\). The operator checks give a map \(P/I\to V\), sending \(p\) to \(p(m_1,\ldots,m_\ell)[1]\). Multiplication on representatives gives \(\pi(m_i v)=x_i\pi(v)\), so its composition with \(\pi\) in the direction \(P/I\to P/I\) is the identity. Evaluation onto \(V\) is surjective because every monomial of degree at most \(D\) acts on \([1]\) by its own class, by induction on its degree. Thus they are inverse.

The quotient by the field equations alone is the product algebra of functions on \(F^\ell\), by repeated one-dimensional interpolation. Further quotienting by the minors leaves one \(F\) factor for each point of \(S_r=\{v\in F^\ell:\operatorname{rank}M(v)\le r\}\). We recover the points without enumerating \(F^\ell\). Refine the simultaneous eigenspaces of the commuting \(m_i\) successively, testing the \(q\) possible eigenvalues at each step. The resulting eigenvalue vectors are exactly the points \(S_r\), and there are at most \(\dim_F V\) nonzero spaces during each refinement. Thus the algorithm computes a polynomial-size exact point list on this certified class. It does not merely find a subset of low-rank points.

A weaker consequence works over every fixed field. If \(S_r\) spans the label space, choose a source basis from it and use ordinary TI queries to match those basis vectors one at a time, trying the polynomial-size target point list. Each pin is a linear arrow from a fixed line. After fixing the label map, constructive ordered module isomorphism gives a transporter. To recover the full determinant coset, use the same basis and the successive stabilizer chain on the source itself. At each prefix try each possible next image and construct one representative for every attainable image. The union of the representatives and the terminal stabilizer generates the source stabilizer. The terminal group has its label map fixed to the identity, so its determinant image is computed by the ordered-pencil end algebra as below. This gives a raw polynomial Turing reduction for the rank-locus class with spanning points. [Typed TI compiler, Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

## 2. Recover candidate label maps nonadaptively from unordered moments

For a pencil over \(F=\mathbb F_3\) that passes the rank-locus checks, remove zero from \(S_r\). Put \(k(v)=\operatorname{rank}M(v)\). Form the total moment
\[
 B_0=\sum_{v\in S_r\setminus\{0\}}vv^{\mathsf T}
\]
and the unary moments indexed by \(k(v)\). Also form pair moments indexed by the signature \((k(v),k(w),k(v+w))\):
\[
 B_\sigma=\sum_{\substack{v,w\in S_r\setminus\{0\}\\
 (k(v),k(w),k(v+w))=\sigma}}v w^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Sums of any of these moment classes are available at negligible extra cost. The entire list of individual ranks and rank triples is polynomial because the ranks are bounded by the mode dimensions and the point list is polynomial. After a genuine tensor transporter with label map \(f\), all the moments transform as \(B'_\sigma=fB_\sigma f^{\mathsf T}\). This follows by change of summation variables, including the rank of \(v+w\). There is no attempt to match or order individual points.

Suppose \(B_0\) is invertible and the simultaneous commutant of \(B_\sigma B_0^{-1}\), for all unary and pair moments, has dimension at most \(C\log_3 L\), for fixed \(C\) and raw input bit length \(L\ge2\). The tensor has a deterministic polynomial-time exact determinant-profile algorithm. Indeed the target must give the same certified rank-locus outcome under a genuine transporter. The relative operators are intertwined by \(f\). Solve their simultaneous linear intertwiner equations over \(F\). If there is one invertible solution \(f_0\), the whole solution space is its left translate of the source commutant. Thus discard dimensions above the bound and enumerate every element of the other spaces. At most \(L^C\) candidates are retained. Check invertibility and the actual original tensor equations.

Given one candidate label map \(f\), test simultaneous ordered module isomorphism of its matrix slices using the two vertex projections on \(X\oplus Y\) and each slice operator from \(X\) to \(Y\). This constructs an actual pair \((g,h)\) whenever any exists. All such pairs for this \(f\) are a coset of \(D^\times\) for the ordered source end algebra \(D\). Compute its radical \(J\) and the explicit quotient \(D/J=\prod_t\operatorname{Mat}_{s_t}(E_t)\). On each radical grade of \(J^aX\) and \(J^aY\), read the multiplicity of the natural simple of factor \(t\) from the dimension of its central-idempotent image divided by \(s_t[E_t:F]\). For the total multiplicities \(m_{X,t},m_{Y,t}\), its contributions to the determinant pair are \((u^{m_{X,t}},u^{m_{Y,t}})\) for all \(u\in F^\times\). This follows by the field norm of a determinant over \(E_t\), norm surjectivity and unit lifting through the nilpotent radical. The product over factors is computed inside the fixed group \((F^\times)^2\). Multiplying by the reference determinants and appending \(\det f\), then taking the union over successful \(f\), gives the exact profile. Inverting a determinant when converting a dualized tensor mode leaves the determinant-one test unchanged. [Brooksbank–Luks, constructive module isomorphism](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf), [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18 on finite-dimensional algebra decomposition](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#S3.SS5.SSS1)

This algorithm never queries ordinary TI. In particular it also yields a polynomial one-instance reduction on this class by outputting a fixed YES or NO ordinary-TI instance after computing the result.

## 3. A growing, irreducible, scalar-stabilizer family

Take positive odd \(m\), label spaces with coordinates \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\) and \(b_1,\ldots,b_l\), where \(k=14m\), \(l=11m\), and square matrix side \(n=154m+1\). Put the \(a\)-linear forms on the diagonal of a matrix. Include ten copies of each \(a_i\) in a contiguous block of ten consecutive positions, filling the first \(10k=140m\) diagonal positions. The tail contains one copy of each \(a_i+a_{i+1}\), \(1\le i<k\), and two extra copies of \(a_1\). Put the \(b\)-linear forms on the second cyclic matching, with the form in position \(j\) in row \(j\) and column \(j-1\) modulo \(n\). In its first \(13l=143m\) positions cycle through \(b_1,\ldots,b_l\) thirteen times. The tail contains one copy of each \(b_i+b_{i+1}\) and two extra copies of \(b_1\). Both lists have length \(n\). Call the resulting pencil \(M\).

The union of the two underlying matchings is one alternating cycle on the bipartite row and column vertices. Any proper square submatrix has at most one perfect matching. Indeed two distinct matchings would have a cycle in their symmetric difference, and the only cycle in the bipartite pattern is the whole one. In particular a chosen matching of size \(s<n\) gives an \(s\)-minor equal up to sign to the product of its chosen entries, without cancellation.

Every point supported on a single coordinate has rank at most 16. The exact ranks on the \(a\)-axes are \(13\) at \(a_1\), \(12\) at interior vertices of the path, and \(11\) at the far endpoint. On the \(b\)-axes they are \(16,15,14\) respectively. For a point supported on two or more label coordinates, choose two occupied coordinates. Two \(a\)'s give at least 20 matching edges from their private diagonal positions alone. Two \(b\)'s give at least 26 from the second matching. For one \(a\) and one \(b\), select the ten private \(a\) entries and then the \(b\) private edges not conflicting with them. The thirteen \(b\) private rows are spaced by \(l>10\), and their columns are the same set shifted by one. Therefore at most one of those rows and at most one column intersects the consecutive ten-position \(a\)-block. At least eleven \(b\) edges remain, for a total of at least 21. These matched entries are all pure private forms, so this lower rank bound holds regardless of other occupied labels. Thus the exact rank-at-most-16 locus consists of zero and the nonzero axis points.

The rank-locus certificate has \(D=19\). For every pair of distinct hidden coordinates \(x_i,x_j\), select 17 of those private matching positions. Both coordinates occur because a single private list has fewer than 17 entries. Their minor is \(\pm x_i^a x_j^b\) with \(a,b>0\) and \(a+b=17\). Multiply by at most one extra power of each variable to make both exponents odd, then reduce by \(x^3-x\) to obtain \(x_i x_j\) in the starting degree-19 span. The stabilized closure then contains every monomial of degree at most 19 involving two distinct coordinates. Reduction by the field equations reduces every remaining monomial to the span of \(1,x_i,x_i^2\). These classes are independent on the low-rank locus: evaluate at zero first, and then at \(\pm e_i\) for each \(i\). Hence the bounded quotient equals the full function algebra on those axis points, with representatives of degree at most two. Its computed multiplication matrices pass all the checks. The certificate calculation is covariant, so the argument proves successful extraction even after arbitrary changes of bases.

Unary moments over rank ranges \(11,12,13\) and \(14,15,16\) give minus the two label projection matrices. The two top-rank unary moments give minus the first coordinate projections within those two blocks. The total moment is \(-I\). For a pair moment restrict \(v,w\) to the same range and put weight one exactly when \(k(v)+k(w)-k(v+w)=1\). For distinct axes in that range which are adjacent on the path, their diagonal or offdiagonal supports share exactly the one path-edge entry. If their signs agree, this entry remains nonzero and the deficit is one. If opposite, it cancels and the deficit is two. For nonadjacent axes the deficit is zero. On the same axis the deficit is at least eleven, even if they have opposite signs. Thus each weight-one moment is minus the adjacency matrix of the corresponding path, on that label block and zero elsewhere. The adjacency of a path with a nonzero offdiagonal has the first coordinate as a cyclic column and cyclic row. Its powers on both sides of the first coordinate projection span the full matrix algebra on that block. Together with the two label projections, the joint commutant is just the two scalar blocks, of dimension two. This proves the polynomial algorithm hypothesis without choosing a coordinate frame from the raw input.

The formal determinant is absolutely irreducible. Its only two terms in the matching expansion are the products \(P_A(a)\) of all the diagonal entries and \(P_B(b)\) of all the offdiagonal entries, with the sign of the \(n\)-cycle equal to \(+1\) since \(n\) is odd. Both products are homogeneous of degree \(n\). Over the algebraic closure, put \(a_1=t\) and \(a_i=t u_i\) for \(i>1\). The determinant becomes \(t^nP_A(1,u)+P_B(b)\). It is irreducible as a polynomial in \(t\) over the field of rational functions in \(u,b\), by Eisenstein at the prime \(b_1+b_2\) in the coefficient polynomial ring in \(b\) over the field of rational functions in \(u\). That factor occurs exactly once in the constant term and does not divide the leading coefficient. In a factorization of the original determinant, no nonconstant factor can be independent of the \(a\)'s, since the coefficients as a polynomial in \(a\) include a nonzero constant from the top \(a\)-degree part. Each factor with positive \(a\)-degree retains positive \(t\)-degree under the substitution, since dehomogenization at \(a_1=1\) is injective on a fixed homogeneous \(a\)-degree. Thus a factorization would contradict Eisenstein.

The matrix-mode adjoint is scalar. At \(a_i=1,b_i=0\), the slice \(D_0\) is invertible and diagonal. Normalize on the left by \(D_0^{-1}\). The generated diagonal algebra contains a coordinate projection for the single position of the line \(a_1+a_2\). Different projective diagonal forms, once divided by their values at that slice, are distinct normalized linear functionals. For each other form use one normalized diagonal slice that separates it from \(a_1+a_2\), subtract its value at the other form, divide by the nonzero difference, and multiply these diagonal slices. The product is exactly the singleton coordinate projection. At \(a_i=0,b_i=1\) the normalized slice is an invertible weighted cyclic shift. Its powers on both sides of that projection produce all matrix units up to nonzero scalar. The normalized slices therefore generate all matrices, so their commutant is scalar and the ordered pencil end algebra is scalar.

A full tensor stabilizer has label map scalar on each of the two label blocks by the moment calculation. Its two scalar values \(c_A,c_B\in F^\times\) multiply the two detector polynomials by \(c_A^n,c_B^n\), respectively. A tensor stabilizer must multiply the determinant polynomial by one common scalar. Since \(P_A,P_B\) have disjoint positive-degree variable supports and \(n\) is odd, it follows that \(c_A=c_B\). The matrix-mode adjoint computation then forces the matrix factors to be scalar as well. The tensor is concise: a single invertible slice gives conciseness in the matrix modes, and the private diagonal and offdiagonal forms give injectivity on the labels. An invertible element of a left or right idealizer would induce a stabilizer fixing the other matrix mode individually, so all idealizer units are scalar. A finite-dimensional unital algebra over \(\mathbb F_3\) with only the two scalar units is \(\mathbb F_3\): a nonzero radical contributes additional units of the form \(1+j\), and any larger finite field, nontrivial matrix factor, or product of two simple factors contributes too many units. Conciseness identifies those two idealizers with the other two pair adjoints. This proves all three scalar-adjoint claims.

## 4. Provenance and scope

The bounded minor identities have a direct source interface in Hex development commit \(3a1b9813b1d1876ae0c89f5cfc20da05547bd854\), especially the exact definitions of minor lists, row/column expansions, and specialization to a rank locus. The bounded-degree saturation and moment tests above supply the new complexity and transporter arguments. [Hex minor expansions](https://github.com/kim-em/hex-dev/blob/3a1b9813b1d1876ae0c89f5cfc20da05547bd854/HexDeterminantalIdeal/Minors.lean), [Hex rank locus specialization](https://github.com/kim-em/hex-dev/blob/3a1b9813b1d1876ae0c89f5cfc20da05547bd854/HexDeterminantalIdeal/MvPoly.lean)

This sufficient class and family require no determinantal factors, supplied basis, or oracle answers. The actual physical determinant profile is checked by module isomorphism. Universal bounded-degree certification and small moment commutants on arbitrary raw scalar-stabilizer tensors remain open. There is no new Lean verification or priority claim.
