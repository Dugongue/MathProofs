# Determinant-constrained tensor isomorphism from radical filtrations

Informal proof over a fixed finite field \(F=\mathbb F_q\). For a dense bimap \(T:X_1\times X_2\to X_3\), the zero tensor and dimensions of zero are decided directly. Otherwise use the action \(g\cdot T(x,y)=g_3T(g_1^{-1}x,g_2^{-1}y)\) of the three independent mode maps. A covariant tensor uses the inverse-transpose convention on the first two modes, which inverts those two determinant coordinates. Fix a character \(\chi:(F^\times)^3\to F^\times\) and a target value \(\eta\).

## 1. Canonical radical filtration

The following constructions apply to any polynomially extracted unital algebra \(R_T\subseteq\prod_i\mathrm{End}_F(X_i)\) which every tensor transporter carries to the other side by simultaneous conjugation and for which \([R_T,R_T]\subseteq D_T\), where \(D_T\) is the Lie algebra of bimap derivations. All brackets here are linear spans.

Two raw-input choices are available. The first is the associative envelope of the derived algebra of \(D_T\), with the stated commutator inclusion tested by linear algebra. The second is
\[
R_T=\{(a,\lambda I,c):cT(x,y)=T(ax,y)\text{ for every }x,y\}.
\]
The latter is the algebra of endomorphisms of the ordered slice maps on modes one and three, with a separate copy of \(F\) on the second mode. The slice span condition is intrinsic under an arbitrary second-mode basis change. The algebra is closed under simultaneous composition and contains the identity. A commutator belongs to \(D_T\), because it has zero middle component and still intertwines all slices. This choice allows an arbitrary radical. There is a second construction fixing the first mode. By dualizing and reordering the bimap conventions one may also use the opposite-algebra slice adjoint for the third fixed mode.

Write \(J=\operatorname{rad}(R_T)\) and \(S_T=R_T/J\). Form, for each mode and each nonzero grade, \(V_{i\ell}=J^\ell X_i/J^{\ell+1}X_i\), including \(J^0X_i=X_i\). Compute these by multiplication and linear algebra. The simultaneous action of \(S_T\) on all these grades is faithful. Indeed the kernel of the action of \(R_T\) on all grades takes each filtration step into its successor. It is a nilpotent ideal in the faithfully represented algebra, so lies in \(J\). The reverse containment holds by construction. A tensor transporter therefore induces one well-defined algebra map \(S_T\to S_{T'}\) and compatible isomorphisms on every grade. The determinant of each raw mode map is the product of its grade determinants multiplied by the ratio of the known target and source adapted-basis determinants. This is block-triangular determinant multiplication.

Compute \(S_T=\prod_j M_{d_j}(E_j)\) explicitly and write \(e_j=[E_j:F]\). The required deterministic finite-field algorithms supply the decomposition, forward map and lifts. For mode \(i\), grade \(\ell\), factor \(j\), write \(m_{i\ell j}\) for the multiplicity of the natural simple module. It is obtained by a matrix unit \(e_{11}^{(j)}\) and the \(E_j\)-dimension of its image on that grade. The factor type is \((d_j,e_j,(m_{i\ell j})_{i,\ell})\), padding vanished grades by zero. A type mismatch rejects the input. Otherwise use correspondences of equal types to obtain polynomial-time \(F\)-linear reference grade maps in standard module bases. For this, find an \(E_j\)-basis of the matrix-unit image by greedy \(F\)-linear independence, then apply all \(e_{a1}^{(j)}\) to get the other natural coordinates. Identify fields of the same degree by finite-field isomorphism. [Constructive decomposition, Ivanyos–Qiao Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03495#page=18), [Lenstra's finite-field isomorphism algorithm](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1991b/art.pdf)

## 2. Normalize the large natural factors using genuine tensor symmetries

For every simple factor in \(S_{T'}\), a natural-coordinate \(\mathrm{SL}_{d_j}(E_j)\) map on all its grades has a lift to an actual tensor autotopism of determinant one on each raw mode. A section \(S_{T'}\to R_{T'}\) exists by Wedderburn–Malcev over the perfect field \(F\). Its computation is unnecessary for the decision. For a natural transvection use the lifts \(X=\lambda e^{(j)}_{ab}\), \(H=e^{(j)}_{aa}-e^{(j)}_{bb}\), \(a\ne b\). Both belong to \([R_{T'},R_{T'}]\), so both are derivations of the full target tensor \(U=T'\). They satisfy \(X^2=0\), \(HX=X\) in all three components.

To verify the transvection, put \(y_0=U(X_1x,X_2y)\). The \(X\)-derivation identity applied to \((x,X_2y)\) gives \(y_0=X_3U(x,X_2y)\), hence \(H_3y_0=y_0\). The \(H\)-derivation identity applied to \((X_1x,X_2y)\) gives \(H_3y_0=2y_0\). Thus \(y_0=0\) in every characteristic. The same \(X\)-identity shows \(X_3U(X_1x,y)=X_3U(x,X_2y)=0\). Expanding proves \((1+X_3)U((1-X_1)x,(1-X_2)y)=U(x,y)\). Its mode determinants are one because \(X_i^2=0\). Transvections generate the natural special-linear group. Their products on the raw tensor give the desired genuine extensions and preserve the original determinant labels.

An \(F\)-algebra isomorphism \(S_T\to S_{T'}\) is a permutation of equal types, followed by independent center Frobenius powers and inner conjugations by matrices \(P_j\) over \(E_j\). The matrix-unit proof of innerness applies after removing the center map. Up to conjugations by target natural special-linear matrices, one need only consider diagonal \(P_j=\mathrm{diag}(a_j,1,\ldots,1)\) for representatives in \(E_j^\times/(E_j^\times)^{d_j}\). Indeed scaling \(P_j\) by a nonzero scalar leaves the conjugation unchanged and multiplies its determinant by a \(d_j\)-th power. Having chosen that scalar, multiply the new representative by a special-linear matrix to attain the chosen diagonal lift. Section 2's genuine autotopisms perform this change in any actual transporter, preserving its determinant data.

The representatives can be constructed deterministically in polynomial time at fixed \(q\), even if \(E_j^\times\) is exponentially large. Let \(k=\gcd(d_j,q^{e_j}-1)\). Factor \(k\le d_j\) by trial division. For each prime \(\ell\mid k\), factor \(X^\ell-1\) to get a primitive \(\ell\)-th root and successively factor \(X^\ell-\zeta\) to take \(\ell\)-th roots of the current root. Stop at the \(\ell\)-valuation of \(q^{e_j}-1\). The resulting element has full \(\ell\)-primary order, so its image generates the \(\ell\)-primary part in the quotient of order \(k\). Multiply these elements and take the first \(k\) powers. Every factoring degree here is at most \(d_j\). Factoring separable polynomials over \(E_j\) at fixed characteristic \(p\) can be performed by computing, in their quotient algebra, the \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear fixed space of the \(p\)-Frobenius. In a squarefree quotient this is a product of copies of \(\mathbb F_p\), one per field factor. When there are at least two factors, a nonscalar fixed vector has distinct values somewhere, and gcd with its differences from each of the \(p\) scalars splits the polynomial. Iterate. This proves the needed running time without assuming a primitive element of the full multiplicative group.

If \(b_\tau\) is the number of simple factors of type \(\tau\), the number of pinned quotient-algebra branches is
\[
P=\left(\prod_j e_j\gcd(d_j,q^{e_j}-1)\right)\prod_\tau b_\tau!.
\]
This can be polynomial even when the raw normalizer orbit and the densor dimension are large. On each branch require the *same* graded action \(g_{i\ell}\) induced by the raw transporter to satisfy
\[
g_{i\ell}\rho_{i\ell}(a)=\rho'_{i\ell}(\psi(a))g_{i\ell}
\]
for the explicit branch map \(\psi:S_T\to S_{T'}\) and every basis element \(a\). These are ordered linear-operator decorations. Link the grade action to the raw action through inclusion and quotient maps of the filtration. The original bimap stays in the instance. Therefore projected witnesses retain the original tensor equation and, by faithfulness, induce exactly the prescribed \(\psi\).

## 3. Read only the multiplicity determinants, even with many radical layers

In a branch, choose the reference \(h_{i\ell}\) implementing \(\psi\) on each grade. An arbitrary feasible grade map is \(h_{i\ell} k_{i\ell}\), where \(k_{i\ell}\) centralizes the source \(S_T\)-action. Its determinant is
\[
\det_F(h_{i\ell})\prod_j
       N_{E_j/F}(\det_{E_j}k_{i\ell j})^{d_j},
\qquad k_{i\ell j}\in \mathrm{GL}_{m_{i\ell j}}(E_j).
\]
This follows by tensoring the multiplicity action with the \(d_j\) natural coordinates and restricting scalars. Set \(\delta_0\) to the triple of the reference products times the raw adapted-basis calibrations. A separate multiplication-predicate instance on the determinant lines enforces the desired \(\chi\) on the genuine raw \(g\).

More explicitly, attach the source and target matrix-unit-image spaces. The target copy uses the source scalar algebra via the known \(\psi\), so the induced map on those spaces is \(E_j\)-linear. Choose reference-aligned bases using \(h_{i\ell}\). Attach exterior chains over \(E_j\). All wedge maps are bilinear over \(F\), scalar multiplication on each sort is also a marked bilinear map, and the field action is fixed by the given ordered \(F\)-basis. On the top line the induced scalar is exactly \(\det_{E_j} k_{i\ell j}\). Its norm is enforced by copies of the line connected via explicit \(q\)-Frobenius maps. These force the successive scalars \(\lambda,\lambda^q,\ldots,\lambda^{q^{e_j-1}}\). Binary multiplication then gives their product, and the \(F\)-line embedded in the final \(E_j\)-line reads this product. The fixed exponent and products from \(\chi\) are enforced by ordered binary field multiplications and a marked terminal scalar. This costs \(L^{O(1)}2^{\max_{i,\ell,j}m_{i\ell j}}\), including the fields and the norm chains. The number of layers is polynomial and their widths are not summed in the exponential.

For polynomial \(P\) and \(m_{i\ell j}=O(\log L)\) this gives a deterministic many-one ordinary TI reduction. Each branch is an ordinary tensor-system test and every witness projects to an actual determinant-constrained transporter. Conversely use Section 2 to normalize a genuine witness into the branch, after which the quotient maps, determinant lines and norms take their induced values. Ordered lists of linear and bilinear maps are encoded by tagged occurrence spaces, algebra idempotents and linkage maps to the original sorts. Index tags are fixed by an ordered shift and a first-index mark. This produces a fixed signature of tensor order at most three. Use the tensor-system compiler. Polynomially many branches are packed by balanced OR. In an OR gate for type equalities \(a=b\) or \(c=d\), equate the two unordered multisets \(\{(a,c),(b,d)\}\) and \(\{(a,d),(b,c)\}\) of ordered pairs. Straight and crossed matchings are exactly the two alternatives. A balanced tree uses polynomially many copies and is encoded with tagged node spaces and incidence maps. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, tensor-system reduction and Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2)

## 4. Remove wide multiplicity blocks using exact correction subgroups

Compute from the target tensor the genuine fixed-mode autotopisms that induce identity on \(S_{T'}\). For example, when the output is fixed, use pairs \((a,b)\) satisfying
\[
T'(ax,y)=T'(x,by)
\]
with product \((a,b)(a',b')=(aa',b'b)\); the real two mode maps associated to an invertible pair are \(a,b^{-1}\). Require both coordinates to preserve their intrinsic filtrations, and on every grade require commuting with the represented algebra \(S_{T'}\). These additional conditions are linear and closed under composition and inversion. They form a unital subalgebra of the adjoint pair algebra, with invertible elements exactly these genuine fixed-output corrections. For a fixed input, the corresponding two moving maps satisfy the direct intertwiner equation for all its slices and form an ordinary product algebra. The faithful grade action ensures that the commuting conditions give the identity action on the quotient algebra.

Compute the determinant image of each unit group from its own radical filtration and semisimple factors. For a simultaneous algebra representation of a unit, a simple factor \(M_d(E)\) contributes \(N_{E/F}(\det_E u)^b\) where \(b\) is the total multiplicity of its simple module on the indicated representation's radical grades. The norm is surjective and every quotient unit lifts to a unit. For an opposite factor, transpose to get a homomorphic representation and invert the determinant as dictated by \(b^{-1}\). The three-coordinate group has constant size, so images and their generated subgroup are computed by finite group multiplication. A desired image value can also be materialized. Construct a diagonal quotient unit using a norm representative (the same primary-root procedure for the primes dividing \(q-1\) finds a norm generator), lift it, and compose the bounded number of images needed in the finite group. Add any explicitly known genuine correction maps that preserve the quotient markings. Denote the generated character subgroup by \(C\).

A multiplicity block \((i,\ell,j)\) is harmless modulo \(C\) if the character contribution of its determinant subgroup \((F^\times)^{d_j}\) on mode \(i\) lies in \(C\). Omit its exterior and norm attachments. For the remaining blocks compute as in Section 3, but enforce the character equation in \(F^\times/C\), using the \(|C|\)-th power of both sides. A successful branch has its actual \(\chi(g)\) in \(\eta C\), because all omitted contributions lie in \(C\). Composing with a genuine correction yields a transporter with exact character \(\eta\). This correction preserves the same \(\psi\), and is constructible from the matrices already computed. Every truly qualifying transporter gives one successful branch by normalizing and then passing to the quotient. Thus only the multiplicity blocks whose determinant behavior is still visible modulo \(C\) need logarithmic width. If no such blocks exist, the whole branch needs only the tensor and its marked quotient actions.

Within a fixed quotient branch, additional ordered symmetric and alternating forms over odd \(F\) can also be retained. Replace each two-mode adjoint algebra by the form-adjoint pairs in both modes. Intersect the linear filtration and intertwiner conditions with their images under the involution. A unitary element and its adjoint inverse satisfy the same conditions, so the construction has exactly the genuine form-preserving corrections. Compute its image by the \(*\)-algebra determinant theorem for exchanged, unitary, symplectic and orthogonal quotient blocks. The same subgroup proof then applies. The relaxed image remains a valid upper bound when extra forms are present. [Ivanyos–Qiao on \(*\)-algebra isometries](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03495)

## 5. Remove all branches when the full grade relaxation is saturated

Without pinning any \(\psi\), the complete profile of the *relaxed grade intertwiners* is a coset \(\delta_0 U\), where \(\delta_0\) is any typed-factor match and its basis calibration. The subgroup \(U\) is computable without enumerating elements of any large matrix group. Include the following generators over all factors \(j\) and equal-type classes.

- A centralizer factor on mode \(i\) contributes the independent subgroup \((F^\times)^{d_j}\) in that coordinate whenever \(m_{i\ell j}>0\).
- A common natural matrix on \(j\) contributes the vectors with \(i\)-th entry \(a^{\sum_\ell m_{i\ell j}}\) as \(a\) ranges over \(F^\times\). Norm surjectivity gives exactly this image.
- The simultaneous \(q\)-Frobenius on \(j\) contributes \(i\)-th entry \((-1)^{(e_j-1)d_j\sum_\ell m_{i\ell j}}\). Frobenius on \(E_j\) has determinant \((-1)^{e_j-1}\), as is seen on a normal basis or by cycling the geometric split coordinates.
- One equal-type factor swap contributes \(i\)-th entry \((-1)^{e_jd_j\sum_\ell m_{i\ell j}}\), the determinant of swapping their identically sized grade blocks.

Every semisimple quotient algebra automorphism and accompanying grade normalizer is generated as above, using coherent field and module coordinates. These generators therefore give the exact relaxed determinant subgroup. Constituent group sizes do not enter the arithmetic cost. Compute the subgroup \(H\le U\) of determinants generated by genuine raw fixed-mode autotopisms, this time without requiring their quotient action to be identity. It can also include explicitly exhibited autotopisms. If \(\chi(H)=\chi(U)\), then the raw unmarked ordinary TI query itself suffices, after checking \(\eta\in\chi(\delta_0)\chi(U)\). Indeed the existence of an ordinary transporter gives one element of the relaxed coset. Every character value in that coset is obtained by correcting it with a genuine autotopism. Failure of the coset test gives a fixed negative pair. This criterion has no width or number-of-factors hypotheses.

## 6. Suppress inner and permutation branches using exhibited autotopisms

For the canonical fixed-middle slice algebra, all quotient-inner changes can be suppressed. Every \(P_j\in\mathrm{GL}_{d_j}(E_j)\) lifts through the unit epimorphism to a pair commuting with the ordered slices. Acting by that pair in modes one and three while fixing mode two is a genuine tensor autotopism. Its simultaneous conjugation on \(S_{T'}\) has exactly the chosen inner action on each factor. Its determinant characters form a subgroup \(C_{\rm in}\), computed by the same norm-and-multiplicity formula for units. In particular there is no inner \(d_j\)-power coset enumeration if one works modulo this known correction subgroup. The number of quotient branches becomes \(\prod_j e_j\prod_\tau b_\tau!\).

There is a further constructive reduction in this count when a type has many factors. For each pair of equal-type factors construct the coordinate interchange on all target grades in coherent standard module bases, and lift it block-diagonally in the chosen raw adapted bases. Test its full raw tensor equation. Each successful map is a genuine autotopism, acting as the named transposition on the quotient with its field and natural coordinates unchanged. Form the graph of these verified swaps on the \(b_\tau\) factors of this type. If its components have sizes \(c_{\tau,1},\ldots,c_{\tau,k}\), the permutation images of the exhibited maps contain the product of the symmetric groups of these components. This is because edge transpositions of a connected graph generate its full symmetric group. The factor \(b_\tau!\) can be replaced by
\[
\frac{b_\tau!}{\prod_u c_{\tau,u}!}.
\]
Enumerate these cosets by assigning the source indices to the target components of the given sizes and then using the canonical increasing matching inside each component. With the certified maps, any existing transporter is changed into an enumerated permutation by left composition.

For these two suppressions enlarge \(C\) in Section 4 by the exact determinant characters of the normalization maps. Normalizing a genuine witness may now change its character by an element of \(C\), which preserves the tested coset. Conversely any accepted witness can be corrected by an explicit product of genuine maps to recover the exact target character. The final corrected map can change the quotient pin, and it retains the original tensor equation. The extra corrections are computed by unit lifts or directly tested swaps, so this composition does not need another TI call. A bound for the remaining quotient branches and the unsuppressed exterior widths suffices. When carrying additional forms, use only corrections preserving them, including the natural lifts used to normalize inner maps. This preservation is automatic if every tensor autotopism preserves the ordered forms. For forms on a fixed branch supplied independently of the tensor, Section 4's \(*\)-algebra image computes the branch-preserving corrections without promising every natural normalization preserves the forms.

## 7. A genuinely nonsemisimple full-width family

Take \(A=F[t]/t^r\) and \(T:A^n\times A\to A^n\), \(T(x,a)=a x\), with \(n\ge2\), \((q-1)\mid n\), and \(\gcd(r,q-1)=1\). The canonical fixed-middle slice algebra is \(M_n(A)\times F\). In fact the slice of \(1\) forces the input and output maps to coincide, and commuting with all other slices gives exactly \(\mathrm{End}_A(A^n)\). The radical of the slice algebra is \(tM_n(A)\times0\) and has length \(r\), which grows without bound. On modes one and three there are \(r\) copies of the natural \(M_n(F)\)-module, one per grade. Mode two has \(r\) scalar multiplicities at grade zero. The quotient branch count is at most \(q-1\). The pinned-branch centralizer determinant group is trivial on modes one and three, since \(n\) is a multiple of \(q-1\), and is full on mode two. The genuine correction \((s^{-1}I,sI,I)\) has exactly these possibilities as \(s\in F^\times\) varies. Thus the full large scalar multiplicity can be omitted.

In fact the full grade relaxation is saturated as well. The only extra determinant vectors come from simultaneous \(M_n(F)\) natural matrices and equal \((a^r,1,a^r)\) for \(a\in F^\times\), and these are realized by genuine common \(A\)-linear changes of basis fixing mode two. There are no factor permutations or center Frobenius variations. Consequently the no-enumeration reduction applies to such tensors and their raw coordinate changes, with all dimensions and radical length unbounded.

## 8. Intersect independent relaxed profiles before using determinant corrections

Several intrinsic algebras can be used simultaneously without choosing a shared decomposition or estimating a product of branch counts. Apply the unpinned calculation of Section 5 separately to each, obtaining \(\delta_j U_j\), and intersect these finite subsets of \((F^\times)^3\). If any type comparison fails or the intersection is empty, no raw transporter exists. On a feasible pair, fix one actual determinant triple \(\delta\); then the intersection equals \(\delta\,\bigcap_j U_j\). If the genuine correction subgroup \(H\) has \(\chi(H)=\chi(\bigcap_j U_j)\), testing \(\eta\) in the character image of the intersection and using the bare raw ordinary TI instance gives the desired reduction. This saturation test applies even if every single relaxation is too large. It uses only covariance, the faithful graded actions, and their exact relaxed profiles. It does not require the commutator-derivation inclusions unless one also uses the pinned special-linear normalization.

For a concrete instance take the raw basis changes of the rectangular matrix multiplication bimap \(M_{a,b}\times M_{b,c}\to M_{a,c}\), with \(a,b,c\) positive multiples of \(q-1\). Take \(a,b,c\ge 2\) so the matrix factors and fixed-mode scalar factors have different simple-algebra types. The two moving modes when mode two is fixed share the natural action of \(M_a(F)\), with multiplicities \(b\) and \(c\). This follows by applying the slice intertwining to all elementary rectangular matrices, which forces the same row-coordinate map on the natural indices. The fixed first mode gives \(M_c(F)\) on the analogous column indices, with multiplicities \(b\) and \(a\). The fixed output mode gives the common \(M_b(F)\) on the contracted indices after dualizing one moving coordinate, with multiplicities \(a\) and \(c\). In each case the independent multiplicity determinants have exponent equal to the shared natural dimension, and the natural determinants have exponents equal to these two multiplicities. All these exponents vanish modulo \(q-1\). So each relaxed determinant group has arbitrary coordinate only in its fixed mode. Their intersection is trivial. Consequently one can recover the unique determinant triple of any feasible pair up to the exact calibration from these three independent computations. This needs no classification of the actual tensor's automorphism group and is unchanged by arbitrary raw basis changes.

## 9. Scope and earlier work

These results apply to the residue tensors in the truncated triangular normalization, and the exact determinant formula there returns the multiplication-tensor character. They retain the same full raw transporter through all filtration and tensor constraints. The source-and-target raw bases only enter by explicit basis calibrations. The federated MathProof DAG search recovered the existing fixed-mode determinant image machinery, radical layers, typed compiler and historical densor benchmark. The derivation-envelope calculation in the preceding work supplied the all-characteristic elementary transvection identity used here. [Brooksbank–Maglione–Wilson, derivation-densor construction](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.04046), [Brooksbank–Maglione–Wilson, inner tensor autotopisms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00275)

The reductions cover arbitrary radicals under the stated width, quotient-branch, or saturation hypotheses. A general residue tensor may have only scalar fixed-mode adjoints and a scalar derived envelope. Then all grades can be large and the genuine correction images can be strictly smaller than the relaxed character image. The universal polynomial-size many-one reduction remains open. The statements here have informal proofs.
