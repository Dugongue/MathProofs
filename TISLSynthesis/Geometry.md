# Determinant cosets for tensor isomorphism with a known projective mode

Let $F=\mathbb F_q$ be an explicitly represented finite field. Write a tensor as a linear family $M_T:W\to\operatorname{Hom}_F(U,V)$, so a transporter from $T$ to $T'$ is a triple $(A,B,C)$ satisfying
$$
 B M_T(w)=M_{T'}(Cw)A\qquad(w\in W).
$$
Dualizing modes in the ordinary tensor convention does not change a determinant-one condition. All three dimensions and the field are preserved when comparing the inputs.

**Theorem.** Given any projective class $[C]$ of invertible third-mode maps, one can decide in deterministic time polynomial in the dense input length and $\log q$ whether there is a transporter with all three determinants one and third map in $[C]$. The same algorithm tests any prescribed triple of nonzero determinants. It uses ordinary module isomorphism and linear algebra over $F$, integer Smith form, and finite-field powers. No field extension, discrete logarithm, or finite-field polynomial factorization is needed.

Consequently any polynomial-size computable complete list of third-mode projective candidates gives a deterministic polynomial-time decision algorithm and a same-field many-one reduction to ordinary tensor isomorphism on that class. In particular this holds over a fixed finite field for unrestricted tensors whose minimum mode dimension is $O(\sqrt{\log L})$, where $L$ is the dense input length. It also holds with unbounded mode dimensions for the determinant-factor frame class below.

## The fixed third-mode transporter

Fix a representative $C_0$, and compare the two tuples of arrows $M_T(e_j)$ and $M_{T'}(C_0e_j)$. Put two vertex idempotents and $d=\dim W$ arrows from the first vertex to the second on $U\oplus V$. The idempotents project to the two summands and the arrows act by those matrices from $U$ to $V$. These are modules for the same finite-dimensional path algebra. A module isomorphism is exactly an invertible pair $(A_0,B_0)$ with the required equations for $C_0$. The constructive module isomorphism algorithm of Brooksbank and Luks decides existence and, on yes instances, supplies one such pair in polynomially many field operations. Rescaling $C_0$ can be absorbed into $B_0$, so a negative answer excludes the entire projective class. [Brooksbank and Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

Let
$$
 E=\{(P,Q)\in\operatorname{End}(U)\times\operatorname{End}(V):
       Q M_T(e_j)=M_T(e_j)P\text{ for every }j\}.
$$
Linear equations give a basis, and componentwise composition gives structure constants. A unit of this algebra is exactly an invertible pair satisfying the displayed conditions, because the block inverse also intertwines. All transporters with $C=\lambda C_0$, $\lambda\in F^\times$, are exactly
$$
 (A_0P,\ \lambda B_0 Q,\ \lambda C_0),\qquad (P,Q)\in E^\times.
$$
This follows by substituting into the equations and by composing any other solution with the inverse of the chosen transporter.

Let $J=\operatorname{rad}E$ be the Jacobson radical and write
$$
 B_E=E/J\cong\prod_\alpha M_{m_\alpha}(F_\alpha)
$$
with $F_\alpha$ finite extensions of $F$. This is Artin–Wedderburn and the commutativity of finite division rings. Regard $M_1=U$ and $M_2=V$ as left $E$-modules. In the direct sum $\widetilde M_i=\bigoplus_{k\geq0}J^kM_i/J^{k+1}M_i$, let $\mu_{i\alpha}$ be the number of simple modules from factor $\alpha$. These modules are direct sums of copies of the standard $F_\alpha^{m_\alpha}$. For a unit $e\in E$ with image $(g_\alpha)_\alpha$, the determinant on $M_i$ is the product of its determinants on the filtration quotients. On a simple factor, restriction from $F_\alpha$ to $F$ gives the field norm of the determinant. Thus
$$
  \det_F(e|_{M_i})
       =\prod_\alpha N_{F_\alpha/F}(\det_{F_\alpha}g_\alpha)^{\mu_{i\alpha}}.
$$
All norm values occur independently, since matrix determinants and finite-field norms are surjective, and every unit of $E/J$ lifts to a unit of $E$ by nilpotence of $J$. Hence the determinant triples of the transporters over $[C]$ are exactly the coset of $(\det A_0,\det B_0,\det C_0)$ generated by the exponent vectors
$$
  (\mu_{1\alpha},\mu_{2\alpha},0)\quad\text{for all }\alpha,
  \qquad (0,\dim V,d).
$$
An exponent vector $(a,b,c)$ here permits all triples $(t^a,t^b,t^c)$ with $t\in F^\times$.

## Computing the exponent vectors

Two elementary procedures suffice to extract the required information without explicitly decomposing finite field extensions.

**The radical.** The following trace-power procedure uses the characteristic $p$ of $F$ and its inverse Frobenius. For a two-sided ideal $I$ of $E$ containing $J$, take the reciprocal characteristic polynomial
$$
 D_a(z)=\det(1-zL_a)\qquad(a\in I)
$$
using left multiplication on the original regular module $E$. Its product over the simple factors in a composition series has the reciprocal polynomial of the simple module of factor $\alpha$ with a positive multiplicity $h_\alpha$. Positivity follows because every simple factor already occurs in $E/J$. The factors on which $I$ has zero image contribute $1$. For all the others let $v=\min_\alpha v_p(h_\alpha)$.

For $k<v$, the coefficient of $z^{p^k}$ in $D_a$ is zero for every $a\in I$, because it is a $p^v$-th power. The negative inverse $p^v$-Frobenius of the coefficient of $z^{p^v}$ is
$$
 \tau(a)=
 \sum_{\substack{\alpha\text{ active}\\v_p(h_\alpha)=v}}
  (h_\alpha/p^v)\operatorname{Tr}_{F_\alpha/F}
                           (\operatorname{tr}(a_\alpha)).
$$
Indeed taking the $p^v$-th root of the reciprocal polynomial replaces each multiplicity by $h_\alpha/p^v$, and its linear coefficient is the corresponding ordinary trace sum. This expression is a nonzero $F$-linear functional on $I$, since each matrix trace composed with finite-field trace is nonzero, the displayed coefficients are units, and $I/J$ is the product of the active factors. Consequently $v$ is found by scanning the coefficients of $z^{p^k}$ on a basis of $I$, for all $p^k\leq\dim E$. If none is nonzero, then $I=J$. The kernel of the bilinear form $(a,b)\mapsto\tau(ab)$ on $I$ removes exactly the displayed minimal-valuation factors. For this, each trace pairing on a matrix algebra over a finite separable field is nondegenerate. Its kernel therefore is again a two-sided ideal containing $J$. Starting at $I=E$ and iterating terminates in at most $\dim E$ steps, with $J$. Characteristic polynomials, kernel calculations, and inverse Frobenius powers over the given finite field take polynomial time. This treatment includes characteristics that kill the unadjusted regular trace. [Friedl and Rónyai, Polynomial time solutions of some problems in computational algebra](https://doi.org/10.1145/22145.22162) · [Rónyai, Computing the structure of finite algebras](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S074771710880017X)

**Center ranks.** Compute $Z=Z(B_E)$ by commutator equations. It is a product of finite fields. At each field component $F_\alpha$, the dimensions over that component of the three $Z$-modules $B_E,\widetilde M_1,\widetilde M_2$ are
$$
  m_\alpha^2,\qquad m_\alpha\mu_{1\alpha},\qquad m_\alpha\mu_{2\alpha}.
$$
These three integers determine the needed exponent vector. It suffices to stratify the components by their three ranks, since repeating an exponent vector does not change the generated subgroup.

To obtain the ranks by linear algebra, present each module over $Z$ using its $F$-basis as generators, and an $F$-basis of the kernel of the resulting map $Z^s\to M$ as relations. These relations span the kernel over $Z$ as well. Compute the componentwise ranks of the relation matrix by elimination over the product of fields. A pivot $a\in Z$ has an idempotent support obtained by decomposing $1$ in $\ker(m_a)\oplus\operatorname{im}(m_a)$ for multiplication $m_a$, where the image component is the idempotent. On this component $a$ is invertible, and the inverse is obtained by a linear solve. On the complementary central component the pivot is zero. Split along the support whenever both parts are nonzero, and continue. The number of nonzero disjoint central parts is bounded by $\dim_F Z$. Each elimination on a part uses a polynomial number of field operations. Performing it successively for the three relation matrices gives a finite partition on which all three ranks are constant. Subtracting relation ranks from the numbers of generators, taking the indicated integer square root, and dividing the other two ranks by that root give the $\mu_{i\alpha}$. This computes the needed list without splitting a product of indistinguishable field factors.

## Testing the determinant coset without logarithms

Set $h=q-1$. Form the integer lattice $L_E\subseteq\mathbb Z^3$ spanned by the displayed exponent vectors and $h\mathbb Z^3$. Compute Smith form with row transformation $P\in\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb Z)$, so $P L_E=\operatorname{diag}(b_1,b_2,b_3)\mathbb Z^3$. Each positive $b_i$ divides $h$, since $P$ preserves $h\mathbb Z^3$. For a proposed determinant triple divide componentwise by the representative triple, obtaining $a=(a_1,a_2,a_3)\in(F^\times)^3$. The coset test is
$$
 \left(\prod_{j=1}^3 a_j^{P_{ij}}\right)^{h/b_i}=1
         \quad(i=1,2,3).
$$
To verify it, choose a generator of the cyclic group $F^\times$ abstractly. The row transformation acts on any corresponding exponent vector, and a coordinate is a multiple of $b_i$ modulo $h$ exactly when the displayed power is one. No generator must be found. Integer Smith transformations and powers have polynomial bit cost. All other spaces used above have dimension polynomial in the dense input size and $\log q$ is the bit scale for field arithmetic. This proves the theorem, including simultaneous treatment of all scalar lifts of $C_0$.

## Obtaining projective candidates from determinant factors

For arbitrary tensors, one can enumerate the $q^{d^2}$ matrices on the smallest mode and run the fixed-class test on the invertible ones. This gives a deterministic $q^{O(d^2)}\operatorname{poly}(L,\log q)$ algorithm and the stated fixed-field small-mode bound. A stronger case uses a frame intrinsic to a square matrix family with a supplied invertible slice. The next conditions and constructions apply for unbounded $d$.

For each input let $M(w_0)$ be a supplied invertible matrix, let $N=\dim U=\dim V$, and let
$$
 D_j=M(w_0)^{-1}M(e_j),\qquad j=1,\ldots,d.
$$
Require that the $D_j$ be simultaneously upper triangularizable over $F$. Over a variable finite field, a supplied complete invariant flag verifies this by polynomial-time linear algebra. Over a fixed field it can also be recognized and constructed. To find a common eigenline, successively intersect with all ordinary eigenspaces $\ker(D_j-\lambda I)$, $\lambda\in F$, keeping the nonzero intersections. At each step the surviving spaces form a direct sum: the previous spaces form a direct sum, and distinct eigenvalue intersections inside each previous space do as well. Thus there are at most $N$ at any step. Any line in a surviving space after all $d$ steps is a common eigenline. Recurse on its quotient. If a complete invariant flag exists, it induces one on the quotient by any invariant line, so the choice of eigenline cannot destroy success.

In a triangularizing basis, each diagonal position gives the covector $w\mapsto\sum_j\lambda_j w_j$ from the diagonal entries $\lambda_j$ of the normalized generators at that position. Group equal covectors as $\ell_\alpha$, with multiplicity $N_\alpha$. The formal determinant factors as
$$
  \det M(w)= \det M(w_0)\prod_\alpha \ell_\alpha(w)^{N_\alpha}.
$$
Each $\ell_\alpha(w_0)=1$, so different eigenvalue tuples give distinct projective covector lines. If $(A,B,C)$ is any tensor transporter, then $\det M_{T'}(Cw)=(\det B/\det A)\det M_T(w)$. Unique factorization in the polynomial ring implies that $C^*$ matches the projective lines and preserves their exponent labels. This is an identity of formal polynomials, so it does not rely on interpolation from evaluations over a small finite field.

Require $d+1$ exponent labels on the source that each occur on a unique covector and whose lines form a projective frame in $W^*$. A frame here is $d$ basis lines and an additional line having all nonzero coordinates in the basis. The labels can be supplied and verified, so no exhaustive frame search is required. A transporter must send the corresponding target labeled covectors to those source covectors via pullback. Verify that the target labels exist uniquely and also give a frame. Matching labeled frames determines one projective class of $C$: rescale basis covectors on each side so the last line is represented by their sum, then match the ordered normalized bases. Any other map matching the first $d$ lines is diagonal in these bases, and preserving the last forces a common scalar. Thus the determinant coset test for this one class decides $\operatorname{SL}^3$-isomorphism. The square size, width, and length of any supplied flags are polynomial in the tensor length.

The class permits arbitrarily large $d$. For $q\geq3$ and $d\geq2$, use diagonal slice blocks with covectors $w_1,\ldots,w_d,\sum_i w_i$ and distinct block dimensions $1,\ldots,d+1$. An invertible slice is obtained by taking all coordinates nonzero with nonzero sum. Starting at the all-ones vector, change one coordinate to a different nonzero value if the sum is zero. The block dimensions give intrinsic unique frame labels, and the total square size is $(d+1)(d+2)/2$. Adding arbitrary strictly upper triangular entries to the slices preserves the determinant factors and regularity of the indicated slice, so the example permits noncommuting generators.

If $q$ is fixed and $d=O(\log L)$, another computable candidate criterion applies without requiring square slices or triangularizability. Enumerate the projective points of $W$ and label each by $\operatorname{rank}M(w)$. Suppose $d+1$ supplied rank values each occur at a unique point, and those points give a projective frame in $W$. Rank is intrinsic under both matrix changes of basis. Matching the corresponding target rank labels and the frames again gives one projective class. This enumeration costs $q^{O(d)}\operatorname{poly}(L)$.

For every class with a polynomial candidate procedure as specified, the exact decision algorithm gives a same-field many-one reduction to ordinary tensor isomorphism by emitting the fixed isomorphic pair $(0,0)$ or the fixed non-isomorphic pair $(0,1)$ of $1\times1\times1$ tensors over $F$, according to the decision. The reduction is a map on instance pairs. It establishes these solvable classes rather than a uniform gadget on individual unrestricted tensors.

## Relation to the literature and controls

Grochow and Qiao already use fixed-mode enumeration and module isomorphism for ordinary small-width tensor isomorphism. The determinant coset is the additional information needed for independent volume constraints, and it can remain a proper subgroup even for a single slice. For example, an invertible square matrix alone has diagonal determinant image $(a,a)$ for its automorphism pairs. A left-right special-linear equivalence therefore preserves the actual determinant although ordinary left-right equivalence does not. [Grochow and Qiao, Tensor Isomorphism-Completeness](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang pose the unrestricted comparison of $\mathsf{TI}_{\mathrm{SL}}$ and $\mathsf{TI}$. Lysikov and Walter also separate determinant constraints from bounded-arity tensor gadgets in their discussion of orbit closure intersection. The present result is an exact decision and reduction theorem in the finite-field classes specified above. It leaves unresolved the unrestricted same-field reduction. Geometry.py checks a quadratic norm example, eight determinant triples in a rectangular projective class, the unique projective frame class across all 11,232 invertible ternary matrices over the field of three elements, and 89 reciprocal characteristic polynomials for modular trace controls. The proofs are in prose, without a Lean verification or a priority claim. [Grochow, Qiao, Tang and Zhang, Actions by Classical Groups, Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135#S1.SS4) · [Lysikov and Walter, Complexity Theory of Orbit Closure Intersection for Tensors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04639)
