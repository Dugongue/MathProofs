# A mixed trichotomy for three-stage Postnikov towers

This fixed-template theorem extends the [quadratic two-stage classification](../MixedPostnikovTrichotomy/) to targets whose final obstruction can distinguish two lifts through the same lower map. The result is an informal proof.

## Targets and instances

Fix an even integer $d\ge 2$, a finitely generated abelian group $G$, and three nonempty finite coordinate lists $a_i,c_j,t_k$. Each coordinate is assigned a scalar integer sort of the fixed multisorted equality–congruence template $M$ of the two-stage theorem. Coefficient data consist of elements $g_{ijk}\in G$ and $s_i\in G[2]$. All these choices are fixed. One can also allow a fixed finite family of such targets and use the worst case in the classification below.

For an explicitly given ordered simplicial pair $(X,A)$, a lower map is encoded by integral degree-$d$ cocycles $a_i,c_j,t_k$ and degree-$(2d-1)$ cochains $b_i$ over $\mathbb F_2$, with $\delta b_i=\bar a_i\smile\bar a_i$. Bars denote reduction modulo $2$. Write $\ell b_i$ for the integral cochain with entries $0,1$ representing $b_i$, and put

$$
w_i=\frac{a_i\smile a_i-\delta\ell b_i}{2},\qquad
J=\sum_{ijk}g_{ijk}\,(w_i\smile c_j\smile t_k)+\sum_i s_i\,(\bar w_i\smile\bar w_i).
$$

The numerator is an even cocycle, so $w_i$ is an integral degree-$2d$ cocycle. Coefficients in the second sum are applied by $1\mapsto s_i$ from $\mathbb F_2$ to $G$. The final lift is a degree-$(4d-1)$ $G$-cochain $q$ with $\delta q=J$. The boundary supplies the entire tuple on $A$, which must be matched literally. The instance may prescribe affine equalities between periods of the integral degree-$d$ coordinates on explicit integral cycles and shared variables of their assigned sorts. It also specifies an arithmetic pp instance over $M$, including finite sorts. Multiple pairs may use the same variables. The constraints can use binary integer coefficients and constants.

These equations represent maps to an actual three-stage Postnikov target. The lower stage is the homotopy fibre of the mod-$2$ cup-square map from the product of the degree-$d$ integral Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces to $K(\mathbb F_2^{\{i\}},2d)$. In the simplicial cocycle model, every displayed operation is natural, including the $0,1$ lift on normalized cochains. Consequently $J$ defines the next $k$-invariant on that fibre itself. Taking its homotopy fibre gives the third stage. The three homotopy groups are the free bottom group in degree $d$, $\mathbb F_2^{\{i\}}$ in degree $2d-1$, and $G$ in degree $4d-1$. The pullback cochain model and the comparison with relative continuous extensions are as in the two-stage theorem.

## Classification

Let $Q(M_0)$ be the exact finite modular pp-image Taylor criterion of the arithmetic core. The complete classification for this class is as follows.

| Condition | Decision complexity |
| --- | --- |
| Some $g_{ijk}$ has infinite order | $\Sigma^0_1$-complete under computable many-one reductions |
| All $g_{ijk}$ have finite order and some is nonzero | NP-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions |
| All $g_{ijk}$ vanish and $Q(M_0)$ fails | NP-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions |
| All $g_{ijk}$ vanish and $Q(M_0)$ holds | Deterministic polynomial time, with polynomial-bit constructive witnesses |

These are effective structural cases. The arithmetic facts used below are constructive tractability under $Q$, hardness when $Q$ fails, and invariance under adjoining fixed cross-sort linear congruences. The last fact is the cyclic-term argument in the two-stage theorem. ### Lower extensions and relative addition

Work on one pair and one index $i$. By integer normal forms find an integral cocycle $a^0$ with the prescribed restriction, or reject. Choose an arbitrary $b^0$ extending $b_A$ as a cochain. On the lattice $L_0=Z^d(X,A;\mathbb Z)$ the map $u\mapsto[\bar u^2]$ to $H^{2d}(X,A;\mathbb F_2)$ is additive. This follows from graded commutativity of relative cup products in characteristic two. Moreover the relative class of $(\overline{a^0+u})^2-\delta b^0$ equals $[(\bar a^0)^2-\delta b^0]+[\bar u^2]$. The two mixed terms cancel in relative cohomology, using the product of one absolute and one relative class. Evaluate this linear map on an integer basis and solve its affine equation over $\mathbb F_2$. A solution gives $a_*$, and a relative finite linear solve gives $b_*$ with the stated boundary. Reject if no solution exists. The integral lattice $L=\{u\in L_0:[\bar u^2]=0\}$ has a polynomial-bit basis by taking the inverse image of the mod-$2$ kernel and applying integer normal forms.

Choose the signed bilinear integral cup-one homotopy $H$ with $\delta H(a,a')=a\smile a'-a'\smile a$ on closed degree-$d$ cochains. Write the group law on lower maps as

$$
(a,b)\star(a',b')=(a+a',\,b+b'+\overline{H(a,a')}).
$$

The coboundary equation follows by expanding the square in characteristic two. This multiplication is associative by bilinearity, with identity $(0,0)$ and inverse of $(a,b)$ given by $(-a,-b-\overline{H(a,-a)})$. It respects restrictions. Every lower extension of the prescribed boundary is therefore $(a_*,b_*)\star(u,v)$ for a relative pair $(u,v)$ with $\delta v=\bar u^2$. Choose one relative pair over each basis element of $L$ by solving for $v$. Add pairs $(0,v)$ for a basis of $Z^{2d-1}(X,A;\mathbb F_2)$. Words in this fixed order with arbitrary integer exponents cover all relative pairs. Indeed their first coordinates cover $L$, and the kernel of the projection to $L$ is exactly the central group of these relative cocycles. Large powers are evaluated by repeated squaring. [Normalized cochain cup-one identities](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpaa.2020.106444)

Write $w(a,b)$ for the displayed divided square. For an absolute lower pair $f=(a,b)$ and a relative pair $e=(u,v)$ there is a relative integral cochain $E$ such that

$$
w(f\star e)=w(f)+w(e)+u\smile a-\delta E.
$$

In fact the difference of the chosen integral lift of the product's second coordinate and $\ell b+\ell v+H(a,u)$ is $2E$, and vanishes on $A$. Modulo $2$ and in relative cohomology, the difference of the squares of $w(f\star e)$ and $w(f)$ is $[\bar w(e)^2]+[(\bar u\smile\bar a)^2]$. Mixed terms cancel by relative graded commutativity. The last term vanishes because $[\bar u^2]=0$ in relative cohomology. Thus $e\mapsto[\bar w(e)^2]$ is additive on the relative pairs, and the same formula gives its action by translation on the obstruction class at any fixed lower extension.

### The zero-coefficient algorithm

Assume all $g_{ijk}=0$. Form an independent origin and relative generator list as above for each $a_i,b_i$. Solve the ordinary integral cocycle extension equations for $c_j,t_k$ and parametrize the relative cocycles by integer bases. Introduce the integer exponents of each lower generator in the sort of its $a_i$, and likewise for the $c_j,t_k$ lattice coordinates in their assigned sorts. The observed periods are affine linear combinations of these exponents, since the first coordinate of the group law is addition. This introduces only same-sort linear equations for the observations.

Choose an arbitrary extension $q^0$ of the last boundary cochain. The class of $J-\delta q^0$ is a known relative origin plus the sum of the top classes of the lower generators weighted by their exponents. All variable classes arise from $s_i[\bar w^2]$, so each can be represented by an actual relative cocycle with entries in $G[2]$. Take Smith normal form of the integer matrix of the relative coboundary in degree $4d-1$. Its row change acts on cochains with coefficients in $G$. In a row of diagonal modulus $t\ge 0$ the resulting condition is

$$
\sum_\nu e_\nu z_\nu=w\pmod{tG},\qquad e_\nu\in G[2].
$$

For each sort and coefficient $e\in G[2]$, sum the exponents in that sort with that coefficient, using its own addition graph. The row now uses a fixed bounded number of scalar arguments. The satisfying parity patterns of those arguments form either the empty set or a coset of the kernel of a linear map from a fixed binary vector space to $G/tG$. Enumerate these patterns in constant size and reject an empty row. Every nonempty pattern set is given by a finite conjunction of affine mod-$2$ equations. There are only finitely many such relations at this fixed arity. They preserve $Q$ after adjoining arithmetic constants, by the prior congruence-invariance lemma. The resulting arithmetic instance has polynomial size. Under $Q$ solve it constructively, realize the words by binary powering, and solve the last relative coboundary equation by integer normal forms. All operations and witnesses use polynomial bit size. Without $Q$, purely arithmetic inputs with no observations already give NP-hardness.

### Upper bounds with finite coefficients

When all $g_{ijk}$ are torsion, choose an even common exponent $m$ annihilating them and the $s_i$. Guess $a_i$ modulo $2m$ and $c_j,t_k$ modulo $m$ on each relevant simplex, along with all $b_i$ and the finite arithmetic variables. These residues determine $\delta b_i=\bar a_i^2$ and determine $w_i$ modulo $m$ by division of an even residue modulo $2m$. They therefore determine $J$ as a $G$-cochain. Check the boundary conditions for the finite data and solve the last relative coboundary equation over $G$ by integer normal forms.

The remaining integral equations and congruences enforce the boundary data, cocycle conditions, observations and guessed residues. On fixing the finite arguments, each arithmetic atom is a Boolean combination of affine equations and congruences. Guess the truth values of the components and, for a failed congruence, a nonzero residue. Solve the resulting equalities and congruences by integer normal forms. On the resulting affine integer lattice a disequality either fails identically, holds identically, or removes a proper affine hyperplane. In free parameter dimension $r>0$, the test vectors $(T,T^2,\ldots,T^r)$ avoid all remaining hyperplanes for some $T$ between zero and $r$ times their number. This follows from the integer polynomial root bound. Thus checking feasibility is polynomial time and every yes-instance has polynomial-bit witnesses. In the infinite-order case, enumeration of all integral and finite cochains and arithmetic variables gives computable enumerability.

### Hardness in the same input model

Choose a nonzero $g=g_{ijk}$. Work on two oriented connected-summed copies of $(S^d)^4$, and use the four sphere axes in each copy as the observed degree-$d$ cycles. Pin all coordinates other than $a_i,c_j,t_k$ to zero on all axes. In the first copy prescribe $a_i$ on the first two axes with periods $x,1$, $c_j$ on the third with period $y$, and $t_k$ on the fourth with period $1$. In the second copy use $(-z,1)$, $1$, and $1$, respectively. All unmentioned periods are zero.

The lower square modulo $2$ vanishes because each sphere generator squares to zero and cross terms occur twice. Thus the lower lift exists. Integral topological cohomology in degree $2d$ is torsion-free, so $2[w_i]=[a_i^2]$ forces $[w_i]$. On the two copies it is $x$ and $-z$ times the product of the first two axis classes. Its square vanishes. The top class evaluates to precisely $g(xy-z)$ on the fundamental class. This evaluation detects the full obstruction since the $G$-cohomology in top degree is $G$. Hence all the observations admit a lift exactly when this equation holds. Replacing $y$ by $1$ in the first copy and using $(-1,1)$ and $y$ in the second gives $g(x-y)=0$, allowing a copy between the sorts of $a_i$ and $c_j$. These are genuine observations on maps from a fixed space. Take empty or basepoint boundary, and reuse as many copies of the carrier as the reduction needs.

If $g$ has infinite order, the gadgets impose exact multiplication and permit exact transfer of the right factors. With addition and the pin $1$, they realize arbitrary integer arithmetic circuits. The Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich theorem gives computably enumerable hardness. If $g$ has finite order $n>1$, take a prime $p\mid n$ and scale one of the first two $a_i$ periods in each copy by $n/p$. This gives the equations modulo $p$. The constraints $x^2=x$, multiplication, and $1-x$ realize Boolean input, AND and NOT. Use exact integer representatives for a satisfying Boolean circuit, and reduce any lift modulo $p$ for the converse. This proves NP-hardness. [Matiyasevich's Diophantine theorem](https://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/~yumat/papers/21_paper/21_eng.pdf)

### Genuine dependence at the third stage

The lower invariant is nonzero: on $\mathbb{CP}^{d}$ a degree-$d$ class given by the $d/2$ power of the standard degree-two generator has nonzero square modulo $2$. The top invariant need not be a pullback from the bottom. For example take one $a$ coordinate, $G=\mathbb F_2$, $g=0$ and $s=1$. On $\mathbb{RP}^{4d}$ take all bottom classes zero and choose $b$ to represent either $0$ or $x^{2d-1}$ for the degree-one generator $x$. Then $w$ is, up to sign, the integral Bockstein of $b$. Reduction sends the latter choice to $Sq^1(x^{2d-1})=x^{2d}$, and the top class is $x^{4d}\ne0$. The zero choice lifts. For a literal boundary choose $A=\mathbb{RP}^{4d-1}$ and prescribe the latter $b$ there with zero bottom and final cochains. The first lift extends over $X$. Every extending lower pair has $[\bar w]$ restricting to $x^{2d}$ on $A$, hence $[\bar w]=x^{2d}$ by restriction isomorphism. The final obstruction therefore cannot vanish. This is a tractable-branch example when the arithmetic template has $Q$.

The higher obstruction can also detect the intermediate lift in the two hard branches. Take the primitive coefficient $g=1$ in $G=\mathbb Z$ or $G=\mathbb F_2$ and the carrier $\mathbb{RP}^{2d}\times S^d\times S^d$. Put $a=0$, let $c,t$ be the two sphere generators, and vary $b$ between zero and the pullback of $x^{2d-1}$. In the latter case $w$ is the nonzero integral Bockstein from the projective factor, which stays nonzero under the indicated coefficient map. Its product with the two sphere classes is nonzero, while its square vanishes by dimension on the projective factor. Thus the same bottom map has two intermediate lifts with different final outcomes.

### Integral and rational multiplication on the targets

The target is an $H$-space exactly when all $g_{ijk}=0$. For sufficiency, use the strict multiplication on the lower cochain pairs and addition on the $c,t$ factors. The preceding identity for $w$ shows that the obstruction class $J$ is primitive on the product of two copies of the lower target. This follows by applying the identity relative to either axis and using that $[\bar a_i^2]=0$ on the lower target. The defect $J(f\star f')-J(f)-J(f')$ vanishes as a cochain on the wedge of the factors. Its relative class also vanishes, since restriction from the product to the wedge in degree $4d-1$ is split-surjective by the two projections. Therefore a relative primitive of the defect gives a correction to addition on the final cochain, zero on the axes. This defines a multiplication on the final homotopy fibre with a unit.

For necessity, choose nonzero $g_{ijk}$ and take $U=V=S^d\times S^d$. Give the map on $U$ the two unit $a_i$ periods and give the map on $V$ the two unit periods for $c_j$ and $t_k$ on separate axes. All other periods are zero. The two topological lifts exist on the separate factors since their lower squares vanish modulo $2$ and their dimensions are $2d$. An $H$-space product would extend their wedge to $U\times V$ up to homotopy. Degree-$d$ cohomology on the product is determined by restrictions to the two factors. Its degree-$2d$ integral cohomology is torsion-free, so the divided squares are also forced there. The resulting top obstruction evaluates to $g_{ijk}$, a contradiction. Rationally the lower torsion stage disappears, each $[w_i]$ becomes $[a_i^2]/2$, and $J$ becomes its sum of quartic monomials with coefficients $g_{ijk}\otimes 1$. The rational target is a product of Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces when these coefficients vanish. When some coefficient survives, the same product-of-factors argument applies with rational coefficients. Thus the rational target is an $H$-space exactly when all $g_{ijk}$ are torsion.

## Additive finite-residue towers of arbitrary fixed depth

There is a separate tractability theorem for deeper targets. Fix $m,d,r$ and a tower $Y_j=\operatorname{hofib}(k_j:Y_{j-1}\to K(H_j,n_j+1))$ starting at $Y_0=K((\mathbb Z/m)^r,d)$, with finite abelian $H_j$ and increasing positive homotopy degrees. Assume every $Y_j$ has a homotopy-associative and homotopy-commutative $H$-group structure with unit and inverse, and every $k_j$ is primitive for the multiplication at its source. Assume the projection from the final $Y_j$ to $Y_0$ preserves multiplication up to homotopy. Compatible towers of infinite loop spaces give examples. Define the observed target by pulling the final $Y_j$ back to $K(\mathbb Z^r,d)$ along reduction modulo $m$. Use literal cochain boundary data in the finite principal-fibre models and observe the periods of the integral degree-$d$ coordinates as before. Then the mixed problem is in polynomial time with polynomial-bit constructive witnesses under $Q(M_0)$, and is NP-complete if $Q(M_0)$ fails.

Use degreewise finite cocycle models for the finite tower. The $k_j$ cocycles and multiplication maps are fixed finite tables in all degrees the algorithms read. A multiplication can be taken strict on the wedge by homotopy extension, since the wedge includes by a cofibration and the target is Kan. For any finite simplicial pair, compute generators represented by actual maps for the abelian groups $[X/A,Y_j]$. At the bottom these are ordinary relative cohomology generators. Given maps representing generators of $[X/A,Y_{j-1}]$, their $k_j$ classes give a homomorphism from a free integer group on the generators to $H^{n_j+1}(X,A;H_j)$. Compute generators for its kernel by integer normal forms with congruences. Realize the kernel basis by fixed-order words, using binary powering, and lift each word by a relative coboundary solve. Add the maps from generators of $H^{n_j}(X,A;H_j)$ in the fibre. The based mapping sequence proves these generate $[X/A,Y_j]$: every class projects to a kernel element, every integer representative of a kernel element belongs to the computed integer kernel, and any ambiguity after lifting belongs to the image of the fibre. A presentation of the group is unnecessary. Primitivity is valid for the relative maps because multiplication restricts to the unit on the wedge and restriction of cohomology from product to wedge is split-surjective in the preceding degree.

Compute one extension of the prescribed finite boundary data at the same time. At stage $j-1$, if $f_*$ is one extension, all relative homotopy classes of extensions come from multiplying it by zero-boundary maps. Indeed translation by $f_*$ and its restriction are homotopy equivalences on the mapping spaces, and restriction is a fibration. Strict unitality gives a map between their literal fibres. The obstruction to extending the last boundary cochain is therefore the obstruction at $f_*$ plus the homomorphism just computed. Solve the resulting affine finite system and lift the chosen solution by a cochain equation. If it fails, reject. Both the obstruction and the relative class of the final projection to $Y_0$ are affine on these words, by the wedge-relative primitivity argument.

Now use integer variables for the exponents of the final generator list and the coordinates of an integral cocycle with the stated boundary. Require its reduction to equal the projected word up to a relative coboundary. These are linear equations modulo $m$, with auxiliary variables for that coboundary. The period constraints are integral linear equations within the assigned sorts. Assign the modular auxiliaries to any one integer sort, and express each modular row by aggregating its terms of each coefficient within each sort. This gives a fixed finite set of bounded-arity congruence relations. Under $Q$ the arithmetic algorithm solves the system and gives polynomial-bit representatives. A relative coboundary between bottom cocycles supplies a simplicial homotopy rel boundary. Lift it through the finite tower, solving on the prism relative to its starting end and side boundary, to obtain a finite lift projecting to the exact reduction of the integral cocycle. Each prism obstruction group vanishes because the end together with the side retracts up to homotopy from the prism. The finite states, linear solves and word operations have polynomial cost. Without $Q$ the arithmetic lower bound and the certificate of a finite lift together with an integral arithmetic witness give NP-completeness, using the affine-lattice witness bound above.

This covers one side of the complexity split at arbitrary fixed depth. The three-stage theorem also proves both hard branches for its displayed nonadditive family. General nonadditive towers are outside these results.
