# Finite-unitary tensor isomorphism inside one Yang–Baxter representation class

**Theorem.** Fix $K=\mathbb F_{q^2}$ with its nontrivial involution $\sigma$. Ordinary ordered three-tensor isomorphism over $K$ is polynomial-time equivalent, on an explicit constructed family, to local equivalence of involutive Yang–Baxter gates by a single Hermitian isometry $g$ acting as $g\otimes g$. The gates themselves preserve the product Hermitian form. At every strand count their representations are simultaneously conjugate to the ordinary flips by a Hermitian isometry. On this family allowing all invertible local changes gives exactly the same yes instances. These assertions hold in characteristic two as well.

**Preserve direction before taking adjoints.** Remove unused supports and compare original and concise dimensions for the two inputs. Nonzero concise tensors have independent slices $M_i:B\to A$ spanning $S$ with common kernel zero and total image $A$. Fix $L=\dim B+1$. Replace every slice by $L$ slices, one with that matrix in each separate output block of $A^{\oplus L}$. The unchanged domain is $B$, and the slice dimension becomes $L\dim S$. Call this ordered tensor $\widetilde T$.

The centroid of a concise ordered three-tensor consists of triples of endomorphisms acting identically on it in the three slots. It is a commutative algebra whose primitive idempotents give its indecomposable summands: equality of the slot actions eliminates all mixed components, and uniqueness of the primitive decomposition gives cancellation. In the amplification the actions on the output and slice slots must agree with one action on $B$. In block coordinates the latter has only matching output and slice block indices; concision forces every off-diagonal block of the other actions to vanish, and each diagonal pair must be the unique pair matching that $B$ action on $T$. Thus the centroid is unchanged and each summand with dimensions $(a,b,t)$ is amplified to $(La,b,Lt)$ in output/domain/slice order. Its output/domain ratio is strictly greater than one.

An isomorphism of two amplifications also implies an isomorphism of the original tensors. Absorb its change on $B$ into one input. With a fixed basis of $B$, read the tensor as a labelled tuple of maps from the dual slice space to the output. The amplification is the direct sum of $L$ copies of this tuple as a representation of a quiver with two vertices and labelled parallel arrows. Krull–Schmidt uniqueness cancels the common repetition factor. The forward implication is immediate. [Tensor centers and decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07915)

**Build the gate.** On $H_0=A^{\oplus L}\oplus B\oplus C\oplus Kz$ use the earlier slice construction for $\widetilde T$: $C$ has one coordinate per amplified slice, $U_i=z e_i^*$, and $s=\sum_i\widetilde M_i\otimes U_i$. Give $H=H_0\oplus H_0^{\sigma,*}$ the hyperbolic Hermitian form, and extend the factors of $s$ by zero on the dual half. Their Hermitian adjoints are supported in that dual half with reversed arrows. All factors in this combined list multiply pairwise to zero. Set
$$s_0=s-s^\dagger,\qquad r=s_0-(s_0)_{21},\qquad X=P(I+r).$$
Here $\dagger$ on operators on $H^{\otimes 2}$ uses the product form. Both $s_0$ and $r$ are skew-adjoint and square to zero. Thus $I+r$ is an isometry. On $k$ strands $J_k=\prod_{i<j}(I+(s_0)_{ij})$ is itself an isometry. The factors commute because overlapping products vanish and disjoint placements commute. Conjugating the adjacent flips by $J_k$ gives the adjacent $X$ operators: swapping one adjacent pair reverses just that pair's factor. This proves the representation claim, the braid relations and involutivity without dividing by two.

**Reflect local equivalence.** Every source isomorphism induces a change $g_0$ on $H_0$ preserving the paired construction; extend it on the dual half by the inverse adjoint to get a local isometry. Conversely even an arbitrary local invertible equivalence identifies the contraction carriers of $r=PX-I$. The original arrows, evaluation arrows, and both adjoint lists are linearly independent across lists, with disjoint blocks as matrices. Therefore the carrier is the direct sum of the four matrix spaces consisting of the amplified slices, evaluation, adjoint amplified slices, and adjoint evaluation. After dividing the domain by their common kernel and using the total image as output, this is a direct sum in all three tensor slots. Cancel both fixed evaluation tensors by primitive-idempotent uniqueness. Adjoint amplified summands have output/domain ratio below one (conjugate transpose switches the two dimensions); amplified original summands all have ratio above one. Keep precisely the latter, then cancel the amplification. This proves reflection without presupposing that an isomorphism respects the hyperbolic halves.

The operations have polynomial size in the dense input. Zero and incompatible formats may be decided directly and sent to fixed yes/no examples. A fixed no pair of the same dimension can also use the flip and any nonzero encoded gate since local conjugation fixes the flip (or choose two inequivalent nonzero concise inputs of the same format). For the upper reduction on the image use the proved agreement between isometric and general local equivalence and the fixed tensor-system compiler for the canonical map $H\times H\to H\otimes H$ and $X$. Finite-unitary tensor isomorphism was already studied in its own right. What is added is an isometric Yang–Baxter realization with all the indicated simultaneous representation constraints. This concerns finite-field Hermitian geometry; over the complex numbers the doubling uses an indefinite form.
