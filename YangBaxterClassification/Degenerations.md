# Directed orbit closure inside the flip representation class

Over $\mathbb C$ the existing square-zero braid construction preserves and reflects the **entire directed degeneration order** on concise three-tensors of any fixed format. Thus local orbit-closure membership is NP-hard for algebraic involutive Yang–Baxter operators on rational input, under simultaneous $g\otimes g$ changes of basis, even among pairs whose strand representations are all conjugate to the flip and whose orbit closures always intersect. The change of basis here is general linear. The NP-hardness conclusion uses the repository's earlier informal degeneration-preserving compiler.

**Setup.** Write a concise tensor as a $t$-dimensional space $S=\langle M_i\rangle\subset\operatorname{Hom}(B,A)$, with specified slice basis. Let $C$ have basis indexed by these slices, let $z$ span a line, and let $H=A\oplus B\oplus C\oplus\mathbb Cz$. Extend all maps by zero and put $U_i=z e_i^*$ on $C$, $s=\sum M_i\otimes U_i$, $r=s-s_{21}$, $X=P(I+r)$ for the flip $P$. The original proof shows involutivity, braid relations and simultaneous conjugacy to flips on all strands. Changing the source bases induces a single basis change on $H$ (the slice basis is paired with its dual on $C$). The encoding is polynomial, so it preserves degenerations. Concision makes the contraction carrier of $r$ exactly $E=S\oplus\operatorname{Hom}(C,\mathbb Cz)$ with total image $I=A\oplus\mathbb Cz$ also equal to its common kernel. Multiplying $A$ and $z$ by a scalar $u$ scales $r$ by $u^2$, so every encoded orbit closure in that dimension contains $P$. **Why a limit cannot create a spurious source degeneration.** Suppose $a=\dim A>1$. Inside $I$ there is exactly one line $L$ for which $E_L=\{f\in E \mid \operatorname{im} f\subset L\}$ has dimension at least $t$. At $L=\mathbb Cz$ it has dimension $t$. A line in $A$ receives fewer than $t$ independent elements of $S$ because their total image is $A$; on any other line different from $\mathbb Cz$ the separate $B$ and $C$ supports force both components to vanish. Put $K=\bigcap_{f\in E_L}\ker f=I\oplus B$. Restriction and projection give an image
$$E\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(K/I,I/L)$$
equal to $S$. All these constructions commute with simultaneous change of basis. If $a=1$, concise tensors of that fixed format are just nondegenerate pairings of two equal-dimensional spaces and have one orbit.

Consider a specialization from the orbit of an encoded tensor to another concise encoding of the same format. By the curve criterion for orbit closure, work over a discrete valuation ring after an extension if needed. The carrier and total image extend as subbundles, since their ranks are equal in both fibers. Properness of projective space and the Grassmannian extends the generic distinguished line and its $t$-plane of maps. The condition that each map in the plane take values in the line is closed. The uniqueness calculation at the special endpoint therefore identifies the limiting line and plane with its distinguished $L,E_L$. The common kernel $K$ has the same rank at both ends and hence extends as a subbundle; the map displayed above also has image of rank $t$ at both ends. Trivialize these bundles and choose a basis of the free image over the valuation ring. The resulting tensor family has generic fiber isomorphic to the original source and special fiber isomorphic to the desired target. This proves reflection. Constant rank is used only along this one-parameter family between endpoints of the stipulated format.

**Unrestricted inputs.** The prior tagged path-algebra compiler sends fixed-order tensors of a common input format to unital associative algebras of a common vector-space dimension depending only on the format. It preserves and reflects directed orbit closure. Passing to the multiplication three-tensor then gives a concise tensor: the unit makes all three flattenings injective in their dual-to-support conventions. On unital associative endpoints algebra degeneration is equivalent to degeneration of ordered multiplication tensors. For the reverse direction, in a degenerating family of isotopes fix the unit $e$ at the special fiber. The maps $R_e:x\mapsto xe$ and $L_e:y\mapsto ey$ are invertible near that fiber. Normalize the product there by $(x,y)\mapsto\mu(R_e^{-1}x,L_e^{-1}y)$. The special product stays fixed. Over the generic fiber an isotope $h(fx\cdot gy)$ acquires, in $h$ coordinates, the product $xcy$ for the unit $c=g(e)^{-1}f(e)^{-1}$, which is isomorphic to the original algebra. Combining these maps gives a polynomial-size reduction on rational structure constants. The published complex tensor orbit-closure hardness theorem now gives NP-hardness of the directed braid problem. **Depth.** The repository constructs a chain of $N^2/8-O(N^{3/2})$ strict degenerations between concise cubic tensors of side $N$. The equivalence just proved gives a strict chain of the same length in local dimension $h=3N+1$. Every strict step drops orbit dimension, and $\dim\operatorname{GL}(H)=h^2$. The quadratic order of growth is therefore sharp along these dimensions, even in one flip representation class. This last conclusion inherits the informal source chain.
