# A recognizable GI-equivalent family of algebraic Yang–Baxter gates

Fix $F=\mathbb F_q$. Given a raw operator $X$ on $H\otimes H$, let $P$ be the ordinary flip and $r=PX-I\in\operatorname{End}(H)^{\otimes2}$. The left and right contraction carriers are the images of contracting its right and left legs. Consider the following basis-independent conditions, treating $r=0$ separately.

* The two carriers coincide in a space $E$, and $r$ belongs to the alternating tensor subspace of $E\otimes E$ (in characteristic two require zero diagonal as well as symmetry).
* $U=\sum_{f\in E}\operatorname{im} f=\bigcap_{f\in E}\ker f$. Thus $E\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(V,U)$ for $V=H/U$.
* The primitive direct summands of the concise ordered tensor associated to $E$ have slice dimensions at most $d$.

For fixed $q,d\ge1$, recognizing these conditions takes deterministic polynomial time. Local equivalence under simultaneous $g\otimes g$ on the resulting family is polynomial-time equivalent to Graph Isomorphism. The total carrier dimension is unbounded. All these $X$ satisfy the involutive braid relations and have the flip representation at every strand count: take a triangular half $s$ of $r$ in any basis of $E$, so $r=s-s_{21}$; all products in $E$ vanish. The earlier explicit intertwiner $\prod_{i<j}(I+s_{ij})$ applies.

**Recover the blocks.** Compute the two carriers, common kernel and total image by elimination. Take a basis of $E$ to form its concise matrix-space tensor. Compute its commutative centroid (equal actions on the three slots) by linear equations. Its primitive idempotents give the intrinsic indecomposable blocks $E_i\subset\operatorname{Hom}(V_i,U_i)$. To find the idempotents in deterministic polynomial time at fixed $q$, solve $z^q=z$ over $F$ in the centroid. In a finite local factor the solutions are exactly the base scalars, as seen first in the residue field and then by nilpotence. Hence this subalgebra is a product of copies of $F$. Simultaneously split its multiplication operators on the centroid into eigenspaces over $F$; the projections of the unit onto the final blocks are the needed idempotents. [Tensor centers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07915)

**Upper reduction.** For each summand make a hub and a vertex for each ordered frame of its slice space. Within each fiber label ordered pairs by their change-of-frame matrix. Give each frame the label of the left-right equivalence class of its ordered tuple of maps $V_i\to U_i$. These labels can be assigned consistently across the two graphs of the reduction by pairwise tests for isomorphism of labelled Kronecker-quiver representations. Module isomorphism supplies deterministic polynomial tests. Include, for each ordered frame pair across different summands, the coordinate matrix of the corresponding component $r_{ij}\in E_i\otimes E_j$ in those frames. Put the diagonal component in the single frame label. Use colors for the hub incidences, dimensions and all labels. Directed finite colored relations of this size encode into ordinary graphs by the usual type and incidence gadgets.

A graph isomorphism permutes summands. Within each fiber, preservation of transitions forces all frame images to come from one linear isomorphism of slice spaces. The ordered-tuple test guarantees that this isomorphism is induced by invertible maps of its $V_i,U_i$ spaces. The cross labels enforce precisely the required equality of $r$. Assemble the maps by direct sum and lift the map on $H/U$ with a choice of complement to get the desired $g$ on $H$. Conversely every local equivalence induces all these frame maps and labels. There are at most $q^{d^2}$ frames per summand; the reduction has size and time $q^{O(d^2)}\operatorname{poly}(\text{input length})$ at fixed $q$. The fixed-width statement also applies to logarithmically bounded $d^2$ at fixed field. **GI lower reduction already at width one.** From a nonempty simple graph $G$, subdivide each edge once and attach three private pendant paths of lengths one, two and three to every original vertex. In the resulting bipartite graph $Y$, the original vertices are exactly those of degree at least three and the subdivision vertices are exactly those adjacent to two originals. Every isomorphism of $G$ extends uniquely to $Y$. Orient from the bipartition side containing all originals to the other and write $A$ for the skew adjacency matrix. Form the nonsingular alternating matrix
$$\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}A&I\\-I&0\end{pmatrix}.$$
Its support graph is $Y$ with one extra private leaf per vertex. Since $Y$ has minimum degree at least one, deleting the degree-one vertices of this last graph recovers $Y$. Empty graph inputs can be handled directly.

Let $m$ be the size of $\Omega$, take independent bases $v_i,u_i$ in complementary $m$-spaces, and let $N_i$ send $v_i$ to $u_i$ and kill the remaining basis elements. Set $r=\sum_{ij}\Omega_{ij}N_i\otimes N_j$. Invertibility makes its carrier exactly the span of the $N_i$. Its rank-one lines are precisely the individual $FN_i$, since the rank of a combination is its support size. Thus any local equivalence permutes these lines and rescales them; every monomial action on the lines is realized on $H$. Equality of $r$ requires monomial congruence of the alternating matrices. This implies an isomorphism of the support graphs, hence of $G$. Conversely a graph isomorphism induces the same signed matrix after its permutation because the orientation is canonical. This covers characteristic two.

**Automorphisms on these encoded graphs.** Put $c$ equal to the number of connected components of $G$. All $g$ preserve $U$; the upper off-diagonal block is an arbitrary map $V\to U$. On $V$ and $U$ the two diagonal blocks are monomial with a common permutation in $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. The $m$ scalars on $V$ are free. The ratios of the corresponding $U$ scalars to $V$ scalars multiply to one along each support edge, giving one free scalar per connected bipartite component. Every graph automorphism has its natural permutation lift. Hence
$$|\operatorname{Aut}_{\rm local}(X_G)|=q^{m^2}(q-1)^{m+c}|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|.$$
The result concerns arbitrary simultaneous linear basis changes of a linear Yang–Baxter operator. The recognition algorithm receives no graph, arrow basis, frame partition, or isomorphism witness.

Over the binary field all diagonal scalars equal one. On these instances the full local automorphism group is the additive group of square binary matrices of side $m$ semidirect the graph automorphism group, acting by simultaneous permutation of rows and columns.
