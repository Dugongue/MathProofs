# Exact query threshold for two unitary Yang–Baxter operators in a hidden basis

Let $G$ be a loopless simple graph on $n$ vertices and put $s_{ij}=1-2A_G(i,j)$. On the complex Hilbert space $H=\mathbb C^n$ with its standard positive inner product, define the gate $R_G(e_i\otimes e_j)=s_{ij}e_j\otimes e_i$. The matrix is real, unitary, Hermitian and involutive. The two sides of the braid equation on a triple acquire the same three signs. On any number of strands the whole representation is simultaneously unitarily conjugate to ordinary flips: on a basis word multiply by the product of $s_{ij}$ over its inverted pairs, for a fixed total order on the alphabet. Exchanging adjacent letters changes that phase by their sign, also when the letters coincide.

**The experiment.** The two known hypotheses are $G=C_{2t}$ and $G=C_t\sqcup C_t$, $t\ge3$. Choose one with equal prior and draw a single Haar unitary $U$ on $H$, held fixed throughout. The black box is the channel induced by $B=(U\otimes U)R_G(U\otimes U)^*$. A query means one use of that channel. Any known ancilla, measurement, adaptation or joint measurement is allowed in the ordinary circuit model. The least number of queries giving any strictly positive advantage over one half is exactly $\lceil t/2\rceil$. The theorem makes no quantitative lower bound on the nonzero advantage at the threshold.

**Invariant functions on the graph gates.** A complete contraction of $d$ copies of $B$ pairs their $2d$ output legs with the $2d$ input legs by a permutation. Schur–Weyl duality, applied after polarization, says these contractions span the homogeneous degree-$d$ polynomial invariants of simultaneous basis change on $H$. This spanning assertion also holds for small $n$, when contractions can be dependent. On the Hermitian orbit complex conjugates of entries can be exchanged for transposed entries, so the same bound applies to mixed polynomials of total degree $d$.

Multiplying by the constant flip converts each gate to a diagonal matrix with entries $s_{ij}$ and merely rewires the contraction. Its index cycles become vertices of a multigraph $F$; each operator gives one edge linking its two index occurrences. The contraction value is the sum over vertex labels of the product of edge signs. Conversely, wiring the edge ends cyclically at each vertex realizes any such sum for a graph without isolated vertices. Isolates contribute known factors of $n$. Substituting $s=1-2A$ and expanding makes these sums linear combinations of ordinary graph homomorphism counts with at most $d$ edges. Loops and parallel edges cause no difficulty, since $A$ is a loopless zero-one matrix. Conversely replacing $A=(1-s)/2$ realizes every ordinary homomorphism count with at most $d$ edges as an invariant of degree at most $d$ on the graph gates. Thus the degree cutoff has an exact graph-theoretic meaning. This uses the same contraction perspective as the repository's orthogonal graph probes. **General threshold.** For any two nonisomorphic simple graphs $G,H$ on the same number $n$ of vertices, let $e(G,H)$ be the least edge count of a probe with different homomorphism counts into the two graphs. It is finite by Lovász's theorem. The two directions above identify $e(G,H)$ with the least separating invariant degree. The query argument below, which only uses Hermiticity and common nonzero trace, gives the exact query number $\lceil e(G,H)/2\rceil$ for this pair. An isomorphic pair has identical distributions at every query number. [Lovász](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02280291)

The same correspondence transfers restricted families of contractions. For every fixed Euler genus bound there exist inequivalent gates in the flip representation class indistinguishable by all contractions whose index graph has that genus bound, at any degree. Apply the bounded-genus homomorphism indistinguishability theorem of Neuen and Seppelt: deleting edges, removing loops and merging parallel edges cannot increase the genus, and the signed expansion only requires these operations. The genus restriction here applies to the graph of shared summation indices; it places no constraint on the physical layout of a quantum circuit. [Neuen and Seppelt, LICS 2026](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.LICS.2026.75)

**First separating degree for cycles.** A homomorphism from any connected simple probe with fewer than $t$ edges to either target lifts to the infinite path after fixing the lift of one root. Indeed each fundamental cycle relative to a spanning tree has length less than $t$, and its winding in the target cycle is zero. The neighbors have distinct signed directions also for $t=3$. The number of maps per choice of root image is therefore the same for both targets. Disconnected probes multiply. At degree $t$ take the probe $C_t$. The signed steps around it have total displacement zero for a map to $C_{2t}$, whereas displacement $+t$ and $-t$ are also possible for maps to either small cycle. These are exactly two extra walks per target vertex, a difference of $4t$ in the count. Thus the first separating invariant degree is exactly $t$.

**From degree to queries, including the converse.** Every outcome probability of an $m$-use protocol is a polynomial of bidegree at most $(m,m)$ in entries of $B,\bar B$. This follows by inserting $X\mapsto BXB^*$ at each call in the unnormalized density-matrix evolution; summing measurement branches preserves the bound. Haar averaging gives an invariant of total degree at most $2m$. No protocol has positive advantage if $2m<t$.

Conversely use $m=\lceil t/2\rceil$ parallel calls on maximally entangled inputs to obtain $m$ normalized Choi states sharing the hidden basis. Take the separating invariant just constructed. Homogenize each term to degree $2m$ with powers of $\operatorname{tr}(B)/n=1$, since every graph gate has trace $n$. In each degree-$2m$ monomial replace $m$ factors by complex conjugates of transposed entries using Hermiticity. The resulting polynomial is a linear functional of the $m$-copy Choi state, up to a known normalization. Its two expectations differ even after Haar averaging. The Hermitian part (or its imaginary counterpart) gives a distinguishing observable; rescale it into a two-outcome measurement. This proves the exact threshold. It does not imply efficient implementation or constant bias. [Local invariants on multiple copies](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9712040) · [Representation classification of unitary involutive operators](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00196)

This threshold concerns a shared Haar-random local basis and ordinary channel queries. The sign-flip family is an instance of classical diagonal-type braidings; the candidate contribution is the query theorem and the exact transfer of invariant degree.
