# Centroid cases for three-to-two determinant signs over F₃

These are informal proofs for explicit dense three-tensors. All reductions are deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions. Two-sign TI tests two determinant-one constraints on the same transporter, and SL-TI tests all three.

## 1. Odd dimensions over an intrinsic residue field

Write a concise tensor as a bilinear map with zero left and right annihilators and spanning image. Its centroid consists of endomorphism triples whose actions in the three positions agree. It is commutative. Apply two centroid actions to different input arguments and move them to the output in either order. The spanning image forces the output actions to commute, and the zero annihilators then give the input commutations.

Compute the centroid C by linear equations. The cube-fixed algebra is a product of one F₃ per local factor. Split its primitive idempotents by refining along the projectors 1 − (x − a)² for basis elements x of that fixed algebra and a ∈ F₃. The idempotent images in the three modes give the primitive tensor blocks. The radical is the kernel of a sufficiently high iterated cubing map.

On a local block of residue field K and degree e, let m_i = dim_F₃(M_i)/e for the three mode modules. A composition series over that local centroid has m_i factors isomorphic to K. A centroid unit u acts in mode i with determinant Norm_(K/F₃)(ū)^m_i. The norm is onto and every nonzero residue lifts to a unit. If m_i is odd, put a norm-negative unit in mode i, its inverse in any other mode j, and identity elsewhere. This is a tensor automorphism with nonzero sign vector b supported on i and j, with entries m_i mod 2 and m_j mod 2. Computing b only needs relative dimensions. Every transporter profile is stable under adding b, so the profile contains zero exactly when its projection along b contains zero. Compile two rows whose common kernel is ⟨b⟩. The argument applies to radical and field centroids, and to any one block in a direct sum. Support deficiency can separately be handled by a reflection in the unused complement. [Exact sign-projection compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/SignProjections.md)

## 2. Heterogeneous direct-sum packing

Suppose all primitive blocks have even size in all three modes. For each source block i let C_i be a list of characters which admit ordinary TI tests of either prescribed value against a target block. Let H_i be the actual stabilizer sign image and set F_i = span(C_i ∩ H_i^⊥)^⊥. Put H = Σ_i H_i and F = Σ_i F_i. A single two-sign reduction works if a nonzero ℓ can be chosen during preprocessing with ℓ ∈ H or ℓ ∉ F on the domain.

Recover and frame the blocks. For each possible matched pair i,j and each b ∈ F₂³, gate a binary variable by ordinary block isomorphism and all the tests χ = χ(b) for χ in C_i. If the actual local sign profile is b_ij + H_i, these gates permit exactly b_ij + F_i. A character nonzero on H_i takes both values and imposes no restriction on b. Impose odd incidence at every source and target block on the enabled variables. Independently keep the original tensor comparison live.

Given a live ordinary matching, choose an abstract representative per ordinary block type. Each b_ij is, modulo H_i, the sum of source and target offsets from its representative. The odd incidence equations count all endpoint offsets exactly once modulo two. They therefore compute precisely the coset b_0 + F where b_0 is the sum of the offsets. A true matching realizes every sum in this coset by independent choices of the enlarged edge labels. The true sign profile is b_0 + H because even block dimensions make block permutations sign-neutral. Write a for b_0 after raw frame calibration. The gated zero-sum equation requires a ∈ F. Testing two rows with common kernel ⟨ℓ⟩ on the live tensor comparison requires a ∈ H + ⟨ℓ⟩. The assumed property gives F ∩ (H + ⟨ℓ⟩) = H, proving the exact verdict. These gates and equations compile as one tensor system with two live signs. [Binary gates and live signs](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SLTensorProjectionPacking/HostProfilePacking.md)

For example, suppose each block has tests for at least six of the seven nonzero characters and all possibly missing characters lie in a known proper dual subspace. Pick ℓ annihilated by that subspace. If F_i differs from H_i, then H_i^⊥ is the line of the missing character, since removing one nonzero vector from a space of dimension at least two leaves a spanning set. In that event ℓ belongs to H_i. If all F_i = H_i, the gates already check the exact third condition.

## 3. Koszul defects in the scalar-centroid case

Write the tensor as B from X to V ⊗ Y, with dim X and dim Y even and c = dim V congruent to 2 modulo 4. Wedge the V label into B to get D from V ⊗ X to Λ²V ⊗ Y. Its kernel and cokernel are modules over the pair-adjoint algebra E, whose pairs (a,b) satisfy B a = (I ⊗ b)B. Compute its radical J and factors M_(d_j)(F_j) of E/J. On the radical grades of the two defects, let m_j be the combined simple multiplicity for factor j. Suppose m_j is even whenever d_j is even. Let W be the sum of all odd-d_j isotypic pieces of the two graded defects. If dim W = O(log L) with a fixed constant, one two-sign instance suffices.

For any actual transporter, the product of the determinants of the source and target maps of D equals det(g_Y). The exponents on g_X, g_Y, and g_V are c, binom(c,2), and dim X + (c − 1)dim Y. Only the middle exponent is odd. In adapted frames the image determinant appears twice and cancels, leaving the product of the two defect determinants times the computable frame ratio. For a factor of degree d, the map over its center has a common conjugating matrix u, a field identification σ and a separate multiplicity map h on each occurrence of multiplicity m. Its three determinant contributions over F₃ are Norm(det u)^m, (det σ)^(dm), and Norm(det h)^d. The even-d pieces therefore cancel in product. Their possible reordering has positive sign, since their dimensions are even. The surviving determinant is exactly that of g_W. Attach a calibrated top volume in the exterior algebra of W to enforce the sign on g_Y, keeping the other two signs on the same transporter. The exterior algebra has polynomial dimension. [Defect calculation and finite-algebra steps](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/RawTensorSignReductions/LargeDefectKoszul.md) · [Two retained signs](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignChain/RetainedSigns.md)

For an unbounded scalar-centroid example, take the two modified pencils in Section 4 of the cited defect proof with c ≥ 6 and c congruent to 2 modulo 4. The repeated-index and distinct-index matrix blocks and the strict intertwiner calculation apply unchanged. Use one copy of the invertible pencil and an even number d of copies of the singular one with shared labels. The pair-adjoint algebra is F₃ × M_d(F₃), and both defects lie entirely on the matrix factor with equal multiplicities because D is square on each copy. Then W = 0 and each defect has size at least d. A centroid element commutes with the pair adjoints by moving its action to the shared label mode. Its X and Y actions are therefore scalar on each pencil family. Injectivity of the label map on each family makes those scalars equal by the shared label action. This gives scalar centroid.

## 4. Radical modules

For a nonempty selection I of modes, form their direct sum W over the local centroid C. Assume every C-linear automorphism of W has positive determinant. Assume also that either every indecomposable type occurs with even multiplicity, or every non-self-dual type occurs with equal multiplicity to its linear dual and the self-dual types occur with even multiplicity. The dual action is (cℓ)(v) = ℓ(cv). Then the product of the raw signs in I is a computable frame ratio on every transporter.

Choose frames on the endpoint decompositions, using identical frames on paired copies and dual frames on dual pairs. A centroid isomorphism twists module types and preserves multiplicities and duality. In these frames a reference semilinear map can match every paired copy by h ⊕ h, and every distinct dual pair by h ⊕ h^(−T). The reference is positive since both maps have positive determinant and reordering even-dimensional summands has positive sign. Every indecomposable is even-dimensional because an individual reflection would otherwise violate the positivity assumption. The actual semilinear map differs from the reference by a C-linear automorphism. Thus the endpoint frame ratio is the only contribution to the product. If the ratio is negative, reject. Otherwise retain two complementary sign rows.

For effective recognition, solve for E = End_C(W) and compute its radical and simple matrix factors. On the radical grades of W, a unit contributes the determinant norm on each simple factor to the power of its total simple multiplicity. Norm surjectivity and lifting units through the radical show positivity exactly when those multiplicities are even. Lift diagonal matrix-unit idempotents in complementary corners to obtain indecomposable summands. An idempotent modulo a nilpotent ideal lifts from x by raising x to a sufficiently high power of three, since x and x² − x commute. Lifts of the off-diagonal matrix units between summands of the same type give isomorphisms, since their compositions are identity modulo the radical. Apply the same method to W ⊕ W* to compare dual types. These operations are deterministic polynomial time at fixed characteristic. [Finite-field algebra algorithms, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

For a nonfree example, take C = K ⊕ K³ with square-zero radical and D = C/I₀ for a two-dimensional K-subspace I₀ of the radical. Use the map ((a,x),(b,y)) ↦ (ab, āy, xb̄) from (C ⊕ D)² to C ⊕ D ⊕ D. It is concise by testing against units and the individual input summands. The centroid equations on C × C kill both C-to-D blocks of the input actions and make their C-to-C actions multiplication by a common element. The mixed-product equations kill the D-to-C blocks and force the D actions to be multiplication by its residue. The spanning image fixes the output action. Hence the centroid is C. For the first two modes W is two copies of C ⊕ D. The two indecomposable types have even K dimensions and endomorphism residue K, so the positivity test passes.

There are two simpler criteria. For a chain centroid, two modes with only even Jordan lengths suffice. In each of their chain frames the sign is the same tangent norm raised to the sum of s(s−1)/2 over the chains. A nonzero combination of those two signs therefore kills the tangent character. One even-chain mode suffices when its exponent is even. For any local centroid of even residue-field length, a free mode of rank r has determinant given by the endpoint frame ratio times (det φ)^r, where φ is the common map on the centroid. Indeed a centroid-linear map has its residue determinant norm repeated an even number of times on the radical grades. Thus two free modes suffice, or one free mode of even rank. The other modes may be nonfree. [Chain frames and free-module signs](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorDeterminantFamilies/ChainCentroid.md)

## 5. Each entire centroid cell contains the full problem

Each cell contains an encoding of arbitrary even concise SL-TI. Consequently, closing the three-to-two reduction on any entire cell closes it for all inputs. Outside the even concise domain there is already a sign correction from an unused support complement or from scalar automorphisms. There is also a prior exact reduction to the even concise domain. [Even concise normalization](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorDeterminantIntervals/ScalarCentroidGlue.md)

**Scalar centroid.** The prior rank-separated marker pencil and evaluation block preserve the determinant profile while making the output centroid scalar, even over an algebraic closure. This already gives the encoding for the scalar cell.

**Multiple blocks.** Append the same multiplication tensor of Mat_k(F₃) at both endpoints, choosing even k with k² larger than all input mode dimensions. Its centroid is F₃, so it is a primitive block. Its format separates it from every original block. Evaluating a multiplication isotopy at the unit shows that it consists of one algebra automorphism together with left and right multiplications by units. Those unit multiplications have positive determinant because they act on k repeated columns or rows. An algebra automorphism is inner by the matrix-unit argument, hence also positive. The appended block preserves every sign profile exactly.

**A fixed proper field centroid.** Fix odd e > 1 and let K = F_(3^e). Interpret the original system over K. Add Sym²(V) and Sym³(V) to each original mode V, with multiplication maps forcing the induced symmetric-power actions. Add the K-linear map taking the chosen basis v_i to v_i³. Its preservation forces every entry of the original mode matrix to satisfy x³ = x. This uses the characteristic-three cubic identity and retains exactly the original F₃ transporters.

Apply the three-sign compiler over K, interpreting sign as quadratic character. Its determinant calculations concern squares and parity, so they apply over K. Before its final optional projection the bits are (a+c,a+b,a+b+c) for original bits a,b,c. This is invertible. The output is concise, has even dimensions, and has coefficients in F₃ in its K-coordinate bases. Apply the scalar-centroid wrapper over K, again using its parity calculation and coefficients in F₃. [Three-sign compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignChain/RetainedSigns.md) · [Scalar-centroid wrapper](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorDeterminantIntervals/ScalarCentroidGlue.md)

Restrict scalars to F₃. The new centroid contains K and is commutative, so all its operators are K-linear. By the scalar-centroid property over K the F₃-centroid is exactly K. A transporter induces one common automorphism of K. Coordinate Frobenius stabilizes each array and has positive determinant because its order divides e. Remove that action to obtain a K-linear map. Its F₃ determinant is the norm of its K determinant, agreeing with quadratic character. On F₃ entries the norm preserves sign because e is odd. Thus the encoded comparison has the exact SL verdict. In particular the cell remains as hard as unrestricted SL-TI with centroid fixed to F₂₇ and even relative dimensions.

**The two radical cells.** Extend a field-centroid encoding coefficientwise by A = F₃[ε]/(ε⁵). The bimap remains concise. Its centroid contains A and is commutative, so centroid endomorphisms are A-linear. Their equations base-change from the original linear equations, giving centroid K[ε]/(ε⁵), where K was the original centroid. The modes are free over that centroid. The intrinsic maximal ideal yields an original transporter \bar g_i by reduction. Its common centroid map takes ε to αε modulo ε² for nonzero α in the target coefficient field. On grade j the actual map is \bar g_i followed by multiplication by α^j. Thus in the coefficient frames its full determinant is (\det \bar g_i)^5(\det m_α)^10 = \det \bar g_i. Conversely each original map extends coefficientwise. This proves exact sign preservation for both radical cases, even when the coefficient field is fixed and the mode modules are free.

## 6. Polynomial-time decision with one relatively small mode per block

Suppose each primitive block has a mode of dimension at most two over its centroid residue field. The choice of mode can vary by block and the field degree can grow. Then SL-TI is decidable in deterministic polynomial time from raw arrays. In particular this gives a single two-sign instance by outputting a fixed yes or no pair.

For each block recover the coefficient field as the unique cube-power-fixed lift of its residue field. More explicitly, if that field has degree e, the fixed space of x ↦ x^(3^e) in the local centroid maps isomorphically onto it. Injectivity holds because a nilpotent fixed by this map is zero. Surjectivity follows by iterating the map on a lift until its nilpotent error vanishes. Thus the block becomes a bilinear map over its field K. For a pair of blocks enumerate the e possible field identifications, then test K-linear transporters in the aligned frames for each. A reference field identification is obtained by splitting the product of the two fields over F₃ given by their tensor product and using one primitive idempotent factor. Each factor has degree e and both field embeddings into it are isomorphisms. The Frobenius maps give all e choices. When moving the small mode to a label role, use its dual when required. This leaves determinant signs unchanged and supplies raw frame calibrations by linear algebra.

For any fixed invertible label map, the remaining task is strict equivalence of the slice tuples. It yields either no transporter or one explicit transporter plus the exact subgroup of sign corrections when the labels are fixed. To construct the transporter, form the endomorphism algebra of the direct sum of the two tuples, written as left and right linear intertwiners. The two summand projections are equivalent idempotents precisely when their images have equal ranks in every simple matrix factor of the radical quotient. In that case choose rectangular corner maps making their two products the two idempotents in the quotient and lift them in the corresponding corners. The products are invertible in those corners since their errors belong to the radical. Correct either product using its inverse to obtain mutually inverse isomorphisms. This proves the criterion and the construction. The stabilizer of the fixed label map is a translate of the invertible endomorphisms of one tuple. On the radical grades of its two mode modules, their signs are the norm characters on the simple matrix factors raised to their multiplicities. Norm surjectivity gives the exact binary subgroup H₀. [Finite-field algebra algorithms, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

A one-dimensional label mode needs only a square and a nonsquare scalar representative. Changing that scalar is lifted by the inverse scalar on one other mode, and multiplying the scalar by a square changes no signs. Now consider label dimension two. The Kronecker form of a pencil has left and right singular indices plus regular elementary divisors at geometric points of the projective line. A projective label change carries the regular points, with their divisor partitions, to the corresponding points and preserves the singular indices. This follows from the homogeneous pencil form. Regular local elementary divisors are transported by a change of local parameter of order one, and the degrees of homogeneous minimal kernel bases on either side are invariant under invertible linear substitution. The data are computable over K by decomposing the two-arrow endomorphism algebra into indecomposables and applying primary decomposition to the ratio on each regular indecomposable. One of the two slices on a regular indecomposable is invertible. [Kronecker decomposition](https://perso.uclouvain.be/paul.vandooren/publications/VDooren79.pdf)

If there are at least three distinct geometric regular points, choose an ordered triple of source points and enumerate ordered triples of distinct target points. Each gives its unique candidate projectivity. The field computation needs only a field containing the chosen six points, of degree at most the product of their six degrees over K. Iterate over triples of irreducible factors and then over their roots in that field. A root of an irreducible polynomial over a finite field brings all its conjugates into the same field. Normalize the projective matrix at its first nonzero entry to test that it is K-rational. For each rational candidate, strict-equivalence tests with this lift and one nonsquare scalar multiple give its entire possible sign profile, by the same scalar correction argument as in dimension one.

If there are fewer than three regular points, test the singular indices and regular point partitions first. With zero points the candidate projectivity is the identity. With one point it is rational, so choose bases to identify the two marked lines. With two rational points try both orderings and identify their two line bases. With a conjugate pair identify the quadratic coefficient fields, choose one root α at the source and a root β at the target, and write β = a + bα with b ≠ 0 to get an affine candidate mapping the pair. This covers both orderings by composing with the conjugate swap if required. If the field types differ there is no match. A strict-equivalence test gives a base transporter for each surviving field alignment.

For each surviving alignment the sign images of all other transporters are the base signs plus the source stabilizer images. The homomorphism from the allowed label action to the sign group modulo H₀ can be evaluated on a small list of representatives. With zero regular points, the label group is GL₂(K), generated by determinant-one elementary unipotents and representatives of K× for the determinant. Its mod-two image is generated by a nonsquare determinant because the unipotents have odd order and squares map to zero. One point gives an upper triangular group, with one nonsquare on each diagonal axis sufficient. Two rational points give that diagonal group and a possible swap, tested against the divisor partitions. A conjugate pair gives a torus of the quadratic field's units and its conjugation swap. In a pair of conjugate eigenline bases the torus has matrices diag(u,u^|K|). One nonsquare u and the swap suffice. For every representative use the constructive fixed-label routine for its lift. This computes precisely the sign subgroup modulo H₀. The known field alignment and the raw endpoint frames give the actual signs over F₃, including any semilinear contribution.

All required field arithmetic, including factoring over fields of polynomial degree over K, is deterministic polynomial time at fixed characteristic. For example, for square-free factorization into irreducibles, solve h^|K| = h in K[x]/(f). This is a K-linear fixed space isomorphic to K^s for s factors. Trace tests Tr_(K/F₃)(ch) for basis choices of c and h separate its coordinates by nondegeneracy of the trace pairing. GCDs against the three F₃ values separate the factors. Repeated factors can be stripped by the derivative and Frobenius-root steps. To find a nonsquare, start from −1 and take successive square roots until none exists. The order doubles on each replacement, so the number of root operations is at most logarithmic in the field order. This avoids enumerating elements of K.

Finally compute the exact profiles for all block pairs. Find one perfect matching using the nonempty profiles as edges. If there is none there is no ordinary transporter. A single matching supplies base signs, including basis calibration and permutation signs. The stabilizer sign group is generated by all local block corrections and by the transposition of two copies of an ordinary type, whenever a type repeats. For the latter choose a block isomorphism and its inverse to realize the transposition. Its determinant vector is precisely the parity vector of that block format, because the internal maps cancel and exchanging equally sized blocks has that sign. Every block permutation factors into those transpositions, and every residual block-preserving automorphism is local. Compute whether zero lies in the resulting affine binary space. All matching, field and profile computations are polynomial in the original dense length.

The reductions on the stated subclasses are complete informal proofs. The unrestricted three-to-two reduction remains open in this work.
