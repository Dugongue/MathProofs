# Exact arithmetic witnesses for one phase-moment law

An observation world is a finite extension $E/K$ of a totally real number field, with $E$ also totally real. A coin bias in $E$ must lie in $[0,1]$ under every real embedding of $E$. The unknown state is a finite list of independent Bernoulli coins with such biases, followed by one fixed finite conditional phase experiment. An observation is the expectation of a fixed rational score on a specified set of the coins. Its value is specified exactly in $K$. Observation arity counts the number of coins in that score. The world maps apply the given embedding to probability coefficients. They preserve admissibility and observations.

**Theorem 1 (three-coin probes).** For any affine system $X$ given by polynomial equations over $K$, one can build such an experiment with observation arity at most three. The response is the same fourth phase moment on four output coordinates for every input. For every world $E$ there is a canonical computable bijection
$$
X(E)\ \longleftrightarrow\
\{\text{completions of the trace over }E\text{ whose fourth response is nonconcave}\}.
$$
The bijections commute with every $K$-embedding. Every world also has a concave completion. These statements concern the actual completions and give a canonical explicit Jensen violation for each nonconcave one. The number of coins and readings is linear in the size of an arithmetic-circuit presentation of the equations. Numeric input constants enter only in one-coin calibration values.

There is a sharp contrast at arity two. In the same independent-coin model, with the same fixed phase channel and with no restriction on biases beyond their being totally in $[0,1]$, existence of a nonconcave completion over $E$ implies existence over the subfield of $E$ generated by its quadratic intermediate fields over $K$. In particular no new positive world can first appear in an odd-degree extension. The arity-three construction realizes first appearance in a totally real cyclic cubic extension.

## 1. Encoding values and arithmetic

For a wire value $x\in E$, use the two biases
$$
u=\frac1{1+x^2},\qquad v=\frac{1+x/(1+x^2)}2,\qquad z=2v-1.
$$
At every real embedding $0<u\le1$ and $1/4\le v\le3/4$. They satisfy $u^2+z^2=u$, and on the open locus $u\ne0$ this equality recovers $x=z/u$ and uniquely recovers the displayed biases from $x$. The remaining possibility $u=0$ forces $z=0$, so a wire can always take the boundary pair $(0,1/2)$.

Take an arithmetic circuit for the polynomial system with input wires, literal constant wires and outputs of binary sum and product gates. Other integer operations can be built with these. Use one pair $(u_i,v_i)$ for each wire, with an observation enforcing its encoding equality, and use these cleared equations for the two gate types:
$$
z_i u_j u_\ell+z_j u_i u_\ell-z_\ell u_i u_j=0
       \quad\text{for }x_\ell=x_i+x_j ,
$$
$$
z_i z_j u_\ell-z_\ell u_i u_j=0
       \quad\text{for }x_\ell=x_i x_j .
$$
Literal constant wires are calibrated by their two biases from the displayed encoding, using two one-coin readings with the known values in $K$. Impose $z_\ell=0$ on every tested output. To ensure every tested output is a gate output, append an addition by the literal constant zero. Include at least one gate output, adding an unused zero sum if needed.

We implement every equality above using only rational scores on at most three independent bits. A signed value in $[-1,1]$ is represented by the expectation of $2B-1$ for a helper bit $B$. The unsigned operand $u\in[0,1]$ can enter this representation by calibrating a helper signed expectation to the unsigned expectation, a two-coin equation. For two signed operands $s,t$, independence makes $st$ the expectation of the product of their centered bits. Force another centered bit to have that expectation using a three-coin score. When an operation uses the same operand twice, use an independent copy with a tied bias, imposed by one two-coin score. Likewise the signed average $(s+t)/2$, negation and rational constant signed expectations are imposed by affine scores using at most three coins. Compute each monomial by signed products, and add the signed monomials by a balanced tree of pairwise averages, padding the list to a power of two by zeros. The resulting signed value is a nonzero known scalar multiple of the desired polynomial, so a final reading of zero imposes the equation. Each template above has bounded size and integer coefficients, with repetitions represented by repeated summands. All helper biases are uniquely determined by their operands and stay in $[0,1]$ at every real embedding. The operations introduce only a constant overhead per wire or gate. This uses the elementary multilinear representation of expectations of functions of independent bits. [Owen, multilinear extensions](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.18.5.64).

Let $s$ be the number of arithmetic wires. Add the designated flag $B$ with bias $q$, and bits of biases $b_i$ subject to $u_i b_i=q/s$ and $\sum_i b_i=1$. Use three-coin product scores for the first equations and affine scores on successive partial sums for the last equation. If $q>0$, all $u_i>0$ and
$$
q=\frac{s}{\sum_i1/u_i}=\frac{s}{s+\sum_i x_i^2},
\qquad b_i=\frac{q}{s u_i}.
$$
All these biases and partial sums are valid under every real embedding. The normalization prevents the flag from decaying as a product of many wire biases.

Any solution $x\in X(E)$ determines the arithmetic wire values, then all their biases, helper biases, harmonic flag and partial sums. These data give a unique state, and $q>0$. Conversely $q>0$ makes every wire recoverable. The encoding and cleared gate equations reconstruct exactly the original input solution and its prescribed circuit values. A completion with $q=0$ always exists: assign all input wires and gate outputs the boundary pair, retain the calibrated literal constants, and set the helper biases to their uniquely determined legal values. Each cleared gate equation vanishes because both cleared output coordinates vanish. The tested outputs also vanish. Put $b_i=1$ at one boundary wire and zero elsewhere to satisfy the flag equations. The positive-witness maps involve only rational arithmetic and the given field constants, so commute with all embeddings.

## 2. The fixed phase response

The output phase alphabet is $\{1,-1,\mathrm i,-\mathrm i\}^4$. Conditional on $B=0$, use independent uniform phases. Conditional on $B=1$, use density
$$
1-\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}
 \bigl(\xi_1\xi_2\overline{\xi_3}\,\overline{\xi_4}\bigr)
$$
relative to that uniform product measure. This is a fixed channel with rational probabilities. Every three-coordinate marginal is independent uniform under either flag value. The response is the fourth moment
$$
F_\mu(x)=\mathbb E_\mu\left|\sum_{j=1}^4 x_j^{1/4}\xi_j\right|^4
       =\sum_j x_j+4\sum_{i<j}\sqrt{x_ix_j}
                 -2q(x_1x_2x_3x_4)^{1/4}.
$$
For the equality expand the fourth moment using two powers and two conjugate powers. Its charges have magnitude at most two in each coordinate. Multiplying by the tilt character or its conjugate leaves magnitude at most three, so reduction modulo four causes no extra Fourier matches. The only nontrivial matches come from the chosen balanced charge and its negative, each with multinomial coefficient four. This is the original phase-moment obstruction on one four-set, now with a finite alphabet and controlled coefficient. [Phase-moment construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/FourFieldProposals/PartiallyObservedLaws.md).

For $q=0$ each summand is concave. For $q>0$ a canonical witness uses the midpoint of $(1,1,1,0)$ and $(1,1,1,t)$ with $t=(q/16)^4$. The midpoint gap is
$$
12(2^{-1/2}-1/2)t^{1/2}
     -2q(2^{-1/4}-1/2)t^{1/4}<0.
$$
Indeed its positive term is below $6q^2/256$, while the magnitude of the negative term exceeds $q^2/32$. The endpoints use rational operations in the flag probability, so the same formula is a violating certificate under every real embedding. No new state parameter comes from the phase output, whose law is fixed given the flag. This proves the natural bijection and explains why the law verdict is forced exactly when $X(E)=\varnothing$, and undetermined otherwise.

If the arithmetic wire values have absolute value at most $H$ at a given real embedding, the harmonic flag has $q\ge(1+H^2)^{-1}$. The displayed midpoint then violates concavity by at least $1/[128(1+H^2)^2]$, uniformly in the number of wires.

There is a converse for noisy readings with a positive flag separated from zero. Fix a real embedding and suppose the error in each expected score and each calibration is at most $\delta$, while the biases remain valid probabilities. If $q/s>\delta$, write $\rho=q/s-\delta$. Each flag-link equation gives $u_i\ge\rho$ since $b_i\le1$. The decoded $x_i=(2v_i-1)/u_i$ have $|x_i|\le1/\rho$. Each arithmetic gate polynomial has residual at most $C\delta$ for an absolute constant $C$: at a centered product the errors from the two inputs add because all signed values have magnitude at most one, averaging does not increase the maximum input error, and each template has a fixed finite number of operations and clone ties. Dividing the cleared residual by $u_i u_j u_\ell$ gives decoded addition and multiplication errors at most $C\delta/\rho^3$. A tested output has error at most $\delta/\rho$, and a literal value $c$ has decoded error at most $(2+|c|)\delta/\rho$. The conclusion is an approximate solution to the displayed circuit equations, with explicit local errors. It makes no assertion that noisy observations give an exact arithmetic point.

## 3. Sharpness for pairwise probes

An exact rational score observation on two independent Bernoulli coins is an equality $a x y+b x+c y+d=0$ in their biases, with coefficients in the trace field $K$. A one-coin observation is unary. This covers tuples of scores by treating their coordinates as separate equations. The response is nonconcave precisely when the designated flag bias is positive. Even if probes of arity at most two are allowed to touch the output phases, their expectations reduce to affine functions of one coin or constants because all proper phase marginals of that size are independent uniform conditional on either flag value.

Given a witnessing solution in $E$, process each nonzero pairwise equation. If $ad-bc=0$, its rank-one coefficient matrix factors over $K$ into one affine factor in each bias. Choose a factor vanishing at the witness, making that equation a unary pin. If $ad-bc\ne0$, the equation reads
$$
y=-\frac{b x+d}{a x+c},
$$
an invertible fractional linear map on its domain. The denominator cannot vanish at a solution because it cannot vanish together with the numerator. In each connected component of the graph of invertible links, choose a root variable $s$. Every other coordinate is a fractional linear function with coefficients in $K$, obtained along a spanning tree. After substitution, off-tree links yield rational equalities whose cross-products have degree at most two in $s$. The unary pins yield linear equalities. The remaining conditions are that all coordinates lie totally in $[0,1]$, all denominators are nonzero, and the flag is positive in its component.

If a substituted equation is nonzero, the witnessing root has degree at most two over $K$, so the whole component already lies in the subfield $E_2$ generated by all such quadratic subfields of $E$. If all equations vanish identically, retain the root if it already lies in $K$. Otherwise no coordinate can be zero or one at any real embedding: an invertible fractional linear function taking those values would put the root in $K$. For each real embedding of $K$, pick an extension to $E$, and a sufficiently small interval around the image of the root. Inside the interval all fractional functions retain their strict bounds and denominator conditions. Choose a new root in $K$ meeting all these intervals at once. Such a choice exists by density of $K$ in the product over its real embeddings. In a primitive-element basis the evaluation matrix is invertible Vandermonde, and the density reduces to that of $\mathbb Q^{[K: \mathbb Q]}$ in Euclidean space. The new component satisfies all requirements using biases in $K$. Combine the independent components. The surviving quadratic coordinates still satisfy all real bounds in $E_2$, since every real embedding of $E_2$ extends to $E$. This proves the pairwise descent claim.

The field $E_2$ has degree a power of two over $K$. For $K=\mathbb Q$, the equation $x^3-3x+1=0$ is irreducible by the rational root test and has three real roots. Its discriminant is $81$, so its splitting field is a totally real cyclic cubic extension. Theorem 1 gives a fixed trace forced over $\mathbb Q$ and undetermined over that cubic field. The pairwise descent proof excludes this profile at arity two.

## 4. Simultaneous certification and uniform input families

Let $L/K$ be a totally real finite Galois extension and let $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$. For each finite $G$-set $S$, one experiment realizes the entire witness diagram
$$
E\longmapsto S^{\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)}
$$
on intermediate fields, including all embedding transports. Indeed each transitive orbit $G/H$ is the root set in $L$ of the minimal polynomial of a primitive element of $L^H/K$. Translate the finitely many chosen primitive elements by ordinary integers to make their polynomials distinct. Their product realizes $S$ as an equivariant set of roots. Use that single polynomial in Theorem 1, and use an inconsistent equation for empty $S$. The natural bijection preserves counts, orbit stabilizers and exactly which witness transports coincide. This transfers the finite descent-complete witness classification to nonconcave completions of a fixed phase law. It uses a low-arity statistical experiment and actual law witnesses for every probe, rather than just matching decision bits. [Galois action on finite étale schemes](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04JI).

The transformation also respects the two operations from simultaneous computability at the witness level. Products are realized by taking disjoint wire lists for the two equation systems and requiring both through one common flag. Disjoint unions are realized by a two-valued selector, multiplying equations in each branch by its selector indicator and fixing inactive input coordinates at zero on that branch. This determines the inactive coordinates uniquely, so the point set is a true disjoint union, and Theorem 1 transports it unchanged. In any computable bounded-format family of input equations, take one padded circuit skeleton computing the full monomial expansions. Use one calibrated constant wire per coefficient. Then all coin scopes, rational score functions, phase channel and response stay fixed, while only the rational or $K$-valued calibration readings change with the shared input. In particular the witness family is a trace family of a single finite observation system when the polynomial dimension, degree and number of constraints are bounded.

There is a variant for integral witnesses. For input equations over $\mathcal O_K$, require each input wire to be of integral precision, meaning either the boundary pair or a pair whose decoded value $z/u$ is in $\mathcal O_E$. This is an additional explicit admissibility condition on the input coins, independent of the equation coefficients. It is stable under embeddings. All gate values of an integral input are automatically integral, and the proof gives the same natural bijection with $X(\mathcal O_E)$, with the same boundary witness and arity. Applied to the bounded-format arithmetic trace realizations on totally real multiquadratic probes, this transfers every monotone c.e. trace profile on a fixed finite poset to forced versus undetermined concavity, with the actual integral point witnesses preserved. The arity-two obstruction above applies to the unrestricted bias model before this additional integral-precision condition. [Arithmetic trace realization](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/FourResearchPrograms/ArithmeticTraces.md), [Simultaneous computability](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/FourFieldProposals/SimultaneousComputability.md).

In the real-valued version of the same rational experiment class, deciding whether the trace admits a nonconcave completion is $\exists\mathbb R$-complete. Membership follows because biases are variables in $[0,1]$, observations are polynomial equalities and nonconcavity is the single strict flag inequality. For hardness, existential real satisfiability reduces to feasibility of polynomial equations, using auxiliary variables for inequalities and polynomial selectors for Boolean choices. Arithmetic circuits implement such encodings with polynomial overhead. Theorem 1 applies verbatim over $\mathbb R$. Probability-logical and parametric Markov models already give other $\exists\mathbb R$-hardness results. The claims here concern an always feasible trace, a fixed fourth-moment law, and the arity threshold and exact witness functor across arithmetic worlds. [Mossé–Ibeling–Icard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13936), [Parametric reachability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13128).

These are prose proofs. The construction relies on the prescribed independence of the hidden coin coordinates. Allowing arbitrary joint fillings would change the model and invalidate the nonlinear arithmetic step.
