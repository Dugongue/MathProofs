# Arithmetic universality inside a rank-two alternating congruence orbit

**Theorem.** Let $X$ be an affine scheme of finite type over $\mathbb Q$, presented by polynomial equations. One can construct a finitely specified alternating matrix, with each specified upper-triangular entry in $\{0,1\}$, whose rank-at-most-two completion scheme is isomorphic over $\mathbb Q$ to a principal open subset of $X\times\mathbb A^s$ for some $s$. The projection to $X$ is smooth and surjective, and every point over every extension field of $\mathbb Q$ has a point above it over the same field. One prescribed entry is $1$, so every completion over a field has rank exactly two. The graph of prescribed off-diagonal entries has maximum degree at most five. The construction is polynomial time in an explicit arithmetic-circuit presentation of the equations and their coefficient bits.

Consequently Hilbert's tenth problem over $\mathbb Q$ is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to deciding whether the congruence orbit of $J_2\oplus0_{N-2}$ meets a given coordinate mask of this kind, where $J_2=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The same holds for special-linear congruence for $N\ge3$. The hardness reduction can also be made to output only masks having a completion over every completion of $\mathbb Q$. These equivalences do not require assuming a decision for rational polynomial solvability. This is an informal proof.

Over every fixed finite field the decision problem is NP-complete even for bipartite prescribed-entry graphs of maximum degree three, with the $1$ entries forming a perfect matching and the $0$ entries forming disjoint paths. In that restricted model the completion problem is exactly projective-line coloring of the graph obtained by contracting the zero-edge paths.

## 1. From a rank-two completion to planar determinant constraints

Use distinct index vertices $H,V$ and prescribe $m_{HV}=1$. The rank-at-most-two scheme is cut out by all three-by-three minors. On the normalized chart with $m_{HV}=1$, write $u_i=(x_i,y_i)=(m_{iV},m_{Hi})$ for every index. The two anchor vectors are $H=(1,0)$ and $V=(0,1)$. The rank equations are equivalent on this chart to
$$
m_{ij}=\det(u_i,u_j)=x_i y_j-y_i x_j
$$
for all indices. In fact eliminate the invertible $H,V$ principal block by block row and column operations. The remaining Schur complement must vanish because each of its entries is, up to a unit, a three-by-three minor including the pivot. Conversely its vanishing gives a factorization through a free module of rank two. This argument works over every $\mathbb Q$-algebra, so the equivalence is scheme-theoretic and gives a unique determinant representation in the normalized chart.

Every rank-two alternating matrix over a field is congruent to $J_2\oplus0$: select a pair of vectors with nonzero alternating pairing, scale one to make the pairing $1$, and split off the orthogonal complement of this nondegenerate plane. In dimension at least three the radical is nonzero, and scaling one radical coordinate changes the determinant of a transporter arbitrarily while preserving the form. Thus the general-linear and special-linear congruence orbits agree on these instances.

## 2. Arithmetic using nonzero scalars

Use nonzero scalar variables with primitive constraints $xy=z$, $z=x+1$, $x=y$, and $x=1$. Every such variable is a unit scheme-theoretically. We first show that this arithmetic language represents any affine scheme over $\mathbb Q$ up to the stated smooth open factors.

Express the input by arithmetic gates for addition, multiplication, the constants $0,1$, and equality. Binary integer coefficients are generated by repeated doubling and addition of one, negative constants by additive inverse, and rational constants by division by an explicitly prescribed nonzero integer. The auxiliary gate values are uniquely determined on each point. These gate constraints give an isomorphic affine scheme.

Represent each gate value $a$ as $a^+-a^-$ using two nonzero variables. Given $a$, the possible choices are an open affine line in the coordinate $a^-$, excluding $0$ and $-a$. The zero gate uses $a^+=a^-$, and the one gate uses $a^+=a^-+1$. For equality of two represented values use $a^++b^-=b^++a^-$. An addition gate $a+b=c$ is equivalent to
$$
c^+ + a^- + b^- = c^- + a^+ + b^+ ,
$$
and a multiplication gate $ab=c$ is equivalent to
$$
c^+ +a^+b^-+a^-b^+ = c^- +a^+b^+ +a^-b^- .
$$
All displayed terms are nonzero, and each product on the two sides is a primitive multiplication.

To implement an equality of two sums of nonzero terms, add the same fresh nonzero shift $t$ to each side. Build its two running sums from $t$ and equate the final results. Require each running sum to be nonzero. For nonzero $x,y,z$, the single addition $x+y=z$ is expressed by fresh nonzero $v,u,w$ with
$$
xv=1,\qquad u=vy,\qquad w=u+1,\qquad z=xw.
$$
These equations give the unique auxiliary values when the three declared operands are units. Conversely they imply the desired sum identity. Once the split parameters $a^-$ are chosen, there are only finitely many excluded shifts $t$, namely zero and the negatives of the two lists of prefix sums. This shows that witnesses lift over every infinite field, including all residue and extension fields of $\mathbb Q$.

More precisely the auxiliary values are unique regular functions of the input coordinates, the split parameters, and the shifts wherever the declared denominators are invertible. Conversely subtracting each pair of split values recovers all raw gate equations, and the reciprocal constraints force exactly the stipulated units. Thus the primitive arithmetic solution scheme is a principal open subset of $X$ times affine space. The open condition is given by the product of the split values, shifts and shifted partial sums. This inverse description takes place by polynomial operations and inversion of units in arbitrary algebras, so it preserves nonreduced scheme structures. Each projection fiber is a nonempty open of affine space. Smoothness and surjectivity follow.

## 3. Determinant gadgets use only the entries zero and one

A local reference $D=(1,1)$ is fixed by the two constraints $\det(H,D)=\det(D,V)=1$ on a normalized frame. Encode a nonzero scalar $x$ by three distinct index vertices $d_x,w_x,r_x$. Impose
$$
\det(d_x,D)=0,\quad
\det(V,w_x)=0,\quad
\det(H,r_x)=0,\quad
\det(d_x,w_x)=\det(d_x,r_x)=1 .
$$
In normalized coordinates these equations uniquely give
$$
d_x=(x,x),\qquad w_x=(0,x^{-1}),\qquad r_x=(-x^{-1},0)
$$
for an arbitrary unit $x$. For a separate vector vertex $u=(a,b)$, specifying $\det(u,w_x)=1$ fixes $a=x$. Specifying $\det(u,r_x)=1$ fixes $b=x$. Coordinate $1$ can be specified by $\det(u,V)=1$ for the first coordinate and $\det(H,u)=1$ for the second. Thus all connections are ordinary prescribed matrix entries.

To implement $xy=z$, take fresh vertices $(x,z)$ and $(1,y)$ with the indicated coordinate constraints, and require their determinant to vanish. For $z=x+1$ use $(z,x)$ and a local $D$ and prescribe $\det((z,x),D)=1$. For $x=y$, use two fresh vertices $(x,1),(y,1)$ and set their mutual determinant to zero. To require $x=1$ add $\det(d_x,V)=1$. All coordinates of every additional vertex are uniquely determined after normalization by the primitive arithmetic solution. The equations are exact even over a coefficient ring because coordinates of the form $x^{-1}$ are imposed by a product equal to $1$.

## 4. Degree and coefficient bounds

Replace each scalar by one copy per occurrence in the primitive constraints, and join copies in a chain with the equality gadget. The copies then have one gate occurrence and at most two equality occurrences. If a variable has no occurrence it may use a single scalar gadget. Each reciprocal axis has two edges in its own scalar gadget and at most three incoming coordinate constraints, giving degree at most five. The diagonal scalar vertex has three gadget edges and at most one further constraint. Each gate coordinate vertex has at most three edges.

Avoid high-degree frame vertices by giving each use of an anchor its own frame $(H_i,V_i)$, sharing a frame for the two distinct anchor edges fixing a local $D$ if desired. Arrange the frames in a chain. Keep $\det(H_1,V_1)=1$ and for each consecutive pair impose
$$
\det(H_i,H_{i+1})=\det(V_i,V_{i+1})=0,\qquad
\det(H_i,V_{i+1})=\det(H_{i+1},V_i)=1.
$$
If $(H_i,V_i)$ is a normalized basis these equations force $H_{i+1}=H_i$ and $V_{i+1}=V_i$, including over coefficient rings. Thus every local frame agrees with the original. Each frame vertex has at most four chain edges and at most one local-use edge. The original normalization edge is incident only to the first frame, whose vertices have at most two chain edges each. A local $D$ used once has at most three edges. This proves the maximum-degree bound five.

Order the horizontal frame vertices first, followed by all gate and diagonal scalar vertices, then all $D$ vertices, then the vertical frame vertices, then the reciprocal axes. Every nonzero request has determinant $1$ in this order. In particular the successor constraint has ordered endpoints $(z,x),D$, and the two coordinate requests pointing to a reciprocal axis have the coordinate vertex first. Reverse orientation changes only zero equations elsewhere. All unrequested entries remain unprescribed. The reduction adds only a bounded number of vertices and edges per primitive arithmetic occurrence and writes a polynomial-size mask.

The determinant chart in Section 1, arithmetic equivalence in Section 2 and uniqueness of all gadget coordinates yield the claimed principal-open isomorphism. A positive-dimensional radical can always be ensured by adding a fresh index whose vector is pinned to zero by two zero anchor pairings, using fresh local anchors in the frame chain.

## 5. Decision, the local promise, and relationship to earlier work

For the reverse reduction to rational polynomial solvability, use variables $x_i,y_i$ and impose each requested determinant equation. A finite list of rational equalities is equivalent to the vanishing of the sum of their squares, with denominators cleared. This proves many-one equivalence. Over the real numbers the same arithmetic construction works for every existential-real equation system. Inequalities can first be encoded by real square and reciprocal variables, giving existential-real completeness for the real counterpart.

For the everywhere-local promise, start with a rational equation $f(\mathbf z)=0$, and replace it by the product equation $f(\mathbf z)(r^2-13)(r^2-17)(r^2-221)=0$, with $r$ fresh. The three new factors have no rational zeros, and at least one has a root over every completion of $\mathbb Q$. Over odd primes other than $13,17$, at least one of the three units has square residue. Hensel lifting gives a square root. At $13$ the number $17$ is a square, at $17$ the number $13$ is a square, and at $2$ the number $17$ is a square since it is congruent to $1$ modulo $8$. The real case uses positivity. Thus the arithmetic and determinant systems lift at every completion, while the rational answer stays the same. This uses the local-promise device in [our rational unit-distance proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/RationalUnitDistance/PROOF.md).

By applying the same theorem to the source hypersurfaces in Corollary 3 of [the rectangular rank-two universality proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/RankTwoGeometricUniversality/PROOF.md), the everywhere-local hardness also holds when the alternating completion scheme is smooth and geometrically integral. The source smoothing in that corollary gives a computable many-one reduction. Its running time remains unbounded by a stated polynomial.

## 6. A sharp finite-field coloring interpretation

Consider a binary mask whose prescribed $1$ edges are a nonempty perfect matching and whose prescribed $0$ edges are disjoint paths and isolated vertices. Interpret the endpoints of each edge in matrix order. Every witness vector is nonzero because it is incident to a determinant-$1$ edge. A zero edge imposes equality of projective directions. A one edge implies distinct directions. Therefore any rank-two completion gives a coloring of the zero-path contraction graph by elements of $\mathbb P^1(K)$, the projective line over the field $K$. A contracted loop means the graph has no coloring.

Conversely, choose a nonzero representative direction at each vertex using any such coloring. Each matched pair has nonzero determinant. Rescale one endpoint of each matched pair so that its ordered determinant is $1$. Because the pairs are disjoint, those rescalings can be made independently. Zero edges still have determinant zero. Determinants of all pairs now fill the matrix with rank at most two, and a matching edge forces rank exactly two.

For hardness, start from an input graph $G$, delete isolated vertices, and replace every remaining vertex by one copy for each incident edge. Each edge of $G$ gives a prescribed $1$ edge between its two endpoint copies. For each original vertex join its copies into a path of prescribed $0$ edges. The $1$ edges form a perfect matching, the $0$ edges form disjoint paths, and contraction recovers $G$ after deleting isolated vertices. To ensure bipartiteness, assign opposite colors to the ends of every $1$ edge. For a $0$ edge with endpoints of the same color, subdivide it with a new vertex of the opposite color, and put a new pendant $1$ edge on that vertex. The new pendant endpoint can always take a projective direction different from its neighbor. The construction preserves the perfect matching and disjoint zero paths. The maximum degree is three. A detached $1$ edge can be added to handle empty input graphs or ensure dimension at least three after adding two such edges. It adds no restriction on colorability.

Over $\mathbb F_q$ there are $q+1$ projective directions, so this construction is a polynomial-time reduction from fixed $(q+1)$-colorability. Fixed $k$-colorability is NP-complete for $k\ge 3$, using 3-colorability and joining the input with a $(k-3)$-clique. A determinant representation is a polynomial-size certificate over a fixed finite field. This proves the claimed NP-completeness, including the binary field. See [Karp's reductions](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

## 7. Relationship to earlier results

The bounded-degree determinant gadgets transfer [the earlier rectangular rank-two universality](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/RankTwoGeometricUniversality/PROOF.md) to a single alternating congruence orbit. The finite-field argument uses the projective-line coloring idea from [the earlier rectangular completion proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/RankTwoAndRingUniversality/RankTwoCompletion.md). The additions here are the alternating form, the subcubic bipartite mask, and the exact contraction rule for that mask model. A prior result on Fano specialization concerns a directed degeneration relation. Here the ambient orbit is fixed by rank, and all input information is in individual masked entries. [Bernstein's rank-two completion paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.06797) studies algebraic-matroid independence for skew-symmetric rank-two matrices. Independence addresses generic data on an observation pattern, while this theorem concerns exact completions at fixed binary observations. [Laurent and Varvitsiotis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.6602) study a real positive-semidefinite completion model. A targeted search did not locate the bounded-degree binary alternating-orbit scheme theorem. Publication priority remains unestablished.
