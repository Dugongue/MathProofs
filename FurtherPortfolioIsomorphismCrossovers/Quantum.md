# Optimal Local-Unitary Overlap of Repeated Code States

## Exact overlap theorem

Let $A$ be a finite alphabet, let $a,b:A^n\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ satisfy $\sum_x a_x^2=\sum_x b_x^2=1$, and let $t\ge2$. Encode $a$ as the state $\Psi_t(a)=\sum_x a_x\bigotimes_{i=1}^n |x_i\rangle^{\otimes t}$, and likewise encode $b$. The local overlap allows an arbitrary permutation $P$ of all $tn$ physical sites and an arbitrary product $U$ of one-site unitaries. Write $\mathcal G$ for the classical action allowing an arbitrary permutation of the $n$ coordinates and a separate alphabet permutation in each coordinate. Then

$$
\max_{P,U}\left|\langle\Psi_t(b),UP\Psi_t(a)\rangle\right|^2
=\left(\max_{g\in\mathcal G}\ \sum_{x\in A^n}a_xb_{g x}\right)^2.
$$

In particular, for binary linear codes $C,D\le\mathbb F_2^n$, let $\Psi_t(C)$ denote the encoding with amplitudes $|C|^{-1/2}\mathbf 1_C$, and put $r=\max_{\pi\in S_n}\dim(\pi C\cap D)$. Their optimal squared overlap is exactly

$$
2^{\,2r-\dim C-\dim D}.
$$

At $t=2$, each length-$n$ code has a polynomially constructible signed stabilizer tableau on $2n$ qubits. Equivalence under local unitaries and qubit permutations is exactly binary permutation-code equivalence. Equal-dimensional inequivalent codes have optimal squared overlap at most $1/4$. With arbitrary dimensions, the bound is $1/2$.

### Proof

Fix any physical permutation $P$. On the physical sites after applying $P$, the source's repetition blocks form one partition into $n$ blocks of size $t$. The target's repetition blocks form a second such partition. Make a bipartite multigraph with source blocks on the left, target blocks on the right, and one edge for each physical site. Both degrees are $t$. The graph splits into $t$ perfect matchings. In fact any subset of left vertices has at least as many neighbors by counting its incident edges with multiplicity, so Hall's theorem gives one perfect matching. Delete its edges and iterate on the regular residual graph. Each matching identifies a set of sites meeting every source and target block exactly once. For $t=2$ this is the alternating-cycle decomposition of the two physical pairings.

Write $i(e),j(e)$ for the source and target blocks of an edge, and let $U_e$ be its one-site matrix. The overlap is
$$
\sum_{x,y}a_xb_y\prod_e (U_e)_{y_{j(e)},\,x_{i(e)}}.
$$
For matching $M_s$, define $f_s(x,y)=\prod_{e\in M_s}|(U_e)_{y_{j(e)},x_{i(e)}}|$. Using the triangle inequality followed by Hölder for the nonnegative measure of weight $a_xb_y$, the absolute overlap is at most
$$
\prod_{s=1}^t\left(\sum_{x,y}a_xb_y f_s(x,y)^t\right)^{1/t}.
$$
Every $f_s$ belongs to $[0,1]$, so $f_s^t\le f_s^2$. For each edge, the squared absolute entries of $U_e$ form a doubly stochastic matrix. By the Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem, it is a convex combination of permutation matrices. Expand those combinations independently along $M_s$. The sum with $f_s^2$ is the expectation of $\sum_x a_xb_{gx}$ for a classical action $g$ built from the matching's coordinate permutation and its sampled alphabet permutations. Thus each factor is at most the $1/t$-th power of the largest classical score. For equality, repeat the maximizing coordinate permutation and alphabet permutations on every physical copy and use their permutation matrices. This also proves the assertion for weighted amplitudes.

For binary codes, alphabet permutations amount to bit flips and hence to translation by a single vector $v$. A nonempty intersection $(\pi C+v)\cap D$ is a coset of $\pi C\cap D$, and the untranslated intersection is always nonempty. Its maximum size at this $\pi$ is therefore $2^{\dim(\pi C\cap D)}$. Normalizing and squaring gives the formula. At an optimal physical permutation, the coordinate permutation supplied by every matching is optimal for the binary intersection objective. Indeed, the same upper bound before maximizing over coordinate permutations is the geometric mean of the matching-specific upper bounds, which are all positive and bounded by the global optimum. Equality forces each one to attain it.

For the tableau, let $E=C^{[t]}$ be the classical linear code obtained by repeating each coordinate $t$ times. The state $\Psi_t(C)$ has stabilizer generators $X^u$ for basis vectors $u$ of $E$ and $Z^v$ for basis vectors $v$ of $E^\perp$. They commute, fix the state, and are independent in total number $tn$. Binary elimination constructs their matrix without listing the codewords.


## Exact invertible-local equivalence

Suppose all one-coordinate probability marginals of the weights $a_x^2$ and $b_x^2$ are uniform. The exact orbit-equivalence conclusion holds if the local unitaries are replaced by arbitrary invertible complex maps and equality is taken up to a nonzero scalar. This follows because the one-site reduced density matrix of a repeated state is diagonal, by tracing another copy of the coordinate, and is the identity divided by $|A|$ under the uniform marginal assumption.

For completeness, if local invertible maps send one such state to the other up to scalar, remove the local unitary parts of their polar decompositions. Divide each positive part by its determinant's positive $|A|$-th root, absorbing the factors into the scalar. The resulting positive operator is $\exp H$ for $H=\sum_i H_i^{(i)}$, where each $H_i$ is traceless Hermitian and $H_i^{(i)}$ acts at a single site. Write $\psi$ for the permuted source and $f(s)=\|\exp(sH)\psi\|^2$. The uniform one-site density matrices at the endpoints imply $f'(0)=f'(1)=0$, since $f'(s)=2\langle\exp(sH)\psi,H\exp(sH)\psi\rangle$. Also $f''(s)=4\|H\exp(sH)\psi\|^2\ge 0$. Thus $f''$ vanishes on the interval, $H\psi=0$, and the positive operator fixes $\psi$. This yields a local unitary transporter.

For binary linear codes, every nonzero coordinate is uniform and every zero coordinate contributes repeated pure one-site factors. An invertible transporter must match the rank-one sites by invariance of Schmidt rank across each one-site cut. Remove those sites and apply the preceding argument on the others. Binary permutation-code equivalence therefore also classifies the exact invertible-local orbits on the repeated family. The tensor has $tn$ modes and is represented succinctly by its tableau. The quantitative overlap theorem uses the unitary action. [Van den Nest, Dehaene and De Moor, Local Equivalence of Stabilizer States](https://ftp.esat.kuleuven.be/pub/pub/SISTA/mvandenn/04-210.pdf)

## Exact complexity transition for a variable overlap threshold

For the states encoded at $t=2$, allow an input dyadic threshold $\theta$ and ask whether optimal squared overlap is at least $\theta$. This problem is NP-complete, even when one of the underlying codes has dimension one. A certificate is a coordinate permutation. Binary elimination computes its intersection dimension and the formula gives a polynomial-time comparison with the threshold.

For hardness use Vardy's binary minimum-distance problem, where the input asks whether a code $C$ contains a nonzero word of weight at most $k$. The cases $k=0$, $k>n$, and $\dim C=0$ are easy, so restrict to $1\le k\le n$ and positive code dimension. Define $C'=C\oplus\mathbb F_2^{k-1}$, let $D$ be spanned by a word of weight $k$ at the length of $C'$, and set $\theta=2^{\,1-\dim C'}$. The maximum intersection dimension with $D$ is one exactly if $C'$ has a word of weight exactly $k$. Any such word must have a nonzero $C$-part, since the free block has length $k-1$. Conversely any nonzero $C$-word of weight at most $k$ can be brought to weight $k$ by adding a word in the free block. The squared overlaps at YES and NO are $\theta$ and $\theta/4$ respectively. Trivial minimum-distance cases can be dispatched to fixed output pairs of the proper answer. For instance, with $C'$ and $D$ distinct rank-one codes at length two generated by words of weights one and two, the threshold is $1$ and the overlap is $1/4$. [Vardy, Algorithmic Complexity in Coding Theory and the Minimum Distance Problem](https://doi.org/10.1145/258533.258559)


The hardness can also be imposed when both states have maximally mixed one-site marginals and the target code has dimension two. For this version, append $k+1$ zero coordinates to $C'$, obtaining $B$ at length $m=\operatorname{length}(C')+k+1$. Put $\widetilde C=B+\langle\mathbf1\rangle$ and $\widetilde D=\langle d,\mathbf1\rangle$, where $d$ has weight $k$ at length $m$ and $\mathbf1$ is the all-ones word. Every coordinate has full binary support since both codes contain $\mathbf1$. Their permuted intersection always contains $\mathbf1$, and has dimension two exactly if $\widetilde C$ contains a word of weight $k$. Such a word is in $B$, since every word of the form $\mathbf1+b$ has weight at least $k+1$ by the appended zero coordinates. The same minimum-distance test follows with threshold $2^{2-\dim\widetilde C}$. The YES and NO overlaps again have ratio four.

The factor-four gap in this reduction is multiplicative at a threshold that can be exponentially small. The separate isomorphism gap for equal dimensions is additive and constant. If density matrices individually lie within trace distance $\varepsilon$ of the encoded inputs, the maximized Hilbert–Schmidt overlap differs by at most $2\varepsilon$, since the other density operator in each trace-difference term is an effect. This uses normalized density matrices and pure-state squared overlap in the noiseless case.

## Recovering a code from raw stabilizer data

Consider the union of the exact product-unitary and site-permutation orbits of the states $\Psi_2(C)$. A valid signed stabilizer tableau in this union can be decoded to a binary code, up to coordinate permutation, in deterministic polynomial time. Membership of a valid raw tableau in the union is also decidable in deterministic polynomial time using a labeled stabilizer local-unitary equivalence algorithm. These assertions concern orbit equivalence of states, up to global phase.

For any state, decompose its projector into Pauli sectors indexed by physical support. The sector for $S$ takes a traceless local operator at every site in $S$ and an identity at every other site. Local conjugation preserves each sector, and a physical permutation permutes them. For a stabilizer tableau, its sector is nonzero exactly when the unsigned stabilizer label space contains a Pauli with that support. This is because the stabilizer projector is a signed sum with one term per unsigned label.

Consider a canonical $\Psi_2(C)$. The one-site supports are exactly the sites repeating zero coordinate functionals on $C$. On the remaining sites, the two-site supports form a disjoint union of cliques. Sites are in the same clique precisely when they repeat the same nonzero coordinate functional. To see this, an exact two-site support with a nonzero $X$-part must consist of the two copies of an original coordinate, already in the claimed clique. With zero $X$-part, $Z_iZ_j$ is a stabilizer exactly when the coordinate functionals at $i$ and $j$ agree. Consequently the original coordinate multiplicities are the clique sizes divided by two, and the original number of zero coordinates is the number of singleton supports divided by two.

Pick one representative per clique, forming a set $R$ of sites. For any stabilizer supported inside $R$, the $X$-part of its canonical Pauli label must be zero, since $R$ never contains both duplicates of an original coordinate. Thus in the canonical state there is at most one unsigned stabilizer label of each support $S\subseteq R$, and it is $Z_S$. Let $C_R$ denote the projection of $C$ to one original coordinate from each clique. These supports, read as bit vectors, are exactly $C_R^\perp$. This follows because membership of $Z_S$ in the stabilizer is equivalent to the sum of the corresponding coordinate functionals vanishing on $C$.

In a raw tableau promised locally unitarily equivalent after a permutation to a canonical state, obtain the singleton and pair supports directly from its unsigned label space. For a given pair, try the nine assignments of nonzero local Pauli axes, and test membership by binary elimination. Do the analogous three tests for each singleton. The support cliques and any representative set $R$ then agree with the canonical construction after reindexing. We now show how to compute $C_R^\perp$ without listing all subsets of $R$. In a nonzero sector on $S\subseteq R$, application of the local unitaries to the unique canonical Pauli word is a rank-one product of traceless local factors. If a factor at site $i$ had two nonzero Pauli coordinates, this product would give two nonzero Pauli coefficients with identical labels except at $i$. The raw stabilizer would then contain the product of those Paulis, giving a singleton support at $i$. That contradicts the choice of $R$. Therefore each occurring axis on $R$ is a single Pauli axis and is independent of the support, since the local rotation used at a site is fixed. A site occurring in no such support imposes no condition on the calculation.

Compute the subspace of unsigned labels whose sites outside $R$ are zero by binary elimination. Its local image at each site of $R$ is of rank at most one. Replace its sole possible nonzero local label by the bit one, and the zero label by zero. This is a linear map on the restricted label space. Its image is $C_R^\perp$ since the nonzero support sectors match those of the canonical state. Dualize by binary elimination and expand the repeated coordinates and zero count to produce a length-$n$ code. Everything is polynomial in the tableau size.

For membership recognition on an arbitrary valid signed tableau, require an even number of physical sites and singleton supports, an even size for every nonzero-site clique, a disjoint union of cliques in the pair-support graph, and local rank at most one in the restricted label space just used. Then construct the decoded candidate and use the clique-based correspondence to place its repeated coordinates on the actual sites. Finally use the labeled local-unitary test on the given tableau and the placed candidate. When the input belongs to the family, all choices in assigning sites inside a clique or among the zero sites give the same placed state. On an arbitrary input, the final check provides soundness directly. The labeled algorithm is applicable to pure stabilizer states, which can be converted to graph states by label-preserving local Clifford operations and binary elimination. [Zhang, Polynomial-Time Local-Unitary Equivalence of Graph States](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00527) · [MathProofs, StabilizerCodeLU](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/StabilizerCodeLU)

Decoding and encoding are therefore polynomial reductions both ways between binary permutation-code equivalence and unlabeled local-unitary equivalence on this recognizable pure-state family. The overlap formula and the threshold complexity result also transfer to raw signed tableaus in the family, as the optimum is invariant under independent local unitaries and relabelings on the two inputs.

## Scope and attribution

This is an informal proof. It combines the support-sector method used in MathProofs' stabilizer local-unitary work with classical permutation-code equivalence and a matching decomposition. Lockhart and González Guillén study hardness of subsystem permutation of quantum states. Zhang studies labeled local-unitary equivalence. Bichmann, Schmid and Wille study equivalence checking for stabilizer and CSS codes under permutation and local Clifford equivalence. The exact optimization identity here allows permutations and arbitrary local unitaries simultaneously and also covers nonnegative weighted amplitudes. A targeted search found these closely related results and did not establish publication priority for the identity. No closure of a published open question is claimed.
