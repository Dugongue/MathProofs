# Third-sign reductions for wide three-tensors over F₃

These are informal proofs for explicit three-tensors over F₃. Two-sign TI asks for one transporter with two designated determinants equal to one. SL-TI requires all three. We write signs additively as elements of F₂. The reductions below are deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions on their stated domains.

If a nonzero sign vector of an actual automorphism is known, quotient the sign profile by its span and keep the two remaining rows. If every transporter satisfies a computable nonzero linear character equation, that equation is either inconsistent with the all-positive signs or can be omitted. Finally, enforcing such a character by an ordinary tensor-system mark allows two complementary characters to be tested on the same transporter by the existing signed compiler. [Projection and signed compilation](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignProjections/SignProjections.md) · [Two retained signs](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorSignChain/RetainedSigns.md)

## 1. Exact determinant counts with small row frontiers

Present a square tensor as a pencil M(z) whose entries are linear in z∈F₃^k. Use an ordering of its rows. A column and a label variable are live from their first row of possible occurrence through their last, inclusive. For a column, occurrence means a structurally nonzero entry in the row. For a variable, it means a nonzero coefficient in at least one entry of the row. Suppose there are at most b live columns and w live variables at every row. There is a deterministic algorithm computing all three counts of values of det M(z), using 3^{w+2^b} times a polynomial in the dense input length. In particular fixed b and logarithmic w suffice. The displayed row order is adequate when it meets the bounds. More generally any computed frames with such an order suffice, since frame factors have known signs.

If a column is identically zero, all determinants are zero. Otherwise order the column basis by last occurrence, with any fixed tie-breaking, and record its permutation sign relative to the desired frames. Compute the wedge product of the rows in sequence. After processing a row, every expired column must already appear in the wedge term for that term to contribute to the final determinant. Project onto the terms containing every expired column and factor off their ordered wedge on the left. The surviving state is a vector in the exterior algebra on the live columns. Initially it is the scalar one. To process the next row, activate its new columns, wedge in the row, and project and factor off the columns expiring there. Because columns are ordered by expiration, each removed ordered wedge is a prefix of the column order then in use. This update keeps the exact signs of wedge products. At the end only the scalar coefficient of the full wedge remains. It is the determinant with the recorded column-frame sign.

For a fixed assignment of the live variables this state has at most 2^b coefficients in F₃. For each boundary assignment store the exact integer number of partial assignments inducing each entire state vector. Enumerate the new variables as they first occur, apply the deterministic exterior update, and sum integer counts when variables expire or states coincide. An induction on rows shows the distribution is exact. In particular the state has to be the whole exterior vector, since the distribution of a linear combination need not be obtained from marginal distributions of its coefficients. Counts have O(k) bits. Variables absent from the pencil multiply all final counts by three each. This proves the claimed runtime.

Let W(T)=#{z:det M_T(z)=1}−#{z:det M_T(z)=−1}. An actual transporter satisfies M_S(gz)=Q M_T(z)P⁻¹. Substitution in the finite sum gives
\[
 W(S)=(-1)^{s_X+s_Y}W(T).
\]
If magnitudes differ, reject. If both counts have a nonzero difference, their relative sign gives the product of the two matrix-mode signs on every transporter. Thus there is a single two-sign query. This sign relation is independent of whether the row frontiers or the frames themselves are invariant.

The same algorithm also handles any fixed odd number of determinant sign factors evaluated at fixed linear combinations of a fixed number of freely varying label vectors. For example, sum the product of signs of det M(x), det M(y), and det M(x+y), with zero determinants contributing zero. Under an actual transporter simultaneously change x and y by its label map. The product acquires the same single matrix-mode character because it has odd length. Compute the sum with a joint state of three exterior vectors and two sets of live variable values. Both blowups are constant, preserving polynomial time for fixed b and logarithmic w. A nonzero correlation therefore gives the sign even when the ordinary signed count vanishes.

There is also a polynomial algorithm when the displayed coefficient graph has bounded treewidth. Its vertices are matrix indices, treating the row and column indices as the same vertex set, and label variables. For each nonzero coefficient c_ij,a z_a make {i,j,a} a clique. Each term is a candidate directed arc i→j of weight c_ij,a z_a. Distinct terms in one entry are separate arcs. A determinant is (−1)^n times the weighted cycle-cover sum with a factor −1 per cycle. A bounded-width tree decomposition can be found in polynomial time and converted to a nice decomposition, with each arc introduced in a bag containing its endpoints and label. [Bodlaender, finding tree decompositions of small width](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539793251219)

For each fixed bag-label assignment, the partial cycle-cover state is a vector over F₃. It records weighted sums indexed by the in- and out-degrees of the bag vertices and the directed matching of open path endpoints among them. All forgotten matrix vertices are required to have both degrees one. Selecting an introduced arc updates these degrees, joins paths, and contributes a factor −1 if it closes a cycle. At a join node, the arc sets from the children are disjoint. Add their boundary degrees, discarding values above one, and compose their endpoint matchings, counting the newly closed cycles. This gives a bilinear merge of the two partial-sum vectors. Already closed cycles need only their existing factors in the sums. The length of a vector depends only on the width. As with the row algorithm, keep the integer distribution of these entire vectors over assignments to internal labels. At a join the two sets of internal labels are disjoint given the bag-label assignment, so multiply the relevant counts and add them for coincident merged vectors. The empty root gives the exact determinant histogram. For the fixed odd correlations use a joint tuple of partial-sum vectors, with assignments to the required fixed number of copies of each live label. This increases the bound by a constant depending on the width and correlation. These width promises are on the displayed coefficients or on a computed presentation.

**An irreducible all-wide family.** Take even n≥4. Use a tridiagonal n by n matrix with independent diagonal variables d_i. Each off-diagonal pair is one independent symmetric variable z_i, except the pair (2,3), whose two entries have independent variables. There are 2n labels. Each column is live for at most three rows and the numbers of live columns and labels are constant. The principal continuants obey
\[
 F_i=d_iF_{i-1}-p_{i-1}F_{i-2},\qquad F_0=1,
\]
where p_j=z_j² except for the split edge, at which p_2 is a product of its two independent entries. Consecutive continuants are relatively prime by induction, since the edge variables at each stage are new. As a polynomial in d_i, F_i is primitive of degree one and hence irreducible. In particular F_n has irreducible degree n.

Write W_i for the signed determinant count of the leading i by i pencil. For even i, summing over d_i gives zero if F_{i-1}≠0. If F_{i-1}=0, summing over d_i and z_{i-1} gives −6 times the integer sign of F_{i-2}, with the sign of zero taken as zero. This last edge is symmetric because i−1 is odd. If F_{i-2}≠0, for each choice of the intervening edge variables there is just one d_{i-1} making F_{i-1}=0. Hence W_2=−6 and W_i=−6c_i W_{i-2} for even i≥4, where c_4=9 and c_i=3 for i≥6. Thus W_n is nonzero.

The strict pair adjoints are scalar. The identity slice equates the two actions, the diagonal slices force a diagonal action, and the off-diagonal slices force its entries to agree. The right and left idealizers consist of diagonal scalars on the consecutive sets {1,2} and {3,…,n}. For the right idealizer this follows by testing the single diagonal slices, which eliminate every possible off-diagonal entry except at the split edge. The adjacent symmetric edges eliminate those two exceptions, by generating a forbidden entry outside the tridiagonal band. Each remaining symmetric edge equates its two diagonal scalars. Transposing gives the left version. For a set of l indices on either side of the split edge, the idealizer scalar occurs on l matrix coordinates and on 2l label coordinates, counting the one directed split entry on its side. Both are even. Therefore the corresponding pair-unit signs are positive, and the same holds for the strict pair. All three mode sizes and all three pair-adjoint commutant dimensions grow. The determinant count thus supplies a sign on a family with growing irreducible determinant degree and trivial signs from the fixed-mode adjoint units. The row-frontier proof applies to the displayed presentation of this example.

A second family makes all three pair adjoints scalar. Instead use the completely symmetric tridiagonal path and add the independent directed corner variable w at entry (1,n). The determinant is F_path±w∏_i z_i by expansion in w. The coefficient and constant are relatively prime since F_path contains the pure diagonal monomial. This makes the determinant absolutely irreducible. Summing its integer sign over w kills every assignment with all z_i nonzero and gives three times the path sign on the remaining assignments. For n=2m the unrestricted path signed count is −6(−18)^(m−1) by the same continuant calculation. The count restricted to all z_i nonzero is 2^(2m−1)(−3)^m, since their squares are then one, and each pair of new rows contributes −3 to the signed sum over the diagonal variables. The two counts differ for m≥2, so the corner-pencil signed count is nonzero. Its row frontier increases by at most one persistent column. Testing individual diagonals forces possible idealizer off-diagonals to lie at the corner, and one adjacent symmetric path edge kills that last entry. The symmetric path then ties all diagonal coefficients. Thus both idealizers are scalar, and so are the strict pair adjoints as before. With all mode dimensions even, their entire unit sign images vanish.

## 2. Many local determinant forms in a normalized slice algebra

Let M from Z to Mat_n(F₃) have nonzero determinant polynomial. Over an algebraic closure choose any invertible slice M(v). Let B be the unital algebra generated by the slices M(v)⁻¹M(z). This algebra does not depend on the invertible anchor. Indeed changing anchors multiplies the normalized slices by a unit of the generated algebra, and the other inclusion follows by reversing the change. It follows that B is invariant under Frobenius and defined over F₃. It is conjugated by the X map of any transporter. In normalized coordinates the Y map supplies the change of invertible anchor.

Write B/rad B=∏_j Mat_(d_j)(E_j) in its descended form, where E_j extends F₃. Let m_j be the multiplicity of the corresponding natural simple in the radical filtration of X. The projected slice map in factor j is linear over E_j after extending Z to E_j. Let r_j be its rank, computed over an extension if necessary. Rank is unchanged by changing the anchor. Consider the following fixed bounds. For every j, either d_j≤D or r_j≤R, with D,R fixed constants. Among the factors with odd m_j, assume every equal-tag group has O(log L) members, for tag (d_j,[E_j:F₃],r_j,m_j). The field degrees, total number of factors and total degrees of the determinant contributions may all grow.

**Theorem.** On this domain, an ordinary tensor system enforces the product of the two matrix-mode signs on the actual transporter. It retains the other two independent signs in one deterministic polynomial-size two-sign instance.

### Finding an anchor and descending the data

Over an algebraic closure the determinant of M(z), up to a scalar, is a product of determinants of the geometric simple quotient factors, repeated by their module multiplicities. This is the radical filtration determinant identity, applied as a polynomial identity. Each factor is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial. It has either bounded degree or depends on a linear quotient of dimension at most R.

There is a deterministic polynomial-size hitting set for such a product. Choose integer weights w_i encoding (1,i,…,i^D) in a radix greater than D times the maximum i^D. Equality of encoded sums for monomials of degree at most D gives equality of their degree and power sums, hence of the multisets of their indices by the Newton identities over the rationals. Thus substituting v(t)_i=t^{w_i} loses none of the nonzero polynomials of degree at most D. The entries of v(t) also span Z as t varies over an infinite field. Substitute
\[
 z=\sum_{a=1}^{\max(1,R)}s_a v(t_a).
\]
A bounded-degree factor remains nonzero by specializing to the single-curve substitution. A factor through a quotient of dimension at most R remains nonzero because the curve spans that quotient. Some R curve points give spanning projected directions, and varying their s_a covers the quotient. The whole product is therefore a nonzero polynomial in a fixed number of parameters, of polynomial degree. Scan a product grid with cardinality per coordinate exceeding that degree. The grid lies in a deterministic finite extension of polynomial bit length, for instance a small cubic Artin–Schreier tower. Obtain each cubic by solving for an element of nonzero absolute trace and using X³−X−a, which has no root and is irreducible. A nonzero value supplies a regular anchor. If no grid point works, the input fails the structural promise.

At a found anchor over K compute B_K by linear-span closure of the normalized basis slices. A Frobenius-stable row span has a Frobenius-fixed reduced echelon basis, since that basis is unique. Thus the same computation descends B to an explicit F₃-basis. Its radical quotient and m_j are computed by explicit finite-field algebra algorithms. The rank r_j for the projected map is measured on a simple component after extension. Components over a fixed E_j are conjugate under Frobenius, so those ranks agree. We can test the bounded-size or bounded-rank conditions and the tag multiplicities on the recovered data. [Ivanyos–Qiao, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495#Thmtheorem18)

The kernel of the projected slice map is Frobenius-stable, since changing anchors acts by invertible left multiplication. It descends to an E_j-linear subspace and gives a quotient P_j of E_j⊗Z of dimension r_j. Choose coordinates on that quotient when using the bounded-rank hypothesis. Write q_j for the determinant of its projected normalized slice, computed in these coordinates or in the original label coordinates as appropriate. Frobenius on K changes every coefficient of q_j by multiplication with the same unit of E_j⊗K, because it only changes the regular anchor. Frobenius permutes the field components of E_j⊗K transitively. Hence a nonzero coefficient is nonzero in every component and is a unit. Divide by the first nonzero coefficient α_j. The normalized form ψ_j is then Frobenius-fixed and has coefficients in E_j.

These forms are computable with polynomial resources. For d_j bounded use the full label space and dense polynomial arithmetic at that degree. For r_j bounded find the kernel first and compute the degree-d_j determinant polynomial on the fixed-dimensional quotient. Both give polynomially many coefficients. There is a unique nonzero constant c_T∈F₃ such that
\[
 \det M_T(z)=c_T\prod_j \operatorname{Norm}_{E_j/\mathbb F_3}(\psi_j(z))^{m_j}.
\]
This follows from the filtration determinant identity. The apparent constant over K belongs to F₃ because both sides have nonzero coefficients defined over F₃. Evaluate its ratio at the found regular anchor to compute it, extending the norms as polynomial norms on coefficients.

### Reading the local signs on the actual label map

On a transporter, the conjugation of B induces field identifications φ_j with the corresponding target factors. Its label map g induces semilinear maps on the quotients P_j. Under these identifications the normalized polynomials satisfy ψ'_(πj)(gz)=h_j φ_j(ψ_j(z)) for units h_j in the target fields. To prove this, compare normalized matrix slices under the actual conjugation, with the anchor change multiplying by an invertible quotient matrix. Its determinant and the coefficient normalizations give the multiplier h_j. The determinant identity now shows
\[
 s_X+s_Y=\operatorname{sgn}(c_S/c_T)+\sum_{j\mid m_j\ {\rm odd}}\operatorname{sgn}\operatorname{Norm}(h_j)
\]
in raw tensor frames.

Include the original tensor, B acting faithfully on X, its radical quotient and its center factors in the ordinary tensor system. Their maps are forced by the original transporter. Include the quotients P_j and the normalized forms. A degree-d form on P can be included as a linear map from the divided power space Γ^d(P), dual to Sym^d(P*). Use multiplication one degree at a time on the symmetric powers and dual pairings to enforce the induced maps. This represents formal polynomial coefficients in characteristic three. The degree-by-degree roles have polynomial size when either degree or number of coordinates is fixed. For fixed d one may use the whole label space instead of P_j. Include a field-line on which the normalized-form map evaluates, so its multiplier is forced to be h_j on a genuine transporter.

For each odd factor, read the norm sign as a flip of a two-element coordinate algebra. Attach two auxiliary field lines, with square-product maps into the form's line having coefficients 1 and a nonsquare η. Field identifications preserve both square classes. The two possible port assignments extend exactly when the port flip equals the quadratic character of h_j. For either allowed port assignment, the necessary scalar multipliers exist by the defining square-class condition on both maps. Fixed-characteristic factoring finds nonsquares deterministically, for example by repeatedly taking a square root of −1 until no root exists. Each successful step doubles the order.

Within an equal-tag group, factor permutations can be arbitrary. Form a coordinate algebra indexed by all bit assignments to that group. Bilinear evaluation on an assignment idempotent and a factor idempotent gives the chosen port idempotent. This forces an assignment to move by the actual factor permutation and the actual port flips. Map each assignment to its total parity bit. The induced flip on the single parity role is therefore exactly the sum of local flips. The group has logarithmic size, so its assignment algebra has polynomial dimension. The different tag groups have a fixed intrinsic order, and a chain of constant-size XOR relations enforces the required total sign from the preceding formula. Roles over permutable factors can be grouped into direct sums with the center actions and degree indices retaining the identifications. Each map uses tensor order at most three and polynomial total size. For an actual transporter all auxiliary maps extend precisely with the stated sign relation, so the other two signs can be retained on that same transporter.

## 3. Coherent local frames without a support-isomorphism search

Suppose an isomorphism-equivariant polynomial procedure recovers direct-sum blocks in all three modes. Let their maximum dimension be O(√log L). For each nonzero block tensor, choose as its color the lexicographically smallest array in its orbit under the product of the three local general linear groups, including the dimensions in the color. These groups and all local orbits have polynomial size. The color is independent of the initial local bases.

Assume each input can be put into these canonical arrays at all nonzero block positions simultaneously, by choosing one common frame per block. This condition is checked constructively in polynomial time. Keep the support fixed and use the direct product of local linear groups. Each local comparison to its canonical array imposes a coset constraint on three factors. Maintain one feasible coset gH with generators of H. For a new local constraint enumerate the projection of H to the three factors, carrying a lift for each projected element. Either no projected element extends or select one and restrict H to the inverse image of the allowed subgroup. Its index is at most the size of the projected group. Compute generators by the polynomial-index Schreier procedure, and prune via permutation-group membership in the faithful action on the disjoint union of local vector spaces. This yields actual coherent frames and also computes the sign subgroup H₀ of support-pointwise tensor automorphisms. [Furst–Hopcroft–Luks, permutation group algorithms](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/f360d936-ad6c-47ab-8ceb-105c662e23d0)

Every color-preserving support isomorphism now lifts by identity maps between corresponding local frames. Any other lift differs from that one by a support-pointwise automorphism. If H₀≠0, its signs give a safe projection. If H₀=0 and one mode consists entirely of even-dimensional blocks, every transporter has a computable constant sign in that mode, since its layout permutation is even. This gives one two-sign query. If all three modes have only even-dimensional blocks, compute the raw endpoint frame offset. The exact SL verdict is that the offset belongs to H₀ and that the colored supports are isomorphic. This gives one ordinary TI instance, via the standard graph-to-tensor reduction, without needing to compute any support isomorphisms. [Graph and tensor isomorphism](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full)

In the all-even case, it is also enough that coherence holds outside an intrinsic exceptional set of O(log L/log log L) support triples. Normalize the other positions by the same coset algorithm. Enumerate only bijections between the exceptional sets, whose factorial count is polynomial. Each consistent bijection prescribes pins on the endpoints of its triples. A color-preserving support isomorphism extending those pins must carry the source array, using identity local maps, to one fully determined array on the target support. The nonexceptional positions have their canonical colors, and the exceptional positions receive the specified source arrays. The sign of any layout permutation is zero. Compute whether the required calibrated sign occurs in a pointwise comparison of that determined array with the target. Keep the pinned support-isomorphism branch exactly when it does. The original verdict is the OR of these polynomially many ordinary comparisons. Ordinary graph isomorphism supports polynomial OR packing. For two equivalence comparisons a≃b and c≃d, compare the multisets of ordered pairs {(a,c),(b,d)} and {(a,d),(b,c)} using tagged graph disjoint-union gadgets. One matching witnesses c≃d, and the other witnesses a≃b. Balanced recursion has polynomial output size.

**A raw family with unrestricted support isomorphism.** For a simple graph of minimum degree at least two, use its bipartite incidence graph with color classes vertex and edge. For each incidence (a,b), introduce a unique third-mode block c_ab. Put one common eight-dimensional tensor at each triple (a,b,c_ab). The local tensor uses indices modulo eight with coefficient +1 at (i,j,i+j), except coefficient −1 at (0,0,0). It is concise and has only scalar infinitesimal stabilizer triples. For the latter claim, the diagonal equations x_i+y_j+z_(i+j)=0 force constant entries because eight is prime to three. At displacement d≠0 an off-diagonal equation has the form X_i+Y_j+Z_(i+j)=0, except at (−d,0),(0,−d),(0,0), where one different coefficient is negated at each position. Two consecutive clean rows imply all cyclic differences of Z are equal and thus zero. Clean positions give constant X and Y. The three exceptional equations then force all three constants to vanish.

The neighborhoods of distinct vertices on either side of the incidence graph are incomparable. For vertex side, their intersection has size at most one, and each has size at least two. For edge side, the neighborhoods are distinct two-element sets. In the raw tensor Lie equations, an off-diagonal block map a→a' in the first mode has a neighbor b of a that is not a neighbor of a'. The corresponding tensor entry at (a',b,c_ab) cannot cancel with a term from another mode, since c_ab is unique to (a,b) and (a',b) is absent. Local conciseness kills that off-diagonal map. The same proof works in the second mode, then uniqueness kills the third-mode off-diagonal maps. The local scalar Lie calculation now shows that diagonal block scalars have the form x_a,y_b,−x_a−y_b, with the first two sets of scalars independent. Their joint eigenspaces recover precisely the three block decompositions after arbitrary raw rebasing. Each block is even-dimensional and all arrays are coherent. For arbitrary input graph, first join it with a triangle. This forces the degree condition and preserves graph isomorphism in both directions, since it adds exactly three isolated vertices to the complement. Thus this family contains ordinary GI while its special-linear comparison reduces to ordinary support GI.

The unrestricted three-to-two reduction remains open. These proofs replace a global transporter search by exact counts, bounded-degree or bounded-coordinate local forms, or a common local-frame normalization. The separate structural conditions specify when the replacement is polynomial.
