# A profinitely invisible halting obstruction for matrix multiplication tensors

**Theorem.** Let $V=M_2(\mathbb Z)$. There is a fixed finite symmetric set of determinant-one integral basis changes of $V$ with the following property. Given a fully specified integral multiplication tensor on $V$, deciding whether a word in those changes carries the standard matrix multiplication tensor to the input tensor is computably enumerable complete under many-one reductions. This holds on a computable family of unital associative multiplication tables, all explicitly isomorphic over the integers to the standard one, with a determinant-one isomorphism supplied. For every tensor in this family, authorized words give the required transport modulo every positive integer. A single element in the profinite closure of the authorized group realizes all the modular transports compatibly. This statement is unconditional.

### Choose a single group

Take a many-one complete computably enumerable set $K$ of positive integers by shifting the ordinary halting set. Form the group $A$ with generators $x_i$ for integer $i$ and commutation relators for pairs at distances in $K$. The shift $x_i\mapsto x_{i+1}$ is an automorphism. Its semidirect product with the infinite cyclic group is generated by $a=x_0$ and the shift $t$. A recursively enumerable presentation has relators $[a,t^n a t^{-n}]$ for $n\in K$. To verify this presentation, send $x_i$ to $t^i a t^{-i}$ and use the translates of the displayed relators. Conversely the semidirect product satisfies all the relators. For a positive $n\notin K$, deleting all generators except $x_0,x_n$ gives a homomorphism from $A$ onto the free group on those two letters. Hence their commutator is nontrivial. Thus the sequence of commutators reduces halting to the word problem in this two-generator group.

Higman's embedding theorem embeds the group in a finitely presented group. Bridson's Theorem B embeds that group in a finitely presented group $Q$ with no nontrivial finite quotients. Alternatively one can take Bridson's single finitely presented universal group with this property. Fix a finite presentation $Q=F/R$ with $F$ free of finite rank and $R$ the normal closure of its finitely many relators. Choose once and for all words in the presentation generators giving the images of $a,t$. Substitution of these two fixed finite strings computes a word $w_n$ in $F$ with $w_n\in R$ precisely when $n\in K$. This is a computable many-one reduction for a fixed group. No algorithm selecting embeddings for variable input presentations is needed. [Higman's theorem](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1961.0132) · [Bridson's controlled embedding, Theorem B](https://msp.org/gtm/1998/01/gtm-1998-01-004p.pdf) · [Bridson's universal group](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08072)

Embed $F$ faithfully and effectively in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$, identifying $F$ with its image. For example, use the free pair of opposite elementary unipotent matrices with nonzero entries 2 off the diagonal. Their freeness follows by ping-pong on the coordinate cones $|x|>|y|$ and $|y|>|x|$. For a free group of rank $k$ use $b^j a b^{-j}$ for $1\le j\le k$ inside that free pair. Reduced words in consecutive distinct such conjugates remain nontrivial by their free normal form. [Sanov's free subgroup and its matrix generators](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2017.01.036)

### Transport the entire multiplication table

Let $H\subseteq F\times F$ consist of all pairs $(u,v)$ with the same image in $Q$. It is generated by the diagonal pairs of free generators and the pairs $(1,r)$ for the finite list of defining relators. Indeed conjugating a relator pair by diagonal words generates $1\times R$, and every pair in $H$ factors into a diagonal pair and one from $1\times R$. This is the classical fibre product construction of Mikhailova. Related orbit-undecidability results for integral linear groups are due to Bogopolski, Martino and Ventura. [Orbit decidability and the conjugacy problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.3104)

Represent $(u,v)$ on $V$ by $L_{u,v}(X)=uXv^{-1}$, which is integral of determinant one since its determinant is $(\det u)^2(\det v)^{-2}$. The chosen generators of $H$ and their inverses supply one fixed symmetric list of allowed moves. Define the order-three tensor $T_s(X,Y)=XsY$ for $s\in F$. Its parameter can be recovered as $T_s(I,I)$, and it has unit $s^{-1}$. Acting on both inputs and the output by a simultaneous basis change gives
$$
 L_{u,v}\bigl(T_s(L_{u,v}^{-1}X,L_{u,v}^{-1}Y)\bigr)=X(vsu^{-1})Y.
$$
In particular the orbit of $T_I$ consists exactly of $T_s$ for $s\in R$. The coefficient arrays for $T_{w_n}$ follow by ordinary integer arithmetic from $n$. Each has the explicit unrestricted transporter $L_{1,w_n}$ and the same matrix multiplication algebra, including its unit. Every mode has flattening rank four because multiplying by the unit realizes the identity on either input and spans the output. Enumerating finite words in the authorized generators semidecides orbit membership on arbitrary given tables. The reduction proves completeness and rules out a computable bound on the shortest authorized transport as a function of input size on reachable targets.

### Why all congruence tests pass

For any homomorphism $\phi$ from $F$ to a finite group, $\phi(F)/\phi(R)$ is a finite quotient of $Q$, hence is trivial. Thus $\phi(R)=\phi(F)$. In particular for every modulus $m$ and every $s\in F$ some $r_m\in R$ has the same matrix entries as $s$ modulo $m$. The word for $L_{1,r_m}$ in the authorized moves carries $T_I$ to $T_s$ modulo $m$.

More strongly, the very same explicit integral transporter $L_{1,s}$ belongs to the closure of the authorized group inside $\mathrm{SL}_4(\widehat{\mathbb Z})$. Its reductions modulo all $m$ are compatible and belong to the corresponding finite images. A negative instance admits such a compatible profinite transporter even though no group word effects the exact integral transport.

### A consequence for every Diophantine encoding of the orbit

The reachable integral coefficient arrays form a computably enumerable subset of $\mathbb Z^{64}$. Integer DPRM gives an existential polynomial presentation $P(c,z)=0$ of this set. One may pass between signed and natural integer inputs by signed coding and Lagrange's four squares. Fix *any* such presentation with integer coefficients. For every hard target $c=T_s$ and every $m$, choose a reachable integral table congruent to $c$ modulo $m$. Its integer witnesses give a solution to $P(c,z)=0\pmod m$. The corresponding closed subsets of $\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\dim z}$ have the finite intersection property by passing to a common multiple of finitely many moduli. Compactness gives a simultaneous profinite solution. Consequently no choice of existential polynomial definition of this orbit makes the collection of all finite congruence tests detect its negative instances. [Jones on universal Diophantine equations](https://doi.org/10.2307/2273588)

The result concerns reachability under a fixed finitely generated subgroup of the integral basis changes. Unrestricted tensor isomorphism on the constructed pairs has an explicit affirmative answer.
