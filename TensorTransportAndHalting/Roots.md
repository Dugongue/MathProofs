# A fixed multiplication tensor with an undecidable square image

**Integral theorem.** There exist a fixed unital commutative associative algebra A, free of finite rank over the integers, and fixed basis vectors a₀,a₁ such that deciding whether a(n) = a₀ + 2n a₁ is a square in A, given n ≥ 0, is computably many-one equivalent to the halting problem. The augmentation ideal J satisfies J³ = 0 and every a(n) annihilates J. The element a(n) has a square root over the reals and over the p-adic integers for every prime p, for every n.

Equivalently, there is a fixed family of commuting nilpotent integer matrices and a single affine line of primitive rank-one, square-zero targets. All targets are integrally conjugate and have explicit integral matrix square roots. Deciding whether any square root commutes with the fixed family is halting-complete. This concerns a membership test for the diagonal evaluation of one fixed order-three multiplication tensor.

**Integral arithmetic.** By the Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich theorem there is a fixed integer polynomial F(t,x) such that machine t halts precisely when F(t,x) = 0 has an integral solution. Replace natural-number witnesses by sums of four integer squares if the chosen presentation is over the natural numbers. Set

    G(t,x,z) = F(t,x)(z²−13)(z²−17)(z²−221).

The three extra factors have no rational root. Each local ring of p-adic integers admits a root of at least one factor. For odd p outside 13 and 17, the Legendre symbols of 13, 17, and 221 have the form u,v,uv, so one is +1 and the root lifts by Hensel's lemma. At 13, the residue of 17 is 4. At 17, the residue of 13 is 8². In the 2-adic integers, 17 is a square since it is 1 modulo 8. A real root exists as well. In any of these local rings, setting x=0 and choosing such a z solves G at every t. [Jones, Universal Diophantine Equation](https://doi.org/10.2307/2273588)

**The single fixed algebra.** Use addition, multiplication and integer constants in a fixed arithmetic circuit for G. Give each intermediate gate its own variable and include the gate equations and the zero-output equation. All equations have degree at most two. Call them E₁,…,Eₕ, leaving t as an independent coordinate. Add one coordinate w and homogenize each equation to degree two as qᵢ(w,v) = w² Eᵢ(v/w), interpreted as a polynomial. Here v includes t, x, z and the gates. Let V be the free lattice with coordinates (w,v), and W the free lattice with h+2 coordinates. Define a symmetric integer bilinear map μ from V × V to W by prescribing its diagonal as

    μ(b,b) = (w², 2wt, 2q₁(b), …, 2qₕ(b)).

For w² use the ordinary product of the w coordinates. For each doubled mixed monomial 2c bᵢbⱼ use the bilinear expression c(bᵢb′ⱼ+bⱼb′ᵢ). Doubled pure squares give integral diagonal coefficients as well.

Take A = ℤ·1 ⊕ V ⊕ W, with 1 a unit, V·V given by μ, and W·(V⊕W) = 0. This multiplication is associative since each triple product from V⊕W vanishes, and bilinearity extends the assertion to A. The fixed integral structure constants form the promised multiplication tensor. Set J = V⊕W and let a₀,a₁ be the first two basis vectors of W. For a domain R among ℤ, ℚ, ℝ, or the p-adic integers, write A_R for this algebra over R. An element squaring to a(n) has scalar part zero since its scalar square is zero. Its W component then disappears on squaring. The remaining equations are precisely

    w² = 1,       2wt = 2n,       2qᵢ(w,v) = 0 for every i.

Each ring in question is a characteristic-zero domain, so 2 cancels even at the prime 2. The unit w equals +1 or −1 and the dehomogenized coordinates v/w give an actual solution of G with input t/w = n. Conversely take w=1 and evaluate all gates on any solution of G. This proves the local assertions, and over ℤ proves equivalence with halting. Integer witnesses are enumerable, giving the reverse computable reduction.

**The fixed matrix constraints.** In the displayed basis of A, let L_b be multiplication by b on the underlying free module. Fix all L_b for basis vectors b of J. They commute, are nilpotent of index at most three, and their joint centralizer is exactly the regular copy of A. Indeed, if X commutes with these matrices it commutes with L_y for every y by linearity and the identity operator. Hence X(y) = X L_y(1) = L_y X(1), which says X is multiplication by X(1). The representation is faithful by evaluation at 1. This proof works over every base ring just used. Thus X² = L_a with X in this centralizer is equivalent to an algebra square root of a.

In contrast, each L_a has a primitive image generated by a=a(n) and annihilates J. In the integral basis beginning with 1, a, the vector a₁ and the remaining basis vectors of J, it sends 1 to a and kills every other vector. This is a basis because the change a₀ ↦ a₀+2n a₁ is unimodular. Thus all L_a are integrally conjugate. A rational or integral matrix square root with no commuting conditions is explicit. Send 1 to a₁, send a₁ to a, and kill all other basis vectors, including a. Squaring gives L_a, as required.

**Rational theorem.** If ℤ has an existential Diophantine definition in ℚ, the same construction with a possibly different fixed multiplication table gives the same halting classification for rational square roots. Begin with the fixed integer DPRM presentation and constrain each integer witness by its existential rational definition. Combine the resulting equations by a sum of squares, clearing fixed denominators. This yields a fixed F whose rational roots at integer input n recognize halting. Apply the same fallback product, gate equations and algebra. The output again has integral structure constants, and local fallback witnesses can even be taken in the p-adic integers. Rational witnesses are enumerable. This fixed-format rational conclusion is conditional on the stated arithmetic premise. [Glazer manuscript and formalization status](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q)

**Boundary.** Over ℚ the algebra is local with residue ℚ. For a unit c+j, c ≠ 0 and j ∈ J, rational square-root existence is decidable by testing whether c is a rational square. If c=d², one root is d(1 + j/(2c) − j²/(8c²)). If instead J²=0, every radical element has square zero, so the square-image test for radical targets is trivial. The hard family occupies the next nilpotence length and consists entirely of targets killed by J. The compatible-root question asks for a potentially singular endomorphism; its undecidability gives no undecidability conclusion for ordinary isomorphism of two tensors. This is an informal proof and priority for the combined restrictions has not been established. [Lenstra and Silverberg on rational commutative algebras](https://escholarship.org/content/qt72w671h6/qt72w671h6_noSplash_c4bd5aa943e97a832a76fa28999ecce1.pdf)

**Conjugacy corollary.** Over the same single fixed algebra, even deciding upper triangularizability for a special-linear matrix of size **two** is halting-complete on an explicit affine line. Take

    M(a) = [[2+a, −1], [1, 0]].

It has determinant one. Suppose it is conjugate over A to an upper triangular matrix. Either diagonal entry u then solves u²−(2+a)u+1=0, by invariance of the characteristic polynomial over a commutative ring. Thus u is a unit and u+u⁻¹=2+a. Modulo J the scalar residue c satisfies (c−1)²=0 in the base domain, so u=1+x for x ∈ J. Since J³=0, the inverse is 1−x+x². It follows that x²=a. Conversely any square root x of a lies in J and gives u=1+x satisfying the same quadratic. The column (u,1) is an eigenvector and extends to the determinant-one matrix with second column (−1,0). This explicitly conjugates M(a) into the upper triangular subgroup. The conclusion for conjugators in SL₂(A) is identical to that for GL₂(A). All these calculations remain valid after the stated local base changes.

On forgetting the A-module structure, **all** these M(a(n)) are integrally conjugate to the same unipotent integer matrix. Indeed the unimodular change of basis conjugating L_a to L_{a₀} in each of the two copies of A transports the displayed block matrix as well. The quadratic relation gives (M−I)²=aM and (M−I)⁴=0. Any nilpotent endomorphism of a nonzero finite free integer lattice admits a primitive kernel vector (divide a nonzero kernel vector by its content), which extends to an integral basis. The induced map on the free quotient is nilpotent. Induction triangularizes the map over the integers. Thus triangularization in an ordinary integral basis is always possible, whereas triangularization in a rank-two A-basis recognizes halting. For the conditional rational version, use the rational algebra constructed above.
