# Complete invariants for subgroup equality with binary matrix generators

**Statement.** Subgroup equality in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ and $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)$, given lists of generating matrices with signed binary integer entries, admits polynomial-time complete invariants. More generally the construction applies to power-word generating lists in a fixed finitely generated virtually free group. A complete invariant is a polynomial-length string equal exactly on equal subgroups. This resolves the particular modular-group candidate discussed by Grochow in his thesis. The more general search for other examples remains separate. [Grochow, chapter 5](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~jgrochow/grochow-thesis.pdf)

**First recover one invariant in a free group.** Fix a free basis and a total order of its signed letters. From a list of power words construct Lohrey's polynomial-size strongly folded power-compressed subgroup graph. Its decompression is folded. Remove any nonbase leaves until none remain. This operation works on the compact graph since new vertices internal to long edges have degree two: an entire hanging path may be removed at once. This leaves the rooted labeled Stallings core, which is uniquely determined by the subgroup. For instance, its based reduced loops read precisely the subgroup; every core edge lies on a reduced based loop, and the deterministic inverse transitions give the unique pointed isomorphism between cores for the same subgroup. [Lohrey, §§3–5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00224-023-10122-2)

The particular compressed presentation need not be unique. Keep the base vertex and the vertices of degree different from two, and replace every intervening path by one directed string label and its inverse. A cycle through the base retains its base; the trivial subgroup retains one vertex. Undirected degree counts loops twice. At any retained vertex, outgoing directed paths have distinct first letters by folding. Number the retained vertices by breadth-first traversal from the base, exploring edges in that letter order. Record for each directed edge its source, target, and full label in a canonical compressed encoding.

To obtain the encoding, represent the path label by a straight-line program, concatenating the compact labels and using repeated squaring for powers. All these descriptions have polynomial size. Encode its output string by the greedy nonoverlapping Lempel–Ziv parse with leftmost source positions and a fixed convention for new letters. This parse depends only on the string and is computable in polynomial time from a straight-line program. Thus two different presentations of the same rooted labeled core give identical output bytes. The output recovers the entire ordinary core upon decompression, proving the converse. This avoids printing a potentially exponentially long path. [Lohrey, compressed strings survey, §3](https://www.uni-siegen.de/fb12/ti/veroeffentlichungen/12-survey.pdf)

**Retain the finite-quotient information.** Fix a normal finite-index free subgroup $F$ of a virtually free ambient group and fixed representatives $t_q$ of its finite quotient. For the input subgroup $H$, compute its finite quotient image. Schreier rewriting gives power-word generators for $K=H\cap F$ and, for each image element $q$, a word $u_q\in F$ with $u_qt_q\in H$. In the power-word presentation these are polynomial-time operations by Lohrey's finite-extension argument. The elements of $H$ in that quotient fiber are exactly $K u_q t_q$.

Adjoin a fresh free generator $a$ to $F$. For each $q$ encode the free subgroup

$$J_q=\langle K,u_q a\rangle\ \le F*\langle a\rangle.$$

Changing $u_q$ within its left coset leaves $J_q$ unchanged. Conversely $v a\in J_q$ exactly when $v u_q^{-1}\in K$. Indeed the inverse of the automorphism fixing $F$ and sending $a$ to $u_q a$ sends $v a$ to $v u_q^{-1}a$ and $J_q$ to $K*\langle a\rangle$; use free-product normal form. Output the list of represented quotient elements in fixed order and the canonical compressed subgroup codes for $K$ and the $J_q$. It determines every coset of $H$, proving completeness. The finite index and the ambient rewriting data are fixed; the input list defining $H$ is unrestricted.

Lohrey converts binary $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ matrices to polynomial-size power words in a fixed virtually free group. For $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)$, lift each projective generator to $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ and adjoin $-I$. The resulting subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ is the full preimage, independent of the lift signs. Equal preimages are equivalent to equal projective subgroups. [Lohrey, §5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00224-023-10122-2)

**Two historical answers.** The explicit truth-table Boolean-function congruence candidate from Fortnow–Grochow also has polynomial-time canonical forms. Given an $n$-input function, use $2n$ vertices for the two literals of each input, put in each opposite pair as an edge, and include one $n$-edge selecting one literal at each coordinate for every satisfying assignment. For $n\ge3$ these two edge types have different sizes. Hypergraph isomorphisms are exactly signed coordinate permutations preserving the truth set. Obtain a canonical hypergraph, order its pair edges by least endpoint and orient each by its smaller endpoint to read a canonical truth table. Small arities admit exhaustive enumeration. Simply exponential canonization in the number of vertices is polynomial in the input table length. This is published-algorithm fallout. Similarly the question asking for natural NP-complete equivalence relations had already been identified as answered by group word problems in Grochow's thesis. Neither is a new problem closure claimed here. [Fortnow–Grochow](https://arxiv.org/html/0907.4775v2) · [Schweitzer–Wiebking](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07466) · [Grochow's retrospective](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~jgrochow/grochow-thesis.pdf)
