# Two adjacent canonization comparisons at every oracle level

Fix any decision oracle $A$. Let Lex, CF, Ker and Eq denote, respectively, equivalence relations with a deterministic polynomial-time-with-$A$ shortlex-first representative, a canonical representative, a complete invariant, and a decision procedure. All are classes of relations on finite strings. Class members may have different lengths. The inclusions are Lex ⊆ CF ⊆ Ker ⊆ Eq.

**Theorem.** Each of Lex = CF, CF = Ker, and Lex = CF = Ker = Eq is individually equivalent to $\mathsf P^A=\mathsf{NP}^A$. For $A$ complete for $\Sigma_i^P$ this is equivalent to $\mathrm{PH}=\Delta_{i+1}^P$, with the ordinary P versus NP comparison at level zero. This answers two of the adjacent comparisons asked in §6.2. Whether Ker = Eq alone implies collapse remains a separate question. [Fortnow–Grochow, §6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/0907.4775v2#S6.SS2)

**Common upper direction.** Given a relation in Eq and an input $x$, there is a member of its class of length at most $|x|$, namely $x$. Existence of an equivalent word of a prescribed such length with a prescribed prefix is an $\mathsf{NP}^A$ predicate. Under the collapse, deterministic polynomial-time oracle computation finds the least possible length and then fills the bits in order. This gives Lex = Eq.

**Lex = CF implies collapse.** Take any $L\in\mathsf{NP}^A$ and fix its oracle verifier with a polynomial bound on witness length. Give each input $x$ a recognizable anchor record and records for all valid witnesses. Include the tagged $x$ in each record. Choose a fixed polynomial anchor padding so every valid witness record is shorter than its anchor. Records are self-delimiting and admit a unique parse. For each $x$ put its anchor and its valid witness records in one class; make every invalid record a singleton. A canonical form for this relation sends all valid records to the anchor, and fixes every invalid record. On an anchor, the shortlex form is a witness on yes-inputs and the anchor itself on no-inputs. Thus Lex = CF puts $L$ in $\mathsf P^A$.

**CF = Ker implies collapse.** Here use one relation permitting *variable* padding. The shared header is $(x,N)$ with $N$ in canonical binary. Valid records are an anchor padded to total length exactly $N$, and witness records of ordinary length containing that header and an accepting witness for $x$. All valid records with a common header are equivalent, and invalid records are singletons. An invariant uses a valid-record tag and the header, or an invalid-record tag and the entire record. Parsing, binary length comparison and verification all take polynomial time with $A$.

Fix a canonical form for that relation and its polynomial output bound. For inputs of length $n$ choose $N=(n+2)^d$. The lengths of valid witness records are bounded by a fixed polynomial in $n$ plus $O(d\log(n+2))$ using length-prefixed fields. Choose the fixed constant $d$ large enough that, for large $n$, the output bound on a witness is strictly less than $N$. A yes-class therefore cannot canonize to its anchor. The canonical form on its anchor must return a witness. On a no-class the anchor is the only valid record. Testing the output tag decides $L$ in $\mathsf P^A$, with finite exceptions hardwired. The relation was fixed before the canonizer and $d$ were chosen. This is the relativizing argument already given in the MathProofs Question 5.10 proof. [Earlier argument](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/CanonicalFormsAndPNP/PROOF.md)

Finally at level $i$, $\mathsf P^A=\Delta_{i+1}^P$ and $\mathsf{NP}^A=\Sigma_{i+1}^P$. Equality makes this level closed under complement, collapsing all higher polynomial-hierarchy levels to it. Conversely, collapse to $\Delta_{i+1}^P$ implies the asserted level equality. The new item here is the explicit oracle-level classification against the authors' comparison list. The variable-padding lemma is reused.

## Equal pair counts with incomparable kernel degrees

A separate construction sharpens the distinction between ordinary many-one and kernel reductions. For every two distinct members of the infinite family below, the relations are Karp-equivalent in deterministic logspace, neither polynomial-time kernel-reduces to the other, and the exact number of accepted ordered pairs agrees for every pair of string lengths. This last statistic is the size of the relation as a set of pairs. The literature on Question 4.14 also considers the growth of the number of classes. The original separation is already published. [Fortnow–Grochow, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/0907.4775v2) · [Finkelstein–Hescott, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.08558) · [Buss and coauthors](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/isomorphReduce/9red_asl.pdf)

Never identify strings of different lengths. At ordinary lengths put all words of that length in one class. Take active lengths $N_0=16$, $N_{j+1}=2^{N_j}$. At an active length $n$ put $k=2^{\lfloor(n-3)/2\rfloor}$, $a=(k^2+k+2)/2$ and $t=2^n-a-k$. There are two partitions of the $n$-bit words in binary numerical order, with contiguous block lengths

$$R=(a,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{k},t),\qquad S=(a-1,k+1,t).$$

They accept exactly the same number of ordered pairs since $a^2+k=(a-1)^2+(k+1)^2$. Relation number $i\ge0$ uses $R$ exactly when the exponent of two dividing $j+1$ is $i$ and $S$ at the other active lengths. All predicates are in L. To check the tower condition one generates the next length only if its exponent is at most $\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$. To classify a word, compare it to the constant number of block boundaries, treating the $k$ singleton words individually by equality. The bits of the boundaries are obtained using indices and carries in logarithmic space since $k$ is a power of two. Ordinary Karp reductions decide the input and output a fixed yes- or no-pair.

Fix distinct $i,i'$ and any candidate kernel reduction with polynomial length bound $p$. Infinitely many active $n=N_j$ have $R$ on the source and $S$ on the target. Eventually $p(n)<N_{j+1}$. Target strings of lengths at most $p(n)$ represent only polynomially many classes: ordinary lengths contribute at most one each, the current active length contributes at most three, and all shorter active lengths together contribute at most $\sum_{h\le\log_2 n}2^h<2n$ classes (an upper bound by *all* strings of those lengths). The source at $n$ alone has at least $k$ distinct classes. Kernel reductions inject classes, which is impossible for large $n$. Interchanging $i,i'$ proves incomparability. This equal-pair-count strengthening is not counted as a newly settled numbered question.
