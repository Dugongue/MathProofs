# Exact determinant extraction in fixed positive characteristic

Fix a field $F$ of characteristic $p>0$. Work with exact formal polynomials, circuit constants in $F$, and division-free arithmetic circuits with polynomially many wires and constant depth. The oracle gate evaluates its polynomial at the argument polynomials. Oracle outputs may feed later calls. Let $\mathrm{VAC}^0_F$ denote the polynomial-degree families with such ordinary circuits and $\mathrm{VBP}_F$ those computed by polynomial-size algebraic branching programs.

**Theorem.** The fixed-field, unbounded-oracle-degree version of Deyâ€“Guo Conjecture 2 holds if and only if $\mathrm{VAC}^0_F=\mathrm{VBP}_F$. It is false over every such field by Forbes's all-field lower bound, as shown by the stronger [linear-degree counterexample](SmallCharacteristic.md). The elementary Boolean lower-bound proof below gives an independent check over the two-element field. In the conjecture the depth and polynomial size bound are uniform bounds on the family, the target dimension is $m(n)=n^{\Theta(1)}$, and each circuit may use the supplied polynomial $f_n$ at its own input dimension. The circuits themselves may be nonuniform.

**Proof.** For even $n$ take any one half-size minor $h_n$ and choose $f_n=h_n^{p^n}$. This is a nonzero member of the indicated ideal. For any oracle call on polynomial inputs, its value has the form $b(x)^{p^n}$. In the polynomial ring modulo the ideal $(x_1,\ldots,x_N)^{p^n}$, this equals the scalar $b(0)^{p^n}$ by Frobenius. This holds even for $b$ formed using nested calls. For each oracle gate take its scalar output on the all-zero external input. Replace the gate by that scalar. Induction in circuit order gives the identical output in the quotient ring.

A stripped ordinary circuit of size $s$ and depth $d$ has degree at most $\max(2,s)^d$. For $s=\mathrm{poly}(n)$ and fixed $d$, both that bound and $m(n)$ are less than $p^n$ eventually. Two polynomials of degree less than $p^n$ equal in the quotient are identical. Therefore exact extraction on just this family yields ordinary polynomial-size constant-depth determinant circuits. To obtain dimension $t$, choose an even $n$ polynomially bounded in $t$ with $m(n)\ge t$, using the constant lower exponent in $m(n)=n^{\Theta(1)}$. Substitute a $t$-square variable block and an identity block. Conversely, ordinary determinant circuits solve the extraction task for every $f$ by computing the target directly.

Determinant is complete for polynomial-size algebraic branching programs under polynomial-size affine substitutions. In particular the sum of path weights in a layered program is an entry of $(I-A)^{-1}$ for its strictly triangular adjacency matrix $A$, and this entry is a signed determinant minor since $\det(I-A)=1$. Constant-depth polynomial-wire circuits unfold to polynomial-size formulas, which convert to algebraic branching programs. These facts give the claimed class equality in both directions.

**An unconditional counterexample over $\mathbb F_2$.** To decide whether a Boolean word of length $k$ has Hamming weight divisible by three, use vertices $(i,j)$ with $0\le i\le k$ and $j\in\mathbb Z/3$. In layer $i$ put an edge of weight $1+x_i$ for remaining at the same residue and one of weight $x_i$ for incrementing it. On Boolean inputs the active path is unique. The path sum from $(0,0)$ to $(k,0)$ is the desired indicator and, by the minor construction, a determinant of size $3k+2$. Polynomial-size constant-depth determinant circuits over $\mathbb F_2$ would turn into Boolean circuits of the same type using parity and AND gates. Substitution of the edge labels adds constant depth. This contradicts Smolensky's lower bound for MOD 3 in $\mathrm{AC}^0[2]$. The argument uses constants in the two-element field; circuits over extension fields are a different model. [Smolensky, STOC 1987](https://doi.org/10.1145/28395.28404)

**Which question this resolves.** Grochow's original Conjecture 6.3 uses unrestricted circuit reductions. The determinant belongs to the half-minors ideal by Laplace expansion and already has polynomial-size ordinary circuits. That literal formulation was recognized as immediate in earlier work. Dey and Guo explicitly impose constant depth and ask about exact extraction with no dependence on the oracle degree. Their positive theorem assumes characteristic zero or sufficiently large characteristic and permits dependence on the degree. The fixed-characteristic theorem above addresses the field-unrestricted reading of their conjecture and makes no change to their positive result. There is a previous MathProofs characteristic-zero argument using Wronskians. The Frobenius proof here concerns fixed positive characteristic. Priority for this formulation remains unverified.
