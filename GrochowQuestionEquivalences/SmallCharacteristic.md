# A linear-degree counterexample to small-characteristic oracle extraction

**Question.** Dey and Guo ask whether their exact extraction results, and the earlier approximate extraction results, extend to small positive characteristic. Their inputs include an arbitrary nonzero polynomial in a determinantal ideal. They already obtain a power of the target in small characteristic. The question here is whether the same oracle model can output the determinant itself, exactly or as a coefficientwise limit. [Dey–Guo, §4, item 2, and Corollary 46](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol362-itcs2026/html/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.49/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.49.html#S4)

**Theorem.** Let $p$ be a fixed prime. For even $n$ let $h_n$ be a single $n/2$-square minor and $f_n=h_n^p$. Over any field of characteristic $p$, including its algebraic closure, no constant-depth polynomial-wire-size family of division-free arithmetic circuits with ordinary $f_n$-evaluation gates computes a determinant of dimension $n^{\Omega(1)}$ and polynomially bounded in $n$. The same holds for border computation with a formal approximation parameter. The oracle polynomial belongs to the required ideal and has degree exactly $pn/2$. Replacing the minor by a generating principal Pfaffian of linearly growing even order gives the corresponding counterexample for Pfaffian targets.

**Stronger counterexample.** The oracle can even be homogeneous and absolutely irreducible, of degree $pn/2+1$. Take minors $h,g$ on disjoint diagonal half-size blocks, two distinct off-block variables $z,w$, and set

$$f=z h^p+w g^p.$$

Both terms belong to the ideal. Over every extension field, $h^p$ is coprime to $w g^p$ because the three sets of variables are disjoint. Thus this is a primitive polynomial of degree one in $z$, irreducible over the fraction field of the remaining variables and hence over the polynomial ring by Gauss's lemma. Its partial derivative in $z$ is nonzero. This example survives a restriction to absolutely irreducible inputs. The modification to the simulation is given below.

The assertion concerns identities of formal polynomials. In the border model the field of constants may include rational functions in a parameter; the coefficients of the final output must be regular at zero with the requested specialization. No bound on intermediate poles is assumed.

**1. The coefficient an oracle cannot change.** Partition some external variables into $d$ blocks. Scale block $i$ by a separate marker $u_i$. In the quotient by $(u_1^2,\ldots,u_d^2)$ every $p$-th power of a polynomial equals its value at all markers zero, raised to the $p$-th power. Indeed every nonconstant monomial gets a marker exponent at least $p\ge2$ after taking the power, and Frobenius eliminates cross terms. Each actual oracle answer, on arbitrary polynomial arguments including earlier oracle outputs, is a $p$-th power. Replace each oracle gate by its scalar output at zero external variables after the chosen input substitution. In circuit order the new ordinary circuit has the identical marked output in the quotient. In particular the coefficient of $u_1\cdots u_d$ agrees. This coefficient selects the part having degree exactly one in each block. The argument works over arbitrary extension fields, including fields of rational functions in the approximation parameter.

For the irreducible oracle, after composing with arbitrary arguments, $h^p$ and $g^p$ again reduce in the marker quotient to their scalar values at zero external variables. Replace this oracle gate by the affine expression $a Z+b W$ in its two actual input wires for $z,w$, with those scalar coefficients $a,b$. Induction also applies when one of these wires comes from an earlier oracle gate. Constant depth and polynomial wire size are preserved, since each replaced gate contributes a constant-size ordinary circuit. For a border family the coefficients $a,b$ may depend rationally on the approximation parameter. Only the selected output coefficients require regularity.

**2. An exact simulation that would contradict a lower bound.** Let $m$ be the extracted determinant dimension. Take width $w=\lfloor\sqrt m\rfloor$ and $d=\lfloor\sqrt{\log w}\rfloor$, ignoring bounded initial sizes. The iterated matrix multiplication entry $\mathrm{IMM}_{w,d}$ on $d$ layers is a path sum in a layered algebraic branching program on $O(wd)$ vertices. For strictly upper triangular adjacency $A$, the path sum is a cofactor of $I-A$ up to sign, since $\det(I-A)=1$. Thus IMM is an affine projection of a determinant that fits inside $m$. Extra rows and columns can be filled by an identity block.

Apply that projection first, then use the $d$ layers as the marked blocks. If extraction were possible, the resulting ordinary stripped circuit $B$ would agree with IMM on its component linear in every layer. That component alone can be computed by an ordinary circuit of size polynomial in $n$ and constant depth. For each $S\subseteq[d]$, zero the blocks outside $S$, scale the others by a common $z$, and retain the degree-$d$ coefficient in $z$. Sum these results with weight $(-1)^{d-|S|}$. Inclusion–exclusion discards every monomial missing a block. A monomial of total degree $d$ using every block uses each exactly once. This works also in characteristic two. The stripped circuit has polynomial total degree because it has constant depth and polynomially many wires. Interpolation therefore costs polynomial size over an infinite extension field, which we may use even for a finite original field. There are $2^d=n^{o(1)}$ restrictions. The resulting circuit computes IMM exactly.

Forbes's Theorem 10 gives a superpolynomial lower bound in $w$ for constant-depth ordinary circuits computing $\mathrm{IMM}_{w,d}$ over **every** field with this choice of $d$. The width $w$ grows polynomially in $n$, so the simulated circuit contradicts the theorem. [Forbes, CCC 2024, Theorem 10](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol300-ccc2024/LIPIcs.CCC.2024.31/LIPIcs.CCC.2024.31.pdf#page=8)

**3. Why the limit version fails too.** An exact lower bound alone need not be a border lower bound. Use the coefficient-matrix rank bound in the *proof* of Forbes's Theorem 10. In his construction there is a hard set-multilinear polynomial $P$ with $d$ specified variable blocks and a fixed coefficient flattening. It is a linear substitution of the preceding IMM. Its relative rank is at least $w^{-O(1)}$ over the field in question (Theorem 9 and the opening of the proof of Theorem 10).

Project the hypothetical border output directly to $P$ and use **its** variable blocks. Let $K$ be the coefficient field with the approximation parameter adjoined. Apply step 1 over $K$. The full set-multilinear component of the stripped ordinary circuit has coefficients regular at parameter zero, since these coefficients equal those of the original output. Their specializations are the coefficients of $P$. Other components and internal gates may have poles.

Forbes's bound on the relative rank of the full set-multilinear projection of a size-$s$, fixed-product-depth ordinary circuit applies over $K$ (proof of Theorem 10, in particular the rank inequality preceding the final specialization). For fixed depth it bounds relative rank by $(d^d s)^{O(1)}w^{-\Omega(d^c)}$ for a constant $c>0$ depending on depth. Here $d^d=w^{o(1)}$ and $s=w^{O(1)}$, so the bound is smaller than $w^{-O(1)}$ for every fixed exponent eventually. Coefficientwise specialization of a regular coefficient matrix cannot increase rank: every minor vanishing over $K$ vanishes after specialization. The same normalization factor is used on both sides. This contradicts the lower bound on the rank of $P$. This use of the universal rank inequality avoids any assumption that exact and border circuit complexity coincide. [Forbes, Theorems 9–10 and their proof](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol300-ccc2024/LIPIcs.CCC.2024.31/LIPIcs.CCC.2024.31.pdf#page=8)

**Pfaffians.** Take a principal generator on $2r$ indices with $r=\Theta(n)$ in the Pfaffian version of the ideal, and raise it to the $p$-th power. Its degree is $pr$. A Pfaffian on $2m$ indices specializes up to sign to $\det_m(X)$ on the alternating block matrix with off-diagonal blocks $X$ and $-X^{\mathsf T}$ and zero diagonal blocks. This identity also holds in characteristic two. The same proof applies in the exact and border models. For an irreducible Pfaffian example use two disjoint equal-sized principal blocks (for instance $n$ divisible by four and blocks of size $n/2$), with two independent off-block entries as $z,w$.

**Scope.** This gives a negative answer to the universal oracle-extraction extension, including polynomial-degree inputs. It permits the oracle to return its actual value; derivative or root gates change the model. The irreducible example shows that excluding oracle polynomials which are themselves powers, or requiring squarefreeness, does not recover the proposed universal reduction. Dey and Guo explicitly identified the root issue and proved a power-output theorem. Their positive theorems and the Forbes lower bound are prior work. Priority for the counterexample and this use of the rank bound has not been established.
