# Exact bridges in simultaneous computability

The objects of study here are the answers, witnesses, and proof costs retained when the same mathematical input is tested in different fields or rings. Each bridge below says exactly which information survives a translation and gives an operation or formula that can be reused.

| Existing results brought together | Exact common statement | What can be reused |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frobenius tests for tensor isomorphism, finite étale root tests, sparse proof certificates, tensor rank and graph fitting rank | Every fixed finite list of these witness-existence tests over a number field has the same answers as a list of finite Galois-set fixed-point tests at all good primes and every residue extension. Each coordinate can be converted into one squarefree polynomial or one ordinary tensor comparison. | Compute implications once on a finite group. Conjunction and disjunction become products and unions of finite sets. The minimum Galois field and minimum possible polynomial degree depend only on the profile. [Frobenius bridge](FrobeniusBridge.md) |
| Finite étale multiplication tensors, central simple algebra witnesses and period–index splitting | Multiplication-tensor witnesses normalize, compatibly with base change, to two units and a center-preserving algebra witness. Over the stated local and global fields a branch of the finite center-isomorphism scheme contributes exactly the multiples of its residue degree times the least common multiple of its relative Brauer periods. | Take a product of center schemes to ask for simultaneous witnesses, then use the same degree formula. In the étale case all period weights are one. A quadratic center and a quaternion obstruction give an exact degree-four example. A parallel integral construction realizes ideal principalization by unmarked side-four multiplication tensors. [Descent bridge](DescentBridge.md) |
| Finite étale TI, norm-one torus torsors, and integral cube principalization | A finite table of Galois-set tests, selected by the residual extension degree, describes the norm-torsor profile. Root polynomials with coefficients in powers of an ideal recover actual generators at integral probes, and every principalizing field supplies one such polynomial. | Use the same operations of subgroup restriction and polynomial-root witnesses on two different arithmetic constructions. The bridge gives exact least degrees and principalizing fields disjoint from protected extensions. [Arithmetic bridge](ArithmeticBridge.md) |
| Exact matroid direct-sum degree and finite-field congruence certificates | Filtering each component's polynomials separately explains why multiplying variables across components can preserve ordinary PC degree. In the literal bracket encoding the exact minimum law extends to matroid 2-sums. In matrix congruence a common symmetric tensor factor preserves an arithmetic obstruction according to its dimension parity in the stated odd- and even-characteristic classes. | Reuse certificates after gluing or tensoring inputs. The transfer applies to both the expensive odd-field examples and the bounded-degree characteristic-two examples. Labelled products also amplify degree two to exact degree $2Q$ in one chosen characteristic. [Certificate bridge](CertificateBridge.md) |

## A worked comparison

For the rational $2\times4\times4$ pencil pair from the portfolio, the two different outcomes now have minimal root tests. Isomorphism corresponds to $x^2-14$, while rank four corresponds to $x^4-18x^2+25$. The first test needs one quadratic field and the second needs the full shared biquadratic field. This compares the two properties over every extension of every good residue field, with the same reductions and Frobenius element. The note checks explicit polynomial substitutions on the original pencils and exhibits an exceptional prime where a new projectivity appears. [Worked comparison](FrobeniusBridge.md#4-what-this-lets-us-reuse)

The finite-group representation of bounded tests is a common decision language. Other bridges retain additional data, including generators of principal ideals, the entries of multiplication-tensor witnesses, and the degree of a PC refutation. These statements specify exactly where that extra information is preserved.

The papers of Fried–Sacerdote and Baker–Lorscheid–Walsh–Zhang give earlier structural inputs. The manuscripts identify the precise portfolio translations, formulas, and resource bounds.
