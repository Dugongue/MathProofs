# Exact bridges between arithmetic extension profiles

Let $K$ be a number field and regard its finite extensions as subfields of a fixed algebraic closure. A field probe tests for a point over $E$. An integral probe tests for a point over $\mathcal O_E$. An ordinary tensor isomorphism allows independent invertible changes in the three modes.

## 1. Norm torsors give finite tables of ordinary tensor probes

Take a locally cyclic Galois extension $L/K$ with finite Galois group $G$, and put $T=R^1_{L/K}\mathbb G_m$. Here locally cyclic means that every decomposition group is cyclic. Let $\boldsymbol\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)$ be a nonempty tuple of everywhere locally trivial $T$-torsor classes, let $D$ be the least common multiple of their orders, and let $X$ be the product of the torsors. For $H\le G$, define
$$
 a_X(H)=\operatorname{lcm}_{i}\operatorname{ord}\big(\operatorname{res}_{L^H/K}\alpha_i\big).
$$
The order of the zero class is one.

**Theorem 1.** For each divisor $b\mid D$ there is an explicitly specified square-free monic polynomial $p_b\in K[t]$ and a pair of finite étale multiplication tensors $(U_b,V_b)$ over $K$. For every finite $E/K$, write $H_E=\operatorname{Gal}(L/(E\cap L))$, $e_E=[E:E\cap L]$, and $b_E=\gcd(e_E,D)$. Then
$$
 X(E)\ne\varnothing
 \iff a_X(H_E)\mid e_E
 \iff p_{b_E}\text{ has a root in }E
 \iff U_{b_E}\simeq_E V_{b_E}.
$$
The polynomial and tensor pairs may all be split over $L$. On probes contained in $L$, or with $e_E$ coprime to $D$, the single branch $b=1$ suffices.

*Proof.* The locally cyclic norm-transfer formula identifies $\Sha^1(K,T)$ with $H_2(G,\mathbb Z)$. Over $E$, the restriction of $\alpha_i$ is identified with $e_E$ times the group homology transfer to $H_2(H_E,\mathbb Z)$. In particular the transfer alone is the restriction to $L^{H_E}$. One way to see the precise extra factor is to dualize the corestriction on the character lattice. Here $H^2(H,X^*(T))\simeq H^3(H,\mathbb Z)$ by the regular permutation sequence, and local conditions are automatic because $H^3(C,\mathbb Z)=0$ for cyclic $C$. Corestriction from $E$ to $K$ on these inflated classes factors as $e_E$ times the subgroup corestriction. The character-group dual of $H^3(H,\mathbb Z)$ is $H_2(H,\mathbb Z)$, by the universal coefficient theorem with divisible coefficients and the sequence $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$. Thus the dual of the stated corestriction is the asserted multiple of transfer. Therefore all classes restrict to zero exactly when $a_X(H_E)\mid e_E$. For $G=(\mathbb Z/m)^r$, the Smith basis transfer formula in exterior powers computes $a_X(H)$ directly. [Tate, cohomology groups of tori](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/nagoya-mathematical-journal/article/cohomology-groups-of-tori-in-finite-galois-extensions-of-number-fields/81A85EFA72AA0B0937DF1019F89373B1) · [Brown, group cohomology and transfer](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~kbrown/papers/cohomology_hangzhou.pdf)

If $J\le H$, further restriction gives $a_X(J)\mid a_X(H)$. Each order divides $D$, so the subgroups with $a_X(H)\mid b$ form a nonempty, conjugacy-invariant downset $\mathcal C_b$. For one representative of each conjugacy class of maximal members, take a monic primitive-element equation for $L^H/K$ and take the product. Distinct representatives give nonisomorphic fixed fields, so the equations are distinct. A root in $E$ is equivalent to $H_E$ lying below a conjugate of one of the representatives.

For the tensor pair use the table of marks of finite $G$-sets. This matrix is triangular after sorting subgroup classes by order, with diagonal entry $[N_G(H):H]>0$ in column $G/H$. Therefore there are two finite $G$-sets having equal marks precisely on $\mathcal C_b$. Indeed solve rationally for a virtual set with mark zero on $\mathcal C_b$ and one elsewhere, clear denominators, then take its positive and negative parts. Add a common fixed point if necessary to make both nonempty. They have equal cardinality because $1\in\mathcal C_b$. On restriction to any $H$, two $G$-sets become isomorphic exactly when their marks agree at every subgroup of $H$, by the same triangular argument for $H$. This holds exactly when $H\in\mathcal C_b$. Take the multiplication tensors of the two associated étale algebras. If $R(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$ is a tensor isomorphism of unital algebras, put $u=P(1)$, $v=Q(1)$. Evaluating at one argument equal to $1$ shows right multiplication by $v$ and left multiplication by $u$ are invertible, so $u,v$ are units. Then $u^{-1}R(-)v^{-1}$ is an algebra isomorphism. Conversely an algebra isomorphism gives a tensor isomorphism. This gives the field test for every branch. $\square$ [Webster, Burnside rings and small equivalent sets](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0610205) · [Earlier finite étale tensor construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/SimultaneousComputabilityAndGrochow/ArithmeticTI.md)

**Corollary 2.** With a common $L$, two such product profiles are ordered by inclusion, $\operatorname{Pos}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Pos}(Y)$, exactly when $a_Y(H)\mid a_X(H)$ for every $H\le G$. Their intersection is the product profile with threshold $\operatorname{lcm}(a_X(H),a_Y(H))$. The union has a branchwise root and ordinary étale tensor realization using the union of the two downsets, with the branch modulus taken to be a common multiple of the periods. If at some $H$ neither threshold divides the other, the union admits no product realization with norm torsors for this $L$.

*Proof.* For any $H\le G$ and positive integer $q$, choose an extension $E/L^H$ of degree $q$ linearly disjoint from $L/L^H$. An Eisenstein polynomial at a prime unramified in $L$ remains Eisenstein at a prime over it in $L$ and provides such an extension. Thus every $(H,q)$ is realized by $(H_E,e_E)$. Inclusion can be checked on $q=a_X(H)$, and conversely follows from divisibility. The intersection and union formulas follow from conjunction and disjunction. If the union of positive multiples of two incomparable integers were all multiples of some integer $c$, then $c$ would divide both and would itself be divisible by one of them, a contradiction. $\square$

**Corollary 3.** If any $\alpha_i\ne0$, no finite collection of ordinary finite étale multiplication-tensor tests or separable polynomial root tests, combined by an arbitrary fixed Boolean rule, reproduces the profile of $X$ on all finite extensions.

*Proof.* Choose one common Galois field $M/K$ splitting the candidate tests and containing $L$. Take a degree-$D$ Eisenstein extension $E/K$ disjoint from $M$, as in Corollary 2. Every candidate test has the same value at $K$ and $E$, since the Galois group over $E$ on the split factors is the full Galois group over $K$. But $X(K)=\varnothing$ and $X(E)\ne\varnothing$ by Theorem 1. $\square$

The possible union obstruction extends beyond one fixed $L$. Take two nontrivial locally trivial norm-one torsors for locally cyclic $(C_p)^2$ and $(C_q)^2$ fields, with distinct primes $p,q$. For each $\ell\in\{p,q\}$, construct its field using two cyclic cyclotomic fields of degree $\ell$, whose conductor primes are unramified in $K$ and split in each other's fields. The factors stay independent after base change to $K$: at each conductor prime the ramification in its own degree-prime factor is total while the other factor splits. At the second prime these conditions are Chebotarev splitting in the first cyclic field, the appropriate cyclotomic field, and the extension adjoining the appropriate root of the first conductor prime. The norm-transfer formula gives the two exact tests $p\mid[E:K]$ and $q\mid[E:K]$. If their union were the profile of one torsor of any torus, its class would be killed by both $p$ and $q$, using restriction–corestriction on any degree-$p$ and degree-$q$ positive respectively. It would already vanish at $K$. The product of the two actual torsors realizes their intersection, the test $pq\mid[E:K]$.

**Example.** Take the compositum of the cyclic quartic subfields of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{17})$ and $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{353})$. Each conductor prime splits in the other's quartic field, since $353\equiv 3^4\pmod {17}$ and $105^4\equiv17\pmod {353}$. Each conductor is totally ramified only in its own factor, and other finite places have cyclic unramified decomposition. Thus $G=C_4^2$ is locally cyclic. Take a generator $\alpha$ of $\bigwedge^2G=C_4$, viewed as a locally trivial norm torsor. The transfer to $H=2G$ sends the generator to the generator of $\bigwedge^2H=C_2$ by the rank-two Smith formula, so $L^{2G}$ is a negative degree-four probe. A cyclic quartic subfield is a positive degree-four probe, because its $H$ is cyclic, and a degree-four probe disjoint from $L$ is also positive, because its $e_E$ is four. This profile distinguishes fields of the same degree and still requires the residual degree information.

## 2. Integral principalization is an exact union of finite étale root profiles

Let $R=\mathcal O_K$ and let $I\ne0$ be an integral ideal. Its class has finite order $d$. A monic polynomial
$$
 f(t)=t^n+c_1t^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n\in R[t]
$$
is admissible for $I$ when $c_j\in I^j$ for all $j$ and $(c_n)=I^n$.

**Theorem 4.** For every finite $E/K$, an element $\theta\in E$ generates $I\mathcal O_E$ exactly when it is the root of an admissible polynomial. The minimal polynomial of a generator is admissible. Consequently the principalization profile of $I$, and hence the earlier split integral cube profile of $I$, is the union of the ordinary finite étale tensor profiles of all irreducible admissible polynomials.

*Proof.* Suppose $f(\theta)=0$ and let $\mathfrak P$ be any nonzero prime of $\mathcal O_E$ with restriction $\mathfrak p$ to $R$. Choose a generator $s\in K^\times$ for $I R_{\mathfrak p}$. Then $s^{-n}f(st)$ is monic over $\mathcal O_{E,\mathfrak P}$ with unit constant coefficient. Thus $\theta/s$ is integral over this discrete valuation ring, so belongs to it. The unit constant coefficient and the polynomial equation then give $(\theta/s)^{-1}\in\mathcal O_{E,\mathfrak P}$. Equality $(\theta)=I\mathcal O_E$ follows at every prime.

Conversely suppose $\theta$ generates $I\mathcal O_E$. In a common Galois closure of $E/K$ every conjugate of $\theta$ generates the same extended ideal. For the minimal polynomial of $\theta$, the elementary symmetric expression for $c_j$ therefore lies in the extended $I^j$, and the constant coefficient generates the extended $I^n$. Both properties descend to $R$, since valuations of elements of $K$ and exponents of extended ideals are multiplied by the same ramification index. The minimal polynomial is separable, and each irreducible polynomial root profile converts by the finite étale construction of Theorem 1, using the downset generated by a root stabilizer in its Galois group. The earlier integral cubes are isomorphic over $\mathcal O_E$ exactly when $I\mathcal O_E$ is principal. $\square$ [Earlier split integral cubes, Theorem 2](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/SimultaneousComputabilityAndGrochow/IntegralCubes.md)

**Theorem 5.** Fix a finite field extension $M/K$, an integer $n\ge2$ divisible by $d$, and a generator $a$ of $I^n$. There is an admissible $f$ of degree $n$ with constant coefficient $a$, whose splitting field has group $S_n$ over both $K$ and $M$ after base change. Its degree-$n$ root field $E$ principalizes $I$, has no proper nontrivial intermediate subfield, and has Galois closure linearly disjoint from the Galois closure of $M$ over $K$.

*Proof.* We can replace $M$ by its Galois closure. Take large finite primes of $K$ split completely in $M$, away from the support of $I$, by Chebotarev. At their residue fields prescribe square-free monic polynomials of factorization types $(n)$, $(n-1,1)$, and $(2,1,\ldots,1)$, all with constant coefficient the residue of $a$. Use only type $(2)$ if $n=2$.

To justify these choices, over a finite field of size $Q$ the norm to that field from its degree-$h$ extension, for $h\ge2$, has $(Q^h-1)/(Q-1)$ elements in each nonzero fiber. For $h\ge3$, the total size of all proper subfields is at most $hQ^{h/2}$, which is smaller than the norm fiber for sufficiently large $Q$. For $h=2$ a norm fiber contains at most two base-field elements, since their norms are their squares, while the full fiber has size $Q+1$. Thus the minimal polynomial of an element of full degree in an appropriate norm fiber is irreducible with any prescribed nonzero constant coefficient. To obtain a mixed type, first select all the needed distinct nonzero linear roots, and then choose the irreducible factor with the constant coefficient that remains.

For each $j<n$, the reduction of $I^j$ at each chosen prime is surjective because the prime does not divide $I$. The Chinese remainder theorem on the module $I^j$ now gives a coefficient with all prescribed residues. Together with constant coefficient $a$, these choices give an admissible polynomial. The factorization types at these primes give Galois elements of the indicated cycle types over $M$, since the primes split completely there and the reductions are square-free. The group over $M$ is transitive by the $n$-cycle. For $n\ge3$, the $(n-1)$-cycle gives a stabilizer transitive on the remaining points, hence the group is doubly transitive. Conjugating a transposition then gives all transpositions. This proves the full group $S_n$ over $M$, and therefore over $K$ as well. Equality of the two groups gives disjointness of its splitting field from $M$. In the splitting field a root has point stabilizer $S_{n-1}$, which is maximal in $S_n$. Indeed a larger subgroup would have to be transitive, and the orbit–stabilizer formula then gives order at least $n!$. Theorem 4 supplies principalization. $\square$

**Corollary 6.** For nonprincipal $I$, no finite Boolean combination of ordinary finite étale tensor tests or root tests reproduces its principalization profile on all $E$, even when restricted to $K$ and degree-$d$ probes. On the other hand an infinite union of irreducible root tests does reproduce it, and each degree $n$ divisible by $d$ requires infinitely many distinct irreducible root tests in any such union.

*Proof.* For the first claim take the field $M$ splitting all the tests and apply Theorem 5 with $n=d$. Its positive root field has the same finite-test value as $K$, a negative. For the last claim, suppose only finitely many degree-$n$ tests occur. Apply Theorem 5 in degree $n$ with $M$ splitting those tests. A positive root test in the resulting field must have degree one or $n$, because the field has no intermediate field. A degree-one test would already be positive at $K$, and all listed degree-$n$ tests are excluded by disjointness from $M$. Also $d\mid[E:K]$ at every positive, either by ideal norm or by the degree of the minimal polynomial in Theorem 4. $\square$

Theorem 5 also supplies disjoint principalizing fields for a finite subgroup $C\le\mathrm{Cl}(R)$, at degree at most $|C|$. Choose cyclic generators of $C$ and principalize their extended ideals one at a time, each time using the current class order and protecting the compositum with $M$. The successive degree bounds multiply to at most $|C|$. The field obtained stays linearly disjoint from $M$: at each step the degree of its compositum with $M$ over the preceding compositum is the degree of that step. Previously principal ideals stay principal. For context, the classical principal ideal theorem and Bosca's abelian-compositum principalization give other existence results. Here the conclusion concerns prescribed degree, a prescribed constant coefficient, full symmetric Galois group, and arbitrary finite disjointness for a single class. No priority claim is made for the polynomial construction. [Bosca, with Gras and Jaulent, principalization in abelian extensions](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.4147)

**Explicit quartic.** Set $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$, $I=(2,1+\sqrt{-5})$. The prime over $2$ is ramified, so $I^2=(2)$. An element of norm two would solve $x^2+5y^2=2$ over the integers, which is impossible. Thus $d=2$. A quadratic accepting field is $K(\sqrt2)$. The admissible polynomial $f=t^4-2t^3+4$ gives an accepting quartic that contains no quadratic. In fact $7,23,89$ split in $K(\sqrt2)$. Modulo $7$ the factors are $t-6$ and $t^3+4t^2+3t+4$, with the cubic having no root. Modulo $23$ the factors are $t-10,t-11,t^2+19t+13$, with quadratic discriminant $10$, a nonsquare. Modulo $89$, the remainder of $t^{89^2}-t$ modulo $f$ is $49t^3+72t^2+49t+28$. The next Euclidean remainders are $20t^2+81t+27$, $72t+77$, and $46$, proving that $f$ has no factor of degree one or two and hence is irreducible. The cycle argument of Theorem 5 applies. The quartic's Galois closure is therefore disjoint from $K(\sqrt2)$, even though both probes accept the same integral cube.

## 3. A common transport problem for norm and integral witnesses

Let $A$ be a commutative finite locally free $R$-algebra of constant rank $n$. Let $P,Q$ be invertible $A$-modules with orientations $\omega_P\colon\det_R P\simeq D$ and $\omega_Q\colon\det_R Q\simeq D$ to a common invertible $R$-module. The oriented transport functor sends a commutative $R$-algebra $S$ to
$$
 \big\{f:P_S\xrightarrow{\sim}Q_S\text{ as }A_S\text{-modules}:
             \omega_{Q,S}\circ\det_S f=\omega_{P,S}\big\}.
$$

**Proposition 7.** Given any un-oriented $A_S$-linear isomorphism $\phi:P_S\to Q_S$, there is a natural bijection between oriented witnesses and
$$
 \{u\in A_S^\times:N_{A_S/S}(u)=a\},
 \qquad a=(\omega_{Q,S}\circ\det_S\phi)^{-1}\omega_{P,S}\in S^\times.
$$
Here the composite in the definition of $a$ is an endomorphism of the invertible module $\det_S P_S$, hence a scalar. Thus the exact obstruction over $S$ first asks whether the two line modules are isomorphic over $A_S$, then asks whether the determinant ratio lies in the norm image.

*Proof.* Every isomorphism is uniquely $\phi\circ m_u$ with $u\in A_S^\times$, since the endomorphism algebra of an invertible module is $A_S$. For each prime $\mathfrak p$ of $S$, the algebra $A_S\otimes_S S_{\mathfrak p}$ is finite over a local ring and therefore semilocal. Its invertible modules are free of rank one, so the determinant of $m_u$ on the module is the determinant of $m_u$ on the algebra there. The determinant equality holds globally because it holds at every prime. This is exactly the displayed norm condition. $\square$ [Stacks Project, finite locally free norm](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BD2)

For $R=K$, $A=L$ and $P=Q=L$, scale the source orientation by $a\in K^\times$ relative to the other. This gives $N_{L/K}(u)=a$ over all base rings with the same witnesses. For a Dedekind domain and an invertible ideal $I$, take $A=R\times R$, $P=A$, $Q=I\oplus I^{-1}$. Use the ordered-factor orientation of $P$ and multiplication $I\otimes I^{-1}\simeq R$ to orient $Q$. Then an oriented witness over $S$ is exactly a generator $u$ of $I\otimes_R S$ together with its unique dual generator $v$ satisfying $uv=1$. This description is natural under arbitrary base change. In the integral setting the corresponding acceptance profile is the split cube profile of Theorem 4. The field setting instead tests the norm torsor itself.

For Galois $L/K$ of degree $n$, the norm equation also has an elementary tensor-diagram compiler. Start with the $K$-space $V=L$, marked by the endomorphism $m_\theta$ for a primitive generator $\theta$ of $L/K$. Use one copy $V_\sigma=L$ for each Galois element and add an arrow $\sigma:V\to V_\sigma$. Fix an ordering $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_n$, set $W_1=V_{\sigma_1}$, and add copies $W_j=L$ with each bilinear multiplication arrow $W_{j-1}\times V_{\sigma_j}\to W_j$ for $2\le j\le n$. Compare the two diagrams with terminal vector $1\in W_n$ and $a\in W_n$ respectively.

Over any extension $E/K$, an isomorphism on $V_E$ commuting with $m_\theta$ is multiplication by a unique unit $u\in(L\otimes_KE)^\times$. The $\sigma$ arrows force multiplication by $\sigma(u)$ on their targets. The multiplication arrows then force multiplication by $\prod_{i\le j}\sigma_i(u)$ on $W_j$, by evaluating one input at $1$ and varying the other. These products also give the isomorphisms on the auxiliary spaces conversely. Since the final product is the scalar norm, the terminal vector condition is precisely $N_{L/K}(u)=a$. The labelled diagram has $2n$ spaces of dimension $n$ and $2n+1$ labelled tensors of order at most three. Its witness bijection works even after $L\otimes_K E$ splits into several factors. The field-uniform Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk construction applied to the diagram yields a single ordinary three-tensor pair with the same existence profile on all extension fields, including infinite extensions. The size statement here concerns the input diagram. [Futorny–Grochow–Sergeichuk, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.09219) · [Grochow–Qiao, Example 10.7](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.00309#S10.SS2)

These are manuscript proofs. The two explicit residue calculations were checked by exact modular arithmetic. The norm transfer and the field-uniform tensor encoding use the cited theorems.
