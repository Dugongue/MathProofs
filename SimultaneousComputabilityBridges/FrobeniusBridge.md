# A common arithmetic representation of bounded witness tests

A squarefree polynomial, an ordinary tensor transporter, a tensor-rank decomposition, a graph fitting-matrix factorization, and a sparse Polynomial Calculus transcript have different witnesses. Their existence profiles over good residue fields and all finite extensions admit the same exact representation. This representation has a smallest Galois field of definition and an exact minimum-degree root test. These two minimizations are independent of the splitting field used to start the calculation.

## 1. From witness systems to finite Galois sets

Let $K$ be a number field and $X_1,\ldots,X_s$ be a finite list of fixed constructible witness sets over $K$. A set may be given by a finite disjunction of lists of polynomial equations and inequations. For simplicity use affine presentations, adding finitely many disjoint charts if desired. Take models over a localization of the ring of integers. Write $k_v$ for the residue field at $v$, $q_v=|k_v|$, and $k_{v,e}$ for its degree-$e$ extension.

**Theorem 1.** There is a common finite Galois extension $L/K$, with group $G$, and finite $G$-sets $D_i$ such that outside one finite exceptional set of $v$, for every $e\ge1$,
$$
   X_i(k_{v,e})\ne\varnothing
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   D_i^{\,\operatorname{Frob}_v^e}\ne\varnothing .
$$
The $D_i$ can be chosen as the sets of strata of finite Galois-equivariant partitions into disjoint geometrically integral locally closed pieces. Give each stratum its dimension. If $d$ is the greatest dimension among the fixed strata and $a$ is their number in dimension $d$, then
$$
    |X_i(k_{v,e})|=a\,q_v^{ed}+O(q_v^{e(d-1/2)})
$$
for $d\ge1$, uniformly in $v,e$. When $d=0$, the count is exactly $a$, and when there is no fixed stratum it is zero.

*Proof.* Work over an algebraic closure of $K$ and take reduced varieties. In a closed set, remove from each irreducible component all the others. These exclusive open pieces are disjoint, geometrically integral and permuted under Galois. Decompose the closed union of intersections by recursion on dimension. Intersect with the constructible witness set, taking a dense open piece on each component where the constructible piece is dense, and use the same recursion for the boundary. Equivalently, first form a partition using the finitely many closed sets in the description, then refine by exclusive components. This gives a finite equivariant stratification after some finite Galois extension. Every algebraic equation, partition identity, descent relation and local-closed presentation spreads after localization. Shrink once more so that every geometric stratum remains geometrically integral of its stated dimension in all fibers, and so that the extension of base rings is étale. This uses generic constructibility of geometric integrality and fiber dimension for finite-presentation models. [Stacks Project, geometrically irreducible fibers](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0559) · [Fried–Sacerdote, general Galois stratification](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1976/104-2/p01).

A point over $k_{v,e}$ belongs to a unique stratum, which is fixed by $\operatorname{Frob}_v^e$. Conversely a fixed stratum descends to a geometrically integral locally closed set over $k_{v,e}$. There are bounds on the complexity of its projective closure and boundary independent of $v,e$, because the finitely many locally closed presentations were spread from the same number-field models. For positive dimension, apply Lang–Weil to the closure and subtract the boundary, whose point count is $O(q_v^{e(d-1)})$ using its bounded degree and smaller dimension. This gives a point for every $e\ge1$ once $q_v$ exceeds one constant. A geometrically integral zero-dimensional stratum is a single point over its field of definition. Sum the counts from fixed strata to get the formula. [Lang–Weil, *Number of Points of Varieties in Finite Fields*](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372655). $\square$

It matters that the strata are disjoint. For instance $x^2-dy^2=0$ has a rational origin even if its two geometric lines are exchanged. The origin is a separate stratum. The earlier tensor-transporter theorem uses Lang's theorem and the disjoint geometric components of a torsor, which makes the stratification step unnecessary in that case. The point-existence part of Theorem 1 is a concrete existential specialization of classical Galois stratification. The use here is to give an exact common decision representation for the portfolio's certificate and isomorphism problems. [Earlier finite-field component argument](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/IsomorphismCrossoversRoundThree/tensors.md).

## 2. Exact translations and the operations they preserve

A decision profile identifies two tests when all their answers at good $v$ and every $e$ agree. For a finite $G$-set $D$, record the conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups $C$ with $D^C\ne\varnothing$. This is downward closed among cyclic subgroups. Conversely any conjugacy-invariant downward family of cyclic subgroups occurs by taking a disjoint union of $G/C$ over its maximal members. For a fixed $L$ the resulting profile poset is therefore exactly that finite distributive lattice, with inclusion as implication, disjoint union as OR, and product as AND.

For arbitrary fixed witness tests, inclusion or equality for a density-one set of base residue fields already implies the same inclusion or equality for every degree over all good places. In fact choose one common $L$ for the tests. If inclusion fails on a group element in $G$, it fails on its conjugacy class, which occurs at positive prime density by Chebotarev. Thus inclusion at density one implies inclusion on all elements of $G$, hence all powers of those elements. OR and AND at the witness level are disjoint union and product, and the fixed-point tests for $G$-sets preserve those operations. The quotient class of $D_i$ therefore retains precisely this logical information, even if the chosen stratification varies.

There are two concrete translations of every such profile.

- **One monic squarefree polynomial.** Use the product of the minimal polynomials of integral primitive generators of $L^H$, one per transitive orbit $G/H$. Choose generators with distinct minimal polynomials by shifting by integral scalars. At good places, the polynomial has a root in $k_{v,e}$ exactly when some orbit of its root permutation has length dividing $e$. Use the constant $1$ for the empty profile. Disjoint union becomes the polynomial product with disjoint roots. Conjunction is the finite étale algebra tensor product, followed by choice of a monogenic presentation.
- **One pair of ordinary multiplication tensors.** If the profile is nonempty, extend the cyclic family to the downward family $\mathcal C$ of all subgroups $H$ whose cyclic subgroups are all allowed. Use the Burnside mark matrix, with rows indexed by conjugacy classes of subgroups and columns by transitive sets. It is triangular up to ordering and has nonzero diagonal, so a nonzero common integer multiple of the zero-one vector of disallowed marks is the mark vector of an integral virtual $G$-set. Write this set as $U-V$. The trivial subgroup is allowed, so $U,V$ have the same cardinality. They restrict to isomorphic $H$-sets precisely when $H\in\mathcal C$, since a finite $H$-set is determined by its subgroup marks and $\mathcal C$ is downward. Translate $U,V$ to finite étale $K$-algebras and take their multiplication tensors. An ordinary tensor isomorphism of two unital multiplication tensors normalizes to an algebra isomorphism by evaluating the tensor witness at the two units. This works over each reduction field. Finite étale algebras spread to good primes and their residue extensions, so the tensors have exactly the specified modular profile. If necessary add one equal split factor to make dimensions positive. For the empty profile use a zero tensor and a nonzero tensor. [Earlier Burnside realization and unit normalization](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/990ed5ac8b956f82c10370d60b7bd54aa4ad5568/SimultaneousComputabilityAndGrochow/ArithmeticTI.md).

These are exact translations of answers, including arbitrary finite lists combined by AND and OR. They have no general complexity-size guarantee. A root witness identifies a positive profile and, when using the stratification model itself, a fixed stratum. It need not give coordinates of a witness in the original problem.

## 3. The minimum Galois field and the minimum root degree

Write $P:G\to\{0,1\}^s$ for the vector of base-prime decisions from Theorem 1. It is a class function. Let
$$
    N=\{n\in G:P(ng)=P(g)\text{ for every }g\in G\}.
$$
This is a normal subgroup. Closure and inverses follow from the stabilizer property under left translations, and conjugation invariance follows because $P$ is a class function. It is the largest subgroup through whose cosets the entire decision vector factors. For a single coordinate define $N$ using that coordinate alone.

**Theorem 2.** The field $L^N$ is the unique smallest finite Galois field over $K$ through which the decision vector factors. It is independent of the witness presentation and initial $L$. For a single nonempty coordinate, put $G_0=\operatorname{Gal}(L^N/K)$, and write $S\subseteq G_0$ for the acceptance set. The minimum degree of a monic squarefree polynomial for this profile, allowing any splitting field over $K$, is the minimum of
$$
       \sum_H [G_0:H]
$$
over collections of conjugacy classes of subgroups $H\le G_0$ for which $S$ is the union of all the conjugates of the chosen $H$'s. Each chosen $H$ is contained in $S$. The minimum is achieved by a polynomial split by $L^N$. Its splitting field then equals $L^N$.

*Proof.* In the Galois group of a compositum of any two candidate fields, the inflated profile vectors coincide by Chebotarev. The kernel of projection to either field is in the translation stabilizer of the common vector. The translation stabilizer of a function inflated from $G$ is the preimage of its stabilizer in $G$. Consequently $L^N$ lies inside every candidate field, and it is itself a candidate by its definition.

For minimum size, start with any finite $G'$-set of roots over a common splitting extension of a competing polynomial and $L^N$, with quotient map $G'\to G_0$ and kernel $J$. Pass to the set of $J$-orbits. A coset $gJ$ fixes such an orbit exactly when some lift of $gJ$ fixes a root. Indeed, if $g x=j x$, then $j^{-1}g$ fixes $x$. Conversely a fixed root gives a fixed orbit. Normality ensures the quotient action is well-defined. Positivity on $G'$ is inflated from $G_0$, so the resulting quotient set has exactly the original profile and has no more points. The [Lean companion](FixedOrbitLift.lean) verifies the fixed-lift calculation.

Every transitive $G_0$-set $G_0/H$ has a fixed point for precisely the elements in conjugates of $H$. Thus any model is a union of these orbits, with the stated total cost, and every allowed cover has a polynomial by the construction in Section 2. A cover exists since the accepted cyclic-family description also descends along the quotient by the orbit construction. Repeated orbits cannot lower cost. Any realizing polynomial's splitting field contains $L^N$ by the first part. $\square$

For two positive predicates recorded jointly, the minimum common field is the compositum of their individual minimum fields, since the stabilizer of a vector is the intersection of the coordinate stabilizers. The minimum field of their conjunction or disjunction can be smaller. For example in a biquadratic group, two different quadratic-root predicates have distinct subgroup supports of size two. Their union has size three and minimum root degree four, achieved by two quadratic polynomials, since no single proper subgroup covers the union. Their intersection has singleton support and likewise requires degree four, now achieved by one irreducible quartic for the full degree-four splitting field. The two degree-four models have different extension profiles.

## 4. What this lets us reuse

The following tests can be mixed in one finite list in Theorem 1.

- **Tensor rank and graph fitting rank.** A rank-$r$ tensor decomposition is a bounded system of equalities between coordinates. A graph fitting matrix of block size $b$ and rank at most $r$ is a factorization $UV$, with identity diagonal blocks and required zero off-diagonal blocks. Each fixed cap, including any one fixed tensor power or graph block size from the growing-prime work, becomes a root test or a finite étale tensor-isomorphism test.
- **Sparse Polynomial Calculus.** Fix literal input equations over $K$, sparse expanded size $s\ge1$, and optionally a degree cap. After discarding zero lines, there are at most $s$ lines. Their degrees never exceed maximum input degree plus $s$. Enumerate the finitely many rule schedules and possible supports in those degrees with total cardinality at most $s$. In a schedule, introduce one unknown per supported coefficient and per inference scalar, imposing nonzero support coefficients by inverse equations. Equate coefficients in every required inference and require its last line to be the constant one. This is a finite union of witness systems, exactly recognizing that sparse cap. It uses a fixed presentation. Field equations whose exponents change with the probe require their own separate treatment.
- **Tensor isomorphism and congruence.** Use the ordinary invertible transporter equations, or the congruence and inverse equations. The exact same group quotient computes the modular decision implications between these and any of the resource thresholds above.

The earlier growing-prime theorem controls a moving list of possible rank or fitting-matrix witnesses by an arithmetic height bound. The fixed-test theorem here gives exact finite-field profiles and minimal polynomial representatives for every member of a fixed list. It does not assert one exceptional set for unboundedly many tests or replace the quantitative estimate needed for a growing list.

For the earlier $2\times4\times4$ quadratic-gate pencils, take the shared biquadratic field $L$ and the prescribed quadratic entry field $K(\sqrt d)$. Away from finitely many primes, isomorphism has support $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K(\sqrt d))$, while rank at most four has support $\{1\}$. To see this directly, spread the distinct labelled determinant divisors, their projectivities and their Galois action. The rational projectivity condition is the same intertwining test for $\langle\operatorname{Frob}_v^e\rangle$. The rank-four condition is splitting of the determinant divisor. The minimum local root tests thus have degrees two and four and different smallest fields, even though the two pencils have identical ranks over every extension. For the earlier everywhere-local pencil example the modular TI decision is eventually constant true, with a degree-one root test, while the rank-four test again retains its biquadratic field. The reduction therefore records the exact information shared by the modular probes and explains how global nonisomorphism can coexist with a constant positive modular profile.

The rational example can be read directly as two different simple root tests. Take the companion pencils with quartics
$$
 2303x^4+4704x^3+2926x^2+672x+47,\qquad
 -124x^4+768x^3+1532x^2+384x-31 .
$$
The isomorphism test becomes $x^2-14$, and the rank-four test becomes $x^4-18x^2+25$, the minimal polynomial of $\sqrt2+\sqrt7$. At $11$, the original homogeneous quartics are projectively equivalent and neither divisor splits. At $19$, the two tests are both negative. At $103$, both tests are positive and both quartics have four distinct rational roots. These checks can be made on the literal binary quartics: at $11$ and $103$, substitute $(x+3y,7x+4y)$ and $(x+31y,8x+85y)$, respectively, in the second form to obtain scalar multiples of the first. The negative at $19$ agrees with the two distinct Klein-four descent actions there.

The finite exclusions can carry real information. At $37$, substitution of $(x+2y,11x+32y)$ in the second homogeneous quartic gives $20$ times the first modulo $37$, though $14$ is a nonsquare there. The reference cross-ratio $9/25$ is equianharmonic modulo $37$, so the geometric projectivity group enlarges. This is a concrete instance in which the compressed generic test is valid at all good primes and a special prime has an additional witness.

The general stratification and point-counting ingredients are classical. The finite quotient, minimum-size conversion, and joint use of the same representation across our existing results are the content of this bridge. They are manuscript results, with the indicated group-action step kernel-checked separately.
