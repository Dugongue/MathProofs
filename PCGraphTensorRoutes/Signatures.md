# Constant-degree reversal of the FGS matrix-space conjugacy encoding

Let $A:W\to\operatorname{End}(V)$ and $B:W'\to\operatorname{End}(V')$ be injective linear maps, with common positive parameter dimension $m$ and common ambient dimension $s\ge2$. Over **every field**, the matrix-space conjugacy equations for these pencils and the ordinary TI equations of the following FGS construction simulate one another in constant-degree ordinary Polynomial Calculus. There is no bound on $m,s$, matrix ranks or weight multiplicities. The simulations use the given two-sided inverse witnesses and have polynomial size per substituted axiom.

Order the axes as coefficient $X$, ambient argument $Y$, dual ambient argument $Z$. There is just one dotted link, between $Y$ and $Z$. In FGS Theorem 4.1 the two linked vertices are in distinct parts, so one identity block implements it in addition to the three scalar strata and their common unit entry. The resulting partitioned tensor is
$$
H_A(x,x_0;y,y_0;z,z_0)=z^{\mathsf T}A(x)y+x_0 z^{\mathsf T}y+x_0y_0z_0 .
$$
The three axes have two parts each, of sizes $(m,1),(s,1),(s,1)$. Apply the printed Lemma 2.2 first to $Z$ with rows $X$ and columns $Y$, then to $Y$ with rows $Z$ and columns $X$, and finally to $X$ with rows $Y$ and columns $Z$. These are the frontal, lateral and horizontal removals of Corollary 3.1, with the remaining matrix axes taken in cyclic order. In each application formula (32) appends the private rows and columns to the first old row and first old column part. Coordinate permutations within a part place the old coordinates first in the formulas below. [FGS, §2.4, Corollary 3.1 and Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.09219)

### Why only arities one and two have to be distinguished

Consider one application with two slice parts. As a quiver pencil the augmentation is the direct sum of the data diagram $D$ and $r,2r$ copies of marker diagrams $N_1,N_2$. Here $r=1+\min(\text{row dimension},\text{column dimension})$. Each $N_i$ has one row at the first row vertex and $p_i$ columns at the first column vertex. Its row pencil reads the $p_i$ coordinates of its own slice part. The following sufficient condition works in arbitrary characteristic:

* On each input $D$ has only scalar endomorphisms and is visibly nonisomorphic to either marker by its dimensions at the vertices.
* The arities $p_1,p_2$ are one and a number greater than one, in the same order on both inputs, and both marker multiplicities are at least two.

Here are the details that remove any characteristic restriction from this specialization of the modular cancellation proof. The endomorphism ring of the augmented diagram has diagonal blocks $K,\operatorname{Mat}_r(K),\operatorname{Mat}_{2r}(K)$. The off-diagonal subspace is a square-zero ideal: distinct markers have zero Hom in either direction, and a nonzero composition through a nonisomorphic indecomposable and back to a single copy would be a nonzero scalar and split it off. This applies also to the brick $D$ since its endomorphisms are scalar. The fixed trace forms $(u,v)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(uv)$ at the diagram vertices have simultaneous kernel equal to this ideal plus any diagonal factors whose vertex dimensions per copy all vanish in $K$. This follows by taking traces in the direct-sum basis. Both marker factors survive, because a copy has one row. Vertexwise conjugation and its given inverse carry these kernels to each other by bounded-degree matrix identities. The induced maps on the centers of the quotients give Boolean selectors for inverse factor permutations. These statements concern kernels of fixed linear forms and their transport, and use no inference that an unknown nilpotent vanishes.

A marker factor cannot map to a visible scalar data factor on a nonzero selector $q$. Indeed on $q$ the images of $E_{12},E_{21}$ in that factor commute; multiplying the resulting equality for $E_{11}-E_{22}$ by the image of $E_{11}$ yields zero for the latter on $q$. Multiplying by the images of $E_{i1},E_{1i}$ and summing kills the entire factor identity on $q$, so $q=0$. The inverse rules out a scalar-to-marker match. Each equality on a selector means polynomial multiplication by its idempotent.

For a proposed marker-to-marker match on $q$, transport the projector onto one copy of the source marker. Its actual row trace is one. After compression to the target marker, the projector is a matrix $C$ on copy indices tensored with the identity of one marker. The other diagonal factors have zero trace on $q$ by the selector or invisibility, and off-diagonal blocks have trace zero. Hence $q\operatorname{tr}(C)=q$. Source slice directions in the kernel of the marker act as zero on the projector. Conjugate, compress and sum the diagonal copy coefficients to see that the slice map carries this kernel into the target marker kernel on $q$. The inverse gives the reverse inclusion. Fixed sections of the two quotient maps now give witnessed rectangular inverses on $q$ between the respective spaces of dimensions $p_i,p_j$. If one is one and the other exceeds one, apply a two-minor to the larger identity: its factorization through one coordinate has vanishing determinant, whereas the principal two-minor of $qI$ is $q$. This rules out the cross-match in constant degree in every characteristic. Each marker must match itself, and its multiplicity on the two sides agrees by construction. The two slice kernels now force the slice transporter and its inverse to be block diagonal.

The residual cancellation is characteristic-free. Project the transporter and inverse to the data diagrams. Any composition from a marker through a data diagram and back to a marker is zero, by the Hom and splitting argument above. Once slice blocks are preserved, their relabeling can be moved onto a marker by its explicit column change (inverse transpose in the row-pencil convention). Thus these vanishing statements are bilinear identities on fixed Hom spaces. The two inverse defects on data square to zero. For residual maps $a,b$ satisfying these identities, $b(2-ab)$ is a two-sided inverse of $a$. The reverse substitution, forward twists, and per-axiom degree bookkeeping are detailed in the cancellation proof. The argument here uses no digit or characteristic-dependent trace of a copy multiplicity. [Selection and cancellation details](Modular.md)

### The three data diagrams

Write $c_{ij}\in W^*$ for the coefficient functional of the $(i,j)$ entry of $A$. These functionals span $W^*$ by injectivity. At each stage a pair of endomorphisms $P,Q$ satisfies $P M_t=M_t Q$ for every slice matrix. The following calculations are ordinary linear equations on the fixed data, so they remain valid over every field extension and for arbitrary entries of $A$.

1. **Remove $Z$.** In the slice $z_i$ the $x_0$ row reads the $i$-th coordinate of $y$. Thus if the $x_0$ endomorphism is $a$, every row of the old $Y$-part of $Q$ is $a$ times its coordinate row. The slice $z_0$ maps $y_0$ to $x_0$, tying its scalar to $a$. In the original $X$ part the remaining equations are $P c_{ij}=a c_{ij}$. Spanning gives $P=aI$. The data therefore has scalar endomorphisms and has a nonzero vertex outside the first row and first column. The two marker arities are $s,1$.

2. **Remove $Y$.** The private rows from the first step are spaces $F_1,F_2$ incorporated into the big $X$ part. The matching private $Y$ coordinates are incorporated into its big part. When those $Y$ coordinates are used as slice directions, their matrices are **all** the units from $F_1$ to the old $s$-dimensional $Z$ part and from $F_2$ to $z_0$. Their equations force $P_Z=\operatorname{diag}(aI_s,b)$, and the $F_1,F_2$ row projections of $Q_X$ are $a,b$ times their respective coordinate projections. For an old $y_j$, the map from old $W$ to the $Z$ part is $x\mapsto A(x)e_j$ and $x_0$ maps to $e_j$. Thus $Q_{x_0}=a$, and the old $W$ projection of $Q_X$ is $a$ times the coordinate projection: apply the identities to all the spanning $c_{ij}$, including on private input coordinates. The $y_0$ slice maps $x_0$ to $z_0$ and gives $b=a$. All endomorphisms are scalar. The additional $x_0$ vertex excludes a marker summand. The slice arities are the enlarged big $Y$ part and its singleton.

3. **Remove $X$.** The second augmentation added private spaces $F_3,F_4$ in $Z$, with the corresponding private coordinates in the big $X$ part. In their $X$ slices there are all matrix units from $F_3$ to the enlarged big $Y$ part and from $F_4$ to $y_0$. Hence $P_Y=\operatorname{diag}(dI,e)$, and the $F_3,F_4$ row projections of $Q_Z$ are the prescribed scalar projections. Take any first-stage marker row basis index in $F_1$. As an $X$ slice it embeds the original big $Z$ coordinates as an identity into its private copy inside the big $Y$ part, and is zero on every other input coordinate. A row index in $F_2$ similarly embeds the old $z_0$ into that big $Y$ part. These equations fix **all** old row projections of $Q_Z$ to $d$ times their coordinate projections, including the zero coefficients from $F_3,F_4$ into the old part. Finally $x_0$ still contains the coefficient $y_0z_0$ and sends $z_0$ to $y_0$, so $e=d$. Again the data endomorphisms are scalar. Its row dimension exceeds one, proving nonisomorphism to the markers. Its slice arities are the enlarged big $X$ part and the singleton $x_0$.

All data diagrams thus meet the sufficient condition and all three augmentations can be undone in ordinary PC. The scalar triple $x_0y_0z_0$ in the recovered $H$ fixes the product of its three change-of-basis scalars to one. Divide each axis by its own scalar via its singleton inverse variable. The block $x_0 z^{\mathsf T}y$ then forces the normalized changes on $Y,Z$ to be inverse transposes. The remaining block is precisely equivalence of the parametrized matrix spaces under an arbitrary parameter change and ambient conjugation. Conversely a conjugating matrix, its inverse, and a parameter change provide the linked witness with all singleton scalars one. This establishes both simulations for the stated FGS encoding. For $s=1$ injectivity forces $m=1$ under the positive-dimension convention; the two full one-dimensional pencils are explicitly equivalent by scaling their nonzero coefficients, giving constant witness substitutions if only PC reductions are required.

For the diagonal incidence Lie pencils the three private unit columns per edge ensure the coefficient functionals span the parameter dual even when there are isolated vertices or repeated weights. Thus the **compiler comparison to linked Lie conjugacy** holds on the raw matrices for all such inputs. Recovering the graph equations from that linked Lie system has the equal-isolate or pendant-preprocessing scope specified in the incidence proof.
