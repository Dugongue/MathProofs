# Evolution algebra embeddings, nilpotence, and functorial tensor reductions

This note gives informal proofs answering Questions 10 and 15 of the [Kaygorodov–Martín González–Viruel survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22248v1) and Questions 2.3 and 4.7 of [Costoya–Fernández Ouaridi–Viruel](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32784v1). Section 5 answers two directions of the functoriality part of [Grochow–Qiao Open Question 7.5](https://epubs.siam.org/eprint/VA7M2DT6DFQMVDVWIBBN/full).

An evolution algebra has a Hamel basis \((e_i)\) for which mixed basis products vanish. Write \(e_i^2=\sum_j c_{ji}e_j\). Its support digraph has an arrow \(i\to j\) when \(c_{ji}\ne0\). All infinite-dimensional vector spaces below are algebraic, and nil refers to principal powers.

## 1. Survey Question 10. Detecting nonzero complex idempotents

**Theorem.** There is an explicit rank test in the structure matrix for the existence of a nonzero idempotent in every finite-dimensional complex evolution algebra. The decision problem is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to feasibility of finite polynomial systems over \(\mathbb C\). The equivalence also applies to rational input coefficients in the ordinary binary model, with solutions still sought over \(\mathbb C\).

Let \(C=(c_{ji})\) be the \(r\times r\) structure matrix, where \(r>0\). In \(2r\) indeterminates \(x,u\), form the equations

\[
(Cx^{[2]})_i-x_i=0\quad(1\le i\le r),\qquad \sum_i u_i x_i-1=0.
\]

The last equation can be satisfied exactly when \(x\ne0\), giving one direction of the feasibility equivalence. For the rank test, square each equation polynomial of degree two, which leaves its complex zero set unchanged. Omit zero polynomials and treat a nonzero constant equation as immediate infeasibility. The remaining polynomials \(h_i\) have degrees one or four. Let \(B=4^{2r}\). Form the matrix \(M\) whose columns are the coefficient vectors of \(z^\alpha h_i\) with \(|\alpha|+\deg h_i\le B\), indexed by all monomials in \(z=(x,u)\) of total degree at most \(B\). [Kollár's effective Nullstellensatz, Theorem 1.5 and Corollary 1.9](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Kollar/kollarnullstellen.pdf) implies that infeasibility gives \(1=\sum_i b_i h_i\) with \(\deg(b_i h_i)\le B\). Conversely, such an identity precludes a common zero. Thus a nonzero idempotent exists exactly when the column for the constant polynomial \(1\) increases the rank of \(M\). The zero-dimensional algebra has no nonzero idempotent.

For the reverse polynomial-time reduction, introduce a variable for each arithmetic gate to make the feasibility instance a system of quadratic or lower-degree equations \(f_j(y)=0\). For a sparse polynomial encoding with binary exponents, repeated squaring gives the needed circuits in polynomial size. Introduce coordinates \((t,y,s)\) with one \(s_j\) for each equation and homogenize each \(f_j\) to degree two, writing it as \(f_j^h(t,y)\). Define a commutative bilinear algebra \(A\) by polarizing the quadratic square map

\[
Q(t,y,s)=(t^2,ty,(t s_j+f_j^h(t,y))_j).
\]

An idempotent has \(t=0\) or \(t=1\). If \(t=0\), its other coordinates first satisfy \(y=0\) and then \(s=0\). If \(t=1\), the equations on \(s\) are exactly \(f_j(y)=0\), and \(s\) can be zero. To place this construction in the evolution class, let \(L\) be the linear map from the coordinate space of \(A\) whose entries are the coordinate linear forms and all pairwise sums of distinct coordinate forms. The identity \(2x_ix_j=(x_i+x_j)^2-x_i^2-x_j^2\) gives a linear map \(D\) with \(Q(x)=D(L(x)^{[2]})\). On the codomain of \(L\) use multiplication

\[
z z'=L D(z\odot z'),
\]

where \(\odot\) is coordinatewise multiplication. The standard basis is natural, \(L\) embeds \(A\) by polarization, and every product lands in \(L(A)\). Therefore every ambient idempotent lies in \(L(A)\), where the equation is precisely the one just analyzed. The output dimension is quadratic in the number of gate variables and equations, and the dense output and coefficient sizes are polynomial. For rational coefficients this follows using only fixed rational scalars and polynomially many sums in constructing the table. This use of an evolution envelope follows the finite-dimensional construction studied by [Costoya–Fernández Ouaridi–Viruel](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32784v1#S2).

The hard instances have a further property. The span of products in \(A\) contains every \(e_{s_j}\) by multiplying \(e_t\) by \(e_{s_j}\). Modulo that span, the products \(e_t e_{y_i}\) are \(e_{y_i}/2\) and \(e_t^2\) is \(e_t\). Hence \(A^2=A\). The envelope \(E\) thus satisfies \(E^2=L(A)=(E^2)^2\). Polynomial-system feasibility remains as hard on inputs whose derived series stabilizes after one squaring.

## 2. Survey Question 15. Nilness and bounded principal index

**Theorem.** Over every field and in arbitrary Hamel dimension, an evolution algebra is nil in principal powers exactly when its support digraph is acyclic. For a nonzero such algebra, bounded principal nil index is equivalent to a uniform finite bound on directed path length. If \(L\) is the maximum path length counted in edges, the common vanishing index for principal powers is exactly \(L+2\), and every product of more than \(2^L\) elements vanishes under every parenthesization. In particular, the finite-generation question has an affirmative answer even without finite generation. A finitely generated example under the bounded-index hypothesis is also finite-dimensional.

For \(x=\sum_i x_i e_i\) of support \(S\), write \(x^{[1]}=x\) and \(x^{[k+1]}=x^{[k]}x\). Expansion of the coordinate at \(i_{k-1}\) is a sum over walks \(i_0\to\cdots\to i_{k-1}\), with weight \(x_{i_0}\prod_{a=0}^{k-2}x_{i_a}c_{i_{a+1},i_a}\). A contributing walk with \(k\ge2\) has all departure vertices in \(S\). In an acyclic graph the departures are distinct, so \(x^{[|S|+2]}=0\). Conversely, take the vertices of a shortest directed cycle as \(S\) and set their coordinates to one. There are no extra arcs between cycle vertices, since an extra arc creates a shorter cycle. Each coordinate on the cycle comes from exactly one walk wholly inside it and is nonzero for every power. The graph criterion consequently holds for every chosen natural basis.

Suppose the graph is acyclic and has a path \(v_0\to\cdots\to v_L\) with \(L\ge1\). Take \(x=\sum_{a=0}^{L-1}e_{v_a}\). Any contributing walk to the \(v_L\) coefficient of \(x^{[L+1]}\) must list all \(L\) departure vertices. They must appear in the displayed order. Reversing any two contradicts acyclicity since the given path already gives a directed route from the earlier vertex to the later one. The unique term is a product of nonzero edge weights. This proves the lower bound on the common vanishing index. The upper bound follows directly by absence of walks longer than \(L\). For \(L=0\) the nonzero algebra has zero multiplication and the index is two.

Expand an arbitrarily parenthesized product of basis elements as a sum over labelings of its binary tree. At each internal node a nonzero term requires equal labels on its two children and an arrow from that label to the label of the node. Thus each leaf-to-root path of a nonzero term traces a directed walk of length at most \(L\). A binary tree of height at most \(L\) has at most \(2^L\) leaves. Multilinearity proves the same vanishing bound on arbitrary inputs. If finitely many elements generate the algebra, only finitely many words and parenthesizations in those generators of length at most \(2^L\) are needed to span it. For the finite-dimensional antecedents and the distinction between fields and coefficient rings, see [Tian–Zou](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.5523) and the references in the survey under Question 15.

## 3. Costoya–Fernández Ouaridi–Viruel Question 4.7. Higher-arity embeddings

Fix \(m\ge2\) and define a commutative \(m\)-ary algebra to have a symmetric \(m\)-linear multiplication \(\beta:V^m\to V\). In an \(m\)-ary evolution algebra a natural basis has zero product unless all \(m\) basis arguments agree.

**Theorem.** Every finite-dimensional commutative \(m\)-ary \(k\)-algebra embeds into an \(m\)-ary evolution algebra if and only if \(k\) is infinite or \(k=\mathbb F_q\) with \(q\ge m\). There is an exact criterion for an individual algebra in the remaining cases. Whenever an embedding exists it can be chosen with every ambient product in the image, so the image is an ideal.

An embedding exists exactly when there is an expansion \(\beta=\sum_{j=1}^h w_j\ell_j^{\otimes m}\), with \(w_j\in V\) and \(\ell_j\in V^*\). For necessity, pull the ambient natural-basis coordinates back to \(V\) and apply a linear retraction to the ambient product formula. The image of a finite basis of \(V\) uses only finitely many of the ambient coordinates, including when the ambient space is infinite. For sufficiency, append coordinate covectors with zero output as needed so that the evaluation map \(F:V\to k^h\) becomes injective. In an ambient natural basis set the pure product of basis vector \(j\) equal to \(F(w_j)\), and set mixed products to zero. The multiplication equation for \(F\) is then the given expansion.

With a basis of \(V\) fixed, index symmetric multilinear coefficients by multi-indices \(\alpha\) of total degree \(m\). The entries of a pure power tensor corresponding to a covector with coordinates \(a\) are \(a^\alpha\). No multinomial factors occur because these are evaluations on basis inputs. Over an infinite field the functions \(a^\alpha\) are linearly independent. Over \(\mathbb F_q\) replace every positive exponent \(d\) by \(1+((d-1)\bmod(q-1))\) and retain exponent zero. The resulting reduced monomials are linearly independent as functions when their exponent vectors are distinct. This follows by inducting on the number of variables and using the root bound on polynomials of degree less than \(q\) in each variable. Consequently the span of pure powers is exactly the subspace of coefficient arrays constant on each class of equal reduced exponent vectors. Indeed, evaluations belong to that subspace, and a linear relation among the functions indexed by the distinct classes would be necessary for the span to be smaller. This proves the criterion for individual \(\beta\) by applying it to each output coordinate. Related projective finite-field vanishing ideals are treated by [Beelen–Datta–Ghorpade](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.09139).

If \(m<q\), every exponent is already reduced. If \(m=q\), the pure degree-\(q\) powers reduce to different degree-one powers, and the other monomials retain degree \(q\). If \(m>q\), the two distinct indices \((m-q,q)\) and \((m-1,1)\) have the same reduction. A two-dimensional algebra prescribing different output vectors for these indices therefore cannot embed. This proves the sharp threshold, including fields of small characteristic with sufficiently many elements.

**Minimum dimension.** Let \(\rho\) be the fewest terms in any pure multilinear expansion and let \(Z=\{z\mid\beta(z,V,\ldots,V)=0\}\). The minimum ambient dimension, when finite, is \(\rho+\dim Z\). To see this, suppose a covector \(\ell_j\) of a minimal expansion is nonzero on some \(z\in Z\). Substituting \(x\mapsto x-z\ell_j(x)/\ell_j(z)\) in every input leaves \(\beta\) unchanged and kills the \(j\)-th term, contradiction. The common kernel of the minimal covectors is therefore exactly \(Z\). Appending \(\dim Z\) covectors that restrict to a dual basis of \(Z\) gives the upper bound. Conversely, for an expansion coming from an embedding, choose \(\dim Z\) ambient coordinate covectors whose restrictions form a basis of \(Z^*\). Project onto their common kernel along \(Z\) in all inputs. The product is unchanged, and these terms vanish, giving the matching lower bound. In the universal case one can choose a basis of \(\binom{\dim V+m-1}{m}\) pure powers containing the coordinate powers and obtain an injective evaluation map directly.

## 4. Costoya–Fernández Ouaridi–Viruel Question 2.3. Infinite-dimensional envelopes

**Theorem.** For a commutative algebra \(A\) of countable Hamel dimension over any field, the finite-dimensional ideal embedding theorem extends exactly to those \(A\) for which every left multiplication \(L_a:x\mapsto ax\) has finite rank. The evolution envelope can be chosen countable-dimensional, with all its products in the embedded copy of \(A\).

Necessity holds in every Hamel dimension. If \(a\) has finitely many nonzero natural coordinates in an evolution algebra, its left multiplication has image in the span of the squares of those basis elements. Restriction gives the same finite-rank property for every embedded subalgebra. This is the argument behind Remark 2.2 of the source paper.

For sufficiency, list a Hamel basis of \(A\) as \(u_1,u_2,\ldots\). Choose decompositions \(A=V_n\oplus K_n\) starting with \(V_0=0\), \(K_0=A\), so \(V_n\) is finite-dimensional and contains the first \(n\) basis vectors. Given the decomposition, the subspace \(H=K_n\cap\bigcap_{v\in V_n}\ker L_v\) has finite codimension in \(K_n\), because a finite basis of \(V_n\) suffices and each multiplication has finite rank. Write \(u_{n+1}=v+k\). If \(k\ne0\), shrink \(H\) by codimension at most one to a subspace \(K_{n+1}\) excluding \(k\). If \(k=0\), take \(K_{n+1}=H\). Pick a finite-dimensional complement \(W_{n+1}\) of \(K_{n+1}\) in \(K_n\) containing \(k\), and put \(V_{n+1}=V_n\oplus W_{n+1}\). Then \(V_n K_{n+1}=0\). In the basis formed by taking bases of the successive \(W_n\), each member multiplies nontrivially with only finitely many members. For members of \(W_n\), all \(W_j\) with \(j\ge n+2\) are in \(K_{n+1}\) and therefore annihilate them. The same construction works with a finite list when \(A\) is finite-dimensional.

In this basis \((a_i)\), take an ambient natural vector \(b_{ii}\) for every \(i\) and an additional \(b_{ij}=b_{ji}\) for every unordered pair of distinct indices with \(a_i a_j\ne0\). Put \(F(a_i)=b_{ii}+\sum_{j\ne i,\,a_i a_j\ne0}b_{ij}\), a finite sum. Define each present \(b_{ij}^2=F(a_i a_j)\) for \(i\ne j\), and \(b_{ii}^2=F(a_i^2)-\sum_{j\ne i,\,a_i a_j\ne0} b_{ij}^2\). The private coordinate \(b_{ii}\) makes \(F\) injective. Distinct images share precisely the coordinate for their pair if it exists, so mixed products agree. The diagonal correction gives the correct squares. All ambient products belong to \(F(A)\). This extends the pair-coordinate construction of Theorem 2.1 of the source paper, using the finite-rank hypothesis to ensure finite support.

Countability is essential to that criterion. Regard the separable real Hilbert space \(H=\ell^2(\mathbb N,\mathbb R)\) as a Hamel space and fix \(z\ne0\). Put \(xy=\langle x,y\rangle z\); each multiplication operator has rank at most one. Any embedding into an evolution algebra, followed on outputs by a linear functional taking the image of \(z\) to one, would exhibit the inner product as the restriction of a diagonal bilinear form on a finite-support coordinate direct sum. There is an uncountable linearly independent family in \(H\), for example the vectors \((1,t,t^2,\ldots)\) with \(0<t<1\), by the Vandermonde determinant. Each finite support contains only finitely many members of an independent family. Choose uncountably many with distinct supports, then thin them by the Δ-system lemma for finite sets to a family with one finite common intersection \(R\) and disjoint parts outside it. Group them into uncountably many disjoint groups of \(|R|+1\) vectors. Each group has a nonzero linear combination vanishing on the coordinates of \(R\), by solving \(|R|\) homogeneous linear equations and using independence. Distinct group combinations have disjoint supports in the putative embedding, so are orthogonal in \(H\). Separability rules out an uncountable orthogonal family of nonzero vectors. The result gives a sharp characterization in countable Hamel dimension and a finite-rank counterexample in uncountable dimension.

## 5. Grochow–Qiao Question 7.5. Two negative directions for all-homomorphism reductions

In the ordinary covariant \(d\)-tensor category a morphism \(T\to S\) is a tuple of linear maps \(f_i\) with \(S=(\bigotimes_i f_i)T\). The tensor orders here are fixed. Consider transformations of objects that reflect isomorphism and send every source morphism to some target morphism. Any functorial objectwise reduction with the standard source morphisms has those properties.

**Theorem.** From graphs with all graph homomorphisms, or from commutative unital finite-dimensional algebras over a fixed finite field with all unital homomorphisms, there is no such transformation into the ordinary covariant tensor category with polynomially bounded output dimensions. The algebra result still holds with augmentation-preserving maps and with input restricted to local algebras with cube-zero radical. The target field can be any fixed field.

Let \(U_i(T)\) be the minimal support of \(T\) in mode \(i\), equivalently the span of the contractions in the other modes. Every arrow \(T\to S\) gives \(U_i(S)\subseteq f_i(U_i(T))\) by composing linear forms with the other mode maps. Arrows in both directions force the mode-support dimensions to agree. Therefore \(f_i\) restricts to a linear isomorphism of corresponding supports. The tensor belongs to the tensor product of these supports, as is seen by completing their bases and examining the coefficients. If the ambient shapes of \(T,S\) also agree, the support isomorphisms extend to ambient isomorphisms and still transport the tensor. Thus mutually homomorphic, pairwise nonisomorphic source objects must be sent to pairwise distinct ambient shapes. With at most \(M\) dimensions per mode there are at most \((M+1)^d\) shapes.

For graphs, every bipartite graph with an edge maps to and from \(K_2\). Taking two labelled sides of size \(n\) gives at least \((2^{n^2}-1)/(2n)!\) distinct unlabelled graph isomorphism classes. This exceeds the number of polynomially bounded shapes as \(n\) grows.

For algebras, let \(U=W=\mathbb F_q^n\) and let \(\beta:\operatorname{Sym}^2 U\to W\) be surjective. Define \(A_\beta=\mathbb F_q\oplus U\oplus W\) by adjoining a unit to the algebra with \(uv=\beta(uv)\) for \(u,v\in U\) and with \(W\) annihilating \(U\oplus W\). It is associative since products of three radical elements vanish. Its radical \(J=U\oplus W\) consists of its nilpotent elements and \(J^2=W\). Augmentation followed by the unit map gives homomorphisms between any two of these objects. An isomorphism induces invertible maps on \(J/J^2\) and \(J^2\) preserving the tensor of \(\beta\). With \(N=n(n+1)/2\), fix the images of the first \(n\) basis elements of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 U\) to a basis of \(W\). There are \(q^{n(N-n)}\) independent choices for the remaining images and every one is surjective. Each orbit under the two general linear groups has at most \(q^{2n^2}\) elements. This gives at least \(q^{nN-3n^2}\) isomorphism classes from explicit inputs of size polynomial in \(n\), again exceeding the number of available shapes. In both cases the conclusion holds even when no preservation of identity or composition is required.

**Scope.** This concerns the graph and algebra directions into ordinary tensor isomorphism, with the indicated homomorphism conventions. The target may use nonconcise tensors. Polynomial-size isomorphism reductions restricted to the isomorphism groupoids can still exist. The other directions and the asymptotic-spectra part of Question 7.5 remain open here. The positive functorial constructions in [Functorial tensor reductions](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/FunctorialTensorReductions/PROOF.md) use other directions or other source morphisms.

## Verification and relation to prior work

These are informal proofs. The finite-coordinate constructions use the same pure-tensor evaluation and vanishing-mixed-products principles as the natural-basis recognition in [Evolution algebra algorithms](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IsomorphismAndGraphComplexity/EvolutionAlgebraAlgorithms.md). The graph argument uses the support graph of an arbitrary natural basis, and the obstruction uses the intrinsic minimal supports of an arbitrary tensor. The cited finite-dimensional and finite-field precedents are used where indicated. Priority for the full statements remains unverified.
