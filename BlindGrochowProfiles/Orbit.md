# When polynomial identities first distinguish locally conjugate permutation matrices

Fix a prime $p$, an integer $r\ge2$, and the standard generators of $E=(C_p)^r$. There exist two tuples of $r$ commuting integral permutation matrices with the following four properties.

1. Every proper subgroup of $E$ admits one permutation conjugator for its entire restriction.
2. The full tuples are simultaneously similar over $\mathbb Z[1/p]$, hence over every commutative ring in which $p$ is a unit.
3. Over every field of characteristic $p$, the least total degree of a scalar polynomial in the named generators which vanishes on exactly one tuple is $d=(r-1)(p-1)+1$. The ordinary Polynomial Calculus (PC) refutation degree of their simultaneous conjugacy system is exactly two.
4. Their common side $N=p\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(1+p^i)$ is smallest possible among permutation representations of this same $E$ with global nonsimilarity and local simultaneous **linear** similarity on every proper subgroup in characteristic $p$.

For $p=2$ and $r=3$, three commuting involutions of side 30 admit simultaneous permutation conjugation on each of seven planes. Scalar polynomial identities first distinguish the triples in degree three, while the conjugacy refutation has degree two. The first degree counts input generators and the second counts unknown transporter entries. The scalar identity threshold makes no assertion about the first rank difference.

## Sharp side and local conjugacy

Index the transitive $E$-sets by their stabilizers $H$. Give $E/H$ signed multiplicity
$$
a_H=\frac{p\,\mu(H,E)}{[E:H]}=(-1)^j p^{1+\binom j2-j},\qquad j=\operatorname{codim}H.
$$
All exponents are nonnegative. Take positive multiplicities for $S$ and absolute negative multiplicities for $T$. At each subgroup $U$ the difference in fixed-point counts (the mark) is $p\sum_{H\supseteq U}\mu(H,E)$, zero off $E$ and $p$ at $E$. The subspace lattice Möbius formula follows by setting $t=-1$ in the finite Gaussian binomial identity $\sum_j{r\brack j}_p p^{\binom j2}t^j=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(1+p^it)$, itself the induction obtained by counting subspaces inside or outside a hyperplane. On any proper subgroup, all restricted marks agree. Triangularity of the fixed-point table, with nonzero diagonal, recovers orbit multiplicities, hence gives the local permutation conjugator. The trivial subgroup mark gives equal sides. Adding the two sizes and evaluating the Gaussian identity at $t=1$ gives $2N$.

To prove minimality under local linear equivalence, consider the invariants of a permutation module under $U$. Their basis consists of the orbit sums. Quotient by the sum of the images of the relative traces $\mathrm{Tr}_L^U(v)=\sum_{g\in U/L}gv$ on $L$-fixed vectors, over maximal proper subgroups $L<U$. Every trace has zero coefficient at singleton orbits in characteristic $p$. Conversely every nonsingleton orbit has stabilizer inside some such $L$, and the trace of one of its $L$-orbit sums is precisely the full orbit sum. The quotient dimension is the integer fixed-point mark. Thus any pair of assertion 4 has equal proper marks and distinct full marks, since equality of all marks gives global set isomorphism. If their full difference is $m$, inversion gives signed multiplicities $m\mu(H,E)/[E:H]$. Integrality at a hyperplane forces $p\mid m$. After cancelling common orbits, each side therefore has size at least $N$.

## Global integral transport after inverting one prime

Write $R=\mathbb Z[1/p]$ and $u_H=|H|^{-1}\sum_{h\in H}h$ in $R[E]$. The coset averages identify $R[E/H]$ with the ideal $R[E]u_H$. A complete family of orthogonal idempotents consists of $u_E$ and $u_L-u_E$ for the hyperplanes $L$. On complex characters these project onto the trivial character and onto all characters with kernel exactly $L$, respectively. The identities descend by injectivity of $R[E]\to\mathbb C[E]$. Each quotient ideal contains precisely the idempotent summands corresponding to characters trivial on $H$, once each. Every cyclic subgroup is proper, so the two complex permutation characters agree elementwise. The multiplicities of the idempotent summands therefore agree. Pair the copies of each ideal to obtain the integral transporter over $R$.

## Exact first polynomial identity

Let $k$ have characteristic $p$, $A=k[E]=k[y_1,\ldots,y_r]/(y_1^p,\ldots,y_r^p)$, $y_i=g_i-1$, and $J=(y_1,\ldots,y_r)$. Exactly one side has a regular orbit, so its representation of $A$ is faithful. The other has a quotient orbit $E/L$ for every line $L$ and only orbits with nontrivial stabilizer, by the alternating signs above. Hence its annihilator is $\bigcap_L(g_L-1)$, where $g_L$ generates $L$. Each orbit with larger stabilizer is a quotient of a line orbit.

Put $n_L=(g_L-1)^{p-1}$. Extending $g_L$ to a basis of $E$, the truncated monomial basis gives $\operatorname{ann}(n_L)=(g_L-1)$. The ideal of all $n_L$ is $J^{p-1}$. Indeed their initial forms in $J^{p-1}/J^p$ are powers $(\sum v_i y_i)^{p-1}$ for the projective directions $v$. Any linear functional killing these powers gives a polynomial in $v$ of individual degrees below $p$ vanishing on $\mathbb F_p^r$ (rescaling a nonzero direction preserves vanishing). Iterated univariate interpolation gives zero coefficients, and each degree $p-1$ multinomial factor is a unit. Thus the initial forms span the full homogeneous component. If $I$ is the ideal they generate before passage to the quotient, $J^{p-1}=I+J^p$ yields $J^{p-1}=I$ by iteration using nilpotence.

The annihilator of the nonregular side is therefore $\operatorname{ann}(J^{p-1})=J^d$. For the last identity the top degree is $s=r(p-1)$, so degrees greater than $s-(p-1)$ annihilate $J^{p-1}$. If a coefficient of $y^\alpha$ of degree at most $s-(p-1)$ is nonzero, multiplication by its complementary monomial $y^{(p-1)\mathbf1-\alpha}\in J^{p-1}$ gives that coefficient at the top monomial. Translating $g_i=1+y_i$ and dropping monomials containing $y_i^p$ cannot increase degree. Thus a scalar polynomial of total degree below $d$ vanishes on both sides or on neither. Conversely every nonzero standard monomial in the $y_i$ of degree $d$ distinguishes. This reasoning extends to noncommutative scalar polynomials filtered by word length because the generators commute.

## The degree-two certificate

Use $X P_i=Q_i X$ and $YX=I,XY=I$ with independent unknown matrices $X,Y$. Telescoping with constant matrix coefficients derives $X P_g=Q_g X$ for every $g\in E$, entirely in degree one. Summing yields $XW_P=W_QX$, where $W_P=\sum_{g\in E}P_g$. On an orbit $E/H$, the norm matrix is $|H|$ times the all-ones matrix. In characteristic $p$ it is zero on every nonregular orbit and has unit entries on every regular orbit. Exactly one of $W_P,W_Q$ is zero. If $W_Q=0$, multiply $XW_P=0$ by $Y$ on the left in degree two. Subtract $(YX-I)W_P=0$ to obtain the false constant equality $W_P=0$ entrywise. The other orientation uses right multiplication by $Y$ and the other inverse axiom. Degree one cannot refute, since the forward equations have a zero solution and the inverse axioms have degree two. Even the expanded norm has at most $N$ summands, because $p^r\le N$.
