# Consequences of the Riemann hypotheses

**Status on November 20, 2026.** Levent Alpöge announced a Lean proof developed with Claude, claiming both classical RH and GRH for Dirichlet $L$-functions. Andrew Sutherland reportedly built and exported the proof. The independent verification record still lists the full comparison as in progress, and Clay lists RH as unsolved. A valid proof discharges the corresponding hypothesis in every previously proved implication. The numbered entries document 105 classical RH consequences and 15 Dirichlet GRH consequences from the literature. [Alpöge’s announcement](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf), [Terence Tao’s report](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/unforced-euler-singularity-unforced-navier-stokes-singularity-and-the-generalized-riemann-hypothesis/), [independent verification record](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/main/verification/NOTES.md#results), [Clay’s problem status](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/riemann-hypothesis/).

**Classical RH** places all nontrivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ on $\Re s=1/2$. **Dirichlet GRH** extends the critical-line assertion to Dirichlet $L(s,\chi)$. Sections one through four use classical RH as the sole unresolved hypothesis. The fifth uses Dirichlet GRH. Recent results sourced from preprints are marked accordingly. Logarithms are natural.

## Classical RH and the distribution of primes

- **P01 · Prime-counting error.** For all $x\ge2657$, $|\pi(x)-\operatorname{li}(x)|<\sqrt{x}\log x/(8\pi)$. [Schoenfeld, Corollary 1](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1976-30-134/S0025-5718-1976-0457374-X/S0025-5718-1976-0457374-X.pdf).
- **P02 · Weighted count of prime powers.** For $x\ge73.2$, $|\psi(x)-x|<\sqrt{x}\log^2 x/(8\pi)$, with $\psi(x)=\sum_{p^k\le x}\log p$. [Schoenfeld, Theorem 10](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1976-30-134/S0025-5718-1976-0457374-X/S0025-5718-1976-0457374-X.pdf).
- **P03 · Weighted count of primes.** For $x\ge599$, $|\vartheta(x)-x|<\sqrt{x}\log^2x/(8\pi)$, with $\vartheta(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\log p$. [Schoenfeld, Theorem 10](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1976-30-134/S0025-5718-1976-0457374-X/S0025-5718-1976-0457374-X.pdf).
- **P04 · Sharper Chebyshev bounds.** The numerator in P02 and P03 may be reduced to $\sqrt{x}\log x(\log x-\log\log x)$ for $\psi$ from $x=101$ and $\vartheta$ from $x=2657$. [Lee and Nosal, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05628).
- **P05 · Inverting the prime count.** For every $n\ge11$, $|p_n-\operatorname{li}^{-1}(n)|<\sqrt n(\log n)^{5/2}/\pi$ for the $n$th prime $p_n$. This is equivalent to RH. [Arias de Reyna and Toulisse, Theorem 6.1](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.847.pdf).
- **P06 · Threshold for the four-term Cipolla lower bound.** The least threshold after which the four-term expression in the paper is always a strict lower bound for $p_n$ lies in $(3.9\times10^{30},3.958\times10^{30}]$. [Arias de Reyna and Toulisse, Theorem 6.2](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.847.pdf).
- **P07 · Comparing the weighted and unweighted prime counts.** $\operatorname{li}(\vartheta(x))>\pi(x)$ for every real $x\ge11$. This is equivalent to RH. [Nicolas](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-68376-8_32).
- **P08 · A prime in every explicit window.** For every $x\ge4$, $[x,x+0.88\sqrt{x}\log x]$ contains a prime. [Carneiro, Milinovich and Soundararajan, Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.04122).
- **P09 · Maximum asymptotic prime gap.** $\limsup_{n\to\infty}(p_{n+1}-p_n)/(\sqrt{p_n}\log p_n)<0.8358$. [Chirre, Pereira Júnior and de Laat, §1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.02393#S1.SS3).
- **P10 · Many primes in windows on the gap scale.** For $\alpha\ge0$ and $c=0.8358(1+2\alpha)$, the lower limit of $[\pi(x+c\sqrt{x}\log x)-\pi(x)]/\sqrt{x}$ exceeds $\alpha$. [Chirre, Pereira Júnior and de Laat, §1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.02393#S1.SS3).
- **P11 · Prime number theorem in every short interval.** $\psi(x+h)-\psi(x)\sim h$ if $h/(\sqrt x\log x)\to\infty$ and $h\le x$. For $\sqrt{x}\log x\le h\le x^{3/4}$ and $x\ge e^{40}$, the explicit Cully-Hugill–Dudek estimate bounds the absolute error by $\sqrt{x}\log x[\pi^{-1}\log(h/(\sqrt{x}\log x))+13/6]$. [Cully-Hugill and Dudek’s bound and Lee’s refinement, Theorems 3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.13457#S3).
- **P12 · Almost all polylogarithmic windows.** If $H/\log^2 X\to\infty$ and $H=o(X)$, the prime count in $(y,y+H]$ is asymptotic to $H/\log X$ for all but $o(X)$ measure of the starting points $y\in[X,2X]$. [Selberg’s result as stated by Matomäki and Merikoski](https://academic.oup.com/imrn/article/2023/23/20337/7111993).
- **P13 · An explicit bound for half the starting points.** For every $x\ge2$, at least half the starting points $y\in[x,2x]$ by measure have a prime in $(y,y+32277\log^2y]$. [Cully-Hugill and Dudek, Corollary 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.00433).
- **P14 · Short windows containing Goldbach numbers.** Every $(x,x+123\log^2x]$ with real $x\ge2$ contains a sum of two odd primes. [Hagen, Theorem 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40993-026-00767-6).
- **P15 · Primes between consecutive cubes.** There is a prime strictly between $n^3$ and $(n+1)^3$ for every positive integer $n$. The sufficiently-large-$n$ result has an unconditional proof. [Caldwell and Cheng](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.4883), [Johnston, Sorenson, Thomas and Webster on the unconditional case](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15564).
- **P16 · Digits of the smallest Mills constant.** The least $A>1$ for which $\lfloor A^{3^k}\rfloor$ is prime for all $k\ge1$ begins $1.3063778838\ldots$, with the prime sequence beginning $2,11,1361$. [Caldwell and Cheng](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.4883).
- **P17 · Transcendence of the Mills constant.** The same least $A$ is transcendental. The proof uses the density hypothesis, which follows from RH. [Saito, Theorem 1.8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11139-026-01443-0), [full theorem text](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16068#Thmtheorem1.8).
- **P18 · Prime gaps as graph degrees.** For every $n\ge2$, the first $n$ entries of $(1,1,2,2,4,2,\ldots)$, formed by prepending $1$ to the consecutive prime gaps, form the degree sequence of a simple graph. [Erdős, Harcos, Kharel, Maga, Mezei and Toroczkai, Theorem 1.6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00208-023-02574-1).
- **P19 · Extending every graph with prime-gap degrees.** Every such $n$-vertex realization contains a matching of size $(p_{n+1}-p_n)/2$. Splitting those edges through a new vertex gives the next prefix while preserving all old degrees. [Erdős et al., Theorem 1.6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00208-023-02574-1), [explicit unconditional large-$n$ thresholds](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10476-026-00175-z).
- **P20 · Total length of large prime gaps.** For $x\ge2$ and $N>0$, the sum of gaps $p_{n+1}-p_n\ge N$ with $p_n\in[x,2x]$ is less than $163x\log^2x/N$. [Erdős et al., Theorem 1.8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00208-023-02574-1).
- **P21 · Mean Goldbach representation counts.** For fixed $k\ge2$, let $S_k(X)=\sum_{n_1+\cdots+n_k\le X}\prod_i\Lambda(n_i)$, with $\Lambda$ the von Mangoldt function. Then $S_k(X)=X^k/k!-k\sum_\rho X^{\rho+k-1}/[\rho(\rho+1)\cdots(\rho+k-1)]+O_k(X^{k-1}\log^3 X)$. The weaker $S_k(X)=X^k/k!+O_{k,\varepsilon}(X^{k-1/2+\varepsilon})$ is equivalent to RH for each fixed $k\ge2$. [Nguyen, Theorems 1.2 and 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13368).
- **P22 · Smoothed binary Goldbach counts.** With $G_2(n)=\sum_{a+b=n}\Lambda(a)\Lambda(b)$, the weighted sum $\sum_{n\le N}(1-n/N)G_2(n)$ equals $N^2/6-2\sum_\rho N^{\rho+1}/[\rho(\rho+1)(\rho+2)]+O(N)$. [Goldston and Yang, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1601.06902).
- **P23 · A prime plus a square in short intervals.** With $H=N^{1/4+\eta}$ and fixed $0<\eta<3/4$, the count of $p+m^2\in(N,N+H]$ weighted by $\log p$ is asymptotic to $H\sqrt N$. The analogous count for $p+q^2$ with primes $p,q$ and weight $\log p\log q$ has the same asymptotic. [Languasco and Zaccagnini, Theorems 1 and 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02271).
- **P24 · Smooth integers in every square-root-length window.** For every $\varepsilon>0$, some $C(\varepsilon)$ ensures that each sufficiently large $[x,x+C(\varepsilon)\sqrt x]$ contains an integer all of whose prime factors are below $x^\varepsilon$. [Soundararajan](https://arxiv.org/html/1009.1591).
- **P25 · Polylogarithmic prime-factor cutoffs.** With $\Psi(x,y)$ counting the integers up to $x$ with prime factors at most $y$, $u=\log x/\log y$ and $\rho$ the Dickman function, $\Psi(x,y)=x\rho(u)\exp(O_\varepsilon(\log(u+1)/\log y))$ uniformly for $1\le u\le y^{1/2-\varepsilon}$. [Hildebrand](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300012481), [precise formulation in Hildebrand–Tenenbaum](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.101.pdf).
- **P26 · Corrected smooth-number asymptotic.** For fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and $(\log x)^{2+\varepsilon}\le y\le x$, $\Psi(x,y)\sim x\rho(u)K(-\log\log x/\log y)$, where $K(t)=t\zeta(1+t)/(1+t)$ and $K(0)=1$. [Gorodetsky, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.08973).
- **P27 · Smooth-number counts in short intervals.** For each fixed $1/2<\theta<1$, a saddle-point relative asymptotic for $\Psi(x+h,y)-\Psi(x,y)$ holds uniformly for $x^\theta\le h\le x$ and $(\log x)^{K(\theta)}\le y\le2x$, with the main term $h\alpha(x,y)\Psi(x,y)/x$ and the error in the theorem. **Preprint.** [Younis, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05761#S1).
- **P28 · Explicit Mertens bounds.** The errors in $\sum_{p\le x}(\log p)/p=\log x+E+\text{error}$ and $\sum_{p\le x}1/p=\log\log x+B+\text{error}$ are bounded respectively by $3\log^2x/(8\pi\sqrt{x})$ for $x\ge43.1$ and $3\log x/(8\pi\sqrt{x})$ for $x\ge24.4$. [Lee and Nosal, Corollary 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05628).
- **P29 · Mertens’s product changes sides.** The normalized error $\sqrt x\log x[\log\prod_{p\le x}(1-1/p)^{-1}-\log\log x-\gamma]$ has a logarithmic limiting distribution. The set where the error is positive has positive lower logarithmic density and upper logarithmic density strictly below one. [Lamzouri, Theorem 1.1 and §4](https://arxiv.org/html/1410.3777).

## Classical RH and arithmetic functions

- **A01 · Robin’s divisor-sum inequality.** $\sigma(n)<e^\gamma n\log\log n$ for every integer $n>5040$, where $\sigma$ sums the positive divisors and $\gamma$ is Euler’s constant. This is equivalent to RH. [Robin (1984)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(84)90065-6), [Lagarias](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0008177).
- **A02 · Lagarias’s harmonic-number inequality.** For $H_n=\sum_{j=1}^n1/j$, $\sigma(n)\le H_n+e^{H_n}\log H_n$ for all $n\ge1$, with equality only at $n=1$. This is equivalent to RH. [Lagarias](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0008177).
- **A03 · Nicolas’s primorial inequality.** With $N_k=\prod_{j\le k}p_j$, $N_k/\varphi(N_k)>e^\gamma\log\log N_k$ for all $k\ge2$. This is equivalent to RH. [Nicolas (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(83)90055-0).
- **A04 · Refined totient extremum.** Put $c(n)=(n/\varphi(n)-e^\gamma\log\log n)\sqrt{\log n}$ and $\beta=2+\gamma-\log(4\pi)$. Then $\limsup c(n)=e^\gamma(2+\beta)$, $c(n)\le c(N_{66})$ for $n>2$, and $c(N_k)>c(2)$ for $k>1$. [Nicolas, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.0729).
- **A05 · Dedekind psi at primorials.** For $\Psi(n)=n\prod_{p\mid n}(1+1/p)$, $\Psi(N_k)/(N_k\log\log N_k)>e^\gamma/\zeta(2)$ for every $k\ge3$. [Solé and Planat](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.1825).
- **A06 · An upper envelope for the number of divisors.** Put $b=\log(3/2)/\log2$. For every positive integer $n$ outside $\{1,2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10,12,18,24\}$, $\log d(n)/\log2\le\operatorname{li}(\log n)+b\operatorname{li}((\log n)^b)$. [Nicolas, Corollary 1.2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-021-00392-0).
- **A07 · Square-root cancellation in the Möbius sum.** For every $\varepsilon>0$, $M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)=O_\varepsilon(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})$. This is equivalent to RH. [Littlewood’s criterion and proof context in Balazard and de Roton](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.1689).
- **A08 · A sharper Mertens bound.** $M(x)\ll\sqrt x\exp(C\sqrt{\log x/\log\log x})$ for some constant $C$, improving the earlier RH bound with a factor $(\log\log x)^{5/2+\varepsilon}$ multiplying $\sqrt{\log x}$ in the exponent. **Preprint for the sharper bound.** [Bui, Florea and Heycock](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27591), [Balazard and de Roton](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.1689).
- **A09 · Cancellation in the parity of prime factors.** If $\lambda(n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)}$, then $\sum_{n\le x}\lambda(n)=O_\varepsilon(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})$. One obtains it from A07 by summing $M(x/d^2)$ over $d\le\sqrt x$. [Björner, §5](https://people.kth.se/~bjorner/files/betti7.1.pdf).
- **A10 · Weighted Liouville sums.** For fixed $0\le\alpha\le1/2$, $\sum_{n\le x}\lambda(n)n^{-\alpha}=O_\varepsilon(x^{1/2-\alpha+\varepsilon})$. For $1/2<\alpha\le1$ it equals $\zeta(2\alpha)/\zeta(\alpha)+O_\varepsilon(x^{1/2-\alpha+\varepsilon})$, with the quotient interpreted as zero at $\alpha=1$. It is eventually negative for each fixed $1/2<\alpha<1$. [Mossinghoff and Trudgian, Theorems 2.1–2.2](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788712000201).
- **A11 · Counting $k$-free integers.** For fixed $k\ge2$, the count is $x/\zeta(k)+O_{k,\varepsilon}(x^{1/(k+1)+\varepsilon})$. An integer is $k$-free when no prime’s $k$th power divides it. [Montgomery–Vaughan as cited and compared with later papers by Mossinghoff, Oliveira e Silva and Trudgian](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.04972).
- **A12 · Squarefree counting error.** For $k=2$, the remainder improves to $O_\varepsilon(x^{11/35+\varepsilon})$. [Liu](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2015.07.013).
- **A13 · Higher power-free counts.** For $k=3,4,5,6$ the respective remainder exponents $17/74,17/94,23/154,4/31$ hold with an added $\varepsilon$. For $k\ge7$ one can use $1/(k+18/11)+\varepsilon$. [Original references and exponents in Mossinghoff, Oliveira e Silva and Trudgian, pp. 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.04972).
- **A14 · Squarefree variance bound.** Let $V(X,H)$ be the mean over $x\in[X,2X]$ of the squared error in counting squarefree integers in $(x,x+H]$ against $6H/\pi^2$. For every $\varepsilon,\delta>0$, $V(X,H)\ll_{\varepsilon,\delta}H^{1/2+\delta}$ uniformly for $1\le H\le X^{1-\varepsilon}$. The family of bounds is equivalent to RH. [Gorodetsky, Matomäki, Radziwiłł and Rodgers, Theorem 3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-021-00557-5).
- **A15 · Squarefree variance asymptotic.** In the range $1\le H\le X^{2/3-\varepsilon}$, $V(X,H)=C\sqrt H+O_\varepsilon(H^{1/2-\varepsilon/16})$, with the explicit $C$ in the paper. This range follows already from classical Lindelöf. [Gorodetsky et al., Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-021-00557-5).
- **A16 · Counting powerful numbers.** If every prime in an integer’s factorization occurs to exponent at least two, count it as powerful. The count up to $x$ is $\zeta(3/2)x^{1/2}/\zeta(3)+\zeta(2/3)x^{1/3}/\zeta(2)+O_\varepsilon(x^{121/860+\varepsilon})$. [Liu](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022314X15002383).
- **A17 · Largest permutation order.** For $g(n)=\max_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{ord}(\sigma)$, $\log g(n)<\sqrt{\operatorname{li}^{-1}(n)}$ for every $n\ge1$. This is equivalent to RH. [Deléglise and Nicolas](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.07664).
- **A18 · Products of distinct primes with bounded sum.** If $h(n)$ is the largest product of distinct primes whose sum is at most $n$, then $\log h(n)<\sqrt{\operatorname{li}^{-1}(n)}$ for all $n\ge1$. [Deléglise and Nicolas](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788718000083).
- **A19 · Determinants of divisibility matrices.** Put $R_n(i,j)=1$ if $j=1$ or $i\mid j$, and $0$ otherwise. The Redheffer identity $\det R_n=M(n)$ gives $|\det R_n|=O_\varepsilon(n^{1/2+\varepsilon})$. [Redheffer identity and proof context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09489).
- **A20 · Euler characteristics of prime-factor complexes.** The reduced Euler characteristic of the simplicial complex of finite prime sets with product at most $n$ is $-M(n)$. A related CW complex has reduced Euler characteristic $-\sum_{j\le n}\lambda(j)$. Both have absolute value $O_\varepsilon(n^{1/2+\varepsilon})$. [Björner, equations (1.1) and (5.2)](https://people.kth.se/~bjorner/files/betti7.1.pdf).
- **A21 · Prime factors of the central binomial coefficient.** The logarithm of the part of $\binom{2n}{n}$ contributed by primes $p\le n$, with multiplicities, is $(2\log2-1)n+O(\sqrt n\log^2n)$. [Lagarias and Yangjit, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.15439#S5).
- **A22 · Prime factors across an entire Pascal row.** For the product $\prod_{j=0}^n\binom nj$, the logarithm of the part contributed by primes $p\le\alpha n$ has explicit main term $f(\alpha)n^2$ and error $O(\alpha^{-1}n^{7/4}\log^2n)$, uniformly for $0<\alpha\le1$. [Lagarias and Yangjit, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1142/S1793042122500373).
- **A23 · Extremal divisor sums under division and multiplication.** The only composite $N$ for which $G(N)=\sigma(N)/(N\log\log N)$ is at least its values at every $N/p$ for prime $p\mid N$ and at every multiple of $N$ is $4$. Exactly 19 integers $N>1$ satisfy the multiples-only condition. [Caveney, Nicolas and Sondow, Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/html/1110.5078), [the companion classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.6010).
- **A24 · Smoothed summation of $n/\varphi(n)$.** For $k\ge2$, its $k$th Riesz mean $(1/k!)\sum_{n\le x}(n/\varphi(n))(1-n/x)^k$ has explicit linear, logarithmic, and constant terms with remainder $O_\varepsilon(x^{-3/4+\varepsilon}/k)$. [Sankaranarayanan and Singh, Theorem 2](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/82550).

## Classical RH and geometry, dynamics, and computation

- **G01 · Farey fractions in quadratic mean.** For the ordered Farey fractions $a_j\in(0,1]$ with denominator at most $N$, put $d_j=a_j-j/m$, with $m$ their count. Then $\sum_{j=1}^m d_j^2=O_\varepsilon(N^{-1+\varepsilon})$. [Franel (1924)](https://eudml.org/doc/59156), [original-paper translations, §5.3](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/farey.pdf).
- **G02 · Farey fractions in absolute mean.** The same displacements satisfy $\sum_{j=1}^m|d_j|=O_\varepsilon(N^{1/2+\varepsilon})$. [Landau, translated in §5.4](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/farey.pdf).
- **G03 · Product of Farey fractions.** For $F_N=\prod_j a_j$, $\log(1/F_N)=\sum_{n\le N}\varphi(n)-\psi(N)/2+O_\varepsilon(N^{1/2+\varepsilon})$. This is equivalent to RH. [Mikolás](https://acta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/13684/1/math_014_005-021.pdf), [Lagarias and Mehta, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1503.00199#S3).
- **G04 · Primitive lattice points in ellipses.** For an ellipse defined by a positive-definite rational quadratic form, the coprime lattice-point count in its $\sqrt{x}$ dilation has main term $6/\pi^2$ times the dilated area and error $O_\varepsilon(x^{221/608+\varepsilon})$. [Zhai, Theorem 3](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa109-1-1).
- **G05 · Primitive lattice points in other curved regions.** For Baker’s planar domains satisfying H1–H4, the corresponding error in the $\sqrt{x}$ dilation is $O_\varepsilon(x^{5/13+\varepsilon})$. [Baker, Theorem 1](https://uamte.math.byu.edu/~baker/PrimLatticePoints.pdf).
- **G06 · Counting primitive right triangles by area.** The count of primitive integer right triangles of area below $N$ has a two-term expansion $cN^{1/2}-c'N^{1/3}+O(N^{127/616}(\log N)^{963/308})$, with explicit constants. [Zhai](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/83882).
- **G07 · Weighted prime lattice points in convex ovals.** For the smooth symmetric convex ovals $\Omega$ with nowhere vanishing curvature in the paper, the von Mangoldt weighted count in $R\Omega$ is $\operatorname{area}(\Omega)R^2+O_\Omega(R^{3/2})$. [Huang and Rudnick, §1.2](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~rudnick/papers/Huang_et_al-2018-Monatshefte_f-r_Mathematik.pdf).
- **G08 · Distribution of the oval-counting error.** The centered error in G07 divided by $R^{3/2}$ has a compactly supported limiting distribution when averaged in $\log R$. [Huang and Rudnick, §4](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~rudnick/papers/Huang_et_al-2018-Monatshefte_f-r_Mathematik.pdf).
- **G09 · Effective equidistribution of closed horocycles.** The average of a smooth compactly supported test function along the closed horocycle at height $y$ on the modular surface approaches its normalized area mean with error $O_{F,\varepsilon}(y^{3/4-\varepsilon})$. [Zagier, p. 279](https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/zagier/files/scanned/EisensteinRiemannZeta/fulltext.pdf).
- **G10 · Inverse spectral theorem for fractal strings.** For every inner Minkowski dimension $D\in(0,1)\setminus\{1/2\}$, a bounded fractal string with a frequency counting function of the form $N_\nu(x)=W(x)-c x^D+o(x^D)$ with $c>0$ and Weyl term $W(x)$ has Minkowski-measurable boundary. Solvability in every such dimension is equivalent to RH. [Lapidus and Maier](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/52.1.15).
- **G11 · Invertibility of a spectral operator.** For the weighted-space operator $\mathfrak a_c=\zeta(\partial_c)$, all frequency truncations are invertible for each $0<c<1$ with $c\ne1/2$. The full operator has a bounded inverse for every $0<c<1/2$. [Herichi and Lapidus](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.0882), [Lapidus, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.05362).
- **G12 · Density in a function space.** The functions $f_\theta(x)=\{\theta/x\}-\theta\{1/x\}$, $0<\theta<1$, span a dense subspace of $L^2(0,1)$. In the strengthened formulation, the indicator of $(0,1)$ can be approximated in $L^2(0,\infty)$ by linear combinations of $x\mapsto\{1/(kx)\}$ with integer $k\ge1$. [Nyman–Beurling and Báez-Duarte](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0202141).
- **G13 · Dirichlet polynomial approximations.** The infimum of the squared error for approximating $1$ by $\zeta(1/2+it)A(1/2+it)$ in $L^2(dt/(1/4+t^2))$, with $A$ a Dirichlet polynomial of length at most $N$, tends to zero as $N\to\infty$. [Bettin, Conrey and Farmer, §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5191).
- **G14 · A concrete machine runs forever.** Matiyasevich’s explicitly presented register machine with 29 registers and 130 instructions never halts. The assertion is equivalent to RH. [Matiyasevich, published corrected version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2019.07.028).
- **G15 · An explicit Diophantine system has no solution.** The specific system constructed from Matiyasevich’s reformulation is unsolvable over the integer domain specified in the paper. [Gaifullin et al., On a Theorem of Matiyasevich](https://www.pleiades.online/abstract/matnt/20/matnt10344_abstract.pdf).
- **G16 · Counting elliptic curves of fixed $j$-invariant.** For rational isomorphism classes of height at most $X$, the remainders after the main terms for $j=0$ and $1728$ are respectively $O_\varepsilon(X^{2/31+\varepsilon})$ and $O_\varepsilon(X^{17/282+\varepsilon})$. For a fixed other rational $j$, the remainder is $O_{j,\varepsilon}(X^{11/210+\varepsilon})$. **Preprint.** [Barquero-Sánchez and Mora-Mora, Tables 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18874).

## Classical RH and the zeta function

Here $\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$ is the completed entire function and $\Xi(t)=\xi(1/2+it)$. Write $\rho=1/2+i\gamma$ for a nontrivial zero and $N(T)$ for their count up to height $T$, with multiplicity.

- **Z01 · The Lindelöf hypothesis for $\zeta$.** For each $\varepsilon>0$, $\zeta(1/2+it)\ll_\varepsilon t^\varepsilon$. A quantitative bound is $\log|\zeta(1/2+it)|\le(\tfrac12\log2+o(1))\log t/\log\log t$. [Chandee and Soundararajan, Theorem 1.1](https://www.math.ntnu.no/emner/MA3001/2020v/2021v/Boundingzeta.pdf).
- **Z02 · Error in counting zeta zeros.** In the Riemann–von Mangoldt zero-counting formula, $S(t)=\arg\zeta(1/2+it)/\pi$ satisfies $|S(t)|\le(1/4+o(1))\log t/\log\log t$. [Carneiro, Chandee and Milinovich, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.1526).
- **Z03 · The integrated zero-counting error.** For $S_1(t)=\int_0^t S(u)\,du$, the upper and lower bounds are respectively $(\pi/48+o(1))\log t/(\log\log t)^2$ and $-(\pi/24+o(1))\log t/(\log\log t)^2$. [Carneiro, Chandee and Milinovich, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.1526).
- **Z04 · Higher antiderivatives of the argument.** For each fixed $n\ge1$, the canonically normalized $n$th antiderivative $S_n(t)$ is $O_n(\log t/(\log\log t)^{n+1})$, with explicit asymmetric constants in the paper. [Carneiro, Chirre and Milinovich](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04099).
- **Z05 · Maximum multiplicity.** A zeta zero at height $\gamma$ has multiplicity at most $(1/2+o(1))\log\gamma/\log\log\gamma$. [Goldston and Gonek, Corollary 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0511092).
- **Z06 · Maximum absolute gap between ordinates.** For every consecutive pair at large heights $\gamma\le\gamma'$, $\gamma'-\gamma\le(\pi+o(1))/\log\log\gamma$. [Goldston and Gonek, Corollary 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0511092).
- **Z07 · A lower bound on the proportion of simple zeros.** At least $19/27$, about $70.37\%$, are simple in the asymptotic lower-limit sense. [Bui and Heath-Brown](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.5018).
- **Z08 · Distinct zeros and the second moment of multiplicity.** At least $84.77\%$ of zeros counted with multiplicity are distinct, and the sum of squared multiplicities over distinct zeros up to $T$ is at most $(1.3208+o(1))N(T)$. [Chirre, Gonçalves and de Laat](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08843).
- **Z09 · Many gaps less than half the mean plus $\varepsilon$.** For every $\varepsilon>0$, at least $c_\varepsilon T\log T$ consecutive pairs with both ordinates in $[T,2T]$ have gap less than $(1/2+\varepsilon)2\pi/\log\gamma$ for sufficiently large $T$, with $\gamma$ the lower ordinate and multiplicities counted. **Preprint.** [Durkan and Pearce-Crump, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28527v1).
- **Z10 · Gaps wider than 3.63846723 local mean spacings.** The upper limit of $(\gamma'-\gamma)\log\gamma/(2\pi)$ for consecutive ordinates exceeds $3.63846723$. **Preprint.** [Durkan and Pearce-Crump, Theorem 3 with modulus 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28527v1).
- **Z11 · Gaps spanning a fixed number of zeros.** For each fixed integer $r\ge1$, the normalized $r$-gap $(\gamma_{n+r}-\gamma_n)\log\gamma_n/(2\pi)$ exceeds $r+0.574271\sqrt r$ infinitely often and falls below $r-0.299856\sqrt r$ infinitely often. [Conrey and Turnage-Butterbaugh](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.00030).
- **Z12 · Sharp orders for all fixed positive moments.** For $k>0$, $\int_0^T|\zeta(1/2+it)|^{2k}dt\asymp_k T(\log T)^{k^2}$. The matching lower bound is unconditional, as is the order for $0<k\le2$. [Harper’s upper bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.4618), [Heap and Soundararajan’s lower bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13154).
- **Z13 · Sharp orders for shifted joint moments.** For fixed nonnegative $\beta_j$ and real shifts $|\alpha_j|\le T/2$, $\int_T^{2T}\prod_j|\zeta(1/2+i(t+\alpha_j))|^{2\beta_j}dt$ has order $T(\log T)^{\sum_j\beta_j^2}\prod_{j<k}|\zeta(1+1/\log T+i(\alpha_j-\alpha_k))|^{2\beta_j\beta_k}$. [Curran’s upper bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10123), [matching lower bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08725).
- **Z14 · Positive moments of derivatives at zeta zeros.** For real $k>0$ and integer $m\ge1$, $\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}|\zeta^{(m)}(\rho)|^{2k}\ll_{k,m} T(\log T)^{k^2+2km+1}$. For positive integer $k$, a matching lower bound holds. [Kirila, Corollary 1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08826), [Benli, Elma and Ng, Corollary 1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13554).
- **Z15 · Sharp constant for the second moment at zeros.** $\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^2\sim T(\log T)^4/(24\pi)$. [Gonek](https://people.math.rochester.edu/faculty/gonek/papers/5-MVZDrvs.pdf).
- **Z16 · Positive moments just off the zeros.** For $k>0$, $N(T)^{-1}\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}|\zeta(\rho+\alpha)|^{2k}\ll_k(\log T)^{k^2}$ uniformly for complex $|\alpha|\le1/\log T$. [Kirila, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08826).
- **Z17 · Tails for unusually crowded zero windows.** For $t$ uniformly distributed in $[T,2T]$, the probability of at least $x$ zero ordinates in $[t,t+1/\log T]$ is $O(e^{-c x\log x})$ for $x\ge2$ and an absolute $c>0$. [Rodgers, Corollary 1.2](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43439).
- **Z18 · Moments of microscopic zeta ratios.** For fixed complex $\alpha,\beta$ with $\Re\beta\ne0$ and every fixed real $m\ge0$, the mean of $|\zeta(1/2+it+\alpha/\log T)/\zeta(1/2+it+\beta/\log T)|^m$ over $t\in[T,2T]$ remains bounded as $T\to\infty$. [Rodgers, Theorem 1.4](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43439).
- **Z19 · Negative moments near the critical line.** For fixed $k>0$ and shifts $\alpha$ in the range of Theorem 1.4 tending to zero, the mean of $|\zeta(1/2+\alpha+it)|^{-2k}$ over $[T,2T]$ is asymptotic to $\sum_n\mu_k(n)^2/n^{1+2\alpha}$, with $\mu_k$ the Dirichlet coefficients of $\zeta^{-k}$. In particular the theorem reaches polylogarithmically small positive shifts. [Bui and Florea, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.07226).
- **Z20 · Universality in every sufficiently high short interval.** For a disk of radius $r$ centered at $s_0$ lying within $1/2<\Re s<1$, zeta translates uniformly approximate every target continuous on the disk, holomorphic in its interior and nonvanishing on the disk. A positive lower proportion of shifts in $[T,T+(\log T)^B]$ suffice if $B>2/(2(\Re s_0-r)-1)$. [Lee and Pańkowski, Theorem 1.1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11139-025-01144-0).
- **Z21 · Typical zeta maxima in long logarithmic windows.** For $\theta>3$ and $t$ uniform in $[T,2T]$, $\max_{|h|\le(\log T)^\theta}|\zeta(1/2+i(t+h))|=(\log T)^{\sqrt{1+\theta}+o_{\mathbb P}(1)}$. The corresponding result for $0\le\theta\le3$ is unconditional. [Arguin, Ouimet and Radziwiłł, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1901.04061).
- **Z22 · Speiser’s criterion.** $\zeta'(s)$ has no zeros in the open strip $0<\Re s<1/2$. This is equivalent to RH. [Speiser (1934)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01448042).
- **Z23 · All real derivatives of $\Xi$ have real zeros.** More precisely, every complex zero of every derivative of $\Xi$ is real. Under RH the function lies in the Laguerre–Pólya class, the locally uniform closure of real-rooted polynomials, and the class is closed under differentiation. [Gröchenig](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.12889).
- **Z24 · Real roots for all Jensen polynomials.** Write $\xi(1/2+z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\gamma(n)z^{2n}/n!$. For every degree $d$ and shift $n$, $\sum_{j=0}^d\binom dj\gamma(n+j)X^j$ has only real roots. For each fixed degree, eventual real-rootedness in the shift is already unconditional. [Pólya’s criterion and the unconditional theorem of Griffin, Ono, Rolen and Zagier](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/116/23/11103.full.pdf).
- **Z25 · Simple and interlacing zeros of shifted completed zeta.** For real $0<|h|<1/2$, the functions $(\xi(s+h)+\xi(s-h))/2$ and $(\xi(s+h)-\xi(s-h))/(2i)$ have only simple critical-line zeros, and their zeros strictly interlace. [Lagarias, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0601653).
- **Z26 · Limiting spacings for the shifted functions.** For each fixed $k$, the joint distribution of $k$ consecutive normalized spacings of the zeros of either function in Z25 converges to a unit point mass at $(1,\dots,1)$. [Lagarias, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0601653).
- **Z27 · Simple and distinct zeros of $\xi'$.** At least $88.25\%$ of the zeros are simple and at least $94.12\%$ are distinct, in the asymptotic lower-limit sense. [Chirre, Gonçalves and de Laat, Corollary 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08843).
- **Z28 · Interlacing for Hardy’s $Z$-function.** For each fixed integer $n\ge1$, between every pair of sufficiently high consecutive zeros of $Z^{(n)}$ there is exactly one zero of $Z^{(n+1)}$. [Matsuoka, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2161).
- **Z29 · Horizontal monotonicity of the completed zeta function.** For every fixed real $t$, $|\xi(\sigma+it)|$ increases strictly in $\sigma>1/2$ and decreases strictly in $\sigma<1/2$. [Sondow and Dumitrescu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.1104).
- **Z30 · Li coefficients.** The numbers $\lambda_n=\sum_\rho[1-(1-1/\rho)^n]$, with zeros summed symmetrically, are nonnegative for every $n\ge1$. This is equivalent to RH. [Li](https://doi.org/10.1006/JNTH.1997.2137).
- **Z31 · A quantitative Li coefficient criterion.** The sequence $\lambda_n/n-(\log n+\gamma-\log(2\pi)-1)/2$ is square summable. [Arias de Reyna](https://personal.us.es/arias/56-2RHB.pdf).
- **Z32 · The de Bruijn–Newman constant is exactly zero.** If $\Lambda$ is the heat-flow threshold for real-rootedness of $\Xi$, RH supplies $\Lambda\le0$. Rodgers and Tao proved the reverse inequality unconditionally. [Rodgers and Tao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05914).
- **Z33 · Total positivity of a Fourier transform.** The Fourier transform of $x\mapsto1/\xi(1/2+x)$ is a Pólya frequency function. In particular, the associated difference kernel has nonnegative ordered minors of every size. [Gröchenig, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.12889).
- **Z34 · A compound Poisson law built from zeta zeros.** With $m_\gamma$ the multiplicity of an ordinate $\gamma$, the function $\exp(\sum_\gamma m_\gamma(e^{-i\gamma t}-1)/\gamma^2)$ is a characteristic function of a compound Poisson distribution. Its Lévy measure is $\sum_\gamma m_\gamma\gamma^{-2}\delta_{-\gamma}$. [Nakamura and Suzuki, Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.08317).
- **Z35 · Positivity of Weil’s quadratic form.** The quadratic form furnished by Weil’s explicit formula is nonnegative on its test-function domain. This is equivalent to RH. [Bombieri’s statement of Weil’s criterion for Clay](https://www.claymath.org/library/proceedings/cmip06.pdf).
- **Z36 · Two-sided bounds on the line $\Re s=1$.** For $t\ge10^{10}$, $|\zeta(1+it)|\le2e^\gamma(\log\log t-\log2+1/2+1/\log\log t)$. Its reciprocal is at most $12e^\gamma/\pi^2$ times the same parenthesis with $14\log\log t/\log t$ added. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, §1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.3595).

## Consequences of Dirichlet GRH

The Dirichlet part of the announcement would supply the hypothesis for the following results, in addition to the classical RH catalog above.

- **D01 · Prime counts in individual residue classes.** For each reduced residue class $a\bmod q$, $\pi(x;q,a)=\operatorname{li}(x)/\varphi(q)+O(\sqrt x\log(qx))$ uniformly in $q$ and $x\ge2$. Explicit bounds track the $x$ and $q$ dependence. [Ernvall-Hytönen and Palojärvi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01925), [Lee’s explicit comparisons](https://academic.oup.com/qjmath/article/74/4/1505/7240642).
- **D02 · Least prime in a prescribed progression.** For $q>3$ and $\gcd(a,q)=1$, the least prime $p\equiv a\bmod q$ satisfies $p\le(\varphi(q)\log q)^2$. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.3595).
- **D03 · A prime in an explicit coset.** For $q\ge20000$ and a subgroup of index $h>1$ in $(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times$, the smallest prime $p$ in each specified coset satisfies either $p\le10^9$ or $p\le((h-1)\log q+3(h+1)+(5/2)(\log\log q)^2)^2$. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.3595).
- **D04 · A small prime outside any proper subgroup.** For $q\ge3000$ with no prime factors below $(\log q)^2$, every proper subgroup of $(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times$ omits some prime $\ell\le(\log q)^2$ coprime to $q$. A version for all $q\ge3000$ uses the explicit bound $(\log q+B(q))^2$. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.3595).
- **D05 · Least quadratic nonresidue.** For every prime $q\ge5$, the least quadratic nonresidue modulo $q$ is below $(\log q)^2$. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Corollary 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.3595).
- **D06 · Primes in residue classes in short intervals.** For $q\ge35$, $x\ge e^q$ and $\gcd(a,q)=1$, there is a prime $p\equiv a\bmod q$ with $|p-x|<h$ for $h\ge\tfrac12\varphi(q)\sqrt x\log(q^2x)$. For $q\ge67$ and $h\ge\tfrac12\varphi(q)\sqrt x\log(q^2x^3)$ there are at least $\sqrt x$ such primes. [Dudek, Grenié and Molteni, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.08616).
- **D07 · Representatives near squares.** For $q\ge2$ and integers $n\ge8\varphi(q)\log q$, each reduced class modulo $q$ is represented by a prime in $[n^2,(n+\varphi(q)(12+2\log(qn)))^2)$. [Dudek, Grenié and Molteni, Corollary 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.08616).
- **D08 · Upper and lower bounds on $L(1,\chi)$.** For primitive $\chi\bmod q$ with $q\ge10^{10}$, $|L(1,\chi)|\le2e^\gamma(\log\log q-\log2+1/2+1/\log\log q)$. Its reciprocal is at most $12e^\gamma/\pi^2$ times the same parenthesis with $14\log\log q/\log q$ added. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.3595).
- **D09 · Effective bounds for imaginary quadratic class numbers.** For $-q$ a fundamental discriminant with $q\ge10^{10}$, multiplying the two bounds on $|L(1,\chi_{-q})|$ from D08 by $\sqrt q/\pi$ bounds $h(-q)$ on both sides. In particular $h(-q)\ge9053$ for $q\ge10^{11}$. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Corollary 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.3595).
- **D10 · Small compositeness witnesses.** Every composite $m$ has a witness at most $2(\log m)^2$ in the sense of Bach’s bounds for the Miller and Solovay–Strassen tests. This gives a deterministic small-base guarantee for these tests. Deterministic polynomial-time primality testing in general already has an unconditional proof. [Bach](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1990-55-191/S0025-5718-1990-1023756-8/S0025-5718-1990-1023756-8.pdf), [Agrawal, Kayal and Saxena](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2004/160-2/p12).
- **D11 · Explicit Chen theorem.** For every even $N>\exp(\exp(15.85))$, the number of representations as a prime plus an integer with at most two prime factors, counted with multiplicity in that integer, exceeds $4\cdot10^{-4}U_N N/\log^2N$, with the explicit positive singular factor $U_N$ in the theorem. The classical sufficiently-large result is unconditional. [Starichkova, Theorem 3 in the arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.08844), [published version](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-024-00866-x).
- **D12 · Large gaps for each fixed Dirichlet $L$-function.** For every fixed primitive Dirichlet character, the upper limit of consecutive zero gaps divided by the local mean exceeds $3.63846723$. **Preprint.** [Durkan and Pearce-Crump, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28527v1).
- **D13 · Critical-line zeros for abelian number fields.** Every nontrivial zero of $\zeta_K$ lies on $\Re s=1/2$ for each finite abelian extension $K/\mathbb Q$. This follows directly from the factorization of $\zeta_K$ into primitive Dirichlet $L$-functions. [The factorization in Sutherland’s number theory notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-785-number-theory-i-fall-2021/mit18_785f21_full_lec.pdf).
- **D14 · Quadratic field splitting primes.** For every quadratic field of fundamental discriminant $d$, the least inert rational prime is at most $(\log|d|)^2$ whenever $|d|\ge3000$ has no prime factors below that bound. Use D04 with the kernel of the quadratic Kronecker character and the Frobenius splitting criterion. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Theorem 1.1(2)](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.3595), [Sutherland on splitting in abelian extensions](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2018fa/LectureNotes20.pdf).
- **D15 · Squarefree variance across reduced residue classes.** For prime $q$ in the range $x\ge q>x^{1/3+30\varepsilon}$ and fixed $0<\varepsilon<1/100$, the variance of squarefree counts up to $x$ across the reduced classes, centered at $(6/\pi^2)(x/q)(1-q^{-2})^{-1}$, is $C(1+2/q)^{-1}\sqrt{x/q}+O_\varepsilon((x/q)^{1/2-\varepsilon/16})$. The extended range follows via Dirichlet Lindelöf. [Gorodetsky, Matomäki, Radziwiłł and Rodgers, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04060).

## Historical implications and limits of the announcement

- **Skewes’s first bound for a crossover.** Under RH, Skewes bounded the first region where $\pi(x)>\operatorname{li}(x)$ by $e^{e^{e^{79}}}$. Much smaller bounds have since been proved unconditionally. [Skewes (1933)](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-8.4.277), [Saouter, Trudgian and Demichel](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstreams/3bbcd8d2-860d-434a-8708-eb7c12e33986/download).
- **Ramanujan’s last exception.** Dudek and Platt proved under RH that the exact last integer exception to $\pi(x)^2<(ex/\log x)\pi(x/e)$ is $38{,}358{,}837{,}682$. An October 2026 preprint claims the same cutoff unconditionally. [Dudek and Platt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.1901), [Padhi’s preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202610.1845).
- **Ternary Goldbach.** Every odd integer greater than five is a sum of three primes. A 1997 proof used Dirichlet GRH. Helfgott supplied an unconditional proof in 2013. [Deshouillers, Effinger, te Riele and Zinoviev](https://www.ams.org/journals/era/1997-03-15/S1079-6762-97-00031-0/S1079-6762-97-00031-0.pdf), [Helfgott](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.7748).
- **Zero simplicity and exact prime-race densities.** Z07 gives a lower bound, and the claim that all zeta zeros are simple is a separate conjecture. The numerical Mertens bias of roughly $0.99999973$ uses RH together with linear independence of the positive ordinates. A number of exact Dirichlet prime-race density results likewise use linear independence assumptions. [Bui and Heath-Brown](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.5018), [Lamzouri](https://arxiv.org/html/1410.3777), [Rubinstein and Sarnak](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.1994.10504289).
- **Artin’s primitive root conjecture.** Hooley’s conditional proof uses RH for Dedekind zeta functions of Kummer fields that can be nonabelian over $\mathbb Q$. That scope is beyond Dirichlet GRH. [Hooley (1967)](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1967.225.209).
- **Binary Goldbach and prime pairs.** The Goldbach window bound P14, average counts P21–P22, and explicit Chen theorem D11 are three distinct conclusions about additive primes. The claims that every even integer greater than two is the sum of two primes and that infinitely many prime pairs differ by two are separate open statements in the cited source. [Helfgott on binary Goldbach](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.7748), [Maynard on bounded gaps](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.4600).

For a longer historical bibliography of equivalent formulations, see [Kevin Broughan’s three-volume Equivalents of the Riemann Hypothesis](https://kevinbroughan.nz/ERH/EquivRH.html).
