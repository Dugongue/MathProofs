# Deterministic tensor witnesses by torus descent

Assume deterministic complete univariate factorization over every explicitly represented \(\mathbb F_q\), of cost \(d^{O(\log(d+1))}(\log q)^{O(1)}\) at degree \(d\). Let \(N\ge2\) measure the size of the input tensors apart from field bit length. All geometric algebraic groups here are taken with reduced structure.

**Theorem.** Consider the ordinary \(a\times b\times k\) tensor isomorphism problem. Let \(H\) be the connected component of the geometric projection of the source stabilizer to the \(k\)-mode. Put \(\mathcal A=\operatorname{span}(H)\subset M_k(\bar{\mathbb F}_q)\), a Galois-stable unital associative algebra, and write its descended semisimple quotient as
\[
\mathcal A/\operatorname{rad}\mathcal A
 =\prod_i M_{r_i}(E_i).
\]
Let \(\delta\colon \mathcal A^\times\to\prod_i\operatorname{Res}_{E_i/\mathbb F_q}\mathbb G_m\) be the determinant map on residue factors. Assume \(H\) contains the geometric kernel of \(\delta\). Then one can decide the instance and return a verified base-field transporter deterministically in
\[
N^{\,2^{O((k+1)^2)}\log N}(\log q)^{O(1)} .
\]
The connected-stabilizer property is a promise. Neither the algebra nor its bases or splittings are supplied. This includes all toral connected projected stabilizers, with arbitrary nonsplit forms and no diagonal basis given. It also includes non-toral connected groups containing the determinant kernel of their span, so the promise permits unipotent radical directions and nonabelian residue special-linear groups.

The preceding bounded-mode result determined every extension degree supporting a witness, including for general connected projected stabilizers, but its Lang step was existential. Its deterministic search covered split diagonal groups or unit groups of matrix algebras. The construction below uses the component information to replace existential descent by deterministic descent through a determinant quotient. [Bounded-mode decision and unit-algebra search](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/DeterministicAverageCaseTI/BoundedMode.md), [Earlier diagonal-torus search](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/QuasipolynomialFactoringFallout/Transporters.md).

## 1. Effective access to a rational component

Write \(k_0=\mathbb F_q\). Use the affine charts of the genuine projected transporter from the preceding bounded-mode proof. They have \(k^2+1\) variables, with one inverse coordinate, polynomially many equations, and degrees polynomial in \(N\). Their nonempty geometric components meet the disjoint translates of \(H\) in dense open sets. From the generic eliminant construction select a chart and a projection \(y\) to \(d\) independent coordinate functions for which an irreducible factor \(g(y,\tau,U)\) is absolutely irreducible. It represents exactly one Frobenius-stable chart component, hence a unique base-field-defined full component \(C\) of the projected transporter. If no such factor occurs, the deterministic decision result certifies nonexistence.

Here is how to recover equations and a point, while remaining uniform in characteristic. In the chart ideal adjoin the coefficients in the indeterminate \(\tau\) of \(g(y,\tau,\ell_\tau(x))\), with \(\ell_\tau\) the formal separating linear form of the previous proof. Work first over \(K=k_0(y)\). The generic fiber is then finite and its geometric points are precisely those of the selected component. Indeed the absolute factors of the generic eliminant have disjoint root sets for \(\ell_\tau\), because the formal \(\tau\) separates distinct points. Vanishing as a polynomial in \(\tau\) picks precisely the points associated to \(g\). This criterion works for an inseparable projection and for chart equations carrying nilpotents.

Compute a monic Gröbner basis over \(K\) of this finite generic-fiber ideal. Take a common denominator \(\Delta(y)\) of its coefficients. Over \(k_0[y,1/\Delta]\) the monic basis has the same reductions and a free quotient on the same standard monomials. In detail the leading monomials are unchanged and the zero reductions of the S-polynomials hold over this localization because every reduction uses monic polynomials. It follows that the quotient is torsion-free over \(k_0[y,1/\Delta]\). Monic division of each original chart and eliminant equation by the basis has zero remainder over \(K\), hence also over this localization. Consequently every specialized point satisfies those equations. Thus the contraction from \(K\) of the generic ideal is computed by clearing the basis denominators and saturating by \(\Delta\), with one auxiliary inverse variable. Its geometric support is the closure inside the chart of the chosen component. Eliminating the original chart inverse gives equations for the projected closure, and within \(\mathrm{GL}_k\) that closure is exactly the full component \(C\).

Specialize \(y\) to any tuple outside \(\Delta=0\) in a finite extension of the base field large enough for a grid of size \(\deg\Delta+1\). Relative finite fields of the needed degrees are obtained with the previous bounded-degree factoring construction. The specialized monic basis defines a nonzero finite quotient of the same dimension. Recover a common point by successively adjoining a root of a coordinate's characteristic polynomial on the current finite quotient, retaining a branch only when fixing that coordinate leaves a nonzero quotient. Each step uses a finite extension of relative degree at most the quotient dimension. There are only \(k^2+1\) coordinates, hence the resulting field degree is bounded in terms of the same fixed-variable polynomial degree bounds. This returns \(a\in C\) over an explicit finite extension, without making a random rational slice.

We also compute \(W=\operatorname{span}(C)\) in matrix coordinates. A linear form vanishes on \(C\) precisely when its class in the finite generic-fiber algebra \(R\) is nilpotent. Write \(m_0=\dim_K R\). If the characteristic \(p\) is at most \(m_0\), take the first power \(P=p^j\ge m_0\), which is at most \(m_0^2\). A class is nilpotent exactly when its \(P\)-th power is zero. For a linear form \(\sum c_i z_i\) in the matrix entries with \(c_i\in k_0\), this is the linear equation \(\sum c_i^P z_i^P=0\) in the known \(K\)-basis of \(R\). Clear denominators, equate \(y\)-coefficients over \(k_0\), then apply inverse Frobenius on the coefficient field. If \(p>m_0\), the nilradical is the kernel of the trace pairing \((x,y)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_K(L_{xy})\) on \(R\). To see this, filter the local factors by their radicals. On each residue field the trace is weighted by its positive composition multiplicity, which is \(<p\). Its extension degree is \(<p\), so the residue field is separable and its trace pairing is nondegenerate. Thus the kernel is exactly the nilradical, and clearing denominators again gives linear equations in the \(c_i\). Taking the annihilator yields \(W\), because the geometric span of the Frobenius-stable component is defined over \(k_0\).

All eliminations above have \(O(k^2)\) variables. Standard fixed-variable Gröbner degree bounds apply over the finite field or the function field. For rational-function arithmetic use the fixed-variable Hermann ideal-membership representation bound together with the Groebner degree bound. The corresponding Macaulay rows span all needed bounded-degree ideal elements, and elimination can choose rational coefficients as quotients of minors of the polynomial coefficient matrices. This keeps their numerator and denominator degrees within \(N^{2^{O((k+1)^2)}}\) throughout. There are a bounded number of these eliminations, and the successive finite-fiber field degrees remain polynomial in this bound with \(k\) fixed. [Groebner degree bounds](https://doi.org/10.1137/0219053), [Hermann representation bound, Theorem 3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0305172), [Previous generic eliminant and finite-extension construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/DeterministicAverageCaseTI/BoundedMode.md).

## 2. The determinant quotient of a component

Since \(C=aH\), over the known field of \(a\) one has \(a^{-1}W=\mathcal A\). The latter space descends to \(k_0\) by Frobenius stability of \(H\) and the uniqueness of its reduced row echelon basis. Thus this equality computes \(\mathcal A\) over \(k_0\), including its multiplication table. It is a unital algebra because \(H\) contains identity and is closed under multiplication. The space \(W\) is a right \(\mathcal A\)-module by matrix multiplication, and over the field of \(a\) it is isomorphic to the regular right module. By Noether–Deuring and deterministic constructive module isomorphism, find a right-module isomorphism \(\mathcal A\to W\) over \(k_0\). Its value \(a_0\) at 1 is invertible. Otherwise every matrix in \(a_0\mathcal A=W\) would be singular over every extension, contradicting \(a\in W\cap\mathrm{GL}_k\).

Compute the radical and the explicit matrix algebra factors of the semisimple quotient by the finite-algebra algorithms already used in the bounded-mode result. Use a finite field \(L/k_0\) containing the coordinates of \(a\) and splitting the center fields \(E_i\). Each center degree is at most \(k^2\), and their least common multiple is bounded in terms of \(k\). This field and its needed embeddings are found by the earlier relative extension and factoring algorithms. Over \(L\) the target of \(\delta\) is a split torus \(Q=(\mathbb G_m)^b\), with geometric coordinate permutations under Frobenius given explicitly by the center embeddings.

The subgroup \(T=\delta(H)\) is closed and connected, since it is the image of a connected algebraic group. As a reduced connected subgroup of \(Q\), it is a torus. The promise gives \(H=\delta^{-1}(T)\) on geometric points. The translated component \(a_0^{-1}C\subset\mathcal A^\times\) has determinant image \(b_0T\), where \(b_0=\delta(a_0^{-1}a)\in Q(L)\). This coset is defined over \(k_0\). Compute equations for it in the split torus by eliminating the chart variables from the graph of the determinant coordinates \(z\mapsto\delta(a_0^{-1}z)\) on the already selected chart component. The linear map \(z\mapsto a_0^{-1}z\) on \(W\) may be extended linearly to ambient matrix coordinates for defining these determinant polynomials. The image of the dense chart open is dense in the coset by continuity, and the full coset is closed inside the torus. Thus restriction of the elimination equations to nonzero coordinates gives precisely \(b_0T\). Substituting \(b_0t\) gives equations of \(T\).

Recover the lattice \(M\subseteq\mathbb Z^b\) of characters trivial on \(T\). Suppose the equations just obtained have degree at most \(D\). For each polynomial defining \(T\), group its monomials by their character restriction to \(T\). Within each group the coefficients sum to zero, by independence of distinct characters. Let \(\Lambda\) be generated by all exponent differences within each group. Its generators have infinity norm at most \(D\) and are in \(M\). If the saturation of \(\Lambda\) were a proper subgroup of \(M\), the same grouped cancellations would define a positive-dimensional enlargement of \(T\) on which all equations still vanish. Thus the short vectors in \(M\) generate it up to saturation. Enumerate every vector \(u\) with infinity norm at most \(D\), and test whether \(t^{u^+}-t^{u^-}\) vanishes on \(T\). This is geometric ideal membership in the radical, implemented by adding an inverse for the candidate difference and testing emptiness, with the torus inverses also imposed. Saturate the lattice of successful vectors by integer Smith normal form. It gives \(M\) because \(M\) itself is saturated, the quotient character group of a split torus being free. Integer linear algebra now gives split coordinates \(T_L\simeq(\mathbb G_m)^r\) and the Frobenius action on the character quotient \(\mathbb Z^b/M\) from the known permutation on \(Q_L\). No root system or given torus basis is used. [Character modules of tori](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/iAG2022.pdf).

The algorithms in this section have \(O(k^2)\) elimination variables. The determinant polynomials, lattice enumeration ranges and factorizations have degrees and sizes within \(N^{2^{O((k+1)^2)}}\). Finite-field radical and matrix splitting algorithms take deterministic quasipolynomial bit time in their dimensions and polynomial time in the logarithmic field size. [Explicit matrix splitting, Theorem 1.8](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-2011-02505-6), [Constructive module isomorphism](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf).

## 3. Deterministic Lang descent for the torus coset

The determinant-coset step can also be obtained directly from the rational spectral monomial algorithm of [Spectral.md, §§2–3](Spectral.md). Take as rows all Frobenius translates of a basis of \(M\), including duplicates if convenient, with target \(b_0^u\) on the row \(u\). These targets form a rational tuple on the row-orbit fields since \(b_0T\) is Frobenius-stable and \(u\) is trivial on \(T\). The row lattice is exactly \(M\), so it is saturated. These monomial equations cut out \(b_0T\) over the algebraic closure and have a rational solution by Lang's theorem. Their Smith torsion is 1. The integer exponent in the equivariant power witness of that algorithm is therefore just the Frobenius permutation order on the columns. Every prime factor of that order is at most the largest center degree, at most \(k^2\). The explicit row fields have degrees dividing this permutation order, which is bounded in terms of \(k\) and already divides \([L:k_0]\). Thus the spectral primary-lifting step constructs a rational determinant point with factoring degrees at most \(k^2\) after preparation. Negative integer entries in the character lattice are allowed in that algorithm. The proof below gives a direct alternative for this coset descent.

The following construction gives a point of a base-field-defined torus coset \(b_0T\), once \(b_0\) and split coordinates of \(T\) are available over \(L=\mathbb F_{q^m}\). Let \(F\) denote the \(q\)-Frobenius action. We use the convention \(\mathcal L(x)=x^{-1}F(x)\), a homomorphism on \(T\). Put \(c=F(b_0)^{-1}b_0\in T(L)\). The cocycle condition is \(\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}F^j(c)=1\), by telescoping in \(Q\). Write \(c_0=1\), \(c_j=\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}F^i(c)\) and
\[
h=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}c_j^{-1}.
\]
Then \(F(c_j)=c_{j+1}c^{-1}\) and \(c_m=1\), so \(\mathcal L(h)=c^m\).

Compute a coordinatewise \(m\)-th root \(z\) of \(h\) in split torus coordinates. Write \(m'=m_{p'}\) for the prime-to-characteristic part. Over a relative extension of \(L\) of degree \(\varphi(m')m'\), the field contains all \(m'\)-th roots of every nonzero element of \(L\): first adjoin the \(m'\)-th roots of unity by degree \(\varphi(m')\), then each Kummer splitting degree divides \(m'\). Use factorization in degrees at most \(m\) and inverse Frobenius for the \(p\)-part. The remaining discrepancy
\[
\eta=\mathcal L(z)c^{-1}
\]
is in \(T[m]\), and its split coordinates have multiplicative orders dividing \(m'\). If \(m'=1\), this discrepancy is trivial and the torsion correction is unnecessary.

In split coordinates \(F\) has an exponent matrix of the form \(qA\), where \(A\) is a known integral matrix with \(A^m=I\), obtained from the Frobenius action on characters with the chosen coordinate convention. The Lang map is therefore the monomial map with exponent matrix \(qA-I\). Compute its integer Smith normal form. It has full rank and each diagonal invariant factor \(d_i\) divides \(q^m-1\), since
\[
(qA-I)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(qA)^j=(q^m-1)I .
\]
After the corresponding monomial changes of coordinates, solving \(\mathcal L(t)=\eta^{-1}\) amounts to taking \(d_i\)-th roots of known roots of unity of orders dividing \(m'\).

These roots do not require degree-\(d_i\) oracle calls. Write \(d_i=d_{i,0}d_{i,1}\), with \(d_{i,0}\) supported on primes dividing \(m'\) and \(d_{i,1}\) coprime to \(m'\). Undo the latter on the right side by its inverse exponent modulo \(m'\). To extract a \(d_{i,0}\)-th root use successive \(\ell\)-th root extractions for primes \(\ell\mid m'\). Work in one additional relative extension of the current field of degree \(m'\). For each relevant \(\ell\), the original field \(L\) already contains all \(\ell\)-power roots of unity up to order \(\ell^{v_\ell(d_i)}\), and the valuation of its multiplicative group order increases by at least \(v_\ell(m')\) after extension by degree \(m'\). This follows by expanding \(Q^\ell-1\) for \(Q\equiv1\bmod\ell\) and iterating, including \(\ell=2\). The larger field therefore contains the required roots of unity of orders dividing \(m'd_{i,0}\). Every extraction uses a polynomial of degree at most \(m\), and the number of extractions is polynomial in \(r,m,\log q\). Reversing the Smith coordinate changes produces \(t\). Then \(\mathcal L(zt)=c\), and \(F(b_0zt)=b_0zt\), so \(b_0zt\in Q(k_0)\) is the desired coset point. Its field components are read by linear algebra in the known center bases.

The extension degrees used here are polynomial in \(m\) and independent of \(q\). The Smith matrix integer entries have bit length polynomial in the input lattice size and \(\log q\). Thus all root extraction costs satisfy the stated quasipolynomial bound and remain polynomial in logarithmic field size at fixed \(k\). The method treats a torus coset with a bounded-degree point and constructive split coordinates. Existing algorithmic Lang results, such as that of Cohen–Murray, address a different split reductive input model and in general use randomized steps. [Cohen–Murray, algorithm for Lang's theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0506068).

Finally lift the rational determinant values to the explicit residue matrix factors using diagonal matrices and lift their tuple through a linear section of \(\mathcal A\to\mathcal A/\operatorname{rad}\mathcal A\). The lift \(u\) is invertible since its residue is invertible. Its determinant quotient is in \(b_0T\). The promise \(H=\delta^{-1}(T)\) now puts \(a_0u\) in \(C(k_0)\). Construct the remaining two mode changes by constructive module isomorphism at that parameter and verify the original three-mode equation. This finishes deterministic search.

## Scope

No unrestricted deterministic fixed-mode transporter search is claimed. If the connected projected stabilizer omits part of the determinant kernel of its span, then the final arbitrary lift need not stay in the component. The result is a written factoring-conditional proof.
