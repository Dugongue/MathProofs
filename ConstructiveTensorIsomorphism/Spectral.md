# Recovering tensor transporters on disconnected spectral support

Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) be explicitly presented. Consider the ordered ordinary action on the simple-spectrum locus of the earlier invertible balanced-cut algorithm for even-order tensors. The proof also applies to any pair with intrinsic modewise conjugation covariants of squarefree characteristic polynomials computable in polynomial time. The usual cyclic identifications make each mode a rank-one module for an étale algebra \(K_i\). The remaining maps are multiplication by units of \(K_i\).

**Theorem.** In the geometric spectral coordinates, let \(V\) be the disjoint union of the eigenlabel sets of the modes and let \(S\subseteq\prod_i V_i\) be the support of the source. Let \(A\) have one row per \(s\in S\), with a 1 in each of its mode positions. To impose a prescribed determinant in a mode, append the row having a 1 at every label of that mode. Let \(m\) be the largest nonzero Smith invariant factor of this integer matrix, or 1 if its rank is zero. Let \(L\) be the order of the permutation of \(V\) induced by \(q\)-Frobenius.

Assume deterministic complete univariate factoring in bit time \(r^{O(\log(r+1))}(\log q)^{O(1)}\) for degree \(r\) over every explicitly presented \(\mathbb F_q\). If \(N\) is the dense input size, including the mode shapes, apart from field bit length, and \(B\) is the largest prime of \(mL\) that divides the order of any source or target coordinate field's multiplicative group, an actual ordinary transporter, including its prescribed determinants, can be found or excluded in
\[
 (N+B+2)^{O(\log(N+B+2))}(\log q)^{O(1)}
\]
bit time. Set \(B=1\) if there are no such primes. There is also a deterministic exact counting algorithm in \(N^{O(\log(N+2))}(\log q)^{O(1)}\) time for all supports and determinant rows, independently of \(B\). Both algorithms use only finite fields of degree polynomial in the dense input size. At fixed characteristic, the time bounds become polynomial in the corresponding parameters.

In particular, every saturated support incidence lattice gives quasipolynomial unrestricted witness search, even with disconnected overlap and nonsplit spectra. More generally the bound is quasipolynomial whenever the relevant prime divisors of the support torsion are polynomially bounded. This includes the parity family in §5, where every overlap graph has two components. ## 1. Reduction to a rational monomial system

Equal mode characteristic polynomials are necessary. For each mode, identify both mode spaces with the same \(K_i=k[Z]/(f_i)\) via cyclic bases. The centralizer of multiplication by \(Z\) consists of the multiplication operators, so after the cyclic basis changes, every valid mode map is multiplication by a unit \(x_i\in K_i\). Tensor components in the geometric eigenframe move only by nonzero scaling. Thus unequal supports rule out isomorphism, and on common support \(S\) the equations are
\[
 f_A(x)_s=\prod_{v\in V}x_v^{A_{s,v}}=r_s,\qquad r_s=u_s/t_s .
\]
The determinant rows have target the ratio of the desired mode determinant to the determinant of the cyclic-basis identification from source to target. The determinant of a multiplication operator is the product of all its geometric eigenvalues, with multiplicities here all one. Denote the combined set of support and determinant rows by \(W\). Frobenius permutes the support rows simultaneously with the eigenlabels and fixes all determinant rows. We regard geometrical coordinates as rational exactly when the value at a Frobenius successor is the \(q\)-th power of the value at its predecessor. In particular the matrix \(A\) intertwines the two permutation actions.

For completeness, the labels and their values can be computed orbit by orbit. Factor \(f_i\) over \(k\), labeling the conjugates of a given irreducible root by successive powers of \(q\)-Frobenius. A tuple of labels of lengths \(a_i\) lies in a field of degree \(\operatorname{lcm}_i a_i\le\prod_i n_i\), where \(n_i=\dim K_i\). Evaluating the two tensors at each tuple in these local fields computes \(S\) and its ratios. One can use coherent models \(F(a)\) for all required degrees: for each prime power choose its nested fields from a displayed field of maximal valuation, using Frobenius fixed spaces, and tensor together the coprime-degree fields. Divisor maps are the tensor products of the nested maps. Identify a displayed field with its model by factoring the minimal polynomial of a generator over the model. A generator can be found without random sampling: in each prime-power subfield choose an element outside its maximal proper subfield by two Frobenius fixed-space computations and add these elements. For a degree \(a\) field and any \(\ell\mid a\), the power \(q^{a/\ell}\) fixes every summand except the \(\ell\)-summand, which it moves. The sum therefore lies in no maximal proper subfield. The same method changes a tower to a polynomial presentation over the prime field if needed. Each tuple field uses prime-power subfields already available from the label fields. In particular the algorithm does not require the common field of degree \(L\), which may be exponentially large. [Prior coherent field models, §4](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GenericOrdinaryFourTI/PROOF.md) · [Deterministic cyclic module isomorphism](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf).

## 2. A rational monomial witness for a power

There is a polynomial-bit integer matrix \(C\) with \(ACA=mA\). If \(UAV\!_0\) is the Smith diagonal, use the diagonal inverse with entries \(m/d_j\) on each nonzero diagonal \(d_j\), and zero on the zero entries, then transform it back. Smith normal form and its unimodular multipliers have polynomial bit complexity. Let \(P_W,P_V\) be the permutation matrices of the two Frobenius actions and form
\[
 C_*=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1} P_V^j C P_W^{-j},\qquad E=mL.
\]
Then \(C_*\) intertwines the two actions, and \(AC_*A=EA\), since \(AP_V=P_W A\). Computing \(C_*\) does not take \(L\) steps: on a pair of label and row orbits of lengths \(a,b\), each entry is a sum over a simultaneous pair orbit of length \(\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\), multiplied by \(L/\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\). This length is at most \(ab\), so the whole matrix is computed with polynomially many arithmetic operations on polynomial-bit integers. The bit length of \(L\) is polynomial, as is that of \(m\) by Smith form. [Kannan–Bachem, polynomial Smith form with multipliers](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040).

If \(r=f_A(x)\), then \(f_{C_*}(r)\) is rational and
\[
 f_A(f_{C_*}(r))=r^E .
\]
This applies whenever a witness exists, which is all that is needed for completeness. To evaluate the output at an orbit representative of length \(a\), group each row orbit of length \(b\) into orbits of the \(a\)-th power of Frobenius. The integer exponents from \(C_*\) are constant on each such group by equivariance. Multiplying all values in that group therefore uses one field norm from \(F(b)\) to \(F(\gcd(a,b))\), followed by a fixed embedding into \(F(a)\) and the relevant exponentiation. This gives a polynomial-time computation in the available coherent models, including for negative exponents.

## 3. From the power to an explicit witness

Let \(M\) be the least common multiple of all source and target coordinate group orders \(q^a-1\) and \(q^b-1\). Its bit length is polynomial. Compute the part \(M_0\) of \(M\) prime to \(E\), for example by dividing out \(\gcd(M,E^{\lceil\log_2(M+1)\rceil})\) using modular powering. A primary projection of any element in these groups is evaluated by powering with the corresponding Chinese-remainder idempotent modulo \(M\). The power witness projected onto the \(M_0\)-part can be raised to the inverse of \(E\bmod M_0\). It then solves the \(M_0\)-part of the target on every positive input.

Only the primes of \(\gcd(M,E)\) remain. Factor this integer by trial division, stopping when there is no remaining factor or the remaining part is prime. This uses time polynomial in \(B\) and the input bit dimensions. For each remaining prime \(\ell\), let \(\ell^t\) be its largest power in \(M\). Choose one available coordinate field whose group order is divisible by \(\ell^t\). Factor \(X^\ell-1\) there and take a nontrivial root, then repeatedly factor \(X^\ell-c\) to lift the current primitive \(\ell^j\)-th root to a primitive \(\ell^{j+1}\)-th root through \(j=t-1\). All steps are possible by cyclicity of the field's unit group. Restrict its powers to the other fields through the coherent gcd-degree subfields. For example, for another group with \(\ell\)-part \(\ell^b\), the power \(\zeta^{\ell^{t-b}}\) belongs to its shared subfield with the chosen field, by the identity \(\gcd(q^a-1,q^c-1)=q^{\gcd(a,c)}-1\). This supplies compatible generators for every local \(\ell\)-group. The residue characteristic cannot divide \(M\).

In each target coordinate group, compute the logarithm of the projected target relative to its explicit \(\ell\)-generator. An order-\(\ell^j\) logarithm requires at most \(j\) order-\(\ell\) logarithms by successive digits: raise the target to \(\ell^{j-1}\) to find the first digit by comparison to \(\ell\) powers, divide off its contribution, and continue. For each domain generator, evaluate \(f_A\) on it alone and compute the same local logarithms. This is polynomial work in \(B\) and the other bit dimensions. The resulting linear system over cyclic \(\ell\)-groups is solved by integer Smith form, using one modulus column per target orbit. An unrestricted integer unknown for a domain generator is valid because its powers already exhaust exactly that coordinate's \(\ell\)-subgroup. Multiply the solved \(\ell\)-parts and the \(M_0\)-part in each domain coordinate. Verify the result against the original equations and the determinant constraints. Verification makes the algorithm sound even on a negative input, and every step on a positive input returns a valid part. No discrete logarithm in a group with unbounded prime divisors is used. [Pohlig–Hellman digit extraction](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications/28.pdf).

All polynomial factoring in preprocessing has degree bounded by a polynomial in \(N\). The primary step uses only factorization of degrees at most \(B\). At fixed characteristic, the \(p\)-Frobenius fixed space of an étale algebra is a product of copies of \(\mathbb F_p\); the eigenspaces for the multiplication maps of its basis elements, trying scalars in the fixed prime field, split those factors deterministically in polynomial time. This applies over polynomial-basis extension fields as well.

## 4. Counting with no large-prime price

The image of the rational monomial map admits a logarithm-free test. For clarity, let \(m_v=q^a-1\) on domain orbits and \(m_w=q^b-1\) on row orbits, with \(a,b\) the orbit lengths. Use abstract coherent generators from a common finite overfield for the correctness proof only. For representatives, let \(A_j\) be the coefficient of the \(j\)-th label of the chosen domain orbit in the chosen row. The exponent-coordinate matrix entry is
\[
 H_{wv}=(m_w/m_v)\sum_{0\le j<a}A_j q^j.
\]
This integer can be computed without ratios of field elements. The \(A_j\) repeat with period \(\gcd(a,b)\), since the row is fixed by the \(b\)-th power of Frobenius. Sum the resulting geometric series to cancel \(m_v\). This also treats the determinant rows, for which \(b=1\) and the nonzero entry is 1.

Compute the integer lattice generated by the columns of \(H\) and the modulus columns \(\operatorname{diag}(m_w)\). Let its Smith row transform be \(U\) and its positive invariant factors be \(d_i\). The abstract target exponents \(\alpha_w\) lie in this lattice exactly when \(\sum_w U_{iw}\alpha_w=0\bmod d_i\) for every \(i\). To test this without recovering the exponents, compute a gcd-free basis for all \(d_i\) and \(m_w\). For each atom power \(D\) in \(d_i\), choose a target field whose group order is divisible by \(D\). Such a field exists because \(d_i\) divides \(\operatorname{lcm}_w m_w\). In each \(w\)-field evaluate \(r_w^{m_w U_{iw}/D}\), an integral exponent because \(d_i\mid m_w U_{iw}\). The result has order dividing \(D\) and \(m_w\), so is in the shared gcd-degree subfield with the chosen target field. Move the values there and multiply. The product is 1 exactly when this atom's congruence holds. This is the earlier Smith membership argument with the determinant rows included.

If any congruence fails the number is zero. Otherwise, the number of witnesses is the kernel size
\[
 \frac{\prod_v m_v}{\prod_w m_w}\prod_i d_i .
\]
Indeed \(\prod_i d_i\) is the cokernel order. The component fields and integer normal forms have polynomial bit size, so this count needs only the preprocessing factoring hypothesis. No integer factorization of the orders \(q^a-1\) or of the Smith factors is needed. [Earlier Smith membership method](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GenericOrdinaryFourTI/PROOF.md).

## 5. A family beyond connected overlap

Take equal even side \(n\ge80\), even order \(d=2h\ge4\), and odd characteristic. Give each mode label in \(\mathbb Z/n\) its parity. Restrict the tensors in the original coordinate bases to even total parity. The earlier sum-preserving weighted permutation witness \(T_0\) belongs to this linear space and has invertible balanced cut and simple spectra. It has \(n^h\) supported tuples, with \(n^{h-1}\) at each mode label. Perturb it by \(\varepsilon Z\), with independent generic entries only in the allowed positions. The simultaneous diagonal sign tuple fixes the perturbation, so every conjugating covariant commutes with its mode sign. Lift its simple eigenvectors to a formal series basis respecting the two parity subspaces. As in the overlap proof, any allowed tuple at Hamming distance at least two from the support of \(T_0\) has first-order spectral coefficient \(Z\) at that tuple, because a first-order frame change acts on only one slot of \(T_0\).

For two labels of the same parity in a mode, choose any parity pattern on the remaining modes that makes both total parities even. There are \((n/2)^{d-1}>2d n^h\) contexts of that pattern. At most \(2d n^h\) put either resulting tuple at Hamming distance less than two from the original support, by the same regular-support count as before. Hence on the perturbation every pair of same-parity labels has a common context. Opposite-parity labels have no common context, and every even parity pattern occurs. These properties persist on a nonempty algebraic open set in the parity-restricted tensor space: one can pass to the finite étale cover labeling the simple eigenlines within each known parity block, express the existence of supporting entries and common contexts there by finite unions of polynomial nonvanishing conditions, and descend the label-invariant open set. Thus over sufficiently large odd finite fields the uniform distribution on this restricted space has a fraction tending to one for fixed \(n,d\). The claim is within the parity-restricted family.

The supported rows generate all differences of labels in the same mode and parity class, by subtracting rows with a common context. After modding out by those primitive differences there are just two generator classes per mode. In coordinates \(b_i\) for the even class and \(\delta_i\) for the odd-minus-even class, the rows consist of \(\sum_i b_i+\sum_{i\in J}\delta_i\) for all even-cardinality \(J\). Their differences from \(J=\varnothing\) generate the even-total lattice on the \(\delta_i\): they contain each \(\delta_i+\delta_j\), their differences \(\delta_i-\delta_j\) using a third index, and hence \(2\delta_i\). The remaining relation on the \(b_i\) is primitive. Consequently the unrestricted incidence torsion has exponent 2, and each overlap graph has exactly two components. After prescribing all determinants the geometric kernel is finite with exponent dividing \(2n\). Indeed its coordinates have the form \(s_i\epsilon^{\eta}\) on parity \(\eta\), with \(\prod_i s_i=1\) and \(\epsilon^2=1\). The determinant relations give \(s_i^n\epsilon^{n/2}=1\). The same calculation works for the diagonalizable group scheme, so bounds Smith torsion even when the characteristic divides \(n\). For a proper subset of imposed determinants, use the product condition to eliminate one unprescribed scalar. The other unprescribed scalars are free, and the selected scalars and the sign form a finite diagonalizable factor killed by \(2n\), since the selected scalars satisfy \(s_i^{2n}=1\) as group-scheme equations. All primes of \(L\) are at most \(n\) because its cycle lengths are at most \(n\). The theorem therefore gives deterministic quasipolynomial search throughout this recognized parity class, including the arbitrary determinant variants.

The parity description also combines with the local lifting theorem. The ordinary residue stabilizer consists of scalar tuples and a rational parity sign, so any one residue witness produces both residue witness classes. Its Lie stabilizer in odd characteristic is the scalar Lie algebra, since the additional sign group has zero Lie algebra. Over every effectively presented finite local ring of odd residue characteristic with such a residue source, the constructive algorithm therefore returns a full ordinary witness or rejects, and exactly counts the full witnesses. This uses the factoring premise only for finding the residue witness. [Local lifting and determinant equations](Lifting.md).

Exact cyclic-coordinate controls compared the orbitwise integer matrix with direct geometric expansion, checked the equivariant power identity and its prime-to-exceptional part, and verified uniform nonempty fibers across 138 systems over fields of orders 2, 3, and 5. The controls covered 17,503 rational domain elements and 4,585 distinct targets counted separately by system.

The split-torus Smith reduction and finite-field powering tests are established techniques. The additional step here is constructive recovery on the nonsplit support torus with factor degrees controlled by geometric support torsion and Frobenius cycles, while keeping field degrees bounded locally. The proof is written mathematics. [Torus orbit algorithms and Smith normal forms](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2021.32).
