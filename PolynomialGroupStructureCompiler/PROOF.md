# Linearizing operations on succinctly presented nonabelian groups

Fix \(p,c,e,r\), with \(p\) prime, and a bound on the arity of operations. Consider named structures on finite \(p\)-groups of nilpotence class at most \(c\) and exponent dividing \(p^e\). The groups are given by word-relator presentations, allowing straight-line relators. Each named operation \(f:C\to B\), where \(C\) is a product of sorts, is evaluable in polynomial time on words, returning words or straight-line programs. Suppose it satisfies the following bounded expansion property. For any \(x,h_1,\ldots,h_s\in C\), set \(h_I\) to the product of \(h_i\) in increasing order for \(i\in I\). Define \(u_J\in B\) recursively by
\[
f(xh_I)=\prod_{J\subseteq I}u_J ,
\]
where the product on the right follows a fixed total order on subsets, increasing by cardinality and then lexicographically. Each equation uniquely determines the term \(u_I\) after the proper subsets. Require \(u_J=1\) whenever \(|J|>r\).

There is a fully faithful polynomial-size encoding of these structures and their operation-preserving group homomorphisms as linear structures over \(\mathbb F_p\), and a polynomial-time many-one reduction of their isomorphism problem to ordinary 3-tensor isomorphism. The output sorts and groups need not be abelian. The property is a promise on the given operations. For abelian output groups it reduces to the usual vanishing iterated additive differences, with the domain allowed to be nonabelian. Separately, arbitrarily long group-word operations can also be included, without a degree bound on the word or an expansion promise. This combines the earlier presented-group jet encoding and the finite-difference encoding.

## The truncated group ring

Let \(D=cp^{e-1}\), \(k=\mathbb F_p\), \(I\) be the augmentation ideal of \(k[G]\), and \(H_L(G)=k[G]/I^{L+1}\) for \(L\ge D\). Record multiplication, augmentation, the unit, the bilinear projection \(q\) to the tensor square truncated in total augmentation degree, and the coproduct \(\Delta([g])=q([g],[g])\). The recognized points \(\epsilon(u)=1,\ \Delta(u)=q(u,u)\) are precisely the images of distinct elements of \(G\). Here is the justification for arbitrary \(L\ge D\).

By the Jennings dimension-subgroup formula the graded restricted Lie algebra \(L_G=\bigoplus_i G_i/G_{i+1}\) is supported in degrees at most \(D\). Indeed the generators of a dimension subgroup come from lower central terms \(\gamma_a(G)^{p^b}\), and \(a\le c,b<e\) for nontrivial terms. Thus \(G_{D+1}=1\), which proves injectivity at cutoff \(L\). The Jennings–Quillen graded theorem identifies the associated graded group ring as a Hopf algebra with the restricted enveloping algebra of \(L_G\). Its primitives are exactly \(L_G\): filter by Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt length and note that a monomial of length at least two with individual exponents less than \(p\) has a nonzero primitive-defect coefficient, obtained by placing one chosen variable in the left factor and the remaining monomial in the right factor. No other monomial contributes that coefficient. Hence the leading homogeneous part of \(u-1\) for a recognized point comes from some \(g\in G_i/G_{i+1}\). Divide by that point's lift \([g]\) to remove the leading degree, and repeat. All actual group points satisfy the equations. This proves recognition. [Jennings](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1941-0004626-6) · [Quillen](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(68)90069-0)

Preserving these marked operations is equivalent to inducing a group homomorphism on the recognized points. The linear action on \(H_L\) is forced, since these points span it. The action on the truncated tensor square is forced by \(q\). Every group homomorphism gives those actions by the functorial group-ring maps.

## Including named operations

Use \(L=\max(D,rD)\). The linear map \([x]\mapsto [f(x)]\) into \(H_D(B)\) kills \(I^{L+1}\). To see this, apply it to \([x]\prod_i([h_i]-1)\) with \(s>L\). The result is the alternating sum of the evaluations at \(xh_I\). Use the ordered expansion in the premise and write each factor as \(1+([u_J]-1)\) in the target group ring, keeping the initial factor \([u_{\varnothing}]\). For each ordered collection of chosen nonconstant factors the alternating sum cancels unless their index sets cover \(\{1,\ldots,s\}\). This uses only inclusion–exclusion and the global order. It does not commute any factors. A collection that covers all \(s\) uses more than \(D\) factors of positive augmentation degree. Its product vanishes at cutoff \(D\). This proves the factorization.

A group-word operation has a linearization that preserves every augmentation cutoff directly. Copying one input is the coproduct into the product-group ring, filtered by total augmentation degree. The inverse on group elements is the antipode and preserves the filtration. Word concatenation evaluates by group-ring multiplication, which is filtration-preserving from the tensor product with its total filtration. Insertion of fixed constants is augmentation-preserving at degree zero and left or right multiplication by those elements preserves the filtration. Composing these steps gives the word linearization. For effective evaluation of a compressed word, one can simply evaluate its specified straight-line program on each of the finitely many basis expansion inputs below.

Include high and low truncations for each sort and product domain and the canonical maps between them. Mark the product projections, so the induced group homomorphism on the domain is the product of the indicated sort maps. Mark each linearized operation. Commutativity with these linear maps on the spanning group points is exactly the original operation-preserving equation by injectivity at cutoff \(D\). Constants are marked points. This gives full faithfulness before compiling to a tensor. No uniqueness assertion concerning all transporters of the final tensor compiler is needed.

## Effective construction

Use the truncated free associative algebra on the named generators, with each group generator represented by \(1+X_i\). A relator gives a polynomial relation by substitution and the finite geometric inverse. Quotient by the two-sided ideal generated by these relations by linear elimination. This yields \(H_L(G)\) by the universal property of the group presentation followed by quotienting by the required augmentation power. Product presentations add pairwise commutations of generators from different factors. For fixed \(L\), the number of words of length at most \(L\) in the generators is polynomial. The filtration subspaces and total-filtered tensor squares are obtained by the same elimination. A monomial of augmentation degree at most \(L\) expands as a sum of at most \(2^L\) group words of length at most \(L\). Hence each operation column can be obtained by evaluating its given program on polynomially many short inputs, substituting the resulting words in the target quotient. Apply the existing compiler for named linear and bilinear maps to obtain one 3-tensor pair. This is an informal proof.
