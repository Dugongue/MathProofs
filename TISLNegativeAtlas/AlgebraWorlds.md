# Regular forms, extraspecial quotients, and determinant corrections

Let \(H(F)\) mean deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction from special-linear to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over a fixed finite field \(F\). Inputs throughout are dense coefficient tables in displayed bases. Group inputs are compact commutator coefficients, with the prime field specified. All equivalence and failure statements below concern these representations. The proofs are informal.

## Audit of the cube-zero and extension-field transfers

I checked the source proof in *Cube-zero commutative rings and finite extension fields*, Sections 1–4. It uses ordered roles, so a swap of the two arguments of a symmetric block cannot silently swap source tensor modes. Three points in the reflection deserve emphasis.

1. The symmetric-power maps span the target symmetric powers, including in positive characteristic. The map on \(\operatorname{Sym}^j_K V\) is forced, rather than merely compatible with a selected subspace. In degree \(|F|\), comparing the linear map \(e_i\mapsto e_i^{|F|}\) with the forced symmetric-power action shows entry by entry that \(a^{|F|}=a\). This is valid in inseparable characteristic because the \(e_i^{|F|}\) are distinct elements of the monomial basis.
2. The trace-edge rank test first takes the quotient by the common radical \(P\) of the marker forms on \(X_0\). Thus the rank sum on \(X_0/P\) has no contributions from pin or dual evaluations. On an edge the nondegenerate trace pairing forces conjugacy between multiplication by \(\theta\) and by \((\alpha_c/\alpha_a)\theta\). A primitive \(\theta\) has no scalar-multiple conjugate over \(K\) except itself. The resulting \(E\)-linear similitude has determinant \(d\) over \(E\) with \(d^2\in K\), hence norm one for the chosen extension degree \(2|K^\times|\), in both characteristics. The quotient flags account for all shears in the full maps.
3. The transporter set for a tensor pair is empty or a single coset of a stabilizer. Characters separate a nonidentity coset in the **quotient by that stabilizer's determinant image**. The constant conjunction is packed only after replacement by ordinary TI queries. In the extension step, restriction of scalars requires the three specified multiplication-by-generator endomorphisms, which are order-two tensors. Without these, an \(F\)-linear comparison may be spurious. This verifies the implication \(H(K)\Rightarrow H(F)\) with both fields fixed.

No gap was found in these steps. Below I give new strengthenings whose additional assumptions and maps are explicit. [Ordinary tensor-system compiler, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2)

## 1. A marked split symmetric form suffices for strictness

For an odd fixed field \(K\), let a marked symmetric matrix space be a surjective symmetric bimap \(\beta:X\times X\to Z\) with a specified \(\lambda\in Z^*\) such that \(B_0=\lambda\beta\) is a split nondegenerate symmetric form. A transporter \((g,h)\) obeys \(\beta'(gx,gy)=h\beta(x,y)\) and \(\lambda'h=\lambda\). Equivalently \(g\) is an isometry between the two displayed split forms and compares the ordered-coordinate spaces of symmetric forms. Define orientation using \(\det h=1\), or equivalently determinant one on the transported space of forms. The inverse in the dual representation has no bearing on determinant one.

**Theorem 1.** Ordinary comparison of these marked symmetric spaces is \(\mathsf{TI}(K)\)-complete. Its determinant-one variant reduces to \(\mathsf{TI}_{SL}(K)\). The two many-one degrees coincide if and only if \(H(K)\). The same is true for **total** determinant one on \(X\oplus Z\) without a mark when a split nonsingular slice exists. A witness to existence may be supplied without restricting the isomorphisms. On the constructed marked instances every ambient transporter has \(\det g=1\).

**Forward map from tensors.** Apply the audited cube-zero construction, viewed on its radical bimap, to one arbitrary character. In its notation, choose each edge multiplicity \(r_e\) even while retaining the rank separation. Take \(\lambda\) to vanish on \(W\) and equal one on every marker vector \(z_a^0\). On \(M\oplus M^*\) the resulting form is the hyperbolic evaluation pairing. On an edge it is \(r_e\) copies of the nondegenerate trace form with multiplier \(1+\theta\). The determinant of this edge form is a square since the number of copies is even. The dimension is divisible by four, so the criterion \(\det(B)=(-1)^{\dim(B)/2}\) modulo squares makes every edge split as well. Thus \(B_0\) is split and its table is independent of the tensor coefficients for a fixed format and character.

Every symmetric bimap transporter still induces the reflected typed maps proved in the source. The restriction \(\lambda'h=\lambda\) forces all marker scalars to be one. Each edge action has determinant one by the original trace argument, and the main input and its dual cancel, so \(\det g=1\). With the mark, the determinant of \(h\) alone is exactly the prescribed character. Without the mark, the proof of the audited construction gives the character as \(\det g\det h\); in that case it is this total determinant that is tested. Conversely the blockwise lift of every typed transporter fixes marker lines pointwise and maps \(W\) to \(W\). It therefore preserves \(\lambda\). This proves reflection and lift with or without the mark.

**Reverse map.** For a marked input, form the typed system consisting of \(\beta:X\times X\to Z\), \(\lambda:Z\to K\), and the field multiplication tensor on a separate scalar line \(K\). Its normalized scalar-line map must be the identity by multiplicativity. This system has one symmetric bilinear tensor per unordered pair of input roles and an individually tagged linear tensor, exactly the hypotheses used by the symmetric cube-zero construction. Apply that construction with weight 1 on \(Z\) and zero on the other roles. Its multiplication table is one ordinary SL-TI instance for \(\det h=1\). For an unmarked input, omit the latter two constraints and use the same proof with weight 1 on both \(X\) and \(Z\). The zero character and the ordinary version give the ordinary reductions, and the fixed character conjunction gives the collapse equivalence. The construction fixes the two compared bases for each copied role. It concerns symmetric spaces with an ambient orthogonal isometry, distinct from ordinary isometry of a tuple whose every label is fixed. [Ordinary compiler and classical tensor actions](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2)

## 2. An alternating compiler and extraspecial quotients

An alternating bimap \(\beta:X\times X\to Z\) is called regular here if its output is spanned and some \(\lambda\beta\) is nondegenerate. For a marked comparison include such a \(\lambda\) and require it to be fixed. The ambient map then lies in a symplectic group and has determinant one in common symplectic coordinates. The orientation uses the displayed determinant on \(Z\).

**Theorem 2.** Over every fixed finite field \(K\), ordinary regular alternating-bimap comparison is \(\mathsf{TI}(K)\)-complete. With a marked nonsingular scalar form in standard symplectic coordinates, orientation on \(Z\) reduces to ordinary comparison if and only if \(H(K)\). In the unmarked regular class, orientation on \(X\oplus Z\) has the same collapse equivalence. A witnessing nonsingular slice may accompany an unmarked input without restricting the isomorphisms. On the marked regular class one may also orient \(X\oplus Z\) with the same answer.

Here is an exact proof of its lower direction, independent of the symmetric trace-edge construction. Start with the typed system in Section 1 of the audited cube-zero proof. For repeated-input constraints, split off a new role \(\widetilde V\), link it to \(V\) by a linear identity, and use \(V\times\widetilde V\) as the input pair. All bilinear constraints are now between distinct ordered roles, with at most one per unordered pair. All linear constraints have separate pin lines. Choose \(k_i\ge1\) with the desired character residues for each original role and zero residue for auxiliary roles. As before, for each variable role \(V_i\), create \(k_i\) output copies and specified distinct pin products identifying them. Represent each bilinear constraint in its corresponding output copy and each linear constraint by its dedicated distinct pin. All nonzero blocks assign a value to one order of their two input roles, with skew symmetry defining the reverse. This construction is alternating in characteristic two as well.

Use three special pin vectors \(e_0,e_1,e_2\) and distinct further pins \(f_j\), at least one. Give \(e_0\) the identity-copy products. Put
\[
 \eta(e_0,e_1)=\eta(e_0,e_2)=\eta(e_1,e_2)=z_*,
 \qquad
 \eta(e_0,f_j)=z_*,\quad \eta(e_1,f_j)=z_j
\]
with all \(z_j\) independent output lines separate from the common line \(z_*\). Use further pin pairs with independent outputs if necessary to make the output dimension \(Q\) divisible by \(m=|K^\times|\). Reserve \(m-1\) distinct pairs among additional pins for this purpose. The input is the sum \(M\) of the variable and pin roles. The bimap has spanned output. A common-radical vector first has no variable component by its separate \(e_0\)-products. Pairing its pure pin part with \(e_1\) detects each \(f_j\) coordinate in \(z_j\). Pairing its remaining part with any \(f_j\) detects the \(e_1\) coordinate in \(z_j\) and the \(e_0\) coordinate in \(z_*\). The \(e_2\) coordinate is then detected by pairing with \(e_0\). This proves joint nondegeneracy.

If all input roles are preserved, the three products amongst \(e_0,e_1,e_2\) force their scalars equal to \(\tau\), and the product of \(e_0\) with \(f_j\) forces every other pin scalar to equal \(\tau\). Write the action on \(V_i\) as \(\tau h_i\). The comparisons of the bimap constraints make the \(h_i\) exactly a typed transporter. An output copy of \(V_i\) has map \(\tau^2 h_i\), and every pure pin output line scales by \(\tau^2\). Thus the \(W\)-determinant is \(\tau^{2Q}\prod_i(\det h_i)^{k_i}\), which is the prescribed character.

To impose the roles intrinsically, attach \(P=M^*\) using evaluations into separate marker lines \(Kz_i^0\), with one \(z_i^0\) per role. Add dummy marker lines to make their total number \(b\equiv1\pmod m\). For every marker add an independent nondegenerate alternating space \(S_i\) of dimension \(2r_i\) with its standard form into \(Kz_i^0\). Its dimension exceeds \(D=\dim M\), the half-ranks are superincreasing, and \(r_i\equiv-(d_i+1)\pmod m\), where \(d_i\) is the role dimension or zero for a dummy marker. These parameters are bounded linearly in \(D\) for fixed fields. Put \(X=M\oplus P\oplus\bigoplus_i S_i\), \(Z=W\oplus Z_0\). The resulting alternating bimap is spanned and has no common radical, by the same radical/evaluation filtration as in the symmetric proof.

Explicitly, rank at most \(D\) first identifies the annihilator of \(Z_0\), as any nonzero marker coefficient has a rank \(>D\) restriction to its own \(S_i\). The common radical of the low-rank slices is \(X_0=P\oplus\bigoplus_i S_i\). On \(X_0\) the ranks are the distinct subset sums of \(2r_i\), identifying each marker coordinate line and its \(S_i\) on the quotient by \(P\). The evaluation between \(P\) and \(X/X_0\) identifies every main role and its dual. On a marker with multiplier \(\alpha_i\) the dual evaluation contributes \(\alpha_i^{d_i}\), the symplectic similitude contributes \(\alpha_i^{r_i}\), and the marker line contributes \(\alpha_i\). The roles contribute no other determinant after cancellation against their duals. Thus the total determinant on \(X\oplus Z\) is precisely the desired character by the chosen congruences. In the marked case, choose \(\lambda|_W=0\) and \(\lambda(z_i^0)=1\). This is a nonsingular slice, symplectic on the \(S_i\) and on \(M\oplus P\). In fact it can be written in standard symplectic coordinates by a fixed determinant-calibrated change of basis for the format. The mark forces all \(\alpha_i=1\), and the output determinant itself is then the desired character. All typed lifts with identity marker actions and \(\tau=1\) obey the mark, proving exact equality of determinant-transporter sets. The construction works also for the zero character.

For the reverse direction, pass to a symmetric cube-zero compiler using the alternating bimap as an ordinary bilinear tensor with **duplicate input roles** linked by a linear identity. Include a marked linear functional using an auxiliary scalar-line multiplication tensor when needed. The weights on \(X,Z\) for the desired character are \(1,1\) in the total-determinant case and \(0,1\) in the marked output case. The construction runs in polynomial time and gives one SL-TI query. Using the zero character gives ordinary TI, and the fixed conjunction gives each claimed collapse equivalence.

There is also a direct unital encoding \(K1\oplus X\oplus Z\) with radical product equal to this alternating bimap, and other radical products zero. It has \(J^3=0\), \(\operatorname{Ann}_J(J)=J^2\), and \(\dim A=1+2D+Q+b+2\sum_i r_i\equiv 1+Q-b=0\pmod m\). Every unit has regular determinant one. On these constructed tables ordinary unmarked isotopy reflects the typed comparison, and determinant one in the output tests the same character as determinant one in all three modes. A marked comparison on arbitrary inputs can use the reverse typed compiler above to enforce its mark.

**Group consequence.** Over \(\mathbb F_3\), the compact special class-two exponent-three group determined by \(\beta\) has Baer coordinates \(X\oplus Z\). Every transporter of \(\beta\) lifts to an additive-linear group isomorphism; all additional central shears have determinant one. A regular slice \(\lambda\beta\) means that \(G/\ker(\lambda)\) has derived subgroup and center both of order three, hence is extraspecial. Marking \(\lambda\) fixes the identification of that quotient's center with \(\mathbb F_3\). The determinant of an invertible map on \(\mathbb F_3^d\) is its permutation sign on the coordinate-ordered set, so Theorem 2 transfers to the ordinary and even isomorphism comparisons for the marked compact group model. It also transfers to the unmarked compact group model promised to admit an extraspecial quotient. Thus in either group model the even degree is strictly larger than the ordinary degree if and only if \(H(\mathbb F_3)\) fails. [Baer's correspondence](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1938-1501972-1)

## 3. A uniform result with two marked scalar forms

**Theorem 3.** Fix \(F\). For every prescribed determinant character on base-field tensor transporters, one construction over \(F\) yields an alternating bimap with two marked independent covectors, the first a nonsingular form, that realizes this character over **every** finite extension \(K/F\) simultaneously. The character image is the same subset of \(F^\times\subseteq K^\times\) in all extensions. The dimensions are polynomial in the original format, independent of \([K:F]\). The two marked form comparison and its outputs over explicitly represented \(K\) use polynomial space and time in the input and extension representation.

Use the alternating construction and always include the symmetric-power constraints over \(F\) to force \(a^{|F|}=a\) in each extension. The coefficients, bases and the Frobenius relation all lie over \(F\). Omit the \(K\)-dependent congruence choices on \(Q,b,r_i\). In addition to the mark \(\lambda_0\) above, take \(\lambda_1\) to annihilate all markers and to be one on \(z_*\) and zero on every other output block. A marked transporter fixes every marker scalar by \(\lambda_0\), while \(\lambda_1\) fixes the scalar \(\tau^2\) on \(z_*\) in \(Z/Z_0\). It therefore forces \(\tau^{2Q}=1\) in every field. The determinants of the auxiliary roles and the wanted character exponents are computed over \(F\), so choose \(k_i\) modulo \(|F^\times|\), independently of \(K^\times\). The rank reconstruction, exact lifts and proof of no other reflected maps are valid over every extension. The nonsingular \(\lambda_0\) gives ambient symplectic determinant one. The marks fix two scalar form labels and their values; the theorem does not infer a full norm oriented reduction when that norm kills the base-field determinant subgroup.

## 4. Exact pointwise determinant correction over odd fields

Let \(\beta:X\times X\to Z\) be symmetric or alternating, with zero common radical over a finite field \(K\) of odd order. Consider the kernel of the action of its automorphism group on \(Z\). Its elements are pointwise isometries of the tuple of scalar forms. Let
\[
 A=\{a\in \operatorname{End}(X):\text{there is }b\text{ with }\beta(ax,y)=\beta(x,by)\}.
\]
The common-radical condition forces uniqueness of \(b\); symmetry or alternation makes \(a^*=b\) an involution on this algebra. The pointwise isometry group is \(U(A)=\{a\in A^\times:a^*a=1\}\).

Write \(R=\operatorname{rad}(A)\) and \(\overline A=\prod_i M_{n_i}(E_i)\), with involution induced by \(*\). Let \(c_i\) be the sum of the regular simple-module multiplicities for factor \(i\) on all \(R^jX/R^{j+1}X\). Here the natural simple module is \(E_i^{n_i}\); \(c_i\) is the dimension over \(E_i\) of the image of the \(11\) matrix unit, summed over the displayed grades. Then the determinant image of \(U(A)\) is generated by the following contributions:

- An orthogonal fixed factor \(i\) contributes \((-1)^{[E_i:K]c_i}\).
- A symplectic fixed factor contributes one by the Pfaffian determinant identity.
- A unitary fixed factor contributes one by the norm-one determinant condition to the involution-fixed center.
- An exchanged pair \(i,j\) contributes \((K^\times)^{c_i-c_j}\). Norm is preserved under the induced \(K\)-isomorphism of the centers. The two matrix determinants are inverses up to this identification, and the field norm is surjective.

These contributions are exact and independent. Indeed the associated-graded regular determinant gives \(\prod_i N_{E_i/K}(\det_{E_i}\bar a_i)^{c_i}\). For surjectivity of \(U(A)\to U(\overline A)\), lift a quotient unitary to \(x\in A^\times\) and write \(x^*x=1+r\). A truncated inverse square root of \(1+r\), polynomial in \(r\), exists because \(2\) is invertible. Its coefficients can be determined recursively by equating powers in \(s^2(1+t)=1\), solving for a coefficient with multiplier \(2\) at each step. It is self-adjoint and commutes with \(1+r\), so \(x s(r)\) is a unitary lift. Unit lifts in the radical kernel have determinant one. On orthogonal factors both determinant choices are attained by the identity and a reflection. All other classical determinant statements are as above. This proves the exact formula for any tuple, including one with no nonsingular scalar form. The radical, quotient, involution types and module multiplicities are computable deterministically in polynomial time over a fixed odd field by linear algebra and the standard fixed-characteristic radical/quotient algorithm. One can detect symplectic versus orthogonal first-kind simple involutions, for example, by the distinct dimensions over the center of their symmetric subspaces. [Ivanyos and Qiao, Sections 2.5–2.6 and Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

**Nondegenerate slice criterion.** If one scalar form is nonsingular, a pointwise isometry with determinant \(-1\) exists exactly when \(X\) has an odd-dimensional common orthogonal direct summand. For sufficiency negate that summand. For necessity let \(g\) be a pointwise isometry of determinant \(-1\). The nonsingular slice pairs generalized eigenvalues \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\) with equal multiplicity over a splitting field. Eigenvalues outside \(\{1,-1\}\) have product one. The generalized \(-1\) eigenspace is therefore odd-dimensional. Its \(K\)-rational primary complement is orthogonal for every slice: \(\beta((g+1)x,y)=\beta(x,(g^{-1}+1)y)\), and the right operator is invertible on that complement while the left is nilpotent on the primary \(-1\) space. This gives the direct summand. In the alternating case a nonsingular slice forces all these summands to have even dimension, so all pointwise isometries have determinant one.

**Collision in the exact cube-zero class.** Over \(\mathbb F_3\), set \(E=\mathbb F_9\), \(X=U\oplus V_1\oplus V_2\) with each role \(E\), and \(Z=E\oplus\mathbb F_3\). Define the symmetric bimap by
\[
 \beta((u,v,w),(u',v',w'))=
 \big(uv'+u'v,\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_3}(uw'+u'w)\big).
\]
Every form is singular, since it pairs a two-dimensional \(U\) against a four-dimensional \(V_1\oplus V_2\) and vanishes within the latter. The common radical is zero and the output is spanned. The radical of the second scalar form is \(V_1\), and the common radical of the two scalar coordinates of the first output is \(V_2\). A pointwise isometry therefore preserves both and induces a map on \(X/(V_1+V_2)\cong U\). The first output, paired against \(V_1\), forces the induced action on \(U\) to be multiplication by \(\xi\in E^\times\), and on \(V_1\) multiplication by \(\xi^{-1}\): set \(u=1\) in the field product relation, then \(v=1\). The trace pairing forces the same inverse multiplier on \(V_2\). These diagonal actions all lift, and arbitrary allowed shears change no determinant. Thus the determinant on \(X\) equals \(N_{E/\mathbb F_3}(\xi)^{-1}\) and both signs occur. Any involution induces \(\xi\) with \(\xi^2=1\), has determinant one, and therefore no odd-dimensional common orthogonal summand exists. The unital commutative cube-zero algebra of \(\beta\) has layer dimensions \(6,3\), satisfying the exact even/odd radical restrictions, with \(\operatorname{Ann}_J(J)=J^2\). This exhibits a genuine difference between the pointwise orientation test with and without a nonsingular slice.

**Small output algorithm.** For two such bimap inputs, when \(z=\dim Z=O(\sqrt{\log L})\) in coefficient input length \(L\), enumerate all \(\mathrm{GL}(Z)\) maps. For each \(h\), use deterministic fixed-odd-characteristic simultaneous tuple isometry to find a \(g\) or reject that \(h\). For a fixed \(h\) the possible \(g\)-determinants form exactly one coset of the pointwise correction subgroup just computed. This yields a deterministic polynomial-time test for any prescribed ambient and output determinant pair, and for total determinant one in the commutative local ring or compact group settings. For ring input in arbitrary coordinates, include the ratio of determinants of the chosen layer-splitting bases before testing the prescribed value. Marked cases keep only \(h\) satisfying their mark equations. The enumeration has size \(|K|^{O(z^2)}\), and every remaining computation is polynomial. This bound is compatible with the strictness results since the lower reductions use output spaces whose dimensions grow linearly in the original tensor format. [Ivanyos and Qiao, Theorems 7 and 18](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.03495)

## 5. Uniform obstruction for the unmarked exact algebra model

Let \(A=K1\oplus X\oplus Z\) be scalar-residue commutative local with \(J^3=0\), \(Z=J^2\), and any complement \(X\) in \(J\). For every \(t\in K^\times\) multiplication by \(t\) on \(X\) and \(t^2\) on \(Z\) is an actual automorphism. Its determinant is \(t^{x+2z}\), where \(x=\dim X,z=\dim Z\). If all automorphism determinants are one and \(A\ne K\), then \(|K^\times|\mid x+2z\) with \(0<x+2z\le2(\dim A-1)\), hence
\[
 \dim A\ge 1+\left\lceil\frac{|K^\times|}{2}\right\rceil .
\]
In particular, an endpointwise exact determinant-image encoding of the trivial character into these unmarked algebras cannot have a dimension bound polynomial in the input tensor format and \(\log |K|\), uniformly over variable finite fields. For any format with two ordinary orbits, each self-comparison forces trivial automorphism determinant image, and the negative comparison forces nonisomorphic outputs. At most one can be the one-dimensional algebra \(K\). The bound concerns that exact, endpointwise unmarked model, while Theorem 3 uses two marks, different dimensions, and a fixed base field.

The transfer and correction theorems prove consequences of the two possible answers to \(H(\mathbb F_3)\). No implication between different characteristics or contradiction to the unrestricted explicit-input separation was used.

## 6. Forgetting the mark and allowing general compact groups

**An explicit collision upon forgetting the mark.** On the symmetric carrier of Theorem 1, fix a scalar \(\alpha\in K^\times\). Keep \(M\) and \(W\) fixed, scale the main duals and marker lines by \(\alpha\), and scale each edge space by a square root of \(\alpha\) in \(E\). The latter exists since the extension degree is even. These maps give an actual unmarked automorphism with output determinant \(\alpha^b=\alpha\), since \(b\equiv1\pmod m\). Its total determinant is one by construction. In the alternating carrier one can instead choose a symplectic similitude of any prescribed multiplier on one marker space, scale the corresponding evaluation dual by that multiplier and the marker center by the same multiplier. It has the same effect on the output and again total determinant one.

Consequently take any trivial-character carrier \(T\) over an odd field and replace its output map \(\beta\) by \(D\beta\) for \(\det D=-1\), replacing the covector by \(\lambda D^{-1}\). The immediate ordinary isomorphism is \((I,D)\). Every marked isomorphism has output determinant \(-1\) by the exact trivial-character profile, so marked output determinant one fails. Forget the covector and compose with the output-determinant \(-1\) unmarked automorphism to obtain output determinant one. This is a collision on explicit coefficient inputs and explains the use of total orientation in the unmarked conclusions.

**Marking only the subgroup with extraspecial quotient.** The compact group result also holds if the input only marks a central subgroup \(N\) with extraspecial quotient, and does not identify its center with particular scalars. Such a comparison preserves \(\ker\lambda\) and allows \(\lambda'h=s\lambda\) for an arbitrary \(s\ne0\). Represent this in the reverse typed compiler by an auxiliary scalar role with unrestricted linear action. The mark on the kernel is intrinsic to the represented subgroup, and for lower reductions the prescribed blockwise lifts preserve the actual functional. Necessity still follows from the exact unmarked **total** determinant calculation. Thus even isomorphism still has the larger degree precisely in the inequality world, even without the center identification.

**All compact exponent-three groups of class at most two.** Let the input be an alternating Baer bracket on a framed space \(L\) with \([L,[L,L]]=0\). Compute the center \(Z\) and derived space \(D=[L,L]\) by linear algebra. Pick complements \(X\) to \(Z\) and \(C\) to \(D\) in \(Z\). The split form is \(L=(X\oplus D)\oplus C\), the first summand giving a special group when it is nonabelian, and \(C\) giving an elementary abelian direct factor. Its bracket on \(X\) has zero common radical and spanned output \(D\). Two such groups are ordinarily isomorphic exactly when the dimensions of \(C\) agree and the special brackets are isomorphic. For necessity use the induced bracket map on \(L/Z\) and \(D\); for sufficiency use a direct sum lift and any isomorphism on \(C\).

When the common dimension of \(C\) is positive, negating one of its coordinates extends to an odd automorphism of the entire group, so ordinary isomorphism implies even isomorphism. Otherwise the sign is exactly the sign on the special bracket after calibrating the determinants of the two chosen framing changes. A required negative calibrated sign can be converted to positive by changing one displayed bracket coordinate at one endpoint. This also handles the abelian case directly, where groups are isomorphic exactly by dimension and always have an even comparison in the coordinate models. Hence both the ordinary and even comparisons for this broader compact class reduce, respectively, to the ordinary and even special group problems, with direct yes/no outputs on elementary cases. Since the special groups are included, the same collapse equivalence holds for the broader class.

## 7. Bounded checks

Direct rank elimination over the six prime fields of orders \(2,3,5,7,11,13\) checked 342 alternating pin configurations with different numbers of pins and adjustment products. Each had spanned output and zero common radical. For the \(\mathbb F_9\)-based symmetric collision, all 288 basis-pair identities for the eight explicit field-multiplier lifts held. Four lifts had odd determinant, while both scalar involutions were even. All 27 scalar slices were singular as predicted.
