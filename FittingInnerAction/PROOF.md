# Epimorphism counting with unbounded inner action on the Fitting commutators

Fix constants \(d,c\). Let \(G,H\) be Cayley-table groups, \(d(H)\le d\), \(F=F(H)\), and assume that \(F'\) is abelian. Put \(C=C_F(F')\), \(Q=H/F\), \(\Gamma=H/C\), and \(D=F/C\). Because \(F'\le C\), the group \(D\) is abelian and a \(Q\)-module under conjugation. Suppose \(D\) has abelian generator rank at most \(c\), or that every simple \(T\) in the socle of each \(D_p\) occurs with multiplicity at most \(c\dim_{\operatorname{End}_Q(T)}T\). Under conjugation \(B_p=(F')_p\) is a module for \(\Gamma\). Assume likewise that each simple \(T\) in its socle occurs with multiplicity at most \(c\dim_{\operatorname{End}_\Gamma(T)}T\). Then the number of epimorphisms \(G\twoheadrightarrow H\) is computable in deterministic polynomial time, and one can construct one when the count is positive. The class of \(F\), the rank of \(F'\), and the order and rank of \(D\) can all grow. This combines Fitting-kernel enumeration and anchored commutator recovery with two different applications of the coinduced rank bound.

The earlier Fitting-subgroup enumeration produces all quotient epimorphisms \(\psi:G\to Q\) that can arise from full epimorphisms. It takes intersections of centralizers of the surviving chief sections along one chief series, giving at most \(|G|\) kernels, then tests quotient isomorphisms by images of at most \(d\) target generators. Fix generators \(h_1,\ldots,h_d\) of \(H\) and enumerate compatible preimage tuples \(x_1,\ldots,x_d\) in \(G\). For each such tuple, enumerate lifts \(\eta:G\to\Gamma\) over \(\psi\) with \(\eta(x_i)=h_iC\). They are solutions to an affine system over \(D\) and are all onto by the anchors. If \(L=\ker\psi\), the homogeneous differences vanish on \(K=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_d\rangle\), and \(G=LK\). A difference is therefore determined by its restriction to \(L\), which is an equivariant map \(L_{\mathrm{ab}}\to D\). The number of these maps is at most \(|G|^c\) by the rank bound on the abelian group \(D\), or by the coinduced module argument just below. Smith form lists the affine solutions to \(\eta\) in polynomial time.

Write \(L=\ker\psi\), \(S=\ker\eta\), and \(M=L'/([L',L']\,[S,L'])\). This is a finite abelian group on which conjugation descends through \(\Gamma\). Every putative epimorphism \(f\) over \(\eta\) restricts to an equivariant map \(b:M\to F'\), since \(f(L)\subseteq F\), \(f(S)\subseteq C\) and \(C\) centralizes \(F'\).

There are at most \(|M|^c\) such \(b\). For each primary component of exponent \(p^a\) in \(F'\), use the group ring \(R=(\mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z)[\Gamma]\) and \(J=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z/p^a}(R,\mathbb Z/p^a)\). The adjunction given by evaluation at 1 identifies the maps into \(J\) with additive characters into \(\mathbb Z/p^a\). This functor extends across inclusions because characters extend into \(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\), and the exponent prohibits values outside its \(p^a\)-torsion. The socle multiplicity of a simple \(T\) in \(J\) is \(\dim_{\operatorname{End}_\Gamma(T)}T\), since its group of additive characters has order \(|T|\). By hypothesis the socle of \(B_p\) embeds in \(J^c\), and the extended map on all of \(B_p\) is injective because its kernel would otherwise contain a simple submodule. Thus, after reducing the source primary component modulo \(p^a\), the number of maps into \(B_p\) is no larger than the order of that component to the power \(c\). Multiply over \(p\). Enumerate the maps by Smith form for the equivariant linear equations, capping the enumeration at \(|G|^c\) on arbitrary inputs.

Express generators \(n_j\) of \(F\) by words in the \(h_i\), giving elements \(u_j\in L\) by the same words in the \(x_i\). For each \(l\in L\), scan \(a\in F\) satisfying
\[
[a,n_j]=b([l,u_j])\quad\text{for all }j.
\]
Each argument of \(b\) is read in \(M\). A nonempty solution set is one \(Z(F)\)-coset, since its elements give the same inner action on the generating tuple of \(F\). Use these cosets and the marked words to specify the map on all \(G\) to \(H/Z(F)\). Check homomorphism equations and compatibility with \(\eta\), which makes sense since \(Z(F)\le C\). Through the abelian kernel \(Z(F)\), count affine lifts with the prescribed anchor values and with \(f|_{L'}=b\). The chosen anchors force surjectivity. Each actual epimorphism occurs at exactly its own \(\psi,\eta,b\) for each preimage tuple. If \(|H|\mid |G|\) divide the sum by \((|G|/|H|)^d\), and otherwise return zero.

The generator hypothesis can be broadened to \(d(H/Z(F))\le d\) when the kernel \(Z(F)\) satisfies the head-endomorphism condition in *Exact counting of epimorphic lifts through nonsemisimple abelian kernels*. Choose \(h_i\) lifting generators of \(H/Z(F)\) and choose the words \(n_j\) only to generate \(F/Z(F)\). Conjugation comparisons on these words still determine the coset because every conjugation by \(F\) fixes its center. Check that the recovered map hits each \(h_iZ(F)\) at \(x_i\), making it onto \(H/Z(F)\). Invoke the head theorem to count its actual epimorphic lifts with the same point constraints. No other count changes.

For an example with all the parameters unbounded, take \(H=(D_{2^s})^Q\rtimes Q\), where \(Q\) ranges over odd-degree alternating groups and acts regularly on the factors, and \(D_{2^s}\) has order \(2^s\), \(s\ge4\). The Fitting subgroup is the base, since it is nilpotent and \(Q\) is Fitting-free. The group \(D=F/C\) is the regular \(\mathbb F_2[Q]\)-module, since the reflections act by coordinatewise inversion on \(F'\). The derived group \(F'=(C_{2^{s-2}})^Q\) has its socle in its subgroup of elements killed by two. On that subgroup the inversion actions are trivial, and the subgroup is the regular \(\mathbb F_2[Q]\)-module inflated to \(\Gamma\). Every centralizer-quotient simple occurring there is therefore an inflated \(Q\)-simple, with the same endomorphisms. The regular module is coinduced, by pairing group-ring elements using the coefficient of 1 in their product, so \(c=1\) suffices for both module hypotheses. Two generators of a base factor and the bounded generators of \(Q\) generate \(H\). Both abelian ranks and the nilpotence class grow. This proof is informal.
