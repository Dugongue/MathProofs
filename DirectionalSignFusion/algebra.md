# Parity of a local algebra exchanges the two directional answers

Let \(F=\mathbb F_3\), \(A=\mathsf{TI}_{SL}(F)\), and \(B_R=\mathsf{TI}(R)\) for dense three-tensors on free modules over a fixed finite commutative local ring \(R\) with residue field \(F\). Write \(\mathfrak m=\operatorname{rad}R\). Fix a nonzero \(s\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(\mathfrak m)\) and fix a determinant triple \(\omega\in(R^\times)^3\) with residue \((-1,1,1)\). The direction can include any principal-unit factors. In particular for \(R=\mathbb Z/3^k\) the nontrivial coordinate of \(\omega\) can be a unit of order \(2\cdot3^a\). Define the two directional answers on ordinary-positive \(R\)-pairs by
\[
 D^R_\omega=[\omega\text{ attained}]\wedge[(1,1,1)\text{ not attained}],
 \qquad \neg D^R_\omega .
\]

The source class consists of the entrywise images under \(c\mapsto s\widetilde c\) of tensors over \(F\) that are independently reframed multiplication tensors of **single** scalar-residue commutative local \(F\)-algebras. Require \(J^3=0\) and \(\operatorname{Ann}_J(J)=J^2\), and require the radical bimap to have scalar centroid and a split nonsingular scalar slice. The source promise fixes the parity of the algebra dimension at both endpoints and requires ordinary isomorphism. The tables may come with the indicated coordinates and a witnessing slice, neither of which constrains the transporter.

**Theorem.** The existence of a single deterministic polynomial-time many-one compiler to unrestricted \(B_R\) has the following exact characterization. The arrows in the table are reductions of **total ternary languages**, independent of the source promise.

| Parity of local algebra dimension | Positive answer \(D^R_\omega\) | Negative answer \(\neg D^R_\omega\) |
|---|---|---|
| Even | \(A\le_m^P B_R\) | \(\overline A\le_m^P B_R\) |
| Odd | \(\overline A\le_m^P B_R\) | \(A\le_m^P B_R\) |

The same statements hold for the target \(B_F\), as well as when the ordinary \(R\) target is required to be supported on \(sF\). In those cases the two conditions are, respectively, the signed collapse \(H\) and the conjunction of \(H\) with closure of ordinary ternary TI under complement. The equivalence for unrestricted \(B_R\) does not require a reduction \(B_R\le_m^P B_F\).

The main new construction is a swap test inside one local radical algebra, followed by a scalar-centroid determinant compiler of independent parity. This avoids requiring that the output preserve a product decomposition. The proof also establishes fixed AND and OR aggregation in ordinary \(B_R\) directly for arbitrary \(R\)-tensors. It is an informal proof.

## 1. Full determinants on the chosen socle line

The map \(\iota:F\to R\), \(c\mapsto s\widetilde c\), does not depend on the chosen lift \(\widetilde c\). It is injective and \(a\iota(c)=\iota(\bar a c)\) for \(a\in R\), where \(\bar a\) is its residue. A nonzero residue lifts to a unit, proving injectivity. Consequently an \(R\)-triple transports two supported tensors if and only if its residue triple transports the extracted ternary tensors. Every invertible residue matrix lifts, since its lifted determinant is a unit. Furthermore any prescribed full unit determinant lifting its residue can be attained by multiplying a single coordinate by a suitable unit congruent to \(1\) modulo \(\mathfrak m\). The supported tensor is unaffected by these corrections, which are independent across the three modes. Thus the complete attainable determinant profile over \(R\) is the full inverse image of the residue profile. Explicit reflection witnesses lift using the specified unit \(\omega\); any other supplied ordinary ternary witness can also be lifted directly.

This proves ordinary-promise preservation and exact preservation of both directional answers, not just ordinary reflection of a comparison. A purely principal direction whose residue is the identity would yield an empty positive answer throughout this support, because feasibility of that tuple equals feasibility of the identity. No assertion about unsupported inputs in that purely principal direction is made. The full profile calculation is the cyclic-ideal restriction of the earlier *Algebra.md*, entries A01–A02, of the 500 atlas.

## 2. Scalar-centroid character realizations of either parity

Use the weighted cube-zero character encoder of *TISLDirectionalGap/WeightedCubeZero.md* and its scalar-centroid refinement in *TISLSquareZeroDirect/ScalarCentroid.md*. For a ternary tensor \(T\) and a determinant character \(\chi:(\mathbb F_2)^3\to\mathbb F_2\), it constructs a local algebra \(C_\chi(T)\). All relative algebra-isomorphism determinant bits between \(C_\chi(T),C_\chi(U)\) are exactly the set \(\chi(P(T,U))\), including the empty case. Every original transporter lifts by explicit graded maps. We use this encoder with independently specified parity of \(\dim C_\chi(T)\), retaining scalar centroid and a split nonsingular slice.

Here is why the scalar-centroid refinement works in both parities. In its main role space \(M\) choose \(\dim M=D\) of the desired parity, adding a further pure pin if needed. The pure pins \(f_j\) have enough unused pairs. The additional products \(f_{j+1}f_{j+2}=z_j=f_j^2\) ensure that all main output coordinates are generated by cross-role products. They are compatible with the common pin scale, and do not change role reflection or the zero common radical since this pair omits \(f_j\). Choose the main output dimension \(Q\) even with a reserve product, and choose the trace-cycle marker count \(b\) with \(b\equiv D\bmod 2\), adding unused markers if needed. Choose all edge multiplicities even and superincreasing. The trace-edge determinant is one, the pin factor \(\tau^{2Q}\) is one, and the dual-evaluation plus marker factor \(\alpha^{D+b}\) is one. This is the complete parity dependence of the determinant calculation. The final dimension \(1+2D+Q+b+\sum_i 4r_i\) is \(1+D\bmod 2\). Thus the opposite choice of \(D\) flips the dimension without altering the weighted result. The centroid computation uses preservation of the edge planes and the fact that the cross-role products span the main output, so it is independent of these parity choices. A scalar slice that is \(1\) on each marker and zero on the main output is nonsingular and split: the evaluation summand is hyperbolic and the trace edges occur in even multiplicities and dimension divisible by four. Both parity versions retain \(J^3=0\) and \(\operatorname{Ann}_J(J)=J^2\).

For normalized multiplication of a unital associative algebra, every isotopy is
\[
 (L_a\phi,\ R_b\phi,\ L_aR_b\phi),
\]
where \(\phi\) is an algebra isomorphism and \(a,b\) are target units. This follows by evaluating at each unit and substituting. Over \(F\) the product of the three signs is \(\det\phi\), regardless of the unit determinants. For a scalar-residue local algebra, left and right unit determinants are each the residue of the unit to the power \(\dim C\) by the radical filtration. Therefore in even dimension normalized signs are \(\{t(1,1,1): t\in\chi(P)\}\). In odd dimension the signs are \(\{v:v_1+v_2+v_3\in\chi(P)\}\), since left and right unit signs are independently arbitrary and generate the even-total plane \(E_0\). In these formulas sign bits are additive, and changing either endpoint's raw multiplication frame translates the whole set by a known sign vector.

Consequently, on every independently reframed ordinary YES in the even class the directional predicate is precisely feasibility of \(e_1=(1,0,0)\): a coset contained in a translate of the diagonal subgroup cannot contain both \(0,e_1\). On every ordinary YES in the odd class the directional predicate is exactly infeasibility of \(0\): the coset is invariant under the even-total plane, and when it omits \(0\) it must be the odd-total plane, which contains \(e_1\). The chosen socle lift carries both conclusions to \(R\).

## 3. Ordinary nonisomorphism already enters on one scalar-centroid local factor

For any ordinary ternary comparison \(x=(T,U)\), first make matching positive formats or handle the syntactic edge cases by fixed inputs. Use the even-dimensional, zero-character scalar-centroid encodings of Section 2. Write their two radical bimaps as \(\beta_T:X_T\times X_T\to Z_T\) and \(\beta_U:X_U\times X_U\to Z_U\). Build **one** local algebra \(P_x\) by taking these two radical products orthogonal to one another, with just one common adjoined scalar unit. The radical of each original \(C_0\) has odd dimension. Each bimap has scalar centroid, spanned output and zero common radical. The centroid of their direct sum is \(F\times F\): its tangent map cannot mix the two summands, because a centroid equality on two orthogonal vectors would force the mixed component into a common radical. Its output map then preserves each spanning output, and on each pair the centroid is scalar. It follows that a bimap transporter permutes the two centroid idempotents and hence either preserves or swaps the two full layers of the radical factors.

On a preserving automorphism both factor signs are positive by the zero character. A swap exists exactly when \(T\simeq U\) ordinarily, since any bimap map lifts to a unital algebra isomorphism of the original cube-zero factors. If a swap exists, choose any factor isomorphism and its inverse. Their internal determinants cancel, and interchanging the odd-dimensional radical blocks has determinant \(-1\). The unit and shears contribute \(+1\). Therefore \(\det\operatorname{Aut}(P_x)=\{1\}\) for ordinary NO and \(\{\pm1\}\) for ordinary YES. This is a switch inside a single scalar-residue local algebra with cube-zero radical, without using central idempotents of the unital algebra.

Now feed the normalized multiplication tensor \(\mu_{P_x}\) as a self-input to the total determinant character of Section 2. Its character image is the exact algebra automorphism determinant image just computed, by cancellation of the left and right units. Output the new scalar-centroid local algebra of the desired dimension parity, and use its normalized multiplication tensor \(M\). Apply the following fixed target reframings.

- For the **odd positive** answer, compare \(M\) with its reflection of bit \(e_1\). Its self-spectrum was \(E_0\) or all of \((\mathbb F_2)^3\) according as \(x\) was ordinary NO or YES. Thus the new positive directional answer is precisely \([x\notin B_F]\).
- For the **even negative** answer, compare \(M\) with its reflection of bit \(e_1+(1,1,1)\). The self-spectrum was \(\{0\}\) or \(\{0,(1,1,1)\}\). After the shift it always omits \(0\) and contains \(e_1\) precisely on ordinary YES, so the new negative directional answer is again \([x\notin B_F]\).

Each gate has an explicit ordinary witness, namely its fixed reflection tuple on a self-pair. Both final tensors are reframed multiplications of a **single scalar-centroid local algebra**. Thus the co-TI obstruction was preserved while the intermediate two-summand radical centroid was removed. Socle lift preserves both gates for the prescribed full \(R\) determinants by Section 1.

## 4. Ordinary fixed AND and OR work over the full local ring target

For an arbitrary dense bimap \(T:U\times V\to W\) of positive free formats over \(R\), use the triangular unital algebra with diagonal \(R^3\), radical corners \(U,V,W\) at \(01,12,02\), and unique corner product \(U\times V\xrightarrow{T} W\). The ideal consisting of the corner spaces and diagonal maximal ideals is nilpotent, and its quotient is \(F^3\). Given an isomorphism, first align the lifted primitive quotient idempotents by a radical unit. If two systems \((e_i),(f_i)\) agree modulo the radical then \(\sum_i f_i e_i\) is a unit intertwining them. Their directed corner order then forces each vertex to its own position, and the remaining equations are exactly \(R\)-linear tensor isomorphism. Conversely every tensor transporter extends.

For tags, replace all three diagonal copies of \(R\) by \(R[t]/(t^j)\), with action on each corner by evaluation at \(0\), and choose different fixed \(j\)'s. Associates acting on diagonal factors still have residue \(F^3\). Their diagonal corners have different free \(R\)-ranks for different \(j\)'s, making these tag types noninterchangeable. Each tagged algebra is centrally indecomposable, because a central idempotent must be diagonal and each nonzero arrow forces the same value at two incident diagonal idempotents. Ordinary AND of two comparisons is the product of their two tagged algebras. The category comparison is exactly the conjunction by tag recovery. The same construction handles any fixed number.

For OR, represent an ordered pair of input tensors by the multiplication tensor of the product of their tagged algebras. Wrap this multiplication tensor again in the triangular construction to give a centrally indecomposable algebra \(D(T,W)\) reflecting the ordered comparison. Given two ordinary input pairs \((T,U)\) and \((W,Z)\), compare
\[
 D(T,W)\times D(U,Z)\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad D(T,Z)\times D(U,W).
\]
One possible matching succeeds exactly when \(W\simeq Z\), and the other exactly when \(T\simeq U\). Every algebra isomorphism gives such a matching because the component central idempotents are intrinsic. Conversion of any of these free unital algebras to its multiplication tensor reflects ordinary algebra isomorphism by unit normalization. If left or right multiplication by an element of a unital algebra is bijective, evaluating its inverse at \(1\) gives a one-sided inverse, and injectivity on the opposite composite gives a two-sided inverse. Thus the normalization remains valid over a local ring. All displayed dimensions are polynomial under a fixed number of operations. This proves positive Boolean aggregation in unrestricted \(B_R\), including mixed characteristic, without using complement or assuming \(B_R\le_m B_F\). The underlying triangular encoding over fields is due to Grochow and Qiao. [Original algebra–tensor comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)

## 5. Completing all four equivalences

The upper bounds follow from Section 2 by pulling back the supplied socle-supported tensor entries to \(F\). On the promise, each easier rail is one signed ternary test, after reflection of the first target mode if needed. Each other rail is one signed ternary NO test on the same pairs. Thus the asserted total language reduction to \(B_R\) suffices. The determinant targets over \(R\) are the original full units by Section 1.

For the easier rails, given an arbitrary ternary pair and a nonzero character \(\chi\), use the selected parity encoder of Section 2 on the two original tensors. In the even case reflect mode one, so the positive rail asks whether the character takes zero. In the odd case leave the normalized pair, so the negative rail asks whether the character takes zero. The ordinary YES promise is preserved in each case. The seven characters detect whether the ternary sign coset contains zero. On ordinary NO, an ordinary guard, carried to \(B_R\) by a socle lift, makes the conjunction false regardless of what the promised compilers do. Fixed AND from Section 4 gives \(A\le_m^P B_R\).

For the other rails use the complements of these same character tests: on ordinary YES they ask whether character zero is missing. A ternary signed NO is exactly ordinary NO, or ordinary YES with some character zero missing. In the first branch substitute the unconditional ordinary-YES co-TI gate in Section 3 on that rail, and in the second use the ordinary guard and disjoin the seven missing-character queries. The whole decoder is positive and fixed. Section 4 compiles it to one ordinary \(R\) instance, proving \(\overline A\le_m^P B_R\). This does not suppose that the target ring has a complement compiler. In particular the ordinary nonisomorphism obstruction survives on these local carriers even when an ordinary source witness accompanies each gate.

For \(R=F\), or for the ordinary target restricted to a single fixed socle line, the two conditions specialize respectively to \(H\), and to \(H\) conjoined with \(\overline B_F\le_m^P B_F\). For the latter, padding unused coordinate lines gives \(B_F\le_m A\), hence \(\overline B_F\le_m\overline A\); if \(\overline A\le_m B_F\) then the complement map converts that same reduction into a reduction of \(A\). The converse applies the ordinary complement map to any reduction of \(A\). This is exactly the original directional-gap equivalence for the unrestricted source, but here it appears on only one of the two rails of each fixed local dimension parity, with the roles reversed at the other parity. [Original directional-gap theorem and character guards](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/TISLDirectionalGap)

No unconditional collapse \(H\), signed co-TI compiler, or unrestricted ring-to-field ordinary reduction is asserted.
