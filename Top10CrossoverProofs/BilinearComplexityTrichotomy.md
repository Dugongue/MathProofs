# Exact complexity of the finite-image and infinite-image bilinear problems

Let \(R\) be a fixed commutative unital algebra finitely generated over \(\mathbb Z\). Let \(U,V,W\) be finitely generated \(R\)-modules, and fix an \(R\)-bilinear map \(B:U\times V\to W\). The raw equations have the form \(\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}B(u_i,v_j)=c\), using coefficients in \(R\) and constant right sides \(c\). Write \(I\subseteq W\) for the \(R\)-submodule generated by the values.

**Theorem.** On instances with right sides in the value module \(I\), the following classification holds.

| Value module | Decision complexity |
| --- | --- |
| Zero | Every instance is solvable. |
| Finite and nonzero | NP-complete. Hardness holds for equations with at most two signed pairing terms and with bounded variable occurrences. |
| Infinite | Computably enumerable complete, even with a fixed bound on the numbers of variables and equations. |

The finite branch uses right sides named in a fixed finite table, and coefficients may be integer polynomials in fixed generators of \(R\) given as sparse monomials with binary exponents or as arithmetic circuits. The same polynomial-time conclusions hold with right sides encoded in another fixed ambient module representation whenever equality to each entry of the finite table is polynomial-time decidable. Right sides outside \(I\) then reject. Computably enumerable completeness holds in the usual effective ambient presentations without this efficiency requirement. The slot of a given finitely presented \(B\) is computable uniformly.

## The finite branch

Quotient by the two radicals. The left quotient embeds as a set into a finite power of \(I\) by testing against a fixed generating tuple of the right module, and conversely. The resulting modules \(U_0,V_0\) are therefore finite. Solutions over them lift to solutions over the original modules. Certificates in the finite value-table representation simply give one value of \(U_0\) or \(V_0\) per variable. Evaluate an input coefficient on the fixed finite module \(I\) by multiplication and addition in its finite scalar action. Binary exponentiation handles sparse monomial powers, and each arithmetic-circuit gate is evaluated in that finite action. This proves membership in NP.

For hardness take frame variables \((a_e)_{e\in U_0}\) and \((b_f)_{f\in V_0}\) indexed by every distinct element of their two finite sets. Impose \(B(a_e,b_f)=B(e,f)\) for every pair. If \(a_e=a_{e'}\), testing the equations against all right indices gives \(B(e-e',f)=0\) for every \(f\), so \(e=e'\). The left assignment is injective and therefore bijective. The same holds on the right. For the left assignment, any \(R\)-linear relation between the \(e\)'s also holds among the \(a_e\)'s, by testing its image against all \(b_f\) and using bijectivity and zero radical. Both frames are thus \(R\)-module automorphisms preserving the pairing. Normalizing a solution by their inverses makes them the standard frames.

Let \(A\) be the algebra of adjoint pairs \((P,Q)\), satisfying \(B(Pu,v)=B(u,Qv)\) and composed in the reverse order in the second coordinate. Let \(C=Z(A)\). It is a finite commutative unital ring with \(1\ne0\), since both quotient modules are nonzero. Encode a variable from \(C\) by its left and right columns \(p_e,q_f\). The two-term tests \(B(p_e,f)=B(e,q_f)\) impose the adjoint equations and also enforce the \(R\)-linear dependencies between the intended columns. For example, if \(\sum r_e e=0\), the pairing of \(\sum r_e p_e\) with any \(f\) is \(B(\sum r_e e,q_f)=0\), so \(\sum r_e p_e=0\). The right argument is the same.

For each of the finitely many adjoints \((E,F)\) impose \(B(p_e,Ff)=B(Ee,q_f)\). This is the equation \(EP=PE\), by zero radical and the use of all columns. The first coordinate of an adjoint determines its second, which makes this test equivalent to commuting in \(A\). All the displayed constants \(e,f,Ee,Ff\) are named by the frame. A multiplication gate \(XY=Z\) is given by

\[
B(p_e^Y,q_f^X)=B(p_e^Z,f).
\]

The adjoint identity and the tests on columns verify the gate exactly. An equality or a successor gate is given by subtracting the left columns tested on \(f\); the right side is respectively \(0\) or \(B(e,f)\). Zero and one are pinned by frame columns.

Addition requires only equality, product, and successor gates. In any commutative ring, the equation \((zx+1)(zy+1)=z^2(xy+1)+1\) says \(z(x+y-z)=0\). The same equation at \((z+1,x+1,y)\) says \((z+1)(x+y-z)=0\). Together they are equivalent to \(x+y=z\), by subtraction. Every expression in these two equations uses only product and successor gates. This gives a fixed two-term gate compiler already used in the integral pairing construction. [Two pairing terms on a path](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/PortfolioTop30/ManinExtensions.md#3-two-pairing-terms-on-a-path-suffice).

Now reduce Boolean 3-SAT. For each Boolean variable require \(x^2=x\) in \(C\), and for each clause with literal values \(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\) require \((1-\ell_1)(1-\ell_2)(1-\ell_3)=0\). Complement literals are imposed by \(x+\ell=1\). A Boolean solution maps to \(0,1\) in \(C\). Conversely reduce any ring solution modulo any fixed maximal ideal of \(C\), where all idempotents are \(0\) or \(1\), so the original clauses hold. The ring and every gate template depend only on \(B\). Using the compiler above gives signed pairing coefficients, the fixed right-side table of zero and frame values, and size linear in the number of circuit gates after Boolean fan-out is split into equality wires.

Bounded occurrences follow by making a private frame for each gate template. Order these templates in a chain and join consecutive frames with the fixed cross tests \(B(a_e^{(h)},b_f^{(h+1)})=B(e,f)\). Comparison with the local equations first identifies the right frames by zero radical, then identifies the left frames. Thus all assignments have one simultaneous normalization. Each frame coordinate is used only in its own template and in the two neighboring cross tests. Split each scalar wire with high fan-out into chain copies joined by two-term equality tests, so each scalar coordinate appears in at most a fixed number of gates. The bounds depend on the fixed bilinear map, not on the instance. Any repeated pairing terms in a gate can be assigned separate variables and identified by equality tests to retain literal signed coefficients. This proves the finite branch.

## The infinite branch

The established finite-image proof gives, for every infinite \(I\), a fixed raw compilation of polynomial solvability over an infinite finitely generated commutative ring \(C'\). More precisely the prime-support step chooses one \(t\in R\) and a prime quotient so \(tB\) has nonzero torsion-free image over an infinite domain. Its domains are finite modules. The fixed-frame and adjoint-center compiler for that pairing interprets the infinite center \(C'\), a finite algebra over the prime quotient. Output equations of the compilation lift to the original \(B\) by multiplying the lifted coefficients by \(t\). The two source quotient maps are surjective, so this preserves solutions in both directions. [Finite-image proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/PortfolioTop30/ManinExtensions.md#1-the-finite-image-dichotomy).

For completeness of the lower bound, the ring-interpretation form of the finitely generated ring theorem supplies one of two fixed computable interpretations in \(C'\). The target is \(\mathbb F_p[T]\) for some prime \(p\), or the ring of integers \(\mathcal O_L\) of a number field. In the latter, \(\mathbb Z\) is Diophantine by Koymans–Pagano and halting admits a fixed Diophantine parameter predicate by DPRM. In the polynomial-ring case, Demeyer's theorem supplies a Diophantine definition of the computably enumerable set of powers \(T^e\) for halting indices \(e\). In either interpretation a representative of the parameter is computable by evaluating the formula for its basic ring operations. Because the operations are fixed existential conjunctions and the domain has an effective enumeration, one can search for a witness representing each computed operation; this search terminates. The fixed existential parameter predicate, translated and compiled, has a bounded number of raw variables and equations with only its constants varying. The compiled right sides lie in \(tI\subseteq I\). This reduces halting to the raw problem. The upper bound enumerates tuples over \(U,V\) and verifies each system by effective module equality. [Ring interpretations, Theorem 2.17](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02573) · [Koymans–Pagano](https://doi.org/10.1090/jams/1081) · [Demeyer](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-007-0078-6).

## Determining the slot

The zero case is tested on the finite pairwise generator values. Compute \(\operatorname{Ann}_R I\) by module syzygies, and put \(S=R/\operatorname{Ann}_R I\). If \(I\) is finite, the faithful action of \(S\) embeds \(S\) into the endomorphism ring of its finite additive group. Conversely a finite \(S\) makes \(I\) finite because \(I\) is a finitely generated \(S\)-module. Thus it suffices to determine finiteness of a presented finite-type \(\mathbb Z\)-algebra.

By elimination compute the kernel of \(\mathbb Z\to S\). If it is zero then \(S\) is infinite. Otherwise its generator \(n>0\) can be factored effectively. By the Chinese remainder theorem one can work on the prime-power characteristic factors. For a factor \(S'\) killed by \(p^a\), it is finite exactly when \(S'/pS'\) is finite. The reverse implication follows because each \(p^jS'/p^{j+1}S'\) is an additive quotient of \(S'/pS'\). The finite-field algebra \(S'/pS'\) is finite exactly when it has finite vector-space dimension, which is decided by the standard monomials of a Gröbner basis. This decides the slot including mixed characteristic and nonreduced presentations.
