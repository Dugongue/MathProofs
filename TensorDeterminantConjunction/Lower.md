# Koszul reduction for signed Frobenius multiplication tensors

**Theorem.** Over $F=\mathbb F_3$, the L5 comparison on raw multiplication tensors whose recovered algebras have complete-intersection associated graded rings reduces by a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism over $F$. The condition on the associated graded ring can itself be recognized in polynomial time. Relation degrees are unrestricted, and the input algebra need not be graded. This gives the same reduction on the corresponding part of L7. The comparison of the unrestricted L5 and L7 languages with the endpoints remains open here. The work is an informal proof.

## 1. Recover the graded ring and the Koszul homology

L5 tests the determinant in the first raw mode when the two tensors are multiplication tensors of commutative scalar-residue local algebras with a one-dimensional socle. Use the earlier raw recognizer to recover each algebra, its multiplication coordinates, and the original first-mode basis calibration. Let $R$ be a recovered algebra, $J$ its radical, $n=\dim R$, $G=\operatorname{gr}_J R=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}G_j$, and $V=G_1$ of dimension $e$. The radical-power filtration gives $\det(\phi)$ as the product of the grade determinants for every isomorphism $\phi:R\to R'$, in frames adapted to that filtration.

The case $e=0$ has $R=F$ and is elementary. For $e>0$, $G$ is a connected graded quotient of $S=\operatorname{Sym} V$, generated by $V$. Write $I=\ker(S\to G)$. If $I$ is generated by a homogeneous regular sequence $f_1,\dots,f_e$, each relation degree $d_i$ is at least two. The regular-sequence Hilbert series gives $n=\prod_i d_i\ge 2^e$. Test this size bound before forming any large exterior space. Under it the Koszul complex
$$
C_i=G\otimes\bigwedge^i V
$$
has total dimension $n2^e\le n^2$. Its differential is multiplication by a tangent generator followed by deletion of that generator from the wedge, with the usual alternating sign. It respects internal degree $m$ and has summand $C_{i,m}=G_{m-i}\otimes\bigwedge^i V$. Compute cycles, boundaries and their quotients by exact linear algebra.

The graded space $W=H_1(C)$ identifies with $I/S_+I$ by the exact sequence obtained from $0\to I\to S\to G\to0$ and tensoring over $S$ with $F=S/S_+$. Thus $\dim W=e$ is the recognition test for a complete intersection. If it holds, homogeneous lifts generate $I$ by induction on degree. They form a homogeneous system of parameters in the polynomial ring because $G$ has finite dimension. A homogeneous system of parameters in a polynomial ring is a regular sequence. This verifies both directions of recognition. [Stacks Project, Cohen–Macaulay local rings](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00N7)

For a complete intersection there is a natural isomorphism of algebras graded by homological degree and internal degree,
$$
H_*(C)\cong\bigwedge W ,
$$
where each $W_d$ in internal degree $d$ has homological degree one. Here “natural” means that the multiplication map from the exterior algebra on $H_1(C)$ gives this isomorphism, so arbitrary original algebra transporters induce its exact actions. To prove it, compute with the double complex $K_S(f_1,\dots,f_e)\otimes_S K_S(V)$. Both augmentations from the total complex, one to $G\otimes_S K_S(V)$ and the other to $K_S(f_1,\dots,f_e)\otimes_S F$, are multiplicative quasi-isomorphisms. Indeed, filter by the degrees of one Koszul factor and use exactness of the other except at degree zero, and the freeness of all the chain modules. The differential of $K_S(f)\otimes_S F$ is zero. Its bigraded algebra is exterior on the classes of the regular sequence, so the multiplication from $H_1(C)$ generates the asserted isomorphism. This also provides a direct check by multiplication of cycle bases within the computed polynomial-size complex. [Stacks Project, Koszul regular sequences](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/062D)

## 2. Move the entire determinant to at most $2e$ dimensions

Fix computed bases of $G_j$, of $V=G_1$ and of $W_d$. Give $C_{i,m}$ the tensor-product basis built from $G_{m-i}$ and the induced monomial wedge basis of $V$. In $H_{i,m}$ use the homology classes of wedge monomials in the ordered homogeneous $W$ basis. This is computed by taking cycle representatives and multiplying them in $C$, then reducing modulo boundaries. For the input pair, reject if the dimensions of corresponding $G_j$ or $W_d$ differ.

For an isomorphism $\phi$, write $s_j=\det(\phi:G_j\to G'_j)$, $v=\det(\phi:V\to V')$ and $w_d=\det(\phi:W_d\to W'_d)$ in the fixed bases. On $C_{i,m}$ the determinant equals
$$
s_{m-i}^{\binom ei}v^{\dim G_{m-i}\binom{e-1}{i-1}} .
$$
Zero summands contribute one. The formula follows from the determinant of a tensor product and the determinant of an exterior power. In $H_{i,m}$ the determinant is a computable monomial in the $w_d$. Indeed the monomial wedge basis decomposes $H_{i,m}$ into tensor products $\bigotimes_d\bigwedge^{k_d}W_d$ with $\sum_d k_d=i$ and $\sum_d d k_d=m$. The induced map is the tensor product of the exterior actions on these factors. Enumerating the wedge-subsets of the $e$ basis positions takes at most $2^e$ trials and computes all required exponents, counted modulo two.

For each fixed internal $m$, choose in $C_{i,m}$ a basis obtained by concatenating a boundary basis, chosen lifts of the $H_{i,m}$ basis, and lifts under the differential of the boundary basis in $C_{i-1,m}$. Each such frame matrix is explicit in the tensor-product basis. Any chain isomorphism acts triangularly in these adapted frames. Its determinant is the product of the two boundary actions and the homology action. Taking products over $i$ cancels all boundary factors because they occur twice and every nonzero scalar over $F$ squares to one. Thus the identity in the original coordinate bases is
$$
\prod_i \det(\phi:C_{i,m}\to C'_{i,m})
   =c_m\prod_i \det(\phi:H_{i,m}\to H'_{i,m}),
$$
with $c_m$ the product of the target-to-source adapted-frame determinant ratios. These constants are computed separately on each object. The $i=0$ factor on the left is $s_m$. All other $s_j$ on the left have $j<m$. Induction on $m$ through the last nonzero algebra grade therefore expresses each $s_m$, and hence the entire determinant in adapted algebra bases, as a computable constant times a monomial in $v$ and $w_d$. This induction only uses parity vectors and scalars in $F$.

An ordinary isotopy of multiplication tensors has first map equal to $L_u\phi$ for a unital algebra isomorphism $\phi$ and a unit $u$ in the target algebra. In fact for an isotopy $p(x)q(y)=r(xy)$, substituting units gives $r(x)=p(x)q(1)=p(1)q(x)$, and $p(1)$ and $q(1)$ are units by invertibility. Hence $\phi=p(1)^{-1}p=q\,q(1)^{-1}$. A unit acts on each radical quotient by its scalar residue. Thus its determinant is positive in even dimension, while in odd dimension its sign can be adjusted freely by multiplying one input and the output map by $-1$. The odd-dimensional branch goes directly to ordinary TI.

In even dimension, include the radical-filtration frame ratios and the raw first-mode basis calibration in the final constant. The raw sign condition is now exactly
$$
 v^{\epsilon}\prod_d w_d^{\epsilon_d}=\delta
$$
with explicit bits $\epsilon,\epsilon_d$ and explicit $\delta\in F^\times$. Take $Z$ to be the direct sum of $V$ when $\epsilon=1$ and the spaces $W_d$ with $\epsilon_d=1$. Then $\dim Z\le2e\le2\log_2 n$, and the condition is $\det(\phi_Z)=\delta$. If $Z=0$ test $\delta=1$ directly.

## 3. Retain the actual algebra map in one ordinary query

Include the original algebra multiplication with its unit, the radical-power inclusions and quotient maps giving the actual $G_j$, and the induced graded multiplication. Include the tensor product maps constructing $G\otimes V$, $G\otimes\bigwedge^2 V$, and the wedge map $V\times V\to\bigwedge^2 V$. These surjections force the actions on the two chain spaces. Add their differentials, the inclusion of the cycle space in $C_1$, and the cycle quotient maps to $W_d$. They force the action on $W$ to be the action induced by the same original algebra isomorphism. The higher Koszul groups used in the determinant computation need no slots in the output.

Link the direct-sum inclusions into $Z$. Include the multiplication of the exterior algebra $\bigwedge Z$ and the inclusion of $Z$ in degree one. The algebra is generated by its unit and $Z$, so its action on all wedge levels is forced. Mark the coordinate top wedge vector on the source and $\delta$ times the coordinate top wedge vector on the target. This pin is preserved exactly when $\det(\phi_Z)=\delta$. Every compatible original algebra isomorphism induces all the required maps. Conversely any diagram isomorphism restricts to an original algebra map of the required sign.

There are polynomially many grades and a fixed number of map types. To keep the tensor compiler signature fixed, pack all grades of a given type in a direct sum and use their projections as actions of a split label algebra $F^b$. Its primitive idempotents are fixed by adjoining a linear directed-path shift on their ordered basis. All tensor slots that use a grade respect that label action and all maps of a given type pack into one linear or bilinear map. Include the corresponding independent inclusions in a packed map to $R$ and the inclusions into $Z$ in a packed map from the chosen grades. This is the directed-label and tensor-map compiler used for apolar volume pins. The exterior algebra of $Z$ has dimension at most $n^2$, and all graded, label and packed spaces are computable in polynomial time. Slot synchronization and the fixed-part tensor gadget give one dense ordinary three-tensor instance over $F$. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4)

As an infinite example with large middle layer, take $F[x_1,\dots,x_{2r}]/(x_1^2,\dots,x_{2r}^2)$. Its middle radical grade has dimension $\binom{2r}{r}$, while the Koszul sign readout needs at most $4r$ coordinates. All computations above use the raw multiplication tensors and need no supplied presentation as a complete intersection.
