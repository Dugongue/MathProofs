# Corrected determinant comparisons and ordinary-controlled sign choices

Work over the fixed field F₃. Write O for ordinary tensor isomorphism, A for one-sign TI, B for two-sign TI, and S for SL-TI. The attainable determinant vectors of a raw tensor comparison form a set D in Γ = F₂³. For an ordinarily isomorphic pair write D = a + H, where H is the source stabilizer sign subgroup. If a pair has no ordinary transporter then D is empty. All decisions below reject such an empty relative profile. Use the sign projection and tagged direct-sum construction from *Determinant Sign Projections and Conjunctions in Tensor Isomorphism* to realize fixed linear images of profiles and their independent sums by explicit raw tensor pairs. A neutral copy of an ordinary comparison is obtained by the zero sign map. Every reduction in this note is deterministic, polynomial time and informal.

The upper comparison U2 asks whether zero belongs to D + QH, where Qh = h₁e₃. Its correction is controlled by a source stabilizer, and the original pair and its self-comparison are both raw inputs to the compiler. The upper balance comparisons U1 and U3–U10 ask whether zero belongs to a sum of projected relative profiles assigned to fixed lines and planes in Γ.

## Two two-sign tests and five one-sign tests for U2

For two independent comparison slots let B^∧2 ask for the conjunction of the two B verdicts. There is a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction U2 → B^∧2. The two slots ask

1. whether some d ∈ D has (d₁,d₂) = (0,0);
2. whether some d ∈ D and h ∈ H have (d₂,d₃+h₁) = (0,0).

The first is B directly. The second is B on the exact sum of two mapped profiles, one from the original pair and one from its source self-comparison. If all h∈H have h₁=0, the first test forces d₁=0 throughout D. The second test then decides precisely whether zero belongs to D. If any h∈H has h₁=1, the original U2 correction is the full line generated by e₃. The first test is then equivalent to U2 and implies the second test, by using the witness of the first test and choosing the correcting bit h₁=d₃. Thus both tests together give the original answer in all cases.

There is also a five-query many-one reduction U2 → A^∧5. Let P = ker(e₁*), and put K = H + QH. The only possible K are zero, any of the three lines in P, any of the three planes through e₃, and Γ. Indeed if H is a line in P then QH=0. Otherwise a line H gains e₃. If H is a plane through e₃, it stays unchanged, and a plane omitting e₃ cannot lie in P, so it gains e₃ and becomes Γ. Each listed K occurs by taking H=K. Evaluate the three nonzero functionals killing e₃, together with e₃* and e₂*+e₃*. For each functional f, test zero in f(D+QH) using one A query. These are raw single-query constructions by linear sign projection and the tagged sum.

For K zero the tested functionals span Γ*. For K a plane its sole nonzero annihilator is tested. The line ⟨e₃⟩ has the three tested functionals in its annihilator. Each of the other two lines in P has one nonzero tested annihilator killing e₃ and one tested annihilator nonzero on e₃. They span its dual annihilator. For Γ there is no condition. Thus for every K the tested functionals that kill K span its entire annihilator, so all five queries pass exactly when a∈K.

Five is exact for conjunctions of fixed affine one-sign probes on independent copies of D and H. With all H=0, each probe is a fixed affine equation in a, or is identically true or false due to fixed slack. Completeness at a=0 lets us normalize its constant to zero, or discard it as true. For general H, its left-hand profile has offset f(a) and direction coming from the images of H in its independent copies. Completeness for every a∈K requires full one-bit direction whenever f(K) is nonzero. This means a probe with coefficient f can only distinguish a from K if f kills K. Opposite cosets of the three possible direction planes force all three nonzero functionals killing e₃. For each K∈{⟨e₂⟩,⟨e₂+e₃⟩}, the offset a=e₃ is outside K and all three of those functional tests pass. To reject it requires another functional killing that K but not e₃. The required two additional functionals must differ since the sum of the two line generators is e₃. This lower bound is for the specified affine probes.

## Exact three-probe compilation of S using U2

For nonempty D=a+H choose a nonzero functional f and the three nonzero u in ker f. Test a ∈ H+u f(H) for each u, using U2 after the corresponding invertible change of sign coordinates, which exists because each rank-one nonzero map u f has square zero. If f(H)=0 all probes are the original comparison. Otherwise if a∉H passed all three, all three nonzero u in ker f would represent the same nonzero class in Γ/H. The sum of two is the third, contradicting that common class. This recalls the three-probe upper bound.

The bound of three also holds against reducing two probes by arbitrary fixed affine sign mixing before each U2 query. Here each probe builds one relative profile out of independently chosen copies of D and H, packed by any constant number of fixed linear maps and fixed offset or slack. Write M for the sum of its relative input maps. On D=a+H the probe profile has offset M a+b and direction W(H) containing M(H). The fixed translation b lies in W(0)+QW(0) by completeness at a=0 and H=0. The test thus accepts whenever M a∈M(H)+QM(H), because W(0) is contained in W(H), and b contributes only to the corrected direction.

For each two-dimensional H, take the opposite coset D=a+H, with a∉H. Among these seven negative comparisons a probe can reject at most three. If M has rank three, it can reject only when H contains M⁻¹e₃. A plane omitting M⁻¹e₃ is carried to a plane omitting e₃ and hence enlarged to Γ by Q. If M has rank two, it can reject only when H contains its kernel line. Otherwise M(H)=M(Γ), which already contains M a. If M has rank one, the same argument limits it to a single H, namely ker M. Rank zero cannot reject any of the seven. In every case there are at most three eligible planes, so two fixed probes cannot reject all seven. Arbitrary affine profiles in this proof are realizable as ordinary sign profiles using a projected zero-tensor comparison with a reflected target. The bound concerns only this probe model. It leaves arbitrary many-one reductions on raw tensor pairs unresolved.

## Independently controlled sign choices from ordinary TI

Choose polynomially many raw ordinary TI questions Gⱼ, which may depend on all the inputs. For any desired vⱼ ∈ Γ the prior ordinary-controlled block swap gives a raw self-comparison with attainable stabilizer signs {0} when Gⱼ fails and the line ⟨vⱼ⟩ when Gⱼ holds. To obtain it, use the equal-format, odd-sided scalar-centroid helpers of *A sign correction controlled by ordinary tensor isomorphism*. Their individual stabilizers are total-even. The ordinary helper swap is available exactly when Gⱼ holds and then contributes an odd total sign by its two inverse block maps and the odd dimensions. The exact sign projection sends the total sign to vⱼ and the other sign information to zero. Each helper comparison is therefore always ordinarily positive and contributes exactly the claimed sign line. Although applying an exact projector to a self-comparison may output two different tensor descriptions, a nonempty relative profile that is a subgroup has that same subgroup as its direction and hence as the source stabilizer sign subgroup.

For a balance comparison U1 or U3–U10, assign each optional line to a slot whose fixed line or plane contains it. Sum its preimage in the slot's sign coordinates into that slot, using separate tags. Then the slot's attainable profile is its old profile plus the independently allowed optional lines. With polynomially many gates choose tags simultaneously in a polynomially bounded range of distinct marker sizes, using one common bound on the formats before tagging. This keeps the full dense output polynomial and its contents explicit. Ordinary comparisons required as additional conjuncts may be added as neutral blocks to any slot. They preserve the slot sign profiles when they hold and make them empty when they fail. In particular every balance candidate has the same many-one degree as its conjunction with an arbitrary polynomial-size list of ordinary TI conditions. The converse sets the list and gates to empty.

For U2 apply the same construction in Γ to the original raw comparison. Incorporating gated lines with total span V replaces its relative profile and its direction by D+V and H+V. The desired corrected profile is therefore

    D + QH + V + QV.

Thus a gate on v contributes the same line if Qv=0, and contributes the plane spanned by v and Qv otherwise. Ordinary conjuncts can again be attached with the zero sign map. In particular, guard on the same ordinary question G for three independent generators. For either a balance candidate whose slot spaces span Γ or for U2, when G holds the signs are fully correctable. When G fails the original sign condition is preserved. The exact decorated verdict is the conjunction of the original ordinary transporter prerequisites with the disjunction of G and the original sign test. This extends the earlier one-sign controlled swap to multiple sign directions and to the cross-mode correction rule. It does not assume an ordinary TI oracle or a precomputed answer to any of the gates.

These constructions give exact reductions and guarded variants on all raw inputs. The new two-sign compilation of U2 uses two B instances, and the constructions above leave the unrestricted many-one equivalences among distinct upper candidates unresolved.
