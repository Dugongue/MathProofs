# Exact determinant-sign projections for tensor isomorphism

Work over F_3 with explicit dense three-tensors. Write O for ordinary TI, A for TI requiring determinant one in the first mode, B for TI requiring determinant one in the first two modes, and S for SL-TI. Every reduction here is deterministic polynomial-time many-one unless its scope says otherwise. The proofs are informal.

**An arbitrary linear projection of the three determinant signs can be realized exactly by one ordinary tensor pair.** In particular, one SL-TI instance can enforce an arbitrary two-sign comparison and an independent arbitrary one-sign comparison. The unrestricted reduction S to B is equivalent to closure of B under two-input conjunction. The three unrestricted reverse reductions considered here remain open.

## 1. Extend the existing sign construction to singular maps

For a pair T,T' write D(T,T') for the set of all determinant vectors of ordinary transporters in F_2^3, using bit zero for determinant +1. This set is empty or an affine subspace. For each prescribed 3-by-3 binary matrix L, we construct a pair with exact sign set L D(T,T'), including preservation of emptiness. The claim is about the sign set of all ordinary transporters in actual coordinate frames.

We reuse Section 2 of [the earlier structural construction](StructuralComparison.md). It first normalizes to concise balanced even inputs without changing the sign set, then implements a matrix M by three alternating bottom spaces on a shared auxiliary space. Let the i-th row of M be (alpha_i,beta_i,gamma_i). Each bottom space contains the four common anchors. It includes the original data space D_0 precisely when gamma_i=1, and signed star terms precisely when alpha_i=1 or beta_i=1. It has a private alternating filler. The size equalization and the filler parity computation depend only on the even input dimensions and the individual row bits.

The same proof works whenever at least one gamma_i is one. Indeed the intersection of the three bottom spaces is the anchor space, with D_0 included in the intersection only if all gamma_i are one. Its support in both cases is precisely the common old space P_0. Intersect an arm with gamma_i=1 with the exterior square of P_0 to recover the anchors and D_0 together. The old anchor rank argument recovers the original tensor and its three maps. In each individual arm, the anchor quotient contributes determinant one. Its data, first star and second star contribute respectively gamma_i d_W, alpha_i d_U and beta_i d_V to its sign, and its private filler contributes zero. No other step uses row independence or distinctness. In particular the suspension has exactly the same parity cancellation and the same intrinsic small spaces when two readout rows coincide or one row is zero. Every original transporter still extends by the same choices on the primed spaces, stubs and fillers. The result therefore reads all three rows of M, with no invertibility requirement except that its third column is nonzero.

For L nonzero, choose v with L v nonzero. Complete v to the third column of an invertible binary matrix Q^{-1}. First apply the existing invertible sign encoder for Q and then apply the preceding construction to M=L Q^{-1}. The third column of M is L v. The exact resulting sign set is M Q D=L D. This uses only two polynomial constructions and constant-size binary linear algebra. If L=0, use the [determinant-neutral multiplication cube](NeutralConjunction.md) in place of the two-stage construction. It reflects ordinary equivalence, and every transporter has all three determinants one. This deals with the empty and nonempty cases for L=0. Malformed encodings with unequal formats can be mapped to a fixed pair with no transporter.

## 2. Pack two independent comparisons into one SL-TI instance

Given a B pair, apply the sign projection L=diag(1,1,0). Exactly its two original signs survive. The third output sign is always zero. Given an independent A pair, use the [one-axis algebra construction](CastlingAndConjunction.md) to produce an absolutely indecomposable pair whose only potentially nonzero sign is the third. Choose its side lengths larger than all sides of the projected B pair, using the available invariant padding. Direct-sum the two pairs.

For concise tensors the intrinsic primitive centroid idempotents determine the direct-sum factors. The enlarged independent A factor has a different format from every factor of the B tensor, so cannot be exchanged or mixed with one. A transporter of the whole tensor exists exactly when both ordinary comparisons succeed. The exact signs of an output transporter are (d_1,d_2,e), with the first two from one B-side witness and the final bit from the independent A-side witness. Both implication directions hold under the same fixed concatenated coordinate order in both endpoints. Thus B AND A reduces to one S. This step does not use any decision queries in its construction.

## 3. Characterize the remaining three-to-two collapse

For a problem X write X^k for the conjunction of k independently encoded instances. The previous exact sign results give B <= A^3 and S <= B^3. The latter uses three line probes with allowed lines generated by u,v,u+v for any independent u,v. If a nonempty affine sign set meets all three but omits zero, it contains u,v,u+v, whose affine sum is zero. Conversely zero meets each line. For B <= A^3, use the three hyperplanes containing its allowed line, transported to one-character queries. In the quotient by that line the same three-line argument applies.

The following are equivalent.

1. S reduces to B.
2. B AND A reduces to B.
3. B^2 reduces to B.

The new packing gives 1 implies 2. Iterating 2 allows one B query to absorb any fixed number of A queries. Replacing the second B instance by three A instances proves 2 implies 3. Iterating 3 twice compresses the three B queries for S and proves 3 implies 1. All iterations have a fixed number of steps, so the polynomial size bound is preserved. In particular the largest-tier collapse follows if B can absorb a single independent A instance. Any such collapse also yields B equivalent to A^3, because the earlier axis assembly gives A^3 <= S and B <= A^3.

The middle gap has the parallel exact criterion B <= A if and only if A^2 <= A. Here A^2 <= B follows from the two independent sign axes, while B <= A^3. Under either condition every fixed conjunction of A instances reduces to A. In particular S <= A, since seven A probes suffice for S. Finally if A <= O, composing all seven probes with that reduction and packing the ordinary conjunction using a polynomial tensor-system construction gives S <= O. See [the published fixed-component compiler, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S4).

## 4. What an objectwise three-to-two map would establish

Suppose a polynomial object map f reduces S-equivalence to B-equivalence. Then A <= O, and consequently S <= O, by the following explicit procedure. This is a sufficient criterion and uses the stronger hypothesis of an object map.

For an A query x,y of the same format, if first-mode support dimensions differ reject. If the first mode has unused support, or its dimension is odd, ordinary TI suffices since a complement reflection or scalar stabilizer corrects the tested determinant. In the remaining branch the first mode U is even and full. Pin the raw format as a shared parameter. For either object independently, pad modes V,W to odd dimensions, adding an unused coordinate in each. Castling in U uses the calibrated annihilator basis. Both padded dual modes of the resulting tensor are concise, because the annihilator contains the whole strip over each unused coordinate. The new mode in U is also concise and is odd-dimensional. The old first-mode determinant is the total sign after castling, by the exact castling formula. Conversely any transporter reflects through double annihilation and restriction to the original supported data and extends over unused untested directions. This holds for every pair of objects in the fixed-format full-U branch.

Apply the one-axis local algebra construction to these all-odd concise tensors. It stores exactly the total determinant in a chosen physical axis and all other signs are neutral. Choose mutually oversized pad dimensions in the three slots of an ordered triple, depending only on the common raw format and the slot, so that the three axis factors have different formats. This produces an object map from triples with coordinatewise A-equivalence to S-equivalence. Compose with f.

Choose five distinct words from {0,1}^3 and substitute x,y for the two symbols. If A(x,y) is true, the five resulting output tensors are all B-equivalent and hence ordinarily equivalent. If A(x,y) is false, the five words are pairwise inequivalent in the coordinatewise relation, and so the five outputs are pairwise B-inequivalent. An ordinary orbit contains at most four B-orbits, by the index of the two determinant constraints in the ordinary acting group. Hence the five outputs cannot all be ordinarily equivalent. Four ordinary comparisons with one common reference determine the verdict, and an ordinary conjunction compiler packages them in one ordinary TI instance. The premise does not follow from an arbitrary pair-to-pair many-one reduction, since its output for an object may vary with the compared partner. See [Fortnow and Grochow on kernel reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4775).
