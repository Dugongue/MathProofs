# The conductor at a pure-cube boundary

Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic three. For a cubic $F$ write $T_F$ for full polarization and call the rank of $v\mapsto T_F(v,-,-)$ its polarization rank. Its support is the smallest subspace of linear forms whose symmetric cube contains $F$. Let $f$ on $e$ variables have polarization rank $e$, let $C$ be its reduced $\operatorname{GL}_e$ orbit closure, and let $X$ be the reduced orbit closure after padding $f$ with $m>0$ unused variables. Suppose $g\in X$ has support dimension $e$ and polarization rank $e-1$.

**Theorem.** At every such $g$ the Grassmannian incidence and its image admit affine charts with coordinate rings
$$
B=A[a_1,\ldots,a_m],\qquad
S=A[\,a_i^3,\ I a_i,\ J a_i a_j\mid1\le i,j\le m\,]\subset B.
\tag{1}
$$
Here $A$ is the coordinate ring of an open subset of $C$ with $(e-1)m$ free coordinates adjoined. In coordinates determined by $e-1$ polarization pivots, write the quotient cubic as $H_0(u)+zQ(u)+z^2L(u)+b z^3$ with $b$ invertible. The ideal $I$ is generated by the coefficients of $Q,L$ and $J$ by the coefficients of $L$. The extension is finite, birational and radicial. Its conductor in $B$ is exactly
$$
(S:B)=(I^2+J)^m B .
\tag{2}
$$
All points of polarization rank $e-1$ on this chart are nonnormal. If the chart on $C$ is normal, $B$ is the normalization. In general, normalize $A$ and then adjoin the $a_i$.

## Recover all but one row of the Grassmannian chart

For each $e$-plane $P$ in the ambient space of linear forms, take the cubics in $\operatorname{Sym}^3 P$ which belong to $C$ in a frame of $P$. This defines a reduced closed incidence over the Grassmannian, since a local frame identifies the closed fibers with the same $C$. Projection to cubics is proper. The locus whose framed cubic is in the orbit of $f$ is dense, and its image is the padded orbit, so the incidence image is $X$.

To check existence of a unique minimal support, take subspaces $P,P'$ with the cubic in both symmetric cubes. Choose a basis of their intersection, extend it to bases of both subspaces, then complete the resulting independent set to an ambient basis. Comparison of monomials puts the cubic in $\operatorname{Sym}^3(P\cap P')$. Iterating in finite dimension gives the minimal support. Every polarization contraction $T_F(v,w,-)$ belongs to it.

Take the support of $g$ as a fixed $e$-plane $W$, and let $Y$ be a complement with basis $y_1,\ldots,y_m$. Choose a basis $(u_1,\ldots,u_{e-1},z)$ of $W$ with the $u_i$ spanning the contraction image of $T_g$. In this frame $g=H_0(u)+b_0z^3$, with $b_0\ne0$. Indeed, the coefficient test on triples of basis vectors has multipliers $1,2$ on mixed monomials and $6=0$ on pure cubes. Thus a variable in the radical occurs only as a pure cube, and full support forces its pure coefficient to be nonzero.

Select $e-1$ contractions on pairs of dual basis vectors whose first $e-1$ coordinate rows have invertible pivot at $g$. For $F\in C$ on this pivot open, normalize the resulting vectors by the pivot inverse to $p_i(F)=u_i+\delta_i(F)z$. Express $F$ in the basis $(p_i(F),z)$ as $H(u,z)$. Also require the coefficient $b$ of $z^3$ in $H$ to be invertible. This gives an affine open $C^\circ\subseteq C$. Every $F\in C^\circ$ has support dimension $e$: a smaller support would have to equal the span of the $p_i(F)$, in conflict with $b\ne0$.

On $X$, apply the same pivot and unit tests to the projection of the ambient cubic to $W$. This projection lies in $C$ because any linear map from its incident $e$-plane to $W$ lies in the closure of invertible maps. Its projected support has dimension $e$. Thus every incident plane above this open projects isomorphically to $W$, and is the graph of a linear $\Phi:W\to Y$. Its image cubic is obtained from $F$ by sending each $v\in W$ to $v+\Phi(v)$. Recover $F$ by setting the $Y$ variables to zero. Recover the vectors $B_i=\Phi(p_i(F))$ by taking the same contractions in the ambient space, with the argument vectors extended by zero on $Y$, then normalizing by the same pivot. The resulting contractions are exactly $p_i(F)+B_i$. Hence $F$ and all entries of the $B_i$ are regular image coordinates. They and $\Phi(z)=\sum_ja_jy_j$ freely parametrize the incidence chart, with $A=k[C^\circ][(B_i)_j]$.

## Compute the image algebra

Use the ambient linear coordinates $(p_i(F)+B_i,z,y_j)$. Their coordinate change and inverse are defined over $A$, with determinant one. In these coordinates the image cubic is
$$
H(u,z+a\cdot y)
=H(u,z)+(Q(u)+2zL(u))(a\cdot y)+L(u)(a\cdot y)^2+b(a\cdot y)^3.
\tag{3}
$$
In characteristic three this expansion has no other terms. Its coefficients linear in $Y$ give every $I a_i$. Its coefficients quadratic in $Y$ give every $J a_i a_j$, using the unit $2$ for cross terms. The pure $Y$ cubics give the $a_i^3$ by dividing by $b$. Thus the ambient coefficients and the localized pivots generate precisely (1), because $A$ was already recovered. Pullback is injective by surjectivity onto the reduced open image.

The $a_i$ are integral with unique cube roots over geometric points. In fact $B^3\subset S$, so the finite extension is radicial. On this open, rank $e-1$ means the normalized contractions span the entire contraction image, equivalently $Q=L=0$ by the polarization coefficient test. Conversely $Q=L=0$ implies rank $e-1$ because the selected contractions are independent. Thus rank $e$ is the open where some coefficient in $I$ is invertible. That locus is dense by the hypothesis on $f$. Inverting any nonzero coefficient $i_0\in I$ recovers all $a_j=(i_0a_j)/i_0$, proving birationality.

## Compute the conductor

Put $D=A[a_1^3,\ldots,a_m^3]$. The ring $B$ is free over $D$ on $a^\alpha$ with $\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}^m$. For $s\ge0$ put $K_s=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}I^{s-2j}J^j$, with $K_0=A$. There is a direct $D$-module decomposition
$$
S=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}^m}(K_{|\alpha|}D)a^\alpha .
$$
Singles with coefficients in $I$ and pairs with coefficients in $J$ realize every product in this formula. For the reverse inclusion, in any product of generators extract cubes and retain a submultiset of the single and double occurrences with the prescribed residue of each variable. A pair whose two occurrences remain contributes $J$. If one remains, its coefficient belongs to $I$ since $J\subset I$. Discarded factors have coefficients in $A$. The same deletion shows $K_{s+1}\subseteq K_s$.

Write $v=\sum_\alpha v_\alpha a^\alpha$, with $v_\alpha\in D$. For a fixed $\beta$, multiplying by $a^\beta$ permutes residues modulo three and multiplies each residue coefficient by a monomial in the independent cube variables. This operation preserves membership in $K_sD$ by coefficient comparison. As $\beta$ varies over the residue basis, each term of $v$ must satisfy every one of the coefficient ideal conditions. Since the $K_s$ descend, this says $v_\alpha\in K_{2m}D$ for every $\alpha$. The identity $K_{2m}=(I^2+J)^m$ proves (2).

At a rank-drop point the conductor belongs to the local maximal ideal. The local extension is strict, since localizing would otherwise make the conductor the unit ideal. A normal domain equals each of its integral birational intermediate extensions, so the image is nonnormal there. Normalizing the finite birational $B$ also normalizes $S$, and polynomial extension commutes with normalization of these finitely generated domains.


## Example and prime-degree extension

Take $f=x^2y+xz^2+z^3$ and $g=x^2y+z^3$. The three first derivatives of $f$ are independent, giving polarization rank three by the same mixed monomial test. Substituting $(x,y,z)\mapsto(sx,s^{-2}y,z)$ gives $x^2y+sxz^2+z^3$, specializing to $g$. The target has support dimension three and polarization rank two. Thus, for every $m>0$, the padded orbit closure is nonnormal at the padded $g$.

For one unused variable $w$, the subfamily $x^2y+sx(z+aw)^2+(z+aw)^3$ has image ring $k[s,a^3,sa,sa^2]$, directly by (3). Its normalization is $k[s,a]$ and its conductor is $(s)$. The specialization $s=0$ retains the coefficient $a^3$.

The chart argument also works at any prime degree $p$ in characteristic $p$ with the same support and adjacent polarization rank hypotheses. Full polarization loses exactly the pure powers, by the factorial coefficient test. Fix $p-1$ arguments for each contraction used in the pivot. Let $J_i$ be the coefficient ideal of the $i$th Hasse derivative in $z$, for $1\le i<p$. These ideals descend because ordinary differentiation sends the $i$th Hasse derivative to $i+1$ times the next, with $i+1$ invertible for $i+1<p$. All multinomial coefficients of degree $i<p$ are units. Hence the image ring is generated by $A$, the $a_j^p$, and $J_i a^\gamma$ for $|\gamma|=i$. Using exponents modulo $p$, the multiset argument gives coefficient ideals
$$
K_s=\sum_{\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}i d_i=s}\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}J_i^{d_i}
$$
and conductor $K_{(p-1)m}B$. Here the pivot-rank drop is exactly the vanishing of $J_1$, and again yields nonnormality.

## Relation to the literature

The Grassmannian map is a homogeneous-bundle collapsing. Lőrincz develops normality and singularity criteria for such maps, including subspace varieties. The equations here compute the finite Frobenius discrepancy and its conductor when one essential direction survives polarization only as a pure power. The theorem is proved in prose.
