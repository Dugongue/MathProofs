# Scheme fibers and normalization of the cubic Grassmannian incidence

Work over an algebraically closed field $K$ of characteristic three. Let $V$ have dimension $n$ and write $R_h$ for the contraction radical of the unnormalized polarization of a cubic $h\in\operatorname{Sym}^3V^*$. Fix $f$ with $f|_{R_f}=0$. Set $r=\dim R_f$ and $c=n-r$, and let $C_0$ be the reduced orbit closure of the induced $c$-variable cubic. Write $X=\overline{\operatorname{GL}(V)f}$, with its reduced structure.

Let $Z$ be the incidence variety in $\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)\times\operatorname{Sym}^3 V^*$ of pairs $(S,h)$ for which $h$ descends to $V/S$ and the quotient cubic lies in $C_0$ after choosing a quotient frame. Its scheme structure is the following one. The cubics on the universal quotient give a vector subbundle of the trivial cubic bundle. In each frame chart take the product with $C_0$ inside this subbundle. These products glue because $C_0$ is invariant under frame change. Projection gives a projective birational morphism $p:Z\to X$. Indeed the part with quotient cubic in the orbit of the inducing form is dense and projects to the orbit of $f$. The projection is an isomorphism on the open polarization-rank-$c$ locus, where $S$ is the kernel of a constant-rank contraction matrix.

**Theorem.** Fix a geometric point $g\in X$ and extend the ground field to its algebraically closed field of definition. Write $g|_{R_g}=\ell_g^3$ and $H=\ker\ell_g\subseteq R_g$, with $H=R_g$ when $\ell_g=0$.

1. The full scheme fiber $F_g=p^{-1}(g)$ is the ordinary $\operatorname{Gr}(r,H)$ if $\ell_g=0$. Otherwise it is the inverse image of $\operatorname{Gr}(r,H)^{(1)}$ under relative Frobenius of $\operatorname{Gr}(r,R_g)$. Given a splitting $R_g=H\oplus Kz$, it is finite flat of degree $3^r$ over $B=\operatorname{Gr}(r,H)$, with multiplication algebra
   $$
    (\pi_g)_*\mathcal O_{F_g}
       \cong \operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal S)/(\text{cubes of local sections of }\mathcal S).
   $$
   Here $\mathcal S$ is the tautological rank-$r$ subbundle on $B$. If $\dim H>r$, then $H^0(F_g,\mathcal O_{F_g})=K$, including in the case of a nonreduced fiber.
2. Let $b$ be the number of geometric points of $C_0^\nu$ over the quotient cubic $g_{V/S_0}$, for any reduced incidence plane $S_0$, where superscript $\nu$ denotes normalization. The reduction of the fiber of $Z^\nu\to X$ at $g$ is a disjoint union of $b$ copies of $\operatorname{Gr}(r,H)$. Consequently the normalization $X^\nu\to X$ has exactly $b$ geometric points over $g$. In particular, if $C_0^\nu\to C_0$ is a universal homeomorphism, so is $X^\nu\to X$.
3. For $f=xy(x-y)$ in variables $x,y,z$, the incidence resolution is smooth. The support of $R^1p_*\mathcal O_Z$ is the pure-cube subspace, and $R^ip_*\mathcal O_Z=0$ for $i\geq2$. The nonvanishing and higher-degree vanishing persist on the induced resolution of the normalization.

## The full incidence fiber

In characteristic three, a mixed cubic monomial is read by polarization with coefficient $1$ or $2$, while a pure cube is killed. This coefficient test is valid over every commutative $K$-algebra $A$. In an adapted decomposition $V=C\oplus R_g$, we therefore have
$$
 g(v+w)=g_C(v)+\ell_g(w)^3\qquad(v\in C,\ w\in R_g).
$$
The contraction kernel after extension to $A$ is $R_g\otimes_K A$, since the matrix is defined over a field and has a split kernel. If a rank-$r$ direct summand $S\subset V\otimes_K A$ is a descent plane for $g$, it lies in this kernel and the pure-cube restriction to $S$ vanishes. Conversely these two conditions imply descent. One chooses local bases splitting $S$ in the radical and applies the same coefficient test. Such a splitting is available locally because $V_A/S$ and $V_A/R_{g,A}$ are locally free, so $R_{g,A}/S$ is locally free as well. Vanishing of the restriction is an identity of formal polynomials over $A$.

Let $F'$ be the descent-plane scheme cut out by these conditions, ignoring the $C_0$ condition. Its reduction is $\operatorname{Gr}(r,H)$. We claim $F'=F_g$ as schemes. First, some $K$-point of this Grassmannian belongs to $F_g$ because $g\in X$. The subgroup $\operatorname{GL}(H)$ acting as the identity on a complementary subspace of $R_g$ and on $C$ preserves $g$. It is transitive on the reduced Grassmannian. Therefore every reduced incidence plane meets the quotient-closure condition.

Work on a graph chart of $F'$ based at any such plane $S_0$. Fix a complement $H_1$ to $S_0$ in $H$. If $\ell_g=0$, the graph takes values in $H_1$, and the triangular automorphism taking $S_0$ to the graph preserves $g$. If $\ell_g\neq0$, choose $z\in R_g$ with $\ell_g(z)=1$. In this case the graph is
$$
 s\longmapsto s+B_1(s)+\lambda(s)z,\qquad
 B_1:S_0\to H_1,\quad\lambda:S_0\to K .
$$
The notation means the corresponding linear maps after scalar extension. The descent equations for this graph are precisely that the $r$ coordinates of $\lambda$ have cube zero. The triangular automorphism defined by the graph and fixing $H_1,z,C$ preserves $g$ over the chart ring. Its effect on the radical summand is to add $\lambda(s)^3$ to $\ell_g(w)^3$, which is zero there. Hence in either case the quotient cubic on the graph is isomorphic, over the whole chart, to the constant quotient cubic from $S_0$. The $C_0$ condition holds scheme-theoretically because $C_0$ is a closed invariant subscheme and the constant cubic belongs to it. The graph charts cover the support, so $F'=F_g$.

In the nonzero case the equations are the pullbacks under relative Frobenius of the incidence equations for planes in $H^{(1)}$. This also follows from the graph coordinates, where those equations are linear in the coordinates normal to the sub-Grassmannian before Frobenius. The fiber is supported in the open set of planes projecting injectively to $H$ along $Kz$. That open set is the total space of $\mathcal S^*$ over $B$, because such a plane is the graph of a functional on its projected plane. Its coordinate algebra over $B$ is $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal S)$. The descent equations kill the cubes of a local basis of $\mathcal S$. Monomials with each exponent at most two give a local basis, proving finite flatness and the displayed algebra formula. In a normal-coordinate chart the equations are $r$ independent coordinate cubes.

To compute global functions, denote the degree $j$ part of the truncated symmetric algebra by $\mathsf T^j(\mathcal S)$. Local splittings of $\mathcal S\subseteq H\otimes\mathcal O_B$ show that $\mathsf T^j(\mathcal S)$ embeds into the trivial bundle with fiber $\mathsf T^j(H)$. Every section of the latter is constant because $B$ is projective and integral. If $r<\dim H$, the only constant in all subspaces $\mathsf T^j(S)$ for $j>0$ is zero. Indeed choose a basis of $H$ and test all coordinate $r$-planes. Each positive-degree monomial uses a basis variable that some coordinate $r$-plane omits. Those monomials form a basis of the truncated algebra, so all coefficients vanish. The degree-zero part contributes $K$, giving the claim. For $r=\dim H$ and $\ell_g\neq0$ the same algebra formula gives $H^0$ of dimension $3^r$, since $B$ is a point.

## Branches and the Stein factor

The normalization $Z^\nu$ is locally the product of an affine Grassmannian chart and $C_0^\nu$. In each graph chart this product is normal by normality of a polynomial ring over a normal domain, and the natural map is finite and birational. Thus it is the normalization there, and these local normalizations glue by uniqueness.

The action of $G=\operatorname{GL}(H)$ on $Z$ lifts to $Z^\nu$. In fact $G\times Z^\nu$ is normal, being locally an open subset of an affine space over a normal variety. The composite with the original action is dominant onto $Z$, so the universal property of normalization gives the lift. Uniqueness gives its group-action identities.

Let $E$ be the reduction of the fiber of $Z^\nu\to X$ at $g$. Its projection to the Grassmannian factors through $B=\operatorname{Gr}(r,H)$, because $E$ is reduced and all its geometric points lie over $B$. The $G$-action is transitive on $B$. Choose $S_0\in B$. On any Grassmannian graph chart the orbit map $G\to B$ has a regular section, obtained by the graph basis change, with an initial constant matrix if the chart is centered at another plane. Applying that section and its inverse to $E$ identifies its restriction to the chart with the chart times $E_{S_0}$. The latter is a finite reduced scheme. Finiteness follows from the finite map $Z^\nu\to Z$ over the fixed pair $(S_0,g)$, and reducedness follows from the product with a nonempty graph chart being reduced. Its points are the points of $C_0^\nu$ above $g_{V/S_0}$ by the normalization product chart.

Transition functions between these local sections lie in the stabilizer of $S_0$ in $G$. It is a connected parabolic, as its block matrices are a product, as a variety, of two general linear groups and an affine space. Its action on the finite reduced fiber fixes every point, since each orbit is the continuous image of a connected variety in a finite discrete space. The local products thus glue to the claimed disjoint union of copies of $B$.

Apply Stein factorization to the proper birational map $Z^\nu\to X$. Because the source is normal and the map is birational, its finite factor is $X^\nu$; the morphism from $Z^\nu$ to this finite factor has geometrically connected fibers. The connected components of $E$ therefore correspond exactly to the points of $X^\nu$ over $g$. This gives the branch count. These calculations are unchanged over algebraically closed field extensions, since normalization of finite-type varieties over the algebraically closed ground field commutes with such extensions. Thus a single point in each geometric fiber gives a finite, surjective and universally injective normalization map, hence a universal homeomorphism. This conclusion allows nontrivial scheme fibers. ## Nonvanishing for a smooth incidence resolution

Take $f=xy(x-y)$ on the three-dimensional space, with passive direction $z$. Its quotient polarization has rank two, since evaluating its contractions on the two repeated-coordinate pairs detects both input directions independently. Its quotient orbit is dense in the entire binary cubic space: the three distinct roots locus is one orbit by projective $3$-transitivity and scalar changes of variables. Thus $C_0=\operatorname{Sym}^3(K^2)^*$, and $Z$ is the total space of the binary cubic vector bundle over $\operatorname{Gr}(1,K^3)$. It is smooth, and $p$ is projective and birational as above.

At $g=x^3$ the polarization is zero. Choose any line in $H=\ker x$; $g$ descends and the quotient is admitted by $C_0$. Here $B=\mathbb P^1$, and the structure sheaf of the scheme fiber has pushforward
$$
 \mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}\oplus
 \mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\oplus
 \mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2).
$$
The degree-two summand has $H^1=K$ by the cohomology of line bundles on the projective line. The other two summands have zero $H^1$. In particular the reduced fiber alone would give zero in this calculation.

For completeness this fiber cohomology yields higher direct-image nonvanishing even without an assumption of flatness near $g$. At the local ring of $X$ at $g$, let $Z_j$ denote the preimage of the base thickening defined by the $(j+1)$-st power of the maximal ideal, for $j\geq0$. The kernel of $\mathcal O_{Z_{j+1}}\to\mathcal O_{Z_j}$ is coherent and supported on the one-dimensional closed fiber, so its $H^2$ vanishes. Thus the maps $H^1(Z_{j+1},\mathcal O)\to H^1(Z_j,\mathcal O)$ are surjective. Their inverse limit surjects onto the nonzero $H^1(F_g,\mathcal O)$. By the theorem on formal functions that limit is the completed stalk of $R^1p_*\mathcal O_Z$, which is therefore nonzero. If $p$ factors as $p^\nu:Z\to X^\nu$ followed by the finite normalization $\nu$, finite pushforward is exact on quasicoherent sheaves, so $R^1p_*\mathcal O_Z=\nu_*R^1p^\nu_*\mathcal O_Z$. The latter higher direct image on the normalization is nonzero as well. [The Stacks Project, theorem on formal functions](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02OC)


In this example the support of $R^1p_*\mathcal O_Z$ is exactly the pure-cube subspace. The only possible polarization ranks on $X$ are zero and two. Rank one would give a nonzero trilinear form on a one-dimensional quotient, whereas every cubic polarization in characteristic three satisfies $T(v,v,v)=0$. Rank two is the isomorphism locus for $p$, so there is no higher direct-image support there. The rank-zero locus is the pure-cube subspace, and every nonzero point of it is in the orbit of $x^3$. Equivariance and closedness of support imply that the whole subspace, including zero, is in the support. At zero the scheme fiber is the reduced $\mathbb P^2$ and has zero $H^1$. Thus this example also exhibits failure of degree-one cohomology to commute with base change at zero.

There are no other nonzero positive-degree direct images in this example. Let $\mathcal Q$ be the tautological quotient bundle on $\mathbb P^2$. The binary cubic bundle $\operatorname{Sym}^3\mathcal Q^*$ is a subbundle of the trivial ternary cubic bundle, so its dual $\mathcal E$ is globally generated, as is every $\operatorname{Sym}^j\mathcal E$. For any such symmetric power, its dual tensored with $\mathcal O(-3)$ embeds into a sum of copies of $\mathcal O(-3)$, so it has zero $H^0$. By Serre duality its $H^2$ vanishes. Higher cohomology vanishes by dimension. The projection of the total space $Z$ onto $\mathbb P^2$ is affine with structure algebra $\bigoplus_{j\geq0}\operatorname{Sym}^j\mathcal E$, and cohomology commutes with this direct sum on the projective plane. Therefore $H^i(Z,\mathcal O_Z)=0$ for $i\geq2$. Since $X$ is affine, this proves $R^ip_*\mathcal O_Z=0$ for $i\geq2$, and faithful finite pushforward gives the same vanishing on the normalization.

## Relation to the literature

Kempf studies homogeneous-bundle collapsings, and Lőrincz gives positive-characteristic results using good filtrations. Samokhin describes Frobenius neighborhoods and the associated truncated symmetric powers. Here the nilpotent radical shear identifies the actual cubic-orbit incidence fiber with a Frobenius neighborhood. It also ensures the quotient orbit-closure equations impose no extra conditions on those nilpotent directions. The branch formula handles arbitrary quotient orbit closures without assuming normality or a good filtration. The higher-direct-image calculation is for the displayed resolution. [Kempf, On the collapsing of homogeneous bundles](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01390321) · [Lőrincz, On the collapsing of homogeneous bundles in arbitrary characteristic](https://doi.org/10.24033/asens.2556) · [Samokhin, The Frobenius morphism on homogeneous spaces I](https://archive.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/id/eprint/1110/)

Grochow, Mulmuley and Qiao use orbit closures to study degenerations of forms in algebraic complexity. The incidence theorem gives branch and scheme-fiber information about an entire characteristic-three family of such orbit closures. It complements the pure-cube orbit analysis and does not depend on the separability of the orbit map. Incidence.py checks 1,092 Artinian graph coordinate values, 126 coordinate Grassmannian planes, the projective-line Čech calculation, and all 2,187 ternary polarizations over the field of three elements. The proofs are in prose, with no Lean kernel verification or claim of literature priority. [Grochow, Mulmuley and Qiao, Boundaries of VP and VNP](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.34)
