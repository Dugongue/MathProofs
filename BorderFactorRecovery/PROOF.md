# Border factor recovery at arbitrary multiplicity

Let \(f\) be a nonzero rational polynomial given by a size-\(s\) arithmetic circuit, and let \(h\) be a rational irreducible factor of degree \(d\) in \(n\) variables, with any multiplicity \(a\). For any positive integer \(q\), there is a nonuniform circuit of size \(\operatorname{poly}(s,n,d,\log(q+1))\) over \(\mathbb Q(\varepsilon)\) that outputs a degree-\(d\) polynomial with coefficients regular at \(\varepsilon=0\), equal to \(h\) modulo \(\varepsilon^q\). The bound does not depend on the input degree or linearly on the factor multiplicity. A circuit with nonzero constant inversions is allowed. This replaces the repeated differentiation in the earlier exact local-jet theorem by multiplicity-corrected Newton iteration. The conclusion is border recovery.

Choose coordinates in which \(h\) is monic of degree \(d\) in its last variable \(y\), and choose a rational specialization \(x=0\) of the other variables where \(h(0,y)\) is squarefree and coprime to the cofactor \(f/h^a\). Such a shift exists from the nonzero resultants over \(\mathbb Q(x)\). Let \(\alpha\) be a root of \(h(0,y)\), and work first in \(E=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), of degree at most \(d\). Put \(M=2d^2+1\). The local simple root \(\zeta(t)\) of \(h(tx,y)\) through \(\alpha\) exists uniquely with coefficients in \(E[x]\) by the scalar-coefficient Hensel recursion.

Locally at \((t,y)=(0,\alpha)\) write \(f(tx,y)=(y-\zeta(t))^a U(t,y)\) with \(U\) a unit. The multiplicity-corrected Newton step in \(y\) is
\[
N(z)=z-\frac{a f(tx,z)}{\partial_y f(tx,z)}.
\]
After cancelling the common power of \(z-\zeta\), the error \(N(z)-\zeta\) is a multiple of \((z-\zeta)^2\) by a regular power series. Indeed the remaining denominator is \(aU+(z-\zeta)U_y\), whose constant is nonzero. Start with \(z_0=\alpha+\varepsilon\). Let \(H\ge M+q\). After each step add \(c_i\varepsilon^H\), choosing \(c_i=0\) or 1 so that the new value at \(t=0\) is not identically \(\alpha\). At most one choice fails. All iterates lie in the formal neighborhood and their errors square in the \((t,\varepsilon)\)-adic order, up to cap \(H\). Thus \(O(\log(M+q))\) steps give error of total order at least \(M+q\).

Compute every step only through \(t\)-degree \(M-1\) over \(E(\varepsilon)[x]\). The \(t=0\) coefficient of the derivative denominator is a nonzero scalar in \(E(\varepsilon)\): the iterate has nonconstant value at \(t=0\), tending to \(\alpha\), and the nonzero univariate derivative cannot vanish identically on such a function. So truncated division is legal. The cancellation identity also shows, analytically, that the final coefficients are regular at \(\varepsilon=0\), regardless of intermediate divisions. Since all retained \(t\)-degrees are below \(M\), the computed jet agrees with \(\zeta\) modulo \(\varepsilon^q\). Truncated circuit arithmetic needs only polynomial overhead in \(M,s\) at each step. The exponents \(H\) and the integer multiplicity \(a\) need only binary-length circuit encodings. In particular a size-\(s\) division-free arithmetic circuit has degree at most \(2^{O(s)}\).

Recover the monic \(y\)-polynomial \(h(tx,y)\) by the earlier linear relation argument. Over \(\mathbb Q(x)\), the true jet uniquely determines the coefficients of \(t\)-degree at most \(d\). Otherwise the difference of two such candidate relations has a nonzero \(y\)-resultant with \(h(tx,y)\), of \(t\)-degree at most \(2d^2\), yet the Bézout identity forces it to vanish to \(t\)-order \(M\). Expand the computed \(E\)-coefficients in a rational basis, choose a full-rank square linear minor at \(\varepsilon=0\), and solve the perturbed system on those rows by determinants. Because the chosen limiting determinant is nonzero, the solutions agree to \(\varepsilon\)-order \(q\) in \(\mathbb Q(x)\). Set \(t=1\).

To remove the remaining variable denominators, choose rational \(\beta\) where the limiting determinant is nonzero, replace \(x\) by \(\beta+u(x-\beta)\), and expand the quotient through total degree \(d\) in the simultaneous scaling variable \(u\) for \(x-\beta\) and \(y\). This uses only scalar inversion in \(\mathbb Q(\varepsilon)\) of a constant regular and nonzero at zero. Evaluating \(u=1\) gives a degree-\(d\) polynomial approximation whose coefficients still agree modulo \(\varepsilon^q\). Restore coordinates and scale. All root, minor and shift choices are nonuniform. This proof is informal and makes no priority claim for border factor closure.
