# Exact linear-size reduction from translation constraints to surface homology localization

Grochow and Tucker-Foltz posed two approximation questions about unweighted 1-Homology Localization on triangulations of surfaces. Question 20 asks for unconditional NP-hardness at some approximation factor above one with binary coefficients. Question 21 asks whether the Unique Games Conjecture implies hardness for every constant factor, with the finite cyclic coefficient group allowed to depend on that factor. Borradaile, Maxwell, and Nayyeri already answered both in 2020, with binary coefficients even for Question 21. Their proof uses minimum uncut and subdivisions of the surface. The result here is a different reduction, with exact optimum preservation for every finite abelian coefficient group and linear simplicial output size. [Questions 20 and 21](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.06800#S6) · [Borradaile–Maxwell–Nayyeri, Theorem 1.6 and §4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.02801#page=12)

## Statement

Let $A$ be a finite abelian group, written additively. An instance $I$ consists of $m$ unweighted translation constraints $x_u-x_v=a_e$ over $A$, with repetitions allowed. Write $U(I)$ for the least number of violated constraints. In deterministic polynomial time one can construct a closed, connected, orientable simplicial surface $S$ with $O(m+1)$ simplices and a 1-cycle $z$ over $A$ such that
$$
\min_{y\in [z]} |\operatorname{supp} y|=3U(I).
$$
An assignment with $b$ violations supplies a homologous cycle with support $3b$. A homologous cycle with support $s$ supplies an assignment violating at most $s/3$ of the original constraints. Conversely, the homology problem on any closed orientable simplicial surface reduces to translation constraints with no change in the optimum. Hence the two minimization problems have the same approximation factors over each fixed $A$.

For an instance with no constraints, take a triangulated sphere and $z=0$. Otherwise discard isolated variables, and join the nontrivial constraint-graph components by a tree of zero-offset constraints. Any assignment before joining can be shifted independently in each component so that the added constraints all hold, by rooting the tree of components and treating its edges in order away from the root. Thus the optimum is unchanged and the number of added constraints is at most $m-1$.

## Exact degree reduction by equality constraints

Replace each variable of incidence count $d$ by the vertices of the Margulis–Gabber–Galil graph on $(\mathbb Z/s\mathbb Z)^2$ for $s=\lceil\sqrt d\rceil$. The case $d=1$ uses just a single vertex. Assign the $d$ incidences to distinct copies. Impose the old constraints between the prescribed copies. Impose six copies of the equality constraint on every edge of the expander, ignoring loops. The original graph may have loops, each of which uses two distinct incidences of the variable. [Margulis–Gabber–Galil bound, Theorem 8.2](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/BOOKS/expanderbookr1.pdf) · [Gabber–Galil, original construction](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(81)90040-4)

The 8-regular expander has second adjacency eigenvalue at most $5\sqrt{2}$. For a set $B$ of at most half the copies, the centered indicator in the Laplacian Rayleigh quotient gives
$$
|\partial B| \ge (8-5\sqrt 2)|B|\bigl(1-|B|/s^2\bigr) \ge h |B|,
\qquad h=(8-5\sqrt 2)/2>1/3.
$$
Orient each permutation edge of the construction arbitrarily as an equality constraint, counting edges of the undirected regular multigraph with their multiplicities. Loops do not belong to any cut, and deleting them preserves this cut bound.

For a labeling of the copies, let $r$ count copies outside a most frequent label class. If that class has size at least half, its complement has at most half, and at least $hr$ expander edges cross the split. Otherwise every class has size less than half. Summing its boundary bound over classes counts every disagreement edge twice, so there are at least $h s^2/2\ge hr/2$ expander disagreements. Rounding every copy to the most frequent label changes at most $r$ old-constraint incidences. It removes at least $6(hr/2)\ge r$ violated equality constraints. Over all variables, rounding therefore weakly decreases the total number of violations. Conversely a labeling of original variables extends by making each copy constant within its group, with no extra violations. The two instances have the same optimum and the assignment conversions have the stated cost properties.

The new degree is at most $49$, since a copy is used by at most one old incidence. There are $s^2\le 4d$ copies for a variable with $d$ incidences. All graphs are generated by their modular affine neighbor formulas, so stage size is linear in the old constraint count. The expander cut bound makes it connected after loop deletion, and the degree reduction preserves connectedness. It now suffices to build a surface with $O(\sum_v d_v^2)$ simplices for a constraint instance of degrees $d_v$, since they are bounded after this step.

## Constructing the surface

For each variable $v$, start a surface piece $S_v$ for its $d_v>0$ incidences. The piece will have one boundary cycle of length three per incidence, with three distinct adjacent triangles. Call these adjacent triangles the terminals. There are $N=3d_v$ terminals. We construct $S_v$ so that for every matching of terminals there are **two** dual paths between each matched pair, with all these paths edge-disjoint. These paths cross only interior edges of $S_v$.

For each incidence, start a triangular disk, insert an interior vertex, and join it to all three outer vertices. This makes three triangular sectors. In each sector insert a further interior vertex and subdivide into three triangles. The triangle containing the outer boundary edge is the terminal. The other two triangles each share a different edge with it. Replace each of those two triangles by an annulus obtained by placing a fresh triangular hole inside the original triangle and triangulating the surrounding annulus. The resulting incidence piece has one outer boundary triangle and six disjoint inner triangular holes. For each terminal its two holes can be reached through two distinct arms in the dual, each starting across the corresponding internal edge of the terminal. Paths for different terminals stay in their own sectors. An annulus used here can be triangulated using the six triangles
$$
(a_i,a_{i+1},b_{i+1}),\quad (a_i,b_{i+1},b_i)\qquad (i\ {\rm mod}\ 3),
$$
where the $a_i$ are outer vertices and the $b_i$ are the fresh inner vertices.

For each terminal $i$ and each of its arm indices $j\in\{1,2\}$, take a separate sphere with $N$ triangular holes. One hole attaches to the corresponding arm. Name the other $N-1$ holes by the remaining terminals. For each unordered pair $\{i,i'\}$ and each $j$, glue the hole named $i'$ on the sphere for $(i,j)$ to the hole named $i$ on the sphere for $(i',j)$. A sphere with $N$ holes is explicit with $O(N)$ triangles. Start with the bipyramid over a cycle of length $\max(3,N)$, select $N$ triangular faces, and replace each of the selected faces by a triangular annulus as above, with all inner vertices fresh.

For a pair $\{i,i'\}$ in a matching, travel from terminal $i$ along arm $j$, into its sphere, across the dedicated connector to the sphere for $(i',j)$, and from there along the opposite arm to terminal $i'$. Within each sphere with holes there is a path between the triangles adjacent to the two specified boundary edges in its interior dual graph. Indeed, in a connected triangulated surface with boundary, interior points of any two triangles can be joined by an interior path avoiding vertices and crossing edges transversely. Use any one edge of every gluing cycle crossed. Distinct pairs of the matching use disjoint spheres and disjoint terminal sectors. The two arms at a given terminal begin by crossing different edges. Thus the paths are edge-disjoint even when they have a terminal in common.

For each constraint glue the outer boundary cycles at its two incidences. Every gluing is by an orientation-reversing boundary simplicial isomorphism. The boundary components in every original piece have disjoint vertex sets. Gluing always pairs components of distinct original pieces, and a pair of original pieces shares at most one such identification. No original piece has a triangle on the three vertices of a boundary component. The only edges between two of those three vertices are the boundary edges. Consequently the glued complex is simplicial, with every identified vertex belonging to exactly two original pieces and with all common simplices precisely the intended boundary edges and vertices. The local link of a glued vertex is the circle made by joining its two former boundary link intervals. Thus the result is a closed orientable simplicial surface. It is connected by connectedness of the constraint graph and of each $S_v$. There are $O(N^2)$ triangles in $S_v$, giving the size asserted above.

## Homology class and exact cost

In the constraint $e=(u,v)$ with offset $a_e$, orient its glued seam cycle $\gamma_e$ by the boundary orientation induced by the piece on the $u$ incidence. This includes the case when both incidences are at one variable. Set
$$
z=\sum_e a_e\gamma_e .
$$
Each $\gamma_e$ is a closed 1-cycle, hence so is $z$. Give the surface a coherent orientation of its triangles. Labeling the triangles by $\lambda$ gives a 2-chain $X_\lambda$. The coefficient of $\partial X_\lambda$ on an edge with positive and negative incident triangles is the positive triangle's label minus the negative triangle's label. Therefore the support of $z-\partial X_\lambda$ counts exactly two kinds of disagreement:

- On an edge interior to a variable piece, the two incident labels are distinct.
- On a seam edge, the $u$-side label minus the $v$-side label does not equal $a_e$.

Every representative of $[z]$ has this form by definition. A variable assignment gives uniform labels within each $S_v$, with exactly three seam disagreements for each violated original constraint and with no internal disagreements.

Now consider arbitrary triangle labels. In a variable piece let $M$ be the largest terminal multiplicity, and let $T=N-M$. Terminals of distinct labels can be matched in at least $\min(T,\lfloor N/2\rfloor)$ pairs. If $M\ge N/2$, pair each non-majority terminal with a majority terminal. Otherwise list terminals by contiguous label classes. Put $q=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ and pair position $i$ with position $i+q$, for $1\le i\le q$. All classes have size at most $q$, so the two positions have different labels. The two paths for each matched pair each contain at least one internal disagreement, and edge-disjointness makes them distinct. Thus the number of internal disagreements is at least $2\min(T,\lfloor N/2\rfloor)\ge T$, using $T\le N-1$ if no class has size $N/2$.

Round all triangles of each $S_v$ to its most frequent terminal label, breaking ties arbitrarily. At most $T$ seam-edge incidences at that variable are changed, since each terminal has precisely one seam edge. All internal disagreements disappear. The total cost therefore cannot increase, and the rounded cost is exactly three times the violation count of the induced variable assignment. This gives both inequalities and the cost-preserving optimum formula. Composing with the degree-reduction rounding and restricting to the original constraints proves the decoding claim.

To decode from a representative $y$, recover face labels by propagating the differences prescribed by $z-y$ along the connected triangle dual graph, fixing an arbitrary starting triangle to label zero. Existence follows from $y\in[z]$. Each edge gives an equation in the group $A$, so traversal and checking take polynomial time. For the reverse reduction, use the surface's triangles as variables and one equation $x_+-x_-=z_e$ per oriented edge. The boundary calculation gives equality of costs in this direction.

## Consequences for the two published questions

With $A=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$, the exact reduction transfers NP-hardness of every approximation ratio below $11/8$ for minimizing violated equations. Constant rational weights in a fixed hardness gadget can be replaced by edge multiplicities. This gives the unconditional inapproximability asked for in Question 20. [Håstad–Huang–Manokaran–O'Donnell–Wright, Theorem 1.7](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v013a019/v013a019.pdf#page=3)

For any constant $c>1$, choose a constant $\epsilon<1/(c+1)$. The Unique Games Conjecture implies NP-hardness of distinguishing translation instances over a fixed cyclic group $\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z$ with $U(I)\le\epsilon m$ from those with $U(I)\ge(1-\epsilon)m$. The source gap is the one for equations $x_u-x_v=a_e$, with the standard unweighted conversion. Since the surface optimum is exactly $3U(I)$, the gap ratio exceeds $c$. This proves Question 21 using the full family of translation problems at the corresponding coefficient groups. The previously published binary Min-Uncut reduction already gives the factor-$c$ hardness with a single coefficient group for all $c$.
