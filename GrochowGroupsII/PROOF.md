# Exact epimorphism counts for central products by weighted matchings

Let \(G,H\) be finite Cayley-table groups. Assume \(H\) is perfect and \(H/Z(H)\) is a product of nonabelian simple groups. For each of at most \(|G|\) candidate quotient kernels described below, the number of epimorphisms with that kernel on the central quotient is a single coefficient of a group-algebra permanent. The cost per kernel is polynomial in the table lengths times \(2^k\max(1,|\Delta|)\), where \(k\) counts the simple factors of \(H/Z(H)\) and the finite abelian group \(\Delta\) is generated by the weights of alternative matching cycles. In particular \(2^k\le|H|\). The theorem allows arbitrary abelian centers and an arbitrary source. It combines the earlier chief-mask algorithm, central-lift equations, and the code formulation of central products. It extends cases of Grochow–Srivastava–Thakkar Questions 2 and 3(5).

Put \(B=Z(H)\). As in the chief-series proof, the preimage \(L\) of \(B\) is among intersections of centralizers of surviving nonabelian chief sections, because the quotient \(H/B\) has trivial solvable radical. Enumerate these at most \(|G|\) intersections. For any viable \(L\), \(G/L\) must be a product of the required simple groups, which can be tested by assembling its minimal normal subgroups and comparing sizes and simple types. Set \(J=G/[G,L]\), \(Z=L/[G,L]\). Then \(Z=Z(J)\), since \(G/L\) is centerless and the quotient made \(L\) central. Every desired map factors through \(J\).

Set \(D=J'\). The equality \(J=D Z\) follows since the quotient by \(Z\) is perfect, so \(D=[D,D]\). For each simple factor of \(J/Z\), let \(E_i\) be the derived group of its preimage. This is again perfect and maps onto that factor. The \(E_i\) commute, since their mutual commutators are central and a homomorphism from a perfect group to the center is trivial. They generate \(D\). Write \(F_j\) for the analogous commuting perfect subgroups of \(H\), which generate \(H\). All of these subgroups and quotients are computable by table scans.

Any desired epimorphism induces an isomorphism of simple-factor products and hence a bijection of factors. On a matched pair \(E_i,F_j\) it induces an epimorphism \(E_i\to F_j\), since the image together with the center covers \(F_j\), which is perfect. Conversely any such local epimorphisms give a homomorphism on the direct product of the \(E_i\). Their center values must agree with one map \(v:Z\to B\). This is the only condition to descend from the direct product of the \(E_i\) and \(Z\) to \(J\): a tuple multiplying to the identity in \(J/Z\) has each \(E_i\)-coordinate central, and then the only remaining condition is the central product relation. Every descended map is onto. A perfect central cover of a two-generated nonabelian simple group is two-generated, since any two lifts generating the simple quotient generate a subgroup whose product with the center is the whole cover, and taking derived groups gives the claim. Thus all local epimorphisms can be enumerated in polynomial table time by two generator images.

Let \(M=\bigoplus_i Z(E_i)\) and \(s:M\to Z\) be the central product map. Form the finite abelian group
\[
C=\operatorname{Hom}(M,B)/s^*\operatorname{Hom}(Z,B).
\]
In entry \(i,j\) put the formal sum of the classes in \(C\) of the central maps of all local epimorphisms \(E_i\to F_j\), extending each central map by zero on the other summands. A full matching chooses a tuple of local maps, and the coefficient of 0 in the permanent counts exactly the choices admitting an extension \(v\). Every such choice has \(|\ker s^*|\) possible central maps, all giving distinct homomorphisms. Multiply by this number, and sum over the possible \(L\). All finite abelian groups and classes here have cyclic presentations obtainable by Smith form. If \(J\) is perfect, \(s\) is onto, so \(C\) embeds into \(\operatorname{Hom}(\ker s,B)\). For elementary abelian centers this already bounds the count parameter by \(p^{\dim(\ker s)\dim B}\).

## Restricting the group-algebra state space

If no unweighted perfect matching exists the count vanishes. Otherwise fix one matching and one local epimorphism on each edge of it, of total class \(b_0\). Index columns by the rows they are matched to in this matching. Regard each local choice in the entry \(i,j\) as a directed edge \(i\to j\) with weight its class minus the base class of row \(i\). Every permutation is a union of directed cycles, so discard edges between strongly connected components. Within each component choose directed paths from a root to \(i\), of total weights \(p_i\), and replace the weight \(w\) on \(i\to j\) by \(p_i+w-p_j\). These changes telescope on cycles. The subgroup \(\Delta\) generated by the new edge weights is exactly that generated by all directed-cycle weights. One containment is telescoping, and for the reverse append a path from \(j\) back to the root to express each new edge weight as the difference of two closed-walk weights. Closed walks decompose into cycles.

Check that \(-b_0\) lies in \(\Delta\) by finite abelian linear algebra. If so, evaluate the permanent by the standard subset dynamic program over the group algebra of \(\Delta\), tracking the coefficient at \(-b_0\). This uses at most \(2^k|\Delta|\) times polynomial work including integer count arithmetic. Backtracking gives a witness and Smith form constructs the central extension if the count is positive. The exact count depends on the stated parameter. This proof is informal. [Published questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7)
