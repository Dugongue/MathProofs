# Rational rank-one projector feasibility with witnesses over every completion

## The dimension and incidence thresholds

A pattern consists of two finite lists of vertices and disjoint sets of cross-list pairs marked zero or nonzero. A realization in dimension $d$ assigns each vertex a symmetric rank-one idempotent matrix $P$ of size $d$. Each marked pair prescribes whether $\operatorname{tr}(PQ)$ vanishes. Unmarked pairs are free. All entries lie in the designated field. Over a subfield of the real numbers the nonzero overlaps are positive; these are exact overlaps of real rank-one quantum projectors. Over other completions use the algebraic zero/nonzero condition.

**Theorem.** Rational feasibility in dimension two has a linear-time decision algorithm. In dimension three, rational feasibility and Hilbert's tenth problem over the rationals are polynomial-time many-one equivalent. The latter equivalence persists even if the input includes explicit valid realizations over each of the three fields $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{13}),\mathbb Q(\sqrt{17}),\mathbb Q(\sqrt{221})$. In the reduction, each of these realizations uses a fixed finite set of projectors independent of the input. Every output therefore has a realization over the reals and over every $p$-adic field.

There are two additional sparsity bounds on the hard instances. One may impose maximum marked degree five on both lists. Alternatively, one may impose at most three zero requirements at each vertex of the first list. With at most two such requirements per first-list vertex, **every** pattern is rationally feasible in dimension three, regardless of the second list's degrees or the number of nonzero constraints. The two sparsity promises on the hard instances are established separately.

## Projective arithmetic on unpinned vertices

A nonzero rational vector $v$ gives the projector $vv^\mathsf T/(v^\mathsf T v)$. Conversely a nonzero column of any rational symmetric rank-one idempotent spans its image, which determines that projector. The overlap vanishes precisely when the two image vectors have zero dot product. Interpret the first list as projective points $[v]$ and the second as lines with equations $w^\mathsf T v=0$. This realizes exactly prescribed incidences and nonincidences. Arbitrary changes of projective coordinates on points act dually on lines and preserve the marked relations. The reasoning applies over each of the three real quadratic fields.

Choose four point vertices forming a projective frame. To enforce this with the allowed labels, for each pair of them require a line through both that excludes the other two. These conditions exclude coincidences and collinear triples. A projective change of coordinates sends the frame, in order, to $O=(0,0)$, the horizontal and vertical infinity points $I,J$, and $(1,1)$. The line through $I,J$ is the line at infinity. Horizontal and vertical projection of $(1,1)$ to the axes gives $E=(1,0)$ and $U=(0,1)$. Each scalar wire $x$ is represented by the affine point $(x,0)$, forced onto the horizontal axis and off the line at infinity. A construction that takes the join of distinct points or meet of distinct lines uses a fresh vertex with the two specified incidences, unless the output is already named.

Given a finite scalar $y$, the line joining $U$ and $(y,0)$ has infinity point $D_y$ in direction $(y,-1)$. For addition, vertically lift $(x,0)$ to $(x,1)$ and follow direction $D_y$ to the horizontal axis. Its intersection is $(x+y,0)$. For multiplication, send $(x,0)$ to $(0,x)$ along direction $(-1,1)$, given by the line through $E,U$. Follow $D_y$ from $(0,x)$ back to the horizontal axis. The result is $(xy,0)$. Every stated join and meet is unique even if either scalar is zero or the two input scalars agree. In particular each join defining $D_y$ uses points at different heights, and its direction has nonzero vertical component. Equality of two wire values can be forced by putting the second wire on the vertical line through the first. These constant-size gadgets interpret any system of rational additions and multiplications over the same coefficient field. Negative values can be represented by imposing $a+b=0$.

## Three finite certificates suffice at all places

Write an input polynomial equation as a system $F_i(z)=0$ of individual gate residuals, including its output constraint. Compile binary constants and exponents by arithmetic gates, using $0,1$ as the only input constants. Each residual therefore has one of finitely many types, namely a wire value, a wire minus a unit, or an addition, negation or product gate discrepancy. Introduce the fixed polynomial
$$
g(s)=(s^2-13)(s^2-17)(s^2-221).
$$
Replace every residual condition by $g(s)F_i(z)=0$ using the projective arithmetic construction. No factor of $g$ has a rational root, so rational feasibility is unchanged. Over each specified quadratic field set $s$ to the corresponding square root and set **every** original wire variable to zero. All the modified equations hold, including those that previously demanded a wire equal to one. This is why the residuals are guarded individually: on the auxiliary solution we never evaluate a long input circuit on the zero tuple. Evaluating an individual residual and the guard on this assignment uses only a fixed finite list of scalars and projective points, because there are only finitely many residual types. The three supplied projector assignments have linear length after compilation and constant bit length per entry. The intersective polynomial itself is classical. [Berend and Bilu, Example 1](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-96-03210-8)

Every completion of the rationals contains at least one of the three fields. For odd $p$ away from $13$ and $17$, either $13$ or $17$ is a square modulo $p$, or their product is a square. Hensel lifting gives the relevant root. At $13$, $17$ reduces to $4$; at $17$, $13$ reduces to $8^2$. At $2$, the odd unit $17$ is $1$ modulo $8$. The real place contains all three. Embedding the supplied matrices into any completion preserves all the marked relations. A single input equation $1=0$ already gives an explicit recipe for a pattern with this full collection of local witnesses and no rational solution.

For the reverse reduction, impose the polynomial equations $P^2=P$, $P=P^\mathsf T$ and $\operatorname{tr}(P)=1$ at each vertex. Over the rationals an idempotent is diagonalizable with eigenvalues zero and one, so trace one enforces rank one. Express a nonzero overlap $u$ with an extra variable satisfying $ut=1$. Package the equations by a sum of squares, or flatten gate conditions first for an explicit sparse polynomial. Correctness of the supplied quadratic witnesses can be checked exactly in polynomial time by field arithmetic. Thus the reverse works as a language reduction if invalid certificates are rejected.

## Sparse and low-dimensional cases

In dimension two the rational lines carry a fixed-point-free involution $L\mapsto L^\perp$. Zero-labeled edges force successive lines to alternate, so a zero-component is determined by its root line. Check parity by graph traversal, rejecting any forced conflict. Within one component reject a nonzero pair with opposite parity. Distinct components can use different pairs of complementary rational lines, of which infinitely many exist. These are all obstructions. With adjacency-list input the checks take linear time.

In dimension three, choose mutually distinct second-list lines with no triple concurrent. For example, take normals $(1,k,k^2)$ for distinct integers $k$; every triple is independent by Vandermonde. A point with two zero requirements uses the unique intersection of its two lines, which is on no third line. With one zero requirement choose a rational point on that line avoiding every other marked line. With zero requirements choose a rational point outside the finitely many forbidden lines. All first-list choices are independent.

To restrict the hard instances to three zero requirements on the first list, make a fresh point copy for every marked occurrence of an old point. Put all copies on two common private distinct lines. Distinguish the lines with a fresh point on the first and off the second. Thus all copies equal their intersection. Conversely any old point admits two distinct rational lines through it. Each copy has two private zero constraints and at most one external zero. The auxiliary distinguishing point has only one zero.

For maximum marked degree five on both sides, replace every vertex by a chain of single-occurrence copies, linking successive copies by the two-distinct-private-lines equality construction; use its dual when copies are lines. A copy uses at most four equality edges and one original marked edge. Auxiliary vertices have degree at most three. Both transformations preserve rational feasibility in both directions. In each finite quadratic-field assignment the new equality witnesses can be selected from a fixed finite list: fix once and for all one rational-over-that-field completion of the gadget for each possible duplicated label.

## Hypotheses forced to agree

These four statements have the same truth value.

1. Rational polynomial solvability is decidable.
2. The decision problem on the triply certified dimension-three patterns of maximum marked degree five is decidable.
3. There is a computable uniform upper bound, as a function of input length, on the least bit length of a rational realization of every feasible such pattern.
4. The rationally infeasible such patterns admit a recursively enumerable sound and complete system of negative certificates.

Indeed, a computable height bound allows a finite exhaustive search. A decider gives a bound by running it on every input through a given length, enumerating solutions to the affirmative cases, and taking a maximum. Positive rational realizations are enumerable, so effective enumeration of all negative certificates decides the problem by dovetailing. A total decider itself supplies a certificate computation for negative answers. For promised inputs, the included local certificates make validity of the promise decidable. If the announced negative answer to rational Hilbert tenth holds, all four statements are false. The equivalences, the dimension-two algorithm, the incidence threshold and the explicit failure of the local-to-global test are independent of that announcement. [OpenAI, rational Hilbert tenth manuscript](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/Hilberts-tenth-problem-over-the-rational-numbers-October-6-2026/main.pdf)

**Status and relation to earlier work.** Informal proof. Projective incidence universality and arithmetic via incidence constructions have a long history. Recent work obtains real existential-theory completeness for Kochen–Specker partial Boolean algebra satisfiability. Our relation specifies a partial pattern of pairwise transition overlaps, permits distinct vertices to receive the same projector, and restricts the desired coordinates to the rationals. The bounded-degree all-places promise, the three uniformly bounded witness lists, and the exact first-list incidence threshold are the claims of this note. Publication priority for their conjunction remains unverified. ## Pathwidth, tree-depth, and approximation

**Conditional localized undecidability.** Assume there is a fixed existential Diophantine definition of the integers over the rationals. Then for some absolute constant $w$ rational projector feasibility is recursively enumerable complete even on the above degree-five, triply-certified inputs with a supplied path decomposition of width at most $w$. The constant may be large. This step uses the integer definition hypothesis; it does not follow merely by changing the word “undecidable” to “complete” in a statement about rational Hilbert tenth. [Claimed integer definition](https://github.com/elliotglazer/diophantine-z-q)

Fix a recursively enumerable complete set $K$ of natural-number encodings. By DPRM there is one fixed Diophantine formula for $m\in K$ over the integers. Natural variables in a natural-number formulation may be replaced by sums of four integer squares. Replace each existential integer coordinate by a rational one plus a fresh copy of the fixed definition. The result is one fixed system over the rationals with $m$ its sole free parameter. Make $m$ a wire produced by a one-register chain $v\gets 2v+b$ reading its binary digits $b$. Flatten and guard every gate residual, including the register updates, exactly as above. In a quadratic bypass solution all register and formula variables are still zero. [Jones, Universal Diophantine Equation](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-symbolic-logic/article/abs/universal-diophantine-equation/113EBF3D928DFC6011C52BECAE81A087)

All gadgets for the fixed formula fit in one constant-size suffix. In the variable-length prefix keep the scalar-frame and guard vertices live throughout; a bit step needs only its old and new register points and its own constant-size incidence pattern. Listing one bag per bit and a suffix bag gives a path decomposition of constant width. The degree-five transformation preserves bounded pathwidth here. Process old edges in bag order, choosing one containing bag for each edge. For each currently live old vertex retain its last occurrence copy until the next one is needed. When processing an edge, create its two endpoint copies, attach each to its preceding copy by its constant-size equality gadget, add the old marked edge, and retire the superseded copies. No more than one retained copy per old live vertex and constantly many extra gadget vertices are needed at any moment. Thus the decomposition itself can be produced in polynomial time. The rational enumeration semidecides YES and the fixed halting set reduces to this single pathwidth slice.

**Unconditional contrast with fixed tree-depth.** For each fixed integer $d$, feasibility on the slice of marked graphs of tree-depth at most $d$ has a polynomial-time algorithm, nonuniform in $d$. First obtain a rooted forest of height at most $d$ whose ancestor closure contains the marked graph; for fixed $d$, recursive root guessing separately on connected components takes polynomial time. Color each vertex by its list and the full zero/nonzero/unspecified profile to its at most $d-1$ ancestors indexed by depth. Bottom-up, keep just one copy of every distinct reduced colored subtree type among siblings, also at the forest roots. Duplicated copies have no constraints between them. Every solution to the reduced graph extends by copying the assignments, since their profiles to each ancestor agree. Conversely restriction preserves feasibility. At fixed height the possible reduced types are finite by induction on height, since each parent stores a subset of the possible child types. Consequently a fixed finite truth table for rational feasibility of the reduced types decides this slice. This argument gives no effective means of obtaining all of those truth tables uniformly in $d$ for the rational target. Supplied quadratic certificates can be validated independently of the pruning. Thus the conditional hardness of a single bounded-pathwidth slice is compatible with unconditional decidability of every fixed tree-depth slice.

**Effective approximate realizations on both sides of the decision problem.** In the reductions above there is an absolute $\delta>0$ such that, for every requested precision $m$, rational projectors with all prescribed zero overlaps at most $2^{-m}$ and all prescribed nonzero overlaps at least $\delta$ can be written in time polynomial in input length and $m$. Use one real quadratic atlas and take the minimum of its finitely many positive overlaps. Approximate a nonzero image vector of each atlas member by a dyadic rational vector to $m+O(1)$ places, and form its exactly idempotent rational projector by the formula above. The projector map has bounded derivative in a small fixed neighborhood of each chosen nonzero vector. Continuity and the finite list of labels give the upper error and a fixed positive lower margin, for example half the smallest positive overlap. This constructs approximate assignments even for inputs with no exact rational solution.
