# An exact–border gap for a rational, irreducible hypersurface in a determinantal ideal

**Theorem (informal).** Work over the complex numbers and let $e=n/2$ for even $n$. There is a family of homogeneous, absolutely irreducible polynomials $F_n$ with integer coefficients in the ideal of the $e$-minors of the generic $n$ by $n$ matrix. Each hypersurface $F_n=0$ is rational. Put $M=\lceil n^{\log\log\log n}\rceil$ for sufficiently large $n$ and $D=eM+1$. For each fixed depth and each target size $t=n^{\Theta(1)}$, exact $F_n$-evaluation-oracle circuits for $\det_t$ need $\Omega(\sqrt M)=D^{1/2-o(1)}$ size. Size counts gates and wires with scalar parameters charged. Circuits may have arbitrary complex constants and nested calls. Andrews and Forbes give polynomial-size, depth-three *border* extraction for this very same family at $t=\Theta(e^{1/3})$. There is no effective height or efficient coefficient-generation assertion about our integer coefficients. [Andrews and Forbes, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00792) · [Dey and Guo, §4, item 1](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.49)

## The polynomials

Take two vertex-disjoint diagonal $e$ by $e$ blocks of the matrix and let $H_1,H_2$ be their determinants. Take four distinct entries $z_0,z_1,u,v$ from outside both blocks. Write $Z_i=z_i^e$. Use four formal coordinates in the order $(H_1,H_2,Z_0,Z_1)$. Let $V$ be the span of the degree-$M$ monomials using at least one of the first two and at least one of the last two coordinates. Let $P,Q$ be forms in $V$ with jointly algebraically independent coefficients over the rationals. Below, evaluating either at the four displayed polynomials is implicit. Define
$$F_n=uP+vQ.$$
It is homogeneous of degree $D$ and every term belongs to the $e$-minors ideal. Its two coefficient polynomials $P,Q$ are coprime over the complex numbers. Indeed, the pairs of homogeneous degree-$eM$ forms having a common factor of positive degree form a closed set in their product of projective coefficient spaces. For each possible factor degree, multiply the common factor by the two cofactors. The image of that projective parameter space is closed, and the union over degrees is finite. Our family avoids containment in that closed set: $H_1^{M-1}Z_0$ and $H_2^{M-1}Z_1$ have disjoint variables and hence are coprime. The universal coefficients may therefore be taken outside the closed set. Since $u,v$ are fresh, $uP+vQ$ is primitive over the polynomial ring in the remaining entries and is linear irreducible over its fraction field. Gauss's lemma gives absolute irreducibility. On the dense open set $P\ne0$, solve $u=-vQ/P$. The affine hypersurface is birational to affine space, and the same calculation on an affine chart proves rationality of the projective hypersurface.

## Interpolation on query points

For $K$ an infinite field, consider $m$ distinct $K$-rational points of projective three-space, each having a nonzero hard coordinate (first pair) and a nonzero easy coordinate (last pair). Normalize representatives by the first nonzero easy coordinate. If $m+1\le M$, evaluation of $V_K$ on these representatives has rank $m$. To isolate any one point, for each other point choose a linear form vanishing there and nonzero at the chosen point. Multiply these $m-1$ forms by one nonvanishing hard coordinate and one nonvanishing easy coordinate of the chosen point. Pad to degree $M$ with that same easy coordinate. This form lies in $V_K$ and isolates the chosen point.

Consider a size-$s$ circuit for $G=\det_t$. Replace each $F_n$ call using one $P$ and one $Q$ call, two multiplications and an addition. The new circuit has $O(s)$ size and depth increased by at most an absolute constant factor plus a constant. Under its actual polynomial semantics, consider each oracle's projective query point over $\mathbb C(X)$, where $X$ are the target variables. If either coordinate pair vanishes identically, the answer is zero. Otherwise use the first easy coordinate nonzero as a polynomial, say $Z_i$, and normalize the four coordinates by dividing by it. This symbolic choice is fixed per gate. Homogeneity gives answer $Z_i^M$ times the normalized value. The divisions occur only in the proof. In the stripped circuit $Z_i^M$ is a power of an actual easy argument, computed by a product of $eM$ copies of that argument.

Hardwire normalized answers at projectively constant points. Among the remaining calls group identical normalized points separately for $P$ and for $Q$. Let $r_P,r_Q$ be their distinct counts and $r=r_P+r_Q$. Cut each normalized answer, using a formal input $Y_l$ for its group and retaining the easy factor $Z_i^M$. This gives an ordinary circuit $H(X,Y)$ of size $O(sD)$ and depth $O(1)$ at every fixed original depth. Its constants together form a list $A$ of length $a=O(s)$, including all normalized constant answers. All these constants can depend arbitrarily on both coefficient vectors.

Use $N=a+1$ independent copies $X_j$ of the input variables, jointly independent over $\mathbb C$. Let $E_0=\mathbb Q(A,X_1,\ldots,X_N)$ and adjoin all $Nr$ actual normalized answers $Y_{jl}$ to obtain $E$. Every actual normalized projective coordinate belongs to $E$: recompute each ordinary input recursively using previous actual answers, evaluate the two determinants in this field, and normalize using its field operations. For a fixed oracle, points in the same copy are distinct by grouping, and points in different copies cannot coincide since their normalized tuples are nonconstant functions on disjoint variables.

Suppose $N\max(r_P,r_Q)+1\le M$. By interpolation, the oracle evaluation equations have rank $Nr_P$ in the coefficients of $P$ and $Nr_Q$ in the independent coefficients of $Q$. Denote by $C$ both coefficient vectors together, of length $b$. Thus $\mathrm{trdeg}_E E(C)\le b-Nr$. Also $\mathrm{trdeg}_{E_0} E_0(C)\ge b-a$, since the copies of $X$ are independent of all constants. By the tower law,
$$\mathrm{trdeg}_{E_0} E\ge Nr-a.$$
If $H(X,Y)-G(X)$ is a nonzero polynomial then substitution $X=X_j$ leaves a nonzero polynomial in the $r$ cut variables for every $j$, and each of the $N$ actual output tuples satisfies its respective equation. For $r>0$ this bounds the joint transcendence degree over $E_0$ by $N(r-1)$, a contradiction. When $r=0$ the circuit has already been stripped. Thus $H(X,0)$ computes $G$. The resulting circuit is division-free. This proves that either $s=\Omega(\sqrt M)$ or $G$ has an ordinary circuit of the indicated size and depth.

For $G=\det_t$, the ordinary circuit alternative is incompatible with $s$ bounded by a sufficiently small constant times $\sqrt M$. Indeed the iterated product of $d=\lfloor\sqrt{\log w}\rfloor$ generic matrices of width $w=\lfloor\sqrt{t}\rfloor$, with one specified output entry, projects from the determinant: its layered path sum is a cofactor of the unitriangular matrix $I-A$ of the layered branching program, with identity padding to size $t$. Forbes's characteristic-independent fixed-product-depth lower bound is $w^{\Omega(d^{c})}$ for a positive depth-dependent $c$. It exceeds $O(sD+\mathrm{poly}(t))$ for these choices. [Forbes, Theorem 10](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2024.31)

For integer coefficients, at each $n$ exclude the common-factor locus above, and exclude exceptional circuit topologies as follows. For a topology, dimension and size with the interpolation inequality guaranteed by its size, and for which the stripped ordinary circuit bound is impossible, the coefficient vectors admitting an exact circuit of that topology form a constructible set over $\mathbb Q$. Correctness equates finitely many coefficients of polynomials, with the circuit constants existentially quantified. The preceding argument excludes the generic point, so this constructible set has proper closure. There are finitely many topologies below the size threshold and finitely many target sizes $t\le M$ at each $n$. Avoid the union of these proper closed sets and the locus of zero forms. Zariski density of integer vectors supplies one choice, simultaneously for all these topologies and dimensions. Any polynomially growing target eventually lies in the tested range. This yields the claimed family.

## Consequences for the hypothesis ledger

The joint assumptions of exact extraction at size $n^{O(1)}D^{o(1)}$, fixed depth, and applicability to all homogeneous absolutely irreducible rational hypersurfaces in these ideals are inconsistent. On the same examples polynomial-size border extraction is a published theorem. Thus restricting the oracle by these three geometric properties simultaneously does not restore a degree-independent debordering theorem. Here rationality means birational equivalence over the complex numbers; it does not provide a small circuit for the birational map. The coefficient family and geometric strengthening are informal results of this analysis. Priority has not been established.


## Two exact equivalences by removing the depth bound

There is an unrestricted-depth consequence over the complex numbers. Let $g$ be any nonzero homogeneous polynomial of positive degree $e$ with rational coefficients. Replace $H_1,H_2$ above by two disjoint copies of $g$, and use four fresh variables. All projective interpolation and irreducibility arguments remain valid. Without a depth restriction, repeated squaring implements each easy factor with $O(\log(eM+1))$ gates. Consequently there is a homogeneous absolutely irreducible rational-hypersurface oracle $F$ with integer coefficients in the ideal of the two copies (when $g$ has integer coefficients), such that for the target $g$, either an exact oracle circuit of size $s$ exceeds $c\sqrt M$, for an absolute $c>0$, or an ordinary circuit computes $g$ with size $O(s(1+\log(eM+1)))$. To obtain one integer oracle for all $s$ below this threshold, use the same finite-topology specialization. For each size where the ordinary upper bound is unavailable, exclude every corresponding oracle topology; for other sizes the disjunction is automatic.

Define a **universal extraction claim** for a family $g$ to mean that, for every family of such homogeneous, absolutely irreducible integer oracles with rational hypersurfaces in the two-copy ideals, the target $g$ has exact circuits using the corresponding oracle at the same index, within the given size budget. The oracles can have unrestricted degree and have no explicitness requirement. The circuits may depend nonuniformly on the oracle family.

* For $g_n=\operatorname{per}_n$ and polynomial size, this claim is equivalent to $\mathrm{VP}=\mathrm{VNP}$ over the complex numbers.
* For the weighted clique polynomial $g_{n,k}$ of Ikenmeyer's equation (4.16), $1\le k\le n$, with parameterized size $f(k)n^{O(1)}$, the claim is equivalent to $\mathrm{VFPT}=\mathrm{VW}[1]$ over the complex numbers.

Indeed take $M=2^n$ and the integer oracles just constructed at every relevant index. For the permanent, any polynomial oracle-size bound eventually lies below $c\sqrt M$, and the ordinary simulation costs only a polynomial factor. Valiant's completeness theorem now applies. For clique, fix $k$ and apply the same reasoning whenever $f(k)n^a<c 2^{n/2}$. All sufficiently large $n$ satisfy this for each $k$. An ordinary explicit monomial enumeration at the finitely many remaining $n$ costs a function of $k$, which can be absorbed into the parameter dependence. If a computable parameter function is required, a threshold can be computed from the given $f(k),a,c$, and the enumeration bound is computable. The ordinary simulation costs $O(n+\log(k+1))$ per gate. Apply parameterized clique completeness. The reverse implications in both cases use ordinary circuits and ignore the oracle. These are equivalences for the extraction claims defined here; neither separates the underlying classes. [Valiant, completeness classes in algebra](https://doi.org/10.1145/800135.804419) · [Ikenmeyer, equation (4.16) and Proposition 7.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08363) · [Bläser and Engels, parameterized Valiant classes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.12287)


**A sharp one-to-two generator threshold.** In the principal ideal $(g)$, an irreducible nonzero member $F$ must equal $cg$ for a nonzero scalar whenever $g$ is nonconstant. Extraction uses one call and rescaling, for any circuit depth allowing that rescaling. For the permanent this case is nonvacuous even with the rational-hypersurface restriction. It is homogeneous and irreducible: in any factorization both factors are homogeneous in each row and column grading (compare the minimal and maximal degrees in a product in a domain). Row and column degrees are one, so each row and each column belongs to just one factor. Every matrix entry occurs, forcing its row and column to belong to the same factor. Thus all belong to one factor. The hypersurface is rational since one can solve for the first entry wherever its nonzero complementary permanent is nonvanishing. Consequently one generator always allows extraction in this geometric class, whereas two disjoint copies already give the equivalence with $\mathrm{VP}=\mathrm{VNP}$ above. Strictness of this threshold in unrestricted polynomial size is conditional on separation of those classes.
