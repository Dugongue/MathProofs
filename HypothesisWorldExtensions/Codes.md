# Low bits of binary-code weight enumerators

Let an affine binary code be given by a syndrome system $Hx=y$, with $n$ columns. For fixed integers $a,b$ set

$$ Z_{a,b}(H,y)=\sum_{Hx=y} a^{n-|x|} b^{|x|}. $$

The precision problem returns this integer modulo $2^t$, parameterized by $t\ge1$. Matrices and syndromes are explicit. Column degree counts the number of checks containing each variable. Our starting points are earlier parity-clique reductions for modular perfect matchings, the literature on sparse solutions of binary equations, and classical hardness of exact code-weight evaluation. The resource here is the number of low bits requested. [Sparse linear equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08270) · [Exact weight enumeration](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0304044) · [Previous modular matching proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/ParameterizedCircuitFrontiers/GeneralMatchings.md)

**Theorem.** If $a,b$ have the same parity, or either vanishes, the precision problem is deterministic fixed-parameter tractable on arbitrary affine binary codes. For every fixed nonzero pair of opposite parity, already on matrices of column degree at most three the precision problem is deterministic fixed-parameter Turing-equivalent to parity clique. The latter equivalence holds also with the promise $y=0$. No separation of parameterized counting classes is assumed. For larger column degree our lower reduction still applies.

**The easy cases.** If one activity is zero, only a constant word can contribute (the empty word contributes one). If both are even, $n\ge t$ forces zero and otherwise at most $2^t$ words need inspection. Suppose both are odd. On a nonempty affine solution space of dimension $h$, expand the product of factors $a+(b-a)x_i$ as an integer sum. A term selecting $j$ coordinates has a coefficient divisible by $2^j$. Its monomial sums to zero or a power of two divisible by $2^{\max(h-j,0)}$, by imposing at most $j$ independent affine conditions. Thus $2^h$ divides the entire answer. Gaussian elimination returns zero for an empty space; when $h\ge t$ return zero, and otherwise enumerate its fewer than $2^t$ points. This also works for differing pairs of odd activities at different coordinates.

**Lower reduction.** Assume $a$ is odd and $b$ is even, and put $v=v_2(b)$. Work first with an affine system. For a clique of size $k$ create one choice slot per position and one per position pair. The former choose a vertex; the latter choose an edge labelled by its endpoints in increasing order of vertex ID. Require the two vertex slots to agree with those endpoint labels in increasing position order. These assignments count ordinary unordered cliques exactly once. Write $s=k+\binom{k}{2}$. Introduce a bit for each available choice in each slot and require odd total parity in every slot. For each incidence of a vertex slot with an edge slot, introduce a separate block of rows indexed by vertex IDs. Each choice puts a one at the indicated ID, and the syndrome on that block is zero. On a selection of total weight $s$ there is exactly one choice per slot. All the incidence equations then enforce literal endpoint consistency. There are no solutions of smaller weight.

Every original column has weight at most $L=\max(3,k)$. Replace its variable by a chain of $L$ copies constrained equal by binary checks. Assign its original row incidences injectively to copy positions. Each new column has weight at most three. Every original assignment has one extension of weight $L$ times its original weight. At precision $t=vLs+1$, all weights larger than $Ls$ vanish and bit $vLs$ of the answer is exactly the parity of the $k$-cliques. Factors from the odd activity and the odd part of the even activity are odd. Empty slots return zero and $k<2$ can be handled directly. Swapping opposite-parity activities on affine systems complements every bit and changes the syndrome by $H\mathbf1$. The construction takes fixed-parameter time.

**Upper reduction.** Normalize to odd $a$, even $b$ by the same affine shift. Only weights $0\le j<t/v$ contribute. Let $N_j$ count weight-$j$ solutions. At column degree at most three, their descriptions are strictly increasing lists of $j$ column indices. If the syndrome has more than $3j$ ones then $N_j=0$. Otherwise checking a description needs at most $3j$ row IDs from table lookups in the input. Compare each of these IDs and the at most $3j$ target IDs by pairwise equality; compare their multiplicity parities, ignoring padding. This certifies the entire syndrome, including rows outside its support.

Bit $i<t$ of $N_j$ is the parity of the number of strictly increasing lists of $2^i$ distinct valid descriptions, since $\binom{N_j}{2^i}\equiv\operatorname{bit}_i(N_j)\pmod2$. Validity and lexicographic order of these lists use a parameter-bounded number of pair comparisons, table lookups and Boolean gates. Each gate has a unique value. For a binary constraint problem take one color class of values per variable and link compatible values across classes. Encode each ternary gate by a separate allowed-tuple variable with projection constraints, which preserves the unique extension property. The resulting cliques taking all colors count the lists exactly. All domains and tables have size $f(t)\operatorname{poly}(|H|+|y|)$ with fixed polynomial exponent. Recover the $N_j$ residues by parity-clique queries and sum $N_j a^{n-j}b^j$ modulo $2^t$; only use $j\le n$. This gives the claimed upper reduction.

**Homogeneous linear codes suffice.** For arbitrary affine column-degree-three inputs use a flag $u$ and replace the syndrome by $yu$, adding $m=\max(1,|y|)$ equal copies of $u$ linked by a chain and assigning each of the $|y|$ incidences to a separate copy. The new system has zero syndrome and column degree at most three. Its enumerator is $a^m Z(H,0)+b^m Z(H,y)$. Subtract the first term via a second homogeneous query. If $b$ is odd invert $b^m$ modulo $2^t$. If $b$ is even, $a$ is odd and a contributing word must have weight less than $t/v_2(b)$; return zero if $|y|>3(t-1)/v_2(b)$. Otherwise ask for $t+m v_2(b)$ bits and divide by the known power of two before inverting the odd part. In both cases the requested precision is bounded in $t$. This transfers the hardness to ordinary linear codes without any promise on the row degrees.

**What changes if precision is unrestricted?** At activities $(1,2)$, polynomial time with $t$ supplied in unary is equivalent to polynomial-time computation of every #P function, even for homogeneous column-degree-three codes. For the lower bound begin with an explicitly listed bounded-arity constraint system. Use one slot for each variable and one for each constraint's satisfying tuple; let $s$ count all slots. Incidence equalities are as in the clique reduction. This is parsimonious for counting Boolean formula solutions by putting in deterministic gate constraints of arity at most three. Let $N$ be the total number of choices. Replace columns by equal chains of common length $L>N$ also exceeding their maximum degree. At precision $Ls+N+1$, all weights above $Ls$ vanish and the window of $N+1$ bits starting at $Ls$ holds the entire solution count (at most $2^N$). The homogeneous conversion above has polynomial overhead. Conversely $Z_{1,2}$ itself counts nondeterministic witnesses: for each selected coordinate guess one independent extra bit, and for each unselected coordinate force it to zero. Bit reduction is polynomial at unary precision. This also proves that a polynomial bound in the numeric precision on this family would put ordinary graph isomorphism in P: count vertex bijections passing the adjacency test as a #P function and test for a positive count. Fixed-parameter tractability alone gives no such bound.

**Status.** Informal. The sparse-equation literature treats the existence of low-weight words as well. The exact classification here concerns modular evaluation for fixed activities and degree-three columns; publication priority has not been established.
