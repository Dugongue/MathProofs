# Which modular homomorphism observations can coexist?

Two connected graphs can agree on every homomorphism count modulo 5 while disagreeing on some probe modulo 2 and some probe modulo 3. No pair of modules over any common finite field can give these answers. Each two-answer restriction occurs, and every graph pattern on any two moduli can be reproduced by a module pair. For every larger number of observations there are also minimally impossible module patterns whose every proper restriction has a concrete realization. [Proof of the threshold, infinite family and decision test](ThreeModuli.md)

The result classifies observations on a *pair* of targets. The realizing pair and the finite field may depend on the observations. The obstruction applies specifically to preserving the answers to the same modular Hom predicates. It leaves ordinary isomorphism decision reductions open. The arguments are informal and publication priority remains unverified.

## Four proof directions

1. **Isomorphism.** The entire divisor profile of a nonisomorphic module pair over a field of order $q$ is governed by one integer $q^a(q^b-1)$. Equal-dimensional diagonal matrices realize every such integer. Connected graphs one vertex away from a forest realize every positive integer. Over fixed $q$ a single modulus occupying $\log_2 n+O_q(1)$ bits suffices in dimension $n$ when polynomial-dimensional probes are available; this is optimal up to an additive constant. The bound is on bits per count. It does not furnish a polynomial-time search for a separating probe. [Structural and precision theorems](Isomorphism.md)
2. **Algebraic complexity.** In a principal ideal generated by one minor, irreducibility permits exact extraction of the minor by one oracle call and rescaling. Two disjoint half-size minors already support homogeneous, absolutely irreducible integer oracles defining rational hypersurfaces for which exact fixed-depth extraction costs $D^{1/2-o(1)}$ at degree $D$. Polynomial-size border extraction remains possible for the same inputs. Without a depth bound, the corresponding universal two-permanent extraction claim is equivalent to $\mathrm{VP}=\mathrm{VNP}$; the two-clique version is equivalent to $\mathrm{VFPT}=\mathrm{VW}[1]$. The oracles have unrestricted degree and no efficient coefficient-generation requirement. [Oracle proof](Algebra.md)
3. **Arithmetic geometry.** Rational feasibility of dimension-three rank-one projector overlap patterns is polynomial-time equivalent to Hilbert's tenth problem over the rationals. The reduction supplies witnesses in three fixed real quadratic fields, collectively giving realizations over every completion, even on infeasible rational inputs. If the integers are Diophantine in the rationals, one fixed pathwidth slice with a supplied decomposition is recursively enumerable complete. In dimension two the decision takes linear time. [Projector proof](Arithmetic.md)
4. **Coding and counting.** For binary linear or affine codes with at most three checks per coordinate, low-bit evaluation at any fixed nonzero pair of opposite-parity integer activities is parameterized Turing-equivalent to parity clique. For odd activities an affine solution space of dimension $h$ gives a sum divisible by $2^h$, yielding a fixed-parameter algorithm even with arbitrarily dense columns. At activities $(1,2)$, polynomial time in unary precision on degree-three linear codes would compute every #P function in polynomial time. [Code proof](Codes.md)

## Reproduction

The expanded [statements](claims.txt) and [implications](rules.txt) extend the [previous ledger](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/GrochowHypoworldsExpansion). Historical source labels, definitions and scope are inherited. The published-only profile excludes informal proof rules, and the local profile includes them. Claims about conjectures are propositional unless a linked proof establishes realizability. In particular, the ledger never assumes the Diophantine hypothesis. Finite modular Hom profiles have the additional realization theorem.

The ledger grew from 211 to 284 propositions. The local profile contains 473 rules and forces 46 true and 36 false propositions. It excludes 550 signed pairs after singleton exclusions and has 302 inclusion-minimal three-premise conflicts. The published-only profile contains 183 rules, forces 12 true and zero false, and has 268 pair-only and 172 minimal three-premise conflicts. These tallies count relations among entries, including inherited and definitional consequences.

For the 13 listed moduli there are 8,192 candidate assignments. Exactly 72 occur for binary modules, 162 for modules with freely chosen finite field, and 192 for graphs. The triples $2,3,5$ give respectively 6, 7 and 8 of the 8 possible assignments. Replays of the full ledger are in [published-summary.json](published-summary.json) and [local-summary.json](local-summary.json). The large model counts encode proposition assignments and cannot be interpreted as probabilities of conjectures.

Run from this folder with Python 3:

```sh
python test_profile.py --agree 5 --disagree 2 3
python test_profile.py --agree 3 7 --disagree 2 63
python profiles.py --all-fields 2 3 5
python worlds.py --profile local --triples --output replay
python worlds.py --profile published --triples --output published-replay
```
