# Two modular Hom comparisons transfer; three can distinguish graphs from every finite field

For a pair of finite graphs let $E_m$ mean that their homomorphism counts from every finite graph are congruent modulo $m$. For a pair of finite-dimensional modules over a common algebra over a common finite field, define $E_m$ using every finite-dimensional module as probe. Allow the algebra, field and target pair to depend on the graph pair and the list of moduli.

**Theorem.** For every list of at most two positive moduli, the attainable truth patterns of the $E_m$ are identical in the two settings. At three moduli they can differ. Two connected simple graphs on 36 vertices each, both of diameter two and of feedback vertex number one, agree at 5 and disagree at both 2 and 3. No pair of modules over any common finite field realizes those three answers. In particular even an arbitrarily expensive pair-dependent translation retaining these three predicates exactly cannot exist.

**Full finite-list classification.** Given any finite list, a pattern is realizable by modules over some finite field if and only if it is the divisibility pattern on that list of an even positive integer or of a number $2^j-1$ with $j\ge1$. For graphs it is the divisibility pattern of any positive integer. All-true on a finite list is included, via a multiple of its least common multiple. [Underlying module and graph realizations](Isomorphism.md)

Indeed, the structural theorem for modules gives the divisor profile of $q^a(q^b-1)$, with $a\ge0$, $b\ge1$. If this integer is odd, $q$ must be even and $a=0$, so it is $2^j-1$. Conversely all such numbers are realized over the binary field. For an even integer $L$ and a finite query list, choose an odd prime $q$ for which $q-1$ agrees with $L$ in prime valuations up to all the precision queried. In detail, for each prime $p$ dividing any query modulus, put $h=v_p(L)$ and require $q\equiv 1+p^h\pmod{p^{h+1}}$. If $p=2$, then $h\ge1$; if $p$ is odd and $h=0$ the chosen residue is 2. In every case the residue is coprime to $p$. The Chinese remainder theorem followed by Dirichlet's theorem gives an odd prime in the simultaneous residue class. Now use $a=0,b=1$ at that field. This realizes the prescribed finite pattern. The field size in this existence proof has no claimed algorithmic bound here. [Dirichlet's theorem](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-785-number-theory-i-fall-2021/mit18_785f21_lec18.pdf)

For two moduli only the case of an odd graph divisor $L$ requires attention. If both moduli are odd, $2L$ reproduces the answers. If both are even, $2^1-1=1$ reproduces the two negative answers. With one even and one odd modulus $m$, the even answer is negative. If $m$ divides $L$, choose $j$ with $m\mid 2^j-1$; if it does not, use $j=1$. The modulus 1 always gives a positive answer. The graph classification realizes every module pattern as well.

For three moduli use the graph construction with $L=5$. An integer giving these answers must be odd. For an odd module profile, $5\mid 2^j-1$ forces $4\mid j$, hence $3\mid 2^j-1$. This contradicts the required negative answer at 3. The three proper two-coordinate restrictions have explicit module witnesses with profile integers $1$ over the binary field, $15$ over the binary field, and $20$ over the field of order five. The obstruction concerns simultaneous preservation of these homomorphism-count predicates. Ordinary many-one reductions of isomorphism decision problems can discard those predicates.

## Minimal contradictions of every arity

**Theorem.** For every integer $h\ge3$ there is a set of $h$ specified modular answers that is realized by a connected pair of graphs and impossible over any finite field for modules. Every proper subset of the answers is realized by a pair of equal-dimensional diagonal matrices over a suitable finite field. Thus no fixed number of simultaneous tests detects all impossible partial assignments of this kind, even if every test of that arity has already been classified.

The triple above gives $h=3$. For $h=s+2$, $s\ge2$, choose distinct primes $p_1,\ldots,p_s$, write $P=\prod_i p_i$, set $m_i=2^{p_i}-1$ and $R=2^P-1$. Specify disagreement at 2, agreement at all $m_i$, and disagreement at $R$. The odd integer $L=\operatorname{lcm}(m_1,\ldots,m_s)$ witnesses the graph pattern. Indeed $L\le\prod_i(2^{p_i}-1)<2^{\sum_i p_i}\le 2^P$, with $L<R$ since $\sum_i p_i<P$ for distinct primes when $s\ge2$. The graph construction gives two connected graphs of diameter two and feedback vertex number one.

Over any finite field, disagreement at 2 forces an odd profile $2^j-1$. Since $\operatorname{ord}_{2^d-1}(2)=d$ for $d\ge2$, the positive tests imply $p_i\mid j$ for every $i$. Hence $P\mid j$ and $R\mid 2^j-1$, contradicting the last answer.

There are explicit certificates of minimality in the classification. Drop the negative answer at $R$ and use the binary module divisor $2^P-1$. Drop the positive answer at $m_i$ and use the binary module divisor $2^{P/p_i}-1$. Drop the negative answer at 2 and use the positive even integer $2L$ in the finite-list construction; $R$ is odd and does not divide $L$. Each deletion therefore gives a realizable module pattern. Any smaller restriction is contained in one of these deletions. For example, agreement at 3 and 7 together with disagreement at 2 and 63 is a four-answer obstruction, and every three of these answers occur together for some module pair.

For arbitrary lists of positive and negative observations there is a direct test. In the even case try the least common multiple of 2 and all positive moduli. Every even candidate is divisible by this number, so if it violates a negative observation, all do. In the odd case an even positive modulus makes this branch impossible. Otherwise try the least positive exponent divisible by the orders of 2 at every positive modulus (use 1 for an empty list). Every candidate exponent is a multiple of it; again any violated negative observation stays violated. The implementation tests the two branches and returns a witness on success. On failure it prunes unnecessary observations and returns a realization for every single-observation deletion from the retained core. It computes multiplicative orders by iteration and makes no polynomial bit-time claim. [Executable test](test_profile.py)

The assertion about bounded tests concerns consistency on subsets of the original observations. Auxiliary variables encoding the exponent or a direct divisibility computation can detect the contradiction. Merely adding a larger fixed cutoff on the number of signed premises will never capture this entire family.

**Status.** Informal. The sharp arity threshold and unbounded minimal contradictions follow from the realization theorem and elementary multiplicative orders together with Dirichlet's theorem. Publication priority remains unverified.
