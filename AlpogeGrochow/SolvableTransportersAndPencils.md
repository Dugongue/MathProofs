# Small-prime local-to-global detection for rational tensor isomorphism

This is an informal proof from the Dirichlet zero-free half-plane theorem stated in the Alpöge release. The release and this application have no independent local end-to-end kernel or axiom audit. The bounds and reductions below are confined to the stated classes.

## Analytic dependency

The release's `lean/src/Main.lean` declares `LFunction_ne_zero_of_half_lt_re : DirichletLNonvanishingRightOfHalf := allChi`. The proposition in `lean/src/Statements.lean` gives nonvanishing for every complex Dirichlet character at every point of real part greater than one half, with the trivial character's point at one excepted. Together with the classical functional equation for primitive characters, this supplies the Dirichlet ERH hypothesis in Bach's small-prime theorem over the rational field. This step uses the arbitrary Dirichlet-character endpoint. The Riemann zeta endpoint alone does not supply it. [Alpöge release announcement](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)

Bach's theorem gives a prime outside any proper subgroup of the units modulo m, coprime to m and at most 3(log m)². Write b(m) for the binary bit length of m and use the integer bound B(m) = 3 b(m)². To detect a nontrivial character of the units modulo m use its kernel. [Bach, 1990](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1990-1023756-8), [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Section 1.1](https://www.math.ksu.edu/about/personal/xiannan/papers/QuaResL1ver2Sound.pdf)

**Solvable splitting detection.** Suppose a nontrivial finite Galois extension of the rational field is unramified outside the prime divisors of a positive integer M, its Galois group J is solvable, and e is a positive multiple of the exponent of J. Then some prime p coprime to m = 4eM with p ≤ B(m) has nonidentity Frobenius. Indeed, J has a nontrivial abelianization. A nontrivial character of that quotient yields a primitive Dirichlet character by Kronecker–Weber. Its conductor is supported on primes dividing M. Its local exponent at odd p is at most 1 + v_p(e), since the units congruent to 1 modulo p have cyclic p-primary quotients. At 2 the conductor exponent is at most 2 + v_2(e), using the cyclic quotients of the units congruent to 1 modulo 4. Thus the conductor divides 4eM. Inflate to modulus m and apply Bach. The selected Frobenius has nontrivial character value and so is not the identity. In particular a solvable extension of possibly nonabelian Galois group needs only the Dirichlet endpoint for this argument.

## Companion pencil theorem

Let f and g be monic squarefree integer polynomials of degree n ≥ 3, with f split over the rationals. Let P_f and P_g be the 2 × n × n tensors whose slices are (I, C_f) and (I, C_g) for the companion matrices C_f and C_g. There is a deterministic bit-polynomial procedure computing a positive integer m of polynomial bit length such that P_f and P_g are GL × GL × GL-isomorphic over the rationals if and only if their reductions are isomorphic over F_p for **every** prime p coprime to m with p ≤ B(m). The list is nonempty, can be computed without factoring m, and is nonadaptive. No assumption of geometric equivalence is needed.

### Pencil equivalence becomes projective equivalence

Over any field where their reductions are squarefree of degree n, tensor equivalence implies projective equivalence of root configurations by the determinant of the homogeneous matrix pencil. Conversely, a projectivity t → (αt + β)/(γt + δ) carrying all f-roots to all g-roots gives the allowed slice change to (γ C_f + δ I, α C_f + β I). Its first matrix is invertible because all target roots are finite. Left multiplication by its inverse gives (I, t(C_f)). The second matrix has distinct eigenvalues over an algebraic closure, hence characteristic and minimal polynomials both equal to g. It is rationally similar over the base field to the companion matrix C_g by the cyclic-matrix criterion. Left-right conjugation then proves tensor equivalence. This identifies the two existence questions over each base field.

### A polynomial-bit integer excluding improvements under bad specialization

The roots a_1, …, a_n of f are distinct integers. They may be supplied or recovered by polynomial-time rational factorization. For each i let U_i = (a_i − a_1)(a_2 − a_3) and V_i = (a_i − a_3)(a_2 − a_1). The homogeneous pairs (U_i,V_i) normalize a_1,a_2,a_3 to 0,1,∞. Put

`N_i = U_i (y − x) z − V_i (y − z) x,`
`D_i = U_i (y − x) − V_i (y − z),`
`D = (x − y)(y − z)(z − x).`

For each ordered distinct triple (x,y,z) of roots of g, the pairs (N_i,D_i) are precisely the images of a_i under the unique projectivity sending the first three roots to that triple. Let G be the homogenization of g and G_i = G(N_i,D_i). Use the finite-dimensional algebra A = Q[x,y,z]/(g(x),g(y),g(z)), with monomial basis x^i y^j z^k for 0 ≤ i,j,k < n. Over the algebraic closure it is the product of one copy of the field for each ordered triple of roots, including triples with repetition.

In this product algebra, the ideal generated by the G_i has an unrestricted coordinate exactly when at least one G_i is nonzero there. Thus D belongs to the ideal if and only if every triple at which all G_i are zero has a repeated entry. Membership descends from the algebraic closure by exact linear consistency over Q. Since D is nonzero precisely for ordered distinct triples, this membership is equivalent to geometric non-equivalence of the two configurations. Indeed, at a distinct triple the projectivity is invertible, every pair (N_i,D_i) is nonzero, and G_i = 0 for all i precisely says that all distinct images are g-roots. Monicity of g excludes an image at infinity.

Compute membership by solving for D as a Q-linear combination of the n generators times the n³ basis monomials. If the system is solvable choose a solution supported on linearly independent columns, use an invertible square minor on those columns, and let K ≥ 1 be the absolute determinant of that minor. Thus KD is an integer linear combination of the products in the integral monic quotient. If the system is inconsistent set K = 1. All matrix entries are computed by integer polynomial arithmetic and monic division on powers of x,y,z of degree O(n) in each variable. Their bit lengths are polynomial in the input length. The matrix has n³ rows and at most n⁴ columns, so exact linear elimination and the determinant calculation are bit-polynomial. Hadamard's bound gives polynomial bit length for K.

Set M = K |disc(f) disc(g)|, e = 2n and m = 4eM. In the geometric NO case any local projectivity at a prime coprime to m would produce a distinct ordered triple with all G_i zero. Specializing the integral identity KD = Σ b_i G_i, where b_i are the integer combinations of basis monomials supplied by the solution, would force D = 0 at this triple. This contradiction shows that every tested prime rejects. The prime list is nonempty by Bach applied to the nonprincipal character modulo 4 inflated to modulus m. Enumeration only needs a sieve up to B(m) and gcd computations. [Lenstra, Lenstra and Lovász on rational factorization](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/9304)

### Recovering a rational orbit from good finite primes

For a geometric YES choose a projectivity T carrying S_f to S_g over the algebraic closure. Every projective automorphism of S_f is rational by its values on three distinct rational points. Consequently the map σ ↦ T⁻¹σ(T) is a homomorphism from the absolute Galois group into the rational automorphism group of S_f. Its kernel fixes exactly the splitting field of g, since the projectivity is determined by three image roots. The rational automorphism group of S_f acts faithfully on the finite set of n real projective points and preserves or reverses their circular order. It therefore embeds in the dihedral group of order 2n. The Galois group of g is solvable and has exponent dividing 2n.

If g does not split over Q, the splitting extension is nontrivial and unramified outside disc(g). Solvable splitting detection provides a tested prime with nonidentity Frobenius in this extension. At that prime g is nonsplit by the Frobenius permutation of its distinct roots. A projectivity over F_p cannot carry the fully split S_f to this nonsplit S_g, so that prime rejects.

If g splits over Q, its roots are integers. T is rational by its values on three distinct rational points. For every prime coprime to m, normalizing the source and target triples uses point differences that are units at the prime. The resulting projectivity therefore reduces to an invertible projectivity over F_p and still carries all source points to the corresponding target points. The pencil correspondence gives tensor equivalence over the rationals and over every tested field. These cases prove both directions.

As a nonabelian example, f = x³ − x and g = x³ − 4x + 1 have geometrically equivalent triples. The latter polynomial is irreducible with discriminant 229, hence splitting group S_3. At p = 7 its discriminant is a nonsquare and its reduction is nonsplit, while f splits. This is a concrete rational NO detected by a good prime in the list.



For a geometric NO example, take root sets (0,1,2,3) and (0,1,2,4). Their rational cross-ratio orbits are disjoint, and the geometric obstruction disappears modulo 5 but remains modulo 7. In the triple algebra for the second root set, the interpolating polynomial equal to D/G_4 on distinct triples and zero on repeated triples has coefficient denominators with least common multiple K = 1,313,832,960. Thus multiplying by K certifies KD = b_4G_4 integrally, and the exceptional prime 5 divides K. This exact computation checks the exclusion mechanism in the example.

## General tensor transporter consequence

Let R = Z[1/M] and G be a product of general or special linear groups acting on a tensor format over R. Suppose X = Isom_G(A,B) is an fppf torsor under a smooth stabilizer H and H has an fppf-exact sequence with smooth kernel C having geometrically connected fibers and quotient the constant group E, and H¹(Q,C) = 1. Assume E is finite solvable of exponent dividing a supplied e. For m = 4eM, a rational transporter exists exactly when all the F_p transporters for p coprime to m and p ≤ B(m) exist.

Indeed the pushforward E-torsor is finite étale over R. Its Galois class over Q is a homomorphism up to conjugacy because E is constant. A trivial image class gives a rational point in the pushforward, above which lies a C-torsor. This fiber has a rational point by H¹(Q,C) = 1. Over every good finite prime, the same implication follows from Lang's theorem for connected smooth groups. A nontrivial homomorphism to E defines its image splitting extension, unramified outside M. Its image is solvable with exponent dividing e, and a detecting Frobenius from solvable splitting detection makes the finite pushforward torsor nontrivial. The converse follows because a trivial generic Galois class is trivial on Frobenius at all good primes. Split-torus kernels satisfy the rational hypothesis by Hilbert 90. This statement applies to ordinary or special-linear tensor isomorphism on the promised transporter locus with M and e supplied. It does not require an algorithm for recovering the stabilizer or establishing the good-reduction hypotheses.

## Scope and prior work

Grochow and Qiao study tensor equivalence as an orbit problem, including comparisons across fields and rings. Their integral cube principality result is a different setting. This application gives bounded finite-prime criteria, including one with explicit bit-polynomial preprocessing for a split-source pencil class. It gives no general algorithm for rational or integral TI and no TI versus SL-TI class equality. The pencil class already admits direct rational decisions by factoring g and checking projectivities of ordered triples. The additional conclusion is finite-field detection at explicitly bounded small primes, including a nonabelian solvable splitting example under Dirichlet ERH alone. The targeted prior-work comparison did not identify these exact tensor formulations. Exhaustive novelty checking and Lean certification remain open. [Grochow and Qiao on tensor isomorphism](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309), [Grochow, Qiao and Stange on commutative rings](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1)

## Special-linear companion pencils

On the same split-source class there is also a deterministic nonadaptive bit-polynomial reduction from rational SL × SL × SL pencil isomorphism to the conjunction of good finite-prime SL × SL × SL tests. The modulus can differ from the ordinary reduction. A uniform explicit bound is max(10⁹, 4096 b(m)²), where b(m) is the bit length of the modulus constructed below. The special-linear conclusion uses the same upstream Dirichlet endpoint, via a bounded-index prime-in-coset result as well as the preceding least-prime argument.

If g is nonsplit over Q, decide that fact by rational factorization. The ordinary theorem already proves rational non-equivalence and supplies a tested finite-prime GL obstruction, which is also an SL obstruction. Take its modulus multiplied by 20,000. The proof of the ordinary theorem supplies a witness coprime to the enlarged modulus too, by applying Bach to the same character inflated to that modulus. The larger prime bound includes the Bach bound. It remains to handle f and g both split.

Write F(x,y) = det(xI − yC_f) and G(x,y) = det(xI − yC_g). Over Q and over any tested finite field, both forms have distinct split projective roots. In the pencil convention P A_f(R(x,y)) Q⁻¹ = A_g(x,y), with P and Q of size n and R of size 2, determinant-one equivalence is equivalent to the existence of R in SL₂ with F∘R = G. Necessity follows by taking determinants. For sufficiency the first slice U = A_f(R(1,0)) has determinant G(1,0) = 1. After left-multiplying by U⁻¹ the pencil has first slice I and second slice given by a matrix with characteristic polynomial g. It is conjugate to the companion pencil. A strict conjugator S can be chosen of determinant one by left-multiplying by a centralizer of C_g of determinant det(S)⁻¹. The centralizer has all determinants because the distinct split eigenspaces are lines, and it can scale one line by any nonzero field element. Then P = SU⁻¹ and Q = S both have determinant one. This establishes recovery over the rational field and each good reduction field.

Enumerate integral matrices T for projectivities carrying the first three distinct roots of G to every ordered distinct triple of roots of F, including candidates that ultimately fail. Use the same homogeneous normalization as in the ordinary proof, with the roles of f and g exchanged. All matrices have nonzero integer determinant d, and their entries have polynomial bit length. Multiplying the initial excluded-prime product |disc(f) disc(g)| by every nonzero |d| ensures these exact matrices survive reduction. For a failed candidate include the absolute value of any nonzero F(T(b_i,1)), where b_i is a root of g whose image fails to be an F-root. This certifies failure even if that image is infinity. Thus every projectivity between the two reduced zero sets is the reduction of a successful rational candidate. Indeed, any such reduced projectivity has one of the enumerated image triples, and an ordered triple determines the projectivity over the good field.

For each successful T compute the nonzero integer u = F(T(1,0)). Equality of the two squarefree split divisors gives F∘T = uG. Include |u| in the excluded-prime product. A representative λT satisfies the SL₂ and form requirements exactly when λ²d = 1 and λⁿu = 1. For odd n = 2k + 1 the system is equivalent to u² = dⁿ, since this identity gives the required λ = dᵏ/u. For even n = 2k the system is equivalent to u = dᵏ and d being a square. These criteria hold over the rationals and good finite fields. Include every nonzero normalization failure |u² − dⁿ| or |u − dᵏ| in the product, according to parity. Then numerical compatibility and projectivity validity specialize exactly, apart from the square condition in the even case. Let M be the whole product and m = 20,000 · 4M. Each factor can be computed by exact integer arithmetic. There are O(n³) candidates and factors, all of polynomial bit length, so m has polynomial bit length. No factoring of m or of these factors is required.

If there are no compatible rational candidates every tested prime rejects, and the set of primes tested is nonempty by Bach and the character modulo 4. In odd degree, any compatible rational candidate already gives a rational SL solution and supplies one over every good finite field. In even degree the same conclusion holds if a compatible rational candidate has d a rational square. At a good prime d is a unit, so a rational square root reduces to a nonzero square root over F_p.

For the remaining even-degree case, let S be the group of projective symmetries of the zero divisor of F, acting by composition on the left of projective transporters. For a representative A of an element of S write F∘A = u_A F. The ratio u_A/(det A)ᵏ is independent of representative and is a multiplicative character of S. The compatible projective transporters, when nonempty, form one left orbit of the kernel S₀ of this character. Indeed, fixing any compatible T₀, all projective transporters have the form A T₀ for A in S. Their normalization ratios are precisely those of A. The determinant squareclasses of the compatible transporters therefore form a coset cH in Q×/Q×², with H the determinant-squareclass image of S₀ and c = [det T₀].

All rational projective symmetries of the split real point set preserve or reverse its circular order in RP¹. They act faithfully on the points, since n ≥ 3. Thus S, and every subgroup including S₀, is cyclic or dihedral. In particular H is generated by at most two elements. When c is outside H there exists a sign character on V = ⟨c,H⟩ taking value −1 on c and +1 on H. The group V is generated by the determinant squareclasses of the compatible candidates and has rank at most three. Each basis squareclass can therefore be represented by the integer determinant of a candidate. Its quadratic Dirichlet character, given by the Kronecker symbol, has conductor dividing four times the absolute determinant and can be inflated to modulus m. Independent squareclasses give independent characters, since every nontrivial product squareclass defines a nonprincipal quadratic character. Hence the map from the residue units modulo m to the dual of V is onto. The desired sign assignment corresponds to a coset of index |V| ≤ 8.

Theorem 1.4 of Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan finds a prime in that coset under Dirichlet GRH. The theorem applies because m ≥ 20,000. With its index h ≤ 8, its count h₂ of order-two characters bounded by 8, and ω(m) ≤ b(m), the bound is at most max(10⁹,(64 b(m))²). Indeed (log log m)² ≤ log m, and their general term C(m) is bounded by 3 b(m) + 10 b(m) + (15/2)b(m) + 13. The (h−1)log m and 2h₂ terms add at most 7 b(m) + 16. The selected prime is coprime to m and every determinant squareclass in cH is a nonsquare there. All compatible candidates therefore fail to give an SL transporter at this tested prime. Sieve the asserted bound and use gcd to generate the query list in bit-polynomial time. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Theorem 1.4](https://www.math.ksu.edu/about/personal/xiannan/papers/QuaResL1ver2Sound.pdf)

For a check with several competing transporters, take f-roots (0,1,2,4) and g-roots (0,−1,−2,−4). The four compatible projectivities have determinant squareclasses −1, 6, −2 and 3. None is a rational square. At the good primes 11 and 13 different candidates have square determinant and the SL test succeeds. At the good prime 31 all four determinants are nonsquares and the SL test rejects. These exact enumerations check that a single failed candidate at a prime is insufficient for rejection.

This proves an SL-to-SL transfer between the rational field and a family of prime fields on the stated class. The earlier theorem is the distinct GL-to-GL transfer. In this special class the rational test is already in P by projectivity enumeration and exact square extraction. The additional result is that small finite-prime tests suffice, including the case where different geometric projectivities can satisfy local orientation at different primes. The positive criterion does not require an unresolved determinant gadget or solve the general TI versus SL-TI question. The argument is an informal deduction from the stated Dirichlet premise.
