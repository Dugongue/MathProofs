# Small-prime certificates for special-linear isomorphism of split matrix-algebra tensors

## Research result and proof scope

Let n = 4r with r ≥ 1, and let μ:(M₂(Q))ʳ×(M₂(Q))ʳ→(M₂(Q))ʳ be coordinatewise multiplication. A GL isotope is a bilinear map τ:Qⁿ×Qⁿ→Qⁿ expressible as Rμ(P⁻¹x,Q⁻¹y) for invertible linear maps P,Q,R. The input is any finite nonempty family (τᵢ) in that single GL orbit, specified by rational structure constants, under the promise of membership. The GL witnesses are absent from the algorithm's input.

Assume Dirichlet GRH, in the exact sense of the released Lean statement DirichletLZerosOnLine. There is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time algorithm that returns a nonempty list S of odd primes at which every τᵢ is defined and such that, for every i,j,

    τᵢ and τⱼ are SLₙ(Q)³-equivalent
    ⇔ their reductions are SLₙ(Fₚ)³-equivalent for every p∈S.

The algorithm computes an integer M divisible by 8 with polynomial bit length from the input alone. It returns at most max(1,s) primes p<3(log M)², where s is the dimension of a span of 2k computed rational squareclasses and k is the number of tensors. The returned primes preserve the whole squareclass span, for which s tests are optimal when s > 0. The original comparisons of a particular batch can require fewer primes. Each completed batch has an independently checkable rank certificate, whose verification does not require GRH. GRH supplies the uniform quadratic-logarithmic upper bound and ensures a batch succeeds within this bounded search. The theorem concerns one explicitly specified GL orbit in each dimension 4r.

## 1. Volume of a split matrix algebra

For a copy of M₂(F), over any field F of characteristic other than 2, write tr for ordinary matrix trace and define the alternating three-form t(X,Y,Z)=tr(X[Y,Z]). Cyclicity of trace makes the form alternating. Define Ω₂=-(tr∧t)/2, where the wedge of a one-form and a three-form is the sum over the four choices for the one-form slot, with alternating signs. On (I,H,E₁₂,E₂₁), with H=diag(1,-1), the wedge has value 4, because [E₁₂,E₂₁]=H and tr(H²)=2. This basis has determinant -2 relative to (E₁₁,E₁₂,E₂₁,E₂₂), so Ω₂ has value 1 on the standard basis. Abstractly tr(X) equals one half of the trace of left multiplication by X. Thus Ω₂ is intrinsic to the algebra. On A=(M₂(F))ʳ take the exterior product of the forms on the simple factors. Swapping two factors of dimension four preserves the product, so the full volume Ω is intrinsic, including under permutations of isomorphic factors. In particular, every F-algebra automorphism of A has determinant one.

## 2. Exact SL³ labels inside the GL orbit

An autotopism (P,Q,R) of μ satisfies R(xy)=P(x)Q(y). Put a=P(1), b=Q(1). From R(y)=aQ(y) and R(x)=P(x)b, both a and b are units. Substituting these equations into the autotopism relation shows that φ(x)=a⁻¹R(x)b⁻¹ is a unital algebra automorphism. Conversely, units a,b and any automorphism φ yield P(x)=aφ(x), Q(y)=φ(y)b, R(z)=aφ(z)b. Left or right multiplication by c∈A× has determinant (∏ⱼdet cⱼ)², using two columns for left multiplication and two rows for right multiplication in each block. Consequently the determinant image of the full autotopism group is precisely

    {(u²,v²,u²v²): u,v∈F×}.

Every u and v is realized using diagonal matrices in the first block. Two GL witnesses to the same tensor differ by an autotopism, and a GL transporter between two tensors can be made special linear in every slot exactly when its determinant triple belongs to the displayed subgroup.

For a tensor τ in this GL orbit, choose α in its first input and β in its second input so that L=τ(α,−) and J=τ(−,β) are bijective. Put λ=det L, ρ=det J, and normalize a product on the output space by x⋆y=τ(J⁻¹x,L⁻¹y). Write α=Pa and β=Qb for the inverse images under any GL witness, and put dₚ=det P, d_q=det Q, dᵣ=det R. The invertibility of L,J implies that a,b are units. The map f:A→(Fⁿ,⋆) defined by f(z)=R(zab) is an algebra isomorphism, so the normalized algebra has intrinsic volume Δ=Ω⋆ evaluated on its coordinate basis. Set u=∏det aⱼ and v=∏det bⱼ. Then

    λ=(dᵣ/d_q)u²,   ρ=(dᵣ/dₚ)v²,   Δ=1/(dᵣ(uv)²).
    η:=λρΔ=dᵣ/(dₚd_q).

The label (η,[λ],[ρ]), where brackets denote squareclasses, is independent of all choices and determines the orbit under coordinatewise SL. Indeed, equality of η fixes the relation dᵣ=dₚd_q for the determinant triple of a transporter, and equality of the two squareclasses makes its first two coordinates squares. This is precisely the autotopism determinant test. The converse follows from the same formulas.

## 3. Computing labels and a set of good primes from the tensor

The determinant polynomial of either slice matrix is a nonzero constant times a product of squares of r homogeneous quadratics in n coordinates. Set B=2n²+1 and eᵢ=Bi+i² for i=0,…,n−1, and substitute xᵢ=t^eᵢ. The exponents eᵢ+eⱼ are distinct for unordered pairs, since their quotient and remainder modulo B recover i+j and i²+j². The latter two numbers recover the unordered pair. Each nonzero quadratic remains nonzero and the substituted slice determinant has degree at most n·max eᵢ. Testing all t from 0 to this degree therefore finds a bijective slice, independently for the two slots. The test vectors have polynomial bit size.

To compute Δ over Q, construct ⋆ by rational linear algebra. Compute its center by commutator equations and choose a generator of the r-dimensional split center. If z₀,…,z_{r−1} is a basis of the center, there are at most (r−1)·binomial(r,2) bad t for z(t)=Σⱼtʲ zⱼ, since any two different coordinate characters give a nonzero difference polynomial of degree ≤r−1. Detect a good t by testing whether powers through r−1 span the center. Its minimal polynomial is split and squarefree. Deterministic rational polynomial factorization gives the r roots, and the Lagrange interpolation idempotents give bases of each four-dimensional simple factor. Compute each Ω₂ by the four-term formula above. Divide the product of these values by the determinant of the concatenated block bases to obtain Δ in the given output basis.

For uniform good reduction, include in M all denominators of the explicitly computed rational tensors, bases, inverse slice maps, normalized multiplication tables, identities, and labels. Also include the nonzero determinant numerators of both slices and the concatenated block basis. For each block include two further nonzero numerators. The first is the determinant of the regular trace form (x,y)↦Tr(L_{x⋆y}); on M₂ it is 2tr(xy), which is nondegenerate. The second is a nonzero rank-three minor of the linear commutator map x↦([x,c_i])ᵢ in a chosen block basis {c_i}. These certificates exist over Q, and Gaussian elimination finds them. Include 8, all label numerators, and the numerator of every nonzero difference ηᵢ−ηⱼ. All factors are taken positive before forming the product.

For p∤M identities and independent block bases reduce exactly. The resulting normalized algebra is a product of unital four-dimensional blocks. In each block the regular trace form is nondegenerate. An element x in the Jacobson radical has xy nilpotent for every y, so L_{xy} is nilpotent and its trace is zero. Nondegeneracy forces the radical to be zero. The rank-three minor makes the center one-dimensional, since the unit is central and the dimension is four. Artin–Wedderburn and Wedderburn's little theorem now identify the block with M₂(Fₚ). The reduced τ is an isotope of this product because τ(x,y)=Jx⋆Ly. Thus the exact same label computation and equivalence test apply over Fₚ and the computed labels are literal reductions of those over Q. Differences between unequal η labels survive at every allowed prime by construction.

Every described rational calculation has polynomial bit complexity. The slice curve has polynomially bounded exponents, central powers have polynomially bounded length, and solving systems or taking determinants uses standard fraction-free linear algebra. Rational polynomial factorization is polynomial time by Lenstra–Lenstra–Lovász. There are polynomially many included factors, including the quadratic number of η differences, so M has polynomial bit length.

## 4. A common rank-optimal panel of primes

Let V≤Q×/(Q×)² be generated by the classes [λᵢ],[ρᵢ]. For an odd p∤M, the Legendre symbols define a linear functional ℓₚ:V→F₂ by 0 for +1 and 1 for −1. Every nonzero v∈V has an associated nonprincipal quadratic Dirichlet character, of conductor dividing M: a squarefree representative has prime support in M, and 8 accounts for the 2-part of its fundamental discriminant. Lift this character to modulus M. Its kernel is a proper subgroup of (Z/MZ)×. The explicit form of Bach's theorem under Dirichlet GRH provides a prime p∤M outside this kernel with p<3(log M)². Thus all the candidate rows in that bound have trivial common kernel on V. They span V*, so greedily retaining independent rows selects s=dim V primes. If s = 0 choose any available good prime in the same bound, which exists by applying the bound to the lifted nonprincipal character modulo 4. The finite list is now nonempty, so unequal exact η labels are always distinguished, and unequal squareclass labels are distinguished by at least one selected row. This proves the equivalence in the root statement.

The actual search can use the integer upper bound 3·ceil(log₂ M)² and then retain rows until the exact rank is achieved. To use the displayed natural-log bound literally in the returned result, scan in increasing order and stop immediately at full rank (or the first good prime at rank zero). The existence proof guarantees that this stopping point is strictly below 3(log M)². The entire scan and Legendre-symbol evaluations are polynomial in the bit length of M. No integer factorization is required.

An independent unconditional certificate for the required rank uses a gcd-free basis. Ignore signs temporarily and list all numerator and denominator factor occurrences, initially one factor per integer greater than one. If distinct factors x,y have gcd d>1, replace every occurrence of each by d and its quotient, omitting ones. The product of all occurrences is fixed, and each step strictly increases their number, which is bounded by the original total base-two logarithm. At termination distinct factors are pairwise coprime. Drop perfect-square factors and take the remaining exponent parities, including a separate sign bit. This gives an exact squareclass matrix because each remaining positive factor has an odd valuation at a prime disjoint from the other factors. Exact GF(2) rank gives s, against which the observed rank is checked. Any completed certificate therefore verifies by rational and finite-field calculations even while the announced Lean endpoint awaits independent audit.

Each prime supplies one bit on V, so fewer than s cannot be faithful on all of V. Sharpness also occurs for actual batches. For d independent rational squareclasses with representative products t, let P=R=diag(t,1,…,1), Q=I. There are 2ᵈ labels (1,[t],1). At each good prime these labels take at most two values. Thus any common prime family separating the actual batch needs at least d primes. As a small example t=1,2,3,6 and primes 5,7 give four distinct profiles (0,0), (1,0), (1,1), (0,1).

## Corollary for products of quaternion algebras

The small-prime certification theorem also holds on any single GL orbit of a multiplication tensor of a product of quaternion algebras over Q, including nonsplit division factors. The algorithm takes only an input batch of rational tensors under the promise that they lie in such a common GL orbit. The shared reference algebra and GL witnesses are absent from the input. It returns the same rank-based number of small primes, conditionally on the released Dirichlet GRH theorem used in the split case.

For each quaternion factor define the four-form using reduced trace in the split proof's formula. Over a splitting field this is exactly the normalized, nonzero four-form on M₂. The formula over Q uses one half of the trace of left multiplication. The product form descends to Q and is invariant under all automorphisms of the product. Block permutations preserve it because every factor has dimension four. The determinant of left or right multiplication by a quaternion unit is the square of its reduced norm. Both identities can be tested over a splitting field, where they are the matrix identities in the split proof.

The isotopy normal form for unital associative algebras applies to the product verbatim. Hasse–Schilling characterizes reduced norms by positivity at each real place ramified in the quaternion algebra. A positive rational meets every such requirement. Given a desired rational square u², one can take the positive reduced norm |u| in any one factor and take norm one in the others. Consequently the determinant image of the autotopism group is still exactly {(u²,v²,u²v²)}. [Voight, Quaternion Algebras, Main Theorem 14.7.4](https://jvoight.github.io/quat-book.pdf)

The split proof's computation of the good slices still works because each reduced norm is a nonzero quadratic form. The center still splits as Qʳ, so its simple factors and their four-forms are obtained by the same algorithms. Let c≠0 be the intrinsic volume on a fixed reference basis of the shared reference algebra. The same normalization gives η=c·det(R)/(det(P)det(Q)), while the other two labels remain the slice determinant squareclasses. The common factor c cancels whenever two exact η labels are compared. This gives the same complete coordinatewise SL classification inside the promised GL orbit.

The explicit good reduction test still produces split M₂ blocks over every allowed finite field. A splitting-field calculation gives nondegeneracy of the regular trace pairing on each quaternion factor over Q. Away from the selected determinant numerator, its reduction therefore kills any Jacobson radical by the nilpotent trace argument above. The surviving rank-three commutator minor forces center dimension one. The resulting four-dimensional central simple algebra over Fₚ is M₂(Fₚ) by finite Wedderburn. The proof of the finite-prime panel therefore applies unchanged to these computed labels. The resulting theorem uses the same top-level Lean theorem LFunction_eq_zero_re from the release's Main module. [Alpöge’s release](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)

This is an informal corollary. It compares SL orbits only under the common GL orbit promise. Its proof uses the reduced norm theorem and the earlier informal certificate theorem. A local kernel or axiom audit of the announced GRH release has yet to be completed.

## Verification

The proof assembles the exact SL³ classifier, explicit good reduction, and the Dirichlet-GRH prime panel in that order. The sign of the canonical volume is fixed by the written matrix-unit basis, and the batch rank check is independent of the existence theorem. The analytic prime bound and local Wedderburn decomposition are invoked from their stated hypotheses. Resource limit for this route is an informal proof and arithmetic checks of the formulas. A complete end-to-end Lean formalization would need a compatible replay of the release and formal bridges for the explicit prime bound and finite-dimensional algebra.

Exact-arithmetic controls passed for 32 Sidon curves with dimension between 4 and 128, 32 randomly chosen invertible basis triples across two dimensions, 128 gcd-free rank comparisons against trial factorization, and the two-prime four-class example. These controls check the specified formulas and require no analytic assumption.
