# Small modular orbit certificates for cyclic étale multiplication tensors

Let $A$ and $B$ be nonzero finite étale algebras over $\mathbb Q$, given by rational multiplication tables. A finite étale algebra here is a product of number fields. Write $T_A$ and $T_B$ for their multiplication tensors, with three independently invertible changes of bases acting on the two input spaces and the output space. Let $\mathcal O_A$ and $\mathcal O_B$ be full-rank orders and let $D=|\operatorname{disc}(\mathcal O_A)\operatorname{disc}(\mathcal O_B)|$. Let $n=\dim_{\mathbb Q}A+\dim_{\mathbb Q}B$.

**Theorem.** Assume Dirichlet GRH in the form specified below. Suppose the field factors of $A$ and $B$ split in a common finite cyclic extension of $\mathbb Q$. There is an absolute effective constant $C$ such that $T_A$ and $T_B$ are in the same rational tensor orbit if and only if
$$
r_p(\mathcal O_A)=r_p(\mathcal O_B)
\qquad\text{for all primes }p\nmid D,\quad p\le Cn^2\log^2(2D),
$$
where $r_p(\mathcal O)$ is the number of degree-one factors of $\mathcal O/p\mathcal O$. Each $r_p$ is obtainable by one matrix rank from the multiplication table over $\mathbb F_p$. A prime at which the ranks distinguish the algebras certifies different tensor orbits over both $\mathbb F_p$ and $\mathbb Q$. The algorithm requires complete multiplication tables and the cyclic splitting promise. It uses neither a splitting field nor its degree as input.

On any common **abelian** splitting extension $K$, let $\rho_A$ and $\rho_B$ be the permutation characters of $G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)$ on the geometric points of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ and $\operatorname{Spec} B$. The same test decides whether $\rho_A=\rho_B$. If $f=\rho_A-\rho_B=\sum_\chi c_\chi\chi\ne0$, set $R=\sum_\chi|c_\chi|/\max_\chi |c_\chi|$. One can replace $n$ in the prime bound by $R$, where $1\le R\le n$.

## From multiplication tensors to ranks

For finite-dimensional unital associative algebras over any field, invertible maps $P,Q,U$ satisfying $U(xy)=P(x)Q(y)$ give an algebra isomorphism. Put $a=P(1)$ and $b=Q(1)$. Then $U(x)=P(x)b=aQ(x)$, so right multiplication by $b$ and left multiplication by $a$ are bijective. A one-sided inverse in a finite-dimensional unital algebra is two-sided. Thus $a,b$ are units and
$$
U(xy)=U(x)b^{-1}a^{-1}U(y)=U(x)U(1)^{-1}U(y).
$$
The map $x\mapsto U(1)^{-1}U(x)$ is a unital algebra isomorphism. A unital algebra isomorphism supplies the same map in all three positions. Unequal dimensions can be rejected immediately.

For $p\nmid\operatorname{disc}\mathcal O$, the reduction $S=\mathcal O/p\mathcal O$ is finite étale over $\mathbb F_p$. Frobenius $F(s)=s^p$ is $\mathbb F_p$-linear. In a field factor $\mathbb F_{p^d}$, the ideal generated by $F(s)-s$ is zero if $d=1$ and is the whole field if $d>1$. Indeed, $X^p-X$ has exactly $p$ roots. Consequently
$$
r_p(\mathcal O)
 =\dim_{\mathbb F_p} S/((F-1)S)
 =\dim_{\mathbb F_p}S-
  \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_p}\bigl[b_j(b_i^p-b_i)\bigr]_{i,j}.
$$
The double-parenthesis notation denotes the ideal generated by the image of $F-1$. The columns use all pairs of basis vectors. Repeated squaring computes the powers in time polynomial in the table size and $\log p$. This formula uses the multiplication table directly.

For a rational input basis $(e_i)$, choose a positive common denominator $m$ of its multiplication constants. The lattice generated by $1$ and all $me_i$ is an order because $(me_i)(me_j)=\sum_k (m c_{ij}^k)(me_k)$. The unit is obtained by solving linear equations. Clearing denominators and using Hermite normal form gives a basis with polynomial bit length. The discriminant is the determinant of the trace Gram matrix, whose entry for $u,v$ is the trace of the multiplication operator by $uv$. In particular $\log(2D)$ has polynomial size in the original input encoding. Enumerating primes to the bound and taking the indicated ranks is deterministic polynomial time given the analytic hypothesis.

## Size of the first distinguishing prime

Replace the common abelian splitting field $K$ by the compositum of the field factors. The two permutation representations factor through its Galois group. Inflating from that quotient preserves character equality and the nonzero Fourier coefficients, so it preserves $R$. Kronecker–Weber and the conductor–discriminant formula put $K$ inside $\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)$ with $q\mid D$. Indeed, the conductor of each abelian field factor is the least common multiple of its character conductors, which divides their product, its absolute discriminant. The conductor of the compositum divides their least common multiple. The order discriminant differs from the maximal order discriminant by an index square. Inflate all characters of $G$ to Dirichlet characters modulo $q$. The decomposition of a permutation representation of an abelian group into one-dimensional characters has integral, nonnegative multiplicities, with sum its dimension. Hence each $c_\chi$ is an integer and $\sum|c_\chi|\le n$.

For a fixed nonzero nonnegative smooth weight $w$ supported in $(1/2,1)$, put $I=\int w(t)\,dt>0$. The smoothed Dirichlet explicit formula under GRH gives, uniformly in all characters modulo $q$ and $x\ge4$,
$$
\sum_{p\nmid D}\chi(p)\log p\,w(p/x)
 = \mathbf 1_{\chi=1}\, I x+O_w(\sqrt x\log(2D)).
\tag{1}
$$
For clarity, first sum over $\Lambda(m)\chi(m)$ with the primitive inducing character. In the explicit formula each nontrivial zero contributes $-x^\rho\widehat w(\rho)$, where $\widehat w(s)=\int_0^\infty w(t)t^{s-1}\,dt$. On the critical line $|\widehat w(1/2+it)|\ll_w(1+|t|)^{-2}$. The standard unit-interval zero count $O(\log(2q(2+|t|)))$ bounds the total by $O_w(\sqrt x\log(2q))$. Trivial zeros contribute $O_w(1)$ because the support is above $1/2$ and $x\ge4$. The pole gives $Ix$ for the principal primitive character. Removing prime powers of exponent at least 2 costs $O_w(\sqrt x)$ by Chebyshev’s bound. Removing the primes dividing $D$ costs $O_w(\log D)$. The inducing and inflated characters agree at every remaining prime, giving (1). The constants are effective. [Bach and Sorenson, explicit formulas for weighted prime sums](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1996-65-216/S0025-5718-96-00763-6/S0025-5718-96-00763-6.pdf)

Choose $\psi$ maximizing $|c_\psi|$. Apply (1) to $f\overline\psi=\sum_\chi c_\chi\chi\overline\psi$. The sum of the weighted values of $f(\operatorname{Frob}_p)\overline\psi(\operatorname{Frob}_p)$ is
$$
c_\psi I x+O_w\left(\left(\sum|c_\chi|\right)\sqrt x\log(2D)\right).
$$
For $x=C R^2\log^2(2D)$ with a sufficiently large absolute effective $C$, its absolute value is at least $I|c_\psi|x/2$. Hence some prime outside $D$ below $x$ has $f(\operatorname{Frob}_p)\ne0$. The same argument gives $\gg_w |c_\psi|x/(\max_{g\in G}|f(g)|\log x)$ witnessing primes in $(x/2,x)$ for every $x$ above the cutoff. Frobenius fixed points on the geometric points of an étale algebra are exactly its degree-one factors after reduction, since $p\nmid D$. This proves the abelian character criterion.

## Cyclic reconstruction and its scope

For cyclic $G$, a transitive $G$-set is $G/H$ for one of its subgroups $H$. The contribution of a copy of $G/H$ at $g$ is $[G:H]\mathbf 1_{g\in H}$. Each subgroup $H$ has a generator $g_H$. Evaluating $f$ at $g_H$ sums the weighted multiplicity differences over all overgroups of $H$. If some multiplicity differs, choose a maximal subgroup with nonzero difference. Its evaluation has exactly one nonzero term, so $f\ne0$. Thus $f=0$ forces equal multiplicities of all transitive $G$-sets and hence isomorphic étale algebras. The reductions of globally isomorphic étale algebras have the same $r_p$ at every good prime, even for different choices of orders, because a prime outside an order discriminant is outside the index of that order in its maximal order. This proves both directions of the theorem.

The abelian statement cannot detect all products of abelian fields. In a biquadratic extension $K$ with quadratic subfields $K_1,K_2,K_3$, the two dimension-six algebras $K\times\mathbb Q^2$ and $K_1\times K_2\times K_3$ have the same character. Its values are $6$ at the identity and $2$ at each other element. Their full cycle types, and therefore their good-prime finite-field tensor orbits, agree because fixed-point counts on all powers determine cycle counts. The rational algebras have different field-factor decompositions.
