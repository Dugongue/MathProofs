# Four results on matrix multiplication tensors

These informal arguments concern polynomial equations, isomorphism and finite restriction. The letter $n$ in the extraction statements denotes the matrix multiplication order; each tensor mode has dimension $m=n^2$. All orbit closures in the extraction and pencil claims are over $\mathbb C$.

## How large a determinant can one equation recover?

For **every** nonzero degree-$D$ polynomial vanishing on the matrix multiplication orbit closure, a nonuniform division-free circuit of constant depth and size polynomial in $n,D$ using evaluations of that polynomial computes the generic determinant of order **$n+1$**. In the border model the size is polynomial in $n$ independently of $D$. More generally any nonzero element of the $r$-minor ideal of a generic matrix yields the full order-$r$ determinant, with these respective resource bounds. Published depth-three results give dimension $\Theta(r^{1/3})$. We use extra constant depth to cancel a product of smaller minors by matched evaluations and to take a normalized root through truncated polynomial interpolation. The exact size still depends on degree. [Proof](Equations.md) · [Dey and Guo](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.49) · [Andrews and Forbes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00792)

**Joint consequence of the extraction and pencil proofs.** Suppose $f$ is an orbit equation and is nonzero at some tensor $T_0$ supported on a two-dimensional subspace of its first mode. Then the exact circuit recovers the determinant of order **$n^2-n+1$** with size polynomial in $n,D$, and the border circuit recovers that determinant at size polynomial in $n$. Also $D\ge n^2-n+1$.

Indeed put $g(Y)=f((I_m\otimes Y\otimes I_m)T_0)$ for a generic $m$-square matrix $Y$. It is nonzero at $I_m$, with degree at most $D$. At $\operatorname{rank}Y\le m-n$ the output still has two slices and the normal rank of the pencil is at most $m-n$. By the pencil criterion below it belongs to the orbit closure, since every required minor vanishes. Thus $g$ belongs to the $(m-n+1)$-minor ideal. That ideal is homogeneous and generated in degree $m-n+1$, giving the degree bound. Full-size extraction now gives both computational claims, with only linear substitutions before each $f$ call. For a different choice of the two-dimensional subspace one may fix bases once. The two-slice hypothesis concerns the equation; e.g., flattening equations that vanish on all two-slice tensors cannot be used in this argument.

As an explicit family satisfying the hypothesis, project onto two fixed slices and take the discriminant of their binary determinant. On the dense family of projected matrix multiplications this determinant is a power of exponent $n$ up to a scalar, since its regular slices have the form of $n$ repeated matrix blocks up to equivalence. Thus for $n\ge2$ the discriminant vanishes there and on its closure. A diagonal pencil with $m$ distinct projective roots gives a nonzero evaluation. Its input degree is $2m(m-1)$. This example demonstrates the stronger transfer with an ordinary polynomial-degree equation.

## Isomorphism can be hard even when every affine product is a matrix algebra

A pair of products on the same vector space can have **every** normalized affine linear combination isomorphic to the split matrix algebra $M_n$, with a common unit, while recognizing equivalence between two such families is TI-complete. This holds on a polynomially constructed promise family even when a change of basis on the two-dimensional parameter space is allowed. The exceptional projective product is associative and every fourfold product in it vanishes. An arbitrary three-tensor sits in the relative position of the products. The independent matrix-mode comparison is a four-tensor problem of format $(2,n^2,n^2,n^2)$. The proof also handles the simultaneous algebra-coordinate action. The claims are algebraic over any field, and give polynomial-time reductions over each fixed finite field. [Proof](Symmetry.md) · [Grochow and Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309) · [Odesskii and Sokolov on compatible multiplications](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0512499)

## When do linear encodings or limits beat routing blocks?

Consider two batches of independent rectangular matrix products with the same total dimension on each input side and with one common outer aspect ratio among the targets. A border restriction exists if and only if a nonnegative integer matrix of block multiplicities satisfies the row, column and inner-index size equations in the proof. Every solution supplies an exact computation by routing coordinate blocks. On input lists of product dimensions, deciding feasibility is strongly NP-complete even when the sources are identical square products. There is also a loss bound when input dimensions are not saturated. For instance, every border restriction from multiplication in the complex group algebra of $S_3^k$ to a single $3\times v$ by $v\times3$ product must leave at least $2^k$ dimensions unused on each input side. [Proof](Algorithms.md) · [Blasiak, Cohn, Grochow, Pratt and Umans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03826)

## An exact membership test for two-slice targets, including singular pencils

For bonds $(a,b,a)$, write $m=ab$ and consider a two-slice target whose two other mode dimensions are at most $m$. It is a border restriction exactly when one binary form $Q$ of degree $a$ has $Q^s$ dividing every minor of size $m-b+s$ for each $1\le s\le b$. Zero minors impose no restriction. This gives coefficient-only set-theoretic equations and a polynomial-arithmetic algorithm using polynomial Smith form and univariate gcds, including when no slice is invertible. For a nonzero pencil with compressed row and column supports of dimensions $u,v$, its minimum equal border bond is one of two consecutive integers starting at $\lceil\sqrt{\max(u,v)}\rceil$. The test chooses which. Bernardi and Gesmundo already determined the generic form and the ambient-filling range; the stated test translates published bundle-closure inequalities into one simultaneous divisor condition for each individual target. [Proof](Pencils.md) · [Bernardi and Gesmundo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15114) · [De Terán, Dopico, Koval and Pagacz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16702)
