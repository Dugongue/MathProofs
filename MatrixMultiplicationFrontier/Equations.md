# Full-size determinant extraction from matrix multiplication orbit equations

Let $M_n(A,B,C)=\operatorname{tr}(ABC)$ over $\mathbb C$, let $m=n^2$, and put $V_n=\overline{\mathrm{GL}_m^3\cdot M_n}$ in the space of $m\times m\times m$ tensors. For every $n\ge 2$ and every nonzero polynomial $f$ of degree $D$ vanishing on $V_n$, the generic **$(n+1)\times(n+1)$ determinant** has a constant-depth division-free $f$-evaluation-oracle circuit of size $\operatorname{poly}(n,D)$. It also has a constant-depth border $f$-oracle circuit of size $\operatorname{poly}(n)$ for arbitrary $D$.

The old extraction via an algebraic branching program gave determinant dimension $\Theta(n^{1/3})$. The improvement comes from recovering a determinant directly from a product of nested principal minors. The exact circuit uses interpolation and a fixed number of additional arithmetic layers. All circuits here are nonuniform, with unbounded-fan-in addition and multiplication, complex scalar constants and charged wires. Border circuits allow rational functions of one parameter as scalar constants and recover the answer as a coefficientwise limit.

## The generic determinantal ideal

Let $I_r$ be the ideal generated by the $r$-minors of a generic $a\times b$ matrix, where $1\le r\le \min(a,b)$. For every nonzero $g\in I_r$ of degree $d$, the generic determinant of order **$r$** has a constant-depth division-free $g$-oracle circuit of size $\operatorname{poly}(a,b,d)$. The corresponding border circuit has size $\operatorname{poly}(a,b)$.

We use two published isolation results. Write $\Delta_j(Z)$ for the determinant of the leading $j\times j$ submatrix. Dey and Guo, Theorem 42, give a depth-three $g$-oracle circuit of size polynomial in $a,b,d$ for a normalized canonical bideterminant
$$
 H(Z)=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\Delta_j(Z)^{e_j},
 \qquad r\le s\le\min(a,b),\qquad e=e_s>0,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{s}j e_j\le d.
$$
Here $e_j$ counts rows of length $j$ in the canonical bitableau. For border computation, Andrews and Forbes, Lemma 3.5, give such an $H$ as the leading coefficient of one linearly transformed $g$ evaluation. After scalar normalization the circuit computes $H+O(\eta)$ coefficientwise with size polynomial in $a,b$ independently of $d$. Each application below can use the exponents belonging to its own isolation result. [Dey–Guo, Theorem 42](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol362-itcs2026/html/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.49/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.49.html) · [Andrews–Forbes, §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.00792#S3.SS1)

Let $X$ be a generic $r\times r$ matrix. Set $A_i=(I_r+tX)_{[i],[i]}$ and $p_i=\det A_i$, with $p_0=1$. For each $1\le i\le r$, form the following two $s\times s$ matrices, extended by zeros to the ambient $a\times b$ format.
$$
 U_i=I_{s-i}\oplus A_i,\qquad
 W_i=I_{s-i}\oplus A_{i-1}\oplus [1].
$$
The leading blocks of every order below $s$ are identical within this pair. The order-$s$ determinants are $p_i$ and $p_{i-1}$. Hence
$$
 H(U_i)p_{i-1}^{e}=H(W_i)p_i^{e}.
$$
All these polynomials have constant term $1$ in $t$. Put $P=\prod_{i=1}^r H(U_i)$ and $Q=\prod_{i=1}^r H(W_i)$. Multiplying the ratios in $\mathbb C[X][[t]]$ telescopes to the exact polynomial identity $P=Qp_r^e$. The circuit forms both products in parallel.

For $\lambda\in\mathbb Q$, define the polynomial $B_\lambda(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\binom{\lambda}{k}(u-1)^k$. If $u(0)=1$, the formal binomial expansion gives $B_\lambda(u)\equiv u^\lambda\bmod t^{r+1}$ with constant term $1$. Therefore
$$
 S(t)=B_{1/e}(P(t)) B_{-1/e}(Q(t))
 \equiv p_r(t)\pmod{t^{r+1}} .
$$
Indeed, the product on the right of the binomial expansions is the unique $e$-th root of $P/Q$ with constant term $1$. Its coefficients are unique recursively because $e$ is invertible. The use of formal division establishes the identity; all operations defining $S$ are polynomial. In particular $[t^r]S=\det X$.

For the exact circuit, $\deg_t P,\deg_t Q\le rd$, so $\deg_t S\le 2r^2d$. Evaluate $S$ at $2r^2d+1$ distinct complex constants and extract its $t^r$ coefficient by one interpolation sum. At each point the matrices supplied to $H$ are affine in $X$. The two products over $i$, the powers up to $r$ inside each $B$, and the outer product use a constant number of layers and polynomially many wires. This proves the exact assertion, including when $e$ grows with $d$.

For the border circuit, choose constants $c_0,\ldots,c_r$ satisfying $\sum_{j=0}^r c_j j^k=0$ for $0\le k<r$ and $\sum_{j=0}^r c_j j^r=1$, using the evaluation matrix on $0,\ldots,r$. Thus $\varepsilon^{-r}\sum_{j=0}^r c_j S(j\varepsilon)=\det X+O(\varepsilon)$, regardless of the degree of $S$. At each $t=j\varepsilon$, replace every $H$ evaluation by its normalized Andrews–Forbes approximation with $\eta=\varepsilon^{r+1}$. Its error after the affine substitutions is $O(\varepsilon^{r+1})$, since the substituted entries have no poles. The polynomial arithmetic defining $S$ preserves that error order, and the final scalar $\varepsilon^{-r}$ leaves error $O(\varepsilon)$. Only $r+1$ sampling points and $2r$ copies of the border isolation at each point are needed. The binomial coefficients and the rational functions from isolation are nonuniform scalars, charged in the usual arithmetic circuit model. This proves the border assertion.

## Transfer to matrix multiplication

Choose a fixed tensor $T_0$ with $f(T_0)\ne 0$. For a generic $m\times m$ matrix $Y$, set $g(Y)=f((Y\otimes I_m\otimes Y)T_0)$. This is nonzero at $Y=I_m$ and has degree at most $2D$. If $\operatorname{rank}Y\le n$, both outer tensor supports have dimension at most $n$. Every such tensor has a trilinear expression $a(x)^{\mathsf T}B(y)c(z)$, with $a$ and $c$ vectors of $n$ linear forms and $B$ an arbitrary $n\times n$ matrix of linear forms. Such an expression is a restriction of $M_n$. For the first coordinate vector $v$, take the three inputs to be $v a(x)^{\mathsf T}$, $B(y)$ and $c(z)v^{\mathsf T}$ and take the trace of their product. The three linear substitutions are endomorphisms on the three $m$-dimensional spaces. The image of every endomorphism triple lies in $V_n$ by density of invertible triples. Thus $g$ vanishes on all matrices of rank at most $n$.

The determinantal ideal theorem gives $g\in I_{n+1}$. Applying the full-size extraction with $r=n+1$ now proves both assertions about $V_n$. Each $g$ evaluation calls $f$ on coordinates that are quadratic forms in $Y$, computable at constant depth with polynomially many wires. The same substitution works over the border parameter field. [Determinantal ideal theorem, Andrews–Forbes, Lemma 2.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.00792#S2.SS3)

## Scope

This is an informal arithmetic reduction for every nonzero equation, with no restriction on how the equation was obtained. The additional step beyond the published canonical isolation theorems is the simultaneous cancellation of smaller minors and extraction of the full-size determinant. The depth-three bounds in the published results apply to their smaller determinant; the circuit above uses additional constant depth. Dependence on $D$ persists in the exact result, and the unrestricted-degree exact size question remains open here. The conclusion concerns equation complexity. It gives no algorithm for matrix multiplication or improvement to its exponent. Publication priority has not been established, and the proof has no Lean certificate.
