# Removing redundant radical directions before the graph reduction

Fix $k=\mathbf F_q$. The short-product graph reduction also applies to raw multiplication tensors of split-basic algebras after removing all radical-annihilating directions disjoint from the radical square. The discarded dimensions remain recorded at their ordered pairs of primitive idempotents. In particular, the enlarged class includes all split-basic square-zero-radical algebras, of arbitrary block width, as well as algebras with large active blocks allowed by the short-product theorem.

Let $A$ be a finite-dimensional associative unital algebra over $k$, with radical $J$ and $A/J\cong k^m$. Compute a complete primitive frame $(e_i)$. Then $S=\bigoplus_i k e_i$ is a subalgebra complement to $J$. Form
$$
K=\{x\in J:xJ=0,\ Jx=0\}.
$$
The conditions are linear equations and $K$ is a two-sided ideal. Its component $K_{ij}$ at the ordered pair $i,j$ has intersection $K_{ij}\cap J^2$. Choose a linear complement $D_{ij}$ of that intersection in $K_{ij}$, and put $D=\bigoplus_{ij}D_{ij}$. Since $JD=DJ=0$ and $S$ acts by the endpoint scalars, $D$ is a two-sided ideal. Write $B=A/D$ and record $d_{ij}=\dim D_{ij}$.

For any other complement $\widetilde D$ constructed in the same manner, each $\widetilde D_{ij}$ is the graph of a linear map $D_{ij}\to K_{ij}\cap J^2$. Choose an $S$-bimodule complement $J_0$ to $D$ in $J$ containing $J^2$, by taking blockwise linear complements. The linear shear which is the identity on $S+J_0$ and adds these graph maps to $D$ is an algebra automorphism. Indeed the shear commutes with the endpoint scalar actions of $S$, is the identity on $J^2$, and changes any radical element by one annihilating $J$ on both sides. Its determinant is one. Thus the pair consisting of $B$ and the entries $d_{ij}$ is well defined up to a frame-respecting isomorphism.

For two split-basic algebras, first use determinant-preserving frame normalization from the short-product reduction. Any normalized isomorphism carries $J,J^2,K$ to their counterparts. A determinant-one shear in the target then makes it carry the chosen $D$ onto the counterpart. It induces a frame-respecting isomorphism of the quotients preserving the recorded dimensions. Conversely $S+J_0$ is a subalgebra, because $J_0^2\subseteq J^2\subseteq J_0$. Its projection to $B$ is an algebra isomorphism. A frame-respecting isomorphism of the quotients preserving the entries $d_{ij}$ therefore extends to an algebra isomorphism of the full algebras, using arbitrary linear isomorphisms between the matched discarded blocks. Those arbitrary maps preserve multiplication because $D$ is killed by $J$ and each $D_{ij}$ has only its two endpoint scalar actions.

If $D=0$, use the determinant character of the quotient isomorphism as before. If $D\ne0$, one discarded block contains a line that can be rescaled independently by any element of $k^\times$. Consequently every ordinary algebra isomorphism can be adjusted to any desired determinant. For raw tensor determinant constraints, the left and right regular unit images must still be calculated using the **full** algebra. The desired three determinants are tested by the formula from the short-product theorem.

For an algorithm, run the short-product recognition and graph encoding on $B$, adding $d_{ij}$ as a color of an ordered endpoint occurrence. The construction and recognition are polynomial time. When $J^2=0$, $B=S$ and the graph remembers exactly the weighted quiver. For $J^2\ne0$, one can start with an active example from the wide short-product theorem and attach arbitrarily many independent $D_{ij}$ directions at any endpoint pair. The proof is informal. Splitting off annihilator components has precedence in the classification of nilpotent algebras. The extension here uses the surviving endpoint actions, the determinant on raw tensors, and the sparse multiplication data on the quotient.
