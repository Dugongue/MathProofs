# Tensor isomorphism inside a smooth rational projective family

**Theorem.** Fix a field $k$. Ordered three-tensor isomorphism over $k$ reduces by a polynomial-size, deterministic construction to abstract $k$-isomorphism of smooth geometrically integral $k$-rational projective varieties. Each output is the blowup of $\mathbf P^N\times\mathbf P^2$ at seven disjoint smooth centers, three determined by tensor spaces and four fixed linear centers. For a fixed tensor format all outputs belong to one smooth projective family over an affine space. They have the same direct sum of Tate Chow motives. Over a finite field their point counts agree over every finite extension. The reduction uses polynomial-size projective equations as well as the compact blowup presentation.

The main step is recovering the entire blowdown from the unmarked variety. The argument works after every field extension, including in characteristic two. It uses the projective reconstruction approach of our trilinear hypersurface result, plus a Segre graph and the smooth blowup formula for the canonical class.

## Encoding a tensor by seven smooth centers

Regard the tensor as a bilinear map $T:V\times W\to U$, or as its linear extension $T:V\otimes W\to U$. We may pad by zeros so that all three dimensions are at least five and $\dim V\ne\dim W$. This preserves and reflects ordinary tensor isomorphism. In the reverse direction an isomorphism of padded tensors identifies in each mode the image of contraction against the other two dual modes. These images are in the original spaces. Restrict to them, then extend the three restrictions to invertible maps of the original modes. The dimensions of corresponding complements agree. One can identify the two input modes with their duals using the input bases to apply the same argument to bilinear maps.

Put $A=V\otimes W$, $D=k^4$, and $Y=\mathbf P(U\oplus A\oplus D)\times\mathbf P^2$. Write $N=\dim U+\dim A+3$, so that the first factor has dimension $N$. Let $e_0,e_1,e_2$ be the coordinate vectors for the base $\mathbf P^2$. Use the four points represented by $e_0,e_1,e_2,e_0+e_1+e_2$ as an ordered projective frame. At these points put, respectively, linear centers of dimensions $0,1,2,3$ in a fixed nested flag in $\mathbf P(D)$.

At the three other base points represented by $e_0+e_1,e_0+e_2,e_1+e_2$, put respectively $\mathbf P(U)$, the standard Segre
$$
S_0=\{[0:v\otimes w:0]\},
$$
and the graph Segre
$$
S_T=\{[T(v,w):v\otimes w:0]\}.
$$
These are scheme-theoretic images of the displayed maps from $\mathbf P(V)\times\mathbf P(W)$. $S_T$ is the linear shear of $S_0$ given by $(u,a,d)\mapsto(u+T(a),a,d)$. All seven marked points are distinct over every field. The centers are smooth, projective and disjoint. Both Segre centers have dimension at least eight and the other data center has dimension at least four, so the four marker dimensions distinguish the four marker centers from all others.

Let $X_T$ be the blowup at the union of the seven centers. Its smoothness follows from the smoothness of the ambient variety and the centers. For example locally at a center choose smooth coordinates in which the center is cut out by coordinate functions, then use the standard affine charts of that blowup. Its rationality follows from the dense open subset isomorphic to the complement of the centers in the rational ambient variety. [Stacks Project, blowups in smooth centers](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FUT)

## Recovering the blowdown intrinsically

Write $H,L$ for the pullbacks of the hyperplane classes of $\mathbf P^N,\mathbf P^2$, and $E_j$ for the seven exceptional divisors. They form an integral basis of $\operatorname{Pic}(X_T)$. Indeed push a divisor forward to the smooth $Y$ and subtract the pullback to leave a linear combination of the exceptional divisors. Independence of the exceptional coefficients follows from a line in each exceptional fiber, where the corresponding $E_j$ has degree $-1$. Independence of the pullback classes then follows by pushforward, since the centers have codimension at least two.

The primitive nef class $L$ is intrinsic. It generates the ray of nef classes $B$ with $B^3$ numerically zero. To prove the claim write $B=aH+bL-\sum_j c_j E_j$, extending coefficients to $\mathbb R$ if desired. On a geometric ordinary fiber $\mathbf P^N$ of the base projection, a linear $\mathbf P^3$ gives intersection $a^3$. Thus $a=0$. A line in any exceptional fiber gives $c_j\geq0$. Choose also a line in the original $j$-th marked fiber $\mathbf P^N$ meeting its center and extending outside it. The strict transform has positive intersection with $E_j$, zero intersection with the other exceptional divisors and zero degree on $L$. Because $a=0$, nefness forces $c_j\leq0$. This yields $B=bL$ with $b\geq0$. Conversely $L$ is nef and $L^3=0$ since the base has dimension two. The morphism of its complete linear system is therefore intrinsic up to a $k$-projectivity. Its space of global sections is the same as on $Y$, because the blowdown pushes forward its structure sheaf to $\mathcal O_Y$.

The marked base fibers and the exceptional divisors are now intrinsic. Away from the marked fibers the morphism has fiber $\mathbf P^N$. Over the $j$-th point its geometric fiber has two irreducible components, the strict transform closure of the complement of the center in $\mathbf P^N$ and the exceptional divisor. The two dimensions are $N$ and $N+1$, respectively. Indeed all points away from the center lie in the first component and all points over the center lie in the projectivized normal bundle $E_j$.

Let $d_j$ denote the dimension of the $j$-th center, and let $c_j^{\mathrm{amb}}=N+2-d_j$ be its codimension in $Y$. The canonical class is
$$
K_{X_T}=-(N+1)H-3L+\sum_j(N+1-d_j)E_j.
$$
The discrepancy is $c_j^{\mathrm{amb}}-1$, as seen from the Jacobian of the smooth blowup chart $x_i=x_1 t_i$ for the center coordinates $2\leq i\leq c_j^{\mathrm{amb}}$. Consider now any abstract isomorphism $X_T\to X_{T'}$ for the same padded format. It matches base classes and exceptional divisors. Comparing canonical classes in the integral Picard basis, every displacement of an exceptional coefficient is a multiple of $N+1$. Each displacement is a difference of center dimensions in the interval $[0,N-1]$, so it is zero. It follows that the first hyperplane classes match and each matched center has exactly the same dimension.

The complete linear system of $H$, together with that of $L$, gives the blowdown to $Y$. This follows from $\pi_*\mathcal O_{X_T}=\mathcal O_Y$, which also follows from extension across the codimension-two center in the normal $Y$. Therefore every abstract isomorphism descends to a product of $k$-projectivities of $\mathbf P^N$ and $\mathbf P^2$. The base projectivity fixes each of the four frame points because its matched center dimension is unchanged. A projectivity fixing the four points of this frame is the identity. Thus all seven centers retain their labels under the descended map.

## Extracting the tensor transporter

The descended projectivity of $\mathbf P^N$ lifts to a linear map $g$ of $U\oplus A\oplus D$. Preservation of $\mathbf P(U)$ and $S_0$ implies preservation of $U$ and of $A$, since the Segre spans $A$. Its restriction to $U\oplus A$ is consequently block diagonal with invertible restrictions $g_U,g_A$. The restriction of $g_A$ to the Segre induces separate projectivities on $\mathbf P(V)$ and $\mathbf P(W)$. Indeed the Picard group of their product has exactly the two primitive nef ray generators given by the coordinate line bundles. They are distinguished by unequal dimensions of global sections. Their complete linear systems recover the coordinate projections. Their action on the Segre hyperplane sections is the tensor product of the two linear lifts up to scalar. Thus we may absorb a scalar into one factor and write $g_A=g_V\otimes g_W$. [Gesmundo–Han–Lovitz, Segre automorphisms and linear preservers](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2025.102449)

The span of $S_T$ is the projectivization of the linear graph of $T:A\to U$, since all decomposables span $A$. Consequently preservation of the graph center from $T$ to $T'$ gives
$$
g_U T=T'(g_V\otimes g_W).
$$
This is an ordinary tensor transporter. Conversely, such a transporter acts projectively on the first factor by $g_U\oplus(g_V\otimes g_W)\oplus I_D$, acts trivially on the base, and carries the center configuration for $T$ to that for $T'$. Functoriality of blowup produces an abstract isomorphism. The zero tensor causes no exceptional case in this equivalence.

## Output size and equations

The compact representation by one ambient product and seven center ideals is polynomial in the dense input size. Each center ideal is generated by linear equations, by $2$-by-$2$ minors of a rectangular matrix for a Segre center, and by two linear equations for the corresponding base point. A graph center replaces the $U$-coordinate $u$ by $u-T(a)$, so all coefficients are explicit.

Here is a polynomial-size projective presentation, also useful if the input model for the target problem insists on equations. For each center, form generators $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ all of bidegree $(2,1)$ by multiplying the stated generators by all monomials of the complementary bidegree. Omit zero polynomials. The generator list generates the center ideal sheaf after the common twist. In $Y\times\mathbf P^{s-1}$, use fiber coordinates $u_i$ and include every equation $g_i u_j-g_j u_i=0$. Off the center these equations cut out the graph of the list.

Cover a Segre center by affine pivot charts, one for each nonzero matrix coordinate $z_{ab}$ and a base coordinate that is nonzero at its marked point. A linear center uses a nonzero coordinate in that center and the same type of base coordinate. On a Segre chart, normalize the pivot and base coordinates to one and trivialize $\mathcal O(2,1)$. A subset of the listed generators now gives a regular system of generators for the ideal. It consists of the forbidden ambient directions, the base-point differences, and the residual matrix coordinates $z_{ij}-z_{ib}z_{aj}$ with $i\ne a,\ j\ne b$. For a linear center only the forbidden coordinates and base-point differences occur. These are a subset of a coordinate system on the chart, via the triangular polynomial change replacing each nonpivot matrix coordinate by its residual. Each dehomogenized $g_i$ is a bounded-degree polynomial combination of the subset. This is obtained by expanding in the chart coordinates and grouping monomials containing one of the ideal generators. Homogenize the corresponding linear relation among the $u_i$ by clearing powers of the pivot and base coordinates. The homogenized relation is a global relation among the $g_i$ because its dehomogenization is such a relation on a dense open set.

Include those relations for every pivot chart. Each holds on the genuine blowup, and on the chart the nonpivot $u_i$'s are eliminated using them. The remaining equations are pairwise proportionalities for the regular sequence generators. A regular sequence ideal is of linear type, with these pairwise equations presenting its Rees algebra. Here the generators are actual chart coordinates, so the formula follows immediately on each projective chart by eliminating all other ideal coordinates. Thus the listed equations present the actual blowup locally on every center chart and off the center. [Vasconcelos, The Arithmetic of Blowup Algebras](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511574709)

The fiber product over $Y$ of the seven single-center blowups is the simultaneous blowup because the centers are disjoint. Use all seven lists of equations in one product of projective spaces. There are only nine projective factors, and every list has bounded multidegree, polynomial length and coefficients of polynomial bit size over standard explicit fields such as $\mathbb Q$ and fixed finite fields. Hence one can also move to a single projective space using the Segre embedding. For each multihomogeneous equation, first multiply by all monomials needed to reach a constant diagonal multidegree, preserving its vanishing as an ideal sheaf, and then express the resulting diagonal monomials in the Segre coordinates. Include the standard quadratic Segre equations. Since the number of factors and degrees are fixed, this is still polynomial size and takes polynomial time over the usual explicit field encodings.

## Rational forms and exact local genera

The encoding can use coprime padded input dimensions. Take $m=\max(5,\dim V,\dim W)$, pad $V,W$ to dimensions $m,m+1$, and pad $U$ to dimension at least five. The entire preceding proof still applies. This choice controls the rational forms of the smooth variety, beyond transport of specific pairs.

Write $G_T$ for the ordinary stabilizer of the padded tensor, with triples $(g_U,g_V,g_W)$ as in the transporter equation. As all seven centers are recovered, a projective ambient automorphism preserves the three summands $U,A,D$, because the fourth marker center is all of $\mathbf P(D)$. Its restriction to $D$ preserves the complete flag from the first four marker centers. Scale its linear lift so that the first diagonal coefficient on $D$ is one. Let $B'_4$ be the subgroup of the upper triangular invertible matrices on $D$ with first diagonal coefficient one. There is now a Galois-equivariant group isomorphism on separable-closure points
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(X_T)\cong (G_T/C)\times B'_4,\qquad
C=\{(I_U,tI_V,t^{-1}I_W):t\in\mathbf G_m\}.
$$
The kernel of the displayed action of $G_T\times B'_4$ is precisely $C$ in the first argument. Indeed an ambient scalar representing the identity must be one on the first diagonal coefficient of $D$, after which the only ambiguity is the factorization of the identity on $V\otimes W$ up to reciprocal scalars. Every automorphism arises from some such action by the linear-system argument.

Coprimality gives integers $r,s$ with $r\dim V-s\dim W=1$. The character $\chi(g_U,g_V,g_W)=\det(g_V)^r\det(g_W)^s$ restricts to the identity on $C$. Since $C$ is central, multiplication gives $G_T\cong C\times\ker\chi$ and $G_T/C\cong\ker\chi$. Hilbert 90 gives $H^1(F,C)=1$ over every extension $F$. We also have $H^1(F,B'_4)=1$, from the split torus quotient and the standard additive filtration of its unipotent upper triangular kernel. Consequently on separable forms the morphism from tensor stabilizers to variety automorphisms induces a bijection on $H^1$ over every extension.

For completeness, padding itself preserves these cohomology sets. Any tensor has a canonical concise core
$$
V/\ker(T:V\to\operatorname{Hom}(W,U)),\quad
W/\ker(T:W\to\operatorname{Hom}(V,U)),\quad
\operatorname{im}(T:V\otimes W\to U).
$$
Choosing complements gives a split map from its stabilizer to the core stabilizer. In each mode a kernel element is block triangular, is the identity on the prescribed core subspace or quotient, and has an arbitrary invertible block on the complementary space and an arbitrary allowed off-diagonal block. The product of the complement general linear groups is the quotient of this kernel by the vector group of off-diagonal blocks. The core section acts trivially on the quotient and linearly on the vector group, so after any cocycle twist the quotient remains split and the vector kernel is a descended vector space. Both have trivial $H^1$. This proves that projection to the core induces a bijection on $H^1$. The padded and original tensors have the same core and the bijections commute with extension of fields.

The ambient product of general linear groups has trivial $H^1$ by Hilbert 90. Therefore its rational tensor orbits in the separable geometric orbit of $T$ are classified by $H^1(F,G_T)$. For the projective variety, a separably split form and a fixed splitting isomorphism yield an automorphism cocycle, and two such forms are isomorphic exactly when their cocycles differ by a coboundary. Conversely each automorphism cocycle here lifts, at the level of classes, to $H^1(F,G_T)$ and hence to a rational tensor form. Applying the blowup construction realizes the proposed variety form. In particular **every separably split form of the variety is rational**, since it is another blowup of the same split product. For perfect fields this covers all geometric forms. The ordinary tensor-to-variety isomorphism equivalence itself holds over arbitrary fields and did not require separable closure.

For a number field $F$, the same bijections at every completion identify the **entire everywhere-local genus** of $X_T$ with that of the input tensor. In particular the earlier side-length-328 construction over $\mathbb Q$, combined with the computation of its two-element local genus, gives exactly two everywhere-locally-isomorphic smooth projective $\mathbb Q$-varieties up to $\mathbb Q$-isomorphism in the whole genus. Both are $\mathbb Q$-rational, have the same Tate Chow motive and are given explicitly by the construction. The two-class calculation uses the norm-one torus of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt{13})$, whose Tate–Shafarevich kernel has order two. The general finite-abelian genus construction transfers in the same way wherever the tensor theorem applies. [Borovoi–Kaletha–Hinich, Corollary 8.3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.04120v4#S8.SS3)

## Fixed format and prior work

For fixed format the seven centers form a disjoint smooth relative family over the affine space of all coefficients of $T$. The varying center is the graph Segre, which is taken to the standard Segre by a shear over that affine base. The blowup is therefore smooth and projective over the same base. The standard Chow-motive formula expresses its motive as that of $Y$ plus the motives of the centers with Tate twists $1,\ldots,c_j^{\mathrm{amb}}-1$. All these terms and multiplicities are independent of $T$. Over a finite field this has an elementary point-count version. Each center point is replaced by a projective space of dimension $c_j^{\mathrm{amb}}-1$ over every finite extension, and the number of center points is independent of $T$. [Jiang on the blowup and projectivization motive formulas](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1474748021000451)

Grochow and Qiao establish the general reduction framework for TI. The closest algebraic-geometric mechanisms located are classical projective stabilizers of Segre embeddings and the ambient-extension argument through line-bundle classes. Yasuda studies general computability of projective scheme isomorphism. Those sources inform the proof. The specific combination of an arbitrary tensor graph with an intrinsic smooth blowdown is this run's deduction. Publication priority remains open. The result is an exact reduction for ordinary TI and gives no general runtime for isomorphism of smooth varieties. [Grochow–Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309), [Hoff–Stenger–Yáñez](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.11150), [Yasuda](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09277)
