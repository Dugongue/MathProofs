# Graph isomorphism for tensor spaces with recoverable rank-one directions

Fix a finite field F_q and a constant s≥3. There is an explicitly recognizable class of raw three-tensors for which ordinary tensor isomorphism is GI-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. For the same class, including harmless zero padding, determinant-one tensor isomorphism is GI-complete under deterministic polynomial-time Turing reductions. The class admits unbounded rank and can have redundant endpoint configurations in both modes. This combines short-dependence presentations, paired point configurations and a quadratic rank-one recovery procedure related to the compound-matrix methods in tensor decomposition.

## Intrinsic input class

Given a tensor, first take the intrinsic support in each mode by flattening. Write its concise core as an injective slice map whose image C is a d-dimensional space in U⊗V. The pulled-back matrix minors of order two are homogeneous quadrics on the d-dimensional coordinate space for C. In a basis of C, linearize every such quadric in the symmetric-matrix variables Z_ii and Z_ij for i<j, replacing x_i² by Z_ii and x_i x_j by Z_ij. Let K be their common linear kernel inside the space of symmetric d-by-d matrices. With this convention the square x x^T evaluates the original quadric. These squares span the symmetric matrices in all characteristics: the diagonal squares give diagonal matrix units, and subtracting the two diagonal squares from (e_i+e_j)(e_i+e_j)^T supplies the off-diagonal basis element. Thus change of slice coordinates acts on the linearization by congruence, including in characteristic two.

The first condition is that C has a basis of rank-one matrices R_i=u_i v_i^T, and dim K=d. In such a basis the pure squares of the coordinate axes all lie in K and span it. A rank-one matrix with coefficient vector x gives xx^T∈K, so x has at most one nonzero entry. The d projective directions of the R_i are therefore intrinsically determined. This conclusion holds after field extension as well.

The second condition concerns the two endpoint configurations indexed by those d axes. The relation kernels of the column arrays [u_1 … u_d] and [v_1 … v_d] must each be generated by relations of support size at most s. One can choose any nonzero endpoint representatives. Rescaling columns changes relation coefficients and preserves their support sizes.

## Deterministically recovering the axes

Compute K by Gaussian elimination and check its dimension. Compute the projection onto X of the linear space of all pairs (X,Y) with XS=SY for every S∈K. If the first condition holds, choose its rank-one coordinates. Using each diagonal matrix unit as S shows that X=Y is diagonal. Conversely all such pairs belong. In the given coordinates this projects to a conjugate of the full diagonal algebra, and its common eigenspaces are exactly the coordinate lines for the R_i.

Split into simultaneous eigenspaces for generators of the projected space by intersecting kernels of X−λI over the q field elements. Keep the current subspaces as a direct-sum list, whose length is at most d. If they fail to span or fail to end in d lines, reject. Check that the recovered line basis produces rank-one matrices. Acceptance proves the first condition directly. This test makes no generic input assumption. Factor each rank-one matrix by a nonzero pivot, and enumerate the two sets of short dependencies to test their spanning condition. These operations are polynomial for fixed q and s.

## Encoding the simultaneous projective maps

Create one node for each axis. For each endpoint and each axis, attach a fiber consisting of the nonzero vectors in that endpoint line. Even when two axes have the same endpoint line, the two occurrences have separate fibers. Record two types of endpoints, the axis links, and multiplication by each fixed nonzero scalar within each fiber. For each endpoint array, encode every ordered relation on distinct axis indices of length at most s, with ordered coefficients and the actual vectors selected from their fibers. Each nonzero coefficient can equivalently be absorbed into its vector. Colored relational structures of bounded arity have standard polynomial many-one reductions to GI.

An isomorphism produces a shared permutation π of axes and two compatible bijections of the scalar fibers. Pick one source vector in each fiber. Its target image is a nonzero scalar multiple of the corresponding endpoint vector. All short dependencies of the two source arrays hold for the corresponding target images and vice versa. By the spanning condition the entire relation kernels correspond. Consequently each bijection extends uniquely to an invertible linear map on the span of the associated endpoint array. Those spans are U and V by concision. The product of the maps carries each R_i to a scalar multiple of R'_π(i), so it carries C onto C′. Conversely a left–right equivalence necessarily permutes the intrinsic rank-one directions and gives exactly such an isomorphism of the encoded structure. The third-mode map is then recovered from the two injective slice coordinate maps. The normalized maps satisfy the original dense tensor equations.

For SL-TI, use GI decision with colored individualization to recover a graph transporter and generators of the source graph automorphism group. The decoded maps on the concise tensor form the corresponding transporter and stabilize the tensor, respectively. The determinants of a transporter form a coset of the subgroup of triples generated by the decoded stabilizer determinants. Check membership of the required triple over fixed q. If a mode of the raw tensor has positive-dimensional complement to its intrinsic support, any nonzero change in the determinant on that mode can be supplied on its complement, independently. Use only the determinant coordinates where support equals the full input mode. This permits zero padding in the theorem, with no assumptions about the original input bases.

## GI hardness and the role of redundant factors

For a graph G form its join H with three universal vertices. This graph is simple and 3-connected when G has at least one vertex, and deleting any three universal vertices recovers the isomorphism type of G. For each edge e of H take its oriented incidence column u_e in the one-coordinate-deleted vertex incidence matrix. Let v_e be an independent coordinate vector, and use R_e=u_e v_e^T as the slice basis. Two such spaces are equivalent exactly when their incidence point configurations are projectively equivalent. Every graph isomorphism gives such a point map, with edge orientation absorbed by scalars. A point map preserves the graphic matroid. For simple 3-connected H, Whitney's theorem recovers H from that matroid.

Every cycle relation among incidence columns is generated by triangle relations through one fixed universal vertex. Indeed subtract appropriate triangles to eliminate all nonstar edges, leaving a relation supported on a spanning star, whose incidence columns are independent. The other endpoint array has no relations. To check the minor-kernel condition, the coefficient of x_e x_f in the minors is, up to sign, the tensor (u_e∧u_f)⊗(v_e∧v_f). The vectors v_e∧v_f are distinct basis vectors and u_e∧u_f≠0 because the incidence directions are distinct. Thus the cross coefficients are independent and dim K=d. This proves hardness by a polynomial many-one reduction. For SL hardness add one unused coordinate to every mode, so determinant corrections are always available. Cases of graphs of bounded vertex count may be handled directly.

There are also input families where both endpoints are genuinely redundant. Take the edges of any finite simple 3-regular bipartite graph with no four-cycle, such as disjoint unions of the incidence graph of the Fano plane. At each left vertex assign its three incident edges the vectors e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2 in a separate two-dimensional block. Do the same at each right vertex in its own block. Use their products as the rank-one slices. Each edge belongs to a different pair of endpoint blocks, so the slices are independent. For any pair of different edges, the two wedge products are nonzero and live in the direct summand indexed by their unordered pairs of left and right blocks, with repeats allowed. Two different edge pairs could occupy the same summand only if there were a four-cycle. Hence all minor cross coefficients are independent. All endpoint relations are generated by local triples. Both endpoint ranks are only two thirds of the number of slices.

## Relation to previous work

The compound-matrix independence condition for canonical polyadic decomposition is established precedent. The paired linear-adjoint computation above recovers the rank-one axes over any fixed finite field, with explicit treatment of characteristic two. The graph step uses short relations of *both* endpoint arrays under a shared axis permutation, then keeps the full determinant coset. The project's earlier survey used sparse relations for one code and recorded the two-matroid interpretation when rank-one elements are already given. This proof supplies recognition from raw tensor coordinates and the joint reduction. GI-hardness via graph incidence and Whitney is also classical in code and matroid equivalence. The scope here is the resulting recognizable tensor class, with the two checkable hypotheses above. This is an informal proof. No claim of priority for the compound criterion, graph gadgets, or code-to-matroid dictionary is made.
