# Determinant-one tensor isomorphism through short relations in wide algebra blocks

**Theorem.** Fix a finite field $k=\mathbf F_q$ and constants $c,\ell\geq1$ and $s\geq3$. There is a deterministic polynomial-time recognizable class of raw cubic tensors with the following properties.

- Ordinary tensor isomorphism on the class is polynomial-time many-one equivalent to graph isomorphism.
- Determinant-one tensor isomorphism on the class is polynomial-time Turing equivalent to graph isomorphism, with polynomial-time many-one hardness.
- The class contains all multiplication tensors covered by the logarithmic Peirce-width theorem, for $s\geq3$, including arbitrary independent changes of basis in their three tensor modes.
- Already at $\ell=2$, the class contains explicit layered quiver algebras with a Peirce block of dimension linear in the size of the full algebra. The large block is generated by nonzero radical products and can have many nontrivial sparse linear relations.

The recognition and upper bounds also work with $s$ equal to one or two. The graph reductions give deterministic quasipolynomial algorithms on the class by Babai's theorem. This proof is informal. The exact new combination is a size bound in terms of generation by shallow products and sparse dependencies, together with determinant recovery from raw tensors. [Babai, graph isomorphism in quasipolynomial time](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03547)

## 1. Recognize the algebra from a raw tensor

Write the input as a bilinear map $T:U\times V\to W$ between three $n$-dimensional spaces, for $n\geq1$, with slices $L_u:V\to W$. Form the paired adjoint algebra
$$
R=\{(P,Q)\in\operatorname{End}(V)\times\operatorname{End}(W):Q L_u=L_u P\text{ for every }u\in U\}.
$$
A basis of $U$ suffices for these linear equations. Componentwise composition makes $R$ a unital associative $k$-algebra. Its two components give $V,W$ the structures of left $R$-modules.

When $T$ is multiplication in a unital associative algebra $A$, evaluating the equation with $u=1$ shows $P=Q$, and then the condition is exactly commutation with all left multiplications. Consequently $P=Q$ is right multiplication by $P(1)$. Right multiplications identify $R$ with $A^{\mathrm{op}}$. Both $V$ and $W$ are left regular $R$-modules. Independent changes of basis preserve these assertions by conjugation.

Conversely, decide whether $V,W$ are isomorphic to the left regular module of $R$, and compute module isomorphisms $b:R\to V$ and $d:R\to W$ when they exist. Put $a(u)=d^{-1}T(u,b(1_R))$. The slices are $R$-linear, so $d^{-1}T(u,b(v))=v a(u)$ in $R$. If $a$ is invertible, $A=R^{\mathrm{op}}$ with the maps $(a,b^{-1},d^{-1})$ is the required explicit algebra model. The map $a$ is invertible for every raw tensor isotopic to a multiplication table, since a left regular multiplication tensor has distinct slices and the slice identity identifies $a$ with the space of those slices. The module checks and invertibility of $a$ therefore recognize the entire GL-isotopy closure of unital associative multiplication tensors. All these operations are deterministic polynomial time for fixed $q$, using the finite-algebra module algorithm.

The project previously recovered raw unital multiplication tables by finding cyclic generators using both paired adjoints. The one-adjoint regular-module derivation gives a concise equivalent interface. The paired adjoint and module algorithms are established tools. [Wilson, adjoint rings of bilinear maps](https://www.math.colostate.edu/~jwilson/papers/Central.pdf), [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, Corollary 3](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf)

## 2. State the recognizable condition

Compute a complete system of primitive orthogonal idempotents $(e_i)$ in $A$, with sum 1. The decomposition $A=\bigoplus_{i,j}A_{ij}$, where $A_{ij}=e_i A e_j$, is its Peirce decomposition. The starting elements are every vector in every $A_{ij}$ with $q^{\dim A_{ij}}\leq(n+1)^c$, together with the idempotents and a zero occurrence for each block. For each block form $P_{ij}$ from the distinct values in it of composable products of at most $\ell$ starting elements, including its zero, and $Q_{ij}$ from the same construction with bound $2\ell$. Word factors carry their starting-block occurrence, so the endpoint pair is determined even if the product is zero.

The conditions in each block are that $P_{ij}$ spans $A_{ij}$, and that the kernel of
$$
k^{Q_{ij}}\longrightarrow A_{ij},\qquad [x]\longmapsto x
$$
is generated by its vectors of support size at most $s$. Gaussian elimination tests both. For the second test enumerate distinct ordered tuples of at most $s$ elements of $Q_{ij}$, try all nonzero coefficient tuples, and compare the rank of the relation vectors found with the kernel dimension. Enumeration is polynomial for fixed $q,c,\ell,s$, since there are at most $n^2$ blocks, each used starting block has at most $(n+1)^c$ vectors, and the product words have constant length. Idempotents are computable by the finite-algebra algorithm. The next section proves that the test has the same outcome for every choice of primitive frame. [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, Proposition 11](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf)

## 3. Normalize frames while preserving determinant

Let $\phi:A\to A'$ be any $k$-algebra isomorphism, and let $J'$ be the target radical. In each simple matrix factor $M_r(K)$ of $A'/J'$, the image frame and the target frame are full ordered sets of rank-one orthogonal idempotents up to permutation. A quotient conjugator matches them. Multiplying that conjugator by a diagonal unit in a basis for the target frame makes its $K$-determinant one without disturbing the match. Each determinant-one quotient conjugator is a product of elementary matrices. An elementary matrix of nontrivial $p$-power order, with $p=\operatorname{char} k$, lifts to a unit of $A'$. Its lift has $p$-power order since the kernel $1+J'$ is a finite $p$-group. The inner linear action of every such lift has determinant one over $k$, since its determinant has $p$-power order in $k^\times$.

After composing the lifts, the corresponding frames agree modulo $J'$. For idempotents $f_i$ now matching $e'_i$ modulo the radical, $u=\sum_i e'_i f_i$ lies in $1+J'$ and satisfies $u f_i=e'_i u$. Its inner action also has determinant one. Hence every algebra isomorphism has a frame-respecting representative with the same determinant. Applying this to the identity algebra isomorphism proves the independence of the condition in Section 2. This reuses the determinant-preserving frame argument of the earlier narrow Peirce reduction.

## 4. Encode multiplication and sparse linear dependence in one graph

For each algebra, make a relational structure with idempotent-index vertices and the element occurrences $x\in Q_{ij}$. Record the ordered endpoints of each occurrence, membership in $P_{ij}$, and the marked vertex $e_i\in Q_{ii}$. Include every bounded-support coefficient relation within a block. For each $x\in P_{ij}$ and $y\in P_{jk}$, record the product triple $(x,y,xy)$, whose output lies in $Q_{ik}$. Relations have bounded arity and there are polynomially many occurrences and tuples. Encode them as colored incidence graphs with a vertex for each relation occurrence and a separately colored port for each ordered argument. Standard color gadgets turn colored graph isomorphism into plain graph isomorphism.

A frame-respecting isomorphism sends the starting vectors, product sets, sparse relations and multiplication records bijectively to their counterparts, so it gives a graph isomorphism. Conversely, a graph isomorphism gives a simultaneous block permutation and bijections $Q_{ij}\to Q'_{\pi(i),\pi(j)}$. The induced permutations of free bases send every short kernel vector to another short kernel vector. The recognition condition and the inverse permutation therefore send each entire kernel onto its counterpart. Passing to quotients yields $k$-linear isomorphisms of the Peirce blocks. The product records imply multiplicativity on the spanning sets $P_{ij}$, and hence on all composable block pairs by bilinearity. All other block products are zero in both algebras. The marked $e_i$ are respected. Thus the decoded graph isomorphism is an algebra isomorphism. The argument also shows that the permutations induced by graph automorphisms give exactly the frame-respecting algebra automorphisms.

The narrow width result is the case in which the starting set already includes every element. In that case, the relation $x+y-(x+y)=0$, expressed among occurrences, plus the scalar relations gives a complete relation kernel in support at most three. To see this, the quotient of the free vector space on all block vectors by those relations has a linear section from the block taking each vector to its occurrence. Evaluation is its inverse.

## 5. Recover the three determinant conditions on raw tensors

Graph isomorphism decision, with colored individualization and point stabilizer chains, gives one graph isomorphism and generators of the source graph automorphism group in polynomially many oracle calls. Decode and take determinants. By Section 3, the determinants of all algebra transporters are precisely a coset $D\subseteq k^\times$ generated by the decoded witness and the determinant subgroup of the decoded graph automorphisms.

Consider desired determinants $(d_1,d_2,d_3)$ of the induced transporter between the two recovered tables. These values are computable as ratios of the three normalization determinants, separately for each raw factor. A normalized-table isotopism satisfies $f(x)g(y)=h(xy)$ and has the form
$$
f=L_u\phi,\qquad g=R_v\phi,\qquad h=L_uR_v\phi
$$
for an algebra isomorphism $\phi$ and arbitrary units $u,v$ of the target algebra. Indeed $u=f(1)$ and $v=g(1)$, the operators $L_u,R_v$ are invertible because $f,g,h$ are, and $\phi=L_u^{-1}R_v^{-1}h$ is multiplicative by substitution. Therefore set $\delta=d_1d_2/d_3$. The determinant-constrained isotopism exists exactly when
$$
\delta\in D,\qquad
d_1/\delta\in\{\det L_u:u\in (A')^\times\},\qquad
d_2/\delta\in\{\det R_v:v\in (A')^\times\}.
$$
The first condition concerns the algebra transporter. The last two concern independent units, with the third determinant forced. Setting $d_i=\det(\text{target normalization in mode }i)/\det(\text{source normalization in mode }i)$ captures the raw determinant-one problem. Dual input conventions replace the corresponding determinants by inverses and leave the determinant-one condition intact.

The two unit determinant groups are computable without enumerating the units. Compute the target radical $J'$. For a primitive $e_i$, let
$$
d_i^*=\dim_k(e_i A'e_i/e_i J'e_i).
$$
The left unit determinant group is generated by the powers $\lambda^{\dim_k(e_i A')/d_i^*}$ for all primitive $e_i$ and $\lambda\in k^\times$. The right unit determinant group uses $\dim_k(A'e_i)$ instead. For verification, use a semisimple complement containing the frame, available by splitting the radical over the perfect field and conjugating lifted idempotents. The corner field at $e_i$ is $K_i=\mathbf F_{q^{d_i^*}}$. The element equal to $a\in K_i^\times$ on that diagonal entry and to 1 elsewhere acts on $e_i A'$ by multiplication by $a$ over $K_i$, and identically on the other Peirce rows. Its determinant is $N_{K_i/k}(a)^{\dim_k(e_i A')/d_i^*}$. The field norm from finite fields is surjective. The right action follows from columns. Such diagonal units and elementary matrices generate the semisimple quotient's units. Elementary lifts and radical units have $p$-power order and contribute determinant one. This gives the full two character images, even when they differ between left and right. [Rónyai, algorithms for finite algebras](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(08)80017-X)

## 6. Wide and GI-hard families

For the wide family take the acyclic quiver with paths $s\to m_i\to t$, for $1\leq i\leq r$, and no other arrows. In its path algebra, the $s,t$ block has dimension $r$, while each starting arrow block is one-dimensional. Permit a quotient by any $k$-linear space of relations on the $r$ length-two paths spanned by vectors of support at most $s$. Every subspace of this terminal path block is a two-sided ideal, since the only possibly nonzero actions on the paths are scalar actions from the endpoint idempotents. The $s,t$ block in the quotient is spanned by the images of the paths $w_i$. When this block stays wide, its $Q_{st}$ consists of zero and scalar multiples of the $w_i$. The relation kernel is generated by the original sparse relations with occurrences mapped to the $w_i$, together with the support-one relation for zero and support-two relations identifying scalar repetitions. For a direct check, use the latter relations to write any kernel vector as the image of a formal combination of the original paths, whose coefficient tuple then belongs to the given relation subspace. When the block is narrow, use all vectors and their support-at-most-three addition and scalar relations. For example, take no path relations, or use disjoint support-three relations killing a fixed fraction of the paths. The large block has dimension $\Theta(\dim A)$.

For hardness, add a disjoint single edge to each input simple graph, which preserves and reflects graph isomorphism by cancellation of connected-component types. Form its directed vertex-edge incidence graph, with arrows from vertex nodes to their incident edge nodes, and take its path algebra. There are no paths of length two. Algebra isomorphism recovers the directed graph from the dimensions of the radical Peirce blocks, and the directed graph recovers the original graph since edge nodes have exactly two incoming arrows. Conversely a graph isomorphism permutes the basis idempotents and arrows and defines an algebra isomorphism. The class condition holds with one-dimensional blocks. There is always an arrow whose scalar can be changed by any $\lambda\in k^\times$ independently, giving any determinant in the algebra stabilizer. Thus an ordinary algebra isomorphism can always be corrected to determinant one for these instances. Its multiplication tensor supplies the many-one reduction to either problem. The ordinary direction specializes the classical graph-algebra correspondence. [Grigoriev, graph and radical-square-zero algebra isomorphism](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/znsl3395), [Grochow–Qiao, Theorem 4.6](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110)

## Research provenance and scope

The combination uses the Peirce and sparse-relation entries of the isomorphism survey and the reduced-norm calculation of determinant images. The existing project work supplied determinant-preserving frame normalization, the graph-stabilizer determinant ledger, raw unital normalization, and finite-field adjoint multiplicity methods. Federated source queries for ten adjoint, Peirce, module, holonomy, and isotypic interfaces each searched 103 registered catalogs with zero reported catalog errors. Representative matches included the double-centralizer index, regular-module ports, and the isotypic module decomposition entries. The sources were used for retrieval and overlap checks. The new combination has no Lean certificate.

The nearest primary comparison is Grigoriev's weighted graph for the square-zero radical case. The layered examples above have nonzero radical square, and the graph remembers actual linear dependencies in the product block. The targeted priority search did not locate the exact bounded-support/short-product recognition condition or its raw determinant-constrained composition. Priority for those claims is unestablished. The theorem concerns its stated recognizable class.
