# Exact cubic transporters over commutative rings

Let \(B\in\mathbb Z^{q\times d}\) be an integer matrix. For a unit tuple \(r\in(R^\times)^q\), over a commutative coefficient ring \(R\), define the Kummer functor
\[
 \mathcal K_B(r)(R)=
 \{u\in(R^\times)^d: \ \prod_j u_j^{B_{ij}}=r_i\text{ for every }i\}.
\]
All cubic equalities below are polynomial identities, including over finite rings.

**Theorem.** There is an explicit family of sparse cubic forms \(F(c)\), with \(c\in(R^\times)^q\), whose transporters \(F(c')\circ g=F(c)\) over every commutative ring are functorially a product of \(\mathcal K_B(c'/c)\) with explicit affine spaces, general linear groups, and split tori. The variable count and number of nonzero allowed monomials are linear in the size of the marked simplicial realization of the matrix \(B\). In particular the construction has size linear in
\[
 d+q+1+\sum_{B_{ij}\ne0}\log(2+|B_{ij}|).
\]
Prescribing \(\det g=\nu\in R^\times\) adds precisely one independent power equation \(v^g=\nu\), with a format-dependent positive integer \(g\) given below. The other factors remain explicit. In particular ordinary and determinant-one cubic equivalence for these forms both decide exactly the specified unit equations over the original ring. No field restriction enters the result.

The theorem gives an exact solution functor, rather than only an orbit-membership reduction. For example the stabilizer schemes include any specified finite diagonalizable group as an explicit scheme factor, including nonreduced \(\mu_{p^a}\) in characteristic \(p\). The determinant prescription preserves these factors. This transfers the earlier contact-lattice constructions into exact arithmetic of a single cubic equation under linear change of variables.

## 1. The labeled input algebra

Use the marked simplicial realization of the integer matrix from [the contact-lattice proof](ContactSeed.md). Its punctured complex has free integral first homology on \(d\) circles, and the marked boundaries evaluate as the rows of \(B\). Its barycentric face flags give strict products \(p_{ij}p_{j\ell}=a_{ij\ell}p_{i\ell}\) on one-dimensional corners of a based algebra. Every unmarked coefficient is one and the marked coefficients are \(c_i\). The orientations are chosen so that the defect \(\eta_{i\ell}/(\eta_{ij}\eta_{j\ell})\) on a flag corresponds to evaluation of the inverse edge cochain on its oriented boundary. There is a connected flag support with a retained face; if necessary attach a fixed filled triangle at a vertex, which changes no first homology. Add missing successor arrows, with no nonzero strict products, in an order refining the barycentric ranks.

Add the label root and private path from [the cubic construction](CubicSeed.md). For the \(v_0\) old vertices there is a new root \(o\) and a path \(b_1,\ldots,b_{v_0}\) before the old vertices in the order. The root arrows to \(b_1\), the remaining path vertices, and the old vertices have dimensions \(4,2,3\), respectively. Add one-dimensional path arrows \(b_i\to b_{i+1}\) and matching arrows \(b_i\to i\). The new positive-degree products vanish. Call the resulting unital algebra \(A_c\). All its strict corners are forward, its \(v\) diagonal corners are the vertex lines, and its strict ideal has rank \(j\). Put \(n=v+j\). Write \(u_e\) for the dimensions of its extra strict corners, including successor and label arrows outside the original simplicial cochain complex. These corners impose no product equations. The original flag products have basis coefficients \(1\) or \(c_i\), and all are units in this theorem.

Use its trivial extension \(C_c=A_c\ltimes A_c^*\), with multiplication \((x,f)(y,g)=(xy,xg+fy)\) and \((xgy)(z)=g(yzx)\). The two-sided ideal \(J\) generated by the commutators equals its full off-diagonal spaces plus the dual vertex lines. To see this, commutation with an \(e_i\) produces each off-diagonal basis vector up to sign. If \(p\) is an original arrow basis vector, then \(pp^*\) and \(p^*p\) are the dual idempotent vectors at its two endpoints. Every vertex is incident to an arrow. Conversely the quotient by this ideal is the commutative split \(R^v\). Hence \(J\) is intrinsic under all base changes. It is nilpotent, its rank is \(2j+v\), and the based radical powers are common to the unit-valued tables. An original flag-output corner in \(J^2\) has its reverse dual line in \(J\) outside \(J^2\), since no strict product can use an original output as an input. These observations apply to the opposite algebra too.

Over a field, the off-diagonal corner dimensions identify the root, the private path with its distinguished endpoint, and then every old vertex by the matching. The two orientations \(C_c\) and \(C_c^{\rm op}\) cannot be exchanged under this labeling: a retained chord has the forward versus reverse grade distinction just described. All these field statements apply over residue fields of any coefficient ring. Thus for an \(R\)-algebra isomorphism the quotient idempotents in \(R^v\) are fixed label by label. In each coordinate this follows because an idempotent reducing to zero in every residue field is zero, and the same holds for its complement. The analogous assertion for \(C_c^{\rm op}\times C_c\) fixes both components and their labels. Each component has connected support, so it has no further factor decomposition over a field. Its center is the scalar line plus dual vertex lines, a square-zero extension, and center idempotents lift uniquely over the nilpotent ideal. This proves the component assertion over \(R\) as well.

## 2. Intrinsic recovery of the vertical space over rings

Put \(D=M_3(C_c)\), let \(s=9\), and use horizontal variables \(X,Y\in D\) and vertical variables \(Z=(Z_{ij})\in(D^s)^*\). The cubic is
\[
 F_c(X,Y,Z)=\sum_{i,j=1}^3 Z_{ij}(XE_{ij}Y),
\]
where the selectors \(E_{ij}\) are constant matrix units. The dimensions and sparsity are linear since the matrix size is fixed. Write \(V=D_X\oplus D_Y\), \(Z_0=(D^s)^*\). Polarization of a cubic by inclusion-exclusion is equivariant over any ring. For the stated form it vanishes on two inputs from \(Z_0\).

Over a field \(Z_0\) is the unique subspace of its dimension with that identity. If an isotropic subspace projects to \(L\subseteq D_X\oplus D_Y\), and has vertical intersection \(I\), then \(I\) annihilates all maps \((U,W)\mapsto(UE_{ij}Y+XE_{ij}W)\) as \((X,Y)\) ranges over \(L\). For the two coordinate projections \(L_X,L_Y\), testing scalar matrix inputs in \(U\) and \(W\) gives dimensions at least \(s\dim L_X\) and \(s\dim L_Y\) in that common span. This uses the ordinary left-right multiplication identification of endomorphisms of \(M_3(k)\) with \(M_3(k)\otimes M_3(k)^{\rm op}\) and then tensors with the vector space \(C_c\). Thus if \(L\ne0\), its dimension is strictly less than the codimension of \(I\) in \(Z_0\), excluding the required dimension. [Characteristic-two cubic recovery](CharacteristicTwoSeed.md)

To see that this also fixes the space over nonreduced rings, let \(g\) transport the two cubics. Its block on \(Z_0\) projected to \(Z_0\) is invertible over \(R\), since it is invertible in every residue field by the preceding argument. Hence \(g(Z_0)\) is the graph of \(h=(h_X,h_Y):Z_0\to V\). For \(z\) supported on slot \(ij\) and \(z'\) on slot \(ab\), the graph's isotropic identity, tested in the \(Y\) slot, gives
\[
 z'_{ab}\big(h_X(z)E_{ab}W\big)+z_{ij}\big(h_X(z')E_{ij}W\big)=0\qquad(W\in D).
\]
Choose \(b\ne j\), \(W=E_{b\ell}\). The second term is zero, while the first is \(z'_{ab}(h_X(z)E_{a\ell})\). Vary \(a,\ell\) and the arbitrary functional \(z'_{ab}\). It follows that \(h_X(z)=0\). Testing in the \(X\) slot instead, use \(a\ne i\) and \(U=E_{\ell a}\). This gives \(z'_{ab}(E_{\ell b}h_Y(z))=0\) for all \(\ell,b\), so also \(h_Y(z)=0\). Since the single-slot vectors span, \(h=0\). This argument makes the residue-field recovery exact in the presence of nilpotents.

## 3. The horizontal and vertical linear parts

The polarized cubic, with its last argument vertical, recovers the symmetric bimap
\[
 ((X,Y),(U,W))\longmapsto (XE_{ij}W+UE_{ij}Y)_{ij}.
\]
Its adjoint algebra, namely pairs \((P,Q)\) satisfying \(b(Px,y)=b(x,Qy)\) with product \((PP',Q'Q)\), is \(C_c^{\rm op}\times C_c\), and its involution exchanging \(P,Q\) interchanges the factors. This familiar construction of adjoints is linear in the matrix entries. To verify the stated calculation over any ring, sum the diagonal selector outputs to obtain \(XW+UY\). As in the [adjoint-block calculation](AdjointSeed.md), comparing its four slot equations and evaluating at the algebra unit forces the two diagonal blocks of \(P\) to be \(R_a,L_b\), and those of \(Q\) to be \(R_b,L_a\). An off-diagonal block would require a simultaneous left or right annihilator of all commutators in \(D\). The two diagonal commutators \(E_{11}-E_{22}\), \(E_{11}-E_{33}\) already have zero simultaneous annihilator, even over a ring of characteristic two. Finally the separate selector outputs force \(a,b\) to commute with all matrix units. This is precisely \(I_3\otimes C_c\).

A transporter conjugates the recovered adjoint algebra and commutes with its involution. The orientation labels from Section 1 pin its two component idempotents, whose first operators project onto \(D_X\) and \(D_Y\). The induced horizontal map is therefore block diagonal. Its conjugation on the adjoints gives one isomorphism \(\phi:C_c\to C_{c'}\), identical on both components after the opposite convention, by the involution. After removing the entrywise action of \(\phi\), the two horizontal maps are a right-\(C_c\)-linear automorphism of \(D_X\) and a left-\(C_c\)-linear automorphism of \(D_Y\). Both modules have free rank \(s\). Conversely these data all occur, because the map
\[
 D_X\otimes_{C_c} D_Y \longrightarrow D^s,\qquad
 X\otimes Y\longmapsto(X E_{ij}Y)_{ij}
 \tag{1}
\]
is an isomorphism of \(R\)-modules. On matrix-unit basis tensors the output matrix position and selector position are exactly the four original matrix indices. The relation over \(C_c\) balances because \(I_3\otimes C_c\) commutes with the selectors. Thus the vertical linear part is uniquely the inverse dual of the resulting map in (1).

## 4. Shears and the exact isomorphism of the associative parts

After removing those three linear blocks, a remaining cubic transporter is precisely a vertical shear \(Z\mapsto Z+\gamma_X(X)+\gamma_Y(Y)\). Its polynomial condition splits into the separate bidegrees \(2,1\) and \(1,2\) in \(X,Y\). Consider \(\gamma_X\). It is equivalently the \(R\)-trilinear functional \(T(x,x';y)=\gamma_X(x)(x'\otimes y)\), balanced between \(x'\) and \(y\) over \(C_c\), with \(T(x,x;y)=0\). This alternation condition is a polynomial coefficient condition: the diagonal entries vanish and the two orders of each distinct pair cancel. With a fixed free basis vector of the left \(C_c\)-module \(D_Y\), write \(T(x,x';r)\) with \(r\in C_c\). Balancing and alternation give
\[
 T(xa,x';r)=T(x,x';ar)=T(x,x'a;r).
\]
Applying these two moves in the two orders shows that \(T(x,x';(ab-ba)r)=0\) for every \(a,b,r\in C_c\). The span of the right multiples of commutators equals \(J\): it contains each off-diagonal vector and, by multiplying an arrow commutator by its dual in the appropriate order, both dual vertex residues. Hence the remaining functional on \(r\) factors through \(C_c/J=R^v\). Conversely every such functional on the alternating pairs of free basis vectors gives a balanced alternating \(T\), by taking products of the two right module coefficients and \(r\) in the commutative quotient. The \(Y\)-shears give the same result using left multiples of commutators. Thus the shears form an affine space of dimension \(2sv\binom{s}{2}\), with no division by two. They have determinant one.

For an isomorphism \(\phi:C_c\to C_{c'}\), align the images \(f_i\) of the vertex idempotents with the written \(e_i\). The unit \(\sum e_i f_i\) in \(1+J\) intertwines them. Multiplying on the left by the inverse of its diagonal part gives an aligning unit with diagonal part \(1\). This unit is unique: two aligning units differ on the left by a unit commuting with every \(e_i\), hence by a diagonal one, and that factor must have diagonal \(1\). The set of such units is an affine space of dimension \(2j\), the off-diagonal dimension of \(C_c\). Given any such unit and an idempotent-fixing map, inverse conjugation reconstructs the original map.

An idempotent-fixing map preserves the forward subalgebra \(A_c\), since all reverse corners are duals, the vertex lines are fixed, and all forward off-diagonal corners are original. It also preserves the ideal \(A_c^*\): its reverse corners are preserved, and its diagonal lines are exactly the diagonal parts of \(J\). Once a map \(A_c\to A_{c'}\) is fixed, its canonical inverse-dual lift to \(C_c\) is an isomorphism. Any other lift differs by an invertible \(A_c\)-bimodule endomorphism of \(A_c^*\). Duality identifies those endomorphisms with central elements of \(A_c\): dualize to a bimodule map on its regular module and evaluate it at the unit. The center of \(A_c\) is \(R1\), because commuting with each \(e_i\) forces the diagonal and the connected strict support identifies all diagonal coefficients. Therefore the difference is one scalar \(u\in R^\times\) on the dual ideal.

It remains to match the original forward lines. On each simplicial corner a map is a unit \(\eta_e\). The ratio \(c'/c\) is its multiplicative defect on the marks, and the defect is one on unmarked flags. Gauge the inverse edge units to one on a spanning tree. The remaining choices are the \(R^\times\)-valued characters on the first integral homology of the punctured complex. In its fixed basis the marked boundary equations are exactly \(\mathcal K_B(c'/c)\). The unnormalized tree contributes \((\mathbb G_m)^{v_0-1}\). The extra strict corners are independent \(\mathrm{GL}_{u_e}\) factors.

## 5. Scheme product and prescribed determinant

The two residual free-module automorphisms in Section 3 contribute \(\mathrm{GL}_s(C_c)\) and \(\mathrm{GL}_s(C_c^{\rm op})\). Each is a product of \(\prod_i\mathrm{GL}_s(R)\) and the affine coordinates in \(M_s(J)\): lift the quotient by its split vertex idempotents and write the remaining factor as \(1+K\) for \(K\in M_s(J)\). The latter is invertible since \(J\) is nilpotent. All the preceding recoveries and constructions are regular and commute with base change. In particular this proves the product description as schemes, without imposing a direct-product group law on stabilizers. Explicitly the ordinary transporter is
\[
 \mathcal K_B(c'/c)\times
 \mathbb A^{2j+2s^2(2j+v)+2sv\binom{s}{2}}\times
 \mathbb G_m^{v_0}\times
 \prod_e\mathrm{GL}_{u_e}\times
 \left(\prod_{i=1}^v \mathrm{GL}_s\right)^2 .
 \tag{2}
\]

For its determinant, put \(w_i=\operatorname{rank}_R(e_i C_c)=\operatorname{rank}_R(C_c e_i)\), which agree by trivial-extension duality. Let \(a_i,b_i\) be the determinants of the two \(\mathrm{GL}_s(R)\) blocks at vertex \(i\). The determinants of the two module automorphisms as \(R\)-maps are \(\prod a_i^{w_i}\) and \(\prod b_i^{w_i}\), respectively: the quotient matrices act on their vertex summands with these multiplicities, and the \(1+M_s(J)\) factor is unipotent in a radical-adapted basis. On the output tensor product (1) each such determinant occurs to the power \(s\), because the other module is free of rank \(s\). The inverse dual on vertical variables inverts that determinant. Also \(\det_R\phi=u^n\), where \(u\) is the dual scaling from Section 4: conjugation by \(1+J\) has determinant one and the determinants of the original and inverse-dual maps cancel. The two horizontal copies have \(2s\) copies of \(\phi\), and the tensor output has \(s^2\) copies. Thus the full determinant is
\[
 u^{\,n(2s-s^2)}\prod_{i=1}^v(a_i b_i)^{(1-s)w_i}.
 \tag{3}
\]
This expression is independent of the Kummer and tree coordinates, all shears, and all extra corner matrices.

Split each \(\mathrm{GL}_s\) as a scheme into a determinant unit and a determinant-one matrix using a diagonal scaling of its first basis vector. Define the positive integer
\[
 g=\gcd\bigl(n(s^2-2s),\ (s-1)w_1,\ldots,(s-1)w_v\bigr).
\]
A unimodular integer coordinate change on the \(1+2v\) determinant variables in (3) makes the character the \(g\)-th power of one variable. This follows from the integer Euclidean algorithm on the exponent row and is valid over all unit groups. The prescribed-determinant transporter therefore replaces those \(1+2v\) variables by the root functor \(\{z:z^g=\nu\}\) and \(2v\) free torus variables. Formula (2) with these replacements and its \(2v\) copies of \(\mathrm{SL}_s\) gives the asserted exact scheme. In particular \(\nu=1\) adds \(\mu_g\) as a scheme factor, with a point for every coefficient ring. Equivalently a specified determinant can occur exactly when it is a \(g\)-th power, independently of the Kummer equations.

For a stabilizer take \(c'=c\). Applying Smith form to \(B\) gives the Kummer factor as a split torus times the \(\mu_{d_i}\) belonging to its nontrivial invariant factors \(d_i\). Choosing \(B\) diagonal realizes any specified finite diagonalizable group in this factor, over arbitrary rings. In characteristic \(p\), for example, the factor \(\mu_{p^a}\) has \(q^{\ell-\lceil\ell/p^a\rceil}\) points over \(\mathbb F_q[z]/(z^\ell)\): its residue is \(1\) and \((1+x)^{p^a}=1+x^{p^a}\). Thus the cubic equivalence retains infinitesimal arithmetic as well as same-field orbits.

These are written proofs. They build on the contact-lattice construction, the cubic polarization and adjoint calculations, and the unital split-corner geometry. The new ring-level step is that an isotropic graph over the recovered vertical space vanishes by explicit matrix selectors, including over nonreduced rings. The determinant computation applies to the entire transporter. [Grochow–Qiao on cubic form equivalence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00309)
