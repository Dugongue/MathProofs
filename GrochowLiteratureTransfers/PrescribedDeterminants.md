# Prescribed determinants for ordered tensor slices over prime-power rings

Fix a prime $p$. Let $R=\mathbb Z/p^\tau\mathbb Z$ with arbitrary $\tau\ge1$. Matrices are dense and residues are encoded in binary. For any two ordered tuples of rectangular matrices $A_i,B_i:U\to V$ over $R$, and any $a,b\in R^\times$, one can decide and exactly count the pairs
$$
(P,Q)\in\mathrm{GL}(U)\times\mathrm{GL}(V),\qquad
QA_i=B_iP,\quad \det P=a,\quad \det Q=b
$$
in deterministic bit time polynomial in the tuple input size and $\tau$. There is no bound on the number of slices or a regularity assumption. The algorithm computes the whole two-coordinate determinant coset by generators. In particular, for an $r$-slice three-tensor over $R$ it gives three-mode determinant-one decision and counting in $p^{\tau r^2}\operatorname{poly}(L+\tau)$ time, where $L$ is the dense input bit length. This yields a polynomial-time many-one reduction to ordinary TI on the regime $\tau r^2=O(\log L)$ by deciding the instance and returning a fixed ordinary yes or no pair. The main result concerns prescribed slice mixing for arbitrary widths and compact ring exponents.

Part V supplies the free-module tensor setting over prime-power rings and uses a radical filtration for isometry over odd local rings. Sun’s Appendix A separates strict left–right tuple equivalence from congruence over $\mathbb Z/2^\tau\mathbb Z$. Here strict transporters are retained and their two determinant characters are computed on both the residue-field and principal-unit parts. The existing fixed-field slice result computes the residue-field part, including for singular rectangular pencils. [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, §§2.5 and 5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S5.SS3) · [Sun, Appendix A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920v1#A1) · [Earlier fixed-field result](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/OpenProblemArchiveAdditions/SpecialLinearPencils.md) · [Part III, Open Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5)

## Strict equivalence over the compact ring

Regard a tuple $M$ as a two-vertex representation with one arrow per slice. Its endomorphisms are pairs of matrices satisfying the displayed relations with $B=A$. For two tuples $M,N$, form $C=\operatorname{End}_R(M\oplus N)$ by linear congruences. Let $\bar C$ be its image by reduction of the ambient matrices modulo $p$. It is possible for $\bar C$ to be smaller than the endomorphism algebra of the reduced tuple. Computing the image of the actual congruence solutions is essential. Smith form over $R$ gives module generators, coordinates and preimages, all in polynomial time. Reduce those generators and use field linear algebra for $\bar C$.

Let $e,f$ be the projections to $M,N$, also denoting their reductions. Test whether the left $\bar C$-modules $\bar C e$ and $\bar C f$ are isomorphic. Use a basis of $\bar C$ as the list of acting matrices. The constructive Brooksbank–Luks algorithm applies to this finite-dimensional module input. An isomorphism is right multiplication by $x\in e\bar C f$ with inverse right multiplication by $y\in f\bar C e$. Indeed its two defining elements are its values at $e,f$. The equalities $xy=e$ and $yx=f$ show that $y$ is an invertible reduced strict transporter. Lift its matrix entries by solving the original linear congruences and take the $fCe$ corner. The vertex maps of the lift are invertible since their reductions are. Their inverses respect every arrow. Conversely an actual strict transporter and its inverse reduce to such a corner pair. This proves the test and constructs a strict transporter $t$ whenever one exists. [Brooksbank–Luks, Testing isomorphism of modules](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf)

## Determinant image of the ordered stabilizer

Write $E=\operatorname{End}_R(M)$. This finite ring is embedded in the product of the two vertex matrix rings. Let $\bar E$ be its actual image modulo $p$ and let $J$ be the inverse image of $\operatorname{rad}(\bar E)$. The finite-field algebra algorithms of Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski and Rónyai compute the radical and an explicit semisimple quotient
$$
E/J\simeq\bar E/\operatorname{rad}(\bar E)\simeq \prod_j M_{d_j}(\mathbb F_{p^{h_j}}).
$$
Their dependence on $p$ is polynomial for fixed $p$. A module generated by a polynomial number of matrix entries has a polynomial-size presentation. Over $R$, one can compute Smith forms by repeatedly pivoting on a nonzero entry of smallest $p$-valuation, which divides every entry in its current submatrix, then clearing its row and column and recursing. The ideal $J$ is nilpotent. For $s$ with $(\operatorname{rad}\bar E)^s=0$, matrices in $J^s$ have entries divisible by $p$, hence $J^{s\tau}=0$. In particular $J$ is the Jacobson radical of $E$, $E^\times\to(E/J)^\times$ is onto, and its kernel is $1+J$. For surjectivity, lift an inverse along with a unit and invert the resulting $1+$nilpotent error by a terminating geometric sum. [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski, §4](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf) · [Rónyai, Computing the structure of finite algebras](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(88)80017-X)

First compute the residue determinant image. On each reduced vertex space, take the filtration by powers of $\operatorname{rad}(\bar E)$. On each graded piece the $j$-th central idempotent cuts out a sum of copies of the standard column module for $M_{d_j}(\mathbb F_{p^{h_j}})$. Count the copies by dividing the dimension of the image of that idempotent by $d_jh_j$. Let $u_j,v_j$ be the sums of these multiplicities at the two vertices. Over the base field the determinant of an extension-field linear map is the norm of its extension-field determinant. Taking determinants on the graded pieces therefore gives the residue image generated by
$$
(z^{u_j},z^{v_j})\quad (z\in\mathbb F_p^\times)
$$
for each $j$. This uses surjectivity of the determinant on $\mathrm{GL}_{d_j}(\mathbb F_{p^{h_j}})$ and of the finite-field norm. It uses no search for a norm preimage. The residue image is precisely the projection to the prime-to-$p$ part of the determinant image. Lift each displayed coordinate to its unique prime-to-$p$ representative by raising any lift in $R^\times$ to $p^{\tau-1}$. This exponent kills the principal part and is $1$ modulo $p-1$.

For the principal part, take the inverse image $S\le E^\times$ of the product of upper unitriangular subgroups of $(E/J)^\times$. This is a Sylow $p$-subgroup because $1+J$ is a $p$-group, and upper unitriangular matrices are Sylow $p$-subgroups of the respective general linear groups. Its determinant image is the full $p$-part of the joint image. Indeed the image of a Sylow subgroup under a surjective homomorphism of finite groups is Sylow, as follows by taking the order of its intersection with the kernel and comparing indices. Generate $S$ with the following elements.

- For each $i\ge1$, compute an $\mathbb F_p$-basis of $J^i/J^{i+1}$ and use $1+x$ for each lifted basis element $x$. The quotient is elementary abelian because $p1\in J$ and $J^{2i}\subseteq J^{i+1}$. Multiplication gives addition on this quotient. Induction down the filtration shows that this list generates $1+J$.
- For each matrix factor lift the transvections in every upper position with entries ranging over an $\mathbb F_p$-basis of the extension field. Such transvections generate the upper unitriangular group by entry elimination. All lifts are units since their images in $E/J$ are units.

Powers and preimages of ideals in this computation are obtained by multiplying module generators and taking Smith forms. There are at most $s\tau$ layers and each layer has at most a polynomial number of basis vectors. The number of transvections is bounded by the algebra dimension. Compute the determinant pairs on the resulting units by elimination over the local ring. This step includes the possible contribution of lifts of residual transvections, which cannot be discarded on the basis of their determinant modulo $p$.

To test membership or compute the size of the subgroup generated in the principal part, use the cyclic coordinates of $R^\times$. For odd $p$, the principal part is generated by $1+p$, of order $p^{\tau-1}$. For $p=2$ and $\tau\ge3$, the generators are $-1$ and $5$, of orders $2$ and $2^{\tau-2}$. For $\tau=1,2$ the corresponding trivial or order-two groups are explicit. In the odd case $v_p((1+p)^{p^i}-1)=i+1$ and in the binary case $v_2(5^{2^i}-1)=i+2$, by binomial expansion and induction. These valuations both verify the orders and give successive digit extraction of the exponent by testing $p$ digits at a time. The sign in the binary case is first read modulo $4$. Thus integer Smith form gives subgroup membership and order in bit time polynomial in $\tau$ for fixed $p$. The residue part has constant order.

## Counting and tensor assembly

If there is an ordinary strict transporter $t$, all others are exactly $tE^\times$. Compose on its source. Their determinant pairs form the translate of the computed subgroup $D$ by the pair of determinants of $t$. Every element of this translate is realized equally often, with fiber size
$$
\frac{|E^\times|}{|D|},\qquad
|E^\times|=|J|\prod_j |\mathrm{GL}_{d_j}(\mathbb F_{p^{h_j}})|.
$$
Here $|J|$ comes from Smith form and the general linear orders come from choosing successive independent columns. The number has polynomial bit length. If the desired pair fails membership, the count is zero. For full three-mode equivalence enumerate determinant-one slice maps and apply the ordered test after each, imposing the other two determinant-one conditions. The two-vertex convention uses $P^{-T}$ and $Q$ on the tensor modes, so the requirement is exactly $(\det P,\det Q)=(1,1)$. Distinct slice maps give disjoint sets of triples. There are at most $|R|^{r^2}$ candidates and the stated bound follows. The general case with unrestricted slice dimension remains open.

A Lean lemma checks the abstract transporter-coset equivalence. The matrix-algebra computations, running time and counting formula are proved above in prose.
