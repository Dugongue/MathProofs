# Literature search through 20 November 2026

The target questions come from Grochow’s publication list and the cited papers. Titles, abstracts, relevant theorem sections, and prior repository proofs were compared using arXiv, ECCC, GitHub, Hexagon and the indexed Lean workspace. The local open-question catalog supplied IDs T6, T7, T8, T10, G03 and G08, all within its first 22 entries. The catalog is organized by topic and supplies no author ranking. The four selections prioritize explicit questions with a recent method that changes the proof. ## Recent sources that enter the arguments

| Primary source | Use in this packet |
| --- | --- |
| Grochow, Qiao, Stange and Sun, _On the Complexity of Isomorphism Problems for Tensors, Groups, and Polynomials V_, arXiv v1, November 2026 | The free-module formulation and local-ring radical approach in §5.3 feed the ordered determinant problem. The determinant proof computes principal-unit characters using a Sylow subgroup above the semisimple quotient. The same paper poses the characteristic-two tuple question and gives the odd-characteristic adjoint approach. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1) |
| Xiaorui Sun, _Faster Isomorphism for Groups of Class 2_, November 2026 | Appendix A separates strict tuple equivalence and alternating congruence over binary prime-power rings. The dyadic proof uses this separation, followed by norm elimination in a finite commutative unit group. The two determinant characters refine the strict equivalence step. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05920v1#A1) |
| Chizewer, Everett, Mithal and Qiao, _Random Tensor Isomorphism under Orthogonal and Unitary Actions_, March 2026 | The exact algorithm diagonalizes simple flattening Gram spectra. The new argument uses Gram equality to remove one full residual isometry group. A second test uses weighted slice Gram matrices to fix another mode up to one scalar phase. [arXiv, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27128v1#S2) |
| Grochow, Srivastava and Thakkar, _Algorithms for Finite Group Epimorphism Testing_, September 2026 | Open Question 3(5) fixes the central-radical target family, and Open Question 2 asks for counting. The proof groups simple-factor assignments by the orbits of restricted extension classes, allowing arbitrary multiplicities under the bounded target radical generator condition. [arXiv, §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07429v1#S7) |
| Zhenyu Sun, _Definability and Lifting in Model Subgroupoids_, Hexagon v1, 11 November 2026 | Sections 6–8 organize existence and enumeration of finite lifts by obstruction classes and orbit types. We use this organization for quotient-factor assignments. The groups and lift problem in the Hexagon paper are different. Its cohomological and enumeration results are inspiration for the decomposition, and the epimorphism proof establishes its own factorization and weights. [Hexagon](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00276v1) |

## Comparison with adjacent work and formal sources

- The existing MathProofs fixed-width pencil proof already computes finite-field determinant pairs by endomorphism algebras. The ordered ring proof adds strict equivalence from actual mod-$p$ reductions, the principal-unit determinant image, and exact fibers over $\mathbb Z/p^\tau\mathbb Z$. The algebra algorithms are standard constructive precedents. [Prior GitHub proof](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/OpenProblemArchiveAdditions) · [Brooksbank–Luks](https://ix.cs.uoregon.edu/~luks/ModIso.pdf) · [Chistov–Ivanyos–Karpinski](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/marek/publications/85169-cs.pdf)
- Qiao–Zhang’s manuscript gives an isomorphism coset for Cayley-table groups. The November follow-up transfers this to finite ring and tensor isomorphism with time measured in the cardinalities of the relevant structures. The ordered determinant proof works in compact prime-power representation and uses a strict left–right calculation. The epimorphism proof counts across variable quotient maps directly. [Qiao–Zhang project](https://group-iso.github.io/) · [Zhang–Wang–Zhuang, §§2 and 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18084v1)
- Classical adjoint-algebra methods reduce simultaneous isometry to a norm equation. The case considered here uses a commutative commutant to make the norm a group homomorphism, including nonidentity involutions, and computes its full fiber over the binary residue ring and characteristic-two fields. [Ivanyos–Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03495)
- Grochow–Qiao’s earlier central-radical isomorphism theorem splits cohomology classes over perfect factors. For a surjection, source factors can be omitted and the kernel map can be noninjective. These are the two changes that lead to the falling factorial in the zero orbit and the asymmetric bound in the target radical rank. [Grochow–Qiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.1776)
- Lysikov–Walter’s orbit-closure results and the CCC 2026 fixed-parameter degree bounds provide relevant invariant-theoretic context. Their orbit-closure equivalence does not compute a prescribed determinant coset over a finite local ring. These papers were shortlisted without using their theorems as premises. [Lysikov–Walter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04639) · [CCC 2026 degree bounds](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.32)
- The Hexagon rank-23 result concerns rank lower bounds rather than the precise transporter questions here.
