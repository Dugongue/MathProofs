# Benchmark verification and solver scope

The verifier in [audit_tensor.py](audit_tensor.py) replays each positive witness by four mode contractions and independently computes the four matrix ranks. Its coverage screen reconstructs both balanced cuts by explicit index loops, solves for their ranks, and constructs the two local commutants using ordinary rational or finite-field row reduction. Its optional negative check independently compares all seven cut ranks and checks the complete intertwiner criterion inside the scalar class. Outside that class it can return unknown. Every positive timing from either method can be checked with the same independent criterion.

For rational coverage, a prime screen can certify the scalar class when it observes two nonsingular cuts and scalar commutants. Tensor entries must have denominators prime to the screen prime. The finite-field determinants certify their rational counterparts. The crossed inverse then reduces to the one used in the screen. Nonzero maximal minors of the commutator linear systems certify their rational counterparts. Since scalar operators always lie in the commutants, these ranks are already the largest possible. Finite-field failure does not determine rational failure. The verifier uses exact Fraction arithmetic in those cases. The comparison verifier operates in the original field.

In a separate synthetic arithmetic check, all 300 binary raw input classifications agreed with the previous bit-packed control. Its 23 accepted inputs were independently scrambled and verified again. An additional 360 binary pair checks compared with the new pipeline and found agreement on all 172 decisions it made. Those checks calibrate the software and are separate from the published-input coverage.

The four-way algorithm of Li, Li, Qiao, Tao and Wang is a relevant specialist comparator with different applicability. In Section 4.1 a fixed square cut with differing ranks supplies a negative certificate. With equal ranks, the algorithm declines outside corank three and also declines when either source kernel matrix space has no invertible matrix. The paper does not link a runnable implementation of this algorithm. The benchmark can measure its rank entry test separately from the substantive solver. [Published algorithm](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00591v2#S4.SS1)

Elasticity has a specific obstruction to that kernel approach on the native axis order. The minor symmetries force the three-dimensional space of alternating $3\times3$ matrices into both native cut kernels. If the rank is six then those are the entire kernels. Every alternating matrix of size three is singular, also in characteristic two. The native cut therefore cannot pass the published kernel procedure on a positive pair with exact minor symmetries. A fixed regrouping of the same tensor describes the two alternative cuts, which can be full rank. The regrouping is an input convention and all results refer to separate mode maps in that convention. For physical rotation of elastic tensors the model uses a single shared orthogonal coordinate map. [MatTen data and field semantics](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8190849) · [Materials paper](https://doi.org/10.1039/D3DD00233K)

Another exact obstruction applies to orthogonal-permutation designs. If the two balanced cuts $F,F^\Gamma$ are permutation matrices, their inverses are their transposes. The crossing $\Gamma$ commutes with full transpose, so $F\Gamma((F^\Gamma)^{-1})=I$, giving zero local powers and full commutants. This identity also holds for dual-unitary tensors over the complex numbers with conjugate transposes. It explains why two full cuts can coexist with zero coverage. Since the local commutants are invariant, arbitrary independent basis changes on the same tensors leave that failure intact.

Brooksbank, Maglione and Wilson report a Magma implementation of the derivation-densor approach. The published polynomial class has classical derivation algebras and one-dimensional densor spaces. The author's Densor package is described as computing densor spaces for bilinear maps. The Gilchrist et al. attack code targets three-way tensors and also uses Magma. The benchmark's runnable external engines are identified separately from these specialist algorithms. [Derivation-densor paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04046) · [Densor package description](https://joshmaglione.com/research.html#software) · [Gilchrist et al.](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/337)

The independent source replay checked both Semeion layouts against the original 1,593 binary pixel records. The row-major factorization had 2 accepted inputs and 3 with two full cuts. The alternative factorization had 1 accepted input. The publisher supplies $16\times16$ images, so each four-way layout is a declared reshape, and the two layouts are separate views of the same records. The independent Sloane coverage pass matched the normalized file checksum and found 7 full-cut inputs and 0 accepted inputs among all 23 selected entries. All 41,104 field-and-layout combinations for MatTen separately matched the first census, including every per-system count. [Semeion](https://doi.org/10.24432/C5SC8V) · [MatTen](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8190849) · [Sloane catalogue](https://neilsloane.com/oadir/index.html)

The Semeion record specifies CC BY 4.0. The MatTen manifest likewise records CC BY 4.0. The Sloane catalogue has no explicit redistribution terms located in its index. Its normalization script and the audit report can point to the original catalogue without bundling the raw or transformed records. The solver wrappers and the corank-three implementation are independent implementations. Their cited solver binaries or Python packages remain separate upstream dependencies. [Semeion license](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/178/semeion%2Bhandwritten%2Bdigit) · [MatTen release](https://zenodo.org/records/8190849)

All 10,276 native elastic tensors satisfy both minor symmetries and the major symmetry exactly as serialized. The three permutations generating these symmetries also generate the eight-element stabilizer of the physical minor partition $P=\{\{0,1\},\{2,3\}\}$. The physically grouped cut has rank six over the rationals. Thus a shared axis permutation can give two nonsingular working cuts only by sending the unused cut to $P$. Exactly eight permutations do so. They differ from the fixed regrouping by left composition with a symmetry of the tensor, and hence give identical arrays. This proves that trying other axis orders cannot increase the MatTen rational count beyond 104. The same argument applies to rounded parity with the physical cut rank at most six.

For Semeion and the Sloane corpus, the [all-layout audit](audit_all_layouts_results.json) first requires two of the three balanced cuts to be nonsingular, then exhausts the 24 shared axis permutations for each remaining input. Only four Semeion records have two nonsingular cuts in any arrangement, and each admits the scalar criterion in at least one ordering. The maximum observed coverage with any ordering is 4/1,593. Seven Sloane inputs have two nonsingular cuts, and all 23 fail the scalar criterion in all orders.

The [timed-ledger replay](audit_timing_results.json) independently verified 75 archived positive witnesses from the crossed and general baseline solvers across their cold and warm runs and the rational solver comparison. It certified 50 archived negative decisions. All general baseline negatives have a cut-rank certificate, and six additional crossed negatives instead have an exact local intertwiner-dimension certificate. The fixed-cut and all-cut published-corank ledgers add 22 rank certificates on 11 pair fixtures. These are counts of answers across repeated configurations, with zero disagreements. The original benchmark checker also replays successful fallback answers before recording hybrid timings.
