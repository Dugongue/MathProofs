# Crossed-flattening tensor isomorphism on published data

The exact crossed-flattening algorithm is faster after eligibility is established for the tested positive pairs. Eligibility is rare in the full corpora. In the unconditioned positive-pair samples, a crossed-first hybrid took longer than the reference solver alone. Every reported positive has an exactly verified four-mode witness.

## Published-input coverage

Coverage is the fraction of individual inputs with the required matched format, two invertible cuts and two scalar commutants. It is counted on original publisher records in each declared view. A decline is a coverage outcome.

MatTen supplies published density functional theory calculations. Its coefficients are used literally as rational numbers. The fixed axis order is $(0,2,1,3)$. The variant over F₂ is a separate arithmetic reduction. Among the rational positives, 57 of 60 triclinic inputs and 47 of 756 trigonal inputs were covered. The other five crystal systems had zero coverage. The benchmark solves independent modewise general-linear equivalence. A common physical rotation is a different question. [MatTen release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8190849) · [Exact coverage ledger](data_elasticity_coverage.json)

All MatTen records have both minor symmetries and the major symmetry exactly in the serialized entries. The native physical cut has rank six. With two distinct cuts required full, the physical cut must be the third one, so any usable axis layout differs from the fixed regrouping by an element of the eight-element subgroup generated by those three symmetries. That subgroup fixes the actual tensor. Thus trying every shared axis permutation cannot increase the MatTen coverage. For the seven Sloane inputs with full cuts, the two cuts are permutation matrices and the crossed operator vanishes, even after independent changes of mode basis. All 24 layouts were tested for Semeion and Sloane. Each of the six order-four entries in FROSTT has four distinct mode sizes, which excludes the matched format without loading its tensor entries. ## Pair construction and comparison

The benchmark uses the same input bytes for competing methods. The binary cohort contains 33 pairs, comprising 16 drawn without coverage selection and then rebased, six rebased from the covered class, and 11 independently rank-certified negatives. Rational timing used 18 pairs, four in the native view and 14 in the regrouped view. The latter includes four unconditioned rebases, seven covered rebases and three same-rank trace-certified negatives. Positive pairs use original corpus tensors and planted coordinate transformations that are withheld from the solvers. The coverage denominators above include all source records. The timed cohorts deliberately include additional covered cases.

CaDiCaL 2.1.2 is the binary reference engine. The rational reference combines CaDiCaL with modular lifting and exact witness verification. Direct Z3 5.4.1 is an additional 3-second-budget comparison. These use independent benchmark adapters. A faithful independent implementation of Li–Li–Qiao–Tao–Wang's published corank-three method handled 11 rank negatives and declined the other 22 binary pairs, with or without trying alternative cuts. Its scope complements the crossed method. No directly comparable Magma run was available. [Published 4-TI algorithm](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00591v2#S4) · [Corank-three ledger](baseline_corank_results.json)

## End-to-end measurements

Cold timings include interpreter launch, JSON reading, arithmetic, external solver calls where applicable, and each solver's exact witness verification. Resident timings keep Python loaded and still include all per-pair work. The hybrid actually runs crossed first and invokes the reference solver on a decline. An unresolved attempt remains unknown. The independent replay of saved witnesses and negatives is outside the timed intervals. [Audit ledger](audit_timing_results.json)

### Binary inputs

CaDiCaL and the hybrid settled all 33 pairs. Crossed alone settled 17. On the six covered positives, median paired speedup over CaDiCaL was **1.56× cold** and **21.0× resident**. Resident median times on those pairs were **3.71 ms** crossed and **71.0 ms** CaDiCaL. The resident hybrid took **1.09 s** versus **1.56 s** for CaDiCaL across the full enriched cohort. In the 16 unconditioned positives it took **1.059 s** versus **0.986 s**, about **7% longer**. Cold hybrid total was **6.14 s** versus **4.83 s** on the enriched cohort. [Binary resident example](results/semeion-native-warm.jsonl) · [Binary cold example](results/semeion-native.jsonl)

### Rational inputs

The first implementation spent most of its time on Fraction arithmetic. The integer-arithmetic accelerator clears denominators and uses adjugates, integer power traces and exact witness checking. Where the linear equations have enough rank modulo good primes, their nonzero minors certify rank and reconstructed vectors are checked over the rationals. If reconstruction fails the solver uses exact elimination. All 18 pair verdicts, coverage profiles and returned positives agreed with the first implementation or its independent verifier.

For the three positives solved by both the crossed method and the rational lifting baseline, the accelerated crossed median was **7.19 ms resident** versus **55.0 ms** for the reference. In an interleaved cold retest with seven repeats per pair, the medians were **133 ms** and **152 ms**, about **1.14×**. The initial exact-Fraction implementation was roughly **241 ms resident** on the same three positives. [Repeated cold measurements](crossed_q_exact_matched.json) · [Exact parity and timing ledger](crossed_q_exact_results.json)

On all 14 regrouped rational pairs, the accelerated crossed solver settled **10**, the lifting baseline **4**, and the hybrid **11**. The resident hybrid took **0.263 s** versus **0.686 s** for the lifting baseline, with different completion counts. Cold totals were **2.51 s** for the hybrid and **2.11 s** for the baseline. On the four unconditioned regrouped positives, the resident hybrid took **0.198 s** versus **0.185 s**, about **7% longer**. The four native rational positives likewise showed no gain from the pre-pass. Direct Z3 settled zero of the 18 rational pairs at this 3-second budget. Its unresolved timings do not establish a solved-to-solved speed ratio. [Resident rational results](results/data_elasticity_regrouped-q-fast-warm.jsonl) · [Cold rational results](results/data_elasticity_regrouped-q-fast.jsonl) · [Budgeted Z3 results](results/data_elasticity_regrouped-q-z3.jsonl)

The result supports a targeted fast path for known eligible inputs, with a potentially useful concentration among the triclinic records in this one release. The benchmark gives no evidence of an across-corpus speedup from enabling the fast path unconditionally. The pairs establish recovery from concealed coordinate changes of published tensors. They do not measure the frequency of naturally occurring duplicate orbits across two independently collected datasets.
