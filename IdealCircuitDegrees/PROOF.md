# Nonminimum ideals, infinitely many minimal circuit degrees, and descending chains

Grochow's Question 6.4 has a negative answer, already for a two-generated family of prime ideals. The descending-chain clause of Question 6.6 has a positive answer in a fixed principal ideal. Question 6.5 has a negative answer when degrees are taken among polynomially bounded families that are nonzero at all sufficiently large indices. The nonminimum and descending-chain results allow representatives of arbitrary degree. These conclusions hold for ordinary nonuniform algebraic circuit reductions with scalar constants over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\), under either polynomially bounded family access or the paper's single-index circuit-oracle convention. [Grochow, *Complexity in ideals of polynomials*, §2.1 and Questions 6.4–6.6](https://eatcs.org/beatcs/index.php/beatcs/article/download/620/633#page=17)

## 1. Conventions and two information bounds

The circuits compute formal polynomials. A gate can compose a supplied oracle polynomial with polynomials already computed by the circuit. A polynomial-size circuit uses only polynomially many scalar constants. The notation \(f\le_c g\) means that polynomial-size oracle circuits for \(g\) compute \(f\), one circuit for each index. We allow multiple polynomially bounded oracle indices for the nonexistence arguments. All affirmative reductions here use only the matching index, except that finitely many fixed small outputs may be computed from constants. Polynomially bounded, abbreviated p-bounded, means that the degree and number of variables are bounded by fixed powers of the index. In the eventual-nonzero convention we omit the zero polynomial at all sufficiently large indices. If finitely supported nonzero families were included as competing representatives, they would give a trivial minimum.

Write \(r(S)=\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q(S)\) for a finite set of scalars. If a circuit uses oracle polynomials having coefficient set \(A\) and uses \(s\) scalar constants, its output coefficient set \(H\) satisfies

$$
r(A\cup H)\le r(A)+s.
$$

Indeed every output coefficient belongs to the field generated by the oracle coefficients and the scalar constants. This follows by induction on arithmetic and composition gates, for every output degree. If two circuits compute the same polynomial from disjoint, jointly algebraically independent sets \(A,B\), respectively, each carrying at most \(s_A,s_B\) extra scalar constants, then

$$
r(H)\le s_A+s_B.
$$

For this, apply submodularity of algebraic rank to \(H\cup A\) and \(H\cup B\). It gives

$$
|A|+|B|+s_A+s_B
\ \ge\ r(H\cup A)+r(H\cup B)
\ \ge\ r(H)+r(H\cup A\cup B)
\ \ge\ r(H)+|A|+|B|.
$$

This is a bound on the information carried by actual oracle coefficients, so it also works if the model permits higher oracle indices, provided the number of additional scalar constants is polynomial.

## 2. A generic graph needs exponentially many coefficient parameters

Fix an integer \(n\), write \(Y=(y_1,\ldots,y_n)\), and put \(N=2^n\). Let \(P_1,\ldots,P_t\) be multilinear polynomials in \(Y\) whose \(tN\) coefficients are jointly algebraically independent over a base field \(L\) containing \(\mathbb Q\). Let \(K/L\) be a field extension within the coefficient field, generated by at most \(s\) further scalars. If a nonzero polynomial \(H\in K[Y,U_1,\ldots,U_t]\) satisfies

$$
H(Y,P_1(Y),\ldots,P_t(Y))=0,
$$

then \(s\ge N\).

Choose a nonzero coefficient of \(H\) regarded as a polynomial in the \(U_i\). This coefficient is a nonzero polynomial in \(Y\). There is a rational grid \(D=\{a_1,b_1\}\times\cdots\times\{a_n,b_n\}\) with each \(a_i\ne b_i\), on which this chosen coefficient is nonzero at every point. For its existence, take the product of the \(N\) evaluations of the chosen coefficient, with \(a_i,b_i\) initially indeterminate, and multiply by \(\prod_i(a_i-b_i)\). Each factor is a nonzero polynomial because the variables selected in that factor are distinct indeterminates. A nonzero polynomial over \(K\) cannot vanish at every tuple of rational inputs, so the rational choices exist.

For each \(P_i\) the evaluations on this grid form an invertible rational linear transformation of its \(N\) multilinear coefficients. This follows by tensoring the invertible two-point interpolation matrices for \(1,y_i\). Thus all \(tN\) evaluations are still jointly algebraically independent over \(L\). At each of the \(N\) points, \(H\) gives a nonzero polynomial relation over \(K\) among the corresponding \(t\) evaluations. Consequently the entire set of evaluations has transcendence degree at most \((t-1)N\) over \(K\), by subadditivity across the points. Its transcendence degree over \(L\) is therefore at most \(s+(t-1)N\), while it is actually \(tN\). This proves the bound and uses no bound on the degree of \(H\).

The same assertion holds when \(K\) has transcendence degree at most \(s\) over \(L\), instead of being explicitly generated by \(s\) elements. Extra ambient variables unused in the graph equations can be specialized to rationals while preserving some nonzero coefficient of \(H\).

## 3. No minimum for a two-generated prime ideal family

For all \(n\), choose generic multilinear \(A_n,B_n\) on \(n\) variables, with all their coefficients jointly algebraically independent over \(\mathbb Q\), including across all indices. In the fixed ambient rings \(R_n=F[Y,u,v]\) take

$$
I_n=(u-A_n(Y),\ v-B_n(Y)).
$$

These are nonzero prime ideals. Indeed, the quotient by the displayed generators is the polynomial ring \(F[Y]\), by substituting the two prescribed values for \(u,v\). In particular every member vanishes on the graph of \((A_n,B_n)\).

Let \(a=(u-A_n)_n\) and \(b=(v-B_n)_n\). If a family \(h\) is circuit-reducible to both \(a\) and \(b\), at each stage the coefficient sets of the two oracle collections are disjoint and jointly algebraically independent after removing the fixed rational coefficients. Section 1 shows that the field generated by the coefficients of \(h_n\) has transcendence degree bounded by a polynomial in \(n\). If \(h_n\) is also a nonzero member of \(I_n\), Section 2 with \(t=2\) shows that this degree is at least \(2^n\). This is impossible for arbitrarily large \(n\).

Thus \(a\) and \(b\) have no common lower degree represented by a member that is nonzero at infinitely many indices. In particular there is no minimum circuit degree in this family, either with p-bounded or arbitrary-degree representatives. This answers Question 6.4.

## 4. A strict descending chain in a principal ideal

Choose multilinear polynomials \(P_n(Y)=\sum_{T\subseteq[n]}\alpha_{n,T}\prod_{i\in T}y_i\), with all \(\alpha_{n,T}\) jointly algebraically independent across indices. For \(j=1,2,\ldots\), put \(w_j(n)=\lceil n/j\rceil\), and in \(F[x,Y]\) define

$$
f^{(j)}_n=xP_n(y_1,\ldots,y_{w_j(n)},0,\ldots,0).
$$

Each family belongs to the principal ideal \((x)\) and is p-bounded and everywhere nonzero. Setting the remaining variables to zero gives \(f^{(j+1)}\le_c f^{(j)}\) already at the matching index.

On the other hand, at stage \(n\) the coefficients with \(T\subseteq[w_j(n)]\) but \(T\not\subseteq[w_{j+1}(n)]\) are absent from every oracle polynomial in \(f^{(j+1)}\). They are independent over the field of all coefficients of that oracle family, because each coefficient is independent of those at every other index. There are \(2^{w_j(n)}-2^{w_{j+1}(n)}\) such coefficients. For fixed \(j\) this number grows exponentially in \(n\). Section 1 implies that no polynomial-size circuit from that oracle family can supply them, even with unbounded query indices. Thus \(f^{(j)}\not\le_c f^{(j+1)}\). This proves the strictly descending chain asked for in Question 6.6.

The construction is a descending chain above the minimum of its principal ideal. Existence of a descending chain and failure of existence of a minimal degree are separate properties.

## 5. Infinitely many minimal degrees in the p-bounded model

For each stage \(n\) choose \(n\) generic multilinear polynomials \(P_{i,n}(Y)\), \(1\le i\le n\), in the \(n\)-tuple \(Y\). Their coefficients are jointly algebraically independent across every stage and generator. Define the prime ideal

$$
J_n=(u_i-P_{i,n}(Y)\mid 1\le i\le n)
\quad\text{in }F[Y,u_1,\ldots,u_n].
$$

For each fixed \(i\), let \(g^{(i)}_n=u_i-P_{i,n}(Y)\) for \(n\ge i\), and use the first generator at earlier indices. The families \(g^{(i)}\) are p-bounded and everywhere nonzero. Let \(L_i\) be the field generated by all coefficients of \(g^{(i)}\) over \(\mathbb Q\). At every \(n\ge i\), the coefficient blocks \(P_{\ell,n}\) with \(\ell\ne i\) are jointly independent over \(L_i\). This holds because the early exceptional indices are strictly below \(i\).

Suppose \(h\) consists of p-bounded members of \(J_n\), is eventually nonzero, and satisfies \(h\le_c g^{(i)}\). All coefficients of \(h_n\) lie in a field over \(L_i\) with polynomially bounded transcendence degree, by the circuit coefficient argument. After substituting \(u_i=P_{i,n}(Y)\), its resulting polynomial \(R_n\) over that same field vanishes on the graph of all \(P_{\ell,n}\) with \(\ell\ne i\). Section 2 says that for all sufficiently large \(n\), \(R_n\) must be the zero polynomial. Indeed a nonzero \(R_n\) would require \(2^n\) transcendence degree over \(L_i\). Hence for all such \(n\), \(h_n\) is divisible by the monic linear-in-\(u_i\) factor \(g^{(i)}_n\), by polynomial division in \(u_i\).

Here is the circuit reduction that recovers the monic linear factor from an oracle for \(h_n\). It uses its p-bounded degree, so we give the construction explicitly. Write \(u=u_i\) and \(P=P_{i,n}\). Let \(r\) be the multiplicity of \(u-P(Y)\) in \(h_n\). Set the other output variables \(u_\ell\), \(\ell\ne i\), to constants preserving this multiplicity. This is possible because the cofactor in \(h_n=(u-P)^r H\) does not vanish identically at \(u=P(Y)\), and its nonzero scalar-polynomial coefficients in \(Y\) cannot all vanish on rational assignments of those other variables. Write \(h^*(u,Y)\) for the specialized polynomial and

$$
Q(u,Y)=\frac{\partial^{r-1}}{\partial u^{r-1}}h^*(u,Y).
$$

Then \(Q(P(Y),Y)=0\) and \(\partial_u Q(P(Y),Y)\ne0\). In fact the last polynomial is \(r!\) times the specialized cofactor evaluated at \(u=P(Y)\). Since the total degree of \(h_n\) is p-bounded, interpolate it in \(u\) at polynomially many distinct scalar points to obtain an oracle circuit for \(Q\) of polynomial size, independently of the circuit complexity of \(h_n\).

Choose a rational point \(a\) for which \(c=\partial_u Q(P(a),a)\ne0\), and let \(Y(t)=a+t(Y-a)\). Its formal parameters range over \(F[Y][[t]]\). The root \(P(Y(t))\) has constant term \(P(a)\), admitted as a scalar constant, and degree in \(t\) at most \(n\). Suppose its truncation \(z_{k-1}(t)\) through \(t^{k-1}\) is computed. Adding \(t^k b\) changes the coefficient of \(t^k\) of \(Q(z_{k-1}(t),Y(t))\) by \(cb\). Therefore compute the next coefficient by

$$
b=-c^{-1}[t^k]Q(z_{k-1}(t),Y(t)).
$$

Obtain the coefficient by interpolation in \(t\) at scalar points. Indeed, the degree in \(t\) of this polynomial before coefficient extraction is \(\deg(Q)\max\{1,k-1\}\), or less, so polynomially many points suffice. All coefficient computations produce polynomials in \(Y\). There are at most \(n\) stages, and the circuits share previously computed coefficients. Substitution \(t=1\) after the last stage gives \(P(Y)\) exactly. Then subtract it from \(u\) to recover the generator. This proves \(g^{(i)}\le_c h\) with polynomially many oracle gates at the matching index. Finitely many initial stages can use fixed explicit circuits.

The degree of each \(g^{(i)}\) is thus minimal in the p-bounded eventually-nonzero model. They are pairwise inequivalent. For \(i\ne j\), the entire current coefficient block of \(P_{i,n}\), once \(n\) is large enough, is independent over the field generated by the coefficients of \(g^{(j)}\). Any reduction would need at least \(2^n\) scalar constants. This gives infinitely many distinct minimal degrees in one ideal family.

## 6. The first clause of Question 6.6 and zero-stage conventions

If admissible families need only be nonzero at infinitely many indices and the reduction allows only polynomially bounded oracle indices, the two-generator family in Section 3 has no minimal degree. For every admissible \(h\), choose positive indices

$$
n_1<s_1<n_2<s_2<\cdots
$$

in its nonzero support. Choose \(n_j\) large enough that the total number of coefficients in \(h_{s_1},\ldots,h_{s_{j-1}}\) is below \(2^{n_j-1}\), and choose \(s_j>n_j^j\). Define \(f_m=h_m\) for \(m\) among the \(s_j\) and \(f_m=0\) otherwise. It belongs to the ideal and has infinite support. It reduces to \(h\) at matching indices. If a polynomial-size reduction from \(h\) to \(f\) existed, then for \(j\) large it could not query any nonzero member of \(f\) at indices \(s_j,s_{j+1},\ldots\) while computing \(h_{n_j}\), since even \(s_j>n_j^j\). Previously retained stages carry fewer than \(2^{n_j-1}\) coefficients. Together with the polynomially many scalar constants these cannot generate the field of coefficients of \(h_{n_j}\), whose transcendence degree is at least \(2^{n_j}\). Thus \(f<_c h\).

This thinning argument uses zero stages and does not answer whether an ideal family can lack any minimal degree when every competing representative must be nonzero at all sufficiently large indices. The infinitely many minimal degrees established in Section 5 use this latter convention. Their proof for arbitrary-degree, eventually-nonzero representatives would need a different factor-recovery bound.
