# Optimal witness costs for two classes of pencil degenerations

Let \(k\) be a field, \(R=k[[t]]\), and \(K=k((t))\). An ordered degeneration from \((I,A)\) to \((I,B)\) uses invertible row and column maps over \(K\) and yields an integral pair reducing to \((I,B)\). A three-mode degeneration also allows an invertible \(2\times2\) label map over \(K\). For a matrix with Smith exponents \(a_1\le\cdots\le a_n\), its width is \(a_n-a_1\), its normalized profile is the sorted list of \(a_i-a_1\), and its reflected profile is the sorted list of \(a_n-a_i\). Its excess determinant valuation is \(\delta=\sum_i(a_i-a_1)\). A symmetric pole bound \(h\) restricts all exponents to \([-h,h]\), hence bounds poles of entries of the matrix and its inverse. A polynomial witness uses generically invertible polynomial factors and outputs \(t^a(I,B)+O(t^{a+1})\). Its approximation order is \(a\), and its error degree is the maximum output exponent minus \(a\). [Ganesh–Koiran–Oliveira, §§2 and 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10179).

## Ordered cyclic source

**Theorem 1.** Let \(A\) be cyclic and \(B\) have the same characteristic polynomial. At each monic irreducible \(p\), let \(d_p=\deg p\) and let \(\mu_{p,1}\ge\cdots\ge\mu_{p,b_p}>0\) be the target primary block lengths. Define \(\mathcal D\) to contain \(j-1\) with multiplicity \(d_p\mu_{p,j}\) for every \(p,j\), in ascending order. Then the ordered degeneration exists. The normalized and reflected profiles of both modes dominate \(\mathcal D\) entrywise, with the normalized bound attained for either chosen mode. The minimum excess determinant valuation in either mode is \(\sum_{p,j}d_p(j-1)\mu_{p,j}\). Both widths can attain the minimum \(w=\max_p(b_p-1)\) at once, with optimal joint symmetric pole bound \(\lceil w/2\rceil\). The minimum polynomial approximation order is \(w\), witnessed by factors of degree at most \(w\) yielding \(t^w(I,B+tE)\) with \(E\) constant.

Suppose \(PAP^{-1}\) is integral and reduces to \(B\). Put \(L_0=R^n\) and \(L=P^{-1}L_0\), stable under \(A\), with induced action \(B\) on \(L/tL\). Scale \(L\) so that its smallest Smith exponent relative to \(L_0\) is zero. Define filtrations
\[
 F_i=((L\cap t^iL_0)+tL)/tL,\quad
 U_i=((t^{-i}L\cap L_0)+tL_0)/tL_0,\qquad U_{-1}=0 .
\]
The coefficient map \(x\mapsto t^{-i}x\bmod tL_0\) on \(L\cap t^iL_0\) has image \(U_i\) and kernel \(L\cap t^{i+1}L_0\). On its intersection with \(tL\) it has image \(U_{i-1}\), by dividing by \(t\). At \(i=0\) that image is zero. Thus \(F_i/F_{i+1}\cong U_i/U_{i-1}\) as \(k[z]\)-modules. In Smith coordinates \(\dim_k((L/tL)/F_s)\) counts the lattice exponents less than \(s\).

Polynomial primary idempotents of \(A\) preserve both lattices and split the filtrations. In the \(p\)-primary component each \(U_i/U_{i-1}\) is a subquotient of a cyclic \(k[z]\)-module, hence cyclic. By lifting generators through \(s\) layers, the \(p\)-part of \((L/tL)/F_s\) is generated by at most \(s\) elements. For any \(s\)-generated quotient \(Q\) of the \(p\)-part of \(B\), each layer \(p^iQ/p^{i+1}Q\) has dimension at most \(s\) over \(k[z]/(p)\) and is a quotient of the corresponding layer of \(B\). Thus \(\dim_k Q\le d_p\sum_i\min(s,\#\{j: \mu_{p,j}>i\})=d_p\sum_{j\le s}\mu_{p,j}\). Summing over \(p\), the count of lattice exponents below \(s\) is at most the count of entries of \(\mathcal D\) below \(s\). This bounds the reflected profile of \(P\). Apply the argument to \(P^{-\mathsf T}A^{\mathsf T}P^{\mathsf T}\) to bound the normalized profile. For a two-sided witness the first slice \(PQ\) is invertible over \(R\), and its two ratios with the second slice are \(PAP^{-1}\) and \(Q^{-1}AQ\), so the bounds apply to both modes.

For construction use the digit basis \(z^i p^j\), \(0\le i<d_p\), \(0\le j<\ell_p\), of the source \(k[z]/(p^{\ell_p})\). Multiplication by \(z\) acts within each digit and carries only into the next digit, by the monic identity for \(p\). Cut the digits into target runs of lengths \(\mu_{p,j}\) and give run \(j\) weight \(j-1\). Conjugating by the diagonal of powers of \(t\) kills exactly the cross-run carries at \(t=0\) and multiplies them by \(t\). After constant changes of frame this yields \(PAP^{-1}=B+tE\). Its row profile is \(\mathcal D\). Reverse the run order within each primary and shift its weights to end at \(w\) to attain \(\mathcal D\) in the paired inverse instead. There is no assumption of separability. Centering the weights globally and reciprocally shifting the inverse gives symmetric pole bound \(\lceil w/2\rceil\), and width \(w\) precludes any smaller bound.

For polynomial witnesses write \(PQ=t^aW\) with \(W\in\operatorname{GL}_n(R)\). Then \(t^aP^{-1}=QW^{-1}\) is integral, so all exponents of \(P\) lie in \([0,a]\). Its ratio with the second slice has reduction \(B\), giving \(a\ge w\). Use the construction with row map \(P\) and column map \(t^wP^{-1}\) to attain the minimum.

## Split semisimple pencils with moving labels

**Theorem 2.** Let \(D\) be split diagonalizable with distinct eigenvalues \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k\) of multiplicities \(m_1\ge\cdots\ge m_k\), and let \(N\) have nilpotent type \(\mu_1\ge\cdots\ge\mu_\ell\). The three-mode degeneration from \((I,D)\) to \((I,N)\) exists exactly when
\[
 \sum_{i\le s}m_i\le \sum_j\min(s,\mu_j)\qquad(1\le s\le k).
 \tag{3}
\]
For a positive pair let \(\mathcal E\) be the sorted multiset union of \(\{0,\ldots,\mu_j-1\}\) over \(j\). Both row and column maps of every Laurent witness have normalized and reflected profiles dominating \(\mathcal E\) entrywise. Either chosen mode attains \(\mathcal E\) and its minimum excess determinant valuation \(\sum_j\binom{\mu_j}{2}\). The common minimum width is \(w=\mu_1-1\). The minimum polynomial approximation order is \(w\), attained by row and column factors of degrees at most \(w\), a label factor of degree at most one, and output \(t^w(I,N+tE)\) for constant \(E\). The minimum joint symmetric pole bound, now including the label map, is \(\max(1,\lceil w/2\rceil)\) for a strict comparison and zero for pairs in the same orbit.

After a constant change take \(D\) diagonal. For label matrix \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) the first and second mixed slices are \(X=aI+bD\) and \(Y=cI+dD\). Since \(PXQ\) is integral with limit \(I\), the matrix \(X\) is invertible over \(K\). The ratio \((PYQ)(PXQ)^{-1}=PZP^{-1}\) with \(Z=YX^{-1}\) is integral and reduces to \(N\). Its eigenvalues \(z_i=(c+d\lambda_i)/(a+b\lambda_i)\) have multiplicities \(m_i\), as the invertible label map induces an injective fractional-linear map on these inputs. Each \(z_i\) lies in \(R\) as a root in \(K\) of an integral monic characteristic polynomial, and lies in \(tR\) since that polynomial reduces to \(x^n\). For any \(s\) spectral labels, the product of the \(s\) matrices \(PZP^{-1}-z_iI\) has generic rank \(n-\sum m_i\) over those labels and specializes to \(N^s\). Rank cannot increase. Taking the largest \(s\) multiplicities proves (3).

In Smith coordinates \(P=U\,\operatorname{diag}(t^{a_i})V\), where \(U,V\) are invertible over \(R\). Subtract the smallest \(a_i\) from all exponents. Then the middle conjugation of \(VZV^{-1}\in tM_n(R)\) by the diagonal powers may have a surviving special entry only from a strictly higher weight to a lower one. In the special matrix, similar to \(N\), the span of weights \(<s\) is killed by its \(s\)-th power. Thus
\[
 \#\{i:a_i\ge s\}\ \ge\ \operatorname{rank}(N^s)=\sum_j\max(0,\mu_j-s),
\]
which is exactly domination of the normalized \(\mathcal E\). Transpose the conjugation to obtain the reflected bound. The opposite ratio \(Q^{-1}ZQ\) proves both bounds for \(Q\). No time-dependent frame was fixed in this argument.

For sufficiency, (3) is precisely the criterion for a zero-one matrix with row sums \(m_i\) and column sums \(\mu_j\). Use a bipartite flow with these end capacities and unit intermediate edges. For a set \(S\) of rows the total usable column capacity is \(\sum_j\min(|S|,\mu_j)\), so the max-flow min-cut inequalities are (3). An integral flow gives the assignment in polynomial time.

Group the source eigenbasis by assigned target blocks. In a block of size \(r\), let \(D_r\) be its diagonal of distinct values and \(J_r\) its nilpotent upper shift. The upper bidiagonal \(D_r+J_r\) is conjugate to \(D_r\) by a constant \(C_r\) because the eigenvalues are distinct. For \(S_r=\operatorname{diag}(1,t,\ldots,t^{r-1})\),
\[
 (S_r C_r)(tD_r)(S_r C_r)^{-1}=J_r+tD_r .
\]
Use label map \(\operatorname{diag}(1,t)\) and the direct sum of these row changes with its paired inverse for the column. This gives row profile \(\mathcal E\). Shifting each row block upward by \(w-(r-1)\) leaves the ratio unchanged and makes the column profile \(\mathcal E\). Both widths are \(w\). Centering the row weights globally and shifting the column reciprocally gives the stated pole bound. In a strict comparison the bound is positive since otherwise all three maps reduce to invertible maps and the pairs are equivalent.

For polynomial witnesses multiply the paired inverse by \(t^w\). Conversely the first slice of any polynomial witness of order \(u\) is \(PXQ=t^uW\) with \(W\in\operatorname{GL}_n(R)\) and \(X,P,Q\) integral. The identities \(t^uP^{-1}=XQW^{-1}\) and \(t^uQ^{-1}=W^{-1}PX\) give widths at most \(u\), so \(u\ge w\) by the profile bound.

## Scope and prior work

These results measure the cost of a single degeneration witness. The earlier Exact Degeneration Depth project computes maximal lengths of strict orbit chains. Classical pencil closure theory classifies allowable ordered transitions, and closures of matrix families with variable eigenvalues include coalescence. The profile estimates quantify the least cost against arbitrary Laurent frames in both the ordered cyclic and moving-label split semisimple cases. In both theorems a strict comparison has minimum error degree one. Zero error would imply equivalence over \(K\), ruled out by differing primary types in the ordered case and by the diagonalizable versus nonzero nilpotent distinction or the slice-span dimension in the moving-label case. The arguments are written proofs. Exact controls checked 1,818 small multiplicity pairs against exhaustive assignments, 14 rational bidiagonal conjugators, and 288 primary digit cuts. [Edelman–Elmroth–Kågström](https://math.mit.edu/~edelman/publications/geometric_approach.pdf) · [Futorny–Klymchuk–Sergeichuk–Shvai](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03213) · [Closures of certain matrix varieties and applications](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40879-023-00673-6).
