# Quantitative recovery of mixed tensor degenerations

Let \(k\) be a field, \(\mathcal O=k[[t]]\) and \(K=k((t))\). For a matrix \(M\) over \(K\), write \(v(M)\) for its least entry valuation, with \(v(0)=+\infty\). For an invertible map put
\[
 \ell(M)=\max(0,-v(M),-v(M^{-1})).
\]
The depth of a tuple is the maximum of its map depths. An \(\mathcal O\)-valued family with value \(y\) modulo \(t\) is a degeneration witness \(x\rightsquigarrow y\) when its generic value is obtained from \(x\) by the indicated tuple. Let \(b(x,y)\) be the minimum witness depth, or \(+\infty\) if there is no such witness over \(K\). This is a measure of a single witness.

Fix a finite mixed tensor signature, with named spaces and ordered words in their primal and dual occurrences. Independently invertible maps on the named spaces act at every occurrence, by inverse transpose on dual occurrences. A record has a coefficient array in each specified word. The dimensions are explicit and the coefficients belong to \(k\).

**Theorem.** The affine-linear top-relation construction from [Mixed tensor degeneration](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/MixedTensorDegenerations/DEGENERATION.md) gives one ordinary three-tensor \(\Phi(x)\) of polynomial side length. For each fixed signature there is an explicit integer \(c_\sigma\) such that, for all pairs of the same format,
\[
 \frac{b(x,y)}6\ \le\ b(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))\ \le\ c_\sigma b(x,y).
\]
Both implications work over the original field \(k\), on arcs in \(k((t))\). Given the mode maps and inverse maps of a tensor witness of depth at most \(b\), a source witness of depth at most \(6b\) can be reconstructed from a number of Laurent coefficients per entry linear in \(b+1\) at fixed signature. The output can be taken to consist of Laurent-polynomial matrices. The algorithm is polynomial in the written dimensions and \(b+1\) in the field-operation model.

In addition, a bound \(D\) on the absolute exponents of supplied Laurent-polynomial tensor mode maps implies depth at most \((2N-1)D\) when \(N\) is their maximum side. This converts bounds on polynomial supports to the symmetric convention. The result does not require a polynomial representation of the inverse as an input if it can be expanded from the supplied maps.

## 1. The labelled top relation

First assume all words are pure. Handle duals by adding one named copy \(V^\vee\) per needed dual and adding a tensor \(\sum_i e_i\otimes e_i^\vee\) to the signature. This pairing tensor has the same value on the two sides. Its role in recovering the mixed action is proved below. Discard any forced zero components and any names that then have no occurrences. Identity maps can be used on the unused spaces.

Write \(s\) for the number of pure records after this expansion, and \(d_0\) for the maximum of one and their word lengths. Use the top-relation algebra \(A(x)\) as follows. For each named space attach a leaf to a hub, put its vector space on the arrow out of the hub, and put one scalar arrow back. Add separate scalar cycles \(A,B,C\) through three more leaves. Keep paths of length at most \(\ell=2(d_0+s+1)\). Write \(t_i=A^iBA^{s-i}\), set \(p_0=C^{d_0}t_0\), and kill the line spanned by the top relation
\[
 p_0+\sum_{i=1}^s x_i C^{d_0-|w_i|}t_i.
\]
The term \(x_i\) is the linear combination of hub-to-hub paths for its word \(w_i\). The tag words are distinct. All terms have length \(\ell\), so the line is a two-sided ideal. Mark each original vertex by a private incoming arrow space of distinct dimension larger than the entire unmarked path count, and kill every positive continuation of a marker. The basis consists of vertices, markers, and core paths other than \(p_0\). The multiplication \(\mu_x\) is affine-linear in the payload. Use it as \(\Phi(x)\), viewed as a tensor for the bilinear action.

Let \(J\) be the ideal generated by all commutators. It is the path ideal, since arrows with distinct endpoints are commutators with their initial idempotents and the quotient by the path ideal is commutative. The dimensions of all its powers and of the arrow corners in \(J/J^2\) are fixed across the payloads. Choosing the marker widths intrinsically labels the ordered quotient idempotents. The top multiplication on the hub path space, defined from arrow classes in \(J/J^2\), has precisely the displayed relation line as kernel. Changing any arrow lift adds a term in \(J^{\ell+1}=0\). If a unital algebra isomorphism acts by \(H_j\) on a named outgoing arrow space and by \(\rho_j\) on its return line, and the full scalar on cycle \(C\) is \(\gamma\), the normalized map on the source name is \((\rho_j/\gamma)H_j\). In fact the reference term acquires the factor \(\gamma^{d_0}a^s b_0\), where \(a,b_0\) are the full scalars on \(A,B\). Each data tag acquires \(a^s b_0\), and its extra \(C\) power accounts for the required division by \(\gamma\) for every occurrence. This calculation uses only the induced maps on \(J/J^2\). In particular it requires no correction by a Laurent conjugator of the actual idempotents.

A pure source transporter extends by using its named maps on outgoing arrows and the identity on the other arrows and vertices. On every path component this acts by a tensor product of at most \(\ell\) maps. The reference path is fixed, and paths of other tags cannot enter its basis line under this extension. The inverse follows the same path formula. Each extended algebra map and its inverse therefore have valuation at least \(-\ell\) times the source depth. For a mixed witness put its inverse transposes on the additional dual copies. It preserves the pairing payloads exactly. The result proves the upper bound with \(c_\sigma=\ell\).

## 2. Recover a unital algebra using integral changes of basis

Write the three tensor mode maps in the bilinear convention
\[
 \nu(Fx,Gy)=H\mu_x(x,y).
\]
This convention differs from the all-covariant convention by inverse transpose on the first two factors and preserves the symmetric depth. Let \(\nu\) be integral with special value \(\mu_y\). Choose \(e\) equal to the written unit of \(A(y)\), and in \(\nu\) let \(R_e(z)=\nu(z,e)\), \(L_e(z)=\nu(e,z)\). Both reduce to the identity, so they and their inverses are integral. Put
\[
 \nu^\natural(z,w)=\nu(R_e^{-1}z,L_e^{-1}w).
\]
This change replaces \(F,G,H\) by \(R_e F,L_e G,H\), preserving their depth bounds. The product is unital associative. Over \(K\), write \(e=F(a)=G(b_0)\) using the original maps. The equations \(R_eF=H R_{b_0}\), \(L_eG=H L_a\) give invertible right and left multiplications in the source algebra, so \(a,b_0\) are units. Transporting the new product by \(H\) gives \(u b_0^{-1}a^{-1}v\), which is associative with unit \(a b_0\). The new product has unit \(\nu(e,e)\) and all these identities hold over the integral ring as well. Its special value is still \(\mu_y\).

Now use \(F,G,H\) for the maps into the unital product. With \(a=F(1)\) and \(b_0=G(1)\) in the target algebra, evaluation at one gives \(H=L_aG=R_{b_0}F\). Both multipliers are units. The algebra isomorphism and its inverse are
\[
  \varphi=G H^{-1} F,\qquad \varphi^{-1}=F^{-1} H G^{-1}.
\]
Indeed \(\varphi=L_a^{-1}F\), so \(F=L_a\varphi\), \(G=R_{b_0}\varphi\), and \(H=L_aR_{b_0}\varphi\). The multiplication identity for \(\varphi\) follows by cancellation. Thus both \(\varphi\) and \(\varphi^{-1}\) have valuation at least \(-3b\). This bound also applies after any integral change of target frame with integral inverse. The passage between multiplication tensors and isotopies uses the classical unital multiplication observation of Bläser and Lysikov. [Degeneration of tensors and algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04253)

## 3. Integral recovery from the radical filtration

Use \(J\) for the commutator ideal of the recovered integral product. Its powers are computed as images of explicit polynomial maps. For example \(J\) is spanned by \(r[a,b]s\) on basis vectors, using the unit for the two possibly absent factors. The generic and special ranks of each power agree with those of the top-relation algebra. Hence all \(J^i\) are free direct summands. Over \(\mathcal O\), choose a minor of full rank modulo \(t\). Row and column elimination using that unit minor puts the map in a block with an identity of that rank. The remaining block is zero by the generic rank. Successive quotients are free too, and the bases can be chosen to reduce to the written bases at \(y\).

The commutative quotient by \(J\) is split. To compute its ordered idempotents, lift each coordinate idempotent from the special quotient and iterate
\[
 z\longmapsto z-(2z-1)^{-1}(z^2-z).
\]
The error squares and the inverse exists at each step. This converges in the complete integral ring. Lifts in the commutative quotient are unique, so the lifted idempotents still sum to one and are orthogonal. Next lift the idempotents to the algebra itself, using the same idempotent iteration in successive complementary corners and the nilpotence of \(J\). The initial representatives can be chosen to reduce to the actual written idempotents at \(y\), by adjusting inside \(J\). These are integral choices and require no extension of the coefficient field. Labeling by the fixed marker widths ensures that the generic \(\varphi\) matches their residues with those of \(A(x)\).

In the second graded layer \(J^2/J^3\), each named cycle space is the image of multiplication of the two degree-one corners through its own labelled leaf. It is spanned by the single excursion along the named arrow and back. The \(C\) cycle is a line of the same kind. There is no relation in degree two, since the relation length \(\ell\) is larger than two. Choose frames by multiplying paired arrow representatives. Each indicated cycle space is a direct summand over \(\mathcal O\). One can extend the chosen product vectors to an integral basis because their reductions are independent and their generic ranks equal the same dimensions. The induced map on each cycle space is a subquotient of the *same* actual \(\varphi\). In integral frames, both its matrix and its inverse have entry valuation at least \(-3b\). This follows by using lifts and quotient projections in adapted integral bases. The map \(M_j\) on the named cycle space is exactly \(\rho_j H_j\), and the map on the \(C\) cycle is exactly \(\gamma\). It is important to read these products from their degree-two spaces, since estimating the separate arrow maps can lose a factor.

Similarly, choose integral arrow frames for the top multiplication. Solve for its sole relation with \(p_0\) coefficient one, using the unit pivot on the other path components. At \(t=0\) it is the relation of \(y\). Over \(K\) the relation is transported from \(x\) by the induced maps on the degree-one arrows. The scalar-tag computation of Section 1 shows that the resulting record is \(g(t)\cdot x\) for
\[
       g_j(t)=\gamma(t)^{-1}M_j(t).
\]
The integral relation coefficients give regularity and limit \(y\). Both \(\gamma\) and its inverse were read from a single line and have valuation at least \(-3b\), hence both \(g_j(t)\) and its inverse have valuation at least \(-6b\).

For an original dual copy, \(\Gamma_j(t)=g_j(t)(g_j^\vee(t))^{\mathsf T}\) is its recovered pairing payload. It is integral and reduces to the identity. Applying \(\Gamma_j(t)^{-\mathsf T}\) to that copy turns its map into \(g_j(t)^{-\mathsf T}\). This changes the regular record by a basis change congruent to the identity and retains its special value. The primal maps \(g_j(t)\) are the desired witnesses. This proves the lower depth bound over the original field.

## 4. Finite precision and actual Laurent polynomials

Write \(d\) for the maximum of one and the original word lengths. Set \(B=6b\) and \(M=(d+1)B\). Truncate each recovered primal map at exponent \(M\), keeping exponents from \(-B\) to \(M\). Write \(\widetilde g=g+E\), with \(v(E)\ge M+1\). Then \(g^{-1}E\in t\,\operatorname{Mat}(\mathcal O)\), so \(\widetilde g\) is invertible and retains the inverse bound. The inverse difference satisfies
\[
 v(\widetilde g^{-1}-g^{-1})\ \ge\ M+1-2B
\]
by the inverse-difference identity. For each word, expand the difference of its two actions by a telescoping replacement of slots. The other factors cost at most \((d-1)B\), and the primal perturbation is at least as deep as the inverse perturbation. Every term therefore vanishes modulo \(t\). This proves that the emitted Laurent-polynomial matrices, with their actual inverse transposes on dual slots, have the same limit.

The reconstruction can use only finite input jets. For example let \(L=M+16b+3\) and supply the tensor maps and inverse maps through absolute exponent \(L+2b\). The product tensor is determined modulo \(t^L\), since each product of three factors loses at most \(2b\) when one factor is perturbed. Normalization, integral elimination, the two idempotent lifts, and the path frames use unit inversions and lose no integral precision. The algebra isomorphism \(G H^{-1}F\) with its integral normalization, and each of the projected degree-two maps, are determined modulo \(t^{L-3b}\). Each occurrence of an integral frame error is multiplied by Laurent factors of total pole bound at most \(3b\). A numerical error in \(\gamma^{-1}\) can lose a further \(6b\) by the scalar inverse identity, and multiplication by \(M_j\) loses at most \(3b\). Thus each resulting \(g_j\) is determined modulo \(t^{L-12b}\), beyond the last retained exponent. All Newton computations can stop at the indicated finite precision. At fixed signature, enumeration of paths, linear elimination, products of integral jets, and unit inversion are polynomial in the written dimensions and \(b+1\). This is an arithmetic time bound in the explicit jet model.

Finally, if the input matrices are Laurent polynomials with support in \([-D,D]\), their nonzero determinants are Laurent polynomials supported in \([-ND,ND]\) for side at most \(N\). Cofactors have valuation at least \(-(N-1)D\), so the entries of the inverse have valuation at least \(-(2N-1)D\). This gives the claimed conversion. The audit companion covers the group identities, depth estimates and integer recurrence used below. The geometric and precision theorems are written proofs.

## 5. A fixed mixed signature already forces exponential witness size

Here is a direct application of quantitative recovery. Take a field \(k\) with at least \(m+1\) distinct elements, put \(V=k^{m+1}\), and choose a diagonal endomorphism \(D_0\) with distinct \(k\)-eigenvalues in the given basis \(e_0,\ldots,e_m\). The source mixed record has three slots
\[
 \left(D_0,\ e_1\otimes e_0^*,\
 \sum_{i=1}^{m-1}e_{i+1}\otimes e_0\otimes e_i^*\otimes e_i^*\right),
\]
and the target keeps \(D_0\) and replaces the other two slots by zero. Use one common \(\mathrm{GL}(V)\) action on all occurrences. A degeneration exists by the diagonal map with weights \(w_0=0\), \(w_i=2^i-1\) before a common integral shift.

For an arbitrary Laurent witness let \(D(t)=g(t)D_0g(t)^{-1}\). It is integral and reduces to \(D_0\). Its rank-one spectral projectors are given by interpolation at the distinct constant eigenvalues. Applying the \(i\)-th projector to \(e_i\) gives an integral eigenvector reducing to \(e_i\). The matrix of those vectors is an integral change of frame with integral inverse and reduces to the identity. After dividing it off, the witness is diagonal and the other target limits are still zero. Thus the valuations of the diagonal coefficients obey
\[
 w_1-w_0\ge 1,\qquad
 w_{i+1}+w_0-2w_i\ge1 \quad (1\le i<m).
\]
The displayed tensor terms are different coordinates, so these inequalities require each coefficient to vanish separately. They force \(w_m-w_0\ge2^m-1\). A common integral shift of the displayed explicit weights attains the minimum symmetric depth \(2^{m-1}\). This construction is related to the weight recurrence of the earlier [Kempf examples](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/KempfDegenerationDegree), with the spectral anchor enforcing the recurrence for all Laurent maps. The quantitative transfer yields ordinary three-tensor pairs with witness depth at least \(2^{m-1}/6\) and side polynomial in \(m\). The separate sparse construction proves a bound exponential in the tensor side itself.

## 6. Quantitative hyperbolic padding

The previous [symplectic orbit-closure result](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/SymplecticTensorFusions/OrbitClosures.md) gives a qualitative retraction in the large-to-small direction. Its polynomial perturbation of a matrix by a scalar identity also gives a quantitative statement.

For ordinary tensors of maximum side \(n\), embed into the first halves of three hyperbolic spaces. An ordinary Laurent witness lifts to a symplectic witness of the same symmetric depth by \(A\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(A,A^{-\mathsf T})\). Conversely suppose a symplectic Laurent witness between the pads has symmetric depth at most \(b\). Its upper-left blocks \(B_i\) give a possibly singular calculation with integral output and the desired limit. Each \(B_i\) has forward entry pole at most \(b\). Replacing each \(B_i\) by \(B_i+t^{a_i}I\) with \(a_i\ge2b+1\) keeps the limit, since a term containing a changed slot loses at most \(2b\) from the other two factors.

For a block of side \(r\), let \(f(u)=\det(B+uI)\). It is monic of degree \(r\). Put \(x_j=t^{2b+1+j}\) for \(0\le j\le r\). Lagrange interpolation gives its leading coefficient as
\[
 1=\sum_{j=0}^r \frac{f(x_j)}{\prod_{i\ne j}(x_j-x_i)}.
\]
Every denominator has valuation at most \(r(2b+1+r)\), and some summand has valuation at most zero. Choose that \(j\). The perturbed block is invertible and its determinant valuation is at most \(r(2b+1+r)\). Its cofactors have valuation at least \(-(r-1)b\). The three resulting maps therefore give an ordinary witness of depth at most \(n(2b+1+n)+(n-1)b\). They retain forward entry pole at most \(b\). In particular an exponential lower bound on the maximum forward pole for sparse ordinary tensors also holds for their symplectic pads. The interpolation points are Laurent monomials in the same parameter, so this argument uses no lower bound on field size.
