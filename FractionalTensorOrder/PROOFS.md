# Fractional-Exponent Tensor Order Reduction

These are informal proofs about ordinary tensor isomorphism with ordered modes. The input is a dense order-$d$ tensor, with $d\geq 4$ and mode dimensions $n\geq 2$, unless otherwise specified. Let $N=n^d$. An objectwise reduction computes a three-tensor from each input separately and preserves and reflects isomorphism. Output size below means the sum of the three mode dimensions. A fixed output format can be used on each stated promise by padding to its public dimension bound.

Grochow and Qiao ask for an all-input polynomial-time reduction of size $\operatorname{poly}(d)n^{(d/3)(1+o(1))}$ in Question 7.8 of the published paper. The existing MathProofs reduction has an absolute $O(n^{\lceil d/3\rceil})$ size bound. For fixed $d$ with $3\nmid d$, the exponent gap persists as $n$ grows. Theorems 1–3 give polynomial-time improvements on specified classes. Theorem 4 identifies the cost of requiring a single transformation lift. The all-input polynomial-time size bound remains open in this note.

## Reused compiler

We use the linear-dimension mixed tensor compiler underlying the existing balanced reduction. Its input is a labelled system of vector spaces and tensors, each with at most three slots, with primal and dual occurrences allowed. It uses one common change of basis for each named space, including across occurrences. It preserves and reflects isomorphism for a fixed system format. Its output dimension is at most an absolute constant times the sum of the named space dimensions, the sum of occurrence dimensions, and the number of labels and components, plus one. Its dense arrays are constructed in polynomial time. All uses below involve bounded arity. An endomorphism uses one primal and one dual occurrence, and a vector uses one primal occurrence. Unused slots can be filled by marked scalar lines, with fixed unit vectors enforcing their basis.

## 1. Compression by intrinsic cut supports

**Theorem 1.** Over any field, for nonzero $T$, define $r_J(T)$ to be its flattening rank across $J\mid [d]\setminus J$. Let
$$
 B(T)=\min_{\mathcal F}\ \sum_{J\in\mathcal F}r_J(T),
$$
where $\mathcal F$ ranges over forests of three ordered full binary trees whose leaf sets partition the ordered input modes. A node $J$ denotes the set of leaves under that node. There is a deterministic objectwise reduction of size $O(\operatorname{poly}(d)B(T))$, in polynomially many field operations. The bound before format marking is $O(B(T))$. For $d=4$ the full bound is
$$
 O\bigl(n+\min_{J : |J|=2} r_J(T)\bigr).
$$

**Proof.** Write $E_J$ for the image of the flattening $(\bigotimes_{i\notin J}V_i)^*\longrightarrow\bigotimes_{i\in J}V_i$. This is the smallest subspace of the $J$-factor supporting $T$. For disjoint $A,B$, membership of $T$ in both $E_A\otimes V_B\otimes V_{\mathrm{rest}}$ and $V_A\otimes E_B\otimes V_{\mathrm{rest}}$ puts it in $E_A\otimes E_B\otimes V_{\mathrm{rest}}$, by choosing complements to the supports. Thus $E_{A\cup B}\subseteq E_A\otimes E_B$. The same intersection argument puts $T$ in the tensor product of the three root spaces.

Compute bases by Gaussian elimination. Encode every inclusion $E_{A\cup B}\to E_A\otimes E_B$ as a mixed tensor and encode the root tensor in the three root spaces. A source isomorphism restricts to every intrinsic support. Conversely, each inclusion forces the change of basis on its image to be the restriction of the product of the two child maps. Starting at the leaves, extend their support maps to ambient isomorphisms of the original modes. Induction up the forest and then equality of the root tensor show that the extended maps carry the original $T$ to the other input. Each node occurs at most twice in this system. Since all the ranks are positive, the reused compiler costs $O(B(T))$ before format marking.

The ordered minimizer is chosen with a fixed tie rule on subsets, so isomorphic tensors select the same forest. Compute all flattenings and the minimum subtree cost for each subset by trying each split into two nonempty subsets. Minimize the sum over ordered partitions into three nonempty roots. There are $O(3^d)$ split and partition choices up to a polynomial factor in $d$, and $3^d\leq N^{\log_2 3}$. Format marking below ensures that outputs across differing chosen formats cannot be isomorphic. The marked descriptor consists of the chosen forest and its node ranks. Ambient dimensions are public for the equal-shape problem. Use a separate zero marker, or the zero three-tensor in a fixed output format.

For $d=4$, each forest has one two-leaf root and two one-leaf roots. Its cost is the sum of the four singleton ranks plus the chosen pair rank. For general $d=3k+r$, use root blocks of sizes $k$ and $k+1$ with $r$ oversized roots. In each block use a comb. The proper subnodes of a block of size $k+1$ cost $O(n^k)$, since the internal dimensions sum geometrically and the leaf cost is $O(kn)$. Therefore, if the oversized roots have ranks $O(n^{d/3})$, the reduction achieves $O(\operatorname{poly}(d)n^{d/3})$. The minimization allows other forests as well. ∎

## 2. Marking formats and aggregating intrinsic direct sums

Here is a common construction for the variable-format issue and for aggregating many blocks. For a three-tensor $X$, record its three original dimensions in the descriptor and restrict $X$ to its intrinsic singleton supports. Interpret that concise tensor as a bilinear map $U\times V\to W$ with zero left and right kernels and surjective linearization, dualizing the first two supports. Form the associative unital algebra with radical $J_X=U\oplus V\oplus W$ and this as its only nonzero radical product. In the zero case use the zero radical. Then $J_X^2=W$. On $J_X/J_X^2$, the left annihilator of the descended bilinear product is $V$, and the right annihilator is $U$. The resulting product $U\times V\to W$ recovers $X$ up to isomorphism. Equal ambient dimensions allow an isomorphism of the concise tensors to extend.

For a fixed-length list of nonnegative descriptor integers $a_j$, choose distinct lengths $\ell_j\geq 4$. Append $a_j$ copies of the nilpotent algebra $(x)\subseteq F[x]/(x^{\ell_j})$ to the radical with zero cross-products, and give the entire algebra a common unit. Bits of a forest descriptor can be included as integers 0 or 1. Let $J$ denote the resulting radical. For $k\geq3$,
$$
 \dim J^k=\sum_j a_j\max(\ell_j-k,0).
$$
Second differences recover all descriptor entries. Put $H=\{h\in J:hJ^2=J^2h=0\}$. Each chain contributes to $H$ only its last two powers, contained in $J^2$, whose products are zero. Thus $H/(H\cap J^2)$ and $H^2$, with the induced bilinear product, recover $J_X/J_X^2$, $W$, and the product above. Conversely any isomorphism of $X$ with equal descriptor gives an algebra isomorphism. The algebra dimension is linear in the tensor dimensions and in $\sum_j a_j$ for fixed descriptor length. For length polynomial in $d$, the cost has a polynomial factor in $d$. In Theorem 1, the compiler format and its dimensions are determined by the chosen forest and ranks, and the marking costs $O(\operatorname{poly}(d)B(T))$.

The ordinary three-tensor of unital algebra multiplication exactly reflects algebra isomorphism. Indeed, suppose invertible maps satisfy $C(xy)=A(x)B(y)$ between two such algebras. Put $a=A(1)$ and $b=B(1)$. Since left multiplication by $a$ and right multiplication by $b$ are bijective, both are units. Then $C(xy)=C(x)(ab)^{-1}C(y)$, and $x\mapsto C(x)(ab)^{-1}$ is an algebra isomorphism. The converse uses the same algebra isomorphism in all three roles. Unital multiplication is concise, so zero padding these tensors to a common upper bound preserves and reflects isomorphism. Products of marked local algebras are isomorphic exactly when their factors match up to permutation and isomorphism, since their central primitive idempotents are intrinsic and each local factor has just one. This aggregates an unordered collection at linear cost in the sum of the marked algebra dimensions.

**Theorem 2.** Over a fixed finite field, consider the intrinsic centroid blocks of the concise restriction of a nonzero input. Write $m_b$ for the largest mode dimension of block $b$, $M=\max_b m_b$, and $s=\lceil d/3\rceil$. There is a deterministic polynomial-time objectwise reduction of size
$$
 O\Bigl(\operatorname{poly}(d)\sum_b m_b^s\Bigr)
 \ \subseteq\ O\bigl(\operatorname{poly}(d)nM^{s-1}\bigr).
$$
Thus $M^{s-1}\leq n^{d/3-1}$ suffices. The bound can also use the sum of the support-forest costs of the blocks, with the format-marking overhead.

**Proof.** On the concise restriction compute the centroid by linear equations. Its elements are tuples of endomorphisms with equal action on the tensor in every slot. For two centroid elements $A,B$, transferring actions between distinct slots shows $(AB)_iT=(BA)_jT$ for $i\ne j$. Using a third slot identifies $(AB)_iT$ and $(BA)_iT$. Conciseness makes the map from an endomorphism in slot $i$ to its action on $T$ injective, so the centroid is a commutative algebra under componentwise multiplication.

A finite commutative algebra over $\mathbb F_q$ splits as a product of local algebras. Its $q$-Frobenius-fixed subalgebra is one copy of $\mathbb F_q$ per local factor. In a local factor the fixed residue is in $\mathbb F_q$; after subtracting it, a nilpotent element fixed by Frobenius is zero, by iteration. Compute the fixed algebra by a linear solve and split its primitive idempotents by the projectors $1-(a-\lambda)^{q-1}$ for basis elements $a$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb F_q$. Each resulting idempotent splits every mode. Equality of its mode actions eliminates tensor entries across differing labels, leaving a direct sum of intrinsic blocks. An isomorphism permutes these blocks and restricts to block isomorphisms. Conversely, blockwise isomorphisms assemble, and the resulting maps on concise supports extend to the fixed original dimensions.

Apply the existing balanced reduction separately to each block and mark its ordered block format and compiled format. Alternatively use Theorem 1 and mark its forest and ranks. The format marking ensures that comparison of the local algebras implements the correct block comparison even when output dimensions coincide for different block formats. Aggregate by taking their product and then its multiplication tensor. The balanced cost is as stated because $\sum_b m_b\leq dn$, and the original shape is fixed. With a prescribed bound on the promise, pad the product tensor to common dimensions. ∎

## 3. A nonlinear reduction using spectral canonization

**Theorem 3.** Fix a finite field $\mathbb F_q$. Suppose each mode has a polynomial-time computable endomorphism $A_i(T)$ which is conjugated by the corresponding source change of basis. Suppose its characteristic polynomial $f_i$ is squarefree. Identify the mode with $K_i=\mathbb F_q[z]/(f_i)$ by a cyclic basis. In these coordinates suppose $t$, the tensor $T$ viewed in the commutative algebra $K=\bigotimes_i K_i$, is a unit. These hypotheses give a deterministic polynomial-time objectwise reduction to ordinary three-tensors of size $O(N^{1/3})$ on the promised class. It requires only the input tensor and the stated covariant algorithms.

**Proof.** Each squarefree characteristic polynomial makes the operator cyclic, and a cyclic generator can be computed by taking one nonzero component in each irreducible primary summand and adding them. A change of cyclic generator is multiplication by a unit in $K_i$. For equal $f_i$, any source isomorphism must conjugate the covariants, so it acts in the cyclic coordinates by multiplication by $\prod_i a_i$ for $a_i\in K_i^\times$, with each factor embedded in $K$. Every such multiplication realizing equality is a source isomorphism. Unit membership is invariant and is checked by solving a multiplication equation in $K$.

Factor each $f_i$ over the fixed field, thereby writing $K_i$ as a product of explicit fields in a deterministic order. Let $\sigma_i$ act by $q$-Frobenius on $K_i$ alone. Put $\delta_i=\sigma_i(t)/t$. View $K$ as a free $K_i$-module with the fixed product basis of the other modes. In each field component of $K_i$, take the first nonzero coefficient of $\delta_i$; together these define $c_i\in K_i^\times$. Each component has a nonzero coefficient because $\delta_i$ is a unit. Under multiplication of $t$ by local units, both $\delta_i$ and $c_i$ multiply by $\sigma_i(a_i)/a_i$. Thus $\eta_i=\delta_i/c_i$ and the componentwise norms of $c_i$ to $\mathbb F_q$ are invariant.

For each component field $L$, fix a deterministic section of its norm, as constructed below. Replace $c_i$ by the section value $c_i'$ of its componentwise norm. Solve $\sigma_i(b_i)=(c_i'/c_i)b_i$ in each component with a nonzero solution, using linear algebra over $\mathbb F_q$. Such a solution exists because in a finite field the image of $b\mapsto b^{q-1}$ is the norm-one subgroup, by counting in its cyclic multiplicative group. Then $y=t\prod_i b_i$ has $\sigma_i(y)/y=c_i'\eta_i$ for all $i$.

For another input in the same orbit, the corresponding $y'$ differs from $y$ by local units $z_i$. Equality of all these normalized ratios forces each $z_i$ to be Frobenius-fixed. It is therefore an independent base-field scalar on each component of $K_i$. Decompose $K$ into cells $K_\alpha$ indexed by tuples of component labels. A cell need not be a field, but its component of $y$ is a unit. Take the first nonzero base-field coefficient $v_\alpha$ of $y_\alpha$ in the fixed product basis. Fix a reference tuple $0$ and write $0[\alpha_i]$ for that tuple with its $i$-th label replaced. Define
$$
 C(t)_\alpha=y_\alpha\frac{v_0^{d-1}}{\prod_{i=1}^d v_{0[\alpha_i]}}.
$$
The multiplier is a product of local scalars, so the representative remains in the orbit. For any residual local scaling $\prod_i z_{i,\alpha_i}$, the denominator scales by $(\prod_i z_{i,0})^{d-1}\prod_i z_{i,\alpha_i}$, which exactly cancels the numerator scaling. Thus $(f_i,C(t))$ is canonical and separates the original orbits.

For the norm section, let $L=\mathbb F_{q^e}$. For each prime $\ell\mid q-1$, put $D_\ell=\ell^{v_\ell(q^e-1)}$. The elementary lifting-the-exponent formula gives $D_\ell=O_q(e)$. For odd $\ell$, the valuation is $v_\ell(q-1)+v_\ell(e)$. For $\ell=2$ and $q$ odd it is $v_2(q-1)$ when $e$ is odd, and $v_2(q-1)+v_2(q+1)+v_2(e)-1$ when $e$ is even. These formulas follow by binomial expansion on raising to $\ell$, using $(q^2-1)=(q-1)(q+1)$ for the first doubling. Compute the roots of $X^{D_\ell}-1$ in $L$ by splitting $L[X]/(X^{D_\ell}-1)$ as an algebra over its prime field. All roots are in $L$, and the polynomial is squarefree. The prime-field Frobenius fixed-space and idempotent splitter from Theorem 2 apply, revealing the individual roots. Select a root of order $D_\ell$ by checking that its $D_\ell/\ell$ power differs from 1. Its norm generates the $\ell$-primary subgroup of $\mathbb F_q^\times$, because the norm exponent is $(q^e-1)/(q-1)$. Products of powers of these norms reach every target in the base field. A deterministic search of constant size for fixed $q$ selects the required product of powers of the corresponding roots. This also explains polynomial-time squarefree factorization of the $f_i$ by fixed-space splitting. No multiplicative group of size $q^e-1$ is enumerated.

All the ambient algebra dimensions are polynomial in the dense input. The invariant string has $N+O(\sum_i n_i)$ field entries. Pack it into the coefficient array of a cube of side $\lceil(N+O(\sum_i n_i))^{1/3}\rceil$, using fixed lengths for a fixed input format. In each of the three named spaces also encode a fixed nilpotent shift and its first cyclic basis vector. The only map commuting with the shift and fixing that vector is the identity. The mixed compiler therefore converts equality of the string to ordinary three-tensor isomorphism at linear cost in the cube side. Since all input dimensions are at least two, their sum is at most their product for $d\geq 2$. ∎

### Computable covariants at orders four and five

For order four, require the $13\mid24$ flattening $M$ to be invertible. Relabel its inverse as a four-way contravariant tensor $S$, so $S_{abcd}=(M^{-1})_{(b,d),(a,c)}$. Write $P$ and $Q$ for the $12\mid34$ flattenings of $T$ and $S$. The matrices $PQ^{\mathsf T}$ and $P^{\mathsf T}Q$ transform by conjugation on the first and second mode pairs respectively. The partial traces of their squares give the four mode covariants. These identities follow from $P\mapsto G_{12}PG_{34}^{\mathsf T}$ and $Q\mapsto G_{12}^{-\mathsf T}QG_{34}^{-1}$.

For an order-five construction, on $W=\bigotimes_i V_i$ form the matrix $B(T):W^*\to W$ with entries
$$
 B(T)_{xy}=\sum_{S\subseteq\{2,3,4,5\}}
             T_{x_S y_{\bar S}}\,T_{y_S x_{\bar S}} .
$$
Each summand permutes matching modes of the two copies in $T\otimes T$, hence $B(gT)=G B(T)G^{\mathsf T}$ for $G=\bigotimes_i g_i$. Where invertible, $U=B(T)^{-1}T$ transforms contravariantly. Contract $T\otimes U$ over the complement of each of the pairs $12,34,15$ to obtain endomorphisms $E_{12},E_{34},E_{15}$. Use the partial traces of their squares to obtain the five covariants, using pair $15$ only for mode 5. This is polynomial time. The unit check in either construction can be done before choosing cyclic coordinates by checking that the $N$ vectors $(\bigotimes_i A_i^{j_i})T$, $0\leq j_i<n_i$, are linearly independent. This tests invertibility of multiplication by $t$ in a basis.

The [exact checker](check_examples.py) verifies order-four examples with all three pair flattenings invertible over $\mathbb F_2$, $\mathbb F_3$, and $\mathbb F_{101}$. It also verifies order-five examples over $\mathbb F_{101}$ at side dimensions 2 and 3. The examples establish nonemptiness at the tested dimensions.

## 4. Exact finite-field obstruction for a fixed group lift

**Theorem 4.** Let $\Phi$ be any map on order-$d$ tensors over $\mathbb F_q$ of equal side $n\geq2$, into a fixed three-tensor format $W_1\otimes W_2\otimes W_3$. Suppose there is one group homomorphism from the source product of general linear groups to the target product giving equivariance for every input. Suppose $\Phi$ is injective within each source orbit. Then
$$
 \max_j\dim W_j\ \geq\ n^{\lceil d/3\rceil}.
$$
In particular, a lift with a left-inverse recovery of source transporters has this obstruction. The theorem assumes neither a linear map nor a polynomial map.

**Proof for $q>2$.** Restrict to the product diagonal torus $D$ of the source. For $x=e_1^{\otimes d}$, write $\chi$ for the product of the first coordinate characters, one from each source mode. Its stabilizer in $D$ is $H=\ker\chi$. Over an algebraic closure of the base field, decompose the target into torus weight spaces. The image $\Phi(x)$ is $H$-fixed but some element of $D$ moves it, by within-orbit injectivity. Thus one nontrivial weight $\chi^k$ occurs. It is a product of three weights, one of each target mode. On each source mode its character has exponent tuple $(k,0,\ldots,0)$, which is nonconstant. At least one of the three target weights is nonconstant there, since a sum of constant tuples is constant. Assign each source mode to one such target weight. One weight receives at least $\lceil d/3\rceil$ modes. Independent coordinate permutations in the source normalize $D$, so all conjugates of that weight occur in that target mode. A nonconstant tuple has at least $n$ permutations. Taking independent permutations gives the bound.

**Proof for $q=2$.** In each source mode decompose into $r=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ two-dimensional blocks and possibly a spare line. Use a $C_3$ on each block acting by multiplication by a primitive element of $\mathbb F_4$. Take $x=\bigotimes_i u_i+\bigotimes_i u_i^{(2)}$, where $u_i$ is an eigenvector of the first block and $u_i^{(2)}$ its Frobenius conjugate. The vectors are chosen over $\mathbb F_4$, and $x$ is rational over $\mathbb F_2$. The two summands are independent and have inverse characters $\chi,\chi^{-1}$, where $\chi$ takes one nonzero block character from every source mode. Again $H=\ker\chi$ fixes the image, and some weight $\chi$ or $\chi^{-1}$ occurs. Decompose it across the three target weights and assign source modes as above, now using nonzero block characters. Block permutations and inversions lie in the normalizer. A weight charged on $t\geq1$ blocks has $2^t\binom rt\geq2r$ conjugates, proving the result for even $n$.

For odd $n$, take a simple composition factor of the charged target mode containing the selected weight, possible because torus restriction is semisimple. Over an algebraically closed field, a simple representation of a product of finite groups is a tensor product of simple representations of the factors. Indeed the radical of each group algebra acts trivially on a simple representation of the product, and the tensor product of the semisimple quotients is a product of full matrix algebras. Each charged source factor needs dimension at least $n$, as follows. If its dimension were at most $2r$, the $2r$-or-larger normalizer orbit would exhaust all dimensions in one-dimensional weight spaces. Choose a weight charged on the last block and fix its character on the first $r-1$ blocks. The resulting joint eigenspace has dimension at most two, since every weight in the orbit has the same number of charged blocks. The subgroup $\mathrm{GL}_3(2)$ on the last block and spare line acts on this eigenspace and acts nontrivially on it because the last block is charged. The group $\mathrm{GL}_3(2)$ is simple, so this representation is faithful. Its order-seven subgroup from $\mathbb F_8^\times$ then has a nontrivial weight. Frobenius on $\mathbb F_8$ normalizes this subgroup and gives an orbit of length three on every nontrivial weight, a contradiction. Taking the product of the charged factor dimensions gives the claimed $n^{\lceil d/3\rceil}$. ∎

For the transporter claim, if $\Phi(gx)=\Phi(x)$, the lift of $g$ is a target stabilizer of $\Phi(x)$. A left-inverse recovery sends that lift back to $g$, which must stabilize $x$. In contrast, the spectral canonizer deliberately identifies all representatives of an orbit.

### Counting and scope

Over a fixed finite field, source orbit count is at least $q^{n^d-dn^2}$. For target maximum side $m$, at most $(m+1)^3 q^{m^3}$ outputs are available, giving $m\geq (1-o(1))n^{d/3}$ for fixed $d\geq4$. With exponential time one can attain $O(n^{d/3})$ on all inputs by enumerating the source orbit, taking its least coefficient string, and applying the rigid cube encoding. The enumeration takes $q^{O(dn^2)}$ time. To hit the bound in polynomial time for every input, the present results still need a compression covering tensors with large intrinsic cut and centroid blocks when usable spectral coordinates are absent.

A concrete family illustrates the gap. For any odd $n\geq3$, index the modes by $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ and put coefficient 1 on $(a,b,a+b,a+2b)$, with other coefficients zero. Every pair determines the other pair since 2 is invertible modulo $n$, so all three pair flattenings are permutation matrices over every base field. Invertibility of $12\mid34$ forces a centroid tuple to be scalar. Equality of its first two mode actions, after canceling the flattening, gives $A_1\otimes I=I\otimes A_2$, hence these are the same scalar, and conciseness forces the same scalar in the other modes. Thus the rank and centroid costs in this note do not improve the order-four exponent on this family.

The spectral unit hypothesis also forces the source stabilizer to consist of scalar tuples. A stabilizer in the cyclic coordinates must multiply by $a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_d$ with $a_i\in K_i^\times$. A unit $t$ can be canceled, leaving $a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_d=1\otimes\cdots\otimes1$; equality of nonzero pure tensors forces each $a_i$ to be a base-field scalar. The displayed family has a nonscalar stabilizer, obtained by translating $a$, $a+b$, and $a+2b$ by 1. So no choice of spectral covariants satisfying Theorem 3 can cover it. This identifies a class for which the present compression guarantees are insufficient.
