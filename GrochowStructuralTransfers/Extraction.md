# An irreducible oracle with one-call border universality and exact elimination

**Theorem.** Over the complex numbers there is a single symbolically specified family of homogeneous, absolutely irreducible integer polynomials defining rational hypersurfaces with the following properties.

* Every VNP family has a one-call border computation using this oracle family, affine inputs, and an affine output map over the Laurent rational function field.
* On polynomial-degree outputs, polynomial-size division-free exact circuits with this oracle compute precisely VP. Polynomial-size constant-depth exact circuits with the oracle likewise compute precisely the ordinary polynomial-size constant-depth class. Exact circuits can nest calls and use different polynomially bounded oracle indices.
* Consequently exact polynomial-size recovery of the permanent from this one fixed family is equivalent to VP = VNP over the complex numbers. The fixed-depth gap is unconditional since the determinant is in VNP and has superpolynomial fixed-depth circuit complexity.

There is also a determinantal version living in the ideal generated by two disjoint half-size minors of a generic matrix. Every algebraic branching-program family admits a one-call border computation from this version. The same exact elimination theorem holds. This provides a constant-depth exact versus border separation in the setting of Grochow's determinantal-ideal question. [Andrews and Forbes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00792) · [Dey and Guo](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.49) · [Forbes, Theorem 10](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol300-ccc2024/LIPIcs.CCC.2024.31/LIPIcs.CCC.2024.31.pdf)

A second construction lets one prescribe exactly how much power a single coordinate derivative adds. Given any homogeneous polynomial family $h$ of polynomial arity and degree, it produces an irreducible rational-hypersurface oracle whose evaluations leave ordinary circuit power unchanged, but whose one designated partial derivative is oracle-equivalent to $h$ on polynomial-degree outputs. This also holds with fixed depth on both sides. The construction is given below.

The construction deliberately uses unrestricted oracle degree. Prelimit border computations may have high degree in the input variables. The output equals the target at the regular limit in the degeneration parameter. Ordinary VP has polynomial-degree outputs, even when intermediate formal degrees are larger. All sizes count gates and wires, and scalar constants have unit cost. The circuits and simulations are nonuniform. The explicit formula below specifies the oracles exactly; their enormous degree precludes polynomial-size ordinary circuits for the oracle polynomials themselves.

## The oracle and its geometry

For positive integers $j$ put $L_j=2^{2^j}$ and $M_j=2^{L_j}$. Let $g_j$ be the permanent of a generic $j$ by $j$ matrix, or the determinant for the determinantal version. Use disjoint copies of its variables and two fresh variables $u,v$. Set

$$F_j=u\,g_j(X)^{M_j}+v\,g_j(Y)^{M_j}.$$

There are no chosen generic coefficients. The two coefficient polynomials of this linear form in $u,v$ have disjoint variables and are coprime. Gauss's lemma proves absolute irreducibility. The equation solves for $u$ on the dense chart $g_j(X)\ne0$, which exhibits rationality (also projectively after fixing a nonvanishing coordinate). In the determinantal version use the two diagonal $j$-blocks of a generic $2j$-square matrix, and take $u,v$ from its off-diagonal blocks. Its unused entries add free variables. Both terms belong to the two-minor ideal. The elimination proof also applies to any sequence of homogeneous nonconstant $g_j$ with polynomially bounded arity and degree and arbitrary scalar coefficients.

## One call in the border model

For a VNP family $h_n(x)$, the family $1+z h_n(x)$, with $z$ a new variable, is in VNP. In the Boolean witness-sum definition of VNP, the constant one can be represented by the term selecting only the all-zero witness. Valiant's projection completeness gives a polynomially indexed permanent $g_j$ whose variables can be set to constants or variables to compute $1+z h_n$. Put $z=\epsilon$, $u=1$, $v=0$. The remaining matrix is immaterial. The output of the one-call circuit after subtraction and scaling is

$$\frac{F_j-1}{M_j\epsilon}=h_n(x)+\sum_{i=2}^{M_j}\frac{\binom{M_j}{i}}{M_j}\epsilon^{i-1}h_n(x)^i.$$

The constant coefficient in $\epsilon$ is $h_n$ and all other terms vanish in the limit. For the determinant version, an algebraic branching program for $h_n$ gives one for $1+z h_n$ by an additional edge and a parallel constant path. A path sum in a finite acyclic graph with weighted adjacency matrix $A$ is a signed minor of $I-A$, since $(I-A)^{-1}=I+A+A^2+\cdots$ and $\det(I-A)=1$. This gives the required affine determinant substitution. [Valiant, Theorem 2](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~billey/colombia/references/valiant.completeness.1979.pdf)

## An exact power-elimination calculation

Suppose a polynomial identity in characteristic zero has the form $T=\sum_{i=1}^R a_i B_i^m$, with $T\ne0$, all $B_i$ nonconstant and $\deg a_i\le D$. Restrict to a generic affine line preserving nonzeroness of $T$ and the degrees of all bases. Choose a minimal sublist of $r$ terms spanning $T$ over the field of scalars on that line. These terms are linearly independent and the coefficients expressing $T$ are nonzero. Their Wronskian is nonzero: row elimination on the coefficient vectors gives a basis of distinct leading degrees, and the leading Wronskian coefficient for that basis is a nonzero Vandermonde. If $m\ge r$, column $i$ has the common factor $B_i^{m-r+1}$. Choose $j$ maximizing $b=\deg B_j\ge1$ and replace its column by that of $T$. Multilinearity changes the Wronskian by a nonzero scalar. Comparing the degree of the forced product of column factors with the sum of replacement column degrees gives

$$(m-r+1)\sum_i\deg B_i\le\deg T+(r-1)D+m\sum_{i\ne j}\deg B_i.$$

Since $\sum_i\deg B_i\le rb$, we obtain $m\le r(r-1)+(r-1)D+\deg T$. The case $m<r$ is covered by the weaker bound $m\le R^2+RD+\deg T$. This derivation also covers repeated or proportional bases. [Earlier power-elimination argument](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/GrochowHypoworlds/POWER-ORACLES.md) · [Related Wronskian lower bounds](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/pascal.koiran/Publis/sum_of_powers.icalp2015.pdf)

## Removing the high-index calls

Let a circuit of size $s\le n^c$ compute a target $H_n$ of degree at most $n^d$. Choose a constant $K$ sufficiently large in terms of these fixed bounds, as quantified below. For large $n$, let $J$ be the largest index with $L_J\le n^K$. Then $J=O_K(\log\log n)$ and

$$L_J>n^{K/2},\qquad L_{J+1}=L_J^2.$$

At each index above $J$, leave the two scalar multipliers and addition in place, and cut both powers, giving each power occurrence a formal variable. Indices at most $J$ remain actual polynomial operations. The resulting output skeleton $S(x,y)$ has at most $2s$ cut variables. Its total degree $D$ satisfies

$$\log_2(D+2)=O\big(s(L_J+\log(s+2)+\log(J+2))\big).$$

Indeed there are $O(s)$ operations in an input-output path. An ordinary product multiplies the running degree bound by at most the fan-in, and a low oracle call by at most $1+M_J\max_{j\le J}\deg(g_j)$. This counts a low oracle as a single polynomial operation. The constant in the bound is independent of $K$.

Under the actual execution hardwire a cut power whose base is a scalar to its actual scalar value. For every remaining cut power its actual value is $B^{m}$ for a nonconstant polynomial $B$, with the common $m=M_{J+1}$, because each higher exponent is a multiple of $m$. This assertion uses the actual gate arguments before cutting, so nesting causes no circularity. Call the scalar-specialized skeleton $S'$ and let $H_0=S'(x,0)$. Expanding $S'$ only in the remaining cut variables shows that $H_n-H_0$ is a sum of at most $R=(D+1)^{2s}$ terms $a_i(x)B_i(x)^m$, with $\deg a_i\le D$. A nonempty product of nonconstant polynomial bases remains nonconstant. Vanishing terms can be discarded. Also $\deg(H_n-H_0)\le\max(n^d,D)$ whenever it is nonzero.

The logarithm of $R^2+RD+\max(n^d,D)+2$ is at most $O(s^2(L_J+\log(s+2)+\log(J+2))+d\log(n+2))$. Take $K>4c+4$. For sufficiently large $n$, $L_{J+1}=L_J^2$ exceeds this bound. The Wronskian inequality now forces $H_n=H_0$. In the circuit for $H_0$ every high power has been replaced by a scalar, including zero, while the scalar multiplier wires are still evaluated. Size in this partially stripped circuit is $O(s)$ and a fixed original depth stays fixed. This is a circuit-existence proof; the simulation may use different scalars for different input lengths.

## Removing the low-index calls and preserving depth

Every $g_j$ with $j\le J$ has a constant-depth dense expansion of size $2^{\operatorname{poly}(J)}=n^{o(1)}$ for each fixed $K$. At unrestricted depth, the remaining powers can be computed with $O(L_J)$ multiplications per occurrence by repeated squaring. This proves ordinary polynomial-size simulation.

There is a separate small-depth simulation. Let $d_0$ be a polynomial bound on the output degree. Work modulo the ideal of input monomials of degree greater than $d_0$. For each low power gate whose computed base is $b(x)$ put $b_0=b(0)$. Replace it by

$$\sum_{i=0}^{\min(d_0,M_j)}\binom{M_j}{i} b_0^{M_j-i}(b-b_0)^i.$$

This agrees in the quotient by the binomial theorem. The scalar for exponent zero is one, including when $b_0=0$. Each summand can use one ordinary unbounded-fan-in product of at most $d_0$ factors. The new circuit has polynomial size, and each replaced gate contributes a bounded number of layers, including the dense expansion of $g_j$. Equality in the quotient is preserved through nested low calls. The final approximant is an ordinary polynomial computed by a polynomial-size constant-depth circuit; its full input degree $B$ is polynomial, bounded by size to depth. Recover the sum of its homogeneous components of degrees at most $d_0$ by interpolating the polynomial in $z$ obtained on input $zx$ at $B+1$ distinct scalars and applying the corresponding one linear functional. This constant-depth operation returns exactly $H_n$. Ordinary circuits give the reverse containment by ignoring the oracle.

## Prescribing the power of one exact partial derivative

Write $e_j=\deg h_j\ge1$ and use the homogeneous base

$$g_j(s,z,X)=s^{e_j+1}+z h_j(X).$$

Form $F_j$ from two disjoint copies as above. It again has a rational absolutely irreducible hypersurface, has integer coefficients when $h_j$ does, and satisfies the entire exact elimination theorem. Consider only the derivative in the first copy's $z$ coordinate. Putting $s=1,z=0,u=1,v=0$ gives exactly

$$\left.\frac{\partial F_j}{\partial z}\right|_{s=1,z=0,u=1,v=0}=M_j h_j(X).$$

Thus a single exact derivative call and scalar rescaling simulate an $h_j$ call. Conversely, that same partial on general arguments equals $M_j u h_j(X) g_j(s,z,X)^{M_j-1}$. In any exact circuit for an output of degree at most $d_0$, this operation can be simulated by an $h_j$-oracle circuit at polynomial overhead, preserving bounded depth when present. Here are the details needed even when the original depth is polynomial. At each gate keep all homogeneous components through degree $d_0$ in the original inputs. Encode these by their polynomial in a marker $t$, with component of degree $i$ multiplied by $t^i$. At an ordinary product, or an $h_j$ call, evaluate the marker input arrays at enough distinct constants, perform the indicated gate on each evaluation, then interpolate the first $d_0+1$ components. The full marker degree is at most $d_0$ times the product fan-in or $\deg h_j$, respectively. For a power $B(t)^k$ use the truncated binomial expression from the preceding section around $B(0)$; after representing $B(t)$ with degree at most $d_0$, this expression has marker degree at most $d_0^2$ regardless of $k$. The component $B(0)$ is a scalar. Intermediate homogeneous components are shared by all later gates. Only polynomially many component wires per gate are needed, and the depth overhead per original layer is bounded. The final sum of components is the target. This proves equality of the two derivative and base oracle classes on the claimed outputs.

Taking $h$ to be the permanent makes exact recovery of the permanent through evaluations equivalent to VP = VNP, while one derivative query recovers the permanent outright. Taking $h$ to be the determinant makes the fixed-depth separation unconditional. A related unconditional unrestricted-depth separation permits an expensive coefficient generator: for each $j$, use the $2^j$ degree-$j$ monomials choosing one variable from each of $j$ disjoint pairs. A size-$s$ topology with $s$ charged scalar parameters maps its constants polynomially into this coefficient space, giving image closure of dimension at most $s$. For $s<2^j$ finitely many such topologies cannot exhaust integer vectors. Choose an integer vector outside these images, for example the first in an effective height enumeration whose circuit nonexistence is certified by algebraically closed field quantifier elimination. The resulting $h$ has no polynomial-size ordinary circuits and is exactly accessible with one derivative query. No efficient coefficient generator is claimed for this last example. Baur and Strassen's theorem supplies derivatives at constant-factor overhead when an ordinary circuit for the source is available. Our constructions concern evaluation-only access to an unrestricted-degree source. [Baur and Strassen](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(83)90110-X)

**Scope and provenance.** The proof combines prior high-power elimination, degree spacing across oracle indices, low-degree binomial truncation, and completeness under projections. The earlier geometric construction used generic coefficient masks and gave stronger quantitative dependence on oracle degree. The present binomial family gives one prescribed oracle, simultaneous elimination on all polynomial-degree outputs, arbitrary polynomially bounded indices, and one-call border universality. It does not separate VP from VNP. Publication priority for this combination remains unverified.
