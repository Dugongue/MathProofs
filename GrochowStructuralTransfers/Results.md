# Sharp witnesses and information bounds in algebraic isomorphism and complexity

Four informal proofs give quantitative answers to different questions about what a restricted observation can recover. Each statement below specifies the observation and the resource being measured. Publication priority for these combinations remains unconfirmed.

## How large must a group probe be?

For infinitely many orders $n=p^5$, a cyclic group and a nonabelian metabelian group of order $n$ agree on the parity of the homomorphism count from *every* group of order below $n$. Among groups of order $n$, there is exactly one distinguishing isomorphism type, and it has two generators. Curran's automorphism classification supplies the group. Kernel–image factorization makes parity detect its occurrences as a quotient. The general upper bound on the size of a distinguishing probe is attained exactly. The same proof packet classifies attainable common divisors of count differences and gives a deterministic logspace minimum-witness routine for each fixed generator budget. [Informal proof](Groups.md) · [Curran](https://doi.org/10.1017/S000497270002757X)

## How much precision must a graph probe return?

On connected graphs of maximum degree 6 and pathwidth at most 19, the optimal worst-case width of one modular homomorphism-count answer is linear in the order cap $N$ for powers of two, $\log_2 N+O_p(1)$ for powers of any fixed prime $p>6$, and $\log_2\log N+O(1)$ when the modulus can vary. One prime selected from the common target order suffices for the last upper bound. The lower bounds allow unlimited probes and adaptive choices. The argument combines finite-group flows on a rigid base graph, free vertex actions on cubic ladders, and prime divisors of graph automorphism groups. The companion script independently builds twelve pairs of uncolored targets and counts lifts of the base by a direct graph dynamic program. [Informal proof](Precision.md) · [Reproduce the finite checks](check_graph.py) · [Curticapean's CFI normalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04767)

## How much more can a border oracle reveal than an exact oracle?

Over the complex numbers an explicit irreducible family of very high degree in a determinantal ideal gives every algebraic branching program in a single border call, where the desired polynomial is the limit of the oracle expression. Even nested exact oracle calls at polynomially many polynomially bounded indices leave both VP and the polynomial-size constant-depth class unchanged on polynomial-degree outputs. Thus the determinant gives an unconditional fixed-depth separation in this oracle model. A second family gives every VNP polynomial in one border call; polynomial-size exact recovery of its permanent is equivalent to VP = VNP. The proof relies on a Wronskian degree bound and the spacing of exponents. The polynomial-degree hypothesis on the oracle itself in earlier positive exact-extraction results is essential to the comparison. [Informal proof](Extraction.md) · [Andrews and Forbes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00792) · [Dey and Guo](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.49)

## What is the value of adaptivity when probing hidden matrices?

Suppose the hidden matrix tuple belongs to a public list of $N$ pairwise nonisomorphic absolutely simple modules of dimension $n$ over a fixed finite field. The observable is the exact number of module maps from a chosen probe, and the resource is total probe dimension. Testing equality deterministically costs exactly $nN(N-1)/2$ for one query. For fixed $k$ the optimal exponents of $N$ are $1+1/k$ with predetermined probes and $2^k/(2^k-1)$ with adaptive probes, with a factor $n$ in both cases. One randomized equality probe with bounded error attains order $nN$, optimal even with additional queries. The key step identifies the information and cost of *every* possible probe from its simple quotients. Matrix entries are hidden in this model. [Informal proof](Representations.md)

## How the library search contributed

The Wronskian degree lemmas in Mathlib and the earlier project oracle-elimination arguments suggested cutting across all sufficiently high oracle indices together. The quotient-kernel equivalence and the checked undirected-reachability work suggested sorting group probes by image size and then recovering a smallest witness. Orbit–stabilizer and incidence-algebra results suggested comparing an integral embedding count with prime-power divisibility of graph counts. The simple-quotient and projective-cover material led to testing what an arbitrary representation probe can see in its semisimple quotient. These ingredients informed the informal arguments. The four composite theorems have no Lean verification in this packet.
