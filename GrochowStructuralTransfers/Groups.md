# An exact generator threshold for modular homomorphism profiles of finite groups

All proofs below are informal. Write \(h(X,G)=|\operatorname{Hom}(X,G)|\). For finite groups \(G,H\), let \(D_d(G,H)\) be the nonnegative gcd of the integers \(h(X,G)-h(X,H)\) over finite groups \(X\) generated by at most \(d\) elements. Write \(D_\infty\) when every finite group is allowed. The gcd of a family of zeros is zero. Thus the divisors of a nonzero \(D_d\) are exactly the moduli for which all such probes give equal counts.

**Theorem.** On pairs of equal-order finite groups the positive values of \(D_1\) are exactly the even integers. The positive values of \(D_2\) are all integers, already on metabelian target groups. More precisely:

* For each even \(L>0\) there are equal-order abelian targets with \(D_1=D_\infty=L\).
* For each odd \(L>0\) there are equal-order metabelian targets with \(D_1=2L\) and \(D_2=D_\infty=L\). Independently of \(L\), one can prescribe the exact minimum order \(p^5\) of a source distinguishing their counts modulo two for any prime \(p>3\) with \(p=2\bmod 3\). There is exactly one isomorphism type of witness of that order, and it is two-generated.

For the pairs in the second assertion every generator budget \(d\ge2\) realizes the unrestricted gcd. This assertion concerns the attainable values on this family. Higher budgets can reveal additional distinctions on other pairs.

## Finite probes and exact arithmetic

Let \(\mathcal S\) contain one representative of each at-most-\(d\)-generated subgroup type occurring in either target. Put \(i(S,G)=|\operatorname{Inj}(S,G)|\) and \(q_X(S)=|\{N\triangleleft X:X/N\simeq S\}|\). Factoring a homomorphism by its kernel gives
\[
h(X,G)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S}q_X(S)i(S,G).
\]
Indeed, for \(X\) generated by at most \(d\) elements every possible image belongs to \(\mathcal S\), and after a choice of isomorphism from each quotient to its representative the remaining choices are exactly the injections. On rows \(X\in\mathcal S\) the matrix of the \(q_X(S)\) is lower unitriangular in increasing order of group size. Its inverse is integral, so
\[
D_d=\gcd_{S\in\mathcal S}|i(S,G)-i(S,H)|.
\]
Equivalently each term is \(|\operatorname{Aut}S|\) times the absolute difference in numbers of copies of \(S\). In particular a minimal-order subgroup type whose injection counts differ modulo \(m\) also distinguishes the homomorphism counts modulo \(m\). If any admissible probe distinguishes, its kernel quotient gives some distinguishing injection count at an order no larger than the original probe. The resulting minimum is therefore global. The same argument works for \(d=\infty\).

## The cyclic obstruction and the even construction

For \(X=C_j\), inversion acts on the elements satisfying \(x^j=1\). When \(j\) is odd the fixed set is the identity. When \(j\) is even the fixed set is all solutions of \(x^2=1\), whose cardinality has the parity of \(|G|\) by inversion on the whole group. Equal-order targets therefore agree modulo two on every cyclic source.

Let \(M>0\) be even. There exist distinct odd primes \(P,Q\), outside any specified finite set, with
\[
\gcd(P(P-1),Q(Q-1))=M.
\]
Choose \(P=1\bmod M\) by Dirichlet's theorem. For each prime \(\ell\mid P(P-1)\) prescribe \(Q=1+\ell^{v_\ell(M)}\bmod\ell^{v_\ell(M)+1}\). For odd \(\ell\) with valuation zero this residue is 2. The valuation at 2 is positive. All prescribed residues are units, so the Chinese remainder theorem and Dirichlet give a sufficiently large prime \(Q\) in these classes. The valuations of the gcd now give exactly \(M\).

Use the two targets \(E=C_{P^2}\times C_{Q^2}\) and \(F=(C_P)^2\times(C_Q)^2\). On a cyclic source the \(P\)-primary counts differ only if the order of the source has \(P\)-adic valuation one, in which case the two counts are \(P\) and \(P^2\). The \(Q\)-primary calculation is identical. Counts into \(E,F\) from any finite source factor through its abelianization and multiply across a cyclic decomposition of that abelianization. Thus every difference is divisible by \(M\). The two sources \(C_P,C_Q\) give absolute differences \(P(P-1),Q(Q-1)\). This proves the even assertion.

## A sharp bound on the size of the first parity witness

Fix any prime \(p>3\) with \(p=2\bmod 3\). Curran's explicitly presented \(K=G_0\) has order \(p^5\), two generators and automorphism group of order \(p^6\). It is metabelian. Indeed a group of order \(p^5\) has class at most four, \(\gamma_2/\gamma_3\) is cyclic in a two-generator group, and \([\gamma_2,\gamma_3]\subseteq\gamma_5=1\). Thus \(\gamma_3\) is central in \(\gamma_2\) with cyclic quotient, so \(\gamma_2\) is abelian. Every nontrivial proper subgroup \(S<K\) has order at most \(p^4\) and class at most three. Curran's Theorem 2 gives it an automorphism of order two, so its automorphism group has even order. [Curran, Theorems 2 and 4 and Corollary 5](https://doi.org/10.1017/S000497270002757X)

For every finite \(X\), the kernel expansion now yields
\[
h(X,K)\equiv 1+q_X(K)\pmod 2.
\]
The trivial image contributes one. Each possible nontrivial proper image type has an even number of injections because its automorphism group acts freely by precomposition. The full image has the odd injection count \(|\operatorname{Aut}K|\). Exactly the same reasoning gives \(h(X,U)\equiv1\bmod2\) for any proper \(U<K\). Also \(h(X,C_{p^5})\) is odd because the set of maps into this abelian group is itself a finite abelian \(p\)-group under pointwise addition.

**Sharpness corollary.** The two targets \(K,C_{p^5}\), both of order \(n=p^5\), agree modulo two for every probe of order strictly less than \(n\). At order \(n\) the unique distinguishing isomorphism type is \(K\), since a quotient of order \(n\) of a group of order \(n\) must be the group itself. Thus the general minimum-witness bound \(\max(|G|,|H|)\) is attained exactly for infinitely many equal orders, even for metabelian \(p\)-groups with one target cyclic.

## Realizing every odd modulus

Fix odd \(L>0\) and choose \(K\) as above, with \(p\) independent of \(L\). Choose an epimorphism \(K\to C_p\) and write \(K_0\) for its kernel. Such an epimorphism exists for every nontrivial finite \(p\)-group. Take \(s\) with \(p,L\mid 2^s-1\), and let \(V\) be the additive group of \(\mathbb F_{2^s}\). Let \(K\) act through its chosen quotient \(C_p\), embedded in the field scalars. Form the groups \(A=V\rtimes K\) and \(B=V\times K\). Both are metabelian: in \(A\) the derived subgroup lies in \(V\rtimes K'\), which is abelian because \(K'\) acts trivially on \(V\). The groups have the same order.

The scalar subgroup of order \(L\) acts on both groups by automorphisms. Every nonidentity scalar fixes precisely the embedded copy of \(K\), so on homomorphisms from an arbitrary \(X\) it acts freely outside the homomorphisms into \(K\). Consequently \(h(X,A)\equiv h(X,B)\bmod L\). For \(L=1\) this congruence is automatic.

Use the separate action of \(V\) by conjugation on \(\operatorname{Hom}(X,A)\). Its cardinality has the parity of the subset fixed by all of \(V\), since each nontrivial orbit has even size. This fixed subset consists of maps into \(C_A(V)=V\times K_0\). Subtract the counts for \(B\) and use the preceding kernel expansion to obtain
\[
h(X,A)-h(X,B)\equiv h(X,V)\,q_X(K)\pmod 2.
\]
Here \(h(X,V)\) is a power of two. It is odd exactly when \(X\) has no quotient \(C_2\), because \(V\) is a nonzero elementary abelian 2-group. Thus \(K\) itself distinguishes by parity, every smaller group agrees, and no other group of its order distinguishes.

Apply the even construction with \(M=2L\), choosing \(P,Q\) different from 2 and \(p\). Put \(G=A\times E,\ H=B\times F\). Counts into direct products multiply, so \(L\) divides every difference. The sources \(C_P,C_Q\) see only \(E,F\), implying \(D_1\mid 2L\). Cyclic parity and oddness of \(L\) give \(2L\mid D_1\), proving equality. The counts into \(E,F\) are always odd because these groups are abelian of odd order. Consequently the displayed parity formula survives unchanged for \(G,H\), including its unique minimum witness \(K\). Thus \(L\mid D_\infty\mid D_2\mid 2L\) with \(D_2\) odd, proving \(D_\infty=D_2=L\).

## Computing the profile and a smallest witness

For any fixed positive integer \(d\), agreement modulo a supplied positive binary integer \(m\) on all \(d\)-generated sources is decidable in deterministic logspace on Cayley-table input. A logspace transducer can output a smallest-order distinguishing source when one exists.

Enumerate all \(d\)-tuples \(a\) from both targets, writing \(U=\langle a\rangle\). For each candidate target \(Y\), enumerate its \(d\)-tuples \(b\). In the ambient direct product generate \(R=\langle(a_i,b_i)\rangle\). Count \(b\) precisely when the two coordinate projections of \(R\) are injective. This is equivalent to checking that neither coordinate kernel contains a nonidentity element. It counts each embedding \(U\to Y\) exactly once because \(R\) is the graph of the unique embedding sending \(a\) to \(b\). Kernel tests use membership in \(R\), implemented by undirected reachability from the identity in the graph of right multiplication by the given generators and their inverses. The direct-product graph has at most \(n^2\) vertices, for \(n=\max(|G|,|H|)\), and its adjacency is computed from the tables. The same membership method computes \(|U|\) by scanning its ambient group. [Reingold, Undirected Connectivity in Log-Space](https://doi.org/10.1145/1391289.1391291)

Each injection count is at most \(n^d\). If the modulus exceeds this bound, equality modulo \(m\) is ordinary equality of these counts. Otherwise \(m\) itself fits in \(O(d\log n)\) bits. Keep a minimum-order distinguishing tuple and its ambient-group identifier. The finite-probe identity proves that its subgroup is a smallest homomorphism-count witness. A table for that subgroup can be streamed by membership and rescanning for output indices. All loop indices and the constant number of tuples take logarithmic space for fixed \(d\). An analogous gcd accumulation computes \(D_d\) itself in logspace.

## Attribution and scope

The kernel/image triangular argument belongs to the Lovász homomorphism-counting tradition. Bounded-generator subgroup profiles in group isomorphism are discussed by Brachter and Schweitzer, including earlier work arising from a question of Gowers. The contributions proposed here are the exact equal-order modular spectrum, its cyclic versus two-generator threshold on metabelian targets, and the explicit minimum modular witness algorithm. The search did not establish publication priority or settle unrestricted group isomorphism. The arithmetic existence proof permits very large targets. [Brachter and Schweitzer, On the Weisfeiler–Leman Dimension of Finite Groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13745)
