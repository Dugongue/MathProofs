# Deterministic interpolation of sparse rational functions

## GP-0596

Fix an individual degree bound $d$. Given oracle access to a rational function $R=N/D$ over $\mathbb Q$ with coprime numerator and denominator, each with at most $s$ terms and individual degrees at most $d$, one can recover sparse representatives of $N$ and $D$ deterministically in polynomial bit time, counting oracle calls and the lengths of their queries and answers. An exact value oracle reports a failed query precisely when $D$ vanishes. The algorithm needs no bounds on total sparsity of cross-products beyond those implied by $s$. Normalize the output by setting one designated nonzero denominator coefficient to one.

### Sparse bounds on bad specializations

Use distinct primes $p_1,\ldots,p_n$ of size polynomial in $n$ and points $a(j)=(p_1^j,\ldots,p_n^j)$ at nonnegative integers $j$. A nonzero $u$-sparse rational polynomial vanishes at at most $u-1$ such points. Indeed, on this sequence its values are a linear combination of $u$ exponentials with distinct positive bases. Dividing by the least exponential and applying Rolle's theorem inductively bounds the number of real zeros by $u-1$. The same statement holds when only a subset of the coordinates varies along its primes and the rest are fixed, provided the specialized polynomial is nonzero.

Assume $N$ is nonzero for the moment. For each variable $x_i$, consider the leading coefficients of $N,D$ as univariate polynomials in $x_i$ and their univariate resultant over the other variables. Each leading coefficient is nonzero, with sparsity at most $s$. Their resultant is nonzero because a positive-degree common factor over the field of the other variables would give a common factor in $\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ by Gauss's lemma. The Sylvester matrix has order at most $2d$, so this resultant has at most $(2d)! \max(1,s)^{2d}$ terms if $d\ge1$. For two nonzero constants in $x_i$, use the test polynomial one in place of their resultant. Also require $N,D$ nonzero at the chosen point. There are $3n+2$ test polynomials, each with polynomially many terms for fixed $d$. A known integer $B=(3n+2)(2d+1)! \max(1,s)^{2d+1}$ bounds the total number of bad sample indices. The algorithm uses $B$ with no need to reconstruct the test polynomials.

### Choosing an anchor

For each $j$ from zero to $B$, and for each axis $i$, restrict the oracle to the line through $a(j)$ varying coordinate $i$. Using at most $3d+1$ distinct integer samples, obtain the reduced univariate quotient if at least $2d+1$ queries succeed. This uses linear rational interpolation in numerator and denominator coefficients followed by cancellation. In fact, if $D$ is identically zero on the line all queries fail. Otherwise there are at most $d$ poles and agreement at $2d+1$ successful points determines the rational function because a cross-product difference has degree at most $2d$. Discard sample points with $R(a(j))$ undefined or zero. If all points are discarded, then $N=0$ by the sparse bound applied to $N,D$ when both are nonzero. Return $0/1$ in that case.

On each surviving line the degrees of the reduced univariates are bounded by the true degrees in $x_i$. They attain both true degrees at every point avoiding the test zeros. Therefore coordinatewise maxima of all observed numerator and denominator degrees are the true degrees. Choose an anchor $a$ attaining them on all axes at once, with $R(a)$ defined and nonzero. At that anchor all leading coefficients and resultants used above are nonzero, as a degree drop or nonconstant cancellation would strictly decrease an observed degree. Take the constant case $d=0$ by evaluating once.

### Transporting the two values separately

For target $a(j)$, move one coordinate at a time from $a$ to $a(j)$ in the order $1,\ldots,n$. On segment $i$, interpolate the univariate quotient on the entire coordinate line. Reject the index if its numerator or denominator has lower degree than the known degrees in $x_i$, or if either of these reduced polynomials vanishes at either endpoint. Also reject a failed interpolation. On an accepted index each quotient has numerator and denominator separately proportional to the true restrictions by a single common nonzero scalar. Taking the ratios of the two endpoints within each numerator and within each denominator gives the exact ratios of their global values. Multiply along the path to obtain $N(a(j))/N(a)$ and $D(a(j))/D(a)$. Supply $R(a)$ to fix their relative normalization.

At most $B'=4B$ indices are rejected. On segment $i$, the leading coefficient and resultant conditions are restrictions in the coordinates less than $i$ with the suffix fixed at $a$. Both endpoint conditions are restrictions of $N,D$ with suffix fixed at $a$, using the prefix of length $i-1$ or $i$, respectively. Each restricted test polynomial is nonzero, since evaluating the varying prefix at the corresponding anchor coordinates yields a nonzero value. Each retains the sparsity bound already proved. The stated bound covers all $7n$ conditions. Thus among the first $(B'+1)(2s+1)+B'$ indices there are at least $2s+1$ consecutive good indices, by separating good runs at the bad indices. This suffices for the next step, and the algorithm can simply look for the first such run.

### Sparse interpolation and bit lengths

On the consecutive run, both unknown normalized polynomials yield exponential sequences with at most $s$ distinct positive integral bases, of the form $\prod_i p_i^{\alpha_i}$ with $0\le\alpha_i\le d$. Recover their shortest recurrences by solving the successive Hankel linear systems and take the roots of the recurrence polynomials by deterministic rational univariate factoring. The usual Vandermonde identity proves this procedure: for $u$ actual terms, the order-$u$ leading Hankel matrix is $V\operatorname{diag}(c_\alpha b_\alpha^{j_0})V^{\mathsf T}$ with both factors invertible. The recovered bases determine the exponent vectors by divisions by the known primes. Vandermonde solves give the coefficients, including the starting-index scaling. [Ben-Or–Tiwari sparse interpolation](https://doi.org/10.1145/62212.62241) · [Deterministic rational univariate factoring](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01457454)

Indices and the exponent bounds are polynomial in $n,s$ for fixed $d$. Values of the two sparse polynomials at the query points have polynomial bit length in the input coefficient bit bound and the query lengths, as each is a sum of at most $s$ rational monomials of total degree at most $nd$. All rational interpolation and recurrence systems have polynomial size and polynomial-bit solutions by determinant bounds. The products of the $n$ exact transport ratios retain polynomial bit length. This yields a deterministic bit-polynomial algorithm in the stated oracle model.
