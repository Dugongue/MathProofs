# Minimum faithful degree from an ordinary character table at odd primes

## GP-0398

For every nontrivial finite odd-order $p$-group, the ordinary character table with power maps determines the minimum faithful permutation degree. It can be recovered with one weighted matroid basis computation in deterministic time polynomial in the exact table size. The lower bound below is valid also at $p=2$. The matching construction uses Ford's odd-prime theorem. [Ford’s theorem, reproduced as Lemma 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05852#S2)

Let $E=\{z\in Z(G):z^p=1\}$, viewed as an $\mathbb F_p$-space. For an irreducible complex character $\chi$, the normalized values $\chi(z)/\chi(1)$ for $z\in E$ give a linear functional $\ell_\chi\in E^*$ after choosing a primitive $p$th root of unity. For each rational Galois orbit of characters with nontrivial restriction, take one representative $\chi$. Give its row the weight
$$
w_\chi=\frac{p}{p-1}\,\chi(1)\,|\operatorname{Gal orbit}(\chi)|.
$$
Changing the representative only rescales $\ell_\chi$ by a nonzero element of $\mathbb F_p$. The answer is the smallest total weight of a basis of $E^*$ drawn from the rows.

For achievability at odd $p$, Ford's theorem gives $\chi=\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\lambda$ for a linear character $\lambda$ of a subgroup $H$ with $\mathbb Q(\lambda)=\mathbb Q(\chi)$. Any $z\in E$ has $\chi(z)\ne0$, since it is a central scalar. The induction formula therefore forces $z\in H$ and gives $\lambda(z)=\chi(z)/\chi(1)$. If $\lambda$ has cyclic image of order $p^a>1$, then $[G\colon \ker\lambda]=\chi(1)p^a=w_\chi$, because $[\mathbb Q(\lambda):\mathbb Q]=p^{a-1}(p-1)$. The kernel of the coset action intersects $E$ in exactly $\ker\ell_\chi$, since $E$ is central. Taking the disjoint union for a basis makes this intersection trivial. Every nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite $p$-group meets $E$ nontrivially by the class equation and taking an element of order $p$ in the central intersection. The action is faithful.

Conversely, consider one transitive permutation orbit of size $d$ and let $D\le E$ be the kernel of its restricted $E$-action. The action of $E/D$ is free on this orbit because central elements have the same stabilizer at every point. Thus its character on $E$ is $d/p^c$ copies of the regular character of $E/D$, where $c=\dim E/D$. The multiplicities of complex irreducible characters in the permutation character are constant on their Galois orbits. For each projective functional line in $(E/D)^*$, the sum of the dimensions of the corresponding Galois-orbit summands, with multiplicities, is $(p-1)d/p^c$. At least one row from that line has weight at most $d/p^{c-1}$. Choosing $c$ independent lines therefore supplies rows spanning the annihilator of $D$ with total weight at most $d$, since $c\le p^{c-1}$ for $c\ge1$. When $c=0$ choose no rows. For a faithful action the intersection of all the subspaces $D$ is zero, so the union of these rows spans $E^*$. Removing dependent rows leaves a basis of no greater total cost. This gives the lower bound.

To implement the calculation, recover class sizes by character column orthogonality and select the singleton classes of order dividing $p$ using the power maps. The normalized character signatures separate their elements: the irreducible characters separate the singleton conjugacy classes. The signature of a product is the componentwise product, since each central scalar is multiplicative. This reconstructs the explicit table of $E$, from which a basis and the row vectors are obtained by elementary linear algebra. Find Galois orbits of rows using the permutations $\chi(g)\mapsto\chi(g^a)$, where $a$ ranges over a generating set of units modulo the group exponent $p^e$. For odd $p$, a lift of a primitive root modulo $p$ together with $1+p$ suffices: the first generates the quotient modulo $p$ and the second generates the kernel for $e>1$. A primitive root modulo $p$ can be found by scanning residues and testing their orders. The table has at least $p$ linear characters, since every nontrivial finite $p$-group has a quotient of order $p$. Hence this scan and evaluating the indicated supplied power maps are polynomial in the table size, even when the exponent is represented in binary. Work with exact character values and their normalized $p$th roots, whose exponents can be read by comparison with the list of all $p$ roots. Sort the weighted rows and insert exactly those that raise rank, which gives a minimum-weight basis by the matroid greedy theorem.
