# Constant-round counting refinement on colored cyclic groups

## GP-0431

The answer depends on the initial coloring version of group Weisfeiler–Leman. For Version II, where the initial tuple color records the group with distinguished generators together with the original colors of those distinguished entries, dimension two and one refinement round identify every finite cyclic group with arbitrary unary vertex colors. For Version I, initialized using multiplication relations on the tuple entries, no fixed dimension and round bound suffices even for prime-order cyclic groups with two distinctly colored singleton markers and a common default color. The lower bound holds for joint replacement multisets as well as coordinatewise refinement. [Group WL versions](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.11487v7)

For the upper bound, choose a generator $g$ of the first group and compare the refined color of $(g,g)$ with a tuple of matching color in the second, assuming their refined histograms agree. The tuple equality pattern and its cyclic marked subgroup identify the matching tuple as $(h,h)$ with $h$ of the same order as $g$. Histogram sizes give equal group orders, so $h$ is a generator. The projection of the refinement multiset to replacements in the second coordinate gives the same multiset of initial colors of $(g,x)$ and $(h,y)$. In Version II the initial color remembers the unique exponent taking the first entry to the second, as well as the second entry's unary color. Therefore $g^j\mapsto h^j$ preserves the colors for every $j$. The group isomorphism follows.

For the lower bound write the group additively as $\mathbb F_p$. The two singleton markers in one group are $1,a$, and in the other $1,b$, with the corresponding colors. A tuple position is matched at coefficient bound $M$ when the two tuples and their two named markers have the same zero tests for every homogeneous linear combination with integer coefficients in $[-M,M]$. Use $M=2$ for the initial color, which determines equalities, multiplication triples and unary colors.

Fix a prospective additional entry $x$ at this bound. Every test with nonzero coefficient of $x$ identifies one candidate value $x=-L/c$, where $L$ is a linear form in the previous entries and constants and $0<|c|\le M$. For $p>2M^2$ such coefficients are units. Two candidates coincide exactly when $c'L-cL'=0$, a test with coefficients bounded by $2M^2$. If the previous tuples match at bound $2M^2$, the finite set of candidate values has an identical equality pattern on both sides. Bijections of the full group matching those candidate classes, and arbitrarily matching their complements, preserve all the bound-$M$ tests on the extended tuples. Tests whose coefficient of $x$ is zero already match.

For a fixed dimension $k$ and $r$ rounds, iterate the transformation $M\mapsto 2M^2$ for $k+r$ steps, starting at two. Choose a prime larger than twice the resulting bound and large enough that at least two distinct residues $a,b$ avoid every ratio $-d/e$ of integers in the final coefficient range with $e\ne0$. The empty tuples then match at the required bound. Spend $k$ extension steps to pair all $k$-tuples bijectively. To refine a paired tuple, apply the extension argument while keeping all its entries, then delete each old entry in turn. The **same** bijection for new entries matches every coordinate replacement, which proves equality of joint multiset colors by induction over the remaining rounds. The two marked cyclic groups are nonisomorphic, since a group isomorphism fixing $1$ is the identity and $a\ne b$. This supplies arbitrarily large prime-order examples for every proposed constant pair $(k,r)$.


### A matching round bound for Version I

At a fixed sufficiently large dimension, Version I identifies every unary-colored cyclic group of order $n$ in $O(\log\log(n+2))$ rounds. In the count-free game, mark a generator $g$. If the reply $h$ has smaller order, the order-finding strategy of Proposition 7.10 wins in this bound. If $h$ is a generator, $g^j\mapsto h^j$ is the only isomorphism consistent with that reply. If it fails to preserve colors, mark a power $g^j$ at which it fails. A reply with different color loses immediately. A matching-color reply is therefore different from $h^j$, and the repeated powering strategy of Lemma 7.9 wins in $O(\log\log(n+2))$ rounds. These strategies use a constant number of extra pebbles. Counting refinement is at least as strong as its count-free version. [Power and order recognition, Section 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.11487v7#S7)

The lower bound above has the same order at any fixed width. After $s=k+r$ extensions the coefficient bound is $2^{2^{s+1}-1}$. Whenever $r\le \lfloor\log_2\log_2 p\rfloor-k-4$, it is at most $p^{1/8}$. The forbidden ratios then occupy only $O(p^{1/4})$ residues and the two marker choices exist for all sufficiently large primes. Thus at sufficiently large fixed width the worst-case number of Version I rounds is of order $\log\log n$, with a lower bound along prime orders.
