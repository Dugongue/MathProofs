# Automorphisms and primitive fusions of coherent configurations

## GP-0772. Exact automorphism groups when valencies recover Hamming adjacency

Let $X$ be a homogeneous coherent configuration on $q\ge2$ points, with rank $r$, smallest nontrivial valency $a$, and largest nontrivial valency $b$. Let $G\le S_d$ act by permuting tensor coordinates, and define $X\uparrow G$ by fusing the basis relations of $X^{\otimes d}$ on the orbits of $G$. Write $G_{[r]}$ for the subgroup of coordinate permutations that preserves each $G$-orbit on words of length $d$ on an alphabet of size $r$. This is the coordinate action on color words, so $G\le G_{[r]}$.

If $d=1$, or if $a^2>db$, the color-preserving automorphism group is
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(X\uparrow G)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)^d\rtimes G_{[r]}.
$$
The formula also holds whenever the distance-one Hamming relation is preserved by every color-preserving automorphism. When $r\ge d$, $G_{[r]}=G$. For $d\ge3$, $r=2$, and $G=A_d$, $G_{[r]}=S_d$. Taking $X$ to be the rank-two scheme on $q\ge d+2$ points gives strict enlargement satisfying the valency hypothesis for every $d\ge3$.

### Proof

A word on the $r$ basis colors describes a tensor-product relation. Its non-diagonal positions are exactly the coordinates in which a pair of points differ. Its valency is the product of its coordinate valencies, with diagonal coordinates contributing one. A fused relation sums the valencies over its orbit of words. A word of Hamming weight one has orbit size at most $d$, so the valency of its fused color is at most $db$. Every word of weight at least two has valency at least $a^2$. The non-diagonal colors with valency at most $db$ therefore give exactly the Hamming graph when the strict inequality holds. For $d=1$ preservation of this graph is automatic.

An automorphism of the Hamming graph acts by a coordinate permutation followed by per-coordinate point permutations. To see this for $d\ge2$, its maximal cliques are the coordinate lines, including the lines of size two if $q=2$. The transitive closure of the relation “opposite edges of an induced square, or edges in the same coordinate line” identifies the $d$ coordinate directions. An induced square changes exactly two coordinates, alternating them. Any two edges of one direction are related by moving between parallel lines along the other directions, and by moving within a line. Once a graph automorphism fixes all directions, its $i$-th output coordinate is constant along lines in every other direction and therefore depends only on the $i$-th input coordinate. The resulting point maps are bijections.

For a pair differing only in coordinate $i$, the base off-diagonal color itself is preserved by a color-preserving automorphism. The fusion can move that color to other positions, but it cannot turn it into another base color. Hence the point permutation on coordinate $i$ belongs to $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$. After removing all these point permutations, the remaining coordinate permutation preserves the fused coloring exactly when it lies in $G_{[r]}$. This proves equality, including the converse extension.

A word with $d$ distinct symbols has trivial stabilizer in $S_d$; preserving its $G$-orbit forces the coordinate permutation to belong to $G$. For binary words and $d\ge3$, each word has a repeated symbol, hence an odd stabilizer. Every $S_d$-translate is then an $A_d$-translate of that word. This proves the stated enlargement and covers all homogeneous base ranks satisfying the valency test. ## GP-0773. Algebraic isomorphisms can destroy an exponentiation with a fixed number of factors

For every $d\ge2$, there is a homogeneous coherent configuration algebraically isomorphic to the exponentiation of the four-point rank-two scheme by $S_d$ that has no exponentiation representation with $d$ factors. Take the distance scheme of the Cartesian product of a Shrikhande graph and $d-2$ copies of $K_4$.

The Shrikhande graph can be defined on $(\mathbb Z/4)^2$ by differences in $\{\pm(1,0),\pm(0,1),\pm(1,1)\}$. Each of the 15 nonzero group elements occurs twice as a difference of distinct members of that six-element set. Consequently the graph has parameters $(16,6,2,2)$. The neighbors of a vertex induce $C_6$, whereas in $H(2,4)$ they induce $2K_3$. Both graphs have diameter two and the same intersection array, with $b_i=3(2-i)$ and $c_i=i$. In $K_4$ the analogous formulas are $b_i=3(1-i)$ and $c_i=i$. Distances add in a Cartesian product, and each neighbor changes one coordinate, so the numbers of neighbors decreasing or increasing distance are the sums of the separate numbers. This gives $b_i=3(d-i)$ and $c_i=i$ in the indicated product, equal to the array for $H(d,4)$. The distance relation matrices of a distance-regular graph are obtained from the adjacency matrix by the three-term recurrence determined by its intersection array. Their multiplication constants are therefore the same in these two schemes. In contrast, their neighborhoods induce $C_6\sqcup(d-2)K_3$ and $dK_3$, respectively. This yields nonisomorphic distance-one graphs. The distance-one valency $3d$ is smaller than that of any other nontrivial Hamming relation. For $2\le i<d$, $3^i\binom di\ge9d$, and at $i=d$ the value $3^d$ exceeds $3d$ when $d\ge2$. Thus no scheme isomorphism can exchange the distance-one relation with a different one.

An exponentiation on $d$ factors with degree $4^d$ must have a four-point base. If the base has $r$ colors, at least $\binom{r+d-1}{d}$ orbits of color words occur, one or more per distinct color multiset. For $r\ge3$ this exceeds the required rank $d+1$. A four-point base has rank at least two, and rank two forces exactly the diagonal and off-diagonal relations. At rank $d+1$, the coordinate group on this rank-two alphabet must have exactly one orbit of each weight. The resulting fusion is the Hamming scheme itself, contradicting the neighborhood calculation. This last rank argument rules out every possible replacement base or coordinate group with $d$ factors. ## GP-0777. Palette cuts bound the rank of every primitive fusion

Let $X$ be a homogeneous coherent configuration and let $U$ be a nonempty proper vertex subset. Suppose $c$ basis relations contain all directed pairs from $U$ to its complement. Every primitive fusion of $X$ has rank at most $c+1$. In particular, deleting one off-diagonal basis color and testing directed strong connectivity gives a polynomial-time certificate that there is no primitive fusion of rank greater than two whenever the deletion destroys strong connectivity.

In a primitive homogeneous configuration, the digraph of each off-diagonal basis relation is strongly connected. For a proof, a relation digraph is finite and every vertex has the same outdegree and indegree. Its condensation therefore contains no edges. In fact a source strongly connected component has indegree zero in the condensation, so degree balance forces its outdegree to be zero, and induction removes all components. The component relation is the support of $\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} A^j$ where $A$ is the adjacency matrix of that basis relation. This sum lies in the adjacency algebra and is constant on each basis relation, so its support is a union of basis relations. The off-diagonal relation has positive valency, and primitivity therefore forces the entire vertex set into one component.

Apply this to a primitive fusion. Each of its non-diagonal basis relations must contain a directed edge leaving $U$, and hence must contain at least one of the original colors in the cut. Distinct fused colors are disjoint collections of original colors. This bounds their number by $c$. If deleting one color leaves a digraph that is not strongly connected, take the vertex set of a sink component. Only the deleted color can leave it. The certificate includes all nontrivial wreath products with a rank-two quotient and one cross-block color. More generally, for any fixed $c$, one can enumerate at most $c$ deleted colors and check strong connectivity to recognize whether the configuration has a cut using at most $c$ colors.
