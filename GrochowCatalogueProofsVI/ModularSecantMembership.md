# Secant membership in small characteristic by equivariant support

## GP-0541

Let $K$ be algebraically closed of fixed characteristic $p>0$, and fix the form degree $d\ge 1$ and the secant bound $r\ge 1$. There is a deterministic polynomial-operation test, in the ambient coefficient size, for the affine $r$-secant cone over the degree-$d$ Veronese. The test also applies to the closure of sums of $r$ ordinary powers of linear forms, where in small characteristic that map can differ from the Veronese. The operations are field arithmetic, equality tests and inverse Frobenius. Over an explicitly represented finite subfield, inverse Frobenius and the test use polynomial bit time. The test uses equivariant support followed by a fixed-dimensional elimination. Its construction does not require splitting a Schur filtration or reading off composition multiplicities.

### The divided-power Veronese

Use the basis-free realization of the degree-$d$ divided power space as the symmetric invariants in $V^{\otimes d}$, with Veronese map $v\mapsto v^{\otimes d}$. All affine scalar coefficients can be absorbed into the $v$ because $K$ is algebraically closed. For a symmetric invariant $T$, let $U$ be its first-mode support, the image of contraction against all linear forms on the other modes. Symmetry makes this the support in every mode. Hence $T\in U^{\otimes d}$ by completing a basis of $U$ to a basis of $V$ and inspecting the tensor coordinates. In a sum of $r$ powers, the support dimension is at most $r$. Since the flattening rank condition is closed, it remains necessary on the full secant cone.

When $\dim U\le r$, let $\pi:V\to U$ be any linear projection restricting to identity on $U$. It sends the ambient power map to the power map on $U$. Its induced linear map on tensor powers carries the closure of sums of $r$ powers into the closure of their projections. Thus for a tensor contained in $U^{\otimes d}$, membership in the ambient secant is exactly membership in the smaller secant on $U$. The other direction uses the inclusion $U\subset V$.

### The ordinary power map

For the map $v\mapsto v^d$ in $\operatorname{Sym}^d V$, write $d=\sum_j d_jp^j$ with $0\le d_j<p$. There is a natural embedding
$$
W=\bigotimes_{d_j>0}(\operatorname{Sym}^{d_j}V)^{(p^j)}
   \longrightarrow \operatorname{Sym}^d V
$$
by multiplying the Frobenius powers of the monomials. Here the twist means that a basis change acts on coefficients by its $p^j$th power. On monomial bases the map sends a tuple of exponent vectors to their digitwise sum with powers $p^j$. In each coordinate the digit is less than $p$, so there are no carries and the exponent tuple is recovered uniquely. The image is the span of the ordinary powers: the expansion of $v^{d}=\prod_j (v^{d_j})^{p^j}$ has each of their coefficients a nonzero constant times a distinct monomial in the coordinates of $v$. Over an infinite field there is no linear relation among these coefficient functions. The first step rejects inputs outside this explicit subspace.

For each group $j$, insert $\operatorname{Sym}^{d_j}(V^{(p^j)})$ into $(V^{(p^j)})^{\otimes d_j}$ by symmetrizing, which is valid since $d_j!$ is invertible. In this partially symmetric tensor, take the one-mode support $U_j$ of each active group. Put $J=\max\{j:d_j>0\}$ and form a matrix of all support columns in a common twist $V^{(p^J)}$ by raising the entries of each group's contraction matrix to the $p^{J-j}$ power. Its column span is the image under the $p^J$ Frobenius twist of the sum $U$ of the inverse-twisted supports. Under a basis change all column spans transform by the same top twist, so this is an equivariant test.

For a sum of $r$ powers, that matrix has rank at most $r$ because each $U_j$ is contained in the $j$th twist of the span of the $r$ underlying vectors. The bound survives closure since the combined matrix uses polynomial entries in the given coefficients and its minors vanish. For any input passing the bound, symmetry within each group implies that the partially symmetric tensor is supported in $U$ in all groups after undoing the twists. This follows by completing bases of the supports and reading the tensor coordinates. In the original polynomial model the input therefore belongs to $\operatorname{Sym}^d U$. Projection to $U$ commutes with the ordinary power map, so the same inclusion and projection argument reduces exact ambient membership to the secant membership problem over $U$.

### The exact final test

In each of the dimensions $m=0,\ldots,r$, precompute the equations of the closure of the $r$-sum map by elimination. If its coordinates are $P_\alpha(v_1,\ldots,v_r)$, eliminate the vector entries from the ideal generated by $z_\alpha-P_\alpha(v_1,\ldots,v_r)$. The elimination theorem gives the vanishing ideal of the closure and evaluating its finite generators is necessary and sufficient. All dimensions, map degrees, and elimination data in this step depend only on $p,d,r$. The polynomial-sized ambient work consists of flattening, support recovery and restricting to a support basis. This is an exact criterion in the fixed-degree, fixed-secant-bound regime. [Characteristic-zero multiplicity context](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04576)
