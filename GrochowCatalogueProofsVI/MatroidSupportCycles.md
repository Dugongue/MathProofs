# Matroid representation classes from cycles of the fundamental support

Fix a labeled basis of a rank-$r$ matroid. Write a representation over $\mathbb F_q$ as $[I_r\mid A]$ using that basis. The bipartite support graph $J$ has one side for basis elements and one for the remaining elements. Join a row and column when the entry of $A$ is nonzero. The supports of any two representations of this labeled matroid agree, since every entry represents a single-element basis exchange. Labeled projective equivalence permits row basis change and independent nonzero column scales. On the displayed charts this is exactly scaling the rows and columns of $A$ independently. Choosing a spanning forest and normalizing its entries to one gives each projective class a unique vector of nonzero chord entries. Indeed a scaling preserving those forest entries has constant row scale and reciprocal column scale on each component.

## GP-0322. Exact class count for cactus supports

Suppose $J$ is a cactus, meaning any two distinct simple cycles share at most one vertex. Let $u$ count the cycles whose square row-by-column submatrix in $A$ has nonzero determinant. The number of labeled projective representation classes of this matroid over $\mathbb F_q$ is exactly $(q-2)^u$, with value one when $u=0$. The formula covers varying field size. It counts representations of the labeled matroid with line scales and all invertible row basis changes factored out.

For a simple cycle of length $2s$, its alternating edge ratio is the product of one alternating set of entries divided by the other product. The cycle has no chord, and its two perfect matchings give a determinant that vanishes precisely for ratio $(-1)^s$. Take the spanning forest by deleting one edge of each cycle. In forest-normalized coordinates each ratio is the independent variable of that cycle or its inverse. It remains to show that the zero-status bits for these cycle minors determine every basis.

Consider an arbitrary square row-by-column submatrix. If its support has no perfect matching, its determinant vanishes. Otherwise choose a perfect matching $M$. The symmetric difference of any other perfect matching with $M$ is a union of vertex-disjoint $M$-alternating cycles. In a cactus, two different $M$-alternating cycles cannot meet at a vertex, since both would have to use the same incident $M$-edge there. All subsets of the available cycles may therefore be flipped independently. The determinant factors as the nonzero signed $M$-monomial times one factor $1+(-1)^{s-1}\rho$ for each available cycle of length $2s$ with flip ratio $\rho$. Its vanishing is determined by the cycle bits. Every square minor gives the basis test obtained by excluding its rows from the displayed basis and including its columns. The ratio choices thus realize exactly the same labeled matroid. Each zero cycle bit forces one nonzero value, and each nonzero bit permits $q-2$. This proves the count. ## GP-0323. Fixed-parameter rigidity beyond cactus supports

Put $k=|E(J)|-|V(J)|+\#\{\text{components of }J\}$. Given two displayed representations with the same support, equality of their labeled matroids is decidable with $2^{O(k)}$ determinant comparisons of polynomial-size matrices. Consequently labeled projective rigidity is decidable with $2^{O(k)}(q-1)^k$ such comparisons. The first bound is independent of the numerical field size. The second gives a nontrivial fixed-parameter algorithm in $(k,q)$ even when the support contains overlapping cycles.

Keep only the edges that lie on cycles. This changes no cycle, gives minimum degree at least two at nonisolated vertices, and leaves cycle rank $k$. In each connected component with a vertex of degree at least three, suppress the maximal paths whose internal vertices have degree two. If its cycle rank is $h$, the resulting multigraph has at most $2h-2$ vertices and $3h-3$ edges by the degree sum. A component consisting of a cycle supplies one whole cycle in place of these paths. There are at most $3k$ paths or whole cycles in total. Every cycle is a union of some of these paths or whole cycles.

Compare the zero status of determinants on square vertex sets obtained as follows. For each subset of these at most $3k$ paths or whole cycles, take the rows and columns at its vertices and keep the test if their counts agree. To see sufficiency, fix any square submatrix and one perfect matching $M$ in its support. If there is none, both determinants vanish. Let $C$ be the union of all its simple $M$-alternating cycles. Every vertex of $C$ is matched by $M$ to another vertex of $C$. Every perfect matching agrees with $M$ off $C$. Conversely a perfect matching on the submatrix induced by the vertices of $C$ extends by the fixed matching edges on the complement. By the determinant expansion, up to a fixed sign, the original determinant is the determinant on $C$ times the product of the fixed matching entries. This argument works for both numeric matrices with the common support, including $C$ empty. The vertex set of $C$ occurs among the enumerated tests. Equality of the zero status of all square minors follows. For rigidity, enumerate the $(q-1)^k$ forest-normalized chord assignments, check matroid equality by these tests, and accept precisely when only one assignment remains. The bit overhead per comparison is polynomial in the matrix size and finite-field encoding length.
