# Cayley-table epimorphisms injective on a marked subgroup

## GP-0350

Suppose the target has the form $H=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t\times B$, with each $S_i$ nonabelian simple and $B$ an arbitrary finite abelian group. Given Cayley tables of $G,H$ and any subgroup $L\le G$, there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm to decide and construct an epimorphism $G\to H$ injective on $L$. The target may have repeated simple factors and cyclic abelian factors of arbitrary prime powers. The algorithm uses no restriction on how $L$ sits inside $G$.

### A lemma on abelian groups

Let $A,B$ be finite abelian groups and $U\le A$. Work prime by prime, writing their $p$-parts as sums of cyclic groups of orders $p^i$ with multiplicities $a_i,b_i$. In the socle $A[p]$ of this $p$-part, let $F_t$ be the span of the elements of order $p$ from summands of lengths $i\ge t$. Here $U[p]$ refers to the $p$-torsion of the $p$-part of $U$. There is a surjective homomorphism $A\to B$ injective on $U$ if and only if for each prime and each $t\ge1$,
$$
\sum_{i\ge t}a_i \ \ge\  \sum_{i\ge t}b_i \ \ge\ \dim_{\mathbb F_p}(U[p]\cap F_t).
$$

Consider a matrix block of a homomorphism from cyclic summands of length $i$ to those of length $j$. Its entry is divisible by $p^{\max(j-i,0)}$. On the quotient modulo $p$ the block can be independently prescribed over $\mathbb F_p$ for $i\ge j$, and is zero otherwise. On the socles the block can be prescribed for $i\le j$, and is zero otherwise. The two prescriptions agree on diagonal blocks, and the strict off-diagonal prescriptions are independent. A homomorphism is onto if and only if its map modulo $p$ is onto, by the finite form of Nakayama's lemma. It is injective on $U$ if and only if the map on $U[p]$ is injective, since a nonzero subgroup of a finite abelian $p$-group contains an element of order $p$. The triangular restrictions give the respective displayed inequalities on the long targets modulo $p$ and on the long source vectors of $U[p]$.

For sufficiency, the socle action induced by automorphisms of $A$ is the whole parabolic subgroup preserving the flag $(F_t)$. Indeed every block-triangular matrix preserving that flag lifts by the socle prescriptions, and invertible diagonal blocks already make the lifted map onto modulo $p$. Such a lift is bijective. Adapt a basis of $U[p]$ to the flag and extend it to an adapted basis of $A[p]$. Thus an automorphism moves $U[p]$ to the subspace spanned in each length $i$ by the first $u_i=\dim(U[p]\cap F_i)-\dim(U[p]\cap F_{i+1})$ coordinate vectors. In each equal-length block match the first $\min(a_i,b_i)$ source and target coordinates by an identity matrix. For the map modulo $p$, match every remaining target coordinate of length $j$ to an unused source coordinate of larger length. This is possible greedily in descending length: after deleting the same number of equal-length pairs from both tails, the first displayed inequality is unchanged. For injection on the marked socle, match each remaining marked source coordinate of length $i$ to an unused target of larger length. The tail inequality is again unchanged by the equal-length marked matches, so the same descending greedy argument applies. The former matches use blocks strictly above the equal-length condition and the latter use the opposite strict blocks. The resulting prescriptions lift together by the block descriptions. Compose with the automorphism putting $U[p]$ in position.

These operations are polynomial in the sizes of Cayley tables. For abelian groups given this way one may use the integer presentation with one variable per element and relations $e_g+e_h=e_{gh}$. Smith normal form with coordinate maps gives the cyclic decompositions. The other steps use finite-field linear algebra, ordered matching and lifts of residues. Empty source or target factors are allowed.

### Reducing the marked problem to the lemma

The simple quotient-kernel procedure enumerates all $k$ distinct nonabelian simple kernels of $G$. It constructs their quotient maps and satisfies $2^k\le |G|$, since the joint map onto the simple factors is surjective. For a repeated simple type $S$ in $H$, enumerate subsets of source kernels of that type of the prescribed size. The total simultaneous enumeration has at most $2^k\le |G|$ choices. Kernel choices match the target simple types in polynomial time. The target table itself supplies its nonabelian simple direct factors among the minimal normal subgroups, and its abelian direct factor is its center. [Simple quotient enumeration and type matching](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/a32630798eebd129ef621986ca4e0b8ce5edfcf3/GrochowCatalogueProofsV/SemisimpleMarkedQuotients.md)

For one admissible choice, let $K$ be the intersection of its simple kernels and set $L_0=L\cap K$. The choice gives a map onto the nonabelian product. Every epimorphism with that simple kernel selection is injective on $L$ exactly when its abelian coordinate is injective on $L_0$. Any abelian coordinate factors through $A=G/G'$. Test whether the projection of $L_0$ into $A$ is injective. If so, let $U$ be its image and apply the lemma to $A,B,U$. The output abelian coordinate is onto. Its joint map with the selected simple coordinates is onto $H$: by Goursat's lemma any proper subdirect product would identify a nontrivial common quotient of a perfect group and an abelian group. The resulting epimorphism is injective on $L$. Conversely every feasible epimorphism supplies one of the enumerated choices and passes the corresponding abelian tests. This proves the algorithm.
