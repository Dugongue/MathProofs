# Presented tensor order reduction with transporter recovery

## GP-0104

Fix any finite commutative ring $R$. Isomorphism of densely encoded tensors of arbitrary input order on finitely presented $R$-modules reduces in polynomial time to a single isomorphism instance for three free $R$-modules. A transporter of the resulting free three-tensors yields a transporter of the original tensors in polynomial time. The input gives dense relation matrices and a dense tensor representative in the free covers. As usual the declared ranks of empty matrices and the names of their basis coordinates are charged to the input size. The dimension and time exponents may depend on $R$. The argument applies to nonfree factors and to unbounded tensor order. It extends the constant-order [presented-module reduction and guard construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/a32630798eebd129ef621986ca4e0b8ce5edfcf3/GrochowCatalogueProofsV/PresentedModuleTensorReduction.md).

### Algebra of free presentations

Write $M_i=F_i/\operatorname{im}D_i$ with free source and target for each $D_i$. For paired factors, pad free covers to the larger rank by adding generators mapping to zero. Add their coordinate vectors to the relations. Pad relation domains by zero columns to align their ranks. Equal-rank free covers of isomorphic finite $R$-modules can be matched by invertible maps lifting any chosen quotient isomorphism. This also holds for two presentations of each relation image. Indeed reduce to the finite local factors of $R$. By Nakayama, a basis of the target modulo the maximal ideal lifts to a subset of the images of a suitably changed source basis. Clear every other source image by subtracting combinations of this subset. Comparing the resulting minimal presentations gives an invertible matrix since it is invertible modulo the maximal ideal, and unused source coordinates may be matched arbitrarily. Combine the maps over the local factors.

Tensors in the quotient are represented in $F=F_1\otimes\cdots\otimes F_d$ modulo the sum $K$ of the relation images tensored with the other free covers. One obtains its generators by tensoring each relation column with all basis products in the other coordinates. Testing whether a representative is zero in the quotient is polynomial time in this dense representation. Membership in the $R$-span can be solved as a finite abelian group linear system: include scalar multiples of generators for an additive generating set of $R$, and use Smith normal form of the resulting integer congruence system. The zero-cover case has zero tensor immediately. The zero ring and tensor order zero have trivial direct comparisons.

### Zero tensors with an unbounded number of factors

If exactly one input tensor is zero in the quotient, output a fixed negative free three-tensor instance. If both are zero, the task is simultaneous isomorphism of the labeled modules. Encode it by the two free spaces $V=\bigoplus_i F_i$ and $H=\bigoplus_i\operatorname{dom}D_i$, one linear map $D=\bigoplus_iD_i$, and $\lceil\log_2 d\rceil$ endomorphisms of $V$. The endomorphisms are diagonal idempotents projecting onto the sums of the $F_i$ for which each successive bit of $i-1$ is one.

A diagram isomorphism preserves the intersections of their kernels and images, which distinguish the individual covers. It carries $\operatorname{im}D$ to its counterpart. Its restriction to each cover therefore descends to the corresponding module isomorphism. Conversely any independent module isomorphisms lift to a block diagonal map on $V$ preserving the labeled relation images. The free-surjection lifting fact gives a map on $H$ completing a diagram isomorphism. Thus there are only $O(1+\log d)$ maps and objects even for zero tensors of arbitrarily large order. A diagram transporter directly gives the requested module maps.

### Nonzero tensors and the logarithmic diagram

Now every free cover of both inputs has positive rank. With $N$ the total input size, the product of the padded cover ranks is at most $N^2$, since $\max(a,b)\le ab$ for positive integers. At most $2\log_2 N$ padded ranks exceed one. The other factors are cyclic modules $R/I_i$. Check that the two annihilator ideals agree at each such position, as required for isomorphism, and thus identify both with the same quotient of $R$. Tensor all these cyclic factors together, leaving a single cyclic factor $R/(\sum_i I_i)$ with one-generator cover, or omit the collapsed factor when there are no cyclic factors. The dense representative of the tensor is naturally read in this shortened presentation because each collapsed cover has rank one.

The collapsed family preserves and reflects isomorphism. Independent automorphisms of cyclic factors tensor to multiplication by a unit on their product. Conversely any unit of $R/I$ with $I=\sum_i I_i$ lifts to a unit of $R$. On a local factor where $I$ is proper this follows because a unit modulo $I$ lifts outside the maximal ideal. On a local factor where $I$ is the whole factor take the lift one. Apply this lifted unit to one of the original cyclic factors and take the identity on the others. This also describes how to extend a transporter of the shortened instance.

Name the shortened free covers $F_1,\ldots,F_m$, where $m=O(1+\log N)$. Include the relation maps in a diagram. Build the full cover tensor product through the named free prefixes $E_i$ with bilinear product maps $E_i\times F_{i+1}\to E_{i+1}$, starting at $E_1=F_1$. If $m=1$, use the single free space without such a map. In the final free product include one extra relation map with image $K+R\tilde t$, where $\tilde t$ is the supplied tensor representative and $K$ the tensor-product relation sum. All spaces and matrices have polynomial size because every prefix dimension is at most $N^2$ and there are $O(1+\log N)$ factors.

Input transporters lift to diagram transporters by the lifting fact. Conversely the product arrows force the map on the final free product to be the tensor product of the cover maps. The relation arrows give quotient transporters taking $Rt$ to $Rt'$ in the target module tensor product. Two generators of the same cyclic submodule of a finite $R$-module differ by a unit of $R$. Over each local factor with nonzero cyclic submodule, a generator is nonzero modulo its maximal-ideal multiple by Nakayama, forcing the linking scalar to be a unit. Choose the scalar one on zero factors. To recover such a unit, test the finitely many units of the fixed ring by the same relation membership algorithm. Multiply one shortened module map by the appropriate inverse scalar to carry the tensor exactly, then lift the collapsed cyclic-factor action back to the original ordered tuple.

### A single ordinary three-tensor

Apply the earlier presentation-diagram encoding to either diagram. Each named free object is copied in the three modes, with identity bridge actions to link its copies. One marked scalar line per linear map encodes that arrow. Each bilinear arrow occupies the block with its named input pair and named output. The named input pairs here are distinct. Each output block is the image of a bridge, as required by the earlier guard proof. The reduction and recovery therefore give the named diagram transporter from the block-preserving bilinear transporter. [Bridge and guard construction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/a32630798eebd129ef621986ca4e0b8ce5edfcf3/GrochowCatalogueProofsV/PresentedModuleTensorReduction.md)

The earlier guard proof needs only that the expanded dimensions remain polynomial. With $s=|R|$ it uses $L=16(s+1)^2$ and private guard dimensions $M L^j$, with $M$ linear in the ranks and the number of guards for fixed $R$. There are two guards per opposite block, hence $O(1+\log N)$ guard lines here. Thus the largest guard dimension is $N^{O_R(1)}$ and the dense free three-tensors can be constructed in polynomial time. The guard proof recovers the underlying block action by restriction and quotient in known coordinates, followed by reduction of the bridge scalars. Given the ordinary free-tensor transporter, dualize the first two modes, apply those restrictions and quotients, and use the zero or nonzero recovery above. All recovered module homomorphisms are output as cover matrices inducing the stated quotient isomorphisms.

This proves the order reduction with actual transporters for each fixed finite ring and dense input. The exponent estimate depends on the fixed ring, and the argument gives no claim for arbitrary infinite commutative rings.
