# A mixed trichotomy for coherent quadratic Postnikov diagrams

The theorem classifies one mixed relative extension problem on finite diagrams. The indexing category can have nontrivial endomorphisms, loops and automorphisms. The three branches use the same coherent boundary data, integral cycle probes and arithmetic language. The proof is informal.

## Fixed data and input

Fix a finite category $I$ and an even integer $d\ge2$. Fix functors $L,G:I\to\mathsf{Ab}$ with $L_i$ finitely generated free abelian and $G_i$ finitely generated abelian at every object $i$. Fix bilinear maps $\beta_i:L_i\times L_i\to G_i$ such that for every arrow $f:i\to j$,

$$
G(f)\beta_i(u,v)=\beta_j(L(f)u,L(f)v).
$$

Write $B_i(u,v)=\beta_i(u,v)+\beta_i(v,u)$ and let $H_i$ denote the subgroup of $G_i$ generated by its values. Bases of $L_i$ are fixed. Put $\kappa_i(a)=\beta_i(a\smile a)$, where the cup product before applying coefficients takes values in $L_i\otimes L_i$. Define the Kan diagram $W$ objectwise by the homotopy fibre of the natural map $K(L_i,d)\to K(G_i,2d)$ represented by $\kappa_i$. Its only possible nonzero homotopy groups are $L_i$ in degree $d$ and $G_i$ in degree $2d-1$.

Fix an equality–congruence arithmetic template $M$ with finitely many scalar integer and finite sorts and a fixed finite signature. Each integer sort has addition and a pin for $1$. After fixing its finite arguments, each basic relation is a Boolean combination of affine equations and congruences. Each basis coordinate of each $L_i$ is assigned one integer sort. Assignments and matrix coefficients may vary across diagram arrows. Let $M^I$ be $M$ with the exact vector relation $R_f(u,v)\iff v=L(f)u$ adjoined for each arrow, with each scalar argument in its assigned sort. Write $Q((M^I)_0)$ for the condition that every exact finite modular pp image of its arithmetic core has a Taylor polymorphism. We use the effective classification of this arithmetic language, including constructive polynomial time when $Q$ holds and NP-hardness of the core when it fails. [Arithmetic theorem and witness construction](../EqualityCongruenceCSP/N_Complete.md) · [Arithmetic hardness proof](../EqualityCongruenceCSP/HARDNESS.md)

The input consists of an explicit finite diagram of finite simplicial pairs $A\hookrightarrow X$ indexed by $I$. Structure maps may identify simplices. The input supplies a coherent boundary map into $W$, represented by a strict map $QA\to W$ on the projective bar resolution defined below. It also supplies integral degree-$d$ cycles on the original $X_i$, with affine equalities tying coordinate periods on those cycles to integer variables in the same sort, and a pp instance of $M$ on shared and auxiliary variables. Coordinates at different objects may share variables of their assigned sorts. Coefficients and integers are binary. Empty boundaries and unobserved objects are allowed. The decision problem asks whether the coherent boundary extends with all observations and arithmetic atoms satisfied.

## Theorem

| Condition on the fixed data | Complexity |
| --- | --- |
| Some $H_i$ is infinite | $\Sigma^0_1$-complete under computable many-one reductions |
| All $H_i$ are finite and at least one is nonzero | NP-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions |
| Every $H_i=0$ and $Q((M^I)_0)$ fails | NP-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions |
| Every $H_i=0$ and $Q((M^I)_0)$ holds | Deterministic polynomial time with polynomial-bit constructive witnesses |

The case is decidable from the fixed finite data. The table conditions are disjoint. The arithmetic relation of every arrow is itself realized in the same geometrical problem, so enlarging $M$ to $M^I$ in the criterion does not change the input rules.

## Coherent maps and finite computation

For a diagram $X$ define $QX$ as the diagonal two-sided bar. A $q$-simplex at $j$ has data

$$
i_0\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow i_q\overset{\alpha}{\longrightarrow}j,
\qquad x\in X_q(i_0).
$$

Its faces apply the face of $x$ and the corresponding bar face, with the first arrow acting on $x$ at face zero. Degeneracies insert an identity and apply the corresponding degeneracy to $x$. The augmentation applies the composite to $x$. In the horizontal augmented bar at each $j$, appending the final arrow followed by $1_j$ gives an extra degeneracy, so the augmentation is an objectwise weak equivalence. A diagonal simplex is degenerate exactly when some internal arrow is an identity and $x$ is in the image of the matching degeneracy. This criterion is independent of the final arrow $\alpha$. Thus each nondegenerate simplex pattern gives a representable cell $I(i_q,-)\times\Delta[q]$ attached along its boundary. The same relative construction makes $QA\hookrightarrow QX$ a projective cofibration. Since $W$ is objectwise Kan, restriction of natural mapping spaces is a fibration, and its fibre over the given $QA\to W$ computes coherent relative extension. At each $i$ the all-identity strings give a simplicial section $s_i:X_i\to QX_i$ of the augmentation. Read cycles on $(s_i)_*z$. These sections are only needed objectwise. [Projective bar models for diagram mapping problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10152)

In normalized cochains a map $QX\to W$ is exactly a pair $(a,h)$ of natural cochains of degrees $d,2d-1$ respectively, with

$$
\delta a=0,\qquad \delta h=\kappa(a),\qquad
a|_{QA}=a_A,\qquad h|_{QA}=h_A.
$$

Here a natural $L$-cochain satisfies $(QX)(f)^*a_j=L(f)a_i$, and similarly for $G$. This representation follows from the cocycle model $K(H,n)_q=Z^n(\Delta[q];H)$ and the contractible path model $C^{n-1}(\Delta[\bullet];H)\xrightarrow{\delta}K(H,n)$. Only degrees through $2d$ need to be listed. The fixed category and degree give only a constant number of bar strings per source simplex in those degrees. The number of derived simplices, and the total size of explicit cochain data needed, are therefore polynomial in the input even when the full bar is infinite.

Natural absolute and relative cochain groups are computed from their integer equations and congruences using presentations of the fixed coefficient groups. On the bar, a cochain can also be specified by its value at the generating object of each representable cell. Working with all objectwise coordinates is convenient for the reduction below. All kernels, cokernels and representatives appearing in the proof can be computed by integer normal forms with polynomial bit length. [Kannan–Bachem normal forms](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

## The relative obstruction identity

Find an integral natural cocycle $a^0$ extending $a_A$, and a natural cochain $h^0$ extending $h_A$. These are linear solves and failure of either gives rejection. Let $U$ be the lattice of relative natural integral $d$-cocycles. Set

$$
T=C^{2d}_{\rm nat}(QX,QA;G)\big/\delta C^{2d-1}_{\rm nat}(QX,QA;G).
$$

For any allowable lower cochain $a$, the upper extension exists exactly when $o(a)=[\kappa(a)-\delta h^0]=0$ in $T$. For any $u\in U$, the relative cup-one identity gives

$$
o(a+u)-o(a)=[\kappa(u)]+[B(a\smile u)],
\qquad 2[\kappa(u)]=[B(u\smile u)].
$$

Indeed cup-one is natural and interchanges the two closed degree-$d$ cup factors up to an integral coboundary, with positive sign since $d$ is even. Applying the natural coefficient map $\beta$ preserves the identity in the natural complex. The primitive for a term with one relative argument restricts to zero, by bilinearity. These identities hold with literal boundary values and do not require a pointwise splitting of $\beta$ into symmetric and skew parts. [Cochain cup-one identities](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpaa.2020.106444)

## Polynomial time and the zero-polar arithmetic hardness

Suppose all $B_i=0$. The map $u\mapsto[\kappa(u)]$ is additive on $U$ by the identity above, has image in $T[2]$, and $o(a^0+u)=o(a^0)+[\kappa(u)]$. Compute an integral basis of $U$ and evaluate this map on it. In Smith coordinates of $T$, a free coordinate or an odd-order cyclic coordinate has no variable contribution, and an even cyclic coordinate has each variable contribution equal to $0$ or half its order. Compare the fixed origin in each row. If it is outside the subgroup that the row could attain, reject. Otherwise the row reduces to an affine parity equation (or a constant check) on basis coefficients.

To express these parity tests in the assigned sorts even for matrices mixing sorts, retain the raw cochain variables. The relative lattice $U$ is saturated in their ambient free integer lattice because it is a kernel of integer equations. Hence the reductions of an integral basis are linearly independent modulo two. Gaussian elimination gives a left inverse there and extends each parity row to a linear parity equation in the raw coordinates of $a-a^0$. Aggregate its terms within each integer sort by ordinary addition. Each resulting parity test has at most one argument per sort, with a fixed finite collection of possibilities. The lower cocycle, boundary and observation equations use addition and pins within sorts, with binary coefficients represented by doubling. Impose naturality with the fixed relations $R_f$ of $M^I$ on each relevant simplex, using zero for any degenerate image. Copy the original arithmetic atoms.

Adjoining fixed cross-sort linear residue relations preserves the exact modular Taylor criterion. For completeness, at any requested modulus $N$ pass first to a common multiple $D$ of $N$ and all the new moduli. The arithmetic Taylor family at $D$ gives a common prime-arity cyclic operation for a sufficiently large prime $p$ on the sorted carriers, by the finite cyclic-term theorem applied to the product algebra. Preservation of addition, cyclicity and idempotence force its numerical action on every sort to be $p^{-1}$ times the sum modulo $D$. It preserves the added linear residue relations. For any pp formula retain all quantified variables, take the arithmetic-only exact image at $D$, intersect with the added residue atoms, and then project. The operation preserves the intersection and descends to $N$. The reverse implication follows by taking the reduct. Arithmetic core retraction keeps numerical variables fixed, so this argument applies to the cores, including after adjoining the numerical relations $R_f$. [Barto–Kozik cyclic terms](https://lmcs.episciences.org/673)

The resulting polynomial-size arithmetic instance thus has the constructive polynomial-time algorithm under $Q((M^I)_0)$. Use the returned $a$ and solve the relative top linear coboundary equation for $h-h^0$. All witnesses have polynomial bit length.

Conversely, for any object $i$ and finite simplicial pair $(C,D)$ let the free representable pair at $i$ have values $I(i,j)\times(C,D)$ at $j$, with arrows acting by postcomposition. Projective cofibrancy and the enriched Yoneda adjunction identify its coherent relative maps with the relative maps $(C,D)\to W_i$. This identification preserves the periods on the identity copy and sends its map to the copy indexed by $f:i\to j$ using $W(f)$. Taking $C=S^d$ and empty boundary, both sides' periods realize exactly $R_f(u,v)$ because every $u$ is allowable on $S^d$. Use one such carrier for every new $R_f$ atom and share its variables with the rest of the arithmetic instance. This gives a reduction from CSP of $M^I$, including its core via the usual retraction equivalence. If $Q((M^I)_0)$ fails, this gives NP-hardness using the same boundary and observations as above.

## Finite-polar NP bound and enumeration

If all polar images are finite, choose a fixed positive even $m$ annihilating every $H_i$. Guess the residues modulo $m$ of the raw lower cochain entries and the finite arithmetic values. Any two natural cocycles with those residues and the given boundary differ by $m u$ for $u\in U$, since the natural cocycle equations are integral. The relative identity gives $o(a+mu)-o(a)=m[B(a\smile u)]+m^2[\kappa(u)]=0$. The first term vanishes since $m$ annihilates the polar values. The second vanishes by the second identity and the fact that $m^2/2$ is a multiple of $m$. This works even when $\beta$ has a skew part of infinite order.

For each residue assignment, first impose the remaining linear lower constraints and the guessed truth values of each Boolean equality–congruence fibre. For a failed congruence guess a nonzero residue witnessing failure. Integer normal forms give the solution lattice to the positive equations and congruences, or reject. Remaining disequalities are constant or remove proper hyperplanes in the free integer parameters. With rank $r>0$, evaluate those forms on $(t,t^2,\ldots,t^r)$. The nontrivial forms become nonzero integer polynomials of degree at most $r$, so one of the first $1+r k$ nonnegative integers works for $k$ forms. A rank-zero lattice is checked directly. This realizes the guessed data with polynomial bit length. Test the obstruction using this representative and a top linear solve. Its value is independent of the representative by the preceding computation. This is an NP verifier. Without the finite-polar condition, enumeration of all integral and finite cochain entries and arithmetic witnesses gives computable enumerability.

## The two topology-induced hardness reductions

Choose an object $i$ and coordinate vectors $u,v$ with $g=B_i(u,v)\ne0$. A basis pair suffices, and an infinite polar image yields a basis pair of infinite order because the basis values generate the image subgroup. Take

$$
C=(S^d\times S^d)\mathbin{\#}(S^d\times S^d)
$$

with compatible orientations. Its four degree-$d$ axis cycles form a basis. The only nonzero pair products among their duals are complementary axes in each summand, with positive sign for both orders. Its $G_i$-cohomology in degree $2d$ is identified with $G_i$ by evaluation on the fundamental class, since $H_{2d-1}(C;\mathbb Z)=0$. The free representable diagram at $i$ reduces the extension question to this carrier and permits the same type of integral period probes as above.

Give the four axis periods of $a$ the vectors $u x,v y,-u z,v$. This is data in the assigned scalar sorts, since the first vector can be supported on one basis coordinate and the second on one basis coordinate. Their prescribed values determine the degree-$d$ class and give the top obstruction exactly $g(xy-z)$. The alternate periods $u x,v,-u,v y$ impose $g(x-y)=0$, a copy between the two sorts. Both gadgets use empty boundary and fixed finite triangulated carriers.

For infinite-order $g$ these are exact multiplication and transfer over the integers. With addition and $1$ they express any system of integer arithmetic gates, giving $\Sigma^0_1$-hardness by the Diophantine representation theorem. For finite $g$ of order $n>1$, take a prime $p\mid n$ and replace $u$ by $(n/p)u$. The two gadgets now enforce multiplication and transfer modulo $p$. Boolean input is expressed by $x^2=x$, conjunction by multiplication and negation by $1-x$. A satisfying Boolean circuit gives integer representatives obeying the constraints, and a solution reduces to a Boolean satisfying assignment modulo $p$. This gives NP-hardness. In both reductions, occurrences are disjoint representable carriers in one input diagram, sharing arithmetic variables. [Matiyasevich's Diophantine theorem](https://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/~yumat/papers/21_paper/21_eng.pdf)

## A coherent example inside the polynomial-time branch

Take the fork $l\to c\leftarrow r$ and the constant target with $d=2$, $L=\mathbb Z$, $G=\mathbb F_2$ and $\beta(x,y)=xy\bmod2$. Its polar vanishes. On the source take $X_l=X_r=S^2$ and $X_c=\mathbb{CP}^2\#\mathbb{CP}^2$, with the two arrows representing the two projective-line generators. Take empty boundary. Pin the period at $l$ to $1$. Link the period at $r$ to a finite parity bit and pin the bit to $0$. The arithmetic language of integer addition and pins with this parity bit satisfies the Taylor criterion, as checked by cofinal even moduli and the induced Mal'cev operations. The coherent arrow equations force central coordinates with parities $1,0$. The square modulo two evaluates to $1$ on the top class. Hence no coherent lift exists. Every individual object is feasible with its local observations, and each one-arrow restriction is feasible by choosing the unobserved central period appropriately. All linear arrow equations are feasible jointly. Replacing the centre by $S^2\vee S^2\vee S^4$ gives a lift with the same groups and degree-two arrow restrictions, since the cup squares on the wedge vanish. Thus one instance uses both coherence and arithmetic to activate a topological obstruction, even in the tractable branch.

For this quadratic family, $B_i=0$ at every object also gives a natural integral homotopy multiplication on the target. On bottom cochains use addition, and on top cochains correct addition by the natural cup-one primitive of the cross term. It vanishes on the axes. Conversely, testing a multiplication on $S^d\times S^d$ at each object shows that any such structure forces $B_i=0$. The same argument over $\mathbb Q$ detects all polar images being finite. Coherence obstructions from general nonquadratic or non-natural higher invariants are outside the theorem.

## A boundary obstruction visible only in higher coherence

Let $I$ be the face poset of a triangulated $S^d$ and take the constant source diagram $(S^d,\{*\})$. Take the constant target whose obstruction is $a\smile b$ on two integral degree-$d$ coordinates. Colimit of the projective bar gives the carrier pair $(N(I)\times S^d,N(I)\times\{*\})$, where $N(I)$ is the nerve. In this model prescribe the boundary $a$ to be a generator on $N(I)$, with $b=0$ and upper cochain zero. Its objectwise and one-arrow boundary data are trivial because the prescribed class is in degree $d\ge2$. At a single object probe the periods of $a,b$ on its $S^d$, prescribing $0,t$, and pin $t=1$ in the arithmetic instance. The boundary and probe force $[a],[b]$ to be the two factor generators. Their product evaluates to $1$ on the top class, so there is no coherent lift. Every restriction to an individual object or arrow is feasible. Keeping the coherent boundary and pinning $t=0$ gives a lift, as does keeping $t=1$ and setting the full coherent boundary to zero. Thus the obstruction depends on arithmetic and on genuinely higher coherent boundary data.

The constant-target observation also extends the earlier [three-stage trichotomy](../HigherPostnikovTrichotomy/) to arbitrary fixed finite indexing categories with constant three-stage target. A map from $QX$ to a constant target is precisely a map from its colimit, the homotopy-colimit carrier. Apply the earlier single-carrier algorithm there with the boundary from the colimit of $QA$ and cycles transported by $s_i$. Only the fixed range of degrees from the three-stage cochain equations is required. The representable sources recover the hardness reductions. This corollary permits all three branches at three stages with a common coherent input interface, while the main theorem permits nonconstant target diagrams.
