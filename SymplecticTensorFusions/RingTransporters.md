# Exact symplectic tensor transport over commutative rings

The [Lean-checked symplectic reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TIEqTISp.lean) uses the hyperbolic module \(H(U)=U\oplus U^*\) with its split alternating form. Over a field, every transporter of the padded tensor induces one on the original tensor by extending isomorphisms of the intrinsic mode supports. Over a ring those supports can have torsion. We give two ring extensions, one preserving literal doubling and one preserving the entire transporter functor after every base change.

## 1. Results and scope

1. **Unmodified doubling over semilocal rings.** Let \(R\) be any commutative semilocal ring. For two tensors of the same finite free format, embedding all three factors into the primal summands of their hyperbolic doubles preserves and reflects isomorphism. The assertion holds for all tensors and prescribed finite lists, including zero tensors and tensors with nonfree mode supports. At each mode a symplectic witness can be corrected to an ordinary witness by a map vanishing on the whole active support.
2. **Exact correction factor over finite local rings.** Let \(R\) now be finite local with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) and residue size \(q\). Let \(M_i\subset R^{n_i}\) be the intrinsic source support in mode \(i\). Put \(e_i=n_i-\dim_k\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Ann}(M_i)\to(k^{n_i})^*)\). Then the literal doubling satisfies
   \[
   \#\operatorname{Trans}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(D(T),D(T'))
   =\#\operatorname{Trans}_{\mathrm{GL}}(T,T')
     \prod_{i=1}^3
     \left(
        |R|^{n_i(n_i+1)/2}
        \prod_{j=1}^{n_i-e_i}(q^j+1)
        \big|\{H\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n_i}(\mathfrak m): H M_i=0\}\big|
     \right).
   \tag{1}
   \]
   The factors are computable in deterministic polynomial bit time in explicit additive ring coordinates and multiplication structure constants. The same formula multiplies componentwise over a supplied product decomposition of a finite ring. Over finite fields, the last factor in parentheses is one, and \(e_i=\dim M_i\).
3. **Transporter schemes and presented modules over arbitrary rings.** Over every commutative ring \(R\), ordinary free three-tensor isomorphism and standard free symplectic three-tensor isomorphism have mutual linear-side-length reductions. With \(s=n_1+n_2+n_3\) for positive input sides, the forward map \(\Psi\) uses side \(2(s+3)\) in each target mode, inserts only input coefficients and constants, and gives the natural affine-scheme isomorphism
   \[
   \operatorname{Trans}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(\Psi(T),\Psi(T'))
   \simeq
   \operatorname{Trans}_{\mathrm{GL}}(T,T')\times
   \mathbb G_m^{6}\times
   \mathbb A^{3s+3(s+3)(s+4)/2}.
   \tag{2}
   \]
   It has a split homomorphism on stabilizers with smooth geometrically connected fibers, and an explicit recovery map for witnesses over any test ring. This yields a two-query polynomial-time reduction for exact counting over any explicitly presented finite commutative ring, without computing its local factors or factoring its characteristic. Composing with the presented-module compiler gives a polynomial-time reduction from ordinary isomorphism of three-tensors in arbitrary finitely presented modules to standard free symplectic three-tensor isomorphism over the same ring. This last decision equivalence commutes with every base change, including nonflat ones. Formula (2) concerns the free input columns, and formula (1) allows their supports to be nonfree.

All tensor actions here have independent transformation matrices in the three modes. The conclusions hold in characteristic two with the stated alternating form.

## 2. Correcting a padded witness

Write \(U_i=R^{n_i}\) and \(H(U_i)=U_i\oplus U_i^*\). The alternating pairing is
\[
\omega((u,\lambda),(v,\mu))=\mu(u)-\lambda(v).
\]
Let \(D(T)\) be the embedding in the three primal summands. The hyperbolic lift \(P\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(P,P^{-T})\) is symplectic and acts on the primal summand by \(P\). This proves the forward implication over any ring.

The support \(M_i\) is the submodule generated by the columns of the \(i\)-th flattening, and \(\operatorname{Ann}(M_i)\) consists of all rows vanishing on it. Since the other factors have bases, a linear map kills the tensor when applied in mode \(i\) exactly when it kills \(M_i\). Invertible mode maps carry the support onto the corresponding target support, by applying their inverse pullbacks to the other two dual factors. These facts use only free ambient modules, with no assertion that the support splits. For a list use the sum of the supports.

Suppose one padded transporter has blocks \(g_i=\begin{pmatrix}A_i&B_i\\ C_i&D_i\end{pmatrix}\). Its bottom-left block \(C_i\) annihilates \(M_i\), since it sends the padded source support into the target primal summand. The primal block of its tensor equation is
\[
 (A_1\otimes A_2\otimes A_3)T=T'.
\]
For a maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\), set \(E_i=(\operatorname{im}\operatorname{Ann}(M_i)\bmod\mathfrak m)^\perp\subset k^{n_i}\), using the ordinary evaluation pairing between rows and columns. Each row of \(\overline C_i\) vanishes on \(E_i\). The column block \(\binom{\overline A_i}{\overline C_i}\) is injective because \(g_i\) is invertible. Therefore \(\overline A_i\) restricts to an injection on \(E_i\). Over the residue field extend that restriction to an invertible full matrix by extending bases. Its difference from \(\overline A_i\) annihilates \(E_i\), so all its rows are reductions of rows in \(\operatorname{Ann}(M_i)\), by double annihilation over the field.

Choose such row lifts for each maximal ideal. The semilocal Chinese remainder theorem supplies coefficients with prescribed values one at one maximal ideal and zero at all the others, so their weighted sum yields one matrix of corrections with the required residues. Its rows still annihilate \(M_i\). The corrected matrix \(A_i'\) therefore agrees with \(A_i\) on the support and is invertible over \(R\), since its determinant is a unit in every residue field. Replacing the three factors one at a time in the tensor equation changes nothing. Thus the three corrected matrices are an ordinary transporter. The same argument applies to an arbitrary invertible \(g_i\) restricted to a submodule that it sends into the primal part. The alternating equation is needed for the canonical forward lift and for the counting argument below.

## 3. Counting raw padded transporters

Let \(U=R^n\), let \(M\subset U\) be any submodule, and use \(\operatorname{Fix}_{\mathrm{GL}}(M)\) and \(\operatorname{Fix}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(M)\) for its pointwise stabilizers in the original and doubled spaces. Partition the ordinary transporter set by its three restrictions to \(M_i\). Each nonempty part is a coset of the product of ordinary pointwise stabilizers. The same partition on the padded transporter has the same realized restrictions by Section 2 and the canonical forward lift. Its parts are cosets of the product of symplectic pointwise stabilizers. Thus the factor in mode \(i\) is the ratio of those two pointwise-stabilizer sizes. This also proves (1) on empty transporter sets once the ratio is evaluated.

Call a Lagrangian here an isotropic free direct summand of rank \(n\) in \(H(U)\). Any such submodule has a symplectic basis completion, over the finite local ring. Given a basis \(x_i\) of it, choose \(y_j\) with \(\omega(x_i,y_j)=\delta_{ij}\), using perfectness of the ambient pairing and the summand projection. Replace \(y_j\) by \(y_j+\sum_a x_a B_{aj}\), where the strictly upper triangular entries of \(B\) are \(B_{ij}=\omega(y_i,y_j)\) for \(i<j\). The new pairings among the \(y_i\) vanish, since the change in pairing \((i,j)\) is \(B_{ji}-B_{ij}\). The resulting square matrix is invertible because its Gram matrix is the invertible standard symplectic form. Thus a symplectic matrix \(w\) takes the standard primal space onto the chosen Lagrangian \(L\).

Every Lagrangian containing \(M\) lies in the orbit of \(U\) under \(\operatorname{Fix}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(M)\). Indeed the inverse of \(w\) just chosen takes \(M\) into the primal summand. Section 2 extends that restriction to an invertible \(h\) on \(U\). Multiplying \(w\) by the hyperbolic lift of \(h\) now fixes \(M\) and has image \(L\) on \(U\). The subgroup of this pointwise stabilizer preserving \(U\) consists exactly of
\[
\begin{pmatrix}P&PB\\0&P^{-T}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad P\in\operatorname{Fix}_{\mathrm{GL}}(M),\quad B=B^T\in M_n(R),
\]
as the alternating block equations show. Hence the ratio of stabilizer sizes is the number of Lagrangians containing \(M\), multiplied by \(|R|^{n(n+1)/2}\).

It remains to count those Lagrangians. Let \(E\subset k^n\) be the subspace defined in Section 2 and \(e=\dim E\). Choose \(n-e\) rows of \(\operatorname{Ann}(M)\) lifting a basis of its residue image. Extend those rows to an invertible matrix by lifting a residue-field basis completion. The common kernel of the chosen rows is a free direct summand \(U_E\subset U\) containing \(M\), with reduction exactly \(E\).

If \(L\) contains \(M\), choose an ambient symplectic \(w\) taking \(U\) onto \(L\). The bottom component of \(w^{-1}|_U\) annihilates \(M\), so its reduction vanishes on \(E\). It follows that \(\overline L\) contains \(E\). Conversely, given any residue Lagrangian \(L_0\) containing \(E\), it has a lift obtained from \(U\) by a symplectic map fixing \(U_E\) pointwise. To construct one, use the hyperbolic decomposition from a basis of \(U\) extending a basis of \(U_E\). The orthogonal quotient of the fixed primal \(E\) is the hyperbolic space of dimension \(2(n-e)\) on the remaining coordinates. The image of \(L_0\) there has a symplectic basis completion. Lift all vectors of that basis and apply symplectic Gram–Schmidt in the complementary free hyperbolic summand: the first prescribed pair has unit pairing, normalize it and subtract its pairings from the remaining vectors, then iterate. Each adjustment preserves the prescribed reductions, and no divided-by-two step is used. Extend the resulting symplectic map by the identity on the hyperbolic summand of \(U_E\). This proves the lift assertion.

For any one such fixed lift \(w\) with \(w(U)\bmod\mathfrak m=L_0\), apply \(w^{-1}\) to an arbitrary Lagrangian reducing to \(L_0\). It then reduces to \(U\). Its projection to \(U\) is invertible, so it is the graph of a unique matrix \(H\in M_n(\mathfrak m)\). Its isotropy says exactly \(H=H^T\). It contains \(M\) precisely when \(H M=0\), because \(w\) fixes \(M\). This counts all lifts of each residue Lagrangian by the same additive kernel size in (1).

The residue Lagrangians containing \(E\) correspond to all Lagrangians of the \(2(n-e)\)-dimensional symplectic quotient \(E^\perp/E\). In dimension \(2r\), double count pairs of an isotropic line and a Lagrangian containing it. Every line is isotropic. Writing \(L_r\) for the number of Lagrangians gives
\[
L_r\frac{q^r-1}{q-1}=\frac{q^{2r}-1}{q-1}L_{r-1},\qquad L_0=1.
\]
Thus the residue count is \(\prod_{j=1}^{n-e}(q^j+1)\), completing (1).

In explicit additive coordinates, equations for the annihilator rows and for \(H M=0\) are additive linear congruences using the listed flattening columns. Their kernels, sizes, and intersections are computed by integer Smith or Hermite normal form. A finite local ring has characteristic a prime power \(p^a\); extract \(p\) as the prime base of the characteristic via exact perfect-power tests. Compute \(\mathfrak m\) as the inverse image under \(R\to R/pR\) of the kernel of a sufficiently high Frobenius power. In this characteristic-\(p\) quotient Frobenius is additive and \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear. An iterate with iteration count \(\lceil \log_2|R|\rceil\) kills every nilpotent and no other elements. The image of the annihilator under reduction is another additive subgroup and has size \(q^{n-e}\). All powers, subgroup computations, and exact integer counts above have polynomial bit cost. The arguments and count apply independently in each supplied local factor. In particular, (1) gives an exact correction even when all the visible support elements are zero after residue reduction.

## 4. Exact transporter schemes over every base ring

To retain witnesses and counts uniformly without a semilocal premise, first use the [scheme-exact three-vertex multiplication encoder](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorIsomorphismAndAlgebraicCrossovers/Rings.md). This replaces a free tensor of sides \(n_1,n_2,n_3>0\) by the multiplication tensor of a finite free unital associative triangular algebra of rank \(m=s+3\). The original three factors are its three positive-rank off-diagonal corners, and their one nontrivial composition is the input tensor in bilinear-map coordinates. The encoder proves that the ordinary transporter, on every test algebra \(S\) over \(R\), is naturally the original transporter times \((S^\times)^6\times S^{3s}\). On stabilizers the projection is split. Its normalizing unit aligns the three diagonal idempotents and its two free units account for unital multiplication isotopy. The directed corner incidence fixes the three labels after testing each residue field, making the parameterization valid over arbitrary test rings.

Each multiplication tensor has full mode support over every \(S\). In the output mode multiplication by the unit yields the whole basis. In each input dual mode contracting the other input against the unit and the output against coordinate functionals yields the whole dual basis. Hyperbolically pad the multiplication tensor in each mode. Any symplectic transporter between the padded tensors preserves the entire primal summand in every mode, by support covariance, and therefore has the displayed upper triangular symplectic form in Section 3. Its primal blocks transport the multiplication tensors, and the three symmetric matrices are completely free. Conversely the hyperbolic lifts of the primal blocks and any three symmetric shears give exactly these transporters. These correspondences are given by matrix algebra and inverses of invertible matrices, and are natural on all test rings. They exhibit the additional factor \(\mathbb A^{3m(m+1)/2}\) and prove (2). Both the triangular algebra encoder and the hyperbolic lift split the projection on stabilizers, so the resulting scheme homomorphism splits and has smooth geometrically connected kernel. Inputs with a zero side have no tensor entries and can be handled as trivial comparisons of equal free formats.

For the reverse reduction, regard a symplectic comparison as a named mixed tensor system. It has the original three-tensor and one fixed order-two alternating form per mode. Free duality identifies preservation of a form tensor precisely with the symplectic matrix equation. The existing [fixed-type linear-length compiler over arbitrary commutative rings](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/IsomorphismCohomologyCrossovers/LinearLengthRings.md) outputs ordinary free three-tensor isomorphism, with linear side lengths and no division or rank tests. The two reductions prove the asserted equality in the free setting, natural under scalar extension. For arbitrary finitely presented source modules, first apply the [stabilized-cover and coevaluation compiler](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorUniversalityApplications/PRESENTED-MODULE-TENSOR-ISOMORPHISM.md). It passes to free three-tensor isomorphism over the same ring, preserving and reflecting existence of a transporter on every test ring even for nonflat base change. Follow with \(\Psi\). Since free modules are among finitely presented modules, the reverse reduction already returns instances in the larger model. This shows equality with the ordinary and symplectic free problems over every ring in the explicit presentation model. All construction steps copy entries and add fixed constants, and their output lengths are polynomial.

## 5. Exact counting without factoring the base ring

Over an arbitrary finite ring, (2) gives the exact correction \(|R^\times|^6|R|^{3s+3m(m+1)/2}\). The integer \(|R|\) is read directly from the additive-coordinate orders. To compute \(|R^\times|\) from the same counting oracle, query the doubled \(1\times1\times1\) tensor with lone entry \(1\) against itself, using literal hyperbolic padding. Its ordinary scalar stabilizer has size \(|R^\times|^2\) since the three factors have product one. Its three independent symmetric shears add \(|R|^3\). Therefore one fixed-dimensional query yields \(|R|^3|R^\times|^2\); divide by \(|R|^3\) and take the exact integer square root. With that correction evaluated, the single query of (2) recovers the ordinary transporter count. This is valid uniformly for rings such as \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\) with binary \(N\) and no prime factors supplied. The number of bits in all counts is bounded polynomially by the explicit input and output dimensions and \(\log|R|\).

## Relation to existing work

The field doubling and the concise field stabilizer formula are already present in the MathProofs symplectic work. Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun study ordinary five-action tensor comparisons over rings, including free and general-module versions. The transport correction here uses the annihilator of the actual support, extends the doubling argument to rings with torsion in those supports, counts every Lagrangian lift by a symmetric additive kernel, and combines exact free multiplication transport with hyperbolic padding to handle arbitrary base change. The result also carries the ordinary module witnesses through the earlier free-cover construction. The new proofs are informal, and priority remains unverified. [Chen–Grochow–Qiao–Tang–Zhang, classical actions](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03135v2#S1.SS5) · [Grochow–Qiao–Stange–Sun, Part V](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00735v1#S1.SS3)
