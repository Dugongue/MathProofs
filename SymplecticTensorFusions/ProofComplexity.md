# Proof complexity of symplectic tensor isomorphism

Hyperbolic padding is the missing reduction in the Lean-checked equivalence [ordinary TI equals symplectic TI](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TIEqTISp.lean). This informal note proves that it also transfers algebraic proof complexity. The reduction preserves lower bounds even for symplectic three-tensors whose intrinsic supports are maximal Lagrangian subspaces. On padded inputs with common coordinate supports, it gives quantitative comparisons in both directions.

## 1. Result and equation conventions

Fix an order \(d\ge3\). Encode ordinary tensor isomorphism of \(T,B\) using action matrices \(X_i\), inverse matrices \(Y_i\), the tensor equations \((\bigotimes_iX_i)T=B\), and both inverse equations \(X_iY_i=Y_iX_i=I\). The coefficient field is arbitrary. For a symplectic space \(H_i=V_i\oplus V_i^*\), take the standard matrix \(J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&I\\-I&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), and use the linear adjoint \(U^\sharp=J^{-1}U^TJ\). Write \(\widetilde T,\widetilde B\) for the tensors supported entirely on the primal halves. The symplectic system has the tensor action equations and both \(U_i^\sharp U_i=I\) and \(U_iU_i^\sharp=I\). This applies in characteristic two, where the same matrix defines a nonsingular alternating form. For finite-field rational-point refutations one may add the field equations to both systems.

1. On **every** ordinary input pair, a variable, zero, and one substitution maps the padded symplectic system to ordinary equations or zero. It transfers Polynomial Calculus (PC) refutations without a degree or sparse-size increase and transfers Nullstellensatz, real Sum-of-Squares (SoS), and circuit IPS certificates by substitution. The lower-bound consequence applies to the weaker symplectic encoding with just \(U^TJU=J\) as well.
2. Suppose the two ordinary tensors have the same intrinsic mode supports, and these are spanned by coordinate vectors in each mode. In particular, either tensor may be fully supported in every mode. Starting from a degree-\(D\), sparse-size-\(s\) ordinary PC proof, one obtains a padded symplectic PC proof of degree at most \(\max(D,2d)\) and sparse size at most \(s+\operatorname{poly}(N)\), where \(N\) is the dense input size. The simulation also preserves IPS size up to polynomial overhead. It increases Nullstellensatz and SoS degree by at most \(2d\). The sparse-size assertion uses a literal coordinate-support hypothesis.
3. In every characteristic there are geometric NO instances of symplectic 3-TI in side length \(2m\) requiring PC degree \(\Omega(m)\) and sparse size \(2^{\Omega(m)}\), with constants allowed to depend on the characteristic. A real family also requires SoS degree \(\Omega(m)\). In both constructions the intrinsic supports of both tensors can be the same three prescribed maximal Lagrangian subspaces, and the tensors on those supports can be chosen to encode multiplication in unital associative algebras. On every fixed finite field the sparse PC and degree claims also hold with the field equations included.

## 2. The projection giving lower bounds

For each mode set
\[
 U_i=\begin{pmatrix}X_i&0\\0&Y_i^T\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 U_i^\sharp=\begin{pmatrix}Y_i&0\\0&X_i^T\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The adjoint identity follows by matrix multiplication. The images of the symplectic identities are ordinary inverse identities, their transposes, or zero. For the presentation \(U_i^TJU_i=J\) the same identities appear with signs and transpositions. The padded tensor equation specializes to the original tensor equation on the primal block, and its other entries are identically zero. Each variable in a field identity specializes to a variable, zero, or one.

This substitution sends every PC line to a polynomial with no more terms or higher degree. Variable multiplication becomes the same rule, the constant zero, or the original line. Polynomial equations which differ by a sign can use the corresponding signed coefficient in a linear-combination rule. The two-sided convention above gives exact copies without signs. For Nullstellensatz and SoS, the substitution maps each multiplier times its axiom to an identical kind of term and preserves squares. For IPS, substitute the corresponding input variable, zero, or signed axiom placeholder. A certificate evaluating to a contradiction continues to do so, and zero placeholders still make it vanish. These statements apply directly to the cubic-action system in the [Galesi–Grochow–Pitassi–She](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2023.4) degree lower bounds. They also apply to the [MathProofs sparse-PC bound](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/tree/main/TensorSparsePCBounds), in its stated characteristic and finite-field scopes.

## 3. Extracting the ordinary certificate from the active blocks

Let \(I_i\) be the common set of active coordinate indices in mode \(i\), viewed in the primal half of \(H_i\). Write \(K_i=U_i^\sharp\). We first derive that both \(U_i\) and \(K_i\) have zero block from \(I_i\) to its full complement in \(H_i\), using only fixed-degree linear algebra. Flatten the padded tensor equation at mode \(i\). With fixed numerical flattenings \(A_i,B_i\), it is
\[
B_i=U_i A_i U_{-i}^T,\qquad U_{-i}=\bigotimes_{j\ne i}U_j.
\]
Set \(K_{-i}=\bigotimes_{j\ne i}K_j\). Multiply on the right by \(K_{-i}^T\) and use each inverse identity in the product \(U_{-i}^T K_{-i}^T=\bigotimes_{j\ne i}(K_jU_j)^T\). This derives
\[
U_i A_i=B_i K_{-i}^T.
\]
To implement the replacement explicitly, write a tensor product of matrices minus the tensor product of their identities as a telescoping sum, replacing one factor at a time. Each term is entries of one quadratic axiom multiplied by products of the other entries. All intermediates have degree at most \(2d-1\). Likewise multiplication of the padded equation on the left by \(K_i\) and cancellation of \(K_iU_i\) gives \(K_iB_i=A_iU_{-i}^T\) in degree at most \(d+1\).

The rows of each numerical flattening on \(I_i\) have full row rank by definition of the intrinsic mode support. Outside \(I_i\), their rows vanish. Multiply the outside rows of the first identity by a constant right inverse of the active rows of \(A_i\), and those of the second identity by the corresponding right inverse for \(B_i\). This derives every sought outside-to-active matrix entry as a degree-one line. Empty active sets produce no entries to derive. Project a formal ordinary pair of inverse matrices to the block-diagonal matrices with active blocks \((U_i)_{I_i,I_i}\), \((K_i)_{I_i,I_i}\) and identity on the remaining original coordinates. The two projected inverse equations follow by taking active blocks of the symplectic identities and removing the cross terms using the degree-one lines. The projected tensor equation is exactly the indicated active block of the padded equation, and its other entries vanish identically. Field equations for the active blocks are original field equations because the upper-left block of \(K_i\) is the transpose of the lower-right block of \(U_i\).

The preparation uses polynomially many expanded monomials for fixed \(d\). Each substitution in the ordinary proof is by a variable or constant, so translated lines add no degree or size. The degree bound for the full preparation and reuse is \(2d\). The same explicit telescoping and right-inverse operations give for every projected input equation an expression as a sum of polynomial multiples of the symplectic equations, with degree at most \(2d\) in the geometric system. The same bound applies to the geometric input equations with field equations present, and each projected field equation is already an axiom or zero. Substitution into a Nullstellensatz or SoS certificate therefore adds at most \(2d\) to the maximum degree. Substituting the corresponding linear axiom-placeholder circuits in an IPS proof incurs only polynomial size. This establishes the two-way quantitative result.

## 4. Hardness on maximal Lagrangian supports

To remove rank-deficiency from the ordinary core of the hard instances, first send each ordinary three-tensor to a concise multiplication tensor. Write the original coefficient array as a bilinear map \(t:V_1\times V_2\to V_3\), interpreting the first two slots contragrediently. Let \(n=\max_i\dim V_i\). Make a unital associative algebra \(A(t)\) with six specified diagonal idempotents \(e_1,e_2,e_3,f_1,f_2,f_3\) adding to one. Each diagonal corner is the ground field. The only nonzero off-diagonal corners are
\[
 e_1 A e_2=V_1,\quad e_2 A e_3=V_2,\quad e_1 A e_3=V_3,\quad
 f_i A e_i=k^{n+i}\ (i=1,2,3).
\]
The product of two elements of off-diagonal corners vanishes except for the designated multiplication from \(V_1\times V_2\) to \(V_3\), where it is \(t\). Each diagonal idempotent acts by the indicated endpoints. Every triple of off-diagonal elements has both bracketings zero because no designated product involves \(V_3\) as an input. Products involving some diagonal entries obey associativity by the endpoint rule. This proves associativity and the unit claim. The dimension is \(O(n)\), with dimension \(6n+12\) when the original dimensions are all \(n\).

Let \(J_0\) be the off-diagonal ideal. It is the ideal generated by commutators, since the diagonal quotient is commutative and every off-diagonal element \(x\) satisfies \([e,x]=x\) for its source idempotent \(e\). Its cube is zero. An algebra isomorphism therefore permutes the primitive coordinate idempotents in \(A/J_0\cong k^6\). The permutation also acts on a chosen set of actual idempotents up to one conjugation. In detail, if \(p_i,q_i\) are the two aligned sets of lifted idempotents with matching residues, then \(u=\sum_i q_ip_i\) is one modulo \(J_0\), hence a unit, and \(q_i u=u p_i\). After this conjugation, corner dimensions distinguish each private arrow and its endpoints because their ranks are exactly \(n+1,n+2,n+3\), while every data corner has rank at most \(n\). The algebra isomorphism now acts separately on \(V_1,V_2,V_3\), and the only data-bearing products give exactly an ordinary tensor isomorphism. Conversely such a triple extends by identities on the diagonal and private corners. This uses the weighted path-algebra method of [Grochow–Qiao within tensor-isomorphism completeness](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.00309), in a constant-vertex form.

For unital associative algebras, an isotopy \(P(x)Q(y)=R(xy)\) gives an isomorphism. Put \(a=P(1),b=Q(1)\). Evaluating at the unit gives \(P(x)b=R(x)=aQ(x)\). Right multiplication by \(b\) and left multiplication by \(a\) are bijective, so \(a,b\) are units. The equal maps \(a^{-1}P(x)=Q(x)b^{-1}\) now define the required algebra isomorphism, directly by multiplying the displayed equation on the left by \(a^{-1}\) and on the right by \(b^{-1}\). Thus taking the multiplication tensor of \(A(t)\) preserves and reflects ordinary TI, including after any field extension. Its first and second supports are full because the corresponding partial multiplication maps are injective by evaluation at the unit. Its third support is full because the image of multiplication contains the entire algebra.

There is also a proof-complexity projection from the multiplication-tensor equations to the original ordinary equations. Let \(P\) act blockwise by \(Y_1^T,Y_2^T,X_3\) on the three data corners and by identity elsewhere. Let the formal inverse \(Q\) act by \(X_1^T,X_2^T,Y_3\) there, and identity elsewhere. The multiplication-tensor action uses \(Q^T,Q^T,P\) and its inverse equations use the inverse triple \(P^T,P^T,Q\). Under these substitutions, data-product equations are original tensor equations, the off-diagonal zero products give zero, and products with one diagonal corner give inverse equations since the diagonal idempotent is fixed. Both-diagonal products are fixed. All substitutions again send variables to variables or constants 0 and 1. Composing this projection with the symplectic one in Section 2 transfers both degree and sparse-size lower bounds directly. The output now has in every mode exactly the primal Lagrangian as its intrinsic support. Neither the upper-bound simulations nor the lower-bound transfer require evaluating these algebraic certificates as algorithms for unrestricted inputs.

One can obtain any sufficiently large prescribed even side length by taking an input lower-bound family at dimension linear in that side length and adding isolated copies of the base algebra \(k\) to each product algebra before hyperbolic padding. The new primitive idempotents in the commutator quotient have no marked corners, so they do not interfere with recovery. The new multiplication equations again project to zero by identity extension. This proves the asymptotic lower bounds with arbitrary large even side lengths. The result concerns explicit ordinary and symplectic equation systems, rather than an encoding-independent lower bound.
