# A sharp symplectic orbit-closure threshold and degeneration transfer

Two informal results build on the hyperbolic padding in the [Lean proof that ordinary and symplectic tensor isomorphism have the same degree](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TIEqTISp.lean).

**Orbit-closure intersection.** Over the complex numbers, orbit-closure intersection for a single ordered tensor under independent symplectic changes in its physical modes is TOCI-complete at every fixed order at least three. Orders one and two admit deterministic polynomial-time algorithms. At order three the reduction from \(\operatorname{End}(U)\otimes\operatorname{End}(V)\) under \(\operatorname{GL}(U)\times\operatorname{GL}(V)\) is affine with rational constants and with every output dimension \(O(\max(\dim U,\dim V)^2)\).

**Orbit-closure inclusion.** Over every algebraically closed field, including characteristic two, the same linear hyperbolic padding as in the TI proof preserves and reflects every ordinary tensor degeneration. The ordinary orbit closure is exactly the linear section of the symplectic orbit closure as a scheme. Its graded Betti groups split into the corresponding symplectic groups, and the minimum separating equation degree is preserved. There is also an explicit projective birational model of the symplectic closure with fibers that are products of isotropic Grassmannians.

## 1. TOCI-completeness in one three-tensor

Use the [single bipartite operator theorem](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/BalancedQuarticOCI/PRODUCT-GROUP.md) as the source. This is the TOCI-complete action of \(\operatorname{GL}(U)\times\operatorname{GL}(V)\) on \(U\otimes U^*\otimes V\otimes V^*\). That proof starts from the two-operator problem of Lysikov–Walter Theorem V.12(2), and packs the two operators using closed conjugation tags and trace-zero payloads. Here we give the next reduction to a pure three-factor tensor. All groups and varieties in this section are complex. By the general linear first fundamental theorem, the source invariants are spanned by contractions of finitely many copies of the source operator, pairing all \(U\)-ports with \(U^*\)-ports and all \(V\)-ports with \(V^*\)-ports. Equivalently, a contraction of \(a\) copies is specified by two permutations of \(a\) indices. Equality of all invariant values is equivalent to intersection of orbit closures. [Lysikov–Walter, Theorem II.7 and Section IV-C](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04639v2)

### A fixed colored graph

Take twelve vertices arranged in alternating color-1/color-2 cycles of lengths two, four, and six. Their labels are \(A,B\), \(C_0,C_1,C_2,C_3\), and \(D_0,\dots,D_5\), respectively. The length-two cycle has two parallel edges of different colors. Complete these cycles to a properly three-edge-colored cubic multigraph \(\mathcal D\) by the color-3 matching

\[
 AC_0,\quad BD_0,\quad C_1D_1,\quad C_2C_3,\quad D_2D_3,\quad D_4D_5.
\]

Cut the edges \(C_2C_3\) and \(D_2D_3\), retaining their four dangling ports. Write \(\mathcal D^\circ\) for the remaining graph with dangling ports. Its internal graph is connected because \(AC_0\) connects the length-two cycle to length four and \(BD_0\) connects it to length six. The following uniqueness property will fix every internal role in the contraction. Write \(w\) for the walk of colors 1,2 in that order. In \(\mathcal D\) the only vertices at which \(w\) returns are \(A,B\). The walk of colors \(3,w,w,3\) returns at \(A\) because its color-3 neighbor is on the length-four cycle, and fails to return at \(B\) because its neighbor is on the length-six cycle. Both returning walks at \(A\) use only edges of \(\mathcal D^\circ\). Thus in any map taking internal edges to same-colored edges of \(\mathcal D\), the vertex \(A\) must be sent to \(A\). Every vertex is connected to it by internal edges, and in \(\mathcal D\) each vertex has exactly one edge of each color. Therefore every other vertex must also be sent to its own label. This argument applies separately to each copy even after its dangling ports are reconnected to other copies.

### One output tensor

Assign \(U,U^*\) to the two ends of the first cut edge and \(V,V^*\) to the two ends of the second. Choose a spanning tree in the internal graph of \(\mathcal D^\circ\). Give each tree vertex weight equal to its number of dangling ports, and root the tree at a weighted centroid. Such a vertex exists by walking into a component of weight greater than half whenever there is one. After a move the component behind has weight less than half, so a finite tree admits no infinite sequence of such moves. Each child subtree therefore has at most two dangling ports.

For a tree edge \(e\) to a child subtree, let \(L_e\) be the tensor product of the free-port spaces in that subtree, in a fixed order. Its two endpoints are assigned \(L_e\) on the parent side and \(L_e^*\) on the child side. An empty product is a trivial line. For each internal nontree edge, assign two dual trivial lines, choosing their order once. The cut edges already have paired assignments. Form the target space \(W_i\) for physical mode \(i\) by taking the direct sum of one hyperbolic space \(L_e\oplus L_e^*\) for each edge of color \(i\). Use the alternating form that pairs its two summands by evaluation, with the opposite sign in the reverse direction. In particular the summands at different edges are perpendicular. The action of \(\operatorname{GL}(U)\times\operatorname{GL}(V)\) on each \(L_e\) has the canonical contragredient action on \(L_e^*\), so the diagonal action on the direct sum is symplectic.

For each vertex, its three ports select one summand in each of \(W_1,W_2,W_3\). At the tree root put the source tensor \(X\), regrouped as the tensor product of the child lanes and the free-port lanes of the root. At each nonroot vertex put the identity tensor between its parent dual lane and the product of its own free-port lanes and child lanes. This product is the parent lane by construction and the choice of reordering. At every nontree internal port append the unit of its trivial line. The resulting vertex tensors occupy their respective three-port subspaces of \(W_1\otimes W_2\otimes W_3\). Define \(F(X)\) to be their sum. Only the root term depends on \(X\), and the other terms are fixed identity tensors. Consequently the map is affine and is equivariant for the indicated homomorphism to \(\operatorname{Sp}(W_1)\times\operatorname{Sp}(W_2)\times\operatorname{Sp}(W_3)\). It preserves intersection of orbit closures. The largest lane dimension is at most \(\max(\dim U,\dim V)^2\), since there are at most two factors in each tree lane. There are six edges of each color, so this also bounds each target side up to an absolute constant. The map uses just input entries and rational constants and is computable in polynomial bit time on dense inputs.

### Pulling back every source contraction

Fix a source contraction of \(a\) operator copies, given by permutations \(\sigma,\tau\) for \(U,V\). Take \(a\) copies of \(\mathcal D^\circ\). Connect the \(U\)-port of copy \(j\) to the \(U^*\)-port of copy \(\sigma(j)\), and the \(V\)-port of copy \(j\) to the \(V^*\)-port of copy \(\tau(j)\). This makes a complete contraction diagram on \(12a\) copies of a target three-tensor. Use the symplectic pairing on every edge of the indicated physical color, with its direction chosen once. This defines a polynomial invariant for the target action.

Expanding the target contraction at \(F(X)\) amounts to assigning one of the twelve vertex tensors to each vertex position of the diagram. A term can be nonzero only if the two summands across every edge are paired. For an edge of a specified color, their base roles therefore lie at the endpoints of the corresponding edge of \(\mathcal D\). These assignments induce maps preserving edge colors on every internal copy of \(\mathcal D^\circ\), and the uniqueness proof above forces the original label at every position. Hence only the single intended assignment can contribute. Contracting its identity tensors along the tree recovers exactly \(X\) on the four dangling ports of each copy, up to the fixed product of pairing signs. All internal nontree lines contribute only signs. The rewired edges then evaluate exactly the selected source contraction, again up to a fixed sign. Thus every source contraction is, up to sign, the pullback of a symplectic invariant. In particular if target closures meet, the source invariant values agree and the source closures meet. This proves TOCI-hardness. Membership follows from the symplectic-to-general-linear tensor-tuple reduction of Lysikov–Walter Theorem V.25. The degree of the simulated target contraction is twelve times the degree of the source contraction.

## 2. The order threshold

At order one all orbit closures contain zero: any nonzero vector lies on an isotropic line that extends to a hyperbolic pair, and a symplectic one-parameter group scales the vector to zero. At order two write a tensor as a matrix \(M\) and choose symplectic form matrices \(J_1,J_2\). A spanning set of invariants is given by contractions with the two forms, by the symplectic invariant-tensor theorem. Every diagram is the union of alternating two-colored cycles, allowing a length-two cycle with parallel edges. A cycle with \(2b\) vertices is a trace of \((MJ_2M^{\mathsf T}J_1)^b\) up to an overall sign: traverse the cycle, multiplying alternately by \(M\) and \(M^{\mathsf T}\) and contracting with \(J_1,J_2\). Reversing any edge pairing changes only a sign. Each such trace is itself a contraction, and under independent symplectic actions the matrix inside it is conjugated in mode one. In characteristic zero traces of all powers are determined by the characteristic polynomial and determine it by Newton identities. Exact rational characteristic-polynomial computation therefore decides the order-two problem in polynomial bit time. [Lysikov–Walter, Sections IV-C and V-B](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04639v2)

For every fixed order \(s>3\), give \(\mathcal D\) one new color per extra physical mode, each a copy of its color-1 perfect matching. All edges in new colors are internal and receive hyperbolic pairs of trivial lines. Append the chosen basis vector of the assigned line to the vertex tensor in that mode. In the invariant diagram on the \(a\) copies, contract these extra edges internally. Their contractions contribute signs, and the first three colors still force exactly the original labelling. The entire argument for membership, hardness, and bit complexity therefore holds at every such order.

## 3. Hyperbolic padding and the scheme-theoretic ordinary section

For the remaining sections let \(k\) be any algebraically closed field. Fix finite-dimensional based spaces \(V_i\), let \(W_i=V_i\oplus V_i^*\) with its hyperbolic alternating form, and put \(V=\bigotimes_iV_i\), \(W=\bigotimes_iW_i\). Let \(j:V\to W\) and \(p:W\to V\) be the tensor products of the primal inclusions and projections. For any \(T\in V\), write \(X\) for its ordinary orbit closure in \(V\) and \(Y\) for the symplectic orbit closure of \(j(T)\) in \(W\). Closures carry their reduced structures.

The usual block-diagonal hyperbolic lift gives \(j(X)\subseteq Y\). For any symplectic tuple \((g_i)_i\), the primal projection of its action on \(j(T)\) is \(\bigotimes_i(p_i g_i j_i)T\), an image of \(T\) under a tuple of endomorphisms of \(V_i\). The latter images all lie in \(X\): the map from the affine product of endomorphism spaces given by applying the tuple to \(T\) is polynomial, and its domain's invertible tuples form a dense open subset. Hence \(p(Y)\subseteq X\). Since \(pj=1\), this proves reflection of every degeneration as well as preservation. It also proves the ideal equalities and inclusions

\[
p^*I(X)\subseteq I(Y),\qquad j^*I(Y)=I(X).
\]

For the equality, inclusion from left to right follows by evaluation on \(j(X)\), and any \(f\in I(X)\) is the restriction of \(p^*f\). Thus the scheme obtained by intersecting \(Y\) with the primal linear subspace \(j(V)\) is exactly \(j(X)\). The two pullbacks induce a coordinate-ring inclusion and retraction.

Both closures are cones, since the ordinary scalar action and its hyperbolic lift scale the padded tensor by every nonzero scalar. Hence the induced inclusions and retractions are graded in the ambient polynomial rings and the quotient coordinate rings. Write \(R=k[V]\), \(S=k[W]\). The Koszul complexes

\[
k[X]\otimes\bigwedge V^*,\qquad k[Y]\otimes\bigwedge W^*
\]

compute \(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_*(k[X],k)\) and \(\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_*(k[Y],k)\) respectively. Extend \(p^*\) and \(j^*\) to the exterior factors to obtain chain maps with composite the identity. These are chain maps because their maps on polynomial coordinates respect multiplication and give the same maps on the linear generators in the differentials. They preserve homological and internal degree. Therefore each ordinary graded Betti group is a vector-space direct summand of the corresponding symplectic group. In particular every lower bound on the number of minimal homogeneous equations or higher syzygies transfers degree by degree. Also, for \(T'\notin X\), the least degree of a polynomial vanishing on \(X\) but nonzero at \(T'\) equals the least such degree for \(Y,j(T')\). Pullback in either direction preserves nonvanishing at the designated point and does not increase degree.

## 4. A projective model with exact fibers

For each mode let \(U_i\subseteq V_i\) be the image of the mode flattening of \(T\). Write \(r_i=\dim U_i\), \(n_i=\dim V_i\), and let \(X_r\) be the ordinary orbit closure of \(T\) in \(\bigotimes_iU_i\). Let \(B=\prod_i\operatorname{IG}(r_i,W_i)\) be the product of isotropic Grassmannians. Each \(E=(E_i)_i\in B\) has an intrinsic copy \(X_r(E)\) of the variety \(X_r\) in \(\bigotimes_iE_i\), obtained by any collection of linear identifications \(U_i\simeq E_i\). It is independent of the identifications because \(X_r\) is stable under \(\prod_i\operatorname{GL}(U_i)\).

The relative subvariety \(\{(E,Z):E\in B,\ Z\in X_r(E)\}\) is closed in \(B\times W\). Indeed \(\bigotimes_i E_i\) is the tautological tensor subbundle of the trivial bundle with fiber \(W\). On any frame chart of the tautological bundles, within that subbundle the relative subvariety is the product of the chart and the closed variety \(X_r\). These conditions agree on chart overlaps. The projection to \(W\) is projective. Its image is \(Y\). To see this, every symplectic orbit point belongs to the image, which is closed. Conversely, for every isotropic \(E_i\) any isomorphism \(U_i\simeq E_i\) extends to a symplectic ambient isomorphism. Extend a basis on each isotropic side to symplectic bases and send one to the other. Thus the embedded orbit of \(T\) in \(X_r(E)\) lies in the symplectic orbit, and its closure lies in \(Y\).

Take \(Z\in Y\) and let \(L_i\) be its flattening image in mode \(i\), of dimension \(s_i\). The choices in the fiber of the model are exactly the isotropic \(E_i\) of dimension \(r_i\) containing \(L_i\). Necessity follows from \(Z\in\bigotimes_i E_i\). One fiber point exists by the preceding image assertion. Any other tuple of planes containing the respective \(L_i\) is reached by linear isomorphisms from that first tuple acting as the identity on \(L_i\), so the tensor \(Z\) still lies in its intrinsic orbit closure. The fiber is therefore

\[
\prod_i \operatorname{IG}(r_i-s_i,L_i^{\perp}/L_i).
\]

This description also holds scheme-theoretically. The condition that a fixed tensor \(Z\) belong to the tautological tensor subbundle is the condition that its flattening images lie in the tautological subbundles in every mode. This can be verified over arbitrary (k\)-algebras by working on charts splitting each tautological subbundle as a direct summand and decomposing the tensor into the corresponding blocks. Since the flattening images of a constant tensor commute with base change, this gives precisely the displayed incidence scheme. It is smooth and reduced as a product of isotropic Grassmannians for nondegenerate symplectic quotient spaces. The additional equations of the relative orbit closure vanish at every closed point of that scheme by the argument above, and therefore vanish identically on it.

On the open stratum where all \(s_i=r_i\), each plane \(E_i\) is recovered as the corresponding flattening image of \(Z\). On a constant-rank chart that image map to the Grassmannian is regular, using minors for its Plücker coordinates. This supplies a regular inverse on the open stratum. The model is birational and gives there a local product of the ordinary intrinsic orbit closure with a smooth Grassmannian chart. The analogous ordinary construction over \(\prod_i\operatorname{Gr}(r_i,V_i)\) gives a local product on the ordinary maximal-support stratum. Thus at corresponding maximal-support points the ordinary and symplectic closures have the same local singularity up to adjoining smooth factors. In particular smoothness at such points is equivalent. The dimension of the symplectic closure is \(\dim X_r+\sum_i\dim\operatorname{IG}(r_i,W_i)\).

The incidence model extends the standard Grassmannian construction for tensor subspace varieties to individual hyperbolically padded orbits. The scheme retraction and the graded syzygy transfer apply to all support strata. Earlier MathProofs work transfers degenerations to matrix subalgebras under symplectic conjugacy. Here the geometry lives in the single tensor representation of a product of symplectic groups. The TOCI threshold uses the independent product action. The single-group symplectic completeness statements in Lysikov–Walter Theorem V.25 concern two cubic slots or one order-seven slot. The related Holant completeness theorem of Cai and Young, Corollary 40, uses two ternary signatures and a binary signature.
