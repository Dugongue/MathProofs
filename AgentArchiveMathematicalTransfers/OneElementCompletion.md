# One-element completion in noncyclic wreath products

In [Question 8.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00133#S8), Collins, Grochow, Levet and Weiß ask for the complexity of minimum generation of finite semigroups given by their multiplication tables, including whether it is NP-complete. The earlier [reduction to one-element completion](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorIsomorphismAndAlgebraicCrossovers/Groups.md) leaves a local problem on composite nonabelian chief factors with noncyclic outer action. We prove two sufficient conditions for polynomial-time search at that step. One covers an infinite family of imprimitive wreath products. The other allows arbitrary outer actions and imposes a condition on the marked subgroups. The unrestricted published question remains open.

The AgentAlphaXiv study on step-decomposed exact verification distinguishes advancing from the current accepted state from substituting a reference outcome. Here the state is the generating tuple in the chief quotient. The new proof establishes when an exact test for advancing can finish by inspecting a single coset. The mathematical proof is independent of the study's empirical results. [AgentAlphaXiv manuscript, version 2](https://huggingface.co/datasets/alphaXiv/AgentAlphaXiv/resolve/a63f46066ac16fcb470f41933415e175825b6bcc/papers/01a1b00f-3a81-791c-98fd-c5daefe85ff8.pdf)

## Local setting and completion criteria

Let $T$ be a finite nonabelian simple group and let $A=T^\Omega$, with $|\Omega|\ge2$. Let $A\le L\le\operatorname{Aut}(T)\wr\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ act transitively on the factors of $A$, where $A$ is identified with its inner automorphism group. Write $P$ for the induced permutation group on $\Omega$. Let $K_1,\ldots,K_c\le L$ be marked subgroups and let $Ay_0$ be a prescribed coset with
\[
\langle A,K_1,\ldots,K_c,y_0\rangle=L.
\]

Each of the following conditions implies that some $y\in Ay_0$ satisfies $\langle K_1,\ldots,K_c,y\rangle=L$. In each case the conclusion also holds after arbitrary independent conjugations of the $K_i$ by elements of $A$.

1. For every distinct $u,v\in\Omega$, there exist $p\in P$, an index $i$, and $h\in K_i\cap A$ such that the coordinates $h_{pu}$ and $h_{pv}$ have different orders.
2. In suitable factor identifications, $L=T^\Omega\rtimes P$ with a pure permutation action and $P$ is noncyclic abelian. More generally, in a split pure permutation action, it suffices that some marked element $h$ has permutation $q$ commuting with $p=\pi(y_0)$ and displacing a $p$-cycle of length $\operatorname{ord}(p)$ to a disjoint cycle.

Condition 1 includes any instance with a nonidentity marked element supported on one sheet. Condition 2 includes $T^\Omega\rtimes(C_2)^d$ with $d\ge2$ and the regular action on $|\Omega|=2^d$ sheets.

## Subdirect products and the first criterion

For a candidate $y$, put $J=\langle K_1,\ldots,K_c,y\rangle$ and $D=J\cap A$. The projection of $J$ modulo $A$ is all of $L/A$, so $J$ induces $P$ on the factors. If $D$ projects onto one copy of $T$, transitivity and normality of $D$ in $J$ show that it projects onto every copy.

A subdirect subgroup of $T^\Omega$ is a product of full twisted diagonal groups, one for each block of a partition of $\Omega$. Induct on the number of copies and apply Goursat's lemma to $T^b\times T$, where $T^b$ is the product of diagonal groups obtained thus far. Either the last factor is independent or an epimorphism $T^b\to T$ links it to an earlier factor. Such an epimorphism factors through one copy since the factor images are commuting normal subgroups of $T$. The partition is intrinsic: two distinct coordinates are in the same block exactly when their joint image is the graph of an isomorphism. For $D=J\cap A$ the partition is $P$-invariant, and coordinates in the same block have equal orders in every element of $D$. A discrete partition implies $D=A$, hence $J=L$.

Under condition 1 take $h\in K_i\cap A$ with a nontrivial coordinate $a$ at a sheet $u$. Let $\ell$ be the length of the cycle of $\pi(y_0)$ through $u$. As $y$ varies in $Ay_0$, the action of $y^\ell$ on the copy at $u$ ranges over a prescribed coset of $\operatorname{Inn}(T)$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(T)$. The transport along each cycle edge can be changed independently by an inner automorphism at its destination. Varying one edge while fixing all the others makes the cycle composite range over the full coset.

The prescribed-coset generation theorem of Burness, Guralnick and Harper gives a value for that composite which, together with $c_a$, generates a subgroup containing $\operatorname{Inn}(T)$. See the existence statement for all nonabelian simple $T$ in Section 1 of Fulman–Garzoni–Guralnick. [Prescribed-coset theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17291#S1)

Choose $y$ accordingly. The image of the sheet stabilizer $J_u$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(T)$ contains $\operatorname{Inn}(T)$. Since $D\lhd J$, its local image $D_u\le T$ is normalized by $\operatorname{Inn}(T)$ and contains $a\ne1$. Simplicity gives $D_u=T$, so $D$ is subdirect. If $u,v$ shared a diagonal block, so would $pu,pv$ for every $p\in P$. Every $h$ used in condition 1 belongs to $D$, so different orders contradict that block. Thus $D=A$ and $J=L$.

Condition 1 is unchanged even by independent $L$-conjugation of the marked subgroups. Such conjugation preserves each coordinate order and permutes the profiles by a single element of $P$. Taking all $P$-translates leaves the collection unchanged.

## A commutator that separates sheets

For condition 2 fix the marked subgroups arbitrarily and put $p=\pi(y_0)$ for the projection $\pi\colon L\to P$. A faithful transitive abelian action is regular because all point stabilizers coincide with the action kernel. Thus every cycle of $p$ has length $m=\operatorname{ord}(p)$. As $P$ is noncyclic and generated by the marked images and $p$, some marked $h$ projects to $q\notin\langle p\rangle$. In the regular action, $q$ commutes with $p$ and moves a $p$-cycle disjointly to another. The following argument uses just that general criterion for the split action.

Choose $u$ on an $m$-cycle $C$ and write $C'=qC$, disjoint from $C$. List their points as $v_j=p^ju$ and $w_j=qv_j=p^jqu$ with indices modulo $m$. Every transport is conjugation by a unique element of $T$. Let $r_j$ be the weight of the $h$ transport $v_j\to w_j$. Choose a generating pair $a,b$ of $T$ where $a$ is an involution and $b$ has odd prime order $s$. King gives an involution and a prime-order generator, and a group generated by two involutions is dihedral and cannot be nonabelian simple. [King's generation theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.04717)

Choose the weights $x_j$ of $y$ along $C$ with ordered product $x_{m-1}\cdots x_0=a$. Along $C'$ prescribe weights $z_j=r_{j+1}x_jr_j^{-1}$ for $0\le j<m-1$. This makes the $hy$ and $yh$ transports from $v_j$ equal for those $j$. Choose the last weight $z_{m-1}$ to give $z_{m-1}\cdots z_0=r_0br_0^{-1}$. Set all weights outside $C$ and $C'$ to $1$. Every assignment of inner weights is available in the coset over $p$. At $v_{m-1}$ the two transports differ, since otherwise telescoping across the cycle would give $z_{m-1}\cdots z_0=r_0ar_0^{-1}$, impossible by the orders of $a,b$.

The elements $y^m$ and $h^{-1}y^mh$ both lie in $D$ and have coordinates $a,b$ at $u$. Hence $D$ projects onto $T$ at $u$ and is subdirect. Each coordinate of $y^m$ along $C$ has order two, and each along $C'$ has order $s$, since cycle products at different starts are conjugate. Elsewhere the coordinates are the identity, even on cycles shorter than $m$. It follows that $y^{ms}$ has support exactly $C$. Any diagonal block meeting $C$ is therefore contained in $C$.

The element $(yh)^{-1}(hy)$ belongs to $D$ because $p,q$ commute. Its action at a source is the ratio of the transports of $hy$ and $yh$ from that source to their common target. Among the points of $C$ the element has a nonidentity coordinate precisely at $v_{m-1}$. Thus the block of $v_{m-1}$, already contained in $C$, is a singleton. Transitivity makes every block a singleton, so $J=L$. This also covers $m=1$, when no preliminary square equations are imposed.

## From the completion test to a Cayley-table algorithm

At a chief step $N\lhd G_0$ with nonabelian minimal normal subgroup $N$, form $L=G_0/C_{G_0}(N)$ whose socle $A$ identifies with $N$. A generating tuple in $G_0/N$ for the mixed problem provides the marked subgroups and prescribed one-element coset in $L$. There are at most $|N|$ lifts for each slot. Tag a marked option by the ordered list of images in $L$ of the original subgroup elements in that slot and deduplicate within the slot, retaining one lift per list. Tag the free options by their values in $L$.

Two prior lifting facts give the assembly. [Earlier one-element reduction](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TensorIsomorphismAndAlgebraicCrossovers/Groups.md) · [Lucchini–Thakkar on chief and crown lifting](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.07633)

- The number of generating lifts is independent of the chosen generating tuple in $G_0/N$. For any supplement $W$ of $N$, left multiplication by appropriate elements of $W$ bijects the allowed conjugator choices for two quotient tuples, and there are always $|W\cap N|$ choices per free slot. Thus the count of lifts contained in $W$ is independent. Subtract counts for proper supplements contained in $W$, in order of subgroup size, to obtain the same statement for the number generating exactly $W$.
- At most $|N|^3\lfloor\log_2 |G_0|\rfloor$ distinct tagged tuples which generate $L$ can fail to generate $G_0$. The associated crown quotient is a fiber power over $L/A$. All earlier coordinate lists are fixed. A generating last coordinate can fail only when its tagged list is an automorphic image of one of the fixed earlier lists under an automorphism inducing the identity on $L/A$. Indeed Goursat's lemma makes such failure the graph of a surjection from the earlier crown to the last $L$. Exactly one of the earlier minimal normal coordinate factors can map onto the last $A$, since those factors commute and $A$ is nonabelian. The graph map therefore comes from the corresponding projection and an automorphism of $L$. There are at most $\lfloor\log_2|G_0|\rfloor$ earlier coordinates. For the automorphism bound, restriction to $A$ is injective. Its permutation of the factors centralizes the transitive $L$ action and has at most $|\Omega|$ possibilities. The factor map on one copy has at most $|T|^2$ possibilities by two-generation. Its outer values on the other copies are then forced by transport, with at most $|T|$ inner choices for each. This bounds the total by $|\Omega||T|^{|\Omega|+1}\le |N|^3$. Representatives of a tagged tuple give the same crown verdict since the earlier coordinate lists are fixed.

Both new criteria guarantee that every complete choice of marked blocks extends to a locally generating list. Enumerate choices of the marked blocks and scan the at most $|N|$ free values for each choice. The delay between locally generating lists is polynomial, including the first and last delays. Test each recovered candidate in $G_0$, stopping once a lift succeeds or the enumeration exhausts itself. The crown bound implies success if more lists occur than the bound, and exhaustion decides the fixed quotient tuple. The first lifting fact extends exhaustion to every quotient tuple.

The criteria are testable in polynomial time from the table. Find the factors of $A$ as its minimal nontrivial normal subgroups by testing normal closures within $A$. Read coordinates by tabulating all $|A|$ products and read $P$ by scanning its induced permutations. Compare coordinate orders and their translates explicitly. To recognize a pure permutation action, choose factor identifications by transporting a root copy. Every factor map becomes inner precisely when the root-factor stabilizer acts through inner automorphisms, a condition that can be checked by scanning the root copy of $T$. In that case $L$ embeds in the split semidirect product, contains the full base, and maps onto $P$, giving equality. Abelianity of $P$ is a table comparison.

There are logarithmically many chief steps. Combining these criteria with the earlier abelian, simple, bounded-order, cyclic-outer and primitive split steps gives a deterministic polynomial-time search algorithm whenever those steps cover every required chief layer. The prior semigroup reduction applies that algorithm to the local groups in the regular classes and produces a minimum generating set in polynomial time under the same conditions. [Earlier primitive split case](https://github.com/Dugongue/MathProofs/blob/main/TopologyAndInvariantCrossovers/Combinatorial.md)

## Scope

The commutator gives one nonidentity coordinate inside a cycle after a power has confined its diagonal block to that cycle. The order-profile criterion supplies another completion test for arbitrary outer actions. The results cover unbounded simple factors and unboundedly many copies. Composite layers outside these and the earlier criteria still leave general Question 8.3 open. The proof is informal.
